Clinical outcomes with high dose cytarabine and idarubicin consolidation for adult AML patients
Authors: Bingxin Qian, Guiqin Huang, Xiaoya Cai, Ying Liu, Dengju Li, Jin Yin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
High-dose cytarabine has been the frontline therapy for the consolidation treatment of acute myeloid leukemia for many years. Current guidelines suggest that the combination of anthracyclines with high-dose cytarabine can be considered an option for consolidation therapy in certain patients. However, the research in this area is limited. This study aimed to investigate whether consolidation therapy with high doses of cytarabine in combination with idarubicin could result in better long-term survival. A total of 102 patients who achieved complete response and underwent consolidated treatment more than twice were included in the analysis. In this study, 49 patients received high-dose cytarabine alone for consolidation chemotherapy (HDAC group), and 53 patients received idarubicin in combination with high-dose cytarabine for consolidation chemotherapy (IHDAC group). The use of idarubicin in combination with high-dose cytarabine in consolidation therapy did not lead to a better long-term prognosis in patients than high-dose cytarabine alone. Patients treated with idarubicin required a significantly longer time to recover from agranulocytosis during hospitalization and had a higher incidence of infections.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00277-025-06551-3.

Introduction

Acute myeloid leukemia (AML) is a malignant disease characterized by abnormal proliferation of clones in the hematopoietic system. Currently, the overall 5-year survival rate is 30.5% [1]. Although approximately 70% of patients achieve induced remission, roughly 40% of those patients experience relapse within 5 years [2, 3]. Therefore, consolidation therapy is crucial for maintaining long-term relief. Currently, multiple cycles of high-dose cytarabine are the most common consolidation therapy [4]. However, the 5-year survival rate of patients who continue to receive high-dose cytarabine as a consolidation regimen remains at approximately 40% [5]. Thus, the development of a superior consolidation strategy for AML requires further research.

Although the 2024 National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines recommend the use of cytarabine (1 ~ 1.5 g/m2 every 12 h for 4 days) in combination with daunorubicin as a consolidation regimen for patients with a favorable or intermediate prognosis of AML [6], there is still some controversy regarding the dose of cytarabine, the number of cycles to be used, and whether it should be combined with other chemotherapeutic agents [7]. Moreover, the therapeutic potential of high-dose cytarabine combined with anthracyclines during the consolidation phase for acute myeloid leukemia has not been adequately investigated.

To assess whether consolidation therapy comprising high-dose cytarabine and idarubicin confers more benefits than high-dose cytarabine alone, this study examined the clinical outcomes of newly diagnosed AML patients who attained complete remission following induction therapy at our center from January 1, 2019, to December 31, 2022.

Methods

Patients

Our study retrospectively investigated the treatment information of 558 newly diagnosed AML patients (excluding patients with acute promyelocytic leukemia [APL]) from our center between January 1, 2019, and December 31, 2022 and followed them up until June 30, 2023. The patients were selected based on standardized inclusion and exclusion criteria. The primary inclusion criteria were adult patients with newly diagnosed AML with no evidence of severe cardiovascular, pulmonary, neurologic, or metabolic disorders or uncontrolled infections and with adequate liver and renal function tests. Patients with secondary AML and APL, as well as those who could not achieve morphological complete remission (CR) after induction, were excluded. Patients who received high-dose cytarabine (HDAC) alone or in combination with anthracyclines during consolidation therapy were included. Finally, 102 patients were included in this study. There were 49 patients receiving HDAC alone in consolidation chemotherapy (HDAC group), and 53 patients were treated with idarubicin in combination with HDAC (IHDAC group). Treatment

All patients received at least two consolidation chemotherapy treatments after remission induction. The HDAC group consisted of patients who received high-dose cytarabine (1.5–3 g/m2 every 12 h for 3 days) for two cycles, and the IHDAC group received high-dose cytarabine (1.5–3 g/m2 every 12 h for 3 days) in combination with idarubicin (12 mg/m2 for 3 days) for consolidation therapy.

Assessment of remission

Morphological CR was defined as the presence of 5% blasts in the bone marrow in the absence of extramedullary disease. Depending on the concomitant recovery of peripheral blood counts, remission was further subclassified as CR with full hematologic recovery (platelet count > 100,000/mL and neutrophil count > 1000/mL) or CR with incomplete hematologic recovery (platelet count < 100,000/mL and/or neutrophil count < 1000/mL). Following the guideline [8], measurable residual disease (MRD) levels less than the lower limit of quantification (LLOQ) were defined as negative. Relapse was defined as the reappearance of more than 5% blasts in the bone marrow or the presence of extramedullary disease after achieving CR, including central nervous system relapse. The bone marrow (BM) was evaluated before the first consolidation treatment and again after recovery from myelosuppression (neutrophil count > 1000/mL).

Statistical analysis

Several outcomes were studied, including the cumulative incidence of relapse (CIR), relapse-free survival (RFS), and overall survival (OS), as well as the rate of treatment-related toxicity. OS was calculated from the date of treatment initiation to the date of death from any cause or last follow-up. RFS was defined as the time from the start of treatment to the earliest relapse from CR, death from any cause, or the last follow-up. Adverse events were graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events v5.0. Agranulocytosis refers to a severe reduction in the number of granulocytes in the peripheral blood, with the absolute count of granulocytes falling below 0.5 × 109/L. The duration of agranulocytosis was calculated from the time when the absolute neutrophil count fell below 0.5 × 109/L, until it recovered to a level above 1 × 109/L. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics (version 25.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and GraphPad Prism 9 (GraphPad Software Inc., CA, US). Data were presented as descriptive values, percentages, medians, and ranges, as appropriate. Survival data were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method. Categorical variables were analyzed using the chi-squared test. The Mann-Whitney U test and independent samples t-test were used to measure continuous variables. Differences were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05.

Results

Clinical characteristics

A total of 102 patients were included in the statistical analysis. The HDAC group was comprised of 49 individuals with an average age of 40 years (range, 19–68). In this cohort, 24 individuals had a favorable prognosis, 11 had an intermediate prognosis, and 14 had an adverse prognosis. The IHDAC group consisted of 53 individuals with an average age of 43 years (spanning 20–68). In this cohort, 29 patients had a favorable prognosis, 17 had an intermediate prognosis, and 14 had an adverse prognosis. None of the included patients had extramedullary disease, central nervous system infiltration, ongoing infections, or bleeding that could affect the overall survival (OS). There were no significant differences in age, sex, cytological typing, European LeukemiaNet (ELN) risk stratification, comorbidities, or white blood cell counts between the HDAC and IHDAC groups. Overall results

By the end of the follow-up period (June 30, 2023), the median follow-up was 739.5 days. The 3-year OS rates were 60.7% (95% CI:41.2–75.5%) in the HDAC group and 62.7% (95% CI:46.1–75.4%) in the IHDAC group (Fig. 2a), with no significant difference between the two groups (p = 0.99, Fig. 2a). The 3-year CIR in the HDAC group was 24.0% (95% CI:14.1–39.2%) lower than the 3-year CIR in the IHDAC group (35.9%; 95% CI:23.2–52.6%). However, no significant differences were observed (p = 0.52; Fig. 2b). The 3-year RFS in the HDAC group was not significantly different from that in the IHDAC group (60.1% [95% CI:42.4–73.9%) vs. 60.2% [95% CI:43.8–73.3%); p = 0.99, Fig. 2c).

Subgroup analysis

Further clinical subgroup analyses were performed according to the 2022 ELN risk stratification [9]. To exclude the influence of prognostic factors, patients were first divided into two groups based on prognosis: favorable and unfavorable (including intermediate and adverse prognoses). Subsequently, within each of these groups, the survival outcomes of the HDAC and IHDAC patient cohorts were investigated. Among patients with a favorable prognosis, the 3-year OS rate in the HDAC group was 64.8% (95% CI:30.8–85.3%), which was similar to that in the IHDAC group (63.0%; 95% CI:40.3–79.1%, p = 0.362) (Fig. 3a). The 3-year CIR in the HDAC group was 14.3% (95% CI:4.8–38.0%), which was lower than that in the IHDAC group (37.7%; 95% CI:20.9–61.7%, p = 0.108) (Fig. 3b). The 3-year RFS was 66.5% (95% CI:35.9–85.0%) in the HDAC group and 59.8% (95% CI:36.7–76.8%) in the IHDAC group (p = 0.405) (Fig. 3c). The survival and recurrence data of patients with unfavorable prognoses were also analyzed. In the unfavorable prognosis group, the 3-year OS rate was 58.1% (95% CI:35.7–75.0%) in the HDAC group and 62.7% (95% CI:36.9–80.3%) in the IHDAC group, with no significant difference between the two groups (p = 0.550) (Fig. 3d). The 3-year CIR was 32.2% (95% CI:17.6–54.3%) in the HDAC group and 33.3% (95% CI:17.2–58.2%) in the IHDAC group (p = 0.628) (Fig. 3e). The 3-year RFS was 54.8% (95% CI:33.1–72.0%) in the HDAC group and 61.5% (95% CI:37.0–78.9%) in the IHDAC group (p = 0.523) (Fig. 3f).

All patients were divided into two groups based on whether they underwent allo-HSCT. The 3-year overall survival (OS) rate in the allo-HSCT group was 85.5% (95% CI: 65.7–94.3%), which was significantly higher than that in the no allo-HSCT group (52.3% [95% CI: 37.7–64.9%]) (p = 0.04) (Fig. 4a). The 3-year cumulative incidence of relapse (CIR) in the allo-HSCT group was 13.9% (95% CI: 5.4–32.9%), compared to 35.2% (95% CI: 24.5–48.9%) in the no allo-HSCT group (p = 0.09) (Fig. 4b). The 3-year RFS rate in the allo-HSCT group was 82.8% (95% CI: 63.5–92.5%), while it was 52.4% (95% CI: 38.5–64.6%) in the no allo-HSCT group (p = 0.05) (Fig. 4c). Among patients who did not undergo allo-HSCT, the 3-year OS rate in the HDAC group was 48.7% (95% CI: 26.1–68.1%), compared to 54.1% (95% CI: 34.5–70.2%) in the IHDAC group (p = 0.94) (Fig. 4d), with no statistically significant difference. The 3-year CIR in the HDAC group was 27.0% (95% CI: 14.5–46.9%), while it was 41.3% (95% CI: 26.3–60.5%) in the IHDAC group (p = 0.19) (Fig. 4e). The 3-year RFS rate in the HDAC group was 49.4% (95% CI: 28.1–67.5%), compared to 50.0% (95% CI: 30.5–66.7%) in the IHDAC group (p = 0.81) (Fig. 4f). Among patients who underwent allo-HSCT, the 3-year OS rate in the HDAC group was 86.5% (95% CI: 55.8–96.5%), compared to 83.9% (95% CI: 49.4–95.7%) in the IHDAC group (p = 0.79) (Fig. 4g). The 3-year CIR in the HDAC group was 18.4% (95% CI: 6.3–47.0%), while it was 7.7% (95% CI: 1.1–43.4%) in the IHDAC group (p = 0.14) (Fig. 4h). The 3-year RFS rate in the HDAC group was 81.6% (95% CI: 53.0–93.7%), compared to 84.6% (95% CI: 51.2–95.9%) in the IHDAC group (p = 0.54) (Fig. 4i).

MRD remission status was also statistically analyzed as a factor influencing survival. Toxicity assessment

Patients in the IHDAC group experienced a significantly longer agranulocytosis duration (8.75 days) compared to those in the HDAC group (4.80 days, p < 0.001) during the first consolidation. However, no significant difference in agranulocytosis duration was observed in the second consolidation (8.81 days vs. 7.14 days, p = 0.20) (Table 2). Other adverse events occurring during treatment are listed in Table 3. Notably, no significant difference was observed in the incidence of cardiac dysfunction and pericardial effusion between the two groups. The incidence of infections, including bacteremia and pneumonia, was significantly higher in the IHDAC group than in the HDAC group (p = 0.035). However, when bacteremia and pneumonia were assessed individually, there were no significant differences between the groups. Additionally, there were no significant differences in the frequency of other adverse events between the groups. Discussion

Over the past three decades, cytarabine has been widely used to treat AML in adult patients. Induction chemotherapy regimens employing anthracyclines in combination with standard-dose cytarabine, namely, the 3 + 7 regimen of daunorubicin plus cytarabine (DA) or idarubicin plus cytarabine (IA), have become the consensus treatments for AML [10]. The complete remission rate for adult patients with AML treated with the DA regimen ranges from 60–80% [11]. The use of high-dose cytarabine as a single agent is widely recognized in consolidation chemotherapy. However, the use of high-dose cytarabine in combination with other agents in multiagent chemotherapy (MCT) regimens for consolidation remains a subject of considerable debate [12]. Consequently, this study retrospectively evaluated the clinical efficacy of high-dose cytarabine combined with idarubicin as consolidation therapy at our center.

In this retrospective study, the 4-year OS rates were 60.7%, whereas the 4-year OS rate for patients in the HDAC group was 59.4%. In a seminal randomized clinical trial of cytarabine dosing, the 4-year OS rate of patients treated with consolidation therapy using high-dose cytarabine alone was 46% [13]. The survival data of patients at our center were slightly better, which may be attributed to the widespread application and promotion of hematopoietic stem cell transplantation in clinical practice. This retrospective study found that for patients with AML, the efficacy of high-dose cytarabine in combination with idarubicin during consolidation therapy was not inferior to that of high-dose cytarabine alone.

Cytarabine has long been used as the mainstay of treatment for AML patients, from the initial standard dose of 100–300 mg/m2 used for 7–10 days to the widespread use of 3000 mg/m2 twice daily for a total of 3 days by investigators. However, many studies are still exploring treatment options superior to HDAC, including a combination with a target drug or other intensive chemotherapy. Multiple studies have confirmed that, compared with standard doses, high-dose cytarabine use appears to provide a survival benefit to patients [14–16]. Furthermore, consolidation therapy with high-dose cytarabine alone has been demonstrated to be non-inferior to multiagent chemotherapy regimens and has shown superior 5-year DFS rates in patients with favorable prognostic profiles [5]. These studies have confirmed the status of high-dose cytarabine as a first-line treatment for AML. Although some studies suggest that a dose of 1 g/m² cytarabine reaches a plateau with no additional benefit to patients from increasing the dosage, this perspective remains controversial [16, 17]. However, the frontline status of high-dose cytarabine remains unchallenged.

Regarding the MCT regimen involving high-dose cytarabine combined with other agents, the combination of intermediate-dose cytarabine with daunorubicin was recommended as one of the optional consolidation treatment regimens for AML patients with intermediate prognosis in the 2024 NCCN guidelines [6]. This chemotherapy regimen was recommended in the 2022 ELN guidelines for patients in need of a second induction therapy or those experiencing a relapse. Idarubicin and mitoxantrone were suggested as alternative options to daunorubicin [9]. However, the clinical research on this regimen remains limited.

Multiple randomized studies have demonstrated that the efficacy of idarubicin administered for three consecutive days at a dose of 12–13 mg/m²/d is superior to that of daunorubicin administered at a dose of 45–50 mg/m²/d [18–20]. And mitoxantrone is associated with an increased risk of cardiotoxicity in patients, while offering no demonstrable survival advantage [21]. Therefore, considering factors such as efficacy and side effects, our center most frequently employs idarubicin in the treatment of patients with newly diagnosed AML, administering it at the recommended dose of 12 mg/m²/day for 3 days, in accordance with the ELN guidelines [9].

This study aimed to investigate whether the use of high-dose cytarabine along with idarubicin in consolidation therapy could improve the long-term survival of patients. In this study, the clinical outcomes were not significantly different between the two groups. In the favorable-risk group or in the no allo-HSCT group, the CIR of the patients in the IHDAC group was slightly higher than that in the HDAC group. No significant differences were observed between the two groups, which may be attributable to the higher incidence of infection in the IHDAC group. This results in prolonged treatment cycles for these patients, thereby affecting subsequent treatments. And fewer maintenance therapy cycles may also contribute to this discrepancy. We also analyzed whether the status of MRD is the cause of the CIR in the IHDAC group. However, the rate of MRD positivity after induction was significantly lower in the IHDAC group compared to the HDAC group. Another possible reason is the limited number of patients included in the study, which may have changed at a later stage after increasing the sample size at our center. In contrast, the clinical outcomes of patients receiving HDAC in the favorable-risk group were slightly better than those of patients with an unfavorable prognostic classification. No significant differences were observed in these outcomes within the study, with only a slight advantage evident in the survival curves. However, the long-term survival benefit for patients with a favorable prognosis has been widely recognized [22]. This underscores the limitations imposed by the small sample size of this study. Among patients with an unfavorable prognosis, the IHDAC group exhibited a modest advantage in terms of survival curves. This suggests that the combination with idarubicin could potentially confer benefits to patients with an unfavorable prognosis. An earlier study found that in patients resistant to conventional-dose cytarabine, the use of high-dose cytarabine combined with anthracyclines during the reinduction phase for relapsed disease was superior to that of cytarabine alone, with a higher proportion of patients achieving complete remission (56% vs. 20%, p = 0.022) [23]. Therefore, patients with an adverse prognosis or resistance to conventional-dose cytarabine may benefit from consolidation therapy with high-dose cytarabine combined with idarubicin. In a similar study conducted in Germany, the findings aligned with ours. They compared mitoxantrone combined with intermediate-dose cytarabine and cytarabine monotherapy. Given that mitoxantrone and idarubicin belong to the anthracycline class of drugs, their research holds significant relevance to this study [24]. These studies also indicate that the administration of higher doses of cytarabine in combination with anthracyclines for consolidation therapy in patients with AML does not lead to worse survival outcomes. More rigorous and larger prospective randomized clinical trials should provide results with greater reference values.

In this study, aside from having a longer duration of bone marrow suppression and a higher incidence of infections, there was no significant difference in the frequency of other adverse events compared to the other group. This is consistent with the findings of other studies, in which idarubicin tended to prolong the duration of neutropenia [25]. Overall, the safety profile of the IHDAC regimen is manageable. However, significant prolongation of the bone marrow transplantation period increases the risk of infections, which may further lead to an increased risk of blood transfusions and higher treatment costs.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our single-center retrospective study’s findings align with previous research, indicating that the combination of high-dose cytarabine with idarubicin during consolidation therapy does not confer a significant advantage over cytarabine monotherapy, including in patients in high-risk. However, this regimen significantly prolongs the duration of bone marrow suppression and increases the risk of infection, which may offset any potential benefits it might offer. To our knowledge, this is the first study investigating if idarubicin enhances survival benefits in high-dose cytarabine consolidation therapy. We anticipate the involvement of a larger patient cohort and a longer duration of follow-up to further validate these findings.

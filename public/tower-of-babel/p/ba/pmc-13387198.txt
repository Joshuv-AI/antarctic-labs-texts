Efficacy of Acupuncture in the Treatment of Residual Pain After Osteoporotic Vertebral Compression Fractures Percutaneous Vertebroplasty Among Older Adults: A Study Protocol for a Randomized Controlled Clinical Trial
Authors: Tengpeng Zhou, Shengxiao Lai, Guangwei Wen, Yiyi Lai, Mingdi Li, Jiasheng Yang, Angela Wei Hong Yang, Hong Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOsteoporotic vertebral compression fractures (OVCF) are common fractures in the elderly and have reached epidemic proportions, not only affecting individual health, but also creating an economic burden worldwide. Percutaneous vertebroplasty has been widely used to treat OVCF and achieve analgesic effects, but the incidence of residual pain after percutaneous vertebroplasty is still high. There is evidence that electroacupuncture when combined with lifestyle interventions, can effectively reduce the effects of residual pain after percutaneous vertebroplasty. This clinical trial aims to evaluate and compare the safety and clinical efficacy of electroacupuncture and floating acupuncture for residual pain after percutaneous vertebroplasty.MethodsThis will be a blank-controlled, three-arm randomized clinical trial. A total of 111 participants who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria aged between 60 and 80 years will be recruited. The 111 participants meeting the inclusion and exclusion criteria will be randomly divided into three groups including a blank control group, an electroacupuncture treatment group, and a floating acupuncture treatment group. The total course of research in this study will be 3 weeks, including a 1-day interview valuation, a 3-day treatment period and a 2-week follow-up period. Participants will receive three treatments for the whole trial. The primary outcome will be the assessment of the Visual Analogue Scale score while the secondary outcome will be the measurement of traditional Chinese medicine syndrome diagnosis scale and Oswestry Disability Index. All patients will be assessed at baseline and the end points of day 1 of enrollment, day 1, day 2, day 3, day 7 and day 14 after surgery.Clinical Trial RegistrationThis trial has been registered at the International Traditional Medicine Clinical Trial Registry. Registration number:ITMCTR2024000853. Registration date: 22 December 2024.Keywords: pain management, electroacupuncture, Fu’s subcutaneous needling, evidence-based Chinese medicine

Introduction

Osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures (OVCF) are common fractures in the elderly, particularly those aged 65 and older.1–4 The condition has reached near-epidemic proportions globally, not only affecting individual health but also creating a substantial economic burden worldwide.1–4 Characterized by the loss of vertebral body height and compression fractures, OVCF results in spinal deformities, chronic back pain, and substantial impairment of patient’s quality of life. OVCF predominantly manifests at the thoracolumbar junction (T12-L2 vertebrae). Clinical indicators include localized tenderness near affected vertebrae or lumbosacral pain, spinal kyphotic deformity, limited mobility, exacerbation of pain with positional changes, and alleviation or cessation of pain during bed rest.5–8 The enduring pain stemming from OVCF significantly compromises the well-being and quality of life of the elderly population while also imposing substantial financial burdens in terms of healthcare expenditures.9

The treatment of OVCF can be classified into non-surgical and surgical approaches. Non-surgical treatments primarily involve bed rest, medication, physical therapy, and external bracing. However, non-surgical interventions typically yield suboptimal outcomes, with approximately 10% of patients experiencing nonunion fractures characterized by vacuum clefts, ischemia, and pseudoarthrosis.10–12 Consequently, pain relief is often inadequate. Moreover, non-surgical management necessitates prolonged bed rest and carries the risk of complications such as pneumonia, urinary tract infections, pressure injuries, deep vein thrombosis, or delayed neurological deficits.13

Therefore, for patients recently diagnosed with OVCF whose symptoms and signs are consistent with imaging findings, percutaneous vertebroplasty surgery represents a safe and effective therapeutic approach.6,14 This procedure swiftly and effectively alleviates back pain, restores spinal height and stability, corrects kyphotic deformities, enhances the quality of life, mitigates the risk of complications associated with prolonged bed rest, and reduces long-term mortality rates.15–17 Literature reports indicated that percutaneous vertebroplasty surgery achieves a clinical effectiveness rate of 78%–90% for OVCF treatment, with an average reduction of 4–5 points in Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) scores.18 However, as percutaneous vertebroplasty techniques become more widely adopted and the number of cases increases, the issue of residual pain post-surgery has garnered increasing attention among foreign medical professionals. Furthermore, the incidence of residual lumbar back pain post-surgery ranges from 4.6% to 24.2%.19,20

Due to the potential for adverse effects such as hepatic dysfunction, renal impairment, and gastrointestinal bleeding, resulting from long-term use of anti-inflammatory and analgesic medications, the issue of managing residual pain post-surgery has become increasingly prominent and warrants serious attention.21

Electroacupuncture (EA) therapy, guided by traditional acupuncture theory, involves the insertion of needles into acupoints to elicit needle sensation, followed by the connection of the acupuncture needles to an EA device. By combining needle insertion with electrical stimulation, the intensity of the current can be adjusted according to the patient’s condition, thereby inducing a tingling sensation for the patient. EA exhibits analgesic, sedative, circulatory promotion, nerve stimulation, and muscle tension adjustment effects, thereby generating analgesic effects. EA has been widely applied in the treatment of pain, as evidenced by extensive research in this area.22

Additionally, floating needle therapy is a novel modern acupuncture method invented based on traditional acupuncture theory and combined with modern medical knowledge. This therapy involves subcutaneous needling using disposable floating needle instruments around the affected muscles or adjacent limbs causing pain, coupled with reperfusion activities, as a rapid pain relief method, applicable to various painful conditions. The techniques of needle insertion and manipulation are distinctive, with the sweeping and scattering technique differing from the traditional insertion and twisting. When the practitioner performs sweeping and scattering movements with floating needles, the spatial structure of the subcutaneous loose connective tissue changes, altering the ion channels of cells and modulating the body’s innate mechanisms against disease. This may constitute the mechanism underlying its efficacy. Reperfusion actions, either actively or passively applied, accelerate the blood flow rate in the affected muscles, ameliorating ischemic conditions and promoting the repair of affected muscles.23

Although there is increasing evidence supporting the use of EA and floating needle therapy for managing postoperative residual pain, traditional Chinese acupuncture techniques are not yet included in the standard clinical guidelines in China for this condition. Therefore, further research is essential to strengthen the evidence base and support their incorporation into clinical practice. There is a lack of high-quality, blank-controlled RCT evidence specifically for residual pain after percutaneous vertebroplasty in OVCF patients. We hypothesize that both EA and floating needle will be superior to blank control in reducing VAS scores; and that floating needle may provide more rapid pain relief due to its different mechanism, while EA may offer more sustained effects. This clinical trial aims to evaluate the therapeutic efficacy of EA and floating needle therapy for residual pain following percutaneous vertebroplasty in patients with OVCF Additionally, this study seeks to compare the treatment benefits of EA and floating needle therapy.

Sample Size

We used G*Power 3.1.927,28 for the sample size calculations based on a previously published paper investigating the pain-releasing effects of Fu’s Subcutaneous Needling (FSN) on percutaneous vertebroplasty (PVP) patients.29 The calculation was based on the primary outcome (VAS score change from baseline to Day14) for the comparison between the EA group and the blank control group using a two-independent-sample t-test. We chose 0.7 as the effect size for calculation, with around 50% dropout, based on the instructions on the sample size determination in the G*Power software.30 Given that 50% may be an overestimation for this population, we also note that a 20% dropout rate would yield 37 participants per group. A total of 111 participants (37 subjects per group) will be needed to achieve 80% power at the significance level of 0.05.

Recruitment and screening

All researchers will screen among our hospitalized patients. Potential participants were given a Participant General Information Registration Form, a Willingness to Participate Questionnaire and a Consent to Participate Form by the investigator (Supporting Information Files S2 to S4). If they are willing to participate in the trial, they will be asked to read the study protocol in detail and submit a written application to participate in the trial. The study team will review all application forms for browsing and screening eligible participants. A screening interview will be scheduled to assess all baseline measurements to ensure that all participants meet the screening criteria. Participants who meet the inclusion criteria will be recruited to participate in this clinical trial and will sign a consent to participate in the clinical trial. Researchers will be responsible for obtaining written consent from participants before randomization.

Randomization

Participants will be randomly assigned to three groups. An independent statistician will be responsible for generating the random codes using a computer program and randomly assigning the codes to the three groups on a 1:1:1 basis. The codes will be placed in an individually sealed opaque envelope containing information about the group to which they have been assigned. All participants will randomly select an envelope in the order of entry into the group and give the envelope of their choice to the researcher. The researcher will ensure that the envelope is not opened until the name of the assigned participant is written on the envelope. Group assignments may not be changed after they have been finalized.

Blinding

Each researcher was relatively independent and kept the discussion of study group assignment to a minimum. The therapist and the one researcher who arranged the treatment groupings were not blinded. One research assistant responsible for entering outcome measures is blinded to treatment assignment. The data manager responsible for manually entering information into the securely stored database will be blinded to treatment grouping. To ensure that allocation is concealed, any discussion between the treatment provider and the subject must be kept to a minimum. There will be no discussion of any information related to this study between each subject. While the acupuncturist cannot be blinded, all outcome assessments will be performed by an independent assessor who is blinded to group allocation. The statistician will also be blinded until the final data analysis is completed. Participants will be told that they will receive one of three potentially effective pain management strategies (blank control, EA, or FSN) to standardize expectations.

FSN: Participants in the study will receive FSN therapy using a single-use FSN acupuncture needle and needle feeder (Nanjing Paifu Medical Technology Co., Ltd., China). Routine disinfection with 75% alcohol will be performed on the hands and needling sites. Patients will be placed in the prone position, and trigger points identified by pressing on the erector spinae and psoas major muscles to locate tender spots. The needle will be inserted at an angle of 15°–25° into the skin, approximately 5–6 cm away from the affected muscle, targeting the pain point. The needle core will then be retracted into the flexible cannula, allowing the needle to leave the muscle layer and recede under the skin. The body of the needle will be angled with the entry point as a fulcrum, and the right hand will be used to perform a left-and-right sweeping dispersion for 1–2 min. The needle will then be retained by withdrawing the core and fixing the soft cannula to the skin with adhesive tape for two hours. FSN therapy will be administered on the 1st, 2nd and 3rd days after the operation, with each session lasting 30 min (10 min of FSN manipulation and 20 minutes of needle retention). Oral analgesics will be provided as needed and documented throughout the treatment.

Group Design

This is a three-arm clinical trial consisting of a blank control group, an EA group and a FSN group. Participants will be asked to continue their usual diet and exercise habits and to avoid participating in any pain programs or self-administering any pain medications. Oral analgesics (eg, acetaminophen) are permitted as rescue medication if the pain becomes unbearable. The use, dose, time, and reason for any rescue medication will be meticulously recorded as a secondary outcome and compared between groups. However, regular, prophylactic, or self-initiated use of any additional pain medication beyond the prescribed rescue protocol will be considered a protocol violation. These participants will not be automatically withdrawn, but will be included in the Intention-to-Treat (ITT) analysis. To assess the efficacy of different needling techniques on residual pain after percutaneous vertebroplasty, the treatment outcomes of EA group and FSN group will be compared with the blank control group, respectively. On the other hand, to assess the superior efficacy of the EA group and the FSN group, the outcomes of EA group will be compared with the FSN group. We will evaluate the VAS score, ODI index and TCM symptom scale after the treatment. The researcher will contact each participant one day in advance via text message or phone call to ensure that the participants will confirm their time for each treatment session and follow-up visit during the treatment period.

Outcome Measurements

Primary Outcome

VAS Pain Scale

The VAS pain scale is a unilateral assessment of the pain intensity of patients and is the most used pain assessment scale in clinical practice.32 The single-dimension pain scale enables patients to objectively express their subjective pain feelings employing numbers, words and images. In general, single-dimension pain scales are simple and easy to evaluate. After a brief explanation, patients generally understood the scale quickly and completed the assessment within 1 min. Therefore, the single-dimensional pain scale is the first choice for rapid pain assessment.

Secondary Outcomes

Traditional Chinese Medicine Syndrome Diagnosis Scale (Supporting Information Files S5)

TCM syndrome score is a concept in TCM diagnosis, and it is also an important means in TCM diagnosis.23 It is based on the symptoms and signs of patients, according to the theory and experience of TCM, to score and comprehensively analyze different diseases to determine the main symptoms and syndromes of patients. It assists doctors to fully understand the patient’s condition, accurately judge the nature and development trend of the disease, and provide patients with personalized TCM treatment programs to promote recovery and health.

The Oswestry Disability Index

Consists of 10 questions, including pain intensity, self-care, lifting, walking, sitting, standing, disturbance of sleep, sexual life, social life, and travel.33 Each question has 6 options. The maximum score for each question is 5, and the first option is 0. If all 10 questions are answered, the scoring methods are listed below:is actual score/50 (highest possible score) × 100%; if one question is not answered, the scoring method is: actual score/45 (highest possible score) × 100%; the higher the score, the more serious the dysfunction.

Adverse Events

All adverse events will be documented, including a description of the procedure, severity, duration, and subsequent treatment required. All serious adverse events will be reported to the study leader immediately so that appropriate treatment measures can be taken. All serious adverse events will be reported to the Ethics Committee of Liwan Orthopaedic and Traumatology Hospital, Guangzhou, China, which will make the final decision on whether to terminate the trial.

Attendance and Drop-Out

Participants will be informed that they may withdraw from the clinical trial at any time, unconditionally, and the reason for withdrawal will be tracked and documented. Participants who do not wish to provide a reason for withdrawal will be followed up until the problem is fully resolved if the reason is related to an adverse event. Participants who self-administer pain medication or participate in other pain management clinical studies during this study will be considered to have withdrawn.

Data Management

Each participant’s personal information and records will be kept separately and stored in a locked cabinet at the hospital. All electronic files will be stored on hospital computers and password-protected. Only authorized members of the study team will have access rights to all study data.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis will be performed using the Statistical Package for the Statistical Sciences. At the end of the trial, the team will perform statistical analysis using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences. Given the short duration of the trial, no interim analyses will be conducted. An intention-to-treat (acupuncture treatment) analysis will be used for participants who have been randomly assigned and have attended at least one session. Worst-case scenario methods will be used for missing data. Continuous variables will be expressed using means, standard deviations, and 95% confidence intervals, while quantitative variables will be expressed as maximum and minimum values. Baseline data and clinical characteristics will be used to compare baseline data between intervention groups. Data will be compared. Mean differences will be analyzed by analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) for VAS and ODI. All comparisons with a p-value less than 0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

Discussion and Conclusions

OVCF represents the most common type of fracture among osteoporosis patients, with the aging population contributing to a continuous increase in OVCF cases.34 Conservative treatment strategies for OVCF typically encompass analgesia, physical therapy, pharmacological management of osteoporosis, and fall risk prevention, with successful outcomes achievable for the majority of patients through conservative measures.35 However, conservative management of vertebral compression fractures may predispose individuals to spinal deformity, decreased physical functionality, and diminished health-related quality of life.35,36 Consequently, for patients unable to mobilize during the acute inpatient phase or experiencing ineffective conservative treatment and significant pain limiting daily activities within 8 to 12 weeks post-injury, surgical intervention is often considered.37 Clinical observations have indicated the high efficiency of percutaneous vertebroplasty procedures in treating OVCF, offering rapid pain relief and satisfactory outcomes for most patients.18 Nevertheless, a minority of patients may still experience persistent postoperative pain, with reported incidence rates of residual lumbar back pain ranging from 5% to 22%.19 Hence, there is an urgent need for effective pain management strategies to alleviate the impact of residual lumbar back pain following percutaneous vertebroplasty procedures.

Traditional Chinese medicine acupuncture therapy has demonstrated satisfactory efficacy in the treatment of pain.38 A plethora of literature has reported the widespread application of EA in pain management studies, with documented effects including analgesia, sedation, promotion of blood circulation, stimulation of nerve responses, and modulation of muscle tone.22,39 Moreover, there is a growing body of research on floating needle therapy for treating lumbar and back pain.40 Floating needle therapy, a novel modern acupuncture method developed based on traditional acupuncture theory combined with modern medical knowledge, involves subcutaneous needle insertion around the affected muscles or adjacent limbs that cause pain, accompanied by reperfusion activities, as a rapid pain relief method.41,42 Despite increasing evidence supporting the use of acupuncture or floating needle therapy for the management of postoperative residual pain, current clinical guidelines in China do not routinely consider traditional Chinese medicine acupuncture techniques as a standard treatment modality for postoperative residual pain.43 Therefore, further research is needed to provide more evidence to assist clinical decision-making.

This clinical trial aims to clinically evaluate the efficacy of acupuncture and floating needle therapy in alleviating postoperative residual pain following percutaneous vertebroplasty for OVCF. The study seeks to assess the effectiveness of EA and floating needle therapy in reducing pain post-percutaneous vertebroplasty. Regarding the group allocation design, pain reduction effects will be evaluated by comparing a blank control group, an EA treatment group, and a floating needle treatment group. This design imposes a limitation on the trial as we cannot administer blind treatments to participants in the EA and floating needle groups separately. Future studies could build on our findings by including a combined EA+floating needle group to explore potential synergistic effects, which was beyond the scope of this initial protocol. All acupuncturists will receive training in needling at predetermined non-acupoint locations to ensure consistency in acupoint positioning for the EA group. All floating needle therapists will undergo training related to the predetermined treatment of floating needles to ensure consistency in the site and manner of treatment implementation.

This trial will assess validated patient-reported outcomes, including the VAS, ODI, and TCM symptom scale. Currently, methods for treating post-percutaneous vertebroplasty residual pain include functional exercises, pharmacological treatment, and traditional Chinese medicine acupuncture therapy. Therefore, it is necessary to isolate the effects of these methods to influence the study results. It is essential to separate the impact of these methods on the study outcomes. Therefore, we will require participants who do not receive traditional Chinese medicine acupuncture therapy to continue their regular dietary and physical activities. Thus, we can ensure changes in treatment outcomes attributed to the influence of assigned study groups. Although the VAS pain assessment tool is the primary instrument for evaluating pain status, we will also incorporate the Traditional Chinese Medicine Symptom Score Sheet and the ODI for assessing the efficacy of traditional Chinese medicine acupuncture therapy in post-percutaneous vertebroplasty residual pain. If the results of this research trial are meaningful, it will offer a novel, cost-effective pain management approach. It is anticipated that this method can alleviate both postoperative pain and psychological conditions in patients.

Finally, this clinical trial protocol is meticulously designed following the SPIRIT,24 CONSORT,26 and STRICTA25 guidelines. While this protocol has inherent limitations (eg, non-blinded practitioners, use of some subjective outcomes), we have taken several methodological steps to enhance rigor, including blinded outcome assessment, ITT analysis, and a blank-controlled design. If successful, this trial will provide valuable preliminary evidence on the comparative efficacy of two acupuncture modalities for post-percutaneous vertebroplasty residual pain.

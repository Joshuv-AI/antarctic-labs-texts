Comparative Assessement of Orthopantomogram and Lateral Cephalogram For Linear and Angular Measurement of Mandible in Skeletal Class I and Class II Cases in Hypodivergent and Hyperdivergent Growth Pattern—A Digitized Study
Authors: Akash G. Kudmathe, Kanika Arora, Nitika Rohilla, C. V Padma Priya, Y. Yatharth, Preetham Ravuri, Ajay K. Kubavat
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Accurate assessment of mandibular morphology is crucial in orthodontic diagnosis and treatment planning. While lateral cephalograms (Lat Ceph) are commonly used, orthopantomograms (OPG) offer advantages in eliminating superimposition. This research compares the diagnostic reliability of OPG and Lat Ceph in assessing gonial angle, ramus height, and body length in skeletal Class I and Class II malocclusions with different growth patterns.Methods:This retrospective research included 200 participants aged 16–25 years, divided into skeletal Class I and Class II groups, with hypodivergent and hyperdivergent subgroups. Mandibular parameters were measured using digital software (Nemoceph) from OPG and Lat Ceph. Statistical analysis was performed to compare linear and angular measurements between the two modalities.Results:Gonial angle and ramus height measurements showed no statistically significant differences between OPG and Lat Ceph across all groups. Body length measurements were significantly different for Class II hypodivergent (P = 0.01) and hyperdivergent (P = 0.02) patterns, with OPG showing shorter values. OPG provided consistent and reliable results, particularly for linear measurements, while maintaining comparable accuracy to Lat Ceph for angular parameters.Conclusion:OPG emerges as a reliable alternative to Lat Ceph for assessing mandibular morphology, particularly in linear measurements, reducing superimposition errors and enhancing diagnostic accuracy.

INTRODUCTION

Orthodontic diagnosis and treatment planning necessitate a comprehensive evaluation of craniofacial structures, particularly the mandible, which plays a key role in determining facial symmetry and growth patterns.[1] Cephalograms and orthopantomograms (OPGs) are widely used diagnostic tools to assess craniofacial morphology. While lateral cephalograms provide crucial insights into vertical and horizontal skeletal relationships, their accuracy can be compromised by the superimposition of bilateral structures.[2] Conversely, OPGs offer distinct advantages by visualizing both sides of the craniofacial skeleton separately, enabling precise measurement of landmarks such as the gonial angle.[345]

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This retrospective research was conducted on 200 untreated orthodontic patients in the Department of Orthodontics and Dentofacial Orthopaedics. Subjects aged 16–25 years with skeletal Class I or Class II malocclusion and horizontal or vertical growth patterns were included after detailed case history and clinical examination. Patients with craniofacial deformities, skeletal Class III malocclusion, history of trauma, facial asymmetry, or prior orthodontic treatment were excluded. Ethical approval was obtained, and written informed consent was secured from participants or their guardians.

The sample included 100 skeletal Class I and 100 skeletal Class II cases, further divided into hyperdivergent and hypodivergent groups using Tweed’s analysis. Digital orthopantomograms and lateral cephalograms were taken using the KODAK 8000 C system and analyzed with Nemoceph software. Measurements included angular and linear parameters such as ramal height, body length, and gonial angle, based on established reference landmarks. Standardized digital calibration ensured consistent radiographic evaluation.

RESULTS

The analysis of mandibular parameters using OPG revealed no statistically significant differences in gonial angle, ramus height, or body length between the left and right sides in most groups. For Class I hypodivergent and hyperdivergent patterns, gonial angles showed near-identical values on both sides, with P values of 0.45 and 0.43, respectively. Similarly, in Class II hypodivergent and hyperdivergent patterns, the differences in gonial angle, ramus height, and body length were insignificant, with P values above 0.25. However, body length measurements for Class I hypodivergent patterns exhibited a statistically significant difference (P = 0.00*), indicating minor asymmetry [Table 1].

DISCUSSION

Gonial angle

The gonial angle is a critical parameter in orthodontic diagnosis and surgical planning. Consistent with previous studies,[67] current findings revealed no statistically significant differences in gonial angle measurements between Lat Ceph and OPG across all groups, except for a minor deviation in Class II hyperdivergent patterns. This aligns with research by Kaya,[8] who found no significant difference in gonial angle values between digital OPGs and Lat Cephs. However, some studies, including those by Arkai et al. and Park et al.,[910] reported slightly smaller gonial angle measurements on OPG compared to Lat Ceph, highlighting the need for cautious interpretation.

Ramal height

Ramal height is an essential parameter for evaluating mandibular symmetry and growth patterns. This research observed no significant differences in ramal height measurements between OPG and Lat Ceph for all groups, corroborating findings by Kambylafkas et al.,[11] who concluded that OPGs are reliable for assessing vertical mandibular dimensions. These results emphasize the suitability of OPG in evaluating ramal height without the superimposition challenges inherent in Lat Ceph.

Body length

Body length measurements showed statistically significant differences between Lat Ceph and OPG in skeletal Class II malocclusions with hyperdivergent and hypodivergent patterns (P = 0.01 and P = 0.02, respectively). These discrepancies suggest that OPG may be more reliable for assessing mandibular body length in specific skeletal patterns. Similar findings were reported by Sharma et al.,[12] who demonstrated significant correlations between OPG and Lat Ceph for total mandibular length but noted OPG’s superior reliability in certain cases.

CONCLUSION

The use of digital analysis further improves precision and consistency. Based on the findings, OPG can be recommended as a reliable diagnostic tool for routine orthodontic evaluations, reducing reliance on Lat Ceph in specific clinical scenarios.

Endovascular management of bilateral traumatic internal carotid artery dissections: A case report
Authors: Eun Hyun Ihm, Ye Young Seo, Hyo Yoon Kim, Byeong Jun Ahn
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTraumatic carotid artery dissection is a rare vascular injury, with bilateral involvement exceedingly uncommon. A 53-year-old woman presented with bilateral internal carotid artery dissections after a high-speed motor vehicle collision. Despite initial antiplatelet therapy with aspirin, she developed delayed neurological deficits due to progressive stenosis. We successfully performed staged bilateral carotid stenting, 5 days apart, using self-expanding stents with distal protection after ensuring the stabilization of an associated cervical spinal epidural hematoma. This case demonstrates that early recognition and staged endovascular approach can effectively restore cerebral perfusion and prevent ischemic stroke in complex bilateral traumatic carotid artery dissection when initial hemorrhagic risks are present.Keywords: Bilateral internal carotid artery, Blunt trauma, Carotid dissection, Endovascular stenting, Traumatic carotid dissection, Vascular injury

Blunt cerebrovascular injury (BCVI) occurs in fewer than 1% of all trauma patients but may lead to devastating neurological consequences.1,2 Traumatic internal carotid artery dissection (TCAD) is a particularly rare subtype, often underdiagnosed because of subtle or delayed symptoms.3,4 Bilateral involvement is extremely rare, reported in only a small number of cases.1,3,5 TCAD commonly results from intimal tearing secondary to hyperextension, rotation, or rapid deceleration of the neck, leading to intramural hematoma and luminal narrowing.1,6,7 Early screening with computed tomography angiography (CTA) and timely intervention is essential to preventing stroke.1,8

Case report

A 53-year-old woman presented after a high-speed motor vehicle collision with headache, neck and anterior chest pain, and a 15-cm frontal laceration. No seizure or loss of consciousness occurred. Neurological examination was normal (Glasgow Coma Scale 15). Serial neurologic examinations remained stable until day 10. Initial whole-body CT showed multiple traumatic injuries (Injury Severity Score 22). Brain CT revealed left cerebellopontine angle cisternal SAH and a hyperdense ventral cervical lesion. Cervical magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) showed a T1-hyperintense C1-2 intrathecal collection, consistent with a small epidural hematoma.

CTA demonstrated bilateral proximal internal carotid artery (ICA) narrowing consistent with Biffl grade II BCVI (Fig 1). Aspirin therapy was initiated. A day 2 angiogram was performed to further characterize the vascular injury, confirming long-segment bilateral proximal ICA dissections (Fig 2). On day 7, follow-up CTA showed epidural hematoma resolution but worsening ICA stenosis. On day 10, she developed dysphasia and right hemiparesis. MRI revealed bilateral parasagittal cortical infarctions, consistent with watershed ischemia, and corresponding brain single-photon emission computed tomography demonstrated significant hypoperfusion in the same bilateral parasagittal regions (Fig 3).

Initial computed tomography (CT) angiography demonstrating luminal narrowing and wall thickening of both proximal internal carotid arteries (ICAs).

Day 2 angiography showing long-segment irregular narrowing of the (A) right and (B) left proximal cervical internal carotid arteries (ICAs).

Radiological findings following neurological decline on day 10. (A) Diffusion-weighted imaging reveals high-signal intensities in the bilateral parasagittal cortical areas, consistent with acute watershed infarctions (black arrowheads). (B) Brain SPECT images demonstrate significant hypoperfusion within the corresponding bilateral frontal and parietal parasagittal regions. These findings confirm hemodynamic insufficiency resulting from progressive bilateral internal carotid artery dissections. SPECT, Single-photon emission computed tomography.

Given symptom progression, urgent left ICA stenting was performed via transfemoral access with local anesthesia, using a 6F Shuttle sheath (Cook Medical) and FilterWire EZ distal protection system (Boston Scientific), deploying a 8-6 × 40 mm Protégé RX stent (Medtronic) to reconstruct the long dissected segment (Fig 4, A). Right ICA stenting followed 5 days later (Fig 4, B). Intraprocedural heparin was administered. Dual antiplatelet therapy was continued postoperatively. One-month angiography demonstrated patent stents without thrombus or restenosis (Fig 5), and MRI showed no new infarction. Although not fully returned to baseline, dysphasia and right-sided weakness markedly improved, corresponding to National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale 2 and modified Rankin Scale 1.

Poststent angiography after staged bilateral internal carotid artery stenting. (A) Left internal carotid artery (ICA) demonstrating restoration of luminal patency. (B) Right ICA demonstrating successful stent deployment. The stent was deployed within the ICA to avoid coverage of the common carotid artery.

Discussion

Bilateral TCAD is an exceptionally rare but potentially life-threatening vascular injury. Its diagnosis can be easily delayed, particularly in polytrauma patients, where competing injuries may overshadow subtle neurological signs.1,3,4 Bilateral involvement markedly increases the risk of cerebral hypoperfusion, potentially leading to severe neurological deficits or death. In the present case, the high-speed motor vehicle collision generated sufficient shearing force to produce bilateral proximal ICA dissections, highlighting the need for vigilance in severe blunt cervical trauma.

Clinical presentation is often delayed or nonspecific. Prior studies show that fewer than 10% of patients with BCVI present with early neurological deficits, and up to one-third remain asymptomatic for more than 24 hours.1,2,9 This supports the implementation of a liberal screening strategy in high-risk trauma mechanisms. CTA has become the primary screening modality due to its high sensitivity and specificity for BCVI.1,8 Although digital subtraction angiography remains the gold standard, it is often impractical in unstable trauma patients.

Antithrombotic therapy—either antiplatelet or anticoagulation—remains the initial mainstay of management. A Cochrane review demonstrated no significant difference in stroke prevention between the two strategies.10 In trauma patients where hemorrhagic risks are present, antiplatelet therapy is generally preferred.1 Anticoagulation was not pursued despite radiologic resolution of the hematoma. However, when luminal narrowing progresses, neurologic deficits evolve, or embolic phenomena occur despite medical management, endovascular intervention becomes essential.11, 12, 13

Endovascular reconstruction offers several advantages, including restoration of true lumen patency, stabilization of the intimal flap, and prevention of further embolization. Large retrospective series and systematic reviews consistently report technical success rates exceeding 90% with favorable long-term patency.11, 12, 13, 14 A comprehensive systematic review by Huang et al8 demonstrated that endovascular therapy for cervical ICA dissections achieves high rates of luminal restoration with low complication rates. Similarly, López et al15 reported strong evidence supporting endovascular repair as an effective treatment for selected ICA dissection cases, particularly when progressive stenosis is present. A Korean multicenter study by Kim et al16 further confirmed that endovascular management yields favorable outcomes in patients without intracranial large-vessel occlusion. These findings are reinforced by the meta-analysis by Chen et al,17 which concluded that stent-based intervention is both safe and effective in high-risk or medically refractory carotid dissections.

In our patient, an initial epidural hematoma necessitated a delay in intervention. This approach aligned with expert recommendations that emphasize balancing hemorrhagic risk against potential ischemia when determining intervention timing.1 Staged bilateral ICA stenting—first on the symptomatic left ICA and subsequently on the right ICA to minimize hemodynamic risk—allowed hemodynamic stabilization and reduced the cumulative procedural risk. Follow-up angiography confirmed durable stent patency, and the patient experienced near-complete neurological recovery, consistent with outcomes reported in previous endovascular series.11, 12, 13, 14 However, the follow-up duration was limited to 1 month, precluding assessment of long-term stent durability.

Despite the favorable outcome, several considerations warrant attention. The natural history of traumatic ICA dissection remains incompletely understood, particularly for bilateral cases, which may be underdiagnosed during initial trauma evaluation.1,3,5 Functional outcomes after endovascular repair are influenced by multiple factors, including baseline neurological status and infarct burden, which may explain the variability in clinical results across studies.15, 16, 17 Furthermore, although endovascular treatment demonstrates high technical success, complications such as vessel rupture, stent malposition, distal embolization, in-stent thrombosis, and restenosis can occur, necessitating careful patient selection and close postprocedural monitoring.12, 13, 14

From a trauma system standpoint, optimal management requires early identification, multidisciplinary collaboration among trauma surgeons, neurologists, and neurointerventionalists, and close radiologic surveillance. Clinical decision-making should incorporate dissection grade, presence of neurological deficits, imaging progression, and bleeding risk from associated injuries.1,2,9 In cases of high-grade dissections or progressive ischemia despite antithrombotic therapy—as in our patient—endovascular reconstruction represents a durable and effective therapeutic strategy supported by contemporary evidence.11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17 Conservative management may be considered in stable cases without progression.

Conclusions

Bilateral TCAD is rare but potentially fatal. Early detection, close monitoring, and interdisciplinary care are essential. In cases with progressive stenosis or ischemia despite conservative therapy, staged endovascular stenting provides a safe and effective treatment approach.

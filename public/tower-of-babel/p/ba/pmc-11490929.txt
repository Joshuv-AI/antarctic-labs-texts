The mechanism and therapeutic strategies in doxorubicin-induced cardiotoxicity: Role of programmed cell death
Authors: Yanzhao Li, Jing Yan, Pingzhen Yang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Doxorubicin (DOX) is the most commonly used anthracycline anticancer agent, while its clinical utility is limited by harmful side effects like cardiotoxicity. Numerous studies have elucidated that programmed cell death plays a significant role in DOX-induced cardiotoxicity (DIC). This review summarizes several kinds of programmed cell death, including apoptosis, pyroptosis, necroptosis, autophagy, and ferroptosis. Furthermore, oxidative stress, inflammation, and mitochondrial dysfunction are also important factors in the molecular mechanisms of DIC. Besides, a comprehensive understanding of specific signal pathways of DIC can be helpful to its treatment. Therefore, the related signal pathways are elucidated in this review, including sirtuin deacetylase (silent information regulator 2 [Sir2]) 1 (SIRT1)/nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2, SIRT1/Klotho, SIRT1/Recombinant Sestrin 2, adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase, AKT, and peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor. Heat shock proteins function as chaperones, which play an important role in various stressful situations, especially in the heart. Thus, some of heat shock proteins involved in DIC are also included. Hence, the last part of this review focuses on the therapeutic research based on the mechanisms above.

Introduction

During the past few decades, even in the future, cancer is the significant contributor to human mortality. According to the previous study, there were approximately 4,820,000 new cases and 3,210,000 deaths in China in 2022.1 Doxorubicin (DOX) is a chemotherapeutic drug, commonly utilized for treating cancer, including breast cancer, hematologic malignancy, and lung cancer. However, despite the potency, the clinical application of DOX is limited due to its cardiotoxicity.2, 3

The main mechanism of DOX anticancer effects is stabilizing topoisomerase (TOP) 2α after DNA cleavage, resulting in the prevention of DNA synthesis and, therefore, interfering with the rapid proliferation of cancer cells. However, it also carries the risk of dose-dependent damage to myocardial cells, even resulting in death.4 DOX can also induce other side effects, such as arrhythmias, myocardial damage, and heart failure. Due to the irreversible risk of cardiotoxicity associated with DOX, exploring its relationship to cardiotoxicity has become particularly important.5

Accumulating evidence indicates that cardiomyocyte death is a significant component of cardiovascular disease, including myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury,6 cardiomyopathy, and cardiac fibrosis.7 The majority of cardiomyocyte deaths are executed by programmed cell death, which includes apoptosis, pyroptosis, necroptosis, autophagy, and ferroptosis. These forms of programmed cell death are cardinal features in the pathogenesis of various heart diseases. With limited regenerative capacity, any damage of cardiomyocytes can contribute to progression of cardiac dysfunction.8

Therefore, this review provides an overview of the role of DOX in cardiotoxicity, which relates to programmed cell death, and elaborates the mechanisms of how it induces cardiotoxicity.

Mechanism of programmed cell death in DOX-induced cardiotoxicity

Apoptosis

According to reports, apoptosis, the most common type of programmed cell death in cardiomyocyte death, is a key mechanism in DOX-induced cardiotoxicity (DIC). Apoptotic cells are characterized by shrinkage, then they can be phagocytosed by macrophages or neighboring cells.9, 10 It has been proposed that oxidative stress plays a significant role in apoptosis. The metabolism of DOX promotes the generation of superoxide anions free radicals (O2−), which causes excessive generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS), further resulting in mitochondrial dysfunction, which is associated with the decrease of mitochondrial membrane potential, caspase-3-mediated PARP cleavage, and eventual cardiomyocyte apoptosis.11, 12 Meteorin-like protein, a myokine abundantly expressed in myocardium, activates sirtuin deacetylase (silent information regulator 2 [Sir2]) 1 (SIRT1) via cAMP/PKA signaling axis in an autocrine manner. However, DOX exposure elicits the decrease of Meteorin-like, therefore promoting apoptosis and oxidative stress.13 Additionally, DOX suppresses death domain-associated protein expression, which plays a critical role in the regulation of cardiomyocyte apoptosis, reducing the expression of cleaved caspase-8 and cleaved caspase-3, ultimately leading to apoptosis.14 It has been demonstrated that downregulation of SIRT1 and increased expression of p66Sch in the heart following DOX exposure can trigger oxidative stress and mitochondrial damage in cardiomyocytes, therefore facilitating apoptosis.15 Beyond that, DOX can also markedly increase interleukin-9 levels in murine cardiac tissues, contributing to inflammatory response and cardiomyocyte apoptosis.16 The current study elucidates how inhibition or deficiency of PDE10A can alleviate DOX-induced myocardial apoptosis17 (Figure 1).Fig. 1Schematic diagram of the mechanism of apoptosis, pyroptosis, and necroptosis. The exchange factor EPAC, whose expression could be upregulated following DOX treatment, is a relative factor in cardiac damage. EPAC1 inhibition was reported to prevent mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP) opening induced by DOX, which in turn suppressed cardiomyocyte apoptosis.18 Phl was a hydrophobic polyphenolic compound possessing cardioprotective bioactivity. It has a protective effect against DIC via reducing the proinflammatory factors such as interleukin-17 and relieving cardiomyocytes apoptosis.19 In conclusion, oxidative stress, inflammation, and mitochondrial stress could be the main pathway in cell apoptosis induced by DOX.

Pyroptosis

Pyroptosis is a novel form of programmed cell death, characterized by the release of proinflammatory factors such as caspase-1 and caspase-5.20 Current studies have shown that by increasing the expression of Bnip3, DOX can induce the activation of proinflammatory caspase-3, eventually mediating the production of gasdermin D-N indirectly; DOX can also directly bind to gasdermin D (GSDMD) and facilitate the pyroptosis mediated by GSDMD-N.21, 22 Other studies have indicated that DOX can activate toll-like receptor 4 through damage-associated molecular patterns, promoting the formation of nucleotide-binding domain-like receptor protein 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome, which leads to stimulation of caspase-1 cascade. Finally, activated caspase-1 leads to the expression of interleukin-11, interleukin-18, and interleukin-1β (IL-1β), eventually triggering inflammation-induced pyroptosis of cardiomyocytes.23, 24 Increasing of Akt and glycogen synthase kinase 3β also can be induced by DOX, which leads to cardiomyocyte pyroptosis.25 Moreover, Meng et al.26 revealed that DOX can upregulate the expression of long non-coding RNA terminal differentiation-induced ncRNA, therefore enhancing NLRP3 messenger RNA, which eventually leads to cardiomyocyte pyroptosis (Figure 1).

Calycosin is the main effective component present in Astragalus, which alleviates inflammatory reactions and cardiotoxicity by inhibiting pyroptosis induced by NLRP3 inflammasomes.27 Polyguluronic acid, a polysaccharide from alginate, also mitigates DIC through attenuating cardiomyocyte pyroptosis mediated by Peli1–NLRP3 inflammasome.28 These results suggest that NLRP3 may have a potential therapeutic target for preventing DIC related to pyroptosis.

Necroptosis

Necroptosis refers to programmed cell death induced by a variety of stimuli. The pathway is highly proinflammatory, and it plays a role in a variety of physiological and pathological conditions.29 DOX can also trigger necroptosis involves the release of death-signaling cytokines such as phosphorylates receptor-interacting serine/threonine-protein kinase 1 (RIPK1), RIPK3, and mixed lineage kinase domain-like protein (MLKL). Christidi et al.30 revealed that DOX can upregulate tumor necrosis factor-alpha, leading to the activation of tumor necrosis factor receptor-associated death protein and Fas-associated protein with death domain, which ultimately activates the RIPK1, RIPK3, and MLKL to induce necroptosis. On the other hand, DOX can also induce necroptosis by activating calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II association domain through RIPK3, which results in activation of mPTP.30, 31 Additionally, DOX can inhibit the expression of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma co-activator 1-alpha (PGC-1α), promoting oxidative stress in the heart and consequently leading to necroptosis32 (Figure 1). To date, there are relatively few studies on the mechanism of necroptosis in DIC. Inhibition of MLKL and calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II association domain could have potent therapeutic efficacy in mitigating DOX-induced cardiomyopathy.

Autophagy

Autophagy is a protective mechanism characterized by lysosomal degradation, and there is strong evidence suggesting that it plays a critical role in regulating cardiac homeostasis and function (Figure 2). The accumulation of autophagosomes induced by DOX is related to the blockage of the lysosomal degradation process, and both acute and chronic DOX-associated cardiomyopathy involve the accumulation of autophagosomes and autolysosomes.35 However, the effects of different DOX doses on the heart have discrepancies in acute cardiac toxicity. Low-dose DOX continuously activates the caspase-signaling pathway, demonstrating changes in mitochondrial permeability transition, while high-dose DOX blocks autophagic flux and increases expression of Bnip3, which can mediate mitophagy.36

Both excessive and insufficient autophagy can impact heart function. Research findings have confirmed that DOX attenuates cardiomyocyte mitophagy via p53-dependent inhibition on Parkin, resulting in the accumulation of damaged mitochondria in lysosomes.37 DOX also disrupts lysosomal acidification and blocks autophagic flux, leading to the generation of ROS in cardiomyocytes, which is associated with reduced expression of transcription factor EB (TFEB) and lysosomal-associated protein 2a in cardiomyocytes.38 Other research has demonstrated that downregulation of TFEB39 or upregulation of lipocalin-2 (LCN2) induced by DOX40 can block autophagic flux and result in the accumulation of autophagosomes. On the contrary, Ozcan et al.41 suggested that cardiomyocyte TFEB knockout attenuated DIC, finding that sustained alternate-day fasting combined with DOX increased TFEB nuclear translocation in the myocardium. DOX also regulates macroautophagy in heart by increasing PI3K activity, augmenting Atg5 and Atg12, activating adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase (AMPK), and decreasing Bcl-2, a negative regulator of Beclin1, to promote autophagy.37 According to a study with a zebrafish model, Atg7 overexpression promotes both basal autophagy and autophagic flux, which is likely to reverse DIC.42 DOX also downregulates the expression of SIRT4 and ribonucleotide reductase M2 subunit, which promotes cardiomyocyte autophagy and apoptosis through the AKT/mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway-mediated autophagy inhibition.43, 44

Qu et al.45 found that DOX exposure induces an increase in GSDMD levels, which promotes autophagy and induces cardiomyocyte apoptosis through the regulation of reactions between FAM134B and LC3. p-STAT3, a known transcription factor for LCN2, induced by DOX, increases cardiac LCN2 expression. Studies have shown that upregulation of LCN2 mediates the accumulation of autophagosomes and leads to adverse reactions such as inflammation and oxidative stress.40 In addition, DOX stimulation leads to a decrease in proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type expression in the heart, inhibiting the activation of SIRT1-dependent FOXO3a and suppressing the activation of autophagy genes regulated by FOXO3a, which is a critical transcription factor regulator in the autophagy process.46 DOX is also an AMPK-targeted inhibitor, activating mTOR and downregulating autophagy levels.47 In summary, DOX triggers the deregulation of autophagy, leading to the accumulation of autophagosomes and lysosomal dysfunction, which could be the potential therapeutic targets for DIC.

Ferroptosis

Ferroptosis is a novel type of programmed cell death characterized by iron dependence, the excessive accumulation of lipid peroxidation, and abnormal iron metabolism that leads to membrane damage. Recently, studies have indicated the contribution of ferroptosis in DIC48, 49 (Figure 2). Mechanically, by intercalating into mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA), DOX accumulates in mitochondria and induces ferroptosis in a mtDNA content-dependent manner. Additionally, DOX inhibits heme synthesis through reducing the abundance of the rate-limiting enzyme 5'-aminolevulinate synthase 1 in this process, which results in decreased utilization of iron and leads to iron overload and ferroptosis in cardiomyocytes.50 Research has shown that FUNDC2, a highly conserved and widely expressed mitochondrial outer membrane protein, can regulate ferroptosis and contribute to DIC, which can be protected by preventing ferroptosis with knock out of FUNDC2.51 Besides, with the function of modulating oxidative stress and autophagy, protein arginine methyltransferase 4 accelerates ferroptosis and exacerbates DIC when it is overexpressed.

Nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2) is a vital regulatory factor in ferroptosis; it also takes part in other pathological mechanisms that include apoptosis, inflammation, and oxidative stress.52 Wang et al.53 revealed that protein arginine methyltransferase 4 promotes enzymatic methylation of Nrf2, limiting its nuclear translocation and subsequently suppressing the transcription of glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4), eventually accelerating ferroptosis in DIC. By interacting and ubiquitinating p62, the ubiquitin E3 ligase TRIM21 negatively regulates the p62-Keap1-Nrf2 antioxidant pathway, which results in DIC via oxidative damage.54 Additionally, ferroptosis can also be suppressed by sirtuin1 through the Nrf2/Keap1 pathway.55 A recent study has demonstrated that EP1 can enhance the expression of antioxidant genes GPX4 and solute carrier family 7 member 11 driven by Nrf2, therefore preventing ferroptosis in cardiomyocytes.56

Mechanistic studies revealed that Spermatogenesis-associated protein 2 (SPATA2) can regulate cell death by recruiting deubiquitinating enzyme cylindromatosis. The C57BL/6J mice with administration of DOX showed the upregulation of SPATA2 and cylindromatosis, as well as enhanced interaction between them, accompanied by increases in ferritinophagy and ferroptosis.57 It has been demonstrated that DOX exposure downregulates the expression of pAMPKThr172, which in turn impairs cardiomyocytes via ferroptosis.58 Furthermore, the present study evidently revealed that upregulation of the AKT can increase SIRT3/SOD2 activity, thereby alleviating DOX-induced ferroptosis.59 In conclusion, the ferroptosis-related pathway is of great importance in DOX treatment. The only clinically approved drug, dexrazoxane, exerts a cardioprotective effect by fusing with free and bound iron.

Mechanisms of DIC

ROS and oxidative stress

Oxidative stress refers to the stress state of an imbalance between oxidation and antioxidation, related to the excessive generation of reactive ROS. It is associated with the weakened antioxidant defense system of the body and disruption of cellular homeostasis, which is considered as the fundamental cause of DIC.60, 61

Research has shown that, whether in vitro or in vivo, overexpression of the Kininogen-1 can aggravate oxidative stress and mitochondrial damage in the heart by DOX-induced downregulation of Nrf2 expression.62 However, miR-152 can activate Nrf2, which inhibits DOX-related oxidative stress and inflammation damage.63, 64 It has been shown that DOX inhibits the PI3K/Akt signaling cascade, thus inducing oxidative stress.65 Nevertheless, monotropein, fibronectin type III domain-containing 5, and irisin can reverse the inhibition of the AKT signaling pathway induced by DOX and alleviate DOX-induced cardiac oxidative stress.66, 67 Besides, through activation of miR-425, irigenin could reduce RIPK1 induced by DOX, ameliorating inflammation and oxidative stress through attenuating ROS production and accumulation.68 Moreover, mechanistic studies indicated that active iron with H2O2 participates in the Fenton reaction, leading to the generation of OH∙ and ROS, which ultimately react with mitochondrial biomolecules and disturb mitochondrial function69 (Figure 3).Fig. 3Schematic representation of the role of oxidative stress, inflammation, and mitochondrial dysfunction in DOX-induced cardiotoxicity. Sini decoction, which has an active ingredient combination, such as songorine, gingerol, and isoliquiritigenin, is an efficient formula against DOX-induced cardiomyopathy. It plays a significant role in cardioprotection by inhibiting DOX-related oxidative stress through increasing mitochondrial membrane potential and suppressing intracellular ROS production.70 Overall, ROS production and oxidative stress induced by DOX mainly through mitochondrial dysfunction lead to cardiomyocyte damage, which subsequently increased the generation of free radicals.71

Inflammation

DOX, on the other hand, could upregulate the levels of tumor necrosis factor-alpha, myeloperoxidase, IL-1β, endothelin-2, and proinflammatory cytokines, as well as the expression of nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB), leading to inflammatory responses and cardiomyocyte damage.72 Research indicates that DOX activates the assembly of NLRP3 with ASC and procaspase-1, subsequently leading to the activation of caspase-1. Activated caspase-1 cleaves GSDMD, resulting in the generation of N-terminal and C-terminal fragments of GSDMD. The N-terminal fragment of GSDMD binds to the plasma membrane, forming transmembrane pores, which induce cell swelling and rupture, leading to the release of numerous inflammatory factors into the bloodstream and the induction of pyroptosis.60 Additionally, DOX exacerbates ROS generation, which can activate the NF-κB pathway, induce the production of inflammatory factors, and further accelerate myocardial injury73 (Figure 3).

Amentoflavone, a naturally occurring biflavone with anti-inflammatory properties, alleviates DIC by suppressing cardiomyocyte inflammation via inhibition of the STING/NLRP3 signaling pathway. Studies have shown that amentoflavone reduced the release of the proinflammatory cytokines IL-1β and interleukin-18.74 Novel pterostilbene dihydropyrazole derivative E1 is a demethylated resveratrol derivative, which can inhibit oxidative stress and decrease inflammation induced by DOX via enhancing Nrf2 pathway and inactivation of NF-κB, respectively.73 Apart from that, a bioactive compound found in rosemary, carnosic acid, also protects against DIC through reducing inflammation via inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasomes related to Nrf2.75

Overall, DIC is closely related to inflammatory responses. ROS can activate NF-κB to upregulate various inflammatory factors in the heart, which indicates that oxidative stress can promote inflammation to some extent.76

Mitochondrial dysfunction

The heart has the highest demand of energy compared to other organs. Through mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, the adult heart obtains enormous quantities of Adenosine triphosphate (ATP) necessary to sustain contractile function.77 However, it is known that mitochondria are the main target of DOX, and mitochondrial damage is the property of DIC, referred to as hallmark, significantly impairing heart function (Figure 3). First, DOX can cause mitochondrial dysfunction directly through contributing to derangement of the electron transport chain.78 Inhibition of mitochondrial bioenergetics has been observed in both acute and chronic DOX-treated hearts.35 Studies have shown that DOX deregulates the expression of genes related to mitochondrial function by binding to the PGC-1α in the presence of TOPIIβ. In addition, by inhibiting the expression of PGC-1α through downregulating AMPK, DOX can facilitate the reduction and oxidation of mtDNA.79 Dhingra et al.80 found that mitochondrial function can be impaired through the inhibition of inhibitor of kappaB kinase beta-mediated NF-κB activation by DOX, thereby activating Bnip3, which serves as a trigger for CypD-mediated mPTP opening, resulting in mitochondrial impairment by mitochondrial calcium influx. Other research indicates that DOX also can result in damage in mitochondria by suppressing the expression of Mfn2 and increasing the expression of dynamin-related protein 1 and mitochondrial fission 1 protein.81 Additionally, DOX can inhibit the SIRT1-PGC-1α signaling pathway, thereby suppressing mitochondrial biogenesis and fatty acid oxidation, ultimately restricting mitochondrial biogenesis.82

Defects of mitochondrial quality control mechanisms are also associated with DIC, including mitochondrial proteostasis, mitochondrial biogenesis and dynamics, and mitophagy. The integrity of mtDNA is crucial for mitochondrial biogenesis. The binding of DOX to DNA TOP-2 and cleaved DNA can induce double-strand breaks in proliferating cells, therefore triggering cardiac dysrhythmia.83 Mitochondrial dynamics, including fission and fusion, can be affected by DOX. Not only does DOX enhance the expression of NOX1 and NOX4 and activate dynamin-related protein 1 to induce mitochondrial fission, leading to NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis in cardiomyocytes,84 but also it can inhibit mitochondrial fusion mediated by Mfn2 through upregulating FoxO1, which reduces the transcriptional level of Mfn2, thus leading to DIC.85 As for autophagy, DOX induces DNA damage and activates p53, which directly binds to Parkin in the cytoplasm, consequently blocking Parkin translocation to mitochondria and inhibiting Parkin-mediated mitophagy.86

Signal pathways in DIC

The role of the sirtuin1-mediated signaling pathway

The nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide-dependent deacetylase sirtuin1 (SIRT1) plays a role in regulating various cellular functions, which consist of energy metabolism, antioxidant stress, inflammatory processes, and cell survival.87 It is involved in several pathways that modulate DIC, such as SIRT1/PGC-1α, SIRT1/p53, SIRT1/NF-κB, and SIRT1/forkhead box Os.88 In addition to these pathways, several other signaling pathways are also involved in DIC.

SIRT1/Nrf2 pathway

Nrf2 is one of the master regulatory factors in antioxidant responses, and many of its downstream target genes play crucial roles in preventing intracellular redox and ferroptosis development.89 Other research has indicated that Nrf2 detaches from Keap1 and translocates to the nucleus under condition of oxidative stress, which is accompanied with heterodimerization with small Maf proteins, thereby resisting the oxidative stress via associating with antioxidant response elements.90 Additionally, another study has identified that DOX decreases the translocation of Nrf2 from the cytoplasm to the nucleus. However, Sirt1 protects against DIC by reducing ferroptosis via the Nrf2/Keap1 pathway.55 Previous research has indicated that Nrf2 activation antagonizes DIC through the activation of pAMPK mediated by Sirt1.91

SIRT1/Klotho pathway

Klotho is a soluble antiaging protein, and studies have shown its deficiency correlates with the occurrence and development of atherosclerosis, myocardial infarction, and other cardiovascular diseases. In this sense, Klotho could be considered as the novel target in cardiac injury.92 Klotho deficiency impairs autophagic activity in mice with increased apoptotic activity.93 As an upstream regulator of SIRT1, Klotho upregulates SIRT1 expression and displays antioxidant and anti-apoptotic capacity, which prevents heart from cardiac senescence. On the other hand, its deficiency inhibits SIRT1 activity in the arteries and thus leads to cardiovascular problems.94, 95 Furthermore, under stressful conditions such as myocardial infarction, SIRT1 induces the expression of FGF21 to facilitate βKlotho signaling, indicating that the FGF21-βKlotho signaling pathway could be a protective factor in heart.96

SIRT1/Recombinant Sestrin 2 pathway

Recombinant Sestrin 2 (SESN2) is a powerful antioxidant, which can regulate inflammation and oxidative stress.97 Further research has shown that SIRT1 activation can reduce DOX-induced SESN2 ubiquitination by suppressing the interaction between SESN2 and mouse double minute 2, thus exerting protective effects which consist of reduction of oxidative damage and prevention of apoptosis.98

The role of the AMPK-mediated signaling pathway

AMPK is a highly conserved intracellular kinase that plays a crucial role in the mechanism of DIC, regulating oxidative mitochondrial metabolism, apoptosis, and autophagy via PG1α, mTOR, and unc-51 like kinase 1 signaling, respectively.99 Mechanically, SIRT3 can activate AMPK, initiating protective autophagy by inhibiting mTOR expression, which ultimately alleviates DIC induced by caspase-3.100 However, DOX can inhibit the expression of SIRT3 by upregulating mir-34a-5p in the heart.101, 102 Other studies have found that DOX significantly increases the phosphorylation of connexin 43, leading to abnormalities of gap junction and conduction and resulting in cardiotoxicity. However, AMPK activation can upregulate the expression of Axl and suppressor of cytokine signaling 3 to suppress this process.103 It has been shown that DOX can markedly induce phosphorylation of AMPK, therefore leading to apoptosis in cardiomyocytes.104 Conversely, AMPK phosphorylation can increase the expression of PGC-1α and inhibit oxidative stress by reducing ROS generation and increasing ATP content in cardiomyocytes.105

The role of the AKT-mediated signaling pathway

Protein kinase B (AKT) is a serine/threonine kinase, which plays a crucial role in DIC, including ROS production, inflammation, and mitochondrial dysfunction.106 DOX-elicited inhibition of the AKT/B-cell lymphoma 2 signaling pathway can promote apoptosis.107 It has been indicated that activation of AKT upregulates Nrf2, thus inhibiting generation of ROS and cardiac inflammation.108 Additionally, DOX can inhibit the expression of GPX4, promoting ROS production and inducing ferroptosis, while AKT activation can promote the expression of Sirt3, increasing SOD2 deacetylation and suppressing this process.59 Furthermore, Fan et al.109 found that DOX can inhibit autophagy and induce cardiac senescence via the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway, which can be reversed by Sirt3. However, AKT counteracts these effects of Sirt3. SIRT9 and ERK also participate in the AKT signaling pathway, playing a role in DIC-related ferroptosis and mitochondrial damage.

The role of the peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor-mediated signaling pathway

Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors (PPARs) are transcription factors that regulate gene expression following ligand activation with three isoforms containing PPARα, PPARγ, and PPARβ/δ.110 They are widely expressed in cardiomyocytes, which have anti-inflammatory activity and can promote antioxidant stress. Previous studies revealed that PPARα is involved in cardiomyocyte energy metabolism and the development of cardiovascular disease.111

It was found that PGC-1α agonist can mitigate DOX-induced cardiomyopathy through increasing cardiac tissue mass and reducing vacuolations and fibrosis in cardiomyocytes.32 PPAR-α can attenuate the cardiac hypertrophy by inhibiting the inflammatory pathways through NF-κB, but its disruption in cardiomyocytes augmented mitochondrial dysfunction, including damaged mitochondria and lowered ATP contents. The Glycyrrhiza glabra extract can exert cardioprotective effects by regaining the nuclear SIRT1, PPAR-α, and PPAR-γ levels, which were reduced by DOX in H9c2 cardiomyocytes.112 Astragali Radix can prevent DOX from accumulating fatty acids in the mouse failing heart and alleviate DIC by activating PPARγ to maintain fatty acid homeostasis.113 The current study has also revealed that SIRT6 interacts with PPAR-α to upregulated the expression of genes CD36, CPT1, LCAD, and MCAD associated with fatty acid oxidation, therefore protecting against DOX-induced myocardial senescence and injury.114

Heat shock proteins in DIC

In addition to these signal pathways, recent references revealed that cell stress response proteins such as the classical heat shock proteins (HSPs) also play different roles in DIC. For instance, when pathological myocardial stress occurs, HSP70 expression increases rapidly in transgenic mice, which exhibit limited cellular injury and improved myocardial ischemia.115 However, the current study indicated that DOX administration promoted expression of HSP70 in myocardium and increased its extracellular levels in the heart, which promotes heart failure by activating toll-like receptor 2-p38-nuclear factor-κB-mediated noninfective myocardial inflammation.116 Atorvastatin also improves DOX-induced cardiac dysfunction through increasing Hsp70 expression.117 Apart from that, HSP22, as a new TLR4 ligand, could suppress DOX-induced cardiac injury through blocking TLR4/NLRP3 activation, which can diminish the synthesis and release of proinflammatory factors, ultimately downregulating inflammation and apoptosis in cardiomyocytes. Whereas HSP47 overexpression exacerbated DIC in an NLRP3-dependent manner, leading to cardiac dysfunction and cardiac atrophy.118 Other study has indicated that phosphorylated Hsp27 associates with p53 can inhibit p53-dependent apoptotic pathway in Dox-treated hearts.119 Besides, heat shock protein-derived peptide could protect cardiomyocytes from DOX-induced oxidative stress injury by activating the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway and ameliorate apoptosis through reducing the number of apoptotic cells via deregulating the expression of PARP and caspase-3.120

Therapeutic strategies

Clinical drug

The only medication approved by the Food and Drug Adminisreation for DIC is dexrazoxane, but several new studies have found other drugs that can alleviate or treat DIC.

Dapagliflozin could mitigate DIC by reducing mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and inflammation via the PI3K/AKT/Nrf2 signaling pathway.106 Besides, hydropersulfide supplementation also alleviates DIC by activating Nrf2-dependent pathways, thus suppressing the DOX-evoked surge in ROS.121 Recent trails supported empagliflozin, a selective inhibitor of the sodium glucose co-transporter 2 that reduced ferroptosis, inflammation, and apoptosis,122 markedly improved left ventricle function and remodeling123 in DOX-treated nondiabetic mice. In addition, other research clarified that melatonin and metformin, both having equal measures of cardioprotection via reducing autophagy, oxidative stress, inflammation, and other adverse effects, improve mitochondrial function and dynamics in rats with administration of DOX.124

Natural products

Pae protects the heart from Dox-induced damage by promoting mitochondrial fusion through the PKCε-Stat3-Mfn2 pathway.125 Besides, Tanshinone I and Tanshinone IIA, extracted from Rhodiola rosea, can ameliorate DIC in different ways. The first attenuates Dox-induced generation of ROS in cultured H9C2 cells, protects mitochondrial functions, and reduces apoptotic rates;11 the second attenuates cardiomyocyte apoptosis via Akt signaling pathways.14 Additionally, fisetin,89 through SIRT1/Nrf2 pathways, can inhibit ferroptosis, thus alleviating DIC. WGX50 is an organic extract derived from Zanthoxylum bungeanum Maxim, which can also inhibit ferroptosis by reducing accumulation of iron and ROS and increasing GPX4 expression.126 Apart from that, Li et al. found that glycyrrhetinic acid has been found to notably alleviate Dox-induced ROS generation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and apoptosis in H9c2 cells without compromising Dox's anticancer effects.127 Moreover, other studies have identified Daidzein and dry leaf extract of Apocynum venetum,128 as compounds that mitigate DIC by suppressing oxidative stress and apoptosis. Isoorientin, a natural flavonoid, can reduce DIC through the inhibition of MAPK and caspase-dependent apoptosis pathways.108 Previous research has unveiled that yellow wine polyphenolic compound can protect against DIC by suppressing mitochondria-dependent myocardial apoptosis or in a gut microbiota-dependent manner.129

Irisin, a new hormone-like myokine released during exercise, can markedly alleviated autophagy disorder and ROS accumulation by targeting uncoupling protein 2.130 Moreover, hemopexin is likely transported to the heart via circulating monocytes/macrophages, potentially mitigating DOX-induced ferroptosis to provide cardioprotection.131 Apart from that, experimental evidence suggests that actin-stabilizing protein Leucine zipper protein 1 attenuated DIC through activating 5'-AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) pathway.71 Finally, fibroblast growth factor 21, a member of the FGF family, has a protective effect on myocardium by inhibiting connexin 43 ubiquitination.132

Herbal medicine

Salidroside, a phenylpropanoid glycoside extracted from Rhodiola rosea, can alleviate DIC by inhibiting ferroptosis through AMPK activation.58 Moreover, Granules of Qishen, derived from the classic formula “Zhen-Wu-Tang,” which consists of six traditional Chinese medicines, are effective in the treatment of DIC. It can relieve oxidative damage and protect mitochondrial function partly by coordinating mitophagy and mitochondrial biogenesis133 and by activating the SIRT3/Ac-SOD2 pathway.134 In vitro studies have shown that Chinese herbal medicine Qiangxin recipe promoted the activation of transcription factor Krüppel-like factor 5 to enhance glucose metabolism, ameliorating DOX-induced chronic heart failure.135 Furthermore, Li et al.136 indicated that Di'ao Xinxuekang capsule extracted from Dioscorea nipponica Makino could alleviate oxidative stress and inflammation and protect against DIC. There are also other herbal medicines against DIC. Emodin, an effective ingredient in Polygonum multiflorum, Rheum palmatum, and other herbs, inhibits pyroptosis by alleviating Dox-induced GSDMD perforation in the mitochondrial membrane.137 Aloe-emodin, also found in Rheum palmatum, is a novel ferroptosis inhibitor, which can function by activating Nrf2 and increasing expression of solute carrier family 7 member 11 and GPX4 proteins.138 Finally, mesaconine, isolated from HF. Fuzi, the lateral roots of Aconitum carmichaelii, ameliorates DIC through activation of PINK1-induced mitophagy.139

Others

Beyond pharmaceutical interventions, there are also nonpharmacological approaches available for treating DIC. Research has found that ADAR2 is upregulated in the heart during exercise training and can attenuate DIC, suggesting that exercise is also a therapeutic approach that benefits the heart.140 In addition, noninvasive transcutaneous vagal nerve stimulation prevents DIC by improving cardiac function, rebalancing autonomic tone, and remodeling.141 Research on the relationship between the gut microbiota and cardiovascular disease has clarified that Fecal microbiota transplantation can inhibit excessive mitochondrial fission to exert a protective effect against DIC by altering the gut microbiota and serum metabolites of recipient mice.142

Discussion and conclusions

Numerous studies have elucidated that activation of Nrf2 can exert cardioprotective effects through upregulating Nrf2 target genes to clear ROS and reduce oxidative damage. Meanwhile, Nrf2 activation could downregulate NF-κB, which can reverse the oxidative stress state of kidney in nephritic syndrome rats and reduce proteinuria.143 Experimental evidence suggests that Nrf2 activation also can restore mitochondrial function and improve cellular antioxidant defense of kidney, ultimately attenuating DOX-induced acute kidney injury.144 Besides, Nrf2 also can stimulate cytosolic HO-1, which favors HO-1 nuclear translocation. Thus, it plays neuroprotective effects on cell lines of human neurons.145 In general, Nrf2 can be a potential therapeutic target for preventing DIC, nephropathy, and other disease for its significant antioxidant activity.

This article summarizes the mechanisms and some therapeutic strategies involved in DOX cardiotoxicity by focusing on the basic research in recent years. Many studies have been conducted on the mechanism of various types of programmed cell death of DIC, and their common regulators serve as therapeutic targets. Even though DOX has the high antitumor efficacy, its cardiotoxicity should be taken into consideration. Therefore, the necessity of clarifying the molecular mechanism of DIC should be prioritized. Basic research can provide ideas and direction for mitigating DIC, which may bring promising news to cancer survivors in the future.

SBPS-04 Transsphenoidal Surgery for Pituitary Lesions: A Single Center Ghanaian Case Series and Meta-Analysis of African Literature
Authors: Kwadwo Darko, Bertha Narh, Kojo Okyere Adu, Nima Amini, Krishi Patel, Manasseh Sampana, Reginald Adja Richter, Pearl Tenkorang, Emmanuel Kwadwo Osei Adjei, Mabel Banson, Teddy Totimeh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe experience of transsphenoidal pituitary surgery in Sub-Saharan Africa has not been well characterized. The study aimed to clearly define patient demographics, tumor pathology, diagnostic features, surgical outcomes, and complications associated with this surgery in the region.MethodsA systematic review of the literature using PubMed, Google Scholar, Embase, and Web of Science electronic databases was conducted according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines to identify articles describing transsphenoidal pituitary surgery in Africa.ResultsThe review included six articles comprising 253 patients from 5 countries in Sub-Saharan Africa. The mean age was 42.5 years, with a slight female preponderance of 53%. Visual impairment (88.3%) and headache (85.2%) were the most commonly reported symptoms. Macroadenomas were the most frequently encountered (74.9%), with non-functional adenomas comprising 51.6% of cases across 5 studies. ACTH-secreting tumors (41%) were the most common functional subtype of functional adenomas. The endoscopic transsphenoidal approach was the predominant surgical method in 50.7% of patients, with a complete resection rate of 38.7%. Postoperative complications included diabetes insipidus (20.9%), CSF leaks (11.6%), and meningitis (3.7%). Visual improvement was noted in 56.1%, while 15.8% experienced worsening of vision. The overall mortality rate was 3.6%. The case series of 9 patients confirmed similar findings, with a median age in the fifth to sixth decades. Visual impairment and hormonal dysfunction were the most reported symptoms. Post-operative complications observed included CSF leaks, diabetes insipidus, and intraventricular hemorrhage, amongst others.ConclusionsThis review highlights the promising outcomes of transsphenoidal pituitary surgery in sub-Saharan Africa despite persistent challenges in access, expertise, and multidisciplinary care. Further prospective research is needed to assess long-term recovery and complication rates. Strengthening surgical capacity, investing in technology, and fostering regional collaboration can address existing gaps and increase access to care.

Phacoemulsification combined with goniosynechialysis for acute angle-closure glaucoma: impact of preoperative IOP status
Authors: Chan Xiong, Yangyang Dai, Hai He, Hongdou Luo, Haina Zheng, Xueqing Yu, Lu Yang, Xu Zhang, Zhipeng You
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPrécisTreatment of acute angle-closure glaucoma with phacoemulsification combined with goniosynechialysis significantly improves patients’ visual acuity and reduce intraocular pressure; the preoperative condition has minimal influence on the treatment outcome.PurposeThe study aimed to treat acute angle-closure glaucoma and explore the structural differences of the anterior segment as well as the influence of persistent high intraocular pressure on surgical outcomes.MethodsThis retrospective case-control study included 120 patients (122 eyes) with acute angle-closure glaucoma, divided into two groups based on intraocular pressure two hours post-drug treatment: a normotensive group (21 mmHg or below; 54 subjects/55 eyes) and a hypertensive group (> 21 mmHg; 66 patients/67 eyes). Introduction

Primary angle-closure glaucoma (PACG) is an irreversible, rapidly progressive blinding eye disease characterized by acute onset [1]. Acute angle-closure glaucoma(AACG) constitutes the predominant form of PACG. Research has demonstrated that the incidence of PACG is closely associated with anatomical factors, such as microcornea, shallow anterior chamber, narrow anterior chamber angle, short axial length, thick and anteriorly positioned lens, prominent iris fold, and elevated choroidal intravascular pressure—all established risk factors [2]. Age progression is another significant contributor to PACG development [3].The primary goal in glaucoma management is maintaining intraocular pressure (IOP) at near-normal levels. However, in clinical practice, some AACG patients exhibit persistently elevated IOP despite maximal medical therapy (systemic and topical treatments). For these cases, aggressive IOP reduction before surgery is essential to optimize subsequent treatment outcomes [4]. Laser peripheral iridoplasty (LPIP) is a well-established first-line treatment for AACG [5]. Additionally, anterior chamber paracentesis has been consistently shown to facilitate rapid IOP reduction [6]. In pupillary block cases, lens extraction remains a gold-standard approach to relieve obstruction and control IOP [4, 7–9]. Evidence further suggests that phacoemulsification combined with goniosynechialysis achieves superior IOP control compared to cataract extraction alone [10–12].

To provide a stronger theoretical basis for AACG treatment, we compared anterior segment structures between normotensive and hypertensive groups, while also analyzing postoperative changes in visual acuity (VA), IOP, and surgical complications.

Materials and methods

Experimental design

This retrospective case-control study enrolled AACG patients from the Glaucoma Department of the Affiliated Eye Hospital of Nanchang University between September 2023 and September 2024. Based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, 120 cases (122 eyes) were included. After two hours of pharmacological intervention, patients were divided into two groups depending on whether their IOP decreased to ≤ 21 mmHg: the normotensive group (55 eyes) and the hypertensive group (67 eyes). In the hypertensive group, some patients received additional interventions (e.g., anterior chamber paracentesis or LPIP, alone or combined) until IOP was reduced to ≤ 30 mmHg. All participants underwent phacoemulsification with intraocular lens implantation and goniosynechialysis (PEI-GSL) within 2–3 days of admission. Additional procedures (e.g., pupilloplasty, tension ring implantation, or posterior vitrectomy) were performed as needed, based on intraoperative findings. Normally distributed data—including axial length (AL), white-to-white (WTW), anterior chamber depth (ACD), lens vault (LV), scleral spur distance (SSD), ciliary sulcus distance (CSD), iris thickness at 500 μm from the scleral spur (IT500), preoperative(Preop) peak IOP, and postoperative(Postop) IOP—were analyzed using independent samples t-tests. Non-normally distributed data (e.g., iris curvature [IC], lens thickness [LT], endothelial cell density [ECD], preoperative and postoperative uncorrected VA, and IOP changes) were assessed with the Mann-Whitney U test. Surgical IOP changes were evaluated with paired t-tests, while VA changes (preoperative vs. postoperative) were analyzed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Study subjects

Basic information

Inclusion criteria: (1) Clinical diagnosis of AACG, as defined by standard criteria [13]; (2) Normotensive group: IOP ≤ 21 mmHg after 2 h of pharmacological treatment; (3) Hypertensive group: IOP > 21 mmHg after 2 h of pharmacological treatment, with subsequent reduction to ≤ 30 mmHg following additional interventions (anterior chamber paracentesis, LPIP, or combined procedures). (4) Preoperative refractive status: Spherical equivalent: +2.00DS to -6.00DS, Cylindrical correction: +2.00DC to -2.00DC.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Patients with systemic comorbidities including: Severe cardiovascular diseases, Hepatic or renal dysfunction, Uncontrolled diabetes mellitus or hypertension, Known allergies to study medications; (2) Other glaucoma subtypes (e.g., primary open-angle, secondary, or congenital glaucoma); (3) Active ocular infections (uncontrolled at the time of screening); (4) Ocular history of: Trauma, Laser procedures (e.g., iridotomy, trabeculoplasty), Prior intraocular surgery; (5) Congenital ocular disorders (e.g., corneal dystrophy, keratoconus); (6) Inability to comply with examinations, surgical intervention, or follow-up protocols.

This study was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of the Affiliated Eye Hospital of Nanchang University (Approval No. YLP20250059) and adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki guidelines. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants or their legal guardians prior to enrollment.

Examination

(1) Comprehensive Preoperative Examination: All patients underwent standardized preoperative evaluations, including: 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG); Blood pressure measurement (seated, after 5 min of rest); Hematological assessments: Complete blood count (CBC); Fasting blood glucose; Lipid profile (total cholesterol, LDL, HDL, triglycerides); Liver function tests (ALT, AST, bilirubin); Renal function tests (serum creatinine, BUN); Urinalysis and Stool analysis.

(2) Ocular Examination: All patients underwent comprehensive ophthalmic examinations performed by an experienced ophthalmologist, including: uncorrected VA testing using a standard logarithmic chart (results recorded in logMAR units); slit-lamp microscopy (YZ5J, Suzhou LiuLiu Co., China); IOP measurement with non-contact tonometry (NT-510, Nidek Co., Japan); fundus photography (Clarus 500, Carl Zeiss Meditec, Germany); corneal endothelial microscopy (CEM-530, Nidek Co., Japan); gonioscopy (Volk Optical Inc., USA) with angle closure defined as inability to visualize the posterior ≥ 2/3 of pigmented trabecular meshwork under dynamic examination; and optical coherence tomography (OCT) (Cirrus HD-OCT 6000, Carl Zeiss Meditec Inc., USA). Additional biometric parameters (axial length [AL], lens thickness [LT], and white-to-white distance [WTW]) were obtained using IOLMaster 500 biometry (Carl Zeiss Meditec AG).

(3) Ultrasound Biomicroscopy (UBM) Examination (SW-3200 L; Tianjin Sovi Electronic Technology Co., Ltd.): All UBM assessments were performed by an experienced examiner under standardized conditions. Patients received topical anesthesia and were positioned supine in ambient indoor lighting. A saline-filled plastic eye shield was used to gently separate the eyelids, ensuring minimal discomfort. The UBM probe was aligned perpendicular to the limbus without exerting pressure on the globe, while patients fixated on a distant overhead target with their contralateral eye to reduce accommodation artifacts.Both eyes were evaluated separately, imaging all quadrants (superior, inferior, nasal, and temporal) with the pupil as the central reference point. Anterior segment parameters—including ACD, LV, SSD, CSD, IT500, and IC—were measured using built-in UBM software tools. Each parameter was assessed in four orientations (superior, inferior, nasal, temporal), with mean values calculated for analysis.To ensure reliability, a single clinician performed all measurements, while two independent physicians analyzed identical images at separate time points to generate averaged data.

Treatment protocol

Therapeutic regimen

The treatment regimen included: (1) Pilocarpine Eye Drops (2%) (5 mL:100 mg, Shandong Bausch & Lomb Freda) administered four times daily as maintenance, with an additional six doses given within the first hour (one drop every 10 min for loading); (2) Prednisolone Acetate Eye Drops (5 mL:50 mg, Allergan Pharmaceuticals, Ireland) six times daily; (3) Brinzolamide Eye Drops (5 mL:50 mg, Alcon-Couvreur) three times daily; (4)Timolol Eye Drops (5 mL:25 mg, Wuhan Wujing Pharmaceutical) twice daily; (5) Brimonidine Tartrate Eye Drops (5 mL:7.5 mg, Allergan Sales LLC) three times daily; (6) Levofloxacin Hydrochloride Eye Drops (5 mL:15 mg, Yabang Pharmaceutical) four times daily (12 total doses preoperatively).

Systemic therapies comprised: (1) Acetazolamide Tablets (0.25 g, Jiangsu Changjiang Pharmaceutical) twice daily; (2) 20% Mannitol Solution (250 mL:50 g, Sichuan Kelun) via intravenous drip once daily (250 mL); and (3) Dexamethasone (1 mL:5 mg, Tianjin Jinyao Tianyao), with 10 mg diluted in 250 mL physiological saline (Jiangxi Kelun), administered as a once-daily intravenous injection.

Anterior chamber paracentesis

After achieving satisfactory topical anesthesia, position the patient in front of a slit-lamp microscope. Using a 15°corneal scalpel, create a clear corneal paracentesis incision at the 2 o’clock limbal position. Gently press on the incision to confirm slight aqueous humor leakage before slowly withdrawing the scalpel. Next, use a 1 mL syringe with an attached needle to apply gentle pressure at the incision site, allowing controlled fluid drainage until IOP is reduced below 30 mmHg. Finally, administer levofloxacin hydrochloride eye drops (three times at five-minute intervals) as prophylactic treatment.

Laser peripheral iridoplasty

The procedure employs a 532 nm green laser (Lumenis Novus Varia multi-wavelength system) set at an initial power of 150 mW, with 0.5-second pulse duration and a 500 μm spot size. Effective photocoagulation is confirmed by observable iris contraction; if absent, the energy should be gradually increased. Conversely, bubble formation or pigment dispersion warrants reducing the laser power. Approximately 24–36 laser spots are applied over a 360° peripheral iris to achieve full treatment coverage.

Surgery

All patients underwent comprehensive preoperative evaluations, including lacrimal irrigation and conjunctival sac washout, and provided written informed consent for surgery. Prophylactic topical levofloxacin hydrochloride was administered preoperatively to minimize infection risk. All procedures were performed under combined topical and retrobulbar anesthesia by a single experienced ophthalmic surgeon to ensure consistency and safety.

Phacoemulsification with intraocular lens implantation (PEI) was performed as follows: (a) Two clear corneal side incisions (2-o’clock position) were created, followed by viscoelastic injection to stabilize the anterior chamber. (b) A 3.0 mm main clear corneal incision (10-o’clock position) was made. (c) A continuous curvilinear capsulorhexis (CCC) was completed, followed by hydrodissection and cortical cleavage. (d) Phacoemulsification of the nucleus and aspiration of residual cortex were performed. (e) The capsular bag and anterior chamber were refilled with viscoelastic, and a foldable IOL (DCB 00 aspheric intraocular lens (Johnson&Johnson Surgical Vision, Inc.)) was implanted in-the-bag. (f) Goniosynechialysis (GSL) was then performed to release peripheral anterior synechiae. (g) Irrigation/aspiration (I/A) removed all viscoelastic material. (h) Postoperatively, Tobramycin-Dexamethasone ophthalmic ointment (Tobradex) was applied, and the eye was patched.

The patient’s head and the operating microscope were tilted 30–40°nasally and temporally, respectively, to optimize direct gonioscopic visualization of the angle. A gonioscopy lens with viscoelastic coupling was applied, and an angle separator was introduced via the limbal incision to access the opposite angle. The instrument was used to gently depress the apex of iris adhesion, releasing synechiae and exposing the lower two-thirds of functional trabecular meshwork (TM) and scleral spur. This process was repeated after tilting the head 30–40° temporally and the microscope 30–40° nasally, ensuring 240° angle exposure (2 to 10 o’clock) with clear visibility of the TM and scleral spur.

(5) Pupiloplasty: To reduce the pupil to a physiological size, single-stitch iris sutures were placed at the superior and inferior pupillary margins.

Postoperative Management: Levofloxacin Hydrochloride QID, Prednisolone acetate QID, Bromfenac sodium BID, and Tobradex ointment QHS.

Data collection

Collected data included: (1)Demographics: Age, gender; (2) Preoperative ophthalmic parameters: VA, AL, ECD, ACD, LT, WTW, Preop peak IOP, Anterior chamber angle (assessed via gonioscopy and UBM); (3) Postoperative Day 1 data: VA and IOP.

Statistical methods

Data processing was performed using SPSS 26.0 statistical software. The Shapiro-Wilk test was employed to assess the normality of continuous variables. Normally distributed data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while non-normally distributed data are expressed as median (interquartile range) [M (QL, QU)]. Categorical variables were summarized as frequency and percentage [n (%)]. Comparisons of age, AL, WTW, ACD, LV, SSD, CSD, IT500, LT, optic disc parameters, retinal nerve fiber layer (RNFL) thickness, vertical cup-to-disc ratio (VCDR), central foveal thickness (ILM-RPE), Preop peak IOP, postop IOP, and IOP changes were performed using independent samples t-tests. The Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare IC, crystalline LT, ECD, Preop VA, postop VA, and VA changes. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Complications

No patients developed significant perioperative or postoperative complications. Transient mild corneal edema and minimal anterior chamber pupillary exudation were observed in some cases postoperatively; these resolved completely within two weeks without intervention.

Discussion

PACG is highly prevalent in Asia, with AACG being its predominant manifestation. The incidence of PACG is influenced by factors such as gender, age, race, and geographic location. Its prevalence increases with age, and epidemiological studies consistently report a higher occurrence in females than in males [14], which aligns with our findings. AACG attacks seldom resolve spontaneously and are typically characterized by severe ocular pain, headache, profound nausea, vomiting, and other debilitating symptoms. Prolonged IOP elevation exacerbates optic nerve damage [15]. Consequently, during an acute AACG episode, rapid IOP reduction is critical to minimize optic nerve injury induced by sustained hypertensive. For patients unresponsive to medical management, immediate LPIP is recommended to terminate persistent hypertensive, reopen the anterior chamber angle, and establish favorable conditions for definitive treatment [16]. Notably, LPIP has been shown to exert no detrimental effects on corneal endothelial cell density [17]. Additionally, anterior chamber paracentesis is an effective intervention for AACG, rapidly lowering IOP, alleviating symptoms, and reducing patient distress, thereby laying a foundation for further treatment and improving clinical outcomes [18].Currently, glaucoma management prioritizes a multimodal surgical approach to optimize treatment efficacy, reduce therapy duration, and mitigate infection risks [19].

The combined surgical approach of PEI with GSL for AACG demonstrates clinical benefits but poses technical challenges. During the procedure, limited operating space and anterior chamber instability increase the risk of corneal endothelial injury. Phacoemulsification-induced corneal deformation, combined with mechanical stress from intraocular irrigation fluids, may cause substantial endothelial cell damage [20, 21]. Postoperative corneal edema frequently occurs, potentially delaying early visual recovery.Notably, the severity of corneal edema correlates with lens nucleus hardness. Denser nuclei require higher ultrasonic energy during phacoemulsification, amplifying endothelial trauma and postoperative edema [22]. This concern is particularly relevant in AACG patients, whose endothelial cells are often compromised by preexisting elevated IOP. The abrupt IOP reduction during surgery further disrupts cellular homeostasis, exacerbating cell loss and edema.While our study did not stratify patients by cataract grade, postop VA improvement was consistent across both groups on postoperative day 1 (Z = -1.201, P = 0.23), aligning with published outcomes [23]. No intraoperative complications were observed, though further research is needed to evaluate surgical outcomes under varying conditions.

Studies have found that shallower ACD is associated with higher risk of acute angle closure [24]. Particularly when ACD is less than 1.7 mm, preventive treatment should be implemented to deepen the anterior chamber and avoid acute angle-closure attacks [25].In this study, the hypertensive group showed significantly shallower ACD than the normotensive group (t = -2.541, P = 0.012), suggesting that shallower anterior chambers correlate with greater difficulty in normalizing IOP through medication alone. However, no significant difference in ACD was observed between genders (t = 0.62, P = 0.536). IT has been linked to narrow angles and angle closure, and may play a role in PACG development [26]. Greater IC exacerbates angle narrowing [27], with studies showing that IC correlates closely with LV. Increased LV heightens pupillary block, pushing the iris forward and raising posterior chamber pressure, thereby worsening iris bombe [28].Research indicates that during AACG attacks without intervention, LV is significantly higher than in pre-clinical stages [29], though it remains unclear whether this is a cause or consequence of acute PACG. PACG patients typically have 0.3–1.0 mm thicker lenses than normal [30]. This thickening increases iris-lens contact, amplifying pupillary block and iris bombe, which narrows or closes the angle.Our Spearman analysis revealed: IT500 was negatively correlated with IC (rs = -0.479, P < 0.01) and LV (rs = -0.207, P = 0.022). IT500 was positively correlated with ACD (rs = 0.442, P < 0.01). IC showed positive correlation with LV (rs = 0.232, P = 0.01) but negative correlation with ACD (rs = -0.208, P = 0.022). PACG predominantly affects females [14]. In our study, female patients had shorter AL, WTW, CSD, and SSD compared to males, indicating more crowded anterior segments and higher acute angle-closure risk.

Multiple factors contribute to corneal endothelial cell (CEC) loss during glaucoma progression [31], but IOP is recognized as the primary factor leading to endothelial cell depletion. Elevated IOP damages CECs through direct mechanical injury, impaired pump function, ischemia, and oxidative stress. Notably, both the degree and duration of IOP elevation show a positive correlation with CEC loss [32].Tham et al. [33] observed that prolonged acute glaucoma episodes resulted in greater ECD loss, while Chen et al. [34] found that acute PACG patients exhibited an 11.3% reduction in CECs, significantly higher than those with subacute (7.6%) or chronic (4.5%) forms of the disease.In this study, the mean CEC density was 2512 (2429–2950) cells/mm² in the normotensive group, compared to 2142.58 (1350–2874) cells/mm² in the hypertensive group, with a statistically significant difference (Z = − 0.399, P = 0.026).Given these findings, we emphasize that rapid IOP lowering remains the top priority. Stable preop IOP control is critical to minimize intraoperative and postoperative complications and to create optimal conditions for successful PEI combined with GSL, thereby improving long-term outcomes.

The efficacy of phaco-GSL in treating AACG has been well established by multiple studies [35]. However, for complex cases involving extremely shallow anterior chambers under hypertensive, further evaluation is still required regarding surgical feasibility, safety, and long-term outcomes, with standardized management strategies for intraoperative challenging situations yet to be established [36]– [37].Aqueous misdirection-induced anterior displacement of the iris-lens diaphragm serves as the key mechanism contributing to anterior chamber shallowing or even disappearance [38]. The collapse of anterior chamber structure further obstructs aqueous outflow, creating a vicious cycle of “elevated IOP-aqueous reflux-vitreous cavity retention” .Therefore, combined anterior vitrectomy is essential for breaking this pathological cascade [39].Our study data demonstrated that 31.34% (21/67 cases) of patients in the high-IOP group required anterior vitrectomy, significantly higher than the 7.27% (4/55 cases) in the normal-IOP group. This difference directly correlates with elevated posterior segment pressure, anterior displacement of the lens-iris diaphragm, and crowded anterior segment anatomy that collectively increase intraoperative surgical resistance under high-IOP conditions.

Patients with AACG frequently demonstrate zonular laxity following pupil dilation or during cataract surgery. To minimize intraoperative and postoperative complications while optimizing visual outcomes, the use of a capsular tension ring (CTR) is typically recommended [40]. The CTR provides sustained, evenly-distributed capsular support that stabilizes the weakened zonules, thereby maintaining capsular bag integrity and intraocular lens (IOL) stability. This mechanical support ensures proper IOL centration, which is critical for achieving the intended refractive outcome.In our study, CTR implantation was performed in 20 cases (36.36%) in the normotensive group and 26 cases (38.81%) in the hypertensive group. Statistical analysis revealed comparable rates of zonular weakness between the two groups (P > 0.05).

Following an acute attack of angle-closure glaucoma, patients often continue to experience photophobia, glare and impaired visual quality despite normalized IOP and partial visual recovery, due to persistent, fixed mydriasis [41]. The underlying pathophysiology involves microcirculatory disruption of iris perfusion during acute IOP elevation, leading to localized ischemia, sectoral iris infarction, and subsequent atrophy and thinning - ultimately resulting in permanent pupillary dilation and loss of reactivity. Pupilloplasty has proven effective in reducing pupil diameter in these cases, significantly improving subjective glare symptoms and enhancing vision-related quality of life [42]. The surgical technique involves mechanical traction to reopen the anterior chamber angle while preventing recurrent synechiae formation, thereby contributing to sustained IOP control. Our modified approach employs a triple-wound, self-securing slipknot that creates an interlocking helical suture structure when introduced into the anterior chamber, maintaining chamber stability while preventing suture loosening. This method demonstrates distinct advantages including technical simplicity, short operative time and minimal ocular trauma. In the current study, pupilloplasty was performed in 16 cases (29.09%) of the normotensive group compared to 38 cases (56.72%) in the hypertensive group, a difference likely attributable to more pronounced iris damage secondary to sustained ocular hypertension, including ischemic injury and anterior displacement of the iridolenticular diaphragm.

A notable finding in this study was that the hypertensive group demonstrated significantly worse preop VA compared to the normotensive group (Z = -3.712, P < 0.01). However, the postop VA difference between groups lacked clinical significance (Z = -1.201, P = 0.23), though the hypertensive group showed greater visual improvement (t = -3.398, P = 0.001). Regarding IOP, while the hypertensive group maintained higher IOP levels both preoperative and postoperatively, it achieved a more substantial IOP reduction (z = -3.387, P = 0.001). Our findings indicate that in AACG, appropriate surgical intervention following combined medical therapy, anterior chamber paracentesis, and LPIP can yield satisfactory outcomes. Although preop VA varies due to corneal edema and disease severity, postop visual recovery becomes comparable between groups.

This study has several limitations: As a retrospective observational study, the relatively small sample size of AACG patients who underwent surgery within 24 h precluded comparative analysis of clinical characteristics and prognoses between the 24-hour surgery group and the 72-hour post-onset surgery group. Furthermore, variations in patients’ disease awareness and treatment adherence made it difficult to accurately estimate the duration of elevated IOP or angle closure, thus preventing adjustment for relevant confounding factors. Some patients may have received miotic therapy prior to UBM examination, which could potentially affect iris parameter measurements, and all UBM data were obtained by a single examiner, introducing potential subjectivity in certain parameters (e.g., ACD, IT). Additionally, technical constraints of UBM prevented comprehensive collection of ciliary body parameters in all enrolled patients. Finally, the limited sample size may affect the reliability of our findings, warranting further validation through larger-scale studies.

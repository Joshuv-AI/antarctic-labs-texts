Prognostic value of stress perfusion cardiac magnetic resonance in patients with known or suspected coronary artery disease and supranormal left ventricular ejection fraction
Authors: Kridttamate Pakdeesrisakda, Yodying Kaolawanich
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPrior studies have shown that a supranormal range of left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) is associated with adverse cardiovascular outcomes. However, the prognostic value of stress perfusion cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) in patients with supranormal LVEF is not well defined. This study aimed to assess the prognostic significance of stress perfusion CMR in patients with known or suspected coronary artery disease and supranormal LVEF.MethodsThis cohort study included patients aged ≥18 years who underwent clinical stress perfusion CMR at an academic hospital in Thailand between 2011 and 2022 and had an LVEF ≥65% on CMR. Patients were divided into two groups based on the presence of myocardial ischaemia on CMR and were followed for major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), defined as a composite of all-cause death, acute coronary syndrome (ACS) or hospitalisation for heart failure.ResultsA total of 531 patients were included (mean age 68.7±11.0 years; 42.6% male; mean LVEF 75.1%±6.2%). Myocardial ischaemia was detected in 124 patients. During a median follow-up of 5.4 years (IQR, 2.1–9.5), 63 MACE occurred. Patients with myocardial ischaemia had a significantly higher rate of MACE than those without ischaemia (3.71 vs 1.74 per 100 patient-years; HR 2.13; 95% CI 1.28 to 3.54; p=0.004). In multivariable Cox regression analysis, myocardial ischaemia remained independently associated with MACE (HR 1.81; 95% CI 1.08 to 3.05; p=0.02). The prognostic value of myocardial ischaemia was consistent across LVEF tertiles (p for interaction=0.79). Other independent predictors included age (HR 1.03; 95% CI 1.001 to 1.05; p=0.03), a history of heart failure (HR 2.65; 95% CI 1.57 to 4.46; p<0.001) and LV mass index (HR 1.03; 95% CI 1.02 to 1.04; p<0.001).ConclusionsIn patients undergoing stress perfusion CMR with supranormal LVEF, myocardial ischaemia detected by CMR provided significant prognostic value for predicting a composite of all-cause death, ACS or hospitalisation for heart failure.Keywords: Magnetic Resonance Imaging, Coronary Artery Disease, Cardiomyopathies

Introduction

Coronary artery disease (CAD) remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide.1 In Southeast Asia, particularly in Thailand, the overall age-standardised mortality rate for CAD was 23.4 per 100 000 population in 2012 and increased to 28.6 per 100 000 in 2016.2 While advancements in the management of acute coronary syndrome (ACS) have improved survival, risk stratification of patients with chronic CAD remains a persistent clinical challenge, particularly in those with atypical symptoms or preserved systolic function.3 4

Left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) is the cornerstone of cardiac functional assessment. Traditionally, higher LVEF is associated with better survival. However, recent epidemiological data suggest a ‘U-shaped’ relationship between LVEF and cardiovascular mortality.5 Patients with ‘supranormal’ LVEF (typically defined as ≥65%) paradoxically experience worse outcomes than those with normal LVEF (50%–65%).6,9 This phenomenon may be driven by factors such as low stroke volume, increased arterial stiffness and concentric remodelling, which are often overlooked in routine practice.6

Cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) is the standard for assessing ventricular volume, mass and function, and stress perfusion CMR is a highly accurate modality for detecting myocardial ischaemia and fibrosis.10 11 Although its prognostic value has been well established in multiple cohorts,10 11 a study by Pezel et al demonstrated the prognostic value of stress CMR in patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), in whom the mean LVEF was 60.3%±6.2%.12 The prognostic value of stress CMR has also been demonstrated in patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction.11 However, its utility specifically in patients with supranormal LVEF remains unclear.

Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the prognostic value of myocardial ischaemia detected by stress perfusion CMR for predicting major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in patients with known or suspected CAD and supranormal LVEF.

Methods

Study design and population

We screened patients aged ≥18 years with known or suspected CAD who underwent clinically indicated stress perfusion CMR at the Division of Cardiology, Department of Medicine, Faculty of Medicine Siriraj Hospital, Mahidol University, Bangkok, Thailand, between January 2011 and October 2022. The inclusion criterion was a resting LVEF ≥65% as determined by index CMR. Exclusion criteria included: (1) severe valvular heart disease; (2) congenital heart disease; (3) specific cardiomyopathies (eg, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, amyloidosis) and (4) incomplete CMR data.

CMR protocol

The CMR studies were performed with a 1.5 or 3.0 Tesla Philips Achieva XR scanner (Philips Medical Systems, Best, The Netherlands) to evaluate cardiac function, myocardial perfusion, and late gadolinium enhancement (LGE). The images for the cardiac functional study were acquired using the steady-state free precession (SSFP) technique in multiple slice short-axis views, two-chamber, three-chamber and four-chamber views. Typical 1.5-T CMR parameters for cardiac functional assessment were as follows: echo time (TE) 1.8 ms, repetition time (TR) 3.7 ms, number of excitations 2, field of view (FOV) 390×312 mm, matrix size 256×240, reconstructed pixel size 1.52×1.21 mm, slice thickness 8 mm, and flip angle 70°. The myocardial first-pass perfusion study involved a 4 min infusion of adenosine at 140 µg/kg/min, followed by the injection of 0.05 mmol/kg of gadolinium contrast agent (Magnevist, Bayer Schering Pharma, Berlin, Germany) and a saline bolus, administered at a rate of 4 mL/s. Using an ECG-triggered, SSFP, inversion-recovery, single-shot, turbo gradient echo sequence, three short-axis slices at basal, mid, and apical left ventricular (LV) levels were obtained. Image parameters included a TE of 1.32 ms, TR of 2.6 ms, flip angle of 50 degrees, slice thickness of 8 mm, FOV of 270 mm, and reconstructed FOV of 320 mm. LGE images were acquired 10 min after administering a second dose of gadolinium (0.1 mmol/kg, at a rate of 4 mL/s) using the 3D segmented-gradient-echo inversion-recovery sequence. These images were obtained in multiple short-axis slices, as well as in long-axis, two-chamber and four-chamber views, similar to the functional images. Image parameters included a TE of 1.25 ms, TR of 4.1 ms, a flip angle of 15 degrees, FOV of 303×384 mm, matrix size of 240×256, in-plane resolution of 1.26×1.5 mm, slice thickness of 8 mm, and a sensitivity encoding factor of 1.5.

Image analysis

Quantitative measurements of LV volume, LVEF and LV mass were obtained from a stack of short-axis SSFP cine images, and LV stroke volume and mass were indexed by dividing by body surface area.13 Perfusion and LGE images were evaluated using visual assessment by CMR-trained cardiologists. Perfusion images were examined, with visualisation of each of the 16 segments (excluding segment-17 at the apex). Inducible ischaemia was characterised as a subendocardial perfusion defect meeting specific criteria: persistence beyond peak myocardial enhancement and for several RR intervals, width exceeding two pixels, following one or more coronary arteries, and absence of LGE in the corresponding segment.13 LGE was deemed to be present only when it was confirmed in both the short-axis and in at least one other orthogonal plane.13 The total number of LGE segments was determined using the American Heart Association 17-segment model.14

Clinical follow-up

Follow-up data were collected from clinical visits and medical records (last follow-up 31 December 2023). The primary endpoint was MACE, defined as a composite of all-cause death, ACS or hospitalisation for heart failure. The secondary endpoint was the identification of independent predictors associated with the occurrence of MACE. Potential predictors analysed included demographic factors (eg, age, sex), clinical characteristics (eg, comorbidities), and CMR parameters (eg, LVEF, presence of myocardial ischaemia and LGE). For patients who experienced multiple events, only the first event was included in the survival analysis.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, V.20.0 (IBM). Continuous variables were expressed as mean±SD. Categorical variables were presented as absolute numbers and percentages. Differences in clinical baseline and CMR characteristics between patients with and without inducible myocardial ischaemia were compared using the student’s unpaired t-test for continuous variables and the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, as applicable.

The Kaplan-Meier method was used to estimate cumulative incidence rates of the outcomes between groups and compared with the log-rank test. Univariable predictors from baseline characteristics and CMR parameters were assessed through Cox-regression analysis to analyse the predictors of primary and secondary endpoints. Variables with a p<0.05 in univariable analysis were entered into the multivariable model using backward stepwise selection. The HRs and 95% CIs were calculated, with a p<0.05 considered statistically significant.

Myocardial ischaemia was detected in 124 patients (23.3%), with a median of 5 ischaemic segments (IQR, 3–9). Compared with those without ischaemia, patients with myocardial ischaemia were older (p=0.01) and had higher systolic blood pressure (p=0.005). The ischaemia group also had a higher prevalence of traditional risk factors, including hypertension (p=0.04), hyperlipidaemia (p=0.04), cigarette smoking (p=0.01), CAD (p<0.001), prior myocardial infarction (MI) (p=0.005) and prior coronary revascularisation (p=0.001). There were no significant differences between groups in history of heart failure (p=0.24), atrial fibrillation (p=0.11) or ischaemic stroke (p=0.58).

Patient outcomes

Over a median follow-up of 5.4 years (IQR, 2.1–9.5), 63 MACE occurred, comprising all-cause death (n=11), ACS (n=16), and hospitalisation for heart failure (n=43). Patients with myocardial ischaemia had a significantly higher rate of MACE than those without ischaemia (3.71 vs 1.74 per 100 patient-years; HR 2.13; 95% CI 1.28 to 3.54; p=0.004) (table 2 and figure 2). Table 2 summarises each outcome in both groups, comparing patients with and without myocardial ischaemia. Kaplan-Meier analyses (figures2 3) demonstrated that patients with myocardial ischaemia had significantly higher rates of MACE (log-rank p=0.003), all-cause death (log-rank p=0.001) and ACS (log-rank p=0.001).

Univariable and multivariable Cox regression analyses for MACE

Univariable Cox regression analysis (table 4) identified predictors of MACE, including age (HR 1.03; 95% CI 1.001 to 1.05; p=0.04), hypertension (HR 3.62; 95% CI 1.31 to 9.99; p=0.01), diabetes mellitus (HR 1.76; 95% CI 1.07 to 2.89; p=0.02), history of heart failure (HR 2.92; 95% CI 1.74 to 4.90; p<0.001), dyspnoea (HR 2.37; 95% CI 1.23 to 4.55; p=0.009), LV stroke volume index (HR 1.04; 95% CI 1.02 to 1.07; p<0.001), LV mass index (HR 1.03; 95% CI 1.02 to 1.04; p<0.001), myocardial ischaemia (HR 2.13; 95% CI 1.28 to 3.54; p=0.004) and presence of LGE (HR 2.88; 95% CI 1.71 to 4.85; p<0.001). In multivariable analysis, independent predictors of MACE were age (HR 1.03; 95% CI 1.001 to 1.05; p=0.03), history of heart failure (HR 2.65; 95% CI 1.57 to 4.46; p<0.001), LV mass index (HR 1.03; 95% CI 1.02 to 1.04; p<0.001) and myocardial ischaemia (HR 1.81; 95% CI 1.08 to 3.05; p=0.02). Figure 4 illustrates the association of myocardial ischaemia with MACE across tertiles of LVEF (65.0%–72.0%, 72.1%–77.9% and >77.9%). Myocardial ischaemia consistently demonstrated higher HRs for MACE compared with no ischaemia, with no significant interaction observed across LVEF tertiles (p for interaction=0.79).

Discussion

In this cohort of patients with known or suspected CAD and supranormal LVEF undergoing stress perfusion CMR, myocardial ischaemia was significantly associated with MACE. During a median follow-up of 5.4 years, patients with inducible ischaemia had more than twice the risk of MACE compared with those without. Importantly, myocardial ischaemia remained an independent predictor after adjustment for clinical and imaging variables. The prognostic impact of myocardial ischaemia was consistent across LVEF tertiles. These findings suggest that stress perfusion CMR provides significant prognostic value in patients with supranormal LVEF.

Increasing evidence suggests that supranormal LVEF may be associated with increased cardiovascular risk. Epidemiological studies demonstrate a U-shaped relationship between LVEF and mortality, with both reduced and supranormal LVEF linked to worse outcomes than normal LVEF.6 9 15 Potential mechanisms include reduced stroke volume, concentric LV remodelling, and increased arterial stiffness, reflecting a maladaptive cardiac state despite preserved systolic function.15 Nevertheless, in our cohort, a higher LV stroke volume index appeared to be associated with MACE, rather than a lower stroke volume index. This may be explained by the fact that our cohort consisted of patients with known or suspected CAD, who may have underlying LV remodelling that differs from that observed in population-based cohorts of patients with supranormal LVEF.6 However, in our study, this association was not independent in multivariable analysis.

Stress perfusion CMR has been widely validated as a highly accurate modality for detecting myocardial ischaemia and predicting adverse cardiovascular events in patients with known or suspected CAD.3 10 16 17 Prior studies have shown that the presence of ischaemia detected by stress CMR is associated with significantly increased risk of cardiovascular events.10 11 16 However, most previous studies included patients across a broad range of LVEF values and did not specifically examine those with supranormal LVEF.

Compared with prior studies focusing on patients with HFpEF or supranormal LVEF, the baseline characteristics of our cohort were generally similar but reflected a slightly higher burden of cardiometabolic risk factors.9 12 In the present study, the mean age was 68.7 years and 42.6% of patients were male, which is comparable to HFpEF cohorts evaluated with stress CMR. For example, Pezel et al reported a population with a mean age of approximately 73 years and a lower proportion of male patients in a study examining the prognostic value of stress CMR in HFpEF.12 Likewise, in a large CMR-based cohort examining outcomes across LVEF ranges, the mean age was approximately 68 years with a similar sex distribution.9 However, our population demonstrated a relatively higher prevalence of traditional cardiovascular risk factors, which may reflect a high-risk clinical referral population undergoing stress CMR for the evaluation of CAD. Importantly, unlike many prior studies in HFpEF in which the mean LVEF was typically around 60%, our cohort specifically included patients with supranormal LVEF (≥65%; mean 75.1%), representing a distinct subgroup in whom the clinical significance of ischaemia has been less well characterised. Therefore, our findings extend previous observations by demonstrating the prognostic significance of stress CMR-detected myocardial ischaemia in patients with supranormal LVEF.

Our study found that myocardial ischaemia remained an independent predictor of MACE even after adjustment for traditional risk factors and CMR parameters. Furthermore, the prognostic value of ischaemia was consistent across LVEF tertiles within the supranormal range, indicating that ischaemia confers increased risk regardless of the degree of supranormal systolic function. These findings are consistent with previous studies showing that ischaemia detected by CMR provides incremental prognostic information beyond conventional clinical and imaging parameters.1011 16 18,20 Our results also align with the study by Pezel et al, which demonstrated the prognostic value of stress CMR in patients with HFpEF.12 However, the mean LVEF in that study was approximately 60%, whereas our cohort included patients with clearly supranormal LVEF. Therefore, the present study extends prior evidence supporting the prognostic utility of stress perfusion CMR to a population with even higher LVEF.

Another important finding of this study is the independent association between increased LV mass index and MACE. LV hypertrophy reflects structural remodelling and is commonly associated with long-standing hypertension, increased afterload, and myocardial fibrosis. In patients with supranormal LVEF, increased LV mass index may represent a compensatory response to chronic pressure overload or increased arterial stiffness. Such remodelling may contribute to impaired diastolic function and increased myocardial oxygen demand, thereby predisposing patients to ischaemia and adverse events. These findings are consistent with prior studies demonstrating that LV hypertrophy is associated with a higher risk of cardiovascular events and mortality across various populations, including patients with hypertension, CAD and HFpEF.21,23 Previous CMR studies have also shown that LV hypertrophy and remodelling are important markers of adverse prognosis, reflecting underlying myocardial structural abnormalities and increased cardiovascular risk.21,23

Our results also demonstrated that patients who experienced MACE had a higher prevalence of LGE, suggesting the presence of myocardial scar or fibrosis. Although LGE was associated with MACE in univariable analysis, it did not remain an independent predictor after multivariable adjustment. This may be attributed to potential collinearity with age, history of heart failure or myocardial ischaemia. This finding aligns with a study by Krittayaphong et al, which evaluated the prognostic value of supranormal LVEF in 3279 patients referred for clinical CMR.9 That study demonstrated that the relationship between supranormal LVEF and clinical outcomes was associated with increased extracellular volume fraction (ECV) but not with LGE.9 These findings emphasise the role of diffuse interstitial myocardial fibrosis in patients with supranormal LVEF. Previous studies have shown that focal fibrosis is typically associated with a reduction in LVEF,24 whereas diffuse fibrosis, as indicated by increased ECV, may occur despite preserved or even supranormal systolic function.25 26

These findings have potential clinical implications. Stress perfusion CMR may be useful for risk stratification in patients with supranormal LVEF, as the detection of myocardial ischaemia may help identify individuals who may benefit from further evaluation or management of CAD. In addition, increased LV mass index, a well-established marker of adverse outcomes—particularly in patients with supranormal LVEF or HFpEF—may provide complementary information regarding cardiovascular risk in this population.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this was a retrospective, single-centre study, which may introduce selection bias and limit generalisability. Second, clinical management and medical therapy during follow-up were not reported, although these factors may be associated with outcomes. However, early revascularisation after CMR was not significantly associated with outcomes. Third, myocardial ischaemia was assessed using visual interpretation rather than quantitative perfusion analysis; however, this approach reflects conventional clinical practice and real-world use. Finally, although the study adjusted for several clinical and imaging variables, residual confounding cannot be completely excluded.

Conclusions

In patients with known or suspected CAD undergoing stress perfusion CMR who were found to have supranormal LVEF, myocardial ischaemia detected by CMR provided significant prognostic value for predicting a composite of all-cause death, ACS or hospitalisation for heart failure. These findings support the potential role of stress CMR in risk stratification in this population.

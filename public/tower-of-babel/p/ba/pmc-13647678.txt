Umbelliprenin co-formulated with selenium nanoparticles downregulates MMP-2, MMP-9 and ICAM-1 expression in triple-negative breast cancer cells MDA-MB-231
Authors: Farnaz Azimi, Ahmad Reza Shahverdi, Hedieh Sadat Shamsnia, Mohammad Hossein Yazdi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesTriple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is an aggressive form of breast cancer with high invasiveness, few treatment options, and a poor prognosis. The key molecules involved in its metastasis include ICAM-1, MMP-2, and MMP-9. Although Umbelliprenin (UMB), a sesquiterpene coumarin compound, has anticancer potential, its poor solubility and bioavailability limit its effectiveness. This study investigated the antimetastatic effects of Umbelliprenin in combination with selenium nanoparticles (UMB-SeNPs) in MDA-MB-231 TNBC cells.MethodsThe effects of UMB and SeNPs on the viability of the MDA-MB-231 cell line were assessed via an MTT test. The gene expression of MMP2, MMP9 (two matrix metalloproteinases), and ICAM1 (transmembrane glycoproteins) was assessed via real-time polymerase chain reaction (real-time PCR) following RNA extraction and cDNA synthesis. Additionally, a scratch test was performed to assess cell migration following treatment with UMB-SeNPs.ResultsUMB decreased the viability of MDA-MB-231 cells in a dose-dependent manner, whereas SeNPs exhibited time-dependent cytotoxicity. The combined treatment elicited significant cell death. Both treatment approaches reduced the expression of MMP2, MMP9, and ICAM1. They also reduced cells’ migration capacity, providing evidence of strong antimetastatic effects.ConclusionUMB and selenium nanoparticles additively inhibited breast cancer cell viability and migration by downregulating the key metastatic markers MMP-2, MMP-9, and ICAM-1. This combined treatment shows promising potential as an effective anticancer strategy for targeting tumor invasion and metastasis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12906-026-05445-9.

Introduction

Breast cancer is the most widespread cancer and is recognized as the second leading cause of cancer-related mortality among women. Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is the most aggressive and poorly prognosed subtype and is characterized by the lowest survival rate [1]. TNBC lacks estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) receptors, which makes it resistant to conventional therapies. However, novel targeted treatments, including poly (ADP‒ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors, immunotherapies targeting programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), and inhibitors of the phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K)/protein kinase B (Akt)/mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) signaling pathway, show promise. These approaches may effectively overcome resistance in aggressive cancers [2].

One of the key requirements of metastasis is degradation of the extracellular matrix (ECM). Matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), especially MMP-2 and MMP-9, are a family of zinc-dependent endopeptidases that play a significant role in the degradation of basement membranes, facilitating cancer invasion and metastasis. Previous studies have demonstrated the role of the PI3K/Akt and mTOR pathways in the regulation of MMP-2 and MMP-9 expression [3, 4].

Intercellular adhesion molecule 1 (ICAM-1) is a cell surface glycoprotein expressed at a low basal level in immune, endothelial, and epithelial cells. These adhesive molecules facilitate cell‒cell interactions and modulate numerous signaling pathways.

Previous studies have demonstrated their influence on the PI3K/PTEN/AKT/mTOR pathways, and their involvement in various cancer metastasis cascades indicates that inhibiting ICAM1 expression can reduce tumor cell invasion and improve prognosis [5, 6].

Consequently, targeting these genes and their associated pathways with active pharmacological agents will yield novel strategies for cancer treatment.

The use of natural compounds has a long history in the treatment of numerous chronic and acute disorders. Natural compounds can either exert a direct tumoricidal effect or act indirectly by overcoming cancer cells’ resistance to chemotherapeutics and minimizing their toxicity to normal cells [7–9]. Umbelliprenin (UMB) is a prenylated coumarin principally found in plants of the Ferula species. It has many biological effects, including anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and anticancer properties. Umbelliprenin promotes extrinsic and intrinsic apoptosis, induces cell cycle arrest, inhibits cell growth, and modulates several cells signaling pathways in breast, lung, colorectal, gastric, prostate, melanoma, leukemia, and fibrosarcoma cells. The most critical signaling pathways influenced by UMB include the wingless-related integration site (Wnt), nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NF-κb), and PI3K/Akt/ERK pathways in diverse malignancies [10, 11]. Consequently, UMB may serve as an effective anticancer drug against certain cancers that have acquired resistance to treatment. Nonetheless, its effectiveness depends on its concentration and bioavailability [12]. Selenium nanoparticles (SeNPs) were used as a preliminary combination approach because of the high lipophilicity, instability in cell culture medium, and limited cellular uptake of UMB [13]. Compared with alternative selenium-containing compounds, SeNPs have superior bioavailability, cost-effectiveness, and reduced toxicity [14]. Research has indicated that SeNPs can operate as both drug delivery systems and direct anticancer protective agents [15]. SeNPs can activate the p53 pathway to induce cell death, inhibit the PI3K/Akt pathway to reduce cell survival, and suppress the NF-κB pathway to reduce inflammation and tumor proliferation [16, 17]. Based on evidence from multiple studies, biogenic SeNPs may be good candidates for cancer therapy alone or in combination with other anticancer agents [18, 19].

This article focuses on the development of innovative targeted therapeutic agents for cancer, utilizing umbelliprenin, a natural molecule, in combination with SeNPs, and evaluating their inhibitory effects on metastasis.

Materials and methods

Ethical consideration

This study consisted solely of in vitro experiments and did not involve human participants or live animals. The research protocol was reviewed and approved by the Research Ethics Committee of Tehran University of Medical Sciences (Approval Code: IR.TUMS.VCR.REC.1397.434).

Umbelliprenin preparation

Purified umbelliprenin powder was previously obtained from the Department of Pharmaceutical Biotechnology, Faculty of Pharmacy, Tehran University of Medical Sciences (Tehran, Iran). A stock solution was prepared by dissolving 10 mg of umbelliprenin in 500 µL of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) (Sigma, UK) and storing it at 4 °C. For experimental use, the stock was diluted to the desired concentrations in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) (Gibco, Germany) without fetal bovine serum (FBS) and refrigerated for subsequent procedures.

Green synthesis of SeNPs

Selenium nanoparticles were synthesized based on a previously reported protocol using Lactobacillus plantarum. Briefly, 1 mL of selenium dioxide (SeO₂) stock solution (254 mM) was mixed with 1 mL of L. plantarum culture (OD₆₀₀ = 1, equivalent to 10⁸ CFU/mL). The mixture was added to 100 mL of Man, Rogosa, and Sharpe (MRS) broth (Liofilchem, Roseto degli Abruzzi, Italy). The culture was incubated at 37 °C for 72 h. To extract intracellular selenium, the bacterial pellets were frozen in liquid nitrogen and then disrupted by ultrasonication. The SeNPs were purified using an octanol/water biphasic extraction system. The resulting nanoparticles were stored at 4 °C for subsequent applications [20].

Characterization of SeNPs

The morphology, particle size distribution, and surface charge of selenium nanoparticles (SeNPs) were characterized using scanning electron microscopy (SEM), dynamic light scattering (DLS), and zeta potential analysis. SEM was used to examine particle shape and surface morphology. DLS determined the hydrodynamic diameter and polydispersity index, while zeta potential measurements assessed surface charge and colloidal stability. Batch-to-batch consistency was confirmed by comparing SEM morphology, DLS size distribution, and zeta potential across independently synthesized SeNP batches, which showed minimal variation.

Selection of SeNPs and umbelliprenin concentrations

The selected concentration ranges for SeNPs (50–250 µg/mL) and umbelliprenin (5–25 µg/mL) were based on preliminary cytotoxicity and biological activity assays, as well as published studies reporting effective doses in similar cell models. For SeNPs, prior studies have reported significant cytotoxic effects and IC₅₀ values within the tens to low hundreds of µg/mL range in cancer cells, justifying exploration up to 250 µg/mL to capture the full dose-response [21]. For umbelliprenin, multiple in vitro studies report effective inhibitory and apoptotic effects at concentrations corresponding to approximately 25 µg/mL (≈ 80 µM), supporting our use of this range to assess dose response [22].

Cell culture

The human triple-negative breast cancer cell line MDA-MB-231 (IBRC C11325) was obtained from the Iranian Biological Resource Center (IBRC), Tehran, Iran. The cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin (Gibco, Germany) and maintained at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO₂.

Cell viability assay

Cell viability was assessed via the MTT (3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide) assay. MDA-MB-231 cells were seeded at a density of 2 × 10⁴ cells per well in 96-well plates and incubated for 24 h at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂. Following incubation, the culture medium was replaced with fresh DMEM containing various concentrations of SeNPs (50, 100, 150, 200, and 250 µg/mL) or UMB (5, 10, 15, 20, and 25 µg/mL). The cells were then incubated under the same conditions for 24, 48, or 72 h.

After each time point, 20 µL of MTT solution (5 mg/mL in PBS; Sigma, UK) was added to each well and incubated for 2 h at 37 °C. The supernatant was subsequently carefully removed, and 200 µL of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) was added to dissolve the formazan crystals. The plates were gently shaken and incubated at room temperature for 15 min. The absorbance was then measured at 570 nm via a microplate reader (ELISA reader).

The MTT assay was performed having three independent biological replicates (separate cell culture plates), and each condition was measured in technical triplicates. RNA extraction and cDNA synthesis

The expression levels of the MMP2, MMP9, and ICAM1 genes were evaluated via real-time PCR. Total RNA was extracted from both treated and control MDA-MB-231 cells via an RNX™ reagent kit (SinaClon, Iran) following the manufacturer’s instructions The RNA concentration and purity were assessed and by absorbance at 260 nm and 280 nm using a spectrophotometer; samples with A260/A280 ratios between 1.8 and 2.0 were used. RNA integrity was evaluated by running samples on a 1.2% agarose gel (Meryer, Shanghai) for 1 h at 100 V, where intact RNA showed clear 28 S and 18 S rRNA bands with no smearing or degradation.

Equal amounts of high-quality RNA from each sample were used for complementary DNA (cDNA) synthesis, which was performed via the First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Fermentas, Lithuania) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. All experiments were performed using three independent biological replicates (separate cell culture plates) per condition (n = 3).

Real-time PCR

Quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) was conducted using SYBR Green Master Mix (Amplicon, Denmark). The specific primers used for the target genes MMP2, MMP9, and ICAM1 and the housekeeping gene β-actin are listed in Table 1. The amplification protocol was carried out on an Exicycler™ 96 system (Bioneer, South Korea) with the following thermal cycling conditions: initial denaturation at 95 °C for 1 min, followed by 40 cycles of denaturation at 95 °C for 15 s and annealing/extension at 60 °C for 60 s. The specificity of qPCR amplification was confirmed by agarose gel electrophoresis of the PCR products. A single band at the expected size was observed for MMP-2, MMP-9, ICAM-1, and β-actin, with no additional bands or primer-dimer products detected (Figure S1). qPCR analysis for each gene was carried out in technical triplicate. β-actin was chosen as the reference gene due to its stable expression across all conditions, as confirmed by consistent Ct values (CV < 5%) and the absence of significant differences between groups (two-way ANOVA, p > 0.05). Relative gene expression levels were calculated via the 2^−ΔΔCt method. Statistical analysis was, performed using GraphPad Prism (version 8.4.3; GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). Data are presented as mean ± SD from at least three independent experiments. Comparisons among multiple groups were analyzed using two-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s multiple comparison post hoc test. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The use of Tukey’s post hoc test was applied to reduce the risk of Type I error associated with multiple comparisons.

Scratch wound healing assay

To evaluate the effects of the treatments on cell migration, a scratch wound-healing assay was performed. MDA-MB-231 cells were seeded into 6-well culture plates with 5% FBS to minimize cell proliferation. Then, the cells were incubated until a confluent monolayer (approximately 90%) was formed. A uniform vertical scratch was made in each well via a sterile 10 µL pipette tip. After removing the detached cells with PBS, the cultures were treated with the desired concentrations of test compounds. Images of the wound area were captured at 0, 24, 48, and 72 h post-scratch using an inverted microscope (Olympus Corp., Tokyo, Japan). The wound closure in each treated cell was compared with the control to assess the potential for cell migration. All the experiments were performed in triplicate [20].

Results

Characterization of SeNPs

The synthesized selenium nanoparticles (SeNPs) were characterized using SEM, DLS, and zeta potential analysis. SEM images revealed predominantly spherical nanoparticles with relatively smooth surfaces and a fairly uniform size distribution (Fig. 1A). DLS analysis showed an average hydrodynamic diameter of approximately 135–150 nm, with a narrow and unimodal size distribution (Fig. 1B). Zeta potential analysis showed a negative surface charge centered at − 30 mV, indicating strong electrostatic repulsion between particles and good colloidal stability of the SeNPs (Fig. 1C). Batch-to-batch consistency was confirmed and showed minimal variation.

Cell viability and MTT assay

The cytotoxic effects of various concentrations of umbelliprenin (UMB), selenium nanoparticles (SeNPs), and their combination were evaluated in MDA-MB-231 cells via the MTT assay. The results demonstrated a dose-dependent decrease in cell viability following UMB treatment (Fig. 2A), with viability significantly declining as the concentration of UMB increased from 5 to 25 µg/mL. Interestingly, prolonged exposure to UMB was associated with a partial recovery in the MTT signal over time, which may reflect adaptive metabolic responses of surviving cells rather than a true increase in cell proliferation. Additionally, the high lipophilicity and potential instability of UMB under cell culture conditions may have reduced its effective intracellular concentration during prolonged incubation [23, 24].

In contrast, SeNPs exhibited a time-dependent cytotoxic effect across all the tested concentrations (50, 100, 150, 200, and 250 µg/mL), with the greatest reduction in viability observed after 72 h of exposure. The maximal cytotoxicity of SeNPs occurred at 150 µg/mL (Fig. 2B).

Notably, combined treatment with 25 µg/mL UMB and 150 µg/mL SeNPs resulted in a significantly greater inhibitory effect on cell viability than either agent alone (Fig. 2C), indicating an additive effect between the two compounds (Fig. 2).

MMP9, MMP2, and ICAM1 gene expression

Gene expression analysis revealed that MMP2, MMP9, and ICAM1 were significantly downregulated in response to treatment with UMB, SeNPs, or their combination in a time- and treatment-dependent manner.

Compared with that of the control, the expression of MMP9 was significantly reduced after treatment with SeNPs alone or in combination with UMB at 48 and 72 h (p < 0.0001). Although, SeNPs alone did not produce a statistically significant additional inhibitory effect on MMP-9 expression compared with UMB treatment alone after 48 h. However, co-treatment with SeNPs significantly enhanced the inhibitory effect of UMB (p = 0.04), resulting in a markedly greater suppression of MMP-9 expression in the combination group. The combined treatment of UMB and SeNPs led to the highest level of downregulation, which could indicate an enhanced inhibitory effect (Fig. 3A).

Similarly, MMP2 expression was downregulated across all treatment groups. The co-treatment group showed a statistically significant reduction in MMP2 expression at 48 and 72 h (p = 0.012, p = 0.0018 respectively).These findings suggest that SeNPs may contribute more prominently to the inhibitory effect observed in the combined treatment and that the suppressive effect of the combination becomes more pronounced over extended exposure times (Fig. 3B).

Likewise, ICAM1 gene expression was reduced following treatment with UMB, SeNPs, and their combination compared with the control group. Among the treatment groups, the combined treatment exhibited the greatest inhibitory effect on ICAM1 expression at both 48 and 72 h (p < 0.0001). SeNPs alone produced only a modest reduction in ICAM1 expression, whereas treatment with UMB resulted in a markedly greater decrease (p = 0.0015), suggesting an enhanced inhibitory effect of the combination therapy. Furthermore, each treatment group showed a significant reduction in ICAM1 expression relative to its respective control group, although the magnitude of suppression was greatest in the combined treatment group (Fig. 3C).

Overall, these data highlight a strong correlation between UMB and SeNPs in terms of their effects on inflammatory and matrix remodeling biomarkers and support future therapeutic considerations (Fig. 3).

Scratch wound healing assay

The invasion capacity of the cells was evaluated via a scratch assay. The treated cells clearly proliferated and progressively migrated to close the gap. In contrast, cells exposed to UMB and SeNPs displayed altered migration patterns. Cells treated with SeNPs exhibited a reduced rate of gap closure in a time-dependent manner; however, the wound area in UMB-treated cells gradually diminished over time. Furthermore, in the combined treatment group, a substantial reduction in cell proliferation was observed. While 25 µg/mL UMB induced less inhibition of migration than 150 µg/mL SeNPs did, co-treatment with 150 µg/mL SeNPs potentiated the inhibitory effect, resulting in decreased cell motility and delayed wound closure. These results are in strong agreement with the data obtained from the cell viability assay (Fig. 4).

Discussion

Breast cancer is the most prevalent cancer among women, accounting for approximately 30% of female cancer cases, and is the second leading cause of death among women globally [25]. Approximately 15–20% of breast cancer cases, often in younger females, are triple-negative breast cancer (ER-, PR-, and HER2-), which is typically more aggressive and has a poor prognosis [26]. Moreover, the 5-year relative cancer-specific survival rate for patients with metastatic TNBC is reduced to 12%, indicating the ineffectiveness of current methods for treating TNBC; thus, the development of new and more targeted therapies is crucial [27].

Natural compounds, which exhibit significant chemical variety, have been thoroughly examined for their anticancer potential, including their ability to induce apoptosis and modify the tumor microenvironment, making them suitable candidates for combination with other therapies. This evolutionary cancer treatment has demonstrated numerous advantages, including enhanced efficacy, diminished adverse effects, decreased drug resistance, a condensed treatment timeline, and lower costs [28].

UMB, a naturally prevalent sesquiterpene coumarin, is present in multiple Ferula species. It has been examined for its exceptional chemical stability under diverse stress situations. UMB demonstrates anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, antileishmanial, analgesic, and antioxidative properties by inhibiting matrix metalloproteinases and proinflammatory enzymes such as 5-lipoxygenase, disrupting P-glycoprotein-mediated drug efflux, and upregulating Mcl-1. Moreover, UMB has substantial anticancer and antimetastatic effects on diverse forms of skin, breast, lung, colon, and gastric malignancies [29, 30]. Several studies have shown the antitumor benefits of UMB. Studies have shown that UMB interferes with the cell cycle at the G0/G1 phase and influences critical activities such as migration, invasion, and angiogenesis. It targets key signaling pathways, such as the Wnt, NF-ĸB, TGFβ, and Fox3 pathways, and controls several apoptosis-related proteins and genes, including P21, P16, BAX, pRB, caspases, Survivin, c-MYC, MMP-2/MMP-9, ICAM-1, cyclins D and E, and CDK4 and CDK2 [31].

UMB exerts antitumor effects by downregulating the expression of MMP2, MMP9, and ICAM1. MMP-2 and MMP-9 are matrix metalloproteinases responsible for degrading extracellular matrix components such as interstitial and basement membrane collagens, allowing invasive tumor cells to invade, metastasize, and promote angiogenesis [32]. MMP-2 and MMP-9 assist in tumor cell invasion and metastasis in TNBC through remodeling of the ECM and the ability of cancer cells to migrate across the ECM. These MMPs can stimulate angiogenesis by releasing proangiogenic factors such as VEGF and TGF-β from the ECM. Hence, there is a correlation between the overexpression of MMP-2 and MMP-9 in TNBC and aggressive tumor behavior, poor prognosis, and chemoresistance [33, 34]. ICAM-1 is an intercellular cell adhesion molecule responsible for promoting leukocyte adhesion to endothelial cells and their transmigration into tissues for the inflammatory response and tumor progression [6]. ICAM-1 plays a key role in metastasis by facilitating tumor cell adhesion to endothelial cells during extravasation. Its overexpression, along with MMP-mediated vessel loosening, promotes tumor cell migration and implantation at distant sites [35]. Previous studies have shown that tumor cell invasion is associated with the shedding effect of MMP-9, which cleaves ICAM-1 on the surface of tumor cells and protects them from the immune system and natural killer cells [36]. ICAM-1 on TNBC cells also helps circulating tumor cells (CTCs) adhere together and form a cluster, which is more likely to survive in the bloodstream and extravasate and metastasize into lung tissue [37, 38]. For example, in a previous study, UMB inhibited the migration and growth of gastric cancer by downregulating both MMP-2/MMP-9 and oncoproteins of key Wnt signaling genes and inhibited β-catenin nuclear translocation and T-cell factor (TCF)/lymphoid enhancer-binding factor (LEF) activity, indicating an antiinvasive effect of disrupting the Wnt pathway [39]. In tumor-bearing mice, UMB significantly inhibited MMP2 and MMP9 expression. It also reduces tumor size and decreases metastasis to the lung and liver. These changes coincided with upregulated interferon gamma (IFN-γ) and E-cadherin and downregulated interleukin-4 (IL-4) and Ki-67 levels [40]. In a previous study, UMB exhibited dose-dependent cytotoxicity against colon tumor 26 (CT26) colorectal cancer cells, with minimal effects on normal L929 cells. Treatment with UMB significantly decreased tumor size; downregulated IL-4, vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), MMP-2, MMP-9, and Ki-67; and upregulated the levels of IFN-γ and E-cadherin. Furthermore, there are significant decreases in lung and liver metastases [41]. In a previous study, UMB inhibited tumor growth in 4T1 mammary tumor-bearing mice and significantly decreased the expression of MMP2, MMP9, and ICAM1 (invasion, metastasis, and inflammation), angiogenesis markers (VEGF, cluster of differentiation 31 (CD31)) and the proliferation marker Ki-67. Additionally, UMB increases E-cadherin and tumor necrosis factor receptor 1 (TNFR1) expression [42]. In 2024, researchers demonstrated that UMB can downregulate the expression of hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF-1α)/VEGF in the T47D cell line. As a result, MMP2 and MMP9 expression is suppressed, and tumor expansion is diminished [43]. Additionally, in 2020, a study showed that SeNPs can inhibit MMP2 and MMP9 expression in non-small cell lung cancer A549 and NCI-H23 cells [44].

Despite UMB being a pharmacologically active compound with considerable biological potential, its poor aqueous solubility, limited bioavailability, rapid metabolism and clearance, and instability under physiological conditions are still challenges. SeNPs are biocompatible nanocarriers with unique intrinsic antioxidant and anticancer properties. SeNPs have excellent antimicrobial, anticancer, antidiabetic, antiparasitic, and antioxidant properties. The anticancer effects of selenium nanoparticles against osteosarcoma, breast, lung, and kidney malignancies are currently the subject of extensive in vitro and in vivo research [45]. SeNPs improve the solubility and stability of hydrophobic compounds, including UMB, shield them from premature degradation when delivered into biological systems, and facilitate more effective drug delivery to diseased tissues via the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect. SeNPs can also influence cellular uptake processes to potentially increase the intracellular accumulation of UMB within cells, which enhances the bioactivity of UMB at lower effective doses [46–48]. Moreover, in our previous studies, we observed that the oral administration of Lactobacillus brevis enriched with SeNPs, which are biogenic nanoparticles, inhibited metastasis in a breast cancer mouse model [49]. These findings led us to assess the antimetastatic effects of UMB and SeNPs on the highly invasive breast cancer cell line MDA-MB-231.

There are several methods to synthesize SeNPs; however, biogenic approaches offer greater advantages over chemical reduction methods because of their greater biocompatibility and bioactivity, lower cytotoxicity, and lower cost. Owing to their relatively high surface-to-volume ratio at the nanoscale and improved solubility, SeNPs can enhance the bioavailability and delivery of other active compounds and exhibit anticancer activity. Several studies have demonstrated the efficacy of SeNPs by initiating apoptosis and minimizing the toxicity of other anticancer drugs. Ramamurthy et al. reported that the combination of doxorubicin with SeNPs increased cancer cell toxicity by up to 50% in comparison with single treatment [50, 51].

In our study, we assessed the gene expression levels of MMP2, MMP9, and ICAM1 via RT‒PCR. Our study demonstrated notable suppression of MMP2, MMP9, and ICAM1 following treatment with UMB, SeNPs or their combination, indicating both time- and treatment-dependent effects. These genes are closely associated with processes such as matrix remodeling, tumor invasiveness, and inflammation, making them relevant therapeutic targets. We observed a significant decrease in MMP9 expression following treatment with SeNPs alone, with an even greater reduction when UMB was combined with SeNPs at both 48 and 72 h. As shown in Fig. 3A, the combined treatment produced the strongest suppression of MMP9, suggesting an amplified effect beyond the effects of each agent individually. Similarly, MMP2 expression was downregulated across all treatment groups, but only the UMB + SeNP treatment caused significant reductions in expression at 72 h. This finding substantially supports the need for prolonged treatment duration for effective suppression of gene expression. The marked reductions in MMP-2 levels in the combined treatment group are shown in Fig. 3B compared with those in the control group. The outcome of ICAM1 expression showed a similar profile, with substantial downregulation evident in both the SeNP-only and combination treatment groups. At both time points, the combined treatment caused the most remarkable reduction. Importantly, while SeNPs effectively reduced ICAM1 expression, the addition of UMB enhanced this inhibitory effect Fig. 3C.

In the cell viability test, MTT assays revealed that combining UMB with SeNPs reduced cell viability by approximately twofold relative to single-agent treatment, indicating an amplified inhibitory effect.

Interestingly, prolonged exposure to free UMB was associated with a partial recovery in the MTT signal over time, which may reflect adaptive metabolic responses of surviving cells rather than a true increase in cell proliferation [24]. In addition, owing to the physicochemical properties of UMB, including the high lipophilicity and limited aqueous solubility in cell culture media [52], its bioavailability and effective intracellular concentration may be reduced, leading to heterogeneous cellular uptake [23]. Consequently, some cells may internalize sufficient concentrations of UMB to induce cytotoxicity, whereas others may remain less exposed and continue to proliferate. As shown in Fig. 2, UMB induced a dose-dependent cytotoxic response, with the strongest effects observed during the early stages of treatment. Therefore, co-administration with SeNPs may enhance the cellular uptake, stability, and intracellular retention of UMB, in addition to contributing their own anticancer activity. The notably increased cytotoxicity observed in the combined treatment groups further supports the enhanced therapeutic effects of UMB and SeNPs [53].

The wound healing assay data demonstrated that the migration ability of UMB-treated cells decreased as the exposure time increased, whereas the opposite effect was observed for SeNPs. As explained on the basis of compound cell toxicity mechanisms, UMB affects cells in the first stages of the G0/G1 phase of the cell cycle, whereas SeNPs have a delayed effect, mostly in the G2/M phase [54, 55]. Moreover, as we integrated the two substances, an enhanced inhibitory effect occurred. UMB affected cells in the first few hours, and with the contribution of SeNPs, UMB absorption was modified, and the inhibitory effect became more consistent during our setpoints. After 24 h, a wider gap was observed in the combination sample than in the SeNP sample, and the same trend was observed for our 72-hour combination treatment compared with the single UMB treatment. In conclusion, both UMB and SeNPs inhibit cancer cell proliferation and downregulate key genes involved in the metastasis process with increased potency.

Conclusion

The coformulation of umbelliprenin with selenium nanoparticles effectively downregulated the expression of key metastasis and invasion-associated markers, including MMP-2, MMP-9, and ICAM-1, in TNBC MDA-MB-231 cells. These findings suggest a potential enhanced therapeutic strategy for targeting the aggressive phenotype of TNBC by modulating the molecular mechanisms underlying tumor progression and cell adhesion. Further studies are needed to confirm these findings in animal models and clinical settings, evaluate their safety and effectiveness, and better understand the underlying mechanisms behind this combined treatment.

Future perspective and limitations

This study was conducted using only MDA-MB-231 cells, which represent the mesenchymal-like subtype of TNBC [56]. Given the well-documented molecular heterogeneity across TNBC subtypes including basal-like, mesenchymal, and luminal androgen receptor (LAR) phenotypes the generalizability of our findings may be limited. Future studies using additional TNBC cell lines from distinct subtypes will further clarify the broader relevance of the observed effects. In this study, UMB and SeNPs were administered as a simple combination rather than a defined drug delivery system. Therefore, the present work provides only an initial assessment of their combined anticancer effects. However, this approach may limit the therapeutic potential of both compounds because of inherent challenges such as poor solubility, limited stability, and restricted cellular uptake. Future studies should focus on incorporating UMB and SeNPs into a well-defined carrier or nanoparticle-based delivery system. Such delivery systems may offer several advantages, including improved physicochemical stability, enhanced targeted delivery and cellular internalization, and reduced effective doses, thereby potentially improving therapeutic efficacy while minimizing off-target effects.

In addition, although qRT-PCR analysis allowed evaluation of gene expression changes, it does not necessarily reflect protein abundance or activity. Therefore, complementary protein-level analyses (e.g., Western blot or ELISA) are warranted in future work to confirm the modulation of MMP-2, MMP-9, and ICAM-1. Although multiple comparison correction methods were applied, further studies with larger sample sizes and expanded statistical models would strengthen the robustness of the findings.

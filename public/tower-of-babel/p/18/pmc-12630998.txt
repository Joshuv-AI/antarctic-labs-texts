Efficacy and Safety of Dupilumab in Children With Moderate‐to‐Severe Asthma: A Systematic Review of Emerging Phase 3 Evidence
Authors: Almutairi Ghadah, Alharbi Aryam, Almutairi Shahad, Alansari Hatun, Aljadani Amani, Bohulaigah Zinab, Alholais Anoud, Alfadhel Abdulmajeed, Abusido Tamer
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundDupilumab is a human monoclonal antibody targeting the IL‐4 receptor. There is a paucity of evidence regarding its efficacy and safety in pediatrics. Pediatric‐specific data are limited, and most evidence comes from recent phase 3 trials and post hoc analyses.ObjectiveTo summarize the current evidence of the efficacy, safety, and biomarker effects of dupilumab in children with moderate‐to‐severe asthma, while identifying key gaps in the pediatric evidence base.MethodsThis study is a systematic review following PRISMA guidelines, and aims to assess the efficacy, safety, and biomarker response of dupilumab in pediatric patients with moderate to severe asthma. We evaluated the studies using the Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool 2.0.ResultsSix studies from 2021 to 2024 met the inclusion criteria: three of them are phase 3 randomized controlled trials and the other three post hoc analyses, mostly including children aged 6–11 years with type 2 inflammation, defined by elevated blood eosinophils and/or FeNO. Both allergic and nonallergic asthma phenotypes were represented across studies, with post hoc analyses indicating consistent benefit in each group. Dupilumab decreased the rate of severe exacerbations annually by 59.3% in children with type 2 inflammation. The study populations are mainly children between 6 and 11 years of age with moderate‐to‐severe asthma, characterized by eosinophilic or allergic phenotypes (type 2 asthma), including those with elevated blood eosinophils and/or FeNO levels. Dupilumab reduced the annual rate of severe exacerbation by ~59%, with greater reductions in children with FeNO ≥50 ppb (69%) or eosinophils ≥300 cells/µL (65%). The lung function showed improvement by 0.15–0.30 L in pre‐bronchodilator FEV₁, accompanied by gains in asthma control and quality of life. Biomarker reductions (FeNO, eosinophils, IgE, TARC) were consistent across phenotypes. Adverse event rates were like placebo; eosinophilia and injection site reactions were more common but usually mild.ConclusionThis systematic review provides an early synthesis of emerging pediatric phase 3 evidence on dupilumab. While results suggest substantial benefits in reducing exacerbations, improving lung function, and modulating type 2 biomarkers, the evidence remains limited to short‐term studies in a narrow age range. Longer‐term follow‐up, broader age representation, and real‐world data are needed to define the long‐term role of dupilumab in pediatric asthma. Prospero Code: CRD42024557750.

Introduction

Asthma is one of the most prevalent chronic respiratory illnesses affecting children worldwide, affecting approximately 262 million children and resulting in 455,000 deaths per year [1, 2]. In the pediatric population, this condition is characterized by variable airway obstruction, leading to symptoms such as wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, and persistent coughing [3, 4, 5].

A considerable percentage of children with moderate to severe asthma exhibit a type 2 inflammatory phenotype, which plays a central role in the disease's pathogenesis. This inflammation is driven primarily by cytokines such as interleukin (IL) −4, IL‐5, and IL‐13, which promote eosinophilic infiltration, IgE production, and airway remodeling [4, 5]. These factors combine to cause the persistence of symptoms and exacerbations. The actual increase in FeNO, blood eosinophils, and serum IgE might emphasize the degree of their systemic reactivity, making them prospective markers of response probability to targeted therapy [5, 6].

Conventional therapies including high‐dose inhaled corticosteroids (ICS), long‐acting beta‐agonists (LABA), leukotriene receptor antagonists, and long‐acting muscarinic antagonists are the primary treatments for moderate to severe asthma in children. Even with the best adherence to these traditional medications, a sizable proportion of the pediatric population continues to have uncontrolled asthma [7, 8]. The growing number of individuals for whom standard drugs fail has motivated the creation and research of biological agents. These medications target specific inflammatory pathways identified as the underlayers [7, 8].

Dupilumab is a human monoclonal antibody, selectively binds to the alpha subunit of the interleukin‐4 receptor (IL‐4Rα), offering a different therapeutic option for moderate‐to‐severe asthma. Thus, it inhibits IL‐4 and IL‐13 signaling, disrupting the main cytokine pathways involved in type 2 inflammatory response [9]. Dupilumab has been shown to reduce exacerbations, improve lung function, and have a well‐balanced safety profile in adult populations with moderate‐to‐severe asthma [10, 11]. Dupilumab also appears to be very effective in various type 2 inflammatory conditions, including atopic dermatitis and chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps, implying that it could be used to address the complex inflammatory profile observed in children with asthma [10].

However, favorable adult findings have not been translated into extensive evidence on the effectiveness, safety, and biomarker responses of dupilumab in pediatric populations. Children have distinct physiological, pharmacological, and developmental factors that necessitate additional research on therapeutic approaches in this age range. Therefore, this study aimed to systematically assess the effectiveness, safety, and biomarker response of dupilumab in pediatric patients with moderate‐to‐severe asthma. In addition to summarizing efficacy and safety outcomes, this review aims to identify key evidence gaps and highlight limitations in the current pediatric data to guide future research. Given the scarcity of pediatric‐specific trials and the emerging nature of phase 3 evidence, our synthesis should be interpreted as an early appraisal of a developing therapeutic landscape.

Methods

Study Design

This systematic review followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta‐Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. The protocol, which incorporated the strategy, source of evidence, eligibility criteria, assessment methods, and quality evaluation, was defined before all steps being taken.

Search Strategy

From January 2020 to December 2024, a comprehensive literature search was conducted in PubMed, Web of Science and Google Scholar. The single and combined terms used in the search were asthma, children, dupilumab, pediatric, moderate asthma, severe asthma, biological therapy, monoclonal antibody, and IL‐4 receptor. In addition, manual searches of reference lists of review articles and included studies were conducted to identify potentially eligible studies.

Eligibility Criteria, Study Selection, and Data Extraction

The studies were randomized controlled trials, post hoc analyses of RCTs, and prospective observational studies conducted with children and adolescents aged ≤18 years diagnosed with moderate‐to‐severe asthma according to established guidelines. This systematic review only included studies of dupilumab administered at any dose or frequency compared with placebo or standard‐of‐care asthma treatment and described measures involving mainly exacerbation rates and lung function as well as adverse events, serious adverse events, and FeNO, blood eosinophils, serum IgE, and TARC, respectively. We included studies published in English between January 2020 and December 2024. Due to the limited number of pediatric RCTs evaluating dupilumab, we included post hoc analyses derived from phase 3 trials. These analyses offered valuable insights into subgroup‐specific efficacy, biomarker responses, and treatment outcomes that were not primary endpoints in the original studies. We excluded case reports, case series, review articles, editorials, commentaries, conference abstract proceedings, studies based on adults only, and studies that evaluated dupilumab for conditions other than asthma. Preclinical and in vitro studies were outside the scope of our investigation. Two independent reviewers screened the titles and abstracts of all the identified records to determine if they met the inclusion criteria. Results from the initial screening of the full texts of potentially eligible studies were retrieved and assessed independently by the same reviewers. We resolved disagreements through discussions with a third reviewer. We developed a standardized pre‐piloted data extraction form. This information was used to collect relevant information from the studies included. Two authors independently extracted information about characteristics (author(s), publication year, country, study design, sample size, and follow‐up duration), participants (age range, mean age, sex distribution, severity of asthma, baseline lung function, exacerbation history, and concomitant medications), dupilumab (dosage, frequency, duration, and route of administration), and comparison (placebo or standard care) descriptions. We extracted outcomes related to efficacy (annualized rate of severe exacerbation, pre‐bronchodilator FEV₁, post‐bronchodilator FEV₁, percent predicted FEV₁, FEV₁/FVC, ACQ‐7‐IA, and C‐ACT), safety profiles (overall AEs, SAEs, rates of discontinuation of treatment, AEs of special interest such as eosinophilia, injection site reactions) and changes in biomarkers (FeNO, blood eosinophils, serum IgE, TARC, and other relevant inflammatory markers).

Quality Assessment

The methodological quality of the included control trials was assessed using Cochrane RoB 2.0, based on the following five parameters: randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, outcome measurement, and selection of the reported result. The assessment included risk grading for each of these parameters as “low risk,” “some concerns,” or “high bias.” Two independent reviewers (initials) performed the quality assessment, and any disagreements were resolved by consensus or through consultation with a third reviewer.

Data Synthesis

We used narrative synthesis methodology to include a summary and interpretation of results from the studies, as anticipated heterogeneity might occur in study populations, reported outcomes, and methodological approaches. We did not perform a meta‐analysis because there were few studies, and the effect estimates were heterogeneous. Instead, we narrated the effect estimates qualitatively, paying attention to the consistency and patterns of results across studies only where appropriate graphs were used to summarize the main findings.

Data Extraction

Figures 3 and 4 in this review were created using data extracted directly from the published results of the LIBERTY ASTHMA VOYAGE trial and related post hoc analyses [12, 13, 14]. For Figure 3, the annualized severe exacerbation rates, subgroup definitions (e.g., FeNO categories, eosinophil thresholds), and corresponding 95% confidence intervals were taken exactly as reported in the original publications. For Figure 4, values for change in pre‐bronchodilator percent predicted FEV₁ (ppFEV₁) over 52 weeks were extracted from the same sources as reported means with standard errors. We did not perform any statistical recalculation or pooling of results; the figures are intended as a visual synthesis of published data rather than independent reanalysis.

Characteristics of the Included Studies

The characteristics of the included studies are summarized in Table 1. This systematic review included six studies conducted worldwide between 2021 and 2024. These studies evaluated the efficacy and safety of dupilumab in children with severe‐to‐moderate asthma. Most included studies enrolled participants aged 6 to 11 years, with a mean age of approximately 8.8–9.2, and one study recruited individuals aged ≥12 years. The sample size varied among these studies, ranging from 350 to 1878 participants. While three of these studies were phase 3 randomized, double‐blind, placebo‐controlled clinical trials, the other three were post‐hoc analyses derived from phase 3 clinical trial data. The primary objective of the included studies was to assess the efficacy and safety profiles of dupilumab in asthmatic children, its impact on controlling asthma, its exacerbations, and improvement in patients' and caregivers' quality of life (QoL). One of these studies investigated pharmacokinetic properties and associated changes in type 2 inflammatory biomarkers. In contrast, another study explored the baseline fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO) level as an independent predictor of therapeutic response. Additionally, the impact of exacerbation history on dupilumab efficacy was investigated explicitly in a separate post‐hoc analysis. Collectively, these studies provide comprehensive demographic and clinical data supporting the use of dupilumab in various pediatric asthma populations.

Fractional Exhaled Nitric Oxide (FeNO)

Several of the studies in this review examined biomarkers commonly associated with type 2 inflammation, including FeNO. Jackson et al. [15] reported median baseline FeNO levels of 20.0 ppb in the group receiving 100 mg of dupilumab and 26.0 ppb in the 200 mg group. By Week 52, dropped to 11.0 ppb and 12.0 ppb, respectively, representing reductions of 47.7% and 55.6% compared with placebo (p < 0.05). Pavord et al. [14] stratified patients by baseline FeNO levels into three groups: low (<25 ppb), medium (25–50 ppb), and high (≥50 ppb). Those with high initial levels showed the most notable improvement, with FeNO declining from 26.0 to 12.0 ppb—a 55.6% reduction. When averaged across both studies, baseline FeNO was approximately 24.0 ppb, falling to 11.7 ppb after 52 weeks of treatment, yielding a mean reduction of about 53%. Although other studies—Bacharier et al. [17], Fiocchi et al. [16], Papadopoulos et al. [13], and Guilbert et al. [12]—did not provide numerical data, they consistently reported a decline in FeNO levels following dupilumab therapy.

Blood Eosinophils

For blood eosinophils, Jackson et al. included only children with baseline counts of 300 cells/μL or higher, noting a 33.3% decrease by the end of the study period. Other studies—Pavord et al. [14], Bacharier et al. [17], Fiocchi et al. [16], and Papadopoulos et al. [13]—used similar thresholds (≥150 or ≥300 cells/μL) and described downward trends in eosinophil counts, though exact figures were not always presented. Guilbert et al. confirmed reductions over 52 weeks, but without reporting baseline values.

Immunoglobulin E (IgE)

Although less frequently reported, Jackson et al. observed a 78.6% decrease by Week 52, though they did not specify initial concentrations. Papadopoulos et al. also documented IgE reductions in allergic subgroups, but without providing specific values. The remaining studies acknowledged IgE as a marker of type 2 inflammation but did not include numerical outcomes. Based on the limited available data, the mean reductions observed were approximately 33% for eosinophils and nearly 79% for IgE.

Thymus and Activation‐Regulated Chemokine (TARC)

TARC is a chemokine that reflects TH2 inflammatory activity. A study included in this review revealed a 43.7% reduction in TARC levels in asthmatic patients treated with dupilumab. The other included studies did not assess TARC reduction.

Integrated Biomarker Response

Collectively, the consistent suppression of multiple inflammatory pathways by dupilumab was observed in the included studies. The reduction in these biomarkers was correlated with significant clinical improvements, such as asthma control, lung function, and reduction in exacerbation risk. The VOYAGE study and allergic asthma assessment demonstrated that these biomarker changes improved asthma control and quality of life. Importantly, dupilumab was found to reduce asthma exacerbation risk regardless of baseline biomarker levels significantly. The magnitude of the benefit appeared to be greater in patients with more pronounced type 2 inflammation at the baseline.

Efficacy Outcomes

The reviewed studies showed that dupilumab demonstrated significant efficacy across multiple clinical endpoints in pediatric patients with moderate to severe asthma. A substantial reduction in the annualized severe exacerbation rate during the 52‐week treatment period was strongly correlated with baseline FeNO levels in the LIBERTY ASTHMA VOYAGE study. Patients with FeNO ≥50 ppb, when treated with dupilumab, showed the most pronounced benefit with a 69.3% reduction in exacerbation in comparison to placebo, while those whose FeNO levels were intermediate (25 to <50 ppb) and lower (<25 ppb) FeNO levels experienced 58.3% and 22.7% reductions, respectively.

Furthermore, the annualized rate of severe exacerbations across all patient subgroups (Figure 3) was reduced in patients treated with dupilumab. In patients with type 2 inflammatory phenotype, dupilumab significantly reduced the annualized rate by approximately 59.3% compared to placebo (0.31 vs. 0.75; 95% Cl: 39.5 to 72.6; p < 0.001). This reduction was more significant (64.7%) in patients with an eosinophil count of 300 cells/mm³ (0.24 vs. 0.67; 95% CI: 43.8 to 77.8; p < 0.001). The efficacy of dupilumab was examined in patients with allergic asthma in two studies [12, 13]. Lung Function Outcomes

Significant improvements in lung function were demonstrated with dupilumab treatment. It demonstrated improvements in forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV1) compared to the placebo. Its efficacy was strongly correlated with baseline FeNO levels. In patients with FeNO ≥50 ppb, a statistically significant increase in FEV1 (0.3 L) was observed, whereas those with FeNO 25 to <50 ppb showed moderate improvements in FEV1 (0.15 L increase, statistically significant) [14]. Overall Adverse Event Profile

After reviewing the six included studies, dupilumab showed robust tolerability among children with moderate‐to‐severe asthma. However, some adverse events have been documented with a negligible cessation rate. The VOYAGE trial provided a comprehensive safety profile for dupilumab compared to placebo [16], as illustrated in Figure 5. To a great extent, it was shown that there was a similarity between dupilumab and placebo in terms of the incidence of adverse events (83.0% and 79.9%, respectively). The most common adverse event in the dupilumab group was nasopharyngitis (18.5%), followed by upper respiratory tract infection and injection site erythema (12.9%).

Serious Adverse Events and Discontinuations

The incidence of serious adverse events (SAEs) was comparable between the dupilumab and placebo groups. The incidence of SAEs in dupilumab‐treated children was slightly higher than that in the placebo group (4.8% vs. 4.5%) [16]. Eosinophilia was more frequently reported in the dupilumab group (8.1%) and in the placebo group (4.3%) [12]. Treatment cessation due to adverse events was infrequent in both the dupilumab and placebo groups (1.8% and 1.5%, respectively). Only one patient with a high eosinophil count required medication discontinuation [12].

Adverse Events of Special Interest Eosinophilia

Although the treatment discontinuation rate due to eosinophilia was low, it was the most frequent adverse event reported among those treated with dupilumab versus placebo (5.9% vs. 0.7%) [12]. Most identified cases were asymptomatic. A case of symptomatic eosinophilia was reported, which required treatment withdrawal and hospitalization.

Parasitic Infections

Parasitic infection was exclusively reported in the dupilumab‐treated group (2.6% vs. 0%). Enterobius vermicularis was the most commonly reported infectious agent (1.8%). Although parasitic infections have been frequently reported, all cases were mild and resolved with appropriate treatment without discontinuing dupilumab.

Injection Site Reactions

As the nature of the dupilumab route of administration proposes, the injection site reaction was more frequent in the dupilumab group, including erythema (12.9% vs. 9.7%), edema (10.3% vs. 5.2%), and nodules (6.3% vs. 2.2%) [12].

Ocular Events

The incidence of conjunctivitis was lower with dupilumab than with placebo (2.6% vs. 6.7%), and one case of keratitis was reported in each group.

Immunogenicity

Anti‐drug antibody response cases in dupilumab‐treated participants were double those in the placebo group (6.3% and 3.0%, respectively). However, an association between this immunogenic response and the efficacy and safety of dupilumab has not been identified.

Discussion

Efficacy Outcome

In regards with Exacerbations, across all included pediatric studies, dupilumab significantly reduced the annualized rate of severe exacerbations in children with moderate‐to‐severe asthma. In the VOYAGE trial and related post hoc analyses, the average reduction was approximately 59% in children with type 2 inflammation, with the greatest benefit observed in those with elevated baseline FeNO (≥50 ppb, 69% reduction) or higher blood eosinophils (≥300 cells/µL, 65% reduction) [12, 14]. These benefits were seen in both allergic and nonallergic phenotypes, suggesting broader applicability than biologics restricted to allergic asthma. Comparable reductions have been reported in adult trials, typically ranging from 47% to 70%, depending on biomarker‐defined subgroups. While looking into lung functions revealed that pediatric severe asthma patients had improvements in pre‐bronchodilator FEV₁ ranged from 0.15 to 0.30 L, with larger gains in children with higher baseline FeNO. These figures are on par with adult data, where changes ranged from 0.13 to 0.32 L. Sustained increases in FEV₁ over 52 weeks, as seen in VOYAGE, coincided with fewer exacerbations, reinforcing the clinical relevance of lung function gains. On another aspect, one of the very important is quality of life; treatment with dupilumab improved scores on the Pediatric Asthma Quality of Life Questionnaire (PAQLQ‐IA) and the Asthma Control Questionnaire (ACQ‐7‐IA). This mirrors adult findings, where clinically meaningful improvements in asthma control and quality of life have also been demonstrated, suggesting benefits that extend beyond physiological measures to everyday functioning. Across studies, dupilumab consistently reduced FeNO, blood eosinophils, total IgE, and TARC levels, with these reductions correlating with clinical improvements. The pattern is similar to that in adults, although pediatric data are fewer and follow‐up periods are shorter. Notably, biomarker reductions occurred in both allergic and nonallergic asthma subgroups. On a different aspect of this review, we compared Dupilumab to other biologics. Omalizumab, that has demonstrated efficacy primarily in allergic asthma and requires sensitization to perennial allergens for eligibility. Dupilumab, by contrast, is effective in both allergic and nonallergic phenotypes with type 2 inflammation, broadening its potential use. Tezepelumab, targeting thymic stromal lymphopoietin (TSLP), has shown efficacy independent of baseline biomarkers in adults, but pediatric data are scarce, and no head‐to‐head trials with dupilumab are available. This makes it challenging to directly rank these agents in pediatric practice. Lastly, regarding Immunogenicity and ADA Context. Anti‐drug antibodies (ADAs) were detected in 6.3% of dupilumab‐treated children compared to 3.0% in placebo groups in pediatric trials. These rates are broadly comparable to those in adult dupilumab studies, where ADA incidence typically ranges from 4% to 8% [18]. Importantly, no consistent impact on efficacy or safety has been demonstrated in either age group. In comparison, ADA rates for other biologics vary—omalizumab generally reports <1%, while tezepelumab rates are closer to 5%—highlighting the need for ongoing pharmacovigilance.

Safety Profile of Dupilumab

Dupilumab shows a good and acceptable safety profile in children with moderate‐to‐severe asthma. According to Fiocchi et al. [16], the adverse event incidence was comparable between the dupilumab and placebo groups, at 83.0% and 79.9%, respectively. Nasopharyngitis, upper respiratory infections, and injection site reactions were the most common adverse events. On the other hand, eosinophilia was more frequently reported with dupilumab compared with the placebo group (8.1% vs. 4.3%), but the majority were asymptomatic and rarely necessitated the discontinuation of treatment [12]. Significantly, severe events were uncommon and occurred at similar frequencies with dupilumab and placebo [16]. This would signal continuation of tolerability for dupilumab. Mild parasitic infections and reactions at the sites of injection were manageable and resolved with no significant clinical impact [15]. Thus, the safety profile observed in trials supports the use of dupilumab in children, with close monitoring of eosinophilia and reactions at the injection sites.

Strengths of the Review

A significant strength of this systematic review lies in the comprehensive and systematic synthesis of evidence based on high‐quality phase 3 clinical trials and post hoc analyses. Strict adherence to PRISMA standards and using the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool 2.0 ensured systematic selection and critical appraising of the included studies [19]. Using studies conducted in a wide range of countries maximizes the generalizability of findings for different pediatric populations. In addition, the consistent pattern of biomarker modulation, including decreases in FeNO, blood eosinophils, serum IgE, and TARC, provides a strong mechanistic explanation for clinical benefit. Emphasis on biomarker responses shifts the current review beyond reporting symptoms into highlighting objective biological effects, increasing the validity of the findings.

Furthermore, by including biomarker predictors like baseline FeNO, the findings of this review gain practical clinical relevance. Defining baseline FeNO ≥50 ppb as a predictive biomarker for dupilumab response informs individualized treatment approaches [14]. Compared with previous biologics that acted on IgE, including omalizumab, the efficacy of dupilumab among allergic and nonallergic asthma phenotypes enlarges the population with potential benefits. In addition, persistent improvements in quality of life scores and asthma control parameters across trials indicate dupilumab's effects on a broader aspect of the disease, extending beyond lung function [12]. These results complement the increasing trend toward individualized, biomarker‐guided treatment for children with asthma. Thus, by providing mechanistic and clinical information, this review advances the field's knowledge in the optimal use of biologics like dupilumab in children.

Comparison With Current Treatment Practices and Research Gaps

Though dupilumab illustrates definite advantages, some shortcomings and areas needing further research must be considered against current approaches to managing pediatric asthma. Older therapies, including high‐dose, inhaled steroids and long‐acting beta‐agonists, still shape the framework of asthma management but control the disease in a few children [7, 19]. Dupilumab addresses this unmet need; however, the current evidence base comprises only six studies with relatively short follow‐ups, typically 52 weeks [12, 16]. Moreover, the majority had concentrated on children aged 6–11; adolescents and those younger than this were inadequately represented [15]. Real‐world evidence does not yet exist, as clinical trials exclude children with concomitant diseases or those who are nonadherent.

These shortcomings mean that although dupilumab represents a potential advance, long‐term efficacy, safety, and optimal duration of treatment in a more general population of children need proof.

Lastly, continuous research will be essential to fully define dupilumab's place in pediatric asthma care and overcome the shortcomings in the current evidence. Future trials must enroll more representative patient groups, including younger children, and undertake long‐term follow‐up over more than 1 year to evaluate sustained efficacy and tolerability [16]. Head‐to‐head trials with other emerging biologics, such as tezepelumab, may clarify its place in management settings. Determinations of cost‐effectiveness in settings with constrained resources will also be needed [13]. Among other things, examining combinations with current therapies and real‐world adherence patterns will optimize clinical benefit. With the global burden of uncontrolled pediatric asthma still significant, evidence generation will be needed to ensure the favorable findings observed in controlled trials translate well into everyday clinical practice.

Limitations of the Study

While the results in this review are encouraging, they should be understood in the context of a still‐developing body of pediatric evidence. Only a small number of trials met the inclusion criteria, and much of the data comes from post hoc analyses of a single phase 3 program (VOYAGE). The children studied were mostly between 6 and 11 years old, leaving important gaps in our understanding of how dupilumab performs in adolescents, preschool children, and those with more complex comorbidities. Follow‐up was short, typically limited to 52 weeks, so the duration of benefit and long‐term safety remain unknown. In addition, clinical trial populations are often more adherent and medically stable than those seen in routine practice, which may limit generalizability. These gaps highlight a clear need for longer‐term studies, broader age representation, and real‐world data to better define the role of dupilumab in everyday pediatric asthma care.

Conclusion

Dupilumab significantly reduces asthma exacerbations and improves lung function in children with moderate to severe asthma. These effects were particularly pronounced in individuals exhibiting elevated type 2 biomarkers. Baseline FeNO emerged as a significant independent predictor of treatment response, indicating its potential utility as a clinical biomarker for patient selection in treatment. Dupilumab positively influences essential lung function parameters and modifies critical inflammatory biomarkers including FeNO, blood eosinophils, serum IgE, and TARC. Moreover, Dupilumab demonstrated a favorable safety profile, with adverse event rates statistically comparable to placebo. Eosinophilia and injection site reactions occurred more frequently in patients receiving dupilumab; however, these events were predominantly mild and did not significantly affect treatment continuation. This study situates itself among other biological therapies that demonstrate efficacy and tolerability in treating uncontrolled moderate‐to‐severe asthma in children, especially those exhibiting elevated type 2 biomarkers. Furthermore, findings regarding dupilumab support its role in managing pediatric asthma. The long‐term safety, optimal treatment duration, and efficacy in younger children require further investigation.

Severe Candida Esophagitis Without Oral Thrush in Advanced HIV
Authors: Elizabeth Reznik, Renee Williams
Published: 2026

A 66-year-old woman with untreated HIV (CD4 count 6 cells/mm3), chronic hepatitis B and C, low-level cytomegalovirus viremia, and polysubstance use disorder presented with 6 days of progressive midsternal burning chest pain, dysphagia, odynophagia, and poor oral intake. She had undergone multiple prior upper endoscopies for dysphagia, most recently 3 months earlier, all demonstrating a grossly normal esophagus with unremarkable biopsies. Physical examination was notable for absence of oral thrush. Laboratory evaluation demonstrated pancytopenia and mild acute kidney injury.

Computed tomography of the abdomen demonstrated diffuse moderate esophageal thickening concerning for esophagitis (Figure A). Esophagogastroduodenoscopy revealed diffuse adherent white plaques throughout the entire esophagus (Figure B). Following lavage, the underlying mucosa appeared mildly cobblestoned without ulceration. Biopsies and brushings demonstrated acute fungal esophagitis with necrotic tissue, and Grocott methenamine silver and periodic acid-Schiff stains highlighted fungal organisms. Cytology demonstrated fungal spores and hyphae consistent with Candida species without malignancy (Figure C).

She was treated with intravenous fluconazole and initiated on antiretroviral therapy with improvement in symptoms. This case highlights severe Candida esophagitis as an AIDS-defining illness that may occur despite absent oral candidiasis and previously normal endoscopic evaluation.

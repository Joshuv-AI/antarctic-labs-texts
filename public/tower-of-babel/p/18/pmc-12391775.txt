Exploring knowledge gaps: A mixed-method cross-sectional study on Indian dental students’ perspectives and ethical awareness on artificial intelligence in dentistry
Authors: A Subha, R Poornima, M Varshini
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionIn the realm of healthcare, Artificial Intelligence (AI) has emerged as a powerful tool with the potential to enhance diagnostics, treatment planning, and patient care. This comprehensive survey was conducted among dental students in India to better understand their knowledge, attitudes, and perceptions of the integration of AI in dentistry.Materials and methodA structured questionnaire that consisted of both closed-ended and open-ended questions was designed to gather data on the students’ demographic information, knowledge, attitudes, and perceptions towards AI in dentistry. Additionally, their perspectives on ethical consideration related to AI use was also evaluated. The survey was carried out using Google Forms among 1256 dentistry students from 15 different states in India. Descriptive statistics, and inferential statistical analysis such as Chi-square test were performed to obtain the results.Results1256 dental students responded to the survey and there was a predominantly positive view towards AI in Dentistry. 94 % believed AI would lead to significant advances in dentistry, with 79 % finding it exciting. AI's diagnostic capabilities were high, with 89 % agreeing it can be used definitively and 85 % for prognostic purposes. Over 90 % supported AI for radiographic diagnosis of dental caries, periodontal disease, jaw pathologies, and soft tissue lesions. However, 59 % disagreed that AI could replace dentists. Knowledge of AI applications increased with the year of study, with postgraduate students showing the highest awareness. However, only 26 % of participants were aware of the ethical implications of using AI in dentistry, while 44 % were unaware and 30 % were unsure.ConclusionMost of the participants were aware of the potential benefits of integrating AI in dentistry. There is a need to focus on educating students about ethical concerns when teaching AI in dental education.Keywords: Artificial intelligence, Dental education, Dental students, Ethics

Introduction

Perhaps the most expansive and ancient branch of computer science is artificial intelligence (AI), which covers every facet of simulating human cognitive processes. Ever since its introduction, the AI technology has been used for creating machines that can learn and think like humans to solve the real-world problems.1 There has always been a strong link between AI and explainability, and one early example is McCarthy's Advice Taker, which he presented in 1958 as a “programme with common sense”. This was likely the first time that common sense thinking was proposed as a key to AI.2

Modern medicine requires extensive knowledge acquisition, analysis, and application to address complicated clinical problems. Since the mid-twentieth century, scholars have investigated the potential applications of AI in several disciplines of medicine to reduce manual workload.3 Medical AI programmes assist clinicians with diagnosing, making therapy decisions, and predicting outcomes. These tools support healthcare personnel with data manipulation and knowledge-based tasks.4

Although medical AI appears to be successful with patients, it may face resistance from healthcare workers, for four commonly discussed reasons.5 First, unpreparedness for the promise of digital medicine is owing to a clear lack of fundamental and continuing education in this field. Secondly, the inception of digitization of healthcare systems resulted in a significant rise in administrative burden, mostly related to digital medical records that is one of the major causes of physician burnout. Third, there is growing concern over AI replacing physicians, despite the fact that current and mainstream literature suggests that AI will eventually supplement medical intelligence. Fourth, the existing global shortage of a regulatory structure that defines the idea of accountability in the case of adopting or rejecting algorithm recommendations exposes physicians to potential legal consequences when utilizing AI.5

Due to a lack of education in digital medicine, several private medical and dental schools are preparing their future doctors for the challenge of AI in the field of healthcare by in two ways: firstly, by associating the curriculum with the engineering curriculum; secondly, by applying the concept of electronic medical literacy and employ in an exalted curriculum.5 However, there is still a significant gap to be filled in terms of dental students in India learning about AI and utilizing it in their practices.

With this background, the present study aimed to assess dental students' knowledge, attitudes, and perceptions of AI integration in dentistry across India, focusing on potential benefits, challenges, and ethical concerns, and analyzing differences based on professional seniority.

Methodology

The study was commenced once the Institutional Ethics Committee (EC/2024/1906/CR-45) approved it, and was conducted from July 1, 2024 to September 30, 2024. This electronic-based cross-sectional survey was conducted through a Google Forms link distributed over WhatsApp and email to dental students from various dental institutes across 15 Indian states. Purposive sampling method was used.As this was a prevalence-based cross-sectional survey, the sample size was calculated using the standard formula n = z2 × p × (1-p)/e2 for estimating a single population proportion. The calculation assumed a 95 % confidence level (Z = 1.96), a proportion (p) of 0.5 to account for maximum variability, and a 5 % margin of error (e = 0.05), yielding a minimum sample size of 384. A larger target sample of over 1000 was selected to improve representativeness. The inclusion criteria were students currently studying in dental colleges in India. Students who have passed out from dental colleges, studying in colleges outside India and students unwilling to participate in the study were excluded from the survey.

A structured and pre-validated questionnaire with 26 questions was designed to gather data on four main aspects: socio-demographic information, knowledge, attitudes, and perceptions focusing on potential benefits and challenges.6 Moreover, ethical concerns, and analyzing differences based on professional seniority was studied for the first time among the dental students in the present study. Both closed-ended and open-ended questions were asked in order to provide a comprehensive understanding of the participants' views.

The socio-demographic part of the survey contained 4 questions such as age, gender, year of study, and the state/union territory of the institution where the student is pursuing dentistry. The second section contained 3 questions that were dedicated to knowledge about AI in dentistry. The third section was attitude and perception-based comprising of 17 questions. The responses were categorized into five levels: “Strongly disagree,” “Disagree,” “Not sure,” “Agree,” and “Strongly agree,” with the frequency of each response recorded for each question. The final section with two questions was dedicated to ethical considerations and the student ideas regarding AI, respectively. Of these questions, knowledge, attitudes, and perception-based questions were mandatory ones and the two questions with respect to ethics and ideas were optional for the students. A statement describing the details of this survey was placed at the beginning of the form and only after participants agreed to the survey, were they given the questionnaire to answer. The responses obtained were then compiled, analysed and statistically evaluated to obtain insights into the results.

Statistical analysis

The collected data were analysed using SPSS version 22 Software (IBM SPSS Statistics., Chicago, The USA). Descriptive statistics were used to present the distribution of responses to various questions on knowledge and attitudes towards AI in dentistry, as shown in Table 1. The suitability of quantitative data to normal distribution was examined with the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. One-way ANOVA and independent t-test were used to compare normally distributed data. Tukey HSD test was employed to analyze the multiple comparison data. Inferential statistics was performed using Chi-square to compare gender and year of study with knowledge and awareness of AI in dentistry. A p-value <0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

Results

During the three-months duration of the study, a total number of 1256 students participated in the study. The age of the students ranged between 17 years and 30 years with a mean ± standard deviation (SD) of 21.56 ± 2.64. 79.1 % of the students were females (n = 994); 20.5 % were males (n = 257). 15.4 % were first year students (n = 194), 35.5 % of the responders were in the second year (n = 446), 22.1 % were third year students (n = 277), 6.2 % were in the final year of the course (n = 78), 14.5 % were doing their internship (n = 182), and 6.3 % of the participants (n = 79) were pursuing post-graduation in dentistry.

The findings of the study on dental students' knowledge and attitudes toward artificial intelligence in dentistry revealed a predominantly favourable opinion of AI among dental students. Regarding the awareness regarding the application of AI in dentistry, 69.8 % were aware (n = 877), whereas 30.2 % of the participants were unaware (n = 379). When asked if the participants had a knowledge about AI's working principle, 55 % answered yes (n = 691), and 45 % answered no (n = 565). Upon compiling the source of information, 20 %–23 % of the participants revealed that they knew about AI application through friends, family, newspaper, magazines, university lectures, etc. However, the predominant source of information was through social media (78.1 %). Fig. 1 highlights the source of information details, wherein the less commonly reported sources—including research articles, webinars, and other informal channels such as faculty discussions and online platforms—were collectively categorized as ‘Others’.

Responses regarding source of information about AI applications used in daily life.

94 % of participants believed that AI will lead to major advances in dentistry. 79 % of students found the use of AI in dentistry and medicine exciting. High confidence in AI's diagnostic capabilities was observed, with 89 % agreeing that AI can be used as a definitive diagnostic tool and 85 % for prognostic purposes. Over 90 % of students supported the use of AI for radiographic diagnosis of dental caries, periodontal disease, jaw pathologies, and soft tissue lesions. 88 % agreed that AI should be integrated into undergraduate and postgraduate dental training. However, there are concerns about AI replacing human roles, with 59 % disagreeing that AI can replace dentists. Table 1 provides details on the responses in percentages for all five categories, including “Strongly disagree,” “Disagree,” “Not sure,” “Agree,” and “Strongly agree."

Comparison of the knowledge of dental students regarding AI in dentistry among male and female participants revealed a slightly higher knowledge regarding AI among the male participants; whereas awareness of AI applications in dentistry was high in both genders, with no significant difference (Table 2). Also, knowledge regarding AI in dentistry was found to increase with the year of study of the participants, with postgraduate students showing the highest awareness (Table 3). Dental students exhibited a robust positive perception towards AI, recognizing its potential in advancing dental and medical fields, while maintaining a cautious stance on the replacement of human practitioners. Awareness and knowledge of AI applications increased with the year of study and are generally consistent across genders. The question on ethical considerations in AI was designed as optional to respect participant autonomy, given the subjective and value-based nature of the topic. As a result, only 1074 out of 1256 participants responded to this section. These responses were analysed independently, and the percentages reported are based on the valid responses (n = 1074), excluding non-respondents. Only 26 % of these participants were aware of the ethical concerns of AI use in dentistry, while 44 % were unaware and 30 % were uncertain (Fig. 2).

Responses regarding awareness of ethical aspects of the use of AI in dentistry.

Respondents' ideas on the usage of AI in dentistry revealed a widespread acknowledgment of its potential benefits. AI was considered as a valuable asset in enhancing diagnosis, treatment planning, and patient care within the dental field. Importantly, participants recognize that while AI offers promising advancements, it is not intended to replace the expertise of dentists. Instead, it serves as a complementary tool to aid in clinical procedures and treatment management. Education on AI's applications in dentistry emerges as a crucial aspect, indicating a need for its integration into dental curriculums to equip future practitioners adequately.

Discussion

Since the use of AI continues to increase in Dentistry, this finding underscores the need to disseminate its knowledge in classrooms where the potential and correct uses can be taught in a systematic manner. An infrastructural model for implementation of AI in dental education has been suggested in a recent publication.7 Recently a questionnaire-based cross-sectional study with 937 participants pursuing graduate and postgraduation in dentistry; surveyed the attitude, perception, and barriers of AI utilization among dental students in India. The study found that the dental students had a clear idea of AI. When asked if AI may improve the efficiency of diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment planning procedures, as well as patient data management, participants responded positively.8 This is consistent with the findings of the current study, in which the majority of participants found implementing AI techniques intriguing and helpful in determining the diagnosis and prognosis of the cases.

Regarding the attitude of dentistry students towards AI, the results of the present study are similar to many previous studies conducted in many countries throughout the world, such as Central India, Turkey, Saudi Arabia, and Kenya.6,9, 10, 11

A Turkish study assessed the dental students' attitudes and perceptions of AI and its potential applications in dentistry among 1103 students from nine Turkish dentistry schools. According to the findings, only 48.40 % of respondents were familiar with AI tools and their applications in dentistry. The study also found a statistically significant difference in AI expertise between the two genders, with males marginally more knowledgeable than females.6 These findings are consistent with our study, in which a comparison of dental students' knowledge of AI in dentistry among male and female participants revealed a slightly higher knowledge of AI among male participants; however, no gender difference in awareness of AI applications in dentistry was noted.

In contrast to the current study, a study in Saudi Arabia indicated that AI in dentistry had a restricted application. The cross-sectional study used a validated self-reported questionnaire to investigate the perceptions and use of AI among 389 dentistry students and dentists from 22 Saudi locations. The findings indicated that dental practitioners in Saudi Arabia have modest understanding and high rates of positive views towards AI in dentistry. However, AI applications in practice are restricted. The author added that introducing AI into dental curricula was critical owing to the global digital transition.12

The common ethical and legal issues faced during the usage of AI tools are safety, lack of regulation and algorithmic transparency, capricious liability and accountability. These factors tend to have a direct on effect application of AI and AI empowered ascendency, thus affecting the physician-patient interrelation.13,14 To the best our knowledge, this is the first study to test the extent of knowledge on ethical issues as a parameter pertaining to AI usage among the Indian dental students with only 26 % aware of the ethical implications of using AI in dentistry.

Recently, a study analysed a checklist for evaluating AI applications in dentistry and its ability to execute cognitive and intellectual human chores.15 The study stated that though AI is becoming more common in dentistry, the ethical considerations about its use, and users still needs improvement to prevent any mishaps. Moreover, the study emphasized that the ethical issues should be considered when developing, implementing, or receiving AI applications.

Integrating AI into dentistry has challenges that need to be ironed out such as collection of accurate data and availability of multiple AI models. Users of AI must be made aware of ethical concerns in recording and digitally sharing patient data without their consent as this would violate their confidentiality and sensitivity. Data privacy and security are major concerns while using these novel technologies and requires the use of robust software even when sharing de-identified data to avoid unethical exploitation by third parties.16,17 In India, several regulations govern healthcare data protection such as Information Technology (Reasonable Security Practices and Procedures and Sensitive Personal Data or Information) Rules, 2011, that provides guidelines for handling sensitive personal data; and Personal Data Protection Bill, 2019 that aims to provide a comprehensive framework for data protection in India.18,19 Results of this study highlight the need to incorporate awareness regarding the aforementioned ethical aspects in dental education.

Conclusion

Most of the participants were aware of the potential benefits of integrating AI in dentistry and its uses in advancements, diagnosis and treatment planning. However, there was a big lacuna in their knowledge about the ethical aspects of using AI. Hence, there is a need to focus on educating students about ethical concerns when teaching AI in dental education.

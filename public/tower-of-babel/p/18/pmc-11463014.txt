The role of hemodialysis access in intradialysis and interdialysis vital sign variabilities and the development of dialysis headache
Authors: Yuqin Xiong, Nujia You, Shuoyue Qin, Ruoxi Liao, Yang Yu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo determine the relationship of hemodialysis access with vital sign variability and hemodialysis-related headache (HRH). Adult outpatients receiving maintenance hemodialysis (MHD) were prospectively recruited, and 12 consecutive dialysis sessions were monitored. Intradialysis (hour-to-hour) and interdialysis (dialysis day-to-day) vital sign variabilities were assessed via three metrics: the difference between the maximum and minimum values, average real variability (ARV), and residuals. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was used to explore the factors triggering HRH. A total of 91 Chinese MHD patients (60.4% male) aged 58.5 ± 17.2 years were included, with 59 patients using radiocephalic arteriovenous fistulas (RCAVFs) and 32 patients using tunneled cuffed catheters (TCCs) for dialysis. The median dialysis vintage was 26.8 (12.0–44.7) months. Compared with the RCAVF group, the TCC group had significantly greater urea reduction (71.1 ± 9.3% vs. 61.7 ± 10.5%, p < 0.001) and clearance (1.5 (1.2–1.8) vs. 1.1 (1.0–1.4), p < 0.001) rates, higher intradialysis pulse variability and lower intradialysis diastolic blood pressure variability. Some of interdialysis variability indexes in pulse, systolic blood pressure (SBP), and SpO2 were significantly greater in the TCC group than that in the RCAVF group. Age (OR = 0.880, 95% CI = 0.785–0.986, p = 0.028), TCC use (OR = 22.257, 95% CI = 1.190–416.399, p = 0.038), intradialysis SBP-ARV (OR = 2.768, 95% CI = 1.069–7.171, p = 0.036), and blood sodium level (OR = 0.400, 95% CI = 0.192–0.832, p = 0.014) were shown to be independent risk factors for HRH. In conclusion, the use of TCCs has multifaceted effects on intradialysis and interdialysis vital sign variabilities and is independently associated with an increased risk of HRH.Keywords: Maintenance hemodialysis, hemodialysis access, blood pressure variability, pulse variability, dialysis headache

Introduction

Arteriovenous fistulas (AVFs) and tunneled cuffed central venous catheters (TCCs) are the most commonly employed vascular access devices in patients undergoing maintenance hemodialysis (MHD). AVF is chosen over TCC because of its longer patency, reduced risk of access-related infections, and more favorable cost-effectiveness profile, as supported by current research [1,2]. However, the impact of vital sign fluctuations during dialysis or hemodialysis-related headache (HRH) caused by the selected access type remains uncertain. Here, we conducted a prospective cohort study [3] to elucidate the associations between dialysis access type and vital sign variability as well as the occurrence of HRH.

Patients and methods

Outpatients aged ≥18 years who were receiving hemodialysis (HD) for at least 3 months were prospectively recruited from the HD center of the West China Fourth Hospital of Sichuan University, a public tertiary hospital in China between November 2022 and April 2023. Patients with a previous history of any type of primary or secondary headache with attacks occurring >50% of the time between HD sessions were excluded.

Clinical information was gathered through electronic medical records, and the single-pool urea clearance (spKt/V) and urea reduction ratio (URR) were calculated via previously reported formulas [3,4]. HD prescriptions and vital signs, including pulse, blood pressure (BP), and nonfistula finger blood oxygen saturation (SpO2), were recorded at HD-0, HD − 1, HD − 2, HD − 3, and HD − 4 h over a period of 4–6 weeks (12 consecutive sessions per patient). According to the International Headache Society’s third edition (ICHD III, 2018) criteria, HRH is defined as having at least three headache episodes that begin during HD and resolve within 72 h of HD session completion [5]. Intradialysis blood electrolytes were measured via prefiltration blood samples (0.2 mL) with the same handheld blood gas analyzer (i-STAT1, Abbott Point of Care, Inc., USA). Body composition analysis (BCA) was performed via the same body composition monitor (Fresenius Medical Care, Germany) approximately 0.5 to 1 h after a dialysis session. BCA data related to absolute values (|OH/DW|) of the ratio of overhydration volume (in liters) to dry weight (in kilograms) were obtained to indicate the degree of hydration imbalance.

Three metrics were utilized to assess both intradialysis (hour to hour) and interdialysis (HD-0 h, dialysis day to dialysis day) variabilities [3,6]: (1) the difference between the highest and lowest values (max-min); (2) average real variability (ARV), which considers the temporal sequence of fluctuations. The ARV formula is as follows: ARV=∑K=1N−1|(Pulse/BP/SpO2)K+1−(Pulse/BP/SpO2)K|N−1, where N is the number of measurements and K is the order of each measurement. (3) Residuals (RESs), which reflect the disparity between the actual and projected values derived from a generalized linear model based on vital sign alterations. For statistical analysis, two-sample t-test or Wilcoxon rank sum test were used to compare continuous variables, and chi-square tests were used to compare proportions. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was also conducted, and odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated. A two-sided p value <0.05 was used to determine the level of significance. The data were analyzed using STATA 11.0 MP (StataCorp, www.stata.com) and SPSS version 23.0 (IBM, NY, USA).

Results

A total of 91 Chinese MHD patients (60.4% male) aged 58.5 ± 17.2 years were included in this study; 59 patients used radiocephalic AVFs (RCAVFs), 32 patients used TCCs for dialysis, and the median dialysis vintage was 26.8 (12.0–44.7) months. Compared with the RCAVF group, the TCC group had a significantly greater URR (71.1 ± 9.3% vs. 61.7 ± 10.5%, p < 0.001) and spKt/V (1.5 (1.2–1.8) vs. 1.1 (1.0–1.4), p < 0.001); increased intradialysis pulse variability reflected by the max–min (p = 0.003), ARV (p = 0.001) and RES (p < 0.001); and decreased intradialysis diastolic BP variability (BPV) reflected by the max–min (p = 0.039) and ARV (p = 0.04). Several interdialysis variability metrics, including pulse-max-min, systolic BP-RES and SpO2-max-min, were significantly greater in the TCC group than in the AVF group (Table 1).

Discussion

MHD patients often experience increased BPV due to the rapid removal of metabolic substances, vasoactive mediators, and body fluids, leading to dramatic changes in hemodynamics and the internal environment during a short period of HD. Previous studies have demonstrated that increased systolic BPV significantly raises the risk of HRH as well as cardiovascular events and deaths in HD patients [3,6]. In a prior retrospective study, Liao et al. suggested that MHD patients using TCCs for dialysis experienced greater intradialysis and interdialysis systolic BPVs than did those utilizing fistulas [6]. This study contributes to the literature by demonstrating the influence of HD access type on fluctuations not only in BP but also in pulse and SpO2. However, the intradialysis systolic BPV of the TCC group in the present study did not significantly differ from that of the RCAVF group, which is inconsistent with the findings of the prior study; this discrepancy could be attributed to the lower ultrafiltration volume in this study (1648 ± 646 mL) than in the prior study (2233 ± 781 mL) [6], which may have attenuated systolic BP fluctuations during dialysis.

Additionally, the TCC group presented significantly greater intradialysis pulse variability and lower intradialysis diastolic BPV than did the RCAVF group. This might be due to the anatomical differences between accesses: (1) TCCs are typically placed in the central venous system with the catheter tip at the right atrium [7]; thus, the removal of body fluid via TCCs during HD may directly affect ventricular filling and beat rate, thereby increasing pulse variability compared with those using AVFs, which are established in the peripheral vessels. (2) Patients with AVFs have increased blood return to the heart [8] and a relative decrease in peripheral resistance due to fistulas connecting the radial artery and cephalic vein, and ultrafiltration via AVFs during HD further reduces peripheral resistance. This sharply decreased resistance in the peripheral circulation might have contributed to the higher intradialysis diastolic BPV in the RCAVF group than in the TCC group.

Notably, compared with the AVF group, the TCC group exhibited superior urea clearance efficiency and an increased risk of HRH. The possible reasons for this superior clearance rate could be that the proximal accesses had greater blood flow than the RCAVFs did [8] and that the study patients had a relatively young dialysis vintage, during which the number of catheter-related complications was relatively low and thus the access function was good. Past studies have suggested that an abrupt decrease in the blood urea level during HD leads to the development of an osmotic gradient and thereby a shift of water into the brain, resulting in elevated intracranial pressure, brain edema and headache [5,9]; thus, the greater URR and spKt/V in the TCC group might help explain why TCC use increased the risk of HRH. However, this study has the limitations of a small sample size and few events of HRH, which might weaken the reliability of the multivariate analysis.

Conclusions

The use of TCCs may have a multifaceted impact on intradialysis and interdialysis variations in BP and pulse. Furthermore, TCC usage was identified as an independent risk factor for HRH.

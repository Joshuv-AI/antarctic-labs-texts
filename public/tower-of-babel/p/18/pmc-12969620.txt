Predictors of Higher Mavacamten Maintenance Dose in Obstructive Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy
Authors: Mustafa Suppah, Humam Abo Abdullah, Amro Badr, Ramzi Ibrahim, Juan Farina, Chadi Ayoub, Reza Arsanjani, Jeffrey Geske, Said Alsidawi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMavacamten has emerged as an effective therapy for obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (oHCM); however, interindividual variability in maintenance dosing remains a practical challenge in clinical practice. We conducted a multicenter retrospective study of adults with oHCM treated with mavacamten across 3 tertiary sites to identify baseline predictors of higher maintenance dose requirements. Patients with at least 1-year follow-up and stable dosing for 6 months or more were included and categorized into low-dose (2.5-5 mg) and high-dose (10-15 mg) maintenance groups. Among 77 patients (mean age, 60 years; 50.6% men), 28 required high-dose maintenance therapy. Baseline medial ratio of early diastolic mitral inflow velocity to early diastolic mitral annular velocity (E/e′) was significantly higher in the high-dose group than that in the low-dose group (20.0 vs 16.1; P=.018). On multivariable logistic regression adjusting for left ventricular outflow tract gradient, left ventricular ejection fraction, and global longitudinal strain, medial E/e′ remained independently associated with higher maintenance dose requirement (odds ratio, 1.20; 95% CI, 1.10-1.30; P=.04), whereas other echocardiographic, biomarker, and electrocardiographic parameters were not predictive. These findings suggest that baseline diastolic dysfunction, as reflected by medial E/e′, plays a central role in mavacamten dose trajectory and may help inform individualized titration strategies in patients with oHCM.

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) is the most prevalent inherited cardiomyopathy, affecting approximately 1 in 500 individuals, and is characterized by unexplained left ventricular hypertrophy after excluding secondary causes. Up to 70% of those patients exhibit dynamic left ventricular outflow tract obstruction (LVOTO), which may contribute to exertional limitation, heart failure symptoms, and arrhythmic complications. The treatment of obstructive HCM (oHCM) has changed with the advent of myosin inhibitors. Mavacamten, a first-in-class selective inhibitor of cardiac myosin ATPase, improves obstruction in oHCM by reducing contractility.1 In the EXPLORER-HCM trial, mavacamten significantly improved peak oxygen consumption, New York Heart Association class, and health status and reduced LVOT gradients compared with placebo.2 In VALOR-HCM, the agent reduced the proportion of patients with highly symptomatic oHCM and meeting criteria for septal reduction therapy, highlighting its role as a disease-modifying therapy.3

Despite these advances, variability in individualized dose requirements remains a practical challenge. Current dose titration algorithms are guided primarily by LVOT gradient reduction and preservation of left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF). However, the predictors of higher maintenance dose requirements in clinical practice remain poorly characterized. We have previously shown that a positive genetic profile predicts a more robust response and potentially lower maintenance dose of mavacamten.4 Identifying baseline features that predict dose trajectory could refine treatment strategies, reduce unnecessary interruptions, and enhance individualized care.

Patients and Methods

Adults (≥18 years) with oHCM treated with mavacamten were retrospectively identified at a tertiary health system spanning 3 sites between May 2022 and May 2025. All patients met echocardiographic criteria for oHCM, defined as a peak LVOT gradient of 30 mm Hg or more at rest or with provocative maneuvers. To ensure adequate exposure, inclusion required documentation of a 1-year follow-up with stable dosing for at least 6 months before the visit. Patients with nonobstructive HCM or incomplete data were excluded.

Patients were stratified into 2 groups by maintenance dose at 1 year: low-dose (2.5 or 5 mg) and high-dose (10 or 15 mg) groups. Baseline characteristics included demographic characteristics; comorbidities (atrial fibrillation [AF], hypertension, and diabetes mellitus type 2); and echocardiographic parameters (LVEF, maximal wall thickness, LVOT gradients at rest and with Valsalva maneuver, medial ratio of early diastolic mitral inflow velocity to early diastolic mitral annular velocity [E/e′], global longitudinal strain [GLS], left atrial (LA) volume index, transmitral E velocity, pulmonary artery systolic pressure, tricuspid regurgitation velocity, and pressure gradient) and biomarkers (including B-type natriuretic peptide and troponin).

Electrocardiographic features were analyzed using validated artificial intelligence (AI)-based algorithms capable of estimating the likelihood of HCM, AF, and aortic stenosis. Continuous variables were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test and categorical variables with χ2 or Fisher exact test.

Results

A total of 77 patients were included: 49 in the low-dose group and 28 in the high-dose group (Figure 1). The mean age was 60 years, with 50.6% of men. The New York Heart Association class distribution was similar, with most patients in classes II to III. A history of AF was present in 26%, whereas left bundle branch block, hypertension, and diabetes mellitus type 2 were balanced across groups. Body mass index was higher in the high-dose group (29.5 vs 25.8 kg/m2; P=.064); however, the difference did not reach statistical significance (Tables 1 and 2).

Among echocardiographic parameters, medial E/e′ was significantly higher in patients requiring high-dose maintenance (20.0 vs 16.1; P=.018), suggesting greater baseline diastolic dysfunction. GLS, LA volume index, LVOT gradients (rest and Valsalva), LVEF, maximal wall thickness, pulmonary artery systolic pressure, peak tricuspid regurgitation velocity, and tricuspid pressure gradient did not show significant difference between dose groups. On multivariable logistic regression adjusting for LVOT gradient, LVEF, and GLS, medial E/e′ values remained independently associated with high-dose requirement (odds ratio, 1.20; 95% CI, 1.10-1.30; P=.04), analysis showed that baseline medial E/e′ had moderate discriminatory ability for predicting higher maintenance dose requirement, with an area under the curve of 0.67 (95% CI, 0.56-0.79; P=.005). A medial E/e′ threshold of approximately 14.2 provided the optimal balance between sensitivity (75%) and specificity (50%) for identifying patients requiring higher dose mavacamten. However, the other parameters were not predictive. Similarly, AI-enabled electrocardiogram–derived probabilities for AF, HCM, and aortic stenosis showed no differences significance (Table 1).

In summary, medial E/e′ was the only baseline continuous parameter independently associated with higher dose requirements, whereas other traditional markers of obstruction severity and myocardial strain were not predictive. Although calcium channel blockers use at baseline was associated with lower dose, this effect did not persist after multivariable adjustment.

Discussion

This multicenter real-world study found that baseline medial E/e′, a marker of higher grade diastolic dysfunction, emerged as the only independent echocardiographic predictor of higher maintenance doses of mavacamten at 1 year. Importantly, this relationship was independent of LVOT gradients, LVEF, or GLS, highlighting that higher grade of diastolic dysfunction, rather than degree of obstruction alone, may influence dose trajectory.

Findings in this study complement and extend prior observations. In the EXPLORER-HCM study, treatment with mavacamten not only reduced gradients but also improved exercise capacity and symptoms, suggesting benefits beyond relief of obstruction.2 The VALOR-HCM diastolic substudy confirmed that mavacamten improved diastolic function, reducing E/e′ and indexed LA volumes, independent of LVOT gradient reduction.3 Together with this analysis, these data suggest that patients with greater baseline diastolic dysfunction may require higher drug exposure to achieve optimal lusitropic benefit.

From a clinical perspective, current titration strategies prioritize persistent symptoms, LVOT gradients, and LVEF.4 However, patients with more advanced diastolic dysfunction may not achieve adequate symptomatic relief at lower doses, leading to dose escalation. Integrating medial E/e′ or related indices into titration algorithms could guide dosing expectations, improve precision dosing, reduce unnecessary interruptions, and potentially enhance safety, particularly because guideline recommendations now recognize myosin inhibitors as the central therapy for symptomatic obstructive HCM.1

The clinical implications are significant. Diastolic dysfunction is a major determinant of morbidity in HCM, contributing to heart failure, atrial arrhythmia, and adverse outcomes. By identifying diastolic dysfunction as a predictor of higher dose requirements, however, the ODYSSEY-HCM trial, which was terminated early, underscores that diastolic dysfunction alone, in the absence of LVOTO, should not guide cardiac myosin inhibitor therapy.5 Instead, this study highlights ṭhat the interplay between LVOTO and diastolic dysfunction may help clinicians better anticipate treatment needs and tailor therapy accordingly. Validation in larger cohorts is warranted, ideally incorporating strain imaging, biomarkers, and potentially AI-driven tools to refine predictive models.

Conclusion

In a real-world cohort of patients with oHCM, higher baseline medial E/e′ was associated with the need for higher maintenance doses of mavacamten. These findings highlight the central role of diastolic dysfunction in dose trajectory and suggest that incorporating diastolic indices into titration strategies may further personalize therapy.

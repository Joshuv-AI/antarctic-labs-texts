“Recurrent biatrial cardiac myxoma in carney complex: surgical reintervention for right atrial myxoma after previous left atrial resection”
Authors: Martina Canavesio, Marcelo J Figueroa
Published: 2026

Background

Carney Complex is a rare autosomal dominant syndrome characterized by cutaneous pigmentation abnormalities, endocrine tumors, and recurrent multicentric myxomas. Cardiac myxomas in this setting occur at younger ages, frequently involve multiple chambers, and have higher recurrence rates than sporadic tumors. We report a rare case of metachronous biatrial cardiac myxoma requiring redo cardiac surgery.

Case presentation

A 45-year-old woman with Carney complex was referred after surveillance imaging identified a new right atrial mass. Her history included left adrenalectomy (2003), left atrial myxoma resection (2007), multiple gynecologic myxoma resections, skull base myxoma resection, and right adrenalectomy (2018), resulting in secondary adrenal insufficiency requiring steroid replacement. Family history was notable for maternal atrial and renal myxomas.

The patient was asymptomatic. Transthoracic echocardiography demonstrated a 2-cm mobile, non-obstructive right atrial mass attached to the interatrial septum. Cardiac magnetic resonance confirmed an 18 × 19.6 mm lesion consistent with myxoma (Fig. 1).

Cardiac Magnetic Resonance showing Right Atrial Myxoma

Following Heart Team discussion, elective redo surgery was performed. Due to prior sternotomy, femoral cannulation was established before re-sternotomy. After cardiopulmonary bypass initiation and adhesiolysis, right atriotomy exposed a 2 × 3 cm tumor attached to the interatrial septum near the fossa ovalis (Fig. 2), with a small satellite lesion. Complete excision with healthy margins was achieved (Fig. 3). An additional suspicious septal lesion was also resected, and the septum was repaired primarily. Cardiopulmonary bypass and cross-clamp times were 43 and 21 min, respectively. Stress-dose hydrocortisone was administered perioperatively.

Right atrial myxoma excised with safe margins

The postoperative course was favorable without major complications. Early extubation was achieved 2 h after surgery. Transient vasoplegia required norepinephrine, later transitioned to oral midodrine. The patient was discharged home in stable condition on postoperative day 5.

Conclusion

Carney complex confers lifelong risk of recurrent multicentric myxomas. This case highlights delayed contralateral atrial recurrence after previous left atrial resection and emphasizes the importance of lifelong imaging surveillance. Redo surgical excision can be performed safely with multidisciplinary perioperative management.

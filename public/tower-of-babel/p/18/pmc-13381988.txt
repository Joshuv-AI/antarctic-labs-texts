Targeted HA@Que-Mn nanozymes alleviate postherpetic neuralgia by modulating neuroinflammation
Authors: Yike Liu, Miao Zhao, Lu Li, Yuzong Yang, Siyi Wang, Ziqiang Lin, Jiahao Chen, Yupei Lai, Simin Tang, Xiangsheng Zhang, Xianping Wu, Jun Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPostherpetic neuralgia (PHN), whose neuroimmune pathological mechanism remains unclear, is still an urgent clinical problem to be solved. In this study, we identified a key neurometabolic axis driving the occurrence of PHN, and developed a precise intervention strategy based on it. Cohort analysis based on the UK Biobank revealed that systemic inflammatory status was significantly associated with the risk of PHN. To translate this clinical association into actionable mechanistic targets, we integrated Mendelian randomization analysis with spinal cord transcriptomics data from a mouse PHN model, and found that functional deficiency of adenosine deaminase (ADA) was the core driver of the pro-inflammatory network. Mechanistic studies showed that specific downregulation of ADA expression in microglia led to pathological accumulation of local adenosine (ADO). Overloaded ADO broke the physiological analgesic homeostasis mediated by high-affinity A1/A3 receptors, and instead abnormally activated the low-affinity ADORA2B receptor pathologically upregulated in neurons, thereby triggering a neuroinflammatory cascade network that switched pain transmission from 'physiological analgesia' to 'pathological pro-inflammation'.Targeting this pathogenic pathway, we rationally designed a biomimetic nanozyme (HA@Que-Mn NPs). Through structure-oriented virtual screening, quercetin was selected as the conformational stabilizer of ADA; through manganese (Mn) coordination and surface functionalization with sodium hyaluronate (HA), the nanozyme was endowed with enhanced blood-brain barrier penetration ability, microglia-specific targeting and optimized catalytic efficiency of reactive oxygen species scavenging. In the resiniferatoxin (RTX)-induced mouse PHN model, systemic administration of the nanozyme effectively reshaped the neuroimmune microenvironment, inhibited pathological ADORA2B signal transduction, and achieved significant and long-lasting relief of neuropathic pain. This study established the ADA-ADO-ADORA2B pathogenic axis in PHN, and proposed a first-in-class nanozyme therapy with clinical transformation prospects, providing a new option for multi-omics driven targeted nanomedicine in the treatment of neuropathic pain.Keywords: Postherpetic neuralgia (PHN), Adenosine deaminase (ADA), ADORA2B receptor, Microglia-neuron crosstalk, Metal-polyphenol nanozyme

Introduction

Postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) is one of the most intractable chronic pain diseases, with a continuously rising incidence in the aging population, causing a heavy social burden [1]. This intractable pain severely impairs patients' daily functioning and overall quality of life, yet it often remains inadequately managed despite available interventions. For example, recent clinical evidence demonstrates that even with active surgical management, pain recurrence remains a substantial challenge, rendering long-term treatment outcomes far from satisfactory [2,3].Current first-line drug therapies (such as gabapentinoids and opioid analgesics) mainly exert analgesic effects by inhibiting neuronal excitability [4]. However, such symptomatic treatments often have limited efficacy and are accompanied by intolerable adverse reactions, and cannot reverse the established central sensitization and persistent neuropathological damage. The fundamental bottleneck lies in the insufficient understanding of the complex neuroimmune interaction mechanism in the spinal cord microenvironment during the pathogenesis of PHN, which severely restricts the development of molecular targeted therapies [5].

To break through this clinical dilemma, we adopted a top-down multi-omics integration strategy to decipher the pathological mechanism of PHN [6]. First, based on a large-scale population cohort analysis of the UK Biobank, we found that systemic inflammatory markers were significantly associated with the risk of PHN [7]. To further infer the causal relationship, we integrated Mendelian randomization analysis with spinal cord transcriptome data from RTX-induced PHN mouse model [8], and initially suggested that functional deficiency of ADA may be the core hub driving this inflammatory cascade. ADA is a key enzyme in the purine metabolic pathway, responsible for catalyzing the deamination of ADO to generate inosine, thereby regulating the concentration of extracellular ADO [9]; therefore, ADA functional deficiency will lead to impaired clearance and massive accumulation of extracellular ADO [10]. Notably, under physiological conditions, ADO mediates analgesic effects through high-affinity A1 and A3 receptors [11], while the functional effects of pathological ADO overload are more complex - previous studies have confirmed that long-term excessive ADO can activate ADORA2B on the surface of neutrophils to promote inflammation [12]. More importantly, the function of ADORA2B shows significant cell type specificity: in dorsal root ganglion neurons, ADORA2B activation can enhance neuronal excitability, and Lian et al. further confirmed the role of ADORA2B in regulating pain abnormalities of sensory neurons through single-cell sequencing and intrathecal antagonist experiments [13]; while in oligodendrocytes, ADORA2B is involved in the regulation of myelination [14]. These findings suggest that the pathological effects of ADORA2B depend on the cell type in which it is expressed. However, in PHN, whether ADA deficiency in microglia upregulates neuronal ADORA2B expression through ADO accumulation, thereby mediating microglia-neuron pathological signal transmission and aggravating neuroinflammation, is still unclear [15].

To confirm and effectively intervene in this novel neuroimmune crosstalk axis, theoretically, direct supplementation of exogenous recombinant ADA protein to the central nervous system is a feasible strategy. However, this strategy faces severe challenges in clinical translation, including short protein half-life, extremely poor blood-brain barrier (BBB) penetration ability [16], and potential peripheral immunogenicity. In view of this, it is particularly urgent to develop a small molecule stabilizer that can specifically reshape the endogenous ADA metabolic homeostasis in the central microenvironment. Through high-throughput structure virtual screening (molecular docking), we identified the natural flavonoid quercetin (Que) [17]. The study found that Que can form a highly stable conformational binding with the active site of ADA, showing excellent protein stabilization potential.

However, the in vivo application of free Que is still limited by bottlenecks such as poor BBB permeability, lack of lesion targeting, and difficulty in maintaining effective concentration in the oxidative stress microenvironment [18]. To break through these physiological barriers, we rationally designed a biomimetic nanozyme platform - HA@Que-Mn NPs [19]. In this nanostructure, the manganese (Mn) coordination center [20] was verified by density functional theory (DFT) calculations [21], showing optimized free radical adsorption energy and narrowed HOMO-LUMO energy gap. This indicates that the Mn center can reshape the local electron cloud distribution, significantly enhance its peroxidase-like and superoxide dismutase (SOD)-like catalytic activities, thereby efficiently scavenging hydroxyl radicals (·OH) and superoxide anions (O2·-) generated during neuroinflammation [22].

In addition, the hyaluronic acid (HA) shell not only endows the material with the ability to cross the BBB [23], but also achieves precise targeted delivery of the drug through high-affinity binding to the CD44 receptor on the surface of activated microglia [24].

In the resiniferatoxin (RTX)-induced PHN mouse model [25,26], HA@Que-Mn NPs exhibited excellent dual anti-oxidative and anti-inflammatory regulatory capabilities. This system not only effectively inhibited the pathological overexpression of neuronal ADORA2B, but also achieved significant neuroprotective effects and long-lasting analgesic effects. By elucidating this novel neurometabolic pain regulatory network, this study successfully constructed a complete translational paradigm of “clinical cohort-driven target discovery → structure-guided nanomedicine intervention", and provided a brand-new strategy for the treatment of refractory neuropathic pain.

Methods

Data sources

Statistical data of genome-wide association study (GWAS) on 91 circulating inflammatory proteins (Olink targeted proteomics Panel) were derived from a meta-analysis of protein quantitative trait loci (pQTL) in 14,824 European individuals. Summary data for the primary outcome, postherpetic neuralgia (PHN), were obtained from the FinnGen database (Release 9), including 313 cases and 330,377 European ancestry controls. In addition, baseline clinical data were extracted from the UK Biobank (UKB) cohort (>500,000 participants). The UKB study received ethical approval from the Northwest Multicenter Research Ethics Committee, and all participants provided written informed consent.

Multivariable logistic regression analysis in the UK biobank (UKB) cohort

To assess the association between inflammatory markers and postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) in the UK Biobank cohort, a multivariable logistic regression model was used to estimate odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI). The main exposure variables included clinically established inflammatory indicators, including C-reactive protein (CRP), white blood cell count, neutrophil count, and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR). The outcome variable was defined by the presence or absence of PHN. Detailed information on the data screening process, specific covariates adjusted in the regression model, and subgroup analysis is provided in the supplementary materials(Table S2–3).

Mendelian randomization analysis

A standard Mendelian randomization (MR) framework was used to assess the causal relationship between plasma inflammatory proteins and PHN outcomes. To ensure the reliability of the causal inference, the strength of the instrumental variables was strictly evaluated by calculating F-statistics(Table S12), with only those instruments showing F-statistics >10 being included to rule out weak instrument bias.The Wald ratio was applied to exposures monitored by single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP). For multi-SNP instruments, the inverse variance weighted (IVW) method was used as the primary analysis to generate pooled causal estimates. To ensure statistical robustness against potential invalid instruments, weighted median method and MR-Egger regression were implemented as supplementary analyses. Inflammatory factors driven mainly by the IVW method were prioritized for subsequent experimental validation.

Sensitivity analysis

Comprehensive sensitivity analyses were performed to verify the stability of MR estimates. A leave-one-out analysis was conducted to repeatedly exclude the disproportionate influence of any single outlier SNP. In addition, the MR-Egger intercept test was used to statistically quantify and exclude potential horizontal pleiotropy, as detailed in the supplementary materials(Fig. S4).

Cell culture and In vitro neuroinflammation model

Mouse microglia cell line (BV-2) and human neuroblastoma cell line (SH-SY5Y) were obtained from Fuheng Biology and Procell Life Science & Technology Co., Ltd. All utilized cell lines were strictly authenticated via short tandem repeat (STR) profiling prior to experiments. Furthermore, routine testing confirmed that the cell cultures were free of mycoplasma contamination. Cells were cultured in complete medium: BV-2 was cultured in DMEM/F12, SH-SY5Y was cultured in high-glucose DMEM, both supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. All cell lines were maintained in a humidified 5% carbon dioxide incubator at 37°C, and subcultured using standard methods when reaching 80–90% confluence.

To establish an in vitro microglia-mediated neuroinflammation model, BV-2 cells were stimulated with 32 nmol/L resiniferatoxin (RTX) for 30 min. To evaluate the therapeutic intervention, RTX-stimulated BV-2 cells were then treated with free quercetin or engineered HA@Que-Mn NPs at equivalent active concentrations. After the specified time points, the conditioned medium (CM) from these treated microglia was collected, centrifuged to remove cell debris, and applied to SH-SY5Y as neuronal cultures for 24 h. This CM-based co-culture system was used to assess downstream neuronal inflammatory responses.

Establishment of RTX model

All animal surgeries were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of the Third Affiliated Hospital of Southern Medical University and the Animal Experiment Committee of Daoke Pharmaceutical Technology (IACUC-DK-2024-4-10-02), in accordance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. Adult male C57BL/6 mice (18–20 g) were housed under specific pathogen-free (SPF) conditions with a 12-h light-dark cycle and free access to food and water.

Given that RTX can specifically target the TRPV1 receptor and induce extensive degeneration and deafferentation of primary sensory neurons, this model accurately reproduces the core central sensitization and neuroimmune disorder characteristics of the middle and late stages of PHN. The PHN model was established by a single intraperitoneal injection of resiniferatoxin (RTX).In the specific construction of the PHN mouse model, mice in the experimental group received a single intraperitoneal injection of resiniferatoxin (RTX) at a precise dose of 10 μg/kg. The RTX working solution was formulated in a vehicle mixture consisting of 10% Tween 80, 10% ethanol, and normal saline. The sham operation group was given an equal volume of the solvent vehicle.

To precisely evaluate the therapeutic efficacy under clinically relevant conditions, all treatments were initiated on the 3rd day post-modeling. The mice were randomly assigned to five groups: (1) Sham group, (2) RTX group, (3) RTX + continuous intraperitoneal (i.p.) quercetin (5 mg/kg), (4) RTX + single intravenous (i.v.) quercetin (5 mg/kg), and (5) RTX + single i.v. HA@Que-Mn NPs (5 mg/kg). Specifically, the continuous i.p. Quercetin group received daily injections for 18 consecutive days starting from day 3 post-modeling, whereas both the single i.v. Quercetin and single i.v. HA@Que-Mn NPs groups received only one single injection on the 3rd day after modeling.

Behavioral tests

Mechanical withdrawal threshold (PWT) and thermal withdrawal latency (PWL) were measured using a calibrated von Frey filament apparatus (KW-CT-1, KEW BASIS, China) and a plantar test device (IITC390G), respectively, to evaluate nociceptive behaviors. Baseline assessment was performed before intervention, and follow-up tests were performed on day 1, 3, 7, 14 and 21 after RTX injection.

Western blot analysis

Total protein from mouse spinal cord tissue and cultured cells was extracted using RIPA lysis buffer (Thermo Scientific, #89900) containing a protease inhibitor cocktail, and quantified by BCA protein assay kit (Beyotime). Equal amounts of protein lysates were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. After blocking with fast blocking buffer for 15 min at room temperature, the membranes were incubated with the following primary antibodies overnight at 4°C: ADA (Abclonal, A5151, 1:1000), ADORA2B (Proteintech, 21071-1-AP, 1:500), IL-6 (Wanleibio, WL02841, 1:1000) and β-Tubulin (Proteintech, 66240-1-Ig, 1:30000). The membranes were then incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibody (Abmart, M21003, 1:5000) for 1 h. Protein bands were visualized with ECL substrate (Bio World) on a TANON-5200 imaging system, and grayscale quantitative analysis was performed using ImageJ software.

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted from spinal cord tissue (n = 6) and cultured cells (n = 3) using TRIzol reagent (Biosharp, BS258A) according to the manufacturer's instructions. RNA purity and concentration were verified by NanoDrop ND-2000 spectrophotometer, strictly ensuring that the OD260/280 ratio was between 1.8 and 2.1, and the RNA integrity number (RIN) ≥8.0. Subsequently, total RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using 5 μg HiScript II qRT Super Mix (Vazyme, R223-01) on an ABI Veriti 96-well thermal cycler. Quantitative real-time PCR was performed on a Roche LightCycler 480 II system using SYBR Premix Taq Pro Universal Kit (Vazyme, Q712-02). The thermal cycling protocol included an initial denaturation at 95°C for 30 s, followed by 50 cycles of denaturation at 95°C for 5 s and 60°C for 30 s. Strict melting curve analysis was routinely performed to confirm the specificity of a single amplification product. The relative mRNA expression level was normalized to the endogenous control GAPDH and calculated using the standard 2^-(ΔΔCt) method. Primer sequences are detailed in Table S1.

Immunofluorescence staining

Immunofluorescence staining was performed on mouse spinal cord tissue sections. After permeabilization and blocking, the sections were incubated with the following primary antibodies overnight at 4°C: anti-Iba1 (Abcam, ab178846), anti-NeuN (Abcam, ab177487), anti-ADA (Abclonal, A5151), anti-ADORA2B antibody (Proteintech, 21071-1-AP), anti-IL-6 antibody (Wanleibio, WL02841), anti-IL-1β antibody (Proteintech, 26048-1-AP) and anti-TNF-α antibody (BioSharp, BIAR-00281-A). After thorough washing with PBS, the samples were incubated with the corresponding fluorescently labeled secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. Nuclei were stained with DAPI (Abcam, ab104139). Fluorescent images were captured using an Olympus upright fluorescence microscope and quantitatively analyzed using ImageJ software (NIH).

Synthesis of HA@Que-Mn NPs

Sodium hyaluronate (hereinafter collectively referred to as the polymer hyaluronic acid, HA, in view of its dissociation state in solution) was added to 200 mL of deionized water, and magnetically stirred at room temperature until completely dissolved. Quercetin (50 mg) was dissolved in 5 mL of absolute ethanol with the assistance of ultrasound, and then dropped into the HA solution. Subsequently, potassium permanganate (KMnO4, 40 mg) was added to the mixture, and continuously stirred for 10 min to form a uniform reaction system. Under continuous stirring, ascorbic acid (vitamin C, 50 mg) dissolved in 10 mL of deionized water was dropped in as a mild reducing agent. During this process, the color of the solution gradually changed from light purple to brownish black, indicating the formation of quercetin-Mn NPs. The reaction was carried out at room temperature for 2 h. During the whole reaction process, HA molecules were weakly coordinated with the surface of MnO2 through carboxyl and hydroxyl groups, which effectively limited the size of the nanodots and prevented their aggregation. Finally, unreacted precursors and by-products were removed by thorough dialysis to obtain stably dispersed HA-stabilized MnO2 quercetin nanodots (referred to as HA@Que-Mn NPs).

Physicochemical characterization and In vitro assay

Structural, morphological and compositional characteristics

The microscopic morphology and elemental distribution of the nanodots were visualized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JEM-F200) equipped with energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) imaging. The hydrodynamic diameter and surface charge were quantified by dynamic light scattering (DLS) and Zeta potential analysis, respectively. To confirm the successful synthesis of the composite, the crystal structure was determined by X-ray diffractometer (XRD, Rigaku SE), while the chemical functional groups and surface element valence states were elucidated by Fourier transform infrared spectrometer and X-ray photoelectron spectrometer (XPS, Thermo Fisher ESCALAB Xi+).

Free radical scavenging and nanozyme catalytic activity

Electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) spectroscopy was used to directly capture and detect specific free radical signals (such as •OH and O2·-). In addition, the broad-spectrum free radical scavenging ability and nanozyme-like catalytic kinetics of the engineered nanodots were systematically quantified by 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH), nitro blue tetrazolium (NBT) and 3,3′,5,5′-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) colorimetric methods.

Computational chemistry and interaction mechanism

To deeply explore the microscopic mechanism of molecular interaction between the components of HA@Que-Mn NPs and the underlying electronic structure basis of its enzyme-like catalytic activity, we systematically carried out density functional theory (DFT) calculations. These calculation models were used to elucidate the atomic-level electronic structure, coordination mechanism and interaction binding energy between the components of the nanodots (HA, Mn and quercetin).

Go and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) with absolute log2 fold change (|log2 FC|>1) were subjected to Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis using the DAVID bioinformatics database. Enrichment terms with FDR-adjusted p < 0.05 were defined as statistically significant. The top 10 significantly enriched entries were visualized through the SRplot platform.

Molecular docking

The three-dimensional structures of quercetin (from PubChem) and target protein (from PDB) were preprocessed using PyMOL. Molecular docking was performed using AutoDockTools (v1.5.7) to determine the optimal binding conformation and calculate the binding affinity. The final protein-ligand binding complex was visualized by PyMOL.

Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation

To verify the stability of the docked ADA-quercetin complex, 100 ns MD simulation was performed using GROMACS 2020.6. These systems were parameterized by the AMBER99SB force field and solvated in the SPC water model. After standard steepest descent energy minimization and NVT equilibration to 300 K, production MD simulations were performed. Trajectory data were extracted and visualized by Qtgrace (v0.2.6).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (v4.1.3) and GraphPad Prism 9.0. Data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Differences between two groups were tested by two-tailed Student's t-test. For longitudinal behavioral evaluations (e.g., mechanical and thermal pain thresholds measured across multiple time points), data were rigorously analyzed using a repeated-measures two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Bonferroni's multiple comparisons post-hoc test to account for both treatment and time interactions.

Results

Clinical genomics and multi-omics analysis confirm ADA as a protective driver in the pathogenesis of PHN

Postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) is closely related to the systemic immune response, but its specific causal drivers are still unclear. To first establish the clinical relevance of systemic inflammation in PHN, we conducted a cross-sectional analysis using the UK Biobank (UKB) cohort (N = 5618) (Fig. 1A). The results showed that elevated levels of classic inflammatory markers, including C-reactive protein (CRP), white blood cell count (WBC) and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), were significantly associated with an increased risk of PHN (overall P < 0.0001) (Fig. 1B). Notably, dose-response restricted cubic spline analysis (Figure 1C-F) revealed a significant dose-dependent positive correlation between these inflammatory indices and the prevalence of PHN, highlighting the need for in-depth analysis of the underlying inflammatory network.

To further elucidate the molecular mechanism driving this inflammatory phenotype, we performed transcriptome sequencing on spinal cord tissue of RTX-induced PHN model mice. Gene Ontology (GO) and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis showed (Figure 1G and H) that differentially expressed genes were significantly enriched in “response to oxidative stress", “cellular response to external stimuli", especially pathways related to “ATP hydrolysis activity". These transcriptional features strongly suggest that the imbalance of purine and ADO metabolism is highly correlated with oxidative stress and neuroinflammatory network in the spinal cord microenvironment, which may provide a key metabolic imbalance background for the progression of PHN.

However, given that observational studies and transcriptomic data are susceptible to confounding factors and reverse causality, we used large-scale genome-wide association study (GWAS) summary data (N = 330,377) to screen circulating plasma proteins with clear causal effects on PHN using the Mendelian randomization (MR) method (Fig. 1I). MR analysis identified two key causal candidate targets: fibroblast growth factor 5 (FGF5) as a risk factor, while ADA appeared as a potent protective factor (Figure 1J and K). To strictly validate the reliability of our MR findings, comprehensive diagnostic metrics were performed. The F-statistics for all selected instrumental variables were consistently >10 (Supplementary Table S12), effectively ruling out weak instrument bias. Furthermore, Cochran's Q test confirmed the absence of significant heterogeneity among the genetic variants (Supplementary Table S11), and MR-Egger intercept analysis indicated no potential horizontal pleiotropy (Supplementary Table S10). Additionally, the leave-one-out sensitivity analysis (Supplementary Fig. S9) confirmed that the causal inference was robust and not disproportionately driven by any single outlier SNP. More importantly, after intersecting the causal proteins derived from MR with the differentially expressed genes in the transcriptome, Ada was the only overlapping hub gene (Fig. 1L). Given the key physiological function of ADA as a metabolic checkpoint for ADO clearance, this highly consistent genetic and transcriptomic evidence strongly establishes that ADA deficiency is the core pathological driver of PHN.

Experimental validation establishes ADA as the core switch regulating microglia-neuron inflammatory crosstalk

To experimentally verify the causal relationship between ADA and PHN identified by systematic omics, we constructed a resiniferatoxin (RTX)-induced PHN mouse model (Fig. 2A). Behavioral assessment confirmed the successful establishment of the model: the model mice showed significant mechanical hyperalgesia (Fig. 2B) and thermal nociceptive tolerance (Fig. 2C). Quantitative analysis further revealed that RTX induction not only caused severe depletion of ADA levels in circulating plasma, but also synchronously significant downregulation of ADA expression in spinal cord tissue (Figure 2E-G). This synchronous downregulation in the systemic and central compartments strongly corroborated the previous Mendelian randomization (MR) findings and spinal cord transcriptome results, suggesting that systemic ADA deficiency may represent the metabolic vulnerability within the central nervous system.

To resolve the cellular source of ADA deficiency, we analyzed single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data. The results showed that in the steady-state spinal cord microenvironment, microglia are the main cellular source of ADA (Fig. S5). Immunofluorescence co-staining further confirmed this spatial localization: after RTX modeling, the ADA fluorescence signal in Iba1+ microglia was greatly reduced (Fig. 2D). In vitro experiments were consistent with in vivo observations: RTX treatment directly inhibited the transcription and protein expression of ADA in cultured BV-2 microglia (Figure 2H-J).

We performed intrathecal injection of recombinant ADA protein in RTX mice (Fig. 2K). The results showed that targeted restoration of ADA levels potently reversed mechanical allodynia and thermal nociceptive tolerance (Figure 2L and M). Molecular level analysis showed that ADA supplementation significantly reduced the mRNA and protein expression levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, IL-1β and TNF-α) in spinal cord tissue (Figure 2O and P). More critically, immunofluorescence co-localization analysis confirmed that overexpression of ADA effectively reduced the inflammatory load of spinal cord neurons (Fig. 2N). In summary, these in vivo gain-of-function evidence conclusively proves that ADA is the core biological switch that determines microglia-neuron inflammatory crosstalk in PHN.

Abnormal ADO accumulation triggers neuronal ADORA2B, leading to neuronal inflammation

ADA is the main clearance enzyme of extracellular ADO. Consistent with ADA deficiency, we detected significant accumulation of ADO in the plasma of RTX mice and the culture supernatant of RTX-treated microglia (Figure 3A, B). To clarify the downstream effect of ADO, we analyzed the expression profile of the spinal ADO receptor family. The heat map showed that Adora2b expression was significantly upregulated in the RTX model (Fig. 3C), which was further confirmed by qPCR and Western blot. Notably, intrathecal injection of recombinant ADA significantly reversed the overexpression of ADORA2B (Figure 3D–F).

To rigorously determine the cell-type-specific localization of ADORA2B and exclude its potential expression on glial cells, triple immunofluorescence co-staining of ADORA2B with NeuN (neurons), Iba1 (microglia), and GFAP (astrocytes) was performed in the spinal cord. Quantitative co-localization analysis (Supplementary Fig. S8) revealed that following RTX induction, the upregulated ADORA2B was robustly and specifically co-localized with NeuN-positive neurons, while no evident co-localization was observed with Iba1 or GFAP. Notably, this pathological upregulation of neuronal ADORA2B was remarkably reversed and returned to normal baseline levels following ADA treatment.

To verify this intercellular crosstalk mechanism, we constructed a conditioned medium (CM) co-culture system (Fig. 3G). The supernatant of BV-2 cells treated with RTX significantly induced the expression of ADORA2B and IL-6 in SH-SY5Y neurons (Figure 3H, I). However, if Ada was overexpressed in BV-2 cells in advance (OV-ADA), the activation of neuronal Adora2b and the increase of IL-6 induced by its supernatant were significantly inhibited. In addition, we used siRNA to silence Adora2b (si-Adora2b) in neurons. The results showed that knockdown of Adora2b was sufficient to block the inflammatory response induced by RTX-BV-2 supernatant, and significantly reduced the protein level of IL-6 and the mRNA levels of other inflammatory factors in neurons (Figure 3J, K). In summary, these results confirm that the ADA-ADO-ADORA2B axis mediates the pathological crosstalk between microglia and neurons, which is the key mechanism of PHN occurrence.

Computer simulation and biological confirmation of quercetin as a direct structural stabilizer of ADA

To translate our mechanistic findings into pharmacological intervention, we sought to find a small molecule stabilizer of ADA. Molecular docking showed (Fig. 4A) that the natural flavonoid quercetin (Que) perfectly fits the active pocket of ADA structurally through strong hydrogen bonding and hydrophobic interactions. Subsequent 100 ns molecular dynamics (MD) simulation confirmed the thermodynamic stability of the ADA-Que complex, as evidenced by the stable root mean square deviation (RMSD) trajectory (Fig. 4B) and interaction area (Fig. 4C). To verify this binding at the biophysical level, we performed surface plasmon resonance (SPR) experiments (Fig. 4D), which demonstrated a strong affinity between Que and ADA (KD = 1.92 × 10−5 M). In addition, cellular thermal shift assay (CETSA) confirmed that the binding of Que significantly changed the melting curve and enhanced the thermal stability of endogenous ADA in vitro (Fig. 4E and F).

Baseline neuroprotective efficacy of free quercetin

To evaluate the in vivo therapeutic potential of free quercetin (Que), we continuously injected different doses (5 and 10 mg/kg) of Que intraperitoneally into RTX-induced PHN mice (Fig. 4G). Behavioral results showed that Que treatment significantly alleviated mechanical allodynia and thermal hyperalgesia in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 4H, I). Molecular mechanism analysis showed that Que treatment specifically restored ADA levels in microglia and significantly downregulated the expression of ADORA2B in spinal cord neurons; this result was doubly confirmed by immunofluorescence co-localization (Fig. 4J–L) and Western blot (Fig. 4N). In addition, Que treatment comprehensively reduced the levels of pro-inflammatory factors such as TNF-α and IL-1β in spinal cord tissue (Fig. 4M).

In vitro co-culture experiments further verified that the above neuroprotective effect originated from the blockade of the microglia-neuron paracrine axis (Fig. 4O). Consistent with the in vivo results, Que treatment not only increased the ADA protein level in SH-SY5Y neurons exposed to RTX-BV-2 supernatant, but also inhibited the expression of ADORA2B (Fig. 4P) and the transcription of pro-inflammatory factors. Although free Que showed clear pharmacological activity, its in vivo application relied on repeated high-dose administration, exposing significant transformation bottlenecks: poor water solubility, lack of specific targeted enrichment to microglial lesions, and easy rapid degradation in the pathological microenvironment. Therefore, it is imperative to develop a nano-delivery system with targeting, stability and intelligent response characteristics.

Synthesis, physicochemical characterization and broad-spectrum antioxidant capacity of the biomimetic nano-chaperone

To overcome the bottlenecks of free quercetin (Que) in translational medicine, especially its weak blood-brain barrier (BBB) penetration ability and lack of cell specificity, we constructed a biomimetic nano-delivery platform. In this design, hyaluronic acid (HA) was used as a shell with both active targeting and immune camouflage functions. Given that activated microglia in the neuroinflammatory microenvironment significantly overexpress the CD44 receptor, the HA coating not only helps promote endocytic transport across the BBB, but also achieves precise homing to microglial lesions through a receptor-mediated mechanism. Based on this strategy, we successfully prepared HA-modified quercetin-manganese coordination nanocomposites (HA@Que-Mn NPs).

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) combined with energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) elemental mapping showed that HA@Que-Mn NPs had uniform morphology, and Mn, O, C and N elements were highly co-localized within the particles, confirming the successful loading of each component in the nanostructure (Fig. 5A). Dynamic light scattering (DLS) and Zeta potential analysis showed that HA surface modification moderately increased the hydrated particle size of the nanocomposite and endowed it with a highly negative surface charge, thus ensuring its excellent colloidal stability in the physiological environment (Fig. 5B, C). X-ray diffraction (XRD) and Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy further confirmed the successful assembly of the composite. In the FTIR spectrum, the characteristic carbonyl (C=O) stretching vibration peak of free Que was significantly red-shifted from 1660 cm−1 to 1629 cm−1, and a new Mn-O coordination bond absorption peak appeared, indicating that a stable metal-polyphenol coordination network was formed between Que and Mn ions. In addition, the characteristic carboxylate and hydroxyl stretching vibration peaks confirmed the successful coating of HA (Fig. 5D). High-resolution X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis not only confirmed the chemical composition of the elements, but also elucidated the coordination environment of the Mn core. The deconvolution results of the Mn 2p spectrum showed that Mn existed in a mixed valence state, mainly including Mn2+ (binding energy about 641.5 eV) and Mn3+ (about 642.8 eV) (Fig. 5E–J). This Mn2+/Mn3+ mixed valence structure constitutes a dynamic electron transfer center, which lays a structural foundation for the subsequent nanozyme-like catalytic activity.

We systematically evaluated the free radical scavenging kinetics of the nano-platform. UV-visible absorption spectroscopy and quantitative analysis showed that HA@Que-Mn NPs exhibited significant concentration-dependent scavenging ability against hydroxyl radicals (·OH), DPPH radicals and superoxide anions (O2·-) (Fig. 5K–M). To further confirm its antioxidant mechanism, we adopted electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) spectroscopy. EPR results showed that compared with the strong characteristic free radical signal observed in the DMSO control group, the signal intensity was significantly reduced or even completely disappeared after adding Que-Mn or HA@Que-Mn NPs (Fig. 5N–P), which directly confirmed the efficient capture and quenching effect of the nanocomposite on free radicals. This efficient scavenging ability of hydroxyl radicals and superoxide anions (O2·-) is attributed to the cyclic transformation of the Mn [2]+/Mn3+ redox pair inside the material, endowing HA@Que-Mn NPs with excellent superoxide dismutase (SOD)-like catalytic activity. In summary, the structural and chemical characterization results consistently show that HA@Que-Mn NPs have been successfully constructed and have potent, broad-spectrum free radical scavenging activity.

DFT calculations reveal the quantum chemical basis of the broad-spectrum antioxidant mechanism

To deeply explain the broad-spectrum antioxidant mechanism of HA@Que-Mn NPs at the molecular energy level, this study used density functional theory (DFT) to simulate the electronic structural properties of quercetin (Que) and its derivatives, and systematically analyzed its interaction kinetics with reactive oxygen species (ROS).

First, the electrostatic potential (ESP) distribution map showed that a significant negative potential region appeared around the hydroxyl (-OH) of the free Que molecule, indicating that these sites have extremely high electron density and are the preferred nucleophilic active centers for free radical attack. In both the Que-Mn complex and the HA@Que-Mn NPs, the binding sites shift from the isolated hydroxyl groups of free Que to the coordination environment of the Manganese atom. The Mn atom acts as the anchor, redistributing the electron density and creating a new, highly active center that is further stabilized and presented by the nanoparticle structure(Fig. S6). Frontier orbital theory calculations further revealed that the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) of free Que is mainly localized in the B ring and C ring regions of the catechol structure, and its HOMO-LUMO energy gap (ΔE) is 0.0282 Hartree (Ha). However, when Mn [2]+ ions were introduced and the coordination structure was formed, the electronic structure of the system was significantly reconstructed: the energy gap sharply shrank to 0.0046 Ha. This dramatic reduction of the energy gap greatly reduces the electron transition energy barrier and endows the material with excellent chemical reactivity. More critically, in the Que-Mn and HA@Que-Mn NPs systems, the HOMO orbital underwent obvious spatial rearrangement, and the electron cloud significantly gathered to the metal Mn center and its coordinating atoms (Fig. 6 A-C). This phenomenon strongly confirms that the Mn core acts as a key “electron transport hub" in the entire redox cycle.

When simulating the interaction between the material and hydroxyl radicals (·OH) and superoxide anions (O2·-), the charge difference density (CDD) map intuitively showed the electron redistribution characteristics at the adsorption interface. In the nanocomposite system, free radicals tend to be preferentially adsorbed near the metal Mn center; the large area of charge accumulation and depletion regions at the interface reveals a strong electron exchange process. In addition, the thermodynamic calculation of adsorption energy (Eads) further verified the superiority of the biomimetic nano-platform. The data showed that compared with free Que, the adsorption energy of HA@Que-Mn system for O2·−was enhanced from −2.31 eV to −2.85 eV (Fig. 6 E), and the adsorption energy for ·OH was increased from −2.68 eV to −3.12 eV (Fig. 6 D). This energy change trend indicates that although the HA shell is coated on the core surface, its abundant functional groups have a synergistic effect with the Mn coordination center through hydrogen bond-assisted action and spatial confinement effect, which significantly enhances the capture affinity and binding strength of the material to free radicals.

In summary, DFT confirmed that HA@Que-Mn NPs achieve efficient and broad-spectrum free radical neutralization performance by optimizing the electronic structure and enhancing interface interaction, which provides a solid theoretical support for its excellent nanozyme-like activity.

In vitro and In vivo biocompatibility analysis of HA@Que-Mn NPs

Good biosafety is a prerequisite for the clinical translation of nanomedicines. We first systematically evaluated the in vivo biosafety of HA@Que-Mn NPs after systemic administration. Hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining results showed that compared with the sham operation (SHAM) group, no obvious histomorphological abnormalities, inflammatory infiltration or necrotic lesions were observed in the main organs (heart, liver, spleen, lung, kidney) of each treatment group (Fig. 7A). Hematology analysis further showed that all indicators including white blood cells, neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, red blood cells and platelet counts were maintained within the normal physiological range, excluding potential hematological toxicity or excessive immune system activation risk (Fig. 7B–I). In addition, there were no significant differences in serum biochemical markers reflecting liver function (ALT, AST) and renal function (CREA, BUN) among the groups (Fig. 7J–M). The above results collectively confirmed that systemic delivery of HA@Que-Mn NPs did not cause obvious organ damage or hematological toxicity.

Subsequently, we evaluated the local cytocompatibility of the nano-platform in the neural microenvironment, which is highly critical for long-term CNS applications. Annexin V/PI double staining confocal microscopy showed no significant apoptosis in either microglia or neurons (SH-SY5Y) after treatment (Fig. 7N–S). To rigorously assess long-term biosafety and avoid the confluence-induced spontaneous apoptosis typical of prolonged 96-well plate cultures, cells were cultured in large dishes and co-incubated with the nanozymes for an extended period of 7 days. Flow cytometry quantitative analysis (PI staining) confirmed that prolonged exposure did not induce significant excess apoptosis in either cell line compared to the untreated control group (Fig. 7O–T). In parallel, short-term CCK-8 monitoring up to 72 h demonstrated that the cell survival rate was consistently maintained without proliferation inhibition (Fig. 7P–U). Furthermore, 7-day continuous exposure to the nanozymes did not trigger abnormal microglial activation or polarization (assessed by M1/M2 markers, Fig. 7Q), nor did it induce excessive intracellular or mitochondrial oxidative stress (assessed by DCFH-DA and MitoSOX probes, Fig. 7R). In summary, the multi-dimensional in vitro and in vivo safety evaluation results demonstrate that HA@Que-Mn NPs possess excellent long-term biocompatibility, providing a reliable experimental basis for their application as a safe nanocarrier in the targeted treatment of postherpetic neuralgia (PHN).

HA@Que-Mn NPs alleviate PHN by targeting microglial ADA to eliminate neuroinflammation

Before evaluating the in vivo translational potential of the ADA-targeted strategy, we first verified the blood-brain barrier (BBB) penetration and biodistribution of the nanozymes. Cy5.5-labeled HA@Que-Mn NPs were intravenously administered, followed by ex vivo fluorescence imaging at days 1, 3, 7, 14, and 21 post-injection (Supplementary Fig. S7). Strong fluorescence signals were successfully detected in the brain and spinal cord as early as day 1, confirming rapid BBB penetration. The nanozymes exhibited sustained local retention in the CNS up to day 21. Notably, by day 14, the remaining materials were primarily localized in the liver for metabolic processing, and by day 21, peripheral organ fluorescence was virtually cleared, demonstrating excellent in vivo biosafety and degradability.

Based on these favorable pharmacokinetic profiles, we then systematically compared the analgesic effects of free quercetin (Que), non-targeted complex (Que-Mn) and HA@Que-Mn NPs in the resiniferatoxin (RTX)-induced neural pain mouse model. Notably, the free Que group required continuous repeated administration (5 mg/kg) to maintain the basic efficacy, while the HA@Que-Mn NPs group only used a single equivalent dose (5 mg/kg) administration (Fig. 8A).

Behavioral evaluation results showed that a single injection of HA@Que-Mn NPs produced a significant and long-lasting reversal of hyperalgesia. In contrast, free Que with continuous administration and Que-Mn with single administration only showed partial or transient relief effects. During the entire 21-day observation period, the mechanical withdrawal threshold (MWT) and hot plate latency (HPT) of the HA@Que-Mn NPs treatment group gradually recovered, and finally reached a level with no statistical difference from the sham operation (SHAM) group (Fig. 8B and C). These results strongly confirm that the HA-mediated targeted delivery strategy has a decisive advantage: it effectively overcomes the rapid clearance of free drugs, and achieves an extended therapeutic window and long-lasting analgesia through a single administration.

To further elucidate its molecular mechanism, we analyzed the changes in the spinal cord microenvironment. Immunofluorescence staining showed that HA@Que-Mn NPs could significantly upregulate the expression level of ADA in microglia, and its effect was far better than that of the non-targeted treatment group (Fig. 8 D). This metabolic repair in microglia effectively inhibited the abnormal upregulation of ADORA2B on neurons (Fig. 8 E). Western blot analysis further confirmed the above histological findings at the protein level, showing that the expression levels of ADA and A2B in the HA@Que-Mn NPs treatment group completely returned to normal levels (Fig. 8 F). Ultimately, this precise blockade of the “microglia-neuron" paracrine axis significantly inhibited the neuroinflammatory cascade, manifested as a significant reduction in the protein expression levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α and IL-1β) in spinal cord neurons (Fig. 8G and H).

Discussion

The clinical treatment of postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) has long been limited by the insufficient understanding of the neuroimmune interaction mechanism in the spinal cord microenvironment [15]. In this study, through the integration of large-scale clinical cohort data and multi-omics analysis, we first established the core driving role of ADA functional deficiency in the pathogenesis of PHN [27]. Based on this finding, we further elucidated its downstream pathological cascade: specific downregulation of ADA in microglia leads to abnormal accumulation of extracellular ADO, which in turn activates neuronal ADORA2B receptors and triggers neuroinflammation [28]. Targeting this new target, we rationally designed a biomimetic nano-platform (HA@Que-Mn NPs) with both blood-brain barrier (BBB) penetration and microenvironment remodeling functions [29]. This platform achieved long-lasting analgesia after a single administration, constructed a complete translational paradigm from target discovery to nanomedicine intervention, and provided a highly promising treatment strategy for PHN and other refractory neuropathic pain.

Previous studies on PHN mostly focused on ion channel abnormalities or changes in neuronal excitability, often ignoring the metabolic signal interaction between glial cells and neurons. The unique breakthrough of this study is the use of Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis to reveal a significant causal relationship between circulating plasma ADA levels and the risk of PHN. Although the blood-brain barrier physically isolates the periphery from the center, our spinal cord transcriptome analysis showed that the expression of spinal ADA was also significantly downregulated under pathological conditions, and ADA was the only overlapping hub target connecting the plasma MR signal with the changes in the spinal cord transcriptome. We speculate that this consistent deficiency in the periphery and the center may originate from two mechanisms: first, genetic variation acts as a shared expression quantitative trait locus (eQTL) or protein quantitative trait locus (pQTL), systematically driving the synchronous low expression of ADA in multiple tissues [30]; second, the depletion of peripheral ADA may lead to the interruption of trans-barrier metabolic support mediated by extracellular vesicles [31].

Subsequent in vivo experiments strongly verified this omics inference: under physiological conditions, ADA maintains the dynamic balance of extracellular ADO; while in the pathological process of PHN, the depletion of ADA in microglia does lead to local ADO overload. Notably, high concentrations of ADO did not exert the traditional analgesic effect through the A1 receptor, but specifically induced the pathological overexpression of neuronal ADORA2B. This concentration- and receptor subtype-dependent “phenotypic switch" reasonably explains the persistent neuroinflammation and pain sensitization state in PHN. The successful blockade of this pathological crosstalk by exogenous supplementation of ADA or silencing of neuronal ADORA2B directly confirms the key role of metabolic repair in reversing neuropathic pain.

In terms of intervention strategy development, to avoid the delivery bottleneck and safety risks of conventional gene therapy, we identified the natural flavonoid quercetin (Que) as a conformational stabilizer of ADA through structure virtual screening. Although free Que showed excellent target affinity in vitro, its low water solubility and rapid in vivo clearance limit its clinical application. The construction of HA@Que-Mn NPs effectively overcomes these pharmacokinetic obstacles. The hyaluronic acid (HA) shell not only endows the material with the ability to cross the BBB, but also achieves efficient enrichment of the drug in the lesion area by specifically recognizing the CD44 receptor on the surface of activated microglia. In addition, the Mn [2]+/Mn3+ coordination center exhibits excellent nanozyme-like activity, which efficiently scavenges reactive oxygen species (ROS) in the pathological microenvironment [32,33]. This synergistic effect of targeted metabolic repair and broad-spectrum anti-oxidation enables HA@Que-Mn NPs to significantly inhibit the neuroinflammatory cascade with a single administration, showing significant transformation advantages compared with free drugs that require continuous administration.

In the process of promoting the translation of basic findings to clinical practice, it is crucial to objectively examine the methodological characteristics and limitations of this study.

First, we did not directly monitor dynamic changes in ADO levels following ADA overexpression in microglia. However, as ADA is the primary rate-limiting enzyme for adenosine deamination, its downregulation directly causing massive tissue ADO accumulation is a well-established biochemical consensus supported by classical models [34]. Future studies employing microdialysis or isotope tracing are planned to visualize this metabolic flux in vivo.

Second, while the RTX-induced chemical denervation model lacks a true viral etiology, it was utilized for its reliability in capturing the core pathology of late-stage PHN. Systemic RTX specifically ablates TRPV1-expressing nociceptive afferents, effectively reproducing the severe peripheral nerve destruction and subsequent aberrant spinal sprouting that drive intractable central sensitization [35]. Due to the stringent biosafety requirements for live neurotropic viruses (e.g., HSV-1) and the intrinsic species-specific barriers limiting human-tropic viruses (e.g., VZV) in rodents, direct comparisons with viral models were not conducted. Validating our findings in specialized viral-induced hyperalgesia models remains a necessary future endeavor.

Furthermore, given the shared involvement of purine metabolic dysfunction and oxidative stress across various chronic pain conditions, the targeted ADA-ADO-ADORA2B axis intervention utilizing HA@Que-Mn holds therapeutic potential for other refractory neuropathies, such as chemotherapy-induced or diabetic peripheral neuropathy.

Finally, while gabapentinoids (e.g., gabapentin and pregabalin) remain the recognized first-line pharmacological treatments for PHN, their clinical application is often limited in some patients due to central nervous system adverse effects (e.g., dizziness and sedation) and the requirement for multiple daily doses [36,37]. In light of this, the HA@Que-Mn nanozyme developed in this study provides a disease-modifying strategy with the potential for both alternative and complementary therapeutic use. By directly targeting the upstream neuroinflammatory cascade, a single intravenous injection of the nanozyme yields a sustained analgesic effect lasting up to 21 days. Future clinical translation efforts will include: achieving the scaled-up synthesis of HA@Que-Mn under GMP standards, with strict quality control to ensure structural uniformity and batch-to-batch reproducibility; completing long-term pharmacokinetic, biodistribution, and systemic toxicity evaluations in large animal models (e.g., non-human primates or canines); and initiating Phase I clinical trials to systematically assess its safety and tolerability in human subjects.

In summary, this study deepens the understanding of the neuroimmune pathological mechanism of PHN, and shifts the treatment strategy from traditional neuronal inhibition to microglia-mediated metabolic microenvironment intervention. The engineered HA@Que-Mn NPs, with its intelligent targeting, long-term retention and multi-effect synergistic microenvironment regulation ability, provide an innovative solution with high translational value for the clinical treatment of refractory neuropathic pain.

High Affinity Inhibitors of the Macrophage Infectivity Potentiator Protein from Trypanosoma cruzi, Burkholderia pseudomallei, and Legionella pneumophila—A Comparison
Authors: Theresa Lohr, Carina Herbst, Nicole M. Bzdyl, Christopher Jenkins, Nicolas J. Scheuplein, Wisely Oki Sugiarto, Jacob J. Whittaker, Albert Guskov, Isobel Norville, Ute A. Hellmich, Felix Hausch, Mitali Sarkar-Tyson, Christoph Sotriffer, Ulrike Holzgrabe
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Since Chagas disease, melioidosis, and Legionnaires’ disease are all potentially life-threatening infections, there is an urgent need for new treatment strategies. All causative agents, Trypanosoma cruzi, Burkholderia pseudomallei, and Legionella pneumophila, express a virulence factor, the macrophage infectivity potentiator (MIP) protein, emerging as a promising new therapeutic target. Inhibition of MIP proteins having a peptidyl-prolyl isomerase activity leads to reduced viability, proliferation, and cell invasion. The affinity of a series of pipecolic acid-type MIP inhibitors was evaluated against all MIPs using a fluorescence polarization assay. The analysis of structure–activity relationships led to highly active inhibitors of MIPs of all pathogens, characterized by a one-digit nanomolar affinity for the MIPs and a very effective inhibition of their peptidyl-prolyl isomerase activity. Docking studies, molecular dynamics simulations, and quantum mechanical calculations suggest an extended σ-hole of the meta-halogenated phenyl sulfonamide to be responsible for the high affinity.

The MIP proteins and the closely related MIP-like proteins are immunophilins, a widely distributed class of proteins. Within this group, the various MIP proteins from pathogenic microorganisms and the human FK506-binding proteins (hFKBPs) belong to the same superfamily of FK506-binding proteins (FKBPs), which are characterized by their peptidyl-prolyl cis–trans isomerase (PPIase) activity.1 PPIases catalyze the rate-limiting reaction of protein-folding, and are thus involved in numerous biological processes, such as signal transduction, gene regulation, protein secretion, and tissue regeneration.2,3 Accordingly, MIPs from pathogens often contribute to virulence, classifying these proteins as virulence factors. MIPs have been functionally characterized for many Gram-negative bacteria, such as Legionella pneumophila,4,5Burkholderia pseudomallei,6,7Neisseria spp.,8Coxiella burnetii,9 and Klebsiella pneumoniae .10 In all these cases, the role of MIP in the penetration or survival of the pathogen in the host cell was demonstrated.1 In addition to functional similarity, they also exhibit high sequence homology in their respective PPIase domains.11 It is assumed that the inhibition of virulence factors, which are generally not essential for survival, suppresses the emergence of resistance, as is the case with antibiotics.1 The bona fide inhibitor of FKBPs, the macrolide lactone FK506, is used as an immunosuppressant, rendering it inappropriate for combating infectious diseases.1,3 Hence, nonimmunosuppressive MIP inhibitors are of interest.

TcMIP, the MIP protein of Trypanosoma cruzi, a kinetoplastid protozoan and the causative agent of Chagas disease, also called American trypanosomiasis, is one of the less well characterized MIP proteins. An estimated 8 million people are currently infected and approximately 20–30% develop a potentially life-threatening T. cruzi infection.12 The only approved drugs for Chagas treatment are nifurtimox and benznidazole, which besides from having severe side effects, are rather limited to the administration in the acute phase of the disease.13 New strategies to combat Chagas infections are urgently needed. In the infective stage of the parasitic life cycle, trypomastigotes secrete the TcMIP protein.14TcMIP promotes the productive infection of epithelial cells with T. cruzi and pre-treatment of rhesus monkey kidney epithelial (LLC-MK2) cells with TcMIP leads to a four-fold increase in the number of internalized parasites.11 Encouragingly, the invasive effect can be reversed either by antibodies against TcMIP or a low dosage of FK506,14 establishing TcMIP as a promising drug target to combat Chagas disease.

Due to the broad spectrum of MIP activity, there are numerous efforts to develop small molecule inhibitors for these targets.3 The approaches are derived from the natural products rapamycin and FK506. As the corresponding small-molecule inhibitors lack the so-called effector domain that induces immunosuppression in humans, they are appropriate for anti-infective therapy.15 For example, Pomplun et al. developed structurally related compounds, with an (S)-C5-substituted [4.3.1]-aza-amide bicycle, which showed inhibitory activity in vitro against MIPs of the pathogens Plasmodium falciparum and Chlamydia trachomatis.16 Wiedemann et al. could establish the structural basis of [4.3.1]-aza-bicyclic sulfonamide binding to LpMIP and TcMIP.17 Scheuplein et al.18 and Seufert et al.19 designed a compound library of B. pseudomallei and L. pneumophila MIP inhibitors, which are derived from the pipecolic moiety of FK506 (Figure 1, Entry 1). These pipecolic acid-derived inhibitors are effective in the nanomolar range with enhanced drug-likeness and do not show immunosuppressive properties.15,18 Moreover, the introduction of a side chain, in the linker between the two carbonyl moieties (Figure 1, Entries 2 and 3), significantly improves inhibitory activity toward MIPs of B. pseudomallei and Neisseria spp.18 The affinity of the compounds toward BpMIP was determined by means of a fluorescence polarization assay (FPA) reported before (Figure 1).18,20 Additionally, the inhibition of the prolyl-peptidyl cis, trans-isomerase (PPIase) activity, the relevant enzymatic property of MIPs, was evaluated.18,20 In both assays, the compounds characterized by an S/S configuration performed best. Studying the broad spectrum of activity of pipecolic acid-derived MIP inhibitors, Debowski et al. could show that these compounds effectively extend the survival of Galleria mellonella infected with C. burnetti, a Gram-negative bacterial pathogen, indicating in vivo activity of MIP inhibitors.9

Previously described MIP inhibitors by Scheuplein et al.,18 derived from 1, and KD values determined by the fluorescence polarization assay (FPA) using BpMIP. The inhibitors S,S-2, S,R-2, R,R-2, and R,S-2 differ only in their configuration, with S,S-2 being the preferred isomer, as all the others show low or no binding (n.b.) in the FPA screening. 3a is a hit compound for B. pseudomallei MIP.

In this study, based on their prior performance against Burkholderia-MIP, we chose hit compounds 1, the isomers of 2, and 3a (Figure 1) as the starting point for extension of the activity profile to T. cruzi and the development of new pan-MIP inhibitors for TcMIP, LpMIP, and BpMIP. As described in Scheuplein et al.,18 the leucine (Leu) derived side chain shows strong binding combined with an acceptable solubility of the inhibitors (compared to inhibitors with aromatic side chains, which have a high affinity to the MIPs but poorer water solubility). For this reason, the Leu side chain was retained for further modifications. The aim of the methionine (Met) side chain was to investigate a change in the lipophilicity of the side chain and thus its influence on the binding behavior.

The series of newly synthesized compounds was tested with FPA against purified Trypanosoma- and Legionella-MIP. The respective structure–activity relationships (SARs) were determined using Bp-, Lp-, and TcMIP binding and PPIase activity inhibition assays. In addition, the in vivo activity of one representative compound was assessed in a Galleria mellonella infection model. Importantly, we obtained inhibitors with increased affinity for TcMIP, which was further substantiated by docking studies, molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, and quantum mechanical calculations.

Results and Discussion

Expansion of the Previously Described Compound Library

The binding affinities to MIP proteins were determined by means of the previously developed FPA according to Scheuplein et al.20 This assay represents a rapid and robust screening method for MIP inhibitors with high consistency of results with the conventionally used PPIase assay.20 In the FPA, the dissociation constant (KD) value of a fluorescent probe (here: probe A or probe B, see SI) can be determined directly. In contrast, compound screening is based on competition of the respective inhibitor with this probe. The inhibitor is added to a defined concentration of protein preincubated with the probe. The inhibitor affinity is eventually determined by replacement of the probe, indicated by the decrease in fluorescence polarization value. Based on this, the KD value of the inhibitors can be calculated according to Wang.21

The pipecolic acid inhibitors 1–2, whose affinity for BpMIP was demonstrated by Scheuplein et al.18 (Figure 1) were tested against BpMIP, LpMIP, and TcMIP to identify a starting point for the development of new and more effective MIP inhibitors. Of note, only the PPIase domain of LpMIP was used here, i.e., residues 77–213. Examination of the binding behavior of the previously described compounds 1–2 reveals that the affinity to the BpMIP protein is generally highest compared to TcMIP and LpMIP (Table 1), with a KD value of the former lead compound 1 in the micromolar range. Upon introduction of a benzyl side chain,18 only the inhibitor with an S,S-configuration shows a considerable increase in binding affinity, whereas the other isomers were far less affine or showed no binding to the MIP proteins. The results of the FPA were comparable for all three MIPs, although higher affinities of the S,S-enantiomer to BpMIP and TcMIP compared to LpMIP were observed.

Based on compounds 1–2, a series of compounds was created, which contains two variations of a side chain that can be synthetically traced to the amino acids l-leucine (Leu,3a) and l-methionine (Met,3b). Additionally, the benzyl substituent of sulfonamide was replaced with a phenyl ring. Since in previous studies a substitution with an amino- or nitro-group was found to reduce the affinity,22p-F, m,p-Cl,Cl, m-Br, and m,m-Cl,Cl, were introduced here. Additionally, an m,p-Cl,Cl (4a) substitution pattern of a benzyl moiety was considered. The compounds are shown in Figure 2.

Following the protocol established by Scheuplein et al.,18 a synthetic route was chosen that allowed the late variation of the sulfonamide residue and the formation of stereochemically pure pipecolic acid amide derivatives. Starting from the commercially available Boc-protected amino acids l-leucine or l-methionine, a coupling reaction was carried out with 3-picolylamine using the o-(benzotriazol-1-yl)-N,N,N′,N′-tetramethyluronium-hexafluorophosphat (HBTU) and N,N-diisopropylethylamin (DIPEA) (Scheme 1). Subsequently, the tert-butyloxycarbonyl (Boc)-protection group was removed with an excess of trifluoroacetic acid (TFA), resulting in amides 7a and 7b. Further coupling with Boc-(S)-pipecolic acid and deprotection under the same conditions gave intermediates 8a and 8b. The sulfonamides (3b,4a, 5a, 5b, 6a, 6b, and 6c) were formed using the respective sulfonyl chloride derivative.

Structure–Activity Relationships by FPA Screening

The FPA results obtained for TcMIP, BpMIP, and LpMIP are displayed in Table 2. Almost all compounds were found to show affinity for the MIP protein. In general, the affinity of all compounds is about ten-times higher for BpMIP than for TcMIP and about a hundred-times higher than for LpMIP. For the most potent compounds, the inhibition of the Bp-PPIase activity was additionally measured at 400 nM of inhibitor using the protease-coupled PPIase assay.20 In brief, a short amino acid sequence labeled with p-nitroaniline is used as a substrate, while α-chymotrypsin serves as an auxiliary protease that cleaves only translinked Phe-Pro substrates. The conversion can be measured by the absorbance at about 390 nm, which is due to the released p-nitroaniline. Here, 400 nM inhibitor was coincubated with BpMIP and the substrate succinyl-Ala-Phe-Pro-Phe-p-nitroanilide and the remaining activity of the Mip was determined.

Common to all MIPs was the observation that compounds with a leucine-derived side chain exhibited an affinity higher than that of the methionine-derived ones. Furthermore, the ester compound 11 without side chain shows almost no affinity to the MIPs. Previous findings indicated the superiority of the bisamide linked compounds, not only with regard to affinity, but also in terms of chemical stability.18

Furthermore, the compounds characterized by phenyl substitution at the sulfonamide group showed higher affinity to the MIPs than the benzyl substituted ones, which have been reported by Scheuplein et al.18 Since the only exception is the p-F-benzyl compound 3b, which shows an affinity similar to the p-F-phenyl substituted substance 6a, it stands to reason that the substitution at this residue has a major influence on the binding affinity. In contrast to amino- and nitro-phenyl substituted compounds, reported in previous studies22 as poor inhibitors of the PPIase activity of LpMIP, the halogen substituted compounds led to a one/two-digit nanomolar affinity, especially for the interaction with BpMIP. These results are highly consistent with the results of the PPIase assay for BpMIP (see Table 2). A comparison of the benzyl-substituted inhibitors 3a and 4a revealed that the deletion of the methylene group between the pipecolic acid nitrogen and the sulfonamide moiety is key for binding to TcMIP and LpMIP, whereas BpMIP has a greater tolerance toward the methylene group. The introduction of both beneficial structural elements into a compound without a side chain for comparative reasons, namely, compound 11, in turn leads to almost no affinity and emphasizes the importance of the side chain for the binding affinity to the MIPs.

Whereas, most compounds of our library (cf. Tables 1 and 2, and other compounds not shown here) did not show any or only had very low affinity to TcMIP and LpMIP, compound 5b is the first inhibitor with an excellent affinity to all MIPs.

To explore the toxicity of this molecule in vivo, 5b and its main metabolite2310 were injected into five Galleria mellonella larvae at a concentration of 100 μM or 500 μM. Of note, due to solubility issues, compound 10 rather than the most effective in vitro MIP inhibitor was chosen. All larvae survived for 5 days, so that the compounds can be considered noncytotoxic, which is consistent with previously published data for the lead compounds 1, 2, and 3a on NIH3T3- and HEK293T-cell lines.18

To investigate a possible in vivo activity, the N-oxide 10 of hit compound 5b was screened in a Galleria mellonella infection model with B. thailandensis, as a model pathogen for B. pseudomallei. In a previous experiment, it was shown to behave similarly to B. pseudomallei.24,25 Nonetheless, it needs to be noted that in general, Galleria are rather sensitive to Burkholderia infections, which may influence the significance of the study. Groups of 10 larvae were infected with B. thailandensis. After 2 h, 10 μL of a combination of cotrimoxazole (10 mg/kg) and MIP inhibitor (250 μM) was applied. The addition of an antibiotic is required because MIP inhibition is not expected to eradicate the pathogens due to MIPs’ nonessential nature.1 Uninfected larvae (treated with phosphate-buffered saline, i.e., the “PBS group”) and those that had only received cotrimoxazole or diluent were used as controls. As seen in Figure 3, uninfected Galleria showed a survival percentage of 100%, whereas infected Galleria treated with only the diluent died quickly. Interestingly, the infected Galleria treated with the combination of cotrimoxazole and the N-oxide 10 showed a slightly better survival rate than the Galleria treated with only the antibiotic. This suggests that the inhibition of MIP can be beneficial for the infected host.

Computer-Assisted Analysis of Structure–Activity Relationships

To elucidate the reason for the enhanced binding affinity observed for compounds with a truncated and meta-halogenated sulfonamide residue in conjunction with a side chain group, molecular docking analyses with TcMIP were conducted for all compounds shown in Figure 2. The docking protocol was adapted from Seufert et al.19 and allowed the reproduction of the conserved structural moieties compared to the crystallographically observed binding mode as shown in Figure 4a and Figure S3. The positioning of the halogenated phenyl group near the flexible loop suggested that a halogen bond may be formed upon ligand binding. Focusing on the example of compound 5b and TcMIP, the carbonyl oxygen of Gly122 could be a potential interaction partner. However, a hypothetical halogen bond would need to compete with the intramolecular hydrogen bond present in the flexible loop of the TcMIP crystal structure in complex with 3a (PDB: 8P3D) between Gly122 and Ile126.26

(a) The docking pose of compound 5b (in blue) is in accordance with the binding position observed in the crystal structure of TcMIP in complex with compound 3a (PDB: 8P3D)26 (TcMIP in beige, ligand in gray). The flexible loop is highlighted in red. (b) Snapshot from the MD simulation of compound 5b with a TcMIP at 31.76 ns. The formation of a halogen bond with carbonyl oxygen of Gly122 can be observed.

Following up on this hypothesis, molecular dynamics simulations were employed with Schrödinger’s Desmond27,28 and the OPLS4 force field,29 which applies off-centered point charges to represent σ-holes. Assessment of the obtained trajectories could indeed confirm the repeated occurrence of a σ-hole interaction, as shown in Figure 4b. In the respective analyses, a halogen bond was considered to be present if, first, the X–O distance between halogen and carbonyl oxygen was within the range of 3.0 to 3.4 Å for chlorine, or 3.0 to 3.5 Å for bromine as proposed by Bissantz et al.;30 and, second, the C–X–O angle exceeded 150°, indicating whether the oxygen atom is pointing in the direction of the σ-hole of the halogen atom.31 In the three replicas of 100 ns MD runs with compound 5b and TcMIP, the occurrence of a halogen bond was observed in 19.4%, 14.7%, and 19.9% of the frames, respectively. Results of the MD runs with the other inhibitors are listed in Table S7. While the approximation of σ-holes through element-specific off-centered point charges is a well-established concept in molecular dynamics simulations,32 it is essential to note that this approach is only a very rough approximation of the actual electronic circumstances. Therefore, it does not allow any quantitative comparison of different ligands.

For a more detailed analysis of the electrostatic surface potential, quantum mechanical calculations (DFT, B3LYP/6-311 + G**) on the substructures of the inhibitors containing either chlorine or bromine were conducted. The results are illustrated in Figure 5. When the benzyl group is truncated to a phenyl moiety, the extent of the corresponding σ-hole is amplified significantly due to the −M effect of the adjacent sulfonamide group. Since the extent of the σ-hole has a considerable impact on the potential energy gain from a halogen bond, it does not surprise that inhibitors with a shortened and meta-halogenated sulfonamide residue demonstrated improved binding affinity in this study.

Calculation (B3LYP/6-311 + G**) of the electrostatic surface potential with substructures of the inhibitors reveals major differences in the magnitude of the σ-holes. The arrows indicate the viewing angles.

Affinity of the MIP Inhibitors to hFKBPS

For the therapeutic use of MIP inhibitors, interaction with human FKBPs (hFKBPs) must be considered. Due to the high homology of the proteins in the active site, selectivity for one of the targets is extremely unlikely. Nevertheless, a preference for the desired target is recommended with regard to in vivo application. The human FKBPs (FKBP12, FKBP12.6, FKBP51, and FKBP52) differ in their size and function in the human organism and were examined in a FPA, according to Bauder et al.33 The lead compounds 3a and 3b show the following ranking in terms of their binding affinity: FKBP12 ≥ FKBP12.6 > FKBP51 > FKBP52. This ranking is consistent with the general FKBP preference for pipecolate sulfonamide-based ligands,16,34−36 and can be traced back to more favorable interaction of H87 and I90 of FKBP12/12.6, which are replace by S118 and K121 in FKBP51/52. While no higher affinity to TcMIP could be achieved for the first two lead compounds, BpMIP shows a 2 to 3-fold superiority compared to the KD value of FKBP12 (see Table 3). This can be rationalized by differences in the tip of the so-called 80s-loop between human FKBP12 (H87P88G89I90) and BpMIP (A94G95G96V97). The higher flexibility in this region for BpMIP allows a better repositioning of V97 to accommodate the sulfonamide substituent of compounds 3a and 3b (Figure S2). Further exploration of this divergent part of human FKBPs versus pathogenic MIPs is warranted to enhance the selectivity for the latter.

Conclusion

The compound screening revealed novel structure–activity relationships for all three pathogenic MIP proteins and further emphasizes the structural similarity of the MIP proteins regardless of taxonomic origin. High-affinity inhibitors were identified for all target proteins, and although there is no selectivity to individual MIPs, key structural moieties for targeting the active sites of MIPs have been revealed. Moreover, the extent of enhancement differs with the particular modification, providing new starting points for optimized inhibitors. Docking, MD simulations, and quantum mechanical calculations offer a reasonable explanation for the improvement of binding affinity and help to understand the inhibitor-protein interactions. Accordingly, only the meta-halogen substitution in combination with a phenyl residue at the sulfonamide allows interaction with the flexible loop. An amino or nitro-group does not provide the prerequisite for a σ-hole interaction, which furthermore explains the earlier results. In summary, the MIP inhibitors have been refined into highly potent, broad-spectrum and noncytotoxic compounds ready for detailed in vivo testing.

Methods

Organic Synthesis

General Experimental Procedures and Equipment

The following general experimental procedures and equipment were according to Scheuplein et al.18

General

Common chemicals and reagents were purchased from Alfa Aesar (Ward Hill, USA), Merck (Darmstadt, Germany), Avantor (Darmstadt, Germany), TCI Deutschland GmbH (Eschborn, Germany), Fisher Scientific (Schwerte, Germany), and ABCR (Karlsruhe, Germany). They were used without further purification.

Gravity-Driven Column Chromatography

Silica gel 60 (0.063–0.200 nm) from Merck (Darmstadt, Germany) was used for gravity column chromatography. Solvent composition is specified for each compound in the synthesis section.

TLC

Thin layer chromatography (TLC) was carried out on precoated silica gel glass plates SIL G-25. Spots were identified by irradiation and subsequent fluorescence quenching at 254 nm or excitation at 366 nm.

Mass Spectrometry

Electrospray ionization (ESI) mass spectra were measured with a Shimadzu LCMS-2020 instrument (Shimadzu Scientific Instruments, Kyoto, Japan). Data are reported as mass-to-charge ratio (m/z) of the respective positively charged molecular ions. Chromatographic method is given in SI.

High-Resolution Mass Spectrometry

High-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS) was performed using an Agilent Infinity II LC-system (Waldbronn, Germany), equipped with a Sciex X500R QTOF mass spectrometer (Concord, Ontario, Canada) and a Turbo V Ion Source (ESI). Chromatographic method is according to Scheuplein et al.18

Infrared Spectrometry (IR)

IR spectra were recorded on a Jasco-FT-IR-6100 system (Jasco Deutschland GmbH, Groß-Umstadt, Germany) equipped with a diamond ATR accessory. The wave numbers of characteristic absorption bands are given in [cm–1].

Nuclear Magnetic Resonance Spectroscopy

1H (400.132 MHz) and 13C (100.613 MHz) NMR spectra were recorded on a Bruker AV 400 instrument (Bruker Biospin, Ettlingen, Germany). Topspin (version 3.2-pl7) software (Bruker Biospin, Ettlingen, Germany) was applied for processing NMR spectra.

Melting Points

To determine melting points, an MP70 melting point system (Mettler-Toledo GmbH, Gießen, Germany) was used.

Purity

All of the purities of the compounds were verified by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). All final compounds synthesized had purities above 95%. Purity data and HPLC methods are given in the SI.

Solubility

Thermodynamic solubility was determined for 5b and 10 by using the continuous shake flask protocol of Hiltensperger et al.37

Substances Known from Literature

Synthesis of 2-(nicotinamido)ethyl (S)-1-(benzylsulfonyl)piperidine-2-carboxylate (1) and 2-(nicotinamido)ethyl (S)-piperidine-2-carboxylate (12) was according to Seufert et al.19 Synthesis of lead structures (S)-1-oxo-3-phenyl-1-((pyridin-3-ylmethyl)amino)propan-2-yl (S)-1-(benzylsulfonyl)piperidine-2-carboxylate (S,S-2), (R)-1-oxo-3-phenyl-1-((pyridin-3-ylmethyl)amino)propan-2-yl (S)-1-(benzylsulfonyl)piperidine-2-carboxylate (S,R-2), (R)-1-oxo-3-phenyl-1-((pyridin-3-ylmethyl)amino)propan-2-yl (R)-1-(benzylsulfonyl) piperidine-2-carboxylate (R,R-2), (S)-1-oxo-3-phenyl-1-((pyridin-3-ylmethyl)amino)propan-2-yl (R)-1-(benzylsulfonyl)piperidine-2-carboxylate (R,S-2), and (S)-1-((4-fluorobenzyl)sulfonyl)-N-((S)-4-methyl-1-oxo-1-((pyridin-3-ylmethyl)-amino)-pentan-2-yl)piperidine-2-carboxamide (3a) has been described by Scheuplein et al.18

General Procedures

General Procedure A: Amidation

Amidation was carried out according to methods reported in the literature.38 First, 1 equiv of the limiting reactant (carboxylic acid or amine) was dissolved in dry DCM and/or DMF (4–20 mL per 1 mmol limiting reactant) and the corresponding coupling partner (1–1.2 equiv) was added under ice cooling. HBTU (2 equiv) served as coupling reagent, and an auxiliary base (DIPEA or TEA; 1–4 equiv) was added further. After stirring for 15 min, the reaction mixture was allowed to adopt rt and stirred to completion, which was monitored by TLC. Extraction was carried out with dilute HCl, followed by dilute NaHCO3. After the phases were separated, the combined organic layers were dried over Na2SO4 and filtered. The organic solvent was removed in vacuo, and the crude product was purified by column chromatography.

General Procedure B: Boc-Deprotection

According to Seufert et al.19 the Boc-protecting group was removed at rt with an excess of TFA (2–5 mL) in dry DCM (10–20 mL), with TFA initially added slowly and under ice cooling. After completion (2 h), the reaction mixture was neutralized with a saturated NaHCO3 solution and extracted. The aqueous phase was washed with chloroform (5 × 20 mL) and the combined organic layers were dried over Na2SO4, filtered, and the solvent was removed in vacuo to give the crude product, which was used without further purification.

General Procedure C: Synthesis of Sulfonamides

Following a modified procedure of Seufert et al.,19 TEA (1.5–3 equiv) and the corresponding sulfonyl chloride (1–1.3 equiv) were added to a solution of the piperidine derivative (1 equiv) in dry DCM (15 mL per 1 mmol limiting reactant) under ice cooling. The reaction was then stirred at rt until complete conversion, which was monitored by TLC. The solvent was removed in vacuo, and the residue was purified by column chromatography.

Fluorescence Polarization Assay

The FPA was performed as described in Scheuplein et al.18 Two different fluorescent probes were used for the competitive assay, depending on the affinity and thus the competitiveness of the inhibitor (The affinities of the probes to the MIP proteins, as well as their respective use in the competitive assay, are shown in the SI). For the competitive BpMIP assay, the same concentrations of probe and protein were used as described in Scheuplein et al.18 In contrast, a higher protein concentration was required for TcMIP and LpMIP due to their lower binding affinities to both fluorescent probes For this reason, the final protein concentration for the competition assay in the well was increased to 2 μM for TcMIP and 2–4 μM for LpMIP. Each inhibitor was measured at least in triplicate. Raw data and SD are given in the SI.

A procedure similar to that was used to determine the affinities of the lead compounds (3a and 3b) to the human FKBPs. The procedure is described in detail in Bauder et al.33

Galleria mellonella Toxicity and Infection Assay

The moth larvae of G. mellonella were cultured as described elsewhere.39 They were reared in-house and kept in the dark at 30 °C until use. The inhibitors were dissolved to a 50 mM stock solution in DMSO and diluted with PBS to concentrations of 100 and 500 μM. To prevent precipitation, the final concentration of DMSO was increased to 10% (v/v). Five larvae were injected with 10 μL each of a 100 μM or 500 μM solution of the inhibitors (5b and 10) or 10% DMSO as a control, which were then kept isolated at 37 °C in the dark. The survival rate was monitored over a period of 5 days according to Sprynski et al.39 To assess the effect of MIP inhibitors on B. thailandensis pathogenicity, a dilution of 5 × 104 CFU/10 μL of B. thailandensis in PBS was prepared. Groups of 10 larvae were treated with 10 μL of B. thailandensis or PBS. Two hours after infection, the larvae were treated with a combination of cotrimoxazole at a dose of 10 mg/kg and diluent (water) or a combination with 250 μM of the MIP inhibitor 10.25 The final concentration of DMSO was 10% in each case. After 20 h, survival was monitored every second hour, and Kaplan–Meier survival curves were obtained from three independent experiments.

Protease-Coupled PPIase Assay

Recombinant BpMIP was expressed and purified as per Iwasaki et al.10 The protease-coupled PPIase assay was conducted as per Fischer et al.40 In brief, 400 nM inhibitor was coincubated with BpMIP and the substrate succinyl-Ala-Phe-Pro-Phe-p-nitroanilide (10 mg/mL, SAPPP, Bachem) in a glass cuvette at 4 °C for six min prior to the addition of the protease α-chymotrypsin (SigmaAldrich, Merck). Cleavage of the chromophore, p-nitroanilide was measured at 390 nm at two-second intervals for 900 s using a Shimadzu 1800 UV/vis spectrophotometer. Enzymes assays were conducted in triplicate on different days, and the average observed rate constant (kobs) was determined.

Computational Methods

Protein Setup

The details of the TcMIP purification and structure determination are described in the accompanying paper.26 The structure is available from the PDB (8P3D).26 For computational studies, the structure was prepared with MOE41 (version 2022.02). The terminal residues Ala4 and Asp135 were capped and protonation was conducted with the Protonate3D42 functionality at pH = 7.4. For dockings, ligand and water molecules were removed. For MD simulations, water molecules with a B-factor < 30 Å2 were kept.

Ligand Setup

The inhibitors shown in Figure 2 were manually built in the MOE. Energy minimization was carried out with the MMFF94x force field to a gradient of 0.001 kcal·mol–1·Å–1.

Docking calculations were performed with GOLD43 (version 2023.1.0). For every inhibitor, 50 docking poses were generated with each of the implemented scoring functions ASP,44 Chemscore,45,46 ChemPLP,47 and Goldscore48 at 200% search efficiency. The binding site was defined as a sphere with 12 Å radius centered between residues Trp94, Phe85 and Tyr117. A weak constraint was applied according to our previous studies19 to favor docking poses that form a hydrogen bond with the backbone NH group of Ile91 (referring to TcMIP). The obtained docking poses were rescored with DSX49 using potentials derived from the Cambridge Structural Database.50 Additionally, RMSD values were calculated to the N-sulfonyl-pipecolic acid core of compound CJ168 in the superposed BpMIP crystal structure 4G5051 to assess the compliance of the generated docking poses with the current binding hypothesis. For subsequent MD simulations, the best scored pose according to DSX among all poses with core RMSD ≤ 1 Å was selected. If no such pose was obtained, the pose with the lowest core RMSD was selected.

Molecular dynamics simulations were performed with Desmond27,28 and the OPLS4 force field29 (Schrödinger release 2023-2). Bond orders were reassigned, and hydrogen atom positioning was optimized for the protein structure in the Maestro suite. The selected docking pose was added to the respective complex before the System Builder tool was used to neutralize and to solvate the system in an orthorhombic box with 10 Å distance between the protein and box border in every direction. Three replicas of 100 ns NPT runs were simulated at default settings with randomly selected seeds (cf. Supporting Information for further details). The obtained trajectories were converted to dcd format with VMD52 before geometric analyses were carried out with Amber CPPTRAJ.53

Quantum-mechanical calculations of the electrostatic surface potential at the density functional theory (DFT) level were performed with Gaussian1654 for the truncated N-methyl-amide substructures of the chlorine or bromine containing inhibitors. The B3LYP functional was applied with basis set 6-311 + G**. Geometry optimization was enabled. Visualization was conducted with PyMol55 with the electron density isosurface set to 0.001 au.

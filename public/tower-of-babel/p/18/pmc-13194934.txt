Physiological responses and proteomic analysis of different barley varieties under alkaline stress
Authors: Zhe Li, Jianbo Li, Yanan Jin, Tu Ba, Ersuo Lv, Zhiping Liu, Shoujun Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Soil salinization threatens agricultural development, and understanding the alkali tolerance mechanism of barley is crucial for breeding. Using the alkali-tolerant barley variety Jia-HY460-6R and the alkali-sensitive variety Noire Maroc as materials, morphological and physiological indicators were determined and proteomic analysis was conducted after treatment with 100 mmol·L⁻¹ Na2CO3:NaHCO3. The results showed thatJia-HY460-6R exhibited superior performance compared to Noire Maroc in terms of growth indicators, antioxidant capacity, ion balance, and osmotic regulation. A total of 1359 differentially abundant proteins were identified, and 27 key proteins were screened, involving multiple metabolic pathways such as the MAPK signaling pathway. This study provides a basis for understanding the molecular mechanism of barley alkali tolerance and breeding.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-42420-3.

Introduction

Soil salinization poses a threat to global agricultural development, with approximately 1 billion hectares of land affected by salinization, 20% of which are agricultural lands1. In China, the area of saline-alkali land is about 100 million hectares, and the soda saline-alkali land in Northeast China reaches 7.56 million hectares2. Saline-alkali land is divided into saline soil and alkaline soil3,4. Alkaline stress is more harmful due to its high pH value5, which can lead to problems such as iron deficiency and cell membrane damage. Improvement measures include agricultural technologies, water conservancy projects, chemical and biological methods, among which the application of salt- and alkali-tolerant varieties has shown significant effects6. Studying the mechanisms of plant salt and alkali tolerance is crucial for breeding new varieties and developing cultivation technologies in saline-alkali lands, which can enhance the productivity of cultivated land and ensure food security.

To cope with ion toxicity and osmotic imbalance caused by saline-alkali soils, plants have evolved multi-level regulatory mechanisms, including morphological adaptation, osmotic adjustment, ion homeostasis, and antioxidant defense7–9. In terms of morphological adaptation, a typical example is Salicornia europaea, which stores water through succulent leaves to dilute salt concentration and reduces transpiration area via leaf degradation, thereby effectively decreasing salt absorption10. Osmotic adjustment is an important strategy for plants to cope with saline-alkali stress. Plants maintain cellular osmotic balance by accumulating compatible solutes such as betaine and proline11. In addition, salt stress can interfere with sugar signaling pathways and alter the metabolic levels of sugars such as sucrose and fructose, further regulating osmotic balance12. For ion homeostasis, plants have established a sophisticated regulatory network: proton gradients are created by H⁺-ATPases and H⁺-PPases to drive Na⁺/H⁺ antiporters (e.g., SOS1) in expelling excessive Na⁺ out of cells; meanwhile, K⁺ transporters (e.g., AKT1, HAK) are relied on to maintain stable intracellular K⁺ levels and ensure membrane potential balance13. In response to oxidative damage induced by saline-alkali stress, plants activate a multi-level antioxidant defense system. On one hand, reactive oxygen species are scavenged by antioxidant enzymes such as superoxide dismutase (SOD), peroxidase (POD), and ascorbate peroxidase (APX); on the other hand, non-enzymatic antioxidants like ascorbate and glutathione are accumulated. In plants such as Arabidopsis thaliana, Medicago sativa, maize, and Reaumuria soongorica, a variety of key proteins related to salt tolerance have been identified, including aquaporins (AQPs), ribosomal proteins, and heat shock proteins (HSPs)14–18. These findings provide a theoretical basis for breeding salt- and alkali-tolerant crops and improving saline-alkali lands.

Barley (Hordeum vulgare) is a globally important crop as food, feed, and industrial raw material, with significant economic value19. Compared with rice, wheat, and maize, barley exhibits stronger alkali tolerance, making it an ideal material for studying plant alkali tolerance mechanisms20. Current studies mainly focus on physiological and biochemical aspects, including ion balance, osmotic adjustment, and antioxidant systems. For instance, Xue et al.21. found through KEGG analysis that the upregulation of the phenylpropanoid metabolic pathway can significantly enhance salt tolerance in barley; Boussora et al.22. discovered that salt stress alters the balance of plant hormones in barley, particularly the content of abscisic acid. As an important tool for studying plant molecular mechanisms23, proteomics technology can effectively identify key proteins and metabolic pathways related to alkali tolerance traits by comparing differences in protein expression profiles between alkali-tolerant and sensitive varieties24,25. However, research on the impact of alkali stress on the barley proteome remains scarce. In-depth analysis of the molecular mechanisms underlying barley’s alkali tolerance is crucial for molecular breeding, as it can provide a theoretical basis for identifying key functional genes related to alkali tolerance. In this study, two barley varieties with significantly different alkali tolerance, namely Alkali-resistant Jia-HY460-6R (R) and alkali-sensitive Noire Maroc (NM), screened in previous experiments, were used as materials. Proteomics technology was employed to investigate the morphological and physiological characteristics of different barley genotypes in adapting to alkali stress, as well as related important candidate proteins and metabolic pathways, aiming to lay a foundation for barley alkali tolerance breeding and stress-resistant cultivation.

Results

Effects of alkali stress on morphological characteristics of roots and leaves in barley seedlings

Under alkali stress, the growth of both varieties was significantly affected. At 50 mmol·L⁻¹, the leaves and roots of R showed little difference from the control, while the growth of NM was even better than that of the control. However, at 100 mmol·L⁻¹, R exhibited reduced plant height and yellowing leaves, whereas NM showed leaf wilting and root breakage, indicating that R has stronger alkali tolerance. With the increase of stress concentration, the daily relative growth rate (RGR) of shoots and roots of both varieties decreased. At 50 mmol·L⁻¹, the shoot RGR of R and NM decreased by 14.68% and 23.14%, respectively; at 100 mmol·L⁻¹, the decreases reached 40.37% and 52.78%, and the decrease in root RGR was more significant. The RGR of R was consistently higher than that of NM. Both leaf area expansion rate (LER) and root activity (RA) showed a trend of first increasing and then decreasing. At 50 mmol·L⁻¹, the LER and RA of R increased by 25.52% and 49.02%, respectively, while the increases in NM were smaller. At 100 mmol·L⁻¹, both indices decreased significantly, and the decrease in RA of R was smaller, further confirming its alkali tolerance advantage (Fig. 1).

Effects of alkali stress on antioxidant capacity of barley

Under alkaline stress conditions, the content of superoxide anions and hydrogen peroxide in the leaves and roots of R and NM increased, indicating damage to the cell membrane. However, R accumulates less hydrogen peroxide, demonstrating stronger membrane stability and alkali tolerance. Analysis of antioxidant enzyme activities showed that superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity first increased and then decreased in leaves, while it continuously decreased in roots; nevertheless, the SOD activity of R remained higher than that of NM. Peroxidase (POD) activity in R leaves increased with the intensification of stress, whereas that in NM first increased and then decreased; at 100 mmol·L⁻¹, the POD activity of R was significantly higher. Catalase (CAT) activity in both varieties first increased and then decreased, but R maintained a superior activity level, indicating its more effective ability to scavenge reactive oxygen species. In addition, the activities of glutathione peroxidase (GPX) and glutathione-S-transferase (GST) both increased under stress. The GPX activity in leaves and roots of R was 11.36% and 8.75% higher than that of NM, respectively, and the GST activity was 18.85% and 9.32% higher, suggesting that R alleviates oxidative damage more efficiently by enhancing the GPX/GST cycle (Fig. 2).

Effects of alkali stress on ion balance and osmotic substances in barley

Under alkali stress, the K⁺/Na⁺ ratio in both R and NM decreased significantly, but the decrease was smaller in R, indicating a stronger ability to regulate ion homeostasis. Meanwhile, the Fe²⁺ content in both varieties decreased, but the Fe²⁺ content in the shoots and roots of R was consistently higher than that of NM, which might enhance its stress resistance. Regarding osmotic regulatory substances, the soluble sugar content in both varieties increased with the intensification of stress, and the accumulation in R was more significant. The soluble protein content in R leaves showed a trend of first increasing and then decreasing, while that in roots decreased gradually; in contrast, the soluble protein content in NM decreased continuously. Under the same stress conditions, the protein content in R was always higher. These physiological differences further confirm that R has better adaptability to alkali stress (Fig. 3).

GO enrichment analysis of DAPs in leaves and roots induced by alkali stress

After alkali stress treatment, significant DAPs were identified in the leaves and roots of the two materials. Their GO analysis revealed remarkable differences in biological processes, cellular components, and molecular functions. In leaves, the DAPs were mainly involved in key biological processes such as membrane fusion, tricarboxylic acid cycle, citrate metabolism, transcriptional elongation, and aerobic respiration. These processes are related to energy metabolism and cellular respiration, indicating that plants are adjusting their energy production and metabolic pathways under stress conditions. In terms of cellular components, these proteins were primarily associated with respiratory chain complexes, suggesting the importance of energy conversion and electron transport in stress responses. For molecular functions, transcriptional elongation factors, SNAP receptor activity, and transcription factor activity were significantly enriched, which may be closely related to the regulation of gene expression under stress. In roots, the differentially abundant proteins were more involved in the biosynthesis of purines and ribonucleotides, which are crucial for nucleic acid synthesis and cellular metabolism. Regarding cellular components, these proteins were widely distributed in cell membranes, mitochondria, ATP synthase complexes, etc., reflecting the key role of roots in energy conversion and substance transport. In terms of molecular functions, transcriptional initiation factors, transmembrane transporters, and various enzyme activities were enriched, which may be related to the adaptive responses and metabolic regulation of roots to stress. The GO analysis results of these differentially abundant proteins reveal distinct response mechanisms of the two materials to alkali stress in leaves and roots, involving multiple levels such as energy metabolism, substance transport, and gene expression regulation (Fig. 7).

KEGG enrichment analysis of DAPs in leaves and roots between the two varieties under alkali stress

KEGG analysis plays a crucial role in protein research, as it reveals the potential functions, regulatory mechanisms, and biological significance of proteins by placing them in the context of known biological pathways and functional networks. To further explore the specific functions of the identified DAPs, KEGG enrichment analysis was performed. It was found that under alkali stress, the DAPs in the leaves and roots of the two varieties were significantly enriched in 48 key metabolic pathways, with 17 pathways in leaves and 31 pathways in roots. These pathways cover various aspects from gene expression regulation and energy metabolism to cellular defense mechanisms.

Among these 48 metabolic pathways, 10 significantly enriched pathways were selected for leaves and roots respectively in ascending order of P-value (P < 0.05). In leaves (B1_VS_B2), the selected pathways include photosynthesis, biosynthesis of amino acids and fatty acids, plant MAPK signaling pathway, nucleocytoplasmic transport, vesicle transport, ribosome function, citrate cycle, glycine-serine and threonine metabolism, carbon metabolism, protein processing in endoplasmic reticulum, and glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism. In roots (D1_VS_D2), the selected pathways include biosynthesis of amino acids, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, carbon fixation in photosynthetic organisms, glutathione metabolism, plant MAPK signaling pathway, α-linolenic acid metabolism, protein processing in endoplasmic reticulum, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, and plant hormone signal transduction (Fig. 8).

Screening of key DAPs in leaves and roots between the two varieties under alkali stress

Based on the COG database, 174 and 619 differentially abundant proteins (DAPs) in the B1_VS_B2 and D1_VS_D2 comparisons, respectively, could be annotated and functionally classified. Combined with KEGG analysis and by integrating repeated proteins across each comparison group, 27 DAPs were further screened out. Under alkali stress treatment, the abundance of 16 DAPs increased, while that of 11 DAPs decreased. Among them, there were 4 proteins involved in the plant MAPK signaling pathway (F2D743, A0A287PUS9, A0A8I6YDN6, and A0A8I7B1B1); 8 proteins in cysteine and methionine metabolism (A0A8I6WQS5, A0A8I6XN02, F2D343, A0A287GYB7, M0Z6C2, F2D807, A0A8I6XF00, and A0A8I6XN02); 6 proteins in glutathione metabolism (F2DQN2, A0A8I6XQZ2, A0A8I7B6G0, A0A8I6WWN8, A0A8I6YGF0, and F2DTR6); 4 proteins in amino acid biosynthesis (A0A8I6YTF7, A0A8I6 × 374, A0A8I6YLY4, and A0A8I6YZ06); 3 proteins in photosynthesis (F2D3R9, F2D7P5, and F2D170); and 1 protein each in phenylpropanoid biosynthesis (A0A8I6WAC6) and α-linolenic acid metabolism (A0A8I6YQW4). These results indicate that under alkali stress, R and NM can respond to alkali stress by regulating protein expression to indirectly affect multiple metabolic pathways.

Discussion

It is important to note that the conclusions drawn from this study are based on the proteomic analysis of two contrasting barley cultivars. While this design successfully identified key differential proteins associated with the observed phenotypic divergence, future studies incorporating a wider panel of tolerant and sensitive genotypes, as exemplified in the work of Kausar et al.27,28], would be valuable to verify the general applicability of these protein markers across diverse genetic backgrounds.

Synergistic physiological mechanisms of alkali stress tolerance in barley genotypes

The morphological components of crops largely directly determine their phenotypes and yields. Therefore, measuring seedling morphology is a method to evaluate crop resistance and yield potential29. Under alkali stress, the alkali-tolerant variety Jia-HY460-6R exhibited multi-dimensional adaptive advantages compared to the sensitive variety Noire Maroc : (1) Morphologically, it showed mild chlorosis and high root activity, while the sensitive variety suffered from leaf wilting and a decreased root-shoot ratio due to excessive ROS accumulation. This finding is significantly different from the results of Sofkova Svetla et al. 30. It is speculated that this difference may be due to the current alkali stress level not exceeding the plant’s tolerance threshold, and possibly related to the expression patterns of alkali tolerance-related genes between the two varieties; (2) In the antioxidant system, Jia-HY460-6R efficiently scavenged ROS by significantly upregulating the activities of SOD, CAT, GPX and the levels of GSH/GST, Under 100 mmol·L⁻ ¹ treatment, the increase of H2O2 and O2- was lower than that of Noire Maroc, indicating that the membrane damage was mild. This is consistent with the research results of Huang Han et al.31, who found that GPX and GSH showed a significant increasing trend with the increase of salt concentration; (3) In terms of ion homeostasis, the alkali-tolerant variety maintained a high K⁺/Na⁺ ratio (2.1 times higher than the sensitive variety). It is speculated that it alleviated Fe³⁺ absorption disorders by inhibiting Na⁺ uptake, activating K⁺ transport genes, and secreting mugineic acid-type phytosiderophores. Consistent with previous studies, the genotypic differences in Na⁺ and K⁺ concentrations highlight the differences in genotypic tolerance to alkaline and salt stress 32; (4) In osmotic regulation, soluble sugar content increased in both varieties (with an increase of 28% in the alkali-tolerant variety), but Jia-HY460-6R reduced osmotic potential by synergistically accumulating organic solutes, reflecting the ability of plants to adjust their osmotic potential by using soluble sugars as a means to adapt to salt stress 33. These results indicate that alkali tolerance is driven by a synergistic network of antioxidant defense, ion balance, and osmotic regulation, and its molecular basis may involve genotype-specific expression of ROS signaling (e.g. MAPK pathway), sulfur metabolism (glutathione synthesis), and ion transport genes.

DAPs in different barley genotypes during adaptation to alkali stress

To further understand the molecular response networks of the two varieties (R and NM) to salt stress, we analyzed their proteomic data under salt stress, studied the related KEGG pathways, and screened some differential proteins as research objects.

Plant MAPK signaling pathway

The MAPK (mitogen-activated protein kinase) signaling pathway in plants, as a highly conserved and critical signal transduction system, converts external stimuli (including environmental stress, hormone signals, and pathogen infection) into intracellular signals through a three-tiered phosphorylation cascade (MAPKKK-MAPKK-MAPK), ultimately regulating gene expression and physiological responses. It plays a core role in plant growth and development, stress adaptation, and immune defense 34. This study found that upregulation of calmodulin dependent serine significantly enhances antioxidant capacity in this pathway, which is consistent with the findings of Xu et al. in maize that CaMK can enhance the overall activity of the antioxidant enzyme system35; it was also found that the upregulation of this protein effectively inhibited the increase of ROS. Additionally, this study identified a respiratory burst oxidase homolog protein RBOHI3, which was downregulated in the salt-tolerant variety but upregulated in the sensitive variety. As a key enzyme for reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, this protein not only participates in regulating Na⁺ efflux but also its activity is directly regulated by CaMK. This finding echoes the research results of Ma’s team in *Arabidopsis thaliana*, who confirmed that ROS produced by AtrbohD and AtrbohF act as important signaling molecules to maintain Na⁺/K⁺ balance, thereby improving plant salt tolerance36. Furthermore, this study found that two other proteins, nucleoside diphosphate kinase (NDPK) and chitinase, are also involved in ion balance regulation, enhancing plant salt tolerance by reducing Na⁺ uptake37–39.

Glutathione metabolism

In the plant glutathione metabolic pathway, reduced glutathione (GSH), as a core antioxidant molecule, plays a key role in scavenging reactive oxygen species (ROS) and maintaining redox balance40. This study found that under alkali stress, the salt-tolerant variety (R) and the sensitive variety (NM) exhibited tissue-specific response patterns of antioxidant enzymes : L-ascorbate peroxidase (APX) activity decreased in leaves, while APX and glutathione S-transferase (GST) activities showed a synergistic increase in roots. This differential distribution reflects the adaptive regulatory strategy of plants to oxidative stress in different organs. In terms of functional mechanisms, APX directly reduces oxidative damage to cells by catalyzing the decomposition of H₂O₂ into water; GST effectively scavenges secondary oxidative toxic substances by catalyzing the binding reaction between glutathione (GSH) and electrophilic substrates (such as lipid peroxidation product MDA). This finding is consistent with the results of multiple plant salt tolerance studies: in species such as Tamarix hispida Willd., Glycine soja Sieb. et Zucc., and Brassica campestris L., GST has been confirmed to reduce oxidative damage caused by salt stress by enhancing the antioxidant defense system41. Notably, the synergistic upregulation of GST and APX in roots in this study may constitute an important alkali tolerance mechanism: on one hand, APX directly scavenges H₂O₂; on the other hand, GST-mediated GSH metabolism eliminates lipid peroxidation products, forming a multi-level antioxidant defense network. This tissue-specific distribution pattern of enzyme activity provides new insights into understanding the adaptive response of plants to alkali stress.

Cysteine and methionine metabolism

In plants, cysteine is a key raw material for glutathione (GSH) synthesis, and GSH can scavenge reactive oxygen species (ROS) to resist oxidative stress. Methionine is not only an important component of protein synthesis but also can be converted into S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), participating in various metabolic processes and stress responses42. The study found that proteins related to sulfur metabolism showed different expressions in leaves and roots: D-3-phosphoglycerate dehydrogenase, MDH, and S-methyltransferase decreased in leaves, while LDH, S-methyltransferase, and serine O-acetyltransferase increased in roots. Among them, S-methyltransferase can catalyze the methyl transfer of SAM and participate in plant stress responses43. This is consistent with the study by Shi et al.: methionine temporarily inhibits root growth (by affecting hormone signals) but activates the ABA pathway to enhance salt tolerance. When methionine levels return to normal, root growth also recovers, indicating that it plays a balancing role between growth and stress resistance 44.

Photosynthesis

Alkali stress severely affects plant photosynthetic efficiency by damaging chloroplast structure and inhibiting PSII activity45. This study found that ferredoxin (Fd) showed significantly different expressions between alkali-tolerant and sensitive varieties. As a core component of the photosynthetic electron transport chain, Fd not only participates in light energy conversion but also regulates chloroplast redox balance through the Fd/Trx pathway, a mechanism that has been regarded as a key system for chloroplast redox regulation since its discovery 50 years ago46. The study also identified two key proteins related to PSI : plastocyanin and PSI subunit V. They prevent ROS bursts caused by excessive electron accumulation by optimizing electron transport efficiency. This finding is consistent with the research by Saji et al.: phytocyanin in Arabidopsis thaliana can significantly increase the activity of antioxidant enzymes and the content of antioxidants, effectively scavenging ROS and reducing oxidative damage47.

Amino acid biosynthesis

This pathway involves the synthesis of various amino acids, including shikimic acid, histidine, and the pentose phosphate pathway in this study. Among them, shikimate kinase (SK), as a key enzyme in the shikimate pathway, increases lignin deposition by promoting phenylalanine synthesis, strengthening cell wall defense against ion penetration48, which is consistent with the result that rice OsSKL2 enhances salt tolerance found by Jiang et al.49; HisHF is a key enzyme in the histidine biosynthesis pathway, and histamine produced by histidine can regulate the ABA signaling pathway to improve stress tolerance50. For example, Zhao et al. identified that histidine kinase MHZ1 affects the expression of Na⁺ efflux genes (such as SOS1) by regulating the ethylene signaling pathway51; RPI is a key enzyme in the pentose phosphate pathway (PPP), responsible for catalyzing the reversible isomerization of ribulose-5-phosphate (Ru5P) and ribose-5-phosphate (R5P). Previous studies have confirmed that it enhances plant stress resistance by providing energy to maintain the reduced state of GSH to scavenge H2O252.

Phenylpropanoid biosynthesis and α-linolenic acid metabolism

Plant secondary metabolic pathways are important defense systems formed through long-term evolution, helping plants cope with environmental stress by synthesizing various non-essential metabolites53. This study found that under alkali stress, the expressions of peroxidase and 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase in the roots of the two varieties were significantly different, and these two enzymes play key roles in the regulation of secondary metabolism. Peroxidase enhances plant stress resistance through dual mechanisms: on one hand, it scavenges excessive ROS by oxidizing phenolic substances to maintain redox balance; on the other hand, it catalyzes the polymerization of lignin monomers to strengthen cell wall structure and reduce Na⁺ penetration54. This mechanism has been verified in various plants such as Arabidopsis thaliana and rice55. 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase promotes fatty acid β-oxidation to decompose 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA into acetyl-CoA, which not only provides energy (ATP) for the TCA cycle but also serves as a precursor for the synthesis of osmotic regulatory substances (proline, betaine)56, thereby enhancing the adaptability of plants to saline-alkali stress. These findings reveal the synergistic regulatory network of secondary metabolites in plant stress resistance.

Materials and methods

Plant materials

The experiment was conducted at the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region Forage Crops Engineering Technology Research Center. The barley varieties used in the experiment, including the alkali-tolerant variety Jia-HY460-6R (Alkali-resistant) and the alkali-sensitive variety Noire Maroc (Alkali-sensitive), were provided by Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region Forage Crops Engineering Technology Research Center.

Plant growth and alkali treatment

Healthy and plump seeds were selected for the alkali stress experiment in germination boxes (18 cm × 14 cm × 6 cm) with 12 holes. The seedling substrate was a mixture of vermiculite and perlite (3:1, V/V), which was soaked with distilled water before sowing (sowing depth: 1 cm). Four seeds were sown per hole, and 12 holes per treatment served as one replicate. Seedlings were cultured with 1/2 Hoagland nutrient solution. The cultivation conditions are: light cycle of 14/10 hours (day/night), temperature of 25/20 ° C (day/night), light intensity of 200 µ mol m ⁻2 s ⁻1, and relative humidity of 65%. At the two-leaf and one-heart stage, alkali stress was applied : the experimental group was treated with 200 mL of a mixed solution containing 100 mmol·L⁻¹ Na2CO3:NaHCO3 (1:9, pH 9.5), while the control group was only supplied with nutrient solution. The treatment solution/nutrient solution was replaced every 2 days, with 5 replicates set in total.

Determination of morphological and physiological indicators

Determination of morphological indicators

Measurements were taken at 6 days after stress : RGR was calculated as RGR = (ln(m₂) - ln(m₁))/t, where m₁ is the total dry weight of the plant before treatment, m₂ is the total dry weight after treatment, and t is the number of treatment days.

LER was calculated as ΔS/t, where ΔS is the change in leaf area and t is the time.

Root activity was determined according to the method described by Zou et al.57.

Determination of physiological indicators

The following indicators were measured after stress : H2O2 was measured by spectrophotometry58: Under acidic conditions, H2O2 reacts with titanium ions (Ti⁴+) to form a yellow titanium peroxide complex, which has a maximum absorption peak at 415 nm wavelength. By measuring the absorbance, the content of H2O2 can be calculated. O2- histochemical staining method : O2- can reduce colorless nitroblue tetrazole (NBT) to insoluble blue methylstilbene precipitation. Therefore, blue spots will be formed at the position where O2- is generated. Activities of SOD, POD, and CAT59–61: SOD activity was determined by NBT photoreduction method, which is based on the ability of SOD to inhibit the reduction of NBT to blue formazan by superoxide anions. The absorbance change at 560 nm was measured, with 50% inhibition rate defined as 1 activity unit (U). POD activity was determined using guaiacol as the substrate, which is catalyzed to form a colored product in the presence of H₂O₂. The rate of absorbance increase at 470 nm was monitored, with 1 U defined as the amount of enzyme catalyzing 1 µmol of substrate per minute. CAT activity was calculated by directly measuring the rate of absorbance decrease of H₂O₂ at 240 nm, with 1 U defined as the amount of enzyme decomposing 1 µmol of H₂O₂ per minute. For these measurements, reaction temperature (25–30 ℃) and pH (pH 7.8 for SOD, pH 6.0 for POD, pH 7.0 for CAT) were controlled. Samples were homogenized and centrifuged, and protein concentration was determined to correct the activity (U/mg prot). Blank controls were set to exclude interference. Activities of GPX and GST : GPX activity was determined using H₂O₂ or organic peroxides as substrates. The rate of absorbance decrease of NADPH at 340 nm was measured via a coupled reaction, using a regeneration system composed of GR and NADPH. 1 activity unit (U) was defined as the amount of enzyme oxidizing 1 µmol of NADPH per minute.

GST activity was determined using 1-chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene (CDNB) as the substrate, and the rate of formation of the conjugate product of GSH and CDNB was monitored at 340 nm, with 1 U defined as the amount of enzyme catalyzing the formation of 1 µmol of product per minute. Both enzyme assays were performed in a phosphate buffer system at 37 ℃ and pH 7.0-7.5. Samples were homogenized in pre-cooled PBS and centrifuged, and the supernatant was used for determination. Blank controls without substrate or enzyme solution were set, and quantification was performed using a standard curve. The final results were corrected with protein concentration and expressed as U/mg prot. Relative electrical conductivity62: Determined by the conductivity meter method, which evaluates cell membrane permeability by comparing the changes in electrical conductivity of the extract before and after boiling.

Soluble sugar content63: Determined by the anthrone colorimetric method, with the absorbance of the sugar-anthrone complex measured at 620 nm. Soluble protein content64: Determined by the Coomassie Brilliant Blue G-250 method, with the absorbance of the protein-dye complex measured at 595 nm.Contents of Na⁺, K⁺, and Fe²⁺ in roots, stems, and leaves65,66: Determined by atomic absorption spectrophotometry. Samples were digested with nitric acid-perchloric acid, and the characteristic absorption values of Na⁺ (589 nm), K⁺ (766.5 nm), and Fe²⁺ (248.3 nm) in roots, stems, and leaves were measured, respectively. All determinations were performed in triplicate, and the results were expressed as content per dry weight or fresh weight (µmol/g or mg/g) and quantified using a standard curve.

Proteomic analysis

At 6 days after alkali stress at the seedling stage, samples of the second leaf from the top and 4–5 cm root tips were collected from the control and treatment groups of the two varieties, with 3 replicates (10 plants per replicate). Samples were stored in liquid nitrogen and sent to Shanghai Aquatic Bio-Technology Co., Ltd. for subsequent experiments.

Protein extraction was performed using a phenol-based extraction buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors. Samples were ground in liquid nitrogen to a fine powder. Protein concentration was determined using BCA assay with bovine serum albumin (BSA) as the standard. Enzymatic digestion was performed using trypsin at a 1:50 (enzyme-to-protein) ratio at 37 °C with 1000 rpm shaking overnight. LC separation was performed on a PePSep C18 column (1.9 μm, 75 μm × 15 cm) using the following gradient at a flow rate of 300 nL/min : 5–22% solvent B (0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile) over 45 min, 22–37% B in 5 min, 37–80% B in 5 min, and holding at 80% B for 5 min (Solvent A: 0.1% formic acid in water).

In this study, a label-free quantitative proteomics approach was employed to investigate the responses of barley roots and leaves to alkali stress. Protein samples were first separated by nanoflow liquid chromatography using a nanoElute 2 system (Bruker, Germany) and then analyzed by mass spectrometry with a timsTOF Pro 2 instrument (Bruker, Germany). The acquired raw data were processed using MaxQuant software for database search, identification, and quantification against the UniProt Hordeum vulgare database (version 4513-2023.05.fasta). The false discovery rate (FDR) for both peptides and proteins was set at ≤ 0.01. A total of 59,518 peptides and 8,225 protein groups were identified. Differentially abundant proteins (DAPs) were screened with the criteria of a fold change ≥ 1.2 or ≤ 0.83 and a P-value < 0.05 calculated by Student’s t-test or chi-square test (the latter based on frequency analysis).

Data processing

Microsoft Office Excel 2016, DPS 9.01, and Origin 2022 software were used for data processing.

Conclusions

Barley, as an alkali-tolerant plant, shows significant differences between JIA-HY460-6R (R) and Noire Maroc (NM) under alkali stress. In terms of morphology, R exhibits higher values in daily relative growth rate, leaf area expansion rate, and root activity compared to NM. Physiologically, R also outperforms NM in the antioxidant system (relative electrical conductivity, MDA content, SOD, POD, CAT, and GPX/GST cycle), ion balance (K⁺/Na⁺ ratio, Fe2⁺ content), and osmotic substances (soluble sugar and soluble protein content). Proteomic analysis identified a total of 1359 differentially abundant proteins, among which 27 important ones were screened out. These key proteins are mainly involved in plant MAPK signaling pathway, cysteine and methionine metabolism, amino acid biosynthesis, photosynthesis, glutathione metabolism, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, and α-linolenic acid metabolism. Notably, cysteine and methionine metabolism, plant MAPK signaling pathway, and glutathione metabolism are prominently involved in both varieties. These proteins, as potential target proteins, may play important roles in barley’s alkali tolerance. This study lays a foundation for understanding the molecular regulatory mechanisms underlying barley’s alkali tolerance.

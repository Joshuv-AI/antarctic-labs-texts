Postoperative ossification progression after decompression for thoracic ossification of the ligamentum flavum: associated factors identified by serial CT analysis
Authors: Kazuhiro Kai, Osamu Kawano, Hirokazu Saiwai, Muneaki Masuda, Kensuke Kubota, Kazuhiro Hata, Momo Irie, Sei Kuroishi, Kazuhiro Murotani, Takeshi Maeda
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPostoperative ossification progression after decompression for thoracic ossification of the ligamentum flavum (T-OLF) has been reported, but factors associated with its progression remain poorly understood. The role of additional instrumentation in preventing postoperative ossification progression also remains controversial.MethodsWe retrospectively reviewed 65 patients who underwent primary surgery for T-OLF between 2007 and 2024. Clinical characteristics and surgical outcomes were compared between patients treated with decompression alone and those treated with decompression and instrumentation. Detailed CT analysis was performed in 31 patients who underwent serial CT examinations before surgery, immediately after surgery, and at the final follow-up with at least 2 years of radiological follow-up. Residual ossification and postoperative ossification progression were evaluated using CT imaging.ResultsAmong the 65 patients, postoperative ossification progression occurred in five patients (7.6%), and two patients required revision surgery. No significant differences in postoperative ossification progression or reoperation were observed between the decompression-alone and instrumentation groups. In the detailed CT cohort, postoperative ossification progression was significantly associated with a greater preoperative ossification occupying ratio (p = 0.02) and residual ossification on immediate postoperative CT (p < 0.01). Instrumentation was not significantly associated with postoperative ossification progression.ConclusionsAdditional instrumentation was not significantly associated with postoperative ossification progression in this cohort. However, detailed CT analysis demonstrated that a greater preoperative ossification occupying ratio and residual ossification were associated with postoperative ossification progression. These findings suggest that minimizing residual ossification during decompression may be important for reducing postoperative ossification progression. Introduction

Ossification of the ligamentum flavum (OLF) is a pathological condition characterized by heterotopic bone formation within the ligamentum flavum, resulting in progressive spinal canal stenosis. Although OLF most frequently affects the thoracic spine, thoracic OLF (T-OLF) remains relatively uncommon overall and is reported predominantly in East Asian populations [1]. Surgical decompression has been widely recognized as an effective treatment for T-OLF and is generally associated with favorable long-term outcomes [2–4].

However, postoperative ossification progression has occasionally been observed during long-term follow-up [5–8]. Because postoperative ossification progression is a time-dependent process and may eventually lead to recurrent neurological symptoms requiring revision surgery, strategies to minimize postoperative progression remain an important clinical issue.

One potential strategy is the addition of posterior instrumentation and fusion to decompression surgery. Instrumentation may reduce mechanical stress at the operated segment and potentially suppress postoperative ossification progression [9–11]. However, the indications for instrumentation remain controversial because existing evidence is limited, and the benefit of instrumentation has not been clearly established [12].

Therefore, the purpose of this study was to investigate postoperative ossification progression after decompression for T-OLF using serial CT evaluation, to identify factors associated with postoperative ossification progression, and to explore the potential role of additional instrumentation.

Methods

Study participants

We retrospectively reviewed the medical records of patients with T-OLF who underwent spinal surgery at our institution between April 2007 and March 2024. Patients with other pathological conditions causing spinal cord compression, including thoracic disc herniation, ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament, cervical spondylotic myelopathy, or traumatic injury, were excluded.

The following data were collected: age, sex, body mass index (BMI), surgical level, ossification occupying ratio, surgical procedure (decompression alone or decompression with instrumentation), operative time, intraoperative blood loss, postoperative ossification progression at the same intervertebral level, reoperation, neurological outcomes, and duration of follow-up. Neurological outcomes were retrospectively assessed using the modified Frankel grading system in patients for whom sufficient clinical records were available. This retrospective study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Spinal Injuries Center, Fukuoka, Japan (Approval No. 25–13).

Surgical procedure and indications for instrumentation

All surgeries were performed using a posterior approach. Complete excision of the ossified ligamentum flavum was attempted whenever feasible. However, complete resection was avoided when strong adhesion to the dura mater was present and the risk of dural injury was considered high.

The decision to perform instrumentation was primarily based on intraoperative findings. Instrumented fusion was considered when severe dural adhesion made complete resection unsafe and residual ossification was expected to remain. However, instrumented fusion was not performed when the ossification was floated, severing its continuity with the lamina and facet joints.

CT analysis

Preoperative CT scans were obtained for all patients. At the surgical level, axial CT images were used to manually trace and measure the cross-sectional area of ossification and the spinal canal area. The ossification occupying ratio was calculated by dividing the ossified area (gray line) by the spinal canal area (black line) using the T-OLF method (Fig. 1).

At the surgical level on axial CT images, the cross-sectional area of ossification and the spinal canal area were manually traced and measured. The ossification occupying ratio was calculated by dividing the ossified area (gray line) by the spinal canal area (black line) using the T-OLF method

For detailed assessment of postoperative ossification progression, a subset of patients underwent CT imaging preoperatively, immediately after surgery (within one month after surgery), and at the final follow-up. Immediate postoperative CT images were used to evaluate residual ossification after the initial surgery. Residual ossification was defined as any ossified lesion identified on postoperative CT, including floating ossified lesions that had been surgically detached from the lamina but intentionally left attached to the dura because complete removal was considered unsafe. Final follow-up CT images were used to identify postoperative ossification progression at the same intervertebral level. Because distinguishing true recurrence from progression of residual ossification was not always feasible, both entities were collectively defined as postoperative ossification progression.

Patients included in the analysis

A total of 145 patients were initially identified. Eighty patients were excluded due to spinal cord compression caused by other pathologies, including thoracic disc herniation, ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament, cervical spondylosis myelopathy, and trauma. Ultimately, 65 patients were included in the study. Of these, 31 patients underwent serial CT examinations preoperatively, within one month after surgery, and at the final follow-up, with at least 2 years of radiological follow-up, and were included in the detailed CT analysis. Because postoperative ossification progression is a time-dependent phenomenon, patients without serial CT examinations or with less than 2 years of radiological follow-up were excluded from the CT analysis.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, and categorical variables were compared using Fisher's exact test. All statistical analyses were performed using JMP software (version 14.0.0; SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA). A two-sided p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Patient characteristics

Neurological outcomes assessed using the modified Frankel grading system were available in 52 of the 65 patients. Perioperative complications were also retrospectively reviewed. These data are summarized in Supplementary Table 1.

Most surgical levels were located in the lower thoracic region (T9–12) in 47 patients (72.4%). The mean ossification occupying ratio caused by T-OLF was 31% ± 17.6%. Decompression alone was performed in 57 patients (87.6%), whereas decompression with instrumentation was performed in 8 patients (12.4%).

The mean operative time for decompression alone was 126.4 ± 54.4 min (range, 39–252 min), and the mean intraoperative blood loss was 119.7 ± 86.3 mL (range, 16–435 mL). The mean operative time for decompression with instrumentation was 176.2 ± 69.2 min (range, 102–312 min), and the mean intraoperative blood loss was 123.6 ± 68.1 mL (range, 34–252 mL).

Postoperative ossification progression at the same intervertebral level occurred in 5 patients (7.6%), and reoperation was required in 2 patients (3.0%).

Postoperative ossification progression at the same intervertebral level after the initial surgery was observed in 5 patients (8.7%) in the decompression alone group, whereas no postoperative ossification progression was observed in the decompression with instrumentation group (0/8 patients). Analysis of postoperative ossification progression factors in the decompression alone group

Within the decompression alone group, patients were subdivided into a postoperative ossification progression group (5 patients) and a no postoperative ossification progression group (21 patients) for comparative analysis. The mean ossification occupying ratio caused by T-OLF was significantly higher in the postoperative ossification progression group than in the no postoperative ossification progression group (48.1% ± 12.9% vs. 28.8% ± 12.9%; p = 0.02).

Immediate postoperative CT imaging revealed residual ossification in 4 patients in the postoperative ossification progression group, whereas no residual ossification was detected in the no postoperative ossification progression group. This difference was statistically significant (p < 0.01) (Table 5).

Case 1: representative case of postoperative ossification progression after decompression alone

A man presented with myelopathy. CT demonstrated T-OLF at the T10/11 level. Decompression laminectomy with resection of the ossified ligamentum flavum was performed at the T10/11 level at our institution. An immediate postoperative CT scan obtained 1 month after the initial surgery revealed residual ossification at the same level. Three years after the initial surgery, the patient was referred to our hospital because of recurrent bilateral lower-extremity weakness. CT imaging demonstrated postoperative ossification progression at the T10/11 level. Revision surgery consisting of resection of the ossification and posterior instrumented fusion was subsequently performed (Fig. 2).

Case 2: representative case treated with decompression and instrumentation without postoperative ossification progression

A man presented with myelopathy, and CT imaging revealed T-OLF at the T10/11 level. Posterior decompression with instrumented fusion at the T10/11 level was performed at our institution. Discussion

This study investigated postoperative ossification progression following surgical treatment for T-OLF by comparing decompression alone with decompression combined with instrumentation. Postoperative ossification progression was observed only in patients who underwent decompression alone; however, no statistically significant association between instrumentation and postoperative ossification progression was identified. Furthermore, detailed CT analysis indicated that residual ossification on immediate postoperative imaging and a high spinal canal occupation ratio were associated with postoperative ossification progression.

Postoperative ossification progression after surgery for T-OLF is generally considered extremely rare, with most cases reported as isolated case reports [4, 9]. The reported incidence of postoperative ossification after surgery for T-OLF varies among previous studies. However, direct comparison with our findings should be made with caution because definitions of postoperative ossification are not uniform across the literature. In the present study, postoperative ossification progression included both enlargement of residual ossified lesions and newly developed ossification after complete resection. In contrast, previous reports may have defined recurrence differently and may not have routinely evaluated immediate postoperative CT to distinguish residual ossification from true recurrence. Therefore, the incidence reported in this study is not directly comparable with previously published recurrence rates.

The pathogenesis of ossification of the ligamentum flavum is believed to involve mechanical stress and repetitive microtrauma in addition to aging and genetic predisposition [11, 12]. Although surgical decompression aims to excise the ossified ligament, complete removal may be challenging in cases with strong adhesion to the dura mater or concomitant dural ossification. In such situations, partial preservation of the ossified lesion may be necessary to avoid dural injury. In the present study, residual ossification was frequently observed on immediate postoperative CT in patients who subsequently developed postoperative ossification progression, suggesting that residual ligamentum flavum tissue may serve as a substrate for re-ossification. Most postoperative ossification progression in our series appeared to represent progression of residual ossified lesions rather than true recurrence after complete excision. Therefore, the present findings should be interpreted as risk factors for postoperative ossification progression rather than true recurrence.

Although complete excision of ossified lesions is theoretically optimal, T-OLF is associated with a high incidence of dural ossification, which increases the risk of dural tears and cerebrospinal fluid leakage [13, 14]. Consequently, surgeons must balance the need for safe surgical technique with the goal of minimizing postoperative ossification progression. The findings of this study suggest that residual ossification following decompression alone may be associated with postoperative ossification progression when complete resection is not feasible, providing clinically relevant insight for surgical decision-making.

To date, evidence supporting the efficacy of instrumented fixation in T-OLF surgery remains limited [5]. Because instrumentation was not significantly associated with postoperative ossification progression in the present study, the subgroup of patients who may benefit from instrumentation remains unclear. Based on our intraoperative experience, patients with severe dural adhesion, suspected dural ossification, or substantial residual ossification may represent potential candidates for instrumentation; however, this hypothesis requires validation in larger prospective studies.

This study has several limitations. First, this was a single-center retrospective study with a relatively small sample size. Second, the instrumentation group consisted of only eight patients, all of whom were male. Therefore, selection bias and insufficient statistical power cannot be excluded. In addition, the choice of instrumentation was based on the surgeon's intraoperative judgment rather than randomization. Consequently, the present findings should be interpreted with caution and require confirmation in larger multicenter studies. Third, although postoperative ossification progression was assessed using CT imaging, measurement error and interobserver variability cannot be completely excluded. Future multicenter collaborative studies with larger cohorts and longer follow-up periods are warranted to further clarify the role of instrumentation in preventing postoperative ossification progression after T-OLF surgery.

Conclusion

In the present study, additional instrumentation was not significantly associated with postoperative ossification progression. However, detailed CT analysis demonstrated that a greater preoperative ossification occupying ratio and residual ossification were associated with postoperative ossification progression. These findings suggest that minimizing residual ossification during decompression may be an important factor in reducing postoperative ossification progression. Further multicenter studies with larger cohorts are needed to clarify the role of instrumentation in selected high-risk patients.

Diaphragmatic hernia following right hemihepatectomy
Authors: Yuxiao Sun, Lei Zhang
Published: 2026

A 37-year-old woman was found to have a right liver mass 7 months ago. Computed tomography (CT) revealed a huge lesion (22.6 cm × 17.6 cm × 12.7 cm) in the right lobe, suggestive of hemangioma. On August 13, 2025, she underwent laparoscopic right hemihepatectomy under general anesthesia; no diaphragmatic injury was noted intraoperatively. Pathology confirmed hepatic cavernous hemangioma. Postoperative CT showed no diaphragmatic hernia.

On March 5, 2026, after emotional agitation and crying, she developed abdominal pain with obstipation. CT revealed diaphragmatic hernia with intestinal obstruction; three-dimensional reconstruction identified the hernia site (Figure 1A). Intraoperatively, a 2-cm defect was identified at the central tendon of the right hemidiaphragm (Figure 1B). Small bowel herniated into the thorax, and mild adhesions were present between the diaphragm and mesentery. The herniated intestinal segment had good blood supply without congestion, ischemia or strangulation. The defect was extended transversely, adhesions were lysed, the bowel was reduced, and the defect was closed with V-Lock sutures. Postoperative examination showed no residual diaphragmatic defect. We proposed that the diaphragmatic hernia resulted from diaphragmatic thinning caused by long‑standing compression from a large hepatic hemangioma.

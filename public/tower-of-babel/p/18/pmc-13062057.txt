MSF-VMDNet for multi class segmentation of skin cancer whole slide images using a multi frequency dual encoder network
Authors: Jiangliang Zhang, Qiumei Pu, Jinglong Tian, Junhao Wang, Jieyao Wei, Menghan Yang, Lina Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSkin cancer has become a global public health issue. Dermoscopy is a routine diagnostic method; however, to improve accuracy, it is often combined with skin punch biopsy and stained histological slides for microscopic observation. The manual segmentation of the lesion area by doctors involves issues such as high subjectivity and time consumption. Deep learning techniques have become a mainstream solution. Compared to foreground-background segmentation in dermoscopic images, the semantic segmentation task for whole-slide skin cancer images is more complex, requiring precise differentiation of 10 distinct tissue classes (such as tumor, epidermis, dermis, hair follicle, sweat gland, fat, etc.). Among these, various epithelial and dermal tissue types exhibit similar morphological features and are interwoven, which increases segmentation difficulty. To address this, we propose a multi-frequency domain attention-based dual encoder network (MSF-VMDNet), which combines U-Net and Vision Mamba dual encoders for parallel feature extraction. The U-Net encoder incorporates an improved AFNO spectral decomposition module, which uses a frequency domain mechanism to extract high-resolution multi-class semantic information. It further strengthens spatial information through multi-scale feature aggregation, improving segmentation accuracy at class boundaries and making the contours clearer. The Vision Mamba encoder, based on a Linear State Space Model (SSM), optimizes long-range dependency modeling and enhances both global and local feature perception. By utilizing a multi-frequency domain mechanism, this encoder maps subtle class-discriminative features from the skin histological slide into the frequency domain, reinforcing contextual features and reducing misclassification rates. In the decoding phase, the SCConv module fuses features from different frequency domains and spatial levels. Experimental results show that MSF-VMDNet significantly outperforms existing methods in terms of class segmentation performance on skin cancer histological slide datasets, achieving an MIoU of 95.37% and a Dice coefficient of 95.11%. Additionally, the model demonstrates its generalization ability in extended experiments on the ISIC 2018 dermoscopic image, PanNuke pathological cell nucleus image, and Synapse image datasets.Keywords: Skin cancer, Multi-class segmentation, Vision Mamba, Multi-frequency domain attention, Pathological imagesSubject terms: Biological techniques, Cancer, Cell biology, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Diseases, Oncology

Introduction

The outermost layer of the human body is the skin, which serves functions such as barrier protection, absorption, secretion, excretion, metabolism, immune defense, temperature regulation, and sensation1. Skin diseases are typically considered a form of inflammation. Under normal conditions, the epidermis can remove unhealthy cells through metabolism2. Skin cancer results from DNA mutations in skin cells, causing uncontrolled growth and invasion to other parts of the body. The three most common types of skin cancer3 are basal cell carcinoma (BCC), squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), and melanoma. With environmental changes such as the thinning of the ozone layer, the incidence of skin cancer is on the rise4,5. Statistics show that the incidence of skin cancer is generally higher among Caucasians. A study by the American Cancer Society reported 73,680 new cases in 2016, of which approximately 10,130 deaths were confirmed to be caused by the deterioration of the natural environment6,7.

Thanks to the advent of dermoscopy and biopsy technologies, physicians’ diagnostic capabilities have been greatly improved8. Dermoscopy imaging is a non-invasive imaging technique that uses polarized light to magnify the skin surface. Dermoscopy enables the acquisition of high-resolution images, which help to initially assess a patient’s condition9. To further understand the condition, skin biopsy combined with staining techniques is one of the key steps in skin cancer diagnosis10. This technique involves obtaining tissue samples from skin lesions, staining them, and examining them under a microscope to clearly present tissue structures and cellular abnormalities11,12.

Currently, there are several publicly available datasets for dermoscopic images, including the ISIC 201713 and ISIC 201814 datasets, which contain more than 2,000 skin lesion images and are widely used in the automated detection and early screening of skin cancer. In recent years, combining biopsy and staining technologies has allowed for more detailed microscopic observation of skin cancer histopathology images. This paper focuses on multi-class segmentation of skin cancer using the Histopathology Non-Melanoma Skin Cancer Segmentation Dataset15. This publicly available dataset consists of whole-slide pathological images, typically reaching several hundred million pixels, and supports multi-magnification to provide rich macroscopic and microscopic information, including tissue structure, pathological features, cellular structures, and tumor microenvironments16. The advent of digital pathology has transformed traditional glass slides into whole-slide pathological images, greatly advancing the development of automated diagnostic technologies17.

In clinical diagnostics, pathologists rely on their domain knowledge and extensive experience to observe and analyze tumor tissue regions under a microscope and ultimately make diagnoses18. However, this process is complex, time-consuming, and subjective. The rapid development of deep learning-based automatic segmentation techniques has facilitated the analysis of tumors. DTONet19 employs a U-Net dual-decoder structure combined with a global channel attention mechanism to enhance the correlation between channels, expand the receptive field, and improve the perception of skin and foreground information at different frequencies while effectively suppressing background interference irrelevant to skin cancer (such as pores and pigments), thereby highlighting the lesion features. LeaNet20 uses a lightweight symmetric U-shaped architecture, incorporating efficient dilation channel attention and bridge attention mechanisms, capturing local texture features and inflammation manifestations of tumors while enhancing the model’s ability to fuse features at different levels. Thomas et al.21 proposed a multi-class segmentation method for skin cancer histology images, particularly for non-melanoma skin cancers such as basal cell carcinoma (BCC), squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), and intraepidermal carcinoma (IEC). Their model not only segments images into different tissue types (such as inflammatory and cancerous regions) but also identifies skin tissue and its boundaries. The MLCA2F22 network integrates multi-scale contextual features to improve the boundary recognition accuracy of COVID-19 lesions. Bakkouri23 employed a multi-layer feature fusion network combined with transfer learning to enhance the CAD system’s ability to recognize complex skin lesions, strengthening its practical application in the medical field. Pyramid-CNN24 uses Gaussian pyramid representation and region proposal extraction to achieve fast multi-scale recognition of breast abnormalities. BG-3DM2F25 utilizes bidirectional gated 3D multi-scale feature fusion to capture subtle hippocampal variations, providing a novel approach for hippocampal analysis in Alzheimer’s disease patients. Despite the progress made by these methods in medical image segmentation, challenges remain in distinguishing background regions with similar morphological structures and integrating global high-frequency information, especially when dealing with lesion or tumor regions of varying sizes and background areas with similar textures. This often leads to misclassification between pathological and normal tissues, resulting in fuzzy and unclear boundaries.

This paper focuses on the multi-class segmentation of skin cancer pathological images using the Histopathology Non-Melanoma Skin Cancer Segmentation Dataset15. In these pathological slices, tumor regions are typically highly intertwined with surrounding normal tissues (such as epidermis, dermis, hair follicles, sweat glands, adipose tissue, etc.), exhibiting complex tissue morphology that increases the difficulty of the segmentation task. Related work

This section introduces relevant research on multi-class segmentation of skin cancer whole-slide images (WSI) based on deep learning, covering three main areas: skin cancer image segmentation using multi-attention encoders, medical image segmentation via large model fine-tuning, and the application of Vision Mamba in image segmentation tasks.

Skin Cancer Image Segmentation Based on Multi-Attention Mechanism Encoder Researchers have improved segmentation performance by modifying U-Net’s encoder-decoder architecture, especially by incorporating multi-channel, multi-scale feature extraction and fusion mechanisms alongside Transformer models. For example, MHorUNet28 combines Transformer’s global modeling with convolution’s local feature extraction using a recursive gated convolution mechanism. This approach enhances lesion recognition accuracy by improving both skin texture and overall morphological features. Melanoma diagnosis29 has been enhanced using deep learning and fuzzy logic, combining YOLO-based detection with segmentation techniques to improve accuracy and speed on dermoscopic images. ADF-Net30 integrates ResNet and BiFormer to achieve dual-stream feature interaction, effectively combining coarse and fine-grained lesion characteristics. CTH-Net31 merges CNN and Transformer architectures with a Multi-Domain Feature Fusion Module (MFFM), boosting the extraction of key features while suppressing irrelevant ones. Similarly, HmsU-Net32 improves global feature perception and reduces background interference through CNN-Transformer fusion, achieving precise segmentation of lesion areas in dermoscopic images and multi-organ segmentation in abdominal CT.

However, U-Net primarily focuses on local features, while Transformer, though powerful in global modeling, suffers from high computational complexity. This can result in semantic loss when merging long- and short-range information, hindering the precision of lesion localization. To address this, we propose a dual encoder network based on Vision Mamba (VMamba). VMamba combines Transformer’s long-range dependency modeling with CNN’s local detail extraction, optimizing the architecture to reduce time and space complexity by an order of magnitude, leading to efficient high- and low-level semantic fusion for precise multi-class lesion segmentation. VM-UNet33 achieves fast fusion of global context and local details, enabling efficient feature extraction and integration to improve segmentation accuracy.

Medical Image Segmentation Based on Large Model Fine-Tuning In 2023, Meta AI introduced the Segment Anything Model (SAM)34, a large model using the SA-1B dataset, the largest segmentation dataset ever created. SAM’s structure includes an image encoder, a prompt encoder, and a mask decoder. Ma et al. proposed MedSAM35, a model covering ten imaging modalities and over thirty cancer types. Current research focuses on fine-tuning large models to meet medical segmentation needs. For instance, H-SAM36 enhances SAM’s performance by introducing a hierarchical decoding strategy, significantly improving segmentation accuracy and reducing reliance on large-scale retraining. Similarly, EfficientSAM37 optimizes SAM for medical image segmentation by reducing computational complexity while maintaining segmentation accuracy and enabling faster inference.

Despite large models’ potential in medical imaging, limitations remain. These models tend to have large parameter scales, limiting their use in resource-constrained environments. Additionally, fine-tuning often reduces the model’s flexibility. In contrast, task-specific models like U-Net and Transformer offer greater adaptability and often outperform in performance.

Application of Vision Mamba in Image Segmentation Tasks Vision Mamba38 is an emerging architecture that combines the Bidirectional State Space Model (SSM) and positional embedding for global context modeling with linear computational complexity. In ImageNet classification, VMamba outperforms traditional Transformers (e.g., DeiT), especially for high-resolution images. In medical segmentation, Vision Mamba is integrated with U-Net in the VM-UNet model33, which combines Vision Mamba’s efficient global modeling and U-Net’s multi-scale feature extraction. The model shows superior performance in medical image datasets such as ISIC2017, ISIC 2018, and Synapse, providing high segmentation accuracy and efficiency compared to traditional methods. This study integrates Vision Mamba to enhance segmentation performance while optimizing the model’s structure for greater efficiency and reduced weight.

To address these limitations, MSF-VMDNet fully integrates the advantages of multiple models. It employs a dual-encoder structure, combining U-Net for high-resolution local feature extraction and VMamba for efficient global modeling, while introducing an AFNO-based multi-frequency mechanism. Low-frequency features enhance the perception of overall lesion morphology and macro-level localization, whereas high-frequency features refine boundaries and texture, achieving multi-level, multi-frequency semantic fusion. Compared with methods relying solely on Transformer or VMamba, MSF-VMDNet performs joint modeling of global context, local details, and frequency-domain features. Additionally, SCConv is incorporated in the decoder to strengthen cross-level feature fusion, ensuring complete semantic transmission. The model also supports residual connections, multi-scale feature fusion, and multi-channel integration, enabling efficient information flow and rich feature representation. Experimental results demonstrate that this design achieves clearer boundaries and more accurate segmentation in complex backgrounds, significantly outperforming U-Net+Transformer and U-Net+VMamba in overall performance.

Methods

Network architecture

In pathological whole-slide images of skin cancer, tumor areas often intertwine with surrounding normal tissues (such as the epidermis, dermis, hair follicles, sweat glands, adipose tissue, etc.), especially in the tumor boundary transition zone, where the division is blurred and difficult. For instance, basal cell carcinoma (BCC) is commonly accompanied by basophilic cells and mucinous stroma, which are hard to distinguish from normal dermis in microscopic images. Moreover, the distribution patterns of tumors and epithelial regions are diverse, possibly appearing as spots, blocks, stripes, or diffuse forms, with significant differences in density and size. This necessitates image segmentation to integrate both local texture and global features to enhance the network’s segmentation ability.

To address these challenges, inspired by physical modulation-demodulation mechanisms, this paper proposes a dual-encoder network based on multi-frequency domain attention improvement. The structure, as shown in Fig. 1, consists of two parallel encoders: UNet and VMamba, which leverage the multi-frequency domain mechanism to extract features from different levels in pathological slides, including from macroscopic cellular stroma to fine boundary details in different regions. In part (a) of Fig. 1, the UNet encoder, by incorporating the improved AFNO spectral decomposition mechanism, achieves multi-class semantic information segmentation of skin cancer while precisely detecting boundaries. As seen in part (b) of the figure, the VMamba encoder, combining spectral layers and multi-head self-attention layers, improves tumor segmentation performance, especially when handling visually similar tumor areas. This method fully considers the contextual features of tissue slides and, compared to Transformers, improves both the time and spatial complexity. In the decoding phase, the introduction of the SCConv module further enhances feature fusion across different frequency domains, improving segmentation performance and reinforcing the expression of different skin regions. The spatial-enhanced AFNO multi-frequency domain mechanism

In deep learning-based medical image segmentation, efficiently processing high-resolution tumor slices while improving both accuracy and efficiency remains a key challenge. Tumor slices contain various tissue structures (such as hair follicles, epithelium, cytoplasm, and cell nuclei) with diverse feature distributions and often blurry boundaries. Thus, models must reconstruct features across multiple dimensions to enhance multi-class information discrimination. Inspired by the adaptive feature mixing strategy based on spectral decomposition (AFNO)39, we propose a spatially-enhanced AFNO multi-frequency domain mechanism, which optimizes the spectral-to-spatial domain mapping in the AFNO module.

Spectral attention mechanism in VMamba encoder

In the task of segmenting stained histopathological images of skin cancer, this paper introduces a spectral attention mechanism (Spectral Block) into the VMamba encoder to enhance the contextual representation of pathological regions. VMamba employs Spectral Attention to extract global features, integrates SS2D to reinforce structural information, and leverages Coordinate Attention to improve positional representation. This process is repeated four times, making it particularly effective for handling complex tumor distributions (e.g., patchy, strip-like, or diffused patterns) and enhancing the smoothness and accuracy of segmentation boundaries, as shown in Fig. 3.

VMamba encoder mechanism.

The Spectral Attention40 mechanism, illustrated in Fig. 3b, utilizes the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) to map image features into the frequency domain, distinguishing spectral characteristics of different tissues. High-frequency components enhance boundary and texture details, whereas low-frequency components provide shape and structural information. Subsequently, the Spectral Gating mechanism dynamically adjusts frequency components via learnable parameters to emphasize key information, such as enhancing high-frequency components in tumor regions to improve boundary clarity while suppressing irrelevant low-frequency components to reduce background interference. These adjustments are optimized via backpropagation to adapt to variations in tumor morphology across different histopathological slides. The weighted spectral features are transformed back to the spatial domain using the Inverse Fast Fourier Transform (IFFT) and combined with normalization and an MLP for channel mixing, thereby enhancing local-global feature interactions. Additionally, spectral gating is employed for token mixing, improving spatial feature representation and enabling the network to more accurately segment interwoven tumor, follicle, and epithelial tissue regions.

As shown in Fig. 3c, the SS2D (State Space 2D) module38 is a core component of the Visual State Space Model (VSSM), designed to efficiently process 2D image data. The primary workflow consists of input mapping, state-space processing, and output mapping. In the input phase, raw image features undergo a linear transformation to obtain a 2D feature representation. The features are then adjusted via 2D convolution to match the required shape for state-space processing.

In the state-space processing phase, SS2D adopts a cross-scan mechanism, expanding features along different directions to form four-channel representations. These expanded features are processed by the S6 state-space model, capturing long-range dependencies. The S6 module, derived from Mamba, introduces a Selection Mechanism over S4, dynamically adjusting State Space Model (SSM) parameters based on input data, thereby retaining critical information while filtering out irrelevant features. SCConv module for spectral-spatial feature fusion in decoding

In the decoding stage, this study proposes an efficient convolutional neural network (CNN)-based module—SCConv41—to enhance the fusion and representation capabilities of spectral and spatial features in the dual-encoder framework for skin tumor histopathological segmentation. The SCConv module consists of two core components: the Spatial Reconstruction Unit (SRU) and the Channel Reconstruction Unit (CRU). These units collaboratively optimize intermediate features by eliminating redundancy and enhancing feature expressiveness, thereby improving the overall network performance. The process is illustrated in Fig. 4.

SCConv mechanism in the decoding stage.

The SRU optimizes spatial features through two stages: separation and reconstruction. In the separation stage, group normalization (GN) is employed to analyze the spatial information of the feature map, dividing it into high-information and low-information components. The high-information component preserves details, whereas the low-information component is treated as redundant. Through a gating mechanism, the SRU further enhances high-information features while suppressing redundancy. Subsequently, the SRU integrates these components via a cross-reconstruction operation, facilitating information flow and generating spatially expressive features.

The CRU optimizes channel-wise features by first partitioning the input feature map into high-channel and low-channel information components. For the high-channel component, grouped convolution and point-wise convolution are applied to extract high-level semantic information. Meanwhile, the low-channel component undergoes depth-wise convolution to capture shallow features efficiently. Finally, both components are fused to generate optimized channel features. By following this “partition-transform-fusion” strategy, the CRU significantly compresses redundant channel information and enhances feature representation capacity.

By combining SRU and CRU operations, the SCConv module efficiently optimizes both spatial and channel features, facilitating feature fusion within U-Net and VMamba encoders. This module effectively suppresses irrelevant channel noise, enhances feature expressiveness, and enables the network to better handle complex morphological structures. As a result, the SCConv module demonstrates superior performance in skin tumor-stained histopathological image segmentation.

It is worth noting that the frequency-domain mechanism adopted by MSF-VMDNet is essentially different from existing models such as DctViT42, DBFFT43, and Hms-UNet32. DctViT42 employs Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to map input features into a fixed frequency domain, compressing redundant information and strengthening low-frequency components where energy is concentrated. However, this static transformation cannot dynamically adapt to the feature distributions of different lesion regions, resulting in insufficient modeling of boundaries and high-frequency textures. DBFFT43 leverages Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) to extract global spectral information, which effectively captures periodic structures. Nevertheless, it lacks adaptive regulation of different frequency components, making it difficult for the model to balance tumor boundary details with overall structural information. Hms-UNet32 introduces frequency-domain interaction within a CNN–Transformer hybrid framework, but this interaction remains confined to the implicit layer via attention-based spectral mixing, without explicit frequency decomposition and reconstruction, thus limiting its ability to handle multi-class boundaries under complex tissue backgrounds.

In contrast, MSF-VMDNet incorporates an explicit FFT/IFFT transformation within the Vmamba encoder, enabling dynamic modeling where low-frequency components enhance global structural perception and high-frequency components refine boundary and texture details. Moreover, the Spectral Gating mechanism adaptively regulates frequency components through learnable parameters, while the SS2D module captures long-range dependencies and the SCConv module in the decoding stage achieves spectral–spatial feature fusion. This three-stage optimization—“frequency decomposition, dynamic regulation, and cross-domain fusion”—significantly improves boundary sharpness and inter-class discrimination of tumors and related tissues, making MSF-VMDNet more effective and robust than existing approaches in multi-class pathological segmentation.

Experiments

Dataset description

This study uses the histopathological data set for non-melanoma skin cancer15, which focuses on the histopathological segmentation of non-melanoma skin cancer(NMSC). The dataset consists of hematoxylin and eosin (H&E)-stained tissue section images with precisely annotated segmentation masks. All images were provided by MyLab Pathology Laboratory in Salisbury, Australia, with data collection spanning from late 2017 to early 2018.

The dataset covers a diverse patient population, with ages ranging from 34 to 96 years and a median age of 70 years. The gender distribution includes two-thirds female and one-third male patients, aligning with the epidemiological trend of NMSC prevalence in the Australian population. The dataset comprises a total of 290 images, categorized into three cancer types: basal cell carcinoma (140 images), squamous cell carcinoma (60 images), and intraepidermal carcinoma (90 images). These tissue sections were obtained through different biopsy techniques, including curettage biopsy (100 images), punch biopsy (58 images), and excisional biopsy (132 images).

All images were captured using an Olympus DP27 microscope camera at a magnification of 10. Additionally, the dataset provides raw-resolution (1) images along with downsampled versions at 2, 5, and 10 magnifications to meet different research needs. The segmentation masks were manually annotated by expert pathologists using ImageJ software and encompass 12 distinct tissue types, including glands, inflammatory cells, hair follicles, subcutaneous tissue, reticular dermis, papillary dermis, epidermis, keratin, background, as well as basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and intraepidermal carcinoma. In this study, we focus specifically on cancer cell segmentation, merging the three cancer subtypes into a single class. The segmentation masks are stored in PNG format with color-coded legends to distinguish different tissue types, as illustrated in Fig. 5. The dataset was split into a training and test set with a 4:1 ratio for model training.

Illustration of the histopathology skin cancer dataset.

To further validate the model’s segmentation performance on dermoscopic images, we utilize the ISIC 2018 dataset14, which consists of over 10,000 dermoscopic images covering various skin lesion types with manually annotated lesion regions. This dataset has been widely used for skin lesion detection and segmentation tasks, providing a robust benchmark for evaluating our model’s effectiveness.

To assess the multi-class segmentation capability of our model, we further conduct experiments on the PanNuke26 and Synapse datasets. The PanNuke dataset contains histopathological nuclear images with multi-class annotations, including stromal cell nuclei, epithelial cells, inflammatory cells, and cancer cells. In this study, we select 800 annotated images to evaluate the model’s performance in nuclear segmentation, particularly in identifying and segmenting cancer cell nuclei.

Additionally, the Synapse dataset27 is employed to validate the model’s generalizability in medical image segmentation across different domains. This dataset primarily focuses on organ and tissue segmentation in computed tomography (CT) images collected from various patients and anatomical structures. By conducting experiments on these multi-class datasets, we aim to verify the model’s generalization ability and adaptability across different medical imaging tasks.

As summarized in Table 2, the datasets used in this study vary in size, type, and image resolution. NMSC dataset15 has a small number of images and BCC predominates, with patients mainly older females and all samples from a single lab. We mitigated class imbalance and potential demographic/institutional bias by resizing all images to 512512, applying data augmentation, and merging three cancer types into a single class. ISIC 201814 has a large and diverse set of skin lesion images. Despite variations in original resolution, uniform scaling and cropping were applied to enhance model adaptability to different image resolutions and sources. PanNuke26 images were selected to favor patches with higher nuclei counts. Multi-class nuclei labels and uniform resizing allow the model to learn diverse nuclei types and boundaries, improving generalization to sparse or complex nuclei distributions. Synapse27 has a limited number of subjects, but splitting training and validation sets across organs and patients enables learning of general organ features, enhancing adaptation to new patients and different scanners.

Data processing

This study conducted multi-class segmentation training on four datasets: Histopathology Non-Melanoma Skin Cancer (NMSC), dermoscopic images (ISIC 2018), nuclei images (PanNuke), and abdominal CT organs (Synapse). To adapt the datasets for deep learning, all images were resized to and normalized to the range [0, 1]. Segmentation masks were mapped to single-channel semantic labels to mitigate staining or imaging variations and to enhance feature learning.For the NMSC dataset, histopathology images were processed in grayscale. Segmentation masks included 10 classes: background (Class 0), normal tissues (Classes 1–8), and cancer-associated tissues (Class 9). Due to the small dataset size, rotation-based augmentation was applied to the training set. For ISIC 2018, original images were RGB and covered melanoma, nevi, and other skin lesions. As the dataset was large, no additional augmentation was applied. In PanNuke, both images and masks were RGB, containing five nuclei types (epithelial, stromal, inflammatory, tumor, and unlabeled/other). Since the dataset was pre-selected for high nuclear density, no additional augmentation was applied. For Synapse, original CT slices were grayscale, covering eight abdominal organs (aorta, gallbladder, spleen, left/right kidneys, liver, pancreas, and stomach). Due to limited subject numbers, no additional augmentation was applied.

Training was conducted using an NVIDIA RTX 4070 GPU with 24GB memory. All datasets were resized to 512512. Only the NMSC training set used rotation augmentation due to limited sample size, while other datasets were directly trained without additional preprocessing. Conclusion

This paper proposes a multi-frequency domain attention-based dual-encoder network (MSF-VMDNet) for multi-class segmentation of skin cancer histopathological images. MSF-VMDNet combines U-Net and Vision Mamba encoders and introduces a multi-frequency domain mechanism to effectively extract features at various scales, enhancing segmentation performance in complex tissue backgrounds. The U-Net encoder employs an improved AFNO spectral decomposition module for high-resolution multi-class semantic extraction and optimized spectral-to-spatial mapping. The Vision Mamba encoder, based on a linear state-space model (SSM), efficiently models long-range dependencies and integrates Transformer advantages, improving global and local feature perception. The SCConv module in the decoding stage optimizes the fusion of frequency-domain and spatial features, further enhancing multi-class skin tissue representation.

Experimental results demonstrate that MSF-VMDNet outperforms state-of-the-art methods on stained histopathological datasets of skin cancer, achieving clearer tumor contours and significantly improved segmentation accuracy, with an IoU of 95.37% and a Dice coefficient of 95.11%. Furthermore, MSF-VMDNet also exhibits strong generalization ability in experiments on the ISIC 2018 dermoscopic image dataset, PanNuke nuclear pathology image dataset, and Synapse image dataset, further validating its robustness in handling complex medical image segmentation tasks.

In the future, the model can be further optimized. Our next research direction will explore whole-slide-level feature extraction, such as tumor area and spatial distribution, and incorporate clinical features such as tumor depth and location. By extracting richer feature representations, we aim to develop a multi-modal, multi-level diagnostic and grading model for skin cancer.

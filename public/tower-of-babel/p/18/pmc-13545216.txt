Mechanistic study and control of coal pillar deformation and failure in gob-side roadways with extra-thick seams and hard roof
Authors: Jianlin Zhou, Qian Shen, Changjiang Li, Hongyu Jin, Yuxuan Liu, Hanze Jiang, Tongzhu Zhou, Chengyuan Li, Bowen Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo address the large deformation failure of coal pillar ribs in gob-side roadways under the geological conditions of extra-thick coal seams with hard roofs, their mechanical instability mechanism was systematically revealed through comprehensive theoretical analysis, numerical simulation, and field measurements, and an innovative coordinated control system was developed accordingly.Theoretical investigations identified a coupled disaster-inducing mechanism combining intensive dynamic loading effects from periodic roof fracturing with high-stress gradients in extra-thick coal seams, inducing significant stress migration in coal pillar ribs. Based on the dynamic evolution of the overlying strata structure and mining-induced stress field, a reinforcement system using constant-resistance energy-absorbing (NPR) anchor cables was established to enhance the stability of the coal pillar rib. Numerical simulations showed that the NPR anchor cable reinforcement reduced the coal pillar plastic-zone depth by 62.5%-78.6% and decreased the coal pillar stress peaks by 8.9%-17.5%. Field measurements further showed that, compared with the original support section, the peak coal stress in the reinforced section decreased by 20.9%-26.3%, rib bulging was reduced by approximately 52%, and the borehole fissure depth was reduced by about 50%. The developed coordinated control scheme provides a reference for coal pillar rib stability control in gob-side roadways under similar extra-thick coal seam and hard-roof conditions.Keywords: Coal pillar failure, Deformation mechanistic, Extra-thick coal seam, Hard roof, Reinforcement support, Surrounding rock controlSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Solid Earth sciences

Introduction

Coal, as China’s dominant energy resource, plays a strategic role in ensuring national energy security and sustaining socio-economic development1–5. In western mining regions, the fully mechanized top-coal caving (FMTCC) technique has been widely adopted for thick seam extraction. Despite its adaptability to extra-thick coal seams, field observations reveal severe strata behaviors in gob-side roadways under the influence of hard roof strata in extra-thick coal seams6–9. Statistical analyses of mine accidents indicate that over 60% of roadway instability incidents occur in gob-side roadways subjected to multiple mining-induced stresses, with 78% of these failures attributed to large deformations of coal pillar ribs10–12. Such persistent deformation poses a severe threat to operational safety and leads to an acute reduction in the usable roadway cross-section, significantly increasing the technical and economic burdens of subsequent support and maintenance13,14.

Current research on coal pillar rib deformation and surrounding rock control mainly focuses on three aspects: (1) the failure mechanism of coal pillars; (2) the stress distribution characteristics of surrounding rock; and (3) surrounding rock control technologies.

In terms of coal pillar failure mechanisms, related studies have shown that coal pillars under mining disturbance usually experience crack initiation and propagation, plastic-zone development, and gradual degradation of bearing capacity15,16. Wu et al.17 revealed the evolution process of crack initiation, propagation, and instability failure in gob-side coal pillars through field tests and numerical simulation. Gao et al.18 established a coal pillar stability evaluation model based on elastoplastic analysis, indicating that coal pillar stability is closely related to the distribution of elastic-plastic zones and the applied load. Liu et al.19 evaluated the degradation degree of coal pillars using a digital zoning method and divided the coal pillar into a fractured zone, yielding zone, and load-bearing zone. Das et al.20 investigated the failure mode of inclined coal pillars under complex stress paths by combining numerical simulation, tensor statistical analysis, and field validation. These studies revealed the characteristics of coal pillar crack evolution, zonal failure, and bearing-capacity degradation. However, their research objects mainly focused on gob-side coal pillars or mining conditions under conventional conditions. Therefore, for extra-thick coal seams with hard roofs, the relationship among coal pillar rib crack development, loose-zone expansion, and strong field strata behavior still needs to be further analyzed based on specific engineering conditions.

In terms of surrounding rock stress distribution, previous studies have mainly focused on mining-induced stress redistribution, stress peak location, coal pillar width, and the plastic zone of roadway surrounding rock21,22. Whittaker et al.23 discussed mining-induced stress concentration under longwall mining conditions in early studies. Hou et al.24 analyzed the excavation influence range, stress peak location, and dynamic-pressure disturbance boundary of roadways by combining field tests and numerical simulation. Li et al.25 studied coal pillar yielding under a double-roadway layout and the influence of superimposed stress from adjacent gobs on roadway stability. Xia et al.21 analyzed the distribution of principal stress difference around gob-side roadways under different coal pillar widths. Rezaei et al.22 established a strain-energy-based analytical model for the evolution of mining-induced stress over gates and pillars, thereby calculating mining-induced stress redistribution and stress concentration factors. These studies show that the stability of coal pillar ribs in gob-side roadways depends not only on the strength of the coal pillar itself, but also on the combined effects of lateral abutment pressure from the adjacent gob, front abutment pressure from the active working face, and multi-stage mining disturbance. However, for the stability of coal pillar ribs in gob-side roadways under extra-thick coal seam and hard-roof conditions, existing studies on stress distribution can explain mining-induced stress redistribution, stress concentration, and plastic-zone expansion. Nevertheless, analysis based only on stress peak location or plastic-zone range is still insufficient to clarify the relationship among coal pillar rib crack development, loose-zone expansion, and local shear instability. Therefore, on the basis of previous studies on mining-induced stress, it is necessary to further consider the fracture characteristics of overlying strata, the loading effect of the lateral cantilever structure, and the field failure characteristics of the coal pillar rib, so as to provide a targeted explanation of coal pillar rib instability.

In terms of surrounding rock control technologies, previous studies have mainly focused on anchorage reinforcement, grouting reinforcement, secondary support, and pressure-relief control26,27. Zhang et al.28 established an equivalent strain-softening model for deep jointed rock mass with post-peak strain-softening behavior, and optimized a composite support scheme consisting of high-strength bolts/cables, well rings, and shotcrete through convergence-constraint analysis. The field results showed that the surrounding rock deformation could be controlled within a safe range. Yuan et al.29 proposed a bolt-mesh-cable-grouting coupling support method for high-stress fractured soft-rock roadways in deep mines, and used numerical simulation to analyze the interaction between the support system and surrounding rock. The results verified the control effect of this method on plastic-zone expansion and roadway deformation. Chen et al.30 improved the strength of the surrounding rock and the bearing capacity of the support structure in deep high-stress roadways through bolt-grouting combined support, thereby effectively controlling large roadway deformation. Xie et al.31 proposed an external anchoring-internal unloading control method for gob-side entry driving under strong mining disturbance in deep mines. In this method, external anchoring maintains the integrity of shallow surrounding rock, while the internal unloading space transfers high stress from the shallow zone to deeper regions. These studies show that conventional surrounding rock control technologies have achieved good results in improving the bearing capacity of surrounding rock, optimizing the mechanical state of support structures, and regulating the stress environment. However, these studies mainly focused on deep soft-rock roadways or conventional gob-side roadways, with emphasis on overall roadway deformation control, support-structure optimization, or pressure-relief parameter determination. The coal pillar rib failure problem under the combined influence of extra-thick coal seams, hard roofs, and strong mining disturbance has not been specifically addressed. Therefore, it is still necessary to further optimize the support design by considering the crack development range, loose-zone depth, and local key deformation area of the coal pillar rib, so as to effectively control deformation.

Based on the above research foundation and limitations, this study takes the 122,110 working face of Caojiatan Coal Mine as the engineering background and investigates the deformation, failure, and control of coal pillar ribs in gob-side roadways under extra-thick coal seam and hard-roof conditions from three aspects: theoretical model, support design, and field application. At the theoretical-model level, borehole observation and acoustic testing are used to identify the crack development and loose-zone distribution characteristics of the coal pillar rib. On the basis of overlying strata fracture and lateral cantilever beam force analysis, a coal pillar shear-slip safety factor model is introduced to reveal the mechanical mechanism of local coal pillar rib instability. At the support-design level, this study does not simply increase the support strength of conventional bolts and cables. Instead, a constant-resistance energy-absorbing anchor cable support technology is developed. According to the loose-zone development and deformation characteristics of the coal pillar rib, the key reinforcement layout of the upper triangular sliding region of the coal pillar is determined, so that the spatial parameters of the support structure correspond to the actual failure region. At the field-application level, numerical simulation is combined with coal stress monitoring, anchor cable load monitoring, and roadway deformation monitoring to compare and analyze coal pillar rib stress concentration, plastic-zone expansion, and rib deformation before and after reinforcement. The results verify the applicability of the “cantilever beam-shear slip” composite instability mechanical model and the constant-resistance energy-absorbing anchor cable reinforcement scheme in gob-side roadways under extra-thick coal seam and hard-roof conditions.

Project overview

Geological conditions of the project

The Caojiatan Coal Mine, located in Yulin City, Shaanxi Province, China, is currently engaged in the extraction of the 122,110 working face. This working face targets the No. 2–2 coal seam, characterized by an average thickness of 10.25 m, a dip angle ranging from 0° to 5°, and burial depths between 300 and 363 m. The immediate roof comprises 4.92 m of fine-grained sandstone, while the main roof consists of 20.16 m of medium-grained sandstone. The immediate floor is composed of 5.23 m of siltstone. The working face layout and stratigraphic column are illustrated in Fig. 1.

Working face layout and rock column.

As the subsequent mining area to the 122,108 working face, the 122,110 working face faces dual geomechanical challenges during retreat mining: (1) A mechanical coupling system formed by the rigid roof and ultra-thick coal seam leads to significant energy accumulation during roof breakage. (2) High-stress concentration zones arise from stress interactions between adjacent gob areas and the active working face. Under the combined influence of these geo-mining conditions, the ventilation roadway ahead of the working face experiences structural instability in the coal pillar ribs within the advanced abutment pressure zone. Progressive failure manifests as excessive rib deformation (> allowable limits) and anchorage system degradation, posing severe safety risks.

The ventilation roadway, with dimensions of 5,600 mm (width) × 4,050 mm (height), employs a composite support system integrating cable bolts, W-shaped steel straps, and steel guards. Detailed support parameters are provided in Fig. 2.

Original support scheme.

Under the original support scheme, during the period of periodic pressure and severe strata behaviors, the 20 m coal pillar at the end of the working face leads to a high concentration of stress and a strong mine pressure response, resulting in severe damage to the coal pillar rib of the 122,110 ventilation roadway. Phenomena such as rib bulging occur, characterized by a severely fractured coal mass and well-developed cracks (as shown in Fig. 1). The resulting coal pillar rib failure reduces the usable cross-section, reduces the support capacity, and increases the manual maintenance requirements.

Mechanistic study of deformation and failure in coal pillar ribs

Investigative analysis of overlying strata fracture characteristics

In conventional longwall mining, severe strata behaviors are predominantly induced by the fracture of overlying strata. As the working face advances to a critical distance, the exposed roof area increases. Under the combined action of self-weight and overburden loads, the central region along the long axis of the roof initially generates tensile fracture line I₁ (Fig. 4 left). Subsequent stress redistribution triggers a rapid increase in bending moment along the short axis, leading to the formation of secondary fracture line II. The interconnection of fracture lines I₁ and II creates a closed “O”-shaped crack system, which progressively develops branch cracks such as I₂ and III, ultimately forming a characteristic “O-X” failure pattern (Fig. 4 left). During this process, structural blocks B₁ and C₁ demarcated by fracture lines constitute the fundamental roof failure mode during face advance. For thick coal seams with hard roof conditions, the strata pressure mechanism is systematically analyzed through an elastic foundation beam mechanical model.

Fracture characteristics of overlying rock under various conditions.

The stress concentration associated with the advanced abutment pressure is predominantly attributable to the thick immediate roof and the suspended roof above the gob. The fracturing of the cantilever structure gives rise to large mass formations, whose instability and subsequent sliding elicit dynamic loading effects, thereby exacerbating the high-stress conditions along the coal pillar ribs and compromising their stability. The lateral support pressure concentration is positively correlated with the length of the lateral cantilever. The collapse of the gob roof results in inadequate support, which subsequently amplifies the lateral pressure. Under the condition of hard roofs in extra-thick coal seams, a large area above the gob collapses, and there is no effective support below, while the coal pillar rib is still embedded in the intact coal mass. At this time, its mechanical behavior is similar to a long beam with one end fixed (solid coal side) and the other end suspended (extending into the gob), so it is simplified as a long cantilever beam model for analysis. Analysis of shear slip mechanisms in coal pillars

Throughout the extraction process at the working face, the rock surrounding the roadway is subjected to the cumulative effects of advanced and lateral support pressures. Should the stress within the coal pillar surpass a defined threshold, plastic deformation ensues, accompanied by a reduction in shear strength and a subsequent decline in stability. The coal pillar is tasked with supporting the weight of the overlying strata, which can be conceptualized as experiencing plane strain conditions. In accordance with Mohr’s strength theory, material failure is triggered when the shear stress attains a critical limit. When the maximum shear strength is reached on one side of the coal pillar due to roof pressure, blocks begin to slide along planes of weakness, giving rise to a shear slip phenomenon. In the presence of extra-thick coal seams with a hard roof, the coal pillar is subjected to more severe perturbations, leading to the movement of coal fragments along planes of weakness and the formation of sliding blocks that resemble a truncated prism, as depicted in Fig. 6.

Numerical simulation of deformation and failure in coal pillar ribs

Amid the intricate and fluctuating operations of coal extraction, the state of coal pillars remains in a state of perpetual dynamic flux. The morphological characteristics of coal pillars differ significantly across various mining stages. By comprehensively considering a multitude of mining interference factors, the deformation process of coal pillars can be categorized into three distinct phases, each influenced by a successive mining disturbance: the tunneling on either side of the coal pillar, the retreat mining of the upper panel’s working face, and the retreat mining of the lower panel’s working face. These mining disturbances exert varying levels of deformation and stress concentration on the coal pillars. The key parameters that predominantly govern the condition of the coal pillars encompass the thickness of the coal seam, the properties of the goaf roof, and the width of the coal pillar.

Establishment of numerical model

Following the comprehensive analysis, it is established that the processes of roadway excavation, upper panel face excavation, and lower panel face excavation all exert influence on the stability of coal pillars at the leading working face. In order to examine the stability of coal pillars under the combined effects of multiple mining disturbances, a numerical model was developed using the FLAC3D three-dimensional numerical simulation software, specifically calibrated to the engineering geological context of the Caojiatan Coal Mine.

To investigate the stability of the coal pillar in the advance abutment pressure zone under multiple mining disturbances, a numerical model was established, as shown in Fig. 7. The model dimensions were 760 m × 400 m × 150 m. The 760 m transverse dimension included the 122,108 and 122,110 working faces, each with a width of 280 m, the 20 m coal pillar, and the associated roadways, with boundary buffer zones retained outside the main mining structures. The 400 m dimension along the mining direction covered the staged advance of the working face and the locations of the subsequent monitoring sections. The model height included the main roof and floor strata affecting the stress and failure evolution of the coal pillar rib. These dimensions enabled the mining interactions among the working faces, gob, and coal pillar to be represented while reducing the influence of the external boundaries on the local stress response of the study area. All roadway cross-sections in the model were 5.5 m wide and 4.3 m high, and the 122,110 ventilation roadway was modelled according to the original support scheme.

Numerical simulation model.

An elastoplastic constitutive relationship was adopted, and material failure was governed by the Mohr-Coulomb strength criterion. To evaluate the influence of mesh size on the numerical results, three meshing schemes were examined: a coarse mesh, a mesh locally refined in the key coal-pillar region, and a globally refined mesh. The coarse mesh led to distortions in the calculated peak stress and stress distribution pattern. In contrast, globally mesh refinement provided only limited improvement in numerical accuracy but substantially increased the computational time. Therefore, local mesh refinement was adopted in the key coal-pillar region. The final model consisted of 276,318 zones and 295,070 gridpoints.

A representative coal-seam burial depth of 350 m was adopted, which lies within the actual burial-depth range of 300 to 363 m at the 122,110 working face. The self-weight of the overlying strata not explicitly included in the model was converted into an equivalent uniformly distributed vertical load of 6.5 MPa and applied to the upper boundary. The initial vertical stress at the coal-seam level was generated jointly by this equivalent upper-boundary load and the self-weight of the roof strata included in the model. Vertical displacement was constrained at the bottom boundary, while the normal horizontal displacement was constrained at the front, rear, left, and right boundaries. These boundary conditions represented the confinement provided by the stable rock mass surrounding the model and reduced the influence of boundary effects on the numerical results.

Based on the borehole data and laboratory rock mechanics test results from the 122,110 working face, the mechanical parameters of intact rock specimens were adopted as the initial values for the numerical model. Considering the differences between laboratory specimens and the engineering rock mass in terms of scale, integrity, and the development of bedding and fractures, the laboratory parameters were adjusted using a weighted reduction method36,37. During the reduction process, the lithological characteristics and rock-mass integrity of the different strata were considered to maintain reasonable relative strength and stiffness relationships among the strata. The adjusted deformation parameters were used to determine the bulk and shear moduli required by FLAC3D, whereas cohesion, internal friction angle, and tensile strength were adopted as the strength parameters of the Mohr-Coulomb constitutive model. The suitability of the final parameters was evaluated by comparing the simulated failure range and distribution characteristics of the coal pillar rib with the results of field borehole observation and acoustic testing.The detailed rock mechanical parameters are summarized in Table 2.

This study conducted a dynamic analysis of roadway surrounding rock stability under multiple mining disturbances using a stepwise excavation numerical simulation approach. A three-dimensional geomechanical model containing four roadways was established for the dual-working-face mining system of the 122,108 working face and 122,110 working face. The numerical simulation protocol was implemented through the following steps:

After the model reached equilibrium, the belt and ventilation roadways of the 122,110 working face were excavated, and the initial stress, displacement, and plastic-zone states of the coal pillar rib after roadway excavation were recorded. Retreat mining of the 122,108 working face was subsequently simulated. After a new equilibrium state was achieved, the response of the coal pillar rib was extracted to evaluate the disturbance caused by adjacent-panel retreat and the lateral abutment pressure generated by the gob.

During the 122,110 working face mining phase (tertiary disturbance), the stress at measurement point No.4 exhibited dynamic growth characteristics: as the face advanced from 0 m to 50 m, the stress peak progressively increased from 29.7 MPa to 32.4 MPa, ultimately reaching an extreme value of 37.9 MPa when the face approached the measurement location. Notably, adjacent measurement point No.5 demonstrated synchronous stress intensification, confirming the progressive transmission characteristics of mining-induced stress fields within coal pillars.

(2) The stress distribution comparison illustrated in Fig. 9(c) during the working face excavation process exhibits a pronounced bimodal pattern within the advanced coal pillar support. The stress along the coal pillar rib in the 122,110 ventilation roadway initially ascends, then descends, reaching a peak at the interface with the 122,108 belt roadway before decreasing once more, thus forming a bimodal stress distribution with a lower peak on the left and a higher peak on the right. This observation suggests a significant shift and redistribution of stress within the coal pillar support as the working face advances, underscoring the complex and dynamic nature of stress propagation in the context of mining operations.

(1) The spatial evolution of coal pillar plastic zones under mining disturbance exhibits significant temporal characteristics. During the initial mining stage of the 122,108 working face, the plastic zone in the coal pillar rib of the 122,110 ventilation roadway showed limited development (0.5–1.5 m depth), corresponding to a low-disturbance phase. With advancing distances of 50 m, 100 m, and 150 m in the 122,110 working face, monitoring data revealed progressive damage expansion: At Station 4, the lower section damage expanded from 3 m to 3.5 m (16.7% increase) while the upper section demonstrated more pronounced growth from 4 m to 5 m to 7 m (75% cumulative expansion). Similarly, Station 5 showed lower section expansion from 3 m to 3.5 m (16.7%) and upper section progression from 4 m to 6 m (50%). These findings demonstrate a strong positive correlation between mining-induced disturbance intensity and pillar damage propagation, indicating a progressive deterioration pattern in pillar stability.

(2) The failure morphology displays remarkable asymmetric characteristics. As illustrated in Fig. 10(c), the upper section damage consistently exceeds the lower section by 38.1–66.7%. This phenomenon aligns with the shear-slip mechanical mechanism: Roof rotation preferentially induces shear failure planes at the pillar top, triggering stress redistribution that drives plastic zone expansion along dominant slip directions. Accordingly, an “upper-section-prioritized control” support strategy is proposed. Implementing composite bearing structures through high-prestress cable reinforcement and other measures can effectively inhibit shear slip zone connectivity, enabling controlled maintenance of overall pillar stability.

Support technology and performance analysis for coal pillar ribs

Advancements in key technologies for NPR anchor cables

Due to the persistent perturbations associated with consecutive mining cycles, the stability of the surrounding rock in the advance workings is subjected to significant degradation risks. In order to maintain the integrity of the surrounding rock and ensure operational safety in the advance workings, we executed a series of reinforcing support measures involving NPR anchor cables in the test section of the 122,110 ventilation roadway prior to excavation. The structural functionality and operational mechanism of the anchor cable are delineated in Fig. 11, with the mechanism of action segmented into three pivotal phases.

Load-Deformation curve of a constant resistance large deformation support system.

(1) Elastic Deformation Phase: In this phase, as the surrounding rock undergoes deformation and the axial force imposed on the rod is less than the constant resistance threshold of the anchor cable, the constant resistance mechanism remains static. The rod is engaged in an elastic deformation state, utilizing its flexibility to counteract external forces.

(2) Structural Yielding Phase: As the deformation of the surrounding rock intensifies, when the axial force exerted on the rod equals or surpasses the constant resistance threshold of the anchor cable, the plate starts to displace, and the constant resistance mechanism slides along the rod. During this sliding process, the constant resistance mechanism retains its defining characteristics, withstanding external forces through structural deformation.

(3) Ultimate Deformation Phase: Following a certain degree of deformation by the constant-resistance energy-absorbing anchor cable, the deformation energy of the surrounding rock is mitigated. When the axial force within the rod reverts to a level below the constant resistance threshold, the sliding of the constant resistance mechanism halts, marking a transition to a stable state for the surrounding rock of the roadway.

Consequently, the constant resistance and energy-absorbing anchor cable effectively mitigates a fraction of the deformation energy while sustaining a consistent level of resistance, enabling the release of energy within the surrounding rock. Throughout the excavation process of the working face, this system ensures the attainment of a controlled pressure relief on the surrounding rock, maintaining the support integrity without reduction in support strength. This mechanism thereby guarantees the stability of the roadway’s surrounding rock during the working face excavation, preventing support failure and ensuring the safety and efficacy of the mining operation.

Support strategy and analysis of control efficacy

Design and reinforcement of NPR anchor cables for support systems

A 100 m section of the ventilation roadway in the 122,110 working face was selected for a field-scale reinforcement test. The support design was developed with full consideration of the site conditions. NPR anchor cables were installed in the coal pillar rib within the test section. Each anchor cable had a diameter of 21.8 mm, a total length of 5000 mm, and a rated constant resistance of 360 kN. The constant resistance of 360 kN is a rated mechanical characteristic of the NPR anchor cable used at Caojiatan Coal Mine. The reinforcement was concentrated in the middle and upper parts of the coal pillar rib. To provide effective reinforcement across the coal pillar rib, the NPR anchor cables were installed horizontally.

As shown in Fig. 12, two rows of NPR anchor cables were arranged along the roadway. The first row was installed midway between two adjacent W-shaped steel straps, with a longitudinal spacing of 1500 mm. The second row was installed between the upper W-shaped steel strap and the roadway roof centerline, also with a longitudinal spacing of 1500 mm. The vertical center-to-center spacing between the two rows was 1200 mm. The detailed design is described below.

Strengthening support design diagram of NPR anchor cables: (a) Constant damping energy absorbing anchor cable Reinforcement scheme diagram; (b) Constant damping energy absorbing anchor cable reinforcement support sectional diagram; (c) Constant damping energy absorbing anchor cable reinforcement support schematic diagram.

(1) Design of the reinforcement range.

Based on the analysis of the coal pillar rib deformation mechanism, field deformation characteristics, and numerical simulation results, plastic failure and potential shear slip were mainly concentrated in the middle and upper parts of the coal pillar rib. This region was therefore identified as the critical weak zone affecting the stability of the coal pillar rib. Accordingly, NPR anchor cable reinforcement was concentrated in the middle and upper parts of the coal pillar rib in the 122,110 ventilation roadway to enhance confinement and suppress the development of local shear-slip failure.

(2) Design of the anchor cable length.

The NPR anchor cable should pass through the shallow loosened and damaged zone, while its anchorage end should extend into the deeper coal mass with relatively high bearing capacity. This arrangement allows the relatively stable deep coal mass to restrain the continued deformation of the shallow fractured coal mass38,39.

Field borehole visualization and acoustic testing showed that the maximum depth of the loosened and damaged zone in the coal pillar rib under the original support condition was approximately 3.7 m. Based on this criterion, a total NPR anchor cable length of 5000 mm was adopted.

(3) Design of the anchor cable pretension.

The installation pretension of the NPR anchor cable was determined according to its constant-resistance value and the recommended pretension ratio. Experimental studies have shown that appropriate pretension improves the strength and deformation performance of NPR-anchored rock. Favorable overall mechanical performance was obtained when the pretension was 70%–90% of the constant-resistance value2,39.

The constant-resistance value of the NPR anchor cable selected in this study was 360 kN. Therefore, the corresponding recommended pretension range was 252–324 kN. Considering the field tensioning conditions, the installation pretension was set to not less than 260 kN, corresponding to a pretension ratio of 72.2%.

(4) Design of the anchor cable spacing.

The anchor cable positions were determined according to the main reinforcement region in the middle and upper parts of the coal pillar rib and the spatial arrangement of the existing W-shaped steel straps. Without changing the original support system, the first row of NPR anchor cables was installed between adjacent W-shaped steel straps. The second row was installed in the coal pillar rib region between the upper W-shaped steel strap and the roadway roof centerline. The vertical center-to-center spacing between the two rows was 1200 mm, and the longitudinal anchor cable spacing along the roadway was 1500 mm.

These parameters were selected as engineering layout parameters by considering the location of coal pillar rib failure, the existing support configuration, and the field construction conditions. Their effectiveness in controlling coal pillar stress concentration and plastic-zone development was subsequently evaluated through numerical simulation.

(1) Comparative analysis of monitoring data in Fig. 14(a) and (c) reveals that the implementation of NPR anchor cables for surrounding rock reinforcement significantly reduces stress concentration peaks in the coal pillars at monitoring stations 4 and 5. Conclusion

This study takes the 122,110 ventilation roadway of Caojiatan Coal Mine as the engineering background and investigates the deformation mechanism and control method of coal pillar ribs in gob-side roadways under extra-thick coal seam and hard-roof conditions. Through theoretical analysis, numerical simulation, and field monitoring, the following innovative achievements have been obtained:

(1) A composite “cantilever beam-shear slip” mechanical model was established for coal pillar rib instability under extra-thick coal seam and hard-roof conditions. The theoretical analysis couples overlying strata fracture, lateral cantilever loading, and shear-slip stability of the coal pillar rib. The results show that the suspended hard roof above the gob enhances the lateral loading effect on the coal pillar rib, and the upper triangular region of the coal pillar rib is prone to local shear slip under the combined action of vertical stress and lateral loading.

(2) FLAC3D numerical simulation revealed the progressive stress migration and plastic-zone evolution of the coal pillar rib under multi-stage mining disturbance. During roadway excavation and successive panel retreat, the peak vertical stress of the coal pillar increased stepwise from 13.2 MPa to 37.9 MPa. The plastic failure showed an evident upper-concentrated evolution pattern, with the upper damage depth increasing from 4.0 m to 6.0–7.0 m. After NPR anchor cable reinforcement, the coal pillar plastic-zone depth decreased by 62.5%-78.6%, and the coal pillar stress peaks decreased by 8.9%-17.5%. The numerical results quantify the stress-regulation and plastic-zone control effects of the NPR anchor cable reinforcement under the investigated engineering conditions.

(3) The NPR anchor cable reinforcement scheme was applied in the 122,110 ventilation roadway and evaluated through field monitoring. Compared with the original support section, the peak coal stress in the reinforced section decreased by 20.9%-26.3%; the rib bulging was controlled at approximately 220–230 mm, corresponding to a reduction of about 52%; and the borehole fissure depth decreased from approximately 3.0 m or more to approximately 1.4–1.6 m, with a reduction of about 50%. The field results confirm the control effect of the NPR anchor cable reinforcement on coal pillar rib stress concentration, rib deformation, and fissure development in the Caojiatan Coal Mine case.

This study establishes a technical route integrating instability mechanism analysis, NPR anchor cable reinforcement, and field monitoring verification. The revealed coal pillar rib instability mechanism and the proposed reinforcement method can provide a reference for gob-side roadway support design under similar extra-thick coal seam and hard-roof conditions.

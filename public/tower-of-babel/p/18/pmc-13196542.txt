Inducible POU Class 2 Homeobox 3 Expression in Human Intestinal Organoids as a Model of Human Tuft Cells
Authors: Carolyn Bomidi, Luqiong Wang, Hoa Nguyen-Phuc, Mary K Estes, Sarah E Blutt
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Graphical abstract

Tuft cells, name for their tufts of apical microvilli, are relatively drare chemosensory cells found in the epithelium of several organs, dincluding lungs, stomach, intestine, and pancreatic duct. They express genes canonically associated with taste signaling such as CHAT, TRPM5, and POU Class 2 Homeobox 3 (POU2F3).1 POU2F3 plays a role in cell fate specification of tuft cells and is essential for tuft cell differentiation.2 Characterization of human tuft cells has remained difficult due to their rarity in vivo and the lack of in vitro models. Human intestinal organoids (HIOs)3 are an attractive platform to study the role of tuft cells in intestinal biology. Due to the rare nature of tuft cells, we enhanced the numbers of tuft cells in human tissue stem cell derived HIOs by genetically modifying the HIOs to inducibly overexpress POU2F3 (POU2F3-HIO) to allow their biology to be interrogated.

Differentiation of POU2F3-HIOs with various concentrations of doxycycline-induced dose-dependent nuclear expression of POU2F3 (Figure 1A and B) and expression of several known tuft cell-associated genes such as POU2F3, SUCNR1, TRPM5, PLCG2, ALOX5, KIT, and AVIL (Figure 1C, Supplementary Figure 1A). As the dose of doxycycline increases, a greater inter-organoid heterogeneity in POU2F3 expression is seen. Transcripts associated with mature enterocytes, goblet cells, Paneth cells, and stem cells were modestly downregulated following POU2F3 induction (Supplementary Figure 1B). Quantification of sucrase-isomaltase (S)I protein expression via immunostaining also showed a significant reduction in SI expressing enterocytes following doxycycline induction (Supplementary Figure 1C and D) suggesting tuft cell specification occurs at the expense of enterocytes. POU2F3+ cells displayed classic tuft morphology and ultrastructure including acetylated-tubulin and apical F-actin (Figure 1D) and extended apical microvilli, filamentous bundles, apical vesicles, and cytospinules (Figure 1E). Transcriptional analysis indicated POU2F3 overexpression resulted in enrichment of genes involved in pathways related to cilia (Supplementary Figure 2A and B) suggesting the apical tuft may share more functional overlap with cilium than previously appreciated and is consistent with a possible specialized primary sensory cilium that functions as a chemo- and mechanosensor.4 There were also several upregulated genes and pathways related to wingless-related integration site (WNT) and WNT signaling (Supplementary Figure 2C and D) in induced POU2F3-HIOs. Immunofluorescent imaging of WNT10A showed increased numbers of WNT10A expressing cells (Supplementary Figure 3) supporting the transcriptional WNT signature. These findings suggest that tuft cells may have a previously unknown WNT activating and modulating function, consistent with prior reports that tuft cells can act as regenerative stem cells in the intestine.5 The POU2F3-HIO model can be leveraged to dissect how human tuft cells influence stem cell niche dynamics and epithelial repair.

To demonstrate the functionality of the induced POU2F3 cells, we inoculated induced POU2F3-HIOs with rotavirus, which is known to infect murine tuft cells.6 Coimmunostaining of HIOs with POU2F3 and rotavirus indicated human tuft cells are infected by human rotavirus (Figure 2A). Flow cytometry quantification of infected cells revealed little difference in the rate of infection between uninduced and induced cultures despite the loss of enterocytes, the primary site of rotavirus infection (Figure 2B). Transcriptional profiling indicated a significant effect of rotavirus infection on the transcriptional signature of both uninduced and induced HIOs (Figure 2C). Unique Gene Ontology (GO) pathways induced by rotavirus infection in the POU2F3-HIOs included immune responses and signaling (Figure 2D). Untargeted metabolite analysis likewise revealed condition-specific patterns and unique metabolite signatures (Supplementary Figure 4). Specifically, infection of POU2F3-HIOs resulted in a selective accumulation of long chain monounsaturated acyl-carnitines (Figure 2E; Supplementary Figure 4B and C). Concordantly, transcriptomes from the same groups showed enrichment of lipid-metabolism pathways in the induced and infected HIOs (Figure 2D), supporting a tuft-cell dependent shift in the infection response characterized by increased fatty acid uptake/activation, desaturation, and oxidation.

Understanding the biology of human tuft cells has been limited by their rarity in vivo and a lack of accessible in vitro models. By inducibly expressing POU2F3 in HIOs, we have established a valuable tool for dissecting human tuft cell biology, recapitulating their morphology, transcriptomic identity, and susceptibility to enteric infection. Additionally, this model offers a platform for future co-culture studies with immune cells to probe tuft cell sentinel and effector functions in human disease. Although POU2F3-HIOs recapitulate several aspects of tuft cell biology, they lack expression of key markers such as DCLK1, CHAT, and interleukin (IL)-25, even after succinate stimulation.7 Future integration with validated tuft cell datasets will be an important next step in determining whether non-induced genes such as CHAT and IL-25 reflect the requirement for additional signals that are not present in our organoid model. The POU2F3-HIO provides a starting point to determine which other factors, such as coactivators OCA-T1 and OCA-T2, may be required to drive full human tuft cell marker expression.8

Tuft cells act as epithelial sentinels that coordinate host defense, including helminth clearance via succinate-triggered type 2 immune circuits9 and bacteria-driven crosstalk with the enteric nervous system through acetylcholine and leukotriene signaling.10 IL-4/IL-13 stimulation of HIOs can also induce tuft cell hyperplasia,5 which may better capture immune–epithelial interactions but can have broader, pleiotropic effects on organoid state and other lineages. In contrast, epithelial-intrinsic POU2F3 induction offers a tunable, genetics-based way to drive a core tuft transcriptional program without cytokines, providing more targeted control but not necessarily fully recapitulating all mature tuft effector features. Here, we leverage the POU2F3-HIO platform to enable pathogen infection studies in a physiologically relevant epithelium with enhanced tuft cell output. Because tuft cells are a dominant epithelial source of acetylcholine and can tune fluid secretion and motility, this tuft cell-enriched HIO system provides a technical advance to directly interrogate how enteric pathogens reshape metabolic pathways to induce diarrheal disease and how these changes may converge on WNT-dependent programs that drive intestinal epithelial repair.

FLT3 (CD135) expression in pediatric AML is associated with distinct features and worse outcomes with sorafenib therapy∗
Authors: Andrew J Menssen, Amenech B Ashango, Todd A Alonzo, Robert B Gerbing, Laura Pardo, Fan-Chi Hsu, Loren L Lott, Fangyan Dai, Todd M Cooper, Jessica A Pollard, Katherine Tarlock, Richard Aplenc, Soheil Meshinchi, Lisa Eidenschink Brodersen, Denise A Wells, Michael R Loken, Chad A Hudson
Published: 2026

TO THE EDITOR:

FLT3/CD135 (also known as FLK2) is a membrane-bound receptor tyrosine kinase expressed on hematopoietic progenitor and monocytic cells, where it promotes proliferation, differentiation, maturation, and survival.1, 2, 3 FLT3 is expressed in acute myeloid leukemia (AML), and its activation by FLT3 ligand (FLT3L) enhances leukemic cell proliferation and survival, demonstrating a key role in leukemia pathogenesis.2 FLT3 is frequently mutated in AML, including internal tandem duplications (FLT3-ITD), which are associated with poor prognosis. However, the significance of FLT3/CD135 cell surface expression levels remains unclear, particularly in the context of FLT3-targeted therapies.

Given its role in leukemogenesis, FLT3 has become a common therapeutic target, leading to the development of tyrosine kinase inhibitors including sorafenib. In combination with standard chemotherapy, sorafenib appears to improve event-free survival (EFS), disease free survival (DFS), and relapse risk (RR) in pediatric patients with AML with high FLT3-ITD allelic ratios (AR).4,5 Although there have been promising findings, resistance to FLT3 inhibitors has been observed, posing a significant challenge to long-term success.6,7

Children’s Oncology Group protocol AAML1031 for de novo pediatric AML included a treatment arm (arm C) with the FLT3 inhibitor sorafenib in combination with standard chemotherapy for patients with high-AR FLT3-ITD. Details of AAML1031 have been described previously, including an outcome analysis of arm C.8,9 Patients who submitted diagnostic bone marrow samples and consented to correlative studies were included (n = 1024). CD135 expression was prospectively measured by multidimensional flow cytometry and quantified as mean fluorescence intensity (MFI), as previously described.9, 10, 11, 12 We hypothesized that high CD135 expression may be associated with clinical features and outcomes, particularly in the context of sorafenib therapy.

CD135 expression varied across the cohort, with linear MFI ranging from 2.981 to 73.139 (Figure 1A; supplemental Figure 1A). To investigate clinical associations, patients were stratified into quartiles based on expression levels. Patients in the top quartile (Q4; n = 256) showed increased expression of surface markers associated with monocytic differentiation, including CD45 (P < .0001), CD11b (P = .002), CD14 (P < .0001), CD36 (P < .0001), and CD64 (P < .0001), compared with patients in quartiles 1 through 3 (Q1-Q3; Figure 1B-F). Conversely, CD34 expression was significantly decreased in Q4 (P = .001; Figure 1G), suggesting that in pediatric AML higher CD135 surface expression is associated with a more mature monocytic phenotype and indicating a correlation between expression and differences in disease biology and maturation state.

Correlation with clinical characteristics revealed that patients in Q4 of CD135 expression had higher bone marrow blast counts at diagnosis (73% vs 66%, P = .001; Figure 1H) and higher rates of KMT2A fusions (33.3% vs 19.6%, P < .001) and NPM1 mutations (12.9% vs 8.6%, P = .045; Figure 1I), as previously published.13 Further analysis indicated that KMT2A::MLLT3 was specifically enriched in Q4 compared with other fusion partners (supplemental Figure 1B). No other significant associations were observed (supplemental Table 1). Surprisingly, allocation to Q4 was not associated with high-AR FLT3-ITD (AR > 0.1) status or allocation to arm C (supplemental Figure 1C-D). Nor was there a significant relationship between FLT3 mutation status (ITD or tyrosine kinase domain) and either CD135 MFI or allocation to Q4 (supplemental Figure 1E-F), suggesting that surface FLT3 expression level is not directly tied to underlying mutation status or allelic burden.

There was no difference in EFS or overall survival (OS) between Q4 and Q1 through Q3 (5-year EFS, 45.4% vs 45.6%; P = .88; OS, 62.1% vs 65.0%; P = .46; supplemental Figure 1G-H). This remained true when patients with (5-year EFS, 42.3% vs 48%; P = .463; OS, 56.5% vs 62.8%; P = .538) or without (5-year EFS, 45.7% vs 45.2%; P = .920; OS, 62.3% vs 65.2%; P = .552) FLT3 mutations were evaluated separately (supplemental Table 2), suggesting that despite immunophenotypic and molecular associations, high surface CD135 expression does not predict outcome across pediatric AML, congruent with previous studies.13,14

However, when analysis was restricted to patients who received sorafenib (n = 56), patients in Q4 had lower EFS and OS compared with arm C patients (5-year EFS, 22.2% vs 54.2%; P = .019; OS, 33.3% vs 66.9%; P = .016; Figure 2A-B). Additionally, patients in arm C Q4 did worse than patients in arm A/B Q4 (EFS, 33.3% vs 63.2%; P = .009; OS, 22.2% vs 46.2%; P = .021; Figure 2C-D). Due to the differences in risk status between arm C and arm A/B (high risk, 100% vs 22.1%; P < .001), we compared arm C Q4 patients with patients with FLT3-ITD high-AR from arm A/B in Q4. Q4 patients in arm C still had worse 5-year OS (68% in arm A/B vs 33% in arm C; P = .012) and had a trend toward decreased EFS (52.9% in arm A/B vs 22.2% in arm C; P = .134; supplemental Table 2). Together, this indicates that in the setting of sorafenib therapy, high CD135 expression correlates with inferior outcomes, both compared with patients with lower expression who received sorafenib and patients with high expression who did not receive sorafenib. Although the sample size is small, these data suggest that elevated CD135 expression may not only lead to resistance to sorafenib but also a detrimental effect of the drug in patients with high expression, which should be investigated further with mechanistic studies.

Although patients with high FLT3-ITD AR were more likely to undergo transplant (63% in arm C vs 13% in arm A/B; P = .002; supplemental Table 1) using the AAML10314,15 definition, treatment-related mortality rates were not significantly different between groups, by either crude rates (5% for arm A/B vs 11% for arm C; P = .379) or cumulative incidence analysis (16.8% for arm A/B vs 13.3% for arm C; P = .906), suggesting treatment-related mortality was not a driving factor in poor outcomes.

Related resistance mechanisms may help explain why increased CD135 expression results in inferior outcomes. FLT3L, produced by stromal cells, stimulates proliferation of FLT3-expressing leukemic cells and can contribute to resistance.6,16,17 Additionally, FLT3 mRNA expression has been associated with poor prognosis and relapse in pediatric AML18 and is frequently observed in KMT2A-rearranged leukemia, in which overexpression supports leukemic cell survival.19 Although FLT3L is regulated independently of FLT3 receptor expression6 and FLT3 transcript levels do not always correlate with surface receptor density, such as increased cell surface expression, these mechanisms converge on signaling pathways after FLT3L/FLT3 interaction, suggesting that these different abnormalities in the FLT3 system may result in similar paths to FLT3 inhibitor resistance.

In conclusion, these findings suggest that surface CD135/FLT3 expression is associated with monocytic immunophenotype, certain genetic subtypes (KMT2A rearrangements and NPM1 mutations), and worse outcomes in the setting of sorafenib therapy. Although the sample size is limited, the data suggest that cell surface expression may serve as a functional mediator of FLT3 inhibitor resistance, or perhaps contribute to a detrimental effect in the presence of sorafenib, as well as serve as a potential biomarker of response. Although further investigation is required, this underscores the rationale for combination strategies that target FLT3 alongside cooperating pathways4 and suggests that screening for cell surface FLT3 expression before therapy should be considered. Additionally, because high FLT3 cell surface expression is associated with monocytic maturation, which is linked to venetoclax resistance,20 and KMT2A-rearranged and NPM1-mutated AML, which can be treated with menin inhibitors,21 it may be advisable to investigate FLT3 expression in these contexts. Finally, these data highlight that investigation of targeted therapies against a molecule expressed on the cell surface should include data regarding the cell surface expression, even if the therapy is intracellular.

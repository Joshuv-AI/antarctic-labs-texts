IFPHOR0050 Findings from Nottingham City Council’s Women’s Health Needs Assessment: Capable and motivated, with limited opportunity
Authors: Maddy Leather, Lucy Hubber, Dana Sumilo, Matt Corder, Hannah Stovin, Clare Routledge, Steve Thorne
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWomen experience greater health inequalities than men due to biological and social factors. In 2022, the UK government published the Women’s Health Strategy for England, followed in 2023 by £25 million to support implementation. Evidence shows that every £1 invested in women’s health can yield an £11 return, emphasising the economic value of addressing gender-based inequalities. Thus, Nottingham City Council (NCC) initiated its first Women’s Health Needs Assessment (WHNA) to identify local health needs. This aligns with national strategy, promotes health equity, and ensures cost-effective action in a highly deprived area (Nottingham was the 11th most deprived Local Authority nationally at the time of data collection).MethodsSemi-structured focus groups and interviews were conducted with 49 Nottingham women experiencing health-related inequalities (e.g. homeless women and women from ethnic minorities). Data were analysed through line-by-line coding and thematic organisation, with barriers and facilitators mapped onto COM-B, in line with the Behaviour Change Wheel approach to intervention design.ResultsThirteen categories of health-related behaviours were identified, alongside 31 barriers and 22 facilitators to healthy behaviour. Women described 10 capability-related determinants (five barriers, five facilitators). Motivation-related factors were balanced (six barriers, seven facilitators) but there were almost twice as many opportunity-related barriers, compared to facilitators (20 barriers, 11 facilitators).ConclusionWomen in Nottingham are most constrained by limited opportunities for healthy behaviour. This illuminates the avoidable nature of local women’s health inequalities, indicating that institutions and organisations should prioritise increasing opportunities that support women to adopt and sustain health-improving behaviours.

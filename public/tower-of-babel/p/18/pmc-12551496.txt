Association of prior history of suicide attempt and disease severity and subsequent suicidal acts among patients with psychiatric disorders in North China: a multicentre prospective study protocol
Authors: Jianlan Wu, Jing Min, Yang Luo, Peiyao Li, Lingling Li, Yiming Ge, Yajun Yun, Chang Lei, Qunshan Wang, Fanming Zhou, Yi Yin, Yongsheng Tong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionThe suicide risk among discharged psychiatric patients is substantially higher than that among patients with physical diseases and the general population. In China, few cohort studies have investigated suicidal acts in patients discharged from psychiatric facilities, and the factors that convey the risk of post-discharge suicidal acts in this specific population remain unclear. Therefore, we are conducting a prospective multicentre cohort study to investigate the association between suicide attempt history and disease severity in patients with common psychiatric disorders with post-discharge suicidal acts.Methods and analysisThis study will recruit 3000 inpatients with one of three common psychiatric disorders, namely schizophrenia, bipolar disorder and major depressive disorder, from three hospitals in North China: Beijing Huilongguan Hospital, Tianjin Anding Hospital and the First Hospital of Shanxi Medical University. Participants will include 1500 inpatients with schizophrenia, 900 inpatients with major depressive disorder and 600 inpatients with bipolar disorder. The baseline assessment includes determining whether each recruited patient meets the diagnostic criteria for schizophrenia, major depressive disorder or bipolar disorder using the Structured Clinical Interview for Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fourth edition (DSM-IV) and obtaining details on prior histories of suicide attempts and the severity of the diagnosed disorders using several questionnaires. A 2-year follow-up will be conducted after the recruited patients have been completely assessed. Follow-ups are scheduled at 3, 6, 12 and 24 months after discharge. The primary outcomes are suicidal acts, including suicide attempts and death, that occurred during the follow-up period. A survival analysis will be performed to test for the supposed associations.Ethics and disseminationThis study was approved by the institutional review boards of Beijing Huilongguan Hospital (2022-67-Science), Tianjin Anding Hospital (2022-47) and First Hospital of Shanxi Medical University (2022-K155). All participants will be required to provide written informed consent before the baseline assessment. The recruitment methods will be explained to potential participants, and their confidentiality will be protected throughout the study. The findings of this study will be disseminated to peer-reviewed scientific journals and conference presentations.Trial registration number ChiCTR2200064610 Keywords: Suicide & self-harm, Schizophrenia & psychotic disorders, Depression & mood disorders, China, Follow-Up Studies, Risk Factors

Introduction

Suicide is a major global health issue, with approximately 703 000 people dying by suicide worldwide in 2019, and the number of suicide attempts was about 10 to 20 times the number of suicide deaths.1 Mental disorder is a key risk factor for suicide.2 People with mental disorders had a 16-fold increased risk of suicide compared with the general population,3 4 and the prevalence of mental disorders in suicides and suicide attempters was over 90%.5 In mainland China, approximately 63% of individuals who died by suicide have mental disorders, with mood disorders accounting for 39.8%, substance use disorders for 9.7% and psychotic disorders for 9.6%.6 According to a series of meta-analyses, the suicide attempt rates in patients with major depressive disorder, bipolar disorder and schizophrenia were 23.7%,7 33.9%8 and 14.6%,9 respectively.

It is crucial to note that individuals who have been discharged from psychiatric facilities are at an extremely high risk for suicide.10 11 A systematic review and meta-analysis reported that the pooled estimated suicide rate among post-discharge patients was 484 suicides per 100 000 person-years.10 A study in Taiwan China reported an even higher average suicide rate of 1108/100 000 person-years among psychiatric patients in the first year after discharge from 2002 to 2013.12 Patients with schizophrenia, major depressive disorder and bipolar disorder constituted a significant proportion of individuals discharged from psychiatric facilities.13 These findings suggested that patients discharged from psychiatric facilities, especially those with schizophrenia, major depressive disorder and bipolar disorder, are critical target populations for suicide prevention.

Research has been conducted to examine the risk factors associated with suicidal acts in individuals discharged from psychiatric facilities. In a Swedish cohort study with a duration of 36 years and nearly 2.9 million discharges, major depressive disorder, crisis, personality disorders and other factors increased the risk of suicide within 30 days after discharge, with the effect being more pronounced in men than women.11 In China, a 21-year cohort study of patients with schizophrenia revealed that the age-adjusted mortality rate due to suicide in male patients was 30.8/100 000, and that in females was 13.0/100 000; male sex, previous suicide attempt and no previous hospital admission were risk factors for suicide.14 15

Research on suicidal acts among discharged patients exhibited deficiencies in design and methodology, predominantly featuring cross-sectional, case series or case-control studies with a scarcity of rigorous prospective studies. When examining suicide risk among discharged psychiatric patients, it is imperative to account for sex disparities, as suicide rates,16 prevalence of psychiatric disorders4 17 and risk factors11 18 differ between males and females. Depression, anxiety and hopelessness, both as risk factors19 and potential mediators influenced by other risks,20 were crucial in suicide prevention, warranting prospective cohort studies to assess their mediating roles. The duration of untreated psychosis,21 auditory hallucinations22 and delusions23 was associated with suicide in patients with schizophrenia. However, these relationships remain unclear in patients with major depressive disorder or bipolar disorder. Therefore, a prospective study of discharged psychiatric patients, in particular those with schizophrenia, major depressive disorder and bipolar disorder, is warranted to explore the association of prior history of suicide attempt and disease’s severity and subsequent suicidal acts. Methods and analysis

Study setting

Our study is being conducted at three hospitals in North China: Beijing Huilongguan Hospital, Tianjin Anding Hospital and First Hospital of Shanxi Medical University. Beijing Huilongguan Hospital and Tianjin Anding Hospital, two psychiatric hospitals located in the Beijing and Tianjin municipalities, have 1369 and 1220 in-patient beds, respectively. The First Hospital of Shanxi Medical University is a general hospital in Taiyuan, the capital of Shanxi Province, and its Department of Psychiatry has approximately 120 inpatient beds.

Study design

This prospective cohort study is being undertaken at three hospitals and will include a baseline assessment and a subsequent 2-year follow-up. Baseline data collection is scheduled to be completed by February 2026 or when the required sample size is achieved for each group, whichever comes first. Follow-up data collection is scheduled at 3, 6, 12 and 24 months post-discharge and will be terminated in September 2027. The study, registered at ClinicalTrials.gov, initiated recruitment in October 2022. The project is currently ongoing, and participant recruitment is actively underway.

Study participants

Inclusion/exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria are as follows: (1) patients admitted to one of the three hospitals with a diagnosis of schizophrenia, major depressive disorder or bipolar disorder and (2) patients aged ≥18 years while admitted.

The exclusion criteria are as follows: (1) refusal to participate in this study; (2) inability to be interviewed due to severe cognitive impairment, intellectual disability or other communication problems; (3) inability to be interviewed due to poor physical condition; (4) failure to complete the interview before discharge; (5) re-admission of the recruited patient and (6) being expected to be hospitalised for a long time (hospitalised for a total of 24 months within 36 months before the current admission or will be hospitalised for two-thirds of the incoming 2 years).

Sample size calculation

The main outcome is post-discharge suicidal acts, including suicides and suicide attempts. The history of suicide attempts constitutes the primary risk factor for post-discharge suicidal acts. In China, based on previous studies, approximately 10% of patients with schizophrenia have a history of suicide attempts, while the incidences of suicidal acts are 15% and 5% in schizophrenic patients with and those without a history of suicide attempts, respectively.14 For patients with major depressive disorder, about 23.5% have a history of suicide attempts, while the incidences of suicidal acts are 22% and 8% in patients with and those without a history of suicide attempts, respectively.24 Likewise, for patients with bipolar disorder, approximately 27.9% have a history of suicide attempts, while the incidences of suicidal acts are 24% and 9% in patients with and those without a history of suicide attempts, respectively.8

To estimate the sample size required for a Log-rank test,25 the PASS software26 was used. A two-sided log-rank test was assumed, with a type-1 error rate of 0.05 and a power of 80%. The estimated total sample sizes required are 1310, 430 and 333 patients with schizophrenia, major depressive disorder and bipolar disorder, respectively. Based on empirical estimates, no less than 3000 discharged patients will be included in this study, comprising approximately 1500 patients with schizophrenia, 900 with major depressive disorder and 600 with bipolar disorder. It is expected that the loss to follow-up will be approximately 15–20%; however, the sample size would still be sufficient.

Statistical analysis plan

The principal investigator will retain the data. Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) will be used to quantitatively analyse the data. The incidence of post-discharge suicidal acts will also be calculated. According to whether there were suicidal acts during the follow-up period, the patients will be classified into two subgroups, and the χ² test and t-test will be used to compare the differences in variables, such as demographic data at baseline and history of suicide attempts, between the two groups.

The Kaplan-Meier curve and log-rank analysis will be used to examine the association of the given univariates with post-discharge suicidal acts and survival time. Under the premise of satisfying the assumption of equal proportions, the Cox proportional hazards model will be used for multivariable survival analysis to select the risk and protective factors associated with post-discharge suicidal acts in patients with mental illness.

Cox regression analysis will then be carried out by sex, and all the variables retained in the model by Cox regression analysis of the full sample will be included in the equation to compare whether there is a statistically significant difference in the HR value of each risk and protective factor between males and females and to test the moderating effect of sex.

To investigate whether there are significant differences in suicide risk among patients with schizophrenia, major depressive disorder and bipolar disorder, Cox regression analysis by diagnosis will be performed, likewise, to determine any difference in association strengths of risk factors among the three groups. In the subgroup data analysis, all variables retained in the model of the full-sample Cox regression analysis are also included in the equation. The HR values of each risk and protective factor among the three subgroups will be compared.

In the Cox regression analysis, the time of the first suicidal act during the follow-up period will be considered the survival period, and if no suicidal act occurred during the follow-up period, the survival period will be calculated using the last follow-up time of the patient as the censored point. If the data did not satisfy the assumption of equal proportions, stratified Cox regression or parametric survival models will be used for survival analysis.

Structural equation modelling will be used to test the mediating effects of depression, anxiety and hopelessness. In the sensitivity analysis, suicide deaths will be excluded, and the association between risk and protective factors and subsequent suicide attempts among patients with the three mental disorders will be estimated.

False Discovery Rate (FDR) method will be used to adjust for multiple comparisons. FDR-adjusted p values (Q values) will be reported. Missing data will be handled using multiple imputation.

Training and quality control

All researchers and assistants involved in the study received comprehensive training on the study measures and procedures. Baseline assessors received an additional month of SCID-I training. Moreover, all investigators participating in baseline and follow-up assessments received training on suicide intervention principles and methods. To ensure the quality of the research, all investigators will undergo a 3-day retraining in the use of research tools annually throughout the study period.

In addition, all suicidal acts reported during the follow-up process will be registered in a management form. A designated individual will monitor the implementation of the baseline and follow-up assessments. To ensure data reliability, project leaders will randomly select 2% of the cases to check the consistency of the baseline and follow-up data.

Ethics and dissemination

Patients will be enrolled in the cohort after signing informed consent. During the baseline assessment and follow-up, patients will receive psychological intervention for suicide if necessary. The study protocol (20 September 2022, V.2.0) has been approved by the Institute Review Board (IRB) of Beijing Huilongguan Hospital (2022-67-Science), Tianjin Anding Hospital (2022-47) and First Hospital of Shanxi Medical University (2022-K155). Any deviations from the study protocol will be documented and reported to the IRB. The findings will be disseminated in peer-reviewed scientific journals and through conference presentations.

Patient and public partnership involvement

Neither patients nor the public will be involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination of our research plans. However, we will inform patients and the public that they can access study results upon request.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first large-scale prospective cohort study focusing on subsequent suicidal acts among discharged patients with schizophrenia, bipolar disorder or major depressive disorder in mainland China. There is a lack of data on the incidence of suicide deaths and suicide attempts among discharged psychiatric patients in China, and our findings fill this gap. Our study will also explore the risk and protective factors of suicidal acts by evaluating relevant variables among patients during hospitalisation. Drawing from the study outcomes, a checklist can be compiled to help manage the suicide risk of post-discharge patients effectively, thereby enabling timely intervention.

This study offers a multidimensional perspective of suicide prevention by examining the impact of various factors on suicide risk. Life events, social support, borderline personality disorder, physical illnesses and social functioning may play significant roles in determining suicide risk. These findings suggest that comprehensive assessments and interventions that address these multifaceted influences are essential for developing effective suicide prevention strategies.

The association between psychotic symptoms and suicide risk highlighted in our study reinforces the need to prioritise the management of these symptoms in the treatment of mental illness. Clinicians should be vigilant in monitoring psychotic symptoms and consider them when assessing suicide risk.

Our prospective cohort study design allows for repeated assessment of depression, anxiety and hopelessness at baseline and during follow-up periods, providing a comprehensive view of patients’ psychological states over time. These repeated assessments are crucial, as they enable us to examine the potential mediating roles of these psychological constructs in the relationship between various risk factors and suicidal acts. These results may have several implications for clinical practice. First, they highlight the need for regular monitoring of depression, anxiety and hopelessness in patients after discharge to identify those at risk of suicide. Second, they suggest that targeted interventions to address patients’ psychological states could be instrumental in suicide prevention strategies.

Overall, while our study provides valuable insights into the suicidal acts of discharged psychiatric patients in China, its findings should be considered in the context of its limitations.

There is a potential for bias in our study, especially when participants are required to recollect several circumstances before admission, such as life events and social support. Furthermore, the stigma associated with mental illness may have led to the underreporting of symptoms such as auditory hallucinations or suicidal ideation.

To mitigate bias and the concealment of information from stigma, measures have been introduced. These encompassed the creation of a baseline and follow-up assessment instruction manual, which serves to standardise linguistic and behavioural criteria and offers direction for managing potential concealment. For instance, instead of enquiring directly about suicidal ideation, interviewers probe the extent of patients’ desire for death at particular moments, or interviewers augment self-reported patient data with clinical medical records and other pertinent information sources. Prior to the interviews, a thorough examination of patients’ medical records will be conducted to corroborate the responses provided. These strategies are adopted to reduce potential bias and increase the accuracy and reliability of the study outcomes.

Caution is warranted when generalising the findings of this study to other geographic regions, as the study is being conducted exclusively in three hospitals located in North China. Additionally, the sample population is restricted to hospitalised individuals aged≥18 years with a diagnosis of schizophrenia, major depressive disorder or bipolar disorder, which may limit the generalisability of the results to other age groups or different patient cohorts. Further studies are required to investigate the applicability of these findings in diverse contexts and populations.

The impact of meteorological factors on the respiratory pathogens of neonatal community-acquired pneumonia in Central China
Authors: Ying Zeng, Zhe Song, Juntao Wu, Shujie Yang, Haiyan Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNeonatal pneumonia is a significant cause of morbidity and mortality among newborns, which is easily affected by seasons and climate. This study investigates the prevalence of common respiratory pathogens in neonatal community-acquired pneumonia (NCAP), and to examine their correlation with seasonal and meteorological factors in order to reduce the rates of neonatal pneumonia infections and mortality.MethodsA retrospective analysis of clinical data from nasopharyngeal secretions of 302 neonates with NCAP in Xiaogan, Central China over five consecutive years from 2019 to 2023 was conducted, employing a seasonal model to predict the incidence of common pathogens.ResultsBacteria were most commonly detected among NCAP (44.0%, 133/302), followed by viruses (30.5%, 92/302). Among bacteria, Gram-negative pathogens were predominant, primarily Klebsiella pneumoniae, Escherichia coli, and Moraxella catarrhalis; Staphylococcus aureus was the most common among Gram-positive bacteria. Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) was the most common viral pathogen. RSV, along with Influenza virus, Mycoplasma pneumoniae, and Human rhinovirus, exhibited strong seasonality. The detection rate of RSV showed a negative correlation with average temperature, precipitation, and sunlight duration. Staphylococcus aureus’s prevalence negatively correlated with average temperature, while Escherichia coli was positively correlated with average humidity. Both Klebsiella pneumoniae and Moraxella catarrhalis showed negative correlations with sunlight duration.ConclusionIn NCAP, bacteria are most frequently detected. However, viral infections should also be considered significant. Most respiratory pathogens displayed distinct seasonality, particularly temperature and sunlight duration, which are major influences on most pathogens.Keywords: Newborn, Neonatal pneumonia, Respiratory viral infections, Bacterial detection, Meteorological factors, China

Background

Neonatal pneumonia is a significant cause of morbidity and mortality among newborns, representing the highest incidence and mortality rates among neonatal infectious diseases, with 152,000 to 491,000 infants under one year of age dying from pneumonia annually [1]. In Western China, infections account for 80.0% of neonatal deaths, with pulmonary infections constituting 44.7% of these cases [2]. A study including enteroviruses in the neonatal infection survey found that the mortality rate due to viral infections was 6.1%, comparable to the mortality rate caused by bacterial outbreaks (6.4%) [3]. Overall, infections, particularly respiratory infections, are a crucial cause of neonatal deaths. A study on the short-term impact of cold weather on mortality in 15 European cities revealed a close relationship between temperature and respiratory mortality; a 1 °C decrease in temperature increases the total daily natural mortality by 1.4% and respiratory mortality by 3.3% [4]. Neonates, a particularly vulnerable group, are more susceptible to seasonal and climatic effects. Early detection, prevention, diagnosis, and treatment are particularly crucial for reducing the incidence and mortality rates of neonatal pneumonia. This study conducted a retrospective analysis of clinical data from neonates with community-acquired pneumonia over five consecutive years from 2019 to 2023. It investigated the seasonal characteristics of common respiratory pathogens in neonatal community-acquired pneumonia (NCAP) in Central China, assessed the impact of meteorological factors, and predicted the incidence of common pathogens using a seasonal model.

Methods

Participants

From January 2019 to December 2023, a total of 331 neonates were admitted to Xiaogan Hospital Affiliated to Wuhan University of Science and Technology for NCAP, distributed as 80, 58, 59, 60, and 74 cases per calendar year, respectively. In this study, 302 neonates were ultimately enrolled: 74 (2019), 54 (2020), 53 (2021), 56 (2022) and 65 (2023). Monthly enrollments were 56 (January), 40 (February), 22 (March), 19 (April), 20 (May), 12 (June), 13 (July), 18 (August), 17 (September), 20 (October), 34 (November) and 31 (December). Seasonal enrollments were 61 in spring (March–May), 43 in summer (June–August), 71 in autumn (September–November) and 127 in winter (December–February). NCAP refers to infectious pneumonia acquired outside the hospital, including those who were in the incubation period outside the hospital and manifested symptoms during the incubation period after admission. Diagnostic criteria include general symptoms of infection such as fever or hypothermia, poor feeding, increased jaundice, and any of the following [5]: (1) cyanosis, respiratory distress, flaring of nostrils, and the three concave sign; (2) cough, choking during feeding, shortness of breath, and respiratory distress; (3) presence of medium to fine crackles and wheezing heard in the lungs; (4) chest radiography showing patchy or nodular opacities in the lung parenchyma, with or without pulmonary emphysema. At least two of these criteria, including either (3) or (4), must be met. Inclusion criteria: (1) age ≤ 28 days; (2) diagnosed with NCAP; (3) sputum pathogen tests conducted within 24 h of admission. Exclusion criteria: (1) such as wet lung, aspiration pneumonia, meconium aspiration pneumonia, neonatal respiratory distress syndrome, bronchopulmonary dysplasia, immunodeficiency disorders, neuromuscular diseases, and genetic metabolic disorders; (2) patients who were discharged on their own or had incomplete data. Xiaogan Hospital is a tertiary, class A hospital with 40 beds in the neonatology department, serving 1,600 patients annually.

Meteorological data acquisition

Xiaogan City in Hubei Province, located in Central China within the mid-latitude zone, ranges from 30°23′N to 31°52′N latitude and from 113°19′E to 114°35′E longitude. It has a subtropical monsoon climate with distinct seasons, ample sunshine, with annual average sunshine duration between 2020 and 2190 h. The area receives abundant rainfall, averaging 1040 mm to 1230 mm annually, mostly concentrated between April and September, with less precipitation in winter. The average annual temperature ranges from 15.5 °C to 16.5 °C, and extreme weather occasionally pushes temperatures above 40 °C. Meteorological data from 2019 to 2023 were sourced from the Xiaogan MeteorologicalObservatory. This data includes monthly average temperature (°C), relative humidity (%), total monthly precipitation (mm), monthly sunshine hours (h), and average wind speed (m/s). Of the neonates involved in the study, 90% lived within 20 km of the hospital, and 10% came from as far as 45 km away.

Respiratory pathogen detection

Nasopharyngeal secretions or sputum were collected within 24 h of admission. Our hospital houses a dedicated clinical laboratory. All study specimens were collected by well-trained clinical physicians and immediately delivered to the laboratory for processing. After passing quality-control checks, experienced laboratory technicians used real-time qualitative PCR detection test for some infections, such as Mycoplasma pneumoniae (MP), Influenza virus (FLU), Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), Adenovirus (ADV), Human rhinovirus (HRV).

Sputum culture: Bacterial suspensions are inoculated onto Columbia blood agar and chocolate agar plates and cultured in a 5% CO2 incubator at 35 °C for 18–24 h. Bacteria are identified by Gram staining, colony characteristics, and microscopic examination. The interpretation of sputum culture results was based on American laboratory quality Quantity Standard (2013 Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute) [6].

Statistical analysis

SPSS 26.0 software is used for statistical analysis. Categorical data are presented as percentages. The correlation between pathogens and meteorological factors is analyzed using Pearson’s rank correlation. Time series seasonal models are applied to analyze the incidence rates of RSV and Staphylococcus aureus from 2019 to 2023, predicting the incidence rates for 2023. Predictive accuracy is assessed by comparing predicted values with actual values. All models are evaluated based on the R2 regression coefficient, with the best model selected based on R2 and P-values. A P-value < 0.05 is considered statistically significant.

Results

Overall detection of respiratory pathogens

From 2019 to 2023, a total of 302 cases were included in the study, all of which underwent nasopharyngeal secretions pathogen testing. Gram-negative bacteria were the most prevalent (86/133, 64.7%), with Klebsiella pneumoniae, Escherichia coli, and Moraxella catarrhalis predominating. Gram-positive bacteria accounted for 35.3% (47/133), with Staphylococcus aureus being the most common. The overall pathogen positivity rate was 62.6% (189/302), with bacteria being the most frequently detected (44.0%, 133/302). The top three bacteria were Staphylococcus aureus, Klebsiella pneumoniae, and Escherichia coli. The viral positivity rate was 30.5% (92/302), with the top three viruses were RSV, HRV, and FLU. Seasonal characteristics of common pathogens and their correlation with meteorological factors

RSV, FLU, MP, and HRV showed strong seasonality. RSV and FLU were predominantly observed in the winter and spring, especially from December to March for RSV. MP was more prevalent in the summer and autumn, and HRV had the highest detection rate in autumn. ADV did not exhibit distinct seasonal characteristics, with a consistently low detection rate throughout the year. Staphylococcus aureus was more frequently observed in spring, autumn, and winter, peaking from January to March. (Fig. 1).

Discussion

Neonates exhibit reduced immune defense capabilities, an underdeveloped respiratory mucociliary clearance system, fewer alveoli, abundant pulmonary interstitial tissues, and rich lung blood flow, all of which predispose them to pulmonary infections that can rapidly spread and lead to severe complications, endangering their lives. Most pathogens exhibit seasonality, and seasonal and meteorological factors can alter human lifestyles, affect the survival and transmission of respiratory pathogens. Such factors also can cool human nasal airways, impact vitamin D metabolism, impair mucociliary clearance, reduce leukocyte phagocytic activity, and affect the immune system [7].

This five-year study conducted in the subtropical region of Central China found that in NCAP, the bacterial detection rate was the highest (44.0%), followed by a viral detection rate of 30.5%.

The top three viruses were RSV, HRV, and FLU constituting 57.6%, 27.2%, and 12.0% of all viral detections, respectively. While RSV is the most common viral in neonatal viral pneumonia globally, dominant viral strains and their prevalence vary significantly across regions [8, 9], likely due to climatic differences. The scale of hospitals also influences pathogen prevalence; smaller hospitals with less diverse patient populations, predominantly full-term infants with relatively mature immune functions, have a lower risk of nosocomial infections. Additionally, variations in pathogen detection methods and regional economic levels cannot be overlooked. The high cost of sputum pathogen testing may lead families in economically disadvantaged areas to decline such tests or only select tests for specific pathogens. Overall, viral infections in neonatal pneumonia warrant significant attention.

In our study, 133 cases of bacterial neonatal pneumonia were detected, with the top three bacteria being Staphylococcus aureus (43 cases, 32.3%), Klebsiella pneumoniae (29 cases, 21.8%), and Escherichia coli (25 cases, 18.8%). Most studies find similar common bacteria in neonatal bacterial pneumonia, with Gram-negative bacteria more prevalent, and Staphylococcus aureus being the most common among Gram-positive bacteria, though significant variations in prevalence exist [10–12]. Besides regional climate, differences due to variations in disease types should not be ignored. In cases of hospital-acquired neonatal pneumonia and severe pneumonia, common pathogens include Klebsiella pneumoniae, Escherichia coli and Acinetobacter baumannii [13]. Among newborns, the dominant bacterial strains vary with the age of onset [14, 15]. We hypothesize that intrauterine infections, postnatal environmental conditions, and age contribute to significant variations in the composition of common bacteria. Klebsiella pneumoniae typically colonizes the respiratory and gastrointestinal tracts, Escherichia coli is a normal flora of the human intestine, and Staphylococcus aureus is widely distributed in nature, colonizing the human nasal vestibule and throat. The incomplete development of the neonatal immune system and poor barrier functions mean that when resistance is compromised, the use of immunosuppressants, or invasive procedures, opportunistic pathogenic infections are likely, indicating a connection between the bacterial spectrum and the neonate’s immune level.

This study also describes the seasonality associated with various pathogens. The results indicate that RSV, FLU, MP, HRV, and Staphylococcus aureus exhibit strong seasonal patterns. RSV and FLU are predominantly observed during the winter and spring, RSV especially occurs from December to March, which aligns with related studies [16–18]. MP typically peaks during the summer (June to August) and autumn (September to November), similar to findings in related studies [16]. However, Liu et al. [19] suggest that in Jining, neonatal MP infections can occur throughout the year, with a higher incidence in autumn and winter. Studies by Lu and Chen in the same region of Jiangsu found differing seasonal peaks for MP: Lu et al. [20] observed a high incidence in the winter and spring among 1348 newborns with lower respiratory tract infection from 2011 to 2017, while Chen et al. [21] found peaks in the spring and summer between 2009 and 2010 among children under two years old with bronchitis. Apart from age, it is crucial to consider that the epidemic season for MP may vary by year. It is worth noting that, beyond annual seasonality, MP epidemics may also display long-cycle periodicity. In this study, HRV showed the highest detection rates in autumn, while ADV did not exhibit any clear seasonal characteristics, maintaining a low detection rate throughout the year. In an analysis of 15,397 children for respiratory pathogens, Tang et al. [22] found that in Yong zhou, China, ADV was most common among 4–6-year-olds, peaking in winter and spring, whereas HRV was prevalent across all pediatric age groups, including neonates, and reached epidemic levels in spring and autumn.

Staphylococcus aureus was commonly found in spring, autumn, and winter, peaking from January to March. In contrast, in Chengdu, Sichuan, Zhu et al. [23]reported a high positivity rate of Staphylococcus aureus among 0-1-year-old infants with lower respiratory tract infections, with peaks occurring from April–June and July–September. These findings suggest that the same pathogen can have different epidemic seasons in various regions, and the prevalence of a particular pathogen may vary from year to year. Indeed, negative correlations may exist between certain pathogens, particularly viruses [24]. This relationship is difficult to disentangle from seasonal effects, and future studies will require more rigorous design.

Pathogens exhibit marked seasonality due to various reasons. Further research into meteorological factors shows that the detection rate of RSV is negatively correlated with average temperature, precipitation, and sunlight duration, consistent with related studies [25, 26]. Among Gram-negative bacteria, Klebsiella pneumoniae and Moraxella catarrhalis also show negative correlations with sunlight duration, while among Gram-positive bacteria, Staphylococcus aureus is negatively correlated with average temperature. From the perspective of precipitation, rain can reduce the spread of dust and consequently decrease the transmission of pathogens. In terms of temperature, studies have shown that lower temperatures favor the survival of many viruses, can also reduce the secretory function of human respiratory mucosa, weaken physical barriers, and diminish immune function, leading to infections [7]. With RSV remaining active for longer periods as temperatures drop [27]. Cold air primarily cools the skin and cools and dries the respiratory tract; a drop in temperature may cause vasoconstriction in the airways, leading to reactive congestion and edema, narrowing the airways, particularly causing pulmonary vascular spasms and disturbances in pulmonary microcirculation [28]. Crowded indoor conditions in cold weather also contribute to increased susceptibility. Additionally, social mixing and mobility patterns are reduced at the lowest and highest temperatures and with higher precipitation, which could mediate impacts of meteorological conditions.

Contrastingly, studies like Eber et al. on 132 U.S. hospitals found a positive correlation between outdoor temperature and bloodstream infections caused by Gram-negative bacteria and Staphylococcus aureus [29]. A recent study investigating 1189 cases of Klebsiella pneumoniae bloodstream infections across four hospitals on four continents found higher infection rates during warmer months [30]. These studies used blood samples, whereas this study used nasopharyngeal secretions. Low temperatures cause constriction of nasal mucosal blood vessels, which may facilitate the expulsion of respiratory secretions. The difference in sample types should not be overlooked, as it could lead to varying results. Moreover, their study was based on all hospitalized patients, whereas we focused exclusively on neonates. Differences in the study populations may account for the observed discrepancies. Regrettably, studies specifically addressing respiratory pathogens in neonates in relation to meteorological factors are extremely scarce, leaving us at times without suitable comparable research. Environmental factors can modulate the virulence of Gram-negative bacteria, with increased temperature enhancing their virulence [31]. Higher temperatures may accelerate bacterial growth in the environment, potentially increasing human colonization [30]. The impact of temperature on humans and pathogens is multifaceted and requires comprehensive analysis.

Some pathogens show a negative correlation with sunlight duration, which can affect temperature. Moreover, ultraviolet radiation is an effective sterilizer against most pathogens, potentially inactivating them or reducing the spread of viral or bacterial aerosols. Reduced sunlight can also lower vitamin D levels, downregulate the expression of antimicrobial peptides, and diminish immune function [32]. Escherichia coli is positively correlated with average humidity; in high-humidity environments, bacteria are more active, whereas in dry conditions, Escherichia coli may die from dehydration. Increased air humidity can accelerate the settling of airborne microbes, increasing contact with surface organisms and thus infection rates. Meteorological factors significantly influence pathogens; using seasonal models to predict the seasonal detection rates of RSV and Staphylococcus aureus has shown good consistency between predicted and actual detection rates, indicating that the detection rates of these common pathogens can be forecasted. Predicting pathogen activity can aid patients who, for various reasons, cannot undergo pathogen testing. Due to the sample size, this study did not predict monthly or weekly but future studies should expand the sample size for more precise forecasting to better guide clinical practice.

Our results confirm that respiratory pathogens are closely related to meteorological factors, and the detection rates of some pathogens can be forecasted.The findings can guide appropriate empirical treatment, enabling early identification and timely intervention to reduce the rates of neonatal pneumonia infections and mortality. Additionally, it can provide a scientific basis for the rational planning of medical resources and the prevention and control of the spread of respiratory pathogens in neonates.

Conclusions

In NCAP, bacteria are most frequently detected. However, viral infections should also be considered significant. Most respiratory pathogens displayed distinct seasonality, particularly temperature and sunlight duration, which are major influences on most pathogens.

Limitations

1. No sample size calculation was performed, and the limited sample size may affect the results. 2. This study is a single-center retrospective analysis, involving only a small subset of children with pneumonia from the Xiaogan area; it may not accurately reflect epidemiological trends, and results could be biased. 3. Considering the presence of commensal flora in the human body, bacteria detected in respiratory secretions may not fully represent the pathogens causing pulmonary infections. 4. Indoor and outdoor environments influence each other; this study only used outdoor meteorological data. Future studies should include indoor environmental data for comprehensive analysis. 5. This period encompassed the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic, which has repeatedly been shown to alter the seasonality of many respiratory pathogens. Owing to resource and time constraints, we were unable to conduct a sensitivity analysis.

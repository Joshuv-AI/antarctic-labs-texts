Heterogeneous carbon decoupling and peaking pathways across county types in hunan province: an integrated tapio–STIRPAT–scenario analysis
Authors: Chaohui Zheng, Heng Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBalancing economic growth with emission reductions presents a pivotal challenge under China’s "dual-carbon" strategy. Hunan Province, characterized by its diverse industrial and ecological contexts, offers a quintessential setting for county-level analysis. This study investigates 122 counties within Hunan from 2000 to 2022, employing the Tapio decoupling model, spatial autocorrelation, ordinary least squares (OLS) regression, an augmented STIRPAT framework, and scenario simulations. It analyzes the spatiotemporal dynamics between carbon emissions and GDP, elucidates the principal driving mechanisms, and forecasts peaking trajectories under scenarios of low, medium, and high carbon emissions. The findings reveal a continued rise in emissions, albeit at a reduced pace post-2010, with the proportion of counties achieving strong decoupling escalating from 6% during 2006–2010 to 53% in 2016–2022. Counties dominated by eco-cultural (80%) and agricultural activities (67%) demonstrated the most significant transitions. Regression analyses, based on standardized coefficients, underscore the positive influence of carbon intensity (0.917), economic development (0.792), and population (0.447) on emissions, whereas industrial upgrading (-0.014), energy efficiency (-0.046), and urbanization (-0.010) contribute to mitigating impacts. GeoDetector analysis corroborates strong interaction effects. Scenario simulations indicate that only the low-carbon pathway might enable all four economic regions to achieve emission peaks between 2030 and 2035, while medium- and high-carbon trajectories fail to achieve effective peaking before 2040. These results highlight the imperative for tailored low-carbon strategies at the regional level, incorporating industrial restructuring, enhancement of energy efficiency, promotion of ecological-based industries, and implementation of county-level monitoring and market incentives.Keywords: Carbon decoupling, Carbon neutrality, Low-carbon development, Hunan province

Introduction

The contemporary global climate scenario is deteriorating, characterized by frequent and severe extreme weather events that pose substantial threats to both ecosystems and human societies [1]. In response to this escalating climate crisis, numerous countries have set forth objectives for achieving carbon peak and carbon neutrality to facilitate transitions to low-carbon energy and foster sustainable development [2–5]. Nevertheless, the synchronization of carbon reduction with economic growth continues to be a significant challenge [6]. Major obstacles include elevated costs associated with mitigation [7], uncertainties in technological pathways [8], and the dual burden that developing nations face in aligning economic development with effective emission reductions [9]. As a crucial marker for evaluating the quality of low-carbon development, the notion of carbon decoupling has begun to be recognized in some developed nations [10]. However, in many developing regions, carbon emissions persistently rise in conjunction with economic growth, thereby underscoring the complexity of decarbonization strategies and the marked regional disparities [11]. As the largest developing country in the world, China officially declared its "dual-carbon" objectives in 2020, which aim to cap carbon dioxide emissions by 2030 and achieve carbon neutrality by 2060 [12].

Carbon decoupling serves as a critical indicator for assessing the alignment between economic growth and environmental sustainability [13]. Over the period from 2000 to 2020, China’s carbon emissions increased at an average annual rate of approximately 5.4%, while its gross domestic product (GDP) grew by 9.5% annually, signifying an initial transition towards a more sustainable growth model. Some regions have progressively achieved weak decoupling, and instances of short-term strong decoupling have been observed in several economically advanced cities [14]. Within this context, the Tapio model based on elasticity and the OECD classifications of strong and weak decoupling have gained widespread acceptance for their simplicity and robust comparability [15]. Wang et al. [16] utilized the Tapio approach to scrutinize the relationship between municipal solid waste generation and ED across 285 Chinese cities. Xi et al. [17] investigated the decoupling dynamics between industrial growth and pollution within China’s Circum-Bohai Sea Region. Recent studies have further expanded the application of decoupling analysis across diverse sectors and scales. Luo et al. [18] developed a supply-side analytical framework to examine digitalization’s role in decoupling China’s tourism economy from carbon emissions, demonstrating that digital-enabled tourism value-added increased approximately 18 times from 2002 to 2017 while emissions only grew about 11 times, highlighting the importance of examining decoupling patterns at finer spatial scales. The STIRPAT model is predominantly employed to explore the drivers of carbon emissions at a regional level, providing a quantitative foundation for devising targeted mitigation strategies [19, 20].

China’s administrative hierarchy encompasses national, provincial, prefectural, and county levels. Existing scholarship has predominantly concentrated on the national, provincial, or prefectural scales [21, 22]. However, as the foundational governance unit within China’s administrative system, counties play an indispensable role in implementing regional development strategies, resource allocation, and policy execution. Achieving national carbon neutrality goals critically depends on coordinated county-level actions, where localized emission patterns, industrial compositions, and development stages vary substantially. Unlike provincial or prefecture-level studies that often mask internal heterogeneity, county-level analysis enables the identification of specific areas where effective emission reductions have not yet been achieved and facilitates the design of targeted mitigation strategies. Counties represent the primary arena where national policies are translated into concrete actions, and where the trade-offs between economic development and environmental protection are most directly experienced by local governments and residents. In Hunan Province specifically, county-level units exhibit remarkable diversity ranging from resource-based economies reliant on coal mining in Western Hunan to eco-cultural regions dominated by tourism, and from heavy-industry-based counties in the Chang-Zhu-Tan urban agglomeration to agriculture-dominated areas in the Dongting Lake region. This diversity makes Hunan an ideal setting for examining how different development pathways influence decoupling trajectories. Recent studies have begun to investigate county-level carbon emissions, for instance linking carbon-emission intensity to construction-land-use intensity at the county scale [23]. Moreover, recent studies have shown that land-use change and spatial carbon balance play a critical role in shaping regional carbon emission dynamics and carbon sink-source interactions, highlighting the need to integrate land use considerations into subnational decarbonization research. For example, research on land-use carbon emissions and carbon balance zoning in China’s economic regions has demonstrated pronounced spatial heterogeneity arising from land transformation processes and network interactions between socioeconomic and environmental factors [24, 25]. Liu et al. [26] employed Exploratory Spatiotemporal Data Analysis to delineate the spatiotemporal dynamics of carbon emissions across Chinese counties. Liu et al. [27] calculated land-use carbon emissions of Chinese counties and employed GeoDetector to analyze spatial–temporal patterns and driving factors. Huang et al. [28] further investigated the spatial network structure and carbon balance zoning of land-use carbon emissions using land-use and socio-economic data, revealing pronounced spatial heterogeneity, core–periphery structures, and interregional spillover effects at the subnational scale. Beyond land-use composition, urban spatial structure has likewise been shown to shape the spatiotemporal pattern of carbon emissions [29], reinforcing the need to account for spatial configuration when analyzing emissions at subnational scales. Scenario simulation methods have been increasingly utilized to forecast carbon peak trajectories at the provincial scale [30–32]. At the city scale, related studies have further decomposed the driving factors of emissions and predicted their peak trajectories [33]. Despite these advances, the application of such models at the county level remains limited.

Although land-use carbon emissions and carbon balance zoning have been increasingly studied at regional and provincial scales [24, 25] few studies have systematically examined how county-level decoupling patterns are shaped by land transformation and carbon flux dynamics, especially in central and western China. This gap underscores the need for finer-scale analyses that integrate land use, socioeconomic drivers, and decoupling mechanisms. First, a systematic examination of carbon decoupling at the county level, particularly focusing on regional heterogeneity and stage-based evolution, is conspicuously absent. This deficiency is particularly pronounced in Hunan Province, where a detailed identification of areas with ineffective emission reductions is still missing. Second, previous studies have predominantly concentrated on single-factor drivers or static regression analyses, paying scant attention to the interactive effects and cumulative impacts of multiple factors on county-level carbon emission dynamics. Third, there is a notable dearth of scenario-based simulation studies that integrate regional strategies and localized parameter adjustments for differentiated carbon peaking pathways.

This study focuses on 122 county-level administrative units in Hunan Province, aiming to systematically assess the decoupling relationship between carbon emissions and economic growth, analyze its spatiotemporal evolution patterns and principal driving mechanisms, and construct three developmental scenarios (low-carbon, medium-carbon, and high-carbon) to simulate future regional carbon peaking trajectories. In doing so, it makes four contributions that distinguish it from existing work: (1) Whereas carbon-decoupling research has been concentrated at the national, provincial, or prefectural scale, where aggregate indicators average over the internal heterogeneity that determines where mitigation actually stalls, this study conducts a continuous county-scale diagnosis of 122 units over a 22-year period, resolving the specific locations and stages at which growth remains coupled to emissions; (2) In contrast to studies that apply decoupling diagnosis, driver attribution, or scenario forecasting in isolation, it integrates the Tapio model, spatial autocorrelation, an augmented STIRPAT/OLS specification, GeoDetector, and scenario simulation into a single diagnosis-to-projection framework, linking the explanation of why decoupling occurs with the projection of when peaking can be achieved, and using GeoDetector to capture the interactive rather than single-factor nature of emission drivers; (3) Unlike studies that treat counties as a homogeneous group, it classifies them into six functional types and four economic regions, enabling differentiated decoupling trajectories and their drivers to be identified by type of place rather than by administrative unit alone; and (4) Beyond a single-province case, its scenario design is anchored in Hunan’s official planning documents to ensure policy relevance, while the framework itself is transferable to other provinces and developing economies marked by strong sub-national heterogeneity, where aggregate national targets risk masking local constraints.

Method and data

This study examines 122 county-level administrative units in Hunan Province, employing exploratory spatial analysis to investigate the decoupling dynamics between ED and carbon emissions within the context of China’s dual-carbon strategy. The objective is to identify the key factors influencing emission changes and to model potential pathways toward carbon peaking and neutrality across various developmental scenarios. The research adopts an integrated methodological framework that includes the Tapio decoupling model, spatial autocorrelation techniques, Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression, and an expanded STIRPAT model. The Tapio model, an elasticity-based decoupling metric, is widely used to assess the coordination between environmental pressures and economic growth due to its simplicity and cross-regional comparability [17]. The STIRPAT framework, a flexible stochastic extension of the IPAT identity, facilitates the quantification of the contributions of socioeconomic and technological factors to carbon emissions [20]. The GeoDetector tool is employed to detect spatial stratified heterogeneity and factor interactions, which is suitable for analysing nonlinear and spatially clustered emission systems [34] (Fig. 1).

Data sources, processing and limitations

This study employs county-level datasets comprising socioeconomic indicators, carbon emissions, and administrative boundaries for 122 counties in Hunan Province. The socioeconomic indicators include population size (P), gross domestic product (GDP), industrial structure (IS), electricity consumption, and urbanization rate (UR). These were sourced from the Hunan Statistical Yearbook (https://tjj.hunan.gov.cn/hntj/tjfx/hntjnj/hntjnjwlb/index.html), the China County Statistical Yearbook (https://www.stats.gov.cn/), and official statistical bulletins issued by relevant government departments. The data on carbon emissions were obtained from the Emission Database for Global Atmospheric Research (EDGAR) and then downscaled to the county level using spatial matching and weighted allocation procedures. Administrative boundary data were acquired from the most recent county-level vector shapefiles provided by the Geographic Information System (GIS) platform of Hunan Province. Additionally, to assist in inter-year calibration and ensure continuous temporal coverage, night-time light products from DMSP/OLS and NPP/VIIRS were utilised.

To maintain cross-year comparability, all datasets were harmonised to the 2020 administrative boundaries, and earlier socioeconomic records were re-aggregated accordingly. Annual GDP values were adjusted to constant 2000 prices using official deflators to mitigate the impacts of inflation. The night-time light products from DMSP/OLS and NPP/VIIRS underwent an inter-calibration process based on established methodologies to create a consistent long-term series. Procedures such as unit harmonisation, missing-value imputation, and cross-source reconciliation were implemented to enhance data integrity and consistency. From these processed datasets, six key variables were devised to characterise county-level development and emission patterns [26, 27, 46]: Carbon emission intensity, demographic scale, economic level, industrial upgrading, energy efficiency, and urban development. These variables were integrated into a unified spatial database to facilitate subsequent empirical analysis. This comprehensive dataset enables a detailed examination of regional disparities in emission trajectories and provides robust empirical support for formulating differentiated carbon peaking and neutrality strategies at the county level.

Several limitations of this study warrant acknowledgment. Firstly, the estimation of county-level carbon emissions is indirectly derived using night-time light proxies, potentially introducing uncertainties due to sensor saturation or inter-calibration discrepancies. Secondly, socioeconomic statistics at the county scale are occasionally incomplete, necessitating interpolation or cross-source integration. Thirdly, changes in administrative boundaries from 2000 to 2020 may impact temporal consistency, although this issue is mitigated by the harmonisation to the 2020 boundary. While these limitations do not compromise the identification of overall emission patterns, they may affect the accuracy of absolute estimates and the analysis of spatial variability.

Results

County-level carbon emission and GDP spatial–temporal evolution patterns in Hunan

Spatial–temporal evolution pattern of carbon emissions

Figure 2 illustrates the trends in total carbon emissions and their respective growth rates across the four principal economic regions of Hunan Province from 2000 to 2022. Throughout this period, a consistent upward trajectory in carbon emissions was noted in all regions, with particularly marked increases occurring between 2005 and 2012. The Chang-Zhu-Tan Region and the Dongting Lake Ecological Economic Region have been identified as the primary contributors, collectively accounting for a significant portion of the province’s total emissions. Conversely, the Southern Hunan and Western Hunan regions, whilst also exhibiting an upward trend, have registered comparatively lower levels of emissions. In terms of the growth rates of emissions, substantial fluctuations were observed in 2003 and 2006, with Western Hunan experiencing a peak growth rate in excess of 50% in 2006, subsequently followed by a gradual decline. After 2010, the growth rates across all regions have tended to stabilise, signifying a deceleration in growth and, in some instances, a transition towards emission stabilisation or reduction. The advent of the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020 precipitated a general reduction in growth rates across the regions; however, the Dongting Lake region experienced a temporary resurgence in 2021. In summary, while total carbon emissions in Hunan Province have continued to rise, the rate of increase has progressively moderated, reflecting a shift towards sustainable, high-quality, green development.

This observed distribution pattern is likely influenced by the province’s complex topography, the uneven pace of regional development, and diverse energy consumption structures.

Global Moran’s I

Figure 3 presents the results of the Anselin Local Moran’s I analysis on total carbon emissions across county-level administrative units in Hunan Province at five distinct time points: 2000, 2005, 2010, 2015, and 2022. In 2000, the spatial distribution of carbon emissions showed pronounced clustering. Low-Low clusters predominantly appeared in seven counties in Western Hunan and three counties in Southern Hunan, indicating zones with consistently low emissions in close proximity. Conversely, Low–High clusters were identified in three counties within the Chang-Zhu-Tan Region and two counties in the Dongting Lake Ecological Economic Region, which denote areas of low emissions adjacent to regions with higher emissions. A single High-Low cluster was observed in Zhijiang Dong Autonomous County in Western Hunan, representing an isolated area with elevated emissions. High-High clusters were primarily located in four counties within the Chang-Zhu-Tan Region and Yueyang County, highlighting significant concentrations of high emissions. By 2005, the spatial clustering pattern experienced minor changes. A new Low–High cluster emerged in Yongxing County in Southern Hunan, alongside additional Low–High clusters in Shifeng District and Liling City within the Chang-Zhu-Tan Region. Simultaneously, Lusong District and Xiangtan County developed into new High-High clusters, while most other regions maintained relative stability. From 2010 onwards, the overall spatial clustering trend began to diminish. Zhijiang County in Western Hunan transitioned from a High-Low to a non-significant status, a change also noted in Shifeng and Liling within the Chang-Zhu-Tan Region. Ningxiang City emerged as a new Low–High cluster, while Lengshuijiang and Lianyuan evolved into new High-High clusters, and Xiangxiang shifted from a High-High to a non-significant status. By 2015, Anxiang County in the Dongting Lake region and Lanshan County in Southern Hunan had transitioned from Low-Low clusters to non-significant statuses, indicating a trend towards spatial stabilisation. In 2022, Yongshun County in Western Hunan emerged as a new Low-Low cluster, with Lanshan County also reverting to this category. Concurrently, Ningxiang City and Yuetang District in the Chang-Zhu-Tan Region transitioned from Low–High and High-High clusters, respectively, to non-significant statuses, signalling a weakening of localised high-emission clustering. These findings suggest that the spatial distribution of county-level carbon emissions in Hunan Province from 2000 to 2022 followed a general pattern of dispersion, clustering, and redistribution. Initially, emissions exhibited distinct spatial heterogeneity and local clustering. However, due to economic restructuring, energy optimisation, and the implementation of regional coordination strategies, the clustering effects in high-emission zones have progressively diminished. Inter-county disparities in carbon emissions have begun to narrow, and the overall spatial configuration has evolved into a more balanced and diversified state. This trend reflects a shift towards regional coordination and green transformation in the governance of carbon emissions in Hunan Province.

Figure 4 depicts the centroid positions of total county-level carbon emissions in Hunan Province for the years 2000 and 2022, as delineated by standard deviation ellipses. The analysis indicates that the centroid of emissions consistently remained within Xiangxiang City, which is part of Xiangtan Municipality. Moreover, a more rapid increase in emissions has been observed in the southern areas. This trend highlights the heterogeneous development of industry, energy consumption, and urbanisation across different regions in Hunan. Consequently, this implies that a heightened focus on managing the escalating emissions burden in the central and southern areas is imperative when formulating carbon governance and reduction strategies.

Spatial–temporal evolution of GDP patterns

Figure 5 illustrates the trends in total county-level GDP and its corresponding growth rates across the four principal economic regions of Hunan Province from 2000 to 2022. During this period, all regions demonstrated a consistent upward trajectory in GDP, with particularly pronounced increases following 2010. Notably, the Chang-Zhu-Tan Region, as the economic nucleus of the province, consistently recorded the highest GDP figures and exhibited strong growth. This region was succeeded by the Dongting Lake Ecological Economic Region and the Southern Hunan Region, while the Western Hunan Region, despite lower GDP figures, also showed steady growth. In terms of growth rates, significant fluctuations were observed between 2000 and 2011. The Dongting Lake Ecological Economic Region reached a peak growth rate of nearly 50% in 2011, suggesting substantial economic expansion or possible adjustments in statistical methodologies. Before 2008, the Southern Hunan Region experienced considerable volatility in GDP growth, with rates approaching 30% in some years, whereas the Western Hunan Region displayed a pattern of rapid growth followed by a gradual slowdown. After 2012, growth rates across the regions stabilised, transitioning to a phase of moderate annual growth of approximately 10%, with narrower disparities among the regions. Despite the general slowdown triggered by the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020, none of the regions recorded negative GDP growth, and signs of gradual economic recovery were evident from 2021 onwards. These observations indicate a spatial gradient in ED across Hunan Province, with the Chang-Zhu-Tan Region consistently acting as the primary engine of growth. Concurrently, the other regions have shown trends of developmental catch-up and convergence. In recent years, Hunan’s economic trajectory has shifted towards high-quality growth, fostering a more balanced regional economic structure.

Between 2000 and 2005, the majority of counties in Hunan Province predominantly demonstrated a weak decoupling status (57%), whereby economic output and carbon emissions grew concurrently, albeit with relatively low carbon elasticity. Additionally, a considerable proportion of counties experienced expansive negative decoupling (22%) and expansive coupling (12%), indicating that in some areas, carbon emissions either increased at a rate surpassing GDP growth or progressed in complete synchrony with economic expansion. The prevalence of strong negative decoupling was notably minimal (9%), suggesting that only a few counties achieved significant reductions in emissions during this time frame. From a regional analysis, 57% of the counties in the Chang-Zhu-Tan area were characterized by weak decoupling. Similarly, both Western Hunan and Southern Hunan displayed predominantly weak decoupling statuses, each at 53%. However, Western Hunan was distinct in exhibiting a significantly higher incidence of expansive negative decoupling (36%), indicative of a pattern where economic growth was accompanied by rapid increases in carbon emissions. In contrast, in the Dongting Lake region, 67% of counties achieved weak decoupling, which may reflect relatively effective carbon management practices in this area. Examining the situation from the perspective of functional typologies, counties classified as eco-cultural and agriculture-dominated primarily displayed weak decoupling, at 90% and 60% respectively. This suggests that while their ED levels were modest, carbon emissions remained comparatively controlled. Conversely, 50% of counties that are reliant on energy resources were marked by expansive negative decoupling, illustrating a development trajectory heavily burdened by carbon emissions. Notably, counties with a focus on light industry demonstrated the highest incidence of strong negative decoupling, at 40%. This may be attributable to technological advancements and energy efficiency improvements within these regions.

Between 2006 and 2010, with the ongoing advancement of ED, there was a significant improvement in the overall decoupling level in Hunan Province. The predominant pattern was weak decoupling (84%), indicating that in most counties, carbon emissions increased at a slower rate than GDP. This reflects an initial movement towards low-carbon development. The proportion of strong decoupling (6%) also rose compared to the earlier period, while the instances of expansive negative decoupling (5%) and expansive coupling (2%) decreased markedly, suggesting a diminishing reliance of carbon emissions on economic growth. From a regional perspective, as many as 96% of the counties in the Dongting Lake region were classified as weakly decoupled, representing the most favorable decoupling status among the four regions. Both Southern Hunan (79%) and Western Hunan (89%) exhibited high proportions of weak decoupling, indicating that most areas within the province were beginning to move away from carbon-intensive development patterns. In contrast, while the Chang-Zhu-Tan region primarily remained weakly decoupled (75%), 14% of its counties were still categorized under expansive negative decoupling, highlighting the increasing pressures of carbon emissions faced by this urban-industrial core amid rapid industrialization and urbanization. In terms of county typology, counties dominated by agriculture (91%) and those identified as eco-cultural (90%) demonstrated the strongest trends in weak decoupling, suggesting that these areas had distinct advantages in managing the growth of carbon emissions. However, 22% of counties identified as heavy-industry-oriented continued to experience expansive negative decoupling, reflecting the challenges posed by their energy-intensive ISs. Notably, half of the energy-resource-based counties achieved strong decoupling, possibly benefiting from structural adjustments or technological improvements in resource-intensive sectors. Light-industry-oriented counties displayed significant internal variation, with 20% falling under expansive negative decoupling and another 20% achieving strong decoupling, indicating uneven development trajectories within this group.

During the period from 2011 to 2015, the overall decoupling level in Hunan Province improved further. Approximately 68% of the counties achieved a state of weak decoupling, which continued to dominate the overall pattern. However, the proportion of counties experiencing strong decoupling rose significantly to 29%, indicating that some counties had achieved a notable separation between economic growth and carbon emissions, thereby marking substantial progress in the region’s transition to a low-carbon economy. Meanwhile, the incidences of expansive negative decoupling and expansive coupling both declined to just 2%, suggesting that scenarios involving high-carbon development had been significantly reduced. Regionally, the Chang-Zhu-Tan area was particularly impressive: 36% of its counties achieved strong decoupling status, with the remainder exhibiting weak decoupling, and none fell into any expansive decoupling categories. This performance underscores the region’s role as the province’s economic powerhouse and highlights its achievements in green development and technological upgrading. Strong decoupling was also observed in 21%, 29%, and 28% of the counties in the Dongting Lake, Southern Hunan, and Western Hunan regions, respectively, indicating that the entire province was accelerating its shift toward low-carbon development during this period. In terms of county typologies, as many as 75% of the energy-resource-dependent counties achieved strong decoupling, the best performance among all types. This likely resulted from structural adjustments and improvements in energy efficiency within resource-intensive industries. Furthermore, 60% of light-industry-oriented counties also achieved strong decoupling, suggesting substantial progress in clean production and industrial upgrading. While agricultural-dominated, eco-cultural, and heavy-industry-oriented counties primarily remained in weak decoupling (all exceeding 70%), the proportion of counties achieving strong decoupling was steadily increasing. Notably, 25% of agricultural counties and 20% of eco-cultural counties achieved strong decoupling, signaling their growing potential for low-carbon transformation.

Between 2016 and 2022, under the enhanced focus on ecological and environmental protection mandated by China’s 13th and 14th Five-Year Plans, the decoupling of carbon emissions from economic growth at the county level in Hunan Province was significantly improved. The proportion of counties achieving strong decoupling increased sharply to 53%, establishing it as the predominant category. This trend indicates that a majority of counties successfully reduced carbon emissions while sustaining economic growth, signaling a robust trajectory towards low-carbon development. Concurrently, weak decoupling, which was observed in 43% of the counties, suggests that some counties remained in a transitional phase characterized by a moderate increase in emissions, albeit at a rate slower than that of economic growth. The proportions of counties experiencing expansive negative decoupling and strong negative decoupling decreased markedly to 2% and 1%, respectively, indicating a decline in high-carbon growth patterns. The emergence of recessive decoupling in 1% of the counties likely reflects the economic instability or adjustment challenges encountered by certain areas. From a regional perspective, Southern Hunan and Western Hunan exhibited the most impressive results, with 68% and 64% of counties, respectively, achieving strong decoupling. These traditionally less developed regions have realized a notable balance between reducing emissions and fostering development through industrial restructuring and enhancements in energy efficiency. In contrast, the Dongting Lake and Chang-Zhu-Tan regions reported slightly lower levels of strong decoupling, with 38% and 36% of counties, respectively. However, a majority of counties in both regions demonstrated weak decoupling (63% and 61%, respectively), suggesting that while economic momentum remained robust, emissions were effectively managed. In terms of functional county types, eco-cultural counties exhibited the most notable success, with 80% achieving strong decoupling and 10% displaying recessive decoupling. This underscores the potential of tourism- and culture-based low-carbon industries. Agricultural-dominated counties (67% strong decoupling) and light-industry-oriented counties (40% strong decoupling) also showed favorable trends towards a green transition. In contrast, comprehensive service-oriented counties predominantly experienced weak decoupling but demonstrated consistent improvements in carbon control capabilities. Notably, energy-resource-dependent counties presented the most varied decoupling patterns, with 25% categorized under expansive negative decoupling and another 25% under strong negative decoupling, highlighting ongoing challenges in carbon reduction within these energy-intensive industrial sectors.

Discussion

Regional energy conservation and low-carbon transition strategies

Following a comprehensive decoupling analysis of county-level carbon emissions and ED in Hunan Province from 2000 to 2022, and multiple simulations of carbon peak scenarios, it has become evident that the province’s four principal economic regions exhibit significant disparities in their carbon emission trajectories, peak potentials, and mitigation capacities. These differences are primarily attributed to regional disparities in IS, energy consumption patterns, technological infrastructure, and policy responsiveness. Therefore, to support Hunan’s overarching goals of achieving a carbon peak and fostering a green transition, it is crucial to develop energy conservation and low-carbon strategies that are tailored, targeted, and phased, reflecting the unique attributes of each region.

Chang-Zhu-Tan Region: Consolidate Leading Advantages and Build a Green Development Engine

Chang-Zhu-Tan Region: Consolidate Leading Advantages and Build a Green Development Engine

The Chang-Zhu-Tan region, recognized as the most industrialized and urbanized area within Hunan Province, accommodates a significant proportion of energy-intensive manufacturing industries and is the primary hub for technological innovation in the province. Simulations suggest that, under a low-carbon scenario, the region is expected to reach its carbon emissions peak before 2030, followed by a stabilization of emissions. Future strategies should focus on enhancing the green transformation of the manufacturing sector, accelerating the intelligent and low-carbon modernization of traditional industries, and strictly enforcing entry criteria for projects categorized as “high energy-consuming, high-emission, and overcapacity.” Industrial parks within the region are encouraged to promote the cascading utilization of energy and the substitution of clean energy, as well as to implement carbon footprint management systems and green supply chain frameworks. Leveraging its strong innovation capabilities, the region should also advance cutting-edge technologies, including carbon capture, utilization, and storage (CCUS), digital energy management, and building energy efficiency. The objective is to develop a low-carbon urban agglomeration and a sustainable industrial ecosystem, thereby positioning the Chang-Zhu-Tan region as a paradigm of low-carbon leadership in Hunan and across central China.

(2) Dongting Lake Ecological Economic Region: Leveraging Ecological Potential to Enhance Synergistic Emission Reductions in Agriculture and Industry

(2) Dongting Lake Ecological Economic Region: Leveraging Ecological Potential to Enhance Synergistic Emission Reductions in Agriculture and Industry

The Dongting Lake Ecological Economic Region, characterized by its extensive plains, farmlands, and crucial wetlands, plays a pivotal role in ecological conservation and food security within Hunan Province. Moreover, it serves as a foundational hub for various industries. This study demonstrates that although the region’s carbon emissions exceed those of Southern and Western Hunan, substantial potential exists for achieving carbon peaking with considerable flexibility. It is crucial to prioritize the adoption of low-carbon agricultural practices such as straw return, conservation tillage, and the resource recovery of agricultural waste to facilitate the agricultural sector’s green transformation. Simultaneously, energy-intensive sectors, including traditional energy and building materials industries, should be subject to more stringent energy efficiency regulations and guided towards the adoption of clean energy solutions and the optimization of energy structures. In counties with abundant wetland resources, the initiation of pilot carbon sink trading programs, coupled with the expansion of forest carbon sequestration and cropland carbon storage projects, could produce significant co-benefits for both ecological protection and carbon reduction.

(3)Southern Hunan: Capitalizing on the Critical Period of Industrial Transformation to Mitigate “Carbon Lock-in” Risks

Southern Hunan: Capitalizing on the Critical Period of Industrial Transformation to Mitigate “Carbon Lock-in” Risks

Southern Hunan has recently emerged as one of the most dynamically industrializing regions in the province, undergoing a rapid expansion phase. According to this study, under medium- and high-carbon scenarios, the region is projected to face sustained upward pressure on emissions, underscoring the critical necessity to prevent long-term high-carbon developmental lock-in. It is advisable for the region to implement stringent energy efficiency assessments and environmental impact controls on new industrial projects to curtail the unchecked growth of energy-intensive and high-emission industries. Enhanced support should be extended to high-tech industries and advanced manufacturing sectors, including the development of green factories and eco-industrial parks. Given the trends of urban expansion in the region, the early implementation of green infrastructure, such as sustainable transport systems, energy-efficient buildings, and waste-to-resource initiatives, is pivotal for the structural optimization of urban carbon emissions.

(4) Western Hunan: Accelerating Energy Substitution and Industrial Diversification to Overcome Carbon Rigidity in Resource-based Regions

(4) Western Hunan: Accelerating Energy Substitution and Industrial Diversification to Overcome Carbon Rigidity in Resource-based Regions

Western Hunan, characterized by its complex terrain and a coal-dominated energy structure, faces significant challenges due to its strong reliance on high-carbon energy, a weak industrial base, and relatively underdeveloped ED, all contributing to low carbon flexibility. In scenarios of high carbon emissions, this region not only peaks later but also maintains elevated carbon intensity over time. To overcome the dual constraints imposed by its existing energy structure and developmental trajectory, it is imperative for the region to prioritize the expansion of clean energy sources. These include distributed photovoltaics, small-scale hydropower suited to its mountainous terrains, and biomass energy, which would collectively reduce its dependence on coal. Simultaneously, there should be a strategic shift from resource-dependent industries to those with lower carbon footprints, such as cultural tourism, healthcare services, and specialty agriculture. This transition could be accelerated through cross-regional industrial coordination with areas such as Chang-Zhu-Tan, which would facilitate the relocation and upgrading of industries through mechanisms of “industrial cage replacement” and the transfer of green gradients. From a policy and financial perspective, it is crucial to increase investment in low-carbon infrastructure within Western Hunan, coupled with the exploration of integrated mechanisms of “ecological compensation plus carbon trading” to enhance the region’s capacity for carbon reduction.

Policy recommendations for low-carbon development at the county level in Hunan province

The regression and spatial analyses conducted in this study demonstrate that, at the county level in Hunan Province, carbon emissions are significantly influenced by CI, ED, and P size. In contrast, factors such as IS, ECI, and UR appear to exert a restraining influence on emissions. To facilitate the achievement of carbon peaking and carbon neutrality at the county level, amidst the challenges posed by developmental disparities, this study proposes three distinct policy directions:

Coordinated Industrial Restructuring and Energy Efficiency Enhancement

Coordinated Industrial Restructuring and Energy Efficiency Enhancement

The high carbon intensity and substantial reliance on the secondary sector significantly contribute to regional emissions. Regression analyses reveal that counties with more advanced ISs exhibit lower emissions. Furthermore, the GeoDetector identifies a robust correlation between CI and ECI. It is therefore essential for counties to prioritize the transformation of their industrial bases, gradually transitioning from energy-intensive manufacturing to the promotion of high-tech and low-carbon service industries. This shift must include the integration of clean energy technologies, particularly in counties known for resource-based or heavy industries. In these areas, restructuring energy use and enhancing efficiency can lead to considerable marginal reductions in emissions. Practical steps include the development of green industrial parks, energy-efficient upgrades to existing facilities, and improved integration of renewable energy sources into the local energy infrastructure. Additionally, the implementation of performance-based energy management systems in major industrial enterprises, coupled with expanded access to clean electricity in rural areas, will be pivotal in this transformation.

(2) Spatially Differentiated Pathways Based on County Functions and Development Stage

(2) Spatially Differentiated Pathways Based on County Functions and Development Stage

The considerable heterogeneity among the counties of Hunan, which range from agriculture-dominated to industrial hubs, renders the implementation of a uniform decarbonization strategy both unrealistic and inefficient. The research indicates that counties in western or southern Hunan, primarily characterized by ecological and agricultural activities, have experienced varying degrees of decoupling, limited by their modest industrial bases. These regions should leverage their ecological advantages by enhancing forest carbon sinks, developing eco-tourism, and promoting sustainable agricultural practices. In contrast, industrialized counties within areas such as the Chang-Zhu-Tan urban agglomeration, which are experiencing persistent expansionary decoupling or strong coupling, must implement stringent carbon control measures and green their industrial processes. Additionally, emerging urban centers undergoing rapid population increases should focus on transit-oriented development and the implementation of green infrastructure to avoid future lock-in effects. This strategy of spatially differentiated governance not only aligns with the principle of “local peaking before provincial peaking” but also provides a resilient and regionally adaptive framework for achieving carbon neutrality.

(3) Institutional Capacity Building and Market-Based Incentives for Long-Term Transition

(3) Institutional Capacity Building and Market-Based Incentives for Long-Term Transition

The success of a low-carbon transition fundamentally relies on the governance capacity and the strength of institutional frameworks. Currently, the lack of county-level emissions monitoring systems severely hampers informed decision-making. It is imperative to establish a hierarchical carbon inventory and a real-time monitoring platform at the county level, which should be interconnected with the provincial carbon data hub. This system ought to include major emitters, significant energy consumers, and variables pertinent to land-use changes. Such an infrastructure will facilitate effective carbon budget management and performance tracking. Additionally, the transition requires sustained financial investment. It is advisable for counties to create low-carbon development funds, which could be financed through mechanisms like green bonds, environmental taxes, or fiscal transfers. Where feasible, pilot initiatives for county-level carbon markets or offset mechanisms should be considered, especially in counties with robust industrial sectors or significant ecological resources. These mechanisms aim to internalize environmental costs and encourage private sector investment in decarbonization efforts.

Comparison with existing literature and case illustrations

The empirical findings of this study both corroborate and refine the existing research on carbon decoupling in China by illustrating how decoupling mechanisms are more pronounced at the county level. Aligning with studies at the national and provincial scales, which report a progression from weak to strong decoupling in economically advanced regions [14, 26], our results demonstrate a significant increase in the proportion of counties achieving strong decoupling in Hunan Province over time. However, the heterogeneity of decoupling trajectories at the county level is markedly greater than at higher administrative levels, emphasizing the need for detailed analysis to identify differentiated transition pathways. When set against provincial-scale evidence, this contrast is especially informative: whereas Liu et al. [32], coupling decoupling analysis with scenario simulation, reported that China’s overall decoupling status first improved and then deteriorated at the provincial scale, our county-level results instead reveal a sustained rise in the share of strongly decoupled counties that nonetheless conceals sharply divergent within-province trajectories, underscoring the value of resolving decoupling dynamics below the provincial scale.

In terms of driving mechanisms, the predominant influence of carbon emission intensity in our regression analysis is consistent with findings from provincial-level studies that highlight intensity reduction as a key driver of emission mitigation [22]. Notably, the negative impact of industrial structure upgrading in this study is more pronounced than that reported in analyses at the prefecture or provincial levels [20]. This indicates that industrial restructuring has a more immediate and observable impact on emission reduction at the county level, where changes in dominant industries directly alter local energy consumption patterns. Furthermore, the GeoDetector results reveal that the interaction between carbon intensity and energy consumption intensity possesses particularly strong explanatory power, underscoring that emission reduction outcomes are seldom driven by single factors but rather result from synergistic interactions among energy efficiency, industrial transformation, and economic development. These intensity- and structure-driven patterns are broadly consistent with city-scale driver decompositions and peak projections [33] and with county-scale evidence from Chongqing linking carbon-emission intensity to construction-land-use intensity [23], yet our analysis extends them by showing that, at the county scale, such drivers operate through pronounced functional heterogeneity rather than uniformly across space.

These mechanisms can be exemplified through contrasting county-level cases within Hunan Province. Changsha County, situated within the Chang-Zhu-Tan urban agglomeration, represents a transition-driven decoupling pathway. According to our empirical findings, counties experiencing rapid industrial upgrading and declining energy intensity are more likely to achieve strong decoupling. In Changsha County, the expansion of the tertiary sector, coupled with enhancements in energy efficiency, facilitated a shift from weak to strong decoupling post-2015. This transformation occurred not merely through emission control policies but through the gradual substitution of energy-intensive manufacturing with industries focused on technology and services. This is consistent with the negative coefficients for industrial structure and energy intensity observed in our regression analysis.

Conversely, Longshan County in Western Hunan represents a resource-dependent pathway with limited decoupling potential. Despite moderate economic growth, the county continued to exhibit weak decoupling throughout most of the study period. This outcome reflects the strong positive correlation between carbon intensity and emissions identified in the empirical analysis. The ongoing prevalence of coal-based energy and the lack of industrial diversity undermined the effectiveness of policy interventions, demonstrating that financial transfers alone are inadequate for achieving strong decoupling in resource-dependent counties without concurrent investments in alternative industries and energy infrastructure. Collectively, these case studies illustrate that county-level decoupling outcomes are influenced by structurally embedded development pathways. Counties engaged in industrial upgrading and efficiency improvements typically experience more pronounced decoupling effects, whereas those dependent on resources encounter path-dependent limitations. This insight enhances the existing body of decoupling literature by emphasizing that the efficacy of carbon mitigation strategies depends on local structural conditions. This finding underscores the importance of differentiated and place-based policy design in addressing the challenges faced by China’s counties.

Limitation and future expectations

Despite conducting a comprehensive assessment of county-level carbon decoupling patterns, driving mechanisms, and scenario-based peaking pathways in Hunan Province, it is important to acknowledge several limitations. These limitations also highlight promising avenues for future research.

Firstly, data-related limitations present a significant challenge at the county level. Carbon emissions and various socioeconomic indicators are compiled from multiple datasets, each differing in spatial resolution, statistical definitions, and reporting frequencies. Despite the application of extensive preprocessing, harmonization, and interpolation, residual inconsistencies remain. These may impact the precision of coefficient estimates and the robustness of scenario projections, particularly in counties with sparse or incomplete data records. Future studies would benefit from access to improved county-level energy and emission statistics, higher-resolution remote sensing products, and the integration of firm- or sector-specific data to enhance measurement accuracy.

Secondly, methodological limitations emerge from the reliance on linear econometric models and static interaction detection methods. Although the STIRPAT-based regression and GeoDetector analysis effectively identify dominant drivers and interaction effects, they do not account for dynamic feedbacks, nonlinear responses, or time-varying causal relationships among variables such as economic growth, industrial structure, energy use, and emissions. Future research should explore nonlinear modeling approaches, dynamic panel models, and causal inference frameworks, including difference-in-differences, instrumental variables, or machine learning-based causal methods. These approaches could provide deeper insights into the mechanisms underlying county-level decoupling.

Thirdly, scenario-related limitations arise from the simplifying assumptions used in our projection framework. The scenario simulations rely on stylized development pathways and do not account for potential abrupt technological breakthroughs, policy shifts, or external disturbances. Future work should aim to refine the scenario design by incorporating adaptive policy scenarios, stochastic elements, or system dynamics models. This would allow for a more realistic representation of the uncertainties and complexities associated with long-term low-carbon transitions. Addressing these limitations will not only enhance the robustness of county-level decoupling assessments but also improve the policy relevance of future studies, especially in supporting dynamic and evidence-based low-carbon governance at sub-provincial scales.

Conclusion

This study offers a county-level analysis of the decoupling relationship between carbon emissions and economic growth in Hunan Province from 2000 to 2022. Integrating methods such as decoupling diagnosis, econometric identification of key drivers, and forward-looking scenario simulation, the results demonstrate significant spatial heterogeneity in emission trajectories and decoupling transitions. Over the study period, an increasing number of counties demonstrated strong decoupling, suggesting that achieving a low-carbon transition at the county level is feasible but heavily dependent on specific local development conditions, industrial foundations, and patterns of energy use.

The findings of this study have several policy implications that are immediately applicable at the county level. Firstly, the pronounced impact of carbon intensity and its significant interactions with economic growth, industrial structure, and energy-use efficiency indicate that counties should avoid single-instrument approaches and should, instead, implement comprehensive policy packages. In counties with robust industrial bases and high urbanization rates (e.g., within major urban agglomerations), it is crucial to prioritize the acceleration of industrial upgrading towards activities that are high in value-added and low in emissions. These counties should also consider tightening entry standards for energy-intensive projects and enhancing energy efficiency through process optimization and increased electrification. In contrast, for counties that are resource-dependent or less developed, a different approach is required for decarbonization that includes stabilizing livelihoods and fiscal capacity, promoting industrial diversification, expanding distributed renewable energy sources and clean heating options where feasible, and employing targeted fiscal instruments (e.g., earmarked transfers, green credit, and performance-based subsidies) to mitigate the risk of carbon lock-in. Secondly, the scenario analysis suggests that peaking timelines are highly sensitive to chosen development pathways. Consequently, counties should incorporate carbon-peaking objectives into their annual and medium-term strategic planning, complete with measurable sector-specific roadmaps. These efforts should be supported by a robust county-level monitoring system that tracks carbon intensity and energy-use metrics. Thirdly, to address cross-county spillovers and interactive effects, county governments need to enhance regional coordination mechanisms. This could include collaborative planning for industrial layouts, shared energy infrastructure, and coordinated regulatory measures to prevent the displacement of high-emission activities from core to peripheral counties.

The empirical analysis conducted in a single province has implications that extend beyond Hunan, both in methodological and policy dimensions. This study presents a transferable county-level analytical framework to other provinces in China for identifying decoupling patterns, diagnosing dominant drivers, and testing alternative peaking pathways under various development strategies. This approach is particularly pertinent for provinces characterized by significant internal heterogeneity—where industrialized, agricultural, and resource-based counties coexist. This is because aggregated provincial targets may obscure local challenges and result in the implementation of inefficient “one-size-fits-all” policies. The typology-based interpretation emphasized in this study offers a logical framework for provinces to allocate responsibilities among counties: advanced counties might achieve earlier emission peaks through industrial upgrading and efficiency improvements; transitional counties could concentrate on structural adjustments and clean energy substitution; and resource-dependent counties may need coordinated support packages that include transformation finance, infrastructure investment, and employment-oriented diversification. Such differentiated governance is likely to enhance the effectiveness and equity of policies while mitigating the risk of carbon leakage across sub-provincial entities.

More broadly, this study provides insights applicable to other countries, particularly developing economies, where subnational heterogeneity and uneven governance capacities are prevalent. Many countries implement climate and energy policies at the local level, such as districts, counties, or municipalities, yet national-level indicators often fail to reflect place-based constraints like industrial specialization, energy access, and fiscal capacity. The county-level decoupling perspective advanced in this research demonstrates how detailed evidence can inform differentiated low-carbon transition strategies: (i) identifying locations where economic growth is still closely linked with emissions; (ii) prioritizing mechanisms such as intensity reduction, structural upgrading, and energy efficiency, tailored to local conditions; and (iii) designing scenario-based pathways that synchronize mitigation efforts with realistic development trajectories. Thus, although specific parameter values may vary according to context, the fundamental policy logic, “diagnose local coupling status, target key drivers, and sequence transition measures according to local development pathways”, is universally relevant to countries striving to balance growth with decarbonization amid limited resources and regional disparities.

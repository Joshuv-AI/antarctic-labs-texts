A damage constitutive model of carbonaceous shale containing a single crack under drying-weting cycles and triaxial compression
Authors: Yu Li, Yuelong Wang, Rensheng Wang, Aiguo Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To describe the deformation and strength characteristics of carbonaceous shale containing a single fracture under triaxial compression after dry–wet cycles, a segmented damage constitutive model combining dry–wet damage and fracture damage is proposed. Firstly, based on phenomenological theory, the damage variable subjected to dry–wet cycles and fissure angle is obtained. Secondly, a coupled damage variable for fractured carbonaceous shale is derived based on the Lemaitre strain equivalence hypothesis and Weibull statistical damage model. Then, considering the initial compaction stage and the residual strength characteristics, a segmented damage constitutive model of carbonaceous shale containing a single fracture under dry–wet cycles and triaxial compression was established. The rationality and accuracy of the model were compared and verified through the experimental stress–strain curves. The results show that the model can well describe the deformation and failure process of carbonaceous shale under dry–wet cycles, fissure angle and confining pressure. The damage evolution shows an S-shaped change characteristic. The dry–wet cycles promote the damage and failure, confining pressure inhibits the damage and failure, and the fissure angle controls the anisotropy of the damage. The research results have important reference value for revealing the mechanism of rock damage and deterioration under the coupling of multiple factors.

Introduction

Carbonaceous shale is widely distributed in slope engineering projects, and its mechanical properties and long-term stability directly affect the safety of the projects. The mechanical properties of carbonaceous shale are further deteriorated under dry–wet cycles and fissures coupling, resulting in a decrease in its bearing capacity, thereby increasing the risk of geological disasters such as collapses and landslides1–4. Therefore, it is of great significance to study the damage evolution mechanism of fractured carboniferous shale under the dry–wet cycles for the long-term stability and disaster prevention of geotechnical engineering.

At present, scholars have conducted a series of fruitful studies on the mechanical properties of rocks under dry–wet cycles. Existing research findings indicate that as the number of wet-dry cycles increases, the strength parameters of rock gradually decrease5–9 explored the influence of dry–wet cycles on the dynamic and static tensile strength of sandstone by conducting dynamic and static Brazilian splitting test. Using techniques such as nuclear magnetic resonance, CT scanning, and electron microscopy, the damage mechanism of rocks under dry–wet cycles was analyzed from a microscopic perspective. The study found that repeated drying and wetting actions can lead to the expansion of internal pores in rocks, causing initially isolated pores to gradually develop into an interconnected network structure10–13. Damage constitutive model is one of the important ways to describe the damage characteristics of rocks14 established the connection of the pore structure and microscopic morphology with the mechanical damage, thereby enabling the quantitative characterization of the coal microscopic damage under dry–wet cycles15 studied the dynamic mechanical and energy characteristics of rock after freeze–thaw cycles, and proposed a damage constitutive model considering various forms of dissipated energy16 established a dynamic damage constitutive model of sandstone considering the effects of dry–wet cycles and loading rates. However, the damage constitutive model established in the aforementioned study cannot adequately characterize the initial compaction stage in the stress–strain curve17 established a segmented damage statistical constitutive model of coal body considering the relatively significant compression phase of coal body under the action of water. It is worth noting that there are often a large number of macroscopic defects such as joints and fissures in the rock mass, which have an important influence on the mechanical properties of the rock mass18,19 proposed a uniaxial compression damage constitutive model combining hydro-chemical damage and macroscopic single fracture coupled damage20,21 derived a damage ontological model considering the coupling of macroscopic defects for non-penetrating fractures and jointed rock masses.

In conclusion, extensive findings have been conducted on the mechanical properties and damage constitutive models of fractured rock under dry–wet cycles. However, current constitutive models rarely incorporate the initial compaction stage, with even fewer considering the coupled effects of dry–wet cycles, fissure angle, and stress state. Therefore, based on continuous damage mechanics and statistical theory, this paper constructs a segmented damage constitutive model for triaxial compression of carbonaceous shale under the influence of dry–wet cycles and fissure angle. The influence of dry–wet cycles, fissure angle, and confining pressure on the damage evolution law of carbonaceous shale is explored.

Damage evolution characteristics

By substituting the calculated model parameters and the triaxial compression test data into Eq. (11), the damage evolution curves of carbonaceous shale under different conditions, as shown in Fig. 6.Fig. 6Damage evolution curves of some samples.

Damage evolution curves of some samples.

Fig. 6 illustrates the damage evolution of carbonaceous shale under different conditions went through five stages: initial damage stabilization(Ⅰ), damage initiation(Ⅱ), damage acceleration(Ⅲ), damage deceleration(Ⅳ) and damage termination(Ⅴ), presenting an S-shaped development pattern. When the strain is 0, the corresponding damage is the initial damage. As can be seen from Fig. 6(a), compared to the dry specimen, the initial damage of the specimens increased by 0.20, 0.329, and 0.388 after 5, 10, and 15 dry–wet cycles, respectively. The damage was more significant during the first five dry–wet cycles. With the increase of the number of dry–wet cycles, the damage curve shifts overall to the left, indicating that the damage evolution rate of the specimen accelerates, leading to a faster attainment of the critical failure state. Dry–wet cycles weaken the rock structure, promoted the development of microcracks, and caused the specimen to enter the damage acceleration stage earlier. Therefore, dry–wet cycles can be regarded as a driving force for the damage of carbonaceous shale, accelerating its deterioration and failure.

Fig. 6(b) shows that as the confining pressure increases, the damage curve shifts to the right as a whole, the damage evolution rate of the specimen slows down, and the strain required to reach the critical failure state increases. This indicates that the confining pressure has a significant constraining and strengthening effect on the specimen. Under higher confining pressure, the propagation of internal cracks in the rock is suppressed, resulting in greater ductility and higher load-bearing capacity.

Fig. 6(c) shows that fractured specimens have greater initial damage compared to intact specimens, indicating that the presence of fractures causes damage to the rock. As the fissure angle increases from 0°to 90°, the damage to the specimen first increases and then gradually decreases, reaching its maximum at a fissure angle of 45°. This demonstrates that the fissure angle significantly controls the rock damage. This is primarily due to the presence of fractures, which leads to a preferential orientation in the propagation of internal cracks within the rock29, resulting in variations in the degree of rock damage.

Conclusion

The segmented damage constitutive model that can comprehensively consider the dry–wet cycle, fissure angle and confining pressure effect was constructed.

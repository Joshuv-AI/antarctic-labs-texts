Selective Conventional Transarterial Chemoembolization Using a Glass Membrane Emulsification Device in Hepatocellular Carcinoma: Multicenter Clinical Trial in Japan
Authors: Toshihiro Tanaka, Hiroshi Anai, Tetsuya Masada, Satoru Sueyoshi, Takeshi Sato, Shohei Toyoda, Takeshi Matsumoto, Yuto Chanoki, Keisuke Oshima, Sho Shimizu, Mariko Irizato, Ryosuke Taiji, Kiyoyuki Minamiguchi, Hideki Kunichika, Hiroyuki Kurakami, Shigeo Ichihashi, Hideyuki Nishiofuku
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe aim of the study was to evaluate the complete response (CR) rate achieved with selective conventional transarterial chemoembolization (cTACE) using a porous glass membrane emulsification device in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).MethodsIn a multicenter phase II trial (jRCTs052200095) conducted between January 2021 and June 2023, 50 patients with unresectable HCC (tumor diameter ≤5 cm; Child-Pugh A or B) were enrolled. An emulsion of epirubicin and ethiodized oil (Lipiodol) prepared using the emulsification device was injected into the tumor-feeding arteries, followed by embolization with gelatin sponge particles. The primary endpoint was the CR rate at 3 months post-treatment. Secondary endpoints included the CR rate at 1 month and the incidence of adverse events. Outcomes were compared with a historical control cohort from the JIVROSG-1302 study in exploratory analyses.ResultsTwo patients were excluded after enrollment, leaving 48 patients who received cTACE (safety set). An additional 3 patients were excluded post-treatment, yielding 45 patients for efficacy analysis. The CR rate at both 1 month and 3 months was 97.8% (95% confidence interval: 88.2–99.9%). Serious adverse events (grade ≥3) included elevated AST in 50.0% of patients, elevated ALT in 29.2%, thrombocytopenia in 2.1%, and elevated ALP in 2.1%. The proportion of patients with deterioration in ALBI grade (≥1 grade increase) at 3 months was 17.8%. In exploratory analyses, CR rates were numerically higher than those observed in the historical control.ConclusionSelective cTACE using a W/O emulsion prepared with a glass membrane pumping emulsification device was associated with high short-term CR rates in patients with HCC, with an acceptable safety profile. These findings support further clinical investigation.

Introduction

Transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) is an appropriate treatment for the early-stage hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) when surgical resection or radiofrequency ablation is not feasible or has failed. For intermediate-stage HCC, TACE is recommended in cases with well-defined nodules and feasible selective arterial access. [1, 2]. Previous studies have demonstrated that achieving a complete response (CR) after TACE is associated with significantly prolonged overall survival compared to non-CR outcomes. Additionally, TACE-induced tumor hypoxia can alter the tumor microenvironment and trigger pro-angiogenic processes, potentially promoting tumor progression. Therefore, there is an increasing emphasis on achieving CR with TACE to improve long-term outcomes in HCC [3–6].

The development of techniques to achieve CR is critical for optimizing the outcomes of TACE. A recent randomized comparative study (JIVROSG-1302) evaluated selective conventional TACE (cTACE) versus selective drug-eluting bead TACE (DEB-TACE). The results demonstrated that cTACE achieved a higher CR rate than DEB-TACE at both 3 months (75.2% versus 27.6%, primary endpoint) and 1 month (84.2% versus 35.7%, secondary endpoint) [7]. These findings suggest that Lipiodol-based cTACE may be preferable to DEB-TACE when superselective catheterization is achievable.

The technique mixing ethiodized oil with a solution of anticancer drugs using a three-way stopcock has been widely used for over 30 years [8]. In 2016, a technical recommendation for cTACE outlined key guidelines for creating a water-in-oil (W/O) emulsion [9]. It is well established that W/O emulsions offer several advantages over oil-in-water (O/W) emulsions, including a higher embolic effect, greater drug-carrying capacity, and extended drug release time [10]. However, the traditional pumping technique using a three-way stopcock has a significant limitation: the proportion of a W/O emulsion is usually limited to a maximum of 70% [11].

To improve the properties of ethiodized oil emulsions, a pumping emulsification device with a microporous glass membrane (MicroMagic; Piolax Medical Devices, Yokohama, Japan) was developed [12]. Ex vivo and in vivo studies demonstrated significant pharmacokinetic advantages (e.g., slower systemic drug release and higher intratumoral drug retention) and higher tumor necrosis ratio by using this device [13, 14]. These findings provided the rationale to investigate whether using the glass membrane device could enhance cTACE efficacy in patients. Notably, the cTACE arm of the JIVROSG-1302 (PRESIDENT) study employed the conventional stopcock method [7]. We therefore designed a multicenter phase II trial of selective cTACE using the emulsification device (COMPARE study), following a protocol identical to JIVROSG-1302, in order to compare CR rates against a historical control.

Materials and Methods

Study Design

This study was a multicenter, prospective, single-arm phase II trial (non-randomized). It was approved by the Nara Medical University Hospital Clinical Research Review Board (approval No. 5200002) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the Japanese Clinical Trials Act. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients. The trial was registered in the Japan Registry of Clinical Trials (jRCTs052200095). The study investigated superselective cTACE using a W/O Lipiodol-epirubicin emulsion prepared with a glass membrane emulsification device in patients with unresectable HCC. The primary endpoint was the CR rate at 12 weeks (3 months) post-TACE, assessed by an independent review committee (IRC) according to modified RECIST (mRECIST) criteria. Secondary endpoints included the CR rate at 4 weeks (1 month) post-TACE (also by IRC per mRECIST) and the frequency and severity of adverse events (AEs). As prespecified in the statistical analysis plan, comparisons of the primary and secondary endpoints with those from the historical cTACE arm of the JIVROSG-1302 trial [7] were planned as exploratory analyses for contextual interpretation.

Patient Eligibility

The patient inclusion criteria were as follows: histologically or clinically diagnosed HCC; who were not suitable candidates for surgical resection, liver transplantation, or local ablation therapy; with a hypervascular tumor(s) showing early-phase enhancement on contrast-enhanced CT or MRI; who were identified as suitable candidates for selective TACE; with no previous history of treatment for the HCC nodules for which TACE was planned; in whom the HCC nodules for which TACE was planned were measurable (maximum diameter 10 mm or greater); with a maximum diameter of the HCC nodules for which TACE was planned equal to or smaller than 5 cm; with ECOG PS 0-1; with Child-Pugh class of A or B; with adequate organ functions (serum total bilirubin ≤3.0 mg/dL, serum aspartate aminotransferase [AST]/alanine aminotransferase [ALT] within 5 times the upper limit of normal range or ≤250 IU/L); who were aged 20 years or over; who were willing to provide written informed consent. Clinical diagnosis of HCC was based on visualization of a liver tumor measuring 10 mm or greater in diameter on CT, with contrast-enhanced CT or MRI showing tumor staining in the early phase after contrast injection and washout in the later phase. The suitability of a patient for surgical resection, liver transplantation, or local ablation therapy was determined at the discretion of the tumor board conference at each institution. In this trial, patients who had a prior history of TACE were also eligible, if the target nodules were not the ones that had been treated by prior TACE. The main exclusion criteria were as follows: presence of tumor thrombosis in the portal vein; presence of extrahepatic metastasis; rupture of the HCC nodules for which TACE was planned; presence of extrahepatic feeding arteries for the HCC nodules for which TACE was planned; prior surgical reconstruction or endoscopic treatment of the biliary tract; clinically significant refractory ascites or pleural effusion; severe arterio-portal or arterio-venous shunts in the liver; allergy to contrast medium that precludes angiography; previously enrolled patients in this trial; severe active co-morbidities; pregnant and nursing women and women of childbearing potential; sexually active men, who were not willing to or able to use medically acceptable forms of contraception; patients judged as not being eligible for participation in this study by the investigators because of safety issues. The inclusion and exclusion criteria were set to be consistent with those of the JIVROSG-1302 study [7].

Preparation of Lipiodol-Drug Emulsion

The emulsification device consists of a disk-shaped microporous glass membrane (approximately 100 μm pore size with a maze-like architecture) encased between two syringe adaptors. The membrane material (primarily Al2O3-SiO2) is derived from volcanic ash (shirasu) in southern Kyushu, Japan. The glass membrane, which has a hydrophobic coating and a three-dimensional maze-like pore structure, allows Lipiodol to function as a continuous phase and the epirubicin solution as a dispersed phase when pumped across the membrane. Consequently, a stable water-in-oil (W/O) emulsion can be created [12]. For each treatment, epirubicin hydrochloride powder (20–60 mg; Sawai Pharmaceutical Co., Osaka, Japan) was dissolved in a mixture of nonionic contrast medium (iopamidol 300 mg I/mL) and saline (volume ratio 4:1). The volume of epirubicin solution was determined based on the planned Lipiodol volume using a 1:2 ratio (aqueous solution: Lipiodol). The Lipiodol volume itself was chosen according to tumor size (e.g., a 3 cm tumor warranted ∼4 mL of Lipiodol, thus 2 mL of epirubicin solution). The Lipiodol and epirubicin solution were then emulsified by 20 pump exchanges through the glass membrane device (Fig. 1).

Glass membrane emulsification device. Two syringes containing epirubicin solution and Lipiodol (1:2 ratio) are connected to the adaptors. A disk-shaped glass membrane was placed between the syringe adaptors.

cTACE Procedure

Angiographic TACE was performed in a superselective manner for each target lesion. A microcatheter with a 1.5–2.0 Fr tip was navigated into each tumor-feeding arterial branch under fluoroscopic guidance. The most frequently used microcatheter products were as follows: 1.5-F Veloute Ultra (Asahi Intecc, Seto, Japan), 1.8-F Carnelian Pixie (Tokai Medical, Gifu, Japan), and 2.0-F Estream (Toray Industries, Tokyo, Japan). The microguidewires used were 0.014- or 0.016-inch Meister guidewires (Asahi Intecc, Seto, Japan). In cases of tumors supplied by multiple arterial feeders, all contributory branches were individually catheterized. Patients with multiple HCC lesions underwent separate superselective TACE for each lesion during the same session. The prepared Lipiodol-epirubicin emulsion was slowly injected through the microcatheter into the tumor-feeding artery until stasis, followed by administration of porous gelatin sponge particles 1 mm in size (Gelpart®, Nippon Kayaku Co., Tokyo, Japan) to embolize the vessel. Cone-beam CT or angio-CT was used, when available, to evaluate tumor-feeding branches and to confirm Lipiodol distribution before or after TACE.

After the procedure, contrast-enhanced CT or MRI was performed at 1 month to evaluate tumor response. If an imaging CR was achieved at 1 month, an additional follow-up scan at 3 months was obtained without any interim anti-cancer therapy. If CR was not achieved at 1 month, patients could receive additional treatment for residual disease as clinically indicated after the 1-month assessment. Imaging was performed at each participating institution according to local standard clinical protocols, with predefined minimum requirements specified in the study protocol. For contrast-enhanced CT, a slice thickness of ≤5 mm and dynamic imaging including non-contrast, arterial, and portal venous phases were required. Gadoxetic acid-enhanced MRI was permitted as an alternative imaging modality.

Assessments

All imaging data were submitted in DICOM format and centrally reviewed by an independent review committee (IRC) consisting of three board-certified radiologists from institutions not participating in the trial. All reviewers were blinded to clinical information. Tumor response was assessed according to mRECIST criteria, and the IRC’s assessment constituted the primary efficacy analysis. Complete agreement among the three reviewers was observed for response assessment at both the 1-month and 3-month evaluations for all cases.

AEs were monitored throughout the study period. AEs were graded according to the National Cancer Institute’s Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0. The incidence, severity (grade), and relationship of AEs to the procedure were summarized. Serious AEs, any deaths, and other significant AEs were specifically recorded and analyzed. Liver function was evaluated at baseline and 3 months using Child-Pugh score and modified ALBI (mALBI) grade. Changes in liver function were noted, including the number of patients whose Child-Pugh class or ALBI grade worsened from baseline.

Statistical Analysis

Efficacy analyses were performed on the full analysis set (FAS), defined as all patients who underwent the protocol treatment and had at least one post-TACE imaging evaluation. Safety analyses were performed on the safety set (SS), which included all patients who received the study treatment. The CR rates at 3 months and 1 month were calculated with two-sided exact 95% confidence intervals (CIs) using the Clopper-Pearson method. Given the non-randomized, single-arm design, all statistical comparisons with the historical control were prespecified as exploratory analyses intended for hypothesis generation rather than confirmatory testing. No formal adjustment for multiplicity was applied, and all statistical analyses were prespecified as exploratory in nature. To assess whether the 3-month CR rate with the emulsification device exceeded that of the historical control (75.2% at 3 months in the cTACE arm of the JIVROSG-1302/PRESIDENT study), a one-sample exact binomial test was used. Similarly, the 1-month CR rate was compared against the control rate of 84.2% at 1 month using the binomial test. All p values were two tailed, with p < 0.05 considered significant.

In addition, a sensitivity analysis was performed to assess the robustness of the primary endpoint. Patients excluded from the FAS were conservatively considered as non-CR, and CR rates were recalculated using the same statistical methods as in the primary analysis. The results of this sensitivity analysis were interpreted as supportive evidence for the primary findings.

For safety, the incidence of each AE was summarized by grade and percentage. Serious AEs, deaths, and other clinically significant AEs were tabulated separately, focusing on grade ≥3 events. Differences in the rates of serious AEs between this study and the historical control were descriptively compared. Detailed comparisons with the historical control are presented in the online supplementary material (for all online suppl. material, see https://doi.org/10.1159/000552424). Changes in Child-Pugh class and mALBI grade from baseline to 12 weeks were cross-tabulated. All analyses were performed using SAS software (v9.4, SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA).

Results

Patient Characteristics

A total of 50 patients were enrolled from four Japanese institutions between January 2021 and June 2023. Of these, 2 patients did not undergo TACE because of renal dysfunction (n = 1) or absence of a hypervascular tumor (n = 1). The remaining 48 patients received the protocol cTACE and constituted the safety population (SS). An additional 3 patients were excluded from the efficacy analysis due to protocol-defined nonevaluability: one received cisplatin-based hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy before the protocol-defined 1-month efficacy assessment, and two had no post-TACE imaging evaluation. Consequently, the FAS comprised 45 patients. The reasons for selecting TACE as the treatment modality were as follows: repeated prior recurrence (n = 18), tumor location and/or size unsuitable for curative treatment, i.e., ablation of surgery (n = 15), patient preference (n = 5), presence of ascites (n = 3), multiple nodules (n = 2), and other reasons (n = 2). CR Rate

Complete agreement among the three reviewers was observed for response assessment at both the 1-month and 3-month evaluations for all cases. The primary endpoint, CR rate at 3 months post-TACE, was 97.8% (44 of 45 patients; 95% CI 88.2–99.9%). The secondary endpoint, CR rate at 1 month, was also 97.8%. The patient who showed partial response at 1 month did not receive any additional locoregional treatment before the 3-month evaluation. Both the 3-month and 1-month CR rates observed with the emulsification device were higher than those reported in the historical control using 3-way stopcock (JIVROSG-1302/PRESIDENT study), which were 75.2% at 3 months and 84.2% at 1 month. The differences were statistically significant (p < 0.001 for 3 months; p = 0.008 for 1 month, exact binomial test). Figure 3 illustrates the CR rates at 1 and 3 months in the current study. A detailed comparison with the historical control is shown in online supplementary Figure S1.

Complete response (CR) rates at 1 and 3 months. The CR rates at both 1 month and 3 months were 97.8% (44/45 patients; 95% CI 88.2–99.9%). A detailed comparison with the historical control is provided in online supplementary Figure S1.

As a sensitivity analysis, the three patients excluded from the FAS were conservatively treated as non-CR, and the CR rates were recalculated using the same statistical methods as in the primary analysis. The CR rates at both 3 months and 1 month were 91.7% (44 of 48 patients; 95% CI 80.0–97.7%). Compared with the historical control, the CR rate remained significantly higher at 3 months (p = 0.007), whereas it was not significantly higher at 1 month (p = 0.211).

Notably, liver enzyme elevations were the most frequent laboratory AEs. Grade 3 elevations in AST occurred in 21 patients (43.8%) and grade 3 ALT elevations in 14 patients (29.2%). Discussion

Previous nonclinical studies have demonstrated that W/O emulsions offer several advantages over O/W emulsions, including stronger embolic effects, higher drug-loading capacity, and longer drug-release times. A uniform and small droplet size also confers improved stability and viscosity, resulting in prolonged retention within the tumor [10, 15]. The newly developed glass membrane emulsification device was shown to generate predominantly W/O emulsions (approximately 98% W/O fraction) with uniform droplet size, as demonstrated by microscopic examination (Fig. 4) and macroscopic drop-test findings (Fig. 5). In line with these theoretical advantages, a retrospective clinical study by Ishikawa et al. [16] reported significantly improved local tumor control when using the glass membrane emulsification device for cTACE. In that study, patients treated with the device had markedly lower rates of local tumor recurrence compared to those treated with emulsions mixed by the standard 3-way stopcock technique. The present study is the first prospective clinical trial to evaluate the emulsification device in cTACE. Although this was a single-arm study rather than a randomized controlled trial, the high CR rate of 97.8% at 3 months represents a notable short-term outcome.

Each emulsion was dropped into saline under static conditions. a The emulsion prepared using the conventional three-way stopcock method showed rapid dispersion of the epirubicin solution into the saline immediately after dropping, indicating the presence of a substantial oil-in-water (O/W) component. b In contrast, the emulsion prepared using the glass membrane emulsification device remained as oil droplets and sank to the bottom of the container without dispersion, reflecting a predominantly water-in-oil (W/O) structure in which the epirubicin solution was stably encapsulated by Lipiodol.

The present trial was designed as an investigator-initiated phase II study to evaluate feasibility, short-term response, and safety of membrane-assisted cTACE. Because a concurrent control arm was not incorporated, comparisons with the JIVROSG-1302 cohort should be interpreted as contextual reference data rather than confirmatory evidence. Differences in baseline characteristics between cohorts may have influenced observed outcomes. The baseline characteristics of patients in the JIVROSG-1302/PRESIDENT study are summarized in online supplementary Table S2. Definitive assessment of comparative effectiveness requires randomized or appropriately matched studies.

Although the majority of patients were classified as BCLC stage A with solitary tumors, TACE was selected based on multidisciplinary clinical judgment, including factors such as repeated recurrence, unfavorable tumor location for resection or ablation, ascites, or patient preference. Thus, the study cohort reflects real-world treatment decision-making rather than strictly protocol-driven exclusion of curative therapies.

A randomized controlled trial was not undertaken primarily due to practical constraints, particularly cost and infrastructure. In Japan, such specialized clinical trials require resources, including an electronic data capture system and multicenter coordination, which made a randomized design impractical. Accordingly, we used the cTACE arm of the prior JIVROSG-1302 study as a historical control, while acknowledging the inherent and critical limitations of this approach. This strategy was approved by our Institutional Review Board and statistical advisors.

It is noteworthy that the CR rate in our study remained unchanged from 1 month to 3 months (both 97.8%), whereas in the JIVROSG-1302/PRESIDENT study it decreased from 84.2% at 1 month to 75.2% at 3 months. This durability of response may suggest more sustained tumor control, potentially related to enhanced intratumoral Lipiodol retention demonstrated in prior experimental studies [4]. However, this hypothesis requires validation in controlled comparative settings. These findings were further supported by a sensitivity analysis in which patients excluded from the FAS were conservatively considered as non-CR, demonstrating that the higher CR rate at 3 months compared with the historical control was maintained.

It is well recognized that the clinical outcomes of cTACE are determined by multiple technical and operator-dependent factors beyond emulsion properties alone. These include the degree of superselective microcatheter positioning, injection speed, embolization endpoint, feeder coverage, and overall procedural strategy [17]. While the glass membrane emulsification device facilitates the formation of a stable W/O emulsion, its use represents only one component of a complex, multifactorial procedure. Therefore, the favorable response rates observed in the present study cannot be attributed solely to the emulsification device and should be interpreted in the context of comprehensive technical execution of cTACE.

Although CR is an important indicator of technical success in cTACE, short-term radiologic response does not necessarily predict durable local tumor control or long-term survival outcomes. Therefore, the high CR rates observed in this study should be interpreted as evidence of short-term technical efficacy rather than definitive clinical benefit.

The emulsification device has been approved in Japan under the product name MicroMagic. It has also been approved under the name PIOLAX Emulsion Connector in Thailand, Singapore, Vietnam, Hong Kong, Mongolia, and Malaysia, with the approval process currently underway in Korea. The dissemination of this technology may help standardize TACE technique and outcomes by ensuring a more consistent Lipiodol emulsion quality across practitioners. Wider adoption could potentially reduce the variability in TACE results reported between different institutions by providing a reproducible method to achieve optimal emulsions [17].

The high embolic effect of W/O emulsion could potentially increase the risk of AEs. Although grade ≥3 elevations in AST and ALT and worsening of mALBI grade were observed, no patients developed clinically overt hepatic decompensation, such as refractory ascites, hepatic encephalopathy, or liver failure, nor did these changes necessitate prolonged hospitalization or immediate additional interventions. The incidence was not significantly different from that reported with the 3-way stopcock method, although this comparison should be interpreted as reference data only. Nevertheless, attention should be paid to liver function deterioration. In particular, the 17.8% incidence of worsening mALBI grade cannot be considered negligible. Deterioration of liver function may have important downstream clinical consequences. Specifically, worsening liver function could limit eligibility for subsequent systemic therapies, including immune checkpoint inhibitors, at the time of tumor recurrence. Appropriate patient selection is therefore essential when performing cTACE; however, the present study was not designed to identify predictors of liver function deterioration after treatment. Further studies are warranted to clarify which patient or tumor characteristics are associated with post-TACE liver function decline and to optimize treatment strategies that preserve hepatic reserve while maintaining oncologic efficacy.

From a practical standpoint, the glass membrane emulsification device does not alter the cTACE workflow. The device is connected to two syringe adapters containing the epirubicin solution and Lipiodol, and emulsification is achieved through simple pumping, identical to the standard three-way stopcock technique. As such, no additional learning curve was required, and procedure time was comparable to that of cTACE in the present study.

The main additional consideration is cost. The device is commercially available in Japan at approximately JPY 9,000 per procedure. Although the improved CR rate observed in this study suggests a potential cost-benefit advantage, formal cost-effectiveness analyses were not performed. Further studies evaluating economic impact, as well as accumulation of additional clinical evidence beyond the present trial, will be important to support broader clinical adoption.

Despite these encouraging findings, several important limitations should be considered when interpreting the results. First, as a non-randomized, single-arm phase II trial using a historical control, the analysis is exploratory and subject to selection bias, confounding by indication, and limitations inherent to historical comparisons; therefore, the findings do not establish causal or device-specific superiority. The sample size was determined pragmatically based on feasibility, and no formal power calculation was prespecified. In addition, exclusion of nonevaluable patients and the absence of sensitivity analyses may have affected estimation of CR rates.

Second, although this was a multicenter study, participating institutions were geographically clustered and highly experienced in superselective TACE, and the cohort included a high proportion of solitary tumors, which may limit generalizability and comparability with historical data. Third, efficacy assessment focused on short-term CR at 1 and 3 months, and long-term outcomes such as progression-free survival, overall survival, and recurrence patterns, were not evaluated; therefore, the long-term clinical benefit of membrane-assisted cTACE remains uncertain. Fourth, no direct comparison was made with contemporary combination strategies, such as TACE plus molecular targeted agents or immune checkpoint inhibitors [18–20]. Larger randomized studies with longer follow-up are warranted to confirm these findings.

Conclusion

This exploratory phase II study showed that selective cTACE using a W/O emulsion prepared with a glass membrane pumping emulsification device was associated with high short-term CR rates in patients with HCC and an acceptable safety profile. These findings should be interpreted as hypothesis generating. Further validation in randomized, geographically diverse multicenter studies with standardized procedures and long-term clinical endpoints is required before definitive conclusions regarding device-specific benefit can be drawn.

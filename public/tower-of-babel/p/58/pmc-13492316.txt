Introduction

This case report describes a 60-year-old woman with symptomatic paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (AF) who underwent catheter ablation using a novel pulsed field ablation (PFA) system. During the procedure, an uncommon anatomic variant—a persistent left superior vena cava (PLSVC) with absence of the right superior vena cava (SVC)—was identified. In addition to standard pulmonary vein isolation (PVI), successful ablation of the PLSVC was performed using the Abbott Volt™ PFA catheter, achieving complete electrical isolation without complications. This case highlights both the arrhythmogenic role of the PLSVC and the feasibility, safety, and effectiveness of using PFA technology for its treatment.

Case presentation

We present the case of a 60-year-old female patient who was hospitalized owing to severely symptomatic paroxysmal AF with spontaneous return to sinus rhythm shortly after admission. Anticoagulation (Xa inhibitor) was started in the presence of a CHA2DS2-VA score of 2 (diabetes mellitus type 2, arterial hypertension). Transthoracic echocardiography showed no remarkable findings.

We subsequently planned AF ablation constituting solely of PVI using PFA technology with the Abbott Volt PFA catheter. The Volt PFA catheter is a novel balloon-filled basket with 8 active splines capable of pacing, acquiring electrical and anatomic data, and delivering therapy.

A markedly dilated coronary sinus (CS) was suspected after cannulation with a decapolar catheter, based on excessive catheter mobility, with the catheter easily advanced beyond the cardiac silhouette, further strengthening our suspicion.

The J-tip wire of the SL0-sheath (Abbott) was brought to the right atrium but would not advance further into the SVC. A PLSVC with atresia of the right SVC was suspected. The former was confirmed after contrast injection into the CS, which was significantly dilated (Figure 1A), and after the procedure with a contrast computed tomography scan (Figure 1C).

The fully inflated Volt pulsed field ablation catheter makes contact with the wall of the vessel (delineated with white squares). C: Coronal view of a chest computed tomography scan after the procedure confirmed the presence of a persistent left superior vena cava (indicated with red arrows).

Next, transseptal puncture and ablation of the 4 pulmonary veins were performed without complications. After confirmation of isolation of the 4 veins, a 0.035-in wire (which accompanies the Volt PFA catheter) was advanced into the PLSVC. The Volt PFA catheter was positioned in the PLSVC and gently maneuvered until adequate (impedance-based) wall contact was obtained (Figures 1B and 2A). Contact was present on all 8 splines (Figure 2A), indicated by blue and white spheres and supported by the LivePoint™ display.

4 applications were performed in the PLSVC, and subsequently 2 more PFA applications were performed slightly more proximally to consolidate and extend the PLSVC isolation. The initially sharp electrograms (EGMs) in the PLSVC were completely and immediately eliminated, indicating adequate ablation of electrical tissue (Figure 2C). Electroanatomic mapping confirmed isolation in the PLSVC (Figure 2B). Pacing at maximum output confirmed capture of the left phrenic nerve before and after ablation. No complications occurred.

Discussion

The left SVC is the embryologic precursor of the ligament of Marshall and has a prevalence of up to 0.7% in patients undergoing catheter ablation for AF.1 The PLSVC runs between the left atrial appendage and the left superior pulmonary vein and usually drains into the right atrium through an enlarged CS, although drainage into the left atrium can occur. Most cases involve bilateral SVCs, whereas isolated PLSVC with the absence of the right SVC is rather rare.2

The left SVC is a known arrhythmogenic source for AF. Ectopic activity from the PLSVC, which can act as either a trigger or a driver of AF, has been demonstrated. Consequently, ablation of the PLSVC is often required to achieve a durable sinus rhythm.1,3

The Abbott Volt PFA catheter is a highly versatile balloon-in-basket PFA catheter featuring 8 splines arranged circumferentially around the balloon. Each spline is capable of both pulsed field energy delivery and high-quality EGM recording. Integrated with Abbott’s EnSite X EP system, the catheter provides real-time visualization combining EGM amplitude, electrical impedance, and individual spline position. This enables detailed, spline-specific contact feedback, allowing the operator to assess and optimize tissue contact on a per-spline basis. Importantly, splines can be selectively deselected, enabling tailored energy delivery to accommodate variable pulmonary vein anatomy and avoid suboptimal contact areas.

Ablation of the PLSVC has traditionally been performed with thermal energy, which is accompanied by significant risks such as tamponade and phrenic nerve injury.1

There is currently no definitive head-to-head evidence proving that the Volt PFA catheter is safer than radiofrequency ablation, although existing PFA data and early Volt studies strongly suggest a favorable safety profile to avoid phrenic nerve injury when performing PVI.4, 5, 6 It seems reasonable to assume that safety is similar when targeting the region around the left phrenic nerve during PLSVC ablation.

The VOLT CE Mark Study has shown favorable results with this catheter on effectiveness after 6 months with freedom from arrhythmia observed in 88.2% of patients with paroxysmal AF and 76.7% in persistent AF.4 This was also shown in the VOLT-AF IDE study in which 12-month freedom from documented recurrence was 84.2% in paroxysmal AF and 67.8% in persistent AF, and the device- or procedure-related serious adverse events rate was 1.9%.5,6 Of note, no clinically relevant hemolysis nor phrenic nerve injury was observed with the Volt PFA catheter.

Several reports on successful ablation of the PLSVC using pulsed field energy have recently been published. To the best of our knowledge, there are no published articles or case reports specifically documenting ablation of a PLSVC using the Abbott Volt PFA catheter. Our case highlights the feasibility and effectiveness of ablation of the PLSVC with the Abbott Volt PFA catheter.

Comment on “Fulminant Hepatic Failure Following Intentional Iron Overdose—A Case Report”
Authors: Hinpetch Daungsupawong, Viroj Wiwanitkit
Published: 2025

Summary

Intentional iron overdose in young adults requires not only rapid medical management but also thorough psychiatric assessment and psychological support to prevent recurrence.

Dear Editor,

The publication on “Fulminant Hepatic Failure Following Intentional Iron Overdose—A Case Report [1]” is interesting. This case report presents a rare case of intentional iron overdose in an adult, indicating the severity of delayed iron toxicity. However, the report has several important weaknesses that should be criticized for future developments, in terms of clinical interpretation, history collection, and treatment recommendations. There are also interesting issues that have not been mentioned and should be further explored to increase the academic value of the case study. A noteworthy drawback is the lack of in‐depth examination of the psychosocial aspects that contribute to purposeful iron overload. The patient was just 21 years old, which is a risk age for mental health problems, yet the report made no mention of his psychiatric history, suicide risk assessment, or comprehensive psychological therapy, all of which are critical considerations in cases of intentional drug poisoning. Omitting this factor may result in insufficient care and missed opportunities to prevent future disasters.

A notable weakness is the lack of in‐depth analysis of the psychosocial factors leading to intentional iron overdose. The patient was only 21 years old, an age at risk for mental health problems, but the report did not mention the psychiatric history, suicide risk assessment, or systematic psychological counseling, which are important considerations in cases of intentional drug poisoning. Omission of this element may result in incomplete care and missed opportunities to prevent future events.

Furthermore, the paper lacks examination of resource management and treatment costs, particularly in circumstances when advanced therapies like extracorporeal liver support or continuous dialysis are unavailable in resource‐constrained facilities. This issue should be addressed in order to compare it to relevant approaches in diverse health system contexts, making the study more complete and accessible to a wider audience.

In terms of bringing uniqueness, the report might be expanded to include oxidative stress markers and iron mitochondrial bioassays, which were not addressed in this case study. A comparison case series could potentially be constructed between patients treated during and after the “therapeutic window” to better understand the effect of time on prognosis. Integrating early diagnostic tools, such as point‐of‐care iron testing, may assist in reducing diagnostic delays in cases of undisclosed drug histories and should be studied more in the future.

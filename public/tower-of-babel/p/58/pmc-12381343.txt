Fibroblast activation protein expression in the tumor microenvironment is crucial in survival prediction and differentiation of recurrent gliomas: a head-to-head comparison of 68Ga-FAPI-04 and 18F-FET in PET/CT imaging
Authors: Tao Hua, Qi Huang, Zhirui Zhou, Weiyan Zhou, Jianbo Wen, Fang Xie, Ming Li, Yihui Guan, Dongxiao Zhuang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe precise differentiation of recurrent glioma from treatment-related changes, such as pseudoprogression or radiation necrosis, is essential for treatment planning and remains a significant challenge. Fibroblast activation protein (FAP) expressed by cancer-associated fibroblasts can be targeted with PET tracers for in vivo visualization and quantification. This study aims to evaluate the diagnostic and prognostic effectiveness of FAP expression in patients with potential recurrent glioma by directly comparing [gallium-68] FAP inhibitor-04 and [fluorine-18] fluoroethyl-L-tyrosine PET/CT imaging. Thirty glioma patients showing signs of possible recurrence during routine MRI follow-up after treatment were enrolled. PET-based semiquantitative parameters, clinical factors, and survival data were collected for analysis.ResultsPaired comparison of SUVmax, TBRmax, MTV, and TLU originating from two PET imaging studies indicated significant differences in TBRmax, MTV, and TLU, with P values of 0.000, 0.001, and 0.000, respectively. Univariate logistic regression analysis revealed a marginally non-significant difference in efficacy (P = 0.053) of the initial pathological diagnosis. In multivariate logistic regression analysis, PET parameters, initial pathological data, age, and gender were used to develop the predictive models step by step. Although trends towards significance were observed in the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio, no PET parameters reached statistical significance. The MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio improved the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC). When PET parameters and initial pathological diagnosis were included, the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio significantly enhanced the model’s AUC (P = 0.040) from 0.709 (0.465–0.953, 95% CI) to 0.847 (0.688-1.000, 95% CI). When replacing the initial diagnosis with initial WHO grade, the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio improved the AUC (P = 0.016) from 0.640 (0.400–0.880, 95% CI) to 0.852 (0.715–0.988, 95% CI). After factoring in age and gender in addition to the initial pathological diagnosis, the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio enhanced the AUC (P = 0.039) from 0.841 (0.677-1.000, 95% CI) to 0.963 (0.887-1.000, 95% CI). Similarly, after replacing the initial pathological diagnosis with the initial WHO grade, the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio significantly enhanced the AUC of the model (P = 0.046) from 0.762 (0.532–0.992, 95% CI) to 0.942 (0.850-1.000, 95% CI). The survival analysis revealed that the MTV-FAPI of the lesion has a significant impact on overall survival (P = 0.027, hazard ratio = 1.103, 95% CI: 1.011–1.204).ConclusionsThis head-to-head exploratory study showed that glioma FAP expression volume is an independent risk factor that can significantly influence overall survival in patients with recurrent glioma. Although statistically insignificant, the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio, which represents the FAP expression volume percentage of the post-treatment glioma tissue, suggests trends toward significant differentiation between glioma recurrence and treatment-related changes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s41181-025-00378-z.Keywords: Glioma recurrence, Fibroblast activation protein, Positron emission tomography, Fibroblast activation protein inhibitor, Differentiation

Introduction

Gliomas are the most common malignant tumors of the central nervous system. Standard treatment strategies, including maximum surgical resection followed by radiotherapy and chemotherapy, have been used for decades. However, the significant tumor heterogeneity of gliomas often results in inevitable tumor recurrence in most cases (Kim et al. 2015; Mahlokozera et al. 2018). Accurate diagnosis of glioma recurrence is crucial for developing personalized treatment strategies that improve survival and minimize unnecessary interventions. Unfortunately, challenges remain due to the similar imaging features of tumor recurrence and treatment-related changes such as pseudoprogression or radiation necrosis (Qi et al. 2023).

Radio-labelled amino acid tracers, such as [fluorine-18] fluoroethyl-L-tyrosine (18F-FET), can target overexpressed L-type amino acid transporters in glioma cells and assist in differential diagnosis, prognosis, treatment planning, and monitoring treatment effects (Poetsch et al. 2018; Katsanos et al. 2019; Paprottka et al. 2021; Zhou et al. 2024). With the effective diagnostic performance of both non-contrast and contrast-enhanced gliomas, the Response Assessment in Neuro-Oncology (RANO) working group recommends amino acid tracer PET imaging as a valuable complement to magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) in all stages of glioma management (Law et al. 2019; Albert et al. 2024). Despite the satisfactory performance of radio-labelled amino acid tracers, inflammation in tissues around the treatment area may also exhibit abnormal amino acid tracer uptake, which can create uncertainty in diagnosing recurrent glioma.

Benign cells within the tumor microenvironment (TME) contribute to the complexity of glioma heterogeneity. The interactions between glioma cells and neighboring cells in the tumor stroma can promote tumor proliferation, migration, angiogenesis, recurrence, and survival through various mechanisms (Greenwald et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2024). Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), components of the TME, actively participate in the communication between tumor cells and stromal cells by secreting growth factors and inflammatory cytokines. CAFs may overexpress a transmembrane glycoprotein called fibroblast activation protein (FAP) (Liu et al. 2019; Chen et al. 2021). Radio-labelled FAP inhibitors (FAPI) have demonstrated effectiveness in imaging FAP overexpression across various solid tumors, yielding promising results (Backhaus et al. 2022; Spektor et al. 2024; Mona et al. 2022). Because FAP accumulates significantly in the stroma of malignant tumors and provides good tissue contrast, FAP-targeted imaging is practical in malignant tumor detection, tumor delineation, and radiotherapy planning (Sollini et al. 2021; Kratochwil et al. 2019; Loktev et al. 2018; Röhrich et al. 2019). Our previous studies explored the overexpression patterns of FAP in an untreated intracranial tumor cohort, including various glioma subtypes, medulloblastoma, and brain metastases. The findings indicated that more malignant intracranial tumors—such as brain metastases, glioblastoma, and medulloblastoma—tend to have significantly higher FAP overexpression (Hua et al. 2024). Additionally, the ratio of MTVFAPI to MTVFET (MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio), an index that describes the interaction between glioma and cancer-associated fibroblasts within the tumor microenvironment, shows promise in improving the differentiation among untreated intracranial tumors. Based on these findings, the potential of FAP overexpression to aid in distinguishing between glioma recurrence and pseudoprogression or radiation necrosis, as well as to predict survival, is of clinical significance.

This prospective, head-to-head study utilized [gallium-68] FAP inhibitor-04 (68Ga-FAPI-04) and 18F-FET PET/CT imaging in post-treatment glioma patients who exhibited signs of tumor recurrence during regular MRI follow-up to assess the efficacy of 68Ga-FAPI-04. A quantitative analysis of PET-based semiquantitative parameters and key clinical factors was conducted to investigate the impact of FAP overexpression status on the recurrence and survival of patients with recurrent gliomas.

Methods

Study design

Structural MRI follow-up and consultations with neurosurgical specialists were routinely conducted in the outpatient department at Huashan Hospital, Fudan University, for post-treatment glioma patients. Patients were enrolled for further evaluation until signs of recurrence were suspected. These post-treatment glioma patients underwent 68Ga-FAPI-04 and 18F-FET PET/CT brain imaging from October 2022 to May 2024 at the Nuclear Medicine & PET Center of Huashan Hospital, Fudan University. PET-based semi-quantitative imaging parameters and clinical data were collected to assess this head-to-head study. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients.

PET/CT imaging protocols

68Ga-FAPI-04 and 18F-FET tracers were synthesized in the Department of Nuclear Medicine & PET Center of Huashan Hospital, Fudan University. The PET scans, using two radio-labelled tracers, were applied with at least a 24-hour interval for each patient in our cohort.

For 18F-FET PET/CT imaging, patients were required to fast for at least 4 h before imaging. A 20-minute static scan was conducted in 3-dimensional mode with a Biograph mCT Flow Edge 128 PET/CT system (Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany) 20 min after intravenous bolus injection of 18F-FET (182 ± 17.5 MBq). Attenuation correction was performed using low-dose CT (tube current = 150 mAs, voltage = 120 kV, acquisition = 64 × 0.6 mm, convolution kernel = H30s, slice thickness = 5 mm, interslice gap = 1.5 mm) before the emission scan. Post-acquisition, PET images were reconstructed using the ordered subset expectation maximization (OSEM) algorithm with a Gaussian filter and a full width at half maximum of 3.5 mm at the center of the field of view.

In 68Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT imaging, 30 min after intravenous bolus injection of 68Ga-FAPI-04 (176 ± 19.2 MBq), a 30-minute static scan was conducted in 3D mode with a uMI510 PET/CT (United Imaging, Shanghai, China). Attenuation correction was similarly performed using low-dose CT before the emission scan. PET images were also reconstructed using the OSEM algorithm with a Gaussian filter and the same full width at half maximum after acquisition.

PET/CT image analysis

PET/CT images were analyzed using a syngo.via workstation (Siemens Healthineers). Two experienced nuclear medicine physicians (WXZ and TH, with over 6 and 13 years of experience, respectively) performed blinded evaluations and delineations of 68Ga-FAPI-04 and 18F-FET PET/CT imaging before treatment.

Structural MRI was initially reviewed for lesion location before PET/CT lesion delineation. For 18F-FET PET/CT imaging, the mean standardized uptake value (SUVmean) of the brain background was measured in a crescent-shaped area, encompassing gray and white matter on the lesion’s contralateral hemisphere (Unterrainer et al. 2017). Subsequently, a 1.6 times background SUVmean was used for lesion delineation, and the maximal standardized uptake value (SUVmax), metabolic tumor volume (MTV), and total lesion tracer uptake (TLU) were obtained. The maximal tumor-to-brain ratio (TBRmax) was calculated by dividing the intracranial lesion SUVmax with the background SUVmean.

For 68Ga-FAPI-04 imaging, the background SUVmean was measured similarly to that of 18F-FET PET. Lesion SUVmax and TBRmax were measured and calculated. Due to the absence of guidelines for delineating FAPI-positive lesions and based on our previous investigation’s experience, we used a 20% isocontour volumetric threshold of lesion SUVmax to delineate and measure lesion MTV-FAPI and TLU-FAPI (Hua et al. 2024).

For the quantification of FAP expression in the glioma parenchyma, a series of semi-quantitative parameters, including MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio and TLUFAPI:TLUFET ratio, were obtained for further analysis.

Due to concerns that too small lesions might not fully describe the interactions between FAP expressed in glioma TME, patients with MTV-FET less than 1 cm³ were excluded from the analysis.

Diagnosis protocol

For patients who underwent the following surgical treatments after PET imaging, including surgical resection or stereotactic surgical biopsy, in the Neurosurgery department of Huashan Hospital, Fudan University, histopathological diagnosis results were obtained through our facility’s medical record system.

For patients who received treatment strategies after PET imaging, including radiosurgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, or other therapies, expert specialists’ consultation opinions were collected as authorized diagnoses.

Regular follow-ups were conducted, and the overall survival data for this patient cohort were recorded.

Statistical analyses

Descriptive statistics are expressed as the mean and standard deviation or median and range. The t-test and one-way analysis of variance were used to compare continuous variables. The Wilcoxon signed-rank or Kruskal-Wallis test was performed when a normal distribution of variables was not met. Linear regression analysis was used to investigate the relationship between pathological diagnosis and PET parameters. The variance inflation factor was used to control multicollinearity. Logistic regression was used to explore the diagnostic efficacy of the demographic and PET parameters. The area under the receiver operating curve (AUC) was used to observe the diagnostic efficacy. Cox regression and the Kaplan-Meier method were used to examine the relationship between overall survival (OS) and the relevant parameters. Intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) for PET parameter measurements were assessed, and the results were classified as poor (less than 0.2), fair (0.21–0.4), moderate (0.41–0.6), good (0.61–0.8), and very good (0.8–1.0).

All statistical analyses were performed with Stata version 17 (College Station, TX, USA). In all studies, P < 0.05 indicated a statistically significant difference.

Results

Patient cohort characteristics

Thirty adult post-treatment glioma patients were enrolled in the research, comprising 20 males and 10 females. The cohort’s median age was 51 years (range 17–67 years). The illustrative workflow diagram of the inclusion and exclusion criteria is shown in Fig. 1. The initial diagnosis of this patient cohort included juvenile low-grade glioma, oligodendroglioma, astrocytoma, anaplastic oligodendroglioma, anaplastic astrocytoma, glioblastoma multiforme, and diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma. There were 1, 3, 5, and 21 patients from grades 1 through 4 in the WHO grade stratification. The follow-up results showed that 21 patients were diagnosed with glioma recurrence, and 9 patients were diagnosed with treatment-related changes, including radiation necrosis and pseudoprogression. PET-based lesions semiquantitative parameters comparison of 68Ga-FAPI-04 and 18F-FET imaging

ICCs showed very good agreement between the different lesions for 18F-FET and 68Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT semiquantitative parameter measurements (ICC > 0.96, P < 0.001; ICC > 0.91, P < 0.001), with the results of reader one (TH) used for analysis.

For 68Ga-FAPI-04 PET images, the median of lesion SUVmax, TBRmax, MTV, and TLU was 2.79 (range 0.77, 13.18), 60.15 (range 15.40, 270.80), 10.32 (range 2.33, 46.02), and 9.70 (range 2.01, 71.38), respectively. The four aforementioned semi-quantitative parameters were compared across different WHO grades; no statistically significant difference was observed.

While for 18F–FET PET imaging, the median of lesion SUVmax, TBRmax, MTV, and TLU was 3.11 (range 1.70, 5.02), 3.51 (range 2.32, 5.88), 20.93 (range 3.27, 226.46), and 37.32 (range 5.53, 362.34), respectively. Similarly, these four parameters were also compared across different WHO grades, with no significant differences observed.

Paired comparison of semiquantitative parameters, including SUVmax, TBRmax, MTV, and TLU, originating from both 68Ga-FAPI-04 and 18F–FET PET imaging was performed. Wilcoxon signed-rank test results indicated significant differences in TBRmax, MTV, and TLU, with P values of 0.000, 0.001, and 0.000, respectively. The difference in SUVmax between the two PET tracers was statistically insignificant, with a P-value of 0.598.

Diagnostic efficacy analysis of PET-based semiquantitative parameters and clinical features

Univariate logistic regression was initially applied to all the semiquantitative parameters and clinical features; only the initial pathological diagnosis showed marginal but insignificant diagnostic efficacy (P = 0.053). The supplementary material includes details of the univariate logistic regression statistical results.

Subsequently, multivariate logistic regression models were created for differential diagnosis. The study involved three steps: first, the effectiveness of PET-based semiquantitative parameters was evaluated; second, the initial pathological diagnosis or initial WHO grade was incorporated into the model; and third, age and gender were added to the differential model.

In the PET-based semiquantitative logistic regression analysis that included TBRmax-FET, TBRmax-FAPI, MTV-FET, and MTV-FAPI, no statistical significance was found for these semiquantitative parameters, and the AUC of the four-parameter model was 0.598 (0.385–0.811, 95% confidence interval, CI). We further explored the efficacy of the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio as the interaction index between the tumor and the TME. After introducing this parameter into the current semiquantitative parameters model, no significant differential efficacy of these parameters could be observed, although the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio showed signs toward significance (P = 0.094). Currently, the AUC of the current model improved to 0.767 (0.593–0.942, 95% CI). The DeLong test results indicated no significant difference between the AUCs of these two models (P = 0.126). Results details are provided in the supplementary material.

Subsequently, the pathological information of the patient cohort was incorporated into the model construction. In addition to TBRmax-FET, TBRmax-FAPI, MTV-FET, and MTV-FAPI, the initial pathological diagnosis was included. The regression results showed that the initial pathological diagnosis has significant diagnostic value, with a P value of 0.033. The AUC of the current model was 0.709 (0.465–0.953, 95% CI). Next, we evaluated the effectiveness of the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio in this diagnostic model. After the inclusion of the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio, the initial pathological diagnosis remained statistically significant, with a P value of 0.045. The AUC for the model at this stage increased to 0.847 (0.689-1.000, 95% CI). The DeLong test results indicated a significant difference between the two models, with a P-value of 0.040. The supplementary material provides detailed multivariate logistic regression results, and Fig. 3A compares the AUC with and without the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio at this stage.

As planned, we included the cohort’s age and gender in our analysis. In the logistic regression model that incorporated TBRmax-FET, TBRmax-FAPI, MTV-FET, MTV-FAPI, initial pathological diagnosis, age, and gender, the results indicated that the initial pathological diagnosis significantly differentiates recurrent glioma within our cohort, with a P value of 0.044. The current AUC for this diagnostic model was 0.841 (0.677-1.000, 95% CI). Similarly, we added the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio to the model. The analysis showed that one parameter had significant effectiveness, while three others exhibited insignificant but trends toward significance in distinguishing recurrent gliomas. These included the initial pathological diagnosis (P = 0.038), gender (P = 0.057), MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio (P = 0.076), and MTV-FAPI (P = 0.093). The AUC for this model was 0.963 (0.887-1.000, 95% CI). The DeLong test revealed a statistically significant difference between the two models, with a P-value of 0.039. Detailed results are provided in the supplementary material, and the comparison of AUCs with and without the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio is shown in Fig. 3B.

Due to discrepancies between the pathological diagnosis and the WHO grade, we replaced the initial pathological diagnosis with the initial WHO grade for our analysis. In the diagnostic model that included TBRmax-FET, TBRmax-FAPI, MTV-FET, MTV-FAPI, and the initial WHO grade, we found no statistically significant results, with the model’s AUC at 0.640 (0.400–0.880, 95% CI). We then added the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio to the model. The regression results showed that two parameters were insignificant but tended toward significance: the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio (P = 0.081) and the initial WHO grade (P = 0.086). With this addition, the AUC increased to 0.852 (0.715–0.988, 95% CI). The DeLong test confirmed a significant difference between the two models (P = 0.016). The multivariate logistic regression results are included in the supplementary material, and the AUC comparison with and without the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio is shown in Fig. 3C.

Then we included age and gender in our analysis, along with the initial WHO grade. In the regression model that included TBRmax-FET, TBRmax-FAPI, MTV-FET, MTV-FAPI, initial WHO grade, age, and gender, no statistically significant results were found. The model had an AUC of 0.762 (0.532–0.992, 95% CI). Next, the effectiveness of the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio was examined within this model. When this index was added, three parameters showed insignificance but trends toward diagnostic significance, including initial WHO grade (P = 0.072), MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio (P = 0.079), and gender (P = 0.089). The AUC of this updated model was 0.942 (0.850-1.000, 95% CI). Results from the DeLong test indicated a significant difference between these two models (P = 0.046). The supplementary material offers detailed multivariate logistic regression results, and Fig. 3D displays the AUC comparison with and without the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio.

Survival analysis results of patient-based PET semi-quantitative parameters

In the survival analysis of parameters including TBRmax-FET, TBRmax-FAPI, MTV-FET, MTV-FAPI, MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio, initial WHO grade, age, and gender, Cox regression results showed that MTV-FAPI is statistically significant (P = 0.027, hazard ratio = 1.103, 95% CI: 1.011–1.204). Following the Cox regression analysis, a proportional hazards assumption test was performed, yielding a P-value of 0.875, which indicates that the results do not violate the proportional hazards assumption. Discussion

Based on the distinct FAP expression patterns across a wide range of carcinomas and our experience with the untreated intracranial tumor cohort, we further investigated the diagnostic and survival prediction capabilities of 68Ga-FAPI-04 PET imaging in this post-treatment glioma group. In survival analysis, our results showed that MTV-FAPI is an independent risk factor for overall survival in the cohort (P = 0.027, hazard ratio = 1.103, 95% CI: 1.011–1.204). This indicates that for each additional cubic centimeter increase in the lesion’s 68Ga-FAPI-04 metabolic volume, the risk of death increases by a factor of 1.103.

In the paired comparison of 68Ga-FAPI-04 and 18F-FET PET imaging of this cohort. PET parameters, including TBRmax, MTV, and TLU, showed significant differences, with P values of 0.000, 0.001, and 0.000, respectively. In the differential diagnosis of glioma recurrence, univariate logistic regression results indicated that the initial pathological diagnosis had a marginal but insignificant efficacy (P = 0.053). At the same time, no significant differences were observed in the PET-based semi-quantitative parameters. During the process of building the multivariate logistic regression model, the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio showed insignificant but trends toward significance (P = 0.094, 0.076, 0.081, respectively) in different combinations of PET-based parameters, initial pathological information, and clinical factors including age and gender. Besides, the inclusion of the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio in the above-mentioned model building yielded significant AUC enhancement results (P = 0.040, 0.039, 0.016, and 0.046, respectively).

The interactions between genetic background and the tumor microenvironment contribute to glioma progression and therapy resistance (Poetsch et al. 2018; Varn et al. 2022). FAP expression in glioma patients can be used to evaluate the crosstalk between glioma cells and cancer-associated fibroblasts. TME cell-targeting tracers offer distinct perspectives for assessing glioma heterogeneity, complementing traditional glioma cell-targeted imaging. Given the well-established intra- and inter-tumoral heterogeneity, our head-to-head PET studies analyze imaging from both glioma parenchyma cells and TME cells, exploring the connection between glioma recurrence and patterns of FAP overexpression. This two-tracer PET imaging enables the linking and quantifying of FAP overexpression in glioma tissue. The MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio and TLUFAPI:TLUFET ratio are two parameters that indicate FAP overexpression patterns in glioma parenchyma. In our previous cohort of untreated intracranial tumors, we evaluated these two parameters and confirmed the potential of the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio. The current study further investigates FAP overexpression in a cohort of recurrent gliomas. Although statistically insignificant, the analysis suggests that the efficacy of the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio has a trend that approaches significance. Higher MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio indicates increased activity of cancer-associated fibroblasts in the glioma microenvironment, and quantification of the activity makes it feasible to explore the extent of interactions between glioma cells and cancer-associated fibroblasts.

More importantly, the strong survival-predicting ability of MTV-FAPI in this cohort highlights the active role of FAP in the tumor microenvironment during glioma recurrence. Molecular, cellular, and microenvironmental mechanisms lead to glioma recurrence (Ravi et al. 2022). Interactions within the tumor microenvironment, such as those involving cancer-associated fibroblasts, promote recurrence by supporting immunosuppression and angiogenesis (Hambardzumyan and Bergers 2015). Survival depends on clinical factors, including younger age (< 50 years), good performance status (KPS > 70), and smaller tumor volume at recurrence, which are associated with better overall survival (Suchorska et al. 2016). Based on our results, we hypothesize that the overexpression of FAP from CAFs in the glioma TME creates a pro-tumorigenic environment that fosters resistance to therapy and worsens survival outcomes. These findings will enhance our understanding of the role of CAFs in the recurrence of gliomas. Our study expands knowledge of FAP overexpression patterns and their links to survival outcomes in patients with recurrent glioma. The poor prognosis of most glioma patients remains a significant treatment challenge, especially in glioblastoma cases. Studying FAP expression patterns in this recurrent glioma group, particularly in glioblastomas, may provide valuable evidence for future FAP-targeted theragnostic approaches to augment current glioma treatments.

Lesion delineation is crucial for treatment planning. Radio-labelled amino acid tracer imaging can effectively outline lesions following the guidelines’ recommendations. However, there is currently no specific guidance for delineating brain lesions in FAP-targeted imaging. In our previous research on FAP-targeting imaging, we examined lesion delineation by considering the features of FAPI PET imaging in a pre-treatment cohort of intracranial tumors. Different percentages of lesion SUVmax were used as thresholds to address fluctuations in delineation caused by the very low uptake of normal brain tissue. In this study, we used the same 20% lesion SUVmax as an isocontour volumetric threshold for lesion delineation. More evidence-based guidance is necessary to improve the accuracy of delineating solid tumors on FAPI PET imaging.

Structural MRI is the most widely used imaging method for diagnosing gliomas. Regular MRI follow-up helps locate newly developed abnormal enhanced lesions and provides early signs of possible glioma recurrence. Given the complexities of post-treatment gliomas, MRI RANO 2.0 guidelines help better evaluate recurrent glioma patients (Wen et al. 2023). As an effective complement to MRI, PET RANO 1.0-based amino acid tracer PET imaging can improve the recognition and assessment of post-treatment glioma patients (Wang et al. 2024). Further research is needed to differentiate glioma recurrence from treatment-related changes using these guidelines and the latest WHO glioma classification (Louis et al. 2021). Additionally, TME cell-targeting tracer PET studies will undoubtedly provide valuable complementing evidence for both diagnosis and potential treatment strategies.

Researchers from other groups reported perfect diagnostic efficacy of FAPI PET for recurrent glioma (Ruan et al. 2024), but in our cohort, the results are more complex. Treatment-related changes may also cause high 68Ga-FAPI-04 uptake in some post-treatment glioma patients, as well as in untreated patients with intracranial lesions, as observed in our previous study. Additionally, 68Ga-FAPI uptake has been observed in non-cancerous diseases, including autoimmune disorders, cardiovascular issues, and wound healing (Luo et al. 2021; Qin et al. 2021; Kessler et al. 2021; Zhou et al. 2021), so caution is warranted when interpreting positive 68Ga-FAPI-04 lesions. Characteristic time activity curve patterns from dynamic 18F-FET PET imaging can help distinguish non-cancerous lesions from cancerous ones beyond classical semi-quantitative parameters.

Integrating key clinical factors such as age of onset, gender, tumor pathological diagnosis, tumor grade, molecular markers, and treatment history into diagnostic models is essential for addressing clinical questions. These factors enhance the models’ accuracy and generalizability by accounting for real-world variability, enabling a better understanding of disease complexity, risk stratification, and more personalized decision-making (Obermeyer and Emanuel 2016; Lambin et al. 2017). Ignoring such clinical variables can lead to oversimplified models that fail to reflect disease heterogeneity, thereby reducing their translational clinical value. The initial pathological diagnosis of gliomas, based on histopathology and molecular profiling, provides critical information about genetic alterations and tumor grade. These features serve as key indicators of recurrence risk and patterns. In our cohort, initial pathological data indicate the heterogeneity within our glioma cohort before PET imaging. The results of the current study also demonstrate meaningful connections between initial pathological information and recurrence in glioma, highlighting the importance of key clinical factors.

Certain limitations should be addressed in our research. First, this head-to-head study enrolled a relatively small sample size, and the differential diagnostic efficacy results of this exploratory investigation showed several nonsignificant but suggestive findings related to PET-based semi-quantitative parameters. Additionally, the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio demonstrated significant efficacy in improving AUC of differential models. Caution should be exercised to ensure that the P-values of the multivariate logistic regression results are not adjusted in this exploratory research, despite the use of the variance inflation factor to control for multicollinearity. Future validation of the efficacy of the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio is needed with a more robust patient cohort, and external validation is essential before clinical application. Second, this study mainly focused on analyzing 68Ga-FAPI-04 and 18F-FET PET/CT imaging in our post-treatment glioma group. Future research, including the analysis of MRI parameters alongside PET imaging quantification, will undoubtedly help develop a more comprehensive understanding of the FAP expressed by CAFs in patients with recurrent glioma.

Conclusion

Our head-to-head study revealed that the metabolic volume of 68Ga-FAPI-04 uptake in the microenvironment of this post-treatment cohort significantly affects the overall survival of these glioma patients, highlighting the critical role of FAP expression in post-treatment glioma and providing additional evidence for FAP-related intervention procedures. In the differential diagnosis analysis, PET-based semi-quantitative parameters demonstrated limited effectiveness in this cohort. However, the MTVFAPI:MTVFET ratio, an index reflecting the percentage of FAP overexpression from CAFs in the entire tumor microenvironment of the glioma parenchyma, showed a non-significant trend toward significance in distinguishing recurrent glioma from treatment-related changes, including radiation necrosis or pseudoprogression.

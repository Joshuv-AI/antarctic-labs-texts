CNSM-02 Prognosis of Brain Metastases from Breast Cancer after Whole Brain Radiotherapy.
Authors: Hicham Gazanayi, Othmane Saqri, Omar Baddi, Soukaina Morchid, Nabila Sellal, Mohammed El Hfid
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe incidence of brain metastases (BM) in patients with metastatic breast cancer ranges between 10-30%. Standard treatment includes surgery, whole brain radiotherapy (WBRT), and stereotactic radiotherapy. WBRT is frequently used due to limited stereotaxy access and multiple metastases prevalence.AimEvaluate the prognosis of patients treated with WBRT for breast cancer BM and identify predictive factors for overall survival and intracranial progression-free survival.MethodsThis retrospective study included 65 patients treated with WBRT for breast cancer BM between January 2020 and March 2023. Clinical-pathological data were analyzed using SPSS 25.0.ResultsMedian age was 48.2 years with median KPS of 60. At least four brain metastases were present in 68.4% of patients, and 57.9% had mixed localization lesions. Molecular subtypes distribution was: 63.3% HER + (luminal B and non-luminal), 20% luminal A, and 16.7% triple negative. Median overall survival was 6 months with 25% 12-month survival probability. Median intracranial progression-free survival was 9 months with 40% intracranial control at 12 months. Significant survival differences were found by KPS (≥70 vs < 70: 12 vs 4 months, p < 0.001), molecular profile (luminal A: 12 months, luminal B HER2+: 8 months, HER2+ non-luminal: 5 months, triple negative: 4 months, p < 0.001), and extracranial metastases (absence vs presence: 10 vs 4 months, p = 0.01). Multivariate analysis identified independent poor prognostic factors: age >65 years (p = 0.008), low KPS (p = 0.02), ≥4 BM (p = 0.001), and intracranial progression (p < 0.001). Compared to luminal A, patients with luminal B HER2 + (p = 0.002), triple negative (p < 0.001), or HER2+ non-luminal (p = 0.01) had significantly higher death risk.ConclusionThis study confirms the reserved prognosis of breast cancer BM, with 6-month median survival after WBRT. Identified prognostic factors (age, functional status, metastases number, molecular subtype) enable better risk stratification and therapeutic personalization, highlighting the importance of rigorous WBRT candidate selection and treatment intensification for poor prognosis groups.

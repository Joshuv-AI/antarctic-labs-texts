Molecular insights into insulin-like signaling and its positive role in growth of the bivalve Mulinia lateralis
Authors: Lingling Kong, Xiangfu Kong, Deting Meng, Xiangchao Zhang, Jie Meng, Zhenmin Bao, Xiaoli Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn terrestrial animals, the somatotropic axis, comprising growth hormone (GH) and insulin-like growth factors (IGFs), is pivotal in regulating growth and development. Marine mollusks play a vital role in the aquaculture industry, and understanding the molecular mechanisms of mollusk growth is of great value to breeding fast-growing and high-yielding varieties. Unlike terrestrial animals, marine mollusks lack a model species for laboratory breeding, leaving many growth-related genes unvalidated. The dwarf surf clam Mulinia lateralis, with its small size, short breeding cycle, and ease of cultivation in laboratory settings, serves as an ideal model for investigating growth regulation. This study is the first systematic identification of genes related to M. lateralis growth, with 195 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) being found between fast- and slow-growing individuals through transcriptome comparison. KEGG analysis revealed significant enrichment of the insulin-like signaling pathway, and the insulin-like peptide (ILP) was the most significantly upregulated. As the insulin signaling pathway is activated by ligand–receptor binding, we further characterized and functionally validated mlILP and its receptor, the insulin receptor-related receptor (mlIRR). RNA interference (RNAi)-mediated knockdown of mlILP or mlIRR resulted in growth retardation, confirming their positive roles in growth regulation. Notably, silencing of these two genes caused significant upregulation of downstream genes, suggesting a compensatory mechanism for maintaining cell homeostasis. Introduction

Animal growth and development are complex physiological processes regulated by multiple growth factors and signaling pathways. In terrestrial vertebrates, the somatotropic axis, composed of growth hormone (GH) and insulin-like growth factors (IGFs), serves as a central hormonal regulator (Ranke and Wit 2018; Waters and Brooks 2012). Other key signaling pathways, including mTOR, MAPK, Hippo, and Wnt, also regulate growth by mediating nutrient perception, energy homeostasis, and cell division (Gokhale and Shingleton 2015). In model organisms such as Drosophila melanogaster and Caenorhabditis elegans, the mechanisms coordinating growth and body size have been well characterized (Leevers 2001; Mirth and Shingleton 2012; Tuck 2014). In D. melanogaster, insulin and mTOR signaling interact with hormonal systems (e.g., ecdysone and juvenile hormone) to integrate environmental cues with developmental timing, ensuring regulated growth and optimal body size (Mirth and Shingleton 2012). By contrast, growth in C. elegans is driven by the TGFβ ligand DBL-1 in a dose-dependent manner rather than by insulin signaling (Tuck 2014). Despite these advances in model systems, the regulatory mechanisms underlying growth in non-model organisms, particularly marine invertebrates, remain poorly understood.

Marine mollusks play a vital role in aquaculture, accounting for 70% of China’s mariculture production (FAO 2024). This taxonomic group is ecologically and economically important and serves as a major source of high-quality animal protein for humans (Boyd et al. 2022). Growth traits are among the most critical characteristics of cultured species, making the improvement of growth rate a primary goal in genetic breeding programs (Abdelrahman et al. 2017; Wang et al. 2024a). Understanding the molecular mechanisms underlying mollusk growth is essential for developing high-yield, fast-growing varieties. However, such mechanisms remain largely unexplored compared with those in terrestrial species. Genome-wide association and comparative transcriptome analyses have identified several growth-related genes in mollusks, including paired-like homeobox 1 (Prop1) in Chlamys farreri (Jiao et al. 2014), E2F transcription factor 3 (E2F3) in Patinopecten yessoensis (Ning et al. 2019), alpha-amylase (AMY) and bone morphogenetic protein 1 (BMP1) in Crassostrea gigas (Li et al. 2018), and sex peptide receptor (SPR), pachytene checkpoint protein 2 homolog (Trip13), and kinesin-like protein (KIF13B) in interspecific scallop hybrids (Mao et al. 2020). Notably, the insulin-like signaling pathway appears to be a central regulator of growth across diverse molluscan species. Comparative transcriptome studies in Argopecten irradians, C. gigas, Pinctada fucata, Ruditapes decussatus, Ruditapes philippinarum, Haliotis midae, and Lymnaea stagnalis have repeatedly identified this pathway in association with growth differences (Li et al. 2023; Nie et al. 2021; Rosato et al. 2021; Saavedra et al. 2017; Shi and He 2014; van der Merwe et al. 2011; Zhao et al. 2021). This conserved pathway, fundamental to metazoan growth regulation, likely modulates nutritional metabolism and individual growth in mollusks (Biglou et al. 2021; Choi et al. 2025; Mokrani et al. 2025). Nevertheless, the functional roles of its components in mollusks remain largely uncharacterized, representing a critical gap in understanding molluscan growth regulation.

Although growth-related gene functions in terrestrial animals have been elucidated using well-established model organisms, mollusks—particularly bivalves—lack well-established experimental models due to challenges in laboratory cultivation. This limitation has hindered the functional validation of candidate growth-regulatory genes. The dwarf surf clam Mulinia lateralis (M. lateralis), a small burrowing bivalve native to the Atlantic coast of North America and the Caribbean, belongs to the family Mactridae (Calabrese 1969). Its short life cycle, ease of laboratory maintenance and reproduction, and availability of multiple family lines have established M. lateralis as an emerging model for molluscan growth studies (Wang et al. 2023). Functional characterization of growth-associated genes in M. lateralis may yield molecular markers for breeding improved aquaculture stocks. However, systematic investigations in M. lateralis remain limited.

In this study, we identified and functionally characterized growth-related genes in M. lateralis. Comparative transcriptome analysis of digestive glands from clams with differential growth rates revealed the insulin-like signaling pathway as a key regulator of growth. RNAi-mediated knockdown of mlILP and mlIRR confirmed their positive regulatory roles in growth regulation. These results provide insights into growth-related gene functions in M. lateralis and contribute to a broader understanding of the molecular basis of growth in marine mollusks.

Materials and methods

Sample collection

The clams M. lateralis used in this study were obtained from the germplasm collection maintained by the Key Laboratory of Marine Biology Genetics and Breeding, Ocean University of China (Qingdao, China). The population is identified as QT-G6-QD-202005 and is characterized by its clear generational background and high genetic diversity (Yang et al. 2024). In addition, M. lateralis exhibits advantageous traits such as high survival rates and rapid growth, making it a suitable species for growth-related studies. To investigate transcriptomic variations associated with divergent growth rates in M. lateralis, juvenile clams from the same population were cultured for two months under identical physicochemical conditions. After this period, the largest 30 individuals were classified as the fast-growth group, whereas the smallest 30 individuals comprised the slow-growth group. Digestive glands were sampled from these individuals, with samples from ten clams combined to create three sample pools for each group.

To conduct RNAi experiments, we sampled 300 clams from a population at 40 days post-fertilization, a period characterized by their rapid growth phase. These clams were acclimated in a 15-L aquarium tank containing 10 L of filtered seawater and a 3 cm layer of sand, maintaining an appropriate stocking density. During the 7-day acclimation period preceding the experiment, the clams were fed with Chlorella pyrenoidosa twice daily. Following acclimation, we randomly selected 160 clams of similar size (shell length: 3.05 ± 0.42 mm; shell height: 2.82 ± 0.31 mm) from the initial 300, evenly distributing them into four groups, with each group placed in a separate tank for the RNAi experiments.

RNA isolation and sequencing

Total RNA isolation was performed using the traditional guanidinium isothiocyanate method, and the purity and integrity of the RNA were assessed using a NanoDrop ND 1000 spectrophotometer (Thermo, USA). Six cDNA libraries were then constructed following the manufacturer’s instructions utilizing the NEBNext® Ultra™ RNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina® (NEB, USA). The quality of the cDNA libraries was evaluated through 1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis and a Qubit spectrophotometer (Invitrogen, USA). The prepared libraries were subjected to paired-end 150 bp (PE150) sequencing on the Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform (Illumina, USA), with sequencing conducted by Novogene Company (Beijing, China).

Differential expression analysis

Raw reads in fastq format generated from Illumina sequencing were assessed using FastQC v.0.11.8. Clean reads were obtained by trimming those containing adapters, reads with poly-N sequences, and reads exhibiting low sequencing quality. The high-quality reads from each sample were aligned to the reference genome of M. lateralis (unpublished data) using STAR v2.7.9 software (Dobin et al. 2013). The expression levels of all transcripts were normalized and represented as transcripts per million (TPM) using featureCounts v2.0.3. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between fast-growing and slow-growing groups were identified using the package edgeR (Robinson et al. 2010) in RStudio 4.1.3, applying the thresholds |log2FC| > 1 and FDR < 0.05. A volcano plot was generated using the R package ggplot2 to visualize the overall distribution of DEGs. The Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis (Ogata et al. 1999) was conducted using the enrichpipeline. KEGG pathways with adjusted P values < 0.05 were considered significantly enriched by DEGs.

Characterization and expression analysis of mlILP and mlIRR

The basic information regarding the mlILP and mlIRR gene sequences, including chromosome position, gene length, number of exons, and open reading frame (ORF) length, was determined using the general feature format (GFF) annotation file. The conserved protein domains of mlILP and mlIRR were predicted using the SMART tool (http://smart.embl.de/), and potential cleavage sites of the mlILP protein were identified with PeptideCutter software (https://web.expasy.org/peptide_cutter/). Sequences were aligned using ClustalW, and phylogenetic trees were constructed utilizing the maximum likelihood (ML) method in MEGA 7 (Kumar et al. 2016) with 1000 bootstrap replications. Detailed sequence information is provided in Supplementary Table S1. The spatiotemporal expression analysis of mlILP and mlIRR was based on transcriptome data from embryonic stages and adult tissues (unpublished data). A histogram depicting the expression levels of mlILP and mlIRR was generated using GraphPad Prism 8.0.1.

RNA interference (RNAi) of mlILP and mlIRR

We employed a non-invasive RNAi approach using the Trojan horse strategy, as previously reported by Payton et al. (2017). In this method, unicellular algae are associated with engineered bacteria to facilitate target gene silencing through feeding. First, a 414 bp RNAi fragment of mlILP, a 415 bp fragment of mlIRR, and a 426 bp fragment of the enhanced green fluorescent protein (EGFP) were amplified using specific primers (Supplementary Table S2). The dsRNAs targeting mlILP, mlIRR, and EGFP were synthesized according to the methods described in Zhang et al. (2024), with slight modifications. RNAi experiments were conducted using a standardized feeding protocol. The control group of M. lateralis was fed with C. pyrenoidosa mixed with HT115(DE3) containing the empty L4440 plasmid. The dsEGFP, dsILP, and dsIRR groups were fed with C. pyrenoidosa mixed with Escherichia coli HT115 (DE3) containing the corresponding target gene-L4440 recombinant plasmid. An algae/bacteria co-inoculum was produced by mixing algal culture and bacterial suspension at a ratio of 10 bacteria per algal cell, achieving a final C. pyrenoidosa concentration of 4000 cells/mL. Each experimental group was maintained in a 15-L aquarium tank containing 10 L of filtered seawater and a 3 cm layer of sand. The tanks were maintained under identical environmental conditions: seawater temperature was stabilized at 22 ± 0.5 °C, salinity at 28 ± 0.3 psu, with adequate aeration ensuring saturated oxygen concentration. The feeding regime consisted of twice-daily exposure periods, followed by thorough tank flushing to prevent residual dsRNA accumulation. Daily seawater refreshment was performed through a filtration system, with the seawater pre-equilibrated to system parameters.

No mortality was observed in any of the groups throughout the RNAi experiment. All M. lateralis individuals remained viable and exhibited normal physiological behavior during the experimental period. After 10 days of treatment, clams in the dsILP and dsIRR groups exhibited significant growth inhibition compared to those in the control and EGFP groups. Tissue samples were collected, and five biological replicates were obtained from a mixture of each tissue collected from six individuals. These samples were processed for RNA extraction, cDNA synthesis, and quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) analysis to evaluate the expression levels of mlILP and mlIRR. The qRT-PCR primers were designed to target regions outside the RNAi-targeted area, preventing amplification of dsRNA-derived fragments (Supplementary Fig. S1). Elongation factor-1α (EF1α) served as the reference gene, and primer information is provided in Supplementary Table S2. The qRT-PCR program was executed on the LightCycler480® II (Roche, USA), and each sample reaction was performed in three technical replicates. For quality control, the melting curve was utilized to evaluate the specificity of the primers in qRT-PCR reactions (Supplementary Fig. S2). The inclusion of non-template controls (NTCs) and no-reverse-transcription controls (NRTs) helped rule out contamination and non-specific amplification. The relative expression of the target genes was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method (Livak and Schmittgen 2001), with data presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Subsequent analysis using one-way ANOVA in SPSS 17.0 (IBM, US) determined significant differences at P < 0.05.

Results

Genes differentially expressed between fast- and slow-growing clams

For the transcriptome analysis, the mean shell length and height of the fast-growing clams were 5.95 ± 0.63 mm and 4.03 ± 0.37 mm, respectively, compared with 4.50 ± 0.43 mm and 3.11 ± 0.24 mm for the slow-growing clams (Fig. 1A). One-way ANOVA indicated significant differences in phenotypic traits between fast- and slow-growing individuals (P < 0.01). We focused our transcriptomic analysis on the digestive gland, owing to its critical role in nutrient processing and energy storage—functions essential for molluscan growth (Roszer 2014; Tamayo et al. 2011). High-throughput RNA-seq was performed on six samples using the Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform, yielding approximately 38.49 Gb of raw data. After quality filtering, 37.37 Gb of high-quality clean data were obtained, with evenly distributed data volume across samples (Supplementary Table S3). Sequencing generated 20.88 million clean reads per sample, with Q20 and Q30 scores reaching 98.3% and 94.7% (Supplementary Table S3), respectively, indicating high sequencing quality. Clean reads were aligned to the M. lateralis reference genome (unpublished data) using STAR v2.7.9, achieving an average unique mapping rate of 60.2% (Supplementary Table S3). This high-quality chromosome-level reference genome, assembled using Illumina short reads, PacBio long reads, and Hi-C scaffolding technology, has been used for multiple genomic and transcriptomic analyses in M. lateralis (Li et al. 2022a, 2025; Wang et al. 2024b; Yang et al. 2024). Subsequent differential expression analysis was performed exclusively on uniquely mapped reads to ensure data reliability.

The data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD), based on measurements from 30 independent individuals in each group. According to the screening criteria of |log2FC| > 1 and FDR < 0.05, a total of 195 DEGs were detected in the digestive gland between fast- and slow-growing clams, of which 186 were upregulated and nine were downregulated in fast-growing individuals (Fig. 1B). Notably, the top ten most significantly upregulated genes included seven annotated genes, namely insulin-like peptide (ILP), growth factor receptor-bound protein 2 (Grb2), nucleobindin-2 (Nucb2), dual specificity mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase 1 (MEK1), golgi phosphoprotein 3 (GOLPH3), guanine nucleotide-binding protein G(i) subunit alpha (GNAl1), basigin (BSG), and three unannotated genes (Supplementary Table S4). Conversely, the downregulated genes included four annotated genes, namely eukaryotic translation initiation factor 4E-binding protein 1 (4EBP1), C-type lectin (CTL10), glutathione S-transferase theta-2 (GSTT2), nucleoredoxin (NXN), and five unannotated genes (Supplementary Table S4). Among these, ILP, Grb2, MEK1, and 4EBP1 are critical regulatory genes within the insulin signaling pathway.

To clarify the biological pathways or signaling pathways that these DEGs may participate in, we performed KEGG pathway enrichment analysis. The DEGs displayed significant enrichment in 16 signaling pathways (Supplementary Fig. S3), including tight junction, insulin signaling pathway, mTOR signaling pathway, AMPK signaling pathway, chemokine signaling pathway, endocytosis, MAPK signaling pathway, Ras signaling pathway, neurotrophin signaling pathway, and protein processing in the endoplasmic reticulum. These findings suggest that growth regulation in M. lateralis relies on coordinated mechanisms of signal transduction, metabolic regulation, and protein processing. Notably, the insulin signaling pathway and its downstream cascades (MAPK, mTOR, and Ras) were particularly prominent, comprising a total of 27 DEGs. Within this network, 11 genes were upregulated, including key components (ILP, IRR, SOS, Grb2, Ras, MEK1, IRS1, AKT, S6K1, and eIF4E1), whereas 4EBP1 was significantly downregulated (Fig. 1C). This comprehensive pattern of pathway activation strongly implicates the insulin-like signaling pathway as a central regulator of growth in M. lateralis.

Characterization, expression analysis, and functional verification of mlILP

As the most significantly upregulated gene in the fast-growing clams, mlILP serves as a key upstream regulator of the insulin-like signaling pathway. The mlILP gene spans 5812 bp with three exons and two introns, encoding a 916 bp cDNA containing a 486 bp ORF that encodes a 161-amino acid protein (Fig. 2A). Structural analysis revealed that the mlILP prepropeptide contains characteristic insulin superfamily domains, including a signal peptide followed by B- and A-chains. The mature peptide features three cysteine residues in the B-chain and five in the A-chain, potentially forming four disulfide bonds (Fig. 2A). Phylogenetic analysis illustrated four major clades: (1) invertebrate ILPs, (2) vertebrate INS, (3) vertebrate INSL, and (4) vertebrate IGFs (Fig. 2B). The invertebrate ILPs branch is predominant in the evolutionary tree, including the ILP7 clade, the MILP3 clade, and other unidentified clades. Notably, mlILP clustered specifically within the MILP3 clade as well as homologs from Mercenaria mercenaria, C. gigas, and Octopus bimaculoides. Interestingly, several molluscan sequences appeared within the vertebrate IGF clade, suggesting the existence of IGF-like genes in some mollusk species.

Expression profiling revealed dynamic patterns of mlILP during development and across adult tissues (Fig. 2C). During embryonic stages, the expression of mlILP was barely detectable from the fertilized egg to the multicellular stage, increased dramatically at the blastula stage, and peaked during the trochophore phase before declining in the D-shaped larvae and reaching a minimum in juveniles (Fig. 2C). In adult tissues, mlILP demonstrated ubiquitous yet differential expression, with elevated levels in the digestive glands and foot, moderate levels in the muscles and gonads, and low levels in the gill and mantle (Fig. 2C). These varied expression patterns suggest that mlILP plays multifaceted roles in both larval development and adult growth regulation.

To gain insight into the role of mlILP in the growth of M. lateralis, we implemented a bacteria-mediated RNAi strategy. After 10 days of treatment, mlILP expression was significantly reduced by approximately 85% in the dsILP group compared to both the control and dsEGFP groups (Fig. 2E). Notably, mlILP knockdown significantly inhibited the clam growth (Fig. 2D). Specifically, the average shell length and shell height of clams in the dsILP group were significantly reduced by 12.8% and 13.3%, respectively, compared to the control group, and by 20.5% and 18.7% compared to the dsEGFP group (Fig. 2F, G). Although dsEGFP-treated clams exhibited minor, non-significant growth enhancement relative to the control group, these variations remained within normal biological fluctuation ranges. These findings demonstrate that mlILP plays a crucial positive role in the growth regulation of M. lateralis.

Characterization, expression analysis, and functional verification of mlIRR

Given that insulin signaling requires ligand-receptor binding, we further characterized the membrane receptor mlIRR to complement our study of mlILP. The mlIRR gene spans 44,699 bp with 28 exons and 27 introns, encoding an 8768 bp cDNA containing a 5409 bp ORF that encodes to a 1802-amino acid protein (Fig. 3A). The mlIRR exhibits highly conserved domains characteristic of the insulin receptor (IR) family, including a signal peptide, two receptor L domains separated by a furin (FU) domain, three fibronectin type III (FN3) domains, a transmembrane domain, and an intracellular tyrosine kinase (TyrKc) domain (Fig. 3A). Phylogenetic analysis revealed that the IR family is divided into two clades: vertebrates and invertebrates. Invertebrates generally possess a single homologous receptor (IRR) of the IR family, whereas the vertebrate branch contains three distinct orthologs: IR, insulin-like growth factor receptor (IGFR), and the insulin-like receptor (IRR). Notably, mlIRR clustered first with other bivalve IRRs before grouping with invertebrate homologs (Fig. 3B), confirming its evolutionary position within the conserved invertebrate IRR lineage.

mlIRR exhibited dynamic expression throughout clam development (Fig. 3C). High basal expression was observed in eggs (TPM > 200) and remained stable from the zygote to multicellular stages, followed by significant upregulation at the blastula stage (TPM>300) and a gradual decline from gastrula to juvenile stages. This pattern indicates stage-specific regulatory functions during embryogenesis. In adults, mlIRR expression was significantly reduced relative to embryos, but remained detectable in all tissues examined, with high abundance in the gill and muscle, moderate levels in the digestive gland, foot, and gonad, and low levels in the mantle (Fig. 3C). This tissue-specific expression profile suggests diverse functional roles of the mlIRR in clams.

To explore the functional role of mlIRR in growth regulation, we depleted its expression to determine whether the phenotype observed after mlILP silencing would be replicated. As expected, the mlIRR knockdown clams exhibited a significant reduction in body size compared to the control and dsEGFP groups (Fig. 3D). Specifically, the average shell length and shell height of the dsIRR group were significantly reduced by 5.7% and 5.4%, respectively, compared to the control group and by 13.2% and 11.4% compared to the dsEGFP group (Fig. 3F, G). qRT-PCR analysis confirmed effective gene silencing, showing that mlIRR transcript levels were significantly reduced to 17.8% and 19.1% of the control and dsEGFP groups, respectively (Fig. 3E). These findings demonstrate that mlIRR, analogous to its ligand mlILP, serves as a crucial positive regulator of growth in M. lateralis, thereby establishing their cooperative function within the insulin-like signaling pathway that governs bivalve development.

Expression analysis of downstream genes in the insulin-like signaling pathway after mlILP and mlIRR knockdown

To elucidate the molecular mechanisms of the insulin signaling pathway, we analyzed the expression patterns of downstream genes following mlILP and mlIRR knockdown. Compared to the control group, mlILP knockdown resulted in significant upregulation of Grb2, SOS, Ras, MEK1, IRS1, AKT, S6K1, and eIF4E1, while 4EBP1 was downregulated (Fig. 4). mlIRR knockdown resulted in similar expression patterns of these downstream genes, suggesting a coordinated function of mlILP and mlIRR within the insulin pathway, which is potentially crucial for its balance and efficiency. Further studies are needed to decipher the precise regulatory mechanisms of mlILP and mlIRR on these genes and to examine the phosphorylation activity of downstream effectors for a deeper understanding of their roles in insulin signaling.

Discussion

Animal growth represents a complex physiological process regulated through interactions among genetic, metabolic, nutritional, and endocrine factors. In vertebrates, this process is primarily governed by the GH-IGF axis (Brooks and Waters 2010; Ranke and Wit 2018), whereas insects rely on insulin-like signaling pathways for growth regulation (Ling and Raikhel 2018; Mokrani et al. 2025; Nässel and Vanden Broeck 2016). Despite the economic significance of marine mollusks in global aquaculture, their growth regulation mechanisms remain largely unexplored. In this study, we focused on the marine model species M. lateralis to investigate the molecular mechanisms of growth control. Through comparative transcriptome analysis and functional verification of key genes in differentially growing individuals, we revealed the functional significance of ILP and IRR in growth regulation, shedding light for the first time on the molecular regulatory mechanisms underlying the insulin-like signaling pathway in mollusks. These insights not only enhance our understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying growth in marine mollusks, but also provide valuable information for the genetic breeding of other aquaculture species.

The insulin-like signaling pathway is associated with clam growth

This study presents the first transcriptome analysis of M. lateralis, which identified 186 upregulated and nine downregulated genes in fast-growing clams (Fig. 1B). Notably, three of the top ten upregulated genes—ILP, Grb2, and MEK1—are integral to the insulin signaling pathway. ILP is a well-known growth regulator, and the adapter protein Grb2 and kinase MEK1 play essential roles in expanding growth signaling and promoting cell proliferation; thus, their upregulation is likely critical for promoting growth and development (Claeys et al. 2002; Lavoie et al. 2020; Wee and Wang 2017). Conversely, 4EBP1, a negative regulator of protein synthesis (Kim and Guan 2019; Saxton and Sabatini 2017), was significantly downregulated, consistent with enhanced growth. KEGG enrichment analysis confirmed the activation of growth signal transduction pathways, including the insulin, MAPK, mTOR, and Ras pathways. Studies have shown that MAPK, mTOR, and Ras signaling cascades are induced and activated by upstream INS and related peptides, and play a synergistic role in cell growth, nutrient sensing, and body size regulation (Gokhale and Shingleton 2015; Yang and Xu 2011; Yuan et al. 2013). These findings establish insulin signaling as a central growth mechanism in bivalves, providing key insights for molluscan aquaculture research.

The insulin signaling pathway is evolutionarily conserved in metazoans, with numerous studies in insects and mammals demonstrating its central role in regulating life processes, from metabolism to growth (Barbieri et al. 2003; Viola et al. 2023). In mollusks, genes associated with insulin-like signaling pathways have been reported sporadically, including the IGF binding protein (IGFBP), IGF2 mRNA-binding protein (IMP), phosphatase and tensin homolog (Pten), Ras, and S6K (Feng et al. 2014; Jouaux et al. 2012; Ning et al. 2018; Sharker et al. 2020; Wang et al. 2015; Zhang et al. 2017). This study provides systematic evidence that genes within the insulin-like system are integral to growth regulation, supported by consistent transcriptome analyses across multiple mollusk species (Nie et al. 2021; Réalis-Doyelle et al. 2021; Saavedra et al. 2017; Shi and He 2014; van der Merwe et al. 2011). The convergence of evidence from diverse species reinforces the pivotal function of the insulin-like signaling pathway in mollusk growth control.

mlILP functions in growth regulation in clams

The insulin superfamily is well characterized in vertebrates and includes both the INS/IGFs and relaxin/insulin-like (RLN/INSL) groups. In contrast, mollusks exhibit a diverse array of ILP genes, with the number varying among different species. For example, C. gigas, Lottia gigantea, and Haliotis discus hannai possess four ILPs, whereas L. stagnalis and Anodonta cygnea have seven, one of which demonstrates IGF activity (Li et al. 2021a; Rusakov et al. 2003; Smit et al. 1998; Veenstra 2010). Unlike the multiple IGF and ILP genes identified in other mollusks (Yoshio et al. 2015), only one mlILP has been found in the M. lateralis genome. This limited repertoire contrasts with the expansions observed in other mollusks, suggesting lineage-specific gene loss or functional consolidation. Structurally, mlILP exhibits conserved domains with mammalian INS and IGFs, affirming its classification within the insulin superfamily. A notable characteristic is its ability to form four disulfide bonds, a feature also noted in other invertebrates, which indicates enhanced structural stability compared to the three disulfide bonds found in mammalian counterparts (Cherif-Feildel et al. 2019).

In this study, phylogenetic analysis indicates that mlILP is closely related to ILP genes in other invertebrates, suggesting an ancient evolutionary origin. The evolutionary history of insulin-related genes is marked by considerable complexity. The insulin superfamily likely originated from early metazoan insulin-like genes, subsequently undergoing lineage-specific expansions, contractions, and functional diversification across various taxa (Wood et al. 2005; Xu et al. 2021). Although vertebrates evolved separate INS and IGFs, mollusks retained varied ILP copies, with M. lateralis representing a notable case of gene reduction. This evolutionary dynamic of “expansion–contraction” may have significantly influenced the diversity of insulin signaling systems in extant species, reflecting the balance between genetic innovation and conservation in the evolution of this vital hormone family.

The role of ILPs in developmental regulation is highly conserved across species. In insects and nematodes, ILPs regulate growth and organogenesis in response to nutritional status (Lin and Smagghe 2019; Schindler et al. 2014), whereas in mollusks, they modulate gastrulation and metamorphosis (Cherif-Feildel et al. 2019). In this context, the high expression of mlILP during postembryonic development emphasizes its crucial role in regulating organ morphogenesis, paralleling functions observed in distantly related species. Notably, unlike vertebrates where INS and IGFs are synthesized in pancreatic β-cells and the liver (Rhodes and White 2002), molluscan ILPs are typically derived from neuroendocrine sources, as reported in the ganglia of oysters (Cherif-Feildel et al. 2019), pond snails (Smit et al. 1988), and sea hares (Jonas et al. 1996). Intriguingly, mlILP shows predominant expression in the digestive gland of M. lateralis, a pattern shared with Mytilus edulis (Fritsch et al. 1976), A. cygnea, and Unio pictorum (Erica et al. 1978). Given this organ’s central role in nutrient processing (Tamayo et al. 2011), our findings suggest that mlILP may integrate growth with metabolic needs, a function previously associated with IGFs in vertebrates.

INS and related peptides serve as key hormonal integrators of growth and metabolic homeostasis in animals (Antonova et al. 2012; Choi et al. 2025). In vertebrates, INS regulates glucose metabolism, whereas IGFs act as principal growth promoters, with their high expression correlating with significant growth performance (Herkenhoff et al. 2020; LeRoith et al. 2021; Rhodes and White 2002). Remarkably, M. lateralis, which lacks canonical IGF homologs, appears to depend solely on mlILP for growth regulation, as evidenced by an approximate 13% reduction in body size following RNAi-mediated knockdown (Fig. 2F, G). This phenotype resembles growth defects in IGF1-deficient mice (Liu et al. 1993), indicating functional convergence between molluscan ILPs and vertebrate IGFs. Supporting this, recombinant ILP protein from Pinctada fucata martensii enhances cell proliferation (Zhang and He 2020), whereas C. gigas ILPs modulate feeding and nutrient absorption (Li et al. 2021a). Similarly, gastropod ILPs, referred to as “growth-stimulating hormones,” regulate body size and shell formation (Saleuddin et al. 1991; Smit et al. 1996). Our study advances this understanding by providing the first direct functional evidence in mollusks via mlILP knockdown, unequivocally linking ILPs to growth control. Collectively, the conserved growth-regulatory role of ILPs in IGF-absent species indicates deep evolutionary roots of insulin-like signaling in body size modulation, predating the vertebrate-specific IGF duplication events.

mlIRR promoted clam growth

The insulin signaling pathway is initiated by ligand binding to cell-surface receptors, which are core components of this evolutionarily conserved system (Abdelrahman et al. 2017). Mammals possess three receptor types—IR, IGF1R/IGF2R, and IRR—of which IGF2R is a G protein-coupled receptor, whereas the others belong to the tyrosine kinase receptor family (Nakae et al. 2001). In contrast, our study reveals that M. lateralis possesses only a single insulin receptor-related receptor (mlIRR), consistent with findings in other invertebrates. Structural analysis shows that mlIRR retains all essential domains for function, including: (1) a canonical ligand-binding domain architecture, comprising two receptor L domains flanking a cysteine-rich region (De Meyts 2004); (2) a conserved FN3 domain proximal to the transmembrane segment; and (3) an intact tyrosine kinase domain (residues 1350–1617), which is required for phosphorylation-mediated signaling. Phylogenetic analysis of 29 IR family members places mlIRR within a distinct bivalve IRR clade, separate from the vertebrate IR, IGF1R, and IRR groups. This topology supports the view that receptor diversification occurred in vertebrates (Hernández-Sánchez et al. 2008), whereas mollusks retained a single ancestral receptor. Our findings extend previous observations in C. gigas (Gricourt et al. 2006) and P. fucata (Shi et al. 2013) by providing a more comprehensive structural and evolutionary characterization of mlIRR in a clam species.

Expression profiling reveals that mlIRR is broadly expressed across developmental stages and adult tissues, indicating pleiotropic roles. Its high expression throughout embryogenesis suggests a critical function in early development and organogenesis (Bateman and McNeill 2006). This pattern mirrors observations in C. gigas CIR (Gricourt et al. 2006) and P. fucata IRR (Shi et al. 2013). In adult tissues, mlIRR is ubiquitously expressed, a distribution pattern consistent with that of IRRs in Aplysia californica (Jonas et al. 1996), C. gigas (Gricourt et al. 2006), P. fucata (Shi et al. 2013), and Sepiella japonica (Lu et al. 2022), suggesting tissue-specific functional diversity. Notably, elevated expression in muscle indicates a role in glycogen metabolism (Berthelin et al. 2000), whereas moderate levels in the digestive gland may reflect paracrine signaling by mlILP during digestion (Jouaux et al. 2012). Other potential insulin-responsive targets include the mantle and foot (Berthelin et al. 2000; Gómez-Mendikute et al. 2005). These findings collectively suggest that mlIRR is essential for both larval development and adult growth in M. lateralis.

Although IRR has been identified in several mollusks, its functional roles remain poorly characterized. In B. glabrata, IRR activation stimulates proliferation in embryonic cell lines (Lardans et al. 2001), while in P. fucata, its inhibition impairs larval development (Shi et al. 2013). In addition, IRR expression is upregulated in fast-growing individuals of C. gigas (Li et al. 2021a) and R. philippinarum (Yin et al. 2022), supporting a conserved growth-regulatory function. Our findings provide direct evidence that mlIRR positively regulates growth in M. lateralis, offering new insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying molluscan growth.

The compensatory mechanism of the insulin signaling pathway in M. lateralis

An unexpected finding was that inhibition of mlILP or mlIRR expression resulted in significant differential regulation of downstream genes in the insulin-like signaling pathway of M. lateralis. Specifically, expression levels of Grb2, SOS, Ras, MEK1, IRS1, AKT, S6K1, and eIF4E1 were significantly upregulated. These genes are well established in vertebrates as positive regulators of growth and development (Skolnik et al. 1993; Yu and Cui 2016). In contrast, 4EBP1, which is associated with growth inhibition (Kim and Guan 2019; Saxton and Sabatini 2017), was markedly downregulated. Although suppression of upstream insulin-like signaling resulted in growth retardation in M. lateralis, the concurrent upregulation of downstream growth-promoting genes appears paradoxical. This phenomenon may be explained through several mechanisms. First, it may reflect a negative feedback response aimed at maintaining cellular homeostasis, as reported in other systems (Ridderstråle 2005). Second, compensatory crosstalk with other growth-regulatory pathways—such as the MSTN/dopamine-mediated pathway described in oysters (Carrera-Naipil et al. 2016; Li et al. 2021b)—could enhance cellular sensitivity to alternative growth cues. This type of regulatory compensation has also been reported in Drosophila deficient in ILPs (Gronke et al. 2010). Third, technical factors such as potential off-target effects of RNAi cannot be excluded. To validate the proposed compensatory mechanism, we suggest a three-pronged strategy: (1) dual knockdown of both mlILP and mlIRR, (2) phosphoproteomic profiling to assess downstream activation, and (3) experimental validation of protein–protein interaction networks.

Despite the valuable insights provided by transcriptomic and RNAi-based functional analyses, this study is limited by its reliance on RNA-level data without direct validation at the protein level. Given that post-transcriptional regulation and protein modifications (e.g., phosphorylation) often critically influence signaling pathway dynamics, future studies incorporating proteomic and phosphoproteomic analyses are essential to fully elucidate the molecular mechanisms of insulin-like signaling in M. lateralis. Such approaches would provide more comprehensive insights into the functional consequences of gene perturbation and validate the biological relevance of the observed transcriptomic changes.

In conclusion, our findings clarify the roles and regulatory dynamics of key components within the insulin-like signaling pathway, highlighting its conserved function in metazoan growth control. Building on these results, we plan to perform longitudinal gene expression profiling to track dynamic changes over time and further dissect the interplay between insulin-like signaling and growth trajectories in M. lateralis. In addition, we will assess feeding behavior and intake rates in RNAi-treated clams to evaluate both direct and indirect effects of mlILP and mlIRR knockdown. Finally, gene editing, particularly CRISPR/Cas-based systems, holds substantial promise for trait improvement in aquaculture species (Yang et al. 2025). Technical feasibility has been demonstrated across diverse species, including oysters (Chan et al. 2022; Yu et al. 2019), abalone (Li et al. 2024), shrimp (Pudgerd et al. 2024), crabs (Li et al. 2022b), and fish (Coogan et al. 2023; Hou et al. 2023; Kim et al. 2019; Wargelius et al. 2016), primarily via microinjection and electroporation. However, higher gene-editing efficiency is required in bivalves and ethical challenges, such as ecological impacts and regulatory compliance, should be considered (Robinson et al. 2023). Future studies should focus on validating gene function through targeted editing while systematically addressing these concerns, thereby advancing the understanding of insulin-like signaling in molluscan development and physiology.

Conclusion

In this study, we identified growth-related genes in the model mollusk M. lateralis and demonstrated that the insulin-like signaling pathway is central to its growth regulation. Most genes in this pathway were upregulated in fast-growing individuals, indicating their critical roles in growth control. Functional validation showed that the upstream genes mlILP and mlIRR positively regulate growth. Interestingly, their knockdown triggered the upregulation of downstream growth-promoting genes, suggesting a compensatory regulatory mechanism. These findings improve our understanding of insulin-like signaling in invertebrates and provide valuable molecular targets for the genetic improvement of aquaculture species.

Dynamic urban land surface monitoring in developing countries using remote sensing and machine learning: real-time investigational approach
Authors: Abdullah Ayub Khan, Asif Ali Laghari, Rex Bacarra, Jamil Abedalrahim Jamil Alsayaydeh, Roobaea Alroobaea, Sultan Algarni, Abdullah M. Baqasah, Majed Alsafyani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Land Surface Changes (LSC) are significantly impacted by dynamic environmental variations, particularly armed conflicts and associat-ed failures, according to a natural perspective. Regarding the current understanding of their relationship, on the other hand, human activi-ties on land usage or land covers may also increase the fluctuations. With a focus on the impacts of environmental restrictions like as land cover, temperature, air pollution, and sustainable water management, this study examines LSC in various metropolitan regions of developing nations. Recently, land cover has changed due to rapid urbanization and environmental pressures such socioeconomic and climatic shifts. We assess patterns of temporal LSC in the central urban regions of under-developing nations using Remote Sensing (RS) technologies, satellite data, and Geographic Information System (GIS). For example, Pakistan’s urbanized land surface is Karachi. We measure and identify the major changes in urban landscapes, as well as the factors related environmental variables, by using Machine Learning-enabled Random Forest Classification (RFC) for image classification and Support Vector Machine for change detection patterns. Critical trends in urban sprawl and deforestation are revealed by the simulation results of the proposed model, underscoring the necessity of sustainable planning for industrialized cities. Furthermore, the outcomes highlight the uniqueness of combining RFC and SVM to categories patterns in terms of change detections from 2000 to 2023, reaching 26.91% and 19.73% higher than previously state-of-the-art techniques.

Introduction

Urbanisation is happening at an unprecedented rate in underdeveloped areas due to population increase, socioeconomic volatility, and survivals from rural migrations1. Land cover changes brought forth by this rapid development frequently have an impact on human livelihoods as well as ecosystems as a whole2. Natural disasters, climate change, and resource shortages are examples of environmental fluctuations that disrupt the dynamics of urban development and are linked to the growth of urban expansion3. Analysis of such Land Surface Changes (LSC) is critical due to the creation of an effective hierarchy of urban planning, along with the management of land resources in a sustainable manner4. Previously, different methods of real-time monitoring of urbanization have been proposed, even several fall into further consideration due to time constraints and the complexities raised while the process of data collection, acquisition, and examination take place5.

On the other side, the advances received in Remote Sensing (RS) technology in terms of providing a reliable, efficient, robust, and powerful alternative as compared to the traditional data exchanging protocols6. In order to leverage satellite imagery and Geographic Information Systems (GIS), experts are able to collect and examine land use/land cover fluctuations in terms of surface changes over time7, as shown in Fig. 1. In this manner, this research is applied to the popular regions of under-developing countries, such as Karachi, offering insights that not only provide a time serious of LSC but also provide an estimation of all essential prospects to draw an informed decision making8,9.Fig. 1The existing lifecycle of satellite imagery and GIS.

The existing lifecycle of satellite imagery and GIS.

The primary objective of this paper is to investigate how LSC reacts to current environmental changes in metropolitan areas of developing nations. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the cooperation of RS and their data management protocols9,10—capturing, preprocessing, filtering, aggregation, examination, presentation, preservation, and ex-change—create a capable platform where we can read and analyse how the changes appear in specific land surface settings. Additionally, it aids in the assessment of deforestation and clarifies hierarchy and organisation in relation to the implications of sustainable smart city initiatives11. By evaluating patterns of urban sprawl, land cover transformation, and deforestation, this paper helps contribute to understanding the deeper prospects of environmental and socio-economic fluctuations that influence urban regions of undeveloped areas.Fig. 2The working cycle of RS technology.

It includes the extreme weather events, seasonal variations, and human-based activities. With the integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) based Machine Learning (ML)-enabled algorithms are proposed that make reading this technology more sophisticated in terms of evaluation, including land cover classification, land use detection, pattern matching from images of time constraints, etc.12,13. However, LSC derives on both prospects, including natural processing and human activities that significantly affect environmental sustainability and urban policymaking and planning. Examining the changes requires more efficient, effective, reliable, accurate, and robust methods of classification and pattern detection14. In fact, the diverse range of ML arrays, Random Forest (RF) classification, and Support Vector Machine (SVM) play an outstanding role in their effectiveness in the land use/ land change environment. In this whole cycle, RF leverages ensemble learning regarding image classification in order to improve the accuracy of prediction and robustness. On the other side, SVM excels in the management of large datasets of high-dimensional captured records and making clear boundaries of decision-making14,15. The integration of RF classification and SVM provides powerful tools for experts and stakeholders (who are part of the sustainable environment and water management ministry) to create regulations, apply monitoring, and understand the dynamic changes of regional surface, especially the areas of under-developing countries.

Motivation

In emerging countries, unplanned urbanisation has led to unprecedented limitations for certain urban regions. This type of expansion in LSC is caused by soil erosion, increased human activity, and deforestation. It is important to note that the reported changes are impacted by dynamic environmental variations, including extreme weather occurrences, climate updates, and other related activities, such human activity. However, the integration of RS makes it possible to monitor and investigate the highlighted changes in real-time over a wide geographic area. The absence of integration of real-time huge environmental data, time series spanning more than ten years, and the need for reactive rather than proactive techniques in the management of historical records are all problems facing contemporary urbanization and planning practices. The pressing necessity to close these identified research gaps is what inspired this work. The main opportunity is to provide urban planners and policymakers with an environment that supports a hierarchy of decision-making based on data in order to encourage resilience and sustainable development. Furthermore, the implementation of RS in urban planning procedures is encouraged by this study in order to help underdeveloped countries create more livable urban environments.

Related work

The primary motivation for implementing machine learning (ML) techniques is to assess the significant computational growth that arises from processing and classifying satellite imagery based on annual activities. This process results in a variety of unclassified sequences with missing values concerning changes in pattern from early 2000 to early 2020, necessitating a comprehensive comprehension of the effects of LSC responses to dynamic environmental fluctuations. The majority of recent research depends on RS temporal data to assess changes brought about by deforestation and human activity. Furthermore, the aforementioned prospects can be readily examined, correlated, and aggregated to aid in the categorization and change pattern detection processes16,17. These processes are not always simple because texture photos are captured while the data label signature is absent18–20. As a result, a lot of specialists are concerned about the aforementioned issue, particularly the need to take the low resolution of temporal imagery into account when creating an evaluation platform that is more robust, dependable, and efficient.

The next subsections elaborate on the recent work on land surface changes and responses to dynamic environmental fluctuation in urban areas, and the role of RS integration in LSC is proposed.

Conclusions and future directions

This paper discusses the existing dynamic environmental variations, particularly armed conflicts and their associated failures, that have a major influence on Land Surface Changes (LSC). However, given the widespread understanding of their relationship, human activities on land covers or usage may potentially increase variability. With an emphasis on how LSC impacts environmental constraints like temperature, air pollution, land cover, and the development of sustainable water management, this study investigates LSC in a variety of metropolitan contexts in developing countries. Rapid urbanisation, environmental pressures including socioeconomic and climatic shifts, and these factors combined have recently caused changes in land cover. We evaluate temporal LSC patterns across the major urban areas of emerging countries using satellite imagery, Geographic Information System (GIS), and Remote Sensing (RS) technology. Karachi is one example of Pakistan’s urbanised land area.

Using Support Vector Machine (SVM) for change detection patterns and Random Forest Classification (RFC) for image classification—both made possible by machine learning—we identify and quantify the factors that associate environmental variables with the changes received in urban environments. The simulation results of the proposed model highlight critical trends in urban sprawl and deforestation, emphasising the need for sustainable design for developed cities. Additionally, while merging RFC with SVM to classify and discover patterns regarding change detections between the years 2000 and 2023, the findings improve uniqueness compared to previous state-of-the-art techniques, reaching marks of 26.91% and 19.73%. The potential of using RS as a significant tool for dynamically monitoring land fluctuations is thus elaborated in this proposed work, along with the benefits of sharing information with related agencies, policy makers, and regulators regarding the implications of environmental fluctuations resistance in urban landscapes, particularly in underdeveloped regions.

But in order to provide a thorough picture of urban dynamics, the research’s future focus will be on improving RS procedures and strategy to integrate with other techniques that aid in managing socioeconomic data. This project will not only improve urbanization planning worldwide, but it will also support sustainable urban growth and fight climate change on a global scale.

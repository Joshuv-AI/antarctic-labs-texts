Plasma exosome-derived tsRNAs as a novel noninvasive biomarker for early detection and prognosis prediction of hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Lin Ding, Yini Li, Te Liu, Xinyi He, Ying Zhao, Baishen Pan, Wenjing Yang, Xinrong Yang, Wei Guo, Beili Wang
Published: 2026

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is frequently diagnosed at advanced stages, leading to poor prognosis.1 Current serum biomarkers, including alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) and protein induced by vitamin K absence-II (PIVKA-II), lack sufficient sensitivity and specificity, particularly for early-stage disease or patients with cirrhosis.2 Reliable and minimally invasive biomarkers are urgently required. Exosomes are nanoscale extracellular vesicles enriched in diverse small RNAs.3 Among them, transfer RNA-derived small RNAs (tsRNAs) are emerging as critical regulators of cancer development and progression,4 yet their landscape and clinical relevance in HCC remain unclear.5

Here, we performed the first large-scale, multicohort investigation of plasma exosomal tsRNAs in HCC. Using microarray screening followed by stepwise validation, we identified four HCC-specific tsRNAs with strong diagnostic and prognostic potential. These tsRNAs displayed a progressive increase from healthy donors (HD) to chronic hepatitis B with liver cirrhosis (CHB + LC), benign hepatic lesions (BHL), and ultimately HCC. Machine learning-based risk models demonstrated excellent accuracy in distinguishing HCC from high-risk populations, outperforming AFP and PIVKA-II, especially in biomarker-negative patients. Moreover, the tsRNA panel served as an independent predictor of time-to-recurrence, offering a minimally invasive approach for early detection, risk stratification, and clinical management.

Exosomes were isolated from the plasma of HD, LC, CHB, BHL, and HCC patients using gradient density centrifugation. Transmission electron microscopy confirmed the typical cup-shaped morphology (Fig. 1A). Nanoparticle tracking analysis showed diameters between 50 and 200 nm (Fig. 1B). Western blotting validated enrichment with positive markers TSG101, CD9, and CD81, and depletion of the negative marker Calnexin (Fig. 1C).

Small RNA microarray analysis, which used uniform differential expression criteria (|fold change| ≥ 1.5, P < 0.05), identified tsRNAs as the most distinctively altered RNA species between HCC and HD plasma exosomes, showing the strongest discriminatory clustering patterns among all small RNA classes (Fig. S1A–S1E). Volcano plot analysis identified the top 10 up-regulated tsRNAs in HCC (Fig. 1D). Verification in discovery cohort II confirmed significant up-regulation of multiple tsRNAs, including mt-tRF3-23-PheGAA, mt-tRF3-24-PheGAA, tRF3b-LeuTAG-2, and tRF5-50-GlyGCC-2 (Fig. S1F and S1G). These results established tsRNAs as promising exosome-enriched biomarkers for HCC detection.

Generalized linear model analysis indicated that a minimal set of four tsRNAs—mt-tRF3-23-PheGAA, mt-tRF3-24-PheGAA, tRF3b-LeuTAG-2, and tRF5-50-GlyGCC-2—achieved the highest diagnostic accuracy (Table S1). In the validation cohort (90 HD, 80 CHB + LC, 90 BHL, and 120 HCC), expression levels of these four tsRNAs increased stepwise from HD to CHB + LC, BHL, and HCC (Fig. 1E and F). Machine learning models were constructed using multiple algorithms. The support vector machine (SVM) performed best in differentiating HCC from high-risk individuals (Fig. 1G; Fig. S2B). Random split of the validation cohort into the training and test subsets confirmed significantly higher predicted scores in HCC patients than in high-risk controls (Fig. 1H). The SVM model identified 91.67% of HCC samples, achieving AUCs of 0.923 and 0.924 in the training and test sets, respectively (Fig. 1I–K; Fig. S2C–S2F).

Risk scores stratified HCC patients into high- and low-risk groups, correlating with advanced BCLC stage and satellite lesions (Table S2; Fig. S2G and S2H). The tsRNA scores consistently outperformed AFP, PIVKA-II, or their combination in distinguishing HCC from all controls and high-risk individuals (Fig. S2I; Fig. 1L). Among 120 HCC patients, 84 (70%) were early-stage (BCLC 0+A). The tsRNA panel exhibited excellent diagnostic accuracy for early-stage disease compared with AFP or PIVKA-II (Fig. S2J; Fig. 1N). Importantly, in AFP- or PIVKA-II-negative patients, the tsRNA panel retained high sensitivity (Fig. 1M). Differential diagnosis was improved for early-stage HCC, as well as for BHL and LC individuals, compared with traditional markers (Fig. 1O; Fig. S2K). The tsRNA panel demonstrated robust diagnostic performance across multiple independently trained binary classification models, each tailored to address distinct clinical diagnostic scenarios across the liver disease spectrum. Specifically, Figure S3A–S3E shows results of the model discriminating HCC from HD, Figure S3F–S3J corresponds to BHL versus LC, Figure S3K–S3O illustrates LC with CHB and BHL versus HCC, and Figure S3P–S3T depicts LC with CHB and BHL versus HD. These classification tasks reflect different clinical decision points: distinguishing malignant from non-malignant liver conditions, differentiating chronic liver disease subtypes, and identifying high-risk individuals who may benefit from intensified surveillance or further diagnostic workup. For external validation, we constructed 39 predictive models using a leave-one-out cross-validation framework. The random forest model achieved the highest concordance index (0.93) and robust performance across cohorts (Fig. 1P; Table S3). Risk scores derived from this model stratified patients into prognostic groups, with the high-risk group showing significantly shorter time-to-recurrence (training, P < 0.001; test, P = 0.048) (Fig. 1Q). Multivariate Cox regression confirmed the tsRNA risk score as an independent prognostic factor, even after adjusting for established clinical variables and biomarkers (Fig. 1R; Table S4).

In conclusion, we established the first plasma exosomal tsRNA-based diagnostic and prognostic model in HCC. The four-tsRNA panel showed excellent accuracy compared with conventional serum biomarkers, particularly for early-stage and biomarker-negative patients. It is noteworthy that tsRNAs, though abundant in exosomes, have been largely underexplored compared with miRNAs; our study highlights their unique clinical utility. In summary, plasma exosomal tsRNAs represent a powerful minimally invasive biomarker class, enabling early detection, risk stratification, and prognosis prediction in HCC, and offering new avenues for personalized management.

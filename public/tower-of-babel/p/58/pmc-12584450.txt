Application of multi-scale dynamic enhancement based on deep neural network and CT urinary tract secretory phase image fusion in the diagnosis of urinary system diseases
Authors: Yuzhe Liang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study proposes a multi-scale dynamic enhancement solution to the clinical implementation bottleneck problem caused by data heterogeneity and small lesion characteristics in urinary system CT (Computed Tomography) diagnosis. A multi-center standardized CT urinary tract dataset is constructed at the data level, and double-blind annotation and elastic deformation enhancement are used. At the network architecture level, a dual-branch 3D convolutional network consisting of a ResNet-18 (Residual Network with 18 layers) plain scan branch and a DenseNet-121 (Densely Connected Convolutional Network with 121 layers) secretory branch is designed to achieve multi-phase feature fusion through a cross-attention mechanism. At the optimization level, an anatomical constraint attention module is introduced at the renal pelvis-ureter junction, and channel pruning is used to achieve lightweight deployment. Experimental results show that the lightweight dual-branch attention model in this paper maintains high performance (Dice 89.7%, IoU 82.9%) while reducing the computational cost to 48.8GFLOPs and the inference speed to 47.9ms. The model accuracy is improved by 1.6% compared with ViT-B/16, and the F1-score is improved by 3.2% compared with EfficientNet-B3. The lightweight dual-branch attention model in this paper can be applied to the clinical diagnosis process, improve the intelligent diagnosis level of urinary system CT images, and has a milestone significance for promoting the development of precision medicine.Keywords: Urinary system CT diagnosis, Multi-scale dynamic enhancement, Dual-branch 3D convolutional network, Lightweight deployment, Cross-attention mechanism

Introduction

Urinary system diseases are common and frequently occurring diseases that seriously threaten human health, and their accurate diagnosis faces major clinical challenges. According to the latest epidemiological survey of the World Health Organization, about 10% of the world’s population suffers from urinary system diseases, and the incidence of serious complications caused by delayed diagnosis is as high as 15–20%. Currently, multi-slice spiral CT urinary tract imaging [1, 2] has been established as the gold standard for the diagnosis of urinary system diseases [3, 4] by international authoritative guidelines due to its submillimeter spatial resolution and multi-planar reconstruction capabilities. In particular, it has irreplaceable value in the diagnosis of the anatomical structure and pathological characteristics of diseases, such as urinary stones, tumors, and congenital malformations [5]. In actual clinical applications, CTU (CT Urography) diagnostic systems still face severe challenges. Multicenter clinical data [6] shows that due to differences in CT equipment models (ranging from 16-row to dual-source CT) and scanning parameters (layer thickness 0.5–5 mm, reconstruction algorithm differences, etc.) among medical institutions, there is a significant shift in the distribution of image features. This causes the performance of existing AI (Artificial Intelligence) diagnostic [7] systems to decline when applied across institutions. For the detection of early microlesions, especially 3–5 mm isodense stones and early urothelial carcinoma [8], the existing systems have insufficient sensitivity and a high misdiagnosis rate. This is mainly because the characteristic expression of microlesions in CT images is weak, and they are easily affected by the partial volume effect. At the same time, although deep learning technology [9] has made great progress, existing 3D convolutional neural network models generally have the defects of high computational complexity and slow inference speed. Their typical computing load exceeds 100GFLOPs; the inference delay is more than 150ms; the video memory occupancy usually requires 8-12GB. This not only makes it difficult to meet the needs of clinical real-time diagnosis, but also seriously limits its promotion and application in areas with underdeveloped medical resources. These technical bottlenecks directly affect the homogenization and standardization of clinical diagnosis. The difference in diagnostic compliance between tertiary hospitals and primary hospitals can reach 20–35%. The realization rate of real-time interactive diagnosis is less than 40%, highlighting the technical difficulties that need to be overcome in the current field of urinary system imaging diagnosis. To improve the reproducibility of the research, this study provides access to the code and dataset. The code is shared through a public GitHub repository after the research paper is published to ensure that other researchers can reproduce the results of this study. The specific code access link is provided in a future update. Solving these key problems has great clinical value and social significance for improving the early diagnosis rate of diseases [10], reducing the risk of medical misdiagnosis, and promoting the implementation of high-quality medical resources.

This study achieves a systematic breakthrough in the intelligent diagnosis of urinary system CT by building a complete technical innovation chain of “data-algorithm-engineering”. At the data level, the first cross-vendor, multi-center standard CT urinary tract imaging dataset is established, covering the imaging data of mainstream CT equipment worldwide. To address the inherent feature distribution shift caused by heterogeneous CT devices and acquisition protocols, the preprocessing pipeline incorporates device-specific response curve calibration to align baseline intensity values across vendors, histogram matching to unify grayscale distribution, and rigid registration for spatial consistency. These steps operate in sequence to minimize structural and contrast discrepancies at the voxel level. During network optimization, the maximum mean discrepancy constraint enforces alignment between plain scan and secretory phase feature distributions originating from different centers, while the multi-task loss jointly optimizes classification, lesion segmentation, and anatomical localization to reduce residual inter-domain variability. The dual-branch fusion further mitigates heterogeneity by learning complementary phase-specific representations that remain stable across hardware platforms and acquisition settings, forming a continuous adaptation mechanism from input normalization to high-level feature integration.The data quality and generalization capability are improved through a double-blind labeling process and elastic deformation enhancement technology based on a biomechanical model. At the algorithm level, a dual-branch dynamic feature fusion architecture is proposed, using an improved ResNet-18 to process plain scan images and extract high-attenuation features such as calcification, which in this context denotes sharply delineated hyperdense regions within non-contrast CT scans corresponding to urinary stones or mineralized tumor components. The DenseNet-121 branch processes secretory phase CT images to extract enhancement pattern features, modeling both the temporal evolution and spatial distribution of contrast medium within renal pelvis, ureter, and associated mucosal structures, thereby highlighting subtle lesions within the urinary tract wall and adjacent tissues. An innovative cross-attention mechanism is used to achieve intelligent fusion of multi-phase features, and a 3D CBAM module combined with urological anatomical knowledge is embedded in the renal pelvis-ureter junction to improve the detection sensitivity of micro-lesions less than 3 mm. At the engineering implementation level, the paper develops a progressive pruning algorithm based on channel importance evaluation and combines it with TensorRT’s INT8 quantization technology. The final model improves the inference speed and reduces the memory usage while maintaining diagnostic performance. It is successfully deployed in the urology clinical workstations of five tertiary hospitals, including Peking Union Medical College Hospital. The system has also improved the detection rate of urinary stones less than 2 mm, shortened the average diagnosis time, and demonstrated significant advantages in the rapid assessment of patients with emergency renal colic, effectively solving the pain point of insufficient urology specialists in primary hospitals. These technological innovations have established new standards for the diagnosis of urinary system diseases, and its technical route of “multimodal fusion-anatomical prior embedding-lightweight deployment” has provided a replicable paradigm for the development of other medical imaging AI systems.

Related works

In recent years, the research on artificial intelligence technology in the field of urinary system disease diagnosis has shown a trend of development with multiple technical routes, but each method still has key technical defects that need to be solved. For example, in the Med4 journal, the literature on the application of artificial intelligence in urological image diagnosis (10.1002/med4.62) provides important references in related fields. In the field of single-phase image analysis [11], the UroSAM (Uro-Segment Anything Model) architecture [12] developed by Leng J et al. performed well in endoscopic video kidney stone detection, but it failed to effectively integrate the diagnostic information of multi-phase CT images.Wang et al. [13] proposed a neural network framework based on multi-view fusion, which automatically segmented the tumor area of glioma using the spatial information of adjacent CT images. It reduced the amount of computation while maintaining high segmentation accuracy, and verified its effectiveness through experiments.Clinical studies have shown that nearly half of the important lesions require multi-phase joint analysis for accurate diagnosis. In terms of feature extraction, the multi-scale feature pyramid network designed by Zhang B [14] improved the accuracy of segmenting target organs from blurred boundary areas and low-contrast adjacent organs, but the segmentation effect of small tubular structures unique to the urinary system was still not ideal. The deformable constraint transfer network developed by Lin W [15] adaptively extracted aortic features to define constraint attributes within the image and guided topological implementation in space, providing new ideas for the design of attention mechanisms, but was not specifically optimized for the urinary system. The dual-path network proposed by Wang J’s team attempted to overcome this limitation. It accurately segmented variable-position lesions or organs with significant differences through a slight dual-path network [16]. However, the simple series connection method adopted by the team results in high model computation and low feature fusion efficiency. Bala S et al. innovatively applied the Vision Transformer architecture [17] to enhance brain tumor detection and classification. Although a high accuracy rate was achieved, its high requirement for GPU (Graphics Processing Unit) video memory seriously restricted its clinical applicability.Liu et al. [18] introduced an improved skip connection and Transformer score matrix enhancement mechanism based on TransUNet and proposed the TransUNet + model, which significantly improved the accuracy of medical image (especially small organ) segmentation and global feature extraction capabilities.

From the perspective of technology development, existing research shows an evolutionary trend from single-phase analysis to multi-phase fusion, from general models to dedicated architectures, and from pure precision pursuit to efficiency consideration. Foundational architectures such as ResNet [He et al., 2016] [5] and DenseNet [Huang et al., 2018] [1] have laid the groundwork for hierarchical feature learning in medical imaging, while recent advances like the Vision Transformer (ViT) [Dosovitskiy et al., 2020] [2] have introduced self-attention mechanisms to enhance contextual feature extraction in CT diagnosis. Wang et al. [19] proposed a universal error correction framework to simultaneously optimize organ segmentation and dose prediction tasks in radiotherapy. By combining the prediction network with the calibration network, they surpassed the performance of existing methods in both tasks. Zhao et al. [20] proposed a universal error correction framework to simultaneously optimize organ segmentation and dose prediction tasks in radiotherapy. By combining the prediction network with the calibration network, they surpassed the performance of existing methods in both tasks.This evolution has obvious faults and lacks organic connection between various technical links. In the field of model compression [21], the progressive channel pruning strategy [22] proposed by Duan Y et al. significantly reduced model parameters while maintaining high accuracy, but inevitably led to accuracy loss and did not consider the spatial distribution characteristics of urinary system lesions. The TensorRT quantization scheme [23] of the Bishnoi V team enabled real-time deployment of the model in applications, opening up a new path for clinical deployment of the model, but it was prone to feature distortion in low-contrast lesion areas. The MEC (Medical Edge Computing) task scheduling and resource allocation mechanism proposed by Sharif Z [24] assigned different priorities to different tasks, but was not yet deeply integrated with the specific workflow of urology. Classic architectures such as ResNet and DenseNet have laid the foundation for hierarchical feature extraction of medical images. By introducing residual connections and dense connection mechanisms, they effectively alleviate the problems of gradient vanishing and parameter redundancy [25, 26]. In recent years, the Transformer structure has been introduced into medical image recognition tasks. Representative works such as ViT have improved the context modeling ability of complex lesion structures through multi-head self-attention mechanisms [27]. In the field of radiomics, studies have used deep learning and CT image fusion to establish a prediction model to assist in tumor metastasis risk assessment and survival analysis, verifying the effectiveness of combining image-pathology information [28, 29].

These studies reflect the common challenges in the current field of AI diagnosis of the urinary system. The multi-phase feature fusion mechanism is not perfect; the attention module lacks anatomical structure-specific design; it is difficult to strike a balance between accuracy and efficiency during model optimization. These innovative methods only optimize specific technical links and lack systematic solutions. They are obviously insufficient in dealing with special anatomical structures of the urinary system [30], and the synergy between various optimization strategies needs to be strengthened. This research status has prompted people to develop more comprehensive technical solutions. A feature interaction mechanism based on contrast agent dynamics is established, which models the enhancement patterns at different stages through temporal attention. The spatial relationships of key anatomical landmarks in the urinary system are encoded as learnable constraints, guiding the network to focus on the clinical focus area. A mixed precision quantization method for lesion perception can be developed to achieve real-time reasoning while maintaining the ability to detect small lesions. By deeply integrating the cross-attention mechanism [31, 32] with anatomical constraints, an end-to-end intelligent diagnosis system [33] is constructed to achieve an organic combination of algorithm innovation and clinical needs, providing a new paradigm for the accurate diagnosis of urinary system diseases.

Construction of multi-scale dynamic enhancement network

Construction of cross-center CT urinary tract dataset

This study establishes a strict multi-center data collection protocol to ensure the comparability of CT images from different medical institutions. The participating institutions include five top tertiary hospitals in China, including Peking Union Medical College Hospital and Shanghai Ruijin Hospital, all of which have internationally certified radiology quality control systems. In terms of equipment selection, only three mainstream high-end CT devices are used: Siemens Somatom Definition AS+, GE Discovery CT750 HD, and Philips Brilliance iCT [34]. The spatial resolution of these devices reaches more than 15 lp/cm, which meets the detection requirements of microlesions.

In terms of scanning parameter settings, a unified imaging protocol is used. The tube voltage is fixed at 120 kV; the tube current automatic adjustment range is limited to 180–220 mAs; the spiral scanning mode is adopted, with a pitch of 0.8–1.2 and a rotation time of 0.5 s/rot. The reconstruction parameters are uniformly set to 1 mm slice thickness, 0.8 mm reconstruction interval, and the standard volume convolution kernel B30f for Siemens, Standard for GE, and B for Philips [35]. All cases must include complete plain scan and secretory phase dual-phase images, where the secretory phase scan time window is strictly controlled within 5–8 min after the injection of contrast agent 350mgI/mL iohexol and is supervised and executed by a dedicated team of technicians. Subtle differences in scanning parameters between different devices will affect image performance. Voltage differences mainly change the contrast performance between tissues, current settings affect the noise level, and layer thickness differences change the volume averaging effect of small lesions. These differences will cause systematic shifts in the distribution of image features in uncorrected data. The three-stage preprocessing process used in this article took these factors into consideration when designing. The baseline intensity drift caused by voltage and current differences is eliminated through device response curve calibration. The grayscale distribution of reconstructed data with different layer thicknesses is unified through grayscale histogram matching. Spatial consistency is then ensured through rigid registration, thereby reducing structural and contrast distribution differences at the voxel level and ensuring consistency of multi-center data. The standardization and unification of these parameters can help ensure the consistency and comparability of data in multi-center data acquisition, and provide a reliable basis for subsequent research or clinical applications such as diagnosis of urinary system diseases based on these data. The inclusion criteria for cases include: ① patients with urinary system diseases confirmed by pathology; ② complete dual-phase CT examination; ③ no severe motion artifacts. A total of 1,024 valid cases are collected, including 326 cases of renal cell carcinoma, 278 cases of urothelial carcinoma, and 420 cases of urinary tract stones. The distribution of cases fully considers the disease subtypes. Renal cell carcinoma include major subtypes such as clear cell carcinoma and papillary carcinoma. Stone cases include different components such as uric acid stones and calcium oxalate stones and samples from different locations such as the renal pelvis and ureter.

Data annotation is completed by a team of deputy chief physicians with more than 10 years of experience in urinary system imaging diagnosis. ITK-SNAP 3.8.0 professional medical image annotation software [36, 37] is used, supporting accurate annotation under three-dimensional multi-plane reconstruction. The annotation content is divided into levels.

The stone annotation uses the point marking method to annotate all visible stones with a diameter greater than or equal to 1 mm. When marking, the location of the stone should be confirmed in the transverse, sagittal and coronal views, and the maximum and minimum diameter measurements should be recorded. For multiple stones, each stone should be marked and numbered separately.

The tumor area is marked using the volume delineation method, requiring precise tracking of the tumor boundary. Semi-automatic segmentation tools [38] are used to assist in marking, and the initial contour is automatically generated by the software, which is then manually corrected by the physician. The boundary area between the tumor and normal tissues is of particular concern, and the boundary error is required to be controlled within 1 pixel. For tumors with obvious heterogeneity, different component areas such as internal necrotic areas and solid areas need to be marked.

Anatomical structure segmentation and annotation are targeted at key urinary tract structures such as the renal pelvis and ureter. A hierarchical annotation strategy is adopted to first annotate the large-scale anatomical structures and then finely segment the key parts such as the ureteropelvic junction (UPJ) [39]. The annotation is based on the Standards of Urinary System Imaging Anatomy to ensure that it conforms to the actual clinical anatomical relationship. To ensure the quality of annotation, a strict double-blind verification process [40] is implemented. Regarding the consistency of small lesion annotation, we used the intraclass correlation coefficient and Cohen’s Kappa statistical methods to quantitatively evaluate the consistency of the three annotators for lesions with a diameter less than 3 mm. The results showed that the intraclass correlation coefficient for stone diameter measurement among the three annotators was 0.91, and the Kappa value for tumor boundary consistency was 0.87, both of which were at high levels. Further error analysis showed that the vast majority of differences were within 0.5 mm and did not significantly shift the distribution of lesions in the final training set. These results indicate that the data annotation was highly reliable.After two physicians independently complete the annotation of all cases, a third senior professor of radiology arbitrates. For cases with inconsistent annotations, a three-party consultation is organized to reach a consensus. The final annotation results are compared with the pathological diagnosis or clinical follow-up results of at least 6 months to ensure the annotation accuracy.

Data preprocessing uses a standardized process dedicated to medical imaging. The N4ITK bias field correction algorithm [41] is used to eliminate the intensity inhomogeneity in CT images. Although originally developed for MRI to correct low-frequency field inhomogeneity, the algorithm is adapted for CT by tuning the bias field smoothing parameter to account for detector-specific gain variations and by constraining the convergence tolerance to suppress noise amplification in high-contrast structures. The primary source of low-frequency intensity drift in multi-center CT originates from variations in detector calibration and reconstruction kernel characteristics rather than coil-induced artifacts. The adapted implementation processes CT volumes in isotropic resolution after resampling, ensuring uniform bias estimation across all orientations. Validation on multi-vendor CT scans confirms consistent baseline intensity restoration without introducing structural distortions, demonstrating that the method is applicable to CT when configured for modality-specific bias sources.This algorithm estimates and corrects the bias field through iterative optimization and is applicable to images collected by different CT devices. Design of dual-branch feature extractor

The plain scan branch is deeply modified based on the classic ResNet-18 architecture to adapt to the three-dimensional characteristics of urinary system CT images. The 2D convolution layers of the original ResNet-18 are all replaced with 3D convolution operations, maintaining the same network depth and connection mode. At the front end of the network, a large 7 × 7 × 7 convolution kernel is used for initial feature extraction, combined with a 3 × 3 × 3 maximum pooling layer to effectively capture the basic anatomical structure features in the plain scan image. The dual-branch network architecture is shown in Fig. 2.

Dual-branch network architecture

Figure 2 clearly shows the bidirectional data flow from ResNet-18 and DenseNet-121 branches into the cross-attention module, followed by feature fusion and final multi-task diagnostic output, providing an intuitive understanding of the entire processing pipeline. The main part of the network contains four residual blocks, each of which consists of two 3 × 3 × 3 convolutional layers with 64, 128, 256, and 512 channels, respectively.A sensitivity analysis of the number of residual blocks showed that while reducing the number of residual blocks reduced computational effort and memory usage, it also resulted in decreased sensitivity and increased localization error in detecting small lesions. Increasing the number of residual blocks improved the capture of detailed features, but the computational cost increased significantly, and the performance improvement was no longer noticeable. A comprehensive comparison of experimental results from different configurations revealed that the current four-layer residual architecture offered the best balance between accuracy and computational efficiency and was therefore selected as the final choice.To adapt to the characteristics of medical images, three key improvements are made to the traditional residual block: a learnable 1 × 1 × 1 convolutional layer is added to the jump connection to achieve a smooth transition of feature dimensions. A 3D batch normalization layer is added after each residual block, and GroupNorm (Group Normalization) is used instead of traditional BN (Batch Normalization) to solve the problem of small batch training. LeakyReLU is used to replace standard ReLU to retain more negative information.

To construct the residual connection, a 1 × 1 × 1 convolution layer is used. The terminal structure of the original model is improved by removing the fully connected layer and replacing it with a three-level 3D convolution feature extraction module. The first level uses a 3 × 3 × 3 convolution kernel with 64 output channels and a stride of 1. The second level still uses a 3 × 3 × 3 convolution kernel; the number of channels is increased to 128; the stride is 2 to achieve downsampling. The third level convolution kernel remains unchanged, and the number of channels is further increased to 256. Each level of convolution is followed by batch normalization and ReLU activation function. Finally, a 256-dimensional feature vector is generated through 3D global average pooling. This design improves the compactness of feature expression while retaining spatial structure information. The specific Formula 4 is shown in the appendix.

The secretory branch uses a 3D DenseNet-121 architecture and is systematically optimized for the characteristics of contrast-enhanced images. The input layer uses a 5 × 5 × 5 enlarged convolution kernel with a 2 × 2 × 2 stride sampling to effectively adapt to the anisotropic voxel spacing of CT images. The initial convolution is followed by a maximum pooling layer with a pooling kernel of 3 × 3 × 3 and a stride of 2 × 2 × 2, reducing computational complexity while retaining key information.

The design decision to choose ResNet-18 for plain scan images and DenseNet-121 for secretory phase images stems from the different characteristics of these two imaging phases. The plain scan phase mainly contains basic anatomical structure information, and the residual learning ability of ResNet-18 is well suited to extract these basic features while having low computational complexity. On the other hand, secretory phase images contain dynamic contrast information, which requires a more complex feature extraction model such as DenseNet-121. DenseNet is particularly suitable for capturing the complex changes and details caused by these contrast agents through its densely connected hierarchical structure, which is crucial for accurate lesion detection. Combining the two models can play to their respective strengths and ensure the best performance at each stage while balancing the computational burden of the network.Different input sizes significantly affect the performance of branch feature extraction. Larger input sizes contain more anatomical context, enhancing sensitivity to complex lesions but significantly increasing computational overhead. Smaller input sizes, while maintaining computational efficiency, reduce the ability to capture subtle lesions. In this study’s experiments, the input size was gradually increased from 128 × 128 × 64 to 256 × 256 × 128. Results showed that the model achieved the best balance between accuracy and computational cost at 192 × 192 × 96.

The network contains 4 dense blocks, each of which has 6 densely connected layers. Different from the traditional DenseNet, each dense layer in this study adopts a Bottleneck structure, which first reduces the dimension through 1 × 1 × 1 convolution and then performs 3 × 3 × 3 main convolution. Between adjacent dense blocks, this module consists of three parallel pooling paths: 1 × 1 × 1 global average pooling is used to retain overall features; 2 × 2 × 2 pooling extracts medium-scale features; 4 × 4 × 4 pooling focuses on local details. The output of each path is spliced after channel adjustment through 1 × 1 × 1 convolution to achieve multi-scale feature fusion. The specific Formula 5 is shown in the appendix.

GAP is global average pooling. A triple attention mechanism is designed at the end of the network. The channel attention module generates channel weights through 3D global average pooling and uses two layers of MLP (Multilayer Perceptron) to model the relationship between channels, and the spatial attention module uses 7 × 7 × 7 convolution to generate a spatial weight map to highlight the key areas. 3D convolutional attention gating multiplies the outputs of the first two with the original features to achieve adaptive feature enhancement. An auxiliary classifier is added after the last dense block to improve the efficiency of gradient propagation through intermediate supervision.In an experimental comparison of different feature fusion timings, the early fusion strategy, which combines the two branches at the shallow feature stage, achieved a Dice of 88.6% and an inference time of 51.4 milliseconds. The late fusion strategy, which combines the two branches after each branch has independently extracted deep features, achieved a Dice of 90.1% and an inference time of 49.7 milliseconds. In comparison, the mid-term fusion strategy, which utilizes a cross-attention mechanism, achieved a combined Dice of 90.3% and an inference time of 49.8 milliseconds, striking a balance between detection accuracy and efficiency. Based on these results, this study chose to perform feature fusion at the mid-term stage.The cross-attention mechanism is used in the feature fusion stage to achieve deep interaction between the plain scan and secretion period features. In the traditional cross-attention mechanism, weighting is usually performed based on global similarity, ignoring the spatial distribution of anatomical structures and the correlation between regions in medical images. The method in this paper combines the anatomical features of the urinary system, such as the kidneys, ureters, and renal pelvis, and uses an adaptive attention weighting mechanism to more accurately capture the key feature differences between images at different stages.

In this model, a cross-attention mechanism is used (see the Appendix for the detailed Formula 6) to perform weighted fusion of image features at different stages. In simple terms, the similarity between each feature is calculated, and based on this, more weight is assigned to important features. In this way, the model can focus on the most important part for disease diagnosis [43].

Different from traditional self-attention, this paper introduces two key improvements. Position encoding uses learnable 3D sinusoidal position embedding to integrate spatial coordinate information into feature representation. The multi-head attention mechanism is divided into 8 heads, and each head processes a 64-dimensional subspace and finally spliced into a full-dimensional feature.

In cross-attention dimensionality testing, different Q, K, and V subspace dimensionality configurations showed significant differences. Smaller dimensionality reduced computational cost but lost some spatial information during feature interactions; larger dimensionality improved fine-grained feature representation but resulted in training instability and significant resource consumption. Comparing the performance and efficiency of different dimensionality configurations, we found that a 64-dimensional configuration per head achieved the optimal balance between accuracy and efficiency, and thus adopted this configuration as the final solution.

Different relationships between features are captured through parallel computation of multiple “heads”. Each head processes a subspace of features and finally merges their outputs to obtain a more comprehensive feature representation (see the appendix for the specific Formula 7). In an experimental comparison of different head counts, 4 heads minimized computational overhead but lacked global context capture. 16 heads provided more detailed feature representation but significantly increased memory usage and inference time. A comprehensive comparison of Dice and IoU metrics with GFLOPs consumption revealed that 8 heads offered the optimal trade-off, maintaining acceptable computational resource consumption while improving performance.To improve the fusion effect, the network training adopts a multi-task learning strategy. In addition to the main classification task, three auxiliary tasks are added. The MMD (Maximum Mean Discrepancy) loss is used to implement the consistency constraint of plain scan-secretion phase features. The Dice loss is used to locate the key anatomical structure, and the Focal loss is used to segment the lesion area.

These auxiliary tasks include kernel function mapping, plane scanning features, secretion period features, category weights, and focus parameters. They help improve the performance of the main task by sharing the underlying feature extractor. The entire network adopts a progressive training strategy: first, each branch is trained separately; then, the fusion module is jointly optimized; finally, the end-to-end fine-tuning is performed to ensure the model’s convergence and stability. Dynamic attention mechanism optimization

This paper constructs a dedicated 3D anatomical segmentation network based on the improved U-Net + + architecture [44] to achieve accurate segmentation of key structures of the urinary system. The network input is 512 × 512×n 3D CT volume data, and a five-layer encoder-decoder structure is used. The encoder part uses 3 × 3 × 3 convolution with 2 × 2 × 2 maximum pooling for downsampling, and the number of channels in each layer is doubled. The decoder uses transposed convolution to achieve upsampling and integrates jump connections from the encoder at each scale. Four sub-U-Nets of different depths are nested in the network, and the deep supervision mechanism is used to ensure the effective learning of features at all levels.

In terms of training strategy, a multi-stage progressive method is adopted. In the first stage, a strong data enhancement pre-training network is used. In the second stage, a curriculum learning strategy is adopted to first learn the obvious anatomical structure of simple samples and then gradually increase the difficulty of fuzzy boundary conditions. In the third stage, adversarial training is implemented to improve the authenticity of the segmentation boundaries through the discriminator network. The labeled data is completed by three radiologists, who finely labels the renal pelvis, ureter, bladder, and other structures, with special attention paid to the boundary determination of the key area of the renal pelvis-ureter junction. The results achieved by the final model on the independent test set provide reliable anatomical priors for the subsequent attention mechanism.

The improved 3D CBAM attention module is embedded in the key position of the feature extraction network. This module contains the attention mechanism in two dimensions: channel and space. The channel attention submodule performs three-dimensional global average pooling on the input feature map to obtain the global representation of each channel.

The channel weights are transformed nonlinearly through two layers of fully connected networks, and the Sigmoid function is used to generate weights between 0 and 1. The specific formula is shown in Appendix Formula (1). To enhance the modeling of inter-channel relationships, layer normalization and Swish activation functions are added between fully connected layers. The spatial attention submodule uses a 7 × 7 × 7 3D convolution kernel to process feature maps and capture long-range spatial dependencies through a large receptive field.

This module uses a three-dimensional convolution operation. Different from the traditional CBAM, this study introduces a residual connection in the spatial attention path to add the original features to the attention-weighted features to alleviate the gradient vanishing problem. GroupNorm normalization and LeakyReLU activation are used after convolution to improve training stability. The specific formula is shown in Appendix Formula (2). The generation of the spatial attention map adopts a channel compression strategy, reducing the number of channels to 1/8 through 1 × 1 × 1 convolution, applying 7 × 7 × 7 convolution and sigmoid activation. This paper uses the probability map output by the anatomical segmentation network as the prior constraint of spatial attention, as shown in Appendix Formula (3).

In the specific implementation, the UPJ segmentation probability map is downsampled to the same size as the feature map and weighted fused with the original spatial attention map. The weight coefficient is designed as a learnable parameter and automatically optimized through back propagation. The schematic diagram of the attention mechanism is shown in Fig. 3.

In key anatomical areas such as the renal pelvis and ureter, the system automatically enhances feature responses and suppresses irrelevant features in non-interest areas, so that the network’s attention is more focused on diagnosis-related areas. To address the problem of uneven quality of clinical CT images, a dynamic adjustment strategy based on image quality assessment is designed. In addition to using the NRSS metric, the dynamic attention adjustment strategy also incorporates signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) as an evaluation metric, establishing a mapping between image quality scores and attention parameters. Results show that as the SNR decreases, the convolution kernel size and channel compression ratio are adjusted accordingly to enhance stability in high-noise conditions. When the SNR is high, the convolution kernel size is kept small to maintain edge refinement. Experimental results

Effectiveness of microlesion detection

In the field of urinary system disease research, accurate evaluation of the detection effectiveness of microlesion detection is crucial. To present the complex relationship between lesion size, detection rate, and positioning error, the role of Grad-CAM (Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping) focus is considered, and a three-dimensional scatter plot is introduced. Figure 4 shows the changing trend of detection rate and positioning error under different lesion sizes from a multi-dimensional perspective and reflects the Grad-CAM focus through color coding, providing a visual basis for in-depth understanding of the performance of small lesion detection.

3D graph of small lesion detection performance

Figure 4 presents the relationship between lesion size, detection rate, and positioning error through a 3D scatter plot, and the color of the scatter plot reflects the focus of Grad-CAM. From the overall trend, the lesion size is positively correlated with the detection rate. As the lesion size increases on the horizontal axis, most of the scattered points show an upward trend in the vertical direction of the detection rate. When the lesion size is small, most of the scattered points are concentrated in the area with low detection rate. When the lesion size becomes larger, a large number of scattered points appear in the position with high detection rate. The lesion size is negatively correlated with the positioning error. As the lesion size increases, the positioning error value corresponding to the scattered points tends to decrease. There is also a certain correlation between Grad-CAM focus and lesion size.Analysis of detection performance across different anatomical locations showed the highest accuracy in the renal pelvis, with the upper ureter in the middle and the lower ureter performing the worst. The renal pelvis is easier to identify due to its high contrast, while the upper ureter suffers from some missed detections due to its smaller diameter. The lower ureter suffers from increased false positives due to motion artifacts and interference from adjacent tissue. By incorporating anatomical position weighting into the attention mechanism, the model’s performance in the lower ureter was improved, but overall results still indicate that positional differences represent a potential area for further optimization. In general, as the lesion size increases, the Grad-CAM focus increases. The regression plane further highlights the linear trend of the positioning error with the lesion size. A closer look can reveal that some of the scattered points do not conform to the above trend. There are some scattered points with large lesion sizes but relatively low detection rates. This is mainly due to the intrinsic characteristics of the data and various external factors. In the data generation process, the simulated data introduces random noise, which causes individual data points to deviate from the overall positive correlation trend due to random factors. In actual detection scenarios, similar random interference also exists widely. Accidental electromagnetic interference during scanning and momentary instability of imaging equipment may lead to the failure to accurately detect large-sized lesions during detection.

In the detection process, there are many other factors that affect the detection rate. The accuracy of detection equipment varies, and equipment with different accuracy has different abilities to identify lesions. The parameter settings during detection, such as the resolution and contrast of the imaging, can also affect the detection results. The special properties of the lesion itself, such as whether the lesion’s shape is regular and whether the density is uniform, can also affect the detection rate. These factors are intertwined and work together in the actual detection process, causing the detection rate of some large lesions to fail to meet the overall trend expectations, resulting in a discrete distribution of scattered points. However, judging from the overall situation presented by a large number of data points, the positive correlation between lesion size and detection rate is dominant.In comparative experiments involving images of varying quality, the high-quality group maintained optimal performance, with an average Dice score of 90.5%. The medium-quality group experienced a slight decline, with an average Dice score of 88.2%. The low-quality group experienced the most significant performance drop, with an average Dice score of 84.1%. This performance degradation is primarily due to increased noise and blurred boundaries in low-quality images, which causes the model’s spatial attention mechanism to mistakenly focus on non-lesion areas, resulting in decreased sensitivity and an increase in false negatives.

In the field of medical detection research, the discovery of this relationship is of great significance. For clinical practice and research, it can help doctors and researchers evaluate the performance of detection algorithms on lesions of different sizes. Small-sized lesions are difficult to detect, with low detection rates and large positioning errors, suggesting that the detection algorithm needs to be optimized to improve the detection capability of small lesions. From the perspective of algorithm development, researchers can adjust algorithm parameters in a targeted manner based on the correlation between lesion size and detection results, so that the algorithm can perform better in detecting lesions of different sizes. The relationship between Grad-CAM focus and lesion size provides direction for the algorithm’s focus characteristics, helps enhance the algorithm’s visualization and interpretability, and improves the algorithm’s reliability and practicality in actual medical scenarios. Paying attention to the inconsistent trend in the scattered points can prompt researchers to explore the complex factors that affect the detection results, improve the detection model, and make the algorithm performance more stable and reliable.

Experimental discussion

This study, through systematic experimental verification and in-depth theoretical analysis, confirms that the deep learning model based on the lightweight dual-branch attention mechanism has significant clinical transformation value in the field of urinary system CT imaging diagnosis. The dual-branch parallel processing architecture adopted by the model realizes deep feature fusion of multi-phase CT images through feature complementarity analysis in the spatiotemporal dimension and cross-modal information interaction, and builds a dynamic feature selection mechanism that conforms to the logic of clinical diagnosis. Its core innovation lies in the attention mechanism guided by anatomical prior knowledge, which improves the localization accuracy of sub-centimeter-level tiny lesions to a clinical practical level. This directly solves the key problem of the high missed diagnosis rate of traditional methods in complex anatomical areas and provides reliable support for clinical decision-making.

In terms of lightweight design, the study reveals the multi-level parameter redundancy that is prevalent in medical imaging neural networks and proposes a structured channel pruning strategy that breaks through the bottleneck of traditional compression methods in terms of accuracy loss in lesion-sensitive areas. This “parameter optimization under anatomical constraints” paradigm has universal value and provides a transferable technical framework for lightweight design of different medical imaging tasks. The stable performance of the model on multi-center data sets verifies the key supporting role of this design strategy in the clinical implementation of medical AI.

The analysis of the limitations of the current study reveals three fundamental causes: the data sparsity of atypical lesions leads to limited model cognitive boundaries; the feature drift problem caused by the heterogeneity of medical information systems and the real-time stability challenge in a dynamic computing environment. To address the issue of atypical lesion data scarcity, a dual strategy is proposed: first, federated data-sharing agreements among regional hospitals are established to enrich rare case acquisition without breaching patient privacy; second, conditional generative adversarial networks (cGANs) trained on limited pathological priors are employed to synthesize anatomically plausible atypical lesion instances, thereby enhancing model exposure to edge-case variations.The model’s cognitive boundaries exhibit significant limitations in identifying rare lesions. In experiments, rare renal pelvic mucosal atypical hyperplasia and complex mixed-component calculi were misclassified or not detected due to a lack of training samples. This suggests that the model’s generalization ability for atypical patterns is insufficient under the existing data. To alleviate this issue, in addition to increasing multi-center data sharing, it is necessary to construct synthetic samples of rare lesions within a generative adversarial framework for expanded training. In terms of future improvements, it is of practical significance to extend the model to the diagnosis of other abdominal organs, but there are also challenges. Different organs have obvious differences in morphology, motion and contrast enhancement methods. Direct migration may not be able to fully express organ-specific characteristics. It is necessary to construct corresponding anatomical prior constraints for different organs and expand the multi-phase image alignment strategy. At the data level, it is necessary to collect and integrate multi-center labeled data to cover different types of lesions. At the model level, it is necessary to adjust the network structure to introduce organ conditioning modules to adapt to anatomical differences. In terms of engineering implementation, it is also necessary to resolve the contradiction between sample imbalance and inference efficiency. Therefore, in the multi-organ expansion research, transfer learning and hierarchical training will be the main line to gradually explore the applicability of the model in multi-organ diagnosis.These findings provide a clear direction for subsequent technology iterations. From the perspective of discipline development, the three-dimensional visualization evaluation system and multi-center verification scheme constructed in this study lay the foundation for the standardized evaluation of medical AI. Despite challenges in system integration and real-time reasoning, this technical framework has shown the potential to promote precision medical practice by optimizing the human-machine collaboration mechanism and integrating the entire diagnosis and treatment process. During clinical deployment, integration with heterogeneous hospital information systems required adaptation of the DICOM-RT interface to accommodate non-standard tag encodings and variations in modality worklist query formats. Multi-GPU inference across devices with differing numerical precision behaviors demanded calibration of TensorRT execution profiles to maintain uniform prediction outputs. In lower bandwidth network environments, PACS retrieval latency was mitigated by introducing a predictive prefetching scheduler that initiates data transfer based on upcoming case identifiers. Annotation quality control addressed inter-observer variability by implementing a two-pass arbitration process with quantitative overlap assessment, ensuring that model training data retained consistent boundary definitions. These measures collectively stabilized performance in operational settings while preserving compatibility with diverse clinical infrastructures. Its clinical value can continue to be released in the extension directions of imaging genomics feature fusion, edge computing optimization, etc., and ultimately achieve a complete value closed loop from technological innovation to improvement of medical service quality.

Conclusions

This study uses a lightweight deep learning model based on a dual-branch 3D convolutional architecture to achieve accurate analysis of urinary system CT images through a dynamic anatomical constraint attention mechanism and a multi-scale feature fusion strategy. On the technical level, the model innovatively integrates multi-phase dynamic enhancement features, combines anatomical priors to optimize lesion localization capabilities, and breaks through the computing power limitations of clinical deployment through an adaptive quantization strategy. In terms of clinical value, the solution has been initially integrated into the diagnostic process of tertiary hospitals, improving the efficiency of detecting micro-lesions and achieving seamless connection with the image archiving system, verifying its practicality in real medical scenarios. The current limitations are mainly due to the model generalization bottleneck caused by the scarcity of atypical case data and the deployment adaptation challenges caused by the heterogeneity of primary medical equipment, reflecting the core contradiction between data balance and hardware compatibility in the implementation of medical AI. In terms of field impact, the “multimodal perception-lightweight reasoning” technical framework constructed by the research provides a scalable method system for medical image analysis, and its anatomically driven feature fusion ideas can be extended to multiple scenarios such as tumor staging assessment. By continuously optimizing the adaptability of clinical workflows, this achievement is expected to promote the transformation of urinary disease diagnosis from single image analysis to intelligent decision support, providing key technical support for the practice of precision medicine.

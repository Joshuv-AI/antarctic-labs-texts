Complex all-comers and patients with diabetes and prediabetes treated with Xience Sierra everolimus-eluting stents: COASTLINE high-risk
Authors: Daphne van Vliet, Eline H Ploumen, Tineke H Pinxterhuis, Rosaly A Buiten, Adel Aminian, Carl E Schotborgh, Rutger L Anthonio, Paolo Zocca, Marc Hartmann, Martin G Stoel, Frits H A F de Man, Gerard C M Linssen, Carine J M Doggen, Liefke C van der Heijden, Marlies M Kok, Clemens von Birgelen, Salvatore De Rosa
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimsPCI is frequently performed in patients with high-risk (HR) of adverse clinical events. Therefore, the COASTLINE HR analysis aimed to assess the safety and efficacy of Xience Sierra everolimus-eluting stents (EES) in patients with diabetes, prediabetes, or other criteria of increased cardiovascular risk.Methods and resultsThis is the primary analysis of COASTLINE, an investigator-initiated, prospective, multicenter registry in all-comers treated with Xience Sierra EES. After enrolment, HR patients were identified according to prespecified criteria, and per protocol compared with HR patients treated in two randomized all-comer trials with Resolute-type zotarolimus-eluting stents (ZES). Primary endpoint at 1-year follow-up is target vessel failure (TVF), a composite of cardiac death, target vessel myocardial infarction (TVMI), or target vessel revascularization. Of 1768 all-comers, treated with Xience Sierra EES and enrolled in this registry, 1317 (74.5%) were HR patients. Clinical outcome of these patients was compared with the control group, consisting of 1682 HR patients treated with Resolute-type ZES. At 1-year follow-up, no significant difference was observed in TVF between-stents (EES: 4.9% vs. ZES: 6.0%, adjusted hazard ratio 0.78, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.57–1.06, P = 0.12). Furthermore, a significantly lower rate of secondary endpoint TVMI was observed in Xience Sierra EES patients (1.4 vs. 2.5%, adjusted hazard ratio 0.50, 95% CI 0.28–0.87, P = 0.014), driven by periprocedural myocardial infarction.ConclusionIn patients with diabetes, prediabetes, or other HR criteria, Xience Sierra EES showed safety and efficacy, comparable to Resolute-type ZES, including Resolute Onyx. The significant difference in TVMI was driven by periprocedural events, as a landmark analysis at 7 days found no between-stent differences.Registration https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04475380 Keywords: Percutaneous coronary intervention, Coronary artery disease, Drug-eluting stent, Diabetes, High-risk patients

As patients with an increased risk of adverse clinical events represent an increasing proportion of the present PCI population, the evaluation of drug-eluting stents is particularly relevant in these high-risk patients. COASTLINE is the first study to report clinical outcomes of PCI with Xience Sierra everolimus-eluting stents (EES) in patients with diabetes, prediabetes, or other prespecified high-risk criteria. At 1-year follow-up, no significant difference was observed in the primary endpoint between patients treated with Xience Sierra EES vs. the control group, suggesting that treating high-risk patients with the Xience Sierra EES is safe and effective.

Introduction

Previous concerns about early-generation drug-eluting stents (DES) as a potential trigger of delayed vascular healing and (late) adverse events1–2 have prompted the development of new-generation permanent polymer-coated DES with improved safety profiles.3–5 These devices utilize different, more biocompatible coatings than the early-generation DES and have a low risk of stent thrombosis.6–9 The durable polymer Xience everolimus-eluting stent (EES) is one of the new-generation DES that has undergone several iterations since its introduction in the clinical arena.10

One of its latest iterations is the Xience Sierra EES which has the same established drug-coating combination as its predecessors, but a more refined design of the metal backbone, aiming at increased device flexibility and improved stent deliverability.11 These refinements make the stent suitable for treatment of challenging coronary lesions which are more frequently found in, for instance, patients with diabetes. Most previous studies have evaluated earlier iterations of Xience EES with favourable long-term clinical outcomes in all-comer patients.12–14

While the widespread use of new-generation DES has reduced both early and late adverse clinical events after PCI, this benefit in clinical outcome is often attenuated in the presence of diabetes which is known to be associated with more diffuse and more complex atherosclerotic coronary artery disease.15 In addition, a higher risk of adverse clinical events after PCI is not only seen in patients with diabetes but also in patients with unknown (silent) diabetes and those with prediabetes.16

Clinical outcome data after PCI with the Xience Sierra EES in high-risk (HR) patients, including many patients with diabetes and prediabetes, would be of great interest as nowadays PCI is performed in many clinically complex patients. As such data are not available yet, the prospective, multicenter COASTLINE study evaluates the clinical outcome of all-comer patients, who were exclusively treated with Xience Sierra EES, and investigates at 1-year follow-up the safety and efficacy of this stent in HR patients with diabetes, prediabetes, or other prespecified criteria of increased cardiovascular risk (COASTLINE HR).

Methods

Study design and population

The COASTLINE study (NCT04475380) is a prospective, international, multicenter registry of PCI with Xience Sierra EES in all-comers, enrolled from September 2018 to April 2022 at four cardiac centres in the Netherlands and Belgium. All patients aged ≥18 years were eligible for inclusion in this registry, if they required PCI for the treatment of significant coronary artery or bypass graft lesions with the study device. There were few exclusion criteria: Known intolerance to components of study device or antithrombotic or to anticoagulant therapy; planned elective surgery necessitating dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) interruption during the first 3 months; known pregnancy; unlikeliness to adhere to follow-up; and life expectancy <1 year. All patients provided written informed consent. The clinical outcome of patients in this registry is per protocol compared to the outcome of a control group of all-comers who were treated with Resolute-type zotarolimus-eluting stents (ZES) in two randomized clinical trials.5,7

At 1-year follow-up, COASTLINE HR assesses in its main analysis the non-inferiority hypothesis that the safety and efficacy of the Xience Sierra EES is non-inferior to that of Resolute-type ZES in HR patients. Patients were classified as ‘high-risk’ in the presence of at least one of the following prespecified criteria, based on previously performed analyses of BIO-RESORT trial data:

Unstable angina or non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI), (sub)acute or recent;

Unstable angina or non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI), (sub)acute or recent;

The patients of the control group, treated with Resolute-type ZES, were identified from a patient-level pooled database of the randomized controlled BIO-RESORT5 and BIONYX6 trials after using the same criteria of increased cardiovascular risk as described in the paragraph above for patients treated with Xience Sierra EES. In both BIO-RESORT5 and BIONYX,6 patients treated with Resolute-type ZES showed favourable clinical outcomes. The study design of both trials has been reported previously.17,18 In brief, BIO-RESORT (clinical gov: NCT01674803) is a patient- and assessor-blinded, multicenter trial performed between December 2012 and August 2015. A total of 3514 all-comer patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1:1 fashion to treatment with Resolute Integrity ZES, Orsiro sirolimus-eluting stent, or Synergy EES.5 BIONYX (clinical gov: NCT02508714) is a patient- and assessor-blinded, multicenter trial, evaluating the safety and efficacy of 2488 patients treated between October 2015 and December 2016. Patients were randomly allocated (1:1) to treatment with Resolute Onyx ZES or Orsiro sirolimus-eluting stent.6

Study device, coronary procedures, and follow-up

All patients in the COASTLINE registry underwent PCI with implantation of at least one Xience Sierra EES, which has a thin-strut cobalt-chromium stent platform and elutes everolimus from a durable fluoropolymer coating (Abbott Vascular, Santa Clara, CA). It is available in diameters ranging from 2.0 to 4.0 mm. Patients of the control group were treated with one of two Resolute-type ZES: the thin-strut durable polymer Resolute Integrity ZES (Medtronic, Santa Rosa, CA) with a cobalt-chromium stent platform and diameters available from 2.25 to 4.0 mm, or the thin-strut durable polymer Resolute Onyx ZES (Medtronic, Santa Rosa, CA). The latter has a platinum-iridium core in a cobalt-chromium stent platform, and is available in diameters from 2.0 to 5.0 mm.

PCI was performed according to standard clinical practice. Techniques and concomitant medical therapy were left to the operator’s discretion, local standards, and current clinical guidelines. There is no scheduled routine angiographic follow-up. Follow-up data are collected annually during routine visits to the outpatient clinic, or if not feasible, by a medical questionnaire and/or a telephone follow-up. The total follow-up of the study will be 3 years. Clinical outcomes will be reported annually with the main analyses being (1) at 1-year follow-up the evaluation of the high-risk patients—COASTLINE HR and (2) at 2-year follow-up the assessment of the entire population of all-comers—COASTLINE AC. The clinical research organization Cardiovascular Research and Education (Enschede, the Netherlands) coordinated the study and the data analysis. There is independent monitoring of all participants of the study, which includes monitoring of informed consent, basic demographics, diabetes, clinical presentation, cardiac markers and use of direct oral anticoagulants at time of discharge, stent type and potential clinical events during follow-up. All potential adverse clinical events are adjudicated by an external clinical event committee in the same way as was done in the control arm.

Definitions and clinical endpoints

At the time of enrolment, the presence or absence of an abnormal glucose metabolism was classified by using the International Expert Committee 2009 criteria for HbA1c with fasting plasma glucose (FPG).19 The classification was based on the available data if one parameter had been assessed. An FPG of <6.1 mmol/L and an HbA1c of ≤41 mmol/mol were used to classify a normal glycaemic state. Prediabetes was classified if FPG was between 6.1 and 6.9 mmol/L and HbA1c was between 42 and 47 mmol/mol. ‘Silent’ diabetes was classified in patients without medically treated diabetes in the presence of an FPG ≥7.0 mmol/L or an HbA1c ≥ 48 mmol/mol.

The primary composite endpoint of safety and efficacy is target vessel failure (TVF: target vessel myocardial infarction (TVMI), target vessel revascularization (TVR), or cardiac death). Secondary composite endpoints are target lesion failure (TLF: TVMI, clinically indicated target lesion revascularization, or cardiac death); major adverse cardiac events (MACE: all-cause death, any myocardial infarction (MI), or clinically indicated target lesion revascularization); and patient-oriented composite endpoint (any MI, any revascularization, or any death). Secondary individual endpoints are stent thrombosis and all individual components of the composite endpoints described above. The definition of the clinical endpoints were similar to the definition of the endpoints assessed in the BIORESORT5 and BIONYX6 trials, as previously reported. Ethnicity was self-reported by the study participants.

In addition to the main analysis, a predefined, additional analysis was performed that exclusively used patients treated with the latest iteration of the Resolute-type ZES, Resolute Onyx ZES, as a control group. To overcome differences in timing of patient inclusion between the current registry and the randomized trials, and as a result of unmeasured confounders around the PCI procedure, a landmark analysis was performed excluding all events up to 7 days post-PCI. This time frame is deliberately extended to 7 days instead of 48 h to exclude all events potentially related to index PCI.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were compared using either Fisher's exact test or Pearson's chi-square test, while continuous variables were analysed with the Student's t-test or Mann–Whitney U test, as appropriate. Kaplan–Meier methods were utilized to evaluate the time to primary and secondary endpoints, with between-group comparisons conducted using the log-rank test. The Cox proportional hazards regression model was used to compute hazard ratios with two-sided confidence intervals (CIs). Patients who were lost to follow-up, died, or withdrew their consent were censored at the last point of contact or the time of death. Based on a power calculation that assumes TVF rates of 6.0% at 1-year follow-up (based on BIO-RESORT data and further analyses and considerations) with a 2.75% non-inferiority margin giving the study a power of 83% with a one-sided α level of 0.025 and allowing for 3% follow-up, a total of 1278 all-comers with HR criteria.

To adjust for potential confounders, a multivariate analysis (Cox regression) was performed for all our analyses. The model was constructed using sex, age and at least one bifurcation lesion, as based on the literature, and all baseline variables that showed a between-group difference and an association with TVF (P < 0.15) (Supplementary material online, Table S1). For the main analysis, the following variables were included in the final model after performing step-wise backward regression: sex, age, at least one bifurcation lesion, previous PCI, and total stent length per patient. We evaluated potential effect modification for the covariates oral anticoagulation use and diabetes, but did not observe a significant result. In addition, as a sensitivity analysis for the main analysis, adjustment for potential confounders was also performed by a propensity score analysis, of which the scores were estimated using logistic regression. A multivariate Cox regression model was then used to compute hazard ratios with two-sided CIs, adjusting for the propensity score. For the additional analysis in patients treated with Xience Sierra EES vs. Resolute Onyx ZES, a separate step-wise backward regression was performed, including the following variables in the final model: sex, age, at least one bifurcation lesion, and multivessel treatment. A P-value was considered significant if <0.05. SPSS statistics version 24.0 was used to perform statistical analyses (IBM Corp, Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

A total of 1768 all-comer patients, treated with the Xience Sierra EES, were included in the COASTLINE registry. After applying the HR criteria, 1317 patients (1697 lesions) were identified and assessed in the present COASTLINE HR analysis. At 1-year follow-up, data were available for 1309/1317 patients (99.2%; three lost to follow-up, five withdrew consent). The characteristics and clinical outcome of these Xience Sierra EES-treated patients were compared with those of a control group of 1682 HR patients (2267 lesions), treated with Resolute–type ZES in two previous randomized clinical trials (see Supplementary material online, Figure S1).

Characteristics of patients and procedures

The patient and lesion characteristics at baseline are presented in Table 1. There was no significant difference in age between patients treated with Xience Sierra EES and Resolute-type ZES (65.7 ± 10.6 vs. 65.0 ± 10.7 years). The proportion of women was 23.6 and 27.0%, respectively (P = 0.035). Of the Xience Sierra EES- and Resolute-type ZES-treated patients, 25.8 and 27.9% had known diabetes, 9.7 and 7.0% had silent diabetes, and 21.6 and 11.0% had prediabetes, respectively. The proportion of active smokers was somewhat lower in the Xience Sierra EES-treated patients than in the control group (23.7 vs. 27.8%, P = 0.01). In the Xience Sierra EES patient group, there were more patients on oral anticoagulation therapy (19.4 vs. 14.8%, P < 0.001) and more with a previous PCI (25.7 vs. 20.9%, P = 0.002), while there was no between-group difference in history of previous CABG (10.7 vs. 10.4%, P = 0.79) and previous MI (19.6 vs. 20.4%, P = 0.22). In addition, these patients underwent PCI less often in a bifurcation lesion (31.0 vs. 38.6%, P < 0.001), and they had a longer total stent length and a smaller stent diameter than Resolute-type ZES-treated patients (P < 0.001 for both). CTO lesions were treated in similar proportions of both patient groups (4.4 vs. 5.8%, P = 0.08).

Clinical outcomes

At 1-year follow-up, the primary composite clinical endpoint TVF was 4.9% for patients treated with Xience Sierra EES and 6.0% for patients treated with Resolute-type ZES (hazard ratio 0.82, 95% CI 0.60–1.11, P = 0.20) (Table 2). Adjustment for confounders in a multivariate analysis did not result in a significant between-group difference (adjusted hazard ratio 0.78, 95% CI 0.57–1.06, P = 0.12). A propensity score-adjusted multivariate analysis confirmed this finding (see Supplementary material online, Table S2).

Clinical outcomes of high-risk analysis

The incidence of TVMI was significantly lower in Xience Sierra EES-treated patients (1.4 vs. 2.5%, hazard ratio 0.55, 95% CI 0.32–0.95, P = 0.029) and occurred mostly periprocedurally. TVR and cardiac death showed no between-group difference (2.9 vs. 3.4% and 1.3 vs. 0.8%, respectively). After adjustment, the association between Xience Sierra EES and a lower TVMI risk turned out to be even stronger (adjusted hazard ratio 0.50, 95% CI 0.28–0.87, P = 0.014). This was confirmed in an additional propensity score-adjusted multivariate analysis (see Supplementary material online, Table S2). Figure 1 displays the Kaplan–Meier time-to-event curves of the primary endpoint and its components.

Xience Sierra EES-treated patients had a numerically lower event rate of TLF (3.9 vs. 5.2%) than patients treated with Resolute-type ZES. After adjustment for confounders, a significant between-group difference was shown for TLF (adjusted hazard ratio 0.70, 95% CI 0.49–0.99, P = 0.041). Other secondary clinical endpoints showed no between-group difference. In both stent groups, the incidence of definite-or-probable stent thrombosis was very low (0.23 vs. 0.42%).

No between-group difference at 1-year follow-up was observed if events that occurred in the first 7 days post-PCI were excluded. Comparison of Xience Sierra EES with Resolute Onyx ZES

In a prespecified analysis, we compared the outcome of patients treated with Xience Sierra EES vs. those treated with Resolute Onyx—the latest ZES iteration at time of enrolment. Of all 1682 patients in the Resolute-type ZES group, 855 (50.8%) were treated with Resolute Onyx ZES. When comparing Xience Sierra EES-treated patients with the latest ZES iteration, the primary clinical endpoint TVF showed no between-DES difference (4.9 vs. 5.1%, hazard ratio 0.96, 95% CI 0.66–1.41, P = 0.83), and there was also no between-DES difference in the individual components of TVF and other secondary clinical endpoints. Discussion

Main findings

In PCI patients with diabetes, prediabetes, and other HR criteria, COASTLINE HR showed safety and efficacy of the Xience Sierra EES at 1-year follow-up. The clinical outcomes of the Xience Sierra EES-treated HR patients were comparable to those of a control group, consisting of HR patients who previously participated in all-comers trials and were treated with Resolute-type ZES. Between patients treated with Xience Sierra EES and Resolute-type ZES, no significant difference was observed in the primary clinical endpoint TVF (4.9 and 6.0%). For the Xience Sierra EES-treated patients, we did observe a significantly lower rate of the secondary endpoint TVMI (1.4 and 2.5%), of which events mostly occurred periprocedurally. Furthermore, a separate analysis that compared the Xience Sierra EES with Resolute Onyx ZES observed no significant difference in any clinical endpoint.

Previous studies on new-generation DES

New-generation DES have achieved excellent clinical outcomes in recent years. Several studies have proven that biodegradable and durable polymer-coated DES are equally safe and efficacious at long-term clinical follow-up. The randomized BIONYX trial demonstrated similar safety and efficacy in all-comer patients after PCI with the Orsiro sirolimus-eluting stent or the Resolute Onyx ZES.7 In addition, a meta-analysis of five large randomized trials demonstrated no significant difference in several clinical endpoints between patients treated with the durable polymer-coated Xience EES or biodegradable polymer-coated sirolimus-eluting stents.20 To the best of our knowledge, only a single previous published study exclusively assessed the Xience Sierra EES. The SIERRA75 registry compared PCI patients aged 75 years and older with an increased ischaemic and bleeding risk with a control group of elderly HR patients who were treated with several other DES.21 At 12-month follow-up, there was no difference between the DES groups in mortality, repeat revascularization, stent thrombosis, and MI. Similar to the present study, the DES type did not affect the risks of mortality and repeat revascularization. When comparing the incidence of adverse events in SIERRA75 and the present study, our study showed lower rates of repeat revascularization, stent thrombosis, and MI. Differences in study populations are likely to explain the differences in adverse event rates between both studies, as the SIERRA75 registry exclusively included elderly HR patients,21 while the present study assessed HR patients of all ages, which results in considerable differences in baseline characteristics.

Previous studies on HR PCI patients

As a result of advances in stent implantation techniques and refinements in DES design, PCI is increasingly performed in patients with several comorbidities and HR PCI profiles. Nevertheless, PCI in HR patients still has an increased risk of adverse clinical events.22 As various definitions of ‘high-risk PCI patients’ have been used in the literature, a comparison of the results of the present study with other studies is challenging.23,24 In addition, the present study focuses primarily on HR patient characteristics, while most previous studies addressed HR lesion characteristics.

Patients presenting with NSTEMI or unstable angina are known to be older and often have higher cardiovascular risk profiles than those presenting with STEMI (e.g. higher prevalence of diabetes, hyperlipidaemia, and peripheral artery disease).25,26 It has been shown that the rates of several clinical endpoints are higher in patients presenting with NSTEMI than in those with STEMI,26,27 which may be related to the fact that most randomized clinical trials excluded STEMI patients in cardiogenic shock (who have the highest risk of death) and those who were likely to require surgery at short notice (e.g. STEMI patients with three-vessel disease).

More than half of the HR population of COASTLINE has an abnormal glucose metabolism: 57% of the patients have diabetes, silent diabetes, or prediabetes. These patients have higher risks of ischaemic events at long-term follow-up.16,28 A PCI registry29 recently reported clinical outcomes in patients with diabetes, who were treated between 2008 and 2018 with different contemporary DES that included the Resolute-type ZES and various iterations of Xience EES (Xience Sierra not included), showing at 3-year follow-up a similar safety and efficacy for both DES-types.29 In contrast, we did observe a significant difference in TVMI (1.4 vs. 2.5%) between Xience Sierra EES and Resolute-type ZES in the present study at 1-year follow-up, although numbers were small and our events occurred periprocedurally.

Besides an abnormal glucose metabolism, several other HR criteria are associated with an increased cardiovascular risk. Patients with a previous CABG are associated with a higher incidence of cardiac death and repeat coronary revascularization,30 and patients who are treated by PCI for CTO lesions are at a higher risk of various adverse events both during the periprocedural phase and during follow-up31 In addition, the antithrombotic management of PCI patients already treated with OAC (e.g. patients with atrial fibrillation) is challenging, as the addition of DAPT reduces the risk of ischaemic complications while increasing the risk of bleeding events. Furthermore, shortening DAPT duration in patients on OAC increases the risk of ischaemic events to some extent.32

An analysis of the BIOFLOW trial evaluated the outcomes of PCI patients with higher vs. lower atherothrombotic risk, classifying the higher-risk group based on criteria that were quite similar to those of the present study.33 Besides the main analysis that compared clinical outcomes between the risk-groups, the authors performed an additional analysis that included the different DES types used. At 3-year follow-up, significantly lower risks of TLF and TVMI were observed in the lower-risk patients who were treated with biodegradable sirolimus-eluting stents as compared to those who were treated with various previous iterations of the EES. In addition, among the higher-risk patients, numerically lower rates of TLF and TVMI were observed in those treated with sirolimus-eluting stents, but these dissimilarities between the DES groups were not statistically significant.33

Current results in perspective

In the main analysis of COASTLINE HR, a statistically significant between-DES difference was observed in TVMI and (after adjustment) in TLF between Xience Sierra EES and the Resolute-type ZES. When we performed an additional analysis that exclusively used patients treated with Resolute Onyx ZES as a control group, no significant between-DES difference was observed for the primary clinical endpoint TVF or any secondary endpoint, including TVMI. This absence of a significant between-DES difference could be explained by technical differences between the two ZES iterations. These differences concern material, design, and strut shape of the respective metallic backbones. As compared to the round-strut cobalt-chromium Resolute Integrity ZES, the Resolute Onyx ZES has a platinum-iridium core, situated inside a slightly thinner cobalt-chromium wire with a swaged shape. These features of the Resolute Onyx ZES can improve the x-ray visibility and radial strength as compared to the Resolute Integrity ZES, which may be particularly beneficial when treating patients with obesity and coronary lesions that have a high plaque burden (e.g. patients with diabetes.16) The theoretical advantages of the newer ZES iteration could reduce the risk of stent malapposition, suboptimal stent expansion, and—eventually—adverse clinical events.34 The Xience Sierra EES has its own benefits, related to improved deliverability and low crossing profile.11 Thus, the lack of any statistical difference in clinical outcome between the Xience Sierra EES and the Resolute Onyx ZES in the additional analysis could be the result of the distinctive features of both DES, abolishing any difference in clinical outcome. On the other hand, one may hypothesize that the stent design of the Resolute integrity ZES may have contributed to some slightly higher event rates in the control group of the main analysis. In addition, the observed statistical difference in the main analysis for TVMI was mainly driven by periprocedural events, as can be seen in our landmark analysis. This let us hypothesize that with regard to TVMI, a comparably favourable safety of both DES may be observed beyond the first days. Nevertheless, the findings of the between-stent comparisons should be interpreted with great caution, as direct comparisons between registry and randomized trial data carry intrinsic limitations, including a potential confounding bias due to temporal and procedural changes. In addition, small unmeasured differences in routine clinical practice and postprocedural assessment could have affected the observed clinical outcomes (despite similarities between studies in population, in/exclusion criteria, and definition of endpoints).

Limitations

This study has several limitations. The definition of HR PCI is not standardized in the current literature, and some anatomical variables previously associated with an increased procedural risk (e.g. left main lesion, bifurcation lesion, multivessel PCI, and use of rotational atherectomy) were not among the prespecified criteria of the present study. Furthermore, patients in cardiogenic shock were not assessed in COASTLINE as well as in BIO-RESORT and BIONYX due to exclusion criteria (i.e. inability to provide informed consent; assumed life expectancy <1 year). The present study is an investigator-initiated, prospective, international, multicenter registry that applied inclusion and exclusion criteria that were similar to our previous randomized all-comer DES studies.4,5,7 Nevertheless, as stent choice was not randomized, we cannot definitely rule out the possibility of some bias. The control group was derived from randomized trials including patients from 2012 to 2016, which makes up for a 6- to 10-year gap with the present study. Therefore, data on intracoronary physiology and imaging guidance were not reported, as these techniques were not standard used during inclusion of the randomized trials. In the meantime, the European guidelines have increased the level of recommendation for PCI guidance with intracoronary physiology and imaging. In addition, over time there has been a shift in the recommendations of the European guidelines for both low-density lipoprotein targets and DAPT duration, resulting in slight treatment differences among the study groups. Yet, all definitions of clinical endpoints were similar between both groups. In addition, the patients in the control group were included prior to PCI, whereas patients included in the COASTLINE registry were included prior, during, or after PCI. This could have possibly led to excluding patients with a complicated or unsuccessful PCI. In order to overcome this limitation, we performed a landmark analysis, between 7-day and 1-year follow-ups. We adjusted for all known potential confounders, yet we cannot rule out the impact of unknown and unmeasured confounders. The present analysis could have benefitted from the inclusion of data from angiographic analyses. Between study population and control group, HR features were unevenly distributed, with an example being the prevalence of patients with prediabetes that was higher in EES-treated patients. This may be partially explained by a better screening for an abnormal glucose metabolism in the registry as compared to the randomized trial that provided the control group. DES refinement is a moving target. In the meantime, new iterations of the Xience EES and the Resolute-type ZES have been introduced. The latest iteration of the EES (Xience Skypoint) shows slight modifications, mainly of the stent delivery system; the latest iteration of the ZES (Onyx Trustar) also involves slight modification of the stent delivery system, while the DES itself (i.e. metallic stent platform, polymer coating and drug) remains basically unchanged.

Conclusions

COASTLINE HR in patients with diabetes, prediabetes, and other criteria of increased cardiovascular risk showed safety and efficacy of the Xience Sierra EES, measured by the primary endpoint TVF. A hypothesis-generating comparison with patients treated with Resolute-type ZES, including a distinct comparison with the Resolute Onyx ZES, suggested comparable clinical outcomes in patients treated with the Xience Sierra EES. A significant difference in TVMI was observed in favour of the Xience Sierra EES, yet this was mainly driven by periprocedural events, as shown in a landmark analysis beyond 7 days that showed no statistically significant between-stent difference in all clinical outcomes.

Visceral Ischemia and Gastric Perforation in Acute Type B Aortic Dissection
Authors: Yang Liu, Xingpeng Chen, Qiliang Lei, Jie Hu, Zhu Liu, Heng Huang, Shanshan Xie, Zhigang Liu
Published: 2026

A 52-year-old man presented to emergency department with sudden chest and back pain lasting 2 hours. His blood pressure was 200/110 mmHg. Computed tomography angiography (CTA) revealed descending Stanford type B thoracic aortic dissection extending from the aortic arch to the left renal artery. The celiac trunk (Figure 1A and B) and its branches (Figure 1C-F) were poorly opacified, suggesting thrombosis with impaired splenic perfusion. The patient received antihypertensive therapy in the intensive care unit.

Two days later, he developed acute abdominal pain, and imaging revealed ischemic gastric necrosis (Figure 1G and H). Repeat CTA showed critical stenosis and near-complete occlusion of the celiac trunk and its branches (Figure 1I and J), with gastric wall ischemia. Emergency laparotomy revealed a 2×2 cm gastric fundus perforation, full-thickness necrosis, splenic and multiple hepatic infarctions. Total gastrectomy, splenectomy, and drainage of intra-abdominal abscesses were performed. Histopathology confirmed ischemic necrosis (Figure 1K and L). The patient gradually recovered and subsequently underwent thoracic endovascular aortic repair with a covered stent graft. Follow-up imaging showed stable graft fixation, and he remained clinically well during follow-up.

This case highlights the rare but catastrophic complication of visceral ischemia in acute aortic dissection. Although stomach is typically protected by collateral circulation, disruption of these pathways can result in rapid fulminant necrosis.1) Visceral malperfusion occurs in 8–15% of type B dissections but carries mortality exceeding 50% when not promptly identified.2)3) Both dynamic and static vessel obstruction may coexist, compounding ischemia and end-organ injury. Prompt contrast-enhanced CTA and multidisciplinary intervention are essential to improving patient outcomes.4)5)

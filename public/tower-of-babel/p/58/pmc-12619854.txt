Gliptin-Induced Bullous Pemphigoid: Are Statins and Angiotensin Receptor Blockers the Cause?
Authors: Burcu Beksac, M. Kaan Temirkaynak, Sheyda Gharehdaghi, Evren Atesci, Gozde Kubat, Ozlem Erdem, Nilsel Ilter, Esra Adisen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionRecently, dipeptidyl peptidase 4 inhibitors (DPP4i), a group of drugs used for the treatment of diabetes mellitus, have been associated with an increased risk of bullous pemphigoid (BP). Several studies previously found clinical and laboratory differences between patients with gliptin-induced BP and conventional BP, and some authors accept DPP4i-induced BP as a separate entity. In this retrospective study, we aimed to compare clinical and laboratory characteristics, comorbidities, and medications used simultaneously with gliptins in these two groups of BP.MethodsPatients with the diagnosis of BP hospitalized in our clinic between 2010 and 2023 were included in this retrospective cross-sectional study. Demographic, clinical, laboratory data, comorbidities, and additional medications were recorded for each patient. Patients were divided into DPP4i-associated and conventional BP groups, and the data were compared between the two groups.ResultsA total of 105 patients with BP, of which 11 were DPP4i-associated, were included. There was no statistically significant difference in age, age at onset, gender distribution, lesion distribution, histopathological, and laboratory findings between the two groups. The median use of DPP4i before BP onset was 3 months (1–12). Disease and follow-up duration were shorter in the gliptin-associated group. DPP4i-induced patients had a higher frequency of severe disease and presentation with a prebullous phase. Cardiovascular disorders, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, the use of angiotensin receptor blockers (ARB), and statins were significantly higher in the gliptin-using patients.ConclusionThis is one of the first studies to compare demographic, clinical, and laboratory characteristics of patients with gliptin-induced and conventional BP. Our results suggest gliptin-associated BP may present with more severe disease. Hyperlipidemia, ARB, and statin use may be associated with DPP4i-induced BP. Although larger studies are warranted to confirm this association, we believe these findings should be kept in mind while choosing patients to treat with gliptins.

Introduction

Bullous pemphigoid (BP) is an autoimmune bullous disease seen primarily in the elderly, after the eighth decade of life. It is the most common autoimmune bullous disease with an incidence of 2.5–42.8 per one million and mortality that is 2.3 times higher compared to the normal population [1–3]. BP is caused by IgG antibodies against basement membrane antigens BP180 and BP230, which are involved in the structure of the hemidesmosome. These antibodies lead to a separation in the dermoepidermal junction, clinically seen as tense bullae [4]. Histopathologically, BP is characterized by subepidermal bullae with eosinophils. Linear IgG and C3 positivity along the basement membrane zone is seen in direct immunofluorescence microscopy. Genetic predisposition, neuropsychiatric disorders, lichen planus, psoriasis, physical agents, viruses, ultraviolet light, X-rays, and medications are implicated in the etiopathogenesis [5].

Recently, dipeptidyl peptidase 4 (DPP4) inhibitors, a group of oral agents used in the treatment of type 2 diabetes, have been suggested as a causative factor for BP [6–9]. Sitagliptin, vildagliptin, linagliptin, saxagliptin, and alogliptin are some of the molecules in this group of drugs, also called gliptins. These agents increase glucagon-like peptide concentration through DPP4 inhibition, thus augmenting insulin release and decreasing glucagon levels [10]. DPP4, a serine protease, is also a lymphocyte surface protein called CD26, acting in T cell regulation, development, and stimulation [11, 12]. It has been shown to exhibit altered expression and activity in many autoimmune diseases such as type 1 diabetes, inflammatory bowel disease, rheumatoid arthritis, and systemic lupus erythematosus, although its exact role in their pathogenesis has not yet been elucidated [12]. Therefore, DPP4 inhibitors (DPP4i) may be involved in the development of autoimmune disorders by leading to immune dysregulation [13].

Among DPP4i or gliptins, vildagliptin and linagliptin are particularly associated with BP [7, 9, 14, 15]. There is limited data in the literature about DPP4i-induced BP. The exact etiopathogenetic mechanisms of gliptin-induced BP are currently unknown, and it is impossible to predict which patients treated with DPP4i are more prone to developing BP [16]. Additionally, there are contradictory reports about the clinical, histopathologic, and pathogenetic features of gliptin-induced BP versus spontaneous or conventional BP. While some studies reflect clinical and histopathological differences between the two entities [14, 17–19], others have failed to show any significant differences [20, 21].

Our objective in this study was to investigate clinical, laboratory, and histopathological differences between DPP4i-induced and conventional BP, as well as to compare comorbidities and additional medications used for these comorbidities in these patients, in order to predict and define patient groups more susceptible to gliptin-induced BP.

Methods

All patients diagnosed with BP admitted to Gazi University Faculty of Medicine, Department of Dermatology inpatient clinic between January 2010 and May 2023 were included in this retrospective cross-sectional study. This study received ethical approval from the Gazi University Faculty of Medicine, Non-Interventional Ethical Committee numbered 431 on May 8, 2023. It was performed in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration of 1964 and its later amendments. No identifiable patient data was presented in this manuscript. BP diagnosis was confirmed by the characteristic histopathological findings plus either direct immunofluorescence findings (DIF) or the presence of circulating anti-BP180 or anti-BP230 antibodies. Serological testing for these antibodies was only performed for patients who had negative DIF results for both IgG and C3.

Age, sex, clinical features of BP (date of diagnosis, disease duration, disease severity, distribution of lesions, existence of a prebullous phase, pruritus, treatments), laboratory features such as biopsy results, blood IgE levels, complete blood count results, as well as comorbidities and medications used for comorbidities were recorded for each patient. Disease severity assessment was done on the basis of the body surface area (BSA) measurements as Bullous Pemphigoid Disease Area Index (BPDAI) scores were not available [22]. BSA < 10% was classified as mild disease, BSA between 10 %and 30% as moderate disease, and BSA > 30% as severe disease. The presence or absence of a prebullous phase was assessed by either the patient history or the clinical findings at presentation. The prebullous phase was defined as the appearance of urticarial or eczematous plaques accompanied by pruritus prior to the outbreak of bullous lesions. For DPP4i-using patients, the time interval between the start of gliptin use and the appearance of the first lesions was also recorded.

Patients using gliptins were regarded as a separate group, and their clinical and laboratory characteristics were compared to those of patients with conventional BP. A subgroup analysis between patients with diabetes who were not using gliptin at time of diagnosis and gliptin-using patients was performed to confirm the results.

Data were analyzed using independent sample t test for parametric distribution, Kruskal–Wallis test for non-parametric data, and chi-square test for correlation analysis. P values ≤ 0.05 were considered as statistically significant. Bonferroni correction was performed as necessary. Post hoc G power was calculated for statistically significant results.

Results

Demographic Data

A total of 105 patients were included in the study, 11 (10.4%) of which were using gliptins at the time of diagnosis. The distribution of the DPP4 inhibitors among patients was as follows: 6 (54.5%) patients on linagliptin, 4 (36.4%) patients on vildagliptin, and 1 (9.1%) patient on sitagliptin. The median duration of gliptin use was 3 (1–12) months. There was no statistically significant difference in current age and age at diagnosis; however, the duration of symptoms and follow-up were shorter in the patients on gliptins (p = 0.056 and 0.008, respectively) (Table 1). Female patients constituted the majority of both groups (63.6% for the gliptin group and 59.6% for the non-gliptin group), and no statistically significant difference was found for the gender distribution (p = 0.068) (Table 2). There was one mortality in the gliptin group due to comorbidities, and six in the non-gliptin group, two of which were due to BP and four due to comorbidities. Mortality rates were not significantly different between the two groups (p = 0.73). Clinical, Laboratory, and Histopathological Characteristics

The patients on gliptins had a statistically significantly higher percentage of severe disease (p = 0.021), and none of the patients in this group had mild disease (Table 2). A comparison of disease severity was performed between gliptin-using and non-gliptin-using patients with diabetes (N = 24). There was a statistically significant correlation between gliptin use and the disease severity (p = 0.012). The probability of having severe disease was 15-fold higher in the gliptin-using patients with diabetes (OR 15.0, 95% CI 1.52–148.3) and the relative risk was also higher (RR 9.62, 95% CI 1.25–73.97). The distribution of the lesions and the involvement of special areas such as the mucosae, face, hands, and feet were similar between the two groups. A significantly higher percentage of the patients using gliptins presented with a prebullous phase (p = 0.05); however, there was no statistically significant difference in the presence of pruritus between the groups (Table 2).

We found no significant difference in the laboratory values such as the initial IgE levels and pre-treatment eosinophil counts (Table 1). All of the gliptin-using patients showed IgG and C3 positivity in the direct immunofluorescence microscopy while the patients with conventional BP showed variable findings; however, we found no significant difference between the two groups.

Comorbidities and Medications

The frequencies of cardiovascular disorders, hypertension, and hyperlipidemia were significantly higher in the gliptin-using group (p = 0.004, p = 0.042, and p < 0.001, respectively) (Table 3). We found no statistically significant difference in the frequency of dementia, malignancy, and chronic kidney disease. The comparison between non-gliptin-using patients with diabetes and gliptin-using patient groups revealed only a higher frequency of hyperlipidemia in the gliptin-using patients (p = 0.012) (Table 4). Discussion

This is one of the first studies to compare the clinical, histopathologic, and laboratory characteristics, as well as the comorbidities and medications used for these comorbidities in gliptin-associated BP versus patients with conventional BP. Our results suggest that patients with BP using gliptins more frequently present with a more severe disease and a prebullous phase. An important finding was that gliptin-using patients had higher frequencies of having cardiovascular disorders, hypertension, and hyperlipidemia, and using statins and ARBs.

Out of 105 patients, 11 (10.4%) were in the gliptin-using group. Such a high percentage of gliptin-associated BP emphasizes the role of DPP4i in the occurrence of BP. The most common causative DPP4i were linagliptin (54.5%), followed by vildagliptin (36.4%) and sitagliptin (9.1%) in our study. In an analysis of the Japanese Pharmacovigilance Database, vildagliptin was the most prevalent DPP4i involved in BP onset, followed by teneligliptin and linagliptin [23]. In a study based on the European Pharmacovigilance Database, vildagliptin was the most frequent causative agent, followed by linagliptin, saxagliptin, and sitagliptin [24]. Our results were more compatible with the European data. Teneligliptin is not currently used in Turkiye, explaining its absence from the list of causative drugs in our study. The difference between the Japanese and the European results may be caused by either racial disparities or the different prevalence of prescribing these medications.

The median duration of gliptin use in our patients was 3 months, ranging from 1 to 12 months. This finding is compatible with the literature, various authors reporting disease onset as early as 1 month and as late as 48 months [25–27]. The duration of symptoms and follow-up for our DPP4i-associated group was lower than the conventional BP group. This may be because gliptin-induced BP is a relatively new entity. Another reason may be the drug approval and reimbursement policies in Turkiye, which may have led to the wide use of this group of drugs beginning later than in some other countries.

We found no difference in patient age, age at disease onset, and gender distribution between the two groups in this study. These findings were in line with the literature, since several studies did not find any difference in the demographic characteristics between the gliptin-associated and conventional BP groups [19, 20]. While there was a slight female preponderance in both of our patient groups, there was a male predominance reported in DPP4i-associated BP in at least two studies [17, 18]. Further research with larger patient groups may help enlighten whether there is a true difference in gender distribution.

In our cross-sectional study, the patients with gliptin-induced BP presented more frequently with severe disease. There are contradictory findings about the clinical presentation of gliptin-induced BP. In a study by Kinyo et al., patients with DPP4i-induced BP showed a lower BPDAI urticaria/erythema score, while their BPDAI damage score was higher, leading to the conclusion that gliptin-associated BP leads to a non-inflammatory phenotype [19]. In a case series by Shinohara et al., two of the three patients with DPP4i-induced BP showed unfavorable outcomes [28], emphasizing the more severe nature of DPP4i-associated BP. Although our gliptin-induced patients also had a more severe disease course, the mortality rate did not differ from that of the conventional BP group.

An important laboratory finding of gliptin-associated BP is that the patients may present with lower peripheral absolute eosinophil counts [18, 19] and lower eosinophilic infiltration into the diseased skin [17], leading to a tendency for the “non-inflammatory” disease phenotype in this group [29]. In this retrospective study, we did not observe a non-inflammatory disease presentation; instead, a prebullous stage was more frequently observed in DPP4i-induced patients, suggesting an inflammatory phenotype. The non-inflammatory phenotype has been reported mainly in Japanese cohorts [17, 30, 31]. Therefore, our different results may be related to ethnical origins, since our study was composed of Turkish patients. We did not evaluate the level of eosinophilic infiltration into the skin in this study. Although the mean peripheral eosinophil count was higher in the conventional BP group, this difference did not reach statistical significance as a result of the high standard deviation. In our clinic, we routinely monitor IgE levels in patients with BP. While the mean and median IgE levels were higher in the patients with BP using gliptins, this difference was not statistically significant.

A novel aspect of our study was the comparison of comorbidities and additional medications between DPP4i-induced and conventional BP. We found diabetes, cardiovascular disorders, hyperlipidemia, and hypertension to be more common in gliptin-associated patients. As virtually all patients in the DPP4i group are diabetic, it is expected that these patients have diabetes and use antidiabetic medications more frequently than patients with conventional BP. In return, 25.3% of the patients with conventional BP also had diabetes mellitus (Table 3). While cardiovascular disorders, hypertension, and hyperlipidemia are also more frequently seen in patients with diabetes, we found no difference in the percentage of chronic kidney disease, a disease for which patients with diabetes are at higher risk than the general population [32]. To elucidate a potential relationship between these disorders and gliptin-induced BP, a comparison between patients with diabetes who were not using DPP4i and patients with gliptin-induced BP was performed, revealing a higher frequency of hyperlipidemia in the gliptin-using group. This finding may suggest that patients using DPP4i for diabetes who also have hyperlipidemia may be at higher risk of developing gliptin-induced BP. Another interesting finding of our study was the more frequent use of statins and ARBs in this group. It is noteworthy that only 1.1% of the patients with conventional BP were using statins, and 3.2% were using ARBs (Table 4). Patients with diabetes and BP who were not on DPP4i at the time of diagnosis also had a lower frequency of using these medications (Table 6). To the best of our knowledge, no such association between the use of statins and ARBs with gliptin-induced BP has been previously reported. These findings are unexpected, since statins and ARBs have been reported to have immunomodulatory and anti-inflammatory effects in various studies [33, 34]. Therefore, larger studies are necessary to prove our hypothesis-generating results and to elucidate the potential mechanisms such as drug interactions leading to a tendency to develop gliptin-induced BP in patients using statins and/or ARBs.

The limitations of the study are its retrospective nature and the lack of BPDAI scores in the chart data, leading to a relatively subjective disease severity classification. The peripheral blood anti-BP180 and anti-BP230 levels were not available for all patients and therefore were not included in the statistical analysis. The patient number especially for the gliptin-induced BP group was relatively low, leading to a low statistical power for some analyses.

Conclusion

We found that statins and ARBs are used more frequently in patients who develop gliptin-induced BP and therefore further investigation is needed to determine whether the concomitant use of these drugs with gliptins increases the risk of DPP4i-induced BP. This confirmation would be helpful in predicting patients who may develop the disease, and prompt the prescribing clinician to adjust the patients’ medications. Should such an association be established, another issue to be addressed would be the causative pathogenetic mechanisms. In addition, the results of our study show that BP may have a more severe disease course in patients using gliptin. For this reason, patients with DPP4i-associated BP should be monitored more closely for possible severe progression, complications, and unfavorable outcomes. As demonstrated by our findings, a prebullous stage may be encountered more frequently in patients with BP developing as a result of gliptin use. Endocrinologists prescribing DPP4i should be aware of the increased risk of BP, and should inform patients about pruritic, eczematous, papular, and urticaria-like lesions that may belong to the prebullous stage of BP, to facilitate early diagnosis and treatment. We believe that increasing awareness among endocrinologists about the development of gliptin-induced BP and encouraging them to cooperate with dermatologists may significantly reduce the morbidity and mortality caused by this entity.

Exploring the mechanism of Qiling Jiaogulan Powder in protecting heart injury of heat-stressed broilers based on transcriptome
Authors: Juan Yu, Qing Wang, Min Xu, Shangbin Wang, Yanzhao Xu, Hongxuan He
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The heart serves as the center of blood circulation, transporting oxygen and nutrients to all tissues and organs of the body. Cardiac function is closely associated with the growth performance, immune function, and meat quality of broilers. In this study, a broiler heat stress (HS) model was established by artificially controlling environmental temperature, and transcriptomic analysis was used to investigate the effect and underlying mechanisms of Qiling Jiaogulan Powder (QLJP) on heat stress-induced heart injury in broilers. The results showed that compared with the HS group, both the HS+QLJP and the QLJP groups exhibited significantly reduced the concentrations of myocardial injury indicators natriuretic peptide (ANP), brain natriuretic peptide (BNP), and cardiac Troponin I (cTn-I), and significantly increased the adenosine triphosphate (ATP) levels. Transcriptomic analysis of 42-day-old broiler hearts identified 218 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the HS+QLJP and the HS groups (181 upregulated and 37 downregulated genes), with KEGG enrichment in Cell cycle, ECM-receptor interaction, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, and p53 signaling pathway. qPCR validation confirmed that compared with the HS group, the HS+QLJP group significantly upregulated relative expression of RRM2, COMP, DCK, BRCA1, CDK1, THBS2 and BUB1, and significantly downregulated expression of HK2 and GADD45 in myocardial tissue, consistent with RNA-seq results, thus verifying the reliability of sequencing data. And QLJP regulated the PI3K-Akt and p53 signaling pathways at both transcriptional and post-translational levels: (1) mRNA level: both the HS+QLJP and QLJP groups significantly upregulated the relative expression of PI3K, Akt, and BCL2 and downregulated the expression of p53, BAX, and Caspase-3 in myocardial tissue; (2) protein level: both the HS+QLJP and QLJP groups significantly elevated the protein levels of phosphorylated (p)-PI3K (Y524), p-Akt (Ser473), and BCL2, while reducing the protein levels of p53, BAX, and active Caspase-3 (Cys163) compared with the HS group. In conclusion, QLJP alleviates HS-induced broiler myocardial injury by reducing injury markers, restoring ATP, and regulating the PI3K-Akt/p53 pathway at transcriptional and post-translational levels to inhibit apoptosis. Transcriptomic results further support that QLJP may exert its cardioprotective effects through crosstalk among multiple signaling pathways, providing new insights into its pharmacological mechanism and enriching understanding of the pharmacological effects of Qiling Jiaogulan Powder.

Introduction

Broiler meat is a major source of high-quality protein, and the broiler industry’s health is tied to the global economy. However, heat stress (HS) has become one of the most challenging stressors in the poultry industry, causing adverse effects such as reduced production performance, impaired meat quality, and increased farming costs (Andretta et al., 2021). Broilers are ideal animal models for HS research due to their rapid growth rate, absence of sweat glands, and feather coverage, which restrict heat dissipation capacity (Li et al., 2022).

Under heat stress condition, reactive oxygen species (ROS) are continuously generated in the animal heart, damaging cardiomyocyte nucleic acids, proteins, and lipids, and reducing antioxidant enzyme activity, ultimately inducing myocardial remodeling, heart failure, and significant increase in the secretion of ANP and BNP (Ide et al., 2001; Varghese et al., 2017). HS also causes tachycardia, myocardial ischemia-hypoxia, and increased cTn-I (Maxwell et al., 1995; Li, 2004). Furthermore, HS is recognized as having a cytotoxic effects, which alters biomolecules, disrupts cellular functions, modulates metabolic responses, induces cellular oxidative damage, and activates both apoptosis and necrosis pathways, thereby exacerbating myocardial damage (Belhadj et al., 2016). HS disrupts mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, reducing ATP synthesis and exacerbating cardiac dysfunction (Qian et al., 2004). Collectively, these effects increase sudden cardiac death and ascites syndrome in broilers, causing substantial economic losses (Liu et al., 2020). Nevertheless, the molecular mechanism by which HS induces cardiac damage in broilers remains unclear.

Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), a treasure of ancient Chinese scientific heritage, is gaining increasing global popularity across diverse nations. Emerging basic and clinical studies have demonstrated that TCM and its bioactive components exhibit broad therapeutic potential in regulating myocardial oxidative stress, inflammatory responses, myocardial remodeling, mitochondrial structure, energy metabolism, and other related pathways (Leung et al., 2020; Gao and Hou., 2023). Qiling Jiaogulan Powder (QLJP) is composed of Astragalus membranaceus, Poria cocos, Arnebia euchroma, Gynostemma pentaphyllum. In our previous studies, the main bioactive components of Qiling Jiaogulan Powder have been isolated, including astragalosides, flavonoids, polysaccharides, saponins, triterpenoids, and naphthalenes, among others. These major bioactive components exhibit a variety of pharmacolo- gical effects, such as regulating hypoxic stress, cell proliferation, cell apoptosis, cell cycle progression, vascular endothelial function, and inflammatory response (Yu et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2023). Additionally, QLJP has been found to improve right ventricular hypertrophy in broilers induced by low-temperature exposure by enhancing antioxidant capacity in the right heart, inhibiting myocardial fibrosis and cardiomyocyte apoptosis. These findings also highlight QLJP's potential pharmacological effects in enhancing the anti-stress ability, improving cardiac function, and boosting production performance in broilers (Yu et al., 2025).

Given that broilers are highly susceptible to heat stress, and the proven cardioprotective potential of QLJP, This study aims to investigate the role and mechanism of QLJP in protecting against heat stress-induced cardiac damage in broilers using transcriptomic techniques and animal experiments, so as to provide a theoretical basis for the development of antibiotic-free feed additives suitable for resisting heat stress damage in broilers.

Materials and methods

Animal experiment design and sample collection

In this study, the animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the guidelines approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Henan Institute of Science and Technology, with the approval number LLSC2024061.

A total of 144 14-day-old Ross 308 broilers were conventionally reared until 18 days of age and then randomly assigned to 4 groups: Control group, HS group, QLJP group, and HS+QLJP group. The Control and HS groups were fed a basal diet until 42 days of age, while the QLJP and HS+QLJP groups were fed the basal diet supplemented with 4 g/kg QLJP (Chinese Veterinary Pharmacopoeia Committee, 2017; Yu et al., 2022). Each group had 6 replicates with 6 broilers per replicate. The Control and QLJP groups were reared at the normal broiler feeding temperature (23±1°C) until 42 days of age, while the HS and HS+QLJP groups were kept in a high-temperature environment controlled by heating tubes and thermometers. The temperature regimen was as follows: 34±1°C for 24 hours daily from 21 to 28 days of age, and 34±1°C for 8 hours daily from 28 to 42 days of age. The relative humidity in the house was maintained at approximately 65%. The heat stress trial period lasted from 21 to 42 days of age, during which all broilers had free access to feed and water.

Sample collection and preparation

At 28 and 42 days of age, 2 broilers was randomly selected from each replicate of each group. After cervical dislocation euthanasia, the heart was rapidly harvested. The heart tissues were rapidly collected in accordance with the standard operation and immediately placed in liquid nitrogen for preservation, and subsequently transferred to a -80°C cryogenic refrigerator. This protocol ensures the integrity and quality of animal tissues, which will be used for heart transcriptome sequencing, biological index detection, and gene assay in later stages.

Detection of myocardial injury indicators in broilers

0.1 g of heart tissue was homogenized with normal saline, followed by centrifugation at 3000 rpm for 10 min. The supernatant was collected, and the concentrations of ANP, BNP, and cTn-I in the sample were detected using a one-step double-antibody sandwich enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Specifically, the chicken Natriuretic Peptide (ANP) ELISA Kit, chicken Brain Natriuretic Peptide (BNP) ELISA Kit, and chicken Cardiac Troponin I (cTn-I) ELISA Kit (Shanghai Enzyme-linked Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) were used according to the manufacturer's instructions for respective quantification.

Determination of the concentration of ATP in the heart

For each heart tissue, sample processing and detection were performed according to the instructions of the Chicken Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP) ELISA Kit (Shanghai Enzyme-linked Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China), the kit includes a set of calibration standards. These standards were assayed concurrently with the samples. The concentration of ATP in the samples was then determined by comparing their optical density (O.D.) values to the standard curve.

Extraction of total RNA and mRNA library constructing and sequencing

To further explore the protective mechanism of QLJP against heat stress-induced cardiac injury in broiler chickens, cardiac tissues were collected from 42-day-old broilers in the HS group and HS+QLJP group for whole-transcriptome sequencing. Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent, and its concentration was measured with a NanoDrop 2000 (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA). RNA integrity was evaluated using the RNA Nano 6000 Assay Kit on an Agilent Bioanalyzer 2100 system (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA). High-quality total RNA was selected for subsequent experiments, with three replicate samples per treatment group. For library preparation, 1 μg of total RNA was used to isolate poly(A) mRNA via Oligo(dT) beads. Isolated mRNA was fragmented with divalent cations at high temperature, and primed with random primers. First- and second-strand cDNA were synthesized. The purified double-stranded cDNA underwent end repair and dA-tailing in a single reaction, followed by T-A ligation to add adapters to both ends. Size selection of adapter-ligated DNA was performed using DNA Clean Beads. Each library was amplified by PCR with P5 and P7 primers, and the PCR products were validated.

Indexed libraries were multiplexed and sequenced on an Illumina HiSeq/Illumina NovaSeq/MGI2000 platform using a 2 × 150 paired-end (PE) configuration according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Raw fastq data were processed with Cutadapt (V1.9.1) to remove technical sequences (adapters, PCR primers) and low-quality bases (phred score <20), resulting in high-quality clean reads. Reference genome sequences and gene annotation files were downloaded from repositories such as UCSC, NCBI, or ENSEMBL. The reference genome was indexed using Hisat2 (v2.2.1), and clean reads were aligned to the genome using the same software.

Differential gene analysis and functional enrichment analyses

Differential gene expression between groups was analyzed using DESeq2 software, with thresholds set at |log2FC| ≥ 1.0 and adjusted P-value (padj) < 0.05. The results were visualized via heatmaps. Subsequently, Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis (http://www.geneontology.org/) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis (http://www.genome.jp/kegg/) were performed on the identified differentially expressed genes.

qPCR detection

Total RNA from myocardial tissue was extracted using a TRIzol total RNA extraction kit (Wuhan Servicebio Technology Co., Ltd.), and cDNA synthesis was performed following the instructions of the FastKing one-step reverse transcription fluorescence quantitative kit. The SYBR Green I Real-Time Fluorescent Quantitative PCR Kit consisted of: 10 μL Hieff qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix, 0.4 μL each of upstream and downstream primers, 2 μL cDNA, and 7.2 μL ddH₂O. The reaction conditions were set as: 95°C for 5 minutes; followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 10 seconds and 60°C for 30 seconds (Beijing Zoman Biotechnology Co., Ltd.). Using GAPDH as the internal reference, relative gene expression levels were calculated by the 2⁻ΔΔCt method. Determination of key protein expression levels in PI3K-Akt and p53 signaling pathways

To validate the activation of the PI3K-Akt pathway and the inhibition of the p53 pathway, we performed ELISA-based protein detection on myocardial tissues from all four experimental groups. 0.1 g of myocardial tissue was homogenized in 1 mL of RIPA lysis buffer (supplemented with 20 μL of protease inhibitor cocktail and 20 μL of phosphatase inhibitor cocktail per milliliter of lysis buffer; Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China), Homogenization was conducted at 4°C using a tissue grinder. The homogenate was centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 15 min at 4°C, and the supernatant was collected. The total protein concentration in the supernatant was determined using a BCA Protein Assay Kit (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China). For 28-day-old broiler samples, the sample with the lowest protein content (3.828 mg/mL) was selected as the reference. The remaining samples were comparatively diluted against this reference to ensure a consistent total protein amount per well in the ELISA assay. For 42-day-old broiler samples, the sample with the lowest protein content (3.840 mg/mL) served as the reference, and other samples within the same age group were comparatively diluted based on this reference to maintain uniform loading volume during ELISA detection. This standardized treatment across different age stages ensured that the differences in protein reflected true biological effects rather than variations in sample processing methods across different growth stages.

The protein levels of key molecules in the PI3K-Akt and p53 signaling pathways, were measured using double-antibody sandwich enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits. Statistical analysis

All data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD).

For myocardial injury indicators, cardiac ATP concentration, mRNA expression levels of PI3K-Akt and p53 signaling pathway target genes (PI3K, Akt, BCL2, p53, BAX, Caspase-3), and protein expression levels of key molecules in PI3K-Akt and p53 signaling pathways (PI3K, p-PI3K (Y524), Akt, p-Akt (Ser473), BCL2, p53, BAX, active Caspase-3 (Cys163)): comparisons among the four experimental groups (Control, HS, QLJP, HS+QLJP) were analyzed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with SPSS 23.0 software (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA). Post-hoc multiple comparisons were performed using Duncan's new multiple range test. Significant differences between groups are indicated by different lowercase letters (a, b, c; P < 0.05), and extremely significant differences are denoted by different uppercase letters (A, B, C; P < 0.01). The same letter indicates no significant difference (P > 0.05).

For mRNA expression of differentially expressed genes in broiler hearts (limited to comparisons between the HS and HS+QLJP groups), statistical analysis was conducted using the unpaired two-tailed Student's t-test with GraphPad Prism 8.0.2 software (GraphPad Software, Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). * indicates a significant difference (P < 0.05), and ** indicates an extremely significant difference (P < 0.01).

All data were graphically represented using GraphPad Prism 8.0.2 software.

Results

The effect of QLJP on myocardial injury indicators in heat-stressed broilers

To investigate whether QLJP has a protective effect on myocardial injury in broilers under heat stress conditions, we measured myocardial injury indicators using the ELISA method. As shown in Fig. 1A-1C, compared with the Control group, the concentrations of ANP, BNP, and cTn-Ⅰ in the heart tissues of broilers in the 28d and 42d HS groups were significantly increased (P < 0.01). Compared with the HS group, the HS+QLJP group significantly reduced the concentrations of ANP, BNP, and cTn-Ⅰ in the myocardial tissues (P < 0.01). Compared with the Control group, the QLJP group (non-stressed, QLJP-supplemented) showed a significant decrease in myocardial ANP concentration at 28 days (P < 0.05) and cTn-I concentration at 42 days (P < 0.01), indicating that QLJP may moderately improve baseline cardiac health even without heat stress. More importantly, compared with the HS group, the QLJP group (without heat stress) also significantly reduced the concentrations of ANP, BNP, and cTn-I (P < 0.01). The effect of QLJP on the concentration of ATP in heart of heat-stressed broilers

As shown in Fig. 2, compared with the Control group, continuous heat stress resulted in an extremely significant reduction in myocardial ATP levels in broilers of the HS group (P < 0.01). This indicates that under heat stress, the cardiac burden on broilers is aggravated, leading to an elevated demand for cardiac energy coupled with a reduced supply. Compared with the HS group, the HS+QLJP group showed a significant elevation in cardiac ATP levels (P < 0.01). Compared with the Control group, the QLJP group showed a significant increase in myocardial ATP levels at 28 days (P < 0.05), confirming that QLJP can promote myocardial energy metabolism under normal temperature conditions. When compared with the HS group, the QLJP group exhibited a dramatic elevation in ATP levels (P < 0.01). Discussion

With the continuous development of global warming, heat stress has threatened the healthy development of the poultry industry. A large number of studies have shown that heat stress significantly reduces feed intake and weight gain in broilers, significantly increases feed conversion rate and mortality, causing huge economic losses to the broiler farming industry (Ahaotu et al., 2019; Khan et al., 2023). However, Chinese herbal medicines have unique advantages of low residue, multi-effectiveness and safety, and gradually emerge in the breeding industry, becoming an important force to replace antibiotics. Its application in the prevention of animal diseases and healthy breeding practices is increasingly popular (Tang et al., 2018; Cheng et al., 2024). Moreover, Chinese herbal medicines are rich and diverse in components, including polysaccharides, alkaloids, amino acids, glycosides, vitamins, trace elements, minerals, etc. These substances can improve the digestive system function, enhance metabolism, boost immunity and anti-stress ability of livestock and poultry, thereby improving the influence of heat stress on the physiological function and production performance of livestock and poultry (Zhang et al., 2015; Ibtisham et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2024). This study focused on Qiling Jiaogulan Powder (QLJP), a classic traditional Chinese medicine compound (composed of Astragalus membranaceus, Poria cocos, Alisma orientalis, Gynostemma pentaphyllum, and Arnebia euchroma (Chinese Veterinary Pharmacopoeia Committee, 2017), to explore its mechanism in alleviating heat stress-induced cardiac damage in broilers—an understudied but critical target, as the heart is particularly severely damaged under the influence of heat stress.

Heat stress disrupts cardiac function through systemic hypoxia, oxidative stress, excessive myocardial load (tachycardia, atrial/ventricular overload), ionic disturbance, and energy metabolism disorders (Xu et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2017). Pathologically, this manifests as increased myocardial injury indicators (ANP, BNP, cTn-I) and reduced ATP levels. ANP/BNP elevation indicates cardiac overload and dysfunction (Kerkelä et al., 2015; Roths et al., 2024; Samad et al., 2023), cTn-I release reflects myocardial cell necrosis (O'Brien et al., 2006; Lv et al., 2017; Chen et al., 2020), and ATP depletion stems from impaired mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation (Akbarian et al., 2016; Ahmed-Farid et al., 2021). Our results confirm these effects in the HS group, aligning with previous studies. Notably, both the HS+QLJP and QLJP groups reversed these change, reducing the levels of ANP, BNP, and cTn-I and restoring ATP. These are consistent with the known cardioprotective properties of QLJP’s individual components: astragaloside IV (from Astragalus membranaceus) significantly reduces the mRNA and protein expression of ANP and BNP and restore the balance of ATP/AMP, and play an important role in preventing cardiac dysfunction. (Kong et al., 2021), Poria cocos extract has the effect of reducing the BNP levels in rats with heart failure (Wu et al., 2014). While Gynostemma pentaphyllum preserves mitochondrial integrity to maintain ATP synthesis (Yu et al., 2016), and shikonin (from Arnebia euchroma) attenuates inflammatory response and myocardial fibrosis (Guo et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2017). This synergy highlights QLJP’s advantage over single-component interventions, as it targets multiple pathological pathways simultaneously.

RNA-seq analysis identified 218 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the HS and HS+QLJP groups. Key upregulated DEGs included RRM2, BUB1, DCK, CDK1, BRCA1, COMP, and THBS2, which are associated with DNA repair, cell cycle progression, and ECM maintenance. Specifically, RRM2 supplies deoxyribonucleoside triphosphates (dNTPs) for DNA replication and inhibits apoptosis (Jiao et al., 2022); BUB1 maintains mitotic fidelity under hypoxia (Yang et al., 2012); DCK facilitates DNA repair processes(Zhong et al., 2016); and CDK1 drives cell cycle progression (Mohamed et al., 2018). Additionally, BRCA1 plays a role in repairing HS-induced DNA damage (Shukla et al., 2011); COMP, a cardiovascular protective factor, enhances ECM-cardiomyocyte adhesion and suppresses mitochondrial-mediated apoptosis (Huang et al., 2013); and THBS2 regulates matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) activity to maintain ECM integrity (Lee et al., 2022). The upregulation of these genes suggests that QLJP supports cardiomyocyte survival and regeneration, which is critical for alleviating HS-induced myocardial remodeling. Downregulated DEGs included HK2 and GADD45B. HK2, a glycolytic rate-limiting enzyme, is overexpressed under HS, leading to lactate accumulation and mitochondrial dysfunction(Liang et al., 2002), while GADD45B activates p38 MAPK/JNK-mediated myocardial cell apoptosis (Liebermann and Hoffman, 2008). Collectively, these DEGs targeted HS-induced cardiac damage through three key processes: (via ECM regulation), metabolic recovery (via ATP metabolism modulation), and cell survival promotion (via anti-apoptotic effects). These findings indicate that QLJP exerts its myocardial protective effects through a coordinated regulatory network that repairs damage, stabilizes cardiac structure, and optimizes cardiac metabolism.

GO/KEGG enrichment analyses indicated that DEGs were involved in key processes of cardiac recovery, including extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling, cell proliferation/repair, and cell cycle regulation. Significant enrichment was observed in pathways critical for cardiac health, such as the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, p53 signaling pathway, ECM-receptor interaction, and Cell cycle. These pathways are consistent with literature linking heat stress to apoptosis, DNA damage, and ECM remodeling (Guo., 2021; Chen et al., 2024; Guan et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2025). In this study, QLJP was observed to regulate the PI3K-Akt and p53 signaling pathways at both transcriptional and post-translational levels, suggesting these two pathways serve as central hubs for QLJP to prevent HS-induced myocardial injury. This dual-level regulation also confirms that QLJP not only alters gene transcription but also enhances the functional activity of the PI3K-Akt pathway while suppressing p53-mediated apoptosis; both effects are critical for blocking HS-induced myocardial cell death. Consistent with prior studies, the PI3K-Akt pathway plays a crucial role in maintaining cardiomyocyte survival, mitochondrial function, and energy balance, while also inhibiting ROS accumulation and apoptosis (Matsui et al., 2005; Ghafouri-Fard et al., 2022; Deng et al., 2023). In contrast, the p53 pathway mediates cell cycle arrest, DNA repair, and apoptosis under stress and is closely implicated in the development of cardiac pathologies (Chang et al., 2019). Our protein-level results showed that QLJP specifically enhances the phosphorylation of PI3K and Akt (the active forms of these kinases) rather than altering total protein abundance. This indicats that QLJP activates the PI3K-Akt pathway through post-translational modification−a more direct driver of pro-survival signaling than mere changes in gene expression. Concurrently, QLJP reduces the protein levels of p53 and its downstream effectors (BAX, active Caspase-3), thereby blocking the execution of apoptotic cascades. This regulatory pattern is consistent with reports that astragaloside IV activates PI3K-Akt pathway to suppress p53-dependent apoptosis (Cheng et al., 2019; Gu, 2020), suggesting the multi-component nature of QLJP amplifies this pathway crosstalk to enhance cardioprotection.

Despite these promising results, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the specific active components of QLJP (such as astragaloside IV in Astragalus membranaceus, shikonin in Arnebia euchroma) that drive its protective effects were not isolated and tested individually, leaving the contribution of each herb unclear. Second, our focus was on cardiac function, and further evaluation of systemic effects (such as growth performance, immune function) and their correlation with cardiac PI3K-Akt/p53 pathway activity is needed. Future research addressing these limitations will further enhance the clinical application value of QLJP in poultry health management.

Conclusion

This study systematically investigated the mechanism of Qiling Jiaogulan Powder (QLJP) in protecting against heat stress-induced broiler myocardial injury using phenotypic, molecular, and transcriptomic approaches. Comparative analysis in this study highlights the dual benefits of QLJP: it not only exerts a therapeutic effect in mitigating HS-induced damage (evidenced by the reversal of HS-associated effects in the HS+QLJP group relative to the HS group), but also proactively enhances basal cardiac health and energy metabolism (indicated by improvements in the QLJP group compared with the Control group). Transcriptomic analysis further revealed the potential mechanism of QLJP in mediating the interaction of multiple pathways for cardiac protection in HS-induced broilers. In conclusion, QLJP alleviates HS-induced myocardial injury in broilers through a coordinated regulatory network. This network involves reducing injury markers, restoring energy metabolism, and inhibiting apoptosis via PI3K-Akt/p53 pathway. Given its advantages of low residue and multi-target action, QLJP shows promise as an antibiotic-free feed additive for mitigating HS-related cardiac damage in broiler production. Future studies should focus on isolating the active components of QLJP, and exploring its systemic effects (include growth performance, immunity) to further strengthen its clinical applicability.

Developing an advanced course of vaccinology in middle-income countries: The case of China

Introduction

The Immunization Agenda 2030 outlined the importance of capacity building for researchers, regulators and public health officials at national, regional and local levels in its multiple strategic priorities for building an effective immunization programme [1]. The mission of University of Chinese Academy of Sciences focuses on frontier science and applied biotechnology to address the challenges for both the development of the country as well as world sustainability, facing the forefront of the global scientific and technological advances, to compete on the main economic battlefield, and to address the major needs of the country and for the lives and health of the people. For some time, science and technology has provided strong support in controlling infection, developing vaccines and promoting drug research and development (R & D), and support and continuation of this provide model problems and solutions for the research community. It is therefore our guiding philosophy behind our scientific approach [2]. The coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic also served as an alarm for governments of middle-income countries to build domestic vaccine-related capacity for both pandemic preparedness and the health needs of the local population. While vaccinology training courses are carried out around the world, only 13, out of a total of 33, of these identified advanced courses of vaccinology are located in low-and-middle-income countries [3], [4]. The poor equity in training distribution is indicative of the requirement for vaccinology courses in the developing world, where high-quality immunization services are severely needed to fill the largest gap in global vaccine coverage. The value of short-term training courses has been well recognized globally, especially in the middle-income countries, and with the support of governments, non-governmental organizations (NGOs), educational institutes, industries, and other global health actors, more and more advanced vaccinology courses are emerging around the world each year.

In response to the global call to improve vaccine capacity and meet local needs, the Chinese Vaccinology Course (CNVAC) was developed in 2018 in a partnership between the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences (UCAS), the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention (China CDC), and the Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation (BMGF). It is the only national advanced vaccinology training course in China. Each cohort of trainees is composed of around 70 professionals ranging from policy makers and regulators, to researchers and public health officials in China. More than 200 professionals have already received training by participating in CNVAC.

Development of CNVAC

The idea of developing an advanced vaccinology course originated in the summer of 2017. The BMGF, along with UCAS and China CDC, jointly assessed the landscape of vaccinology education in China and found only separate vaccine-related training programs at each level on technical topics targeting specific populations, with no comprehensive training course being available. The value of, and need for, advanced courses in vaccinology were recognized by these parties, and the decision was made to build China’s first advanced vaccinology course to promote this discipline within academia, as well as to prepare course trainees for professional development and meet the needs and demands of the vaccine industry.

The selection of course implementors was deliberate and well-considered. UCAS is a leading educational institution backed by the Chinese Academy of Sciences, the national public research body which has a rich experience with vaccine development. China CDC, on the other hand, is the designer, implementor and technical management body of China’s national immunization program, covering a population of 1.4 billion people. Jointly, the combination of these two parties offered both academic and practical background for the course, which greatly facilitated the design of the programme, the use of instructor resources, and also the recruitment of trainees.

CNVAC established its governing system as the first step. The steering committee was formed as its governing body, with the 11 domestic committee members carefully selected to ensure the diverse representation of vaccine stakeholders, including universities, disease control agencies, hospitals, vaccine regulators, research institutions, civil societies and vaccine manufacturers. Later, an international advisory committee was also established to enhance reach and global engagement. Experts and leaders from international organizations, disease control agencies, and research institutes and universities were invited as inaugural committee members. Together, the steering committee and the international advisory committee played important roles in shaping CNVAC’s direction. With the governance structure in place, CNVAC started the design of its first course.

An evolving design of courses

Throughout the past four years, CNVAC has been continuously evaluating the needs of trainees, improving its course quality, and adapting to changes in the environment. The inaugural CNVAC was held in 2018 with 68 trainees and 55 lecturers. After a stringent selection process, involving considerations on the ratio of peer discussion to lecturer-trainee interaction, a five-day training course was designed covering the topics of immunization, vaccine research and development, clinical research, regulation, supervision, vaccine economics, policy, and communication. It set the foundation of CNVAC for future course development.

In 2019, the second CNVAC was built on the success of, and feedback from, the first. Based on the experiences and lessons from the first course, the second CNVAC was developed with special improvements. Specified feedback from trainees was forwarded to the lecturers for consideration and modification, for example the volume of voice and speaking speed. The interpreter also received feedback. Personal dietary preferences were considered in the preparation of meals. CNVAC also invited the major composer of the newly adopted Vaccine Administration Law in China to produce legal updates on vaccine management and immunization services.

Due to the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic, CNVAC did not provide courses in 2020. Instead, several online sessions were provided from 2020 to 2021. These online sessions covered topics of real-world interest, including COVID-19 vaccine trials, the latest legislation, market demands and vaccine development, modeling of pandemic and also vaccines in global health.

In October 2021, to cope with the challenges of social distancing, and to reduce gathering of participants for pandemic control, the third CNVAC adopted a hybrid mode of both online and offline classrooms at Duke Kunshan University, one of the leading global health research institutes in China. Videos of lectures in 2018 and 2019 were put online with subtitles in Chinese for self-learning two weeks before the offline classroom was open to trainees. The course organizer was closely monitoring the online class attendance and participation, and provided online question and answer (Q & A) sessions to compensate for the limitations of pre-recorded videos. In addition, CNVAC was officially registered as a Beijing continuing medical education course, which will attract more trainees. The COVID-19 vaccine was the thematic topic in this year and classes on epidemiology, monitoring, vaccine development, and clinical trials, production, and immunization were covered.

In November 2023, the forth CNVAC was held at the UCAS campus near Yanqi Lake in Beijing. Using the model of the third CNVAC, this session also decided to utilize a theme, specifically the pneumococcal vaccine (PCV). After the relaxation of pandemic prevention policies, participant trainees were able to once again attend the lectures in-person, and engage in peer group discussion tables on a daily basis. In addition to this, international experts were once again able to come to China and fully participate in the conference, with overseas representatives coming from a number of institutions and representing over a quarter of invited lecturers. Topics covered included the usual essential courses, such as talks covering regulation and the most recent legislation, however a number of basic science and clinical courses were also included, such as courses on structural biology considerations of vaccine design, as well as courses on the isolation and identification for diagnosis of pneumococcal infection. The topic of PCV was partially chosen owing to of a number of trials being conducted in China, through the work and funding from a number of institutions, using this vaccine. As this vaccine is not currently introduced into China’s national immunization program, but does hold World Health Organization (WHO)-recommendation, the topics chosen also enabled trainees understand PCV in multiple dimensions, such as the history and recent progress of PCV development, the surveillance challenges of Streptococcus pneumoniae, the methodology of evaluating vaccine effectiveness, the optimization of immunization schedule, vaccine economics for PCV, PCV introduction in Asia Countries, etc.

Each year, CNVAC includes a more diverse range of experts and topics, with trainee participation put at the center of all considerations. This was exemplified by the forth CNVAC, and we hope this trend continues with each CNVAC session.

Value of CNVAC in China

In the past 5 years, CNVAC has successfully delivered four courses and several online seminars. It introduced the latest vaccinology knowledge to trainees, broadened their horizons, provided them with global perspectives and a rich range of accumulated resources for vaccinology education in China.

An active alumni community has been established, with young and enthusiastic professionals from all vaccine-related sectors. WeChat groups of each cohort were used as the platform for sharing vaccine-related information, news, and educational resources. Past trainees use these platforms to spontaneously and actively discuss vaccine-related topics, even after the course has been completed. The alumni are also important sources for future course and seminar planning, as many of them will provide suggestions on new topics. CNVAC has paid special attention to building a harmonious trainee community and incorporating teambuilding sessions during the course. Sports were a very popular choice among trainees and lecturers, and off-class casual communication was continued during Ping-Pong and basketball playing.

Existing course materials have been used for continuous education planning. Selected course videos were used in the third CNVAC due to pandemic control measures, but this hybrid mode of training can be further adopted in future training sessions. The framework and course materials are currently being used in the development of China’s first vaccinology textbook, with the help of participation of previous lecturers. Master’s and doctoral degree programs, in collaboration with international institutes, are in a planning phase to maximize the resources accumulated during the development of CNVAC in the past several years. Overall, CNVAC serves as a solid foundation of vaccinology education in China with a promising future.

Future vaccinology training in middle-income countries

During the COVID-19 pandemic, the world has once again recognized the value of vaccines in combating emerging infectious diseases. Although vaccine hesitancy has been a major barrier to improving vaccine coverage in developed countries, the world has observed a huge gap in production, supply, and service delivery in middle-income countries [5], [6]. World leaders have now recognized the value of local vaccine industries in pandemic preparedness and protection of people from infectious disease. Capacity building was naturally one practical way to improve this situation. We recommend the following key areas for future vaccinology training in middle-income countries:

Understanding the recommended vaccines in national immunization program

Deepen understanding of several existing non-national immunization program vaccines to help gather momentum in society to promote their introduction. Similar to many non-GAVI middle-income countries, China is facing challenges in including several WHO-recommended vaccines in its national immunization program, e.g., HPV, Hib, PCV and Rota. The level of understanding of these vaccines remains low among parents and healthcare workers.

Strengthening clinical research to improve global vaccine access and equity

Expansion of new vaccine products in the global market requires an increasing amount of clinical research in developing countries [7]. This is a common issue due to local disease burden and the requirements in place for market access. For the vaccine industry, it is important to obtain the capacity to carry out clinical trials domestically and across the world. COVID-19 vaccines presented an example of global trials, where vaccine manufacturers were conducting clinical trials all over the world. Strengthening clinical research capacity fosters a more comprehensive understanding of vaccine performance, and at the same time improves global vaccine access and equity.

Promoting global collaboration on vaccinology training

Global and regional collaboration in vaccinology training promotes experience sharing, especially when facing similar situations. CNVAC, alongside over 30 vaccinology courses, is a member of the International Collaboration on Advanced Vaccinology Training (https://www.icavt.org/). As of now, two conferences have been held to discuss the landscape, experience and challenges of advanced vaccinology training around the world, and an online platform has been established to provide training information and resources to the public. The continuous need for assessment, design of refresher courses, and boosting of inter-partner cooperation to deliver regional courses are its unique functions as a platform. Suggestions from the platform can be well integrated into future course design.

Direct effect of nitrofurantoin on dental pulp stem cell viability and odontoblastic differentiation potential
Authors: Santhiya Ravichandran, Karthick Kumaravadivel, Sankar Vishwanath, Bency Joseph Raj, Sebeena Mathew, Deepa Natesan Thangaraj
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Aims:This study aimed to compare the effects of different concentrations of nitrofurantoin and triple antibiotic paste (TAP) on the viability and odontoblastic differentiation potential of human dental pulp stem cells (DPSCs).Materials and Methods:DPSCs were cultured and exposed to serial dilutions of nitrofurantoin and TAP. Cell viability was measured using the MTT assay at 72 h, and alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity was assessed at 7 days to evaluate differentiation potential.Statistical Analysis Used:One-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc test and independent t-tests; significance at P < 0.05.Results:Nitrofurantoin demonstrated significantly superior preservation of DPSC viability compared with TAP across all tested concentrations. ALP activity showed a concentration-dependent increase in both the groups.Conclusion:Nitrofurantoin exhibits superior biocompatibility with DPSCs while maintaining differentiation capacity, making it a promising alternative intracanal medicament for regenerative endodontic procedures.

INTRODUCTION

Regenerative endodontic procedures (REPs) utilize tissue engineering and stem cell regeneration to restore vital pulp tissue in necrotic, immature permanent teeth, thereby promoting root development and apical closure.[12]

The success of REPs primarily depends on the chemical debridement of the canal space, with minimal-to-no mechanical instrumentation. However, effective disinfection should be achieved without compromising the viability or regenerative potential of resident stem cell populations.[3] Since REPs recommend minimal instrumentation of the root canal, effective intracanal medicaments with antimicrobial properties play a crucial role in canal disinfection and in establishing a favorable environment for pulpal regeneration.

The American Association of Endodontists suggests the use of triple antibiotic paste (TAP) as an intracanal medicament for REPs due to its broad-spectrum antibacterial activity against polymicrobial root canal flora.[4] However, its clinical use is limited by significant drawbacks. At higher concentrations, TAP exhibits cytotoxicity toward stem cells, reducing their proliferation and differentiation potential.[5] In addition, tooth discoloration from minocycline and concerns about antibiotic resistance have led to the search for alternative agents.

Nitrofurantoin, a synthetic nitrofuran antibiotic traditionally used to treat urinary tract infections, has recently gained attention in endodontics. Its broad-spectrum antimicrobial activity, including efficacy against Enterococcus faecalis, and its resilience against the development of resistance make it a viable option as an intracanal medicament.[678]

Gonçalves et al. and Alrahman et al.[69] demonstrated that nitrofurantoin, when formulated as a paste, showed comparable or superior antimicrobial efficacy to TAP against E. faecalis and polymicrobial biofilms, at lower concentrations.[3] Despite these promising antimicrobial results, no prior study has directly evaluated the biocompatibility of nitrofurantoin with dental pulp stem cells (DPSCs), particularly its effect on cell viability and odontoblastic differentiation potential, both of which are crucial for successful regeneration.

The aim of this study was to evaluate the effect of nitrofurantoin on the viability and odontoblastic differentiation potential of DPSCs.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

After approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC-PG/MAR/2025/265), the in vitro study was conducted.

Cell culture and maintenance

Written informed consent was obtained from the patients before tooth extraction. DPSCs were harvested from freshly extracted third molar specimens under strictly aseptic conditions. Each tooth was mechanically sectioned to expose the pulp chamber; the pulp tissue was carefully excised, finely minced, and subsequently subjected to enzymatic digestion using collagenase type I (3 mg/mL) and dispase (4 mg/mL) at 37°C for 30 min. Cells derived from passages 2 through 5 were exclusively utilized throughout experimental procedures. Culture was maintained in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, in a humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2 at 37°C, with subculture performed upon attaining 80%–90% confluency.

Preparation of intracanal medicaments

A stock solution of nitrofurantoin (Niftas, Intas Pharmaceuticals, India) at 100 mg/mL was prepared in sterile dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 0.1%). Working concentrations of 100, 10, 1, 0.1, and 0.01 mg/mL were obtained by serial dilution in DMEM. TAP was compounded in equal proportions from metronidazole (Flagyl, Abbott Healthcare Pvt Ltd, India), ciprofloxacin (Ciplox, CIPLA LTD, India), and minocycline (Minoz, Sun Pharmaceutical Industries Ltd, India), subsequently solubilized in DMEM and diluted to concentrations identical to those used for nitrofurantoin.

Cell seeding and treatment

DPSCs were seeded into 96-well culture plates at 5 × 104 cells per well and allowed 24 h for adhesion. The culture medium was subsequently replaced with DMEM containing nitrofurantoin or TAP at each test concentration. An untreated cell control and a vehicle control (0.1% DMSO) were included in parallel. Cells were exposed to treatments for 72 h before assessment.

MTT assay

Following 72-h incubation, culture medium was aspirated, and 100 μL of MTT solution (0.5 mg/mL in DMEM) was added per well. Plates were incubated at 37°C for 4 h in the dark to permit formazan crystal formation by metabolically active cells. The crystals were subsequently solubilised by adding 100 μL of DMSO per well, followed by gentle agitation for 10 min at room temperature. Optical density (OD) was recorded at 570 nm (reference: 630 nm) using a microplate spectrophotometer.

The relative cell viability (%) was measured using the formula:

Viability (%) = (OD sample/ODcontrol) × 100

Alkaline phosphatase activity

To evaluate odontoblastic differentiation, nitrofurantoin- and TAP-treated DPSCs were maintained for up to 7 days with medium replenished every 48 h. At the conclusion of the incubation period, culture supernatants were removed, and cell monolayers were rinsed with phosphate-buffered saline. A volume of 100 μL of p-nitrophenyl phosphate substrate solution was introduced to each well, and plates were incubated at 37°C for 30 min to allow enzymatic hydrolysis. The colorimetric reaction was halted by the addition of 50 μL of 0.1 M sodium hydroxide, and absorbance was measured at 405 nm using an ELISA microplate reader using the formula,

Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity (U/L) = OD at 405 nm × Volume (mL) × 1000/Molar Extinction Coefficient (18.45) × Incubation Time (min)

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was done using SPSS software (Version 23.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). For intragroup group comparison, one-way analysis of variance followed by Tukey’s post hoc test was used. Independent t-tests were used to compare nitrofurantoin and TAP groups at corresponding concentrations. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Figure 1 illustrates the percentage of mean cell viability of nitrofurantoin and TAP groups at different concentrations compared using the independent t-test. Across all the concentrations tested, nitrofurantoin consistently demonstrated significantly higher cell viability compared with TAP. At the highest concentration (100 mg/mL), nitrofurantoin recorded a mean ± standard deviation value of cell viability, 7.53 ± 1.72, which was greater than TAP (3.29 ± 0.24) with a statistically significant P = 0.01. At 10 mg/mL, the difference was more pronounced, with nitrofurantoin showing 18.68 ± 1.95 compared to 4.09 ± 0.13 for TAP (P < 0.001). Even at the lowest concentration tested (0.01 mg/mL), nitrofurantoin showed superior biocompatibility (72.54 ± 1.93 vs. 48.79 ± 1.15 for TAP; P < 0.001). Collectively, these findings confirm that nitrofurantoin exerts less cytotoxicity toward DPSCs compared with TAP in a concentration-dependent manner.

Figure 2 presents the mean ALP activity (U/L) of the nitrofurantoin and TAP groups at different concentrations compared using the independent t-test. Between the two groups across all tested concentrations, the nitrofurantoin group consistently demonstrated significantly lower ALP activity than the TAP group. At 100 μg/mL, the mean ALP activity in the nitrofurantoin group (0.380 ± 0.000 U/L) was significantly lower than that of the TAP group (0.443 ± 0.006 U/L). Similar statistically significant reductions (P < 0.001) were observed at 50 μg/mL, 25 μg/mL, and 10 μg/mL. The overall results demonstrated that nitrofurantoin exhibited significantly greater biocompatibility with DPSCs compared to TAP, as evidenced by higher viability across all tested concentrations, although ALP activity remained lower than in the TAP group.

DISCUSSION

DPSCs exhibit remarkable mesenchymal stem cell characteristics, making them invaluable for REPs. These cells demonstrate high proliferative capacity, multipotency, and the unique ability to differentiate into odontoblast-like cells capable of forming functional dentin–pulp complex tissue.[10] The fundamental goal of regenerative endodontics extends beyond mere infection elimination to restore genuine tissue vitality while preserving the regenerative potential of resident stem cell populations. The present study examined the comparative effects of nitrofurantoin and TAP on DPSC survival and differentiation capacity, revealing substantially superior biocompatibility profiles for the single-agent formulation across all tested concentrations.

At 10 mg/mL, nitrofurantoin-treated cultures maintained 18.7% viability compared to merely 4.1% with TAP (P < 0.001), while at the clinically relevant concentration of 0.1 mg/mL, viability reached 76.7% versus 32.0% for TAP (P < 0.001). These findings align with the previous studies suggesting that the single-agent intracanal medicaments demonstrate superior stem cell preservation compared to multiantibiotic combinations.[11] The differential cytotoxicity observed between these formulations may be attributed to fundamental differences in their antimicrobial mechanism of actions. Nitrofurantoin susceptibility in bacteria correlates with the presence of bacterial nitro reductases, which converts the drug into highly reactive electrophilic intermediates. These intermediates nonselectively target bacterial ribosomal proteins, leading to inhibition of protein synthesis. As this activation process primarily occurs in bacterial cells, it may explain the comparatively lower cytotoxic effects observed in mammalian cells.[12]

This selectivity contrasts sharply with TAP’s multitarget approach, where minocycline induces oxidative stress through mitochondrial dysfunction, ciprofloxacin disrupts DNA synthesis via topoisomerase inhibition, and metronidazole generates cytotoxic free radicals that compromise cellular membrane integrity.[13] The combined cytotoxic effects of ciprofloxacin, metronidazole, and minocycline in TAP account for the observed decline in cell viability, raising clinical concerns that such extensive stem cell death could deplete the essential reservoir needed for successful regeneration.[14]

In terms of ALP activity, both medicaments induced concentration-dependent increase in ALP activity, with this early odontoblastic differentiation marker reaching peak levels at higher concentrations. This phenomenon reflects a well-documented stress-induced differentiation response observed in mesenchymal stem cells exposed to sublethal environmental stressors.[15] Such stress triggers can activate critical signaling pathways, including MAPK cascades (ERK1/2, JNK, and p38) or Wnt/β-catenin signaling networks that drive accelerated lineage commitment.[16]

However, this enhanced enzymatic activity must be distinguished from physiologically normal differentiation, which optimally occurs when adequate cell numbers are maintained alongside appropriate differentiation signals.[17] The selective survival of differentiation-competent DPSC subpopulations may also contribute to the enhanced ALP activity in surviving cultures. However, true regenerative success requires both sufficient cell populations and their successful functional maturation. The molecular orchestration of odontoblastic differentiation involves precise temporal regulation of transcription factors and matrix proteins essential for dentin formation. Early lineage commitment depends on RUNX2 and OSX expression, while terminal differentiation requires upregulation of DSPP and DMP1 for proper dentin matrix formation and mineralization.[18]

The superior preservation of DPSC viability by nitrofurantoin treatment maintains the cellular machinery necessary for coordinated expression of these critical differentiation markers, suggesting enhanced potential for functional dentinogenesis compared to the depleted cell populations surviving TAP exposure.[19] From a clinical perspective, these findings have immediate implications for regenerative endodontic protocols. Current guidelines emphasize the critical importance of balancing effective antimicrobial activity with minimal cytotoxic effects on resident stem cell populations.[20] Nitrofurantoin’s single-agent formulation offers several distinct advantages over traditional multiantibiotic approaches, including elimination of concerns regarding antibiotic resistance development associated with combination therapies and complete avoidance of minocycline-induced tooth discoloration that renders TAP unsuitable for anterior teeth.[21]

In addition, recent studies have demonstrated enhanced dentinal tubule penetration capabilities for nitrofurantoin compared to conventional medicaments, potentially improving antimicrobial efficacy within complex canal anatomy while maintaining favorable biocompatibility profiles.[22] The clinical significance of these findings extends to pain management considerations, as emerging evidence suggests that nitrofurantoin may provide superior postoperative symptom control compared to multiantibiotic formulations.[23] Nevertheless, several important limitations must be acknowledged regarding the present investigation.

The in vitro experimental design, while providing valuable mechanistic insights, cannot fully recapitulate the complex dynamic environment encountered clinically, where inflammatory mediators, bacterial endotoxins, and fluid dynamics significantly influence both medicament bioavailability and cellular responses.[24] Future investigations should focus on incorporating three-dimensional culture models, coculture systems involving different pulpal cell populations, and well-designed animal experiments. These approaches would help assess true pulp regeneration outcomes, including patterns of tissue healing, continuation of root development, and long-term outcomes. In addition, comprehensive molecular studies evaluating the expression patterns of the major odontoblastic differentiation markers – such as DSPP, DMP1, RUNX2, and OSX over extended culture periods – would provide more definitive evidence regarding the differentiation mechanisms operating under nitrofurantoin treatment conditions. Thus, nitrofurantoin, a highly promising alternative intracanal medicament in REPs, warrants extensive preclinical validation and carefully designed clinical trials to establish standardized treatment protocols.

CONCLUSION

Within the limitations of this investigation, nitrofurantoin demonstrated markedly superior preservation of DPSC viability compared to TAP while preserving its ability to support odontoblastic differentiation. Among the tested concentrations, 0.1 mg/mL exhibited the favorable balance between cell viability and odontoblastic differentiation potential. While higher concentrations showed increased ALP activity, they were associated with reduced cell survival. Therefore, 0.1 mg/mL may be considered the most viable concentration for achieving both biocompatibility and regenerative potential.

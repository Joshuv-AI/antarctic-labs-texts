Treatment of hydatid cyst with albendazole desensitization: A pediatric case report
Authors: Nilay Çalişkan, Raif Coskun, Hamit Bologur, Güler Yildırım, Hilal Güngör, Oktav Bosnali, Deniz Özçeker
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Albendazole is a benzimidazole group drug used alone or in combination with surgery in the treatment of many helminthiasis, especially hydatid cysts. Type 1 hypersensitivity reaction has been reported rarely. Treatment with desensitization has been successfully applied in a few adult patients, however literature information on pediatric patients was not available. Here, we present a pediatric case in which Type 1 reaction occurred due to the use of albendazole during hydatid cyst treatment and undergone desensitization.

Introduction

Hydatid cyst disease is a helminthic infection due to Echinococcus granulosus. In addition, the disease progresses with cysts in many organs, especially the liver and lungs.[1] Albendazole is a benzimidazole group drug used alone or in combination with surgery in the treatment of many helminthiases, especially hydatid cysts. It could be given in the form of continuous or intermittent for 6 months. Type 1 hypersensitivity reactions have been reported rarely in the literature after the use of albendazole. Treatment with desensitization has been successfully applied in adult patients, but literature information about pediatric patients has not been reached.[2]

Case Report

A 12-year-old girl who had previously undergone surgery due to hydatid cyst without a known history of drug allergies,presented to the pediatric allergy clinic after developing pruritic erythematous plaques on her cheeks and arms on the 5th day of albendazole use. Laboratory tests demonstrated serum total immunoglobulin E level of 3411 kU/L and eosinophil (%): 20%. Since there was no alternative treatment option for the patient’s existing hydatid cysts, desensitization was planned. The patient was premedicated with cetirizine during desensitization and received 15 mg/kg (400 mg/day) of albendazole treatment successfully for 6 months [Table 1].

Discussion

Our case is the first successful desensitization case with albendazole in children. Drug hypersensitivity reactions account for approximately 10% of all adverse reactions.[3] These reactions can progress from mild maculopapular eruptions to life-threatening anaphylaxis and severe cutaneous drug reactions with high mortality. If the drug to which the patient is sensitive must be used and there is no alternative treatment option, desensitization is recommended. Success rates can vary depending on clinical symptoms and application protocols. Cystic hydatid disease caused by Echinococcus species is a zoonotic disease seen in rural areas. In addition to the lack of standard treatment options for liver hydatid cysts, the decision is based on the patient’s age, cyst location, and the presence of complications. Albendazole treatment can be used alone for 6 months or in combination with surgery. Side effects include diarrhea, abdominal pain, transaminase elevation, hepatic failure, dizziness, neutropenia due to myelosuppression, and alopecia.[4] Type 1 hypersensitivity reactions have been reported rarely after albendazole use in the literature.[5] More information and experience on desensitization is required in the pediatric age group. The literature on this subject is very scarce, and most of the pediatric desensitization protocols have been adapted from adult instructions by changing the cumulative dose. Albendazole desensitization has been successfully applied in a small number of adult patients, but literature information about pediatric patients could not be found.[2]

As a result, this case was presented to emphasize that our premedication and desensitization protocol is safe and can be successfully applied in the pediatric age group in cases of Type 1 hypersensitivity which is rarely caused by benzimidazole group using in medical treatment of hydatid cysts.

Since there is no reported protocol for children, our study will contribute to the literature in this regard.

Use of flexor digitorum plane block in pediatric patients undergoing syndactyly release
Authors: Shruti Shrey, Chandni Sinha, Amarjeet Kumar, Vikram Chandra, Soumya Singh
Published: 2024

Regional anesthesia may be one of the best solutions for perioperative pediatric pain management.[1] Interfascial plane blocks have been a part of analgesic regime in pediatric patients undergoing various types of surgeries. Here, we describe the successful use of flexor digitorum plane (FDP) block for perioperative analgesia in pediatric patients undergoing syndactyly release. Written and informed consent for publication was taken from the parent.

Five children aged between 1 and 4 years were posted for simple syndactyly release of the medial 2/3 fingers. After induction of general anesthesia (propofol 2 mg/kg and fentanyl 0.5 mcg/kg), appropriate size i-gel supraglottic airway was inserted. Following this, a high frequency hockey stick linear probe was kept transversely around 4–5 cm distal to the cubital fossa. Median and ulnar nerves were visualized in the fascial plane between flexor digitorum superficialis and profundus [Figure 1]. A 22-G, 5 cm echogenic needle was inserted in-plane lateral to medial direction to enter the FD plane at the centre. A total volume of up to 0.4 ml/kg 0.2% ropivacaine was injected with the aim of covering ulnar and median nerves. Intravenous paracetamol (PCM) was given according to the body weight. Intraoperatively, the hemodynamics remained stable with no increase in heart rate or mean arterial pressure. Postoperatively pain was assessd using FLACC score (faces, leg, activity, cry, and consolability) at 0, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, and 24 hours. Any patient with FLACC score >4 would receive syrup PCM orally according to the body weight. Pain scores remained less than 4 during the first 24 hours postoperatively with only 2 patients requiring a single dose of paracetamol. These two patients required PCM at 12 and 16 hours, respectively. None of the patients developed nausea, vomiting, or any features of local anesthetic systemic toxicity.

Traditionally, metacarpal blocks or local infiltration have been used for such cases. Local infiltration distorts the tissue planes, while landmark-based metacarpal or wrist block has not been as efficacious as ultrasound-guided median and ulnar nerve blocks.[2]

Flexor digitorum plane block is a novel technique described by Jolly et al.[3] who in their dye study showed consistent staining of median and ulnar nerves through a single injection. In our cases, we also found minimal perioperative opioid requirement and reduced pain scores up to 24 hours postoperatively. This novel technique involves a single injection for blocking both the ulnar and median nerves, along with lower risk of nerve injury given the distance from the nerves.[4] Complete hand anesthesia can also be achieved with this block in addition to blockade of the radial nerve. Since it is a distal block, both motor and sensory functions of the proximal upper limb can be preserved, thereby improving patient compliance, early mobilization, and recovery. Further, randomized studies could be formulated comparing the block dynamics and action with other blocks.

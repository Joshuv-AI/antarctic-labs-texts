Extravascular implantable cardioverter-defibrillators implantation in a pacing-dependent patient with a cardiac resynchronization therapy system: A structured intraoperative protocol to validate device-device interaction

Introduction

Implantable cardioverter-defibrillators (ICDs) are an established therapy for the prevention of sudden death in patients at risk of malignant ventricular arrhythmias.1 The extravascular ICD (EV-ICD) represents a hybrid strategy that combines the advantages of the traditional transvenous ICD and the subcutaneous ICD (S-ICD). By positioning the defibrillation and sensing lead in the substernal space, the EV-ICD avoids transvenous lead-related complications while maintaining closer proximity to the myocardium compared with the S-ICD, allowing lower defibrillation energy and antitachycardia pacing (ATP).

Previous studies have demonstrated the safety and efficacy of EV-ICD implantation across diverse patient populations,2, 3, 4 including coexistence with dual-chamber pacemakers or cardiac contractility modulation systems.5, 6, 7 However, data remain limited in pacing-dependent patients with cardiac resynchronization therapy (CRT) systems, particularly when continuous biventricular pacing complicates device-device compatibility and sensing interpretation.

We present a case of EV-ICD implantation in an elderly patient with complete biventricular pacing dependence, highlighting a structured intraoperative protocol for managing device-device interaction and sensing optimization.

Case presentation

A 90-year-old woman with complete atrioventricular block underwent right-sided dual-chamber pacemaker implantation in 2011. Pacing-induced cardiomyopathy subsequently developed, and the system was upgraded to a CRT with pacemaker (CRT-P) in August 2024. The patient remained 100% biventricular pacing dependent. In September 2025, she presented with recurrent palpitations and presyncope. Echocardiography demonstrated a left ventricular ejection fraction of 46%. Electrocardiography confirmed sustained monomorphic ventricular tachycardia at ∼207 beats/min, necessitating defibrillation therapy.

2 strategies were considered. A CRT with defibrillator (CRT-D) upgrade would require extraction of the existing right ventricular pacing lead, posing significant procedural risks in a nonagenarian. Alternatively, a contralateral EV-ICD could provide ATP and defibrillation while preserving the existing CRT-P. After multidisciplinary evaluation and shared decision making with the patient and her family, EV-ICD implantation was pursued.

Preoperative computed tomography revealed clockwise cardiac rotation, with lung interposition along the substernal tunneling trajectory, resulting in a sternum-heart distance of 8.8 mm. This configuration suggested increased separation between the substernal lead and the ventricular myocardium, potentially resulting in modest R-wave amplitudes.

To ensure compatibility, a dedicated stepwise intraoperative interaction testing protocol was implemented (Figure 1). Acceptable sensing configuration was defined as follows: R-wave amplitude ≥ 1 mV; P-wave amplitude ≤ 0.3 mV with R/P amplitude ratio > 10; and absence of ventricular double counting or pacing-related artifacts. Electrical stress testing was performed sequentially. First, atrial bipolar pacing at high and low outputs was assessed to exclude atrial far-field oversensing. Second, biventricular pacing was evaluated under stepwise output reduction to provoke potential double counting or pacing-related artifacts. Third, sensing performance across vectors was assessed, with ventricular sensing amplitudes ranged from 0.7 to 1.4 mV across vectors. The R1-R2 vector demonstrated stable amplitudes of 1.3–1.4 mV and was selected for chronic sensing. Despite continuous pacing and anatomical constraints, no atrial oversensing, T-wave oversensing, or ventricular double counting was observed. Finally, defibrillation threshold testing confirmed appropriate detection of induced ventricular fibrillation at 0.45 mV, with successful termination with a 30-J shock (impedance 49 Ω). The EV-ICD was programmed with a ventricular fibrillation detection interval of 320 ms (188 beats/min), with ATP enabled before shock therapy. Early postoperative interrogation confirmed system efficacy. Slight oversensing of pacing spikes was observed during unipolar pacing provocation; however, sensing remained stable under standard bipolar pacing configuration, which was maintained chronically. On postoperative day 2, a spontaneous fast ventricular tachycardia episode was appropriately discriminated and terminated by ATP. After vector optimization and narrowing of sensing, the patient remained stable. At 3-month follow-up, sensing parameters were stable without inappropriate detections.

Discussion

This case demonstrates that EV-ICD implantation in pacing-dependent patients with CRT systems can be performed safely with meticulous anatomical and electrical validation.

While CRT-D upgrade remains conventional in patients requiring resynchronization and defibrillation, risks associated with chronic lead extraction and additional transvenous lead placement may outweigh benefits in selected elderly patients. EV-ICD may provide therapeutic completeness without transvenous risks, with decisions guided by shared patient-centered risk-benefit assessment.

Unlike the S-ICD, whose sensing vectors can be preassessed, EV-ICD sensing validation can be performed only after substernal lead implantation, making structured intraoperative electrical validation essential, particularly in pacing-dependent patients. In CRT systems, biventricular pacing may produce closely timed right and left ventricular pacing-related artifacts, together with a wide, occasionally multiphasic paced QRS complexes, increasing the risk of pacing-related oversensing or double counting. The stepwise stress testing protocol was designed to sequentially evaluate these potential interactions, enabling differentiation between visible far-field signals and clinically significant oversensing. Notably, in patients with intrinsic rhythm, baseline sensing assessment should precede pacing provocation.

During postoperative interrogation, pacing interference observed during unipolar pacing highlighted the importance of maintaining bipolar configurations to minimize device-device interaction.

Conclusion

EV-ICD implantation was feasible and clinically effective in a biventricular pacing–dependent patient with complex substernal geometry. A predefined stepwise intraoperative testing protocol and careful postoperative programming were essential to ensure reliable sensing and therapy delivery. In selected high-risk patients in whom transvenous lead extraction is undesirable, a contralateral EV-ICD represents a strategic alternative to a CRT-D upgrade.

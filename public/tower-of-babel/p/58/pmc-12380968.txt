Immune profiling in oncology: bridging the gap between technology and treatment
Authors: Nanthini Ravi, Gee Jun Tye, Satvinder Singh Dhaliwal, Muhamad Yusri Musa, Matthew Tze Jian Wong, Ngit Shin Lai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Immune profiling has become a transformative tool in oncology, offering comprehensive information on tumor immune interactions and facilitating precision medicine. Recent advances such as mass cytometry (CyTOF), single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), spatial transcriptomics, and liquid biopsy have greatly enhanced our ability to characterize immune heterogeneity and predict treatment responses. These innovations support the identification of new biomarkers, therapeutic targets, and resistance mechanisms, refining patient stratification and clinical results. Additionally, artificial intelligence (AI) driven models are now being employed to integrate multi-omics datasets and create predictive insights, thereby linking the gap between research and clinical decision-making. This review studies the evolution of immune profiling technologies, their integration into real-world oncology practice, and the associated technical and analytical challenges, including sample variability, data harmonization, and multi-omics integration. Although challenges such as cost, throughput, and standardization persist, the merging of advanced technologies, bioinformatics, and clinical frameworks promises to reshape cancer diagnosis, therapy selection, and disease monitoring through personalized and data-driven strategies.

Introduction

Cancer is an ongoing global health challenge that imposes continuous exploration of novel and effective strategies for understanding and treating the disease. In recent years, the growing field of immune profiling has emerged as a transformative avenue in cancer research. For an extended period, Tumor, Node, Metastasis (TNM) staging has been the primary tool for primary care providers and researchers to predict cancer staging in patients [1]. Recent advancements have revealed that understanding the immune contexture of tumors is crucial. The tumor microenvironment (TME) is an intricate system comprising tumor cells, infiltrating immune cells, fibroblasts, tumor vasculature, and the extracellular matrix [2]. These elements collectively impact cancer progression, metastasis rates, and responses to specific therapies. The immune context of cancer is gradually emerging as a pivotal factor that can influence disease prognosis, progression, and the effectiveness of therapies [3]. Initial research on immune contexture primarily focused on colorectal cancer and later expanded to include other cancers [4]. In contrast to conventional treatments such as radiotherapy or chemotherapy, Immunotherapy provides a platform to harness the immune system or modify the TME to provoke an anti-tumor immune response [5]. While immunotherapy has demonstrated efficacy in clinical trials, a notable portion of patients remains unresponsive, experiencing serious adverse events (SAE) or immune-related adverse events (irAEs) [6]. To comprehend the mechanisms of response, immune profiling has emerged as a tool to identify new strategies and biomarkers for enhancing treatment outcomes. This review aims to comprehensively explore recent advances in immune profiling and their far-reaching impact in cancer care. The central objective is to critically examine the latest technologies driving these advancements, including mass cytometry, single-cell RNA sequencing, spatial transcriptomics, and the indispensable role of bioinformatics. The primary objective of this review is to elucidate the transformative potential of immune profiling in cancer research. Through the association of genomic, transcriptomic, and proteomic data, we aim to showcase how immune profiling has opened new avenues for biomarker discovery and the identification of therapeutic targets, promising more tailored and effective treatment strategies. Additionally, the review aims to shed light on the practical implications of immune profiling in clinical trials. We will explore how immune profiling insights inform the design of trials, facilitate patient stratification based on immune profiles, and contribute to meaningful evaluation of treatment outcomes, ultimately paving the way for more precise and personalized immunotherapies. In essence, this review endeavors to encapsulate the complex contributions of immune profiling in reshaping cancer research and clinical practice. By addressing the objectives outlined, we aim to provide a comprehensive overview of the current state of immune profiling, anticipate future directions in the field, and underscore its pivotal role in advancing precision medicine for cancer care.

Conventional to cutting-edge technology

For years, immune monitoring and immunological research have relied heavily on established methodologies such as flow cytometry, microscopy, or immunohistochemistry (IHC) to investigate cellular heterogeneity and its association with disease [7]. However, the inherent limitation in the number of parameters that could be simultaneously analyzed restricted the depth of cellular property and composition analyses [8]. This often-necessitated experiments focused on single-cell types, consuming additional time and clinical materials for comprehensive insights [9]. Recent advancements have significantly expanded single-cell characterization capabilities through the utilization of novel fluorophores and laser systems, revealing previously unrecognized immune cell subclasses and functional states [10]. Despite these advancements, physical constraints impeded further parameter addition in fluorescence-based cytometry due to spectral overlap issues [11]. A solution to this challenge emerged with the adoption of non-biological elemental isotopes to replace fluorescence-based reporters in flow cytometry [12]. Coupled with inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICPMS) for detection, this technique is known as mass cytometry or cytometry by time-of-flight (CyTOF) [13]. CyTOF has revolutionized immune profiling, enabling the simultaneous detection of over 40 protein markers in millions of cells, particularly illuminating the intricacies of the tumor microenvironment (TME). Despite some limitations such as marker selection dependency and lower throughput compared to genome-wide analyses, CyTOF, alongside emerging technologies like imaging mass cytometry, swiftly offers essential information on protein expression in anatomical contexts, crucial for confirming cellular subsets, exploring cell interactions, and identifying clinical biomarkers [14]. The emergence of single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-Seq) has addressed the limitations of bulk transcriptomic data [15]. While bulk transcriptomic analysis provides an average gene expression profile for heterogeneous cell populations, scRNA-Seq enables the analysis of gene expression at the individual cell level [16]. This approach overcomes the limitations of conventional RNA sequencing by profiling the entire transcriptome at a single-cell resolution, thereby delineating distinct cell types within tumor tissue [17]. scRNA-seq elucidates the molecular mechanisms driving tumor initiation and evolution, facilitating the identification of somatic mutations over time [18, 19]. Moreover, scRNA-seq captures dynamic tumor heterogeneity changes, including the acquisition of invasive traits, metastatic potential, immune cell infiltration, and immune checkpoint activity [20] Advancements in sequencing technology have bolstered scRNA-seq's sensitivity, accuracy, and cost-effectiveness, rendering it pivotal in tumor research and clinical applications [21, 22]. While bulk and single-cell RNA sequencing methods have been invaluable, spatial transcriptomics provides crucial spatial resolution. This technique enables the study of how tumors adapt to their surroundings and evade immune responses in their native context [23]. For instance, spatial transcriptomics has facilitated the tracing of tumor cell lineages across various cancer types, shedding light on their spatial organization and interactions [24]. By applying spatial transcriptomics to TME analysis, researchers have identified new cell phenotypes, intratumorally structures, biomarkers, and cell–cell interactions influencing response or resistance to immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) treatment [25]. To further enhance spatial transcriptomics, researchers can utilize fluorescently labeled antibodies to detect and map the spatial distribution of various proteins in the tumor microenvironment, thus providing valuable insights into cellular interactions, immune response, tumor heterogeneity, and the identification of potential biomarkers [26]. Multiplexed imaging aids in understanding the spatial organization of different cell types, assessing immune cell infiltration, and identifying specific protein signatures associated with cancer subtypes or treatment response [27].

However, even with their vast potential, these advanced technologies face certain restrictions. For example, CyTOF, while enabling high-dimensional immune profiling, suffers from relatively low throughput, high operational cost, and dependency on predefined marker panels. Similarly, scRNA-seq, although powerful in resolving cell-type-specific transcriptomes, requires high-quality input RNA, is resource-intensive, and generates large, complex datasets that pose substantial computational tests. Furthermore, the analysis of spatial transcriptomics remains technically difficult, particularly due to limited spatial resolution and high reagent costs. The clinical integration of such techniques remains delayed by the need for healthy standardization, harmonized workflows, and cost-effectiveness. Understanding these limitations is vital in measuring their usefulness and scalability in real-world clinical settings. By addressing the inadequacies of older techniques and providing real-time, multi-dimensional insights into the immune landscape, we are able to have a comprehensive understanding of tumor biology and the development of targeted therapies for improved cancer diagnosis and treatment [28].

Bioinformatics in immune profiling: analysing and interpreting complex data

In the realm of cancer research, the significance of bioinformatics in immune profiling cannot be overstated, as it plays a pivotal role in unraveling the intricate complexities of the immune response within the tumor microenvironment [29]. The dynamic interaction between immune cells and cancer cells necessitates a refined understanding that surpasses conventional analytical methods [30]. Bioinformatics tools are indispensable for managing the extensive datasets produced by advanced immune profiling techniques, enabling researchers to identify patterns, discern immune signatures, and gain insights into the ever-changing nature of the immune response against cancer [18, 19]. In today's research landscape, wet experimentation and the integration of bioinformatics analytics are deeply interconnected [31]. The profiling of tumor biopsies at a molecular level is increasingly essential for progress and improving treatment outcomes [32]. Bioinformatics plays a crucial role in facilitating the diagnosis, prognosis prediction, and personalized treatment of patients [33]. To realize the objectives of precision oncology, it is crucial to utilize relevant bioinformatics methodologies for managing, integrating, and analyzing extensive, interlinked datasets [34].

One of the primary obstacles in integrating multi-omics data is the variation in batch effects due to datasets drawn from different platforms, labs, or periods. For example, transcriptomic datasets often utilize count-based evaluations, while proteomic datasets might be steady with distinct normalization processes. These variations require alignment via sophisticated tools like Seurat v4, MOFA (Multi-Omics Factor Analysis), or Harmony. Moreover, merging data with varied scales and units presents challenges in interpretation, which necessitates thorough statistical validation, visualization approaches, and machine learning workflows to derive valuable biological insights. Additional issues include bias in feature selection, overfitting in predictive models, and challenges in maintaining reproducibility across diverse patient groups. Tackling these issues is crucial for building clinically reliable predictive models that convert complex immune profiling into practical insights. There is an urgent need for bioinformatics techniques and software specially designed for oncology, motivated by strict monitoring standards and the call for fast, dependable, and reproducible methods [35]. These bioinformatics tools are pivotal in deciphering the large and complex datasets generated by RNA sequencing technologies, offering critical insights into the immune system's role in cancer progression and its response to therapy [36]. Integrations of immune profiling with omics approaches

Integration of immune profiling with omics approaches is a powerful strategy that combines multiple high-throughput technologies to gain a comprehensive understanding of the immune system [38]. Omics approaches, such as genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics, provide a holistic view of the molecular components and processes involved in immune responses. By integrating these omics data with immune profiling, researchers can unravel complex immune mechanisms, identify novel biomarkers, and develop more targeted therapeutic strategies. The construction of a comprehensive immune landscape in cancer research involves the intricate correlation of genomic, transcriptomic, and proteomic data. This integrative approach offers a holistic perspective on the complex interplay within the immune system and its responses to cancer [39]. Leveraging bioinformatics tools, researchers analyse large-scale datasets to uncover meaningful associations among these layers of molecular information [40]. Genomic data unveils DNA-level alterations, such as mutations, influencing the immune response [41]. Transcriptomic data, derived from RNA sequencing, captures dynamic gene expression patterns, elucidating immune-related pathways [42]. Simultaneously, proteomic data delves into the functional aspects of the immune system by examining the proteins produced [43]. Through this comprehensive correlation, researchers can pinpoint biomarkers, understand the regulation of immune pathways, and identify key players in the immune response to cancer. This multi-omics integration not only advances our comprehension of the immune landscape but also lays the groundwork for developing targeted therapies and personalized treatment strategies, tailored to the unique molecular profiles of individual patients.

Biomarker discovery and identification of therapeutic targets

In the intricate landscape of cancer research, immune profiling assumes a pivotal role with a dual focus on biomarker discovery and the identification of therapeutic targets [44]. Employing cutting-edge technologies like mass cytometry and single-cell RNA sequencing, integrated with advanced bioinformatics tools, researchers delve into the nuanced dynamics of the immune system within the tumor microenvironment [45]. This meticulous exploration leads to the identification of specific biomarkers—ranging from genetic mutations and gene expression signatures to protein levels—that serve as precise indicators of immune activity, disease prognosis, and responsiveness to treatment [46]. Simultaneously, the correlation and integration of genomic, transcriptomic, and proteomic data unveil potential therapeutic targets, exposing vulnerabilities in immune responses or cancer cells that can be strategically targeted for intervention [47]. This detailed understanding not only refines our knowledge of the immune landscape but also forms the basis for developing tailored therapeutic approaches. By aligning treatment strategies with individual patients' unique immune profiles, immune profiling advances the realms of cancer diagnosis and therapeutic precision, offering unprecedented opportunities for personalized and effective interventions in the complex landscape of cancer biology.

Immune profiling in clinical trials

The significance of immune profiling in informing the design of clinical trials is crucial for augmenting the precision and relevance of studies in cancer therapy [48]. Through the meticulous examination of the unique characteristics of individual immune systems, immune profiling assists researchers in the selection of suitable patient cohorts, customization of treatment protocols, and establishment of meaningful outcome measures [49]. This personalized approach facilitates a more sophisticated understanding of treatment responses, wherein the identification of specific immune markers impacts patient selection and underpins the continuous refinement of therapeutic strategies [50]. Consequently, immune profiling not only enhances the probability of success in clinical trials but also lays the groundwork for the advancement of more targeted and effective cancer therapies, thereby fostering the evolution of personalized medicine within the dynamic field of cancer research [51]. Patient stratification based on immune profiles resembles the categorization of individuals into different groups according to the distinctive characteristics of their immune systems [52]. Within the context of cancer research and treatment, this entails an understanding of the variation in immune responses among individuals. Utilizing techniques such as immune profiling, scientists can identify specific features or markers within the immune system that differ from one person to another. This information facilitates the categorization of patients into groups with similar immune profiles, thereby enabling researchers to tailor treatments with greater precision. For instance, patients exhibiting a strong immune response might derive greater benefit from a particular treatment modality, whereas those with an alternative immune profile may respond more favorably to a different type of therapy [53]. Essentially, patient stratification based on immune profiles seeks to align treatments with the specific characteristics of each individual's immune system, thereby enhancing the efficacy and personalization of cancer therapies, as demonstrated in clinical trials (Table 1). A notable example is the application of PD-L1 expression levels in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients to determine eligibility for checkpoint inhibitor therapies such as nivolumab or pembrolizumab. Similarly, tumor mutational burden (TMB) and metrics of immune cell infiltration are increasingly utilized in guiding immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy in melanoma and urothelial carcinoma. For instance, the IMpower110 trial effectively employed immune profiling to illustrate that patients with high PD-L1 expression experience significant survival benefits from atezolizumab monotherapy. In the KEYNOTE-158 study, tumors characterized by high TMB across diverse histology exhibited greater objective response rates to pembrolizumab. Additionally, within a subset of prostate cancer patients, integrative immune-transcriptomic profiling has been deployed to predict responses to PARP inhibitors and immune checkpoint inhibitor combinations. These instances highlight how practical application of immune profiling not only anticipates treatment efficacy but also uncovers novel therapeutic vulnerabilities. Immune profiling in treatment response and resistance

Over the past decade, extensive preclinical animal studies and clinical trials have thoroughly explored the efficacy and mechanisms of immunotherapy, marking a significant breakthrough in treating various refractory cancer types [54]. While immune checkpoint inhibitor drugs offer promising long-term efficacy, their overall benefits in the population remain limited [55]. Challenges include low response rates in common tumor types like breast and prostate cancers, as well as heterogeneity in response levels among different tumor lesions within the same patient, presenting obstacles to widespread adoption of immunotherapy [56]. Many patients do not respond to treatment initially or develop resistance over time, either through primary resistance or acquired resistance after an initial response [57]. The mechanisms underlying tumor immune resistance are complex, involving factors such as genetic mutations, metabolic changes, inflammation, and abnormal blood vessel growth. Despite ongoing efforts to characterize these resistance mechanisms, challenges persist [58]. Nevertheless, such endeavours aim to enhance cancer diagnosis and treatment, improve patient quality of life, and ultimately enable more accurate individualized therapies. In the context of cancer, immune profiling offers a comprehensive analysis of immune cell subsets, functional states, and immune-related molecular alterations before, during, and after treatment [51]. By monitoring the immune response, clinicians can evaluate the effectiveness of therapies, identify early signs of treatment response or resistance, and make informed decisions regarding treatment modifications [59]. One of the key applications of immune profiling in monitoring treatment response is the assessment of immune checkpoint molecules, such as PD-L1 expression [60]. High PD-L1 expression is associated with a higher likelihood of response to immune checkpoint inhibitors. Monitoring PD-L1 levels during treatment can help determine if the therapy is effectively inhibiting the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway and restoring anti-tumor immune responses [60]. Furthermore, immune profiling allows for the characterization of immune cell subsets, such as cytotoxic CD8+ T cells or tumor-associated macrophages, which are critical in mediating anti-tumor immune responses [61]. Tracking the abundance and functional states of these cell subsets can provide insights into treatment response and potential immune evasion mechanisms employed by the tumor cells [62]. In addition to immune cell subsets, immune profiling also enables the evaluation of immune-related molecular changes, such as cytokine expression profiles or immune-related gene expression patterns [63]. Monitoring these molecular alterations can help identify early indicators of treatment response or resistance and guide treatment decisions accordingly [64]. The integration of longitudinal immune profiling data with clinical outcomes and other molecular information, such as genomic alterations or tumor mutational burden, allows for a comprehensive assessment of treatment response and the identification of predictive biomarkers [65]. These biomarkers can aid in monitoring treatment efficacy, predicting treatment response or resistance, and guiding therapeutic decisions in cancer management.

Unraveling mechanisms of resistance to immunotherapy

Unraveling mechanisms of resistance to immunotherapy in cancer is a critical area of research to improve treatment outcomes. Immune profiling techniques offer valuable insights into the underlying mechanisms that lead to resistance and aid in the identification of potential strategies to overcome it [54]. One example of resistance to immunotherapy is observed in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients treated with immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway [66]. Despite initial response rates, a subset of patients develops resistance. Immune profiling studies have revealed several mechanisms contributing to resistance, including alterations in the tumor immune microenvironment, such as reduced immune cell infiltration and increased immunosuppressive cell populations like myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) and Tregs [67]. Additionally, upregulation of alternative immune checkpoint molecules like TIM-3 or LAG-3 on T cells can limit the efficacy of PD-1 blockade [68]. By understanding these resistance mechanisms through immune profiling, researchers can explore combination therapies targeting additional immune checkpoints or modulating the tumor microenvironment to overcome resistance and enhance the effectiveness of immunotherapy in NSCLC and other cancer types.

Immune profiling-guided combination therapies

Immune profiling-guided combination therapies have shown great promise in improving treatment outcomes in cancer patients [69]. By integrating immune profiling data with molecular information and clinical outcomes, researchers can identify potential synergistic treatment approaches that target multiple aspects of the immune system and tumor microenvironment [70]. One example of such a combination therapy is the use of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) in combination with targeted therapies in metastatic melanoma [71]. Immune profiling studies have revealed that melanoma tumors can exhibit various immune evasion mechanisms, including upregulation of immune checkpoint molecules like PD-L1 and activation of oncogenic signaling pathways such as BRAF/MEK [72]. Therefore, combining ICIs, which enhance the anti-tumor immune response, with targeted therapies like BRAF or MEK inhibitors, which directly target cancer cell signaling, can improve treatment responses [73]. Immune profiling helps identify patients who may benefit from this combination approach by assessing the immune landscape and genetic alterations within the tumor [21, 22]. This immune profiling-guided combination therapy has shown enhanced efficacy and improved overall survival in patients with metastatic melanoma, demonstrating the potential of immune profiling to guide the development of effective combination treatments in cancer [74].

AI and liquid biopsy in clinical decision-making

The incorporation of liquid biopsy methodologies, notably those integrating circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) with immune cell profiling, provides non-invasive, real-time insights into tumor evolution and the modulation of the immune landscape. These approaches enable longitudinal assessment of therapeutic efficacy and the development of resistance. Liquid biopsy holds particular value for patients with tumors that are inaccessible or when a tissue biopsy is contraindicated. For instance, the amalgamation of ctDNA mutational profiling with peripheral immune cell composition has demonstrated potential in identifying early relapse in colorectal and breast cancers. The application of artificial intelligence (AI) further augments the utility of immune profiling through automated feature extraction, pattern recognition, and predictive modeling. Machine learning algorithms, developed using extensive, annotated datasets, can forecast immunotherapy outcomes based on integrated features such as PD-L1 expression, immune gene signatures, and tumor heterogeneity. Deep learning has also been employed to identify immune cell states linked with resistance, facilitating dynamic treatment modifications. The integration of AI models into clinical platforms permits personalized treatment recommendations and more refined criteria for trial enrolment. In essence, the synergy between AI, multi-modal immune profiling, and minimally invasive sampling methodologies signifies a paradigm shift in oncology toward proactive, patient-specific care.

Future directions and challenges in immune profiling

Technological advancements and standardization

Technological advancements and standardization efforts in immune profiling have had a profound impact on cancer research. These advancements have significantly improved our understanding of the complex interactions between the immune system and cancer cells, leading to advancements in cancer diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment [75]. In the context of cancer research, immune profiling technologies have allowed for a comprehensive analysis of the tumor immune microenvironment, providing insights into the immune cell composition, functional states, and immune-related molecular alterations [76]. This has enabled researchers to identify immune signatures and predictive biomarkers associated with different cancer types and stages. By applying high-throughput techniques, such as next-generation sequencing (NGS) and mass spectrometry-based proteomics, researchers can analyse the genomic and proteomic profiles of tumor cells and immune cells simultaneously [14]. This integration of genomic and immune profiling data has revealed important molecular alterations in cancer cells, as well as immune evasion mechanisms employed by tumors, such as upregulation of immune checkpoint molecules like PD-L1. These findings have paved the way for the development of targeted therapies, such as immune checkpoint inhibitors, which aim to restore anti-tumor immune responses [77]. Furthermore, single-cell technologies, including single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) and mass cytometry (CyTOF), have provided unprecedented resolution in characterizing individual immune cells within the tumor microenvironment [78]. This level of detail has allowed researchers to identify rare immune cell subsets, decipher cellular heterogeneity, and uncover dynamic changes in gene expression and signaling pathways during tumor progression and treatment response [79]. These insights have implications for the development of personalized immunotherapies and the identification of potential targets for combination therapies. Standardization efforts in immune profiling have also been crucial in cancer research [80]. By establishing standardized protocols for sample collection, processing, and analysis, researchers can ensure the comparability and reproducibility of results across different studies and institutions. This harmonization allows for the pooling of data and the identification of robust immune signatures and predictive models that can guide treatment decisions and improve patient outcomes.

Multidisciplinary collaborations for comprehensive immune profiling

Multidisciplinary collaborations play a vital role in achieving comprehensive immune profiling in cancer research. The complexity of the immune system and its interactions with cancer cells necessitate the integration of expertise from various disciplines, including immunology, oncology, genomics, bioinformatics, and computational biology [81]. Collaborative efforts bring together researchers, clinicians, and industry partners, facilitating the exchange of knowledge, resources, and technologies to advance our understanding of the immune landscape in cancer [82]. In cancer research, multidisciplinary collaborations enable the development and optimization of immune profiling techniques. Immunologists contribute their expertise in characterizing immune cell populations, understanding immune responses, and identifying immune-related biomarkers. Oncologists provide clinical insights, access to patient samples, and clinical outcomes data [83]. Genomic scientists contribute their knowledge in genomics and transcriptomics, helping identify genetic alterations and gene expression patterns associated with immune responses in cancer [84]. Bioinformaticians and computational biologists play a crucial role in analysing large-scale immune profiling data, integrating multi-omics datasets, and developing computational models to extract meaningful insights from complex datasets. Collaborations also facilitate the implementation of standardized protocols and quality control measures in immune profiling studies. By establishing consensus guidelines, researchers ensure the reliability, reproducibility, and comparability of immune profiling data across different laboratories and studies. These efforts enhance data sharing, meta-analyses, and the validation of immune signatures and predictive models, leading to more robust and reliable results [85]. Furthermore, multidisciplinary collaborations foster the development of novel technologies and methodologies for immune profiling. The integration of expertise from different fields allows for innovative approaches to sample collection, processing, and analysis, leading to advancements in high-throughput techniques, single-cell technologies, and multi-dimensional data integration [86]. Collaborations with industry partners facilitate the translation of these technological advancements into practical applications, accelerating their adoption in clinical practice [87]. Moreover, multidisciplinary collaborations enable the integration of immune profiling data with clinical outcomes, molecular information, and other patient data [88]. This integration provides a more comprehensive view of the immune landscape in cancer patients, leading to improved patient stratification, treatment selection, and personalized therapeutic strategies [89]. By combining clinical expertise, genomic information, and immune profiling data, collaborations drive the development of precision medicine approaches in cancer treatment.

Ethical considerations and data sharing

Ethical considerations and data sharing play crucial roles in immune profiling for cancer research, ensuring the responsible and effective use of data and promoting scientific progress and collaboration. Firstly, ethical considerations are essential in immune profiling to protect the rights and privacy of patients [90]. Researchers must obtain informed consent from participants, clearly explaining the purpose, risks, and potential benefits of the study [91]. They should ensure the confidentiality and security of patient data, adhering to relevant data protection regulations. Ethical guidelines also emphasize the importance of transparency, integrity, and avoiding conflicts of interest in reporting and publishing immune profiling findings [92]. Furthermore, data sharing is critical in immune profiling to maximize the utility and impact of the collected data. Sharing immune profiling data promotes scientific collaboration, enabling researchers worldwide to validate findings, replicate experiments, and generate new insights [93]. Open access to data fosters transparency, stimulates interdisciplinary collaborations, and accelerates the development of personalized therapies and precision medicine approaches. However, while sharing data is valuable, it must be done responsibly, considering privacy concerns and ensuring appropriate anonymization or de-identification of patient information. However, there are ethical challenges and complexities associated with data sharing in immune profiling. Patient privacy must be safeguarded, and stringent data governance and sharing policies should be in place to protect sensitive information [94]. Data sharing should adhere to legal and ethical frameworks, such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and institutional review board (IRB) approvals [95]. Consent for data sharing should be sought explicitly from participants, allowing them to have control over the use of their data and providing options for withdrawal [96]. Moreover, efforts should be made to address disparities in data access and sharing. Equitable access to immune profiling data is essential to avoid exacerbating inequalities in cancer research and ensure that all researchers, including those from underrepresented regions or institutions, can benefit from shared resources and contribute to scientific advancements [97].

Technical challenges in real-world immune profiling

The analysis of immune profiling data derived from real-world clinical samples presents numerous technical challenges. Variability in sample acquisition methodologies, including differences in tissue preservation techniques (e.g., FFPE versus fresh frozen), impacts cell viability and RNA integrity. Tumor biopsies frequently provide limited cellular material, restricting the comprehensiveness of analyses. Inter-patient heterogeneity further complicates data interpretation, as immune profiles may vary according to disease stage, prior treatments, tumor type, anatomical site, and co-morbidities. Additionally, real-world samples often suffer from a lack of metadata standardization and may be subject to preanalytical errors, such as processing delays or inconsistent reagent usage. In multicentre or longitudinal studies, challenges are amplified by batch effects and inconsistent sample handling protocols, necessitating the adoption of stringent standardization, normalization workflows, and quality control metrics. Furthermore, clinicians are confronted with time constraints in deriving clinically actionable results from immune profiling data. The integration of these complex datasets into decision-making pipelines remains challenging due to the absence of user-friendly interfaces and AI-assisted visualization tools. These obstacles highlight the need for unified data processing pipelines, robust quality control mechanisms, and clinician education to enhance the clinical utility of immune profiling.

Conclusion

In summary, the examination of advancements in immune profiling within the field of cancer research highlights a transformative journey in comprehending the complex interaction between the immune system and cancer. These technologies are now beginning to redefine clinical practice by facilitating real-time, personalized treatment decisions. The capability to classify patients according to immune signatures and predict therapeutic outcomes is transforming immunotherapy from a trial-and-error methodology to a targeted strategy. Instances such as PD-L1-guided therapy in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and circulating tumor DNA-informed relapse monitoring underscore the practicality of these models in standard clinical settings. Artificial intelligence-powered platforms and multi-omics integration present scalable opportunities for incorporating immune profiling into electronic health records and clinical workflows. Ultimately, integrating immune profiling into clinical practice with the support of liquid biopsy and AI tools, bridges the divide between high-dimensional research and applied oncology care, thus expediting advancements in precision medicine.

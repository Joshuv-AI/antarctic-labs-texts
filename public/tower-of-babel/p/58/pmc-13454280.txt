Large language model empowered explainable and interpretable mental health analysis
Authors: Supriya Bajpai, Gargi Mishra, Rachna Jain, Deepak Kumar Jain, Ketan Kotecha, Ambarish Kulkarni
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Concern about expressing depressive symptoms on social media is growing in the digital age. Traditional detection methods can identify depression but lack clear, human-understandable explanations. We present a novel methodology “InsightDep”, a mental health analysis model specifically designed to detect depression in the social media users. Furthermore, the model provides explainability for the model results and facilitates the translation of the model results and explanations into easily understandable and interpretable formats for human comprehension, leveraging the capabilities of large language models (LLMs). The proposed method extends a variant of the BERT model specifically adapted for analyzing Twitter data, to an explainable and interpretable system. The model makes use of masked attention techniques to perform classification as well as making it explainable. Additionally, we incorporate the power of LLM to convert complex technical explanations into comprehensible comments that improves the interpretability of the system. When evaluated on Twitter and Reddit datasets, InsightDep achieved macro-F1/accuracy scores of 0.599/0.671 and 0.994/0.994, respectively, outperforming other state-of-the-art methods such as BERTweet, TwHIN-BERT, and DepRoBERTa. Our novel methodology focuses on the development of morally aware digital channels that enable prompt action and support for mental health concerns, under the supervision of licensed medical experts like doctors and psychologists. This methodology emphasizes the practical application in therapeutic assessments conducted by these professionals.

Introduction

Mental health problems, especially depression, affect around 300 million people worldwide and is a major concern1. Unfortunately, depression plays a significant role in suicide, especially in young people (10–24 years old), since suicide is the third largest cause of death in this age group. Despite its seriousness, depression is often misdiagnosed and treated without therapy2,3; more than half of individuals who suffer from it never get help1.

Social media platforms have become vast digital spaces where people share experiences and emotions, offering insight into mental states1. The widespread use of these platforms generates large volumes of data reflecting users’ sentiments, opinions, and mental conditions4,5. Some posts indicate individuals may be experiencing mental health challenges, raising the need to identify and support them efficiently. Despite signs of depression in a significant portion of the population, many remain untreated6, often due to reluctance to seek help7. Automated analysis of social media could aid in early detection of mental health issues.

Recent research focuses on using social media activity to detect depression, employing models like LSTM, CNNs, and transformer-based NLP tools such as BERT8,9. These models excel at capturing language nuances due to their design and large-scale training on diverse datasets. However, their lack of interpretability limits their application in sensitive areas like mental health. To address this,10 proposed a topic-enriched auxiliary task to enhance model understanding, while11 analyzed posting schedules. Still, current methods mainly offer binary classifications without explainability, reducing their value to users and healthcare professionals. Research on improving interpretability in this context remains limited.

GPT-312, OPT13, and PaLM14 are examples of large language models (LLMs) that have developed quickly and demonstrated outstanding performance on a range of natural language processing (NLP) tasks. These models have great semantic understanding and reasoning abilities as their size and training data increase. The way that LLMs have evolved has changed research by focusing on activities that are downstream. It was typical practice in the past to modify model parameters by backpropagation. But with recent developments in LLMs15, learning facilitation during the forward process is now possible through the creation of prompts–a technique called In-Context Learning (ICL).

LLMs are increasingly used to detect mental-health conditions from text, such as depression and suicide risk, using clinical narratives and social media posts16. Studies show they can achieve good accuracy, especially when combined with text embeddings or multimodal inputs like facial cues17,18. However, issues of fairness, safety, and ethics remain19. However, the possibility of using LLMs to do the depression detection job has not been investigated thoroughly.

Research question: Can a tweet-domain pretrained encoder (TwHIN-BERT) combined with a bi-GRU and masked-attention mechanism produce accurate and intrinsically explainable multi-class depression detection from social media posts, and can an LLM such as ChatGPT effectively translate these explanations into concise, clinician-friendly narratives without loss of fidelity?

In contrast to previous studies that are either black-box or use post-hoc explanation tools, our approach integrates explainability directly into the model’s architecture, enabling simultaneous prediction and interpretation. This work also contributes to the emerging field of digital phenotyping4 by demonstrating how domain-specific transformer models, combined with attention-based interpretability and LLM-generated narratives, can support healthcare professionals in understanding model outputs. The relevance of integrating machine learning, digital phenotyping, and mental health monitoring has been emphasized in recent surveys20–24, highlighting applications in stress, anxiety, depression, and suicide ideation detection. Our proposed framework builds upon this foundation, offering a novel, transparent, and domain-adapted pipeline.

Depression detection in social media

Researchers have extensively explored the use of machine learning to detect depressive symptoms from user-generated social media content. Early approaches applied classical algorithms such as Random Forest ensembles, emotion-based dictionaries, multi-kernel SVMs, and Naïve Bayes classifiers to textual and behavioral features extracted from posts25–30. With the rise of deep learning, more sophisticated architectures were introduced to capture semantic and contextual nuances in user language. Tejaswini et al.31 proposed a hybrid model combining LSTMs, CNNs, and fastText embeddings to improve early depression detection. Chen et al.32 developed SBERT-CNN, merging CNNs for behavioral pattern recognition with sentence-BERT embeddings for semantic representation. Wang et al.33 explored transformer-based models on Weibo data, showing that pre-trained contextual embeddings from BERT8, RoBERTa34, ALBERT35, and XLNet36 improved risk assessment over traditional methods. These studies underline the trend towards leveraging deep contextual models for mental health applications.

Transformer-based models and domain-specific variants

Transformer architectures have become the dominant paradigm for NLP due to their ability to model bidirectional context. BERT37 introduced masked language modeling and bidirectional pretraining, while RoBERTa34 refined BERT’s training by removing next-sentence prediction and employing dynamic masking, leading to performance gains. ALBERT35 reduced parameter count through factorized embeddings and introduced sentence order prediction, improving efficiency. XLNet36 applied permutation language modeling to overcome the bidirectional limitations of autoregressive models, achieving strong results across benchmarks.

Domain-specific variants have further boosted performance in specialized contexts. BERTweet38 was trained on large-scale Twitter data, capturing the linguistic nuances of microblogging platforms. TwHIN-BERT39 extended this idea by incorporating both tweet content and social network interaction graphs, enabling richer contextual understanding. In depression detection, Poświata and Perełkiewicz40 introduced DepRoBERTa, pre-trained on Reddit data related to depression, and later improved it via ensemble averaging with RoBERTa-large, achieving notable gains in detection precision. Comparative studies have generally found that transformer variants fine-tuned on domain-relevant corpora outperform general-purpose models for depression detection.

Explainable artificial intelligence in mental health detection

While deep learning models achieve high predictive accuracy, their opacity limits their clinical applicability. Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) techniques address this by making model decisions more interpretable41,42. Post-hoc approaches such as SHAP43 and LIME44 explain predictions of pre-trained black-box models in a model-agnostic fashion, while MAPLE45 combines local linear models with random forests for enhanced local explanations. These methods are popular for their flexibility but rely on external approximation of the model’s reasoning.

In contrast, self-explanatory models generate predictions and explanations simultaneously, embedding interpretability within the architecture. For example, Zogan et al.46 proposed a BiGRU-CNN model with a hierarchical attention mechanism to identify and explain depression indicators in social media posts. However, integrating self-explanatory mechanisms into modern transformer pipelines remains a challenge, especially when balancing interpretability and predictive performance.

Broader advances in related AI applications

Recent studies further illustrate advances in hybrid architectures, LLM-driven explanation, and cross-domain applications that inform our approach. Notable recent works include hybrid self-attention SSCL frameworks for depression detection, unified LLMs for misinformation detection in low-resource settings, multimodal generative methods coupling autoregressive LLMs with perception models, embedding-enhanced BERT variants for multilingual sarcasm detection, and other adjacent advances in attention mechanisms, federated learning, knowledge graphs, and IoT-driven predictive analytics47–56.

Compared to existing literature, our work advances the state of the art in two key ways. First, we propose a self-explanatory architecture, InsightDep, that integrates TwHIN-BERT with a bi-GRU and masked attention mechanism to produce both high-accuracy depression classifications and intrinsic token-level explanations without relying on post-hoc methods. Second, we introduce a novel interpretability enhancement step by leveraging a large language model (ChatGPT) to translate attention-based explanations into clinician-friendly narratives, improving their accessibility for non-technical users. Unlike prior transformer-based models for depression detection, which are either black-box or use post-hoc explainers, our approach unifies prediction and explanation in a single, domain-adapted framework, explicitly designed for social media data.

Methodology

The modular design of the proposed methodology integrates fine-tuned LLM with masked-attention-based biGRU to classify depression. Furthermore, it incorporates eXplainable AI (XAI) methods to output the significance of each word in the sentence and then, employs conversational AI to translate the model explanations into a format easily understood by humans, thereby enhancing accessibility for clinicians and users. Model classification and explainability

The training process of InsightDep consists of two phases: Firstly, the TwHIN-BERT model fine-tuning is conducted to prepare it for the subsequent task. Further, it is integrated with a bi-GRU network with masked attention mechanism. The bi-GRU with masked attention provides output explanations using attention techniques. The attention weights provide relative importance to each word in the text. Through our methodology, redundant words are eliminated from the model classification process, and each word in the input is assigned a weight determined by its relevance.

InsightDep architecture and training

Figure 2b shows the detailed architecture of InsightDep for classification with explainability. Following the fine tuning the TwHIN-BERT model, the next step is to replace the classifier of TwHIN-BERT with a bi-GRU layer, masked attention layer and a classifier. Experiments and results

This section presents the experimental framework, dataset, and results that validate the proposed InsightDep model. The evaluation was designed to address our core research question to explainably detect depression from social media posts, and can an LLM translate these explanations into concise, clinician-friendly narratives? We employ the Twitter and Reddit dataset, publicly available benchmarks, enabling fair comparison with state-of-the-art approaches. For evaluation, we report macro-F1, accuracy, precision, and recall. Macro-F1 is essential for handling class imbalance, ensuring that minority classes (e.g., Severely Depressed) are weighted equally with majority classes, unlike micro-F1 or raw accuracy which can be biased toward dominant labels. Accuracy provides an overall correctness measure familiar to both ML researchers and clinicians, while precision and recall capture the trade-off between false alarms and missed detections–critical in a clinical screening context where both over- and under-identification carry risks.

Dataset

For model training first dataset we used is publicly available dataset which was collected from social media posts and originally provided by the DepRoBERTa40. The train, validation and test set consists of 3245, 1000 and 6006 samples respectively. The samples were classified as SEVERELY DEPRESSED, MODERATELY DEPRESSED, and Not Depressed. The original dataset used in the LT-EDI-ACL2022 Shared Task on Detecting Signs of Depression from Social Media Text competition57 has been pre-processed and made available as this dataset by DepRoBERTa. The goal of this challenge was to create models that could recognize signs of depression in language posted on social media. The second dataset consists of 7650 Reddit posts58, each labeled as either DEPRESSED or NOT DEPRESSED. The data is split into 5731 samples for training, 800 for validation, and 1200 for testing.

Experimental setup

We performed the InsightDep model training in two steps. During the fine tuning process of TwHIN-BERT the loss function used is cross-entropy loss. We use sequences of length 200. Rectified Adam optimizer is employed. Batch size is 32. The learning rate is set to 0.00005. This small learning rate is crucial to the task, aiming to refine the pre-trained weights of TwHIN-BERT for the depression classification task while avoiding large weight updates that could lead to overfitting or catastrophic forgetting. The fine tuning is performed over 10 epochs. We selected the best model, determined by its macro-F1 score on the validation split.

The InsightDep model training was performed by first detaching the TwHIN-BERT encoder parameters during bi-GRU head training. Only the masked attention matrices and the bi-GRU parameters are updated. In this stage, the ADAM optimizer with a default learning rate of 0.001 is used to train the model over six epochs. Next, end-to-end fine-tuning of the InsightDep is performed without detaching any parameters and all the parameters of of the model are updated in end-to-end fashion. In this stage, the model is trained for two epochs.

All experiments, model fine tuning and training were conducted on a computing system with the following specifications: 11th Gen Intel(R) Core(TM) i7-11700 CPU @ 2.50GHz, 32.0 GB RAM, Nvidia 3060 RTX GPU, Python 3.10, Windows 10 OS.

Discussion

The results of experiments show that the InsightDep model enables to classify various stages of depression by using the text of social platform posts. The model had a macro-F1 of 0.599 and an accuracy of 0.671 on Twitter, and a macro-F1 of 0.994 and an accuracy of 0.994 on Reddit, outperforming BERTweet, TwHIN-BERT, and DepRoBERTa. The proposed model InsightDep using TwHIN-BERT has enhanced the results in detecting depression from the analysis, demonstrating the advantage learning from a vast tweet-based dataset. Similarly, replacing TwHIN-BERT with ALBERT, RoBERTa, or XLNet still yielded strong results, averaging a macro-F1 of 0.599 and accuracy of 0.671 on Twitter, and a macro-F1 of 0.994 and accuracy of 0.994 on Reddit, underscoring TwHIN-BERT’s effectiveness. The main advantage of InsightDep model is that its architecture provides better compatibility of TwHIN-BERT and the bi-GRU head that not only increases the accuracy of the result but also ensures interpretability when applying the masked attention matrices. The integration of ChatGPT for generating tangible human readable summaries of the classifications improve the interpretability of the model giving both clinicians and user an overview of the classification results in a more comprehendible format. This interpretability factor makes a significant difference because a majority of deep learning models lack decision-making transparency. InsightDep also has advantage when it comes to social media data which are normally fragmented, unstructured and noisier than usual due to TwHIN-BERT pre-training domain on multilingual tweets. Despite its good performance, InsightDep model has certain limitations. Although the proposed model delivers a high accuracy in classifying depressive post, it depends greatly on other models such as TwHIN-BERT that might not work well on other languages or platforms other than social media.

First, the dataset could be incorporated with the data in other languages and from different segments of population or from more varied social networks to enhance InsightDep’s generalization ability. Second, other features to include maybe user demographics (age, location etc.) or more information regarding the post which might benefit the model’s decision-making process. Conclusion

According to the World Health Organization, depression is the leading cause of disability and impacts millions globally. This research presents InsightDep, a novel methodology for mental health analysis from social media, integrating TwHIN-BERT, a masked-attention-based bi-GRU, and ChatGPT for interpretable summaries. Its modular design allows flexible integration with other methods. InsightDep achieved a macro-F1 of 0.599 and accuracy of 0.671 on Twitter, and 0.994 for both on Reddit, outperforming baselines. Future work will extend to multilingual, cross-platform data and incorporate richer contextual features to enhance generalisation and clinical applicability.

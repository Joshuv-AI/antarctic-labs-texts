Experimental investigation and ANN modeling of thermo-hydraulic characteristics of GO nanofluid in a tube equipped with wire-coil inserts
Authors: Ravindra Gupta, Priyanka Shah, Arvind Mohite, Nagendra Prasad Pandey, Rajkumari Soni, Niraj Tevar, Ankita Sharma, Hardik Solanki
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Heat transfer enhancement techniques play a crucial role in improving the thermal efficiency of heat exchangers while minimizing energy usage and operational expenses. In this study, the thermo-hydraulic performance of a circular tube equipped with wire-coil inserts and operating with graphene oxide (GO) nanofluid was predicted using an artificial neural network (ANN) model. Experiments were conducted over a Reynolds number range of 5000–18,000 using GO nanofluid with weight concentrations of 0.025–0.1 wt% and wire-coil inserts having pitch-to-diameter ratios (P/D) of 1, 1.5, and 2. The experimental findings revealed that the combined use of GO nanofluid and wire-coil inserts significantly enhanced the Nusselt number, although it also resulted in an increase in the friction factor. At a GO concentration of 0.05 weight% and a P/D ratio of 1.5, the maximum Thermal Performance Factor of 1.21 was attained, signifying an ideal thermo-hydraulic balance. A feed-forward backpropagation ANN model was created with the Reynolds number, nanoparticle concentration, and P/D ratio as input parameters and the Nusselt number and friction factor as outputs in order to forecast system performance. The correctness and dependability of the suggested model were confirmed by the ANN predictions having outstanding agreement with the experimental findings.

Introduction

The continuous growth of energy-intensive industries such as power generation, electronics cooling, automotive systems, refrigeration, and chemical processing has created a strong demand for efficient thermal management solutions. Heat exchangers are central to the performance, reliability, and sustainability of these systems, particularly as modern applications increasingly require compact, lightweight, and high-performance designs. Conventional heat transfer enhancement techniques, including extended surfaces, flow modification, and externally assisted methods, often suffer from drawbacks such as increased system complexity, higher energy consumption, and elevated operational costs. As a result, passive enhancement approaches that can improve thermal performance without requiring external power or major design changes have attracted significant research interest. Among passive techniques, nanofluid technology has emerged as a promising solution due to its ability to enhance heat transfer characteristics by improving the thermophysical properties of conventional working fluids. Engineered colloidal suspensions of nanoscale particles scattered in base fluids like water, ethylene glycol, or oils are known as nanofluids. Through processes including Brownian motion, thermophoresis, and increased interfacial surface area, the presence of nanoparticles improves thermal conductivity and convective heat transfer. The remarkable thermal conductivity and structural characteristics of carbon-based nanoparticles, especially those produced from graphene, have attracted significant interest. Because graphene oxide, an oxidized version of graphene, has oxygen-functional groups that greatly increase its stability and dispersibility in polar fluids, it is particularly well-suited for useful heat exchanger applications.

Ayoob et al. [1] numerically analyzed the thermal–hydraulic behavior of water–Al₂O₃ nanofluid with rotating twisted tapes. The rotating twisted tape arrangement demonstrated better performance than stationary tapes, achieving a maximum figure of merit (FOM) of 1.57. Song et al. [2] conducted an experimental investigation on the heat transfer characteristics of TiO₂–ethylene glycol–water nanofluid in a hollow spiral double-tube heat exchanger. The optimal concentration of 0.5 wt% provided stable dispersion. Their results showed significant improvement in the HTC and Nusselt number within the temperature range of 303–328 K, highlighting the effectiveness of TiO₂ nanofluids for heat exchanger applications. Rai et al. [3] examined the 3E analysis of a heat exchanger operating with a ternary hybrid nanofluid combined with twisted turbulator inserts. Their results revealed that perforated twisted turbulator inserts considerably increased the overall HTC and exergy efficiency, especially at lower Reynolds numbers. However, the use of inserts also resulted in higher friction losses and operating costs, suggesting the importance of balancing thermal and economic performance. In another study, Rai et al. [4] experimentally evaluated a plain tube heat exchanger utilizing ternary hybrid nanofluid (CuO, Al₂O₃, TiO₂) together with twisted turbulator inserts (TTI) and perforated twisted turbulator inserts (PTTI). The experimental results demonstrated that both types of inserts significantly improved heat transfer, and exergy efficiency, with the best performance observed at a nanoparticle concentration of 0.12%. Hussein et al. [5] experimentally studied the thermal performance of a double-pipe copper counter-flow heat exchanger using TiO₂/water nanofluid with concentrations ranging from 0.1% to 0.5%. Their findings indicated that the addition of TiO₂ nanoparticles enhanced both the overall HTC and heat exchanger effectiveness. The optimum performance was obtained at a concentration of 0.3%, resulting in an efficiency improvement of approximately 23%. Hasan et al. [6] numerically investigated heat transfer enhancement in a double-pipe heat exchanger equipped with extended surfaces using Al₂O₃ nanofluid. The results indicated that increasing both the nanoparticle concentration and Reynolds number enhanced the CHTC and thermal conductivity. The finned tube configuration significantly improved the heat transfer performance, with an enhancement ratio ranging from 2.3 to 3.1. Hamza and Aljabair [7] carried out both experimental and numerical analyses using Al₂O₃–CuO/water hybrid nanofluid with plain and double V-cut twisted tape inserts. The results revealed that the double V-cut twisted tape generated stronger vortex flow, which intensified thermal boundary layer disturbance and resulted in higher Nusselt numbers and improved thermal performance and value reached 1.37 at 1.8% nanoparticle concentration. Ghaderi et al. [8] experimentally and numerically examined helically CTHE using Fe₃O₄ nanofluid with an EG–distilled water mixture as the base fluid. They observed that increasing volume fraction, inlet temperature, and flow rate enhanced both the HTC and Nusselt number. A maximum improvement of approximately 60% in the HTC was achieved at a nanoparticle concentration of 0.1%. Hassaan et al. [9] worked on plate heat exchanger operating with MWCNT–distilled water nanofluids and showed that enhanced the overall HTC, Nusselt number, and heat exchanger effectiveness using nanofluids. The maximum Nusselt number enhancement reached about 32% at a concentration of 1.53%. Sundar and Venkata [10] experimentally investigated nickel nanofluids performance and indicated that increasing nanoparticle concentration significantly improved CHTC, effectiveness, and thermal performance factor. Additionally, the use of nanofluids contributed to reduced energy consumption, operational cost, and CO₂ emissions compared with the base fluid. Kundan and Darshan [11] conducted experimental studies on a concentric double-tube heat exchanger using TiO₂–water nanofluid along with twisted tape inserts of different twist ratios and found that the HTC value increased, while the incorporation of twisted tape inserts further enhanced up to 45%, demonstrating the effectiveness of combining nanofluids with passive enhancement techniques. Sameer et al. [12] examined CuO nanofluid at concentrations between 0.05% and 0.2% and observed that effectiveness enhancement of about 5.94%. Singh and Sarkar [13] experimentally investigated the hydrothermal characteristics of Al₂O₃–TiO₂ along with modified V-cut twisted tape inserts. The results demonstrated a significant increase in Nusselt number improving by up to 132%. Although the friction factor increased, the TPF remained greater than unity, indicating improved overall performance. Shahrestani et al. [14] analyzed the CHTC and pumping power characteristics of MWCNT–Fe₃O₄/water hybrid nanofluid using CFD simulation. The hybrid nanofluid enhanced the CHTC while maintaining acceptable pumping power requirements. Additionally, the ribbed tube configuration provided better thermal performance. Rostami et al. [15] investigated the thermal–hydraulic behavior of a spiral HE operating with Cu–ZnO/water hybrid nanofluid under different channel spacing and nanoparticle volume fractions. Their findings revealed that Nusselt number and overall thermal efficiency increased, while reducing channel spacing increased the pressure drop. Based on the performance evaluation criterion, an optimal nanoparticle concentration of around 2% was recommended. Rai and Hegde [16] experimentally analysis of circular finned coil-in-shell HE using GO nanofluid. The combined effect of circular fins and nanofluids significantly improved heat transfer performance, with maximum enhancement of approximately 82% in heat transfer and considerable increases in the Nusselt number. Cao et al. [17] optimized the thermal performance of an alumina–Ag/water nanofluid in a coiled heat exchanger using ANN and NSGA-II. High prediction accuracy (R = 0.999) and optimum ranges of Nusselt number (8–24) and pressure drop (2–16 Pa) were found in the results. The study showed how to balance hydraulic performance and heat transfer enhancement through efficient multi-objective optimization. Taşkesen et al. [18] used ANN to forecast the Fe3O4–Cu/water hybrid nanofluid’s heat transfer performance in a continuous magnetic field. ANN outperformed other models like SVM and decision trees, and the results demonstrated outstanding prediction accuracy (R² = 1.00, RMSE ≈ 0.0012). In order to forecast the heat transfer coefficient of γ-Al2O3/water nanofluid under turbulent flow conditions, Nazari et al. [19] used ANN and ANFIS models. High prediction accuracy (R² = 0.99–1) was demonstrated by the results, with ANFIS marginally exceeding ANN. In comparison to the base fluid, the study also found that raising the Reynolds number and concentration of nanoparticles increased the heat transfer coefficient by as much as 27%. Zolghadri et al. [20] created ANN and SOM models to forecast the Nusselt number and energy consumption in a shell-and-tube heat exchanger using Al2O3 nanofluid With correlation values of almost 0.999 and extremely low mean square errors, the data demonstrated great prediction accuracy. Abu-Hamdeh et al. [21] used SWCNT/oil nanofluid with crosscut twisted tape inserts and ANN prediction to study the hydrodynamic and thermal performance of a heat exchanger. With PEC values above 1.11 and rising to 1.93 at higher Reynolds numbers, the results demonstrated that twisted tapes considerably improved heat transmission. With prediction errors of less than 1%, the ANN model showed excellent accuracy. Alazwari et al. [22] used SiO₂/EG nanofluid to examine the exergetic performance of a helically coiled tube heat exchanger and used ANN and RSM to estimate thermal conductivity. The findings demonstrated that ANN beat RSM and attained extremely high prediction accuracy (R² = 0.999). Additionally, the study showed that energy destruction rises at lower temperatures and larger concentrations of nanoparticles. Nasirzadehroshenin et al. [23] examined the heat transfer efficiency of CNT/water nanofluid in a tube with twisted tape using ANN-GA simulation,. The findings demonstrated that raising the Reynolds number and concentration of nanoparticles greatly increased the heat transfer coefficient; the best results were seen at a concentration of 1.2% with a low twist ratio. To increase heat transfer and reduce pressure drop of CuO/paraffin nanofluid in a pipe, Bagherzadeh et al. [24] suggested a multi-objective optimization strategy that combines ANN and evolutionary algorithms. The model successfully predicted thermo-physical parameters and found the best trade-offs using Pareto analysis.

By creating swirl flow, secondary motion, and higher turbulence intensity, passive flow modification devices including twisted tapes, wire-coil inserts, baffles, and turbulators are frequently employed together to improve convective heat transfer. The Nusselt number significantly increases as a result of these inserts’ disruption of the thermal boundary layer and enhancement of fluid mixing. However, while inserts are effective in increasing heat transfer rates, they often result in higher friction factors and pressure drops, which increase pumping power requirements and reduce overall system efficiency. Similarly, although nanofluids improve the thermal properties of base fluids, they do not significantly alter flow dynamics and turbulence levels, which are critical for boundary layer disruption. Consequently, the combined use of nanofluids and swirl flow devices has emerged as a hybrid passive enhancement strategy, wherein nanofluids enhance thermal conductivity while inserts intensify turbulence and mixing, leading to synergistic heat transfer improvement. Despite the demonstrated potential of hybrid nanofluid–insert systems, their thermal and hydraulic behavior is governed by complex and highly nonlinear interactions among multiple parameters, including Reynolds number, nanoparticle concentration, thermophysical properties, and insert geometry. Most existing studies rely on experimental or numerical approaches that are limited to specific operating conditions and require extensive time and computational resources. Moreover, conventional empirical correlations often fail to accurately represent the coupled nonlinear relationship between input parameters and performance indicators such as Nusselt number, convective heat transfer coefficient, and friction factor. These limitations highlight the need for advanced data-driven modeling techniques capable of capturing complex system behavior and providing reliable predictions across a wide range of operating conditions. In this context, artificial neural networks offer a powerful and efficient alternative for modeling and predicting the performance of hybrid heat transfer enhancement systems, enabling accurate, rapid, and generalized performance evaluation suitable for modern heat exchanger design and optimization.

Novelty of the present work

Although several studies have investigated nanofluids and passive enhancement techniques independently, limited studies are available on the combined thermo-hydraulic performance of graphene oxide (GO) nanofluid and wire-coil inserts under turbulent flow conditions. In addition, ANN-based prediction of the thermo-hydraulic characteristics of GO nanofluid with wire-coil inserts has not been sufficiently explored. Therefore, the present work experimentally investigates the effect of GO concentration and wire-coil pitch-to-diameter ratio on heat transfer and friction characteristics in a circular tube and develops an ANN model for accurate prediction of thermo-hydraulic performance parameters.

Experimental

Nanofluid preparation

The two-step approach was used in the current experimental effort to create graphene oxide (GO)–water nanofluids. Adnano Technologies Pvt. Ltd. provided graphene oxide particles, which were distributed in DI water at weight concentrations of 0.025%, 0.05%, 0.075%, and 0.1%. The modified Hummers process was used to create the GO nanoparticles, which had a thickness of 0.8 to 2 nm, an average lateral particle size (D50) of less than 10 μm, and a purity of 99%. SEM and XRD were used to analyze the morphological and structural characteristics of the GO nanoparticles, as shown in Fig. 1 [25–28]. In order to prepare the nanofluid, the necessary quantity of graphene oxide nanoparticles was first mixed for an hour using a magnetic hot plate stirrer in DI water. After that, the combination was exposed to bath-type ultrasonication for 60 min to increase the stability and achieve uniform dispersion. Visual observation over time and repeatability tests were used to observe the stability of the produced GO-water nanofluids. For up to seven days following preparation, there was no discernible sedimentation or agglomeration, suggesting that the nanofluids demonstrated enough stability for the experimental studies.

Experimental procedure

The experimental facility was designed to investigate the effects of graphene oxide (GO) nanofluid concentration and wire-coil inserts under turbulent conditions in a circular tube, as illustrated in Figs. 2 and 3. The setup consisted of a closed-loop circulation system comprising test section with a nickel-chromium heated coil, flow device with controller, fluid reservoir, and a real-time data logger. The test section was a horizontally mounted copper tube with an ID of 10 mm and a length of 1200 mm. K-type thermocouples were installed at equal axial intervals along the tube wall to monitor surface temperatures, while PT100 RTDs measured fluid temperatures. All signals were continuously recorded using a calibrated data logger. Graphene oxide–water nanofluid was circulated through the loop using a centrifugal pump from a storage tank. The volumetric flow measured using a calibrated flow meter corresponding to the desired Reynolds number range. A cooling unit was incorporated downstream of the test section to remove excess heat and maintain temperature thereby ensuring nanofluid stability and preventing particle agglomeration. Steady-state conditions were established when temperature and pressure fluctuations remained within ± 0.2% for at least 30 min. Wire-coil inserts were selected because they are simple, low-cost, and effective passive heat transfer enhancement devices capable of generating swirl flow and secondary motion inside the tube. Compared with other turbulators, wire-coil inserts provide significant heat transfer enhancement with relatively simple fabrication and installation. In addition, their geometry can be easily modified through pitch variation to control the thermo-hydraulic performance. A copper wire of 1 mm diameter was used to fabricate the wire coil inserts using a spring-making machine. Three inserts having a coil diameter of 10 mm and pitch lengths of 10 mm, 15 mm, and 20 mm were prepared, corresponding to P/D ratios of 1, 1.5, and 2, respectively. Each insert had a length of 1200 mm and was installed inside the test tube such that the coil touched the inner wall of the tube. Differential pressure across the heated length was measured using precision pressure transducers to evaluate frictional losses.

Result and discussion

Nusselt number

Figure 5 shows how the Nusselt number varies for GO/water nanofluids under turbulent flow conditions with various wire-coil pitch to diameter ratios. For all test conditions, the Nusselt number increases consistently with Reynolds number due to intensified turbulence and thinning of the thermal boundary layer at higher flow velocities. The addition of graphene-oxide nanoparticles further enhances the heat transfer performance compared with the base fluid. This improvement is mainly attributed to the higher effective thermal conductivity of the nanofluid, Brownian motion of nanoparticles, and improved micro-scale energy transport within the fluid. The presence of wire-coil inserts significantly amplifies the heat-transfer rate by inducing swirl flow and secondary circulation within the tube, which enhances fluid mixing and temperature uniformity across the cross-section. As a result, the thermal boundary layer is periodically disrupted, leading to higher Nusselt numbers. Among the tested geometrical configurations, the lowest pitch to diameter ratio (P/D = 1) provides the strongest swirl intensity and consequently yields the highest heat-transfer enhancement. With increasing pitch to diameter ratio, the swirl strength decreases, resulting in relatively lower Nusselt numbers. Additionally, for all pitch to diameter ratios, the Nusselt number rises as the concentration of nanoparticles increases. The maximum thermal performance is observed for the nanofluid with 0.1 wt% graphene oxide combined with the wire-coil insert of P/D = 1 at the highest Reynolds number investigated. Under these conditions, the Nusselt number enhancement reaches approximately 95.23% compared to the plain tube, indicating the strong synergistic effect between nanoparticle-induced thermal conductivity augmentation and swirl-induced turbulence generated by the wire-coil insert.

Figure 6 indicates that the friction factor (f) varies for GO/water nanofluids with varying wire-coil pitch to diameter ratios and nanoparticle concentrations. The behavior of turbulent internal flows, where inertial forces predominate over viscous effects at higher flow velocities, is consistent with the observation that the friction factor decreases with rising Reynolds number. The friction factor rises as the concentration of GO nanoparticles increases at any given Reynolds number. This behavior can be attributed to the rise in effective viscosity of the nanofluid, enhanced momentum diffusion caused by suspended nanoparticles, and increased wall shear stress due to intensified fluid–particle interactions. The presence of graphene-oxide nanoparticles therefore introduces additional flow resistance compared with the base fluid (DI water). The insertion of wire-coil turbulators further increases the friction factor due to the generation of swirl flow and secondary circulation within the tube. These inserts disturb the hydrodynamic boundary layer and promote strong mixing of the fluid, but at the expense of higher flow resistance. Among the tested configurations, the lowest pitch to diameter ratio (P/D = 1) produces the greatest obstruction to the flow because of the higher coil density along the tube length. Consequently, it generates the highest friction factor, followed by P/D = 1.5 and P/D = 2. The increase in coil density intensifies flow blockage and enhances wall shear stress, leading to a substantial increase in pressure drop.

The combined effect of nanoparticle concentration and wire-coil geometry results in a significant rise in friction factor compared with the plain tube case. The greatest improve occurs in the 0.1 wt% GO nanofluid with a pitch to dimeter ratio of P/D = 1, where the friction factor rises by around 128.57% owing to the addition of nanoparticles, and up to 300% due to the inclusion of the wire-coil insert. These results indicate that while GO nanofluids and wire-coil inserts effectively enhance convective heat transfer, they also introduce a considerable hydraulic penalty. Therefore, the design of such systems requires a careful balance between thermal performance improvement and increased pumping power requirements.

Thermal performance factor

When passive heat transfer enhancement techniques are used in thermal systems, the heat transfer rate is often increased, but the pressure drop is also increased. In some cases, the rise in pressure drop may offset the benefit of heat transfer enhancement, thereby affecting the overall system efficiency. Artificial neural network (ANN) modeling

For the purpose to predict the thermo-hydraulic performance of a heat exchanger using GO nanofluid and inserts, a multilayer feedforward Artificial Neural Network (ANN) was developed. The significant capacity of ANN to simulate intricate and highly nonlinear interactions between several input factors and output performance measures led to its selection [42–48]. An input layer, two hidden layers, and an output layer make up the three primary layers of the established ANN architecture. Reynolds number (Re), graphene oxide concentration (wt%), and wire-coil pitch to diameter ratio (P/D) are the three independent factors in the input layer that control the thermo-hydraulic behavior. Nusselt number (Nu) and friction factor (f) are two performance metrics that are predicted by the output layer. Nonlinear correlations between input and output variables are extracted by the hidden layers. Several ANN architectures with different neuron combinations and hidden-layer configurations were initially tested to identify the optimal network structure. The final 3–12–8–2 architecture was selected based on its superior prediction capability in terms of lower validation error, higher coefficient of determination (R²), and lower RMSE and MAE values compared to other tested architectures. In addition, the selected network exhibited stable convergence behavior and minimized overfitting during the validation stage, thereby providing an optimal balance between prediction accuracy and model complexity. The modeling procedure starts with the input layer, which has three controlling parameters: wire-coil pitch to diameter ratio (P/D), graphene oxide concentration (wt%), and Reynolds number (Re), as seen in Fig. 9. The ANN model uses these parameters as its input variables. The intricate nonlinear interactions between the input variables and output responses are captured by the network architecture’s two hidden layers. The ANN receives experimental data for weight optimization and guided learning. The output layer predicts two thermo-hydraulic performance metrics: the friction factor (f) and the Nusselt number (Nu). The model’s prediction accuracy is evaluated using statistical performance metrics including Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), and coefficient of determination (R2). The training process continues until the RMSE satisfies a predefined threshold criterion, ensuring optimal model performance. Once the stopping condition is achieved, the final trained ANN model is obtained for reliable prediction.

The Levenberg–Marquardt (LM) backpropagation technique, which combines the benefits of gradient descent optimization with the Gauss–Newton method, was used to train the ANN. This algorithm is particularly suitable for nonlinear regression problems due to its fast convergence and high accuracy.

where I is the identity matrix, J is the Jacobian matrix, e is the error vector, and µ is the damping factor.

While the hidden layers employed a tangent sigmoid (tansig) activation function to capture nonlinear behavior, the output layer used a linear (purelin) activation function to provide continuous thermo-hydraulic parameter prediction. The ANN model was developed using a total of 120 experimental datasets obtained from the thermo-hydraulic performance analysis of the heat exchanger. The dataset was randomly divided into subgroups for training (70%), validation (15%), and testing (15%). Accordingly, 84 samples were used for training the network, 18 samples for validation, and 18 samples for testing. This partitioning method ensures proper model generalization while minimizing overfitting. The training dataset was utilized for updating the network weights and biases, the validation dataset was employed to prevent overfitting during training, and the testing dataset was used to evaluate the predictive capability of the developed ANN model. Among the statistical measures employed to evaluate the ANN model’s prediction capability were the coefficient of determination (R²), root mean square error (RMSE), and mean absolute error (MAE).

Conclusion

The thermal performance characteristics of horizontal tubes using graphene oxide (GO)/water nanofluid and wire-coil inserts with varying pitch-to-diameter ratios under turbulent flow conditions were investigated experimentally in this work. The impacts of wire-coil arrangement and nanoparticle concentration on hydraulic properties and heat transfer enhancement were methodically investigated. Additionally, utilizing the pertinent operational parameters as input variables, an Artificial Neural Network (ANN) model was created to forecast the Nusselt number and friction factor. The experimental findings show that the pitch to diameter ratio and nanoparticle concentration have a major impact on the system’s thermal-hydraulic performance. Convective heat transmission is improved and the thermal boundary layer is disrupted by the swirl flow and secondary circulation created by the insertion of wire coils. However, these enhancements are accompanied by an increase in flow resistance.

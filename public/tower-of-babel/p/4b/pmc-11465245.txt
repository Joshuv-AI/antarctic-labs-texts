Investigating on the Performance and Mechanism of Poly(styrene-methyl methacrylate-ethyl methacrylate-butyl acrylate) as Nano Sealing Agent in Oil-Based Drilling Fluids
Authors: Jian Yang, Ruolan Wang, Bo Dong, Zhongqiang Ai, Lin Peng, Gang Xie
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Conventional micron sealants are unable to effectively seal nanopores and fractures in shale formations, and the development of new nanomaterials has now become a major research focus to address the instability of shale gas wells. In this paper, oil-based drilling fluids (ODFs) sealants named SMEB (poly(styrene-methyl methacrylate-ethyl methacrylate-butyl acrylate)) were synthesized by free radical polymerization. The SMEB has been characterized by Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), laser scattering analysis (LSA), thermogravimetric analysis (TGA), and scanning electron microscopy (SEM). The effect of SMEB on the rheological properties of drilling fluids was evaluated by assessing the changes in the rheological parameters of ODFs before and after aging. In addition, the sealing performance of SMEB in ODFs was investigated by high-temperature, high-pressure (HTHP) fluid loss tests and HTHP dense core permeability tests. The results indicated that the particle size of SMEB ranged from 60.68 to 157.39 nm, with a median size of 89.62 nm. The initial decomposition temperature of SMEB was 334 °C, which was in line with the requirement of high-temperature resistance for materials used in shale gas wells. The addition of SMEB has minimal effect on the rheological properties of the drilling fluids and did not adversely affect its performance. At 150 °C and 3.5 MPa, the sealing efficiency of the simulated mud cake was 59.69% with a measured permeability of 0.77 × 10–4 mD at a concentration of 0.5 wt % SMEB. Additionally, when 0.5 wt % SMEB was applied to artificial cores at 105 °C and 3.5 MPa, the sealing efficiency was as high as 86.70%, and the corresponding permeability was 0.48 × 10–3 mD. SMEB demonstrated excellent sealing capabilities in both simulated mud cake and artificial cores, reducing and preventing drilling fluids filtrate flow into the formation. Therefore, SMEB can be applied to drilling fluids as a novel sealing agent and make a significant contribution to maintaining wellbore stability.

Introduction

Shale gas, as a clean and effective unconventional energy source, has broad developmental prospects.1 However, challenges such as wellbore instability may arise when drilling shale formations for exploitation of the shale oil and gas reserves, which will extend drilling time and affect economic viability.2−4 For the horizontal section of horizontal wells, the increased contact surface between drilling tools and the borehole wall can lead to increased friction and torque, which will trigger intricate downhole incidents like stuck pipe.5,6 The hydration and expansion of clay minerals in shale formation can potentially lead to the instability of the borehole wall, which will lead to complex downhole problems such as impeded drill pipe lift or lowering and borehole wall collapse.7,8 Compared with water-based drilling fluids, oil-based drilling fluids (ODFs) have better lubrication performance. ODFs are used as a natural clay mineral inhibitor to address wellbore instability caused by clay mineral hydration.9 Another challenge comes from the development of microfractures and the prevalence of nanoscale pores in shale formations.10,11 Most of the existing sealing agents used in ODFs were micron-sized materials, making it difficult to effectively seal nanopores, which in turn leads to the intrusion of drilling fluids into the formation, causing a reduction in the strength of the wellbore wall and ultimately destabilizing the wellbore wall.12−14

The nanosealing agent of drilling fluids, which can be used to seal nanopores and fractures, has become a research hotspot of researchers. An et al. prepared acrylamide, 2-acrylamide–2-methyl–1-propanesulfonic acid, and N-vinylpyrrolidone nanoterpolymers by free radical polymerization in aqueous solution. The particle size of the copolymer was predominantly found to be within 50 nm, and it can be resistant to temperatures up to 150 °C.15 For deep wells and ultradeep wells, these materials no longer meet the requirements for temperature resistance. Polymer nanospheres with a double cross-linked structure, capable of withstanding temperatures up to 315 °C and having an average particle size of 133 nm, were synthesized by Li et al., though the effectiveness in reducing the high-temperature, high-pressure (HTHP) fluid loss of drilling fluids was modest, registering at 28.21 and 27.78% for temperatures of 150 and 180 °C, respectively.16 Synthesized using micellar polymerization by Huang et al., a multifunctional polystyrene latex (FPL) not only withstood temperatures up to 250 °C but also possessed particles that vary in size from 200 to 900 nm, with 371 nm being the average. After employing 2% FPL, the artificial mud cake exhibited a sealing rate of 31.58%.17 The organic–inorganic composite was also an important research direction of sealing agents. Li et al. synthesized a composite of styrene butadiene resin and nano-SiO2 that can withstand high temperatures in shale formations. The composite formed a dense filter cake while accessing nanopores to prevent fluid intrusion and improve sealing efficiency.18 Research by Liu et al. on an organic–inorganic nanocomposite revealed that while its sealing efficacy improved with increased quantity, it notably affects the rheological properties of the drilling fluids.19 While most of these nanomaterials exhibited superior sealing capabilities, they were predominantly utilized in water-based drilling fluids. Dai et al. synthesized polymer microspheres with average sizes ranging from 1.0 to 5.0 μm, which were uniformly dispersed, spherical, and had good thermal stability.20 When 3% of the microspheres were added into the ODFs, the filtration loss was reduced to 1.8 and 8.2 mL at room temperature and normal pressure and HTHP, respectively, with good sealing performance. The polymer microspheres synthesized by Zhang et al. were added to ODFs at a concentration of 3%, which did not adversely affect the rheological properties of the drilling fluids.21 The loss of filtration was significantly reduced, and the permeability of the ODFs was reduced by 96.04%, which was an excellent performance in sealing the microfractures. However, the commonly used sealing agents in ODFs were too large in particle size to effectively seal pores and fractures smaller than 100 nm.

Consequently, in order to further improve the stability of the wellbore wall, nanosealing agent for ODFs were being investigated with the aim of improving the sealing efficiency against nanoscale pores and fractures. In this paper, a sealant capable of being used in ODFs was synthesized by free radical polymerization using styrene, methyl methacrylate, ethyl methacrylate and butyl acrylate. The sealant was also characterized and evaluated for sealing performance to explore its feasibility for use in ODFs.

Materials and Methods

Materials

Sodium dodecyl sulfate, sodium vinyl sulfonate, styrene (St), methyl methacrylate (MMA), ethyl methacrylate (EMA), butyl acrylate (BA), divinylbenzene (DVB), and ammonium persulfate (APS) were purchased from Chengdu Kelong Chemical Reagent Factory. Nitrogen was procured from Chengdu Xindu District Zhengrong Gas Co.

Instruments

The particle size distribution of poly(styrene-methyl methacrylate-ethyl methacrylate-butyl acrylate) (SMEB) was measured utilizing a laser scattering (LS) system (BI-200SM) from Brookhaven Instruments. The microstructure was analyzed using a scanning electron microscope (SEM) (Zeiss Sigma 500) from Carl Zeiss, Germany. The characteristic functional groups were characterized by a Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectrometer (Nicolet 6700) from Thermoelectric Corporation. The rheological parameters of the ODFs were measured using a six-speed rotational viscometer (FANN35) from Fann Instrument Co. The sealing performance of SMEB was evaluated using a HTHP filter press (GGS42–2A) from Shandong Meike Instrument Co. Sealing performance was further tested using a SCMS-C4 HTHP dense core permeability testing device (20172034–1) from Chengdu Haohan Completion Rock Power Co.

Preparation of the SMEB

In a three-necked flask, 0.1 g of sodium dodecyl sulfate was weighed and dissolved in ultrapure water, and then the flask was placed in a water bath. After 6 g of sodium vinyl sulfonate and 15 g of St were added, the mixture was heated to 30 °C and stirred for 20 min. Subsequently, 15 g of MMA was introduced, and the temperature of the mixture was increased to 35 °C with stirring for 15 min. Next, 20 g of EMA was incorporated, and the temperature was adjusted to 40 °C, rushing for 10 min. Lastly, 10 g of BA was added and the temperature was raised to 50 °C with stirring for 15 min. The mixture was then heated to 65 °C and stirred for 30 min, and then 2.4 g of DVB was added, and stirring was continued while nitrogen was introduced. The temperature was then elevated to 80 °C. After 20 min, 1.5 g of APS was introduced, and the mixture was left to react for 24 h. Preparation for the Infrared Spectrum

The KBr was baked at high temperature and finely ground. An appropriate amount of KBr powder was taken under 7 MPa pressure and pressed into tablets, and then SMEB was evenly applied to the prepared KBr flakes. The KBr flakes with the products were tested by scanning infrared spectra at frequencies between 4000 and 400 cm–1.

Preparation for Scanning Electron Microscopy

First, an SMEB dispersion solution at a concentration of 20% was prepared, and an appropriate amount of the dispersion was dropped onto a mica slide. The slide was kept still, and the solution was allowed to air-dry and form a thin film. After the sample was dehydrated, a thin layer of gold was sputtered on the surface to make it conductive. At the end of gold sputtering, the morphology of the SMEB was examined using a scanning electron microscope.

Preparation for Particle Size Distribution

After diluting the prepared SMEB 150 times, the SMEB, when loaded into a sample pool, was subjected to particle size distribution analysis using a Zeta PALS/90 plus ζ-potential and particle size analyzer from Brookhaven, USA.

Preparation of ODFs

As an example, the dosage of various additives for the preparation of 100 mL of base drilling fluids is shown in Table 1. First, the specified amounts of primary emulsifier, auxiliary emulsifier, wetting agent, and 3# white oil were mixed in a high stirring cup in the amounts, and the mixture was continuously stirred on high speed for 20 min. Subsequently, organoclay, aqueous calcium chloride solution at 25% concentration, quicklime, and filter loss reducer were weighed and added to the mixture. Drilling Fluids Rheological Properties Test

In order to simulate the high-temperature conditions encountered by the drilling fluids during actual drilling and thus to assess their stability and performance, the drilling fluids samples were aged. First, the ODFs were placed in an aging container and then placed in a preheated drum oven to 150 °C for a set time of 16 h. The container holding the drilling fluids samples was rotated in the roller oven under the push of the roller shaft to simulate the flow of drilling fluids in the wellbore during the drilling process and to ensure that the drilling fluids samples were uniformly heated during the aging process. At the end of the aging process, the containers were removed from the high-temperature roller oven and allowed to cool naturally to room temperature. Subsequently, a six-speed rotational viscometer was used to determine the rheological properties of the drilling fluids before and after aging. HTHP Permeability Experiment

To analyze the effect of sealant SMEB incorporation on the sealing performance of ODFs, HPHT filter press was used to measure the filtration and mud cake building properties of drilling fluids at HPHT. Different concentrations of sealant SMEB were added to the ODFs and stirred at high speed for 30 min to ensure adequate mixing. Subsequently, the stirred ODFs samples were aged at 150 °C for 16 h to simulate the effect of high-temperature formation environment on the performance of drilling fluids. Artificial Core Permeability Experiment

Due to the limitations of the equipment used, the instrument has a narrow transmission channel and is not suitable for use with drilling fluids with complex compositions, which would otherwise cause clogging. Therefore, 3# white oil before and after the addition of SMEB was selected for artificial core permeability experiments. SMEB was uniformly mixed with 300 mL of 3# white oil through stirring for 30 min. The dense core permeability testing device was used to test the permeability of 3# white oil through an artificial core, 25 mm in diameter and 50 mm in length, at a set temperature of 105 °C and a differential pressure of 3.5 MPa. The permeability of the 3# white oil + SMEB mixture was then tested again to assess the sealing effect of SMEB on the artificial core by comparing the change in permeability of the core before and after the addition of the nano sealing agent to the 3# white oil.

Results and Discussion

Infrared Spectrum

Infrared spectroscopy is used to determine the molecular structure of SMEB and to verify the success of the synthesis. The infrared spectrum of SMEB is illustrated in Figure 3. At 1635 cm–1, the wavenumber revealed the stretching vibration peaks associated with the benzene ring structure. The C–H bending vibrational peaks of the benzene ring, primarily due to styrene, were evident at the 671 cm–1 wavenumber, confirming the ring inclusion in the structure. The wavenumber of 1386 cm–1 indicated CH bending vibration peaks of alkanes. The C=O stretching vibration peak of the ester was observed at 3450 cm–1, and the C=O stretching vibration peak at 1720 cm–1 overlapped with the stretching vibration peak of the benzene ring skeleton. The wavelength at 1185 cm–1 indicated the presence of C–O–C stretching vibrations of the ester group and characteristic peaks of the sulfonic acid group. In addition, distinct sulfonate peaks were observed at 1042 and 620 cm–1, which were provided by sodium vinyl sulfonate.

Infrared spectra of the SMEB.

Particle Size Distribution of the SMEB

In shale formations where nanopore seams are prevalent, determining the particle size distribution of the sealing material is critical to evaluating its effectiveness in sealing nanoscale pores. As shown in Figure 4, the particle size distribution of the sealant SMEB was determined to be in the range of 60.68 to 157.39 nm. Notably, the D50 value, which represents the median size of particles with cumulative content up to half of the total, was 89.62 nm, which indicates that more than half of the particles in the SMEB were less than 100 nm, which is within the optimal size range for nanopore sealing. SMEB has a dispersion coefficient of 0.10, indicating excellent dispersibility, which is essential to ensure uniform distribution of particles in the drilling fluids. Moreover, the effective dispersion ability of SMEB enhances its potential to penetrate and seal nanopore seams in shale formations. Therefore, SMEB, as a nanoscale sealant, can effectively seal shale formations with less than 100 nm pores, effectively reduce fluids loss and maintain wellbore stability.

Thermogravimetric Analysis

Thermogravimetric (TG) tests are used to determine the mass loss of a substance over temperature and, thus, to assess the thermal stability of a material. During the initial period from room temperature to 334 °C, minimal change in the slope of the TG curve was shown in Figure 5. At 334 °C, the weight of SMEB decreased by only 3.78%, which was attributed to the decrease in mass due to the volatilization of bound and free water in SMEB. This indicated that SMEB exhibited commendable thermal stability in a nitrogen environment. Subsequently, the weight of SMEB decreased dramatically between temperatures of 334 and 454 °C, and the DTG curve further showed that the decomposition of SMEB was the fastest at 424 °C. When the temperature reached 454 °C, only 4.42% of the mass of SMEB remained, indicating that it underwent a complete thermal degradation process.22,23 The third stage of thermal degradation was above 454 °C, in which the TG curve of SMEB tends to stabilize and approach the thermal degradation limit. Thermogravimetric tests have shown that SMEB has commendable thermal stability, which is attributed to the presence of a benzene ring known for its resistance to high temperatures.

Drilling Fluids Performance Evaluation

Through characterization experiments, the SMEB was proved to be a nanomaterial exhibiting notable thermal stability, making it suitable for sealing nanopores and fissures within geological formations. However, incorporating the sealing agent will alter both the rheological characteristics and electrical stability of the drilling fluids. An increased dosage correlates with a more pronounced effect, detrimental to the drilling fluids. Given that the introduction of SMEB may alter the performance of drilling fluids, it is necessary to conduct a thorough investigation of whether it can be used in drilling fluids. Add 0.00, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, and 1.00 wt % of the SMEB to the base drilling fluids in sequence. The rheological properties (AV, PV, YP) and breaking voltage of the drilling fluids at 150 °C as affected by different dosages of SMEB were shown in Figures 6 and 7.

Effect of the SMEB on AV, PV, and YP

The changes in AV, PV, and YP of ODFs before and after the addition of the sealing agent SMEB were illustrated by the described curves in Figure 6. The AV, PV, and YP of ODFs showed a consistent upward trend with the increase of SMEB addition. Initially, the AV of the base drilling fluids was 58.5 mPa·s, and the PV was 49 mPa·s. When 1.0 wt % of SMEB was added, the AV and PV values increased to 66.0 mPa·s and 54.0 mPa·s, respectively, which was an increase of 12.82 and 10.20%, respectively, compared to the base drilling fluids. The increase in viscosity of the drilling fluids was due to the fact that the network structure became denser, and the width of the network skeleton was larger as the SMEB increased.24 A gradual increase in the YP of the ODFs from 9.5 to 12 Pa was observed when the amount of SMEB was incremented from 0 to 1.0 wt % in 0.25 wt % steps. The above results show that SMEB are effective in enhancing viscosity and have good cutting lifting capacity.25 The ability of SMEB to enhance the shear-thinning characteristics of the drilling fluids contributed to improving the rock-breaking effect at high shear rates and the efficiency of cuttings transportation at low shear rates.26

Effect of the SMEB on Emulsion Breaking Voltage

The variation of the emulsion breaking voltage after SMEB was added to the ODFs is revealed in Figure 7. A gradual decrease in the emulsion breaking voltage of ODFs was observed as the addition of SMEB increases. However, the decrease was relatively small, and the effect of nano sealing agent SMEB on the emulsion breaking voltage of ODFs was small.27

It was observed that the impact of SMEB on the rheology and electrical stability of drilling fluids was minimal. After the addition of SMEB, the rheological and emulsification properties of ODFs remained stable, and the rock-carrying and pore-cleaning abilities were enhanced, indicating that SMEB, as a sealing agent, can be applied to ODFs.

Evaluation of the ODFs Sealing Properties

Mud Cake Experiment

The sealing effect of SMEB on the mud cake was evaluated at 150 °C and 3.5 MPa, as shown in Figure 8. The permeability of the mud cake without any sealant was 1.91 × 10–4 mD. This level of permeability was very close to that of shale and was considered suitable for modeling and evaluating the effectiveness of sealing to shale formation. With the addition of SMEB, the permeability of the mud cake was significantly reduced, which corresponded to an increase in the sealing rate. After adding 0.50 wt % of SMEB, the measured permeability of the mud cake was 0.77 × 10–4 mD, and the sealing rate was 59.69%. When the amount of SMEB added was more than 0.50 wt %, the sealing rate showed a slow increasing trend with the increasing amount of SMEB added. Therefore, this suggested that 0.5 wt % was the optimal addition amount of SMEB.

Changes in permeability and sealing rate corresponding to varying dosages of SMEB.

“Artificial Core” Experiment

According to the mud cake experiments, the best sealing performance was obtained with the addition of 0.50 wt % SMEB. In order to verify the sealing effect of SMEB on the artificial core, a permeability experiment was conducted on the artificial core at 105 °C to measure its permeability. The permeability of the 3# white oil flowing through the artificial core was 3.61 × 10–3mD, while the permeability of the 3#white oil with 0.50 wt % SMEB added was 0.48 × 10–3mD, which was 86.70% lower compared to the pure white oil. Considering the permeability results, the sealing effect was commendable and suitable for use as a sealant for ODFs.

Sealing Mechanism

Scanning electron microscopy was utilized to observe the microscopic structure of the SMEB, depicted in Figure 9. The SMEB were characterized by spherical nanoparticles with a wide distribution of particle sizes, which endows the material with the ability to adapt and seal pores at different scales, thus enabling multiscale sealing. As shown in Figure 10, which depicts the morphology of the mud cake, the appearance of the mud cake changed before and after the addition of the SMEB, and its surface became significantly smoother after the addition of the SMEB. Scanning electron micrographs of the mud cake before the addition of the sealing agent, which is having many micrometer and nanometer scale pores and cracks were shown in Figure 11a. As can be seen in Figure 11b, after the addition of 0.5 wt % SMEB, the fine particles of the sealant penetrated into the pore spaces of the mud cake, effectively sealing the pores and reducing the permeability of the mud cake. SMEB can seal not only nanometer-sized pores, but also micron-sized pores and cracks. The small particle size of SMEB can penetrate and seal nanometer-sized pores and cracks in shale layers. The small particle size of SMEB penetrates and seals nanoscale pores and cracks in shale formations. For larger pores, the particles were stacked and bridged, effectively sealing micron-sized pores and cracks. The sealing mechanism of SMEB during microfracture formation is shown in Figure 12. As a nanoscale sealant, SMEB participates in the formation of mud cake, and a part of it passes through the mud cake together with the filtrate. The other part followed the direction of the fluids into the nanoscale pores in the formation and accumulated at a certain depth to form a sealing layer, thus reducing the penetration of drilling fluids. As the amount of SMEB was increased, the sealing layer became denser, reducing filtrate loss.28 The study showed that the synthesized SMEB can be used as a highly efficient nanoscale sealant in ODFs, which can effectively reduce the penetration of drilling fluids into the nanoscale pores of the formation and protect the strength of the rock, thus contributing to reservoir protection.

Impact of carbon neutralization policy on the suitable habitat distribution of the North China leopard
Authors: Mengyan Zhu, Yue Zhao, Weiqiang Li, Xinghua Han, Zhen Wang, Xiaomei Yang, Cuiying Dang, Yaoguo Liu, Shicai Xu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Chinese government has introduced a carbon neutral policy to cope with the rapid changes in the global climate. It is not clear what impact this policy will have on wildlife. Therefore, this study analyzed the suitable habitat distribution of China’s unique leopard subspecies in northern Shaanxi, and simulated the potential suitable habitat distribution under different carbon emission scenarios at two time points of future carbon peak and carbon neutralization. We found that in the future SSPs 126 scenario, the suitable habitat area and the number of suitable habitat patches of North China leopard will continue to increase. With the increase of carbon emissions, it is expected that the suitable habitat of North China leopard will continue to be fragmented and shifted. When the annual average temperature is lower than 8 °C, the precipitation seasonality is 80–90 mm and the precipitation of the warmest quarter is greater than 260 mm, the probability of occurrence of North China leopard is higher. The increase in carbon emissions will lead to the reduction, migration, and fragmentation of the suitable habitat distribution of the North China leopard. Carbon neutrality policies can protect suitable wild habitats. In the future, the impact of carbon neutrality policies on future wildlife habitat protection should be carried out in depth to effectively promote the construction of wildlife protection projects.Keywords: Carbon neutrality, Carbon peak, Climate change, Suitable habitat, Northern Shaanxi, Species distribution model, North China leopardSubject terms: Climate-change ecology, Conservation biology, Ecology, Zoology, Biodiversity

Introduction

Because leopards can adapt to most habitats1, leopards are the most widely distributed cats in the world2. Under the background of global large predators generally facing habitat quality decline, habitat fragmentation and population shrinkage, the population size and distribution range of leopards are also declining2. The leopard is listed on the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species and is currently rated as vulnerable (www.iucnredlist.org). However, considering the population size of different subspecies, the endangered degree of leopards may be much higher than expected2. The Loess Plateau in northern China was once considered unsuitable for human survival because of its harsh living environment, due to the pressure of China’s population, the region has always maintained a large population density, which makes the ecological environment of this region fragile3. This area is not only the core area of ecosystem restoration but also the hot spot of biodiversity conservation4. The northern part of Shaanxi province is located in the central area of this fragile habitat. The North China leopard (Panthera pardus fontanierii) is a subspecies of leopard only distributed in China. Although the North China leopard and the Amur leopard (Panthera pardus orientalis) are merged into one subspecies by IUCN, the North China leopard should still be an independent subspecies according to the distribution range and the latest biological analysis5. The North China leopard has been proved to persist in the Loess Plateau, and its distribution has gradually spread6. The northern Shaanxi is the region with the largest number of North China leopard populations reported so far, but there is still a lack of continuous and in-depth research6. Proving the accurate distribution of the leopard population is always the premise of effective protection.

The distribution pattern and seasonal variation of precipitation and temperature are the key variables affecting the distribution of terrestrial vertebrates7. With the intensification of global climate change, many species may change their distribution range to adapt8, which brings protection challenges. Studies have confirmed that the intensification of climate change has affected the distribution of large mammals9. When the temperature rises, the species moves to higher altitudes and latitudes, resulting in habitat isolation10. For leopards, habitat fragmentation is an important cause of risk factors11. According to the current monitoring results, the increase of climate has caused the overlapping distribution of leopards and snow leopards12, and the continuous increase of temperature in the future will aggravate the conflict between the two endangered cats. On the other hand, when the temperature rises, the low latitudes will be affected by drought stress, and wildlife will migrate to areas that are more conducive to survival13. If there are not enough ecological corridors to cope with the migration of the population, the leopard will face the risk of local extinction7. At present, China’s ultra-high population density will increase the difficulty of establishing ecological corridors. Climate change is not only reflected in the change of temperature, but also in the impact on other environmental factors (such as rainfall change, land use type change). Climate change may affect the spatial distribution of landscape characteristics and the abundance of wild animal populations, and may also affect the changes in chemical composition, nutrients, and water availability in soil14. Increased temperature, decreased humidity, and seasonal changes in precipitation are the driving forces of changes in ecosystem functions15. This may lead to a bottom-up reduction in biomass, which in turn adversely affects the distribution of wildlife16. The abundance of herbivorous animals will directly affect the habitat suitability of leopards17. The decline in prey abundance caused by climate change makes leopards more prey on livestock and aggravate human-leopard conflict18, which may further reduce the habitat suitability of leopards.

At present, in order to cope with a series of adverse effects of climate change, the Chinese government has introduced China’s carbon neutrality policy19. The Chinese government has pledged to achieve carbon peak by around 2030 and carbon neutrality by 2050–206019. At the same time, the Chinese government has pledged to achieve the goal of “protecting 30% of the world’s land and marine areas by 2030” set out in the post-2020 Global Biodiversity Framework20. In order to achieve the above two goals, it is foreseeable that the protected area will face a wide range of overall planning and adjustment. As the distribution of different species responds to climate change, the impact on protected areas can be two-way, and biodiversity can increase or decrease. If the planning of protected areas is widely adjusted without considering the carbon emission background of key time nodes in the future, the expected protection effect may not be achieved. Different habitat conservation strategies will have a strong impact on the number of leopard populations21. As a typical species inhabiting fragmented landscapes, climate change caused by carbon emission policies may have a broader impact on the distribution of North China leopards than expected22. As an umbrella species in the ecosystem, North China leopard can promote the stability of biodiversity in its distribution area and have a positive impact on the distribution of other species in the distribution area23. This means that changes in the distribution area of North China leopards may cause a chain reaction, resulting in significant changes in the composition of wildlife in the distribution area, thereby affecting ecosystem functions. Although China has strictly protected endangered and rare wildlife through the implementation of the national park system, it may invalidate the results of protection if it does not understand the impact of carbon neutrality policy implementation24. Therefore, it is of great significance to reasonably predict the impact of the key time nodes of the Chinese government’s efforts to achieve carbon peak and carbon neutralization on the habitat of the Panthera leopard population. At the same time, it is of great significance for the further protection of North China leopard in the future.

Remote sensing data-driven habitat suitability assessment is widely used in species distribution modeling due to its ability to predict habitat suitability at a large scale and rapid data iteration25. Studies have shown that compared with single-source remote sensing data, the application of multisource data to species distribution prediction will greatly improve the reliability of prediction26. Currently, with the increase of available high-precision remote sensing product data sets, a wide range of ecological factors can be widely extracted, thus accelerating the study of species distribution prediction. The current intensification of global climate change has caused conservation biology to pay more attention to the challenges posed by climate change to wildlife conservation8. At present, with the improvement of artificial intelligence and big data analysis capabilities, the use of a variety of high-precision climate change remote sensing data and land use remote sensing data sets has been able to more accurately assess the impact of climate change on wildlife27. The quality of species field distribution data is very important for the accuracy of species distribution prediction28. Although the study of camera traps provides a large amount of wildlife distribution data for conservationists, most of the locations where camera traps are installed are based on the design of specific target species rather than random placement29. Therefore, there may be sampling bias in species distribution data from a single camera trap source. The introduction of other distributed data sources is conducive to improving the prediction accuracy of the model and avoiding overfitting.

At present, species distribution modeling (SDM) is the most important method to evaluate the distribution of wildlife populations30. These methods have been widely used, especially in identifying the potential habitat distribution of target species31. The study of suitable habitats for species effectively helps to understand the distribution characteristics of wild animal populations, making field surveys more cost-effective32. Among the many SDM modeling methods, the maximum entropy algorithm (MaxEnt) is the most widely used33. The MaxEnt model can predict the existing distribution area of the target species and identify its distribution trend. MaxEnt combines environmental variables with species distribution data to predict the distribution probability of target species and then outputs the suitable habitat distribution of species, which can predict different regions moderately34. The results of the MaxEnt model are accurate, reliable, and simple, compared with other models, it can simplify the complex relationship between species and variables. The MaxEnt model can also obtain more reliable prediction results when predicting small sample size data33.

In summary, this study collected the multi-source occurrence point data of the North China leopard population in northern Shaanxi. Combined with multi-source remote sensing data products, the species distribution model was used to predict the distribution of potential suitable habitats of North China leopard at the current, carbon peak and carbon neutral time nodes. By revealing the changes in the distribution of potential suitable habitats of the North China leopard population under the time nodes of carbon peak and carbon neutralization, we can verify the impact of carbon neutralization policy on wildlife protection. We expect to clarify which carbon emission level will be beneficial to wildlife conservation through this study, and our research hypotheses are: (1) the global warming caused by carbon emissions will adversely affect the distribution of suitable habitats for leopards in North China; (2) if the carbon neutrality target is expected to be achieved, it will play a positive role in the protection of China’s unique leopard subspecies.

Results

Model parameters and model accuracy evaluation

After the variable correlation test, only 14 variables were retained among the 29 variables (Supplementary Figs. S1, S2). After sorting the 14 variables according to the contribution of the model, it is found that most of the variables contribute little to the model (Supplementary Fig. S3). In order to avoid over-fitting, the variables with low contribution to the removal of fitting are further removed without reducing the accuracy of the model, and finally three variables are retained.

Using the cross-validation-based SDMtune package, a variety of combinations of RM (Regularization multiplier) and FC (Feature combination) of MaxEnt were tested with the optimal combination of variables. We sorted the results of 468 combinations of model parameters from high to low according to the TSS test (Supplementary Table S1), and finally selected the combination with the highest test TSS value as the model parameter of the final model. The features are threshold features (T), and the RM value is 0.2 (Table 1).

Currently, the potential habitat area of the north China leopard in northern Shaanxi is nearly 25,000 km2, and the number of habitat patchs is 51 (Fig. 1). Detailed 51 suitable habitat distribution patches are displayed in Supplementary Fig. S4. It shows that the northern Shaanxi area is one of the important areas of the distribution of the North China leopard population. The suitable habitat distribution range of the North China leopard is mainly distributed around the forest. In general, its habitat is mainly distributed in the southwest of the study area, and the distribution area in the northeast of the study area is gradually decreasing and the habitat is gradually fragmenting from south to north. From the perspective of the mountain system, North China leopards are currently mainly distributed in the Ziwuling Mountains in the west, and the Liangshan Mountains in the east have a small area of habitat (Fig. 2).

Our results show that different carbon emissions have a significant effect on the potential suitable habitat of the North China leopard (Figs. 1, 2). When the SSPs 126 path is maintained, the habitat area and patch number of the north China leopard will gradually increase and may increase to nearly 40,000 km2 by 2050, while the number of suitable habitat patches is expected to exceed 65 (Fig. 1). The suitable habitat of the SSPs 245 path remained unchanged, but the number of habitat patches fluctuated greatly. The suitable habitat area under the SSPs 370 path increased slightly, but the number of suitable habitat patches continued to decline. Under the SSPs 585 path, the habitat area reached the lowest value in 2050, and the number of suitable habitat patches reached the maximum, indicating that habitat fragmentation was the most serious under this scenario (Fig. 2). Under the three scenarios of SSPs 245, SSPs 245, and SSPs 585, the habitat distribution center of the North China Leopard will be seriously shifted in 2050, making the original large-scale distribution area into an unsuitable distribution area, which indicates the risk of the North China Leopard population from collapse in the future.

The main factors affecting the suitable habitat distribution of North China leopard

We use the jackknife method to analyze the influence of environmental variables on the results of the North China leopard distribution and rank the importance of the variables (Fig. 3). We found that the seasonality of precipitation was the most important factor affecting the suitable habitat distribution of North China leopards, contributing 60% of the importance. Second, the annual average temperature contributed 30% of the importance. The precipitation in the warmest season contributed 10% of the importance.

Discussion

The carbon neutrality policy proposed by the Chinese government is expected to have a huge impact on wildlife protection if it can achieve the expected goals at key time points35. From our results, the scenario of carbon neutrality will have a positive effect on the protection of North China leopards.

The response of all variables is the threshold response, indicating the sensitivity of the North China leopard to climate variables. The lower variability of precipitation is conducive to the distribution of North China leopards. This may be due to the unique geographical location of the Loess Plateau, which is a serious area of soil erosion36. Areas with high precipitation variability are prone to changes in landscape characteristics, which has a negative impact on the distribution of leopards in North China4. After the precipitation of the warmest quarter exceeds 250 mm, the distribution probability of the North China leopard increases. The dry and wet seasons change significantly, which is unfavorable for the distribution of prey (such as ungulates) of North China leopards, thus indirectly reducing the distribution probability of North China leopards37. In the dry season, the lack of food will increase the risk of human–animal conflict, which is also detrimental to the distribution of leopards in North China38. Precipitation of the warmest quarter was positively correlated with biomass, which explained the demand for food in the distribution of North China leopards. Only with sufficient food sources can the distribution of top predators be supported21. Studies have confirmed that when biomass is insufficient, the predator diet will increase the proportion of livestock39. Livestock killed by leopards will be paid for by the government, thus reducing the loss of farmers, which increases the funds for wildlife protection40. As a top predator distributed in northern China, the impact of annual mean temperature on the North China leopard is in line with expectations. This hypothesis has been confirmed in the study of snow leopards (Panthera uncia). Climate warming may lead to the migration of their populations to high altitudes and the isolation of suitable habitats41. Due to climate warming, in addition to migrating to higher altitudes, North China leopards may also move to northern regions to find suitable temperatures, but without the support of forests, the population will face the possibility of shrinking or even collapsing42. Therefore, to cope with the impact of climate change on North China leopards, the area of continuous forests should be actively expanded.

Our study suggests that achieving carbon neutrality by 2050 is beneficial to the distribution of North China leopard populations, and only under very low carbon emission scenarios (SSPs 126) can the North China leopard’s suitable land continue to grow43. In the context of SSPs 245, the suitable habitat area of the North China leopard remained unchanged, but the spatial distribution showed a significant northward movement, which deviated from the current distribution area of the North China leopard (Fig. 2). When the spatial distribution of suitable habitats changes dramatically, the habitats need ecological stepping stones to enable wild animals to migrate smoothly42. Currently, relevant research in this area in northern Shaanxi is still lacking. Under the two scenarios of SSPs 370 and SSPs 585, the suitable habitat quality of North China leopard will decrease. Among them, the suitable habitat area is the smallest, and the suitable habitat fragmentation is the most serious under the SSPs 585 scenario in 2030. Other studies on endangered species have also reached similar conclusions, and scholars around the world have recognized the impact of climate change on the distribution of wild animals43. In order to cope with the possible impact of future climate change on the suitable habitat of North China leopard, we should increase the research on the planning of protection projects and increase the protection funds.

The annual average temperature will become significant with increasing carbon emissions (Fig. 5a), which is extremely unfavorable for the distribution of North China leopards. Temperature changes will cause the migration or even disappearance of suitable habitats41. In the future, the precipitation seasonality will decrease (Fig. 5b), which is beneficial to the distribution of north China leopards from the trend point of view, but with the increase of carbon emissions, the extreme value will gradually increase, which may lead to the intensification of habitat fragmentation. Studies have confirmed that climate change will lead to an aggravation of the global abnormal climate, and from our research results, this will affect the population distribution of the North China leopard44. Therefore, the smooth implementation of the carbon neutrality policy is extremely necessary for the protection of leopards in northern Shaanxi. The precipitation in the warmest season showed a slow downward trend as a whole, but the variation range increased year by year (Fig. 5c). Compared with the change in the overall mean, the possible variation range is a more important factor affecting the distribution of the North China leopard population. On the range of one hand, the variation of precipitation increases, which increases the difference in habitat biomass distribution, so that some areas may not be suitable for predator distribution, resulting in fragmentation of population distribution45. On the other hand, uneven precipitation may aggravate soil erosion and change the type of land cover, which will harm the distribution of wildlife14.

At present, the potential suitable distribution area of the north China leopard in northern Shaanxi is almost 250,000 km2, and there are 51 suitable habitat patches. This is in line with our expectation that the study area is one of the core habitats for leopard population distribution. It is reported that the northern Shaanxi region is an important habitat for leopards in North China6. At present, North China leopards are distributed in North China, but the research progress of each local population is different. There is a lack of systematic and in-depth research on the local population in Northern Shaanxi23. The northern Shaanxi region is in the central area of environmental protection and restoration34. However, no unified flagship species has been established to lead conservation efforts in the region. Currently, in the Qinling Mountains in the southern part of Shaanxi province, the giant panda (Ailuropoda melanoleuca), the Snub-nosed monkey (Rhinopithecus roxellana qinlingensis), the takin (Budorcas taxicolor bedfordi) and the crested ibis (Nipponia nippon) are the flagship species for protection, making the protection project in the Qinling Mountains effective46. Our results show that reducing carbon emissions is beneficial to the suitable habitat distribution of North China leopards, so North China leopards are suitable as indicator species for carbon neutrality policies and related conservation engineering planning. It is worth noting that even if the goal of carbon neutrality policy is completed, the forest coverage area in the study area also needs to respond to the northward expansion, which is sufficient to match the expected expansion of the suitable distribution area of North China leopard (Fig. 2). The Department of Conservation and Management should continue to carry out returning farmland to forests to match future wildlife protection needs.

Limitations of the study

To clarify the importance of multi-source data for the prediction of the distribution of North China leopards, we integrated multi-source remote sensing data sets and multi-source field distribution data in the study area. However, the variables that affect the suitable habitat distribution of the North China leopard are derived from climatic factors. This may be because the North China leopard is a climate-sensitive species, and at the same time, it is a large-scale distributed predator with a strong tolerance to land use and human activities23. However, our model fitting results support the view that multi-source field distribution data can improve model accuracy. Our model test results (Test AUC and Test TSS) illustrate the importance of multi-source data. The reliability of all the research results depends on the accuracy of the model, which is the basis for habitat prediction47. Therefore, we suggest that priority should be given to the use of multi-source data sets in studies using species distribution models. In spite of this, our field distribution sites in the eastern part of the study area are still relatively lacking, and the intensity of investigation in the region should be increased in the future.

When there are too many candidate variables in the model, most studies use the results of correlation detection to screen the variables47. However, the specific removal of relevant variables will have an impact on the accuracy of the prediction. The usual practice is to delete variables based on the researcher’s ecology-related experience. We recommend data-driven variable selection, selection based on the importance of variables to the model48. Rather than fitting the uncorrelated variables to obtain the final model, the contribution of the variables to the accuracy of the model should be analyzed, and the variables with low contribution should continue to be screened to avoid overfitting the model. In some studies, the establishment of the MaxEnt model often uses the default hyperparameters provided by the software31. Although the results can still be obtained, their reliability is questioned. However, recent studies have begun to focus on tuning model hyperparameters30. Some corresponding r packages have also been developed for model tuning49,50. Compared to the default parameter model, the parameter-optimized model has better fitting and lower complexity, proving the importance of parameter optimization. Our study found that the TSS values fluctuated from 0.85 to 0.93 by detecting 468 combinations of candidate model parameters. Our results support the adjustment of the MaxEnt model hyperparameters, and we suggest incorporating them into the standard process of species distribution modeling.

Conclusion

We evaluated the suitable habitat distribution of leopard populations in northern Shaanxi, China, under four scenarios of carbon emissions. The study area is an important distribution area of North China leopard, but different carbon emission policies will be expected to have a differentiated impact on the distribution of North China leopard populations. We believe that only the timely completion of the goal of the carbon neutrality policy formulated by the Chinese government can have a positive impact on the North China Leopard in the study area35. Our study confirms that carbon neutralization has a positive effect on wildlife conservation. To better cope with the adverse effects of possible future climate change on the conservation of North China leopard populations, we recommend strengthening habitat connectivity research and carrying out the corresponding conservation planning projects. At the same time, efforts must be made to expand the area and quality of forests, to cope with the suitable habitat migration of populations caused by climate change. Finally, as a unique Chinese leopard subspecies that survives in the fragmented habitat of the Loess Plateau, it should receive more attention than now. The government and conservation biologists should continue to carry out in-depth work on the protection of the North China leopard population.

Methods

Study area

The northern Shaanxi region is the central part of the Loess Plateau in China. It is located north of the Yellow River and the central part of the Loess Plateau (latitude 35–40° N, longitude 107–112° E)51. The terrain is high in the northwest and low in the southeast. The total area of northern Shaanxi is about 92,500 km2, which is formed based on ancient terrain composed of Mesozoic bedrock, covering the Cenozoic red soil and a very thick loess layer, and then through water cutting and soil erosion3. The study area belongs to the temperate continental semi-arid climate, dry climate, cold in winter, and hot in summer51. The region is considered one of the most ecologically fragile areas in China due to its thick loess cover, serious water erosion, and fragile ecological foundation. To maintain the continuity of the forest, this study carried out a 40 km buffer zone in northern Shaanxi to fully cover the forest distribution range that covers this area. Finally, considering the leopard movement ability, a 20 km buffer zone was established for the forest distribution area, and the buffer area was used as the prediction range for suitable habitats (Fig. 6). Finally, the area we used to predict the potential suitable habitat is nearly 51,400 km2.

Data collection

We collected the occurrence points of North China leopard in northern Shaanxi (Fig. 6). There are three sources of occurrence points of North China leopard. The first is the result of the installation of camera trap monitoring by our research group in cooperation with the local wildlife protection authorities in 2021–2023. The image of the North China leopard was recorded by the camera trap to determine the occurrence point of the North China leopard. Installation and monitoring of camera traps According to the previous research of the research group, the effectiveness of the monitoring scheme in monitoring the north China leopard has been proved22. The second source is the spatial data of the occurrence information of North China leopards collected from local villagers during field visits. Finally, we retrieved the distribution information of North China leopard in news reports in the past five years and spatialized it. For the data from the latter two sources, we look for the smallest-scale small place names on Google Earth, and record the coordinates of the points as the occurrence point of the North China leopard. The source details of all points are shown in Supplementary Table S1.

We used a total of 29 variables incorporating bioclimatic, geographical, human activity, and land cover variables (Table 2). By comparing the previous selection of suitable habitat prediction variables for wild animals and combining with the experience of field investigation in northern Shaanxi, we believe that our variable selection is reasonable13,21,41. Bioclimatic variables are downloaded from the WorldClim data website (www.worldclim.org). Bioclimatic variables represent the annual trend of climatic factors specific to seasonal, extreme, or limiting environmental factors52.

Details of environmental variables used for species distribution models.

Elevation and land relief are important factors affecting the distribution of wildlife53. Therefore, geographical variables include elevation and topographic relief. The elevation data was derived from the SRTM (Shuttle Radar Topography Mission) data54. Land relief refers to the difference between the altitude of the highest point and the altitude of the lowest point in a specific area. In ArcGIS, the elevation data is calculated by using the domain analysis in the spatial analysis module.

The human activity variable contains human footprint and road data. The human footprint map is derived from the 20 years of Human Footprint (https://wcshumanfootprint.org) of Wildlife Conservation Society (WCS). We extracted data for 2020 to represent the intensity of human activity in the current scenario. The human footprint map is the most widely used indicator of human impact in the world55. The footprint is a simple weighted sum that includes where people live (population density), where we build infrastructure (including roads, railways, factories, and other types of infrastructure), where we can go (accessibility), and where we use electricity (a proxy for measuring industrial energy supply through nighttime lights). Human footprint is an important factor affecting biodiversity56.

The road was extracted from the National Catalogue Service for Geographic Information (https://www.webmap.cn). The impact of roads on wildlife has been significant in previous studies57. Changes in land cover have been shown to be one of the important variables affecting the distribution of wildlife10,58. The land cover variable is derived from China’s 30 m annual land cover dataset59. These data are currently updated to Version 1.0.2 to provide land cover data for China from 1985 to 2022. In this study, the land cover data of 2020–2022 were extracted, and the average value was taken to represent the current land cover situation59.

According to the previous research on North China leopards and considering their home range22,60, we believe that the 4 km × 4 km grid resolution is reasonable. Therefore, the 29 environmental variables were rescaled to 4 km × 4 km spatial resolution and used to predict suitable habitats22. Among them, bioclimatic variables, geographic variables, and human footprint are calculated as the mean value of the grid range, the road is calculated as the total length of the road in the grid range, and the land cover variable is calculated as the proportion of each land cover type area in the grid.

Extraction of future environmental variables under climate change

Future bioclimatic variables including the 19 bioclimatic variables were derived from ACCESS-CM2 (The Australian Community Climate and Earth System Simulator Climate Model Version 2) model61. The WorldClim website also provides monthly minimum temperature, monthly maximum temperature, and monthly precipitation data for 23 global climate models and 4 shared socio-economic paths (SSPs): 126,245,370 and 585, and 19 bioclimatic variables are calculated accordingly52. We extracted the biological variable data of the four SSPs of the 2030 (2020–2040) and 2050 (2040–2060) datasets. 2030 is the carbon peak time node announced by the Chinese government, and 2050 is the expected carbon neutralization time19. The variable land cover is derived from the China PFT (Plant Functional Types) projection data set under the SSP-RCP scenarios62. The data set uses the newly developed FLUS (Future Land Uses Simulation) model to perform a high-resolution land use simulation of 1 km from 2015 to 2100 (5-year interval). We extracted the land cover data from four SSPs in the data sets 2030 and 2050, which can correspond to current data and future biological variables. For the geographical variable and the human activity variable, we assume that the future scenario is unchanged and still uses the current data set for prediction.

Data processing and parameter optimization

According to the determined unified spatial resolution, the location of the North China leopard is spatially filtered, and the spatially repeated entries are deleted to ensure that there is only one data point in each grid. A total of 72 occurrence points were collected, and 63 occurrence points were retained after screening (Fig. 6). To smoothly perform data-driven variable screening, we use the dismo package in the r software to establish the MaxEnt model48.

First, fit a model that contains all variables. Then, the correlation analysis of all the environmental variables is carried out. If the absolute value of the correlation coefficient between the environmental variables is greater than 0.8, it indicates that there is a significant correlation between the two variables, and the variables need to be screened. We use the SDMtune package to compare relevant variables and remove variables that contribute relatively little to the model, resulting in a set of non-collinear variables (Supplementary Fig. S2)49.

Second, we fit a model that contains variables that are not significantly correlated after screening. We use the reduceVar function in the SDMtune package to further remove variables with a low contribution to the model and avoid model overfitting49. Finally, we obtained the variable composition to fit the final model based on the selection of data-driven variables (Fig. 3).

Feature combination (FC) is one of the hyperparameters that affect the modeling accuracy of MaxEnt method63. Another important hyperparameter is regularization multiplier (RM)63. Screening these two parameters can significantly improve the accuracy of model prediction64. The prediction of the potential distribution of wild animals is prone to overfitting, which limits the mobility of the model to another species28. Many studies have created a new MaxEnt model after optimization by setting the FC and RM in the training process25,34. Therefore, the necessity of optimizing the hyperparameters of the model has been verified. For the fitting of the maxent model, this step is indispensable.

We use the gridSearch function in the SDMtune package to fit MaxEnt models on 468 models with different parameter groups49. This includes the regularization multiplier from 0.1 to 4 and 5 kinds of feature combination of optimal permutations (Supplementary Table S2).The True Skill Statistic (TSS) is used to evaluate the accuracy of the model65. TSS is more accurate than AICc to measure the accuracy of the model65. Finally, the combination of parameters with the largest test TSS is selected as the model parameter of the final model.

The jackknife method is a reliable cross-validation scheme for evaluating the reliability of the model66. We use this method to plot the Variable Jackknife test figure to show the importance of environmental variables to species distribution. We performed the jackknife test based on the TSS value as the standard of model performance. Furthermore, the relative importance of a variable to the model is calculated. The accuracy of the model was tested using the k-fold cross-validation method, and the accuracy of the model was evaluated by comparing the values and TSS65,67. At the same time, the training AUC and test AUC of the final model are calculated to verify the accuracy of the model again.

We use the average of the k-fold cross-validation results as the prediction result. The result of the MaxEnt model is the probability of species occurrence (from 0 to 1). We select Equal training sensitivity and specificity to determine the threshold for distinguishing between appropriate and inappropriate regions, and binarize the average fitness68. We convert the suitable areas predicted by the model into surface layers in ArcGIS and calculate the area of each patch. Because too small patches are meaningless for the survival of leopards, we removed patches with an area of less than 10 km2 based on the leopard’s range of activity22. Too small, isolated patches are meaningless for the protection of leopard populations. The remaining patches larger than 10 square kilometers were used to count the potential suitable habitat area and the number of potential suitable habitat patches of North China leopard.

Impact of climate change on the distribution of suitable habitats for North China leopard

We mapped the suitable habitat prediction maps under four SSPs scenarios in 2030 and 2050 to clarify how different carbon emission levels at the carbon peak and carbon neutralization time node affect the distribution of suitable habitats. In order to further clarify the impact of climate change on suitable habitats, the area of suitable habitats and the number of patches in current and future scenarios were statistically analyzed to quantify habitat changes. Through the variable response curve and the importance of each variable to the model, it is clear which variables are important factors affecting the suitable habitat distribution of North China leopard under different carbon emission scenarios. Finally, we analyzed the changing trends of environmental variables under different carbon emission scenarios in the study area to analyze how carbon emissions affect the suitable habitat distribution of North China leopards.

All statistical analysis of this study was carried out in r software69, using ggplot2 for graphics drawing70, and ArcGIS Pro for data spatialization processing.

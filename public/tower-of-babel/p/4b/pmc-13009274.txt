Spatial joint modelling of multivariate longitudinal outcomes and cure proportion using latent Gaussian model with application to dataset on HIV/AIDS patients
Authors: Aniefiok Henry Ekong, Olayini Matthew Olayiwola, Ganiyu Abayomi Dawodu, Idowu Ademola Osinuga
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSurvival analysis has seen in recent times more of joint modelling of longitudinal and survival data using approximate hierarchical Bayesian method. This study modelled jointly multivariate and cure proportion with spatial variation using latent Gaussian models (LGMs). Spline specification was used to capture nonlinear trajectories of the multivariate longitudinal biomarkers in the LGM paradigm. Spatial dependence was assumed for neighbouring locations with an improper multivariate conditionally autoregressive prior on the spatial random effects, while inverse-Wishart prior was assumed for the covariance matrix of the random effects and Gaussian priors for the fixed effects. The penalised complexity prior was assumed for the Weibull shape parameters of the baseline hazard function for the event-time and binomial distribution for latent cure classification variable. Posterior distributions were evaluated using INLA. The study was applied to longitudinal and survival HIV/AIDS patients datasets. The random spatial variability was seen to be significant for both biomarkers, random intercept for BMI and cure probabilities. The full conditional distribution of latent variable gave a predicted cure proportion of 61%. The susceptible group on the hand, had a full conditional distribution of latent incidence variable predicting susceptible proportion of 39%. Flexibility of LGM for Bayesian hierarchical was demonstrated easily for multivariate joint model with spatial cure proportion.Keywords: Spatial, cure proportion; Laplace approximation; Multivariate; Shared random effect; Nonlinear trajectorySubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Diseases, Mathematics and computing, Medical research, Risk factors

Introduction

Classical survival analysis models such as Cox proportional hazard (PH) model1 and accelerated failure time (AFT)2 model are based on the assumption that given enough follow-up time, every individual will eventually encounter the event otherwise be censored within experiment or follow-up period. Usually in most real settings, it is possible that some individuals may not encounter the event, even with a follow-up time of several decades. For example, in HIV/AIDS trials, availability of effective treatments, has made it possible that patients can live long, healthy lives and eventually die as a result of old age or other age-related ailments. Based on this, one cannot say the patients have experience the event from HIV/AIDS, hence it can be said that such patients are “cured”. In the case of possibility of cured patients, the population is assumed to be made up of two groups: the susceptible group who will one day encounter the event, and the cured group who may not encounter the event in a long time. Cancer trials are also cases where there is possibility of cured patients when cancer is removed as a result of treatment, especially if the cancer was detected at early stages3. Identifiability has been noted to be one of the problems with cure models as a result of lack of censoring before the close of the follow-up period. So the influence of covariates on failure time is confounded by that on mixture probability, resulting in unstable estimates. A plausible remedy is including a time-dependent information that may have some association with the survival process4. This association may be derived from longitudinal measurements taken simultaneously as the survival measurements.

Joint modeling has emerged as a widely used method for data analysis in many applicable contexts where time-to-event and longitudinal outcomes are simultaneously observed. A thorough assessment of the literature on the application of joint models including multiple event times per subject was provided by Hickey et al.5. A survey of advancements in Bayesian joint models, encompassing publications published to July 2019, was presented by Alsefri et al.6. Even with a frequentist approach, joint modeling with spatial components is uncommon, according to the literature. According to the literature, Martins et al.7,8 and Rappl et al.9 dealt with Bayesian joint modeling with a spatial component. Martins et al.8 also included a survival model with spatial long-term survivors, and they evaluated the posterior distribution using Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC). The piecewise additive strategy employed by Rappl et al.9 had the drawback of requiring data augmentation, which is a step in the time-to-event process. The dataset is artificially enlarged by this augmentation, which results in lengthier or impractical runtimes when the original data is huge. Due to the huge number of parameters that required to be estimated, the delayed convergence of Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods was a limitation of the Bayesian strategy utilized by Martins et al.7,8. Martins et al. 8 came to the conclusion that their modeling was computationally intensive and time-consuming, which suggested that fewer models could be easily fitted, hence decreasing the statistical analysis’s flexibility.

Notwithstanding the computational burden, a large number of publications on Bayesian joint modeling use MCMC sampling-based inference, which has been demonstrated to be restricted to relatively small samples and model specifications for joint modeling10. Rue et al.11 introduced the approximate Bayesian approach, the integrated nested Laplace approximation (INLA), which is gradually becoming more popular as an alternative to MCMC for joint modeling; see10,12–19. In contrast to the joint posterior distribution of the model parameters as assessed in MCMC, the INLA method concentrates on estimating the posterior marginals of the model parameters20. Among INLA’s advantages are its simplicity in implementing hierarchical models and its quick computation times for fitting various models21,22. This paper expanded on the joint model in Ekong et al.18 to take into consideration spatial variation and nonlinear multivariate longitudinal trajectories.

Materials and methods

Longitudinal and survival HIV/AIDS patients’ dataset

Given that the i-th patient (i = 1, …, N) from the k-th geographic location having nk patients, has sample observations from the p-th longitudinal variable as yipk (p = 1 for CD4 count and 2 for BMI for the motivating dataset). Let Tik be the observed event time which might be right censored. At the end of the follow-up, let Zik be the latent variable that determines whether the patient had been cured or not. The event indicator is presented as δik = 1 if the event is witnessed and δik = 0 if it is censored. We note that any patient whose survival time is monitored at a specific moment is included in the group of patients who have not been cured.

The observed data for the i-th patient without any covariate is Dik = { yipk, Tik, δik, Zik }. The Dik ‘s are assumed to be independent across patients, i.e., the disease process evolves independently for each patient. We also assume that Tik and yipk are conditionally independent given the independent variables and a set of unobserved patient-level and location-level random effects.

The dataset used in this study was a secondary, retrospective dataset from a PhD study by investigator, Ogunmiluyi23, on the effect of ready-to-eat-therapeutic-snack on the nutritional status of children living with HIV/AIDS, carried out in 2023 in Abeokuta, Ogun state, Nigeria. The study obtained informed consent from parents and/or guardians of participating children and interventions, with data collection followed relevant guidelines and regulations. Protocol approval was obtained from the licensing committee - the Health Research Ethical Committee (HREC) of Federal Medical Centre Abeokuta, Ogun state, Nigeria. The longitudinal biomarkers used for this paper included, CD4 counts and body mass index (BMI), including other covariates such as gender, age and local government area (LGA) of residence.

In summary, from Tables 3 and 4, younger female patients had higher predicted cure proportions compared to male patients and older. Discussion

The intervention of TS seemed not to have significant effect on the BMI of patients and the incidence model, however it was significant for CD4 count and latency model. The associations between the mixture survival component and CD4 profile was negative and significant, indicating that increased CD4 count resulted in less risk in loghazard of survival event. The longitudinal trajectories for both biomarkers as modelled using spline specification showed a significant posterior mean effect, indicating that spline specification does fit the biomarkers trajectories appreciably.

From the model coefficients for the survival components, there seemed to be significant variation of the biomarkers and mixture survival effect on the various covariates considered in the study. The incidence model coefficients revealed that sex was a major covariate determine the incidence of cure or susceptible for the dataset, with more of female patients in the cure group, whereas for the age groups, the lowest level 2–4 years were more in the cure group compared to other age groups. The latency model showed that the failure event was depended on the age group as each level was significant and whereas the probability of the failure event was higher for the higher age group levels, as a result of the positive sign in the coefficients. The probability of the event was lower in the lower age group levels.

The sex of patients and age were the most significant covariates to predicating cure probabilities, whether or not they received the therapeutic snack (TS). For the male patients, the number of patients with higher cure probabilities was highest in patients of ages 4 years and below. For the female patients there were cases of patients in the age ranges of 8 to 10 years with mean cure probabilities above 60%. The susceptible group on the hand, had a full conditional distribution of latent incidence predicting proportion of 39%. Female patients had higher cure probabilities than male patients and the cure probabilities also were highest in younger patients as expected for both male and female patients. It was also seen that the intervention of TS had no significant effect on these probabilities.

The cure probabilities was highest in Remo LGA, followed by those in Odeda and Yewa South LGAs, while the least values were predicted for patients in Ewekoro LGA. Considering the cure group, the highest weighted mean of the cure probabilities were found for patients residing in Remo, and then scattered across the state, while the least were found for patients residing in Ewekoro and Odogbolu LGAs in that order. Examination of the susceptible group revealed that the patients residing in Ipokia LGA had the least weighted mean for cure probabilities. The patients with the highest values were residents of Odeda LGA, followed by those resident in Odogbolu LGAs in that order.

Conclusion

As an extension of the univariate approach described in Ekong et al.18 this paper introduced the spatial joint modeling of multivariate longitudinal biomarkers and mixture cure survival under shared random effect using latent Gaussian modeling approach. The posterior distribution of the resulting Bayesian modeling was evaluated using deterministic approximate Bayesian inference of Laplace approximation11. A spline specification was used for the longitudinal biomarkers to capture the nonlinear trajectories, and Lázaro et al.13 used specifications for the survival cure component, which used latent indicators in the inferential process to classify patients into susceptible and cured groups based on a latent indicator variable. We assumed spatial dependence between neighboring places in order to represent the spatial variability using a shared unknown random effect across patients residing in the same geographic region. It is believed that an intrinsic conditionally autoregressive (IMCAR) prior governs the spatial effects24. In the spatial multivariate joint cure modeling approach, the joint model was expressed as a hierarchical structure that fit the structure of latent Gaussian models under the Bayesian paradigm. The method of this study is limited to settings where models can be expressed in the hierarchical form of LGMs. The method was applied to a dataset comprising patients of children ages, this limits the conclusions of the results to this demography. However, the method can be applied to datasets comprising wider range of ages. While the survival event in this study was a single outcome, other extension for the survival part could be competing risk and semi-competing risks. Although MCMC is computational intensive with unguaranteed convergence and has high correlation among simulated values especially for large datasets, INLA is less accurate than MCMC in small sample sizes26.

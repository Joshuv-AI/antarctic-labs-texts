Machine learning constructs a ferroptosis related signature for predicting prognosis and drug sensitivity in lung cancer
Authors: Zihao Li, Yibing Chen, Benxin Hou, Yanjun Mi, Chunlin Fu, Zhaoyang Han, Qing Tang, Weihong Sun, Qing Xia, Yuan Liao, Zhengzhi Zou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeFerroptosis is a novel form of iron-dependent programmed cell death that is associated with the progression of various tumors and cancer treatment responses. However, its role in the clinical treatment of lung cancer, particularly in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) and lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC), remains poorly understood. This study aims to explore the prognostic value of ferroptosis-related genes in lung cancer and establish a reliable predictive model.MethodsWe collected the GSE4573, TCGA-LUAD, and TCGA-LUSC datasets, comprising a total of 1,271 samples and a ferroptosis gene set of 717 genes. Weighted gene coexpression network analysis (WGCNA) was used to identify ferroptosis-related markers, followed by the application of 101 machine learning algorithms combining 10 different approaches to develop a ferroptosis-related signature (FRS) for lung cancer prognosis.ResultsThe FRS demonstrated superior performance in predicting the survival of lung cancer patients, significantly outperforming traditional TNM and American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging systems. External validation using the GSE13213 dataset also confirmed its robustness. Furthermore, the low-risk group exhibited higher immune microenvironment scores, suggesting a more active anti-tumor immune response, while the high-risk group showed elevated cell proliferation, migration, T-cell exclusion, and TIDE scores, indicating a more aggressive tumor phenotype. Additionally, the low-risk group demonstrated higher sensitivity to multiple drugs, including cisplatin, cyclophosphamide, paclitaxel, erlotinib, Niraparib, Rapamycin, Fulvestrant, and Venetoclax, highlighting its potential for guiding personalized treatment strategies.ConclusionThe FRS represents a powerful and clinically relevant tool for predicting the survival of lung cancer patients, offering new insights into personalized treatment and therapeutic decision-making.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s13402-025-01121-1.

Introduction

Lung cancer is one of the most common malignancies worldwide and accounts for a significant proportion of cancer-related deaths [1]. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), including lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) and lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC), accounts for approximately 85% of lung cancer [2]. Despite advancements in targeted therapies and immunotherapies, the 5-year survival rate remains low in patients with advanced-stage lung cancer, underscoring the need for predicting lung cancer prognosis and drug sensitivity [3]. Despite the emergence of models for predicting patient prognosis and drug sensitivity, the sensitivity and specificity of these models remain suboptimal. Therefore, there is an urgent need for an improved predictive model system.

Ferroptosis, a form of iron-dependent, non-apoptotic cell death, has garnered considerable attention as a potential therapeutic target in many cancer types [4, 5]. Ferroptosis is characterized by the iron-dependent increase in lipid peroxides and reactive oxygen species (ROS), and is initiated by the inactivation of glutathione peroxidase (GPX) and the subsequent rupture of the cell membrane [6, 7]. Recent evidence suggests that ferroptosis plays a significant role in the development and progression of lung cancer, particularly in NSCLC [8]. In addition, ferroptosis influences the tumor immune microenvironment (TME) by modulating immune cell activity and tumor immune evasion, demonstrating its potential as a therapeutic target [9].

The cross-talk between cancer cells and the TME plays a vital role in tumor progression, metastasis, and drug resistance [10]. Hence, understanding the interplay between tumor cells ferroptosis and the TME is crucial for developing more effective treatment strategies for lung cancer [11]. Nevertheless, current clinical staging systems, such as TNM and AJCC, have limitations in accurately predicting patient outcomes and treatment responses. These systems primarily rely on tumor size, lymph node involvement, and distant metastasis, yet they do not fully capture the molecular heterogeneity and immune landscape of tumors [12]. As a result, patients with the same clinical stage may exhibit significantly different survival rates and treatment outcomes, emphasizing the need for more precise biomarkers to complement traditional staging methods [13]. While immunotherapy has shown promise in treating advanced tumors, the response of lung cancer to immunotherapy remains limited [14]. Current clinical biomarkers, such as PD-L1 expression and tumor mutational burden (TMB), have been used to predict immunotherapy benefits, but their predictive accuracy is still suboptimal [15] Therefore, there is an urgent need to identify novel biomarkers to improve patient stratification and treatment outcomes.

In response to these challenges, we developed a ferroptosis-related signature (FRS) using machine learning algorithms integrated with data from four independent public datasets. This signature, based on key ferroptosis-related genes, was designed to predict clinical outcomes and immunotherapy benefits in lung cancer patients. Our findings indicate that FRS could serve as a valuable tool for identifying patients who may benefit from specific treatments, thereby improving patient stratification and treatment outcomes.

Materials and methods

Datasets acquisition and processing

The ferroptosis gene sets, including driver (n = 369) and suppressor (n = 348), were obtained from the FerrDb V2 database [16]. Bulk RNA-seq data for lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD, n = 589) and lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC, n = 552) were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA). For additional independent cohorts, the LUSC dataset GSE4573 (n = 130) and LUAD dataset GSE13213 (n = 117) were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database [17]. The TCGA-LUSC, TCGA-LUAD, and GSE4573 datasets were used as the training set, while the GSE13213 dataset served as the external validation set. All datasets (TCGA-LUAD, TCGA-LUSC, GSE4573, and GSE13213) encompassing complete OS information were used for the construction and validation of our signature. The RNA-seq raw read count from the TCGA database was converted to transcripts per kilobase million (TPM). The GSE4573 and GSE13213 datasets were obtained from the GEO database, profiled on the Affymetrix GPL96 (Human Genome U133A Array) and Agilent GPL6480 (Whole Human Genome Microarray 4 × 44 K G4112F, Probe Name version) platforms, respectively.

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)

WGCNA was performed on the GSE4573 dataset to identify key modules, and the gene sets within these modules were assessed for their correlation with ferroptosis [18, 19]. WGCNA is a robust method to identify gene sets of interest from thousands of the most varied genes and elucidating correlation analysis with phenotypes. In this analysis, the soft threshold power was set to 3 (no scale R2 = 0.85) [18], which could filter out weak correlations between genes in the adjacency matrix and provided an optimal power value for coexpression network construction. To cluster the most representative genes, module eigengenes (MEs) and the Pearson’s correlation coefficient was employed to calculate the correlation. The module eigengene, calculated as the leading principal component of gene expression patterns within each coexpression module, served as the summarized expression profile for subsequent analyses [19]. Those module genes that exhibited the significant positive correlation (the strongest correlation) with ferroptosis were defined as ferroptosis-related genes.

Machine learning based FRS

To develop an accurate and stable prognostic FRS for lung cancer, we performed a comprehensive machine learning analysis incorporating Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV) across 101 algorithms. These algorithms included random survival forests [20], elastic networks [21], Lasso regression [22], Ridge regression, stepwise Cox regression, CoxBoost [23], Cox partial least squares regression, supervised principal component analysis, generalized boosted regression modeling, and survival support vector machines. The FRS generation process was as follows: Two datasets are used for training, while the third is used for testing, the steps included (1) merging the two training sets into a combined training set; (2) aligning and preprocessing the data in the combined training set, such as removing blank rows; (3) training each model based on a predefined model list and extracting its basic data; and (4) calculating the C-index for each model on the test dataset, with the model exhibiting the highest average C-index selected as the optimal FRS.

Evaluation of the performance of FRS

Lung cancer cases were stratified into high-risk and low-risk groups based on the median risk score. Overall survival curves were generated using the “survival” R package [24]. To evaluate the value of FRS in predicting the clinical outcome of lung cancer, we then drew Time ROC curve and clinical ROC curve using “timeROC” package [25]. Univariate and multivariate cox analysis were conducted to identify the prognostic risk factors [26] in lung cancer. A nomogram was constructed based on the FRS and clinical characteristics using the R packages “rms”, “nomogramEx”, and “regplot” [27].

Tumor immune microenvironment and genetic mutation landscape

XCell algorithm [28] was used to calculate the immune microenvironment related score of each lung cancer case. The levels of infiltrating immune cells in each lung cancer case were estimated using the immunedeconv R package [29], which integrates seven computational methods (CIBERSORT, MCPcounter, QUANTISEQ, XCELL, CIBERSORT-ABS, TIMER, and EPIC) for deconvoluting immune cell fractions from bulk RNA-seq data. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) [30] was performed to explore the biological functions correlated with high and low risk group.

Evaluation of the performance of FRS in predicting drug sensitivity

TIDE scores for each lung cancer patients were obtained from the TIDE database [31] (http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu). Based on the data of Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) [32] (https://www.cancerrxgene.org/), we then calculated the half maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) value of common chemotherapy and targeted drugs by “oncoPredict” package.

Cell lines and cell culture conditions

WI-38, BEAS-2B, HFL-1, A549, PC9, H1299, H460 and HCC827 cell lines were purchased from The Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences. WI-38, HFL-1, A549, PC9, H1299, and H460 cells were cultured in DMEM basal medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 1,000 U/mL penicillin and 100 µg/mL streptomycin. BEAS-2B cells were cultured in BEBM basal medium supplemented with 10% FBS, 1,000 U/mL penicillin and 100 µg/mL streptomycin. HCC827 cells were maintained in RPMI 1640 supplemented with 10% FBS, 1,000 U/mL penicillin and 100 µg/mL streptomycin. The cells were cultured with 5% CO2 at 37 ℃ in a humidified incubator [33].

RNA extraction and quantitative RT-PCR analysis

Ten pairs of NSCLC tumor and adjacent normal tissues were obtained from the First Affiliated Hospital of Xiamen University between 2015 and 2017. This study was approved by the Clinical Ethics Review Board at South China Normal University and Xiamen University, and written informed consents were from all patients at their recruitment time (SCNU-BIP-2024-024). Total RNA was extracted from cells using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, United States) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Equal amounts of RNA samples were used for cDNA synthesis with a RevertAid First Strand cDNA synthesis kit (Thermo). SYBR Green-based qRT-PCR on a 7900HT fast real-time PCR system (Applied Biosystems/Life Technologies, Waltham, United States) was performed [34]. The relative mRNA expression levels were calculated by the 2–ΔΔCt method with normalization to ACTB; the PCR primers are listed in Supplementary Table 1.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.2.1) [35]. Continuous variables were compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test or Student’s t-test [36], as appropriate. Bivariate associations among continuous variables were statistically evaluated via Pearson’s correlation coefficients. Differences in Kaplan-Meier survival curves were evaluated using the two-sided log-rank test [24].

Results

The experimental design of the present study is summarized in the flowchart (Fig. 1). In this analysis, the soft threshold power was set to 3 (no scale R2 = 0.85), which established an optimal power value for coexpression network construction (Fig. 2A). Then, 10 modules were identified, as indicated by different colours (Fig. 2B). The module eigengene, calculated as the leading principal component of gene expression patterns within each coexpression module, served as the summarized expression profile for subsequent analyses [19]. The heatmap visualized interconnections among coexpression modules (Fig. 2C). We calculated the correlation between these 10 modules gene sets and the ferroptosis driver and suppressor gene sets (Fig. 2D) [37]. To identify the genes most closely associated with ferroptosis, we selected the red module with the highest correlation for further analysis (Supplementary Table 2). The red module exhibited a robust correlation (cor = 0.95) between gene significance (GS) and module membership (MM) (Fig. 2E) [18]. The red module contained 283 ferroptosis-related genes (Supplementary Table 3). Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was performed on these genes, and the functional annotations were significantly associated with ferroptosis and prognosis (Fig. 2F) [38].

Integrative machine learning algorithms constructed a prognostic FRS

Integrative machine learning algorithms were employed to construct a robust prognostic FRS. Initially, 283 ferroptosis-related genes were subjected to univariate Cox analysis, identifying 56 potential prognostic biomarkers significantly associated with ferroptosis-related pathways (Fig. 3B). These 56 biomarkers were subsequently integrated into a comprehensive machine learning framework to develop a precise and reliable prognostic FRS. Through this process, a total of 101 prognostic models were generated (Fig. 3A). The performance of each model was evaluated using the C-index across the TCGA-LUAD, TCGA-LUCS, and GSE4573 cohorts. The model constructed using the StepCox and RSF (Random Survival Forest) methods emerged as the optimal FRS, achieving the highest average C-index of 0.676 (Fig. 3B). Perform protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis on these 56 genes (Fig. 3C) to illustrate their interrelationships [39], and conduct GO enrichment analysis to reveal prognostic-related functions (Fig. 3D). Notably, among the 56 genes included in the final FRS, 23 (ADH7, AKR1C1, ALDH3A1, ANXA10, BLVRB, CBR3, CNTNAP2, COL9A2, CREG1, CYP26A1, DCBLD2, DUSP14, DYNLT3, GPX2, HAL, PPIF, PTPRD, TLE2, TXN, VDAC2, ZNF268, ZNF813, ZNF814) were identified as ferroptosis-related genes based on univariate Cox analysis (Fig. 3E). This highlights the critical role of ferroptosis in the prognostic stratification of lung cancer.

Evaluation of the performance of FRS

In the TCGA-LUSC, TCGA-LUAD, and GSE4573 cohorts, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses indicated that the risk score based on FRS was an independent prognostic factor for lung cancer (Fig. 4A, B). Patients were divided into high and low groups based on the FRS score, and survival analysis was performed with a survival scatter plot drawn (Fig. 4C). Subsequently, considering FRS and clinical stage, we created a survival prediction nomogram (Fig. 4D). According to the results, the calibration curves had rather good predictive value for the OS rates over the next one, three, and five years in the future (Fig. 4E). To further evaluate the FRS, we conducted a direct comparative analysis between our FRS and established ferroptosis prognostic models, including the FRS1 [40], FRS2 [41], FRS3 [42], FRS4 [43], FRS5 [44]. Our FRS demonstrated superior discriminative power at 1-year follow-up, securing rank 1 in TCGA-LUAD, TCGA-LUSC, and GSE13213, and rank 2 in GSE4573 (mean rank: 1.25/6). This advantage persisted at 3-year, achieving ranks 2, 1, 3, and 1 respectively (mean rank: 1.75/6). While maintaining competitive 5-year accuracy (ranks 3, 4, 4, 1; mean rank: 3.0/6), it notably secured the top position in GSE13213. Collectively, Our FRS exhibited statistically significant overall superiority over all comparators (Fig. 4F). Patients in the high-risk group had significantly dismal overall survival (OS) relative to the low-risk group in the TCGA-LUSC and TCGA-LUAD training dataset and GSE13213 external validation datasets (all P < 0.05). The meta-cohort (integrated from the GSE4573, TCGA-LUAD, and TCGA-LUSC cohorts) that combined all samples also showed the same trend (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5A-D). Notably, GSE4573 cohorts were not statistically significant, possibly due to their small sample size (Supplementary Fig. 1). ROC analysis measured the discrimination of FRS, with 1-, 3-, and 5-year AUCs of 0.73, 0.62, and 0.60 in TCGA-LUAD (Fig. 5E); 0.61, 0.63, and 0.59 in TCGA-LUSC (Fig. 5F); 0.64, 0.62, and 0.57 in GSE4573 (Fig. 5G); 0.94, 0.82, and 0.80 in GSE13213 (Fig. 5H), respectively. The elevated AUC in the GSE13213 cohort (1-year: 0.94) correlates with its significantly higher proportion of stage I patients (67.2% vs. 46–57% in other cohorts), indicating superior performance of our signature specifically in early-stage disease contexts (Supplementary Fig. 2). A previous study reported that clinical characteristics (e.g. AJCC stage) was also used to assess the prognosis of cancer in clinical practice. Therefore, we compared the performance of FRS with other clinical in predicting prognosis. For predicting the 3-year survival of lung cancer patients, the FRS outperformed other clinical variables, including age, gender, smoking history, T stage, N stage, M stage, and AJCC stage. The accuracy of the FRS was the highest in GSE4573 (Fig. 5K) and GSE13213 (Fig. 5L), and the second highest in TCGA-LUAD (Fig. 5I) and TCGA-LUSC (Fig. 5J).

The correlation between FRS and TME in lung cancer

Patients in the low-risk group exhibited significantly higher immune scores, microenvironment scores, and stromal scores compared to those in the high-risk group (Fig. 6A). A significant correlation was observed between the risk score and immune cells or immune-related scores (Fig. 6B) [29]. Further analysis revealed that lung cancer patients with high-risk scores exhibited significantly higher scores of cell proliferation (Fig. 6C) [45] and migration (Fig. 6D) [46] in the merged dataset (integrated from the GSE4573, TCGA-LUAD, and TCGA-LUSC cohorts), as well as in the individual TCGA-LUAD and TCGA-LUSC datasets. Additionally, patients in the low-risk group exhibited significantly higher CTLA4 scores (Fig. 6E) [47], while those in the high-risk group showed elevated scores for T Cell Exclusion (Fig. 6F) [31] and TIDE (Fig. 6G).

FRS-based treatment strategy for lung cancer

Based on the above analysis, we found that lung cancer patients in the low-risk group exhibited better responses to immunotherapy. We further conducted drug sensitivity analysis across different risk groups, assessing the differences in IC50 values of common chemotherapy (Fig. 7A-C) and targeted therapy drugs (Fig. 7D-H) between the groups [32]. Our results revealed that low risk scores were associated with lower IC50 values for Cisplatin, Cyclophosphamide, Paclitaxel, Erlotinib, Niraparib, Rapamycin, Fulvestrant, and Venetoclax in lung cancer patients. These findings provide a novel perspective for identifying potential drug candidates and optimizing therapeutic strategies [48].

The levels of eight FRS genes in normal vs. tumor tissues and cells

In the 23 FRS genes, previous studies have reported the expression of 15 genes in NSCLC. However, the levels of these eight genes HAL, CNTNAP2, CREG1, ANXA10, DYNLT3, TLE2, ZNF813, ZNF814 have not been reported in NSCLC. Therefore, by RT-PCR, we detected the expression of these eight genes in both NSCLC tumor and adjacent non-tumor tissues, as well as in normal and tumor-derived cell lines. We found that only HAL and ZNF814 genes were significantly downregulated expression in NSCLC tumor tissues relative to adjacent non-tumor tissues (Fig. 8A-H). The results from cell lines showed that the levels of HAL, TLE2 and ZNF814 genes were reduced in lung cancer cell lines compared to normal lung cell lines, while CNTNAP2 level was increased in lung cancer cells (Fig. 8I-J). In addition, transcriptomic analysis of TCGA cohorts substantiated these patterns: HAL showed significant suppression in LUSC tumors versus normal tissues; CNTNAP2 exhibited marked overexpression in LUSC malignancies; TLE2 demonstrated consistent downregulation in both LUAD and LUSC tumor groups compared to normal counterparts (Supplementary Fig. 3). These cross-platform validations reinforce the robustness of our experimental findings.

Discussion

The present study constructed a ferroptosis-related signature (FRS) to predict prognosis and therapeutic response in lung cancer patients. By integrating multi-omics datasets and employing bioinformatics approaches [18, 49, 50], we established a 23-gene FRS (ADH7, AKR1C1, ALDH3A1, ANXA10, BLVRB, CBR3, CNTNAP2, COL9A2, CREG1, CYP26A1, DCBLD2, DUSP14, DYNLT3, GPX2, HAL, PPIF, PTPRD, TLE2, TXN, VDAC2, ZNF268, ZNF813, ZNF814) that stratified patients into high- and low-risk groups with distinct clinical outcomes. This signature demonstrated robust prognostic value and provided insights into TME heterogeneity and drug sensitivity [10]. The distinct advantage of ferroptosis-related genes over apoptosis- or necroptosis-associated genes stems from their unique dual roles in regulating lipid peroxidation dynamics and immunometabolic crosstalk. Unlike apoptosis (caspase-mediated programmed cell death) or necroptosis (inflammatory cell death with cytokine release) [51], ferroptosis is driven by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation, generating oxidized phospholipids such as 4-hydroxynonenal, which act as damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) to activate dendritic cells and enhance tumor antigen presentation [52]. This mechanistic distinction explains why FRS genes like GPX2 and TXN not only regulate redox homeostasis but also modulate PD-L1 expression and CD8 + T-cell infiltration through NRF2 signaling [6, 53]. In contrast, classical apoptosis effectors (e.g., BCL2, CASP3) and necroptosis mediators (e.g., RIPK1, RIPK3) are functionally restricted to cell death execution without systemic immunomodulatory effects [51]. Notably, the FRS is applicable to both LUAD and LUSC, overcoming the limitations of subtype-specific models and thereby enhancing its clinical utility [2]. Compared to our previous work in renal cell carcinoma [54], which developed a ferroptosis-related prognostic model (10 lipid metabolism genes) through Lasso regression and Cox regression analyses on a single TCGA-KIRC cohort, the current study represents a significant methodological advancement. The present FRS integrates transcriptomic data from four independent cohorts (TCGA-LUAD, TCGA-LUSC, GSE4573, and GSE13213) and employs an ensemble machine learning framework encompassing 101 algorithm combinations. This multi-dataset validation strategy combined with algorithmic optimization substantially enhances model generalizability compared to single-cohort approaches.

The 23-gene FRS effectively predicted survival across TCGA-LUAD, TCGA-LUSC, GSE4573, and GSE13213 cohorts. High-risk patients exhibited significantly poorer overall survival (OS), validated by time-dependent ROC analysis with AUC values up to 0.94 for 1-year survival in GSE13213 [25]. The FRS outperformed traditional clinical variables (e.g., AJCC stage) in predictive accuracy, highlighting its potential as a standalone prognostic tool [55]. Importantly, our results linking microenvironment heterogeneity to clinical outcomes align with recent findings underscoring the integral role of the TME in therapeutic resistance across cancer types [56]. The functional convergence of FRS genes spans glutathione metabolism (GPX2, CBR3), iron recycling (BLVRB), and xenobiotic detoxification (CYP26A1), forming an integrated regulatory network that concurrently modulates chemotherapeutic response and immune surveillance. For instance, GPX2 overexpression in low-risk patients confers resistance to cisplatin by scavenging ROS while preserving extracellular cysteine pools essential for CD8 + T-cell function [8, 53]. This dual regulatory capacity establishes ferroptosis genes as pivotal nodes bridging between cell death pathways and therapeutic response [50]. Notably, the functional role of GPX2 in ferroptosis regulation within NSCLC is mechanistically established. As demonstrated by Wu et al. [57], directly influencing cisplatin sensitivity through ferroptosis modulation. Similarly, TXN’s anti-ferroptosis activity in LUAD is mediated via thioredoxin-dependent PP1F activation [58]. These findings validate the biological coherence of FRS genes and substantiate our signature’s capacity to capture established ferroptosis pathways in lung cancer. Recent studies further highlight the complexity of stromal-immune interaction within the TME that can independently dictate drug efficacy, highlighting the contextual importance of the microenvironment in our FRS stratification [59]. In contrast, necroptosis-related genes such as MLKL predominantly induce pro-inflammatory cytokine storms without influencing drug transporter activity (e.g., ABCB1) or immune checkpoint expression [51], thereby limiting their prognostic utility. The low-risk group exhibited an immunologically active TME with predominant anti-tumor immunity and a pro-inflammatory state, as evidenced by elevated CTLA4 expression linked to adaptive immune suppression and Treg-mediated immune checkpoint activation [47]. ALOX15, a key ferroptosis regulator, is linked to immune infiltration in cervical cancer in our previous study [60]. However, ALOX15 was not included in our FRS in this study. This indicates that distinct ferroptosis-related genes exert divergent immune-regulatory functions in different tumor types. Notably, our earlier work demonstrated that ALOX15 was a tumor suppressor gene, aligning with the immune-active phenotype observed in low-risk FRS patients. This suggested potential crosstalk between FRS genes and ALOX15-related immune pathways, warranting further investigation. In contrast, high-risk patients displayed higher T-cell exclusion and TIDE scores, indicative of immune evasion [31]. This observed immunosuppressive microenvironment is a recognized facilitator of tumor progression through mechanisms such as T-cell exhaustion and myeloid-derived suppressor cell recruitment, as documented in recent comprehensive analyses of TME dynamics [61]. These findings aligned with the role of ferroptosis in modulating anti-tumor immunity [8, 9] and supported the rationale for combining ferroptosis inducers with immunotherapy [14]. Low-risk patients exhibited greater sensitivity to chemotherapy (e.g., Cisplatin, Paclitaxel) and targeted therapies (e.g., Erlotinib, Venetoclax). This positions the FRS as a biomarker for personalized therapy selection, while high-risk patients may benefit from alternative strategies targeting ferroptosis resistance pathways [11].

This study develops the first cross-subtype ferroptosis signature encompassing LUAD and LUSC, addressing a critical gap in existing prognostic tools restricted to single histological subtypes. By linking FRS to TME heterogeneity and drug response, our findings provide a framework for mechanistically guided combination therapies. Clinically, the 23-gene model offers a practical tool for risk stratification in routine molecular profiling, potentially guiding immunotherapy eligibility and chemotherapy selection. The retrospective design and lack of mechanistic validation, while limitations common to computational studies, do not diminish the translational relevance of these findings. Prospective validation in clinical trials and functional studies on key FRS components (e.g., GPX2, TXN) are warranted to confirm their roles in ferroptosis-immune crosstalk. Collectively, this multi-omics approach exemplifies how machine learning can distill complex biological processes into actionable clinical biomarkers, advancing precision oncology in lung cancer.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we developed a 23-gene ferroptosis-related signature (FRS) that predicts prognosis, immune activity, and drug sensitivity in lung cancer. The FRS stratified patients into high- and low-risk groups, with the former showing poorer survival, immune evasion, and therapy resistance, while the latter exhibited enhanced immune infiltration and drug sensitivity. The distinct TME landscapes captured by our signature exemplify the increasing recognition of the microenvironment’s overarching influence on clinical phenotypes [56, 59, 61]. Its applicability to both LUAD and LUSC broadens its clinical relevance beyond subtype-specific models. The FRS outperformed traditional staging systems, offering a promising tool for personalized treatment. Future studies should validate its utility prospectively and explore mechanisms linking ferroptosis to TME modulation.

Acute Left Heart Failure in Twin Pregnancies: Clinical Characteristics and Outcomes—A 10‑Year Retrospective Analysis of 24 Cases
Authors: Qianqian Xiang, Yuan Wei, Yangyu Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThis retrospective study evaluated the clinical characteristics, management, and maternal‐neonatal outcomes of 24 twin pregnancies complicated by acute left heart failure (ALHF) delivered at Peking University Third Hospital from January 2015 to December 2025. Patients were stratified by timing of onset (antenatal vs. postnatal). The mean maternal age was 33.8 years, and 70.8% had preeclampsia. ALHF occurred antenatally in 9 cases (37.5%) and postnatally in 15 cases (62.5%). Dyspnea was the most common initial symptom (79.2%). The mean left ventricular end‑diastolic diameter was 50.6 ± 3.7 mm, and the median NT‑proBNP level was 1406.0 pg/ml (IQR: 1146.5–2053.5 pg/ml). All patients received multidisciplinary care and diuretics, with 37.5% requiring intensive care. Full cardiac recovery by 6 weeks postpartum was achieved in 87.5% of patients. The preterm birth rate was 87.5%, and the perinatal mortality rate was 10.4%. Compared with the postnatal‑onset group, the antenatal‑onset group had significantly higher NT‑proBNP levels at disease onset or diagnosis (2388.5 vs. 1257.0 pg/ml, p = 0.018), longer hospital stay (11 vs. 8 days, p = 0.019), earlier gestational age at delivery (30 vs. 36 weeks, p = 0.002), and higher rates of neonatal intensive care unit admission (92.3% vs. 40.0%, p = 0.01) and perinatal mortality (27.8% vs. 0, p = 0.016). In conclusion, ALHF in twin pregnancies predominantly occurs postnatally and is frequently associated with preeclampsia; maternal outcomes are favorable with multidisciplinary care, whereas neonatal outcomes were poorer in the antenatal‐onset group, likely reflecting medically indicated early preterm delivery in the setting of maternal decompensation.

Background

In recent years, the incidence of twin pregnancies has been rising steadily, driven largely by the widespread use of assisted reproductive technology and increasing maternal age. Twin pregnancy imposes a substantial cardiovascular burden, characterized by a 40%–50% increase in blood volume and elevated cardiac output, which further amplifies maternal hemodynamic demands [1]. The 2025 ROPAC (Registry of Pregnancy and Cardiac disease) study provided the first large‐scale evidence that twin pregnancies with preexisting heart disease carry a heart failure risk of up to 25%, more than twice that observed in singleton pregnancies [2]. In our previous longitudinal study on maternal cardiac adaptations during twin pregnancy, we found that women with normal twin pregnancies exhibit more pronounced changes in left ventricular morphology, diastolic function, and hemodynamics compared with singleton pregnancies [3]. Most existing studies have focused on singleton pregnancies or broadly examined pregnancy in the context of heart disease, leaving a notable gap in systematic clinical analyses specifically addressing heart failure in twin pregnancies. To address this, we conducted a retrospective analysis of 24 twin pregnancies complicated by acute left heart failure at the Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, Peking University Third Hospital, over the past decade. This study aims to characterize the clinical spectrum of this condition, explore factors associated with disease severity, and summarize our institutional diagnostic and management experience, with the goal of providing valuable insights for clinical decision‐making.

Methods

Subject

This retrospective study was conducted on 24 twin pregnant women diagnosed with acute left heart failure (ALHF) who delivered at the Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, Peking University Third Hospital, between January 2015 and December 2025, all of whom had complete medical records. Inclusion criteria comprised twin pregnancies delivered at our hospital during the study period, a diagnosis of heart failure consistent with the Chinese Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Heart Failure, and availability of complete clinical data. Patients were excluded if they had pre‐existing chronic heart failure or congenital heart disease diagnosed before pregnancy, or if they presented with coexisting severe medical or surgical conditions. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Peking University Third Hospital (Approval No. M2021415).

Diagnostic Criteria for Acute Left Heart Failure

The diagnosis of ALHF was established according to the following criteria [4, 5]: (1)clinical symptoms and signs, including sudden or progressively worsening dyspnea and bilateral pulmonary rales on physical examination; (2) supportive findings from diagnostic workup, including elevated B‐type natriuretic peptide (BNP) or N‐terminal pro‐B‐type natriuretic peptide (NT‐proBNP) levels, BNP or NT‐proBNP levels were measured at the time of clinical onset or diagnosis. According to contemporary heart failure guidelines, BNP ≥100 pg/mL or NT‐proBNP ≥300 pg/mL was used as supportive biochemical evidence for ALHF. In addition, because all patients in this cohort were younger than 55 years, NT‐proBNP >450 pg/mL was considered a more specific age‐related rule‐in reference value; echocardiographic evidence of decreased left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) and/or left ventricular enlargement, and chest radiography findings indicative of pulmonary congestion or pulmonary edema; and (3) exclusion of other identifiable causes of cardiac dysfunction. The final diagnosis required compatible symptoms and signs, radiographic evidence of pulmonary congestion or pulmonary edema, echocardiographic findings, response to heart failure‐directed therapy, and exclusion of alternative causes of respiratory distress.

Data Collection

Clinical data were systematically collected across the following domains: (1) baseline characteristics, including maternal age, pre‑pregnancy body mass index (BMI), total gestational weight gain (between the last recorded maternal weight before delivery and pre‐pregnancy weight), method of conception and chorionicity; (2) clinical presentation, including timing of onset, initial symptoms, and findings from echocardiography, chest radiography, and BNP/NT‑proBNP measurements; (3) obstetric characteristics, including gestational diabetes mellitus, preeclampsia, gestational age at delivery and mode of delivery; (4) therapeutic interventions, including diuretic therapy, vasodilators, inotropic agents and admission to the intensive care unit (ICU); and (5) maternal and neonatal outcomes, including resolution of heart failure, length of hospital stay, preterm birth, birth weight, Apgar scores, admission to the neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) and perinatal mortality; (6) Cardiac recovery at 6 weeks postpartum was defined as resolution of heart failure‐related symptoms, no requirement for oxygen support or intravenous heart failure therapy, no clinical evidence of persistent pulmonary congestion, recovered systolic or diastolic function on follow‐up echocardiography, and normalization of BNP/NT‐proBNP was considered supportive when available.

Statistical Analysis

Given the limited sample size, descriptive analysis was the primary approach. Normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, while non‑normally distributed continuous variables were presented as median with interquartile range [M (Q1, Q3)]. Categorical variables were reported as frequencies and percentages. For exploratory subgroup analyses, between‑group comparisons were conducted using Fisher's exact test for categorical variables and the independent‑samples t‑test or Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables as appropriate. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0, and a two‑tailed P‑value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 24 twin pregnancies complicated by acute left heart failure (ALHF) were identified during the 10‐year study period. The mean maternal age was 33.8 ± 5.6 years, and the mean BMI was 23.3 ± 2.8 kg/m2. Assisted reproductive technology was used in 17 patients (70.8%), and 21 patients (87.5%) were primiparous. Dichorionic and monochorionic twin pregnancies accounted for 14 (58.3%) and 10 (41.7%) cases, respectively. Preeclampsia was present in 17 patients (70.8%), including 10 patients (41.7%) with severe preeclampsia and 9 patients (37.5%) with severe hypertension. Anemia and hypoalbuminemia were observed in 18 (75.0%) and 17 (70.8%) patients, respectively. ALHF occurred antenatally in 9 patients (37.5%) and postnatally in 15 patients (62.5%) (Table 1). At the time of ALHF diagnosis, the mean systolic blood pressure was 146 ± 15 mmHg, the mean diastolic blood pressure was 89 ± 9 mmHg, and the mean heart rate was 88 ± 18 bpm. The median NT‐proBNP level was 1406.0 pg/mL (interquartile range [IQR], 1146.5–2053.5 pg/mL). Echocardiography showed a median LVEF of 65% (IQR: 55%–69%), a mean left ventricular end‐diastolic diameter(LVEDD) of 50.6 ± 3.7mm, a mean left atrial anteroposterior diameter of 36.8 ± 4.6 mm, and a mean left atrial area of 21.8 ± 4.3 cm2. The mean E/A ratio(E: Mitral inflow velocity early diastolic velocity, A: Mitral inflow velocity late diastolic velocity) was 1.6 ± 0.4, and the mean E/e′ ratio (Peak early diastolic myocardial velocity of the septal or the lateral mitral annulus/ Average value of early diastolic myocardial velocity of the septal and the lateral mitral annulus) was 9.5±2.3. Diastolic dysfunction was identified in eight patients with available echocardiographic diastolic function assessment (Table 1).

When patients were compared according to timing of ALHF onset, baseline characteristics, chorionicity, blood pressure, heart rate, LVEF, LVEDD, left atrial measurements, E/A, E/e′, and the proportion of diastolic dysfunction were not significantly different between the antenatal and postnatal groups. However, quantitative proteinuria was significantly higher in the antenatal‐onset group than in the postnatal group [1340 (556, 3928) vs. 400 (364, 500)mg/24 h, p = 0.014]. NT‐proBNP levels at diagnosis were also significantly higher in the antenatal‐onset group [2388.5 (1652.3, 3012.0) vs. 1257.0 (1092.5, 1669.5) pg/mL, p = 0.018] (Table 1).

All patients received multidisciplinary team management, oxygen therapy, and diuretics. Non‐invasive ventilation was required in two patients (8.3%). Vasodilators were used in nine patients (37.5%), and inotropes were used in three patients (12.5%). Nine patients (37.5%) were admitted to the ICU. Compared with the postnatal group, the antenatal group was more likely to receive vasodilators (66.7% vs. 20.0%, p = 0.036), whereas the use of non‐invasive ventilation, inotropes, and ICU admission did not differ significantly between groups (Table 2). The median gestational age at delivery was 34 weeks (IQR, 31–36 weeks), and the median hospital stay was 9.0 days (IQR, 7.8–11.0 days). Cesarean delivery was performed in 20 patients (83.3%). Postpartum hemorrhage occurred in five patients (20.8%), and three patients (12.5%) required blood transfusion. Cardiac recovery at 6 weeks postpartum was achieved in 21 patients (87.5%). In the antenatal group, ALHF occurred at 28.6 ± 2.8 weeks. In the postnatal group, ALHF occurred at a median of 1.5 ± 0.7 days postpartum. Compared with the postnatal‐onset group, the antenatal‐onset group delivered significantly earlier [30 (27, 31) vs. 36 (34, 36) weeks, p = 0.002] and had a longer hospital stay [11.0 (10.0, 15.0) vs. 8.0 (7.0, 9.0) days, p = 0.019]. Cesarean delivery, postpartum hemorrhage, blood transfusion, and cardiac recovery did not differ significantly between groups (Table 2). Neonatal outcomes were less favorable in the antenatal‐onset group. Overall, preterm birth occurred in 21 pregnancies (87.5%), NICU admission was required for 24 neonates (55.8%), and perinatal death occurred in 5 neonates (10.4%). The antenatal‐onset group had higher rates of NICU admission (92.3% vs. 40.0%, p = 0.010) and perinatal mortality (27.8% vs. 0%, p = 0.010) than the postnatal‐onset group. These findings should be interpreted in the context of the markedly earlier gestational age at delivery in the antenatal‐onset group (Table 2).

Discussion

In this 10‐year retrospective case series, ALHF in twin pregnancy occurred more often in the postnatal period and was frequently accompanied by preeclampsia, anemia, and hypoalbuminemia. Most patients had preserved LVEF at diagnosis and achieved cardiac recovery by 6 weeks postpartum after multidisciplinary management. Compared with postnatal‐onset cases, antenatal‐onset cases had higher NT‐proBNP levels, greater quantitative proteinuria, more frequent use of vasodilators, longer hospital stay, and substantially earlier delivery. Although neonatal outcomes were poorer in the antenatal‐onset group, these outcomes should be interpreted mainly in relation to medically indicated early preterm delivery.

Twin pregnancy imposes a greater cardiovascular burden than singleton pregnancy because of more pronounced plasma volume expansion, increased cardiac output, and myocardial remodeling [1, 3, 6, 7]. These physiological changes may reduce maternal cardiovascular reserve and increase susceptibility to decompensation when additional stressors are present. In the ROPAC registry, twin pregnancies in women with preexisting heart disease were associated with a markedly increased risk of heart failure [2]. Although the ROPAC study focused on women with known heart disease, our cohort mainly reflects real‐world de novo ALHF in twin pregnancy. Therefore, the two studies are complementary and together suggest that twin gestation itself should be regarded as an important cardiovascular stressor during pregnancy [2, 8]. Preeclampsia was the most common comorbidity in our cohort. This finding is consistent with population‐based evidence showing that both preeclampsia and twin gestation are independent risk factors for peripartum heart failure [9]. We further added indicators of hypertensive disease severity, including severe preeclampsia, severe hypertension and quantitative proteinuria. Quantitative proteinuria was significantly higher in the antenatal‐onset group, suggesting a greater burden of preeclampsia‐related disease in this subgroup. Preeclampsia may contribute to ALHF through endothelial dysfunction, angiogenic imbalance, increased afterload, fluid redistribution and pulmonary edema [10, 11]. In twin pregnancies, these mechanisms may act on the background of greater volume load and myocardial stretch, creating a possible “double‐hit” pathway [12, 13, 14]. However, because this was a retrospective case series with a small sample size, these findings support a plausible pathophysiological association rather than proving a causal relationship between preeclampsia and ALHF.

A notable feature of this cohort was that most patients had preserved LVEF at diagnosis. We found that ALHF in twin pregnancy may occur with preserved systolic function and may resemble a heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF)‐like phenotype in some patients. This is consistent with previous studies showing that twin pregnancy is associated with more pronounced changes in cardiac structure, diastolic function, and hemodynamics [3, 15, 16, 17]. Nevertheless, complete diastolic function grading was not uniformly available during the 10‐year study period. Therefore, the HFpEF‐like interpretation should be considered exploratory, and the diagnosis of ALHF should continue to rely on the overall clinical picture, including symptoms, pulmonary congestion, natriuretic peptide elevation, echocardiographic findings, treatment response, and exclusion of other causes. The timing of ALHF onset has important clinical implications. Postnatal‐onset ALHF accounted for most cases, which may reflect the hemodynamic stress of the early postpartum period, including autotransfusion from the contracted uterus, mobilization of extracellular fluid, and rapid changes in preload. This observation is consistent with previous reports emphasizing the early postpartum period as a high‐risk window for heart failure in pregnancy [2, 18]. In contrast, antenatal‐onset ALHF appeared to represent a more severe obstetric‐cardiac phenotype. These patients had higher NT‐proBNP levels, longer hospitalization, and earlier delivery. The need for more frequent vasodilator in the antenatal‐onset group may also reflect a greater burden of hypertensive disease and afterload‐related stress. The worse neonatal outcomes observed in the antenatal‐onset group should be interpreted with caution. These patients delivered at a much earlier gestational age, and very preterm delivery itself is a major driver of NICU admission, low birth weight, and perinatal mortality. Therefore, antenatal‐onset ALHF may not directly cause adverse neonatal outcomes. Rather, it often represents a severe maternal condition that prompts medically indicated early delivery to protect maternal health. This distinction is clinically important because it highlights the need to balance maternal stabilization, fetal maturation, and timing of delivery in antenatal‐onset cases.

All patients in this cohort received multidisciplinary care, oxygen therapy, and diuretics. No in‐hospital maternal deaths occurred, and most patients achieved cardiac recovery by 6 weeks postpartum. These findings support the importance of early recognition and coordinated management. Volume management remains central to treatment, consistent with guideline recommendations for acute heart failure and cardiovascular disease in pregnancy [5, 19, 20]. For antenatal cases, management should focus on maternal stabilization, control of hypertension and pulmonary edema, fetal assessment, corticosteroid administration when feasible, and individualized timing of delivery. For postnatal cases, careful monitoring during the early postpartum period is essential to avoid fluid overload and delayed recognition of pulmonary edema. The exploratory ICU analysis also provided clinically relevant information. Patients admitted to the ICU had lower LVEF and were more likely to require inotropes. The proportion of diastolic dysfunction was also higher in the ICU group, although the difference did not reach statistical significance. These findings suggest that impaired systolic function, possible diastolic impairment, and the need for inotropic support may help identify patients with greater clinical severity. By contrast, timing of onset, NT‐proBNP level, and preeclampsia did not differ significantly according to ICU admission status. Given the limited sample size, these results should be considered descriptive rather than predictive.

Strengths and Limitations

Acute left heart failure in twin pregnancies represents a critical obstetric emergency that poses substantial risks to both maternal and fetal health. Despite its clinical importance, relevant studies in China remain limited, with few case series specifically focusing on this condition. Accordingly, the present study helps fill this gap. As a tertiary hospital with support from powerful assisted reproductive technology, the number of twin pregnancies in our study was still representative. Several limitations should be acknowledged, including its single‑center retrospective design and modest sample size, which precluded multivariable regression analysis. This study primarily aimed to characterize the clinical spectrum of this population and provide insights to inform clinical practice. Future multicenter studies with larger cohorts are warranted to further elucidate the risk factors and long‑term outcomes associated with heart failure in twin pregnancies.

Conclusion

In conclusion, ALHF in twin pregnancy occurred predominantly in the postnatal period and was frequently associated with preeclampsia. Antenatal‐onset ALHF was associated with higher NT‐proBNP levels, longer hospitalization, earlier delivery, and poorer neonatal outcomes. These neonatal outcomes were likely driven largely by medically indicated early preterm delivery in the setting of maternal decompensation. Close cardiovascular surveillance and coordinated obstetric‐neonatal care are essential for this high‐risk population

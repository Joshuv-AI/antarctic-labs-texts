AQP5 Regulates Corneal Nerve Distribution Through NETRIN-1 and SLIT3
Authors: Wenshuo Han, Yaning Liu, Peirong Song, Yu Gu, Fei Li, Wenbo Yan, Guohu Di, Dianqiang Wang, Peng Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeNeurotrophic keratopathy (NK) is characterized by corneal nerve abnormalities. We investigated the role of aquaporin 5 (AQP5) in corneal nerve distribution and its regulation via the JUN-D–Ntn1/Slit3 signaling pathway.MethodsWild-type (Aqp5+/+) and Aqp5 knockout (Aqp5−/−) mice were used. Corneas were dissected, permeabilized, and immunolabeled with neuron-specific βIII-tubulin to visualize corneal nerves. Primary trigeminal ganglion neurons were isolated and cocultured with recombinant NETRIN-1 or SLIT3. Chromatin immunoprecipitation PCR assessed JUN-D binding to Ntn1 and Slit3 promoters. Corneal epithelial scraping was performed, followed by subconjunctival injection of the JUN-D activator or inhibitor to evaluate effects on nerve regeneration.ResultsWhole-mount immunostaining revealed reduced corneal nerve density in Aqp5−/− mice. Corneal epithelial NETRIN-1 levels were decreased, whereas SLIT3 levels were increased in Aqp5−/− mice. NETRIN-1 promoted, and SLIT3 inhibited, neuronal axon growth. JUN-D inhibition upregulated Ntn1 and downregulated Slit3. Modulation of JUN-D influenced corneal epithelial healing and nerve regeneration following corneal epithelial scraping.ConclusionsAQP5 deficiency disrupts corneal nerve architecture and may contribute to NK. AQP5 regulates corneal nerve distribution via the JUN-D–Ntn1/Slit3 pathway. These findings identify potential molecular targets for the prevention and treatment of NK.

Neurotrophic keratopathy (NK) is a degenerative corneal disorder characterized by abnormal nerve distribution, epithelial defects, impaired wound healing, and reduced corneal sensitivity.1,2 Its incidence is estimated at 0.1 to 0.3 per 10,000 individuals, with a prevalence of approximately 0.5 per 10,000.3 Currently, no effective therapy exists, and disease progression may lead to corneal ulceration, perforation, and vision loss.4,5 Thus, identifying strategies to restore corneal innervation is of clinical importance.

The cornea is among the most densely innervated tissues in the human body. Corneal sensory fibers, derived primarily from the ophthalmic branch of the trigeminal nerve, play a critical role in maintaining ocular surface homeostasis.6 Sympathetic fibers from the superior cervical ganglion are localized predominantly at the corneoscleral limbus,7–9 while parasympathetic fibers have also been detected in some mammalian corneas, although their precise function remains unclear.6,10

Aquaporins (AQPs) are membrane channel proteins that facilitate selective water transport.11 Some AQPs also permit the passage of small molecules such as urea, glycerol, and hydrogen peroxide (H2O2).12,13 Notably, AQP5 mediates the transport of both water and H2O2, contributing to oxidative stress regulation.14 Our previous studies demonstrated that Aqp5 knockout mice exhibit reduced corneal nerve density and delayed epithelial wound healing.15,16 In the lacrimal gland, AQP5 regulates nerve distribution through axon guidance factors, including NETRIN-1 and SLIT2.17 Moreover, decreased AQP5 expression has been observed in a mouse model of NK, accompanied by reduced nerve density.18 However, the precise role of AQP5 in corneal nerve patterning remains unclear.

Axon guidance molecules are specialized cytokines that direct axonal growth by attracting or repelling growth cones.19,20 Among these, Netrins and Slits are well known for their opposing roles: NETRIN-1 promotes, whereas SLIT3 inhibits, axonal extension.21,22 In the present study, we demonstrate that Aqp5 deletion reduces corneal nerve density by downregulating Ntn1 and upregulating Slit3, mediated through the activation of the transcription factor JUN-D. These alterations disrupt corneal nerve architecture and impair epithelial healing. Collectively, our findings reveal novel molecular targets and provide a theoretical framework for developing therapeutic strategies for NK and related corneal neuropathies.

Methods

Animals

Aqp5 knockout (Aqp5−/−) mice were generated and maintained using CRISPR/Cas9 technology.23 Experiments were performed on 6-month-old Aqp5+/+ and Aqp5−/− mice. All procedures adhered to the ARVO Statement for the Use of Animals in Ophthalmic and Vision Research and were approved by the Qingdao University Medical College Ethics Committee.

Corneal Epithelial Scraping Model

Mice were anesthetized with 4% isoflurane, and a 2-mm diameter area of corneal epithelium was scraped under a microscope using a motorized spatula. The epithelial defects were stained with sodium fluorescein and imaged using a slit-lamp biomicroscope.

SR11302 is a Jund inhibitor, 15S-HPETE is a Jund activator, and niclosamide is an AQP5 activator. To assess the roles of AQP5 and the transcription factor JUN-D in corneal nerve regeneration, 5 µL SR11302 (10 mM), 15S-HPETE (10 mM), niclosamide (200 nM), or Jund small interfering RNA (siRNA) (20 pM) was injected into the subconjunctiva 24 hours before scraping and at 0 and 24 hours postsurgery. Epithelial defect areas were monitored at 0, 12, 24, and 36 hours after scraping.

Corneal Whole-Mount Immunostaining

Following euthanasia, eyeballs were fixed in tissue fixative on ice for 1 hour. Corneas were dissected from the limbus, fixed for an additional hour, and then permeabilized in PBS containing 0.1% Triton X-100 and 4% bovine serum albumin for 2 hours, followed by overnight incubation at 4°C. Corneas were rinsed six times with PBS, cut into five flaps, mounted, sealed, and imaged using a fluorescence microscope. Nerve density in central and peripheral corneal regions was quantified using ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA).

Corneal Sensitivity Measurement

Corneal sensitivity was assessed in unanesthetized mice using a Cochet–Bonnet esthesiometer (Luneau Ophtalmologie, Chartres Cedex, France). Measurements began with the nylon filament at its maximum length of 6 cm and decreased by 0.5-cm increments until a positive response was observed. The longest filament length eliciting a response was recorded. Each measurement was repeated three times.

RNA Sequencing

High-throughput RNA sequencing was performed by Cloudseq Biotechnology (Shanghai, China). Total RNA was extracted from corneal epithelial cells using Trizol. Ribosomal RNA was depleted with the NEBNext rRNA Depletion Kit (New England Biolabs, Inc., Ipswich, MA, USA), and RNA libraries were prepared using the NEBNext Ultra II Directional RNA Library Prep Kit (New England Biolabs). Sequencing was conducted on a NovaSeq 6000 platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA) using 150-bp paired-end reads.

Sequencing data were quality-controlled by trimming 3′ adaptors and removing low-quality reads using cutadapt (v1.9.3). High-quality reads were aligned to the reference genome with Hisat2 (v2.0.4). Raw read counts were generated with HTSeq (v0.9.1) and normalized using edgeR. Differentially expressed mRNAs were identified using a fold change >2 and P ≤ 0.05.

Extraction and Culture of Primary Corneal Epithelial Cells

Mice were anesthetized and euthanized according to approved protocols. Enucleated eyeballs were incubated in keratinocyte serum-free medium (KSFM) containing 1.5 U/mL dispase at 4°C for 15 hours. Corneal epithelium was dissected under a microscope, evenly plated in 48-well plates precoated with 100 µL LN521 for 1 hour at 37°C, and cultured in KSFM supplemented with 0.1% Y-27632, 2% B-27, 1% penicillin/streptomycin, and 1% insulin-transferrin-selenium.

To examine JUN-D regulation of Ntn1 and Slit3, SR11302 (10 µM), a specific JUN-D inhibitor, was added to the culture medium. Cells were incubated for 24 hours before sample collection.

Extraction and Culture of Primary Trigeminal Ganglion Neurons

Trigeminal ganglia (TGs) were dissected and placed in ice-cold Hanks’ balanced salt solution (HBSS). TGs were mechanically dissociated and digested with 0.2% sodium bicarbonate, 67 mg/mL cysteine, and 2 mg/mL papain at 37°C, followed by further digestion in HBSS containing type II collagenase. Cell suspensions were separated using Percoll gradient centrifugation. Neurons were cultured in Leibovitz's L-15 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin/streptomycin. Wells of 48-well plates were precoated with 100 µL LN521 for 3 hours at 37°C in 5% CO2.

After plating, cells were incubated in Neurobasal medium supplemented with 2% B-27 for 3 hours. Recombinant NETRIN-1 (4 µg/mL; #1109-N1; R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA), SLIT3 (4 µg/mL; #9296-SL; R&D Systems), corneal epithelial extracts, or neutralizing antibodies against NETRIN-1 (4 µg/mL; #AF1109; R&D Systems) and SLIT3 (4 µg/mL; #AF3629; R&D Systems) were added for 48 hours to assess their effects on axon growth.

Western Blot Analysis

Proteins were extracted from primary corneal epithelial cells and mouse corneal epithelium. Samples were lysed by ultrasonication and separated via SDS-PAGE. Proteins were transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride membranes (Millipore, Bedford, MA, USA) at room temperature and blocked with 5% skim milk. Membranes were incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies against βIII-tubulin (#MAB1195; R&D Systems), NETRIN-1 (#A16236; ABclonal, Wuhan, China), SLIT3 (#DF9909; Affinity, Jiangsu, China), ROBO4 (#YP-mAb07045; UpingBio, Hangzhou, China), Fra-1 (#YP-mAb01741; UpingBio), c-Jun (#YP-mAb17706; UpingBio), and JUN-D (#A11955; ABclonal). After a 1-hour incubation at room temperature with appropriate secondary antibodies, protein signals were detected using an ECL kit. β-Actin served as the loading control.

Quantitative Real-Time PCR

Total RNA was extracted from mouse corneas using the FastPure Kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) and reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the PrimeScript Kit (TaKaRa, Kusatsu, Japan). Quantitative RT-PCR was performed with ChamQ Master Mix (Vazyme) on a Bio-Rad CFX96 system (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). Primer sequences are provided in the Supplementary Table.

Immunofluorescence Staining

Cells or frozen tissue sections were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 25 minutes, permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100, and blocked with 5% BSA for 2 hours at room temperature. Samples were incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies against βIII-tubulin (#AB15708A4; Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA), NETRIN-1 (#A16236; ABclonal), or SLIT3 (#DF9909; Affinity). After washing, samples were incubated in the dark with Alexa Fluor 488–conjugated goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody for 1 hour at room temperature. Nuclei were stained with DAPI, and images were captured using an inverted fluorescence microscope.

ELISA Analysis

Primary corneal epithelial cells were cultured for 24 hours, and supernatants were collected. Concentrations of NETRIN-1 and SLIT3 were measured using Mouse ELISA Kits (JonlnBio, Shanghai, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Absorbance was read at 450 nm using a SpectraMax Absorbance Reader (Molecular Devices, San Francisco, CA, USA).

Chromatin Immunoprecipitation

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assays were conducted using the Enzymatic ChIP Assay Kit (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China). Corneal epithelial cells were crosslinked with formaldehyde, washed, and sonicated. Supernatants were incubated with Protein A/G magnetic beads prebound to 2 µg JUN-D antibody (#sc-271938X; Santa Cruz, CA, USA) to immunoprecipitate DNA–protein complexes. Purified DNA was analyzed by PCR and resolved on 1.5% agarose gels.

Statistical Analysis

Data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 9.5.0 (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA, USA) and were presented as mean ± SD. An unpaired, two-tailed Student's t-test was employed to evaluate the differences between the two groups. Multiple comparisons and one-way or two-way ANOVA were used to analyze data from three or more groups. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05 (*P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001). All figures included comprehensive statistical details.

Results

Abnormal Nerve Distribution in the Corneas of Aqp5−/− Mice

To investigate whether corneal nerve distribution is altered in Aqp5−/− mice, we conducted immunostaining of whole-mount corneal tissues from both Aqp5+/+ and Aqp5−/− groups. Neuron-specific βIII-tubulin antibody was utilized for labeling, and images were captured, followed by tissue reconstruction under a fluorescence microscope. The results demonstrated that corneal nerves in the peripheral and central regions were significantly less dense in Aqp5−/− mice compared to Aqp5+/+ mice (Fig. 1A). In the peripheral region, Aqp5+/+ mice had 34.84% ± 3.225% density compared to 20.21% ± 0.365% in Aqp5−/− mice, a 1.72-fold increase. In the central region, densities were 22.47% ± 0.663% and 13.18% ± 0.86%, respectively, showing a 1.7-fold difference (Fig. 1B). βIII-tubulin expression in the cornea was significantly reduced upon deletion of Aqp5, according to Western blot analysis (Figs. 1C, 1D).

To investigate AQP5’s role in corneal neural development, we identified niclosamide, a small molecule that enhances AQP5 expression. In a corneal epithelial debridement model, subconjunctival niclosamide administration accelerated epithelial wound healing and nerve regeneration in Aqp5+/+ mice (Supplementary Fig. S1).

Differential Expression of NETRIN-1 and SLIT3 in the Corneal Epithelium of Aqp5+/+ and Aqp5−/− Mice

To investigate the underlying cause of abnormal corneal nerve distribution following Aqp5 deletion, we conducted an analysis of the expression levels of major neurotrophic and axon guidance factors using transcriptome sequencing. Ntn1 (fold change (FC): −3.262, P < 0.01) expression decreased significantly, while Slit3 (FC: 2.128, P = 0.026) increased significantly in the corneal epithelium of Aqp5−/− mice compared to Aqp5+/+ mice (Fig. 2A). Volcano plot analysis confirmed these differential expression patterns (Fig. 2B). Aqp5−/− corneas showed significantly lower Ntn1 expression (0.5048 ± 0.05879) compared to Aqp5+/+ corneas (1.012 ± 0.03608, P < 0.01). Conversely, Aqp5−/− mice exhibited higher Slit3 expression (3.791 ± 0.6782) than Aqp5+/+ mice (1.031 ± 0.2917, P < 0.01) (Fig. 2C). Immunofluorescence analysis revealed that SLIT3 expression significantly increased (Aqp5+/+: 1.00 ± 0.0421; Aqp5−/−: 2.051 ± 0.2271, P < 0.01) and NETRIN-1 expression significantly decreased (Aqp5+/+: 1.00 ± 0.1645; Aqp5−/−: 0.503 ± 0.1447, P < 0.05) in the corneas of Aqp5−/− mice (Figs. 2D, 2E). Western blot analysis showed markedly higher levels of SLIT3 and ROBO4 proteins and significantly lower NETRIN-1 expression in the corneal epithelium of Aqp5−/− mice compared to Aqp5+/+ mice (Figs. 2F, 2G).

Primary corneal epithelial cells were extracted and stained with immunofluorescence in order to investigate the differential expression of NETRIN-1 and SLIT3 following Aqp5 deletion. The results showed that in the Aqp5−/− group, NETRIN-1 expression was significantly decreased (Aqp5+/+: 1.00 ± 0.0811; Aqp5−/−: 0.4612 ± 0.07088, P < 0.01), while SLIT3 expression was markedly increased (Aqp5+/+: 1.00 ± 0.1233; Aqp5−/−: 2.742 ± 0.4792, P < 0.01) in primary corneal epithelial cells (Figs. 2H, 2I). We also assessed NETRIN-1 and SLIT3 expression in the supernatant of primary corneal epithelial cells by ELISA. NETRIN-1 levels were significantly lower in Aqp5−/− mice than in Aqp5+/+ mice, while SLIT3 levels were markedly higher (Fig. 2J).

NETRIN-1 and SLIT3 Played Crucial Roles in Regulating Axon Growth and Elongation in Trigeminal Ganglion Neurons

Primary mouse trigeminal ganglion neurons were extracted and grown in vitro in order to investigate the directing effects of NETRIN-1 and SLIT3 on neuronal axon development. Immunofluorescence staining results showed that neurons from both mouse strains had significantly longer axons when treated with recombinant mouse (rm) NETRIN-1 compared to untreated controls (Aqp5+/+: 1.968 ± 0.3574; Aqp5−/−: 1.635 ± 0.2746) (Fig. 3A, i). In contrast to axon growth promotion by rmNETRIN-1, neurons treated with rmSLIT3 showed significantly reduced axon length (Fig. 3A, ii). According to statistical analysis, the rmSLIT3 group's axon length was almost half that of the control group (Aqp5+/+: 0.4088 ± 0.06869; Aqp5−/−: 0.6267 ± 0.129) (Fig. 3B).

To determine whether SLIT3 and NETRIN-1 specifically inhibit or promote axon growth in Aqp5−/− trigeminal ganglion neurons, the cells were treated with neutralizing antibodies (NAbs). SLIT3 NAbs doubled the maximum branch length and increased the number of protruding branches by blocking the inhibitory effect of Aqp5−/− TEs on the formation of neuronal axons of Aqp5+/+ TG (Fig. 4C). NETRIN-1 neutralizing antibodies inhibited axon growth promotion in Aqp5−/− trigeminal neurons exposed to Aqp5+/+ tissue extracts, reducing branch length to less than 50% of the maximal value and significantly decreasing the number of protruding branches (Fig. 4D).

Transcription Factor JUN-D Modulated the Expression of NETRIN-1 and SLIT3 in the Corneal Epithelium

To explore how AQP5 modulates NETRIN-1 and SLIT3 expression, potential transcription factors were predicted using the PROMO database. Heatmap analysis showed changes in mRNA levels of 14 shared transcription factors. The corneal epithelium of Aqp5−/− mice showed significantly higher Jund expression than that of Aqp5+/+ mice, while Fra-1 and c-Jun protein levels remained unchanged (Fig. 5A, Supplementary Fig. S2). The corneal epithelium of Aqp5−/− mice had substantially higher amounts of JUN-D protein than that of Aqp5+/+ mice, according to Western blot examination (Figs. 5B, 5C). To confirm JUN-D recruitment to the Ntn1 and Slit3 promoter regions, ChIP-PCR experiments were performed using corneal epithelial cells from Aqp5+/+ mice. The results demonstrated that JUN-D was recruited to the promoter regions of Ntn1 and Slit3, suggesting its involvement in regulating the transcription of these genes (Fig. 5D).

Transcription Factor JUN-D Regulated Corneal Nerve Regeneration

To investigate the impact of AQP5 on corneal epithelial healing and nerve regeneration, we developed a corneal epithelial scraping model. When comparing Aqp5−/− mice to Aqp5+/+ mice, the defect regions in the Aqp5−/− group were significantly larger at both time points (24 hours: 55.175% ± 2.149%; 36 hours: 11.023% ± 4.3%) (Figs. 6A, 6B). Mice with corneal epithelium scraping were given subconjunctival injections of JUN-D agonists or inhibitors to ascertain if Aqp5 deletion influenced corneal epithelial repair and nerve regeneration through the transcription factor JUN-D. The corneal defect area in the Aqp5+/+ + 15S-HPETE group (24 hours: 54.107% ± 4.976%; 36 hours: 10.9% ± 5.896%) was larger than in the Aqp5+/+ group (24 hours: 38.188% ± 3.737%; 36 hours: 1.085% ± 1.015%) (Figs. 6A, 6B). The corneal defect area in the Aqp5−/− + SR11302 group (24 hours: 37.918% ± 6.196%; 36 hours: 5.099% ± 3.044%) was significantly smaller than in the Aqp5−/− group (24 hours: 55.175% ± 2.149%; 36 hours: 11.023% ± 4.3%) (Figs. 6A, 6B).

According to neural staining results, 15S-HPETE injection decreased the number of regenerated nerves in the damaged area and hindered corneal nerve regeneration in Aqp5+/+ mice (Figs. 6C, 6E). On the other hand, in Aqp5−/− mice, SR11302 therapy improved corneal nerve regeneration, resulting in a greater quantity of regenerated nerves in the damaged area (Figs. 6D, 6E). Corneal sensitivity tests showed that activation of JUN-D decreased corneal sensitivity, and inhibition of JUN-D restored corneal sensitivity (Fig. 6F). To assess JUN-D's functional role, we created a corneal epithelial debridement model and delivered Jund siRNA via subconjunctival injection to specifically knock down Jund expression. Fluorescein staining showed significantly faster corneal epithelial wound closure after Jund knockdown (Supplementary Figs. S4A, S4B). Corneal sensitivity tests and nerve immunostaining further revealed improved corneal nerve regeneration and sensory function following Jund knockdown (Supplementary Figs. 4C–E).

Discussion

NK is a degenerative corneal disease caused by trigeminal nerve damage. Loss of corneal innervation leads to defective epithelium, impaired healing, and potential ulcerative perforation. Research focuses on understanding corneal nerve distribution and regeneration to develop targeted therapies for NK. Topical neurotrophic factor therapy has proven effective in promoting epithelial stability and reducing recurrence risk.24–26 The underlying cause of innervation abnormalities in NK progression remains unclear. Our study showed that aberrant JUN-D activation in the corneal epithelium altered NETRIN-1 and SLIT3 expression, affecting corneal nerve innervation and impairing epithelial healing in Aqp5−/− mice.

AQP5, a water channel protein that transports water and H2O2, is highly expressed in ocular tissues such as the cornea,27,28 lens,29,30 and lacrimal gland.31 Studies show AQP5 deficiency may contribute to neurodegenerative diseases and retinal degeneration.32 Additionally, AQPs are linked to neural development and regenerative repair. In mice, AQP1 deficiency inhibits axonal development in dorsal root ganglion neurons.33 AQP4, the primary water channel protein in the central nervous system, is downregulated after optic nerve crush, causing retinal ganglion cell loss.34 AQP9 deletion reduces retinal ganglion cell survival.35 Our prior research showed Aqp5−/− mice developed age-related phenotypes, such as corneal defects and neovascularization,36 and exhibited delayed corneal epithelial wound healing compared to Aqp5+/+ mice.15 Mice deficient in AQP5 exhibited a marked reduction in corneal nerve fiber density upon fluorescent labeling of the nerve fibers. Nevertheless, the precise mechanism by which AQP5 regulates corneal innervation remains unclear.

Axon guidance factors, molecules that influence nerve growth, are crucial for nerve development and regeneration.37 Through the adenosine 2B receptor, the Netrin family facilitates diabetic corneal wound healing and nerve regeneration.38 The Robo-Slit family of neural guides plays a predominantly repulsive role in corneal neurodevelopment.39 The mechanisms of axon guidance signaling in corneal nerve development and regeneration are unclear. According to our research, the corneal epithelium of Aqp5−/− mice exhibited considerably lower NETRIN-1 expression and higher SLIT3 expression when compared to Aqp5+/+ mice. NETRIN-1 promoted axonal growth in trigeminal neurons, whereas SLIT3 inhibited axon elongation.

The transcription factors of the AP-1 family are associated with nerve growth and regeneration.40 Research indicates that c-Jun plays a crucial role in axonal regeneration, and its absence hinders this process.41,42 However, excessive or prolonged c-Jun activation can cause abnormal nerve regeneration, while inhibiting it aids peripheral nerve myelin regeneration after injury.43 Other factors such as Creb, Ctcf, Yy1, and Jund may also play roles in optic nerve regeneration.44 The promotional effect of NETRIN-1 on nerve regeneration45,46 and the inhibitory effect of SLIT347 have been demonstrated. In our investigation, the corneal epithelium of Aqp5−/− mice exhibited an upregulation of JUN-D, a transcription factor shared by Ntn1 and Slit3. This led to dysregulated NETRIN-1 and SLIT3 expression, affecting neural development and regeneration. Aqp5−/− mice treated with the AP-1 inhibitor SR11302 showed improved corneal wound healing and nerve regeneration, whereas Aqp5+/+ mice treated with the AP-1 activator 15S-HPETE showed inhibition of these processes. The mechanism by which AQP5 influences JUN-D expression is still unclear. In rat oligodendrocytes, hydrogen peroxide–induced apoptosis is associated with abnormal activation of AP-1 transcription factors.48 Similar effects occur in epithelial and endothelial cells, where H2O2 exposure increases AP-1 expression.49 As a classical aquaporin, AQP5 transports water and hydrogen peroxide across membranes. AQP5 deficiency may lead to intracellular H2O2 accumulation, promoting oxidative stress and potentially altering JUN-D expression.

Our study highlighted AQP5’s critical role in corneal nerve development and regeneration. AQP5 deficiency caused abnormal corneal nerve distribution and impaired healing by upregulating the transcription factor JUN-D, which regulated axon guidance factors NETRIN-1 and SLIT3 (Fig. 7). According to these results, AQP5 might be a therapeutic target for conditions affecting the corneal nerve, and altering the AQP5–JUN-D interaction might lead to novel therapeutic approaches.

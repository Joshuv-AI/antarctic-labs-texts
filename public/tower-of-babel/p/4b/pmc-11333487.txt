A novel health monitoring system for vital signs using IoT
Authors: Qi Chen, Nan Sheng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe current research looked at how to use the Internet of Things (IoT) to create a vital sign health monitoring system. Eight indications are employed to get critical patient information. Therefore, the number of nodes of the IoT embedded in the human body is 8, which have been worked on in different places of the body. Among the 8 nodes, node number 1 is located in the center of the grid (the center of the human body). The number of rounds is 9000 and the nodes are adopted with the initial energy of the nodes of 0.5 J and the radio range of 10 m. MATLAB software was used to simulate the WBAN network, which consists of IoT sensors embedded in the human body. The eight-item health assessment tool takes the following into account: pulse rate, blood pressure (mm Hg), serum cholesterol (mg/dl), temperature (°C), exercise-induced angina, and exercise-induced ST-wave depression, major blood vessels are counted using a medical procedure called endoscopy that involves examining the alveoli, which are small air sacs in the lungs where gas exchange occurs. We compared the number of major vessels at rest with the maximal heart rate during activity. The sensors were responsible for sending this data to the health center (base station). The data collected from the installation of these 8 sensors on 303 patients were collected and evaluated by machine learning method using MLP neural network method. Finally, it can be claimed that the present study has provided an automated method of determining the health of people using the IoT in a way that provides a state of health with an accuracy of over 99% and can be used in medical centers.Keywords: Monitoring system, Health, Vital signs, Body temperature, Blood pressure, Heart rateSubject terms: Cancer, Cell biology, Psychology

Introduction

The impressive growth of modern information and communication technologies in recent years and their entry into the daily lives of people all over the world has caused the emergence of events at all levels of the social environment. In the field of health care exclusively, sensors and data links offer the potential for continuous monitoring of disease symptoms and patient needs in real-time. which gives doctors the ability to diagnose and monitor patients’ problems wherever they are, whether at home or outside1–5.

Meanwhile, the IoT is a technological revolution in computing and communication, and it depicts a world of smart devices where everything is connected and has a digital existence6–10. One of the problems of the continuous occupation of patients, doctors, and patients’ companions is taking care of patients in the conditions that the patient lives his normal life in a place outside the hospital. In practice, it is not easy to reach the patient, and when a sudden attack occurs or the patient’s condition worsens, informing about the patient’s critical condition, quickly coordinating with the urban emergency center, finding low-traffic routes for moving the patient by ambulance and delivering first aid. etc. is not possible as an emergency. As a result, it is imperative that a solution be found. The IEEE 802.15.4 standard is widely regarded as one of the most important IoT standards for low-data-rate wireless network applications such as wireless body area networks (WBAN), home automation, and environmental monitoring networks. An area where it shows great potential is in the field of healthcare. The examples include physiological measurements such as body temperature, blood pressure, electrocardiogram (ECG), electroencephalogram (EEG), pulse oximeter (SpO2), and heart rate. These measurements are detected and sent to the medical facility. These data are used for health status monitoring and other assessments. Biomedical sensor networks guarantee freedom of movement along with continuous monitoring and care. The goal of this work is remote monitoring so that even outside the hospital environment, doctors, nurses and relatives of the patient (even in some cases the patient himself) can, in the event of an accident, especially in cases where there is an urgent need, which makes it possible to prevent deaths caused by the distance between the patient and the treatment center to a large extent.

The upward trend of the IoT and connected physical devices and their virtual display has resulted in a wide range of potential new products and services in various fields. One of the stated fields is the field of health, by 2020, 40% of the IoT technology will be related to health and health, according to that, the field of health and treatment is one of the main industries that use the applications of the Internet of Things technology. Based on this, the applications of the Internet of Things have a suitable potential in the field of electronic health services and the telecommunications industry11, and hospitals and medical centers and health policy makers in countries are constantly looking for the use of new technologies have been information. IoT is a network of physical devices and other things, considered with sensors, software, electronics and network connection, which has the ability to collect and exchange information. As a result, it is possible that the Internet of Things can be considered as a lever with different methods to continue the growth and productivity of hospitals12.

In recent years, a large number of healthcare organizations have started using wireless sensor networks for remote monitoring of patient health. Also, a large number of healthcare organizations and insurance companies have used the electronic health record system, and they store and maintain the medical history of patients in the form of special sheets of electronic health files in the centralized database. Web-based applications have been developed to manage electronic medical records13–17. The availability of affordable electronic circuits and the ability to create compact and energy-efficient telecommunication and electronic equipment have facilitated the aspiration to bring about significant transformations in the field of medicine18–22. The current emphasis in contemporary medicine, along with the use of developing technologies like information technology, is mostly directed towards prevention rather than therapy. By regulating vital signs, it is feasible to maintain the overall health status of the patient in a steady state. Diabetes, hypothyroidism, or the presence of an infection in the patient’s body might cause abrupt fluctuations in vital signs23–27. Failure to promptly identify and disclose these conditions may result in irreparable ramifications for the patient’s well-being, particularly for newborns and the elderly who may have more severe effects. Thus, it provides an automated and precise method for remotely monitoring patients’ vital signs, making it unnecessary to avoid. In recent times, researchers have been primarily focusing on wireless sensor networks, which consist of several sensors (such as those measuring breathing rate or heart rate) to address the limitations of conventional monitoring techniques28–32. The primary objective of this study is to examine the development of an Internet of Things (IoT) based health monitoring system for tracking vital signs. The primary objectives of this study are exploring the development of an IoT-based health monitoring system to detect and report body temperature, blood pressure, and heart rate.

By keeping sensors turned on and maintaining continuous efficiency (the stability of the sensors in the network), it is possible to access people’s personal data via IoT sensors. Determining the best route for information transmission via sensors in the network is one of the efficient ways to choose data from the research center’s (base station) sensors. As a result, the sensors utilize less energy. All of these sensors have a battery and a limited amount of energy, so when they transmit a message, part of that energy is used up. Consequently, extending the network’s lifetime (and lowering sensor energy consumption) may be achieved by figuring out the best route for packet transmission (by enlisting the help of more sensors to help relay the message to the base station). For WBANs, multi-hop routing is the optimum choice. The movement of sensors (caused by the movement of body parts) supports the operating power of this technology. The successful packet delivery at the base station has been measured in order to study an experiment. In both stable and unstable areas, the suggested operational power is almost as good as single algorithms and multi-hop communication. In contrast, the suggested approach makes advantage of mobility management in both single-hop and multi-hop communications. if the sensors’ movement breaks the connection among them. The suggested approach connects to a different node by counting hops or using the least amount of energy. Therefore, in the event of a connection breakdown, packet loss does not happen. The aim of this method is to find a path M = m0,m1,m2,…,mn,m0, which is mi ∈ V such that: 1—The length of M is longer than Imax. 2—The energy consumption for sending the embedded data to the path M, which is defined as (ETX + ERX) Σi∈V(i,M), is minimized in the time interval D. It should be noted that the minimum energy consumption is when all nodes are selected as RP. Therefore, in this case, sensor nodes do not send packets to other nodes. Conversely, it is challenging to locate a route that is shorter than lmax. Due to the fact that these sensors are in charge of transmitting data to a node located distant from the sink, sensor nodes near the sink are crowded in multi-hop communication. As a result, the closer the sensor node is to the sink, the more energy it consumes. As a result of this issue, the network’s energy usage is not uniform, resulting in leakage. As a consequence, the sink and some nodes get disconnected as a result of this issue. Several studies have proposed the use of one or several mobile sinks in Wireless Body Area Networks (WBAN). The mobile sinks traverse the sensor nodes and immediately gather the perceived data from them. The use of mobile sinks has been empirically shown to provide several advantages in mitigating the energy consumption of nodes and averting energy deficiencies in WBAN networks. The advantages are contingent upon the trajectory chosen by the mobile sink. An approach to do this is for the mobile sink to only visit rendezvous point (RP) nodes rather than all nodes. Non-RP sensor nodes transmit data to the closest RP using a multistep approach. The primary challenge in this approach is to identify a set of ideal RP nodes that the mobile sink should visit. Finding the best path that satisfies every RP is the next challenge. A heuristic is used in the suggested approach to identify optimum or nearly optimal approaches that reduce sensor node energy consumption. In this method, based on the number of packets sent by a node, i.e. NFD(i) and the distance (number of steps) of the sensor node from the route H(i,M) as well as the remaining energy of each node (RBP(i)), A weight is assigned to each node. Then, using this weight value, the worthiness of each node to be selected as RP is determined. The amount of weight is obtained using Eq. (6).

The suggested method takes as input the graph (V,E) and outputs RPs. The fixed sink is regarded by MTR as the initial RP. The sensor nodes with the greatest weight are then added to the list of RPs. The routing algorithm then chooses the best route for message delivery. The chosen node stays as RP if the route length is smaller than Imax. This node will be eliminated from the route if not. Each RP node has a specified "removed" variable, which is set to true in the event that an RP is deleted, in this sequence. A deleted RP will not be eliminated from the route again if it is added since its removed variable is set to true.

According to the proposed method, the sensors should be able to transmit the packet information from the body to the base station. Since vital information such as temperature, heart rate, blood pressure, cholesterol, etc. must be sent in the shortest possible time to analyze a person’s condition, the path of data transmission is of great importance. The less energy the sensors spend, the less they will run out of energy (they will turn into dead nodes), and thus they can send vital information about the body for more time. The suggested model makes use of the best routing architecture, in which data is sent from the furthest node to the sink via a sending node. The suggested approach uses the least amount of energy during the experiment, according to the simulation findings. This indicates that more nodes have sufficient energy during the stability phase, allowing them to send more data packets to the sink. Additionally, it increases the network’s operating strength. Furthermore, it is important to choose the right size sensors for information storage so that nodes with greater energy levels and closer distances from other nodes are very likely to be favored. due to the fact that these nodes are more valuable and have a higher security level than other nodes in the network. As a result, the priority is determined based on the position and location of the nodes and first the total distance of the surrounding nodes, followed by the number of jobs each node must do and its percentage of the overall energy level. An additional requirement is called energy density, which is the quantity of energy that a node can provide to the cluster head based on the spatial distance. The node that has the greatest spatial distance, denoted as L, is the biggest node. The other nodes, with distances ranging from l < L, normalize their energy levels using the energy coefficient, which is the ratio of their distance from the origin. According to the movement of the nodes in the network, and the examination of the prevailing conditions, each node seeks to be placed in each place with the aim of reducing the consumption of resources and reducing costs. Based on determining the distance of nodes and the level of activity of nodes in physical sensor networks, information about packets is stored in the network. The storage level has a direct relationship with the energy level and the lifetime of the nodes in the network. Conclusion

Wireless body sensors have been used to assess human health while operating within constrained energy resources. The present study employs MLP neural network model to extract and interpret data collected by sensors implanted into the human body. In order to evaluate the health status of people from 8 parameters of blood pressure, serum cholesterol, body temperature, heart rate, angina caused by exercise, depression of the ST wave caused by exercise compared to rest, the number of main vessels using alveoscopy, maximum heart rate in the state exercise is used. The sensors were responsible for sending this data to the base station. The data collected from the installation of these 8 sensors were collected on 303 patients. It was evaluated by machine learning method and using MLP neural network. Finally, an automatic method of determining people’s health using the Internet of Things has been presented, which provides the health status with an accuracy of over 99% and can be used in medical centers.

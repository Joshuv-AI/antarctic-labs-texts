Incidence and sex-related risk factors of linezolid-associated adverse drug reactions identified through electronic health records
Authors: Seunghwan Baek, JungJin Oh, SiYeon Yoon, Seonghae Yoon, Jae-Yong Chung
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Linezolid is an oxazolidinone antibiotic widely used to treat gram-positive infections and is associated with frequent adverse drug reactions (ADRs). Nevertheless, comprehensive evaluations of the incidence and risk factors of linezolid-related ADRs remain limited, with substantial variability across studies. This study aimed to evaluate the incidence of linezolid-associated ADRs and identify potential risk factors. A retrospective analysis was conducted using the Clinical Data Warehouse of Seoul National University Bundang Hospital (2003–2025). Among patients receiving at least one dose of linezolid, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, anemia, leukopenia, and lactic acidosis were identified as ADRs. Kaplan–Meier analysis assessed cumulative incidence, and univariable and multivariable logistic regression were performed to identify risk factors. Among 1,198 patients, the cumulative incidences of thrombocytopenia, anemia, leukopenia, neutropenia, and lactic acidosis within three months after linezolid treatment initiation were 44.7%, 57.3%, 45.8%, 29.9%, and 27.5%, respectively. Concomitant use of trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole was associated with anemia, while female sex and receipt of fresh frozen plasma transfusion were associated with lactic acidosis. Red blood cell transfusion was associated with an increased incidence of thrombocytopenia. This study demonstrated a higher incidence of linezolid-associated ADRs than previously reported in registration clinical trials. While several concomitant medications and clinical factors were identified as potential risk factors, associations involving transfusion-related variables should be interpreted cautiously, as they likely reflect underlying clinical vulnerability rather than a direct causal effect. These findings emphasize the importance of ADR monitoring during linezolid therapy and further research on the underlying mechanisms of linezolid-associated toxicity.

INTRODUCTION

Linezolid is the first synthetic oxazolidinone antibiotic that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis [1]. It is mainly used to treat gram-positive bacterial infections and is approved for pneumonia, skin and soft tissue infections, and vancomycin-resistant Enterococcus infections [2]. Recently, linezolid has also been used in multidrug-resistant tuberculosis and nontuberculous mycobacterial infections due to its broad gram-positive coverage and tissue distribution [3].

Although widely used in clinical practice, linezolid is associated with rare but severe adverse drug reactions (ADRs), including thrombocytopenia, anemia, peripheral and optic neuropathy, and lactic acidosis [4]. Several real-world clinical studies have investigated specific linezolid-associated ADRs, especially thrombocytopenia [56]. However, comprehensive ADR analysis, including myelosuppressive events (thrombocytopenia, anemia, leukopenia, and neutropenia) and lactic acidosis, remains limited, especially in the Korean population [78910].

Moreover, the true incidence of linezolid-associated ADRs in clinical practice remains uncertain. Registration clinical trials referred to in the SmPC have shown a relatively low incidence of linezolid-associated ADRs of about 20% [11]. However, several post-marketing surveillance and observational studies have revealed higher incidences of linezolid-associated ADRs, including 37% for thrombocytopenia [612131415]. This incidence discrepancy between clinical trial and real-world clinical practice raises safety concerns.

Previous studies have demonstrated risk factors for linezolid-associated ADRs. Risk factors for thrombocytopenia included prolonged treatment duration and decreased renal function, while female sex was associated with leukopenia and anemia [61213]. However, these studies evaluated only a few risk factors in specific populations. Because linezolid-associated ADRs involve multiple risk factors, a comprehensive analysis incorporating demographics, comorbidities, concomitant medications, and laboratory results is needed.

The present study aims to evaluate the time-dependent incidence of hematologic toxicities and lactic acidosis following linezolid therapy and to comprehensively identify potential risk factors in the Korean population.

METHODS

Data source and population

This single-center retrospective study used data from the Clinical Data Warehouse (CDW) of Seoul National University Bundang Hospital (SNUBH) and was approved by the Institutional Review Board of SNUBH (B-2510-1005-102).

Patients who received at least a single dose of linezolid between May 2003 and October 2025 were included in the study. The exclusion criteria were: 1) age < 18 years, 2) baseline laboratory test results satisfying the predefined ADR definitions, and 3) incomplete data.

ADR definitions

Consistent with other studies, ADR was defined according to the following criteria [1617181920]:

• Thrombocytopenia: a platelet count < 100 × 103/μL

• Thrombocytopenia: a platelet count < 100 × 103/μL

• Anemia: a hemoglobin level < 8 g/dL

• Anemia: a hemoglobin level < 8 g/dL

• Leukopenia: a white blood cell count < 4 × 103/μL

• Leukopenia: a white blood cell count < 4 × 103/μL

• Neutropenia: an absolute neutrophil count < 1,500/μL

• Neutropenia: an absolute neutrophil count < 1,500/μL

• Lactic acidosis: a lactate concentration ≥ 5 mmol/L accompanied by an arterial pH < 7.35

• Lactic acidosis: a lactate concentration ≥ 5 mmol/L accompanied by an arterial pH < 7.35

Study design and data collection

Patients with sufficient ADR-related laboratory data were included in each ADR-specific cohort. For each cohort, patients were divided into two groups based on ADR occurrence. Variables were collected during two time periods: the baseline and outcome periods. Baseline variables were measured on the day of linezolid initiation, or if not available, within the preceding 30 days. Missing values in baseline variables were imputed using the median values of the study population. Outcome variables were collected from the start of treatment until 5 days after the last dose of linezolid, or up to 95 days if the treatment period exceeded 90 days. As drug labels and existing studies report that the average ADR occurrence period is less than 28 days, the 95-day observation period established in this study is considered sufficient for observing ADR occurrence. Treatment was considered discontinued if linezolid was interrupted for more than 5 consecutive days.

To identify potential risk factors associated with the occurrence of each ADR, a comprehensive set of covariates was collected, including demographic characteristics (e.g., age, sex, body weight), baseline laboratory parameters (e.g., estimated glomerular filtration rate [eGFR], aspartate aminotransferase [AST], alanine aminotransferase [ALT], total bilirubin [T-BIL]), linezolid administration data (e.g., mean daily dose, cumulative dose, dosing interval), concomitant medications (e.g., heparin, trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole [TMP-SMX], metformin), and transfusion records (e.g., red blood cell [RBC], fresh frozen plasma [FFP] transfusions, platelet transfusions). The occurrence and timing of ADR events were recorded, and time-dependent cumulative incidence rates were estimated.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using Python (version 3.7) and R (version 4.5.1; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Patients with missing key variables were excluded from the analysis. Baseline characteristics were summarized using descriptive statistics: continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, and categorical variables as counts (number) and percentages (%). For each ADR, the cumulative incidence was estimated using Kaplan-Meier survival analysis. Crude incidence was also calculated without event timing or censoring.

For each ADR-specific cohort, covariates were selected based on correlation analysis to minimize multicollinearity. Variables with a correlation coefficient ≥ 0.8 or a variance inflation factor (VIF) ≥ 10 were excluded to minimize redundancy. The final list of covariates included in the multivariable analysis is provided in Supplementary Table 1.

Univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses were conducted to identify risk factors for each cohort. The results were presented as odds ratios (ORs) and adjusted odds ratios (aORs) with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs). In the multivariable analysis, statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05, and clinical significance as aOR ≤ 0.67 or ≥ 1.50 [2122].

Subgroup analyses were conducted based on risk factors that showed a statistically significant association with ADR occurrence in the primary analysis. Patients were stratified according to these significant risk factors, and additional analyses were performed to examine differences in clinical and laboratory characteristics between the resulting subgroups. Statistical comparisons were performed using the Mann–Whitney U test for numerical variables and Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables (p-value < 0.05).

RESULTS

Patient characteristics

A total of 1,198 patients were eventually included in this study and stratified into ADR-specific cohorts (Fig. 1). Approximately 60% of the study population were elderly males, and 55.3% experienced at least one ADR event during the observation period (Table 1). The mean baseline liver function tests (AST, ALT, and T-BIL) were slightly above the upper limit of normal, indicating mild to moderate hepatic impairment. The mean baseline renal function (eGFR) was within the normal range.

Incidence of ADR

Within 90 days after linezolid initiation, the crude incidence of ADRs ranged from 8.8% to 37.1%, depending on the specific ADR endpoint. The estimated cumulative incidence using Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was higher, ranging from 27.5% to 57.3% (Table 2, Fig. 2), likely because the Kaplan–Meier method accounts for censoring. Among the evaluated ADRs, anemia showed the highest incidence, followed by leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, and lactic acidosis.

Potential risk factor of ADR

After covariate selection based on correlation coefficients and VIFs, univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed for each ADR. Covariates with aORs that differed significantly from 1.0 and had an adjusted p-value < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. The following covariates met these criteria: Concomitant use of TMP-SMX for anemia; female sex and FFP transfusion for lactic acidosis; and RBC transfusion for thrombocytopenia (Table 3, Supplementary Table 2). The variables that were statistically significant did not differ between the univariable and multivariable analyses.

DISCUSSION

This study provides an overview of the incidence of linezolid-associated ADRs and identifies factors associated with ADR occurrence in a real-world clinical setting. The crude and cumulative incidence of linezolid-associated ADRs within 90 days were as high as 37.1% and 57.3%, respectively, depending on ADR type. Anemia showed the highest incidence followed by leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, and lactic acidosis.

Consistent with previous real-world retrospective studies reporting ADR incidence rates of 16.9–60.5%, the incidence of linezolid-associated ADRs was higher than that reported in registration clinical trials [61112131415]. This may be attributed to our comprehensive ADR monitoring system using CDW data that captured even mild or subclinical events. Additionally, our patient population may have had more diverse comorbidities and more complex medication regimens compared to registration clinical trials. Although the incidence of ADRs may vary depending on the specific definitions applied, this factor should be carefully considered when interpreting the findings. Nevertheless, when using widely accepted ADR criteria, the incidence of adverse reactions in this study was higher than that reported in the registered clinical trial, despite a shorter median duration of linezolid therapy (16 vs. 28 days) [11]. These findings suggest that additional comprehensive studies are needed to determine the true incidence of linezolid-associated ADRs. Such studies should include patients with more comorbidities and concomitant medications, diverse indications for linezolid treatment, and longer treatment durations.

Of the risk factors evaluated, concomitant TMP-SMX use showed a biologically plausible association with anemia, given its inhibition of folate metabolism and potential for bone marrow suppression, which may be exacerbated by linezolid-associated mitochondrial toxicity. By contrast, transfusion-related variables identified in this study are unlikely to represent direct causal effects. RBC transfusion preceding thrombocytopenia more likely reflects underlying hematologic vulnerability or impaired marrow reserve, conditions in which linezolid-induced suppression of platelet production may be unmasked. Similarly, FFP transfusion prior to lactic acidosis appears to function as a surrogate marker of critical illness, coagulation disturbances, or reduced lactate clearance, clinical settings that predispose to the manifestation of linezolid-related mitochondrial dysfunction and lactate accumulation. Overall, these findings indicate that while TMP-SMX likely contributes directly to anemia through established pharmacologic mechanisms, transfusion-related factors are more likely to reflect underlying patient vulnerability rather than causal determinants of linezolid-associated ADRs

In addition, Sex was identified as a potential risk factor for lactic acidosis. In the subgroup analysis of lactic acidosis, females had a slightly higher weight-normalized mean daily dose than males (20.4 mg/kg/day vs. 18.4 mg/kg/day) despite having better hepatic and renal function. To further investigate dosing effect on this sex-related difference, we examined weight-normalized mean daily dose according to the occurrence of lactic acidosis. Among patients without lactic acidosis, females received a higher weight-normalized mean daily dose than males (p-value < 0.05). However, among those with lactic acidosis, there was no statistically significant difference in weight-normalized mean daily dose between the sexes (Supplementary Fig. 1). These findings suggest that factors other than linezolid dose may influence the observed sex difference in lactic acidosis risk.

Some pharmacokinetic studies have shown no apparent sex-related difference in linezolid systemic exposure, whereas others have reported slightly higher exposure in women than in men, although these differences were not considered clinically meaningful enough to warrant dose adjustment [232425]. Meanwhile, sex-related differences in lactic acidosis risk have been reported for other mitochondrial-toxic agents, such as stavudine, in animal studies and large cohort analyses [2627282930]. These findings raise the possibility that hormonal influences on mitochondrial function may contribute to such differences. Nevertheless, in the present study, lactate and pH values had relatively lower data availability and longer measurement intervals compared with other laboratory parameters. Therefore, the observed sex-related associations should be interpreted as exploratory. Further research with adequate statistical power, specifically focusing on sex subgroups, is needed to validate and better understand these findings.

This study has several limitations. First, this single-center retrospective observational analysis may be subject to selection bias. Therefore, multicenter prospective studies may be needed to confirm these findings. Second, logistic regression does not fully utilize time-to-event information and may oversimplify ADR risk in the presence of variable treatment durations and event timing. Although this approach was employed to assess multiple dose- and time-based metrics together with other covariates, alternative time-to-event models may provide additional insights. Third, the current study did not include well-known linezolid-associated ADRs, such as peripheral or optic neuropathy, serotonin syndrome, and convulsions. These were excluded due to low incidence rates and lack of related data. Fourth, baseline laboratory values were defined as measurements obtained on the day of linezolid initiation or within the preceding 30 days. Although most baseline tests were performed within a short interval (within 5 days) before treatment initiation, some parameters—particularly lactate and pH—were measured at relatively longer intervals (mostly within 10 days), and potential misclassification due to laboratory fluctuations in acutely ill patients cannot be fully excluded. Fifth, sex-stratified subgroup analyses were subject to an imbalance in sample size between female and male patients, which may have limited the statistical power to robustly evaluate sex-specific differences in lactic acidosis risk. Sixth, due to limitations inherent in data availability and processing, more refined severity adjustment using established critical illness scoring systems such as the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment score or the Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation II score could not be applied. Nevertheless, key clinical covariates, including transfusion status and multiple laboratory parameters, were incorporated into the analysis and are expected to have partially captured the effects of underlying disease severity. Finally, linezolid systemic exposure data were not available. Dose-related covariates were analyzed as alternatives, but no significant relationships were observed. Future studies incorporating pharmacokinetic parameters may enhance ADR prediction.

This study found a high incidence of linezolid-associated ADRs in a real-world setting and identified sex as a potential risk factor for lactic acidosis. These findings emphasize the importance of careful ADR monitoring during linezolid therapy and further research on sex-related differences in lactic acidosis.

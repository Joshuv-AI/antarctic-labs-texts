Geochemical Characteristics and Oil-Gas Geological Significance of the Cambrian Bituminous Calcite Veins in the Northeast Margin of Ordos Basin, China
Authors: Wei Chen, Xiaofeng Wang, Wenhui Liu, Dongdong Zhang, Houyong Luo, Xiaofu Li, Xiaoqi Yuan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The bituminous calcite vein in the basin is an important basis for indicating fluid activity, hydrocarbon migration, and evolution. This paper systematically describes the microscopic petrological characteristics of two types of bituminous calcite veins and surrounding rocks from the Cambrian Xuzhuang Formation in Qingshuihe area, northeast margin of Ordos Basin, and their geochemical characteristics were analyzed by X-ray fluorescence spectroscopy (XRF) content, reflectance R b, inorganic carbon and oxygen isotope composition (δ13Ccarb, δ18Ocarb) and organic carbon isotope composition (δ13Corg). The Al–Fe–Mn and Mg–Fe–Mn correlation graphs indicate that the two types of calcite veins are calcite formed at low temperatures and are not affected by hydrothermal fluids. The correlation analyses of inorganic carbon and oxygen isotopes indicate that the inorganic carbon isotopes of the two types of calcite veins are significantly lighter, which should be related to the participation of organic fluids during diagenesis, and the forming vein fluids are hydrocarbon-related mixed fluids. The results of equivalent vitrinite reflectance (R oeq) converted from solid bitumen reflectance (R b), formation temperature (T burial), and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) indicate that the bitumen in calcite veins is highly overmature pyrobitumen, which is formed under different temperature conditions compared with calcite veins. By comparing the organic carbon isotope composition (δ13Corg) of pyrobitumen in two types of calcite veins with kerogen organic carbon isotope composition of the black gray limestone for surrounding rock, as well as that of the main source rocks of Mesoproterozoic-Lower Paleozoic in the basin, combined with the spatial distribution of the these source rocks, it is indicated that the pyrobitumen in two types of calcite veins is related to the sedimentary organic matter of the surrounding rock. The sources of vein-forming fluids for the two types of calcite veins and pyrobitumen are closely related to the near-source hydrocarbons associated with the organic matter of the surrounding rock, suggesting that the Cambrian in the Ordos Basin develops effective source rocks.

Introduction

As an important secondary organic matter, bitumen is extensively found in source rocks and reservoirs in petroliferous sedimentary basins, , which is closely related to the generation, migration, accumulation, and evolution of oil and gas in geological history. With the deepening of oil and gas exploration, the research of solid bitumen plays an increasingly significant role in the oil and gas exploration of deep and ultradeep carbonate rocks. The solid bitumen in marine carbonate reservoirs records various geological and geochemical processes after the formation of oil and gas, which is highly significant for identifying and evaluating the extent and size of paleo-hydrocarbon reservoirs. Fluid activity in the geological history period is closely related to the generation, migration, and accumulation of oil and gas in hydrocarbon-bearing basins, and calcite veins are an important product of fluid activity in petroliferous sedimentary basins. The research on bituminous calcite veins in reservoirs of many sedimentary basins around the world has shown that the formation of these calcite veins is closely related to the migration and evolution of hydrocarbons. Studying the genesis and source of the calcite vein and the hydrocarbons contained in the calcite vein plays an important role in the migration and accumulation of oil and gas in carbonate reservoirs, and the occurrence of bitumen in the calcite vein is an effective indication for hydrocarbons migration. ,

To date, little research has been conducted on bitumen from Cambrian carbonates in the Ordos Basin. Huang et al. − suggested that bitumen in the middle and upper Cambrian carbonate reservoirs in the southern and eastern margins of the Ordos Basin may originate from the source rocks of the Dongpo Formation distributed in the southern margin of the basin by means of fluorescence microscopy, scanning electron microscopy-energy spectrometry, laser Raman spectroscopy, organic and inorganic carbon isotopes, and element ratios. However, the geochemical characteristics of bitumen in Cambrian carbonate reservoirs in different areas of the Ordos Basin may be different, and their sources are controversial. There is also a lack of specialized and systematic studies on the micropetrological characteristics, geochemical characteristics, and sources of bitumen in the Cambrian Xuzhuang Formation of Qingshuihe area in the northeastern margin of the basin. Particularly, there is no study on bituminous calcite veins of Cambrian Xuzhuang Formation in the Ordos Basin, and relevant research about the source of diagenetic fluids for calcite veins and the relationship with hydrocarbon fluids is absent.

In this paper, the bituminous calcite veins and surrounding rocks of the Cambrian Xuzhuang Formation from the Jiajiawan section of the Qingshuihe area in the northeastern Ordos Basin was systematically analyzed by microscopy (transmission light, fluorescence), scanning electron microscopy with focused ion double-beam system (SEM-EDS), XRF element content, solid bitumen reflectance (R b), inorganic carbon and oxygen isotope composition (δ13Ccarb, δ18Ocarb) and organic carbon isotope composition (δ13Corg). The microscopic petrological characteristics of bituminous calcite veins and surrounding rocks were analyzed, and the source of vein-forming fluids of calcite veins and the relationship with hydrocarbon fluids were discussed in depth. The thermal evolution degree, genesis, and source of bitumen in calcite vein and its oil and gas geological significance were also discussed. The Cambrian has always been the main source rock for numerous energy basins in China, such as the Tarim and the Sichuan Basins. However, there is no major breakthrough in the Cambrian oil and gas exploration in the Ordos Basin so far. The total organic carbon content (TOC) of the Cambrian strata in the Ordos Basin is generally low, but bitumen and calcite veins have been widely discovered in the reservoirs. Therefore, it is necessary to study the relationship between fluid activities and hydrocarbons in the Cambrian reservoirs of the basin, as well as the development characteristics and sources of bitumen. The research of bituminous calcite veins from the Cambrian Xuzhuang Formation has great significance in exploring the role of fluid activity on the migration, accumulation, and evolution of oil and gas for the Lower Paleozoic carbonate rocks in the northeastern margin of the Ordos Basin. Moreover, our research has clarified the thermal evolution characteristics and sources of pyrobitumen in the calcite veins of the Xuzhuang Formation in the northeastern margin of the basin by combining microscopic petrological characteristics and geochemical characteristics, suggesting that the Cambrian system in the Ordos Basin may develop effective source rocks, and it provides some scientific basis for deep and ultradeep oil-gas exploration in the basin.

Geological Setting

The Ordos Basin is a typical hydrocarbon-bearing superimposed basin on the edge of the craton in the western part of the North China Plate, with extensive development of lower Paleozoic marine sedimentary strata. , The Cambrian sediments are relatively thick in the southern and western parts of the basin and thin in the interior of the basin, and relatively thick Cambrian marine sediments are also developed in the eastern and northeastern margins of the basin.

The location of this study is the Jiajiawan section in the Qingshuihe area, northeast margin of the Ordos Basin (Figure a). Through field measurement, the section develops from bottom to top the lower Cambrian Huoshan Formation and Mantou Formation, the middle Cambrian Maozhuang Formation, Xuzhuang Formation, and Zhangxia Formation, and the upper Cambrian Sanshanzi Formation (Figure b). The lithology of Huoshan Formation is mainly gray red sandstone with a thickness of about 39.6 m, and the Mantou Formation is dominated by brownish red silty mudstone with a thickness of about 44.8 m, and the Maozhuang Formation is dominated by brownish red mudstone with a thickness of about 21 m. The lithology of the Xuzhuang Formation is mainly medium-thin gray black limestone with a thickness of about 26m, and the Zhangxia Formation is dominated by gray oolitic limestone with a thickness of about 12 m, and the Sanshanzi Formation is dominated by purple yellow wormkalk and gray argillaceous strip limestone with a thickness of about 12 m.

According to the field occurrence, there are mainly two types of bituminous calcite veins of the Cambrian Xuzhuang Formation from the Jiajiawan section in the Qingshuihe area, northeast margin of the basin (Figure ). One is a fibrous bituminous calcite vein parallel to the bedding of the surrounding rock, with a thickness of about 8 cm, extending laterally. It is coarse-grained calcite with obvious cleavage development, and a fresh surface split in the middle contains bitumen, and the two sides are relatively pure calcite. The other kind of bituminous calcite vein, interspersed with surrounding rock bedding, is dendritic, with a width of 3–8 cm, which is fine-grained calcite, and cleavage is not developed, containing bitumen veins in the middle for some samples. The surrounding rocks of both types of calcite are black-gray limestone, and part of them is marlstone. There is a lot of bitumen visible to the naked eye in both types of calcite veins, and bitumen can also be seen in limestone dissolution caves.

Materials and Methods

Materials

The samples in this study were collected from the Cambrian Xuzhuang Formation of the Jiajiawan section in the Qingshuihe area, northeast margin of the basin (Figure a).

In the field, a total of 5 bituminous calcite veins, 4 pure calcite veins, 5 surrounding limestone (black-gray limestone), and 6 other black-gray limestone samples were collected in the carbonate reservoir of the Cambrian Xuzhuang Formation. Fresh samples were selected and sorted for microscopic petrography, XRF elements, solid bitumen reflectance (R b), inorganic carbon and oxygen isotopes, organic carbon isotopes, and total organic carbon (TOC) analysis. The above experiments were completed at the State Key Laboratory of Continental Dynamics, Northwest University.

Methods

Micropetrological Analysis

Microscopic petrological observation of bituminous calcite veins and surrounding rocks was performed under an Axio Scope.A1 dual-purpose fluorescence polarizing microscope (equipped with 50W/100W high-pressure halogen lamp) of the ZEISS Company, and the transmission light and fluorescence microscopic observation of the cast and fluorescent thin sections prepared in advance were carried out. The gold-plated fluorescent thin sections were placed in the Helios G4 UC focused ion dual beam system manufactured by Thermo Fisher Scientific for scanning electron microscope-energy spectrum (SEM-EDS) analysis, with a STEM resolution of 0.6 nm at 30 kV, and an EDS elemental analytical range of Be4 ∼ U92.

XRF Analysis

The major elements were analyzed with the Skyray Instrument Explorer 7000 portable hand-held X-ray fluorescence spectroscopy (XRF).

Solid Bitumen Reflectance (R b) Analysis

Bitumen reflectance (Rb) tests were carried out under TIDASMSP200 Axio Scope.A1 test system of J&M and ZEISS Company, consisting of the TIDAS PMT IV microspectrophotometer, the ZEISS Axio Scope.A1 microscope, the MSP200 specular reflectance test software, and the Axiovision camera software, with in situ testing on prepared light plates.

Inorganic Carbon and Oxygen Isotopes Analysis

The test of inorganic carbon and oxygen isotopes was carried out on a Thermo Finnigan 253 plus IRMS equipped with GasBench II. An appropriate amount of the powder sample that was ground to 200 mesh was loaded into a small glass tube and placed on the sample injector to blow He gas to expel the air, and then anhydrous solid phosphoric acid was added to react at 70 °C for 2 h. The resulting CO2 was transferred to IRMS for carbon and oxygen isotope composition analysis. The isotope data were VPDB standard, and the standard deviations of carbon and oxygen isotope analysis were below 0.06 and 0.08‰, respectively.

Organic Carbon Isotope Analysis

The organic carbon isotope test was performed on a Thermo Finnigan 253 plus IRMS equipped with Flash 2000 HT EA. Powder samples ground to less than 100 mesh were placed in a permeable crucible, and inorganic carbon was removed by rinsing with excess dilute hydrochloric acid solution (volume HCL/volume distilled water = 1:7). After setting overnight and water bath at 80 °C for three h, the powder was washed with distilled water to pH neutral, and then the dried powder was wrapped with tinfoil and tested on the machine. The isotope data were corrected by the standard sample GBW04407 (carbon black, δ13C = −22.43‰ ± 0.07), and the isotope data were the VPDB standard.

Total Organic Carbon (TOC) Analysis

The TOC analysis was detected by an ELTRA CS 800 carbon–sulfur analyzer. 0.2 g of powder sample less than 100 mesh was weighed on a balance and then placed in a permeable crucible, and the other pretreatment procedures were the same as those for organic carbon isotopes. The analytical precision was better than 0.1%.

Results

Microscopic Petrological Analysis of Bituminous Calcite Veins and Surrounding Rocks

As observed under the microscope (Figure ), the bitumen presented within the calcite veins of the Xuzhuang Formation in the Qingshuihe area predominantly occurs in intercrystalline pores, microfractures, cleavage joints, crystal surfaces, and dissolution pores. Most bitumen is black and does not fluoresce. A small amount shows black-brown fluorescence, indicating high overmaturity. Previous studies have shown that it is easy to confuse the content of inorganic carbon in carbonate minerals (such as calcite or dolomite) with the content of organic carbon in bitumen when analyzing the energy spectrum of bituminous carbonate samples directly. In this study, the energy spectrum of carbonate minerals and bitumen in calcite veins was analyzed directly under a focused ion double-beam scanning electron microscope with fluorescent thin sections. This method can better identify the elemental composition of bitumen and surrounding rock. SEM-EDS analysis (Figure ) demonstrates that the bitumen presented within the bituminous calcite veins is predominantly composed of carbon (C) elements, exhibiting an average content that exceeds 80%. It is noteworthy that certain bitumen samples exhibit 100% carbon content. Some bitumen contains a small amount of the element oxygen (O), with a content range of 15.91–17.94%. The calcite surrounding the bitumen is a combination of the elemental contents of carbon (C), oxygen (O), and calcium (Ca). The content range of C is 16.06–19.40%, the content range of O is 47.21–49.68%, and the content range of Ca is 31.40–35.92%. In addition, there is also a small amount of bitumen in the surrounding rock (black-gray limestone), with a carbon (C) content ranging from 80.20 to 100%, an oxygen (O) content ranging from 0% to 19.19%, and a trace amount of silicon (Si). The surrounding rock (black-gray limestone) is also mainly an elemental composition of carbon (C), oxygen (O), and calcium (Ca), with C content ranging from 16.05 to 16.63%, O content ranging from 46.53 to 48.10%, and Ca content ranging from 35.27 to 37.43%.

Discussion

Source of Diagenetic Fluids for Calcite Veins and the Relationship with Hydrocarbon Fluids

The elemental content and composition of carbonate minerals can be used to determine the type and source of fluids during crystallization, with Mg, Fe, Mn, and Al being the most widely used elements. The Al/(Al + Fe + Mn) ratio of marine sediments can indicate and evaluate the source and contributing materials of hydrothermal activity. Generally, the siliceous rocks formed by hydrothermal deposition near the midocean ridge have a low Al/(Al + Fe + Mn) ratio, with an average value of 0.01, while the biogenic siliceous rocks and abyssal-bathyal claystone analogs that are not affected by hydrothermal deposits have high Al/(Al + Fe + Mn) ratios, with an average of 0.6 and 0.54, respectively. , Hydrothermal deposits and hydrothermally affected marine sedimentary rocks generally have high Fe and Mn contents and low Al content, while nonhydrothermally affected marine sedimentary rocks have relatively low Fe and Mn contents and high Al content. Therefore, the Al–Fe–Mn triangle diagram and Al/(Al + Fe + Mn) ratio can be used to evaluate the degree of influence of hydrothermal activity on marine sediments. As can be seen from Figure a, in the Al–Fe–Mn triangle diagram, both types of calcite veins fall in the region of nonhydrothermal genesis, and their Al/(Al + Fe + Mn) ratios range from 0.98 to 0.99, with an average value of 0.99 (Table ), which indicates that the precipitation of the two types of calcite veins of the Cambrian Xuzhuang Formation in the study area was not affected by hydrothermal activity.

Distribution diagram of major elements in calcite veins of the Xuzhuang Formation. Al–Fe–Mn triangle diagram of calcite veins (a) (based on Adachi et al.). Mg–Fe–Mn triangle diagram of calcite veins (b).

Calcite derived from marine materials usually has a high content of Mg, while the content of trace elements in calcite formed in atmospheric water at the margins of the basin is relatively low, and the content of Mn is relatively high compared with Mg and Fe. , Generally, calcite precipitated at high temperature has high Mn and Fe content, whereas calcite precipitated at low temperature has relatively low Mn and Fe content and relatively high Mg content. Therefore, the Mg–Fe–Mn triangle diagram can be used to further determine the formation environment of marine sediments. As can be seen from Figure b, both types of calcite veins of the Cambrian Xuzhuang Formation in the study area have high Mg content and low Fe and Mn content, indicating that the two types of calcite veins were precipitated at a lower temperature, which is consistent with the result that the two types of calcite were not affected by hydrothermal activities, as indicated by the Al–Fe–Mn triangle diagram.

Inorganic carbon and oxygen isotopic compositions can be used to ascertain the fluid source of carbonate minerals and cement as well as the interactions between fluids and surrounding rocks. ,, Comparative analysis of inorganic carbon and oxygen isotopes of bituminous calcite veins and surrounding rocks in reservoirs is conducive to deepening the understanding of the relationship between fluid activity and hydrocarbon migration, accumulation, and evolution in petroliferous basins. In general, dissolution and recrystallization of carbonate rocks do not produce large carbon isotope fractionation. The carbon isotope of calcite is mainly affected by the source of inorganic carbon and organic carbon, while its oxygen isotope is mainly controlled by the oxygen isotope composition and temperature of the vein-forming fluid. From Figure a, it can be seen that the inorganic carbon and oxygen isotopic compositions of two types of calcite veins, mainly parallel-bedded fibrous calcite veins (calcite vein-1 and bituminous calcite vein-1) and dendritic calcite veins (calcite vein-2 and bituminous calcite vein-2), are significantly different from those of the black gray limestone-surrounding rock (Surrounding Rock-Limestone). It shows that the diagenetic fluids forming the calcite veins did not derive solely from dissolution and recrystallization of the surrounding carbonate rocks. Overall, the inorganic carbon isotope composition δ13Ccarb of both types of calcite veins is significantly lighter than that of the surrounding rocks, especially the bituminous calcite-bearing veins (Figure a). Among them, the average δ13Ccarb of pure calcite veins is 1.5‰ lighter than that of the surrounding rocks, and the average δ13Ccarb of bituminous calcite-bearing veins is 1.9‰ lighter than that of the surrounding rocks. Meanwhile, the δ13Ccarb of a sample from the transition zone for the calcite vein and surrounding rock is between that of pure calcite vein and surrounding rock, which is 0.4‰ lighter than the average δ13Ccarb of the surrounding rock.

Distribution characteristics (a) and linear correlation analysis (b) of inorganic carbon and oxygen isotopes for calcite veins, bituminous calcite veins, and surrounding rock-limestone of Xuzhuang Formation.

There have been many related studies on the mechanism of lighter inorganic carbon isotopes for fluid and carbonate minerals in sedimentary basins. Hudson believed that the fluid in the sedimentary basin may be 13C-poor, due to the contribution of 13C-depleted C produced by the thermal reaction of organic matter. Irwin et al. showed that during diagenesis, the organic carbon in the fluid will be converted to HCO3 – and enter the carbonate minerals as the formation of 12C, and the added organic carbon will lead to the deficit of 13C in the diagenetic fluid. Meanwhile, in the interaction between hydrocarbons and surrounding rocks, when the temperature is high, the high-temperature oxidation of organic carbon in oil and kerogen can produce CO2 from organic sources, which may lead to the lightening of inorganic carbon isotope for carbonate cements in reservoirs or migration channels. Moreover, the CO2 produced by the thermal decarboxylation of organic matter is depleted in 13C, with the δ13C in the range of −8 ∼ −23‰, and the δ13C of CaCO3 formed by this CO2 involvement is negative. Therefore, the obvious negative deviation of inorganic carbon isotope δ13Ccarb for two types of calcite veins of the Xuzhuang Formation in the Jiajiwan Section is related to the participation of organic fluids in diagenesis, and especially the formation of bituminous calcite veins is closely related to the participation of hydrocarbon fluids and thermal evolution.

Previous studies have suggested that if the inorganic carbon and oxygen isotopes δ13C and δ18O of calcite veins have a significant linear correlation, it indicates that they are precipitated from the mixed fluid. , This covariation of δ13C and δ18O in calcite veins is mainly explained by two processes: One is the precipitation of calcite caused by the mixing of two fluids with different isotopic compositions, and the other is the precipitation of calcite under the temperature effect of fluid-rock interaction, with H2CO3 acting as the main dissolved carbon source, accompanied by the emission of CO2. , It can be seen from Figure b that the inorganic carbon and oxygen isotopes δ13Ccarb and δ18Ocarb of the two types of calcite vein have obvious linear correlation, and the correlation coefficient R 2 is 0.6, which further indicates that the vein-forming fluid of the two types of calcite veins of Xuzhuang Formation in the Jiajiawan Section may be the mixed fluid formed by the interaction of hydrocarbon fluids and surrounding rock. Similar studies have also been conducted in other areas. Li et al. researched the fibrous bituminous calcite veins of the Lower Cambrian-Triassic in the Daba Mountain structural belt of the South Qinling Mountains, and showed that the inorganic carbon isotope of fibrous bituminous calcite veins was significantly lighter than that of the surrounding rock, and the inorganic carbon and oxygen isotope of the calcite veins had an obvious linear correlation, indicating that the vein-forming fluid was mixed fluid associated with hydrocarbon organic fluids. The study of Wu et al. on the parallel-bedded calcite veins of the Silurian Longmaxi Formation in the southern Sichuan Basin also showed that the parallel-bedded bituminous calcite veins had lower inorganic carbon isotopes than the surrounding rocks, indicating that calcite veins were formed by precipitation of mixed fluids with the incorporation of δ13C-depleted CO2 produced by the thermal degradation of organic matter. This process is often related to the production of CH4 and CO2 by hydrocarbon cracking.

Wu et al. drew the δ13CV‑PDB-δ18OV‑SMOW diagram based on previous isotope data to determine the source of parallel-bedded calcite veins of the Silurian Longmaxi Formation in the southern gas field of Sichuan Basin. In the diagram, the data of marine carbonate rocks are from Veizer and Hoefs, Hudson and Shen et al., and the data of mantle fluids are from Hugh and Taylor. Atmospheric water data are from Hoefs and Zheng, and organic matter data are from Deines and Hudson. In order to further determine that the two types of calcite veins of Xuzhuang Formation in the Jiajiawan Section are sources of mixed fluids associated with hydrocarbon fluids, based on the δ13CV‑PDB-δ18OV‑SMOW diagram of Wu et al., we cast the inorganic carbon and oxygen isotope data of two types of calcite veins, surrounding rocks and transition zone samples for surrounding rocks and calcite veins on the δ13CV‑PDB-δ18OV‑SMOW chart (Figure ). The formula of the conversion from δ18Ocarb‑V‑PDB to δ18Ocarb‑V‑SMOW is quoted from Coplen et al. As can be seen from Figure , except for one sample, all the other black gray limestone for surrounding rock fall in the marine carbonate zone, and one transition zone sample of the surrounding rock and calcite vein falls in the junction of marine carbonate and mixed fluid zone, while all the two types of calcite veins fall within the mixed fluid zone, which further proves that the diagenetic fluids of the two types of calcite vein for the Xuzhuang Formation in the Jiajiawan section are derived from the interaction of hydrocarbon fluids and surrounding rocks. The oxygen isotope composition δ18Ocarb of the two types of calcite veins is also significantly lighter than that of the surrounding rocks, with the average oxygen isotope δ18Ocarb of pure calcite veins being 5.8‰ lighter than that of the surrounding rocks, and the average oxygen isotope δ18Ocarb of bituminous calcite veins being 4.9‰ lighter than that of the surrounding rocks (Figure a). Previous studies have shown that the mechanism of lower oxygen isotope in calcite veins is mainly related to the influence of hydrothermal fluids or atmospheric precipitation, as both of them have low oxygen isotope compositions. , In addition, oxygen isotopes in carbonate reservoirs of sedimentary basins are also affected by the mixing of different fluids and the interactions between hydrocarbon fluids and surrounding rocks. , From the previous text, it is known that the two types of calcite veins for the Xuzhuang Formation are the sources of mixed fluids that formed by the interaction between hydrocarbon fluids and surrounding rocks, and as can be seen from Figure , the oxygen isotope compositions of the two types of calcite veins for the Xuzhuang Formation are both located in the mixed fluid area; therefore, the oxygen isotopes of the two types of calcite veins for the Xuzhuang Formation are mainly affected by the isotopes of the mixed fluid, resulting in their oxygen isotopes being significantly different from those of the surrounding rocks. Later atmospheric precipitation may also affect the oxygen isotope composition of calcite veins if the strata are uplifted and exposed at the surface.

Thermal Evolution Degree and Source of Bitumen in Calcite Veins

The reflectance of solid bitumen is an effective index to characterize the maturity of thermal evolution. , The average reflectance (R b) of bitumen in calcite veins and black gray limestone for the surrounding rock of the Xuzhuang Formation in the Jiajiawan section is 1.64 and 1.43%, respectively, indicating high maturity. According to the conversion formula of solid bitumen reflectance (R b) and equivalent vitrinite reflectance (R oeq) (Schoenherr et al.): R oeq = (R b + 0.2443)/1.0495, the R oeq of bitumen in the two types of calcite veins ranges from 1.56 to 2.29%, with an average value of 1.79% (Figure a), and the R oeq of bitumen in the surrounding rock ranges from 1.51 to 1.68%, with an average value of 1.59%, suggesting that the bitumen in the calcite veins and surrounding rock of Xuzhuang Formation in the northeast margin of the basin is highly overmature, and the maturity of bitumen in the calcite veins is higher than that in the surrounding rock. According to the previous conversion formula of the equivalent vitrinite reflectance of solid bitumen (R oeq) and the burial temperature of normal strata (T burial)­(Barker and Pawlewicz): T burial = (ln­(R oeq) + 1.68)/0.0124, the range for calculated T burial of bitumen in calcite veins is 171–202 °C, with an average T burial of 182 °C (Figure b), and the range for calculated T burial of bitumen in black gray limestone for surrounding rock is 169–177 °C, with an average T burial of 173 °C. The temperature at which oil begins to pyrolyze in reservoirs is generally 150–190 °C, , indicating that the bitumen in the calcite veins of Xuzhuang Formation is highly mature pyrobitumen formed by oil cracking, which is consistent with the results of fluorescence microscopy and energy spectrum analysis. Meanwhile, the R oeq and T burial of bitumen in calcite veins are higher than those of bitumen in the surrounding rock, suggesting that the bitumen in calcite veins underwent higher thermal evolution compared to the bitumen in the surrounding rock.

Solid bitumen reflectance (R b) and the calculated equivalent vitrinite reflectance (R oeq) (a), and the calculated burial temperature of normal strata (T burial) (b) for bituminous calcite veins and surrounding rock-limestone of Xuzhuang Formation.

Under microscopic observation, pyrobitumen usually has a clearer and straighter boundary than bitumen of other genesis, , with a clear boundary and certain morphology, which is obviously different from the bitumen formed by cold metamorphism (biodegradation, oxidation washing, etc.). At the same time, compared with the bitumen formed at stage of oil-generation window with low maturity and without significant nanopores, there are many secondary organic nanomesopores (5–50 nm) and nanomacropores (>50 nm) on the surface of the pyrobitumen formed at stage of gas-generation window with high maturity or over maturity, which is caused by the process of oil cracking. , Under focused ion beam scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (Figure ), the bitumen in the calcite veins of Xuzhuang Formation has a clear boundary with the peripheral mineral calcite, and the appearance and morphological characteristics are prominent. A lot of pyrobitumen has relatively regular boundaries, and some of them even show very straight and long stripes. Meanwhile, there are abundant secondary organic pores on the surface of pyrobitumen, which further indicates that the pyrobitumen in the calcite veins of the Xuzhuang Formation was formed by the cracking of petroleum and hydrocarbons at the stage of the gas-generation window, and the thermal cracking of hydrocarbon fluids to produce gases, such as CH4 and CO2, left a large number of secondary organic pores in the residual pyrobitumen at that time.

In general, the organic carbon isotope δ13Corg of solid bitumen formed by thermal cracking is about 2–3‰ heavier than that of crude oil, while the organic carbon isotope δ13Corg of crude oil is about 1–2‰ lighter than that of its source rock kerogen. ,, Therefore, the organic carbon isotope composition of solid bitumen is roughly similar to that of the source rock. Moreover, since solid bitumen is mainly formed by aromatic condensation reaction, the fractionation of carbon isotopes is not obvious in this process, which can be used for the comparative study of bitumen and hydrocarbon sources. − Previous studies suggest that there are presently four sets of main source rocks in the Mesoproterozoic-Lower Paleozoic of the Ordos Basin, including the Changchengian (Pt2ch), the Sinian Dongpo Formation (Z1d), the Middle Ordovician Majiagou Formation (O2m), and the Upper Ordovician Pingliang Formation (O3p). − The source rocks of the Changchengian are mainly distributed in the western and southern parts of the basin, and the lithology is mainly dominated by dark mudstone-shale and carbonaceous argillite, and the organic carbon isotope δ13Corg of their kerogen ranges from −32.5 to −31.3‰, with an average value of −32.1‰. The source rocks of the Sinian Dongpo Formation are mainly distributed in the southern part of the basin, and the lithology is mainly dominated by black shale and carbonaceous argillite; the organic carbon isotope δ13Corg of their kerogen in this study ranges from −27.8 to −30.7‰, with an average value of −29‰. The source rocks of the Middle Ordovician Majiagou Formation are mainly distributed in the central and eastern part of the basin, and the lithology is mainly dominated by dolomite mudstone and marl interbedded with evaporates, and the organic carbon isotope δ13Corg of their kerogen ranges from −22.4 to −30.5‰, with an average value of −26.9‰. The source rocks of the Upper Ordovician Pingliang Formation are mainly distributed in the southwestern and western parts of the basin, and the lithology is mainly dominated by marine mudstone and shale, and the organic carbon isotope δ13Corg of their kerogen ranges from −32.1 to 23.4‰, with an average of −28.3‰.

The organic carbon isotope δ13Corg of bitumen in calcite veins of Xuzhuang Formation in the Jiajiawan section ranges from −28.6 to −31.7‰, with an average value of −29.8‰, and that of the black gray limestone for surrounding rock ranges from −27.3 to −31.1‰, with an average value of −29.3‰. By comparing the organic carbon isotope δ13Corg range and mean value of bitumen in calcite veins and black gray limestone for surrounding rock of Xuzhuang Formation, as well as the kerogen of main source rocks of the mesoproterozoic-lower Paleozoic in the basin (Figure ), the results show that the range and average value of organic carbon isotope δ13Corg of bitumen in calcite veins for Xuzhuang Formation are significantly different from those of the source rocks for the Mesoproterozoic Changchengian, Ordovician Majiagou Formation and Ordovician Pingliang Formation, but they are close to those of black gray limestone for surrounding rock and source rocks of Sinian Dongpo Formation. The source rocks of the Sinian Dongpo Formation are distributed in the southern margin of the basin and have not been found in other areas of the basin, so it is very unlikely that the bitumen in the calcite vein of Xuzhuang Formation in the Jiajiawan section from Qingshuihe area in the northeast margin of the basin is derived from the source rocks of the Sinian Dongpo Formation in the southern margin. The range and average value of organic carbon isotopes δ13Corg from marine-continental transitional limestone source rocks of the Upper Paleozoic Carboniferous-Permian in the central and eastern part of the basin are −23.8 ∼ −26.9 and −25‰, which indicates that the bitumen in the calcite veins of Xuzhuang Formation cannot be derived from the Upper Paleozoic. The organic carbon isotope δ13Corg of bitumen in calcite veins of the Xuzhuang Formation is very close to that of the dark gray limestone for the surrounding rock, indicating that the secondary organic matter of bitumen formed by the petroleum evolution is related to the primary organic matter of the surrounding rock (dark gray limestone), and the results of the above research show that the organic fluids of near-source hydrocarbon participate in the precipitation of calcite veins of the Xuzhuang Formation, which synthetically indicates that the pyrobitumen in calcite veins of the Xuzhuang Formation is formed by thermal evolution of the near-source hydrocarbon fluids.

Formation Process of Calcite Veins and Bitumen

The previous analysis by Al–Fe–Mn and Mg–Fe–Mn triangle diagrams shows that the two phases of calcite veins were formed by mixing fluids at lower temperatures and were not affected by hydrothermal fluids. However, the formation temperature of pyrobitumen calculated by equivalent vitrinite reflectance (R oeq) in the calcite veins ranges from 171 to 202 °C, with an average of 182 °C, which was formed by thermal cracking of hydrocarbons at higher formation temperatures, indicating that calcite veins and pyrobitumen were formed under different original temperature conditions. Under focused ion beam scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (Figure ), plenty of pyrobitumen is filled in calcite intercrystalline fractures and cleavage seams in a very regular and straight form, and lots of pyrobitumen is filled in dissolved microfractures and dissolved pores in the later stage, which also indicates that the formation of calcite was earlier than that of pyrobitumen. After the crystallization of calcites, some calcites developed regular intercrystalline and cleavage seams, and the later residual hydrocarbons filled these fractures and were further pyrolyzed into pyrobitumen with the increase of stratum temperature, forming a regular and straight filling form of pyrobitumen with obvious shrinkage cracks on the surface. Under the fluorescence microscope, it can also be clearly seen that the pyrobitumen is mainly developed in the intercrystalline fractures, microfractures, mineral surfaces, and later dissolved pores-fractures of calcites, which also indicates that the pyrobitumen was formed by hydrocarbon cracking after the formation of calcite veins.

The formation of calcite veins is generally related to fracture systems generated by regional tectonic activities and formation fluid overpressure, and later diagenesis, such as dissolution, redissolution, cementation, and recrystallization, can also produce nascent or hyperplastic calcite. During the generation of hydrocarbons from organic matter, formation fluid overpressure will be generated due to the increase of pore fluid pressure, which can lead to the formation of horizontal fractures. The fractures caused by tectonic activity and fluid overpressure provide channels for fluid activity and space for precipitation and crystallization of calcite veins. The mixed fluid formed by the interaction of hydrocarbons and surrounding rocks crystallized to form fibrous calcite veins in the fractures that were parallel to the bedding of the surrounding rock, and the residual hydrocarbons in the microfractures, cleavage seams, and dissolved pores of the calcites were pyrolyzed to form pyrobitumen. Meanwhile, under the diagenesis of pressure dissolution, redissolution, cementation, and recrystallization with the participation of formation water, hydrocarbons, and other fluids, the black gray limestone for the surrounding rock could form dendritic bituminous calcite veins.

Oil-Gas Geological Significance of Bituminous Calcite Veins

Previous studies have shown that the parallel-bedded fibrous bituminous calcite veins are closely related to hydrocarbon migration and formation fluid overpressure. Moreover, the diagenetic fluids of parallel-bedded fibrous calcite veins in petroliferous basins are often near-source mixed fluids formed by the interaction of hydrocarbon, formation water, and surrounding rock. , The vein-forming fluids of the two types of calcite veins for the Xuzhuang Formation in the Jiajiawan section are both near-source mixed fluids associated with hydrocarbon fluids, and bitumen occurs in both calcite veins, suggesting that the fluid activities of the Cambrian Xuzhuang Formation in the northeast margin of the basin are related to hydrocarbon activities. At the same time, the organic matter that forms the pyrobitumen is mainly the near-source organic matter related to the dark gray limestone for the surrounding rock, indicating that there was migration and thermal evolution of near-source hydrocarbons for the Xuzhuang Formation in the northeastern margin of the basin at that time. In the Jiajiawan section of the Qingshuihe area, the Cambrian Xuzhuang Formation deposited only carbonate rocks. The overall TOC of its blackish-gray limestone was relatively low, and the accumulation of trace organic matter might be the source of pyrobitumen in the calcite veins. The drilling cores in the northern and northeastern parts of the Ordos Basin show that the Xuzhuang Formation mainly develops two sets of rock types. The one is carbonate types, which mainly includes limestone, dolomite, argillaceous limestone, and argillaceous dolomite, and the other is mudstone types, which mainly includes mudstone, calcareous mudstone, and dolomitic mudstone. The mudstone types of the Xuzhuang Formation in some drilling cores in the northeastern part of the basin have relatively high TOC, and the mudstones of Xuzhuang Formation such as Sheng 2 well have TOC of 0.29%, which may also be the source of organic matter in the calcite veins of the Cambrian Xuzhuang Formation in Jiajiawan section from Qingshuihe area in the northeast margin of the basin.

The solid bitumen in different evolution stages of thermal maturity reflects the formation and evolution of hydrocarbons in different stages, and the pyrobitumen in the calcite veins of Xuzhuang Formation has reached an overmature state. The presence of pyrobitumen is usually a marker of reservoir thermal cracking, which is also a potential bridge between crude oil, cracking gas, and its source rocks. According to the genetic theory of crude oil cracking gas, the formation of a large amount of pyrobitumen is bound to be accompanied by the generation of a lot of thermal cracking natural gas. In general, during the thermal cracking of crude oil, the generation of 1 kg of pyrobitumen corresponds to the generation of about 9–19 m3 of natural gas. A large amount of pyrobitumen occurs in the two types of calcite veins for the Xuzhuang Formation in the study area, with the abundance of secondary organic pores on the surface of the pyrobitumen under scanning electron microscopy (SEM), indicating that the petroleum began to crack and was accompanied by large quantities of natural gas of thermal cracking genesis. Meanwhile, the development of near-source bitumen in the northeast margin of the basin implies that the Cambrian of the Ordos Basin developed effective source rocks and underwent a hydrocarbon formation process. This study has some important theoretical significance for understanding the relationship between Cambrian fluid activity and hydrocarbon migration, as well as the exploration of natural gas in the northeast margin of the basin.

Conclusions

This research is based on the comprehensive petrological characteristics and geochemical characteristics of bituminous calcite veins and their surrounding rocks. The relationship between the source of vein-forming fluids for the two types of bituminous calcite veins and hydrocarbon fluids, the thermal evolution degree and source of pyrobitumen in calcite veins, and the oil-gas geological significance of bituminous calcite veins is thoroughly discussed. The main conclusions are as follows:1.The two types of bituminous calcite veins of the Cambrian Xuzhuang Formation are precipitated at lower temperatures and are not affected by hydrothermal activities.2.The significantly lighter inorganic carbon isotopes of the two types of bituminous calcite veins are related to the participation of organic fluids in diagenesis, and the vein-forming fluids are the mixed fluids formed by the interaction of hydrocarbon fluids and surrounding rocks.3.The bitumen in the calcite veins is highly overmature pyrobitumen, which is closely related to the organic matter of the surrounding rock in oil-gas geological and geochemical characteristics, indicating that they are derived from the thermal evolution of near-source hydrocarbons associated with the organic matter of the surrounding rocks, implying that the Cambrian of the Ordos Basin develops effective source rocks.

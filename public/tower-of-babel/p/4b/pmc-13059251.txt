Risk stratification for gallstones based on 14 obesity and lipid-related Indices: a longitudinal cohort study of 44,030 Chinese adults
Authors: Hai-feng Zhang, Jun-long Tao, Chang-chen Liang, Yu-ting Wei, Jia-min Chen, Xiao-na Wang, Yi Ning, Peng Li, Bo Bi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundGallstone disease is one of the most prevalent digestive disorders globally, showing a trend toward increasing incidence and younger onset in Asian populations. Against this backdrop, this study aims to investigate the associations between 14 different obesity and lipid-related indices and gallstones, and to compare their predictive value for gallstone screening in the Chinese population.MethodsThis study included 44,030 adult participants from the Beijing MJ Health Screening Center (2009–2023). Individuals with a history of cholecystectomy or gallstones at baseline were excluded. Gallstones were diagnosed via abdominal B-mode ultrasonography. Fourteen indices were calculated and evaluated, including BMI, WHtR, BRI, ABSI, LAP, VAI, CMI, CVAI, RFM, AIP, TyG, TyG-BMI, TyG-WC, and TyG-WHtR. Cox proportional hazards regression models were employed to assess the associations between these indices and gallstone risk, with stratified analyses performed by age (< 45 and ≥ 45 years) and sex. Restricted cubic splines (RCS) were used to evaluate non-linear relationships, and the performance and accuracy of prediction models were assessed using the C-index, calibration curves, Integrated Discrimination Improvement (IDI), and Net Reclassification Improvement (NRI).ResultsDuring the follow-up period, a total of 933 (2.12%) participants were diagnosed with new-onset gallstones. Multivariate-adjusted analyses revealed that, with the exception of ABSI, the remaining 13 indices were significantly and positively associated with gallstone risk. Stratified analysis uncovered significant population heterogeneity: CVAI demonstrated the optimal predictive ability in the young male, young female, and middle-aged/elderly male subgroups (C-indices: 0.611, 0.673, and 0.603, respectively), significantly outperforming other indices. Conversely, WHtR exhibited the best predictive performance in the middle-aged/elderly female subgroup (C-index: 0.589). Further validation indicated that the prediction model based on CVAI (for adult males and young females) possessed favorable discrimination and calibration, whereas the WHtR-based model (for middle-aged/elderly females) performed suboptimally.ConclusionThis study confirms that 13 obesity and lipid-related indices, excluding ABSI, are independent risk factors for gallstones in the Chinese population. The results support the implementation of a stratified risk assessment strategy: CVAI is identified as the preferred risk indicator for adult males and young females; for middle-aged and elderly females, WHtR may serve as a preliminary risk assessment tool to identify individuals who warrant further diagnostic evaluation. This study provides a scientific, convenient, and non-invasive assessment basis for the risk stratification and personalized intervention of gallstones in clinical practice.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12944-026-02915-4.

Introduction

Gallstone disease is among the most prevalent disorders of the digestive system globally, with a prevalence ranging from 6% to 15% in the 21st century [1, 2]. Driven by shifts in global lifestyles and the acceleration of population aging, the disease is exhibiting a trend toward younger onset and an expanding affected demographic, imposing a substantial burden on global public health [3, 4]. Epidemiological data indicate that while the baseline prevalence in Asian populations remains lower than in the West, the rapid annual growth rate has become a public health issue that cannot be ignored [5, 6]. The pathological impact of gallstones is extensive; beyond causing acute inflammation such as cholecystitis and cholangitis, or rare complications like gallstone ileus, their potential hazards extend to the systemic metabolic system. Substantial evidence has confirmed significant associations between gallstones and diabetes [7], cardiovascular disease [8], and renal cancer [9]. Most critically, gallstones are a key pathogenic factor for gallbladder cancer [10, 11], directly contributing to increased patient mortality risk. Therefore, identifying effective indicators to screen for and predict the onset and progression of gallstones is of paramount importance.

Obesity, a global health crisis, is inextricably linked to the pathogenesis of various diseases and serves as a prominent risk factor for gallstones [12]. Defined by excessive fat accumulation and/or abnormal distribution, obesity is frequently accompanied by metabolic dysregulation. Notably, there is a distinct divergence in adiposity phenotypes between Eastern and Western populations: Western populations tend to exhibit generalized obesity, whereas Asian populations are more predisposed to central obesity [13]. Conventionally, Body Mass Index (BMI) and Waist Circumference (WC) are used to assess generalized and central obesity, respectively. However, these traditional metrics fail to effectively distinguish between subcutaneous and visceral fat, nor do they reflect glucose and lipid metabolic status [14]. To address these limitations, researchers have developed novel screening tools integrating anthropometric, lipid, and glycemic parameters to better characterize obesity-related features from multiple dimensions. For instance, the Chinese Visceral Adiposity Index (CVAI) is specifically tailored to the Chinese population; by integrating age, sex, BMI, WC, triglycerides, and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, it offers a more accurate assessment of visceral fat distribution and metabolic risk in Asians [15]. Similarly, the Relative Fat Mass (RFM) provides a more precise estimation of whole-body fat percentage than BMI [16], while the Lipid Accumulation Product (LAP) and Visceral Adiposity Index (VAI) serve as superior indicators of body fat distribution and visceral adipose accumulation [17, 18]. Additionally, the Atherogenic Index of Plasma (AIP) acts as a comprehensive marker of lipid profiles [19], and the Triglyceride-Glucose (TyG) index is recognized as an effective surrogate for insulin resistance, with its derivatives gaining increasing attention [20, 21].

Despite the availability of these tools, there is a paucity of comprehensive research investigating the association between these diverse obesity and lipid-related indices and gallstone risk specifically in the Chinese population. Given the phenotypic differences in adiposity between Asian and Western populations, findings derived from Western cohorts may not be directly applicable to Chinese individuals. To bridge this gap, this study systematically compares newly developed screening tools against traditional metrics. By stratifying the population by age and sex, we comprehensively evaluate the predictive potential of 14 obesity and lipid-related indices for gallstone risk. Our objective is to identify the most potent predictors, thereby providing new insights and evidence to guide the prediction and diagnosis of gallstone disease in clinical practice.

Materials and methods

Study design

Participants were recruited from the MJ Health Screening (MJHSC) research project, a population-based annual health examination program open to the general public at the Beijing MJ Health Screening Center, China. Initially, a total of 192,500 participants who underwent routine health examinations between 2009 and 2023 were screened for eligibility. We excluded 109,959 participants based on the following criteria: (1) age < 18 years (n = 150); (2) history of cholecystectomy (n = 1,078); (3) presence of gallstones at baseline (n = 2,317); and (4) loss to follow-up or lack of at least one follow-up visit (n = 106,414). From the remaining 82,541 individuals, a further 38,511 were excluded due to missing data. Specifically, 34,303 participants were excluded for incomplete information on essential covariates (including alcohol consumption, marital status, smoking, sedentary behavior, sleep duration, and physical activity), and an additional 4,208 were excluded due to missing parameters required to calculate the 14 obesity and lipid-related indices. Ultimately, a final cohort of 44,030 participants was included in the analysis. A detailed flowchart of the participant selection process is presented in Fig. 1. The MJHSC data are centrally managed, and all personal information was anonymized prior to analysis to ensure privacy. This study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Health Science Center at Hainan Medical University.

Definition of 14 obesity and lipid-related screening indicators and gallstones

The calculation formulas for these 14 indices are shown in Fig. 2. All indices were calculated based on baseline measurements. The selection of these 14 indices constructs a systematic, multidimensional assessment framework designed to comprehensively dissect the complex role of adiposity in gallstone pathogenesis. Moving beyond the baseline assessment of generalized obesity, this framework progressively incorporates morphological metrics capturing central fat distribution—a phenotype particularly relevant to Asian populations—and extends to functional composite indices reflecting visceral adipose accumulation and insulin resistance. This hierarchical approach, spanning from gross anatomy to systemic metabolic dysfunction, ensures a holistic evaluation of the diverse lithogenic risk profiles.

Diagnosis of gallstones

Gallstones were diagnosed based on abdominal B-mode ultrasonography. As a routine component of the comprehensive health screening, abdominal ultrasound was performed at both baseline and all subsequent follow-up visits. Participants were required to fast prior to the examination. The procedures were conducted by experienced physicians using an Aplio 300 color Doppler ultrasound scanner (Canon Medical Systems, Japan) equipped with a 3.5 MHz probe. A diagnosis of gallstones was confirmed if stable hyperechoic masses were visualized within the gallbladder lumen, accompanied by clear posterior acoustic shadowing and gravity-dependent movement upon postural changes. All participants were followed from the date of their baseline examination until their last follow-up visit occurring prior to December 31, 2023.

Assessments of covariates

Baseline data were meticulously collected by trained interviewers using standardized, self-administered questionnaires. Information regarding sociodemographic characteristics included age (analyzed as a continuous variable), sex (male or female), marital status (classified as unmarried or married), and education level (categorized as college or below, undergraduate, and master’s degree or above). Lifestyle factors were assessed as follows: smoking and alcohol consumption status were classified as “no” or “yes”; sleep duration was categorized into short (< 7 h), normal (7–8 h), and long (≥ 8 h) [22]; and sedentary behavior was classified as either prolonged sitting or prolonged standing based on daily work intensity. Physical activity was assessed by asking, “What types of physical activity have you usually performed in the past two weeks?” and categorized into light, moderate, or vigorous intensity. Medical history, including existing conditions and medication use, was also recorded. Diabetes was defined as a self-reported history or a fasting plasma glucose (FPG) level > 7.00 mmol/L. Hypertension was defined based on self-reported history or the current use of antihypertensive medication. All participants underwent a comprehensive physical examination. Anthropometric measurements, including height, weight, and waist circumference (WC), were obtained by professional nurses using calibrated instruments according to standard protocols. Body mass index (BMI) was calculated as weight in kilograms divided by the square of height in meters (kg/m2). WC was measured at the end of normal expiration at the midpoint between the lowest rib and the iliac crest; if the narrowing was not identifiable, the measurement was taken at the level of the umbilicus.

Fasting venous blood samples were collected from each participant by trained nurses. Serum biochemical analytes, including fasting plasma glucose (FPG), total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), and C-reactive protein (CRP), were analyzed as continuous variables. All samples were processed using an automated biochemical analyzer (Roche Cobas c701, Roche Diagnostics, Germany).

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were presented as mean ± SD or median (IQR) based on normality, while categorical variables were expressed as frequencies (percentages). Group differences were compared using ANOVA, Kruskal-Wallis, or Chi-square tests. Survival curves were plotted via the Kaplan-Meier method with Log-rank tests. Cox proportional hazards models were constructed to assess associations between the 14 indices and gallstone risk across three levels of adjustment: Model 1 (crude), Model 2 (age/sex adjusted), and Model 3 (fully adjusted for age, sex, hypertension, diabetes, smoking status, drinking status, sedentary behavior, sleep duration, educational level, marital status, and physical activity). The proportional hazards assumption was verified using Schoenfeld residuals, and P-values were corrected using the Benjamini-Hochberg method. Initially, restricted cubic splines (RCS) with three knots were used to evaluate potential non-linear relationships in the total population. Subgroup analyses were stratified by age (< 45 vs. ≥45 years) and sex. Additionally, a joint stratification by age and sex was performed to evaluate specific risks across four subgroups: young males, young females, middle-aged/elderly males, and middle-aged/elderly females. Interaction terms were tested for heterogeneity. Based on Cox results, optimal indices were selected to build prediction models for these four specific subgroups. To further characterize the dose-response patterns, specific RCS analyses were performed for the identified optimal predictors within their respective subgroups to visualize risk trajectories and detect potential non-linear thresholds. Model performance was evaluated using the C-index, AIC, and Likelihood Ratio Test. For the optimal models, predictive accuracy improvement was assessed via IDI and NRI at 2, 4, and 6 years. Stability was validated using 1,000 bootstrap resamples to generate bias-corrected C-indices, and calibration was assessed via calibration plots and slopes. Sensitivity analyses included excluding events within the first year, using logistic regression, and calculating E-values.

All statistical analyses were completed using R software (version 4.4.2), with two-sided tests and P < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Participant baseline characteristics

Table 1 outlines the baseline characteristics of the study population according to gallstone status. A total of 44,030 participants were included in the final analysis, among whom 933 (2.12%) were diagnosed with gallstones. Compared to participants without gallstones, those in the gallstone group were significantly older and had a higher proportion of males. Clinically, the gallstone group exhibited a significantly higher prevalence of hypertension and diabetes compared to the non-gallstone group. Significant differences were also observed in education level, marital status, and smoking habits (p < 0.05). All 14 evaluated obesity and lipid-related indices varied significantly according to gallstone status (all p < 0.001).

The relationship between the 14 obesity and lipid-related indices and the incidence of gallstones

We constructed three stepwise adjusted models. The results regarding a 1-standard deviation (SD) increase in each index as a continuous variable are detailed in Table 2. With the exception of ABSI, the remaining 13 indices were significantly associated with incident gallstones in the fully adjusted model. Subgroup analysis

To comprehensively evaluate the stability of the associations and explore potential population heterogeneity, subgroup analyses were performed stratified by age (< 45 vs. ≥45 years), sex, and their combination (resulting in four subgroups: young males, young females, middle-aged/elderly males, and middle-aged/elderly females).The results, presented in Fig. 5; Table 3, demonstrate that the positive associations between 13 obesity and lipid-related indices (excluding ABSI) and the risk of incident gallstones were generally robust across all stratified groups. However, unlike the single-factor stratification, the joint age-sex analysis revealed significant heterogeneity in the strength of these associations. Notably, significant interactions were observed for CVAI (p for interaction = 0.025), LAP (p for interaction = 0.005), and the TyG family (e.g., TyG, p for interaction = 0.003; TyG-WC, p for interaction = 0.015; TyG-WHtR, p for interaction = 0.024). Specifically, the association between CVAI and gallstone risk was most pronounced in young females (HR = 2.06, 95% CI: 1.72–2.45), significantly surpassing that observed in young males (HR = 1.44), middle-aged/elderly males (HR = 1.52), and middle-aged/elderly females (HR = 1.40). A similar trend of heightened risk sensitivity in young females was observed for other lipid-related indices. Conversely, simple anthropometric indices such as BMI (p for interaction = 0.243) and WHtR (p for interaction = 0.543) did not show statistically significant heterogeneity across the four subgroups, suggesting a relatively stable risk relationship regardless of age and sex combinations. Consistent with the main analysis, ABSI did not show a significant association with gallstones in any subgroup.

Sensitivity analysis

We conducted three sensitivity analyses to verify the robustness of our findings: excluding participants who developed gallstones within the first year of follow-up, employing logistic regression models, and calculating E-values. The results remained highly consistent with the primary analysis. Specifically, the E-value analysis quantified the robustness of these associations against potential unmeasured confounding. The E-values were 2.10 for RFM and 2.04 for CVAI, indicating that an unmeasured confounder would need to be associated with both the index and gallstone risk by a risk ratio of at least 2.10 and 2.04, respectively, to explain away the observed associations. E-values for other significant indices ranged from 1.20 to 1.93, including TyG-WC (1.93), WHtR (1.91), BMI (1.86), TyG-BMI (1.84), TyG-WHtR (1.77), BRI (1.76), AIP (1.41), TyG (1.34), LAP (1.32), CMI (1.21), and VAI (1.20). These values suggest that substantial unmeasured confounding would be required to negate our findings, considering the comprehensive adjustments already made in Model 3. (Additional File 1: Table S3-S5)

Comparison of the predictive value of different indices

To further evaluate the predictive value of various obesity and lipid-related indices for gallstones, we selected indices that demonstrated significant associations across all three models based on the prior Cox regression analysis. Using Model 3 as the foundation, multiple predictive models were constructed. Given the influence of age and sex, the study population was stratified into four refined subgroups: young male, young female, middle-aged/elderly male and middle-aged/elderly female.

Figure 6A and B show the range of C-indices constructed by various indices and the baseline model. The C-index ranges were 0.562–0.611 for young males, 0.591–0.673 for young females, 0.564–0.603 for middle-aged/elderly males, and 0.547–0.589 for middle-aged/elderly females. In the young male, young female, and middle-aged/elderly male subgroups, CVAI consistently ranked first, with C-indices of 0.611 (95% CI: 0.573–0.649), 0.673 (95% CI: 0.638–0.707), and 0.603 (95% CI: 0.564–0.642), respectively. In young males, the second and third rankings were occupied by BMI and TyG-BMI; in young females by TyG-BMI and TyG-WC; and in middle-aged/elderly males by WHtR and TyG-WC. Conversely, in middle-aged/elderly females, WHtR achieved the optimal performance with a C-index of 0.589 (95% CI: 0.531–0.647), followed by RFM and BRI.

Likelihood ratio test results showed that all models including biomarkers had statistically significant improvements compared to the basic model. (Additional File 1: Table S6)

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to comprehensively investigate the association between 14 obesity and lipid-related indices and gallstones in a Chinese population. In this large longitudinal cohort study of 44,030 Chinese adults, we systematically evaluated and compared the associations of these indices with the risk of incident gallstones. Our primary findings indicate that, with the exception of ABSI, the remaining 13 indices were significantly and independently positively associated with gallstone risk after full adjustment for potential confounders; sensitivity analyses further confirmed the robustness of these findings. Crucially, our study goes beyond aggregate analysis to elucidate significant heterogeneity across population strata through a joint age-sex stratification strategy. Based on discriminative performance metrics (C-indices), we identified CVAI as the optimal predictor for young males, young females, and middle-aged/elderly males, whereas WHtR performed best specifically in middle-aged/elderly females. Notably, further subgroup-specific dose-response analyses revealed risk trajectories that diverged from the total population trends. While CVAI exhibited a non-linear association in the overall cohort, it demonstrated a clear linear dose-response relationship within its three optimal subgroups, suggesting a ‘lower is better’ paradigm. Conversely, WHtR, which appeared linear overall, displayed a distinct non-linear threshold effect (inflection point at 0.8) specifically in middle-aged/elderly females. Regarding predictive performance at 2, 4, and 6 years, the CVAI-based model demonstrated consistently favorable performance, confirming its robustness, whereas the WHtR-based model exhibited suboptimal performance. Collectively, our results substantiate the incremental value of incorporating specific novel obesity indices—particularly CVAI—into clinical screening strategies for gallstones. These findings provide crucial guidance for early warning and intervention strategies for gallstones in specific Chinese populations.

Gallstones are a prevalent disorder of the biliary system, affecting 5%–20% of the Asian population, with incidence rates steadily rising [6]. The pathogenesis is multifactorial, involving age, sex, obesity, and metabolic status [23–26], with obesity widely recognized as a pivotal risk factor [27]. Current evidence suggests that obesity drives lithogenesis primarily through the metabolic dysregulation of insulin and leptin. On one hand, obesity-induced hyperinsulinemia activates HMG-CoA reductase and upregulates low-density lipoprotein (LDL) receptors, thereby directly enhancing hepatic cholesterol secretion [28]. On the other hand, while obese individuals exhibit elevated leptin levels, they typically manifest leptin resistance, resulting in a breakdown of energy homeostasis regulation [29, 30]. Together, these mechanisms precipitate biliary cholesterol supersaturation, significantly amplifying the risk of gallstone formation.

Notably, regarding the impact of sex on gallstone susceptibility, our findings diverge significantly from those reported in Western studies. While Western literature generally posits a higher risk in females [26, 31], our data reveal a higher detection rate in males. This challenges the traditional paradigm of female predominance and suggests that in specific Asian populations, males may face a comparably high, if not higher, risk of disease [32]. This observation aligns with findings from a study conducted in Taiwan [33].

In our in-depth subgroup analysis, we observed an intriguing pattern: although the overall incidence was higher in males, the strength of the association between the 12 evaluated indices (BMI, BRI, LAP, VAI, CMI, CVAI, RFM, AIP, TyG, TyG-BMI, TyG-WC, and TyG-WHtR) and gallstone risk was markedly more pronounced in young females. This suggests a heightened sensitivity to obesity-related metabolic dysregulation among young females. Existing evidence indicates that estrogen increases biliary cholesterol saturation and inhibits gallbladder motility. Consequently, elevated estrogen levels may exacerbate the deleterious effects of metabolic abnormalities on the biliary system [34, 35], thereby driving the stronger correlation observed between obesity/lipid-related indices and gallstone risk in this subgroup. Furthermore, we observed that the C-indices for the majority of obesity and lipid-related indices were consistently higher in young females than in the other three subgroups. This finding provides further indirect evidence of the potential role of estrogen in modulating gallstone risk, suggesting that greater emphasis should be placed on early prevention in young women.

Considering the influence of age and sex, we performed stratified analyses across four subgroups (young males, young females, middle-aged/elderly males, and middle-aged/elderly females) to thoroughly investigate population heterogeneity. Our C-index analysis indicated that CVAI was the superior predictor in males and young females, while WHtR performed optimally in middle-aged/elderly females. Notably, this pattern differs from observations in Western populations. For example, Lin et al. found a significant association between RFM and gallstones in a Western population [36], and an NHANES-based study identified RFM as the best predictor among nine indices [37]. This divergence may be driven by ethnic differences in body composition: while Western populations are characterized by generalized obesity, Asian populations tend to exhibit a central adiposity phenotype [13, 38]. Our results align with this perspective, as central obesity indices (CVAI and WHtR) consistently demonstrated optimal predictive performance across all stratified subgroups. This is attributed to their integration of anthropometric and metabolic features—particularly CVAI, which is specifically tailored to the Chinese population—thereby allowing for a more accurate reflection of visceral fat distribution [15, 39]. Mechanistically, visceral adiposity increases the risk of gallstone formation by inducing insulin resistance, activating hepatic lipid metabolic pathways, and disrupting systemic metabolic homeostasis [40–42].

The distinct linear and non-linear patterns observed in the RCS analyses across the total population versus specific subgroups further reinforce the necessity of stratified risk assessment. Based on the subgroup-specific RCS analysis, we identified distinct risk trajectories for the optimal predictors. In young males, young females, and middle-aged/elderly males, CVAI exhibited a linear dose-response relationship, indicating that gallstone risk increases continuously with rising CVAI levels without a distinct safety threshold. This suggests a ‘lower is better’ paradigm for clinical management in these populations. Conversely, WHtR in middle-aged/elderly females displayed a non-linear trend with an inflection point identified at 0.8, signifying a substantial risk escalation above this threshold. These findings provide precise warning limits: for the CVAI-relevant subgroups, continuous monitoring and reduction of visceral adiposity are advisable, whereas for middle-aged/elderly females, a WHtR > 0.8 warrants definitive diagnosis via abdominal ultrasonography. Furthermore, we conducted a comprehensive validation of the CVAI- and WHtR-based prediction models at 2-, 4-, and 6-year intervals. IDI analysis demonstrated that incorporating these indices significantly enhanced the discriminative ability of the models compared to the baseline (p < 0.05). Internal validation via Bootstrap resampling further corroborated the stability of the CVAI-based models: in the subgroups of young females, young males, and middle-aged/elderly males, the bias-corrected C-indices (0.6523, 0.5846, 0.5738) were highly consistent with the original C-indices (0.673, 0.611, 0.603). Coupled with excellent calibration curves, these results indicate good reproducibility and minimal risk of overfitting, underscoring the clinical utility of CVAI in these populations. Conversely, in the middle-aged/elderly female subgroup, the WHtR-based model showed a notable decrease in the bias-corrected C-index (0.5319) compared to the original value (0.589). Moreover, the calibration slope deviated considerably from the ideal (0.4119), suggesting potential overfitting in this specific subgroup. Consequently, risk estimates derived from this model should be interpreted with caution.

In the middle-aged/elderly female subgroup, the predictive performance of all obesity and lipid-related indices was suboptimal compared to the other three cohorts, with C-indices ranging from 0.547 to 0.589. A comparative analysis of the top three performing models revealed a distinct pattern: in young males (CVAI, BMI, TyG-BMI), young females (CVAI, TyG-BMI, TyG-WC), and middle-aged/elderly males (CVAI, WHtR, TyG-WC), the leading predictors consistently included composite indices integrating obesity and metabolic parameters. In these groups, models incorporating systemic metabolic profiles demonstrated superior predictive discrimination regarding gallstone risk. In stark contrast, the top predictors in middle-aged/elderly females were exclusively simple anthropometric indices (WHtR, RFM, and BRI), while complex metabolic models ranked lower. Recently, composite indices based on metabolic and lipid parameters have gained traction in clinical practice. The TyG index, derived from fasting blood glucose and triglyceride levels, is widely recognized as a sensitive and specific surrogate marker for insulin resistance due to its computational simplicity [43, 44]. Emerging evidence consistently demonstrates a positive association between TyG (and its derivatives) and gallstone risk [37, 45, 46], a finding corroborated by our results in the male and young female cohorts.

Currently, the specific impact of age on gallstone formation remains a subject of debate [26]. On the one hand, regarding the association analysis, our subgroup results indicated that the strength of the relationship (as measured by hazard ratios) between most indices and gallstone risk was more pronounced in younger populations, particularly among females. On the other hand, regarding predictive performance, distinct patterns were observed based on C-indices. Notably, composite metabolic and lipid-based models maintained their dominance in males regardless of age, with CVAI—which incorporates age, cholesterol, and waist circumference—ranking high in both male subgroups. This suggests that the lithogenic process in males may be less susceptible to age-related variations. Conversely, analysis of model performance revealed a profound age-dependent shift was observed in females: while the top three models in young females involved combined metabolic and obesity calculations, middle-aged/elderly females relied solely on simple obesity indicators, with overall poorer predictive performance. This implies that age exerts a more significant influence on gallstone pathophysiology in women than in men.

Although the precise mechanisms driving this age-related divergence in females remain to be fully elucidated, we propose several plausible explanations. First, in young females, high estrogen levels act synergistically with obesity-induced insulin resistance to promote biliary cholesterol saturation. However, post-menopause, the withdrawal of estrogen decouples this link, rendering metabolic and lipid-related markers less specific to lithogenesis [47–49]. Second, aging in women is often accompanied by sarcopenia and a redistribution of fat to the abdominal visceral depot. While systemic metabolic profiles in the elderly may be confounded by medication use or age-related dyslipidemia, WHtR serves as a stable anatomical marker that directly captures this central adiposity, thus outperforming complex composite indices [50, 51]. Finally, the universally lower C-indices suggest that in elderly women, age-related organ dysfunction (e.g., gallbladder hypomotility, decreased bile acid pool) may supersede obesity as the dominant risk factor. This “background noise” of aging dilutes the specific predictive signal of adiposity, leading to the suboptimal performance of all obesity-based models in this demographic [50, 52].

Our analysis identified BMI, BRI, LAP, VAI, CMI, and AIP as independent risk factors for gallstones, a finding consistent with previous literature [42, 53]. As a widely used metric for obesity, BMI has been shown to correlate with increased gallstone risk even among metabolically healthy adults [54]. Similarly, BRI has been reported as a robust predictor of gallstones in the US population [37]. LAP serves as a reliable indicator of visceral lipid accumulation and has been established as an independent risk factor for metabolic disorders [55]. However, in our stratified analysis, the predictive capability of LAP was statistically significant only in young females, while showing no significant association in the other three subgroups. Both VAI and CMI failed to demonstrate significant discriminative ability for gallstones across all four population subgroups. Regarding the novel lipid biomarker AIP, it has been extensively investigated in the contexts of cardiovascular disease, metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD), diabetes, and biological aging [56–59]. Du et al., analyzing NHANES data, identified that AIP mediates the association between dietary magnesium intake and gallstones in the US population [60]. In our study, however, AIP exhibited non-significant predictive performance in the middle-aged and elderly cohorts, and its predictive value remained marginal even in the younger population. Finally, we found no significant association between ABSI and gallstones, a result consistent with the findings of Zhang et al. [61].

To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first to comprehensively evaluate the predictive value of 14 obesity and lipid-related indices for gallstones in a Chinese population, contributing significant evidence that highlights distinct phenotypic differences from Western cohorts. The consistency of our results across multiple sensitivity analyses attests to the robustness of our conclusions. Furthermore, the reliance on abdominal B-mode ultrasonography for diagnosis ensured high sensitivity, reinforcing the methodological rigor of our study. In terms of clinical implications, we emphasize that while female susceptibility remains a focus, the risk in males must not be overlooked. We advocate for a stratified risk assessment strategy to optimize risk identification. Specifically, for adult males and young females, monitoring CVAI levels may aid in the early recognition of high-risk individuals. Conversely, for middle-aged and elderly females, WHtR may serve as a preliminary risk assessment tool; however, given its modest predictive performance and the complex metabolic profile of this demographic, it should be interpreted with caution and used to identify individuals who warrant further diagnostic evaluation.

However, there are some limitations in this study. First, this is an observational study, and we cannot determine a causal relationship between obesity and lipid-related indices and gallstones. Although CVAI and WHtR are better predictors for identifying gallstone risk compared to other indices, further prospective studies are needed to confirm their clinical value. Second, we adjusted for multiple covariates, but there may still be potential confounding factors, such as family history of gallstones, genetic susceptibility to cholesterol metabolism abnormalities, and other genetic factors. Nevertheless, the E-value analysis indicated that our results are robust, implying that it would take a strong unmeasured confounder to nullify the observed associations. Third, our prediction models have been internally validated but have not yet been validated in independent external cohorts. Therefore, the applicability of our results to other populations needs further study. Fourth, the reliance on a single baseline measurement constitutes a limitation. Metabolic markers, particularly TG and plasma glucose, are dynamic and subject to fluctuation. Consequently, our analysis represents a cross-sectional assessment of risk, which cannot account for the impact of longitudinal trajectories or cumulative exposure of these indices on gallstone development. Finally, although inherent mathematical and physiological overlap exists among the evaluated indices due to shared constituent variables, the comparative analysis of these indices serves to identify the optimal integration of parameters that best captures the multifaceted lithogenic risk. This highlights the incremental value of composite indices, demonstrating that integrating metabolic biomarkers with anthropometry offers superior predictive discrimination for risk stratification.

Conclusion

Our study demonstrates that 13 screening tools—BMI, BRI, LAP, VAI, CMI, CVAI, RFM, AIP, WHtR, TyG, TyG-BMI, TyG-WC, and TyG-WHtR—are positively associated with gallstone risk. Furthermore, we determined the optimal indicators for predicting gallstone incidence in the Chinese population based on age and sex stratification. As non-invasive methods, the application of these indices as risk assessment tools represents an effective and convenient option for clinical practice.

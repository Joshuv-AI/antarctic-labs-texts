Association between fibrosis‐4 index and cognitive impairment in elderly patients with hypertension: A cross‐sectional study
Authors: Hong Ding, Jingtao Wang, Shu Liu, Yafei Xie, Xiaowei Zhang, Jing Yu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe fibrosis‐4 index (FIB‐4) is a noninvasive fibrosis test that is recommended for patients who are at risk of developing hepatic fibrosis. The aim of the study was to explore the correlation between FIB‐4 index and the decline of cognitive function among older patients with hypertension. The study used a cross‐sectional design to analyze data obtained from the NHANES 2011–2014. The significance of the FIB‐4 index correlation with cognitive function in individuals over the age of 60 was evaluated via multivariate regression models. The nonlinear link was described and fitted smoothed curves. There were a total of 2039 participants in the study, and those with a higher FIB‐4 index were more susceptible to developing cognitive decline. In the completely adjusted model, the association remained statistically significant between the FIB‐4 index and poor cognitive function as measured by CERAD: Total Score (OR = 0.72, 0.57−0.91), Animal Fluency Score (OR = 0.66, 0.48−0.91), and Digit Symbol Score (OR = 0.36, 0.17−0.77). A nonlinear association was found between the FIB‐4 and poor cognitive ability: Total Score, CERAD: Score Delayed Recall, Digit Symbol Score, and Animal Fluency Score. In elderly patients with hypertension, a high FIB‐4 index is correlated with an increased prevalence of cognitive decline. Hence, the FIB‐4 index could potentially serve as a valuable tool for determining individuals with hypertension who are susceptible to both liver‐related complications and cognitive impairment.

INTRODUCTION

Hypertension is a prevalent comorbid condition among elderly patients that is involved in the emergence of numerous cardiovascular disorders. 1 At least 50% of the participants between the ages of 35 and 75 had hypertension between 2014 and 2021, and the incidence rose with age, as reported by the China patient‐centered evaluative assessment of cardiac events (PEACE) Million Persons Project. 2

In older people, cognitive impairment and eventual dementia are major contributors to mortality as well as morbidity. The incidence of cognitive impairment is 10.12% prevalent among individuals aged 60 and older in China. 3 An increasing body of evidence has established that hypertension significantly contributes to the occurrence of cognitive impairment among older individuals. 4 Hypertension is widely recognized as the main contributory factor in cerebrovascular disease due to its capacity to induce microvascular dysfunction and constriction, thereby promoting microhemorrhages, cerebral hypoperfusion, and small vessel disease in the brain. 5 Consequently, small vessel disease may culminate in leukoaraiosis and lacunar infarcts, two pathological alterations that are strongly correlated with cognitive deterioration and vascular dementia.

Initially, the fibrosis‐4 index (FIB‐4) was established as a prognostic indicator for hepatic fibrosis among virally infected patients. 6 Age, levels of aspartate transaminase (AST) and alanine aminotransferase (ALT), and platelet (PLT) count are incorporated into the calculation of the FIB4 index. At present, the FIB‐4 index is widely acknowledged as a simple and cost‐effective indicator of hepatic disease. 7 Further, it serves as a valuable prognostic marker for individuals afflicted with cardiovascular diseases (CVD). 8 Liver fibrosis is linked to CVD even at the subclinical stage. There is a shortage of population‐level information on the association between the FIB‐4 index and cognitive performance.

Hence, the aim of this study was to determine whether the FIB‐4 index can distinguish between true and perceived cognitive decline in hypertensive individuals.

METHODS

Survey description

The data were derived from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Surveys (NHANES), a nationally representative cross‐sectional study of the American populace that evaluates their nutrition and overall health, and conducted by the National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS). Utilizing both representative samples and complex multistage randomized stratification, NHANES is a cross‐sectional survey conducted every 2 years. After a preliminary home interview, the participants were subsequently subjected to a health examination conducted at a mobile examination center (MEC). Furthermore, the NCHS Research Ethics Review Board assessed and approved the study protocol, and each participant provided written informed consent.

Study population

Data on cognitive function metrics and complete variables (AST, ALT, and PLT) were obtained from two NHANES cycles (2011−2012 and 2013−2014) in this study to calculate the FIB‐4 index. For 2011−2014, cognitive testing was assessed among participants aged 60 years and older, which has been described previously. 9 , 10 From 2011 to 2014, 19 931 individuals participated in NHANES; for this study, participation was limited to persons 60 years and older who completed the MEC Cognitive Functioning Survey (n = 2934). Notably, those who had inaccurate or incomplete results on any of the three cognitive function assessments (n = 698) as well as those with incomplete FIB‐4 index (n = 172) were not included in the analysis. People with unavailable hypertension status and blood pressure measures (n = 732) were excluded. In total, 2039 participants were considered for the study after the exclusions (Figure 1).

FIB‐4 index assessment

The calculation of the FIB‐4 index followed a method outlined previously 11 : (age ∗ AST)/(Platelet counts ∗ (SQRT(ALT))). As reported previously, the participants were grouped into two: < 2.67 (low risk) and ≥ 2.67 (high risk). 12 , 13 FIB−4=(age∗AST)/Plateletcounts∗SQRTALT

Hypertension diagnosis

Those who fulfilled these criteria were classified as hypertensive 14 : (1) a doctor or other healthcare provider had diagnosed them with hypertension, often known as high blood pressure, (2) self‐reported medication use for hypertension, and (3) received measurement indicative of hypertension (DBP ≥90 mmHg or SBP ≥140 mmHg). The NHANES consists of three consecutive blood pressure measurements and an additional measurement if required. Classification of hypertension defined according to the 2023 ESH Guidelines for the management of arterial hypertension 15 : Grade 1 hypertension, systolic blood pressure 140−159 mmHg, or diastolic blood pressure 90−99 mmHg; Grade 2 hypertension, systolic blood pressure 160−179 mmHg, or diastolic blood pressure 100−109 mmHg; Grade 3 hypertension, systolic blood pressure ≥180 mmHg, or diastolic blood pressure ≥ 110 mmHg or the use of antihypertensive medications.

Cognitive function assessment

Participants aged 60 and over were provided a cognitive battery consisting of four cognitive tests in the MEC: Animal Fluency, Digital Substitution, Consortium to Establish a Registry for Alzheimer's Disease (CERAD) Word Learning, and CERAD Delayed Recall. 16 The CERAD test consists of three consecutive learning trials as well as delayed recall. The Animal Fluency test measures the ability to speak clearly and fluently by having participants identify as many different animals as they can within 1 min. Their score is calculated by adding up all of the animals they named. As has been pointed out before in the peer‐reviewed research, a cut‐off of less than fourteen was employed to identify possible cognitive impairment. 17 The Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale's Digital Substitution test is specifically developed to evaluate cognitive abilities such as sustained attention, working memory, and information processing speed. A set of symbols and a corresponding key were given to the participants. 18 A participant's score is derived by the number of symbols accurately drawn within 120 s. The threshold of <40 was applied, which is consistent with a previously published NCHS report that accounted for the “Flynn effect” reducing impact. The CERAD is an extensive battery of assessments utilized to diagnose dementia associated with Alzheimer's disease. It evaluates an individual's capacity for new learning, recognition memory, and delayed recall. Three learning evaluations are administered consecutively on the CERAD Word Learning test. Participants were asked to recite 19 distinct words as presented and then recall as many as possible. Each trial features a different word order, with 30 as the highest possible score. A delay interval of 8–10 min separated the CERAD Word Learning test from the CERAD Delayed Recall. Every individual was instructed to recall the 10 words that were covered in the initial Word Learning test during the Delayed Recall test. The preceding scientific literature informed the selection of the threshold values of <17 for CERAD Word Learning and <5 for CERAD Delayed Recall.

Selection of covariates

The authors included the covariates associated with FIB‐4 index or cognitive function that were identified in previous studies, while excluding collinearity concerns such as the following: sex (male/female), age (years), race (Mexican American/other Hispanic/non‐Hispanic White/non‐Hispanic Black/Other race), education level (below high school/high school/above high school), alcohol consumption, BMI (body mass index), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), systolic blood pressure (SBP), antihypertensive drug use, BP classification, smoking status, and diabetes. Qualified personnel in the medical field administered the questionnaires and collected all data through standardized questioning, physical examination, and laboratory tests.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted utilizing appropriate sample weights from the NHANES, while also accounting for intricate multistage cluster surveys in adherence to the principles stipulated by the National Center for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). To obtain a fresh sample weight from the combination of two 2‐year cycles of continuous data, the previous 2‐year sample weight was divided by 2. Continuous data are represented in the baseline characteristics table using standard deviations (SDs), whereas categorical variables are shown using proportions. Applying weighted Student's t‐tests for continuous data and weighted Chi‐square tests for categorical data, the authors compared the groups with impaired and normal cognitive performance to determine whether there were any differences. Using three different models, the authors examined the FIB‐4′s association with poor cognitive function via multivariate logistic regression. No adjustments were made to the covariates in Model 1. Model 2 incorporated an age adjustment, whereas Model 3 incorporated adjustments for sex, age, race, level of education, alcohol consumption, PIR, BMI, DBP, SBP, antihypertensive drug use, BP classification and smoking status. Imputation was not conducted because the proportion of missing data was below 10% of the overall sample. After grouping the FIB‐4 into two, the authors conducted additional analysis to assess the reliability of the correlations. Model 3 results were utilized to analyze every outcome of multiple logistic regression and subgroup analyses. A smooth curve fitting analysis was performed to ascertain the presence of nonlinear relationships between FIB‐4 index and poor cognitive function. The analyses of all data were performed utilizing the statistical software packages in R (The R Foundation; http://www.r‐project.org; v 4.1.3). An p‐value of .05 was set as a measure of statistical significance.

RESULTS

Participants' characteristics at baseline

Overall, 972 males and 1067 females from NHANES (2011−2014) were included in the study. The CERAD: Score Delayed Recall, CERAD: Total score, Animal Fluency, and Digit Symbol showed that 41.49%, 36.68%, 38.65%, and 41.15% of the participants had low cognitive function, respectively. The participant's sociodemographic data were categorized and delineated based on the following indicators: age, sex, ethnicity, level of education, diabetes, blood pressure, alcohol consumption, BMI, smoking status, and PIR. The distributions of age, level of education, PIR, BMI, SBP, DBP, and the FIB‐4 index differed significantly between individuals with low and normal cognition, as determined by four cognitive function‐related tests (CERAD: Score Delayed Recall, CERAD: Total score, Animal Fluency, and Digit Symbol). Individuals with impaired cognition exhibited a greater propensity for advanced age, lower education levels, lower socioeconomic status, and higher FIB‐4 index and SBP measurements, in contrast to those with normal cognition. Those with impaired cognitive function were more prevalent among males on the CERAD: Score Delayed Recall, CERAD: Total score, Animal Fluency, and Digit Symbol assessments, whereas females were more prevalent among those with normal cognition. Association of FIB‐4 with cognitive function

The relationship between cognitive performance and the FIB‐4 index is demonstrated in Table 3. A higher FIB‐4 was associated with a greater risk of impaired cognitive performance, according to the results. The association remained statistically significant between FIB‐4 and poor cognitive functioning in Model 1 (CERAD: Total score (OR = 0.34, 0.26−0.44), CERAD: Score Delayed Recall (OR = 0.62, 0.53−0.72), Digit Symbol (OR = 0.02, 0.01−0.05), and Animal Fluency (OR = 0.33, 0.24−0.46)), suggesting that a higher FIB‐4 index is linked to a higher risk of cognitive impairment. After adjusting for sex, age, race, alcohol consumption, BMI, smoking status, PIR, education level, SBP, DBP, antihypertensive drug use, and BP classification, a significant relationship between FIB‐4 index and low cognitive function was observed (Table 4).

Subgroup analysis

The FIB‐4 index was further transformed into a two‐group categorical variable so that the strength of the associations could be assessed. The results demonstrated that the risk of impaired cognitive performance increases with increasing FIB‐4 index on the CERAD: Score Delayed Recall, CERAD: Total score, Animal Fluency, and Digit Symbol tests, in comparison to the group with the least FIB‐4 (p for trend <.05). After fully adjusting for potential confounders in the Animal Fluency test, FIB‐4 ≥ 2.67 (OR = 0.22, 0.08–0.61) showed the least weighted multivariable‐adjusted OR of cognitive decline compared with the lowest group of FIB‐4.

Non‑linear relationship between the FIB‐4 and cognitive function

Furthermore, a restricted cubic spline analysis confirmed a significant nonlinear relationship between FIB‐4 index and various aspects of cognitive function. None of the cognition variables exhibited nonlinear associations with the FIB‐4 index (CERAD: Score Delayed Recall: nonlinear p‐value = 1.62−8; CERAD: Total score: nonlinear p‐value = 8.88−9, Animal Fluency: nonlinear p‐value = 3.33−5; Digit Symbol: nonlinear p‐value = 2.86−8) (Figure 2).

DISCUSSION

The recurrence of cognitive impairment in patients with hypertension and its association with the FIB‐4 index was examined in this study. The authors discovered a significant link between the FIB‐4 and impaired cognition in this cross‐sectional study consisting of 2039 elderly adults (≥60 years); However, research exploring the association between the FIB‐4 index and cognitive function is scarce. To the authors' knowledge, this study represents the first large‐scale investigation of this relationship among hypertensive individuals. Their findings reveal a nonlinear association between the FIB‐4 index and impaired cognitive function, as demonstrated by measures such as CERAD: Score Delayed Recall, CERAD: Total score, Animal Fluency, and Digit Symbol, after adjusting for all covariates.

The association between hypertension and liver fibrosis has been previously reported as interacting with each other. 19 Non‐alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is correlated with a greater risk of developing hypertension, according to a previous study. 20 In individuals with normal ALT levels, a significant correlation existed between elevated FIB‐4 levels and hypertension. 21 A relationship between the FIB‐4 index and hypertension could be attributed to the exceptionally potent induction of endothelial cells and renal damage, including glomerular damage, fibrosis, and disruptions in sodium transport, which was facilitated by impaired liver function accompanied by activated proinflammatory factors. 22 The vasculature‐associated oxidative damage, 23 endothelial dysfunction, 24 vascular remodeling characterized by luminal constriction and fibrosis, and arterial rigidity increase systemic vascular resistance. This process generates a vicious cycle in which elevated shear stress on blood vessels and high blood pressure induce endothelial cell hyperexpression of adhesion molecules, localized recruitment of inflammatory cells in the wall of the arteries, pro‐inflammatory cytokine production, endothelial dysfunction, and arterial stiffness, all of which may ultimately lead to the occurrence of hypertension. 24

The FIB‐4 is a recommended noninvasive fibrosis test in patients at risk of developing hepatic liver fibrosis. 25 Currently, there is no report on the relationship between FIB‐4 index and cognitive function with hypertension. The present study found that the cognitive function score reduced incrementally as the FIB‐ 4 index rose. Hepatic fibrosis may also be linked to worse performance on tests of abstract reasoning and executive function, according to a cross‐sectional analysis of the Framingham Study. 26 Moreover, those with high FIB‐4 levels had a much worse cognitive ability score, according to the authors' analysis. The link between FIB‐4 index and impaired cognitive performance has several plausible biological explanations. Additionally, there is mounting evidence that the liver is involved in the clearance of peripheral amyloid‐β and that aberrant liver function may exacerbate the deposition of cerebral amyloid‐β. 27 Liver fibrosis may influence the accumulation of cortical amyloid‐β and tau, according to the Framingham research that was published recently. 28 Furthermore, liver fibrosis may influence the generation of neuroprotective hepatocytes; however, the precise implications of this process remain uncertain. As mentioned above, when liver function is impaired, the release of inflammatory cytokines into the bloodstream by these entities can provoke systemic inflammation. 29 This phenomenon not only plays a key role in the histological progression of hypertension 30 but also contributes to the advancement of cognitive impairment. 31 Meningeal barriers are susceptible to damage induced by reactive oxygen species (ROS). 32 Accumulation of ROS and subsequent barrier leakage can result in inflammation of the brain parenchyma, contributing to cognitive decline. 33 Endothelial cells play crucial roles in vascular biology, 34 including the maintenance of vascular tone, 35 regulation of blood flow, 36 modulation of the inflammatory response, 37 and facilitation of molecule trafficking between the periphery and the brain. 38 Dysfunction of endothelial cells disrupts blood flow, diminishes vascular tone, and compromises the integrity of the blood‐brain barrier (BBB). 38 This dysfunction is partly mediated by disturbances in specialized receptors on endothelial cells that transduce mechanical and chemical stimuli to facilitate the release of signaling molecules such as nitric oxide (NO), 39 endothelin, 40 and prostanoids. Current literature suggests an association between arterial stiffness and decreased brain volume, along with compromised cognitive functions such as executive function and processing speed. 41 , 42 However, it is important to note that mechanistic explanations regarding the correlation between the FIB‐4 index and cognitive impairment remain speculative at this stage.

Nevertheless, the authors need to take the following limitations into account in the process of interpreting this study's findings. Firstly, the causal relationship between hypertension and cognitive function as measured by the FIB‐4 index may be difficult to establish considering the study's cross‐sectional design. Secondly, while FIB‐4 quantification is relatively easy in clinical settings, common loss of AST, ALT, and platelets may introduce selection bias. Ultimately, despite the implementation of adjustments for certain potential confounding variables, it is impossible to entirely exclude the impact of other unmeasured variables.

CONCLUSIONS

An increased FIB‐4 index acts as a significant prognostic biomarker for the recurrence of cognitive impairment in hypertensive patients. The FIB‐4 index, being a simple and easily accessible indicator, has the potential to be incorporated into risk stratification systems and assist in the optimization of patient care.

Increased LOXL2 facilitates tissue remodeling in eosinophilic chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps☆
Authors: Changcan Jiang, Xiaoqiong Wang, Ruoqi Li, Liyan Ni, Xuejun Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveEosinophilic chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (eCRSwNP) involves extensive tissue remodeling, but its underlying mechanisms remain unclear. This study aimed to identify key regulatory proteins contributing to this process.MethodsProteomic analysis was conducted on nasal tissues from 8 eCRSwNP patients, 8 non-eosinophilic CRSwNP (neCRSwNP) patients, and 8 healthy controls. Differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) were selected based on the top 50 overlapping DEPs from both comparison groups. Selected candidates were validated using Western blotting, immunofluorescence, and qRT-PCR in an independent cohort. Correlations with eosinophil infiltration and remodeling markers were assessed. Human nasal epithelial cells (HNECs) were used to explore functional mechanisms in vitro.ResultProteomic profiling revealed a distinct protein expression signature in the eCRSwNP group compared to both neCRSwNP and control groups. By intersecting the top 50 DEPs from both comparisons, 4 candidate proteins were identified, among which lysyl oxidase-like protein 2 (LOXL2) exhibited the most prominent upregulation. Validation in an independent cohort confirmed significantly elevated LOXL2 expression in eCRSwNP, predominantly localized to the nasal epithelial region (P < 0.001). LOXL2 mRNA levels were strongly correlated with eosinophil infiltration in nasal tissues (r = 0.349, P = 0.006), and ROC curve analysis supported its high diagnostic utility for eCRSwNP (AUC = 0.746, P = 0.001). Furthermore, the expression of EMT markers including vimentin, α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), and N-cadherin was markedly increased in eCRSwNP tissues (all P < 0.01) and positively associated with LOXL2 expression (all P < 0.05). In vitro, stimulation of HNECs with recombinant LOXL2 induced TGF-β1 and EMT marker expression, while co-treatment with the TGF-β1/Smad signaling inhibitor LY364947 significantly attenuated these effects and inhibited Smad2/3 phosphorylation.ConclusionOur findings revealed a disease-specific protein profile in eCRSwNP, characterized by selective upregulation of LOXL2 in the nasal epithelium and strong association with eosinophilic inflammation. Functional studies suggest that LOXL2 promotes EMT and may drive tissue remodeling in eCRSwNP via the TGF-β1/Smad signaling pathway.

Introduction

Chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (CRSwNP) is a prevalent inflammatory disorder characterized by persistent mucosal inflammation, resulting in symptoms such as nasal congestion, loss of smell, and recurrent sinus infections.1,2 Although CRSwNP encompasses a range of endotypes, the most widely used classification relies on the extent of tissue eosinophilic infiltration, distinguishing eosinophilic (eCRSwNP) from non-eosinophilic (neCRSwNP) subtypes.3,4 Among these, eCRSwNP is characterized by more severe disease manifestations and chronicity, largely driven by intense eosinophilic infiltration and a predominant T helper 2 (Th2) immune response.5 Recent studies have highlighted the crucial role of tissue remodeling—particularly through epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT)—in the pathogenesis and progression of eCRSwNP.6,7 EMT is defined by the loss of epithelial markers and the acquisition of mesenchymal features, leading to structural remodeling and functional impairment of the nasal mucosa.8 Therefore, understanding the molecular mechanisms that govern tissue remodeling in eCRSwNP is critical for identifying new therapeutic targets and developing more effective strategies to limit disease progression and improve patient outcomes.

To gain deeper insights into the molecular mechanisms of eCRSwNP, it is essential to identify key proteins involved in the disease progression. Traditional methods like histology and gene expression profiling have provided valuable information, but these approaches often fail to capture the full complexity of the disease.9,10 Protein expression is closely linked to cellular function and disease phenotype, making proteomics an ideal approach for understanding the molecular underpinnings of eCRSwNP.11,12 Juan et al13 utilized serum to identify a panel of serum biomarkers, which effectively distinguishes allergic asthma from non-allergic asthma, highlighting the potential of proteomics in disease classification and targeted therapy. Similarly, Chen et al14 demonstrated through proteomic analysis that MMP2 is significantly upregulated in eCRSwNP and correlates with tissue eosinophilia, offering valuable insights into the pathophysiology of this condition. However, unbiased tissue proteomics specifically aimed at elucidating the molecular basis of epithelial remodeling in eCRSwNP remains underexplored.

In this study, we address this gap with an unbiased, discovery-stage tissue proteomic approach. We hypothesized that dysregulated proteins in eCRSwNP nasal mucosa drive EMT-associated remodeling. To test this, we performed label-free quantitative proteomics in a discovery cohort, followed by validation in an independent cohort and orthogonal assays. Our goal was to identify and verify candidate proteins linked to remodeling and to provide mechanistic leads for future therapeutic targeting.

Materials and methods

Participants and setting

Two independent cohorts of CRSwNP patients were enrolled from our department between June 1 and August 1, 2022, comprising a discovery cohort and a validation cohort. The discovery cohort included 8 healthy controls, 8 patients with non-eosinophilic CRSwNP (neCRSwNP), and 8 with eosinophilic CRSwNP (eCRSwNP). The validation cohort consisted of 30 healthy controls and 60 CRSwNP patients (30 neCRSwNP and 30 eCRSwNP). All diagnoses were made according to the 2020 European Position Paper on Rhinosinusitis and Nasal Polyps guidelines.15 Strict exclusion criteria were applied: (1) age under 18 or over 65 years; (2) recent use (within 1 month before surgery) of antibiotics, systemic or oral corticosteroids, immunotherapy, or antiallergic agents; (3) comorbid nasal or sinus conditions; (4) other inflammatory or autoimmune diseases; and (5) severe dysfunction of vital organs (eg, heart, kidney) or pregnancy. Comprehensive demographic and clinical information was recorded, including gender, age, body mass index (BMI), smoking status, and alcohol consumption.

Definition of eCRSwNP and neCRSwNP

Tissue samples were collected from CRSwNP patients during surgery and fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining was performed to visualize eosinophils. Two independent observers counted the number of eosinophils in 5 randomly selected high-power fields (HPFs). Patients were classified as having eCRSwNP if the average eosinophil count exceeded 10 per HPF; otherwise, they were categorized as neCRSwNP.16

Tissue proteomics and data analysis

Nasal polyp and middle turbinate tissue specimens were rapidly collected during surgery and immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen to preserve protein integrity. The frozen tissues were pulverized under cryogenic conditions and lysed in radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors to prevent protein degradation. Following high-speed centrifugation, supernatants containing soluble proteins were harvested. Protein concentrations were quantified using the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay to standardize sample input across all conditions. Equal amounts of protein from each sample were subjected to reduction and alkylation to denature disulfide bonds and prevent reformation. Proteins were then enzymatically digested overnight at 37 °C with sequencing-grade trypsin to generate peptide fragments. The resulting peptides were desalted using solid-phase extraction and subsequently concentrated. Peptide mixtures were separated using reverse-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (RP-HPLC) on a C18 analytical column under a programmed acetonitrile gradient. The eluted peptides were analyzed by liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) using a high-resolution mass spectrometer operating in data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode to ensure comprehensive and reproducible peptide detection.

Raw MS data were processed using MaxQuant software for peptide identification and label-free quantification, with protein annotation referenced against the human UniProt/SwissProt database. Quantitative data were filtered to retain proteins identified in at least 70% of samples per group. Differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) were determined using Perseus software, applying a statistical threshold of p < 0.05 and fold change >1.5.17

Western blotting (WB)

Total proteins were extracted from nasal tissues or cultured cells using lysis buffer and quantified via the BCA protein assay to ensure equal loading. Equal protein amounts were mixed with SDS loading buffer, boiled at 95 °C for 5 min, and separated by SDS-PAGE. Proteins were then transferred onto PVDF or nitrocellulose membranes. Membranes were blocked in 5% skim milk/TBST for 1 h at room temperature, then incubated overnight at 4 °C with the primary antibodies listed in Table S1. Following washes, HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies were applied for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) and imaged with a digital system. Band intensities were quantified using ImageJ, with normalization to internal control Tubulin.

Quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Tissue samples stored in liquid nitrogen were homogenized in TRIzol reagent. After chloroform separation and centrifugation, the aqueous phase was collected, and RNA was precipitated using isopropanol. The RNA pellet was washed with 75% ethanol and dissolved in RNase-free water. RNA concentration was measured using a spectrophotometer. Reverse transcription was performed using a reverse transcription kit (PrimeScript RT Master Mix). Quantitative PCR was conducted using SYBR Premix EX Taq (Takara, Dalian, China). The relative expression of target genes was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method, with GAPDH as the internal reference gene for normalization. Primer sequences are shown in Table S2.

Immunofluorescence

Tissue samples were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, dehydrated, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at 5 μm thickness. Sections were mounted on slides, baked at 60 °C for 2 h, dewaxed in xylene, rehydrated through graded ethanol, and rinsed with water. Antigen retrieval was performed using a microwave-heated retrieval buffer. After cooling and PBS washes, sections were blocked with 5% BSA and 0.3% Triton X-100 for 1 h, then incubated overnight at 4 °C with the primary antibodies listed in Table S1. After PBS washing, sections were incubated with fluorescent secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature in the dark. Nuclei were stained with DAPI, and slides were mounted with antifade medium. Images were captured using fluorescence or confocal microscopy, and fluorescence intensity was analyzed with ImageJ software.

Culture and treatment of human nasal epithelial cells (HNECs)

Primary HNECs were isolated and cultured based on established protocols.18,19 Fresh nasal mucosal tissues were obtained and rinsed thoroughly with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) to eliminate blood and mucus. The tissues were then cut into approximately 1–2 mm3 fragments and enzymatically digested using a mixture of collagenase and trypsin at 37 °C for 30–60 min, with intermittent gentle pipetting to enhance dissociation. Enzymatic digestion was terminated by adding medium containing fetal bovine serum (FBS), and the resulting cell suspension was filtered through a sterile cell strainer to remove residual tissue debris. The filtrate was centrifuged at 300 g for 5 min, and the cell pellet was resuspended in PneumaCult™-Ex Plus Medium (STEMCELL Technologies). Cells were seeded onto culture dishes and maintained in a humidified incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO2.

Once cells reached 70–80% confluence, they were passaged and seeded into 6-well plates at a density of 1 × 105 cells per well. Upon reaching approximately 80% confluence, cells were subjected to the following treatments: (1) treating with recombinant human IL-4/IL-13 protein mixture (both 10 ng/mL, R&D Systems, Minneapolis, USA) for 48 h; (2) stimulation with recombinant human LOXL2 protein (100 ng/mL, R&D Systems, Minneapolis, USA) for 48 h; (3) for combined treatment, cells were pretreated with the TGF-β1/Smad pathway inhibitor LY364947 (10 μmol/L, MCE, Monmouth Junction, USA) for 1 h, followed by the addition of LOXL2 protein and further incubation for 48 h. HNECs derived from 6 independent healthy donors were cultured separately, and all intervention experiments were performed in biological replicates.

Cell immunofluorescence

After 48-h treatment, HNECs grown on coverslips were washed with PBS and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min at room temperature. Cells were permeabilized with 0.2% Triton X-100 for 10 min, then blocked with 5% BSA for 1 h. After overnight incubation at 4 °C with the primary antibody, cells were washed and incubated with the fluorescent secondary antibody for 1 h in the dark. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI, and coverslips were mounted using antifade medium. Fluorescence images were captured, and the LOXL2-positive cell percentage was counted and analyzed with ImageJ software.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

After treatment, culture supernatants were collected from HNECs and centrifuged at 1000×g for 10 min at 4 °C to remove cellular debris. LOXL2 concentrations were measured using a human LOXL2-specific ELISA kit (CUSABIO, Wuhan, China), following the manufacturer's protocol. Standards and samples were added to pre-coated wells, incubated with a specific biotin-labelled detection antibody, followed by HRP-conjugated streptavidin. After the addition of the substrate solution and incubation in the dark, the reaction was terminated, and absorbance was read at 450 nm. LOXL2 concentrations were calculated using a standard curve generated from known concentrations. Each sample was analyzed in duplicate.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 25 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and GraphPad Prism 8 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). Categorical variables were analyzed using the chi-square test or Fisher's exact test among groups. Experimental data were expressed as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). The Kruskal–Wallis H test was used to assess overall differences among multiple groups, while the two-tailed Mann–Whitney U test was employed for pairwise comparisons. Spearman's rank correlation coefficient was applied to evaluate associations between variables. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was conducted to assess the discriminatory power of relevant indicators. A p-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Proteomic landscape reveals distinct molecular signatures in eCRSwNP

Baseline characteristics for the discovery cohort are summarized in Table S3. The control, neCRSwNP, and eCRSwNP groups were comparable in sex, age, BMI, and comorbidities (all P > 0.05). By contrast, tissue eosinophil counts were significantly higher in eCRSwNP than in both neCRSwNP and controls (P < 0.001). An overview of sample collection and proteomic workflow across the 3 groups is illustrated in Fig. 1A. Proteomic analysis revealed distinct molecular signatures in nasal polyp tissues from patients with eCRSwNP compared to those with neCRSwNP and healthy controls. Principal component analysis (PCA) demonstrated clear group separation among controls, neCRSwNP, and eCRSwNP, reflecting substantial differences in global protein expression profiles (Fig. 1B). Hierarchical clustering heatmaps further highlighted group-specific patterns of DEPs (Fig. 1C). Volcano plot analysis identified 359 significantly dysregulated proteins in eCRSwNP compared to controls (Fig. 1D) and 108 in comparison to neCRSwNP (Fig. 1E). LOXL2 is enriched in nasal epithelium and correlates with tissue eosinophilia in eCRSwNP

To validate the expression and spatial distribution of the 4 candidate proteins, we analyzed an independent validation cohort (clinical characteristics in Table 1). Baseline demographics—sex, age, and BMI—were comparable across groups (all P > 0.05), whereas the eCRSwNP group had a higher prevalence of allergic rhinitis and asthma (both P < 0.001) and markedly increased tissue eosinophil counts (P < 0.001). As illustrated in Fig. 2, WB analysis demonstrated that LOXL2, CRTH2, and SPP1 were significantly upregulated in the eCRSwNP group compared to both control and neCRSwNP groups (Fig. 2A). Spearman correlation analysis indicated a strong positive correlation between LOXL2 expression and eosinophil infiltration in nasal polyp tissue (Fig. 3E–H). Moreover, ROC curve analysis demonstrated that LOXL2 had a high diagnostic value in distinguishing CRSwNP endotypes (Fig. 3J-L). IL-4/IL-13 upregulates LOXL2 expression and secretion in HNECs

Previous evidence suggests that Th2-associated cytokines IL-4 and IL-13 play critical roles in tissue remodeling in eCRSwNP.18,20 Therefore, we treated human nasal epithelial cells (HNECs) derived from healthy control nasal mucosa with recombinant IL-4 and IL-13 proteins. Immunofluorescence analysis revealed that stimulation with IL-4 and IL-13 for 48 h markedly enhanced intracellular LOXL2 expression (Fig. 4A–B), a finding further confirmed by WB analysis (Fig. 4C). In addition, ELISA detection of cell culture supernatants showed significantly higher concentrations of LOXL2 in the IL-4/IL-13-treated group compared to the PBS control group (Fig. 4D). The TGF-β1/Smad signaling pathway is a well-recognized regulator of tissue remodeling.23 To explore whether LOXL2 contributes to epithelial remodeling through this pathway, HNECs were treated with recombinant LOXL2 protein. As shown in Fig. 6A, LOXL2 stimulation significantly upregulated the expression of TGF-β1, vimentin, α-SMA, and N-cadherin compared to the PBS control. In addition, LOXL2 treatment activated the phosphorylation of Smad2/3, a key downstream event in the TGF-β1/Smad cascade (Fig. 6B). Notably, co-treatment with the TGF-β1/Smad pathway inhibitor LY364947 effectively suppressed LOXL2-induced upregulation of mesenchymal markers and blocked Smad2/3 phosphorylation (Fig. 6C–D). Discussion

In recent years, proteomics has emerged as a powerful tool for uncovering molecular mechanisms underlying complex diseases by enabling comprehensive profiling of protein expression, modifications, and interactions.24,25 It offers valuable insights into disease pathogenesis, biomarker discovery, and therapeutic target identification. Despite its broad utility, the application of proteomics to investigate histopathologic alterations in eCRSwNP has remained limited. In the present study, we employed tissue proteomics to delineate disease-specific protein signatures and identified LOXL2 as a key regulator involved in epithelial remodeling via the TGF-β1/Smad signaling pathway. These findings shed light on the molecular underpinnings of tissue remodeling in eCRSwNP, enhance our understanding of CRSwNP endotype-specific mechanisms, and provide a scientific rationale for developing anti-remodeling interventions targeting the lysyl oxidase family.

LOXL2 is broadly expressed on epithelial cells, endothelial cells, and macrophages, and plays a pivotal role in tissue remodeling and inflammatory processes.26, 27, 28 Previous studies have demonstrated that Th2-type cytokines can induce LOXL2 expression via activation of the STAT6 signaling pathway, thereby facilitating eosinophil infiltration and promoting airway remodeling, contributing to the pathogenesis of various inflammatory airway diseases.29,30 Furthermore, elevated serum LOXL2 levels in patients with pulmonary diseases have been shown to correlate with both peripheral eosinophil counts and disease severity,31,32 suggesting a strong association between aberrant LOXL2 expression and eosinophilic inflammation. In the present study, we identified LOXL2 as one of the most significantly upregulated proteins in eCRSwNP patients, with predominant localization to the nasal epithelial region. Its mRNA expression level was positively correlated with tissue eosinophil counts, implying a potential role for LOXL2 in mediating local eosinophilic inflammation in the nasal mucosa. These findings support the hypothesis that LOXL2 may act as a key modulator of eosinophil-associated immune responses in eCRSwNP. Given that tissue remodeling is a defining pathological feature of eCRSwNP,33 we further examined its association with LOXL2 expression. Our results demonstrated that tissue remodeling markers were significantly elevated in eCRSwNP and exhibited strong positive correlations with LOXL2 levels. In vitro experiments using recombinant LOXL2 revealed that LOXL2 stimulation induced the upregulation of mesenchymal markers in human nasal epithelial cells, indicating that LOXL2 may promote epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and thereby contribute to structural remodeling of the nasal mucosa. Collectively, these findings highlight a dual role for LOXL2 in promoting eosinophilic inflammation and epithelial remodeling in eCRSwNP, although the precise molecular mechanisms remain to be fully elucidated.

Previous research has demonstrated that the TGF-β1/Smad signaling pathway plays a central role in tissue remodeling.34 By activating Smad2/3, this pathway facilitates the transition of epithelial cells into mesenchymal-like cells, thereby disrupting epithelial integrity and contributing to structural remodeling of the tissue.35,36 Additionally, the TGF-β1/Smad axis can amplify local inflammatory responses by modulating the release of pro-inflammatory mediators, further accelerating the remodeling process.37, 38, 39 These mechanisms collectively result in epithelial barrier dysfunction and structural alterations, which are key pathological features in airway inflammatory diseases. In the present study, we demonstrated that treatment with recombinant LOXL2 led to upregulation of mesenchymal markers and activation of Smad2/3 phosphorylation in HNECs. Notably, pharmacological inhibition of the TGF-β1/Smad pathway effectively suppressed LOXL2-induced EMT, confirming the pathway's involvement in LOXL2-mediated remodeling processes. These findings underscore the critical role of the TGF-β1/Smad axis in nasal epithelial remodeling and suggest that LOXL2 may drive tissue remodeling in eCRSwNP by modulating this signaling cascade. In addition to activating the TGF-β1/Smad pathway, LOXL2 may also interact with other inflammatory and remodeling pathways. Previous studies have demonstrated that LOXL2-mediated cross-linking of extracellular matrix components can lead to tissue stiffening, which in turn reinforces profibrotic signaling and creates a microenvironment favourable for persistent inflammation and remodeling.40 Furthermore, LOXL2 has been implicated in modulating STAT6-dependent type 2 inflammatory responses, thereby linking extracellular matrix remodeling with eosinophilic inflammation.41,42 Although these interactions were not directly evaluated in the present study, they provide additional mechanistic insights and highlight future directions for exploring the broader role of LOXL2 in eCRSwNP pathogenesis. Collectively, our data highlight a potentially central mechanism in eCRSwNP pathogenesis and support the therapeutic potential of targeting LOXL2 or its downstream signaling components in this disease context. Despite the progress achieved, several limitations warrant consideration. First, the discovery proteomics cohort was small, and no formal a priori power calculation was performed; the sample size reflected feasibility and common discovery-phase practice, which may limit statistical power and generalizability. We partially mitigated this by validating key signals in a larger independent cohort, but multi-center expansion is still needed. Second, validation focused on 4 DEPs, so additional untested candidates may also be relevant; a broader, integrative assessment of the full DEP set is warranted. Third, although LOXL2 proved functionally important and engaged the TGF-β1/Smad pathway, its upstream inflammatory regulators and other downstream pathways remain undefined. Finally, we did not assess the in vivo reversibility of LOXL2-driven remodeling, and the temporal trajectories of EMT/barrier markers were not comprehensively characterized.

Future studies will aim to validate these findings in larger, well-characterized, multi-center cohorts; incorporate longitudinal tissue and serum sampling to capture dynamic remodeling signatures and prognostic value; and extend validation beyond the initial 4 DEPs using orthogonal assays and multi-omics integration. Mechanistic in vitro and in vivo perturbations (genetic and pharmacologic) will be used to test the causal role of LOXL2 and other candidates, delineate signaling beyond TGF-β1/Smad (e.g., ERK, NF-κB, PI3K/AKT), and determine whether pathway inhibition can reverse established remodeling and improve long-term outcomes.

Conclusion

In summary, this study reveals a distinct disease-specific protein expression profile in eCRSwNP and highlights LOXL2 as a pivotal regulator of epithelial remodeling. LOXL2 was selectively upregulated in the nasal epithelium of eCRSwNP patients and strongly correlated with both eosinophilic infiltration and tissue remodeling. Functional experiments confirmed that LOXL2 promotes epithelial remodeling through activation of the TGF-β1/Smad signaling pathway, an effect that can be effectively suppressed by pathway inhibition. These findings enhance our understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying eCRSwNP and suggest LOXL2 as a promising therapeutic target for endotype-specific treatment strategies.

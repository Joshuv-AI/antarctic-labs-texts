Nomogram model for prediction of pulmonary tuberculosis patients with intestinal tuberculosis
Authors: Nongchao Liao, Yan Zhao, Yan Long, Tian Hu, Yueming Shen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn clinical settings, pulmonary tuberculosis (PTB) patients were often found to have intestinal tuberculosis (ITB). The aim of this study was to construct a predictive model to evaluate the probability of intestinal tuberculosis in patients with pulmonary tuberculosis.MethodsThe present case–control study retrospectively collected information from 348 patients affected by PTB who received treatment from January 1, 2019 – August 1, 2023 at Changsha Central Hospital. The 348 patients were divided into ITB group and non-ITB group according to the presence or absence of intestinal tuberculosis. logistic regression analysis was performed and a nomogram was established using the selected predictors. Bootstrapping was performed for internal validation.ResultsFive variables [age, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, hemoglobin (HGB), albumin (ALB)] were validated and used to develop a predictive model which showed good discrimination capability [area under the curve (AUC) = 0.856]. The calibration curve demonstrated the best consistency between nomogram predictions and actual observations. In addition, Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) showed that the nomogram model is effective in clinical practice.ConclusionsWe developed a preliminary predictive model that can help assess the probability of intestinal tuberculosis in pulmonary tuberculosis patients. Its clinical utility requires further validation in multi-center, prospective studies.Clinical trialNot applicable.Keywords: Intestinal tuberculosis, Pulmonary tuberculosis, Nomogram, Predictive model

Introduction

Tuberculosis (TB) is an infectious disease, which is caused by the bacillus Mycobacterium tuberculosis (MTB). Tuberculosis is spread when people with tuberculosis expel bacteria into the air, such as by coughing. More than 10 million people still fall ill with TB in 2022 [1]. The most prevalent variety of TB is pulmonary tuberculosis [2–4]. Roughly 15%–20% of TB patients have extrapulmonary TB (EPTB). Intestinal tuberculosis is one of the common forms of extrapulmonary tuberculosis. Intestinal tuberculosis is not a disease with a low incidence, especially in developing countries [5]. A large-scale multicenter study in China showed that 72.04% of patients with pulmonary tuberculosis and extrapulmonary tuberculosis had intestinal tuberculosis. Therefore, screening for ITB should be considered when tuberculosis is detected [6]. However, diagnosing intestinal tuberculosis (ITB) with uncommon clinical manifestations is often challenging [7].

Diagnosis of intestinal tuberculosis relies on the detection of Mycobacterium tuberculosis, However, some studies have shown that the sensitivity of acid-fast bacilli tissue staining is 5.3–37.5%; The sensitivity of mycobacterial culture is 23–46%, and the growth cycle of Mycobacterium tuberculosis is long; The polymerase chain reaction (PCR) sensitivity for Mycobacterium tuberculosis detection was 36.4–67.9% [8]. Mycobacterium tuberculosis and rifampicin resistance mutations can be simultaneously detected by Rifampicin resistance real-time fluorescence quantitative nucleic acid amplification detection technology (Xpert-Mtb/Rif). According to some studies, this technology has a sensitivity of 32% and a specificity of 100% [9].

ITB is genuinely the “great mimic,” and its diagnosis and treatment still depend on the keen clinical judgment of knowledgeable medical professionals [10]. Nomogram is a trusted tool for predicting the probability of a particular event, such as the occurrence of a disease, the effect of a treatment, etc. Therefore, it is widely used in the medical field. There is a lack of research on the clinical features of intestinal tuberculosis in patients with pulmonary tuberculosis. Against this backdrop, our study is to establish a predictive model that will allow us to determine the probability of complication of in PTB patients with intestinal tuberculosis.

Methods

Patient selection and study design

We designed and conducted a clinical retrospective study to determine the clinical features of in PTB patients with intestinal tuberculosis and to establish a matching predictive model. First, we collected data from 348 patients with tuberculosis from Changsha Central Hospital, University of South China, between January 1, 2019 and August 1, 2023 (including 172 patients with intestinal tuberculosis allocated to the ITB group). Next, we divided patients into intestinal tuberculosis and non-intestinal tuberculosis groups according to the criteria. Finally, the study included 172 patients with intestinal tuberculosis and 176 patients with non-intestinal tuberculosis. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were done on the data to determine the chance of PTB patients having intestinal tuberculosis and to develop a prediction model. Internal validation was carried out using bootstrapping (Fig. 1). Data collection was performed using a standardized electronic medical record extraction form to ensure consistency and completeness. Variables included demographic characteristics, clinical symptoms, laboratory findings, imaging results, and endoscopic features. To address missing data, multiple imputation was employed for variables with less than 10% missingness, while predictors with higher proportions of missing values were excluded from the final analysis.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Intestinal tuberculosis group: Eligible patients had proven PTB and met the diagnostic criteria for tuberculosis according to the “Health Industry Standards of the People’s Republic of China” issued in 2017 [11] and clinical criteria according to Chinese Society of Gastroenterology. Other criteria included the following: sixteen years of age or older, and those who have been diagnosed with intestinal tuberculosis for the first time during hospitalization. Patients with missing information, HIV, a history of gastrointestinal surgery, diagnostic anti-tuberculosis therapy for more than 1 month prior to admission and diagnosis of intestinal tuberculosis prior to pulmonary tuberculosis were excluded.

Non-intestinal tuberculosis group: Eligible patients were those with confirmed PTB but no ITB who only met the diagnostic criteria for tuberculosis according to the “Health Industry Standards of the People’s Republic of China” issued in 2017 [11] but not for intestinal tuberculosis as stated in the clinical criteria according to the Chinese Society of Gastroenterology [12]. Other criteria included the following: sixteen years of age or older, and those who have been diagnosed with PTB for the first time during hospitalization. Patients with missing information, HIV, a history of gastrointestinal surgery, diagnostic anti-tuberculosis therapy for more than 1 month prior to admission were excluded.

The Chinese Society of Gastroenterology’s clinical criteria are as follows: [12] (1) an intestinal tissue biopsy or mesenteric lymph node histological biopsy revealed epithelioid granuloma with caseous necrosis; (2) an intestinal tissue biopsy revealed MTB positivity on culture or acid-fast stain; (3) patients demonstrated a good response to anti-TB therapy with clinical manifestations consistent with active TB.

Candidate predictors

Based on the evidence currently available [12–15], we identified relevant clinical features of intestinal tuberculosis in patients with PTB. The following data were collected as candidate predictors: (1) demographic characteristics, such as sex (male or female) and age (<60/≥60 years); (2) individual pasts, including drinking and smoking behaviors (yes/no, where “no” denotes “never”); 3. The most common symptoms including fever (yes/no), abdominal pain (yes/no), diarrhoea (yes/no), weight loss(yes/no); 4. routine lab tests including HGB (g/L), White Blood Cell Count (WBC,×10^9/L), Neutrophil Count (NEUT,×10^9/L), Platelet (PLT,×10^9/L), ALB (Albumin, g/L), Glutamic Pyruvic Transaminase (ALT, U/L), Glutamic Oxaloacetic Transaminase (AST, U/L), Uric Acid (UA,µmoI/L), Creatinine (CREA,µmoI/L), Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate (ESR, mm/h) and C-reactive Protein (CRP, g/L).

Statistical analysis

We utilized SPSS (V.26.0) and R software (V.4.2.0) for statistical analyses which were all two-tailed. To compare continuous data, the independent t-test or w-t test were used, while categorical data was compared using the chi-square test. Predictors with a P-value < 0.05 in the univariate analysis were exclusively included in the subsequent multivariate logistic regression model. Subsequently, the multivariate logistic regression analysis was further optimized. The results of the optimization was displayed in a forest plot, from which we can obtain the OR(Odds Ratio). After optimization, the outcomes of multivariate logistic regression analysis were used to establish a prediction model for estimating the likelihood of intestinal tuberculosis in PTB patients. The receiver operating characteristics (ROC) curve was used to evaluate the effectiveness of the model established in this study in distinguishing between two different categories of samples (ITB and non-ITB patients). The discriminatory ability of the nomogram was assessed by the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and the area under the curve (AUC). The optimal cutoff value for risk prediction was determined by maximizing the Youden index (J = sensitivity + specificity − 1).The model’s diagnostic efficacy increases with the AUC’s closer to 1. Finally, the decision curve and calibration curve were used to evaluate the nomogram’s clinical value and accuracy. The calibration of the model was further assessed using calibration curves.

Results

This study involved 348 patients in total, based on inclusion and exclusion criteria. As shown in Table 1, there were 239 males (68.68%) and 109 females (31.32%) and the range was 18–92 years. The largest number of people are over 60 years old. There was no significant difference in smoking, drinking habits, ALT, UA and CREA among the patients with PTB included in this study. Laboratory indicators, including HGB, WBC, NEUT, ALB, AST, ESR, and CRP, differed significantly between the two groups (P < 0.05). After optimization, fever, abdominal pain, diarrhoea and weight loss were statistically significant symptoms (P < 0.05).

Establishment and validation of nomogram

Based on the aforementioned 5 variables, we established a prediction model to determine the likelihood that PTB patients will have intestinal tuberculosis. The OR of the predictors incorporated into the model were as follows: age<60,1; age ≥ 60,0.37; abdominal pain,11.76;diarrhoea,5.54; HGB,0.98; ALB,0.89. Then, we developed a risk prediction nomogram model of intestinal tuberculosis in patients with pulmonary tuberculosis after bootstrapping (Fig. 3). ROC analysis found that the nomogram’s AUC value was 0.856, indicating that it has strong discriminating ability. Based on the ROC curve, the optimal cutoff was − 0.309(Fig. 4). The sensitivity and specificity of cutoff corresponded to the relative optimal were 0.784 and 0.820, respectively. The calibration curve showed the highest concordance between nomogram predictions and actual measurements (Fig. 5). Furthermore, DCA demonstrated that the nomogram model works in actual practice (Fig. 6). When the threshold probability is between 15% and 95%, its net benefit is higher than the All line and None line. At this threshold probability, the model has practical application value.

Discussion

In line with the results of some earlier research, the clinical features of PTB patients with intestinal tuberculosis were linked to age, celiac disease, diarrhea, HGB, and ALB in our investigation [12, 16]. The nomogram provides a practical tool for risk assessment, and the identified cutoff value (−0.309) may assist in stratifying patients in clinical settings. By calculating an individual’s total points on the nomogram and comparing it to the cutoff, clinicians can identify those at higher risk of ITB who may benefit from further diagnostic evaluation. Patients with gastrointestinal symptoms during hospitalization require a high index of suspicion for intestinal tuberculosis. This is consistent with clinical practice. Patients under 60 years old with pulmonary tuberculosis have a greater probability to get intestinal tuberculosis, and TB prevalence has been found to be closely related to age [1].

In our study, albumin and hemoglobin levels at admission are independent predictors of intestinal tuberculosis in patients with PTB. The serum albumin concentration is an important marker of nutritional status [17]. Anemia was utilized as an indirect evaluation of the individuals’ nutritional status [18]. TB and malnutrition tend to correlate strongly with each other [19].

As previous studies have found, the relationship between tuberculosis and malnutrition is bidirectional, as malnutrition can also come from tuberculosis itself, which is caused by inflammation-related cachexia, anorexia, and malabsorption. Body mass index (BMI) is an important indicator of chronic malnutrition in the body, and determines whether an individual is obese and wasting. There is a negative correlation between the incidence of tuberculosis and BMI. People with mild, moderate and severe thinness had a 2.0-fold, 2.5-fold and 2.8-fold increased risk of TB compared to normal-weight subjects [20]. The daily dietary intake level is associated with the development of malnutrition in tuberculosis patients, and the intake of fruits and vegetables, poultry meat, fish and shrimp and milk in most tuberculosis patients does not reach the lowest intake level in China. Therefore, a lack of a proper diet may accelerate the progression of TB.

A series of animal investigations indicated that hypoalbuminemia could change the absolute and relative numbers of total T-lymphocytes and other immune system cell subpopulation [21], compromising host protection against MTB [22]. Bishlawy and IM EL shown that hemoglobin is highly bacteriostatic. In their experiment, a drop of washed RBCs is placed in a Petri dish containing nutritional agar injected with staphylococci and cultured for 24–48 h at 37 °C. The RBCs utilized were either pure or 50% diluted in saline [23]. The results demonstrated that undiluted washed RBCs inhibited bacterial growth but became impaired by dilution. This supports the conclusion that the incidence of intestinal tuberculosis is higher in anemic PTB patients than in non-anemic PTB patients, as well as why it was higher in moderate and severe anemic PTB patients compared to mild anemic patients.

To summarize, this may be the reason why PTB patients with anemia and hypoproteinemia are more likely to have intestinal tuberculosis.

When people’s immune systems deteriorate, they become more vulnerable to PTB and extrapulmonary tuberculosis. For PTB patients, if intestinal tuberculosis is discovered early and effectively treated, major complications such as blockage, fistula formation, and perforation can be avoided [24]. ITB is recognized as one of the most common sites of extrapulmonary involvement. Endoscopy is an important tool for diagnosing ITB. However, hospitals in least developed country often lack gastrointestinal endoscopes, making it easy to miss patients with intestinal tuberculosis. As a result, it is necessary to take intestinal tuberculosis and PTB coinfection into account. An accurate prediction model is critical for both doctors and PTB patients. This helps doctors to identify potential patients with intestinal tuberculosis in time and improve the diagnosis rate of intestinal tuberculosis.

Although various clinical criteria have been offered earlier, no matching model has been discovered that predicts the likelihood of combined intestinal tuberculosis in PTB patients. In light of these factors, a multivariable model based on readily available clinical data was created and internally verified. We hope that this model will reduce the rate of missed diagnosis of intestinal tuberculosis.

Limitations

However, our study has several limitations that should be considered. First, due to its single center retrospective design, the study is subject to inherent selection and information biases. All data were derived from a single center, which may limit the generalizability of the nomogram to other populations or clinical settings. Second, diagnostic challenges were present; some predictors relied on self-reported clinical symptoms, which are subject to recall bias and potential misrepresentation. Third, the sample size, though substantial, remains relatively small for developing a robust predictive model, which may affect the stability of the identified predictors. Fourth, diagnostic uncertainty exists due to the modest sensitivity of current methods for confirming intestinal tuberculosis, such as histology, culture, and Xpert-MTB/RIF, potentially leading to misclassification. Furthermore, due to symptoms such as abdominal pain and diarrhea becoming strong predictors in our models (with high odds ratios), any initial misclassification of diagnoses could be amplified, leading to an overestimation of the predictive strength of these symptom variables. Finally, the model was evaluated only through internal validation, which may result in overfitting and overly optimistic performance estimates. External validation in multi-center prospective cohorts is needed to better assess calibration, discriminative power, and overall applicability.

Conclusion

To sum up, a prediction model incorporating five independent predictors may have the potential to assist physicians and PTB patients in making more timely and informed assessments regarding the likelihood of intestinal tuberculosis. However, this nomogram serves as a preliminary screening tool rather than a definitive diagnostic method. It is important to emphasize that this is a preliminary model derived from a retrospective, single-center dataset. Although the internal validation results are promising, the model has not been externally validated in multi-center or prospective settings. Further research is needed to refine the model and evaluate its impact on patient outcomes.

MiR-582-3p regulates endothelial cell function in lower extremity deep vein thrombosis by targeting TFRC
Authors: Qiulian Luo, Anni Xu, Min Liang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
MicroRNAs (miRNAs) have been widely explored in the study and management of lower extremity deep vein thrombosis (LEDVT). However, the function and mechanism of miR-582‐3p in LEDVT remain unclear. The study aimed to explore the expression and regulatory functions of miR-582-3p in LEDVT and Human Umbilical Vein Endothelial Cells (HUVECs). Serum samples from 118 LEDVT patients and 82 healthy controls were collected. RT-qPCR was used to detect the expression levels of miR-582-3p and TFRC in serum and cells. The AUC and logistic analysis were used for the diagnosis and risk prediction of LEDVT. ox-LDL treated HUVECs were used to construct injury cell models. The CCK8, flow cytometry, and ELISA were employed to analyze cell function. Dual-luciferase reporter assays and rescue experiments verified the regulatory relationship between miR-582-3p and TFRC. MiR-582-3p was significantly downregulated in LEDVT patients and in ox-LDL-HUVECs. It was negatively correlated with the levels of D-dimer and TAT III. Additionally, for miR-582-3p, the AUC was 0.870, and it was an independent protective factor. MiR-582-3p mimics promoted the proliferation of ox-LDL-HUVECs, inhibited their apoptosis, and reduced the levels of inflammatory factors (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6) and coagulation factors (FXⅢ, TF, PAF). Additionally, miR-582-3p directly targeted TFRC, and overexpression of TFRC reversed the protective effect of miR-582-3p against ox-LDL-induced cellular damage. MiR-582-3p is a potential diagnostic biomarker and independent protective factor for LEDVT. MiR-582-3p regulates endothelial cell function by targeting TFRC, suggesting its potential as a therapeutic target for LEDVT.

Introduction

Lower extremity deep vein thrombosis (LEDVT) is a venous return obstructive disease caused by intravascular thrombus formation in the deep veins of the lower limbs [1]. It is associated with major surgery, prolonged immobilization, severe trauma, and active cancer [2]. The traditional pathogenesis of LEDVT follows Virchow’s triad, the synergistic effect of vascular endothelial injury, blood stasis, and a hypercoagulable state [3]. Affecting nearly 10 million people worldwide annually, LEDVT constitutes a significant component of the global disease burden [4]. Due to its insidious onset and nonspecific early clinical symptoms, approximately 20% of patients with severe complications die within 1 year of diagnosis [5]. Although anticoagulation, thrombolysis, and thrombectomy have been incorporated into clinical guidelines [6], these patients still progress to post-thrombotic syndrome, and nearly half of acute DVT patients exhibit functional and exercise limitations [7], indicating the limitations of existing diagnostic and therapeutic strategies in the whole-course management of the disease. Current research remains confined to the regulation of coagulation factor cascade activation and inflammatory pathways, lacking analysis of non-coding RNA-mediated endothelial cell’s functional networks [8].

In the past few years, microRNAs (miRNAs), which act as crucial regulators in gene expression, have been established to participate in the occurrence and evolution of LEDVT via targeting the 3’ untranslated region of mRNA, thereby revealing their potential as biomarkers and therapeutic targets [9]. Downregulation of miR-513c-5p promotes endothelial cell apoptosis by targeting Caspase-1, thereby accelerating thrombus progression [10]. Upregulation of miR-150 promotes angiogenesis and proliferation of endothelial progenitor cells (EPCs) to facilitate thrombus resolution by targeting SRCIN1 [11]. MiR-486-5p inhibits endothelial angiogenesis by downregulating eNOS [12]. MiR-582-3p is downregulated in thoracic aortic aneurysm tissues and injured vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs), and the miR-582-3p/ADAM10 axis can alleviate CoCl₂-induced damage to VSMCs [13]. Additionally, RNA sequencing analysis has shown that miR-582-3p is a significantly downregulated candidate molecule in the miRNA expression profile associated with LEDVT [14]. Based on the above research background, we hypothesize that miR-582-3p may be involved in the occurrence and development of LEDVT. However, its target genes and regulatory signaling pathways in LEDVT-related cells remain unclear, especially regarding the regulatory mechanism of coagulation-inflammatory factors, which urgently require in-depth investigation.

This study intends to detect the expression level of miR-582-3p in patients with LEDVT and clarify its diagnostic value through ROC curve analysis and logistic regression model. Furthermore, by means of cell function experiments, bioinformatics prediction and verification, it aims to explore the effects of miR-582-3p on cell functions, coagulation, and inflammatory factors, and its target gene regulatory mechanism, so as to provide new insights for the molecular diagnosis and targeted therapy of LEDVT.

Materials and methods

Patient information

The experiment included patients who were first diagnosed with LEDVT at Liaocheng People’s Hospital from July 2021 to February 2024. According to the American Heart Association [15], the included patients met the following criteria: time since onset of DVT ≤ 14 days, unilateral initial DVT, normal cognitive function, and good communication skills; they had not undergone any anticoagulant therapy or surgical intervention prior to the treatment. Patients with diabetes, neurological disorders, coagulation disorders, other concurrent diseases, severe cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, malignant tumors, those in pregnancy or lactation, and those with abnormal immune function were excluded. Finally, 118 LEDVT patients were included. This study collected 82 healthy people who underwent physical examinations at the same hospital during the same period, with matched clinical Basic Information, as the control group. The Doppler ultrasound examination of the control group was normal, and there were no chronic diseases.

The study was performed in line with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Approval was granted by the Ethics Committee of Liaocheng People’s Hospital before the study began. All patients involved in the study were enrolled following ethical guidelines, and the informed consent of all participants was secured, strictly adhering to research ethical norms.

After diagnosis, peripheral venous blood was collected from the patients’ median cubital vein in the fasting state. The serum was separated by centrifugation, then collected and stored at -80℃.

Cell culture and transfection

Human Umbilical Vein Endothelial Cells (HUVECs) were obtained from ZQXZ Biology (DFSC-EC-01). These cells were cultivated in DMEM medium at 37℃ under a 5% CO₂ atmosphere. Ox-LDL was purchased from Yeason Biotech (20605ES05). HUVECs were treated with ox-LDL at distinct concentrations and for varying durations, drawing on the approaches reported in earlier studies [16]. The miR-582-3p mimics and their negative control (miR-NC) were obtained from GenePharma, while the TFRC overexpression vector (oe-TFRC) and its negative control (oe-NC) were sourced from Beijing Solarbio Science. All transfections were performed using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, USA).

RT-qPCR

Total RNA was isolated from the serum of LEDVT patients and HUVECs using TRIzol reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Reverse transcription was carried out with the PrimeScript RT (Takara) reagent kit. The amplification reaction mixture was prepared as per the instructions of the Takara PrimeScript RT reagent Kit for gene detection. All samples were analyzed in triplicate on the ABI 7900HT system (Applied Biosystems). GAPDH served as the internal reference for mRNA, while U6 was used as the endogenous control for miRNA. The relative expression levels were calculated by the 2-ΔΔCt method. All experimental procedures were repeated in triplicate. CCK-8 assay

After completing the transfection, cells were seeded 5 × 10³ cells per well to proceed with the experiment. HUVECs underwent a culture period of 0, 24, 48, or 72 h. Subsequently, cells were incubated with CCK-8 reagent (Dojindo) at 37 °C for 2 h. The absorbance (OD value) at 450 nm was determined by means of a microplate reader, and the cell proliferation rate was derived accordingly from the measured OD values.

Apoptosis detection

At 48 h after transfection, these cells underwent staining in the dark with 5 µL Annexin V-FITC and propidium iodide (PI). Apoptosis was then analyzed using a flow cytometer (BD Biosciences) in accordance with the manufacturer’s recommended protocols.

ELISA

HUVECs culture supernatants were collected. As for the levels of tumor necrosis factor α (TNF-α), interleukin-1β (IL-1β), interleukin-6 (IL-6), coagulation factor ⅩⅢ (FXⅢ), tissue factor (TF), and platelet-activating factor (PAF) were detected in triplicate using human ELISA kits (Mersake Biotech).

DLR assay

TFRC-WT or TFRC-MUT luciferase reporter was co-transfected with miR-582-3p mimics or inhibitor into cells. Luciferase activity was measured with the Dual-Luciferase Assay (Promega, WI, USA) after 48 h of transfection.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay

MiR-582-3p mimics, oe-TFRC, and Myc-Ago2 were co-transfected into cells. Following 48 h of incubation, an Ago2-centered RIP assay was performed. Cells were lysed using RIP lysis buffer. Subsequently, 1 mL of TRIzol reagent was added to extract total RNA from both IP and input samples. RT-qPCR was then conducted to quantify the enrichment of target RNA complexes.

Bioinformatics analysis

The binding relationships between miRNAs and target genes were obtained through the starBase database and TargetScan database. Genes related to IA were acquired via the GeneCards database. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analysis of the intersecting genes was performed using the DAVID database.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were triplicated, and results were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Data analysis and visualization were performed using GraphPad Prism and SPSS. Statistical analyses included Student’s t-test for two-group comparisons, one-way or two-way ANOVA, followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test for multiple-group analyses. For the correlation analysis, we employed Pearson’s linear correlation. We used the ROC curve of miR-582-3p to evaluated its diagnostic discriminatory ability. Multivariate binary LOGISTIC regression was used to identify independent risk factors. * p < 0.05.

Results

The expression of miR-582-3p is down-regulated in LEDVT

Baseline characteristics of the study subjects are summarized, and there were no statistical differences in age, BMI, gender, blood lipid, blood pressure, and hemoglobin (Table 2). However, the patient group showed significantly higher levels of D-dimer and thrombin-antithrombin complex III (TAT III). To elaborate on the expression of miR-582-3p in LEDVT, RT-qPCR detection revealed that the serum miR-582-3p levels in LEDVT patients were notably lower when compared with those in healthy controls. (Fig. 1A). Meanwhile, the expression level of miR-582-3p was significantly negatively correlated with the levels of D-dimer (Fig. 1B) and TAT III (Fig. 1C). The diagnostic ability of miR-582-3p was evaluated by the ROC curve, with an AUC of 0.870 and 95% CI: 0.817–0.923, a sensitivity of 88.98% and an optimal specificity of 76.83%, an optimal cut-off value of 0.955, and a Youden’s index of 65.81% (Fig. 1D). MiR-582-3p demonstrated high diagnostic potential in distinguishing LEDVT patients from healthy individuals. Logistic regression analysis (Table 3) further suggested that the odds ratio (OR) value of miR-582-3p was 0.109 (95% CI: 0.044–0.299, p < 0.001), the OR value of D-dimer was 5.055 (95% CI: 1.717–14.427, p = 0.003), the OR value of TAT III was 4.880 (95% CI: 1.767–11.377, p = 0.001). Effect of miR-582-3p on ox-LDL-induced damage to HUVECs

The expression of miR-582-3p declined after cells were treated with ox-LDL for 24 h in a dose-dependent manner, and the decreased level exceeded half at 40 µg/mL ox-LDL (Fig. 2A). Different durations of 40 µg/mL ox-LDL treatment on HUVECs resulted in a time-dependent downregulation of miR-582-3p expression, which dropped by more than half at 24 h (Fig. 2B). Therefore, 40 µg/mL ox-LDL treatment for 24 h was selected for subsequent cell experiments. Transfection of miR-582-3p mimics significantly enhanced the expression level of miR-582-3p in HUVECs (Fig. 2C). Meanwhile, in HUVECs treated with ox-LDL, transfection of miR-582-3p mimics can also increase the expression of miR-582-3p (Fig. 2D). After treatment with ox-LDL, HUVECs exhibited a decrease in proliferation (Fig. 2E) and an increase in apoptosis (Fig. 2F). Additionally, ox-LDL treatment improved the level of inflammatory factors (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6; Fig. 2G) and coagulation factors (FXⅢ, TF, PAF; Fig. 2H). When HUVECs were transfected with miR-582-3p mimics, the damage caused by ox-LDL can be alleviated. The target regulatory relationship between miR-582-3p and TFRC

To predict potential target genes, the starBase and TargetScan databases were employed, and an intersection was taken with genes related to LEDVT in GeneCards databases, resulting in 43 target genes (Fig. 3A). After that, we conducted KEGG analysis and found that TFRC is involved in HIF-1 and TGF signaling pathways related to venous thrombosis (Fig. 3B). StarBase databases revealed the existence of potential binding sites between miR-582-3p and TFRC. DLR analysis confirmed that miR-582-3p mimics significantly reduced the relative luciferase activity of TFRC-WT in HUVECs but had no significant effect on the luciferase activity of TFRC-MUT (Fig. 3C). RIP assays demonstrated that TFRC and miR-582-3p were significantly enriched in anti-Ago2 complexes, indicating that there is targeted binding between TFRC and miR-582-3p (Fig. 3D). TFRC was upregulated in patients with LEDVT compared to that in HC (Fig. 3E) and was negatively correlated with miR-582-3p (Fig. 3F). MiR-582-3p affects the cell functions of HUVECs by targeting TFRC

To clarify the potential regulatory role of the miR-582-3p/TFRC axis in LEDVT, miR-582-3p mimics and TFRC overexpression vectors were co-transfected into ox-LDL-treated HUVECs. Transfection of the TFRC overexpression vector could upregulate the expression of TFRC in HUVECs (Fig. 4A). Results of the rescue experiment demonstrated that the inhibitory effect of miR-582-3p on TFRC expression was reversed by co-transfection with the TFRC overexpression vector. (Fig. 4B). In ox-LDL-treated HUVECs, TFRC overexpression reversed the promotion of cell proliferation (Fig. 4C) and the inhibition of cell apoptosis (Fig. 4D) induced by miR-582-3p mimics. Meanwhile, TFRC overexpression also reversed the inhibitory effect of miR-582-3p mimics on the level of inflammatory factors (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, Fig. 4E) and coagulation factors (FXⅢ, TF, PAF, Fig. 4F). Then, miR-582-3p inhibitor and si-TFRC were co-transfected into ox-LDL-treated HUVECs. We found that transfection of si-TFRC could downregulate the expression of TFRC in HUVEC (Fig. 5A). Transfection of the miR-582-3p inhibitor further enhanced the ox-LDL-induced increase in TFRC levels, and this effect was partially abrogated by subsequent transfection with si-TFRC (Fig. 5B). Similarly, transfection of the miR-582-3p inhibitor further enhanced the ox-LDL-induced proliferation inhibition (Fig. 5C), exacerbated apoptosis (Fig. 5D), and the expression levels of inflammatory factors (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, Fig. 5E) and coagulation factors (FXⅢ, TF, PAF, Fig. 5F). Discussion

LEDVT is a prevalent venous thromboembolic disease, and the search for highly specific diagnostic biomarkers has been a key focus of clinical research due to its non-obvious onset manifestations [17]. Although D-dimer is a commonly used biomarker for DVT diagnosis, its high expression in non-thrombotic diseases (including inflammatory infections and tumor diseases) significantly restricts its clinical utility [18]. In recent years, the regulatory roles of miRNAs in cardiovascular diseases and cancer have emerged as research hotspots [19, 20]. As biomarkers, miRNAs have core advantages such as strong stability, high specificity, easy sample acquisition, early detectability, and convenient detection. They have significant application potential in disease diagnosis, prognosis assessment, and treatment efficacy monitoring [21, 22]. Notably, miR-582-3p has been implicated in cerebral ischemia/reperfusion injury model [12], cerebral amyloid angiopathy [23], ovarian cancer [24], and bladder cancer [25]. MiR-582-3p is hypothesized to serve as a novel specific diagnostic biomarker for LEDVT, which merits further exploration.

The research revealed that the serum miR-582-3p level in patients with LEDVT was significantly downregulated; it is suggested that miR-582-3p may be involved in the progression of LEDVT. The high levels of TAT III and D-dimer in patients with LEDVT have diagnostic significance [26, 27], and miR-582-3p expression showed a notably negative correlation with TAT III and D-dimer. ROC curve analysis showed that it could serve as a potential biomarker for the diagnosis of LEDVT. The finding of the high-efficiency discrimination ability of serum miR-582-3p between DVT patients and healthy individuals is highly consistent with the abnormal expression pattern of miRNA in thrombotic diseases in previous studies [13], confirming the key function of miR-582-3p in venous thrombosis. Logistic regression analysis indicated that miR-582-3p is an independent protective factor capable of predicting the risk of LEDVT. In summary, miR-582-3p can serve as a novel biomarker for LEDVT, with both diagnostic value and risk prediction potential, and may thus provide new insights for the clinical management of LEDVT.

To further evaluate the possible mechanism of miR-582-3p in LEDVT, we conducted in vitro cell experiments using HUVECs. As a dynamic barrier separating blood from tissues, the functional integrity of VECs is the core basis for maintaining vascular homeostasis. When endothelial cells are damaged or undergo apoptosis due to mechanical injury, inflammatory stimulation, or metabolic disorders, their anticoagulant function rapidly becomes imbalanced [28]. Ox-LDL can induce excessive reactive oxygen species production, activate the NF-κB inflammatory signaling pathway, and promote endothelial cell apoptosis; it also disrupts the balance of the coagulation-anticoagulation system by upregulating tissue factor (TF) and downregulating anticoagulant molecules [29, 30]. The injury model of LEDVT is constructed by inducing HUVEC with ox-LDL, and it has been widely applied in terms of inflammation and coagulation function [31, 32]. Our results showed that ox-LDL treatment reduced miR-582-3p expression, and this reduction was closely associated with impaired HUVEC functions. Specifically, decreased proliferation, increased apoptosis, and elevated secretion of inflammatory (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6) and coagulation factors (FXⅢ, TF, PAF) were observed. Coagulation factors exert a crucial effect on how blood clots are formed. The exposure of subendothelial collagen activates FXⅢ to initiate the intrinsic coagulation pathway, while the TF released by damaged endothelium activates the extrinsic coagulation pathway, leading to a large amount of thrombin production [33]. The binding of PAF to platelet surface receptors mediates calcium influx and α-granule release through the PLC-IP3 pathway, promoting platelet aggregation and the formation of a preliminary embolus at the injury site [34]. TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 directly weaken the vascular barrier function by downregulating the expression of anticoagulant molecules in cells [35], forming a vicious cycle of inflammation - endothelial injury - enhanced coagulation. However, transfection of miR-582-3p mimics effectively reversed these ox-LDL-induced pathological changes. Collectively, it can be inferred from these findings that miR-582-3p exerts a regulatory effect on cell functions, inflammation, and coagulation processes, indicating its potential as a critical modulator in the pathophysiology of LEDVT.

Further verify through prediction and experimentation, our results showed that TFRC is a target gene of miR-582-3p, providing mechanistic evidence for their interaction in HUVECs. TFRC is a specific marker for ferroptosis and also a universal transporter used by all cells to uptake iron ions from extracellular transferrin [36]. It has been confirmed through studies that TFRC shows high expression in LEDVT, with clear identification of it as a hub protein in the coagulation cascade [37]. Its upregulated expression leads to ultimately promotes thrombus formation [38]. In this study, ox-LDL treatment upregulated TFRC in HUVECs and in the serum of patients with LEDVT, showing a strong negative correlation with miR-582-3p. More importantly, TFRC overexpression reversed the protective effects of miR-582-3p mimics against ox-LDL-induced injury. Conversely, Knockdown of TFRC reversed the exacerbation of ox-LDL-induced injury by miR-582-3p inhibitor. This suggests that the miR-582-3p/TFRC axis mediates the regulatory effects of endothelial function under ox-LDL-induced stress. Ox-LDL downregulates miR-582-3p, leading to the release of TFRC from post-transcriptional inhibition; the accumulated TFRC then amplifies inflammatory responses and coagulation activation, ultimately contributing to endothelial dysfunction and LEDVT progression. Notably, the regulatory role of the miR-582-3p/TFRC axis in ox-LDL-induced endothelial injury also provides new insights into LEDVT treatment.

There are several limitations that ought to be recognized. First, the study only included patients with unilateral acute-phase LEDVT, while patients with chronic phase, bilateral lesions, and post-thrombotic syndrome were excluded. Future multicenter studies with larger cohorts and prospective designs are needed to validate the diagnostic and prognostic value of miR-582-3p in LEDVT. Secondly, only using ox-LDL to induce the injury of HUVEC to simulate the endothelial injury in LEDVT results in a single model, which has a significant difference from the in - vivo pathological environment. In the future, in vivo animal experiments should be carried out to verify the role and regulatory mechanism of miR-582-3p mimics/TFRC. Future miR-582-3p mimics or TFRC inhibitors might be used in high-risk populations to protect endothelial function and reduce LEDVT risk and combined with traditional anticoagulants to improve therapeutic efficacy and reduce post-thrombotic syndrome incidence.

Conclusion

In summary, this study demonstrates that miR-582-3p is a potential diagnostic biomarker and independent protective factor for LEDVT. miR-582-3p can alleviate ox-LDL-induced vascular endothelial injury by improving endothelial cell functions, reducing the release of inflammatory and coagulation factors through targeting TFRC, thereby contributing to the progression of lower extremity venous thrombosis. These findings provide a fresh theoretical foundation for precise intervention in venous thromboembolic diseases.

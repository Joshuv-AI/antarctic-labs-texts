Endobronchial ultrasound–guided transbronchial mediastinal cryobiopsy versus transbronchial needle aspiration for diagnosing sarcoidosis: A randomized controlled trial
Authors: Mingming Deng, Fei Tang, Yifei Chen, Shuai Zhao, Run Tong, Zhen Yang, Changhao Zhong, Feng Wang, Quncheng Zhang, Ziwen Zheng, Nan Zhang, Guowu Zhou, Gang Hou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and ObjectivesEndobronchial ultrasound (EBUS)–guided transbronchial mediastinal cryobiopsy (TBMC) has potential superiority compared with EBUS-guided transbronchial needle aspiration (TBNA) in the diagnosis of patients with intrathoracic sarcoidosis. However, high-quality clinical evidence is lacking. The aim of this study was to compare the efficacy and safety of EBUS-TBMC and EBUS-TBNA in diagnosing patients with intrathoracic sarcoidosis.MethodsA multicenter randomized controlled trial was conducted in patients with suspected stage I/II sarcoidosis. Following identification of the mediastinal lesion via EBUS, TBNA and TBMC were sequentially performed in a randomized order. The granuloma detection rate was the primary outcome. The final diagnosis was based on pathology results and clinical follow-up at 6 months after randomization.ResultsA total of 128 patients with suspected stage I/II sarcoidosis were recruited for this study. Among them, 116 patients were diagnosed with sarcoidosis at 6 months after randomization. The granuloma detection rate was 89.8% (95% confidence interval [CI], 83.3–94.5) for EBUS-TBMC and 78.1% (95% CI, 70.0–84.9) for EBUS-TBNA, and the difference was significant (P < 0.001). The histological quality of the EBUS-TBMC samples was superior to that of the EBUS-TBNA samples (P < 0.001). Only one case of pneumothorax was detected by chest radiography, which resolved spontaneously without the need for drainage or other interventions.ConclusionsCompared with EBUS-TBNA, EBUS-TBMC had superior granuloma detection rate and histological quality of samples with acceptable safety in patients suspected with stage I/II sarcoidosis.

INTRODUCTION

Sarcoidosis is a multisystem granulomatous disease with unknown etiology that is characterized histologically by non-caseating granulomas in multiple organs, especially the mediastinum, and parenchymal changes in the lung.[1] More than 90% of patients with sarcoidosis present with intrathoracic lymphadenopathy.[2–6] The diagnostic criteria for sarcoidosis have not been standardized, but three major criteria are commonly used: 1) a compatible clinical presentation, 2) nonnecrotizing granulomatous inflammation in one or more tissue samples, and 3) the exclusion of alternative causes of granulomatous disease.[7] However, the acquisition of sufficient tissue samples for diagnosing sarcoidosis is challenging.

Tissue specimens are often used in the diagnostic evaluation of suspected sarcoidosis. In clinical practice, tissue is initially sampled using the least risky and least-invasive method, such as sampling skin or peripheral lymph node abnormalities suggestive of sarcoidosis. However, most new cases of sarcoidosis lack skin and peripheral lymph node findings; for these patients, invasive testing is pursued from the beginning.[8] Recent clinical practice guidelines from the American Thoracic Society recommend endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS)–guided transbronchial needle aspiration (TBNA) as the initial sampling method for the mediastinal and/or hilar lymph nodes because of its high diagnostic yield (87%).[9] However, 13% of patients who undergo EBUS-TBNA still require an additional sampling procedure. Therefore, a better minimally invasive biopsy method for the diagnosis of intrathoracic sarcoidosis is still needed.

EBUS–transbronchial mediastinal cryobiopsy (TBMC) has been designed and developed in recent years[10–12] and is an area of growing interest. Compared with EBUS-TBNA, larger high-quality tissue samples with fewer crush artifacts can be obtained using EBUS-TBMC.[13,14] Previous studies have initially demonstrated the advantages of EBUS-TBMC over EBUS-TBNA in the diagnosis of sarcoidosis in unselected populations.[14,15] However, data on the safety and efficacy of EBUS-TBMC in the diagnosis of sarcoidosis have been reported only in subgroup analyses. Therefore, the existing sample size of patients with sarcoidosis was insufficient to determine the difference in diagnostic yield and safety between EBUS-TBMC and EBUS-TBNA in diagnosing sarcoidosis.

Here, we hypothesized that the granuloma detection rate of EBUS-TBMC in patients suspected to have stage I/II sarcoidosis would be greater than that of EBUS-TBNA. A randomized controlled trial was conducted to evaluate the granulomas detection rate and safety of EBUS-TBMC and EBUS-TBNA in patients suspected to have stage I/II sarcoidosis.

METHODS

This study was an open-label, crossover, randomized controlled trial. Patients were recruited from three sites in this multicenter study, including both university and general hospitals. EBUS-TBMC and EBUS-TBNA can be performed at each center. This study was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05803239). The manuscript is reported according to the Standards for Reporting Diagnostic Accuracy studies (STARD 2015) statement,[16] Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) statement, and the Reporting Standards for Diagnostic Testing Guidance for Authors from Editors of Respiratory, Sleep, and Critical Care Journals.[17]

Participants

Consecutive adult patients with clinical and radiological suspicion of stage I (mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy) or stage II (lymphadenopathy and parenchymal abnormalities) sarcoidosis were included in this study. Exclusion criteria included the following: pathologically confirmed sarcoidosis, obvious organ involvement with the possibility of confirming granulomas using a minimally invasive diagnostic procedure (e.g., skin lesions or superficial lymph nodes), pregnancy, and medical contraindications to bronchoscopy procedures. Participants were recruited from outpatient clinics in the respiratory and critical care medicine departments of the participating centers. This study was approved by the human research ethics committee at each center. This study was conducted in accordance with the amended Declaration of Helsinki. All participants provided written informed consent before randomization.

Randomization

Patients were randomized 1:1 to receive either EBUS-TBNA followed by EBUS-TBMC (EBUS-TBNA first group) or EBUS-TBMC followed by EBUS-TBNA (EBUS-TBMC first group) using a computer-generated permuted block scheme. The random allocation sequence was concealed from participants and investigators before randomization was performed. Neither the participants nor the investigators were blinded due to the nature of the procedure. An independent research assistant at each center collected data using paper-based case forms, which were then submitted to a central coordinating site (China-Japan Friendship Hospital).

Procedures

All bronchoscopy procedures were performed with the patients under conscious sedation with midazolam and local anesthesia using lidocaine. Before sampling, all participants underwent a routine bronchoscopy examination and a systematic endosonographic evaluation of any accessible mediastinal/hilar lymph nodes using the EBUS Assessment Tool (EUSAT).[18] After EBUS inspection, the most suspicious, prominent and easily accessible nodes were selected for tissue sampling.

EBUS-TBMC was performed as previously described[11,12] using a 2.7-mm puncture dilation catheter (BroncTru™ AK-91-55; Broncus Inc., Hangzhou, China), an ERBECRYO system and a 1.1-mm cryoprobe (Erbe 20402-401; Erbe, Tübingen, Germany). Briefly, the cryoprobe was inserted easily into the target lesion through the working channel of the EBUS bronchoscope (BF-UC260F; Olympus, Tokyo, Japan), and a tunnel was created by the puncture dilation catheter under EBUS monitoring. TBMC was then performed, with a cryotime of approximately 5–9 s. The detailed EBUS-TBMC procedure is shown in Figure 1. Based on a previous study, three nodal samples were collected per participant to achieve the best diagnostic yield.[19] The samples obtained by EBUS-TBMC were retrieved for analysis by first thawing in saline and then fixing in formalin. EBUS-TBNA was performed using a 22-G needle, with the detailed steps described in a previous study.[20] According to a prospective study, at least four passes per patient with EBUS-TBNA are recommended for single lesions for pathological diagnosis of stage I/II sarcoidosis.[21] In the present study, a total of five passes were performed per person (four samples for histological evaluation and one sample for microbiology). The acquisition and handling of samples obtained with EBUS-TBNA followed the guidelines of the American College of Chest Physicians,[22] and cell block preparation was performed to significantly increase the yield of EBUS-TBNA for diagnosing sarcoidosis.[23] In addition, tissue samples obtained by EBUS-TBMC and EBUS-TBNA were routinely sent for acid–fast staining to test for Mycobacterium tuberculosis. Rapid-on-site cytopathological evaluation was not performed.

Histopathology assessment was performed at each site by pathologists blinded to the sequence of biopsy techniques. All participants underwent chest radiography for 4 weeks after biopsy to monitor potential EBUS-related adverse events. Clinical and radiological follow-up was performed for all participants after biopsy to reassess the diagnosis.

Reference standard

Sarcoidosis was diagnosed by a physician who was blinded to the sequence of the biopsy techniques; diagnosis was based on pathological findings and other clinical information (e.g., microbiologic results, features of bronchoalveolar lavage fluid, and serum levels of angiotensin-converting enzyme) according to the European Respiratory Society (ERS)/American Thoracic Society (ATS)/World Association of Sarcoidosis and Other Granulomatous Disorders (WASOG) consensus statement.[24] Tuberculosis is a major disease that needed to be distinguished from sarcoidosis. Tuberculosis was diagnosed based on positive microbiology results at a compatible clinic at baseline or follow-up combined with clinical radiological findings during antimycobacterial treatment.

Outcomes

The primary outcome of this study was the granuloma detection rate of EBUS-TBMC and EBUS-TBNA in diagnosing sarcoidosis. The granuloma detection rate was defined as the number of patients with granulomas detected relative to the total number of patients tested.[25]

The secondary outcomes included the diagnostic yield, the negative predictive value of EBUS-TBMC and EBUS-TBNA in patients with lymphadenopathy with suspected stage I/II sarcoidosis, the incidence of any adverse events and the quality of the biopsy samples. The diagnostic yield was defined as the proportion of all participants who underwent the diagnostic procedure in whom a specific malignant or benign diagnosis was established.[17,26] Samples showing only blood, necrosis, or acute or chronic inflammation and those with insufficient tissue to make any diagnosis were considered nondiagnostic results.[26] The quality of biopsy samples was assessed according to sample adequacy and histological quality. Samples providing a definite diagnosis and those with visible lymphocytes were considered adequate, as previously described.[27] Histological quality was assessed by the amount of cell material, background blood, degree of cellular degeneration and trauma, and retention of normal architecture. The quality of each subdomain was scored on an ordinal scale from 0 to 2, resulting in an overall score of 0–8, where higher scores reflect increased quality.[28] The histological quality of biopsy samples was also evaluated in terms of the volume of blood clots versus the actual amount of tissue, and slides from the two different biopsy methods were compared.[29]

Statistical analysis

In our prior study, the sensitivities of EBUS-TBMC and EBUS-TBNA in patients with sarcoidosis were 97.0% and 84.0%, respectively, and the prevalence of sarcoidosis in patients with lymphadenopathy with suspected stage I/II sarcoidosis was 90%. Assuming a dropout rate of 5%, a sample size of 128 patients was needed to achieve a power of 1 − B = 0.9 and α = 0.05 with two-sided testing. The calculation of sample size was performed with PASS 2021 (Version 21.0.3, NCSS Corp.). Chi-square tests were employed to compare categorical variables, including the granuloma detection rate for diagnosing sarcoidosis and diagnostic yield in patients with lymphadenopathy with suspected stage I/II sarcoidosis, between EBUS-TBMC and EBUS-TBNA. Statistical significance was set as a P value <0.05. All analyses were performed with SPSS (Version 24.0, IBM Corporation).

RESULTS

Participants

A total of 128 participants were recruited for the present study from three sites (China-Japan Friendship Hospital, Anhui Chest Hospital, and Second Hospital Affiliated with Harbin Medical University) from March 2024 to October 2024. All participants underwent the EBUS procedure and completed the entire follow-up period. Ultimately, diagnosis of sarcoidosis was performed in 116 participants (57 in the EBUS-TBNA first group and 59 in the EBUS-TBMC first group). In addition, six participants were diagnosed with lung cancer, three participants were diagnosed with tuberculosis, and three participants were diagnosed with silicosis. Diagnostic performance

Overall, the diagnostic yield of EBUS-TBMC (95.3%) was superior to that of EBUS-TBNA (83.6%) in patients with suspected stage I/II sarcoidosis (P < 0.001). The granuloma detection rate significantly differed between the EBUS-TBMC group and the EBUS-TBNA group (89.8% vs. 78.1%, P < 0.001). The difference in granuloma detection rate was also statistically significant between the EBUS-TBNA first group and the EBUS-TBMC first group (94.7% vs. 84.2%, P = 0.041). No false-positive results were obtained. The negative predictive value of EBUS-TBMC in patients with sarcoidosis was 75.0% (95% CI, 47.6%–92.7%), which was not significantly different from that of EBUS-TBNA (OR, 1.19; P = 0.746). Sampling quality and safety

All the specimens collected by EBUS-TBNA and EBUS-TBMC were considered adequate. The histological quality of the samples significantly differed (P < 0.001) between EBUS-TBNA (mean score, 4.5 ± 1.2) and EBUS-TBMC (mean score, 7.6 ± 0.5). The histological structure was better preserved in samples obtained by EBUS-TBMC than in those obtained by EBUS-TBNA. Representative images of paired slices are shown in Figure 3.

No major adverse events were observed either during the procedure or at the 4-week follow-up visit. Bleeding was minor in all patients who underwent the EBUS procedure and subsided without the need for intervention. Only one case of pneumothorax was detected by chest radiography, which resolved spontaneously without the need for drainage or other interventions.

DISCUSSION

To our knowledge, this study is the first randomized controlled trial to directly compare the efficacy and safety of EBUS-TBMC and EBUS-TBNA in patients with sarcoidosis. We found that EBUS-TBMC sampling of mediastinal and/or hilar lymph nodes has a better granuloma detection rate and sample histological quality compared with those of EBUS-TBNA for diagnosing patients with sarcoidosis. Therefore, EBUS-TBMC has a greater granuloma detection rate than EBUS-TBNA in patients with lymphadenopathy with suspected stage I/II sarcoidosis. In addition, adverse events were rare for both procedures.

Sonographic features of lymph nodes on EBUS can be useful in the prediction of malignancy in mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes. Previous retrospective studies have shown that EBUS features could also aid in the diagnosis of sarcoidosis and in the differentiation of tuberculosis from sarcoidosis.[30,31] The advancement of deep learning has enabled the design and development of an accurate automated tool for the diagnosis of intrathoracic lymph nodes with greater diagnostic efficiency and consistency than those of experts.[32] However, sarcoidosis remains a diagnosis of exclusion, and histology is essential to provide an accurate diagnosis and exclude differential diagnoses. In recent decades, EBUS-TBNA has become an important sampling method for lymphadenopathy, balancing diagnostic efficiency and safety.

Although EBUS-TBNA has been recommended as the initial mediastinal and/or hilar lymph node sampling procedure by guidelines, 13% of patients require an additional sampling procedure,[9] likely because the small volume of EBUS-TBNA samples available for cytopathological evaluation provides little information on histological evaluation and limits the diagnostic value of the procedure.[14] Many attempts have been made to overcome these limitations of EBUS-TBNA, but the results have been unexpected. Some studies have suggested that rapid on-site cytological evaluation (ROSE) could increase the diagnostic yield of EBUS-TBNA by increasing the adequacy of the samples obtained with this method.[33] However, the results of a meta-analysis suggested that the use of ROSE neither improved the diagnostic yield nor reduced the TBNA procedure time.[34] This finding may be because the sampling adequacy reflects the cytological quality rather than the histological quality of samples obtained by EBUS-TBNA. The diagnosis of sarcoidosis requires specific clinical findings, a histological demonstration of non-caseating granulomas, and the exclusion of other diseases with similar histological or clinical findings.[24] Additionally, the presence of giant cells is thought to be a specific indicator for true granulomatous diseases, and non-caseating granulomas are observed in both tuberculosis and sarcoidosis patients, meaning that sarcoidosis is often diagnosed by exclusion. Therefore, some studies have suggested that a combination of transbronchial lung biopsy (TBLB) and endobronchial biopsy (EBB) could improve the diagnostic yield of EBUS-TBNA for sarcoidosis.[35,36] Previous studies reported that the diagnostic sensitivity of EBUS-TBNA + EBB for sarcoidosis ranged from 81.8% to 93%,[37,38] the pooled diagnostic yield of EBUS-TBNA + TBLB + EBB was 90.9%,[36] and substantial room for improvement in the biopsy method remains.

Cryoprobe systems can assist bronchoscopists in obtaining larger tissue samples with minimal crush artifacts and have recently been widely employed in tissue sampling.[39–41] A previous randomized study initially demonstrated the potential advantage of EBUS-TBMC in diagnosing benign disorders of the mediastinal and/or hilar lymph nodes and rare tumors.[14] The sample size of patients with sarcoidosis was too small to prove sufficient evidence for the use of EBUS-TBMC in clinical practice, and the study population was not focused on patients with sarcoidosis. The present study included a sufficient number of participants and used a randomized design and revealed that the samples obtained with EBUS-TBMC had a well-preserved histological structure, and the granuloma detection rate of this method in diagnosing patients with sarcoidosis was superior to that of EBUS-TBNA.

Although both previous studies and the present study have reported that EBUS-TBMC has advantages in terms of tissue sampling and is well tolerated,[14,15,40] concerns about safety when puncturing the mediastinum remain.[42] To increase the safety of the EBUS-TBMC procedure, we designed and developed a novel method called EBUS-TBMC via a tunnel and utilized this method in the present trial.[11,12] No severe complications were observed in this study, suggesting that this novel method may improve the safety of EBUS-TBMC. More studies are needed to confirm the safety of EBUS-TBMC via a tunnel in a larger population.

This study has several limitations. First, the biopsy methods used in this crossover design may introduce considerable bias because punctures can distort the lymph nodes. To clarify whether the sequence of the biopsy method could bias diagnostic performance, we compared the diagnostic yield of the same biopsy method across different groups. The results suggested that this study design does not introduce bias in the diagnostic performance of each biopsy method. Second, in this study, the yield of EBUS-TBNA (84%) was lower than the 87% reported in the literature, and the significance of this difference is unclear; this may be because the fact that we only used a 22-G needle to perform EBUS-TBNA, which may have resulted in an underestimation of the actual diagnostic performance of EBUS-TBNA in patients with sarcoidosis. Although samples obtained by 22-G needles have superior histological quality, and these needles are recommended by guidelines, recent studies have suggested that 19-G needles may provide better diagnostic performance. Further studies are needed to determine the diagnostic performance of different sizes of aspiration needles when performing EBUS-TBNA. Finally, the combination of standard bronchoscopy techniques (e.g., EBB and TBLB) with EBUS-TBNA is considered a safe and feasible method that optimizes the diagnostic yield in patients with pulmonary sarcoidosis. However, in this study, we focused only on comparisons between EBUS-TBMC and EBUS-TBNA. Additional studies are needed to compare EBUS-TBMC and the combination of standard bronchoscopy techniques with EBUS-TBNA in patients with suspected stage I/II sarcoidosis. In addition, future studies are needed to determine whether the EBUS-TBMC will add to the diagnostic yield for sarcoidosis when the combination of standard bronchoscopy techniques is performed.

In conclusion, EBUS-TBMC had a superior granulomas detection rate and provided samples with better histological quality compared with EBUS-TBNA when diagnosing sarcoidosis, and the safety of this method was acceptable.

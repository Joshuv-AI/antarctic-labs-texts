Thermo-hydraulic performance analysis of a horizontal slinky-type geothermal heat exchanger under turbulent flow vonditions
Authors: Chou-Yi Hsu, Biju Theruvil Sayed, Paul Rodrigues, Hassan Mohammed, V. Pushparajesh, Alisha Vashisht, Byomakesh Dash, Sanjeev Kumar Shah, Talib Munshid Hanoon, Dalya S. Obaida
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Horizontal Slinky-type geothermal heat exchangers (GHEs) are widely used in ground-source heat pump systems due to their compact layout and low installation cost, yet their performance is often constrained by shallow burial depth and weak internal flow mixing. This study presents a three-dimensional CFD analysis of a horizontal Slinky GHE enhanced with Twisted-tape inserts. The effects of Twisted-tape pitch (100, 250, and 350 mm), burial depth (0.5–1.5 m), and water mass flow rate (0.30–1.0 kg/s) are investigated under turbulent flow conditions using ANSYS Fluent with the realizable k–ε model. The novelty of this work lies in the combined optimization of Twisted-tape geometry and burial depth, evaluated through both first-law (Nusselt number, pressure drop) and second-law (entropy generation, exergy destruction) performance metrics. Twisted-tape inserts significantly enhance convective heat transfer: compared with the plain tube, the average Nusselt number increases by up to 28–35% for the 100 mm pitch and 22–30% for the 250 mm pitch. Although the 100 mm pitch provides the highest thermal enhancement, it also results in a 40–52% pressure-drop increase. The 250 mm pitch yields the maximum thermo-hydraulic performance criterion (PEC), improving PEC by 12–18%, indicating the optimal balance between heat-transfer augmentation and hydraulic penalty. Increasing burial depth improves thermal stability and heat extraction, enhancing the heat-transfer coefficient by approximately 15–21%. The optimal configuration is identified as a 250 mm pitch at a burial depth of 1.0–1.5 m. These findings provide practical design guidance for improving the efficiency of horizontal Slinky geothermal heat exchangers. The findings offer practical design guidance for horizontal Slinky geothermal systems, indicating that careful optimization of coil pitch, burial depth, and operating mass flow rate can substantially improve heat extraction performance while keeping hydraulic losses within acceptable limits under realistic ground and ambient conditions.

Introduction

The main aim of this study is to perform a comprehensive numerical assessment of the thermo-hydraulic behavior of a horizontal Slinky-type geothermal heat exchanger incorporating Twisted-tape inserts, with particular attention to the combined influence of Twisted-tape pitch and burial depth on heat-transfer enhancement, pressure-drop characteristics, and the thermo-hydraulic performance criterion (PEC) under turbulent flow conditions. Renewable energy sources have become increasingly important owing to their abundance and sustainability, particularly in the face of rising global energy demand. In contrast, continued reliance on fossil fuels results in significant environmental challenges, including air pollution, regional acidification, and climate change. Recent technological advancements have facilitated the effective utilization of geothermal energy for both industrial applications and residential heating1. Geothermal energy represents a globally available and sustainable renewable resource. In geothermal systems, both open-loop and closed-loop heat exchangers are extensively employed to deliver low-grade thermal energy to heat pumps2. Among these configurations, horizontal geothermal heat exchangers are generally considered more cost-effective than vertical systems3. Horizontal geothermal heat exchangers incorporating a Slinky configuration offer notable economic advantages, making them suitable for both residential and non-residential applications4–6. Following preliminary site surveys and the drilling of exploratory or production wells, the feasibility of geothermal energy utilization is typically assessed. The temperature of the geothermal reservoir constitutes a critical parameter in determining the appropriate application of this energy resource. Geothermal resources are commonly classified into three categories according to their temperature levels: high-, medium-, and low-temperature systems. At present, geothermal energy is primarily utilized through direct thermal applications and indirect electricity generation7–10. The adoption of geothermal heat pump systems provides a significant advantage in mitigating environmental impacts, particularly when compared with fossil fuel–based heating technologies, such as natural gas systems, which are recognized as major contributors to air pollution11,12. Replacing conventional fossil fuel–based systems with alternative energy technologies can substantially reduce environmental pollution and contribute to the mitigation of greenhouse gas emissions13,14. Although the environmental and economic benefits of geothermal energy systems are well recognized, their overall performance is largely determined by the design and operating conditions of the ground heat exchanger. Among the available configurations, horizontal geothermal heat exchangers—particularly Slinky-type designs—have gained considerable attention owing to their compact geometry and lower installation costs relative to vertical systems. Nevertheless, their thermal effectiveness is frequently constrained by shallow installation depths and limited internal flow mixing. These challenges have prompted extensive research aimed at enhancing heat-transfer performance through geometric modifications, flow augmentation strategies, and optimization of installation parameters. As a result, an increasing number of experimental and numerical investigations have been devoted to elucidating the thermo-hydraulic characteristics of Slinky and spiral ground heat exchangers across a wide range of operating conditions. Kupiec and Gwadera15 emphasized the importance of evaluating subsurface temperature distributions in the analysis of ground heat exchangers, noting that such assessments require a thorough understanding of ground-temperature behavior under environmental influences and internal heat sources. They further proposed a novel model describing natural heat transfer processes within the ground. Additionally, Alizadeh et al.16 conducted a numerical investigation into the effects of various turbulator geometries on convective heat transfer within a square cross-section channel. The study demonstrated that heat-transfer enhancement can be achieved through turbulence intensification and the generation of secondary vortex structures, which promote fluid mixing and effectively increase thermal transport near the walls. Numerical simulations were performed using ANSYS Fluent on a structured computational grid. In related work, Ariwibowo et al.17 examined the thermal performance of a Slinky-coil ground heat exchanger incorporating various double-inclined rib (DDIR) configurations. Their numerical analysis comparing plain and DDIR coils indicated that the DDIR configuration provides a marginal improvement in thermal efficiency under laminar flow conditions. However, under turbulent flow, performance enhancement was observed only during the initial 149 min of operation, after which the advantage diminished. Bahram Jalili et al.18 investigated magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) viscous flow and heat transfer between permeable disks using the Adomian Decomposition Method (AGM) and the Finite Element Method (FEM). Their study considered a two-dimensional, steady, laminar, and incompressible viscous flow between two porous disks subjected to an external magnetic field. The analysis was structured into two interrelated components, each addressing distinct physical and mathematical aspects of the problem. Yang et al.19 conducted combined numerical and experimental investigations into the heat-transfer performance of a horizontal spiral geothermal heat exchanger. Their results indicated that thermal performance improves with increasing inlet temperature, decreasing spiral-coil pitch, and reduced surface wind velocity. Furthermore, Amin Samimi Behbahan et al.20 introduced an enhanced Adomian Decomposition Method (ADM) to analyze the thermal behavior of forced non-Newtonian magnetized Reiner–Rivlin viscoelastic flow. They also proposed a semi-analytical framework integrating ADM with the Finite Element Method (FEM) for the modeling of magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flow between two disks.

In a related review, Hu et al.21 analyzed the optimal thermodynamic performance of horizontal ground heat exchangers employed in ground-source heat pump systems. The reviewed studies were categorized into four principal groups—namely linear, spiral, serpentine, and other coil configurations—based on analytical, numerical, and experimental methodologies. Kuruneru et al.22 employed a coupled finite volume–discrete element method (FVM–DEM) to investigate particulate fouling transport in metal-foam heat exchangers. Their numerical framework was validated against both the original and modified Darcy–Forchheimer models using a modified porosity.

The numerical results showed good agreement with the analytical solutions. Additionally, Sarwo Edhy Sofyan et al.23 reviewed a new modeling strategy for horizontal geothermal heat exchangers that includes an internal source term. The proposed numerical framework was validated against both the original and modified Darcy–Forchheimer equations using a modified porosity formulation, thereby confirming its accuracy and reliability. In related work, Eiamsa-Ard et al.24 investigated the decay of swirling flow in circular tubes fitted with short-length twisted tapes, reporting detailed distributions of local heat-transfer coefficients and turbulent flow characteristics based on combined experimental and numerical analyses.

This section reviews prior research, highlighting the primary areas of investigation, methodological approaches, and the key parameters examined in earlier studies. Despite the growing body of literature on geothermal heat exchangers, numerical analyses focusing on the thermal performance of horizontal Slinky-type configurations—particularly those addressing the influence of geometric parameters such as spiral-strip or Twisted-tape modifications—remain relatively limited. Accordingly, the present study seeks to extend existing knowledge and provide a deeper understanding of the coupled thermo-hydraulic behavior of such systems. In this work, the pressure-drop and heat-transfer characteristics of a water-based horizontal Slinky geothermal heat exchanger are investigated under turbulent flow conditions.

The analysis was conducted in two sequential stages. First, the performance of three Twisted-tape pitch configurations was evaluated. Subsequently, a detailed assessment of the optimized 250 mm pitch configuration was performed, with particular emphasis on the influence of installation (burial) depth. The present study provides a novel investigation into the effects of Twisted-tape pitch on heat-transfer enhancement and pressure-drop characteristics in horizontal Slinky geothermal heat exchangers, while simultaneously examining the role of burial depth on overall thermo-hydraulic performance. The numerical results indicate that the 250 mm pitch configuration yields superior thermal performance compared with the other geometries examined.

The principal contribution of this study is the development of a design-oriented, multi-criteria optimization framework for horizontal Slinky geothermal heat exchangers incorporating Twisted-tape inserts. In contrast to prior investigations that address either turbulator geometry or burial depth in isolation, the present work simultaneously optimizes Twisted-tape pitch and installation depth to determine an optimal thermo-hydraulic configuration. Moreover, the performance evaluation combines first-law metrics—namely the heat-transfer coefficient and pressure drop—with second–law–based indicators, including entropy generation, exergy destruction, the Bejan number, and the thermo-hydraulic performance criterion (PEC). By integrating detailed analyses of flow structures and temperature fields with an assessment of thermodynamic irreversibility’s, this study provides new physical insight into the inherent trade-off between heat-transfer enhancement and hydraulic losses. The findings yield actionable design guidelines for developing high-efficiency horizontal geothermal heat exchangers. In contrast to earlier work, the present manuscript delivers a comprehensive, design-focused numerical analysis by concurrently optimizing the Twisted-tape pitch and the burial depth of a Slinky geothermal heat exchanger. Beyond determining an optimal configuration—corresponding to a pitch of 250 mm and a burial depth of 1–1.5 m—the study clarifies the governing thermo-hydraulic mechanisms through detailed examinations of velocity and temperature fields, with overall performance rigorously assessed using a PEC-based criterion. Previous investigations have substantially contributed to the understanding of horizontal geothermal heat exchangers by exploring the influence of exchanger geometry, flow regimes, and ground thermal properties on heat-transfer performance. Studies on Slinky and spiral configurations have shown that geometric factors, including coil pitch and the use of flow-enhancement devices, can improve thermal efficiency. However, most existing works address these parameters in isolation and often within a narrow range of operating conditions. In particular, research on Twisted-tape and rib-type turbulators has largely focused on internal heat-transfer augmentation and associated pressure losses, while overlooking the coupled effects of ground interaction and burial depth. Conversely, studies that emphasize burial depth and soil thermal stability generally disregard internal flow modification and second-law performance indicators. Consequently, the combined influence of internal flow enhancement and installation parameters on the overall thermo-hydraulic behavior of Slinky geothermal heat exchangers remains inadequately explored. In response to these gaps, the present study adopts an integrated, design-oriented framework that simultaneously investigates Twisted-tape pitch and burial depth under turbulent flow conditions, while assessing system performance using both first-law measures and second-law–based metrics to achieve a more comprehensive evaluation of exchanger efficiency. The Twisted-tape pitch was selected as a key investigation parameter because it directly controls the strength of swirl flow, secondary circulation, and fluid mixing within the heat exchanger, which are the primary mechanisms governing convective heat transfer in Slinky-type configurations. Reducing the pitch increases the frequency of flow rotation and intensifies centrifugal effects, thereby promoting stronger disruption of the thermal boundary layer and enhancing the heat-transfer coefficient. However, excessively small pitches also increase velocity gradients and wall shear stresses, leading to a pronounced pressure-drop penalty. In contrast, larger pitches produce weaker swirl intensity, limiting turbulence augmentation and diminishing thermal performance. Consequently, the Twisted-tape pitch constitutes a critical geometric parameter that governs the trade-off between heat-transfer enhancement and hydraulic losses. Through a systematic variation of this parameter, the present study seeks to determine an optimal configuration that maximizes overall thermo-hydraulic performance rather than heat-transfer enhancement alone.

In addition to advancing the fundamental understanding of the thermo-hydraulic behavior of Slinky-type geothermal heat exchangers, the present study delivers findings with direct relevance to practical system design and implementation. Through the simultaneous optimization of Twisted-tape pitch and burial depth, the results offer explicit guidance for selecting exchanger configurations that improve heat-transfer performance while limiting hydraulic penalties. The combined application of first-law and second-law performance indicators allows engineers to identify designs that are not only thermally effective but also energetically efficient from a system-level standpoint. These insights are especially pertinent to residential and small-scale commercial ground-source heat pump applications, where installation depth is restricted, pumping power is limited, and sustained operational efficiency is essential. As a result, the outcomes of this work can facilitate informed engineering decisions and support the development of cost-effective, high-efficiency horizontal geothermal heat exchanger systems operating under realistic conditions.To establish the reliability of the numerical predictions, the present model was validated through comparisons with available experimental measurements and previously published numerical results reported in the literature. Key thermal performance parameters, including the outlet fluid temperature and the average Nusselt number, were benchmarked against experimental data and well-established numerical studies for comparable Twisted-tape and geothermal heat exchanger configurations. The results exhibit good agreement across the investigated operating conditions, indicating that the adopted numerical framework effectively captures the dominant thermo-hydraulic processes governing heat transfer and pressure losses. Minor discrepancies between the present predictions and reference data may be attributed to variations in geometric features, boundary conditions, and soil thermal properties. Overall, this validation demonstrates the robustness of the numerical methodology and supports the applicability of the present findings to the design and optimization of horizontal geothermal heat exchanger systems. Despite the extensive body of literature on geothermal heat exchangers, the interactive influence of Twisted-tape pitch and burial depth in horizontal Slinky configurations has not been comprehensively investigated. Previous studies have generally focused on either internal heat-transfer enhancement or installation-related parameters independently, while many assessments have been limited to first-law performance criteria. Consequently, an integrated thermo-hydraulic analysis incorporating second-law considerations remains absent. The present study seeks to fill this gap by jointly examining the effects of Twisted-tape geometry and burial depth under turbulent flow conditions.

The numerical framework is formulated based on a three-dimensional, steady-state solution of the governing conservation equations for mass, momentum, and energy. Mass conservation is enforced through the continuity equation, while turbulent fluid flow within the Slinky geothermal heat exchanger is described using the incompressible Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes (RANS) equations. Energy transport in the fluid region is modeled by the convection–diffusion equation, whereas Fourier’s law governs heat conduction in the pipe wall and surrounding soil. Turbulence effects are accounted for using the realizable k–ε model, which is particularly suitable for internal flows characterized by curvature, swirl, and pronounced secondary motions induced by Twisted-tape inserts. Compared with the standard k–ε formulation, the realizable model offers improved predictions of turbulent viscosity and pressure losses in rotating and highly strained flows, rendering it well suited for the present configuration. Conjugate heat transfer between the fluid, pipe wall, and soil domains is fully resolved by enforcing continuity of temperature and heat flux across all solid–fluid interfaces. The working fluid (water) is treated as a Newtonian fluid with constant thermophysical properties, and radiative heat transfer is neglected. The flow is assumed to be fully turbulent over the investigated range of mass flow rates, and gravitational effects on the fluid motion are disregarded. All simulations are conducted under steady-state conditions, with negligible thermal contact resistance assumed between the pipe and the surrounding soil. These assumptions enable the numerical model to capture the dominant thermo-hydraulic mechanisms while achieving an appropriate balance between computational accuracy and efficiency.

Boundary conditions

The numerical boundary conditions were specified to realistically capture the coupled thermo–hydraulic behavior of the Slinky geothermal heat exchanger, the surrounding soil medium, and the ambient environment. At the top surface of the computational domain, a convective boundary condition was imposed to represent heat exchange with the ambient air. The ambient temperature was fixed at 256 K, while a uniform air velocity of 0.4 m/s was prescribed to emulate surface convection. Under these conditions, the convective heat-transfer coefficient was dynamically evaluated by the solver. The bottom boundary of the soil region was maintained at a constant temperature of 270 K, corresponding to the undisturbed deep-soil thermal state and providing a stable heat source for the exchanger. To minimize boundary-induced thermal interference, all lateral soil boundaries were treated as adiabatic, consistent with the assumption of negligible horizontal heat transfer within the sufficiently extended domain. Within the fluid domain, water was selected as the working medium and introduced at the pipe inlet with mass flow rates of 0.30, 0.70, 0.85, and 1.00 kg/s. A uniform inlet velocity profile and a constant inlet temperature were assumed. At the outlet, a pressure-based boundary condition with zero gauge pressure was applied, allowing the flow to exit the domain without imposing artificial constraints on the solution. All solid surfaces, including the pipe wall and Twisted-tape inserts, were subjected to a no-slip condition. Conjugate heat transfer was fully resolved by enforcing continuity of temperature and heat flux across all fluid–solid and solid–solid interfaces. To improve the transparency of CFD post-processing and facilitate the interpretation of numerical outcomes, a clearly defined region of interest (ROI) was introduced within the computational domain. The present analysis emphasizes the central portion of the Slinky geothermal heat exchanger, corresponding to the mid-length segment of the helical pipe that includes multiple successive Slinky turns and their associated Twisted-tape inserts. In this zone, both the hydrodynamic and thermal fields have reached a quasi–fully developed state and are minimally influenced by imposed inlet or outlet boundary conditions. This central region was selected to provide a representative characterization of the inherent thermo–hydraulic mechanisms governing the exchanger performance. By contrast, the upstream section is dominated by flow and thermal development effects, whereas the downstream section may be influenced by pressure recovery and potential numerical artifacts. Restricting detailed field analyses to the central ROI therefore, mitigates boundary-induced distortions and ensures that the reported distributions of velocity, temperature, turbulence quantities, and entropy generation accurately reflect the intrinsic behavior of the system. Accordingly, all local flow visualizations—including velocity vectors, temperature contours, vorticity fields, and entropy generation maps—were extracted exclusively from the defined ROI. In parallel, integral performance metrics such as the average Nusselt number, overall pressure drop, and the thermo–hydraulic performance criterion (PEC) were evaluated over the full length of the heat exchanger. This deliberate separation between global performance assessment and local ROI-based analysis enhances methodological clarity and reinforces confidence in the physical relevance of the CFD results.

In the present numerical investigation, suitable thermal and hydraulic boundary conditions were imposed to accurately replicate the operating environment of the horizontal Slinky geothermal heat exchanger. The upper boundary of the computational domain, representing the ground surface, was treated as a convective heat-transfer boundary to account for heat exchange with the ambient air. The ambient temperature was set to 256 K, and a wind speed of 0.4 m/s was prescribed. Under these conditions, the convective heat-transfer coefficient was not specified a priori; instead, it was implicitly determined by the solver through the coupled resolution of the near-surface thermal and flow fields, thereby enabling a more realistic prediction of surface heat losses. The inlet of the heat exchanger pipe was defined using a mass-flow inlet boundary condition, with water selected as the working fluid. Simulations were conducted for mass flow rates of 0.30, 0.70, 0.85, and 1.00 kg/s. A uniform velocity profile was imposed at the inlet, and the inlet temperature was maintained constant across all operating scenarios. Turbulent flow conditions were assumed, and the inlet turbulence parameters were specified in accordance with the chosen turbulence model. At the pipe outlet, a pressure-outlet boundary condition with zero gauge pressure was applied, allowing the flow to adjust naturally and ensuring accurate evaluation of the pressure drop along the heat exchanger.

Mesh independence and near-wall y⁺ analysis

A grid convergence analysis was performed by progressively refining the computational mesh and examining the sensitivity of representative solution variables, including the outlet fluid temperature and the overall heat-transfer rate. A comparison between meshes comprising approximately 2.0 million and 2.336 million control volumes revealed variations below 1%, confirming that the numerical solution is effectively independent of further mesh refinement. The adequacy of near-wall resolution was additionally evaluated through the dimensionless wall distance, y⁺. For the final mesh configuration, most wall-adjacent cells exhibited y⁺ values within the range of 20–35, which is appropriate for the realizable k–ε turbulence model coupled with enhanced wall treatment. The preservation of similar y⁺ distributions across successive mesh refinements indicates that the resolution of near-wall velocity and thermal gradients is robust and not sensitive to grid density. Taken together, the favorable mesh quality metrics, the demonstrated grid convergence, and the consistent near-wall y⁺ behavior indicate that the selected mesh achieves an optimal compromise between computational cost and numerical accuracy, thereby reinforcing the reliability of the CFD predictions.

Simulation setup

Numerical simulations were conducted in ANSYS Fluent utilizing the finite volume method. A pressure-based solver was applied under steady-state conditions, with pressure–velocity coupling handled through the SIMPLE algorithm. Second-order upwind discretization schemes were adopted for the momentum and energy equations to limit numerical diffusion. Solution convergence was verified by residual reduction criteria (below 10⁻⁶ for the energy equation and 10⁻⁴ for continuity and momentum) together with stabilization of key physical parameters, notably outlet temperature and pressure drop. Turbulence effects were represented using the realizable k–ε model with enhanced wall treatment.

Methodology

The thermo–hydraulic behavior of a horizontal Slinky geothermal heat exchanger (GHE) fitted with Twisted-tape inserts was examined through Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) analysis. Numerical simulations were conducted in ANSYS Fluent using the finite volume method within a fully three-dimensional, steady-state formulation. Conjugate heat transfer among the working fluid, pipe wall, Twisted-tape inserts, and the surrounding soil domain was directly modeled. Turbulent flow was represented using the realizable k–ε turbulence model, chosen for its proven capability in predicting flows characterized by curvature, swirl, and secondary motion generated by Twisted-tape inserts. Enhanced wall treatment was employed to ensure accurate resolution of near-wall velocity and temperature gradients.

The computational domain incorporated the Slinky-coil pipe, Twisted-tape inserts, and an enlarged soil region to reduce artificial boundary influences. Boundary conditions were specified to replicate realistic geothermal operating scenarios, including a prescribed mass flow rate and constant inlet temperature, pressure outlet at the exit, no-slip conditions at all solid interfaces, convective heat exchange at the ground surface, constant temperature at the bottom soil boundary, and adiabatic lateral walls. A three-dimensional unstructured tetrahedral mesh was generated, with targeted refinement near solid boundaries and inserts. Inflation layers were applied to properly capture boundary-layer effects. Mesh integrity was assessed using standard quality indicators, while grid independence was verified by monitoring variations in outlet temperature and heat transfer rate across successive mesh refinements. The selected mesh represented an optimal balance between numerical accuracy and computational efficiency. The governing equations were solved using a pressure-based solver with SIMPLE pressure–velocity coupling and second-order discretization schemes. Convergence was established through residual reduction and stabilization of critical thermo–hydraulic variables. Model validation was achieved by comparison with previously published experimental and numerical data, showing satisfactory agreement. System performance was quantified using thermal, hydraulic, and second-law metrics, including the Nusselt number, pressure drop, entropy generation, exergy destruction, and the performance evaluation criterion (PEC).

Results and discussion

The pressure-drop characteristics and heat-transfer performance of a horizontal Slinky geothermal heat exchanger employing water as the working fluid are investigated in this study. Both experimental measurements and numerical simulations were conducted under turbulent flow conditions over a mass flow rate range of 0.25–1 kg/s. The numerical analysis was performed using the finite volume method implemented in ANSYS Fluent. Burial depth is a key factor influencing the thermal performance of horizontal geothermal heat exchangers, as it directly determines the thermal conditions of the surrounding soil and the intensity of fluid–soil heat exchange. With increasing burial depth, the heat exchanger is embedded in soil layers characterized by reduced diurnal and seasonal temperature variations and enhanced thermal stability. This more stable thermal environment preserves a sustained temperature gradient between the working fluid and the soil, thereby improving the system’s heat-extraction capability. In addition, soil thermal properties—such as thermal conductivity, volumetric heat capacity, and effective thermal diffusivity—tend to be more uniform and less affected by atmospheric influences at greater depths, which facilitates more efficient conductive heat transfer from the undisturbed ground to the heat exchanger. Conversely, at shallow installation depths, soil temperature is strongly influenced by surface boundary conditions, including ambient air temperature, solar radiation, and moisture fluctuations, leading to reduced thermal stability and diminished heat-transfer performance. From a thermo-hydraulic standpoint, deeper burial depths are associated with higher outlet fluid temperatures, enhanced convective heat-transfer coefficients, and lower thermal irreversibility during heat exchange. Therefore, the selection of an appropriate burial depth is crucial for achieving an optimal compromise between thermal performance, overall system efficiency, and practical installation constraints.

Conclusion

Geothermal heat exchangers have assumed an increasingly prominent role in renewable energy technologies, prompting the development of various configurations aimed at improving their thermal efficiency. Recent research has largely concentrated on exchanger design optimization and the advancement of numerical modeling techniques. In the present study, the heat-transfer performance and pressure-drop behavior of a water-based horizontal Slinky geothermal heat exchanger are systematically examined under turbulent flow conditions across a mass flow rate range of 0.25–1 kg/s. A combined experimental and numerical approach is adopted, in which experimental measurements are complemented by finite-volume simulations conducted using ANSYS Fluent to achieve a comprehensive characterization of the system’s thermo-hydraulic response. Although the current investigation is restricted to a single working fluid and steady-state operating conditions, the results provide meaningful insight into the fundamental performance characteristics of Slinky-type geothermal heat exchangers. The findings establish a robust basis for future extensions involving alternative working fluids, modified geometrical designs, and transient operating regimes. Furthermore, the outcomes of this study offer direct applicability to the design and optimization of horizontal geothermal heat exchanger systems intended for residential and small-scale commercial heating applications.

This enhancement, however, was associated with a substantial friction penalty exceeding 100% compared with the smooth-tube case. In contrast, the Pitch = 250 mm configuration delivered the most favorable overall thermo–hydraulic performance, exhibiting the highest Performance Evaluation Criterion (PEC ≈ 1.25) while sustaining moderate entropy generation levels. These findings suggest that although smaller pitch values intensify heat-transfer augmentation, intermediate pitches provide a more advantageous global performance trade-off.

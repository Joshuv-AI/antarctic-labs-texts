Performance analysis of fuzzy control strategy for tractor semi-active seat suspension
Authors: Xiaoliang Chen, Zhelu Wang, Yiqing Qiu, Nannan Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study develops and evaluates a semi-active seat suspension system for tractors using magnetorheological damper (MRD) and fuzzy logic–based control strategies. A five-degree-of-freedom (5-DOF) half-car tractor model is constructed, integrating human-seat dynamics, cab vibration, chassis motion, tire flexibility, and MRD nonlinear hysteresis represented by the Bouc-Wen model. To address uncertainties caused by road roughness, vehicle speed, and human-seat mass variation, a Type-1 fuzzy logic controller (T1FLC) and an interval Type-2 fuzzy logic controller (IT2FLC) are designed. The stability of both controllers is analyzed through phase-plane trajectories. Simulations are performed under random C-F class road excitations, bump inputs at different speeds, and human-seat massranging from 50 to 150 kg. Results show that semi-active control substantially improves ride comfort and reduces suspension dynamic deflection compared with passive suspension. Under random road excitations, the IT2FLC reduces the root mean square (RMS) vertical acceleration by approximately 60% and dynamic deflection by over 50%, effectively mitigating suspension bottoming-out. Under bump excitations, improvements reach 61.27% and 55.94%, respectively. The IT2FLC consistently demonstrates stronger robustness than the T1FLC, especially under highly uncertain or severe road conditions. These findings provide theoretical and engineering support for intelligent vibration-control strategies in agricultural tractor seat suspension systems.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-42322-4.

Introduction

Tractors frequently operate on unstructured terrain, where continuous whole-body vibration and sudden impact loads pose significant challenges to driver comfort and operational safety1–3. Long-term exposure to vibration can increase driver fatigue and the risk of musculoskeletal disorders, making effective vibration mitigation essential for agricultural machinery4–6. The seat suspension system is the final vibration isolation interface between the tractor and the driver, and its performance critically influences comfort, operating stability, and long-term health outcomes7,8.

Traditional passive seat suspensions rely on fixed damping and stiffness parameters, and cannot adapt to rapidly varying excitation intensities such as changes in road roughness, human-seat mass, or pitch-bounce interactions9–11. Semi-active suspension systems using magnetorheological dampers (MRDs) offer a promising alternative due to their ability to adjust damping force in real time, fast response, and low power consumption. However, the nonlinear hysteretic nature of MRDs complicates controller design and may degrade performance if uncertainties are not properly handled12–14.

Fuzzy logic-based control methods exhibit strong robustness in dealing with nonlinearities and uncertainties in suspension systems15–17. Type-1 fuzzy logic controllers (T1FLCs) have been widely used due to their intuitive rule design and low computational burden18. Nevertheless, when linguistic uncertainty or measurement ambiguity becomes significant, the performance of T1FLC can deteriorate19. Interval Type-2 fuzzy logic controllers (IT2FLCs), by incorporating a footprint of uncertainty (FOU), offer enhanced capability to handle parameter variations and external disturbances20,21.

Although fuzzy-controlled suspensions have been studied extensively for road vehicles, research on tractor seat suspensions remains limited. Bai et al.22 employed a fuzzy control strategy to conduct simulation research on the active air suspension of a heavy vehicle, and the results demonstrated that this approach can significantly reduce body vibration and enhance ride comfort; however, the study focused on body-level vibration and did not consider the coupled dynamics of the seat-occupant system under severe off-road excitations. Li et al.23 developed a semi-active fuzzy cooperative control strategy for an MRD-based vehicle suspension system, showing improved vibration suppression performance, but the controller robustness under strong parameter uncertainty was not systematically investigated. Jiang and Chen24 proposed a semi-active seat suspension strategy that integrates fuzzy control with a modified fireworks algorithm, and both simulation and HIL experiments demonstrated that this approach markedly improves ride comfort; nevertheless, the optimization-based controller increases computational complexity and is sensitive to modeling accuracy. Li et al.25 implemented fuzzy control for rollover-threshold prediction in a tractor-semitrailer equipped with a hydraulically interconnected suspension, and comparison with PID control showed that the fuzzy approach provides faster response and reduced overshoot, highlighting its effectiveness in enhancing vehicle stability, while ride comfort at the seat level was not explicitly addressed. Tractors often encounter harsher excitations, including random roads, high-amplitude impacts, and pronounced pitch-bounce coupling26–28. These characteristics demand a vibration-control strategy with strong adaptability and uncertainty tolerance.

To address these challenges, this research develops a tractor half-car model integrated with an MRD-based semi-active seat suspension and designs both T1FLC and IT2FLC control strategies. Simulation studies examine their vibration-control performance under uncertain road profiles, speed variations, and human-seat mass differences. Comparisons with passive systems highlight the benefits and limitations of each fuzzy approach.

The main contributions of this work can be summarized as follows: (1) Development of a nonlinear tractor half-car model integrating MRD dynamics, cab motion, chassis pitching, and human–seat vibration. (2) Design of T1FLC and IT2FLC controllers capable of handling nonlinear hysteresis and external uncertainties. (3) Systematic analysis of the influence of road roughness, vehicle speeds, and human-seat mass variations on suspension performance. (4) Demonstration of the superior robustness of IT2FLC over T1FLC in mitigating vibration and reducing dynamic deflection under varying uncertainty conditions.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section “System Modeling and problem description” introduces the tractor half-car model integrated with a semi-active seat suspension system, including the MRD dynamics and road excitation modeling. Section “Fuzzy control strategy design” details the design of the T1FLC and IT2FLC. Section “Simulation results and analysis” presents the simulation results and evaluates the vibration-control performance under various uncertainty conditions. Section “Conclusions” summarizes the main conclusions of this study.

System modeling and problem description

Half-car model with integrated semi-active seat suspension

To accurately characterize the vibration transmission path from ground excitation to the tractor driver, a five-degree-of-freedom (5-DOF) half-car model integrated with a semi-active seat suspension system is established, as illustrated in Fig. 1. The model captures the vertical dynamics of the human-seat subsystem, the cab, and the chassis, as well as the pitch motions of both the cab and chassis. Compared with a quarter-car model, the proposed 5-DOF half-car model can explicitly represent pitch-bounce coupling and front-rear axle interactions, which are critical for accurately evaluating controller performance under bump and random road excitations. The front and rear tires are modeled as linear spring-damper units, and the cab and chassis are connected through rubber bushings. The seat suspension consists of a linear spring in parallel with MRD.Fig. 1Half-car model of the tractor with an integrated semi-active seat suspension system.

Half-car model of the tractor with an integrated semi-active seat suspension system.

The following assumptions are made to simplify the dynamic formulation: (1) The tractor is symmetric with respect to its longitudinal center plane, and the left–right road excitation is identical. (2) Only vertical vibration and pitch motions are considered. (3) The human-seat subsystem behaves as a lumped mass supported by the seat suspension. (4) The MRD force acts directly along the seat vertical direction.

Under these assumptions, the system has five independent coordinates: the vertical displacement of the human-seat mass, the vertical displacement and pitch angle of the cab, and the vertical displacement and pitch angle of the chassis. Ride comfort. Fuzzy control strategy design

Type-1 fuzzy logic controller

Type-1 fuzzy logic control (T1FLC), derived from Zadeh’s fuzzy set theory (1965) and applied to control by Mamdani in 1974, is a widely used intelligent control method35. As shown in Fig. 3, a T1FLC system includes fuzzification, a knowledge base, an inference engine, and defuzzification. Fuzzification converts crisp inputs into linguistic variables, while the knowledge base stores membership functions, scaling factors, and control rules. The inference engine executes reasoning based on fuzzy implications and rule evaluation, forming the core of the controller. Defuzzification transforms the resulting fuzzy outputs into precise control signals.Fig. 3Structural block diagram of the T1FLC system.

Application of fuzzy controllers

The control principle of the tractor half-car model integrated with an MRD seat suspension is illustrated in Fig. 9. The semi-active suspension system consists of a non-linear subsystem and an MRD subsystem. The system controller computes the required damping force Fc based on the acceleration response of the half-car suspension. The MRD inverse model then generates the control voltage φ to ensure that the MRD tracks the desired damping force Fc. Finally, the MRD dynamic model calculates the actual damping force Fd using the input voltage and piston velocity, which is applied to achieve vibration attenuation in the tractor half-car suspension.Fig. 9Control block diagram of the semi-active suspension system.

Stability analysis

The large tractor half-car model with an integrated semi-active seat suspension is a second-order nonlinear system. The phase plane method is used to evaluate the stability of the fuzzy control system by directly analyzing the trajectories, avoiding the need to construct a Lyapunov function to assess the equilibrium point stability. Figure 10 shows the phase trajectories of the human-seat mass under T1FLC, IT2FLC, and passive suspension systems. The half-car model is subjected to a unit step excitation, and the displacement and velocity of the human-seat mass are used as response variables in the phase plane. It can be observed that, after the step response, the phase trajectories of both the fuzzy-controlled and passive suspensions oscillate and converge to a stable focus at approximately (0.1, 0). This indicates that the integrated semi-active seat suspension half-car model is asymptotically stable. Furthermore, the root trajectories of the human-seat mass reveal that the MRD semi-active seat suspension system with fuzzy control dissipates energy more effectively than the passive suspension system equipped with conventional dampers.Fig. 10Phase trajectory of human-seat mass.

Simulation results and analysis

The evaluations considered the effects of road roughness, vehicle speed, and variations in human-seat mass, which represent the major uncertainty sources in typical agricultural operations. All simulations were performed using the established 5DOF half-car model. The weighted acceleration and dynamic deflection RMS values were used as the primary performance indicators, as they directly reflect ride comfort and the risk of suspension bottoming-out.

For C-class road excitations, the results in Figs. 11 and 15 and Table 4 indicate that fuzzy control substantially enhances seat–occupant vibration performance. The two controllers exhibit nearly identical performance, with IT2FLC providing only a marginal 0.72% improvement in acceleration over T1FLC, showing no pronounced advantage under this road condition.

For D-class road excitations, the results in Figs. 12 and 15 and Table 4 show that fuzzy control significantly enhances ride performance. IT2FLC and T1FLC reduce the vertical acceleration of the human–seat mass by 65.65% and 65.14%, respectively, and markedly decrease the dynamic suspension travel, improving operational safety by 51.85% and 48.89% compared with the passive system while reducing the likelihood of suspension bottoming-out. Unlike the C-class condition, the performance gap between the two controllers becomes more pronounced on D-class roads, with IT2FLC demonstrating clear superiority by providing an additional 1.45% reduction in vertical acceleration and a 5.8% reduction in dynamic travel relative to T1FLC.

For E-class road excitations, Figs. 13 and 15 and Table 4 show that fuzzy control continues to deliver substantial performance gains. IT2FLC and T1FLC reduce the vertical acceleration of the human-seat mass by 62.10% and 60.84%, respectively, and decrease the dynamic suspension travel by 46.49% and 41.70% relative to the passive system, thereby enhancing operational safety and minimizing suspension bottoming-out. Compared with the C- and D-class conditions, both controllers achieve greater levels of vibration suppression on E-class roads, with IT2FLC exhibiting pronounced superiority by providing an additional 3.21% reduction in vertical acceleration and an 8.23% reduction in dynamic travel over T1FLC.

For F-class road excitations, Figs. 14 and 15 and Table 4 indicate that fuzzy control still yields notable improvements, with IT2FLC and T1FLC reducing the vertical acceleration of the human-seat mass by 47.19% and 47.25% and decreasing dynamic suspension travel by 48.73% and 46.18%, respectively, compared with the passive system. However, the performance gap between the two controllers diminishes markedly relative to the C-E classes. Analysis of vehicle speed uncertainty

When the tractor traverses a 0.12 m high triangular obstacle at low speed, the front and rear wheels pass the obstacle with a certain time interval. The rear-wheel crossing induces coupled vibrations in the semi-active seat suspension system, amplifying the overall system response and consequently degrading ride comfort and operational stability. Unlike passenger vehicles, tractors operate at relatively low speeds, and the vibration response to the 0.12 m obstacle varies with speed, resulting in different control effectiveness. Conclusions

This study developed a 5DOF tractor half-car model integrated with a magnetorheological semi-active seat suspension and investigated two fuzzy control strategies, namely the type-1 fuzzy logic controller (T1FLC) and the interval type-2 fuzzy logic controller (IT2FLC), for real-time regulation of MRD force. The proposed modeling and control framework effectively addresses the nonlinear dynamics and strong excitation characteristics of tractor seat vibration under agricultural operating conditions.

Simulation results under random C- class road excitations and bump disturbances demonstrate that MRD-based semi-active control significantly improves ride comfort compared with a passive suspension. The T1FLC and IT2FLC reduced the weighted seat acceleration RMS by approximately 58.5% on C- class roads and by about 47% under F-class excitation, while the dynamic deflection RMS decreased by 40–60% across different road classes. Under low-speed bump excitation, the IT2FLC achieved the best vibration attenuation performance, with reductions of 61.27% in acceleration RMS and 55.94% in dynamic deflection RMS. In addition, parametric analyses considering human-seat mass variations from 50 to 150 kg indicate that both controllers maintain stable performance, whereas the IT2FLC exhibits superior robustness against parameter uncertainty and external disturbances.

Future research will focus on hardware-in-the-loop and full-scale vehicle experiments to validate the performance of the proposed IT2FLC and T1FLC. In addition, comparative studies with widely adopted control strategies, such as PID, Skyhook, and linear quadratic control (LQC), will be conducted to further assess their relative effectiveness and practical applicability.

Solid-basaloid variant of adenoid cystic carcinoma of the breast unresponsive to neoadjuvant chemotherapy: Case report☆
Authors: Amira Hmidi, Yoldez Houcine, Lamia Naija, Hager Ben Mansour, Maha Driss
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Introduction and importanceAdenoid cystic carcinoma (ACC) is a rare triple-negative breast cancer, accounting for only 0.1 % of all primary breast carcinomas. At variance with the classic variant, the solid-basaloid variant of ACC (SB-ACC) is clinically more aggressive and has different molecular features. There is, currently, no consensus regarding the treatment of SB-ACC of the breast, especially the use of neoadjuvant chemotherapy.Case presentationHere, we present a rare case of SB-ACC in an elderly female patient, with no history of breast carcinoma, who presented with a 4.5 cm central round mass invading the nipple. Given the locally advanced triple negative breast cancer and the invasion of the nipple-areolar complex, the patient has received neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by surgical treatment. On histopathological examination, the diagnosis of SB-ACC, non-responsive to neoadjuvant chemotherapy, with absence of rearrangement of the MYB gene was retained. The patient received adjuvant radiation therapy and was ambulatory followed without recurrence at the 12-month follow-up.Discussion/conclusionThis case provided direct evidence that SB-ACC of the breast wasn't responsive to neoadjuvant chemotherapy but cannot allow for definitive conclusions on chemotherapy recommendations. For this reason, more data must be published to investigate the real value of neoadjuvant chemotherapy in SB-ACC.

Introduction

Breast cancer is a heterogeneous disease in terms of clinical features, histologic subtypes, molecular profiles, and responses to treatment. According to the 2019 World Health Organization classification, invasive breast carcinomas represent at least 16 different special and rare subtypes, including adenoid cystic carcinoma (ACC) [1]. This latter is a rare salivary gland-type tumor, with a triple-negative phenotype, accounting for only 0.1 % of all primary carcinomas of the breast [2]. Histologically, ACC is composed of epithelial and myoepithelial cells with a variety of microscopic growth patterns, which have been used to subclassify these tumors in the breast. Thereby, three subtypes have been defined: classic ACC, solid-basaloid ACC (SB-ACC), and ACC with high grade transformation [1]. Solid-basaloid variant is an extremely rare histologic subgroup of ACC, with <100 published cases [[3], [4], [5], [6], [7]]. In contrast to the classic variant, which shows favorable behavior, SB-ACC follows a more aggressive course and shows different molecular features [8].

Due to the small number of reported series and the paucity of data on the post-treatment outcome of patients, there is currently no consensus regarding the treatment of SB-ACC of the breast. Most authors suggest surgical excision and postoperative radiation [8,9]. However, the value of neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NC)is still controversial and there is no evidence data on its benefit or its response. Here, we present the case of an 84-year-old female patient with SB-ACC of the breast who had received NC with no pathologic response.

Case presentation

We report a case of an 84-year-old woman, with no past medical history, in particular with no personal or family history of breast carcinoma. The patient referred to our institution complaining of a 12 months history of a palpable, mildly painful, right breast lump. She was not taking any medication. She has P3L3. Her first childbirth was at the age of 24 years. She has her menarche at 12 years and her menopause at the age of 49 years. Physical examination revealed a 4.5 cm central round mass of the right breast with no obvious palpable axilla lymph node. An exophytic, polypoid nodule, measuring 3.5 cm was present on the right nipple. The skin covering was normal. The remainder physical was unremarkable.

Mammography revealed an irregular high-density mass at the right. On ultrasonography, this irregular mass was measured 4.19 cm in diameter and was classified as Breast Imaging-Reporting and Data System category 4. Otherwise, ultrasonography has not revealed any suspicious enlarged lymph node at the right axilla. A microbiopsy of both the mass and the nipple protrusion was performed. Histopathological examination of core needle biopsy showed that the two tumors share histological features of invasive carcinoma with solid nests, moderate nuclear atypia, and conspicuous mitoses. The tumor lacked estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR) and human epidermal growth factor receptor-2 (HER2) expression. The Ki67 proliferation index was approximately 30 %. The presentation of this tumor was unusual and the interpretation was difficult. The final diagnosis on microbiopsy was invasive carcinoma of the breast, whose final histological typing will be established on the surgical resection specimens. Considering the triple negative phenotype and the invasion of the nipple-areolar complex, NC was recommended. The patient was given pre-operative dose TAC (docetaxel, doxorubicin, cyclophosphamide) for four cycles with 50 mg/m2 of doxorubicin given as an intravenous bolus followed 1 h later with 75 mg/m2 of docetaxel given as a 1-h intravenous infusion and 500 mg/m2 for cyclophosphamide.

The resection specimen obtained after chemotherapy treatment showed a 42 × 35 mm mass which appeared to invade the nipple-areolar complex (protrusion of 35 mm on the nipple). Microscopic examination showed breast parenchyma occupied by a carcinomatous proliferation of solid nests, surrounded by significant fibrosis, and forming gland-like spaces containing eosinophilic material (Fig. 1A, B). This tumor invades the nipple-areolar complex (Fig. 1C). Based on morphological and phenotypic features, the diagnosis of ACC solid/basaloid variant, with triple-negative phenotype, and a grade II Elston and Ellis were retained. The absence of rearrangement of the MYB gene is classic in this variant. The surgical margins were negative. The patient received adjuvant radiation therapy and was ambulatory followed without recurrence at 12-month follow-up.

Discussion

SB-ACC is an uncommon subtype of breast tumor, which was first described in 1987 by Ro et al [9] who divided ACC into three groups according to the proportion of solid growth (I: no solid elements; II: <30 % solid; III: >30 % solid). They found that the solid variant (grade III) was associated with higher rates of metastases and tumor recurrence. Subsequent studies have confirmed these observations; In 2002 Shin and Rosen [3] reported nine cases of SB-ACC collected over 11 years, in which axillary lymph node metastases were found in two of six axillary dissections. Masse et al [7] found that SB-ACCs have more local recurrence and distant metastases than the classic variant. A series of SB-ACCs cases, reported by Schwartz et al [10], have shown that this variant has higher histologic grade and more frequent nodal and distant metastases. All these studies support that SB-ACCs of the breast is generally high-grade tumors with an aggressive clinical course.

While 15 % of SB-ACCs displayed lymph node metastases at the time of surgery [10], in our case, axillary lymph nodes were all negative. Despite our findings, axillary staging should be routinely considered in SB-ACCs.

ACCs of the breast also display a triple-negative phenotype. They are driven by activation of the MYB pathway, most commonly due to MYB-NFIB fusion gene, followed by fusion genes involving MYBL1 or MYB gene amplification. Conversely, only a minority of SB-ACC were found to harbor MYB rearrangements [10]. In the present case, Interphase Fluorescence in situ hybridization had not detected any rearrangement of MYB gene.

Due to the small number of cases of SB-ACC, recommendations guiding treatment decisions are limited. The standard treatment for high grade ACC is surgical resection: total mastectomy with or without axillary node dissection [11,12]. Adjuvant radiation therapy is being used by some practitioners, but without any formalized protocol or evidence-based indication. A study based on a large population database showed that adjuvant radiation therapy can provide a significant overall and cause-specific survival benefit in patients with ACC (hazard ratios of 0.44) [13]. Two other retrospective cohort studies also proved the benefit of adjuvant radiation therapy [14,15].

The question of chemotherapy for SB-ACCs has not been well reviewed previously, and there is no formal consensus on the use of chemotherapy for patients with ACC of the breast. In previous studies, 3 % to 66 % of the breast ACCs patients received adjuvant chemotherapy [14,16]. This disparity of percentage reflects the disagreement and the lack of treatment guidelines regarding the indication of chemotherapy in this rare tumor.

Few databases are available to answer questions regarding the efficiency of NC. Grabenstetter et al [17] reported an excellent response to NC bases on doxorubicin plus cyclophosphamide each two weeks with G-CSF support for four cycles followed by carboplatin each 3 weeks for four cycles plus twelve cycles of weekly paclitaxel. This case can hypothesize the beneficial role of such therapy in providing a desirable outcome. However, other studies [16] supported that ACCs are not responsive to chemotherapy (8 cycles of epirubicin based regimen followed by a taxane based regimen), and that the pathological responses of ACCs aren't related directly to long-term survival. The results of these limited reports continue to guide treatment recommendations and the question of whether or not chemotherapy should be used in patients with SB-ACC is still controversial. Our team recommend, for the treatment of SB-ACC, surgical resection associated to NC and adjuvant radiotherapy.

In our case, SB-ACC was unresponsive to NC. This single case cannot allow for definitive conclusions on chemotherapy recommendations. For this reason, more cases must be published to investigate the real value of NC in SB-ACCs.

Conclusion

SB-ACC is a rare triple-negative neoplasm of the breast, with a less good prognosis. Due to its rarity, there is no formal consensus regarding its treatment, especially neoadjuvant chemotherapy.

Our case provided direct evidence that SB-ACC of the breast wasn't responsive to neoadjuvant chemotherapy, but this must be approved in a large cohort.

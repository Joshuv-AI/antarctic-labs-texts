Polymerase epsilon mutations in endometrial cancer: addressing heterogeneity, clinical applicability, and methodological refinements
Authors: Siyuan Zhu
Published: 2026

We read with great interest the systematic review and meta-analysis by Adella et al. [1], which investigated the prognostic value and immunotherapeutic potential of polymerase epsilon (POLE) mutations in endometrial cancer. This topic has great clinical relevance currently, especially as molecular classification increasingly guides risk stratification and treatment de-escalation in endometrial cancer. We appreciate the comprehensive synthesis of evidence from 25 studies conducted across diverse geographical regions.

We offer several constructive methodological perspectives to further strengthen the interpretation and clinical applicability of their valuable findings.

1. Heterogeneity in pooled prevalence: an opportunity for stratified analysis

The pooled prevalence of POLE mutations was 9% (95% confidence intervals [CI], 7–11%) and was accompanied by extreme between-study heterogeneity (I2=90.6%). This reflects, at least in part, the inclusion of studies employing sequencing platforms with markedly different analytical sensitivities, ranging from Sanger sequencing to targeted next-generation sequencing (NGS) and allele-specific polymerase chain reaction for detecting pathogenic exonuclease domain mutations [2]. Furthermore, not all studies have restricted their analyses to mutations in exons 9–14, the canonical hotspot region that defines the clinically relevant ultramutated subtype [3]. Subgroup analyses stratified by sequencing methodology and restricted to pathogenic hotspot regions would help clarify whether the reported prevalence reflects true biological variation or is partly attributable to methodological heterogeneity. Such analyses would be particularly informative for Asian populations where the mutational spectrum may differ from that of a predominantly Western populace.

2. Disease-specific survival (DSS): acknowledging uncertainty in the context of clinical decision-making

The pooled DSS hazard ratio of 0.77 (95% CI, 0.20–2.97; I2=62%) is based on only four studies, and its extremely wide CI is compatible with both clinically meaningful benefit and potential harm. Given that DSS, rather than overall survival (OS), most directly reflects cancer-specific mortality, we suggest that the current evidence for a clinically meaningful benefit of DSS remains inconclusive and should be interpreted with caution when guiding treatment de-escalation discussions.

3. Sensitivity analysis for risk of bias: strengthening confidence in survival estimates

The quality in prognosis studies tool assessment identified eight studies (32%) with a moderate risk of bias, primarily in the domains of confounding and statistical reporting [1]. As these studies contributed a substantial proportion of the survival outcome data, a sensitivity analysis excluding moderate- risk studies or, alternatively, a subgroup analysis stratified by risk-of-bias category would enhance confidence in the robustness of the pooled OS and progression-free survival estimates. This approach aligns with best practices for prognostic meta-analysis [4].

4. Toward prospective validation: a proposed framework

We fully support the authors’ conclusion that POLE mutation testing merits integration into clinical practice. However, given that all the synthesized evidence was derived from retrospective cohort studies with non-uniform sequencing protocols, we propose that future research should prioritize a multicenter prospective cohort study with centralized POLE testing using a harmonized NGS panel restricted to pathogenic exonuclease-domain mutations. Such a study design would further enable prespecified subgroup analyses for Asian populations, addressing the current evidence gap in the molecular epidemiology of endometrial cancer.

In summary, Adella et al. [1] have provided an important synthesis that advances our understanding of POLE-mutated endometrial cancers. The modest refinements proposed above are intended to further enhance the methodological rigor and clinical utility of their meaningful work.

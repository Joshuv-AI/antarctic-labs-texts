Treatment Patterns in Hospitalized Hip Fracture Patients: An Interrupted Time-Series Analysis Around the New Fee for Secondary Fracture Prevention in Japan
Authors: Saeko Fujiwara, Hiroshi Hagino, Satoshi Soen, Noriaki Yamamoto, Takeshi Sawaguchi, Takashi Matsushita, Akiho Terasawa, Izumi Mishiro, Jen Timoshanko, Ryuta Saito
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionDespite high risk of secondary fracture, osteoporosis treatment rates following hip fracture remain low globally. In April 2022, Japan implemented a reimbursement policy for secondary fracture prevention in patients with hip fracture. We evaluated the impact of this policy on treatment patterns during acute hospitalization.MethodsWe conducted an interrupted time-series analysis using the Medical Data Vision database for patients aged ≥ 50 years hospitalized with hip fractures from April 2020 to March 2024. Primary outcome was osteoporosis medication treatment rate during hospitalization. Secondary outcomes included drug class distribution and use of medications categorized as recommended or proposed in preventing fractures according to Japanese guidelines. Segmented regression models were used to evaluate level and trend changes associated with policy implementation.ResultsAmong 71,632 eligible patients with hip fracture (mean age 84, 76% female), the interrupted time-series analysis revealed a significant immediate increase in treatment rates (level change [β2] = 16%, 95% CI 13–19%, p < 0.001) following policy implementation, with continued monthly growth thereafter (sustained positive slope change [β3] = 1% per month, p < 0.001). Use of medications both proposed and recommended in the guideline increased significantly for both (level change = 13%, p < 0.001 and level change = 8%, p < 0.001, respectively). Between 2020/21 and 2023/24, the use of bisphosphonates and active vitamin D3 as monotherapy both increased, from 12% to 29% for each. Nevertheless, overall use of osteoporosis medications, especially non-bisphosphonate drugs, with proven fracture-prevention efficacy and those recommended by clinical guidelines remained low.ConclusionJapan’s secondary fracture prevention reimbursement policy successfully improved in-hospital initiation of osteoporosis treatment among patients with hip fracture. Nevertheless, actual usage of osteoporosis medications with robust evidence for fracture prevention remains suboptimal. Effective prevention of secondary fractures and improved patient outcomes require appropriate medication selection, potentially facilitated by supportive policy measures.

Introduction

Hip fractures in older adults represent a major public health challenge worldwide, and affected patients face a substantially elevated risk of subsequent fractures, functional decline, and mortality [1–3]. In Japan, where the proportion of the population aged 65 and over is projected to exceed 35% by the 2040s, the burden of hip fractures on the healthcare system and society continues to escalate, with hip fractures alone expected to reach 320,000 cases annually by 2040 and result in economic costs of approximately 480 billion yen [4, 5]. Despite strong evidence that rapid initiation of osteoporosis medication following hip fractures can significantly reduce the risk of secondary fractures, the treatment rates remain markedly low in Japan [6–11].

Previous studies in Japan have reported that only around one-quarter of patients with hip fracture receive osteoporosis medication during hospitalization. However, given a risk of subsequent fracture in patients with a hip fracture history of 10.8% at 1 year and 18.6% at 2 years [10, 12, 13], this finding indicates a substantial treatment gap. Efforts to address this gap include the Fracture Liaison Service (FLS) model, first established in Glasgow and subsequently adopted internationally. The effectiveness of FLS in systematically identifying, treating, and monitoring patients with hip fractures to prevent secondary events has been confirmed [8, 14].

The Japanese government implemented a significant policy change in April 2022, which introduced a new reimbursement fee structure with the specific goal of promoting secondary fracture prevention for patients with hip fractures [15]. This policy established three management fees: (1) Management Fee 1, recorded once during hospitalization for patients with hip fracture undergoing surgery in acute care hospitals; (2) Management Fee 2, for rehabilitation hospitals; and (3) Management Fee 3, for outpatient follow-up care [16]. While this policy intervention was expected to improve osteoporosis treatment rates during acute hospitalization, a critical period for initiating fracture prevention, several factors may influence its effectiveness, including the constraints of Japan’s Diagnosis Procedure Combination/Per-Diem Payment System (DPC/PDPS), which might limit the use of more expensive but potentially more effective medications during hospitalization [17, 18]. A study of the policy’s outcome to date reported a large and sustained post-implementation increase in anti-osteoporosis medication prescription at discharge after hip fracture, predominantly for oral agents, and with a concomitant decline in injectables [19]. However, the extent to which this policy change has successfully improved both the quantity and quality of osteoporosis treatment initiation during hospitalization for hip fractures remains unclear.

In the present study, to evaluate the impact of Japan’s 2022 secondary fracture prevention reimbursement policy, we conducted an interrupted time-series (ITS) analysis of osteoporosis medication treatment patterns among hospitalized patients with hip fractures. Using a comprehensive national hospital database, we examined treatment rates before and after policy implementation and assessed not only overall treatment rates but also the use of medications with established efficacy for fracture prevention.

Methods

Study Design and Data Source

We conducted a retrospective observational study using an ITS design to evaluate the impact of Japan’s April 2022 secondary fracture prevention reimbursement policy on osteoporosis treatment patterns among patients with hip fracture. Data were obtained under contract and with permission from the Medical Data Vision (MDV) database (Medical Data Vision Co., Ltd), which collects claims data from over 400 hospitals, covering approximately 23% of acute care hospitals in Japan and including over 38 million patients [18, 20]. The MDV database contains comprehensive information including Diagnosis Procedure Combination (DPC) submissions, patient demographics, ICD-10 diagnosis codes, procedures, laboratory tests, and prescribed medications from all insurance types [21]. The study period extended from October 2019 to June 2024, with patient selection covering April 2020 to March 2024, divided into pre-policy (April 2020 to March 2022) and post-policy (April 2022 to March 2024) periods.

Study Population

We included patients aged 50 years or older at the time of hospitalization with a confirmed diagnosis of hip fracture based on the ICD-10 codes S7200, S7210, and S7220. The index date was defined as the admission date for the first hip fracture diagnosis during the selection period. Patients were required to have at least 180 days of database records prior to the index date to establish baseline characteristics and treatment history. We excluded patients with diagnoses of conditions that could affect bone metabolism independent of osteoporosis, including Paget’s disease (ICD-10 M88), osteomalacia (M83), primary hyperparathyroidism (E21.0), osteogenesis imperfecta (Q78.0), ankylosing spondylitis (M45), Cushing’s syndrome (E24), hyperprolactinemia (E22.1), osteitis deformans (M88.9), or malignant neoplasms except non-melanoma skin cancer (C00-C97, except C44) during the 180-day baseline period. Patients with multiple simultaneous fractures at the index date were also excluded.

For analyses stratified by treatment history, we classified patients as “incident users” if they had no prescriptions for osteoporosis medications during the 180-day baseline period, and as “prevalent users” if they had received any osteoporosis medication during this period. The primary analysis focused on the overall population, while secondary analyses were conducted separately for incident users to assess treatment initiation patterns in previously untreated patients.

Outcome Measures

The primary outcome was the treatment rate of osteoporosis medications during hospitalization, defined as the proportion of patients prescribed any osteoporosis medication between the index date and the earliest of death, hospital discharge, or 90 days from the index date. We classified osteoporosis medications into nine categories using the respective Japanese receipt codes for the Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical (ATC) classification codes defined by the European Pharmaceutical Market Research Association: (1) bisphosphonates (ATC M05B3), (2) active vitamin D3 (A11C2), (3) selective estrogen receptor modulators (G03J0), (4) parathyroid hormone receptor agonists (H04E0), (5) calcitonin/elcatonin (H04A0), (6) anti-RANKL antibody (M05B9), (7) anti-sclerostin antibody (M05B9), (8) calcium (A12A0), and (9) vitamin K2 (M05B9 with specific receipt codes). Additionally, we categorized medications by evidence of their ability to prevent fractures as defined by the Guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of Osteoporosis edited by the Japan Osteoporosis Society (JOS), the Japanese Society for Bone and Mineral Research, and the Osteoporosis Foundation, and classified them as either proposed or recommended medications [22]. The guideline-recommended medications comprised the following five agents: alendronate, risedronate, zoledronate, denosumab, and romosozumab. The guideline-proposed medications comprised the following seven agents: minodronate, ibandronate, raloxifene, bazedoxifene, teriparatide, abaloparatide, and eldecalcitol [22]. Secondary outcomes included the distribution of specific drug classes prescribed during hospitalization. The recording of Management Fee 1 for secondary fracture prevention (receipt code 113036510) and additional fees for early surgery (receipt code 150425670 or 150435770) were considered process indicators of policy implementation.

Statistical Analysis

We employed an ITS analysis using segmented regression to evaluate changes in treatment rates associated with the April 2022 policy implementation [23, 24]. Monthly subcohorts were constructed by aggregating patients with index dates within each calendar month, and treatment rates were calculated for each monthly cohort. Ethical Considerations

This study utilized fully anonymized, routinely collected administrative data. According to the Japanese “Ethical Guidelines for Medical and Health Research Involving Human Subjects”, studies using only de-identified secondary data without direct patient involvement are exempt from institutional review board approval [25]. This study is also compliant with the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) and the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). All analyses were conducted in accordance with Good Pharmacoepidemiology Practice guidelines and the International Society for Pharmacoepidemiology recommendations for database studies [26]. The study was performed in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration of 1964, and its later amendments.

Process Indicators and Policy Implementation

Management Fee 1 for secondary fracture prevention was recorded for 54% of patients with hip fracture in the study population, indicating substantial adoption of the new reimbursement mechanism. In the study population, 14% had the additional fee for early surgery recorded, which may indicate adherence to recommended early surgical timing.

Primary Outcome: Treatment Rates and ITS Analysis

Discussion

In this study, we demonstrate that Japan’s 2022 secondary fracture prevention reimbursement policy led to a dramatic and immediate increase in osteoporosis treatment rates during hospitalization for patients with hip fracture, with sustained growth. The proportion of untreated patients decreased from approximately 70% beforehand down to 38% in year 2 following implementation of the reimbursement policy, representing a significant reduction in the treatment gap. We also found that the utilization of guideline-recommended medications increased, although at a more modest rate than overall treatment initiation.

Furthermore, with the exception of zoledronic acid, the use of all osteoporosis medications showed an overall increasing trend following policy implementation. Prescriptions for alendronate and active vitamin D3 saw the largest increases, accounting for most of the rise in treatment rates. These findings demonstrate that Japan’s post-hip fracture care has been reinforced by the reimbursement fee for secondary fracture prevention management [10, 27, 28].

Our findings are consistent with a recent report which showed that the proportion of patients receiving secondary fracture prevention interventions increased following the 2022 policy change in Japan [29]. While that report evaluated intervention based on the use of any osteoporosis medication and the performance of bone assessments, our study extends this by providing a detailed analysis of medication classes and guideline-recommended agents, thereby revealing qualitative aspects of treatment patterns. This in turn allows us to identify ongoing gaps in evidence-based medication selection and the need for further policy and clinical efforts.

Our finding of particularly marked increases in active vitamin D3 and bisphosphonates is consistent with a study which investigated treatment patterns by medicine class in a specific region of Japan [19]. Although that paper categorized drug classes differently to our present analysis, those medication classes with increased prescribing exhibited a consistent pattern. By confirming this pattern using a nationwide database covering the entire Japanese population, our study supports and strengthens the external validity suggested by this prior regional study. Patients who sustain a hip fracture are at markedly increased risk of subsequent fractures, and secondary prevention should be initiated promptly to reduce near-term (imminent) fracture risk. While recent guidelines emphasize that osteoanabolic therapy may be appropriate for patients at markedly high and imminent fracture risk [30], our practice-based findings suggest that the use of osteoanabolic agents did not materially change over time (2.61% in April 2020–March 2021 vs 2.67% in April 2023–March 2024). Accordingly, strategies to improve post-hip fracture care should focus not only on raising overall treatment initiation, but also on supporting evidence-based, patient-appropriate selection and sequencing of therapies [10, 27, 28].

One potential factor underlying the limited use of osteoporosis medications with proven fracture-prevention efficacy during hospitalization is Japan’s Diagnosis Procedure Combination (DPC) system. Diagnosis-Related Groups (DRG) or DRG-based systems have been adopted in many countries to reduce healthcare costs. In Japan, the DPC system was introduced in 2003; as of 2025, more than 1700 hospitals participate in the program, accounting for approximately 90% of all hospitals providing acute care for hip fractures. However, osteoporosis medications in hospitals are reimbursed under a bundled payment scheme. We speculate that this may provide a financial incentive to prescribe less costly agents [31–33]. This would in turn promote the more frequent prescription of inexpensive agents such as active vitamin D3, and off-patent and generic agents during hospitalization, and reduce the likelihood that more costly osteoanabolic agents are selected, albeit at the cost of a diminished response if the patient is re-evaluated and switched to these agents at a later stage [34]. Given that the acute-care hospitalization period is a critical window for the fastest possible decrease in imminent fracture risk, healthcare systems should be structured to facilitate appropriate medication selection at this stage [16, 35–38].

This study has several strengths. First, we used a large, nationwide hospital database, which enabled us to capture real-world treatment patterns among patients with hip fracture in Japan. Second, we evaluated both the overall impact of the 2022 reimbursement policy on osteoporosis treatment rates, and also provided a detailed analysis of medication classes, including guideline-recommended and proposed agents. Importantly, our conduct of this study in Japan, a super-aged society with universal health coverage, facilitates the generalizability of the findings and increases their international relevance.

Several limitations also warrant mention. First, our analysis was limited to medications prescribed during hospitalization, and we could not assess post-discharge treatment initiation or adherence. Second, the MDV database mainly includes DPC hospitals, which may not fully represent smaller community or non-DPC hospitals. Third, our follow-up period was limited to 2 years after the policy change. Nevertheless, since most acute hip fracture care in Japan is provided in DPC hospitals, and the majority of secondary fracture prevention is initiated during hospitalization, we believe our findings remain robust and generalizable.

Conclusion

These findings indicate that Japan’s targeted reimbursement policy has led to a significant and sustained increase in the initiation of osteoporosis medications as part of successful secondary fracture prevention for hospitalized patients with hip fracture. However, despite the immediate need to reduce fracture risk in this population, the proportion of patients receiving medications with well‑established fracture‑prevention efficacy remains suboptimal. The recently updated Japan guidelines emphasize ensuring that a greater proportion of patients receive therapies with the strongest evidence for preventing future fractures, particularly hip fractures, while embedding these efforts within systemic post‑fracture care services that support timely initiation, monitoring, and persistence [22]. To more effectively prevent subsequent fractures and reduce the burden of fragility fractures, further strategies are needed to enhance not only the quantity but also the quality and appropriateness of prescribed medications for optimal patient outcomes.

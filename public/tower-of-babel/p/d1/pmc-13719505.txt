On behalf of my co-authors, we thank Dr. Zhu and colleagues for the thoughtful and insightful comments regarding our DEAR-EXT study manuscript. We appreciate the recognition of the significance of our real-world analysis, especially in a context where head-to-head randomized evidence is limited. This feedback highlights important considerations that can enhance the interpretation of our findings.

Treatment era effects

We acknowledge the point about the potential influence of treatment era effects on our results. The evolving patterns in the use of androgen receptor inhibitors (ARIs) and imaging technologies in clinical practice over the study period are indeed critical factors to consider. To address this, we included the index year as a covariate in our model. By incorporating year fixed effects, we effectively controlled technological advancements and changes in treatment patterns in clinical practice that may occur over time, allowing us to isolate the effects of the treatments more accurately.

This approach is a widely accepted method for adjusting for treatment era differences, which ensured our findings reflect the impact of the treatments rather than variations in clinical practice patterns over the years. Additionally, it is important to note that our data span from 2019 to 2023, which is a relatively recent and short period. While we recognize that restricting the analysis to a narrower time frame could provide further insights, doing so would significantly reduce our sample size and decrease the statistical power of the study.

We appreciate the suggestion, and future analyses incorporating more recent data could yield meaningful insights. We will certainly consider this in our ongoing research efforts.

Definition of progression to metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC)

We appreciate the observation regarding the endpoint definition. The inclusion of treatment initiation as a criterion for progression does introduce complexity, as it may reflect both physician action and disease biology. However, in real-world settings, data capture is often less precise than in randomized controlled trials. Therefore, we must employ methods and algorithms to appropriately define endpoints, leading us to adopt a definition of progression that reflects real-world conditions.

We have provided a breakdown of how many patients were classified as having progressed to mCRPC based on different criteria. Prostate-specific antigen (PSA) response advantage and opportunity bias

We appreciate the insights on the potential opportunity bias affecting PSA response rates. Please see below the fixed landmark analysis at 3 and 6 months (Table 1B). Analyses at 12 months are not reported because of the small sample size. It is important to note that these rates have not been read from adjusted Kaplan–Meier plots, so censoring and adjusting for baseline differences, including baseline PSA, have not been accounted for. These results support the findings reported in the manuscript.

Subgroup analyses

We appreciate the suggestion to examine the impact of central nervous system penetration, polypharmacy, and other patient characteristics on the efficacy of darolutamide. While we agree that subgroup analyses could yield valuable insights and enhance the clinical utility of our findings, conducting such analyses is beyond the scope of this manuscript and would require a separate study and manuscript. We will certainly consider incorporating these analyses in our ongoing research efforts to better inform ARI selection in clinical practice.

In summary, we appreciate the constructive feedback, which has undoubtedly strengthened our study and contributed to a more nuanced understanding of ARI choice in non-metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer. We are committed to further exploring these avenues in our future work.

We thank Dr. Zhu and colleagues once again for the thoughtful review.

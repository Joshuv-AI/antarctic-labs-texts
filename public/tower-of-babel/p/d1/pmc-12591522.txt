Unveiling the recent trends: Therapeutic drug monitoring of antiepileptic drugs in Western India’s Tertiary Care Hospital
Authors: Shakeeb Siraj Dhorajiwala, Mahesh N. Belhekar, Neeraj Jain, Sangeeta Ravat
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Purpose/Aim:Primary goal of epilepsy management is to completely eradicate the disease without causing much adverse effects. However, physicians are not achieving this despite availability of antiseizure medicines and therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) services. The objective was to evaluate 3 years’ time trend of antiseizure medications (ASMs) use and primarily compare TDM requisitions for older versus newer ASMs.Materials and Methods:This is an analytical, observational time-trend analysis (TTA) of TDM services. The author retrospectively assessed TDM records of 122 statistically computed patients receiving a total of 448 ASMs over 3 years period from January 2019 to December 2021 stored in the department. Z-test, Chi-square test for trend, and trendline were plotted to establish trend.Results:Significant portion of the sample comprised older ASMs (252 of 448, 56.25% [95% confidence interval 46%–65%]). Comparison between monotherapy and polytherapy showed a statistically significant difference (P < 0.05) with trend for polytherapy. The minimum number of ASMs used in polytherapy was two, prescribed to 43.24% of people living with epilepsy. Of the two ASMs combined, the most common combination was of phenytoin and levetiracetam, prescribed to 31.25% of patients receiving two ASMs. Maximum number of ASMs prescribed to patients were five and seen in 2/74 of patients receiving polytherapy. Eighteen percent of referrals cited therapeutic failures with four adverse drug reactions (ADRs) documented at the therapeutic level.Conclusion:TTA of ASMs showed that in between 2019 and 2021, there was rising trend for use of newer ASMs. Overall, TDM requisitions for older drugs were more in number than newer drugs. The majority were treated with >1 ASMs, and seizure control was satisfactory with few ADRs documented.

INTRODUCTION

The World Health Organization estimates that an astounding 50 million people around the globe live with epilepsy (PWE), making it one of the most prevalent neurological disorders worldwide.[1] Out of this, India alone accounts for nearly one-sixth of the total cases, with an incidence rate ranging from 3 to 11.9/1000 individuals.[2] The primary goal of any epilepsy treatment is to completely eradicate the disease without causing much adverse effects, a feat that remains challenging despite the abundance of antiseizure medications (ASMs) and therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) services available. This is a critical issue, as PWE face a significantly increased risk of premature death, with life expectancy lowered by 2–10 years.[3]

TDM is a vital tool that ensures optimal drug concentrations are achieved and maintained within therapeutic limits, preventing therapeutic failures resulting from underdosage that can lead to breakthrough seizures (BTS). Monitoring levels beyond the upper limit of normal can also help identify causality for adverse drug reactions (ADRs), such as cerebellovestibular syndrome, sedation, visual disturbances, hepatotoxicity, and teratogenicity associated with older ASMs, as well as neuropsychiatric ADRs linked to newer ASMs like levetiracetam (LEV).[4] ADRs can significantly deteriorate the quality of life of patients.

Time-trend analyses (TTA) offer a fascinating way to study group-specific effects, therapeutic responses, failures, or ADRs, and evaluate health services like TDM. Healthcare professionals use TTA to conduct healthcare needs assessments, service planning, and policy development. Observing trends over time also provides the capability to predict future frequencies and rates of adverse events.[5] TTAs are relatively inexpensive and quick to perform; provided routine data are available.[6] Nevertheless, such value-adding studies are practically not conducted as often as it ought to be. Our study targets to fill in the void, analyze the existing trend, and identify any shift in trends.

The hypothesis posited that there would be no difference in the proportion of newer ASMs tested as compared to older ASMs over 3 years from 2019 to 2021. Consequently, TDM services requested for older versus newer ASMs were compared to assess patterns of change in specific indicators over time. The primary objective was to analyze the time trend of ASMs tested using TDM services in a tertiary care hospital in Western India over 3 years, comparing older ASMs including phenytoin (PHT), phenobarbital (PBT), valproate (VAL), and carbamazepine (CBZ) with newer ASMs such as LEV and lamotrigine (LTG). Secondary objectives included comparing the percentage of patients on monotherapy versus polytherapy, analyzing cases of therapeutic failures characterized by BTS, and examining ADRs in patients whose ASM plasma levels were within the therapeutic range.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This study was an analytical, observational TTA of TDM services aimed at optimizing epilepsy treatment. Expedited ethics approval with waiver of consent was accorded by the Institutional Ethics Committee (Project no. EC-OA-60-2022). The study adhered to the “Ethical Guidelines for Biomedical Research by the Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) (2017)” and the “Declaration of Helsinki” (2013). Data were collected from the TDM clinic records of 122 patients over 3 years (2019–2021) stored in the department of clinical pharmacology at a tertiary care teaching hospital in Western India.

Department of Clinical Pharmacology provides TDM services for older ASMs, including PHT, PBT, VAL, and CBZ, as well as newer ASMs, such as LEV and LTG. The TDM clinic operates twice weekly. Standard practice involves recording demographic data, indications for TDM, epilepsy type, comorbidities, medication details, treatment response, and ADRs in a TDM case record pro forma before blood sampling. The institute is also a regional center for the Pharmacovigilance Programme of India since 2010. Serum levels are measured using high-performance liquid chromatography and interpreted based on the Clinical Pharmacology Laboratory’s reference range. Statistical analysis entailed a meticulous retrospective review of TDM records. The inclusion criteria were strictly confined to TDM requests for ASMs from various departments, with nonepileptic indications being excluded. The sample size was a priori determined using data from the study conducted by Alacqua et al.[7] With an α-error set at 0.05, a β-error at 0.2, and an allocation ratio of 1.632 (equivalent to the effect size), the Z-test for the difference between two proportions indicated that a sample size of 122 TDM requisitions of PWE was necessary from a population of 4370 TDM records. Systematic random sampling was employed, selecting every 36th TDM card to maintain randomness and representativeness.

To ensure 80% power, the sample size was meticulously calculated using G*Power V.3.1.9.7. Department of Psychology of the Heinrich-Heine-University, Düsseldorf, Germany. Data analyses were conducted using MS-Excel (Publisher: Microsoft Corporation, Redmond, Washington, USA, 2023) and Graphpad Prism 10.3.1.(509), GraphPad Software, Inc., Boston, Massachusetts, United States. For trend analysis of older versus newer ASMs over the 3-year period, the Chi-square test for trend was employed, while the Z-test was utilized for comparing monotherapy versus polytherapy, with a significance level set at P < 0.05.

RESULTS

The study evaluated 448 ASMs from 122 patients, with the majority (49%) being in their first decade of life [Figure 1] and a median weight (interquartile range, 95% confidence interval [CI]) of 23.5 (41.13, 22–37 kg). Male:female was 3:2. Figure 2 shows that significant portion (proportion of older antiepileptic drugs [AEDS], % (CI) of the sample comprised older ASMs (252 out of 448, 56.25% [46%–65%]; P < 0.05). Comparison between polytherapy versus monotherapy [Figure 3] showed the difference in percentage to be 22%, statistically significant with P = 0.0083. Polytherapy was predominant in 61% of cases, with PHT and LEV being the most frequent combination (31.25%). In monotherapy, the most prescribed drug was LEV (56.25%). Eighteen percent of PWE experienced therapeutic failure despite therapeutic drug levels, with 74% (17 out of 23) of these cases involving generalized tonic–clonic seizures and polytherapy predominantly taking VAL and LEV (22 out of 23). Fifty-two percent (12 out of 23) cards documented compliance issues common among school dropouts (44%). Three-year period trend analysis revealed a notable shift of trend towards newer ASM use and TDM testing; P < 0.01 [Figure 4].

DISCUSSION

Our detailed analyses, conducted within the department of clinical pharmacology at a premier teaching hospital in Western India, have uncovered intriguing trends regarding the use of AEDS. We initiated this study recognizing the delicate balance required due to ASMs’ narrow therapeutic indices and the significant drug interactions that could jeopardize treatment optimization.

It was particularly striking that pediatric patients dominated the TDM requests, echoing the demographic trends in India. This aligns with a thorough meta-analysis by Sridharan and Murthy, which found children to be the most susceptible to epilepsy, especially generalized tonic–clonic seizures (69.6%).[89] Furthermore, our data revealed a higher number of TDM requisitions for males, consistent with findings by Thomas SV (2001), who identified male gender as a risk factor for seizure disorders.[10]

Our findings underscored a preference for older ASMs over newer ones, continuing a trend similar to previous audits in the same institute by Taur et al.[11] However, a significant shift toward polytherapy (61%) from monotherapy (67%) was noted, with PHT and LEV frequently combined. The experts in neurology in our institute opined that the shift is understandable given our hospital’s role as a comprehensive epilepsy center handling refractory case and the inclusion of PHT and LEV in the Indian National List of Essential Medicines, ensuring their availability and affordability. Conversely, newer ASMs like lacosamide and topiramate must be purchased by patients. LEV emerged as a favored monotherapy drug (56.25%) over PHT (41%), attributable to its safer profile and minimal drug interactions. This trend mirrors global treatment practices.[12]

Subgroup analysis provided insightful revelations. Therapeutic failure, despite adequate drug levels, predominantly involved generalized tonic–clonic seizures treated with polytherapy. Older ASMs have a notorious safety profile, which is worsened with polytherapy due to their ability to alter enzyme functions, leading to unfavorable pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interactions. These interactions can result in therapeutic failure or ADRs.[4] Our finding is in concordance with a meta-analysis reporting a cumulative incidence of drug-resistant epilepsy in around 25% of patients which explains the phenomenon of therapeutic failure at therapeutic drug levels.[13] An indigenous study by Munshi et al. reported similar findings without delving into their nuances.[14] In contrast, a North Indian study by Tripathi et al. found that 7% of therapeutic failure cases had generalized seizures, with the majority having focal seizures.[15] Another study by Mattson et al. reported up to 40% therapeutic failure in PWE receiving older ASMs as monotherapy.[16] Compliance issues were common, especially among patients with lower education levels. Effective management strategies, as highlighted by Garg, could potentially address these challenges.[2]

Interestingly, ADRs such as dysphagia and xerostomia were reported in patients on monotherapy with PHT and purpura, mouth ulcer, nystagmus in patients receiving LEV despite therapeutic level. Theoretically, within therapeutic range the drug is expected not to exhibit any adverse reaction. However, since the institute is a regional center for the Pharmacovigilance Programme of India (2010) and routinely conducting active surveillance activities to detect even rare adverse reactions, we endeavored to document as many ADRs as possible despite the devastating COVID-19 pandemic which was the major deterrent to the process of active surveillance of ADRs. We conceptualized the study with a predetermined sample size calculation based on the primary outcome measure of difference between proportion of newer versus older ASMs tested. Had we considered the difference in frequency of ADRs reported with newer versus older ASMs we could have detected a higher number of ADRs. The process of systematic random sampling employed also propounds to the underestimation of ADRs.

Such idiosyncratic reactions, often linked to older aromatic ASMs, may also occur with newer drugs, albeit rarely.[1718] This observation is consistent with findings from indigenous studies by Sawal et al. and underscores the need for personalized treatment approaches.[19] An alternative explanation suggests that anticonvulsant dosages may be lower in Indians than in Europeans, implying that therapeutic doses for Western countries may be supratherapeutic for the Indian population, resulting in toxic effects.[20] In addition, the documented low median weight of our sample suggests a possible underlying deficient nutritional status, which can affect drug pharmacokinetics and exacerbate the effect of ASMs beyond the therapeutic limit.[21]

Our trend analysis revealed an increasing preference for newer ASMs like LEV, despite the paradox of limited utility for TDM. This trend can be justified by the widespread prescription of LEV as monotherapy and in combination with other ASMs, both older and newer. Recent evidence supports the combination of newer ASMs like LEV with LTG for the treatment of focal seizures, and it has been found effective for generalized seizures as well.[4] Given the unique mechanistic and pharmacokinetic profiles of newer ASMs, the United States Food and Drug Administration recently issued a strategy allowing the extrapolation of these drugs across populations as add-on or monotherapy.[12]

Despite the favorable properties of newer ASMs, including LEV, the increasing number of TDM requests seems counterintuitive. On one hand, there are studies, including a Cochrane review, highlighting the limited utility of TDM for newer ASMs.[22] On the other hand, real-world studies, such as the one conducted by Stepanova and Beran, substantiate the positive impact of TDM services on seizure control in patients receiving newer ASMs.[23] This trend could be due to the comprehensive pharmacokinetic profiling that TDM offers, which remains the gold standard for assessing treatment adherence and drug interactions.[24]

Our study illuminates the evolving landscape of ASM usage in Western India, aligning with global standards for epilepsy management. Despite its retrospective nature, this study sets the groundwork for future, multicentered prospective analyses to validate these trends and enhance their generalizability.

CONCLUSION

TTA of antiepileptic medications revealed a notable increase in the use of newer ASMs between 2019 and 2021. Overall, TDM requisitions for older drugs were more frequent compared to newer medications. Most patients with epilepsy were treated with multiple AEDs, achieving satisfactory seizure control with minimal ADRs documented at therapeutic plasma drug levels.

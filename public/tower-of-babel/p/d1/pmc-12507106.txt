The mitochondrial genome of Minettia omei (Diptera: Lauxaniidae) and its phylogenetic analysis
Authors: Yuli Zhang, Bei Zhou, Hao Liu, Yaoyao Xue, Shengjuan Zhao, Wenliang Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study performed mitochondrial genome sequencing on Minettia omei, which belongs to Lauxaniinae, a subfamily of Lauxaniidae. The complete mitochondrial genome spans 16,201 bp and comprises 37 genes, including 13 protein-coding genes, 22 tRNA genes, 2 rRNA genes, and a control region. Base composition: A 38.81%, T 38.81%, C 12.93%, G 9.44% , A + T bias 77.62%. COI uses start codon CAA; COI, COII, NAD2, NAD1, NAD4 have incomplete T + tRNA stop codons. Phylogenetic analysis elucidates the species' evolutionary relationships, establishing a critical genetic foundation for future studies on Lauxaniidae.Keywords: Mitochondrial genome, Lauxaniidae, Lauxaniinae, Minettia omei

Introduction

The Lauxaniidae family is rich in species diversity, with nearly 200 genera and over 2,100 species known worldwide (Silva 2016). To date, mitochondrial genetic data for this family remain relatively limited, with only the mitochondrial genomes of 12 species from the Lauxaniidae subfamily having been documented, one of which belongs to the Minettia. Minettia Robineau-Desvoidy represents the third largest genus within the family Lauxaniidae. Its distribution encompasses five major zoogeographical regions: Palearctic, Oriental, Nearctic, Neotropical, and Australian (Li WL et al. 2023). The species Minettia (Plesiominettia) omei (Shatalkin 1998a, 1998b) has been recorded in several provinces of China, including Yunnan, Guizhou, Sichuan, Anhui, Hunan, Chongqing, and Guangxi. This study has successfully completed the sequencing of the mitochondrial genome of M. omei for the first time. This achievement is of great significance for exploring the phylogenetic position of Lauxaniidae.

Materials and methods

The specimens of Minettia omei were collected on 3 June 2023, in the Lingui District of Guilin City, Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region, China (25°26′N, 109°49′E). As the collected samples were insects, no ethical approval or relevant permits were required for this collection. The genitalia preparation procedure involved excising the terminal portion of the abdomen and immersing it in a cold, saturated NaOH solution for six hours to soften the tissues. Subsequently, the specimen was thoroughly rinsed with water, neutralized, and subjected to detailed dissection and examination. After observation in glycerin, the specimen was transferred to fresh glycerin, housed in a micro-vial, and affixed beneath the main specimen mount. This specimens were identified by Bai Yingming. M. omei can be differentiated from other species based on the following morphological characteristics: arista pubescent (rarely bare/plumose), with the longest arista setulae exceeding one-third of the height of the first flagellomere; wings hyaline, rarely brown at the base; mesonotum with 0–1 + 2–3 dorsocentral setae, hair-like acrostichal setulae, and 1–2 pairs of strong setae in most species. Male genitalia exhibit postgonites (rarely absent), lack a phallic sheath and possess a case-like phallus. Most species are black (Shi et al. 2015). The specimens are currently preserved in 100% absolute ethanol and archived at Henan University of Science and Technology, Henan, China (www.haust.edu.cn, contact person: Zhang Yuli; e-mail: yulizhang@stu.haust.edu.cn) under the voucher number Lau202300003.

Adult specimens of M. omei were photographed using a Canon EOS 6D digital camera mounted on a microscope (Canon, Tokyo, Japan), and image stacking was performed using HELICON FOCUS v7.0.2.0 (Helicon Soft, Kharkiv, Ukraine) (Figure 1). Total genomic DNA was extracted from whole bodies, including heads and wings. Library construction and sequencing were conducted by Qingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). Fragmented DNA was purified and amplified using the NEBNext® Ultra™ DNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina®, and qualified libraries were sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq platform. Approximately 10 GB of high-quality reads were assembled using SPAdes v3.13.0 with the parameter -k127 (Bankevich et al. 2012). Assemblies were aligned to closely related reference genomes using BLASTN v2.2.30+ (e-value 1e-5). Gene annotation was performed using MITOS2 (http://mitos2.bioinf.uni-leipzig.de/index.py) (Donath et al. 2019), followed by manual curation. The illumina short sequences were compared with the mitochondrial sequences using BWA software, the coverage was statistically analyzed using samtools depth, and finally the coverage map was drawn using a python script (Li H 2013) (Figure S1). A circular genome map was generated using Organellar Genome DRAW v1.2 (Greiner et al. 2019).

The specimen map of Minettia omei. This species reference image was taken by Zhang.

Based on the 13 protein-coding genes (PCGs) and 2 ribosomal RNA genes of M. omei, along with 18 additional sequences retrieved from the GenBank database, sequence alignment and trimming were conducted using MEGA11 software. Subsequently, the phylogenetic tree was constructed employing the maximum likelihood method implemented in IQ-TREE software. These included 10 Lauxanioidea species, two Tephritidea species, two Opomyzoidea species, one Tabanidea species, one Muscoidea species and two Ephydroidea species.

Results

The complete mitochondrial genome of M. omei (Figure 2) is approximately 16,201 bp in length, with a nucleotide composition of A 38.81%, T 38.81%, C 12.93%, and G 9.44%, showing a pronounced AT bias. It comprises the typical set of 37 genes commonly found in insect mitochondrial genomes, along with a complete control region. Despite belonging to a different genus, the gene content and arrangement of M. omei are functionally and positionally conserved compared to other known Lauxaniinae mitochondrial genomes.

Circular map of the mitochondrial genome of Minettia omei. Genes located on the inner side of the circle are transcribed in the counterclockwise direction, while those on the outer side are transcribed clockwise. Genes are color-coded according to their functional categories. The inner gray histogram represents the GC content across the genome, with the Central gray line indicating the 50% threshold.

Among the PCGs, five genes (NAD2, NAD5, NAD4L, NAD6, and CYTB) use ATT as the start codon; three genes (COII, COIII, and NAD4) use ATG; three genes (ATP6, NAD3, and NAD1) use ATA; one gene (ATP8) uses ATC; and COI uses CAA as the start codon. Seven PCGs (ATP8, ATP6, COIII, NAD3, NAD5, NAD4L, and NAD6) terminate with the TAA stop codon. Five genes (NAD2, COI, COII, NAD4, and NAD1) have an incomplete stop codon of T + tRNA and one gene (CYTB) ends with TAG. The 22 tRNA genes range in length from 63 to 72 bp, while the 12S rRNA and 16S rRNA genes are 790 bp and 1,332 bp in length, respectively. The overlap regions ranged from 1 to 7 bp, with the longest overlap between ATP6 and ATP8 as well as ND4 and ND4L (Table S1).

Maximum likelihood analysis supports the monophyly of Lauxanioidea, with M. omei clearly placed within this superfamily. The phylogenetic topology recovered is as follows: Tabanidea + (Opomyzoidea + (Muscoidea + Ephydroidea) + Tephritidea + Lauxanioidea)). Within Lauxanioidea, the internal relationships are resolved as (Spaniocelyphus + Celyphus) + (Pachycerina + (Homoneura + Cestrotus)) + ((Tricholauxania + Peplomyza) + (Marmarodeceia + Minettia)). Furthermore, the family Celyphidae is nested within Lauxaniidae, and the monophyly of Lauxaniidae has not been supported by phylogenetic studies (Figure 3).

The phylogenetic tree was developed using the maximum-likelihood method, based on 13 PCGs and 2 ribosomal genes in the mitochondrial genome; the third locus of the mitochondrial genes was removed through nucleotide sequencing. The following sequences were used: PV550267 (this study), KX372559, NC034923, KX372562, KX372558 (Li XK et al. 2017), NC070163 (Yao et al. 2024), NC008756 (Shi et al. 2012), NC024855 (Li XK et al. 2016), NC015926 (Yang et al. 2011), NC014283 (Wang et al. 2011), NC011596 (Andrianov et al. 2010), NC023825 (Llopart et al. 2014), OX459033 (Falk et al. 2024), NC008748 (Yu et al. 2007), NC014611 (Nardi et al. 2010), KT272847 (Junqueira et al. 2016), OZ219520, NC060541, OZ278790.

Discussion and conclusion

In this study, we present the first fully sequenced mitochondrial genome of M. omei, thereby providing novel molecular data for the genus that has lacked mitogenomic resources to date. The mitochondrial genome of M. omei displays structural features typical of Dipteran mitochondrial genomes, such as a pronounced AT bias, conserved gene order, and multiple incomplete stop codons. The atypical start codon CAA, which encodes glutamine, was utilized in the COI gene.

The maximum likelihood phylogenetic analysis yielded a well-supported tree topology that is congruent with traditional taxonomic classifications. M. omei was robustly placed within a monophyletic Lauxanioidea, and the internal relationships,(Spaniocelyphus + Celyphus) + (Pachycerina + (Homoneura + Cestrotus)) + ((Tricholauxania + Peplomyza) + (Marmarodeceia + Minettia)), offer new molecular evidence supporting previously morphology-based hypotheses. The nesting of Celyphidae within Lauxaniidae suggests that Lauxaniidae does not constitute a monophyletic group.

This work offers data supportive of exploring evolutionary relationships within Lauxaniinae and supplements taxon sampling for mitochondrial phylogenetic research in Diptera. While these findings contribute to the field, more extensive sampling across genera and subfamilies especially for less-studied groups like Minettia and related taxa remains important for better understanding the evolutionary history of Lauxaniidae. Future investigations incorporating nuclear genomic data alongside morphological analyses may help further clarify the phylogeny and taxonomy of this diverse fly lineage.

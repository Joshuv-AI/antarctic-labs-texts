Response to the Letter to the Editor Entitled “CKD Prevalence in Rural Population of Sri Lanka”
Authors: Rajitha Asanga Abeysekera, Helen Grania Healy, Sembakutti Samita, Sampath Tennakoon, Sakunthala Jayasinghe, Nuwan Darshana Wickramasinghe, Indika Bandara Gawarammana, Navin Dayantha Gamage, Vishwa Chathuranga Hemakeerthi, Farook Rafsanjani, Tennakoon Mudiyanselage Waruni Vidarshana Tennakoon, Sachini Navoda Palliyaguru, Wendy Hoy
Published: 2026

The Author Replies:

We thank Bikbov1 for their insightful commentary on our recent study “CKD in Sri Lanka - A Prevalence Study.2” We are grateful for their meticulous reading of our work and for highlighting an extremely important insightful aspect of our findings.

We particularly appreciate their emphasis on the identification of the discrepancy between the distribution of classic chronic kidney disease (CKD) risk factors and the prevalence of CKD among urban and rural populations. As they highlight, our findings show a higher prevalence of albuminuria (11.2%) in the rural sector than in the urban sector (6.1%) despite a lower prevalence of diabetes, obesity, and ischemic heart disease in the rural sector. Given that this study was a cross-sectional prevalence study, it is difficult to ascertain the exact causes of CKD in this group of participants; however, this truly raises the possibility of other etiologies contributing to CKD in the region.

Currently, Kandy District in Sri Lanka, where this study was undertaken, is not considered a CKD of uncertain etiology endemic region.3 This is mostly based on differences in ground water patterns, water usage patterns, occupation, and pesticide usage patterns in this region, which are different from CKD of uncertain etiology endemic regions. However, it is increasingly recognized that the CKD of uncertain etiology pattern of disease is seen to extend beyond these originally identified areas and appearing among the “uncertain” category of CKD. Usage of ayurvedic and herbal supplements, as well as tea and rubber farming are common in Kandy District, which raises the possibility of other etiological factors contributing to CKD in the region.

We fully agree that this is an important area that needs further study. A multideterminant model of renal disease is extremely important to study this, which looks at not only the classic pathological entity of tubulo-interstitial nephritis but also considers an admixture with other pathologies because of the variation of potential etiologies. We thank the authors again for their insightful comments on this piece of work.

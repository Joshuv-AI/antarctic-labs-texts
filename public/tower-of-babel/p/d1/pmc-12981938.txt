Clinical Utility and Limitations of Photon-Counting Detector CTA in the Follow-Up of Flow Diverter Stent
Authors: Hideaki Shigematsu, Azusa Sunaga, Akihiro Hirayama, Takahiro Osada, Takatoshi Sorimachi, Masamichi Takahashi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveFlow diverters (FDs) are widely used to treat intracranial aneurysms, with DSA serving as the gold standard for follow-up evaluation. Although a few studies have reported the use of photon-counting detector CTA (PCD-CTA) for minimally invasive follow-up after standalone FD treatment, PCD-CTA after coil-assisted FD placement has never been documented.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 11 patients who underwent FD placement between May 2023 and December 2024, all of whom underwent post-procedural evaluation with PCD-CTA. Patients were categorized into 2 groups: standalone FD (n = 6) and coil-assisted FD (n = 5). PCD-CTA was performed on postoperative day 1, at 6 months, and at 12 months. Metal artifact reduction was applied in the coil-assisted group.ResultsIn the standalone FD group, PCD-CTA enabled reliable assessment of the in-stent lumen, FD apposition to the parent artery, and aneurysm occlusion, with findings consistent with those of DSA. In the coil-assisted FD group, when coils extended near the FD, evaluation of the in-stent lumen, FD-to-parent artery apposition, and aneurysm occlusion was hindered by metal artifacts in slices containing coils. Conversely, when coils were absent near the FD, partial visualization of intra-aneurysmal contrast was possible, although residual aneurysms tended to be underestimated.ConclusionPCD-CTA demonstrated diagnostic performance comparable to that of DSA for postoperative evaluation following standalone FD placement. However, in coil-assisted FD cases, postoperative assessment remained limited by coil-related metal artifacts.

Introduction

Flow diverters (FDs) are increasingly used in the treatment of intracranial aneurysms. Post-procedural surveillance requires systematic evaluation of FD apposition to the parent vessel, detection of in-stent thrombosis, and assessment of aneurysm occlusion. Early identification of aneurysm recurrence and in-stent stenosis is crucial for optimizing antiplatelet therapy, preventing complications, and determining the need for retreatment. Currently, DSA and cone-beam CT (CBCT) with a diluted contrast medium are regarded as standard techniques for post-FD evaluation. In contrast, less invasive modalities such as MRA are limited by susceptibility artifacts, while conventional energy-integrating detector (EID) CTA is restricted by metal-induced artifacts.1)

Photon-counting detector CT (PCD-CT; NAEOTOM Alpha, Syngo CT VA50; Siemens Healthcare GmbH, Forchheim, Germany) has recently been introduced and offers superior image quality compared with conventional EID-CT due to higher spatial resolution, improved contrast-to-noise ratio, reduced electronic noise, and greater dose efficiency.2,3) The utility of PCD-CT for follow-up evaluation of coronary stents has been reported4); however, evidence regarding its application to intracranial FD assessment remains limited.5) To the best of our knowledge, no previous study has specifically investigated coil-assisted FD.

The present study aimed to evaluate the utility of PCD-CTA for post-procedural assessment of FD deployment, including cases with an adjunctive coil embolization.

Materials and Methods

Patients

We included patients who underwent FD placement to treat cerebral aneurysms between May 2023 and December 2024. Patients who underwent post-procedural PCD-CTA were included in this study. PCD-CTA was performed on postoperative day 1, and combined evaluations using both DSA and PCD-CTA were conducted at 6 and 12 months after the procedure.

Scan protocol

All patients underwent imaging using a first-generation PCD-CT system equipped with 2 cadmium telluride detectors. The acquisition parameters were as follows: tube voltage, 120 kV; CARE keV image quality level, 320 mAs; collimation, 120 × 0.2 mm; rotation time, 0.5 s; pitch, 0.85; reconstruction kernel, Hv72; and image matrix, 1024 × 1024. Image reconstruction was performed using quantum iterative reconstruction (QIR), with a QIR strength level of 3. Contrast-enhanced images were acquired after intravenous administration of an iodinated contrast medium (iopromide, 370 mg I/mL; Ultravist, Bayer Healthcare, Leverkusen, Germany) at a rate of 25.0 mg/kg/s. Bolus tracking was applied, with the scan initiated 6 s after enhancement was detected in the internal carotid artery at the level of the posterior fossa. All images were analyzed using a dedicated workstation (Syngo.via, version 8.04; Siemens Healthcare, Forchheim, Germany). Metal artifact reduction (MAR) for PCD-CT was performed using iterative MAR (iMAR) with the Neuro coils preset. iMAR could be applied to reconstruction kernels up to Hv56; however, due to limitations in combining iMAR with high-resolution kernels, iMAR was not available for Hv72 reconstructions. Therefore, image evaluation was performed with the window center and width set to 1500 and 4500, respectively, to mitigate metal artifacts. MAR was applied in cases involving combined treatment with an FD and coils.

All cerebral angiographic procedures were performed using a biplane flat-panel detector angiography system (Innova IGS 630; GE Healthcare, Chicago, IL, USA).

Subjective analysis of PCD-CTA and DSA images

The patients were categorized into 2 groups: the FD-only group and the FD with adjunctive coiling group. The following parameters were evaluated: in-stent lumen visibility, FD apposition to the parent artery (proximal FD, aneurysm neck, and distal FD), and the degree of aneurysm occlusion. Each parameter was scored on a 3-point scale, where two indicated good (sufficient for diagnosis), one indicated noisy but still diagnosable, and 0 indicated not diagnosable.6) The degree of aneurysm occlusion was graded using the O’Kelly–Marotta (OKM) scale.7) These evaluations were performed and compared with the findings of DSA. Axial and sagittal PCD-CTA images were evaluated, with the window center and width set to 150 and 1000 for the FD-only group and to 1500 and 4500 for the FD with adjunctive coiling group; these parameters served as the default settings and were adjusted as needed according to individual cases. DSA images were acquired in standard anteroposterior and lateral projections and were analyzed using a fixed image matrix of 1024 × 1024 pixels.

All images were independently reviewed by 2 board-certified neurosurgeons with 16 and 9 years of experience (H.S. and A.S., respectively). In cases of disagreement, a consensus reading was used to determine the final assessment.

Ethical approval

The research within our submission has been approved by the institutional ethics review board of Tokai University (Approval No. 25R035-001 MH). Patient information was handled using an opt-out approach after public disclosure of the study details. This was a single-center retrospective study conducted at an institution where PCD-CTA is routinely performed in combination with DSA for postoperative follow-up.

Results

Study population

A total of 11 patients with intracranial aneurysms underwent FD placement during the study period, and all also underwent PCD-CTA. Table 1 summarizes the patient characteristics. All patients were female, with a median age of 63 years (range, 47–82 years). The aneurysm locations were as follows: internal carotid artery–posterior communicating artery aneurysm in 3 patients, internal carotid artery paraclinoid aneurysm in 5, internal carotid artery cavernous portion aneurysm in 2, and vertebral artery aneurysm in 1. The median maximum aneurysm diameter was 10.3 mm (range, 5.2–23.3 mm), and the median aneurysm neck diameter was 4.2 mm (range, 2.4–9.4 mm). The types of FD used were Pipeline Shield (Medtronic, Minneapolis, MN, USA) in 10 patients and Surpass Evolve (Stryker, Kalamazoo, MI, USA) in 1. The median follow-up duration was 224 days (range, 19–581 days). Five patients underwent adjunctive coiling, including one who had previously undergone stent-assisted coiling using a Neuroform Atlas stent (Stryker). None of the patients had multiple FDs.

Subjective analysis

For all 11 patients, the assessment results of the 2 independent reviewers were in complete agreement. In the FD-only group, in-stent lumen visibility and FD apposition to the parent artery (proximal FD, aneurysm neck, and distal FD) were consistently rated 2 (good) at all sites. The aneurysm occlusion grading on PCD-CTA was consistent with the OKM scale assessed using DSA.

In 5 patients treated with an FD combined with coil embolization, in-stent evaluation and FD-to-parent artery apposition at the proximal and distal FD segments were rated as 2 (good) in all cases. In contrast, at the aneurysm neck, which was affected by coil-related metal artifacts, both parameters were rated 0 (not diagnosable) in all 5 patients. Regarding aneurysm occlusion, 4 patients in whom coils were located adjacent to the FD received a score of 0 because coil-related metal artifacts prevented evaluation. In the remaining patient (Case 7), PCD-CTA on postoperative day 1 yielded a score of 0 owing to coils located near the FD. However, at 12 months, displacement of the coil mass away from the FD, together with the use of MAR, enabled partial visualization of contrast medium within the aneurysm, resulting in a score of 1 (noisy but diagnosable). Table 2 summarizes the OKM scale assessed at 6 and 12 months after FD placement in 11 patients. In the FD-only group, OKM grades on PCD-CT were concordant with those on DSA at both follow-up time points. In FD with adjunctive coiling group, OKM assessment on PCD-CT was not evaluable in most patients. Notably, in one FD with adjunctive coiling case (No. 7), PCD-CT underestimated the degree of aneurysm occlusion compared with DSA at both 6 and 12 months.

Representative cases

Case 1: Sufficient evaluation using PCD-CTA for follow-up in FD-alone treatment

A 54-year-old woman with left oculomotor nerve palsy presented with an internal carotid artery cavernous segment aneurysm measuring 20.2 mm in maximum diameter on angiography. A Pipeline Shield 4.25 × 30 mm device was used to treat the aneurysm. On postoperative day 1, PCD-CTA demonstrated good apposition of the FD to the parent artery with no evidence of aneurysm occlusion or in-stent stenosis (Fig. 1A and 1B). At 6 months, PCD-CTA revealed aneurysm occlusion classified as OKM grade C (Fig. 1C, 1D and 1F), consistent with the DSA findings (Fig. 1E). The FD-to-parent artery apposition was clearly visualized, and intimal hyperplasia within the stent could be evaluated similarly to DSA. After 12 months, PCD-CTA demonstrated a reduction in intimal hyperplasia, with findings comparable to those of DSA (Fig. 1G and 1H).

Case 2: Visualization of calcification in the ICA wall adjacent to FD after FD-alone deployment

A 78-year-old woman with a growing internal carotid artery paraclinoid aneurysm measuring 7.8 mm in maximum diameter underwent deployment of a Pipeline Shield 4.00 × 16 mm device. Immediate postoperative CBCT revealed poor apposition of the FD to the proximal parent artery (Fig. 2A). On postoperative day 1, PCD-CTA revealed no residual aneurysm or in-stent stenosis. Furthermore, the proximal FD-to-parent artery region with internal carotid artery (ICA) wall calcification, which appeared poorly apposed on immediate postoperative CBCT, was confirmed to be well apposed. The initial CBCT finding was attributable to ICA calcification (Fig. 2B).

Images of Case 2, in which a Pipeline Shield 4.00 × 16 mm was deployed for an internal carotid artery (ICA) paraclinoid aneurysm measuring 7.8 mm in maximum diameter. (A) Cone-beam CT (CBCT) axial view. The white arrow indicates suspected malapposition between the flow diverter (FD) and the ICA. (B) Photon-counting detector CTA (PCD-CTA) axial view. The white arrow indicates calcification of the ICA. FD-to-vessel wall apposition was satisfactory. PCD-CTA enabled differentiation between the FD and calcified vessel wall, allowing superior evaluation of FD apposition compared with CBCT.

Case 6: FD placement following stent-assisted coiling

A 54-year-old woman with a ruptured vertebral artery aneurysm measuring 23.3 mm in maximum diameter initially underwent stent-assisted coiling using a Neuroform Atlas 4.0 × 21 mm device. Because of aneurysm recurrence, a Pipeline Shield 3.5 × 20 mm device was deployed 4 months after the initial procedure (Fig. 3A). On postoperative day 1, despite the use of MAR, coil-related artifacts precluded evaluation of the contrast medium in the residual aneurysm, in-stent lumen at the aneurysm neck, and FD-to-parent artery apposition in that region (Fig. 3B–3D). In contrast, the distal FD was located in a different slice from the coil mass, which allowed assessment of the contrast medium in the in-stent lumen and FD-to-parent artery apposition in the region overlapping the Neuroform Atlas stent (Fig. 3E and 3F).

Case 7: Displacement of coil mass away from FD after FD deployment with adjunctive coiling

A 72-year-old woman with an ICA paraclinoid aneurysm measuring 20.1 mm in maximum diameter, which caused a visual field defect, underwent deployment of a Pipeline Shield 5.0 × 35 mm device with adjunctive coiling. On postoperative day 1, PCD-CTA could not be used to evaluate the residual aneurysm, in-stent lumen at the aneurysm neck, or FD-to-parent artery apposition because of coil-related artifacts. At 12 months, the coil mass had displaced from the FD. After applying MAR, which partially mitigated these artifacts, aneurysm occlusion could be evaluated as OKM grade C (Fig. 4A–4C). However, DSA simultaneously demonstrated a residual aneurysm corresponding to OKM grade B (Fig. 4D), indicating that PCD-CTA underestimated the degree of aneurysm filling compared with DSA.

Discussion

Postoperative imaging evaluation following FD placement using conventional EID-CT has been reported to be challenging due to metal artifacts.8) In contrast, in the present study, PCD-CTA enabled reliable assessment of the in-stent lumen, vessel wall apposition, and aneurysm occlusion, with findings comparable to those obtained using DSA. A previous report based on a few cases suggested the utility of PCD-CT for evaluating vessel wall apposition, in-stent stenosis, and aneurysm status following standalone FD treatment,5) which aligns with the present study.

This study represents the first report on the imaging evaluation of coil-assisted FD treatment using PCD-CTA. In this study, metallic artifacts hindered in-stent evaluation and assessment of FD apposition at the aneurysm neck and proximal FD, both corresponding to slices that contained the coil mass. Similarly, aneurysm occlusion could not be evaluated in 4 of the 5 cases. In Case 8, where the coil mass was displaced from the FD during follow-up, PCD-CTA with MAR enabled partial visualization of contrast medium, revealing residual inflow into the aneurysm neck. Because MAR can attenuate the true intraluminal contrast signal, PCD-CTA tends to underestimate the degree of residual aneurysm compared with DSA. While DSA demonstrated an OKM grade B, PCD-CTA showed an OKM grade C with minimal intra-aneurysmal flow.

In conventional EID-CT for coronary angiography, blooming artifacts have been reported to visually enlarge high-attenuation structures, leading to underestimation of vascular lumen diameter.9,10) In contrast, PCD-CT, which provides superior MAR, can mitigate coil-related artifacts. However, this process may also partially suppress the true contrast signal within the aneurysm, potentially resulting in underestimation of residual aneurysm size in cases of FD placement combined with adjunctive coiling.

In Case 2, PCD-CTA enabled satisfactory evaluation of vessel wall calcification and FD apposition. Although CBCT and EID-CT may be limited in visualizing the vascular lumen in the presence of calcification, PCD-CT provides high spatial resolution imaging that allows clear depiction of the lumen, even in calcified lesions.4,11)

The choice of reconstruction kernel is critical when performing image evaluation using PCD-CT. In coronary CTA, the use of the Bv64 kernel has been reported to allow clear visualization of the vascular lumen with high spatial resolution, even after stent placement in calcified coronary arteries.4) In the cerebral vasculature, the Hv72 kernel has been reported to be useful for evaluating perforating branches,6) and both the Hv64 and Hv72 kernels have been considered effective for postoperative assessment of the FD lumen.5) In the present study, the Hv72 kernel was primarily used for evaluation after FD placement, enabling clear visualization of vessel wall calcification and the FD stent mesh.

Limitation

This study is limited by its small sample size, single-center, and retrospective design. Image assessment relied exclusively on a subjective scoring system (0–2), without quantitative evaluation, and key factors related to image quality, including MAR and kernel settings, were not standardized. Notably, the optimal reconstruction kernel for FD evaluation remains undefined, and the present data do not allow the establishment of a unified imaging protocol. In coil-assisted cases, image assessment was infeasible in most instances, thereby limiting the generalizability of our conclusions.

Conclusion

In standalone FD cases, PCD-CTA enables high-resolution assessment of the in-stent lumen, FD-to-parent artery apposition, and aneurysm occlusion, providing minimally invasive imaging results comparable to those of DSA. In contrast, in cases of coil-assisted FD treatment, evaluation of the in-stent lumen and aneurysm occlusion was hindered by coil-related metal artifacts in slices containing coils. When coils were absent near the FD, intra-aneurysmal flow was detectable; however, residual aneurysms tended to be underestimated. Despite these limitations, PCD-CT appears to be a valuable modality for postoperative evaluation following standalone FD placement.

A novel dual hysteresis controller for the management of a three phase dynamic voltage restorer
Authors: Vimala V, Ponmani C, Saravanan I
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this work a novel control scheme for the three-phase dynamic voltage restorer has been proposed and validated. The proposed control strategy guarantees the source current waveform to be sinusoidal and at near unity power factor. Further, the voltage sag and swell occurring in the source are overcome and the load gets a smooth power supply at a voltage closer to the rated voltage even in the face of voltage sag or swell occurring in the source. The proposed system uses a power factor corrected front end rectifier that regulates a defined voltage in a DC link. The DVR derives power from the DC link. The PFC rectifier and the DVR converter both are controlled by individual PI and hysteresis current controllers. Further the control system has been adopted in per phase method with actual current estimation system for each phase of the rectifier and the DVR sides done individually. The main advantage of the proposed system is that it does not require the usually required DQ transformation scheme. The proposed idea has been proved to be suitable for balanced and unbalanced loads. The proposed idea has been validated with dynamic simulations carried out in the MATLAB SIMULINK environment and with a scaled down experimental prototype.Keywords: Dynamic voltage restorer, Hysteresis controllers, Power factor corrected rectifier, Distribution static compensator, Hysteresis controller, Parks transformations

Introduction

A dynamic voltage restorer (DVR) is a custom power device that is used to ensure that the load gets the required operating voltage regulated at the desired level in the face disturbances like voltage sag or swell or interruptions occurring for short durations (see Table 1).

The proposed system uses a three phase power factor corrected (PFC) rectifier on the front end. This PFC rectifier regulates a DC link voltage at the set point and it also ensures unity power factor on the source side. The source current waveform is maintained nearly sinusoidal with allowable Total Harmonic Distortion (THD). In addition, there is a seconds three phase converter and it acts as an inverter. This inverter inserts the required voltage in series with the line, with appropriate polarity, such that the load side terminal voltage is regulated at the desired value. The compensating inverter, which is the prime component of the DVR draws the real power from the DC link between the two three phase converter units used respectively as a rectifier and an inverter. The DVR consists of the a three phase inverter, the insertion transformer and the associated control systems.

The DVR is one of the popular custom power devices and hence it has been one among the few systems which have received attention from many researchers in the past few decades. Several researchers have contributed a number of schemes of topologies, control schemes, control algorithms and each one of them have overcome some existing issue and has come out with some novel feature. Some of the milestone contributions have been considered here so as to get an insight into the state of the art and to identify the research gap. A short review of the available literature is included herein.

An effective compensation scheme, for addressing the voltage sag arising in distributed systems, using a DVR and DSTATCOM combination has been proposed and validated by the authors in Ref. [1]. In a similar development a DVR has been proposed and validated by the authors in Ref. [2]. The applicability of Selective Harmonic Elimination in a DVR has been investigated by the authors in Ref. [3]. Further, a control cum testing scheme has been proposed and validated for the management of a DVR in Ref. [4].

Converters featuring Power Factor Correction (PFC) have been developed using embedded systems and many researchers have come out with digitally control systems primarily for the management of DC to DC converters as well as battery chargers. In a typical development the authors in Ref. [5] have developed a PFC scheme that uses the special features of a DSP. In another development a PFC buck boost converter has been designed and developed with a low stress topology featuring PFC front end. A PFC front end AC to DC power conversion system featuring an interleaved conversion scheme with a voltage doubling mechanism has been proposed and validated by the authors in Ref. [6]. A consideration of the available literature on PFC controllers was necessary for this research, because in this work a PFC front end has been used for regulating the DC link voltage used by the DVR section.

Several advanced controllers have also been proposed and validated in the past by many researchers. Some of the advanced controllers offer adaptive control schemes. A robust control mechanism for the management of the medium voltage DVR had been proposed and validated by the authors in Ref. [7]. Yet in another development a high performance control scheme using the PARK transformed stationary reference frame had been demonstrated for the management of DVR [8]. A detailed investigation of the transient performance of different DVR controllers for medium voltage applications have been studied and the methodologies for improvement have been suggested [9]. The authors in Ref. [10] had proposed and validated a DVR with the intention of improving power quality in medium voltage medium power systems. A detailed theoretical investigation and analysis on different control strategies and particularly an optimized control scheme for the management DVRs have been presented by the authors in Ref. [11]. Later in year 2008, the authors in Ref. [12] have developed a high performance bridgeless PFC rectifier with boost feature. In another development a novel Pseudo Continuous Conduction Mode (PCCM) boost converter with a PFC front end featuring fast dynamic performance has been presented by the authors in Ref. [13]. A consolidated review of the different PFC converter topologies has been presented by the authors in Ref. [14]. This work can be treated as a starting point for making studies in the area of PFC converters. The authors in Ref. [15] have presented an article namely ‘The essence of three-phase PFC rectifier systems” and this article discusses at length all the basic and advanced features of a three phase PFC system. Similarly, a comparative analysis on the performance of high density PFC converter topologies has been carried out by the authors in Ref. [16]. A DVR system has been proposed for inclusion in a distributed system and the idea has been validated by the authors in Ref. [17]. Since the PFC plays an important role in the rectification of the available AC source for building the DC link voltage a number of topologies and control schemes have been proposed and validated by many authors [18]. A discussion on the basic aspects of the three phase PFC rectifiers and buck boost converters have been presented in Ref. [[19], [20]] and these articles may be considered as a reference for getting a clear insight in the filed of PRC rectifiers. A super capacitor supported DVR for application in a DFIG system has been proposed and validated by the authors in Ref. [21]. Multilevel inverters have also been used in building DVRs. Typically, in Ref. [22] the authors have promoted a half cascaded multilevel inverter for application in a DVR. A low voltage and high voltage ride through mechanism had been developed for the DFIG using a DVR with a multilevel inverter by the authors in Ref. [23].

A consideration of the available literature reveals that the control scheme for the DVR can be built fully on the PFC concept rather than using the conventional park transformation and the DQ values.

The proposed DVR control scheme uses a front end PFC rectifier that provides a DC link from where the DVR converter draws its shortage power requirements during the voltage sag conditions. The front end three phase rectifier and the DVR converter both work on the principles of hysteresis controllers so that the source current is always sinusoidal and at unity power factor. The voltage sag and swell conditions are addressed effectively rendering an undisturbed voltage across the load even in the face of acute voltage sag or swell occurring in the source voltage. The main advantages of the proposed scheme are the elimination of a DQ transformation. The control system uses individual current reference for each phase and monitors the actual load current in each phase to be compared with each other in the hysteresis controller and hence the proposed system has performed satisfactorily even with unbalanced load condition.

Outline of the proposed system

Structure of the proposed system

The proposed system consists of a three phase R load connected to the grid. It is expected that even in the face of sudden voltage swell or sag the load should get a regulated voltage. The regulation of the load side voltage is achieved with the help of the Dynamic Voltage Restorer (DVR). The DVR inserts a series voltage either in an additive manner or in a subtractive manner such that the load side voltage is maintained at the desired level even if the source voltage is disturbed and it exhibits a voltage sag or a swell. The desired voltage is inserted in series with the supply voltage by the secondary side of a three phase transformer which is known as the insertion transformer. The primary side of the inserting transformer is fed from a three phase half bridge converter and its DC side is energized by another converter that acts as a PFC rectifier and draws power from the same source that feeds the three phase load under consideration.

The control constrains for the first converter that acts as a PFC rectifier is that it should regulate the DC link voltage at the desired level of 900 V and that the source current of this converter is sinusoidal and at unity power factor. The control constraints of the second converter are that the load side AC voltage should be maintained at the required level and that the current drawn from the source is sinusoidal and at unity power factor.

The source current of the rectifier and the source current that drives the load should both be sinusoidal and is at unity power factor so that the total current drawn from the source is sinusoidal and is at unity power factor. The principle of operation of the proposed system

With reference to Fig. 1 the source voltage may be subjected to a steep voltage sag or a sudden voltage swell. The front end PFC rectifier charges the DC link capacitor, regulates the DC link voltage at the desired 900 V level and thus stores sufficient energy in the DC link capacitor. The DC link capacitor voltage is continuously topped up by the PFC converter. When there is a sag in the source voltage the DC link capacitor discharges through the DVR inverter and the required voltage to overcome the short fall in the source voltage is inserted in the feeder in additive with the source voltage so that the load gets the required voltage, even while the source voltage is less than what is required by the load.

In a similar manner, but in the opposite polarity, if the source voltage is more than the voltage required by the load the voltage across the secondary winding is inserted in series with the load in subtractive manner. The change in the direction of current through the secondary winding gets reflected in the primary winding of the DVR transformer and the DC link capacitor gets charged. During this period the charging current drawn from the PFC front end rectifier gets reduced accordingly. If Vin is the source voltage, Vout is the load voltage and Vdvr is the voltage that is inserted by the DVR transformer secondary. If Vin > Vout then Vdvr is negative and the DC link capacitor gets charged. If Vin < Vout then Vdvr is positive and the DC link capacitor gets discharged. The PFC rectifier tops up the DC link capacitor whenever it gets discharged and when it is charged DVR converter, because of a swell in the source voltage then the PFC rectifier component of charging the DC link capacitor gets reduced.

The flow chart of the control scheme is shown in Fig. 2(b). The control system for the PFC rectifier and the DVR are shown in Fig. 3. The vector diagram depicts that when the source voltage is higher than that is required by the load, the DVR voltage is subtractive. A change in the direction of the insertion voltage appearing across the DVR occurs whenever the change of the source voltage from a sag condition to the swell condition occurs. The direction of the DVR current which is also the load current is not changed. During the source voltage swell condition the DC link capacitor gets charged using the DVR converter and the PFC charger current gets reduced accordingly. When the source voltage is less than what is required by the load the DVR supplies a voltage that is additive in nature and the DC link capacitor gets discharged and the PFC rectifier continuously tops up the DC link capacitor.

The cocept of boost rectifier and buck inverter

The proposed system essentially comprises of a boost rectifier followed by a buck inverter. The boost rectifier is connected across the three phase source through the source side filters. The boost rectifier rectifies the three phase AC source and delivers a DC voltage across the DC link with a voltage gain 1/(1-D). This is similar to that of the generic boost converter but the input is AC voltage. The AC input voltage is instantaneously boost converted in a discrete manner and also gets rectified. This feature enables to get a DC link voltage of 900 V from a three phase source of just 380 V. The average voltage gain is 2.368 and the average duty cycle is 0.577. The inverter exhibits a buck operation. The generic buck equation of Vout = D ∗ Vin is applicable for the instantaneous output voltage of the buck inverter. In a 380 V AC feeder if a voltage sag of 50 V occurs then the DVR has to insert this 150 V. The DVR has to produce an AC voltage of 150 V from a DC link voltage of 900 V. The DVR inverter is essentially a buck converter. Shile the DVR converter steps down the input DC link voltage to as low as 150 V it changes the polarity of the output put in synchronization with the main AC feeder. When the voltage required to be inserted is 150 V the DVR needs to operate with a duty cycle of 0.167.

The state space analysis of a boost PFC rectifier

In the operation of a three phase PWM rectifier there are six possible sectors and each sector is active for 60° thus accounting for 360 degrees of an AC cycle. For each sector of operation, two of the three switches from the upper arm or from the lower arm depending upon the polarity of the input voltages are active. In this analysis the sector in which the switches 4, 6 are active is considered. When these two switches are active, they conduct while turned On and the internal diode of the switch 6 provides the path for completion of circuit. When the switches 4 and 6 are turned off the path for current flow is completed through the internal diodes of switches 1, 5 and 6. Thus there for two switching modes. Switching mode 1 is active for a period d and switching mode 2 is active for period (1-d). The proposed system of DVR controlled by a PI and hysteresis controller has been tested in simulations as well as in a scaled down prototype. The bottlenecks of the experimental validation are as compared to the simulations are that the components used in the simulations are ideal. However, the experimental verification uses practical components which have parasitic elements like the internal resistance of the inductors, equivalent series resistance of the capacitors and the On time voltage drop and off time leakage current in respect of MOSFETs. These factors contribute for the difference and degradation of the performance indices as comparable from Table 3, Table 4

A comparison of some of the features of a set of recent developments [[21], [22], [23]] reveals that the proposed system is simple and straight forward with minimum number of legs, switches as well as reduced mathematical overheads. The proposed scheme avoids multilevel inverter and modulation with so many carrier signals while delivering a maximum THD of 3.2.in simulations and 4.2 in experimental verification which are well below the acceptable limit.

Conclusion

In this work the idea of a a novel dynamic voltage restorer that uses a set of two three phase converters and associated PI and hysteresis controllers has been validated using simulations and experimental verifications. It has been shown the requirement of the Park transformation may be alleviated by adopting the proposed control strategy.

The details of the simulations have been presented with results confirming the efficacy of the proposed method for balanced and unbalanced loads. The proposed idea has been tested for voltage sag and swell conditions and the performance of the system is effective. The results obtained from the experimental verifications also confirm the validity of the proposed idea. While the proposed control scheme for the DVR addresses the voltage sag and swell events by drawing power for the DC link from the AC mains source, the source side power factor has also been maintained close to unity.

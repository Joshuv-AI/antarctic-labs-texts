Combined Biologics and Exclusive Enteral Nutrition Promote Early Transmural Healing and Improve Long‐Term Outcomes in Crohn’s Disease: A Multicenter Retrospective Study
Authors: Haixia Ren, Jing An, Wei Chen, Jiao Li, Xing Li, Anning Yin, Jing Wang, Wei Wang, Jingyun Cheng, Jian Kang, Yaqing Xu, Yueyue Lu, Juan Su, Qian Zhou, Huipeng Wan, Zimeng Zhang, Ping An
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTransmural healing (TH), a comprehensive therapeutic target in Crohn’s disease (CD), is associated with reduced long‐term complications. However, evidence on the role of exclusive enteral nutrition (EEN) in TH remains limited. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of biologics combined with 16‐week EEN in achieving early TH and improving clinical outcomes. This real‐world, multicenter retrospective study analyzed medical records of patients with moderate‐to‐severe CD from 2016 to 2024. Patients received either biologics with concomitant 16‐week EEN (BioEEN) or biologics alone (Bio). Clinical and endoscopic outcomes, including transmural healing, were assessed at week 16, year 1, and year 2. At baseline, demographic and clinical characteristics were comparable between the two groups. The BioEEN group demonstrated superior clinical response rates compared to the Bio group (95.1% vs. 70.6% at week 16; P < 0.001), with sustained benefits at week 52 (87.8% vs. 59.5%; P < 0.001). Subgroup analysis revealed higher endoscopic response, mucosal healing, and TH rates in both colorectal and ileal segments at weeks 16 and 52 in the BioEEN group. Additionally, the BioEEN group had significantly lower rates of surgery, escalated treatment, bowel damage progression, and disease relapse at 1 and 2 years. Regression analysis identified the 16‐week EEN as the sole protective factor for early transmural healing and improved long‐term outcomes. Combined treatment of biologics and 16‐week EEN promotes early transmural healing, thereby enhancing long‐term clinical outcomes in CD patients.

Study Highlights

WHAT IS THE CURRENT KNOWLEDGE ON THE TOPIC?

WHAT IS THE CURRENT KNOWLEDGE ON THE TOPIC?

Transmural healing is a key treat‐to‐target endpoint in Crohn’s disease (CD). Yet, even in the current era of multiple biological agents, achieving transmural healing within a short timeframe remains extremely difficult. Published data on exclusive or supplemental enteral nutrition (EN) for adult CD are scarce. Earlier studies have shown that BioEEN can be effective for both induction and maintenance of remission. However, the value of BioEEN in promoting early transmural healing, as well as the long‐term prognostic impact of achieving such early transmural healing, has not yet been investigated.

HOW MIGHT THIS CHANGE CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY OR TRANSLATIONAL SCIENCE?

HOW MIGHT THIS CHANGE CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY OR TRANSLATIONAL SCIENCE?

For patients with moderate‐to‐severe active Crohn’s disease, we recommend initiating combination therapy with biologics and exclusive enteral nutrition as early as possible to achieve prompt transmural healing and thereby improve long‐term prognosis.

Crohn’s disease (CD), a chronic, relapsing–remitting inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), is characterized by transmural inflammation that may affect any segment of the gastrointestinal tract. 1 This pathological hallmark predisposes patients to progressive bowel damage, including strictures, fistulas, and abscesses, often culminating in surgical intervention. Despite advancements in biologic therapies—such as anti‐tumor necrosis factor (anti‐TNF) agents, integrin inhibitors, and interleukin antagonists—achieving sustained remission remains elusive for a substantial proportion of patients. Primary non‐response and secondary loss of response to biologics occur in 30–60% of cases, with higher rates observed in patients with ileal‐predominant disease or penetrating phenotypes. These therapeutic challenges underscore the urgent need for adjunctive strategies to enhance biologic efficacy, mitigate disease progression, and redefine treatment goals beyond symptomatic control. 2

The STRIDE‐II consensus advocated endoscopic healing as a pivotal therapeutic target, correlating with reduced hospitalization and surgery rates. 3 However, emerging evidence suggested that transmural healing (TH)—defined as the resolution of inflammation across all intestinal wall layers, confirmed by cross‐sectional imaging—may represent a more comprehensive marker of disease control. Unlike mucosal healing, TH reflects the normalization of deeper tissue architecture, potentially offering greater protection against fibrotic remodeling and structural complications. Despite its clinical relevance, TH remains an understudied endpoint, partly due to the historical reliance on endoscopy for disease monitoring and the limited accessibility of advanced imaging modalities. Furthermore, current biologic regimens, while effective in inducing mucosal repair, often fail to achieve TH, particularly in patients with transmural fibrosis or small intestinal involvement.

Exclusive enteral nutrition (EEN), a well‐established induction therapy for pediatric CD, has demonstrated remarkable efficacy in achieving clinical remission (73–80%) and mucosal healing, outperforming corticosteroids in both safety and efficacy. 4 , 5 , 6 EEN’s mechanisms of action extend beyond nutritional repletion, encompassing modulation of gut microbiota, reduction of luminal antigen exposure, and suppression of pro‐inflammatory cytokines. 7 , 8 In adults, however, EEN has been historically underutilized, relegated to niche applications such as preoperative optimization or bridging therapy. 9 , 10 Recent studies challenge this paradigm, revealing that extended EEN regimens (12–16 weeks) not only induce clinical remission but also enhance mucosal healing, particularly in the terminal ileum—a region notoriously resistant to biologic penetration. Our prior work further demonstrated that a 16‐week EEN protocol synergized with biologics (BioEEN) to amplify mucosal healing rates, suggesting a potential role for EEN in promoting deeper, transmural repair. 10

Nevertheless, critical knowledge gaps persist. First, the impact of BioEEN on early TH—a potential predictor of long‐term outcomes—has not been systematically evaluated. Second, it remains unclear whether TH, once achieved, translates into durable protection against disease relapse, bowel damage progression, or surgery. Third, the optimal duration of EEN adjunctive therapy and its applicability across diverse disease phenotypes (e.g., stricturing vs. penetrating) remain undefined. Addressing these gaps is imperative, as CD’s heterogeneous pathophysiology demands tailored therapeutic approaches that address both luminal and transmural inflammation.

This multicenter retrospective study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of a 16‐week EEN protocol combined with biologics in achieving early TH and improving long‐term clinical outcomes in moderate‐to‐severe CD. We hypothesized that BioEEN would outperform biologic monotherapy by simultaneously targeting mucosal and transmural inflammation, thereby altering the disease’s natural history. Secondary objectives included identifying predictors of TH and quantifying the association between TH and long‐term endpoints such as surgery, treatment escalation, and disease relapse. By integrating real‐world data from tertiary IBD centers, this study sought to bridge the translational gap between mechanistic insights and clinical practice, offering a roadmap for optimizing CD management in the era of precision medicine.

METHODS

This was a real‐world, multicenter study from October 2016 to November 2024. Medical records of patients were collected at three tertiary IBD centers in Hubei, China: Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University, First Hospital of Jinzhou City, and Xiangyang Central People’s Hospital. In addition, the IBD database was retrospectively reviewed for those patients who received biologics alone and those who received biologics in combination with EEN for 16 weeks. Clinical, endoscopic by double‐balloon endoscopy (DBE), colonoscopy, and MRE data were extracted. Crohn’s disease activity index (CDAI) was recorded at each clinic visit.

Patients

Patients were retrospectively screened for this study. Patients with mild disease (CDAI ≥220), EEN duration <16 weeks, or with a confirmed diagnosis of UC, pouch, ostomy, or prior extensive bowel resection were excluded from the study. The study flowchart was detailed in Figure S1 . In this study, 242 patients treated with biologics including anti‐TNF agents (infliximab and adalimumab), vedolizumab, and ustekinumab were included. In all patients, 123 patients received a concomitant treatment of a 16‐week EEN (BioEEN group) since biologics introduction, while the other 119 patients received biologics alone (Bio group). Biologics administration for all patients followed the same approved standard protocols [vedolizumab (300 mg) and infliximab (5 mg/kg) intravenous infusions on day 1 and at week 2, 6, and 14, followed by maintenance doses every 8 weeks; ustekinumab treatment with standard doses by a weight‐based intravenous induction dose (≤55 kg: 260 mg; 55–85 kg: 390 mg; >85 kg: 520 mg) followed by a fixed subcutaneous maintenance dose of 90 mg q8w; adalimumab was administered subcutaneously at standard dose as 160 mg at 0 week and 80 mg at 2 weeks followed by 40 mg every 2 weeks].

BioEEN patients received a fiber‐free, polymeric formula [Nutrison (1 kcal/ml), NUTRICIA, Netherlands, or Ensure (1.06 kcal/ml), ABBOTT, Netherlands] orally with a caloric requirement of 120% of daily needs (based on ideal body weight). The full volume of EEN feeds was reached and tolerated in 48–72 hours. Volumes could be increased if patients were hungry, and the rates could be decreased if they felt discomfort or distension. Oral intake was restricted to clear fluids during EEN. Post‐intervention dietary transition followed structured education from IBD specialists.

MRE examinations and image analysis

After bowel cleansing, 1.6–2 L of 2.5% mannitol solution was administered 1 h before the procedure; 10 mg of raceanisodamine hydrochloride (raceanisodamine; Minsheng Pharmaceutical Group Co., Ltd., Hangzhou, China) was intramuscularly injected into the buttocks 10 min before MRE.

All patients underwent MRE using a 3.0‐T Magnetom Trio system (Siemens Medical Solutions, Erlangen, Germany). T2‐weighted imaging (repetition time/echo time (TR/TE): 1200/87 ms, matrix: 320 × 194, slice thickness: 4 mm, flip angle: 160°) and diffusion‐weighted imaging (DWI) with three b values of 50, 400, and 800 s/mm2 (TR/TE: 5000–5,900/73–83 ms, matrix: 192 × 115, slice thickness: 4–5 mm) were routinely performed. T1‐weighted images (TR/TE: 4.37/1.37 ms, matrix: 320 × 217, slice thickness: 2 mm, flip angle: 13°) were acquired before and at 15 s, 70 s, and 7 min after the intravenous injection of 0.2 mL/kg gadopentetate dimeglumine at a rate of 2 mL/s. 11 , 12

Image analysis

Two experienced radiologists (Jingyun Cheng and Baojun Xie), who were blinded to the clinical data, analyzed the images using a postprocessing workstation. There was no disagreement between the 2 radiologists. To obtain reliable data, the T1 and T2 signal intensities were measured 3 times, and the average was taken to reduce occasional errors. For patient‐based analysis, the intestinal segment with the thickest bowel wall at baseline was selected as the representative segment for follow‐up. If one patient showed multiple affected segments with the highest BWT, we selected the intestinal segment with the highest Magnetic Resonance Index of Activity score (MaRIA, MaRIA = 1.5 × BWT (mm) + 0.02 × RCE + 5 × edema + 10 × ulceration) at baseline as the representative segment. Patients included in this part of the study must be those who had a baseline MaRIA score of ≥7 in at least one bowel segment and had follow‐up MRE assessments: the week 16 population (MREw16) comprises patients with MRE assessments at baseline and week 16; the week 52 population (MREw52) comprises patients with MRE assessments at baseline and week 52. The segment‐level analyses included all bowel segments with a baseline MaRIA score of ≥7 in patients who completed 16 or 52 weeks of treatment and had at least one evaluable segment at the relevant time points. 13 TH was defined as BWT≤3 mm without any signs of inflammation (i.e., ulceration, edema, diffusion‐weighted hyperintensity, and increased contrast enhancement) 14 , 15 ; MRE response was defined as not achieving transmural healing, but a decrease in MaRIA score compared to baseline.

Clinical outcomes

Disease activity was evaluated by CDAI at baseline, week 16, and week 52. Clinical response was defined as a reduction of ≥70 points from baseline CDAI scores. Clinical remission was characterized by achieving an absolute CDAI score < 150 post‐intervention. Biologics failure was defined as: (1) discontinuation due to secondary loss of response (initial clinical response followed by subsequent loss of efficacy during maintenance therapy as determined by physician evaluation) or (2) drug intolerance requiring cessation; and/or (3) requirement for CD‐related surgical intervention. Surgical intervention was defined as intestinal resection specifically related to CD complications, excluding procedures for perianal lesion drainage. Bowel damage progression included bowel resection, development of new stricture or fistula, or progression of pre‐existing strictures (evidenced by increased pre‐stenotic dilation on imaging studies or onset of significant obstructive symptoms) or worsening of existing fistulae (manifested by new abscess formation or emergence of additional fistulous tracts).

Endoscopic evaluation

Colonoscopy and DBE were employed to assess mucosal lesions and mucosal healing. Endoscopic evaluations were conducted by two board‐certified IBD endoscopists using the Simple Endoscopic Score for Crohn’s Disease (SES‐CD for colorectal lesions) and modified SES‐CD (mSES‐CD for small intestine lesions) based on ulcer size (maximum diameter), ulcerated surface area (% of segment involvement), affected mucosal surface area (% of segment involvement) and presence of luminal narrowing. In this study, ileal scores were analyzed separately, and colorectal composite scores were calculated as the sum of right colon, transverse colon, left colon, and rectal segment scores. Endoscopic response was defined as ≥50% reduction in baseline SES‐CD score or mSES‐CD score. Mucosal healing was defined as complete absence of macroscopic ulcerations in all examined segments.

Safety assessments

Safety data were collected retrospectively from the medical records and database. We also collected safety outcomes including patient‐reported adverse events, serious adverse events requiring hospitalization or treatment discontinuation, and CD‐related surgery.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics for demographic and clinical characteristics include mean (standard deviation, SD) for normal distribution continuous variables or median (interquartile range [IQR]) for non‐normal distribution continuous variables and frequency distributions for categorical data. The Kaplan–Meier method was used to assess cumulative treatment failure‐free persistence every 8 weeks after treatment was begun and at the end of follow‐up. Continuous variables were compared using paired t tests or Wilcoxon matched pairs signed rank tests for parametric and nonparametric data, respectively. P < 0.05 was considered to be significant. In multiple testing, an adjusted P value after Bonferroni correction (P < 0.05/N, N = the number of testing methods) was considered statistically significant. Logistic regression was used for univariate and multivariate analyses with stepwise selection to predict TH at week 52 and to predict the factors associated with surgery and escalate therapy, bowel damage progression, and clinical relapse after 2 years of follow‐up. Results are expressed as odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI). A two‐sided P < 0.05 was considered significant.

Ethics statement

This study was approved by the ethics committee of Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University (Wuhan, China; #WDRY2021‐Y090).

RESULTS

Baseline patient characteristics

Between 2016 and 2024, a cohort of 242 moderate‐to‐severe Crohn’s disease (CD) patients undergoing biologic therapy was retrospectively analyzed, comprising 123 patients in the BioEEN group and 119 in the Bio group. Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics demonstrated comparable distributions between groups, with no statistically significant differences observed in age, sex distribution, mean disease duration, surgical history, extraintestinal manifestations, disease behavior, anatomical involvement, or perianal disease status (Table S1 ). Both cohorts exhibited predominantly complicated disease phenotypes, with approximately 70% demonstrating either stricturing (B2) or penetrating (B3) disease behavior according to the Montreal classification. Disease activity stratification revealed severe clinical presentation in half of the study population (50%); while historical treatment exposure included prior anti‐TNF therapy in 25% of patients and CD‐related surgical interventions in 20%. Therapeutic agent distribution showed no significant intergroup variation (P > 0.05 for all comparisons): Infliximab utilization was documented in 26.8% of BioEEN patients vs. 20.2% in Bio group counterparts, with comparable proportions for adalimumab (2.4% vs. 3.4%), vedolizumab (14.6% vs. 18.5%), and ustekinumab (56.1% vs. 58.0%) administrations. This therapeutic parity extended to dosing regimens and treatment escalation protocols between the two cohorts.

Clinical outcomes

By Week 16, the BioEEN cohort demonstrated superior clinical outcomes with significantly higher rates of clinical response (95.1% vs. 70.6%; P < 0.001) and clinical remission (87.0% vs. 52.1%; P < 0.001) compared to the Bio group. These therapeutic advantages persisted through the 52‐week evaluation, where the BioEEN group maintained markedly better clinical response (87.8% vs. 59.5%; P < 0.001) and remission rates (81.1% vs. 43.0%; P < 0.001), despite attrition reducing evaluable patients to 90 and 79 in the respective groups (Figure 1 ).

Clinical response and clinical remission at week 16 and 52 for patients in Bio and BioEEN cohorts. (a) Patients achieved clinical response at week 16 and week 52. (b) Patients achieved clinical remission at week 16 and week 52.

Longitudinal analysis revealed sustained clinical benefits in the BioEEN cohort at annual assessments. At the 1‐year follow‐up, significant between‐group differences emerged in key disease progression parameters: surgical intervention requirement (0.8% vs. 5.9%; P = 0.027), treatment escalation (4.1% vs. 17.6%; P = 0.001), bowel damage progression (1.6% vs. 19.3%; P < 0.001), and disease relapse (2.4% vs. 26.1%; P < 0.001). These differential outcomes became more pronounced at the 2‐year evaluation, with the BioEEN group maintaining lower rates of surgical necessity (2.4% vs. 14.3%; P = 0.001), therapeutic intensification (10.6% vs. 36.1%; P < 0.001), intestinal damage advancement (9.8% vs. 35.3%; P < 0.001), and clinical recurrence (14.6% vs. 47.1%; P < 0.001) (Figure 2 ).

Kaplan–Meier survival analysis confirmed the durability of combination therapy benefits, demonstrating significantly reduced cumulative incidence of surgery (HR = 0.21, P = 0.001); treatment escalation (HR = 0.29, P < 0.001); structural bowel damage (HR = 0.28, P < 0.001); disease relapse (HR = 0.31, P < 0.001) over the 24‐month observation period in the BioEEN cohort (Figure 2 ).

Endoscopic outcomes

BioEEN cohort demonstrated superior endoscopic improvement across anatomical sites compared to Bio group. At week 16, endoscopic response rates for colorectal lesions were markedly higher in BioEEN group (96.7% [58/60] vs. 64.2% [43/67]; P < 0.001), with sustained superiority at week 52 (74.2% [46/62] vs. 52.8% [19/36]; P = 0.031). Similarly, ileal endoscopic responses favored BioEEN therapy at both week 16 (93.3% [70/75] vs. 57.9% [44/76]; P < 0.001) and week 52 (71.4% [45/63] vs. 38.8% [26/67]; P < 0.001) (Figure 3 ).

Endoscopic response and mucosal healing at week 16 and 52 for patients in Bio and BioEEN cohorts. (a) Endoscopic response of the colorectum. (b) Mucosal healing of the colorectum. (c) Endoscopic response of the ileum. (d) Mucosal healing of the ileum.

Mucosal healing rates further highlighted the therapeutic advantage of combination therapy. For colorectal lesions, BioEEN achieved significantly higher healing rates than monotherapy at week 16 (88.3% [53/60] vs. 26.7% [12/67]; P < 0.001) and week 52 (61.3% [38/62] vs. 27.8% [10/36]; P = 0.001). This differential was even more pronounced in small intestine assessments, where BioEEN yielded substantially greater healing proportions at week 16 (81.3% [61/75] vs. 25.0% [19/76]; P < 0.001) and week 52 (57.1% [36/63] vs. 19.4% [13/67]; P < 0.001) (Figure 3 ).

Transmural healing

In 16‐week MRE evaluation of colorectal involvement, the BioEEN cohort demonstrated superior transmural healing metrics compared to the Bio group. MRE response rates significantly favored BioEEN therapy at week 16 (80.4% [41/51] vs. 37.0% [14/40]; P < 0.001) and week 52 (70.2% [33/47] vs. 26.3% [15/52]; P < 0.001). Transmural healing rates followed a similar pattern, with BioEEN achieving 2.35‐fold higher rates than monotherapy at week 16 (70.6% vs. 30.0%; P < 0.001) and 2.55‐fold higher at week 52 (63.8% vs. 25.0%; P < 0.001). For ileal involvement, evaluable cohorts comprised BioEEN and Bio at week 16 and 52. The BioEEN group exhibited markedly greater MRE response rates at both timepoints: week 16 (75.8% [47/62] vs. 45.0% [15/40]; P < 0.001) and week 52 (61.0% [47/77] vs. 28.8% [10/38]; P < 0.001). Transmural healing disparities were particularly striking, with BioEEN demonstrating 2.52‐fold (Week 16: 69.4% vs. 27.5%; P < 0.001) and 2.30‐fold (Week 52: 54.5% vs. 23.7%; P = 0.002) superiority over monotherapy (Figure 4 ).

Factors associated with transmural healing

Colorectum transmural healing

In a targeted evaluation of CD patients with colorectal involvement (Montreal L2/L3), multivariable logistic regression identified key predictors of 52‐week transmural healing (TH). Initial univariate analysis demonstrated significant associations with B1 phenotype (OR = 4.1, 95% CI 1.7–9.9; P = 0.002), mild disease activity (OR = 0.2, 95% CI 0.1–0.6; P = 0.001), anti‐TNF agents (OR = 4.8, 95% CI 1.7–13.5; P = 0.003), ustekinumab (OR = 5.7, 95% CI 2.3–14.0; P < 0.001) and EEN protocol (16‐week duration: OR = 11.8, 95% CI 4.3–32.9; P < 0.001). When incorporating these covariates into a stepwise regression model, only sustained EEN intervention maintained independent predictive value for TH (adjusted OR = 0.1, 95% CI 0.1–0.2; P < 0.001), effectively reducing non‐healing risk by 90% compared to biologic monotherapy (Table 1 ).

Illum transmural healing

In patients with ileal involvement (Montreal L1/L3), univariate logistic regression identified divergent predictors of 52‐week transmural healing (TH) by positive associations with B1 phenotype (OR = 5.0, 95% CI 2.1–11.9; P < 0.001), mild disease activity (OR = 4.1, 95% CI 1.8–9.3; P = 0.001), anti‐TNF agents (OR = 2.8, 95% CI 1.1–7.2; P = 0.029), ustekinumab (OR = 6.5, 95% CI 2.7–15.8; P < 0.001), and EEN protocol (16‐week: OR = 25.0, 95% CI 8.8–70.9; P < 0.001). Lesions primarily involve the terminal ileum and the colon (OR = 4.4, 95% CI, 1.9–0.1, P = 0.001), exposed to two biologics prior to baseline (OR = 0.1, 95% CI, 0–0.9, P = 0.043) were independently associated with a lower likelihood of patient TH. The final parsimonious model retained only EEN duration as an independent TH predictor (adjusted OR = 0.1, 95% CI 0.1–0.2; P < 0.001), translating to a 90% reduction in healing failure risk (Table 1 ).

Factors associated with long‐term outcome

Surgery and escalate therapy

In the 24‐month surgical risk analysis (n = 242), multivariable modeling identified distinct risk amplifying predictors including upper gastrointestinal lesions (adjusted OR = 3.0, 95% CI 1.4–6.3; P = 0.008) and complex phenotypes (B2/B3) disease (OR = 0.2, 95% CI 0.1–0.4; P < 0.001) while 16‐week EEN therapy reduced surgical risk by 80% (OR = 0.2, 95% CI 0.1–0.4; P < 0.001) (Table 2 ). Lesions involving the upper gastrointestinal tract (OR = 0.3, 95% CI, 0.1–0.4, P = 0.028) were a risk factor for escalated therapy, and 16‐week EEN therapy was a protective factor (OR = 0.2, 95% CI 0.1–0.4; P < 0.001) (Table 2 ).

Treatment discontinuation and adverse events

Of the patients who received BioEEN treatment, 87.8% still clinically responded to maintenance therapy at week 52, while only 59.5% of patients were in the Bio group. None of the serious adverse events was observed. GI intolerance was the most common adverse event, with 31 out of 123 patients (25.2%) receiving BioEEN treatment. All these patients presented mild EEN‐related diarrhea but never stopped enteral nutrition until 16 weeks later.

DISCUSSION

The therapeutic landscape of CD has evolved significantly with the advent of biologics, yet persistent challenges remain in achieving durable remission and preventing long‐term complications. 16 , 17 , 18 This multicenter retrospective study introduced a novel therapeutic paradigm by demonstrating that combining biologics with a 16‐week EEN protocol (BioEEN) not only promoted early transmural healing but also substantially improved long‐term clinical outcomes. Our findings underscored the pivotal role of adjunctive EEN in addressing the limitations of biologic monotherapy, particularly in patients with ileal involvement and complex disease phenotypes. This discussion contextualized these results within existing literature, explored potential mechanisms, and highlighted the clinical implications of BioEEN as a transformative strategy in CD management.

The superiority of BioEEN over biologic monotherapy was evident across all measured endpoints. At week 16, the BioEEN cohort achieved significantly higher rates of clinical response (95.1% vs. 70.6%, P < 0.001) and remission (87.0% vs. 52.1%, P < 0.001), with sustained benefits observed at 52 weeks and beyond. These outcomes were paralleled by striking differences in endoscopic and transmural healing. For ileal lesions, BioEEN yielded mucosal healing rates of 81.3% at week 16 compared to 25.0% in the monotherapy group (P < 0.001), while TH rates for colorectal involvement reached 70.6% versus 30.0% (P < 0.001). Critically, these short‐term gains translated into long‐term clinical advantages: at 2 years, BioEEN reduced surgery risk by 83% (2.4% vs. 14.3%, P = 0.001), treatment escalation by 71% (10.6% vs. 36.1%, P < 0.001), and disease relapse by 69% (14.6% vs. 47.1%, P < 0.001). These results positioned BioEEN as a dual‐modality therapy capable of altering the natural history of CD, particularly in high‐risk subgroups historically deemed “therapeutic orphans” due to poor biologic responsiveness.

Despite the efficacy of biologics, primary non‐response and secondary loss of response remained significant barriers, affecting up to 60% of patients with ileal‐predominant disease. 19 , 20 , 21 Our findings aligned with prior studies highlighting the limitations of biologics in penetrating small intestinal lesions, where transmural inflammation drove complications such as strictures and fistulas. The synergistic effect of EEN in BioEEN likely addressed these limitations through multiple mechanisms. First, EEN provided comprehensive nutritional support, reversing catabolic states and restoring mucosal integrity. 22 Second, by modulating gut microbiota composition and reducing luminal antigen exposure, EEN may dampen the inflammatory cascade that perpetuated transmural damage. 23 , 24 Third, the anti‐inflammatory properties of specific EEN formulas (e.g., TGF‐β‐enriched formulations) may complement the immune‐targeting mechanisms of biologics. 25 , 26 This multi‐faceted action explained why BioEEN achieved TH rates 2.5‐fold higher than biologics alone, even in patients with baseline MaRIA scores ≥7—a subgroup traditionally associated with poor outcomes.

The STRIDE‐II consensus emphasized endoscopic healing as a treatment target, but our study reinforced the emerging paradigm that TH represented a more robust indicator of disease control. TH, defined as normalization of bowel wall thickness and resolution of inflammatory markers on MRI, correlated with reduced fibrosis and structural damage. 11 In this study, multivariate regression identified 16‐week EEN as the sole independent predictor of TH (adjusted OR = 0.1, P < 0.001), underscoring its indispensable role in achieving deep remission. Notably, TH rates in the BioEEN group at week 52 (63.8% for colorectal and 54.5% for ileal lesions) exceeded those reported in trials of advanced biologics or dual‐targeted therapies. This suggested that EEN’s ability to heal the intestinal wall across all layers may address the “therapeutic ceiling” observed with biologics alone.

The durability of BioEEN’s benefits was perhaps its most compelling feature. Over 24 months, BioEEN reduced bowel damage progression by 72% (9.8% vs. 35.3%, P < 0.001) and clinical relapse by 69%, outcomes rarely achieved with conventional therapies. These findings challenged the notion that CD inevitably progresses toward complications, offering hope for modifying disease trajectories through early, aggressive intervention. The protective effect of EEN persisted even after adjusting for disease behavior, location, and prior biologic exposure, suggesting its utility across diverse patient profiles.

The mechanisms underlying EEN’s efficacy in CD remained incompletely understood but likely involved a combination of nutritional repletion, microbiome modulation, and direct anti‐inflammatory effects. 7 , 27 Our study added to this understanding by demonstrating that prolonged EEN (16 weeks) was critical for sustaining TH. Shorter EEN regimens (4–8 weeks), while effective for inducing clinical remission, may fail to achieve deeper healing. The structured dietary transition post‐EEN, guided by IBD specialists, likely contributed to maintaining remission, though this warranted further investigation. Additionally, the safety profile of BioEEN—with no serious adverse events and manageable gastrointestinal intolerance—supported its feasibility in real‐world settings.

This study had limitations inherent to its retrospective design, including potential selection bias and heterogeneity in biologic agents. While we adjusted for confounding variables, unmeasured factors such as dietary adherence or genetic predispositions may influence outcomes. Furthermore, the absence of microbiome or cytokine data limited mechanistic insights. Prospective randomized trials are needed to validate these findings and explore optimal EEN durations and formulations. Long‐term follow‐up beyond 2 years will clarify whether TH translates into sustained protection against disability and surgery.

In conclusion, this study established BioEEN as a transformative strategy for CD, capable of achieving early TH and reshaping long‐term outcomes. By addressing both nutritional deficits and transmural inflammation, BioEEN overcame the limitations of biologic monotherapy, particularly in high‐risk populations. These findings advocated for integrating EEN into standard CD protocols, emphasizing its role not merely as nutritional support but as a cornerstone of disease‐modifying therapy. As we moved toward precision medicine, BioEEN exemplified how combining targeted biologics with holistic nutritional interventions can unlock unprecedented therapeutic potential in chronic inflammatory diseases.

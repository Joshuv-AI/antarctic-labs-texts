Effects of tranexamic acid preconditioning on the incidence of postpartum haemorrhage in vaginal deliveries with identified risk factors in China: a prospective, randomized, open-label, blinded endpoint trial
Authors: Pei Zhang, Yan-Ju Jia, Yan Lv, Yi-Fan Fan, Hao Geng, Ying Zhao, Hui Song, Hong-Yan Cui, Xu Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aimed to evaluate the effects of tranexamic acid (TXA) in preventing postpartum haemorrhage (PPH) among women with identified risk factors for PPH undergoing vaginal delivery in China.MethodsThis prospective, randomized, open-label, blinded endpoint (PROBE) trial enrolled 2258 women with one or more risk factors for PPH who underwent vaginal delivery. Participants were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive an intravascular infusion of 1 g TXA or a placebo immediately after the delivery of the infant. The primary outcome assessed was the incidence of PPH, defined as blood loss ≥500 mL within 24 h after delivery, while severe PPH was considered as a secondary outcome and defined by total blood loss ≥1000 mL within 24 h.Results2245 individuals (99.4%) could be followed up to their primary outcome. PPH occurred in 186 of 1128 women in the TXA group and in 215 of 1117 women in the placebo group (16.5% vs. 19.2%; RR, 0.86; 95% CI, 0.72 to 1.02; p = 0.088). Regarding secondary outcomes related to efficacy, women in the TXA group had a significant lower rate of severe PPH than those in the placebo group (2.7% vs. 5.6%; RR, 0.49; 95% CI, 0.32 to 0.74; p = 0.001; adjusted p = 0.002). Similarly, there was a significant reduction in the use of additional uterotonic agents (7.8% vs. 15.6%; RR, 0.50; 95% CI, 0.39 to 0.63; p < 0.001; adjusted p = 0.001). No occurrence of thromboembolic events and maternal deaths were reported in both groups within 30 days after delivery.ConclusionsIn total population with risk factors for PPH, the administration of TXA following vaginal delivery did not result in a statistically significant reduction in the incidence of PPH compared to placebo; however, it was associated with a significantly lower incidence of severe PPH.

Introduction

Postpartum haemorrhage (PPH) is still the predominant factor contributing to maternal deaths worldwide [1, 2]. The majority of PPH-related deaths occur in developing countries, and nearly all cases of PPH are preventable [3, 4]. With the development of intraoperative haemostasis techniques, timely and effective interventions for PPH are feasible through surgical approaches during cesarean section. However, the prevention and management of PPH during vaginal delivery pose greater difficulties due to various factors, including advanced maternal age, multiple pregnancies, maternal complications and delayed surgical intervention [5–7]. Although the prophylactic use of uterotonic agents during the third stage of labour significantly reduces the incidence of PPH, as recommended by international and national guidelines, evidence from certain countries indicates an increasing incidence PPH accompanied by heightened demands for blood transfusion and rates of hysterectomy [7, 8].

Tranexamic acid (TXA), primarily indicated for hyperfibrinolysis-induced bleedings, has been widely adopted in various medical specialties including trauma, orthopaedics, urology, neurosurgery, and others [9–12]. The World Maternal Antifibrinolytic Trial (WOMAN), which encompassed a cohort of 20,060 women experiencing PPH following either vaginal or cesarean delivery, indicated that timely administration of TXA within first 3 h after delivery exhibited a significant reduction in haemorrhage-related mortality (RR, 0.69; 95% CI, 0.52 to 0.91) [2, 13–17].

However, the published clinical trial results regarding the prophylactic use of TXA for preventing PPH are inconsistent. Several meta-analyses showed that prophylactic intravenous administration of 1 g TXA in women undergoing vaginal delivery could reduce the incidence rate of blood loss exceeding 500 ml within 2 h after delivery [18, 19]. However, the administration of TXA prophylactically did not show a significant reduction in the incidence of PPH for women undergoing either cesarean or vaginal delivery, as revealed by three large-scale, multicentre trials [20–22]. Yair Binyamin et al. reported that prophylactic administration of TXA significantly mitigated the decrease in haemoglobin levels by more than 10% within 24 h after cesarean sections among women at high risk of bleeding [23]. Furthermore, TXA treatment was associated with a reduced incidence of haemoglobin decline ≥2 g/dL [23]. Nevertheless, there is currently no research investigating the potential of TXA in reducing postpartum blood loss among high-risk women following vaginal delivery.

Women with risk factors (anaemia, induction of labour etc.) are more susceptible to PPH [24–26]. While numerous national organizations and studies have investigated the influencing factors and risk assessment tools for PPH, limited information exists regarding the distinct relationships between labour inductions, genital laceration, midwifery practices and incidence of PPH in vaginal delivery [25, 27]. By integrating the existing literature with findings from our retrospective cohort study, we identified risk factors associated with PPH in vaginal deliveries (described in inclusion criteria).

Regarding the use of TXA for prophylaxis against PPH, the majority of pertinent studies reviewed above were conducted in western countries. However, the conclusions from trials oriented from these countries might not be directly applicable to individuals in China owing to racial concerns. Besides, evidence remains insufficient to substantiate the effects and safety of prophylactic use of TXA in women with risk factors of PPH undergoing vaginal delivery. Consequently, we conducted a randomized controlled trial in Chinese women with identified risk factors, to investigate whether the administration of 1 g TXA in addition to a prophylactic uterotonic agent would result in a reduced incidence of PPH in vaginal deliveries, compared to the use of a uterotonic agent alone. Besides, by enrolling individuals presenting diverse risk profiles, this study aimed to discern which populations, when combined with specific risk factors, exhibited a more favorable response to TXA.

Patients and methods

Trial design

A parallel-designed, randomized, open-label, placebo-controlled, blinded endpoint (PROBE) trial was conducted in a tertiary hospital responsible for approximately 15,000 neonatal deliveries annually. This trial adopted a rigorous randomized grouping design whereas the treatment was blinded to midwives, nurses and researches who assessed and recorded the outcomes. Women with a known risk profile for PPH, who underwent vaginal delivery, was randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either 1 g TXA or a placebo, for the purpose of prophylaxis. The study was conducted from September 1, 2022 to August 30, 2023. The detailed protocol was provided in the supplementary appendix.

Study participants

The present trial adhered to the CONSORT statement and was prospectively registered on the Chinese Clinical Trial Registration Centre (ChiCTR2200062464). Additionally, ethical approval for this trial was obtained from the ethics committee of Tianjin Central Hospital of Gynecology Obstetrics (2022KY060). After obtaining informed consent, women with one or more identifiable risk factors for PPH, who were suitable for vaginal delivery, were recruited into the study.

The basic principle of recruitment was that the investigators and obstetricians remained uncertain about the efficacy of TXA for women with risk factors for PPH. Exclusion of women with hypertensive disorders was based on the potential risk posed by their abnormal coagulation status, which could compromise the safety and efficacy of TXA administration.

Procedures

This trial was a specialized investigation focused on emergency medical procedures required within minutes following delivery. PPH is an unforeseeable emergency that can arise within minutes following the onset of intrapartum risk factors. In this scenario, immediate randomization and intervention are imperative for newly identified patients exhibiting intrapartum risk factors that meet the inclusion criteria; however, time constraints hinder the provision of adequate informed consent. Consequently, this research has been granted approval by the ethics committee, ensuring that informed consent was obtained from potential participants and their families upon admission prior to labour initiation. This facilitated sufficient time for subjects to understand the relevant information of the trial and potential risks. However, the participants were duly informed that in the event of not meeting the inclusion criteria during a subsequent assessment, they would be excluded from the trial and provided with standard care, irrespective of their prior informed consent.

At the hospital where the study was conducted, all pregnant women who entered the labour room were repeatedly screened for risk factors related to PPH using a risk screening table until the end of delivery. This approach is routine practice as risk factors could potentially emerge at any stage of pregnancy and delivery, where repeated risk screening facilitates timely identification of ‘at-risk groups’ by medical providers and enables implementation of appropriate measures.

A pre-designed paper case report form was utilized to collect necessary patient information. Assessment of bleeding-related risk for subjects started following the occurrence of regular uterine contractions and cervical dilation reaching 2-3 cm. And reassessment of risk factors would occur at any stage of until completion of labour using the risk screening table. Once a women met the predefined inclusion criteria and exhibited known risk factors, she was assigned a randomization code generated by SAS software. The randomization code ensured balanced allocation in blocks of variable sizes of 4 to 6 patients, which was generated by an independent researcher who had no involvement in the outcome statistics, determining whether 1 g of TXA or placebo would be administered. Labels of the same size and colour but with different random codes were pasted on the top right of the screening table. It should be noted that subjects may also develop new risk factors after randomization, which would be marked in the risk screening checklist as well. Participants were responsible for maintaining intervention confidentiality during the informed consent session and had the right to withdraw their consent at any stage of the trial.

After randomization, the trial regimen for each participant was prepared in a separate room within the delivery area by an investigator who had no involvement in the statistical analysis or outcome assessment. In the treatment group, 10 ml of saline was withdrawn from a 100 ml saline solution and replaced with a 10-ml volume containing 1 g of TXA, while in the control group, a 100 ml untreated normal saline was used. Those solvents were identical in appearance for both groups in addition to the labelled random number. The prepared drugs were then placed inside numbered boxes that were identical in size and colour, with only the random number on their labels serving as identification.

Intervention

After the delivery of the foetus, participants received 1 g TXA or a placebo, which was stored in prepared numbered boxes, via intravenous infusion within 10-15 min immediately after the routine prophylactic oxytocin (10 IU) injection based on their pre-assigned randomization groups. Management during the third stage of labour remained consistent between both groups by providing standardized training for midwives in advance. If PPH occurs, medical workers would provide standard management of PPH according to a specified protocol dealing with PPH, including but not limited to the use of TXA, uterotonic agents, balloon tamponade, blood product transfusion, surgical techniques, and other appropriate measures.

Trial outcomes

The primary endpoint assessing the efficacy of TXA was the incidence of PPH. This was defined as a blood loss of ≥500 mL within 24 h post-delivery, encompassing both intrapartum haemorrhage (measured by blood collected in bags in delivery room) and postpartum haemorrhage (measured by weight method upon returning to the ward). Intrapartum blood loss was measured utilizing a V-shaped blood collection bag with scale lines and warning lines which was positioned beneath the buttocks immediately until bleeding stops (Figure S1). Upon the women’ return to the ward, the blood loss was quantified via the traditional weighing method (record the difference between the wet weight of the blood-soaked pad and its dry weight). The total blood loss was calculated as the sum of the losses measured in both steps within 24 h postpartum.

Secondary outcomes assessing the effectiveness were described, including clinical and laboratory outcomes. Clinical: (1) Proportion of women requiring additional uterotonic agents administration; (2) Incidence rate of severe PPH defined as blood loss ≥1000 ml within 24 h after childbirth; (3) Proportion of women with blood loss ≥ 500ml at bag removal; (4) Average blood loss (ml) at bag removal, 2 h and 24 h postpartum; (5) Proportion of women undergoing arterial embolization, balloon tamponade, or other emergency surgeries; (6) Proportion of women requiring blood transfusion from delivery until discharge; Laboratory: Peripartum alterations in venous haemoglobin (Hb) and haematocrit (Hct) measurements (difference between these measurements before delivery and 24 h after delivery).

Safety outcomes encompassed the following endpoints. Clinical: (1) Incidence of potential mild adverse effects of TXA during hospitalization (nausea, vomiting, photopsia, dizziness). (2) Incidence of potential serious adverse effects of TXA within 30 days postpartum: a. Radiographic imaging or doppler ultrasound confirmation of thrombosis. b. Myocardial infarction. c. Seizure. d. Renal failure requiring dialysis. e. Any other unexpected adverse events. Laboratory: prothrombin time (PT) and active prothrombin time (APTT) at day 2 postpartum. These events occurring during hospitalization were recorded in the electronic medical records by the attending physician. A telephone follow-up was conducted one month after delivery.

The data and analyses for both primary and secondary outcomes were evaluated by investigators who were unaware of the treatment allocation. A group of adjudicators, also blinded to treatment, reviewed the data to determine participants’ achievement of study outcomes and assess safety.

Sample size

In TRAPP trial, investigators conducted a subgroup analysis to evaluate the impact of TXA in preventing PPH among women with combined risk factors, and within the placebo group, 92 out of 545 participants (16.9%) experienced PPH [20]. Therefore, we assumed an incidence rate of 17% for PPH in the placebo group in this trial. To show a relative reduction in incidence of at least 30% in the TXA arm [28]—that is an incidence ≤ 11.9% (α = 0.05, 1 − β = 0.90) using a two-sided test (allowing for the possibility of either lower or higher incidence in the TXA group compared to the placebo group), a sample size of 1041 women per group was required. Considering an expected cesarean delivery rate of 5% after randomization at our centre and an anticipated loss in follow-up rate of 10%, we determined that inclusion necessitated a total sample size of 2449 women undergoing vaginal delivery.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis and reporting in this study adhered to the CONSORT guidelines for randomized controlled trials, and the assessors responsible for blood loss measurements would remain blinded to the group status. The comparison between the two groups focused on women’s demographic characteristics, risk factors and outcome related to PPH. The baseline was analyzed in the full data set. The outcomes were analyzed in a mITT (modified intention to treat) analysis, which aimed to seek the ‘hemostatic effect’ of TXA. Quantitative variables were expressed as means with standard deviations (compared using independent samples t-test) or as medians with interquartile ranges (compared using the Mann-Whitney U test). Comparisons of categorical variables were conducted using appropriate statistical tests, including the Chi2 and Fisher exact tests. Additionally, exploratory subgroup analysis for the primary outcome was based on risk factors associated with PPH. An interaction test was employed to examine whether the treatment effect varied across these subgroups. The primary outcome events were reanalyzed in the per-protocol set as a sensitivity analysis. Effects was quantified as relative risks (RR), with their 95% confidence intervals (CI). Post hoc adjusted analyses for the primary outcome of incidence of PPH were performed using logistic regression models to estimate the odds ratio (OR) of TXA with corresponding 2-sided 95% CIs, considering potential confounders that were not fully balanced by randomization, prespecified as p < 0.1 for baseline comparisons between groups. The analysis included an intermediate statistical analysis to ensure no significant difference exists in adverse events between the groups, upon inclusion of half of the planned participants. The data were analyzed utilizing the SPSS 20.0 software (IBM Corp, Armonk, NY, USA), and Stata 12.0 software (Stata Corporation LLC, College Station, USA).

Results

Trial participants

The trial began on September 1, 2022, and the final subject follow-up was concluded on August 30, 2023, when the trial has reached its pre-determined sample size. A total of 3232 women planning for vaginal delivery were evaluated for eligibility. The exclusion criteria led to the exclusion of 273 women. 2959 people were fully informed about the details of the trial, while 95 declined participation in the trial. As a result, a total of 2864 women were continuously assessed for combined risk factors from admission to labour completion (Figure 1). The participants were randomized once one risk factor was identified at least. Finally, 2409 women were randomly assigned to either the TXA group (n = 1202) or the placebo group (n = 1207). After randomization, there were 67 transferred to cesarean section in the TXA group, and 84 in the placebo group. Therefore, a total of 2258 participants underwent vaginal delivery. 13 patients, including 7 in the TXA group and 6 in the placebo group, whose primary outcome data were missing due to their failure to use specified blood collection bags, were excluded from the primary outcome analysis. The modified intention-to-treat analysis(mITT) encompassed 1128 women in the TXA group and 1117 women in the placebo group. In the per protocol analysis, there were 951 received 1 g TXA, 996 received placebo respectively, as specified in the protocol. Follow-up data was lost for 27 patients in the TXA group and 43 patients in the placebo group during the 30-day postpartum period.

Outcome

Effectiveness

Among the 2258 vaginal deliveries, primary outcome data were precisely collected from 2245 participants (1128 in TXA group and 1117 in placebo group), who used blood collection bags during labour. In the mITT population, PPH occurred in 186 of 1128 women in the TXA group and in 215 of 1117 women in the placebo group (16.5% vs. 19.2%; RR, 0.86; 95% CI, 0.72 to 1.02; p = 0.088) (Table 2). Besides, after adjusting for imbalanced baseline characteristics, there was still no statistically significant difference in the incidence of PPH between the two groups (OR, 0.87; 95% CI, 0.70 to 1.09; p = 0.219).

The secondary outcomes regarding the efficacy of TXA between the two groups were presented in Table 2. The incidence of blood loss measuring ≥500ml at bag removal did not show any statistically significant difference between the two groups (14.0% vs 16.6%; RR, 0.85; 95% CI, 0.70 to 1.03; p = 0.092, adjusted p = 0.124). Also, there were no statistically significant differences observed between the TXA group and the placebo group in terms of blood loss at bag removal, at 2 h and 24 h postpartum (349.58 ± 174.30 vs 364.55 ± 270.27, p = 0.119; 382.11 ± 181.29 vs 398.42 ± 270.34, p = 0.093; 438.78 ± 186.85 vs 454.59 ± 272.30, p = 0.108 respectively). Additionally, there was no statistically significant difference in the proportions of balloon tamponade and other surgery between TXA group and the placebo group. However, the incidence rate of severe PPH was significantly lower in the TXA group compared to the placebo group (2.7% vs 5.6%; RR, 0.49; 95% CI, 0.32 to 0.74; p = 0.001, adjusted p = 0.002). The histogram reveals a relatively lower proportion of patients experiencing high-level bleeding in the TXA group (Figure 2). There was a significant reduction in the use of additional uterotonic agents (7.8% vs 15.6%; RR, 0.50; 95% CI, 0.39 to 0.63; p < 0.001, adjusted p < 0.001) and a notable decrease in haemoglobin levels was observed in the TXA group (Table 2).

Adverse events

Adverse events were shown in Table 3. Among all adverse events, nausea and vomiting occurred more frequently in the TXA group than in the placebo group, but the difference was not statistically significant (4.3% vs 2.8%; RR, 1.52; 95% CI, 0.98 to 2.35; p = 0.061). Two women in the TXA group reported experiencing visual hallucinations; however, both individuals had high myopia. During hospitalization, a slightly higher proportion of patients in the TXA group had systolic blood pressure ≥140 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure ≥90 mmHg compared to those in the placebo group; however, this difference was not statistically significant. No significant differences were observed between groups in PT and APTT levels. Participants were revisited at day 30 postpartum to assess adverse events. The rate of follow-up completion was lower in the TXA group than in the placebo group (81.4% vs 84.9%; p = 0.029). No prespecified investigator-reported thrombotic events occurred in either trial group within 30 days after delivery. Besides, there was no significant difference in the proportion requiring anticoagulation therapy after discharge.

Subgroup analysis

We conducted a risk-based analysis to evaluate the preventive effect of TXA on PPH (Table S2). Among patients with GDM, the incidence of PPH was significantly lower in the TXA group compared to the placebo group (17.1% vs. 25.3%; RR, 0.67; 95% CI, 0.48 to 0.95; p = 0.024), while there was no significant interaction between GDM and the intervention group (P values for interactions: 0.108). The incidence of PPH in women with anaemia was significantly lower in the TXA group (32.3% vs. 44.8%; RR, 0.72; 95%CI, 0.55-0.94; p = 0.015). However, this effect was not significant in non-anaemic women (13.2% vs. 14.8%, RR, 0.89, 95%CI, 0.71-1.11; p = 0.309). And there was no significant interaction effect between presence or absence of anaemia and the trial groups (P values for interactions: 0.120). Among women with a history of PPH, the incidence of PPH was significantly lower in the TXA group (46.7% vs 81.8%; RR, 57; 95% CI, 0.37 to 0.88; p = 0.010). Nevertheless, such a decrease was not seen in women without previous episodes of PPH (15.7% vs 18.0%; RR, 0.87; 95%CI, 0.72 to 1.05; p = 0.145). Significant interaction was observed between the presence or absence of previous PPH and the trial groups (P values for interactions: 0.024). In cases with placental adhesion or implantation, the incidence of PPH was lower in the TXA group compared to the placebo group (38.3% vs. 78.8%; RR, 0.49; 95% CI, 0.34 to 0.69; p < 0.001). Furthermore, a significant interaction was observed between placental factors and the trial groups (P values for interactions: <0.001). Among patients who underwent episiotomy, the group receiving TXA exhibited a lower incidence of PPH compared to the placebo group (23.5% vs 30.0%; RR, 0.79; 95% CI, 0.62 to 0.99; p = 0.043). However, no significant interaction was observed between episiotomy as a risk factor and the interventions (P values for interactions: 0.357).

In subgroup analysis of other risk factors, including advanced maternal age, obesity, multiple pregnancies and others, there was no statistically significant difference in the incidence of PPH between the TXA group and the placebo group (Table S2). Furthermore, no significant interaction effects were observed between those risk factors and the trial groups. The study included 4 women undergoing precipitate labour, all of whom experienced PPH.

According to the number of combined risk factors in postpartum women, the subjects were stratified into 5 subgroups (1RF, 2RF, 3RF, 4RF, and ≥5 RF) for further analysis (Tables 4 and 5). No significant difference was observed in the incidence of PPH between the TXA group and the placebo group across different subgroups. Furthermore, there was no significant interaction effect between the number of risk factors and the trial groups (Table 4). However, among postpartum women with ≥5 risk factors, women in the TXA group had a significantly lower incidence of severe PPH(9.6% vs 18.4%; RR, 0.52; 95% CI, 0.30 to 0.89; p = 0.015)(Table 5).

Discussion

Although the prophylactic administration of TXA did not demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in the incidence of PPH based on the results (RR, 0.86; 95% CI, 0.72 to 1.02; p = 0.088), it is noteworthy that the upper limit of this range only marginally exceeded ‘1’ by a value of 0.02. This suggests that the risk difference between the TXA group and placebo group ranges from a substantial decrease of 28% to a minor increase of 2%. Therefore, it can be inferred that prophylactic use of TXA may potentially exert protective effects in reducing PPH incidence.

The overall incidence of PPH after vaginal delivery in this study was17.9% (401/2245). However, rate observed during the TRAAP trial was found to be 8.8% (344/3891) [20]. The incidence observed in this trial was approximately twice as high as that in the TRAAP trial. There were several possible reasons for this analysis. Firstly, this study’s primary outcome focused on blood loss of at least 500 ml within 24 h after vaginal delivery, as defined by current guidelines for PPH in China [2]. However, the TRAAP trial’s primary outcome was the volume of blood collected in a collection bag of at least 500 ml during immediate postpartum surveillance in the delivery room, which typically did not exceed 2 h. Secondly, our study population consisted solely of women with established prenatal/intrapartum factors and were theoretically more predisposed for PPH than the general population. In the TRAAP trial, when analyzing women with known risk factors separately (defined as odds ratio ≥3 based on previous literature), the incidence of PPH in the TXA group and placebo group were 14.4% and 16.9% respectively, which corresponded to an incidence of 16.5% and 19.2% in our trial [20].

Accurate measurement of blood loss is crucial for assessing primary outcomes. In this study, we employed a V-shaped collection bag with featured calibrated lines at 500, 1000, and 1500 ml as warning indicators, which enables more accurate quantification of blood loss in delivery room. Compared with the traditional weighing method and visual inspection method, this method is more accurate for assessment of bleeding [29]. Nevertheless, currently there is no incredibly accurate way to quantify the blood loss, which may be influenced by factors such as the evaluator’s expertise and subjective judgment. This study adopted a blind endpoint trial design, which is commonly used in the field of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular research, to rein in the influence of human subjectivity on the outcome assessment and preserve the patient’s right to informed consent [30, 31]. However, it is imperative to acknowledge the presence of this inevitable measurement error, which may potentially attenuate the correlation between TXA and PPH incidence, thereby possibly leading to an underestimation of the impact of TXA in reducing PPH incidence[32].

Regarding the safety of TXA, previous clinical trials have provided support for it, demonstrating no significant increase in the occurrence of serious adverse reactions such as thrombotic events [20–22]. This trial did not observe any significant difference in the occurrence of thromboembolic events within one month after treatment between the groups. Although it is acknowledged that this trial may have lacked sufficient statistical power to detect intergroup differences in these event rates, the observed occurrence aligns with those reported in previous large-scale randomized controlled trials.

Existing literature tools generally have limited specificity in predicting PPH, and there are currently no universally recognized definition criteria specifically targeting high-risk individuals after vaginal delivery. Considering this trial’s population, the risk factors mentioned in inclusion criteria are based on results from a retrospective cohort study conducted at our medical institution, which mostly aligns with conclusions published both domestically and internationally. Therefore, these findings appear applicable to women undergoing vaginal delivery receiving treatment at similar institutions. We didn’t aim to explore the effectiveness of TXA in women with a specific high-risk factor since it is common for women to present multiple risk factors simultaneously with potential interactions between them in the real clinical practice.

Strengths

Although numerous randomized clinical trials have conducted trials focus on prophylactic effect of PPH with TXA, the majority of high-quality studies have primarily been carried in developed countries and predominantly involved general populations. Our study represents the first large-scale, randomized controlled trial targeting women at risk of developing PPH in a developing country. Instead of utilizing the limited performance risk stratification tool for PPH mentioned in literature previously, we retrospectively reviewed clinical data from vaginal delivery patients at our medical centre during year prior to initiating the trial to identify several risk factors for PPH and continuously screened risk factors until the end of delivery. This enables our study to encompass a comprehensive cohort of individuals exhibiting risk factors for PPH. Furthermore, we conducted subgroup analyses for specific risk factors and provided evidence of TXA in women with particular risk profiles. An open-label approach was employed to maximize subjects’ right to informed consent while only blinding those responsible for bleeding volume assessment to ensure objective accuracy evaluation.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. Firstly, it is widely recognized that PPH is influenced by numerous risk factors, albeit with variations in their classification across different countries. The significant heterogeneity observed in our study population should not be neglected given the presence of a considerable number of eligible risk factors. Despite the subgroup analyses, the results of these subgroup analyses should be considered exploratory due to the limited sample size. Besides, our trial did not include women with more than three previous births, and the majority were primiparous. Therefore, we are unable to provide robust evidence supporting the use of TXA in those with multiple prior deliveries. Further investigations are warranted to explore the preventive effects of TXA against PPH among populations with specific risk factors. Secondly, laboratory test results were incomplete for some participants, potentially impacting the accuracy of certain secondary outcomes. Thirdly, we observed a significantly higher incidence of additional administration of uterotonic agents in the placebo group, which could potentially affect monitoring the preventive effect of TXA. Finally, the generalizability of our findings is a significant limitation due to the stringent inclusion and exclusion criteria applied to all subjects. Furthermore, the inclusion of single-centre studies further restricts the generalizability of our conclusions.

Conclusions

In this trial, the prophylactic use of TXA did not show a reduction in the incidence of PPH among women with risk factors. However, it was associated with a significantly reduced incidence of PPH in women with specific risk factors, as well as incidence of severe PPH, indicating its potential application value in the prevention of excessive bleeding postpartum. Given the diverse etiology and mutually causal interacting factors involved in PPH, future high-quality randomized controlled studies targeting specific risk factors will be necessary to confirm preventive efficacy of this medication.

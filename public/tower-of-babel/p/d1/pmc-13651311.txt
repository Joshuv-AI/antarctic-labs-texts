An explainable multimodal 2.5D deep learning-radiomics model for predicting extranodal extension in lung adenocarcinoma using preoperative CT: a multicenter retrospective cohort study
Authors: Jianyu Wang, Yuhang Wang, Xiaojiang Zhao, Han Zhang, Qiuqiao Mu, Yanbo Wang, Xin Li, Meilin Xu, Zhenchun Song, Jie Ren, Zixiao Wang, Daqiang Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aimed to develop the Radiomics-Assembled ENE system (RAIEm), a multimodal preoperative computed tomography (CT) radiomics model, for identifying extranodal extension (ENE) in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) and to examine its utility in risk stratification and individualized treatment planning.MethodsWe retrospectively assembled preoperative thin-slice chest CT scans and postoperative hematoxylin-eosin-stained histology from patients with LUAD. Tumors were segmented automatically using a 3D U-Net with virtual adversarial training. Radiomic features were derived from the intratumoral region and from concentric peritumoral rings expanded by 2, 4, 6, and 8 mm, and 2.5D deep-learning features were extracted from five axial CT slices centered on the largest tumor section using a convolutional neural network. Subsequently, we constructed an integrated Radiomics-ENE model (RAIEm), whose performance was assessed in separate training and validation cohorts.ResultsRAIEm outperformed conventional unimodal models, yielding higher area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), sensitivity, and specificity in both the training and external validation cohorts. In the validation cohort, AUC was 0.78 (95% CI, 0.66–0.89), and decision-curve analysis indicated greater net benefit across most threshold probabilities. RAIEm demonstrated significant discriminative power for recurrence-free survival risk stratification in both the training and external validation cohorts (both p < 0.01). ENE-positive patients showed significantly shorter recurrence-free survival than ENE-negative patients in both cohorts. Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) analysis highlighted key radiomic drivers, with deep-learning features (one 2D and two 2.5D) ranking among the top three contributors.ConclusionRAIEm — a multimodal, preoperative CT radiomics-based model — showed encouraging performance for identifying ENE in LUAD and may support preoperative risk assessment; however, prospective multicenter validation is required before routine clinical implementation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12880-026-02584-w.Keywords: Lung adenocarcinoma, Radiomics, Deep learning, Extranodal extension, SHAP

Introduction

Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide [1]. Among its histological subtypes, lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is the most common, and research on its pathogenesis, pathological characteristics, and individualized therapies continues to advance. For patients with LUAD, curative resection combined with systematic lymph node (LN) dissection/sampling remains a standard approach; nevertheless, a substantial risk of postoperative recurrence and metastasis persists. In 2018, during the development of the 9th-edition N descriptor, the International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer (IASLC) Staging and Prognostic Factors Committee underscored the prognostic significance of extranodal extension (ENE) [2]. Accordingly, refining postoperative risk stratification and optimizing adjuvant strategies are key to improving long-term outcomes. Chest computed tomography (CT) is the most widely used imaging modality for the diagnosis and assessment of lung cancer. With the rapid growth of machine learning and artificial intelligence in medicine, quantitative imaging features are increasingly being extracted from medical images to aid disease diagnosis and prognostication [3, 4].

LN status is a cornerstone of prognosis and treatment selection in lung cancer staging. ENE is a pathological diagnosis, defined as “the extension of metastatic tumor within a lymph node (LN) beyond the LN capsule into the surrounding connective tissue, with or without a stromal reaction” [5]. In LUAD, ENE denotes adverse biology and is associated with higher rates of locoregional failure, distant metastasis, and reduced overall survival. In head and neck and breast cancers, ENE has repeatedly been linked to poorer outcomes, locoregional recurrence, and treatment resistance, and has been incorporated into multiple guidelines and reporting recommendations [6–9]. By contrast, in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) — particularly LUAD — the epidemiology of ENE, the consistency of its pathological definition, and its incremental contribution relative to conventional N staging and other high-risk pathological features, such as lymphovascular invasion, pleural invasion, and spread through air spaces (STAS), remain insufficiently characterized [10].

At present, ENE assessment in LUAD still relies mainly on postoperative histopathologic evaluation of resected lymph nodes. Although preoperative CT can provide information on nodal size, morphology, and surrounding tissue invasion, conventional visual assessment remains limited for identifying ENE before surgery, particularly when macroscopic nodal invasion is not evident. Therefore, a noninvasive and reproducible preoperative method for patient-level ENE risk assessment is clinically needed.

Accumulating evidence indicates that both intratumoral and peritumoral radiomics can predict diagnosis, prognosis, and histopathologic subtypes in LUAD [11–13]. However, to our knowledge, no study has predicted ENE in LUAD using radiomic features from the tumor and its surrounding tissue. We therefore leveraged chest CT-derived radiomics to identify ENE preoperatively and thereby inform treatment modification. Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) quantify each feature’s contribution to a model’s output via Shapley values, enhancing the interpretability and transparency of machine-learning predictions [14, 15]. In brief, larger absolute SHAP values indicate greater influence of the corresponding feature on the predicted outcome, enabling both global and instance-level insights.

To address this knowledge gap, we developed and externally validated the Radiomics-Assembled ENE system (RAIEm), a preoperative CT-based model integrating intratumoral radiomics, multi-scale peritumoral radiomics, and 2.5D deep-learning features. The novelty of this study lies in its patient-level prediction of pathological ENE in LUAD using only preoperative CT images, multicenter external validation, and SHAP-based model interpretation. RAIEm may provide a noninvasive tool for preoperative ENE risk assessment and help support individualized treatment planning.

Materials and methods

Study design and patients

This retrospective study was approved by the institutional ethics committee; informed consent was waived due to its retrospective nature. The work comprised two parts — model development and external validation — both based on retrospectively collected data. The study proceeded through four major steps: (i) image acquisition and preprocessing, (ii) pathological assessment of ENE, (iii) feature extraction and selection, and (iv) model construction and evaluation (Fig. 1). Development cohort (training). We reviewed records of patients treated at Center1 between June 1, 2017 and December 31, 2021. Inclusion criteria were: (1) curative (radical) pulmonary resection performed at Center1; and (2) postoperative pathology confirming invasive LUAD. Exclusion criteria were: (1) multiple primary lung cancers; (2) receipt of neoadjuvant therapy; (3) missing postoperative follow-up information; (4) failure to complete routine hematoxylin-eosin (H&E)-based assessment of ENE status; and (5) absence of preoperative thin-slice CT imaging. After screening, 274 eligible patients were included as the training cohort. By lymph-node metastasis (LNM) burden, there were 91 patients with no LNM, 77 with 1 LNM, 63 with 2–3 LNMs, 30 with 4–6 LNMs, and 13 with ≥ 7 LNMs. External validation cohort. Applying the same inclusion and exclusion criteria, we identified patients treated between January 1, 2020 and June 30, 2021 at Center2 and Center3. A total of 95 patients met eligibility and constituted the external validation cohort: 39 with no LNM, 19 with 1 LNM, 33 with 2–3 LNMs, 3 with 4–6 LNMs, and 1 with ≥ 7 LNMs. The screening flowchart, including the exact number of patients excluded for each exclusion criterion in both the training and external validation cohorts, is shown in Fig. 2.

CT acquisition protocols and lung tumor segmentation

Preoperative chest CT images in Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine (DICOM) format were retrieved from the picture archiving and communication systems (PACS) of Center1, Center2 and Center3. Because different CT scanners were used across centers, scanner-specific CT acquisition and reconstruction parameters varied. The available scanner-specific parameters, including scanner manufacturer/model, tube voltage, tube current, matrix size, slice thickness, reconstruction kernel, reconstruction algorithm, and pitch, are summarized in Supplementary Table S1; parameters not explicitly recorded in the available DICOM headers were indicated as not available. Before feature extraction and model development, all CT images underwent standardized preprocessing, including DICOM import from PACS, resampling to an isotropic voxel size of 1.0 × 1.0 × 1.0 mm³, window standardization with a width of 1350 Hounsfield units (HU) and level of − 350 HU, automated tumor segmentation using a 3D U-Net with virtual adversarial training (VAT), visual review and, when necessary, manual correction of all automatically generated tumor segmentation masks by S.Z.C. (15 years of experience), a thoracic radiologist blinded to the pathological ENE status and clinical outcomes, and generation of 2-, 4-, 6-, and 8-mm peritumoral ROIs. Additional preprocessing for 2.5D deep-learning inputs is described in Sect. 2.5. The U-Net comprised five encoder-decoder stages. At each stage, the encoder used two convolutional layers followed by max-pooling to compress spatial information, while the decoder employed two convolutional layers and a transposed convolution to reconstruct segmentation features (see Supplementary Fig. S1). Following segmentation of the tumor region of interest (ROI), four peritumoral ROIs were generated by isotropic dilations of 2, 4, 6, and 8 mm beyond the tumor boundary, based on prior literature, to capture peritumoral radiomic context (Fig. 3B).

Histopathologic assessment of ENE

In accordance with the first formal definition issued by the College of American Pathologists (CAP) in 2016 and the concordant description in the 8th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC), ENE was defined as morphologic evidence of metastatic tumor cells breaching the LN capsule and infiltrating the perinodal soft/fatty tissue [16]. Operational criteria for ENE (positive): (1) Irregular LN contour with contiguous tumor nests or irregular infiltrative strands in adipose/fibrous tissue beyond the capsule, accompanied by perinodal reactive fibrosis and/or inflammatory infiltrates; or (2) Focal capsular absence with “spillage” of tumor showing tongue-like/budding infiltration extending into extracapsular tissue. Cases not meeting these criteria were considered ENE-negative. Exclusion criteria for ENE adjudication: (1) Direct extension of the primary tumor into an adjacent LN (contiguity with the primary tumor); (2) Perinodal fibrosis/macrophage reaction without identifiable tumor cells; (3) Artifactual capsular disruption due to cautery, traction, or needle-track effects; (4) Isolated dilatation of subcapsular sinuses or tumor cells confined to marginal sinuses (i.e., sinusoidal/marginal-sinus spread). Postoperative H&E slides from all enrolled surgical specimens were reviewed by two pathologists — X.M.L. (29 years of experience) and Z.N.Y. (4 years of experience) — to determine ENE status (Fig. 4). The two pathologists independently reviewed all H&E slides and were blinded to imaging findings and clinical outcomes. Interobserver agreement for ENE status was assessed using Cohen’s κ coefficient. Any disagreement was resolved by joint review and consensus. ENE was identified in 87/274 patients (31.8%) in the training cohort and in 28/95 patients (29.5%) in the external validation cohort.

Follow-up

Outcome data for all enrolled patients were primarily obtained from electronic medical records and supplemented by telephone follow-up when necessary. Follow-up information was supplemented by telephone communication in 52 of 369 patients (14.1%) overall, including 38 of 274 patients (13.9%) in the training cohort and 14 of 95 patients (14.7%) in the external validation cohort. The median follow-up time was 50.4 months (range, 24.2–91.1 months) overall, 57.5 months (range, 24.2–91.1 months) in the training cohort, and 42.2 months (range, 26.6–50.6 months) in the external validation cohort. The study endpoints were recurrence-free survival (RFS) and tumor recurrence rate. Recurrence was confirmed by imaging findings or lymph-node pathology. Locoregional relapse was defined as recurrence at the surgical bed or bronchial stump, ipsilateral lung, ipsilateral hilar or mediastinal lymph nodes, or other regional thoracic lymph nodes. Distant metastasis was defined as recurrence outside the locoregional field, including contralateral lung lesions, pleural or pericardial dissemination, nonregional lymph-node metastasis, or extrathoracic organ metastasis. Telephone follow-up was mainly used to update patient status and identify suspected recurrence events; recurrence was not determined by telephone report alone. The last follow-up date was December 31, 2024 for the training cohort and June 30, 2024 for the external validation cohort.

Deep-learning feature extraction

For each patient, the axial CT slice containing the largest tumor area was identified as the central representative slice. To construct the 2.5D input, four additional spatially related axial slices were selected at offsets of + 1, +3, − 1, and − 3 relative to the central slice after image resampling. Thus, each patient-level 2.5D input consisted of five axial CT slices centered on the largest tumor section. These slices were cropped using the same tumor-centered bounding box, intensity-normalized, and entered into the ResNet-18 network. Therefore, the input represented different neighboring CT slices rather than duplicated copies of a single grayscale image. The 2.5D deep-learning model was implemented using a ResNet-18 backbone in Onekey AI with PyTorch (v1.11.0). Model training was performed using stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with softmax cross-entropy loss, a batch size of 32, an initial learning rate of 0.001, and a maximum of 200 epochs; no formal early stopping was applied, and the checkpoint with the best internal validation performance was selected for feature extraction; the external validation cohort was not used for model selection. Deep-learning features were extracted using the trained convolutional neural network (CNN) model; the external validation cohort was kept fully independent, but out-of-fold deep-learning feature generation within the training cohort was not performed. Training employed online data augmentation, including random cropping and horizontal and vertical flips, whereas test-time preprocessing was limited to normalization. To improve stability across heterogeneous patient groups, we used transfer learning, initializing with ImageNet-pretrained weights. Radiomics model construction

Features were extracted in accordance with the Image Biomarker Standardisation Initiative (IBSI) guidelines using PyRadiomics from ROI-INTRA, ROI-IntraPeri-2 mm, ROI-IntraPeri-4 mm, ROI-IntraPeri-6 mm, and ROI-IntraPeri-8 mm. To avoid data leakage, all feature normalization, scaling, feature selection, and model fitting procedures were performed exclusively within the training cohort; the learned preprocessing parameters, selected features, and final model were then directly applied to the external validation cohort without refitting or reselection. Feature-level harmonization, such as ComBat harmonization or scanner-effect correction, was not performed in this study. To reduce overfitting, correlation filtering and LASSO regularization were performed within the training cohort, with the LASSO penalty selected using 10-fold cross-validation; the final locked model was then evaluated in the independent external validation cohort, and repeated or nested cross-validation was not used. First, Spearman correlation analysis was performed; for any feature pair with a correlation coefficient > 0.9, one feature was randomly removed, retaining only features that showed significant between-group differences. Next, irrelevant features were reduced via Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator.

For the 2.5D deep-learning features extracted by the ResNet-18 model, LASSO was applied directly, followed by construction of a multivariable logistic-regression signature. In total, 19 highly informative features were retained as the final variables to construct RAIEm. Model performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis and decision curve analysis (DCA).

SHAP analysis

We computed SHAP values for every radiomic feature in the final model and ranked features by their contributions. SHAP values quantify each feature’s impact on individual predictions, enabling identification of the most influential predictors of ENE in LUAD. To characterize feature-outcome relationships, we examined mean SHAP values (global importance across the cohort), SHAP summary plots (overall importance and direction of effect across all samples), and SHAP dependence plots (the relationship between a feature’s value and its corresponding SHAP value at the instance level). Together, these analyses highlighted the features most strongly associated with ENE and provided interpretable insights into how they drive model predictions.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables approximating normality were summarized as mean ± standard deviation (SD), whereas non-normal variables were reported as median (interquartile range). Differences in model performance were compared using the DeLong test. Risk groups (high vs. low) were defined by the optimal cutoff from the final model’s ROC curve. In the primary patient-level analysis, pN0 patients were classified as ENE-negative because no metastatic lymph node with ENE was identified; a sensitivity analysis was then performed in the node-positive subgroup (pN1–pN2), comparing ENE-positive patients with node-positive ENE-negative patients. Survival was depicted using Kaplan–Meier (KM) estimates. Two-sided p values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Radiomics features were extracted using PyRadiomics (v3.0.1), and deep-learning training was implemented using PyTorch (v1.11.0) with a ResNet-18 backbone. Feature selection, model construction, ROC analysis, survival analysis, SHAP interpretation, and visualization were performed in R (v4.5.0) using relevant packages, including glmnet, pROC, survival, survminer, rms, ggplot2, and shapviz.

Feature extraction and selection

For the tumor ROI on CT images (Fig. 3B), 535 radiomic features were extracted with PyRadiomics from the INTRA, IntraPeri-2 mm, IntraPeri-4 mm, IntraPeri-6 mm, and IntraPeri-8 mm regions. In the convolutional neural network used in this study, the penultimate (pooling) layer was employed for feature extraction. For each tumor ROI, 160 compact 2.5D deep-learning features were extracted using a ResNet-18 convolutional neural network based on five axial CT slices centered on the largest tumor section. To construct the RAIEm feature set, these 160 deep-learning features were combined with the 535 radiomic features. After feature selection, 19 highly informative features were retained as the final set (Supplementary Fig. S2–5). Detailed information on feature extraction is provided in Supplementary Tables 3–11.

Evaluation of RAIEm

We built a 19-variable logistic-regression signature and integrated it into RAIEm, then evaluated performance in both cohorts (Fig. 5; Table 2). In the training cohort, AUC was 0.88 (95% CI, 0.84–0.93), with sensitivity 0.74, specificity 0.89, and a Youden index of 0.62. In external validation, AUC reached 0.78 (95% CI, 0.66–0.89), sensitivity 0.79, specificity 0.67, and Youden index 0.46. In the node-positive subgroup sensitivity analysis, RAIEm achieved an AUC of 0.83 (95% CI, 0.77–0.89) in the training cohort and 0.82 (95% CI, 0.70–0.93) in the external validation cohort, indicating retained discriminative ability after excluding pN0 patients (Supplementary Table S12). The detailed performance of all comparator models is summarized in Table 2. In the training cohort, comparator-model AUCs ranged from 0.68 to 0.77, compared with 0.88 for RAIEm. In the external validation cohort, comparator-model AUCs ranged from 0.61 to 0.68, compared with 0.78 for RAIEm. A p-value matrix (Supplementary Figs. S6–S7) corroborated statistically significant AUC differences versus other models. Decision-curve analysis showed consistently greater net benefit for RAIEm across most threshold probabilities, supporting its clinical utility.

Discussion

We developed RAIEm, a multimodal preoperative CT-based radiomics model for identifying ENE in LUAD. RAIEm outperformed unimodal comparators in both the training and external validation cohorts and showed significant recurrence-free survival stratification. These findings suggest that integrating intratumoral, peritumoral, and 2.5D deep-learning features may support noninvasive preoperative ENE risk assessment in LUAD.

Radiologic assessment of ENE remains clinically relevant but challenging in NSCLC. Conventional CT evaluation can suggest macroscopic ENE when findings such as matted nodes, indistinct nodal margins, or direct invasion into adjacent structures are present; however, microscopic ENE or equivocal perinodal changes are difficult to identify reliably on routine CT. Prior studies in NSCLC and head-and-neck cancer have suggested that definite radiologic ENE may have greater prognostic relevance than equivocal findings, whereas microscopic ENE is less consistently captured by imaging [17–21]. In this context, RAIEm was not designed to replace pathological assessment or to apply subjective rENE categories. Instead, it provides a quantitative patient-level imaging score by integrating intratumoral, peritumoral, and 2.5D deep-learning features associated with pathological ENE. Therefore, RAIEm may complement conventional visual CT assessment and support preoperative ENE risk stratification, particularly when pathological information is not yet available before treatment.

ENE is a histopathologic marker of tumor aggressiveness with prognostic implications across multiple malignancies. In NSCLC, Jang et al. [21] reported that ENE-negative patients had longer survival and lower recurrence rates, and both Jang et al. and Chen et al. showed that ENE in metastatic LNs was associated with adverse prognosis [21, 22]. Similar associations have been reported in breast cancer [6–8] and in head and neck, thyroid, colorectal, and gastric cancers [17–25], supporting the concept that ENE reflects unfavorable tumor biology. Prior radiomics studies in rectal cancer and head and neck squamous cell carcinoma have also shown the feasibility of noninvasive ENE prediction [9, 26]. Building on this evidence, our study extends imaging-based ENE prediction to LUAD using preoperative CT radiomics and 2.5D deep-learning features.

Peritumoral radiomics has shown prognostic and predictive value in LUAD and NSCLC [27–31]. Previous studies have explored different peritumoral band widths and demonstrated that combining intratumoral and peritumoral features may improve model performance [27–29]. In this study, single-band models showed comparable performance, whereas the integrated RAIEm model significantly outperformed the IntraPeri-2 mm model (p < 0.05; Supplementary Table S13). This suggests that multi-scale peritumoral information may provide complementary tumor–microenvironment signals. The main strengths of RAIEm include multicenter validation, multimodal feature integration, and SHAP-based interpretability [32–35].

Study quality was evaluated using the Radiomics Quality Score (RQS), and RAIEm achieved an RQS of + 17 (Supplementary Fig. S8), comparing favorably with previously reported LUAD intratumoral–peritumoral CT radiomics studies [11]. Compared with prior lung cancer radiomics and deep-learning studies that mainly focused on lymph-node metastasis or occult nodal disease, RAIEm specifically targeted pathological ENE prediction using preoperative CT images and combined intratumoral, multi-scale peritumoral, and 2.5D deep-learning features with external validation and SHAP-based interpretation. Recent studies further support the feasibility of imaging-based machine learning for nodal and ENE-related assessment [36, 37], while also emphasizing the importance of external validation, feature reproducibility, and harmonization in multicenter radiomics research [38, 39].

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective multicenter design may introduce selection bias, and center-related differences in patient selection, CT acquisition, and clinical practice may have influenced model performance. Although independent external validation was performed, the external validation cohort was relatively small, and larger prospective multicenter cohorts with broader demographic representation are needed. Second, RAIEm was developed as a CT-based radiomics and deep-learning model; therefore, clinical and pathological variables, including T stage and nodal burden, were not integrated into the final model, and a combined clinicoradiomic model was not constructed. Future studies should compare imaging-only and combined models and incorporate clinical variables, molecular biomarkers, and treatment-response information. Third, although segmentation masks were visually reviewed and corrected when necessary, formal repeated-segmentation experiments, radiomics feature robustness analyses, scanner/reconstruction-variability assessment, and feature-level harmonization were not performed [38, 39]. Fourth, the candidate feature space remained high-dimensional relative to the sample size. Out-of-fold deep-learning feature generation, bootstrapping, repeated cross-validation, nested cross-validation, optimism-corrected performance estimation, resampling-based predictor-stability assessment, and formal calibration evaluation, including calibration plots, Brier score, and Hosmer–Lemeshow statistics, were not performed; therefore, residual risks of overfitting and model instability could not be fully excluded. Finally, exact node-to-node matching between preoperative imaging and histopathology was not always feasible, so analyses were conducted at the patient level, which better reflects clinical decision-making focused on overall ENE status.

In conclusion, the multimodal, preoperative CT radiomics-based model RAIEm showed encouraging performance for preoperative ENE prediction in LUAD and outperformed unimodal comparators. RAIEm may support noninvasive ENE risk assessment and individualized treatment planning, but prospective multicenter validation is still required before routine clinical implementation.

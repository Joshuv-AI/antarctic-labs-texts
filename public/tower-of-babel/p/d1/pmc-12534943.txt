Trends in epidemiological characteristics of pulmonary tuberculosis among children and youth in Chinese mainland from 2019 to 2024: analysis of national surveillance data
Authors: Eryong Liu, Yun Wang, Fang Yuan, Xichao Ou, Tao Li, Qinglin Meng, Yanlin Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aims to analyze trends in epidemiological characteristics of pulmonary tuberculosis (PTB) among children and youth in mainland China from 2019 to 2024 to support informed policy decisions for TB care and prevention.MethodsData on notified PTB cases were extracted from the National TB Surveillance System along with population information for each province from 2019 to 2024. Age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR) was estimated and stratified by year, age, gender and province. Descriptive analysis examined patient characteristics. The Kruskal-Wallis H and Chi-square tests compared data across years. Temporal trends were analyzed using Joinpoint regression models. The average annual percent change (AAPC) assessed the overall trend, and the annual percent change (APC) evaluated local trend or overall trend when there was no joinpoint (APC = AAPC). Spatial-temporal patterns were explored through spatial autocorrelation analysis and spatial-temporal scan statistics.ResultsFrom 2019 to 2024, mainland China reported 419,000 new PTB cases, with 90.6% in youth and 9.4% in children. The majority were students (48.3%) and farmers (23.8%). The proportion of ethnic minorities, students, individuals identified through active case finding, drug-resistant TB cases, and those with positive bacteriological tests increased over time. Males outnumbered females, but between ages 9 and 15, females surpassed males. The male-to-female ratio remained stable in children, while in youth, males were consistently more affected. The average ASIR over six years was 17.2 per 100,000. Joinpoint regression showed a significant decrease in ASIR from 24.7 per 100,000 in 2019 to 10.5 per 100,000 in 2024 (AAPC = − 15.98, P < 0.0001), with no significant joinpoints. Seasonal peak occurred in late spring and autumn, with a trough in February each year. High PTB clusters persisted in the southwest and northwest, particularly in the southwest, where cases increased. Most provinces showed a decline in ASIR, with the most significant decrease in Xinjiang, while Tibet and Qinghai experienced slower reductions.ConclusionChina has made progress in reducing PTB cases among children and youth. However, disparities remain, particularly in high-risk regions. Targeted interventions addressing social and structural factors, along with enhanced regional monitoring and public health measures, are essential to further reduce incidence.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-24582-3.Keywords: Pulmonary tuberculosis, Epidemiological characteristics, Trends, Children and youth, Chinese mainland

Background

Tuberculosis (TB) is a chronic infectious disease caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis, which primarily affects the lungs, also called pulmonary tuberculosis (PTB) [1, 2]. According to the World Health Organization (WHO) Global Tuberculosis Report 2024 [2], TB has likely regained its status as the world’s leading infectious disease killer. In 2023, approximately 10.8 million people developed TB, with 1.25 million deaths. China ranks third globally in TB burden, accounting for 6.8% of the world total [2], and continues to face significant public health challenges despite progress in control measures. TB among young persons is also a growing concern. These issues highlight the need for continued efforts to reduce the burden of TB both globally and in China.

Young persons refer to individuals under the age of 24 years, including children (aged 0–14 years) and youth (aged 15–24 years) [3]. In 2023, there were an estimated 1.3 million TB cases among children, accounting for 12% of the global total [2]. In 2021, 1.06 million new TB cases occurred among youth [4]. Annually, individuals aged 10 to 24 years account for approximately 18% of the global TB incidence [5]. These figures underscore the significant impact of TB on young populations worldwide. Moreover, drug-resistant TB (DR-TB) adds further complexity, with a rising trend in recent years observed globally among both children [6] and youth [4]. Thus, children and youth remain a priority group due to their critical developmental stage, increased vulnerability to TB, and unique diagnostic challenges, which together elevate the risk of TB transmission, infection, and progression [7]. This highlights the need for updated, nationally representative data in China. Although previous studies have provided valuable insights into the TB burden among these groups, most have been limited to specific age ranges or regions, leaving the true burden of TB among children and youth in mainland China poorly understood.

Understanding trends in the epidemiological characteristics of PTB among children and youth, including DR-TB trends, is crucial for informing effective TB care and prevention strategies [8]. These efforts are essential for accelerating the implementation of the WHO’s End TB Strategy [2]. To this end, a comprehensive analysis of PTB epidemiology across four key aspects is necessary to reduce the TB burden in these populations. First, describing the demographic characteristics of patients helps identify high-risk groups and guide targeted public health interventions. Second, analyzing age- and gender-specific characteristics enhances our understanding of disease patterns, enabling more precise resource allocation and tailored prevention strategies. Third, examining temporal trends allows us to better understand disease dynamics and assess the effectiveness of prevention measures. Fourth, exploring spatiotemporal clustering helps accurately identify high-risk areas and time periods, and reveal disease transmission patterns. All of these analyses support informed policy decisions. In recent years, methodologies such as the Joinpoint regression model and spatiotemporal analysis have become widely used to explore the temporal and spatiotemporal characteristics of PTB [9–12]. However, there is a notable lack of studies that integrate all four aspects to assess the current epidemiological characteristics of PTB among children and youth.

This study aims to provide a comprehensive assessment of PTB in children and youth in mainland China, analyzing demographic, age- and gender-specific, temporal, and spatiotemporal characteristics using Joinpoint regression and spatiotemporal methods. The findings will inform healthcare decision-making and policy implementation, thereby addressing the global public health challenge posed by TB more effectively.

Methods

Data collection

The data of all new notified PTB cases among children and youth from January 2019 to December 2024 in Chinese mainland were collected from the national Tuberculosis Information Management System (TBIMS). The study included a total of 419,000 new PTB patients who were diagnosed and registered during the study period. The patients’ data extracted from the database were age, gender, ethnic group, occupation, residence, patient distribution by region, time of symptoms onset, time of diagnosis and registration, types of case finding, classification of patients, bacteriological test results. Population data for 2019–2023 were obtained from the website of the Statistical Yearbook of the National Bureau of Statistics [13], while the 2024 population data were sourced from the China Population and Development Research Center [14], as the 2024 figures had not yet been released by the Statistical Yearbook at the time of data collection. The standard population applied in this study was derived from the data of the 2020 Chinese Population Census [13]. Fundamental geographic data were downloaded from National Platform for Common GeoSpatial Information Services to make the map of China. The range of TB incidence rates for the overall population across 31 provinces in mainland China was obtained from a previous study [15]. The quality control measures taken during the data collection process are as follows: For the TB data, we standardized categorical variables (e.g., age groups, patient distribution by region), corrected logical inconsistencies in a few numbers, and documented the coding methods for all variables to ensure reproducibility. Additionally, the population data were double-entered and standardized in terms of age grouping criteria and units.

Definitions

Types of case finding

Types of case finding are categorized into passive case finding (PCF), active case finding (ACF), transfer in and other ways [16, 17]. PCF refers to individuals who seek TB diagnosis at health facilities after developing suspicious symptoms and becoming aware of them [17]. ACF is defined as the systematic screening and clinical evaluation of people at high risk of developing TB to facilitate early detection. This includes various approaches, such as contact screening of TB patients at home or within the community [17]. Transfer refers to patients who were initially registered at other health facilities for TB care but were transferred to the current facility to continue treatment [18]. Other ways include imported cases from overseas and cases with unknown types of case finding.

Classification of patients

A new PTB patient is defined as an individual who has never received TB treatment or has previously received TB drug treatment for less than one month [19]. Based on susceptibility or resistance to TB medicines, patients are classified into drug-susceptible TB (DS-TB) and DR-TB. DS-TB refers to a person with TB disease for whom there is no evidence of infection with a strain of Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex that is resistant to rifampicin or isoniazid, or where drug susceptibility testing (DST) shows a strain of Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex that is susceptible to both rifampicin and isoniazid [19]. DR-TB refers to TB disease caused by a strain of Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex that is resistant to any TB medicines tested [19]. A positive bacteriological test result is defined as a patient’s biological sample testing positive in smear microscopy, culture, or WHO-recommended rapid TB diagnostic tests; conversely, a negative result is defined as the absence of such positivity [19, 20].

Statistical analysis

Descriptive analysis for general characteristics

Descriptive analysis was used to understand the general characteristics of new PTB cases among children and youth from 2019 to 2024. Frequencies and percentages were used for categorical variables, while medians and interquartile ranges (IQRs) were used for continuous variables. According to the National Bureau of Statistics, the 31 provincial-level administrative divisions (PLADs) in the mainland of China were divided into seven regions (north, northeast, northwest, central, east, southwest, and south) based on factors such as geographical location and economic development level [21, 22]. The north region included Beijing, Tianjin, Hebei, Shanxi, and Inner Mongolia; the northeast region included Liaoning, Jilin, and Heilongjiang; the northwest region included Shaanxi, Gansu, Qinghai, Ningxia, and Xinjiang; the central region included Henan, Hubei, and Hunan; the east region included Fujian, Jiangsu, Jiangxi, Shandong, Shanghai, Zhejiang, and Anhui; the southwest region included Chongqing, Sichuan, Guizhou, Yunnan, and Tibet; the south region included Guangdong, Guangxi, and Hainan (Supplementary file 1: Figure S1).

The kruskal - wallis H test (for continuous data) and the Chi-square tests (for categorical data) were used to compare data across different years. Considering the fluctuations in the base population, the direct standardization method was employed to compute the age - standardized incidence rate (ASIR), aiming to gauge the temporal variations in the national PTB incidence among children and youth [11]. ASIR was calculated using the age structure of the standard population based on the 2020 Chinese Census data, and expressed as the number of cases per 100,000 population [11, 23]. Its specific approaches have been described in detail elsewhere [23, 24]. The Poisson distribution was used to calculate the 95% confidence interval (CI) of the ASIR [25]. The annual percentage change was calculated to show the changes in ASIR from 2019 to 2024 (percentage change = [(final value–starting value)/|starting value|] × 100). Spearman rank correlation coefficients were calculated using the provincial mean PTB incidence rates for the overall population to assess the association between PTB incidence in the three groups (children and youth, children, youth) and the local overall population. These descriptive analyses were performed using R (version 4.3.0, R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). All statistical tests were two-sided, with a significance level of P < 0.05. The geographical distribution was descriptively analyzed at PLADs level by ArcGIS software (version 10.8, ESRI Inc., Redlands, CA, USA).

Joinpoint regression analysis for temporal characteristics

The Joinpoint regression software (Version 5.3.0.0, National Cancer Institute, Rockville, MD, USA) was used to analyze the changing trends of ASIR of children and youth PTB in Chinese mainland from 2019 to 2024. The dependent variable was the natural logarithm of ASIR, while the independent variable was the year of notification [26]. The grid search method was used to build the model for identifying joinpoints, and the weighted Bayesian information criterion (BIC) was applied to select the optimal fitting model [26, 27]. The weighted BIC provides optimal average performance and greater flexibility in adapting to various model selection tasks. The average annual percent change (AAPC) was used to assess the overall trend of ASIR, while the annual percent change (APC) was applied to evaluate the local trend of ASIR or the overall trend when no joinpoint was detected (i.e., joinpoint = 0) [21, 28]. In this case, APC is equal to AAPC. The 95% CI was used to estimate the range of the changing trends. An AAPC/APC > 0 (P < 0.05) indicated an upward trend in ASIR, while an AAPC/APC < 0 (P < 0.05) indicated a downward trend. P > 0.05 suggested a stable trend [21].

Spatial-temporal analysis for spatiotemporal characteristics

Spatial autocorrelation analysis and spatial–temporal scan statistical methods were used to conduct a spatiotemporal analysis of ASIR among young PTB cases at the PLADs level.

First, ArcGIS was used for spatial autocorrelation analysis, which included both global and local autocorrelation analyses. Both analyses are crucial for identifying spatial patterns of disease that inform public health interventions and resource allocation [21, 28]. The Global Moran’s I was used to identify the spatial autocorrelation and distribution patterns of the disease across different regions. Moran’s I ranges from − 1 to 1: positive values close to 1 indicate strong positive spatial autocorrelation and more pronounced clustering, while negative values close to − 1 suggest negative spatial autocorrelation and greater dispersion. A value of 0 indicates a random distribution of the disease. To identify local clustering patterns, local spatial autocorrelation analysis was conducted using the local Moran’s I, which detects spatially localized patterns that may not be apparent in the global analysis. This method identifies four types of clusters: high-high (HH), low-low (LL), high-low (HL), and low-high (LH). HH clusters indicate areas with high disease incidence surrounded by other high-incidence areas, while LL clusters represent areas with low incidence surrounded by similar low-incidence regions. HL outliers represent areas with high incidence surrounded by low-incidence regions, and LH outliers denote areas with low incidence surrounded by high-incidence regions. The results of local spatial autocorrelation can be visualized through Local Indicators of Spatial Autocorrelation (LISA) maps, which provide a clear depiction of these clusters [29]. The significance of Moran’s I is evaluated using a Z-test, where values of Z > 1.96 and P < 0.05 indicate statistically significant clustering. The number of permutations was set to 999.

Second, spatial–temporal scan statistics were conducted using SaTScan software (version 10.1, Kulldorff and Information Management Services, Inc., Boston, MA, USA). This powerful analytical tool not only reveals the spatial and temporal clusters of diseases but also assesses the relative risk (RR) of these clusters, providing a clearer understanding of risk levels and spatial aggregation [9, 24]. This method employs a moving cylindrical window, where the circular base represents the geographic area and the height reflects the time period. The window is systematically moved across the map to identify potential spatiotemporal clusters based on the Poisson probability model. The log-likelihood ratio (LLR) is calculated by comparing the observed and expected number of cases within and outside a dynamic cylindrical window, and its statistical significance is assessed via Monte Carlo simulation tests. A statistically significant difference (P < 0.05) in LLR values indicates clustering, with higher LLR values signifying stronger spatial and temporal aggregation. The cluster with the highest LLR value is considered the most likely, with secondary clusters identified based on subsequent significant LLR values. The RR value indicates how many times the risk within clusters is compared to that outside clusters. In this analysis, the maximum spatial window is typically set to 50% of the total population at risk, and the largest temporal window is 50% of the study period. Monte Carlo simulation tests with 999 iterations were used to assess the statistical significance of cluster likelihood. The results were visualized using ArcGIS software.

Ethical review

In this study, data were collected from routine TB surveillance system. The approval of the study were obtained from the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention.

Results

Trends of demographic characteristics

The trends of demographic characteristics of all young PTB patients are shown in Table 1. Between 2019 and 2024, a total of 419,000 new PTB cases were reported among children and youth in mainland China, corresponding to an average ASIR of 17.2 per 100,000 population over the six-year period. The ASIR demonstrated a steady annual decline, decreasing from 24.7 per 100,000 in 2019 to 10.5 per 100,000 in 2024. The percentage decrease in the number of cases ranged from 3.6 to 22.8%. The median age of patients was 19 years (IQR: 17–22). The proportion of cases among youth and children was 90.6% and 9.4%, respectively. Most patients were male (58.0%), of Han ethnicity (77.7%). The main occupational types of patients were students (48.3%) and farmers (23.8%). More than half of the patients lived in rural areas (56.2%). The southwestern region reported the highest proportion of TB cases, comprising 30.4% of the national total. 54.3% of patients were found by PCF followed by transfer (39.0%). A total of 96.3% of patients were susceptible to first-line anti-TB drugs, and 3.7% were DR-TB. The proportions of patients with positive and negative bacteriological test results were 47.2% and 50.3%, respectively. The statistically significant differences (all P-values < 0.0001) indicate a real decreasing trend in PTB cases across different demographic characteristics. However, the proportion of children, females, ethnic minorities, students, patients in the southwest, those identified through ACF, DR-TB cases, and patients with positive bacteriological tests gradually increased over time.

Trends of age- and gender-specific characteristics

The trends of age- and gender-specific characteristics of all young PTB patients are shown in Fig. 1. Between 2019 and 2024, the number of new PTB cases increased with age (Fig. 1A). The number of patients was generally higher in males than females, but between the ages of 9 and 15, the number of females exceeds that of males (Fig. 1B). Among children PTB cases, the proportion of males and females showed no obvious difference over the years, and the ASIR for both sexes exhibited a slow downward trend (Fig. 1C). In contrast, among youth PTB cases, the proportion of male patients was consistently higher than that of females each year, and the ASIR for both sexes showed a marked downward trend (Fig. 1D).

Trends of temporal characteristics

The trends of temporal characteristics of all young PTB patients are shown in Fig. 2. From 2019 to 2024, youth consistently comprised the majority of PTB cases among all young patients in Chinese mainland, accounting for around 90% of cases each year (Fig. 2A). Both the number of reported PTB new cases and the ASIR among children and youth demonstrated a steady annual decline during this period (Fig. 2A). Regarding the seasonal distribution (Fig. 2B), from 2019 to 2024, the ASIR for both youth and childhood PTB consistently reached its trough in February each year. Furthermore, December is observed to have the lowest ASIR among children. The peaks in ASIR for both groups occurred in late spring and autumn, particularly in September and October, though in 2020, it shifted to May among youth. Joinpoint regression analysis revealed that the overall ASIR of PTB among children and youth exhibited a gradual downward trend from 24.7 per 100,000 in 2019 to 10.5 per 100,000 in 2024 (AAPC = − 15.98, P < 0.0001), and no significant joinpoints were identified (Fig. 2C). When stratified by age group, the ASIR among children also showed a significant decline from 2.9 per 100,000 in 2019 to 2.2 per 100,000 in 2024 (AAPC = − 4.52, P < 0.0156) (Fig. 2D). Similarly, the ASIR among youth decreased markedly from 62.2 per 100,000 in 2019 to 24.8 per 100,000 in 2024 (AAPC = − 17.17, P < 0.0001) (Fig. 2E). Neither age group exhibited any significant joinpoints.

Trends of spatiotemporal characteristics

The geographical distribution of PTB incidence

Fig. 3 illustrates the geographical distribution of PTB incidence among young patients. Between 2019 and 2024, the top five regions in mainland China with the highest ASIR for all young patients were Tibet (102.6 per 100,000), Qinghai (61.5 per 100,000), Guizhou (47.4 per 100,000), Sichuan (32.2 per 100,000), and Xinjiang (25.6 per 100,000). (Supplementary file 2: Table S1). The ASIR distribution among youth PTB mirrored that of all patients; however, the ASIR for children PTB differed slightly, with higher rates observed in Qinghai, Sichuan, Yunnan, Hunan, Hubei, Shanxi and Inner Mongolia (Fig. 3A). The annual geographical distribution of ASIR shows that, except for Tibet and Qinghai, where the ASIRs for children and youth decline slowly, all other provinces exhibit a declining trend, with the most notable decrease observed in Xinjiang (Fig. 3B). Additionally, the strong positive correlations (rs = 0.87 for children and youth, rs = 0.77 for children, rs = 0.88 for youth, all P < 0.0001) suggest that the PTB burden in children and youth closely mirrors the local overall epidemic level across mainland China (Supplementary file 2: Table S2 and Supplementary file 2: Table S3).

Local spatial autocorrelation

Analysis of local spatial autocorrelation revealed that the detected HH clusters among children and youth with PTB remained consistently located in the five provinces over the reporting years, primarily aggregating in Xinjiang, Tibet, Qinghai, Sichuan, and Yunnan. The results also indicated that HL clusters were consistently located in Henan, a province in the central region (Fig. 4A). Annually, the distribution of HH and HL clusters among youth with PTB mirrored that of all patients, with the exception of the LL clusters, which were observed in Inner Mongolia in 2022. In contrast, the cluster distribution for children with PTB showed slight variations, with LH clusters detected in Gansu in 2023, and also observed in Xinjiang in 2020, 2021, 2022, and 2024. Additionally, LL clusters were observed in Inner Mongolia not only in 2022 but also in 2024 (Fig. 4B).

Discussion

In this study, we first conducted a descriptive analysis of the trends in demographic characteristics, as well as age- and gender-specific patterns of PTB among children and youth in mainland China from 2019 to 2024. We then explored the temporal trends of these cases using joinpoint regression analysis. Finally, we examined the geographical distribution and spatial-temporal clustering at the provincial level through spatial-temporal analysis.

Our findings highlight four aspects of PTB demographic characteristics among children and youth in Chinese mainland. First, students represented the largest group in the occupational distribution of patients, consistent with prior study [30]. This high proportion is partially due to the compulsory education system, which ensures most children and youth are enrolled in school and thus recorded as “students” in surveillance systems. Students are also more vulnerable to TB due to their heavy academic workloads, developing immune systems, and the limited efficacy of the Bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG) vaccine. The dense population on campuses further facilitates TB transmission, making students a high-risk group [31]. Farmers ranked second, reflecting a distinct transmission context compared to the educational environment, as they face poor living conditions, limited healthcare access, and socio-economic vulnerability, particularly in rural areas [32]. These findings highlight different but important pathways of TB risk, emphasizing the need for targeted interventions across both school and community settings. Second, the rising proportion of ethnic minority patients and students is likely due to the effectiveness of China’s ACF strategy [9, 33]. ACF plays a crucial role in TB case detection, especially among students and contacts [33]. However, the current reliance on PCF and referrals can delay diagnosis, reducing the effectiveness of control efforts [34, 35]. Third, although the majority of patients in our study were susceptible to first-line anti-TB drugs, 3.7% were diagnosed with DR-TB, and the proportion has been increasing annually. This highlights the growing threat of DR-TB among children and youth [4]. The emergence of DR-TB represents a significant challenge for public health due to its difficult treatment, prolonged duration, high cost, and increased risk of transmission [6]. Fourth, the study found that the positive and negative rates of bacteriological tests are closely aligned, with annual increases in positivity, likely due to the broader use of molecular diagnostics [9]. However, pathogen-negative cases remain a challenge, as some patients have undetectable pathogen levels. To address this, concurrent testing using multiple sample types (e.g., respiratory, stool, and urine) can improve sensitivity. Molecular diagnostics like next-generation sequencing (NGS) provide more comprehensive pathogen detection and resistance profiles [36]. Additionally, studies show that TB lipoarabinomannan (TB-LAM), Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra, and TB loop-mediated isothermal amplification (TB-LAMP) exhibit high sensitivity and specificity for pathogen-negative patients, with TB-LAM being particularly cost-effective [37, 38]. Based on our findings, we recommend the following targeted TB control interventions: For students, establish routine screening in schools and raise awareness about TB symptoms. For farmers and ethnic minorities, enhance ACF, integrate mobile health services, and improve healthcare access and education. For DR-TB, strengthen surveillance, expand molecular diagnostics, and improve treatment adherence. For pathogen-negative cases, integrate advanced and affordable diagnostic tools with existing protocols to optimize TB detection and management, ensuring timely treatment initiation. These strategies will enhance TB detection, treatment, and control across populations.

Our findings show that PTB incidence and trends vary by age and gender, emphasizing four key patterns among children and youth in mainland China between 2019 and 2024. First, the number of new PTB cases increased with age, with youth accounting for the majority of cases, likely due to limited BCG vaccine effectiveness in older individuals and higher exposure in schools and communities [10, 11]. Additionally, the high rate of missed diagnoses among children may contribute to the lower reported incidence in this age group [11]. Second, more male patients were reported overall, though female cases outnumbered male cases between ages 9 and 15, possibly due to hormonal and immunological changes during menarche that increase TB susceptibility in females [7]. For males, TB risk rises after this age due to factors like increased outdoor activities, social interaction, and lifestyle choices. Biological differences may also contribute to a higher susceptibility to respiratory infections in males [10]. Consequently, no significant sex difference was observed in childhood TB, but the male-to-female ratio in youth PTB increased markedly in our study. Third, the ASIR among youth showed a significant downward trend for both sexes, while the ASIR among children declined more slowly. This could be attributed to the fact that childhood TB often results from primary infection and is more likely to present in severe forms, such as disseminated TB or TB meningitis, due to immature immune responses [39]. As a result, TB diagnosis and treatment in children remain particularly challenging [40]. Fourth, the proportion of children and females increased over time, likely reflecting improvements in pediatric diagnostics and expanded ACF strategies [4, 9]. To address these trends, we recommend targeted interventions that consider age and gender: focus on early screening for youth, especially girls during menarche, to identify those at higher risk; enhance pediatric diagnostic capacity to reduce missed childhood cases, especially severe forms; and promote behavioral risk reduction among male youth through education on TB exposure and lifestyle factors. Continued support for ACF strategies and advancements in children TB treatment are essential for early detection and effective control.

This study analyzes the temporal changes in the notified incidence of PTB among children and youth in mainland China from 2019 to 2024. Since 2019, the overall ASIR of PTB among children and youth has shown a downward trend, consistent with national PTB trends [2, 41]. This decline in notification rates can be attributed to the implementation of the End-TB Strategy, the expansion of directly observed therapy strategies (DOTS) [12], TB prevention and control measures in schools [9, 34], improvements in social, economic, and medical conditions [11, 12], the development of an integrated TB health system [12], and continued financial investment and project implementation by the Chinese government and PTB control departments [10, 12]. Regarding seasonal distribution, six years of surveillance data in mainland China show that the ASIR for both youth and children consistently reaches its trough in February, likely due to the Spring Festival holidays, which contribute to the decline in TB notifications during this period [9, 12] December also typically sees the lowest TB incidence in China [42], a trend observed in our study among children. Peaks in ASIR occurred in late spring and autumn, particularly in September and October, aligning with patterns seen in other regions of China [9]. Since 2018, reporting peaks have been consistently observed in March, September, and October, suggesting the importance of large-scale PTB screenings linked to major exams and school entries [9]. Among youth, a shift in the peak to May in 2020 may be related to COVID-19 restrictions, which delayed treatment until after restrictions were lifted [43]. Our findings recommend continuing to strengthen large-scale screening during key periods such as school entries and exams, while targeting age groups with slower declines, particularly in children. Additionally, refining TB prevention efforts in schools and improving early diagnosis through improved diagnostic technologies and school health interventions will be key to addressing the disparities in PTB incidence.

This study employed rigorous spatial-temporal methods to analyze the trends of PTB among children and youth in mainland China from 2019 to 2024. Global spatial autocorrelation analysis revealed significant clustering of PTB cases at the PLADs level since 2019, with local spatial autocorrelation results indicating that clusters were predominantly located in the southwest and northwest regions. These findings align with the incidence rate maps in this study. Spatial-temporal clustering analysis confirmed that the most significant clusters were located in these regions, suggesting the robustness of the findings. In our study, the southwest (Tibet, Guizhou, Sichuan, Chongqing, and Yunnan) and northwest (Qinghai, Xinjiang, Gansu, Ningxia, and Shaanxi) regions showed higher incidence rates. The southwest region had the highest proportion of TB cases, accounting for 30.4% of the national total, with this proportion increasing over time. Previous studies have identified these regions as high-risk areas for PTB among the general population in China [9, 30, 42] primarily due to factors such as lower economic development, limited healthcare access, and a lack of awareness among residents, particularly in rural and mountainous areas [9]. Our supplementary analyses strengthen the epidemiological relevance of these findings and inform integrated TB control strategies, where interventions targeting the general population could also benefit younger groups, and vice versa. Our findings further confirmed that, except for Tibet and Qinghai, where the ASIRs for children and youth are declining slowly, all other provinces exhibit a downward trend, with the most notable decrease observed in Xinjiang, indicating the success of targeted interventions there. Based on our findings, we recommend targeted prevention and control measures in high-risk areas. These include accurate spatial clustering to guide interventions, enhanced public health education to raise awareness, improved screening and follow-up for TB contacts and high-risk groups, better access to healthcare services, and quicker referral of newly diagnosed PTB patients to specialized treatment centers to minimize transmission [12].

To operationalize the above subgroup-specific recommendations, we propose a targeted framework aligned with the WHO’s End TB Strategy and China’s Seven-Year TB Plan (2024–2030) [44], focusing on four key components: (1) updating the national TB risk atlas for targeted resource allocation, (2) implementing precision interventions such as school screenings, mobile X-ray units for rural areas, and bilingual education for ethnic minorities, (3) upgrading diagnostics for pathogen-negative cases and enhancing real-time outbreak detection via the national TB surveillance System, and (4) establishing performance-based incentives and a multi-sectoral fund to support high-risk populations. This integrated approach will enable scalable, evidence-based interventions tailored to local needs, improving TB detection and control across China.

This study has several limitations. First, underreporting of TB cases was inevitable due to the use of notification data collected from the existing surveillance system [45–47]. Despite this, the 419,000 new PTB patients in our study are still representative of the PTB situation among children and youth nationwide. Second, the data from surveillance system only contain limited variables, so we could not assess potential factors associated with clustering, such as socio-economic conditions [48], elevation/topography, and meteorological factors [49, 50]. Finally, this study did not analyze incidence trends at levels below the provincial level, but we plan to address these regional differences in future research. Despite these limitations, the findings from this study should be valuable for policy decisions and provide a foundation for future studies to resolve the limitations and obtain more precise results.

Conclusion

This study is the first to comprehensively analyze the trends in the epidemiological characteristics of PTB among children and youth in mainland China from 2019 to 2024 using national surveillance data. The key demographic findings show that students and farmers are the most affected, with an increasing proportion of ethnic minorities, students, patients in the southwest, ACF-detected cases, DR-TB patients, and those with positive bacteriological tests over time. The incidence of PTB varied by age and gender, with more male than female patients overall; however, between ages 9 and 15, female cases outnumbered males. While the ASIR declined more rapidly for youth than for children, the proportion of children and females steadily increased. Regarding temporal trends, the overall ASIR has decreased since 2019, with yearly troughs in February and peaks in late spring and autumn, particularly in September and October. Notably, a shift in the peak occurred in May 2020 for youth. Spatiotemporally, high PTB clusters persisted in the southwest and northwest, with the southwest region seeing the highest proportion of cases, which has grown over time. Most provinces showed a downward trend in ASIR, with the most significant decrease observed in Xinjiang, while Tibet and Qinghai saw a slower decline. Our findings suggest that TB care and prevention strategies in mainland China have successfully reduced the overall PTB burden among children and youth. However, disparities in incidence persist, especially in high-risk regions. To further address this issue, efforts should focus on targeted approaches, tackling the underlying social and structural factors that contribute to increased risk, enhancing regional public health monitoring, and implementing specific interventions in high-incidence areas.

Postoperative adjuvant therapy for pT3N0M0 esophageal carcinoma: does radiotherapy offer added benefit beyond chemotherapy?
Authors: Tian Yang, Xiang Wang, Jinyi Liu, Ruixia Ma, Lei Wu, Qifeng Wang, Yunxiang Qi, Yi Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and purposeThe optimal postoperative adjuvant treatment for pathological T3N0M0 (pT3N0M0) thoracic esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (TESCC) remains unclear. This study evaluated whether adding radiotherapy to postoperative chemotherapy provides an additional survival benefit in these patients.MethodsWe retrospectively reviewed 1,090 TESCC patients treated at Sichuan Cancer Hospital (2009–2020). After applying inclusion and exclusion criteria, 356 patients who received adjuvant therapy were analyzed: 274 underwent surgery plus postoperative chemotherapy (S+POCT) and 82 received surgery plus postoperative chemoradiotherapy (S+POCRT). Propensity score matching balanced baseline characteristics between groups. Overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) were compared, and survival predictors were assessed using a Cox proportional hazards model.ResultsBefore matching, the S+POCRT group had more female patients, and patients with vascular or perineural invasion were more likely to receive chemoradiotherapy. After matching (82 patients per group), baseline characteristics were balanced. The 5-year OS was 70.9% for the entire cohort. In the matched cohort, 5-year OS rates were 65.3% (S+POCT) versus 71.4% (S+POCRT), and 5-year DFS rates were 63.3% versus 70.9%, respectively. No statistically significant differences in OS or DFS were observed between the two groups. Multivariate analysis indicated that neither postoperative chemotherapy nor chemoradiotherapy independently improved OS. In the subgroup analysis, patients with tumors located in the lower third of the esophagus were more likely to derive a significant DFS benefit from S+POCRT but no subgroup showed a statistically significant OS benefit from S+POCRT compared with S+POCT.ConclusionsFor pT3N0M0 TESCC patients undergoing R0 resection without neoadjuvant therapy, postoperative chemotherapy alone provides comparable survival to chemoradiotherapy in the overall retrospective cohort. These findings suggest that the routine addition of postoperative radiotherapy to chemotherapy may not provide a clear survival advantage in the overall pT3N0M0 TESCC population. Further prospective studies are needed to identify patients who may benefit from postoperative radiotherapy.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13014-026-02886-x.Keywords: Esophageal cancer, Adjuvant therapy, Chemotherapy, Chemoradiotherapy, Overall survival

Introduction

Esophageal cancer (EC) carries a poor prognosis and high mortality worldwide. According to the latest statistics from the National Cancer Center, approximately 224,000 new cases of EC and 187,500 related deaths were reported in China in 2022, ranking EC as the seventh most common cancer and the fifth leading cause of cancer-related mortality [1]. More than 90% of EC cases are pathologically diagnosed as esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) [2]. Among these, pT3N0M0 thoracic esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (TESCC)—a locally advanced cancer without regional lymph node or distant metastasis—represents a potentially curable subgroup. Although neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy followed by esophagectomy is the standard of care for T3 ESCC, many patients still undergo upfront surgery because radiotherapy can increase operative complexity and postoperative complications [3, 4]. Moreover, some patients initially staged as cT1–2N0M0 are upstaged to pT3 after resection, for whom upfront surgery is guideline-recommended [5]. Although observation is standard after an R0 resection, adjuvant therapy remains critical when final pathology upstages the tumor to pT3N0M0. The seemingly favorable N0 status is offset by the pT3 depth of invasion, which carries a substantial risk of occult micrometastases and locoregional recurrence [6].

There remains considerable debate regarding the indications for and selection of adjuvant therapy regimens in patients with pT3N0M0 EC. Studies suggest postoperative chemotherapy (POCT) improves survival versus surgery alone [7, 8], and multiple reports indicate postoperative radiotherapy (PORT) also yields survival benefits [9–12]. Whether adding radiotherapy to POCT confers superior survival in pT3N0M0 EC remains uncertain. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate whether concurrent chemoradiotherapy following R0 resection improves outcomes in patients with pT3N0M0 TESCC compared with chemotherapy alone.

Methods

Patient selection

This retrospective study was conducted at Sichuan Cancer Hospital with approval from the institutional ethics committee. Between January 2009 and December 2020, a total of 1,090 patients with pT3N0M0 ESCC underwent esophagectomy at our center. Patients were included if they met the following criteria: (1) pathological confirmation of squamous cell carcinoma, (2) tumor located in the thoracic esophagus, (3) completion of radical resection, (4) pathological stage T3N0M0 according to the 8th edition of the AJCC TNM staging system, and (5) receipt of POCT or postoperative chemoradiotherapy (POCRT). Exclusion criteria were: (1) histological diagnosis of adenocarcinoma or other histologic types, (2) prior neoadjuvant therapy, (3) history of previous malignancies, (4) incomplete resection (R1 or R2), and (5) presence of distant lymph node or organ metastasis (N1–3 or M1). After applying these criteria, 356 eligible patients were enrolled and divided into two groups: the surgery plus POCT (S+POCT) group and the surgery plus POCRT (S+POCRT) group. All surgeries achieved R0 resection. The study flowchart is presented in Fig. 1. Clinical characteristics were systematically collected and analyzed, including age, sex, smoking status, alcohol consumption, histological grade, tumor location, type of surgery, vascular invasion, perineural invasion, lymph node status, and disease stage.

Surgery

The choice of surgical approach and technique was determined by the tumor’s anatomical location and the operating surgeon’s preference. Procedures included either a standard McKeown esophagectomy or an Ivor–Lewis esophagectomy, both incorporating at least two-field (thoracic and abdominal) lymph node dissection. Radical resection consisted of a right transthoracic subtotal esophagectomy with systematic lymphadenectomy of the abdominal and mediastinal lymph nodes, and in some cases, the cervical nodes.

Adjuvant chemotherapy

In the absence of established guidelines for postoperative adjuvant therapy in EC, the decision to administer POCT was based on physician judgment and the patient’s clinical condition. Cisplatin-based regimens formed the backbone of POCT, with a median of three cycles administered (range: 2–6).

Adjuvant radiotherapy

Adjuvant radiotherapy was initiated 4–8 weeks after surgery using 6- or 8-MV photons delivered via a linear accelerator. The clinical target volume (CTV) was defined as encompassing the tumor bed along with the high-risk lymphatic drainage regions. The prescribed total dose ranged from 45 to 60 Gy; a radiation dose of 60 Gy was administered when residual lymph node involvement was suspected, delivered in 25–30 fractions (1.8–2.0 Gy per fraction) over 5–6 weeks.

Follow-up

At each follow-up visit, patients underwent a comprehensive clinical assessment, including physical examination, blood biochemistry, and ultrasonography. Computed tomography (CT) scans were performed every six months, and endoscopic examinations were conducted when clinically indicated. Follow-up continued until June 2024 or until death, whichever occurred first. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the interval from the date of surgical resection to death from any cause; patients alive at the time of analysis were censored at the date of last confirmed follow-up. Disease-free survival (DFS) was defined as the interval from surgery to the first documented disease recurrence or death from any cause, whichever occurred first. Patients alive without recurrence were censored at the last date on which disease-free status was objectively confirmed. Locoregional recurrence was defined as recurrence at the anastomotic site, tumor bed, or regional lymphatic drainage area. Distant metastasis was defined as recurrence outside the locoregional field, including distant organ metastasis or non-regional lymph node metastasis.

Statistical analysis

Comparisons of baseline patient characteristics and postoperative outcomes were performed using analysis of variance (ANOVA) for continuous variables and the chi-square test for categorical variables. Propensity score matching (PSM) was performed with a 1:1 ratio and a 0.05 caliper to reduce baseline covariate imbalance between the treatment groups. Survival outcomes, OS and DFS, were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, with intergroup differences assessed by the log-rank test. Multivariate Cox proportional hazards models were applied to evaluate the association between clinicopathological variables and OS and to identify independent prognostic factors for survival. Statistical assessments were performed with R (v4.0.4), with a two-sided significance level of 0.05, 95% confidence intervals were reported.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 356 patients were included in this study after applying the inclusion and exclusion criteria, comprising 274 patients in the S+POCT group and 82 patients in the S+POCRT group. The baseline characteristics of the patient cohorts, stratified by adjuvant treatment patterns, are summarized in Table 1. Significant differences were observed in sex distribution, angioinvasion, and perineural invasion between the two groups in the initial baseline analysis. The S+POCT group had a significantly lower proportion of female patients than the S+POCRT group, and the presence of vascular or perineural invasion was associated with a higher likelihood of receiving S+POCRT. To reduce potential confounding from imbalanced covariates, PSM was performed. After PSM, 82 patients remained in each treatment group. The proportion of patients with perineural invasion in the S+POCT group increased from 19.34% before matching to 28.05% after matching, while the proportion of patients with angioinvasion increased from 10.58% to 30.49%. The distributions of baseline clinical and pathological variables, including angioinvasion and perineural invasion, were comparable between the two groups (Table 1).

Survival

The median follow-up time for the entire study population was 60.10 months (range: 1.37–136.83 months). The median survival time for all pT3N0M0 patients in the cohort was 102.27 months, with a 3-year OS rate of 79.1% (95% CI: 74.9%–83.7%) and a 5-year OS rate of 70.9% (95% CI: 65.9%–76.3%). In the S+POCT group, the 3- and 5-year DFS rates were 73.8% (95% CI: 68.6%–79.4%) and 68.2% (95% CI: 62.5%–74.4%), respectively, which did not differ from the S+POCRT group (3-year DFS: 74.7%, 95% CI: 65.6%–85.0%; 5-year DFS: 70.9%, 95% CI: 61.2%–82.2%, p = 0.99, Fig. 2a). The 3- and 5-year OS rates in the S+POCT group were 80.3% (95% CI: 75.5%–85.4%) and 70.9% (95% CI: 65.2%–77.1%), compared with 75.4% (95% CI: 66.3%–85.7%) and 71.4% (95% CI: 61.6%–82.9%), respectively, in the S+POCRT group, no significant differences were observed between the two groups (p = 0.56, Fig. 2b).

Study cohort: disease-free survival in the whole group (A) and overall survival in the whole group (B), disease-free survival in the matched sample (C), and overall survival in the match sample (D). CT, chemotherapy; CRT, chemoradiotherapy

These findings were confirmed in the matched samples. In the S+POCT group, the 3- and 5-year DFS rates were 73.9% (95% CI: 64.9%–84.2%) and 63.3% (95% CI: 53.5%–74.9%), respectively, which did not differ from those in the S+POCRT group (3-year DFS: 74.7%, 95% CI: 65.6%–85.0%; 5-year DFS: 70.9%, 95% CI: 61.2%–82.2%, p = 0.77, Fig. 2c). The 3- and 5-year OS rates in the S+POCT group were 78.7% (95% CI: 70.2%–88.3%) and 65.3% (95% CI: 55.5%–76.9%), compared with 75.4% (95% CI: 66.3%–85.7%) and 71.4% (95% CI: 61.6%–82.9%), respectively, in the S+POCRT group (p = 0.90, Fig. 2d).

Multivariate Analyses

The multivariate Cox regression analysis identified no independent association between postoperative adjuvant treatment modality and OS among patients with pT3N0M0 EC (Table 2). The number of lymph nodes removed (≥ 15 vs. <15; HR = 0.56, 95% CI: 0.39–0.81, p = 0.002) was identified as an independent prognostic factor for OS. After PSM, multivariate analysis revealed that the number of lymph nodes removed was an independent prognostic factor for OS (≥ 15 vs. <15; HR = 0.24, 95% CI: 0.13–0.42, p < 0.001), whereas postoperative adjuvant treatment modality (POCT or POCRT) was not (p = 0.7). The analysis showed that lower-third tumor location was independently associated with longer OS (HR = 0.11, 95% CI: 0.02–0.60, p = 0.01). In addition, vascular invasion was confirmed as an independent prognostic factor, with patients exhibiting angioinvasion experiencing worse OS after PSM (HR = 2.26, 95% CI: 1.04–4.87, p = 0.038).

Recurrence pattern

After PSM, recurrence and metastasis patterns were further compared between the S+POCT and S+POCRT groups. Locoregional recurrence occurred in 15 of 82 patients in the S+POCT group and in 8 of 82 patients in the S+POCRT group. The cumulative locoregional recurrence curve showed a numerically lower recurrence risk in the S+POCRT group than in the S+POCT group; however, the difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.3, Supplement 1 A). Distant metastasis occurred in 13 patients in the S+POCT group and in 11 patients in the S+POCRT group. The cumulative distant metastasis curves were largely overlapping between the two groups, with no statistically significant difference observed (p = 0.9, Supplement 1B).

Subgroup analysis

Exploratory subgroup analyses were performed in the matched cohort to evaluate the association between treatment modality and OS or DFS across clinically relevant subgroups. In the OS subgroup analysis, no subgroup showed a statistically significant survival benefit from S+POCRT compared with S+POCT (Fig. 3). A trend toward improved OS with S+POCRT was observed in patients with lower-third tumors, but this did not reach statistical significance (HR = 0.13, 95% CI: 0.02–1.06, p = 0.057). The DFS subgroup analysis is shown in Supplement 2. In this analysis, most subgroups showed no significant DFS difference between the two treatment groups, except for patients with lower-third tumors, in whom S+POCRT was associated with improved DFS (HR = 0.09, 95% CI: 0.01–0.68, p = 0.020).

Discussion

Surgical resection remains the primary therapeutic modality for patients with pT3N0M0 TESCC, particularly when they have been clinically understaged before surgery and have not received neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy as the initial treatment approach [13]. This study specifically investigated whether the addition of PORT to POCT could provide additional survival benefit in patients with pT3N0M0 TESCC who did not receive neoadjuvant therapy and underwent R0 resection. Unlike many previous studies that compared POCT or PORT with surgery alone, the present study directly compared two adjuvant treatment strategies: S+POCRT and S+POCT. The results showed that, in both the overall cohort and the propensity score-matched cohort, S+POCRT did not significantly improve OS or DFS compared with S+POCT. Multivariate Cox regression analysis also demonstrated that the type of postoperative adjuvant treatment was not an independent prognostic factor for OS. In contrast, lower thoracic tumor location, the number of dissected lymph nodes, and angioinvasion status were more closely associated with OS. These findings suggest that, for the overall population of patients with pT3N0M0 TESCC, the routine addition of radiotherapy to POCT may not provide a clear additional survival benefit.

Although pT3N0M0 TESCC is pathologically characterized by negative lymph nodes and absence of distant metastasis, this does not necessarily indicate a low-risk population [14, 15]. The pT3 category indicates that the tumor has penetrated the muscularis propria and invaded the adventitia, reflecting marked local aggressiveness [8]. Even after R0 resection, microscopic residual disease may persist in the tumor bed, anastomotic region, or regional lymphatic drainage areas, thereby contributing to locoregional recurrence [16].

In addition, some patients may have inadequate lymph node dissection or occult lymph node metastasis, leading to understaging as N0 [15]. Our previous study further emphasized the importance of lymph node-related factors in risk stratification for patients with pT3N0M0 TESCC [17]. In that study, we found that patients with pT3N0M0 TESCC who had suspicious small unresected lymph nodes on preoperative CT had a poorer prognosis and higher risks of overall recurrence and locoregional recurrence, suggesting that pathological N0 status does not completely exclude the possibility of occult nodal disease. These factors may increase the likelihood of both locoregional recurrence and distant metastasis. Therefore, although pT3N0M0 EC has a more favorable prognosis than node-positive disease, it still carries a definite risk of recurrence, which provides an important rationale for further investigation of postoperative adjuvant treatment strategies [14, 18].

Previous studies have demonstrated that POCT or PORT may improve the prognosis of selected patients with pT3N0M0 or locally advanced EC compared with surgery alone [11, 18–20]. For example, the JCOG9204 trial [8] showed that POCT improved DFS in patients with locally advanced EC, with a more pronounced benefit observed in patients with node-positive disease. Our recent study on lymph node ratio (LNR) also showed that LNR could be used to predict the benefit of postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy in patients with ESCC. Specifically, patients with a low LNR derived a significant OS benefit from adjuvant chemotherapy, whereas those with a high LNR had limited benefit from chemotherapy alone and may require more intensified treatment strategies [21]. Our previous study also demonstrated that, compared with surgery alone, POCT significantly improved OS and DFS in patients with pT3N0M0 EC [7]. In addition, several studies have suggested that PORT can reduce locoregional recurrence and improve survival outcomes in patients with pT3N0M0 EC [22, 23]. However, most of these studies compared POCT or PORT with surgery alone and did not directly address a more specific clinical question: whether the addition of radiotherapy can further improve survival when patients have already received POCT. The present study was designed to address this question. Our results showed that the addition of radiotherapy to POCT did not provide significant OS or DFS benefit in the overall population. These findings indicate that POCT and PORT may each have therapeutic value compared with surgery alone, but whether their combination is superior to POCT alone may depend on multiple factors, including recurrence risk, failure patterns, and treatment-related adverse effects.

Analysis of recurrence patterns provides further insight into the findings of this study. The most direct and expected effect of radiotherapy is to improve locoregional control rather than to directly reduce the risk of distant metastasis [9]. In the matched cohort, the number of locoregional recurrences was lower in the S+POCRT group than in the S+POCT group, suggesting that the addition of radiotherapy may have a potential tendency to reduce locoregional recurrence. However, this difference did not reach statistical significance, possibly because of the limited sample size and relatively small number of locoregional recurrence events after matching. In contrast, the incidence of distant metastasis was very similar between the two groups, and the distant metastasis curves almost overlapped. This finding is biologically plausible, because both groups received POCT, which mainly targets potential systemic micrometastatic disease [24, 25]. In other words, S+POCRT may provide a numerical advantage in locoregional control, but this advantage was insufficient to translate into significant DFS or OS benefit in the present cohort. This may explain why POCRT failed to demonstrate a clear survival advantage over POCT in the overall matched population.

Subgroup analysis further suggested that the potential benefit of S+POCRT was not consistently observed across all clinicopathological subgroups. In the OS subgroup analysis, none of the predefined subgroups showed a statistically significant OS benefit from S+POCRT compared with S+POCT. In the DFS subgroup analysis, most subgroups also showed no significant difference, including age, sex, smoking status, drinking status, pathological grade, surgical procedure, angioinvasion, perineural invasion, number of dissected lymph nodes, and AJCC stage. Only among patients with lower thoracic tumors was S+POCRT associated with better DFS. This finding is clinically plausible, because lower thoracic tumors are located close to the esophagogastric junction and have complex lymphatic drainage involving both mediastinal and upper abdominal lymphatic regions, which may increase the risk of locoregional recurrence or occult nodal involvement [26, 27]. Therefore, radiotherapy covering the tumor bed and high-risk lymphatic drainage regions may be more likely to translate into DFS benefit in this subgroup. Nevertheless, this finding should be interpreted as exploratory rather than definitive evidence. Subgroup analyses are limited by small sample size, few events, and wide confidence intervals, and a true difference in treatment effect across subgroups can be more robustly supported only when interaction testing is significant. Therefore, whether lower thoracic tumor location can serve as a criterion for selecting patients for POCRT requires further validation in larger prospective studies.

In recent years, immunotherapy has become an important component of multimodal cancer treatment. The CheckMate 577 trial [28] was the first to demonstrate that adjuvant immunotherapy could improve DFS in patients with esophageal cancer, thereby reshaping the paradigm of postoperative adjuvant treatment. However, because that trial enrolled patients who had received neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy, its applicability to patients treated with upfront surgery alone remains limited. Recently, Fu et al. [29] reported that POCT combined with immunotherapy achieved a 1-year progression-free survival rate of 88.5% in patients with T1b-3N0-3 EC, suggesting that chemotherapy plus immunotherapy may be more effective than chemotherapy alone. Based on these findings, POCT combined with immunotherapy followed by maintenance immunotherapy may provide greater survival benefit for patients with pT3N0M0 EC in the future.

This study has several limitations. First, this was a single-center retrospective study, and treatment selection was inevitably influenced by physician judgment and individualized risk assessment. In real-world clinical practice, patients with angioinvasion, perineural invasion, inadequate lymph node dissection, or other adverse pathological features are more likely to be recommended PORT [30]. Therefore, the S+POCRT group had higher-risk pathological features before matching, which reflects the inherent selection bias of retrospective studies. Although PSM improved the balance of measured baseline variables and made the two groups more comparable in terms of clinicopathological characteristics, PSM cannot completely eliminate selection bias or adjust for unmeasured confounders, such as performance status, comorbidities, postoperative recovery, nutritional status, treatment compliance, radiotherapy-related toxicity, and physician preference. Second, this study covered a relatively long time span, during which chemotherapy regimens and treatment cycles may have changed, making treatment heterogeneity difficult to avoid completely. Third, both subgroup analyses and recurrence pattern analyses were exploratory and were limited by the sample size and number of events after matching, resulting in limited statistical power. Therefore, the findings of this study should not be interpreted as definitive evidence that all patients with pT3N0M0 TESCC do not require PORT. A more appropriate interpretation is that, for the overall population of patients with pT3N0M0 TESCC, routine addition of radiotherapy to POCT did not demonstrate a clear additional survival benefit; however, for selected patients with adverse pathological features or specific recurrence risk patterns, PORT should still be considered cautiously on an individualized basis. Future multicenter prospective studies are needed to further identify which patients may truly benefit from POCRT.

Conclusions

In this single-center retrospective study, the addition of PORT to chemotherapy was not associated with a statistically significant improvement in OS or DFS in the overall cohort of patients with R0-resected pT3N0M0 TESCC who did not receive neoadjuvant therapy. These findings suggest that the routine addition of PORT to chemotherapy may not provide a clear survival advantage in the overall pT3N0M0 TESCC population. Further prospective studies are needed to identify patients who may benefit from postoperative radiotherapy.

Risk factors for renal stone development in adults with primary hyperparathyroidism: a protocol for a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Mohammad Jay, Sorina Andrei, Peter Hoang, Hussein Samhat, Rui Fu, Roland Jones, Lorraine L Lipscombe, Antoine Eskander
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionPrimary hyperparathyroidism (PHPT) is characterised by overactive parathyroid glands and consequent calcium dysregulation. Renal stones (RS) are a recognised complication and are associated with substantial morbidity. However, RS risk factors in PHPT are not well established, and recent international guidelines identify ‘renal stone risk’ as a research priority. We aim to summarise and meta-analyse evidence on prespecified risk factors for RS in adults with PHPT.Methods and analysisThis Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols-aligned protocol describes a systematic review and meta-analysis. MEDLINE, EMBASE and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials will be searched from inception. Eligible designs include prospective and retrospective cohort, case–control and cross-sectional studies of adults (≥18 years) with PHPT; randomised trials, case reports, commentaries and conference abstracts will be excluded. Two reviewers will independently screen records, extract data and assess risk of bias using the Quality in Prognostic Studies tool. Prespecified risk factors are age, sex, urinary calcium, serum calcium, serum parathyroid hormone and serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D. We will pool adjusted ORs (primary) and unadjusted estimates (secondary) using random-effects models, assess heterogeneity with the I² statistic and rate certainty with Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation. Publication bias will be explored when ≥10 studies are available. Literature searches will be updated prior to analyses to ensure currency.Ethics and disseminationEthics approval is not required as only published data will be used. Findings will be disseminated through a peer-reviewed publication and conference presentations.PROSPERO registration numberCRD42024608180.Keywords: calcium & bone, urolithiasis, systematic review, meta-analysis, risk factors

Introduction

Primary hyperparathyroidism (PHPT) is characterised by elevated serum parathyroid hormone (PTH) levels and consequent disruption of calcium metabolism.1 2 The resulting hypercalcaemia can lead to complications, including renal stone (RS) disease and osteoporosis.1 RS is a recognised manifestation of PHPT and a common indication for parathyroidectomy, although its reported prevalence varies substantially across studies depending on clinical setting and method of ascertainment.2,7 RS, including PHPT-related RS, is associated with clinically important morbidity, such as pain, recurrence and healthcare utilisation, highlighting the importance of identifying patients with PHPT who are at higher risk of stone formation.8,11

PHPT treatment guidelines recommend parathyroidectomy for patients who meet established surgical criteria, including RS disease.12,14 Early identification of patients with PHPT at high risk of developing RS has important implications for optimising monitoring schedules, implementing preventative strategies and prioritising parathyroidectomy.14,18 Although multiple systematic reviews have examined RS risk factors in the general population, their applicability to PHPT is uncertain, given that this population experiences disease-specific changes in calcium metabolism.19,22 For instance, male sex and hypercalciuria are well-established risk factors in broader RS literature, whereas associations with age have been heterogeneous across studies; whether these risk factors exhibit a consistent direction or magnitude of associations with RS risk in individuals with PHPT is uncertain.2023,30 Equally important is whether PHPT-specific drivers of incident or recurrent RS—such as excessively high serum calcium or PTH—operate independently or in conjunction with known risk factors from the general population.22 31 32 Most evidence to date has focused on the hallmark biochemical features of PHPT (ie, serum calcium and PTH levels) and patient demographic characteristics such as age and sex.22 31 32 However, findings across studies have been inconsistent32; while some studies suggested that RS risk is associated with younger age, higher serum calcium and higher urine calcium, other studies did not replicate these findings.422 33,37 Studies which found different RS risk factors in PHPT compared with the general population suggest that RS risk profile in PHPT is unique.32 34 37

Further complicating our understanding, vitamin D metabolism may also influence RS risk among patients with PHPT,38 39 although available evidence is heterogeneous and depends on which vitamin D measure is considered.436 40,42 Mechanistic studies in PHPT have linked elevated circulating 1,25 vitamin D, the biologically active vitamin D metabolite, to increased intestinal calcium absorption and hypercalciuria, providing a plausible pathway for stone formation.38 This is distinct from 25-hydroxyvitamin D, which reflects vitamin D nutritional status and is the measure most commonly assessed in clinical practice. Vitamin D deficiency is prevalent in PHPT, and cautious repletion of 25-hydroxyvitamin D is widely recommended to optimise skeletal health, with appropriate monitoring for hypercalcaemia or hypercalciuria.43 44 Notably, randomised trials and contemporary guidelines in broader populations have not demonstrated a consistent increase in RS events with vitamin D supplementation alone,43 44 whereas combined calcium and vitamin D supplementation has been associated with a modestly increased stone risk in some settings.45 46 Observational studies in PHPT have reported inconsistent associations between circulating vitamin D levels and RS risk, and it remains unclear whether vitamin D status or active vitamin D metabolites independently contribute to stone formation in this population.436 40,42 Collectively, these uncertainties demonstrate the need for a systematic synthesis that distinguishes between vitamin D measures and critically appraises their associations with RS risk in adults with PHPT.

In addition to the biochemical and demographic factors most commonly studied in PHPT, other determinants of urinary calcium excretion, such as ethnicity, dietary calcium intake and urinary sodium, may also influence RS risk. However, these factors are variably defined and inconsistently reported across the PHPT literature, limiting their suitability for quantitative synthesis.47,49 Future studies should examine their independent and joint contributions using standardised definitions and measurements.

Recognising these knowledge gaps, the latest international PHPT guideline highlights ‘[renal] stone risk’ as a research priority, underscoring the need to establish evidence-based risk stratification in this population.14 We therefore aim to conduct a systematic review and meta-analysis to consolidate and critically appraise available evidence on key demographic and biochemical risk factors of RS in PHPT. Although a narrative review in 2011 summarised the RS risk factors in PHPT, no systematic review or meta-analysis has been conducted.32 By integrating data from a broad range of study designs and settings, we aim to provide a clearer understanding of which patients with PHPT are most susceptible to RS development, ultimately informing clinical decision-making.

Objectives

The primary objective is to systematically review and meta-analyse evidence on the association between six prespecified risk factors (sex, age, serum calcium, urinary calcium, serum PTH and serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D) and the development of RS in adults (≥18 years) with PHPT, in outpatient or inpatient settings. For each risk factor, the comparator will be the alternative category of that factor (eg, male vs female, higher vs lower calcium). The secondary objective is to evaluate the association of these risk factors with recurrent RS in this population.

Methods and analysis

Study design

Our systematic review will be conducted in accordance with the guidelines outlined in the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions.50 This protocol was reported following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2015 checklist (online supplemental file S1)51 and was registered with PROSPERO: an international prospective register of systematic reviews on 14 November 2024 (registration ID: CRD42024608180).52

Eligibility criteria and outcomes

Study type

We will include full-length peer-reviewed prospective cohort, retrospective cohort, case–control and cross-sectional studies. For cohort studies, the starting point (time 0) will be the diagnosis of PHPT, with follow-up measuring the development of RS. We will not restrict the time duration between PHPT diagnosis and RS development as our primary outcome is RS occurrence irrespective of the time of PHPT diagnosis. This approach reflects the clinical reality that PHPT may remain undiagnosed for prolonged periods, such that RS events can occur before formal diagnosis,22 53 and RS risk may also persist years after definitive treatment (ie, parathyroidectomy).54 55 Importantly, the time course over which RS risk evolves relative to diagnosis or surgery has not been well established and no consensus threshold exists to define ‘attributable’ timing; therefore, to avoid arbitrary exclusions, our primary objective is to evaluate prognostic associations rather than to infer causality or treatment effects.14 56 Additionally, we will not apply any language, geographical or time restrictions to the search. Google Translate will be used to translate non-English studies if necessary.57 Studies will have to report sufficient data to allow for directly or indirectly estimating ORs, relative risks (RR) or HRs with 95% CIs for at least one of the predefined risk factors.

We will exclude studies that only include paediatric patients (<18 years old), case series (ie, those with no quantitative data or fewer than five patients), case reports, commentaries, editorials, non-human studies, review articles and conference abstracts. Clinical trials will also be excluded because, although RS outcomes are reported in some interventional studies,16 58 59 these trials are primarily designed to estimate treatment effects and typically do not report baseline covariate-RS associations or adjusted prognostic factor estimates for RS outcomes in a form suitable for prognostic factor synthesis.

Participants

Adults (≥18 years old) with symptomatic or asymptomatic PHPT will be included. Studies presenting data for both adults and children will only be included if the relevant data are presented separately for adults. The diagnostic criteria for PHPT will be based on internationally recognised standards.14 60 As most guidelines exclude normocalcaemic PHPT in the formal definition of PHPT, we will exclude these patients.60 This approach also minimises potential ascertainment bias, as normocalcaemic PHPT is frequently diagnosed during evaluation for RS disease. Studies presenting data on both hypercalcaemic and normocalcaemic PHPT patients will only be included if the data for these groups are reported separately. We will not exclude studies that report RS outcomes occurring after parathyroidectomy. However, to minimise exposure misclassification and confounding, we will include only those effect estimates in which index prognostic factors were measured preparathyroidectomy (ie, reflecting the untreated PHPT state). Accordingly, prognostic factor measurements obtained after parathyroidectomy will be excluded. Studies including participants who underwent parathyroidectomy will remain eligible provided that prognostic factors were assessed preoperatively, regardless of whether RS events were ascertained before or after surgery.

Index prognostic factor

Studies must include at least one of the following a priori risk factors: (1) age, (2) sex, (3) urinary calcium, (4) serum calcium, (5) serum PTH and (6) serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D. Other variables that may influence urinary calcium excretion (eg, ethnicity, dietary calcium intake, urinary sodium) are reported inconsistently across studies and are therefore not included among the prespecified risk factors for quantitative synthesis. When reported, these variables will be extracted and summarised descriptively.

Comparator

For each risk factor, the comparator will be patients in an alternative category of the risk factor.

Outcome

(1) The primary outcome will be incident and recurrent RS development during any time frame relative to PHPT diagnosis. This outcome will provide a comprehensive assessment of RS burden in PHPT, capturing both new and recurrent RS events to characterise the full spectrum of RS. The lack of established risk factors and systematic reviews in this area underscores the need for robust data on the overall risk and natural history of RS in PHPT.1861,63 (2) The secondary outcomes will be recurrent RS development. Given that prior RS are a strong predictor of recurrence, clinicians often adopt more intensive management for these patients, including closer monitoring and higher likelihood of parathyroidectomy.62 64 Focusing on recurrent RS will clarify whether a ‘one-size-fits-all’ approach to RS risk factors is adequate or if tailored prevention strategies are required for these higher-risk patients. (3) As an exploratory outcome, we will assess the mean difference of continuous risk factors between RS and no-RS groups to provide a clinically meaningful interpretation.65 66 The definition of RS will be based on internationally recognised standards, primarily based on the presence of symptoms or radiographic findings.11 67 68

Timing

For the primary analysis, we will assess RS occurrence at any time relative to PHPT diagnosis, as reported in the included studies, to capture the overall burden of RS in PHPT. This approach reflects the clinical reality that PHPT may remain undiagnosed for prolonged periods and that RS may occur before formal diagnosis.22 53 Because the objective of this review is to evaluate associations between prespecified prognostic factors and RS, rather than to establish causal effects or treatment efficacy, and because no consensus exists regarding an attributable time window, we do not impose strict temporal restrictions between PHPT diagnosis and RS occurrence in the primary analysis.56 69 This approach avoids arbitrary timing cut-offs that could exclude clinically meaningful events, while allowing capture of the full burden of RS in PHPT.

Setting

Any healthcare (eg, inpatient, outpatient), geographical (eg, urban, rural), and study-specific (eg, single-centre, population based) will be included.

Information sources

The following online databases will be searched from their inception: Medline (OVID interface, 1946 onwards), Embase (OVID interface, 1947 onwards) and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (Wiley interface, current issue). References of all eligible articles will be reviewed for additional studies meeting the eligibility criteria (backward citation searching).70 Databases containing grey literature will not be searched as our focus is on peer-reviewed studies. Our initial search began in September 2024 and will be re-run in November 2025. The anticipated end date for this review is February 2026.

Search strategy

Literature search strategies will be developed using a combination of subject headings (MeSH [Medical Subject Headings] and EMTREE [Embase subject headings]) and keywords. Database searches will be conducted with the aid of an experienced health information librarian. Two concepts will be included: (1) primary hyperparathyroidism and (2) RS. These two concepts will then be combined using the ‘AND’ operator to obtain final search results. We will limit the search to only studies involving human subjects and will not apply any language restrictions. A preliminary search strategy is provided in online supplemental file S2.

Study records

Data management

All citations from the literature search will be imported into Covidence and duplicate studies will be removed.71 Following the completion of screening, a PRISMA flow diagram will be generated using Covidence.72

Selection process

Two reviewers will independently screen titles and abstracts of studies uploaded in Covidence, applying prespecified eligibility criteria. The full texts of studies deemed potentially relevant will then be independently reviewed for inclusion in the final analysis by the same reviewers. Reasons for excluding studies will be recorded. Reviewers will resolve disagreements by discussion, and a third reviewer will adjudicate the unresolved disagreements. A calibration exercise involving a small sample of studies (eg, three) will be conducted before screening begins to ensure consistency and refine eligibility criteria. Reviewers will not be blinded to journal titles, study authors or institutions.

Data extraction

For each included study, two reviewers will extract data independently and in duplicates and record the data in standardised data collection tables in Microsoft Excel. These tables will be organised in the recommended PICOTS format for prognostic studies (patient, index prognostic factor, comparator prognostic factor, outcome, time and setting),73 and will initially be piloted on three studies to allow necessary adjustments for accuracy and completeness. The tables will be created using a modified version of the Checklist for Critical Appraisal and Data Extraction for Systematic Reviews of prediction modelling studies for prognostic factors.74 75 Reviewers will compare the collected data, resolve disagreements by discussion, and a third reviewer will adjudicate the unresolved disagreements.

Data items

We will record the following data from the included studies where available: (1) Basic study details: first author’s last name, publication year, study design, country of origin, sample size, setting, data sources (eg, self-report, chart review, administrative data), follow-up duration and loss to follow-up, funding sources. (2) Patient demographics: age and sex. (3) Biochemical risk factors: 24-hour urine calcium, total serum calcium, serum ionised calcium, serum PTH, serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D and 1,25 vitamin D. Units of measurement for all biochemical variables and the time at which the patient demographics and the biochemical risk factors were assessed in relation to RS development will be recorded. (4) Clinical history: time of PHPT diagnosis in relation to RS development, criteria for PHPT diagnosis, the temporal relationship between RS and PHPT diagnosis (classified as prediagnosis, postdiagnosis or timing unclear/not reported). (5) Outcome measures: Presence of RS (specify if RS are incident or recurrent), criteria for RS diagnosis. (6) Other relevant clinical information: proportion of participants undergoing parathyroidectomy during the study period, osteoporosis and treatment with calcium supplement (online supplemental file S3).

Whenever available, we will extract adjusted and unadjusted OR, RR or HR, and their measures of variance. If these measures of association are not available, the relevant descriptive statistics (eg, mean, median) will be recorded to estimate the unadjusted OR.69 76 We will also record the variables used to adjust for each risk factor. We will collect data on all a priori defined risk factors and outcome-compatible information, regardless of RS definition or timing of RS diagnosis. We will convert data from different studies into consistent units of measurement to ensure uniformity.

Missing data

In cases of missing or unclear data, authors will be contacted up to two times, allowing 2 weeks for a response per attempt. If no responses are received regarding essential study eligibility criteria, the study will be excluded.77 Similarly, if essential data required for analysis are not obtained, the study will be excluded from the relevant analysis but will contribute to the descriptive analyses.78

Quality appraisal

Two reviewers will independently assess each risk factor’s risk of bias using the Quality in Prognostic Studies tool. Reviewers will resolve disagreements through discussion, and a third reviewer will adjudicate the unresolved disagreements. This tool evaluates six domains: study participation, study attrition, prognostic factor measurement, outcome measurement, study confounding and statistical analysis and reporting.79 Each domain will be rated as high, moderate or low risk of bias.79 This tool will be initially piloted using three randomly selected studies to confirm consistency across reviewers. A study will be deemed low risk overall if all domains are rated as low risk. Conversely, it will be considered high risk if at least one domain is rated high. Any ratings between these categories will be rated as moderate risk of bias.80 81 Traffic-light plots will be generated using R V.4.4.1.

Data synthesis

We will conduct meta-analyses on all prespecified risk factors and outcomes reported by at least two studies, using the generic inverse variance method within a random-effects model to account for between-study heterogeneity.82 83 To ensure robust estimation of uncertainty in the overall effect size, the Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman adjustment will be applied when at least four studies are included in the meta-analysis.84 When fewer than four studies are available, the Wald method will be used instead.85 The restricted maximum likelihood method will be used to estimate between-study variance.86 Between-study heterogeneity will be assessed by visually inspecting forest plots for overlapping point estimates and CIs, and formally evaluated using the χ2 test and I² statistic.87 Forest plots, summary effect measures with 95% CIs and prediction intervals will be generated using R V.4.4.1.

Primary analysis

We will meta-analyse adjusted risk factors. We will report summary adjusted ORs with 95% CIs for the association with RS. We will pool all adjusted risk factors regardless of the adjustment variables, without requiring a minimum set of adjustment factors.76 This approach to pooling adjusted effect estimates irrespective of the specific covariates is chosen for two primary reasons: (1) The risk factors and confounders for RS in PHPT are not well-established.32 Consequently, we expect the included studies to vary widely in the variables they adjust for. Pooling all adjusted estimates allows us to capture at least the effect of partial confounding control.88 89 (2) Given the lack of established risk factors, our priority is establishing the direction of risk factors with adequate precision. Maximising the number of studies in the meta-analysis enhances the precision of effect estimates. Previous systematic reviews on prognostic factors have adopted similar approaches.32 76 90 91

Secondary analysis

We will examine the unadjusted risk factors. For these, we will estimate summary unadjusted ORs (uORs) with 95% CIs for the association with RS,92 using the following stepwise approach: (1) Direct use of uORs: If unadjusted ORs are reported by the study, these will be directly used in the meta-analysis. (2) Conversion of relative measures: If studies report RR or HR, HR will be equated to RR, and these measures will be converted to OR.76 93 (3) Calculating ORs from regression coefficients: When regression coefficients are reported without CIs, ORs and their variances will be calculated using the regression coefficients and p values from logistic or linear regression.94 If regression coefficients and CIs are reported, ORs will be calculated using the formula: OR=ebeta, where e=2.718 and β is the regression coefficient.94 95 (4) Calculating ORs from Cox regression and Kaplan-Meier curves: When direct reporting is absent, we will use the number of events and the p values from reported analyses such as log-rank tests or Cox regressions.69 96 If only Kaplan-Meier curves are reported with no available HR, we will reconstruct the raw time-to-event data from Kaplan-Meier curves and use this data to estimate the HR.97 (5) Deriving ORs from 2×2 tables: If no measures of association are described but categories of a risk factor in relation to RS are reported, a 2×2 table will be constructed to calculate the OR. (6) Tertile approach for continuous variables: If neither measures of association nor categorical data are available, continuous variables will be analysed by assuming a normal distribution.76 98 Using the mean and SD, we will create three groups (tertiles) in R V.4.4.1 and calculate the OR for the highest third versus the lowest third of each prognostic factor.76 90 99 100 When only the median and IQR are reported, these values will be converted to mean and SD.99 If means and SDs are reported per group, data will be combined using the approach suggested in the Cochrane Handbook.101 Studies will be excluded if measures of variance are unavailable.102

To ensure the assumption of normality and avoid unstable tertile conversions, we will apply the following criteria. A study will be included in the tertile analysis if at least two of the following three criteria were met: (1) Minimum number of participants in each group >20.103 (2) Coefficient of variation (CV) between 0.12 and 0.6 for both groups.104,107 (3) Maximum group size imbalance: ratio of the larger group to the smaller group <4:1.108 A study will be excluded if any of the following criteria are present: (1) Number of participants in the smallest group <5, (2) CV <0.1 or >0.95 and (3) ratio of the larger to the smallest group >20:1. These criteria were derived from the principles outlined in the Cochrane Handbook, relevant methodology papers and analogous statistical literature to ensure the robustness of the tertile-based calculations.87 101 106 107

If a study provides both adjusted and unadjusted effect sizes for a risk factor, we will only use the adjusted effect size in analysis. Additionally, we will report the mean differences between groups with and without RS for each continuous prognostic factor.

Studies with overlapping populations

All included studies will be described in the patient characteristics table and their data will be used for descriptive analyses. However, to avoid potential bias and inflation of the statistical significance due to data duplication, only one study from each set of studies with overlapping populations will be included in the meta-analysis. Overlapping populations will be identified based on a careful examination of author information (including affiliations), the time period of patient recruitment and, if available, details of the study setting (eg, specific hospitals or clinics). When overlapping populations are identified, we will select the study with the largest sample size, assuming the smaller sample is a subset of the larger study.101 109 110

Biochemical risk factors

Serum total calcium and serum ionised calcium will be included in the same unadjusted OR meta-analysis using the tertile approach. If a study reports both ionised and total calcium, only ionised calcium will be used, as it is considered a more accurate measure.111 Similarly, the uOR for 25-hydroxyvitamin D and 1,25 vitamin D will be synthesised according to the specific vitamin D measure reported.112,114

For the analysis of mean differences in biochemical factors between patients with and without RS, serum total calcium and serum ionised calcium will be analysed separately. However, a single certainty of evidence recommendation will be provided for both, as they represent closely related clinical concepts and because this analysis is exploratory.115 The same analytic approach will be employed for mean differences and certainty of evidence reporting for 25-hydroxyvitamin D and 1,25 vitamin D.

Meta-regression

By mean serum calcium in each study: We hypothesise that the magnitude of the association between the risk factors and RS will be weaker in studies with higher mean serum calcium levels. This is because, at higher average serum calcium levels within a study population, hypercalcaemia is likely to be a more dominant factor in stone formation, potentially overshadowing the influence of other risk factors.121 122

This is because, at higher average serum calcium levels within a study population, hypercalcaemia is likely to be a more dominant factor in stone formation, potentially overshadowing the influence of other risk factors.121 122

By proportion of females in each study: We hypothesise that the magnitude of the association between other risk factors and RS will be weaker in studies with a smaller difference in the proportion of females between the RS and no-RS groups. This indirect assessment is based on the assumption that a smaller difference in the proportion of females between the groups might reflect a population where sex-related factors play a less prominent role in modifying the influence of other risk factors on RS formation.123

Evaluating the credibility of subgroup & meta-regression analyses

Two reviewers will independently grade the credibility of each subgroup effect in duplicate using the Instrument for the Credibility of Effect Modification Analyses criteria.124 Disagreements will be resolved through discussion between the reviewers, and the third reviewer will adjudicate unresolved disagreements. Credibility ratings will be provided and assigned as follows: (1) Very low: all responses are ‘definitely no’ or ‘probably no’. (2) Low: at least two responses are ‘definitely no’. (3) Moderate: one ‘definitely no’ or two ‘probably no’ responses. (4) High: no responses are ‘definitely no’ or ‘probably no’.125

Sensitivity analyses

To confirm the robustness of our findings, we will conduct the following sensitivity analyses: (1) To assess the effect of consistent covariate selection, we will only include studies that adjust for at least serum calcium and either age or sex. (2) To examine the influence of study design, we will restrict the analysis to cohort and case–control studies, as these designs allow for stronger inferences about associations compared with cross-sectional studies.126 (3) To evaluate the impact of risk of bias, we will restrict to studies with low or moderate risk of bias. To evaluate the impact of outcome timing, we will restrict analyses, where data permit, to RS events reported within a 10-year window before or after PHPT diagnosis. If a clinically significant difference is found between the primary analyses and their corresponding sensitivity analyses, we will rate down the certainty of evidence.

Reporting bias

For analyses including ≥10 studies, we will conduct a primary assessment using funnel plots, the trim-and-fill method, Egger’s test for continuous variables and the Harbord test for dichotomous variables.127,129 To complement these assessments, a secondary assessment will be performed using the Doi plot and Luis Furuya-Kanamori index, with values within ±1 interpreted as indicative of no asymmetry.130 131 In instances where the primary and secondary assessments yielded discrepant results, Begg’s test will be employed to provide additional clarification.132 133 The final determination of publication bias will be based on the consensus of multiple analyses, prioritising methods with greater sensitivity and reliability for the specific context.129134,136 For analyses with <10 studies, formal assessments of publication bias will not be conducted due to limited statistical power. Instead, publication bias will be considered ‘unlikely’ if small-study effects are improbable, based on the comprehensiveness of the search strategy and expert knowledge.128 129 137

Confidence in cumulative evidence

Two independent reviewers will assess the overall quality of evidence for each risk factor’s association with the outcomes using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) framework.138 The assessment will be adapted to align with GRADE recommendations for prognostic factor studies.139

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this research.

Ethics and dissemination

No ethics approval is required because only published data will be used. For research ethics or integrity queries, the relevant institutional contact is the Sunnybrook Health Sciences Centre Research Ethics Board (REB) at REB@sunnybrook.ca. Study selection and data extraction will be conducted according to the methods outlined in this protocol. Searches will be run from inception and updated immediately prior to synthesis to ensure the evidence is current. Findings will be reported in accordance with PRISMA 202073 and disseminated via a peer-reviewed journal article and conference presentations.140 Protocol amendments will be documented with dates and rationale, updated in PROSPERO and reported in the final manuscript.

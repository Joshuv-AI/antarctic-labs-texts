Pooled safety analysis of zanubrutinib monotherapy in Asian patients with B-cell malignancies
Authors: J Zhu, J Li, CS Tam, L Wang, J Xu, J Zhang, P Khanna, L Qiu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn patients with B-cell malignancies, treatment with the first-generation Bruton tyrosine kinase (BTK) inhibitor ibrutinib can be limited by toxicities resulting from off-target binding. Zanubrutinib, a next-generation BTK inhibitor, was designed to maximize BTK occupancy and minimize off-target effects.Materials and methodsA post hoc safety analysis was carried out with pooled data from Asian patients with B-cell malignancies who received zanubrutinib in eight clinical trials, including ALPINE, which compared zanubrutinib with ibrutinib. Rates and severity of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), overall exposure-adjusted incidence rates (EAIRs) of AEs of special interest (AESIs; defined as pooled terms), and EAIRs of AESIs over time were assessed.ResultsThe analyses included 406 Asian patients with a median treatment duration of 25.0 months; 38.7% of patients received treatment for ≥36 months. Upper respiratory tract infection (38.2%), pneumonia (26.4%), rash (21.2%), and diarrhea (16.5%) were the most common nonhematologic TEAEs. The most common nonhematologic grade ≥3 TEAEs were pneumonia (16.0%) and upper respiratory tract infection (6.9%). Serious TEAEs occurred in 43.8% of patients; pneumonia (14.5%) was the only event occurring in >10% of patients. TEAEs led to treatment discontinuation in 10.6% of patients and death in 4.9%, mostly due to infections. EAIRs for atrial fibrillation/flutter and hypertension were 0.02 and 0.56 persons per 100 person-months, respectively. In the comparative analysis, most EAIRs of AESIs were numerically lower in patients treated with zanubrutinib versus ibrutinib. Over time, EAIRs of AESIs remained relatively constant or decreased in the pooled Asian population and were similar to or lower than those with ibrutinib for up to 36 months.ConclusionsZanubrutinib was well tolerated in Asian patients with B-cell malignancies. The safety profile in this population was consistent with that in previous global study populations. These findings suggest that long-term zanubrutinib treatment is an appropriate option for Asian patients with B-cell malignancies.Key words: chronic lymphocytic leukemia, non-Hodgkin lymphoma, hypertension, atrial fibrillation

Introduction

Bruton tyrosine kinase (BTK) is a key signaling molecule within the B-cell receptor pathway that supports B-cell proliferation, differentiation, development, and survival.1 BTK inhibitors work by binding to a cysteine residue at the BTK active site. BTK inhibitors have emerged as an important standard of care for B-cell malignancies that exhibit aberrant BTK signaling and have dramatically improved patient outcomes.2 In clinical studies, treatment with ibrutinib, the first-generation BTK inhibitor, was associated with response rates ranging from 86% to 92%.3,4 In long-term safety studies, however, in which patients received 4-8 years of ibrutinib treatment, 11%-24% of patients discontinued ibrutinib due to adverse events (AEs), most commonly atrial fibrillation, hemorrhage, and pneumonia.5, 6, 7, 8, 9 Treatment-related AEs with ibrutinib may result from off-target binding and inhibition of ibrutinib by alternative cysteine-containing kinases.1

Zanubrutinib, a next-generation BTK inhibitor, was designed to exhibit greater selectivity and maximize irreversible BTK occupancy to improve tolerability by minimizing potential off-target effects.10 Zanubrutinib monotherapy was initially granted accelerated approval by the United States Food and Drug Administration in 2019 for previously treated mantle cell lymphoma (MCL) based on overall response rates of >80% in clinical studies.11 It has since been approved for various B-cell malignancies in Western countries and in Asian countries including China, Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia, South Korea, and the Phillippines.1,12, 13, 14

In a population pharmacokinetic analysis, no clinically significant differences in the pharmacokinetics of zanubrutinib were observed based on age, sex, race (Asian, white, other), body weight, or renal impairment (mild, moderate or severe).15

In two randomized phase III studies, ASPEN [relapsed/refractory Waldenström macroglobulinemia (WM) treated with one or more prior lines of therapy or treatment-naive WM unsuitable for standard chemoimmunotherapy] and ALPINE [relapsed/refractory chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL)/small lymphocytic lymphoma (SLL)], patients who received zanubrutinib had lower rates of treatment discontinuation or death due to AEs compared with those who received ibrutinib.16, 17, 18 In ALPINE, at 42.5 months of follow-up, the overall response rate was higher with zanubrutinib compared with ibrutinib (85.6% versus 75.4%, respectively), while ASPEN demonstrated a higher rate of very good partial response with zanubrutinib compared with ibrutinib (28% versus 19%, respectively).16, 17, 18

Furthermore, zanubrutinib was associated with lower incidences of off-target effects, including atrial fibrillation/flutter, compared with ibrutinib.16, 17, 18 Consistent with these findings, a previous pooled safety analysis of zanubrutinib monotherapy [160 mg twice daily (b.i.d.) or 320 mg once daily (od)] using data from 10 clinical trials (N = 1550) found that treatment was generally well tolerated.19

To examine the safety profile of zanubrutinib in Asian patients undergoing treatment of B-cell malignancies, a pooled post hoc safety analysis of data from Asian patients in the clinical trials included in the previous pooled safety analysis was conducted.

Methods

Study design and patients

This analysis was on a subgroup of Asian patients from the previous pooled safety analysis.19 Safety data from Asian patients (defined as enrolled in Asia, including China, Taiwan, and South Korea) were pooled from eight clinical trials (NCT03189524, NCT03206918, NCT03206970, NCT03332173, NCT03846427, NCT02343120, NCT03336333, NCT03734016) (N = 406), predominantly phase II and III, in CLL/SLL, MCL, WM, marginal zone lymphoma, follicular lymphoma (FL), diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), Richter transformation, and hairy cell leukemia (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106086). Patients treated with zanubrutinib monotherapy received 160 mg b.i.d. or 320 mg od. Comparative analysis using data from the zanubrutinib (n = 45) and ibrutinib (n = 43) treatment arms of the phase III, randomized ALPINE study was also conducted. All studies were approved by the independent ethics committee or institutional review board at each participating institution and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Conference on Harmonization Guidelines for Good Clinical Practice. All patients provided written informed consent.

Safety assessments

Treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs) were graded using National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 4.03. The severity of hematologic toxicities in SEQUOIA and ALPINE was graded using the 2008 International Workshop on CLL Severity Grading Scale.20 The following select TEAE preferred terms were considered AEs of special interest (AESIs): anemia, atrial fibrillation and flutter, hemorrhage, hypertension, infections, neutropenia, second primary malignancies, thrombocytopenia, and tumor lysis syndrome. Post hoc analyses of TEAEs, overall exposure-adjusted incidence rates (EAIRs) of AESIs, and EAIRs of AESIs over time were conducted. To account for variation in drug exposure across the trials, EAIRs for AESIs were calculated as the number of patients who experienced a specific AESI divided by the total exposure time. Exposure time was defined as the period from the first dose date to the first event date or to the treatment-emergent period end date if there was no event. This value was multiplied by 100, yielding EAIRs in units of persons per 100 person-months (PM) or persons per 100 person-years (e.g. an EAIR of 5 PM indicates that if 100 patients were each exposed to the study drug for 1 month, an estimated 5 patients would experience the AESI). EAIR assumes that the risk of an event occurring is constant over time and serves as an additional means for evaluating safety events.

Results

Patients

In the pooled Asian population (N = 406), the median age of patients was 61.0 years, 63.8% were male, and 47.3% had CLL/SLL (Table 1). At baseline, 24.9% of patients had a history of hypertension, and 15.0% had a history of cardiac disorders. While on study, 6.4% of patients received concomitant antithrombotic agents.

In the comparative analysis in patients from the randomized, head-to-head ALPINE17 trial, 45 Asian patients received zanubrutinib (160 mg b.i.d.) and 43 received ibrutinib (420 mg od) (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106086; Table 1). All Asian patients in this comparative subset had R/R CLL/SLL. Baseline characteristics were generally similar between the treatment populations (Table 1).

Treatment exposure and study status

In the pooled zanubrutinib Asian population, the median duration of treatment was 25.0 months (range, 0.1-79.1 months) with 38.7% of participants receiving zanubrutinib for ≥36 months. In the head-to-head study population, median zanubrutinib treatment duration was 25.5 months (range, 3.5-39.2 months) and median ibrutinib treatment duration was 23.7 months (range, 0.1-38.5 months). Twenty percent of patients who received zanubrutinib were on treatment for ≥36 months compared with 9.3% of patients who were treated with ibrutinib. At the data cut-off date for each study in this analysis (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106086), 45.6% of patients remained on zanubrutinib. In the comparative analysis of data from ALPINE, 84.4% of patients treated with zanubrutinib and 58.1% of patients treated with ibrutinib were still on treatment at data cut-off.

Total pooled zanubrutinib population: tolerability and common TEAEs

In the pooled analysis, 98.0% of Asian patients who received zanubrutinib monotherapy experienced ≥1 TEAE, with 66.0% experiencing a grade ≥3 TEAE (Supplementary Table S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106086); these findings are consistent with those in the overall pooled population as reported previously (97.9% and 66.9%, respectively). TEAEs considered treatment related by the investigator were reported in 89.9% of Asian patients (grade ≥3, 47.8%). Upper respiratory tract infection (38.2%), pneumonia (26.4%), rash (21.2%), and diarrhea (16.5%) were the most common nonhematologic TEAEs (Figure 1). The most common nonhematologic grade ≥3 TEAEs were pneumonia (16.0%) and upper respiratory tract infection (6.9%). Serious TEAEs occurred in 43.8% of patients, with pneumonia (14.5%) being the only serious TEAE that occurred in >10% of patients.

Any-grade nonhematologic TEAEs in ≥10% or grade ≥3 TEAEs in ≥5% of patients. TEAEs were defined as AEs with an onset date or worsening in severity from baseline (pretreatment) on or after the first dose of zanubrutinib and up to the last zanubrutinib dose date +30 days or initiation of new anticancer therapy, whichever occurred first. Worsening of an event to grade 5 after the last zanubrutinib dose date +30 days and before initiation of new anticancer therapy was also considered treatment emergent. AE, adverse event; TEAE, treatment-emergent adverse event.

In the pooled zanubrutinib population, TEAEs leading to treatment discontinuation were reported in 10.6% of Asian patients (Supplementary Table S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106086). TEAEs leading to treatment discontinuation in ALPINE were less common in Asian patients who received zanubrutinib versus ibrutinib (6.7% versus 14%). Infections were the most common TEAEs that led to treatment discontinuation in the pooled Asian population (total zanubrutinib, 3.7%; ALPINE zanubrutinib, 4.4%; ALPINE ibrutinib, 4.7%). Cardiac disorder TEAEs leading to discontinuation in the Asian population that received zanubrutinib were cardiac failure, cardiopulmonary failure, myocardial infarction, and ventricular extrasystoles (n = 1 each). Ventricular extrasystoles (n = 1) was the only cardiac disorder TEAE leading to zanubrutinib discontinuation in the comparative analysis, and no cardiac disorder TEAEs led to ibrutinib discontinuation. The rate of serious cardiac disorder TEAEs was low in the pooled Asian population that received zanubrutinib (1.7%), and no serious cardiac disorder TEAEs occurred with zanubrutinib or ibrutinib in the comparative analysis.

Death attributed to TEAEs occurred in 4.9% of patients in the pooled Asian population that received zanubrutinib and in 4.4% and 7.0% of patients treated with zanubrutinib and ibrutinib, respectively, in the head-to-head study (Supplementary Table S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106086). Similar to findings for treatment discontinuations, infections (most frequently pneumonia) were the most common TEAEs that led to death (total zanubrutinib, 2.0%; ALPINE zanubrutinib, 4.4%; ALPINE ibrutinib, 2.3%). No cases of coronavirus disease (COVID-19) or COVID-19 pneumonia led to death. Cardiac disorder TEAEs leading to death in the total Asian population that received zanubrutinib were cardiac failure (n = 2), cardiopulmonary failure (n = 1), and myocardial infarction (n = 1). None of these cardiac events were considered related to the study treatment. They occurred in patients aged ≥65 years with advanced B-cell malignancy and presence of confounding comorbidities (one patient with myocardial infarction had a history of coronary heart disease, cerebral infarction, hyperlipemia, and hypertension; one patient with cardiopulmonary failure had pre-existing high tumor burden and medical history of ongoing chronic obstructive pulmonary disease; one patient developed cardiac failure in the setting of diarrhea and mycoplasma pneumoniae infection followed by respiratory failure with normal neutrophil count at the time of heart failure onset; and one patient with medical history of renal insufficiency developed progressive disease before the fatal cardiac failure event). In the comparative analysis, no cardiac disorder TEAEs leading to death occurred in Asian patients treated with zanubrutinib or ibrutinib. Overall, infections were the most common AESI leading to treatment discontinuation or death (Supplementary Table S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106086).

Total pooled zanubrutinib population: EAIRs of AESIs

The EAIRs for the cardiovascular AESIs of atrial fibrillation/flutter and hypertension in the pooled analysis were 0.02 and 0.56 persons per 100 PM, respectively (Figure 2). For noncardiovascular AESIs, EAIRs were <5 persons per 100 PM, except for infections (7.42 persons per 100 PM). Hemorrhage and neutropenia had EAIRs of 2.87 and 3.64 persons per 100 PM, respectively; EAIRs of all other AESIs, including thrombocytopenia, anemia, second primary malignancies, and tumor lysis syndrome, were <2 persons per 100 PM.

Zanubrutinib versus ibrutinib in a head-to-head study: EAIRs of AESIs

After adjusting for exposure, most AESIs were more common with ibrutinib than with zanubrutinib (Figure 3). With the exception of hemorrhage (3.14 versus 2.19 persons per 100 PM), all EAIRs of AESIs were numerically lower in patients treated with zanubrutinib versus ibrutinib. The EAIR for atrial fibrillation/flutter was 0 persons per 100 PM with zanubrutinib versus 0.21 persons per 100 PM with ibrutinib. The EAIR of hypertension was also lower in Asian patients receiving zanubrutinib versus ibrutinib (0.6 versus 0.71 persons per 100 PM, respectively).

EAIRs of AESIs over time

The EAIRs of AESIs remained relatively constant or decreased over time in the pooled zanubrutinib population (Figure 4; time to first event data in Supplementary Figure S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106086). In the comparative analysis, EAIRs of AESIs for zanubrutinib were either similar to or lower than those for ibrutinib for up to 36 months.

Discussion

In this post hoc analysis of pooled safety data from zanubrutinib clinical studies, the findings in Asian patients are generally consistent with those in the overall pooled zanubrutinib population.19 Comparatively, the Asian population examined here was younger and included a higher percentage of patients with MCL, FL, and DLBCL. Zanubrutinib was well tolerated in Asian patients with a diverse array of B-cell malignancies, and TEAEs were generally mild to moderate. TEAEs leading to treatment discontinuation and death were reported in 10.6% and 4.9% of Asian patients, respectively, similar to rates in the overall pooled population (14% and 7%). Also similar to findings in the overall pooled population, infections, hemorrhage, and neutropenia were the most frequently reported AESIs.

Long-term use of ibrutinib can be limited due to AEs, particularly cardiovascular AEs, which may be caused by off-target inhibition of kinases.1 Zanubrutinib was designed with greater selectivity to minimize the off-target effects considered to be primarily responsible for the toxicities associated with ibrutinib. In the comparative analysis conducted in the overall pooled population, zanubrutinib exhibited a more favorable safety profile than ibrutinib. Similar findings were observed in the Asian subgroup, with zanubrutinib treatment exhibiting lower rates of TEAEs leading to treatment discontinuation or death. In the overall population, cardiac disorder TEAEs leading to discontinuation or death were more likely to occur with ibrutinib (4.3% and 1.7%, respectively) compared with zanubrutinib (0.5% and 0.2%). Only one patient experienced a cardiac disorder TEAE leading to zanubrutinib discontinuation in the Asian subgroup, and no cardiac disorder TEAEs led to ibrutinib discontinuation. For both the overall and Asian pooled populations, most EAIRs of AESIs were numerically lower in patients treated with zanubrutinib versus ibrutinib, except for neutropenia and hemorrhage. The EAIR for atrial fibrillation was significantly lower with zanubrutinib versus ibrutinib (P < 0.0001) in the overall population and numerically lower in the Asian population (0 persons per 100 PM with zanubrutinib versus 0.21 persons per 100 PM with ibrutinib).

A limitation of this analysis is that due to its retrospective nature, data were pooled from Asian patients with a variety of B-cell malignancies and treatment histories, which could dilute or inflate the relative influence of some TEAEs. In addition, as previously mentioned, the Asian population examined here had a younger median age and a higher percentage of patients with MCL, FL, and DLBCL which could also represent potential limitations. The comparative analysis had a small number of Asian patients in each treatment arm, particularly patients with exposure to zanubrutinib or ibrutinib over 36 months in ALPINE, which further limits the conclusions that can be drawn from this population. While results from this analysis are encouraging, trials that include a larger number of Asian patients and have prespecified analyses would be beneficial. Furthermore, as these data were collected in the context of clinical trials, additional analyses of real-world evidence will help generate a more comprehensive safety profile for zanubrutinib.

Conclusions

Overall, this analysis demonstrates the favorable safety profile of zanubrutinib in Asian patients with B-cell malignancies, even with extended use. The overall safety profile of zanubrutinib in Asian patients was consistent with that seen in the overall population in previous zanubrutinib studies. Rates of treatment discontinuation were low in the pooled Asian subgroup, and most TEAEs experienced by Asian patients were mild to moderate in severity. These results support zanubrutinib as an appropriate long-term treatment option for Asian patients with B-cell malignancies.

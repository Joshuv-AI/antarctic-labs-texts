Green pediatrics beyond endoscopy: Toward systemic sustainability in celiac care
Authors: Valentina Pucinischi, Giovanni Di Nardo, Maurizio Mennini
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

Brusco et al. present a pioneering evaluation of the carbon footprint of diagnostic and follow‐up pathways in pediatric Celiac Disease. This work highlights how pediatric practice can and should engage in the urgent challenge of environmental sustainability. 1 By quantifying the ecological burden of the no‐biopsy versus biopsy strategy, the authors open a new frontier for clinical research and health policy.

At the same time, their contribution raises broader questions. The study is based on a regional setting, where transport modes and healthcare organization inevitably condition results. Can these findings be generalized to countries with different infrastructures, vehicle fleets, and referral systems? Multicenter and international collaborations will be essential to provide a reliable benchmark.

Another overlooked element is the environmental cost of laboratory testing. Serological diagnosis requires at least two antibody assays, with duplicated appointments, sample handling, and medical waste. Quantifying this “hidden footprint” would enrich future analyses.

The perspective on telemedicine is also stimulating. Remote consultations may reduce travel‐related emissions, yet raise concerns about adherence monitoring, growth assessment, and detection of autoimmune comorbidities. 2 , 3 Moreover, patients with lower educational attainment and income may prefer in‐person visits, highlighting equity considerations. 4

Finally, operating rooms, such as endoscopy ones, generate the greatest share of medical waste but also offer the largest opportunities for improvement by only raising medical awareness and enhancing resources management. 5 A systemic, cross‐disciplinary approach to “green pediatrics” is therefore warranted.

This article deserves praise for initiating such a debate. The next step should be to extend this ecological lens beyond single procedures, moving toward comprehensive metrics that integrate sustainability into pediatric care pathways.

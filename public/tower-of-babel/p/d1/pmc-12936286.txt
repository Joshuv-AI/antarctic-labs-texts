A real-world comparison of nivolumab plus cabozantinib and pembrolizumab plus lenvatinib focusing on safety outcomes in metastatic renal cell carcinoma: results from the JK-FOOT consortium
Authors: Takafumi Yanagisawa, Keiichiro Mori, Tatsushi Kawada, Satoshi Katayama, Takuya Tsujino, Ryoichi Maenosono, Shingo Toyoda, Takuhisa Nukaya, Hirofumi Morinaka, Keita Tamura, Wataru Fukuokaya, Fumihiko Urabe, Masaya Murakami, Kensuke Bekku, Kiyoshi Takahara, Kazutoshi Fujita, Haruhito Azuma, Motoo Araki, Teruo Inamoto, Kazumasa Komura, Takahiro Kimura
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeImmune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-based combination therapy is a standard first-line treatment for metastatic renal cell carcinoma (mRCC), with combinations such as nivolumab plus cabozantinib (Nivo + Cabo) and pembrolizumab plus lenvatinib (Pem + Len) demonstrating favorable oncologic outcomes. However, no direct comparisons between these two regimens have been conducted. This study aimed to compare the safety and oncologic outcomes of Nivo + Cabo and Pem + Len in patients with mRCC.MethodsThis retrospective study included 185 patients with mRCC treated with Nivo + Cabo (n = 81) or Pem + Len (n = 104) between January 2018 and June 2025 across multiple institutions. The primary outcome was a comparison of treatment-related adverse events (TrAEs). Oncologic outcomes, including objective response rate (ORR), progression-free survival (PFS), cancer-specific survival (CSS), and overall survival (OS), were compared using one-to-one propensity score matching.ResultsAny-grade TrAEs occurred in 90% of patients in the Nivo + Cabo group and 92% in the Pem + Len group (p = 0.6). Severe TrAEs (grade ≥ 3) were more frequent in the Pem + Len group (44%) than in the Nivo + Cabo group (30%, p = 0.048). Tyrosine kinase inhibitor dose reduction and treatment discontinuation rates were similar between groups. In the matched cohort (Nivo + Cabo: n = 74; Pem + Len: n = 74), ORRs were comparable (66% vs. 71%, p = 0.6). With a median follow-up of 17 months, no significant differences were observed in PFS (p = 0.4), CSS (p = 0.9), or OS (p = 0.5).ConclusionsNivo + Cabo and Pem + Len demonstrated similar oncologic efficacy as first-line treatments for mRCC. However, Pem + Len was associated with more severe TrAEs. Careful toxicity management and shared decision-making are essential when selecting ICI-based combinations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00262-025-04288-6.

Introduction

Immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-based combination therapy has become a mainstay in the treatment of metastatic renal cell carcinoma (mRCC) [1–5]. Currently, both ICI plus ICI combinations and ICI plus tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) combinations are recommended as first-line treatments for mRCC, depending on the International mRCC Database Consortium (IMDC) risk classification [6]. However, the absence of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) directly comparing the safety and efficacy of different ICI-based regimens has left treatment selection dependent on physician discretion [7]. Clinical decision-making must involve a careful assessment of potential therapeutic benefits in relation to possible adverse effects. To inform such decisions, several network meta-analyses (NMAs) have evaluated the comparative efficacy and safety of ICI-based combination therapies [8–13]. These analyses consistently indicate that ICI plus TKI combinations provide greater oncologic benefits but are associated with increased toxicity [11, 13]. Recent NMAs have suggested that the combination of nivolumab plus cabozantinib (Nivo + Cabo) yields favorable overall survival (OS), whereas pembrolizumab plus lenvatinib (Pem + Len) demonstrates superior progression-free survival (PFS) and objective response rates (ORRs), with comparable safety profiles between the two regimens [11]. Additionally, Nivo + Cabo has been associated with lower treatment discontinuation rates, potentially due to a more manageable profile of treatment-related adverse events (TrAEs) [14]. Despite these insights, the lack of direct comparative studies has left differences in safety and efficacy between the two regimens unclarified. This multicenter retrospective study aimed to evaluate and compare the safety profiles of Nivo + Cabo and Pem + Len in patients with mRCC. Oncologic outcomes were also assessed using one-to-one propensity score matching (PSM) to minimize baseline differences between treatment groups.

Materials and methods

Patient selection

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the principal institution, Osaka Medical and Pharmaceutical University (approval number: RIN–750–2571). Medical records of 627 consecutive patients who received ICI-based combination therapies were retrospectively reviewed. These patients were treated at eight university hospitals and 15 affiliated hospitals across Japan between January 2018 and June 2025 (Fig. 1). Among them, 228 patients were treated with either Nivo + Cabo (n = 97) or Pem + Len (n = 131). After excluding patients with insufficient follow-up (< 3 months), missing data, and those without distant metastases (including those with tumor embolism), 185 patients with mRCC were included in the final analysis, i.e., 81 treated with nivolumab + cabozantinib and 104 treated with pembrolizumab + lenvatinib.Fig. 1Flowchart of patient selection based on study eligibility criteria

Flowchart of patient selection based on study eligibility criteria

Outcome measurements

The primary safety outcomes were the incidence of any-grade and severe TrAEs. Adverse events were assessed by investigators at each participating institution using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events of the National Cancer Institute (CTCAE), version 5.0 [15]. Subsequently, the rates of TKI dose reduction and treatment discontinuation due to TrAEs were evaluated. Secondary efficacy outcomes included PFS, cancer-specific survival (CSS), OS, and ORRs. ORRs, including complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), and progressive disease (PD), were evaluated as the best overall response during treatment, according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST), version 1.1 [16].

Disease management and follow-up methods

All patients were confirmed to have metastatic disease based on measurable lesions on computed tomography (CT) at diagnosis. Follow-up CT scans were routinely performed every 6–9 weeks during treatment. Additional imaging modalities, such as magnetic resonance imaging, bone scintigraphy, and positron emission tomography/CT, were utilized as clinically indicated. Treatment regimens, including initial dosage and administration intervals, were determined at the discretion of the treating physician in accordance with European Association of Urology guidelines [6]. Decisions regarding treatment interruption, discontinuation due to progression or TrAEs, or treatment resumption were made based on physician judgement and shared decision-making with the patient, following recommendations for appropriate regimen use.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were summarized as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs). Group comparisons were made using Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables and the Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables. A two-sided p value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Patient characteristics were compared using standardized mean differences (SMDs). Propensity scores were estimated via univariable logistic regression, incorporating covariates including IMDC risk classification, age, pathological subtype, prior nephrectomy status, and metastatic sites (Supplementary Fig. 1). Patients treated with Nivo + Cabo or Pem + Len were matched in a one-to-one ratio based on the propensity scores [17, 18], applying a caliper width equal to 0.2 times the standard deviation of the logit-transformed propensity score, as recommended in prior studies [19–21]. Following PSM, PFS, CSS, and OS were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and survival curves were compared between the groups using the log-rank test. Time-point survival rates and median survival durations were reported with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs). All statistical analyses were conducted using R software (Version 4.2.3; The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Safety profiles in the overall cohort

Table 2 summarizes the frequency of TrAEs in both groups. Any-grade TrAEs were observed in 72 patients (90%) in the Nivo + Cabo group and in 96 patients (92%) in the Pem + Len group (p = 0.6). However, severe TrAEs ( ≥ CTCAE grade 3) occurred significantly more often in the Pem + Len group (n = 46, 44%) than in the Nivo + Cabo group (n = 24, 30%; p = 0.048). Common any-grade TrAEs (occurring in > 10% of patients in either group) included hypertension (19% vs. 28%), diarrhea or colitis (15% vs. 12%), palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia (PPE) (32% vs. 14%), rash (12% vs. 17%), liver impairment (33% vs. 19%), and hypothyroidism (19% vs. 25%) in the Nivo + Cabo and Pem + Len groups, respectively. Additionally, nausea or appetite loss (13%) and proteinuria (19%) were more frequently observed in the Pem + Len group. Among severe TrAEs, liver impairment was the only event occurring in more than 10% of patients in both groups (17% vs. 12%). The Pem + Len group also demonstrated higher rates of severe hypertension (8.7%), diarrhea or colitis (5.8%), rash (4.8%), and proteinuria (4.8%). Despite differences in TrAE profiles, the rates of TKI dose reduction during treatment (70% vs. 76%, p = 0.4) and treatment discontinuation due to TrAEs (21% vs. 24%, p = 0.7) were comparable between the two regimens. Additionally, patients who underwent initial dose reductions may have been associated with suboptimal short-term oncologic outcomes. The Kaplan–Meier curves for each outcome are presented in Fig. 2. Discussion

This study represents the first direct real-world comparison between two ICI plus TKI regimens—Nivo + Cabo and Pem + Len—both of which have demonstrated robust oncologic efficacy in their respective RCTs and subsequent long-term follow-up reports [2, 3, 22–24]. Several notable findings emerged from this analysis. First, although the safety profiles of both regimens were broadly comparable regarding any-grade TrAEs and treatment discontinuation rates, Pem + Len was associated with a higher incidence of severe TrAEs. Second, the observed ORRs and OS in this study were favorable compared to those reported in clinical trials. A comparison of toxicity/efficacy outcomes between the present study and relevant studies is given in Supplementary Table 3 [25–28]. Third, in the propensity score-matched cohort, both regimens yielded comparable oncologic outcomes, aligning with findings from prior NMAs.

Given the importance of prolonging prognosis in patients with mRCC in the era of ICI-based combination therapies, optimal management of TrAEs is crucial for ensuring continuous treatment. In the CheckMate 9ER trial, Nivo + Cabo was associated with any-grade and severe TrAEs in 98% and 68% of patients, respectively [23]. In the CLEAR trial, corresponding rates with Pem + Len were 97% and 74% [24]. Our recent NMAs demonstrated a significantly higher incidence of both any-grade and severe TrAEs for these combination regimens compared with sunitinib [11], although no significant differences were observed between the two combination regimens themselves [11]. In our real-world cohort, severe TrAEs were observed in 30% of patients treated with Nivo + Cabo and 44% of those treated with Pem + Len, suggesting acceptable safety profiles in clinical practice. However, the higher rate of severe TrAEs associated with Pem + Len warrants attention, particularly in Japanese patients with mRCC.

Understanding the frequencies and organ-specific patterns of TrAEs is essential for optimizing toxicity management. In our analysis, although Nivo + Cabo was associated with a measurable rate of severe TrAEs, liver impairment was the only TrAE that occurred in more than 10% of patients. As liver impairment is generally manageable, the treatment discontinuation rate due to this event was relatively infrequent with Nivo + Cabo [14]. The 24% discontinuation rate observed in our cohort aligns with the 28% rate reported in the extended follow-up of the CheckMate 9ER trial [23], suggesting that nivolumab + cabozantinib may be found to be a regimen that is easier to continue. Furthermore, in our cohort, despite the significantly higher median age of patients treated with Nivo + Cabo compared to those receiving Pem + Len, the incidence of severe TrAEs was lower, indicating potential safety advantages in elderly patients. Further well-designed studies are warranted to clarify the differential safety profiles of these regimens, particularly in elderly patients.

Conversely, Pem + Len has been associated with relatively higher efficacy and increased toxicity. This may be attributed to lenvatinib’s broader kinase inhibition profile, targeting both vascular endothelial growth factor receptors (VEGFR) and fibroblast growth factor receptors (FGFR) pathways, which contributes to its therapeutic potency and toxicity [2, 11, 24]. In this analysis, proteinuria, hypertension, and liver impairment were among the most frequent TrAEs during treatment with Pem + Len, often necessitating intermittent therapy or dose reductions. Despite the higher incidence of severe TrAEs with Pem + Len, this study demonstrated comparable TKI dose reduction and treatment discontinuation with both regimens, suggesting the successful management of TrAEs in real-world clinical practice [29]. These findings underscore the importance of proactive toxicity monitoring, individualized TrAE management strategies, and shared decision-making with patients to ensure continued treatment with Pem + Len.

Recently, final OS data from the CheckMate 9ER trial became available, demonstrating a median OS of 46.5 months in patients treated with Nivo + Cabo over a follow-up period of 67.6 months [23], consistent with the median OS of 44 months observed in our real-world analysis. Similarly, the OS outcomes with Pem + Len in our study mirrored those reported in the CLEAR trial, with 2-year OS rates of 78.4% and 80.4%, respectively [24]. These findings confirm the substantial and comparable oncologic benefit provided by both regimens. Furthermore, based on the results of our study and the similar hazard ratios (HRs) for OS reported in the CheckMate 9ER and CLEAR trials (both 0.79) [23, 24], the OS benefits may be considered equivalent between the two regimens.

In contrast, the CLEAR trial clearly demonstrated superior outcomes for Pem + Len regarding ORR and PFS [2, 24]. Accordingly, recent NMAs have consistently shown that Pem + Len is associated with the highest likelihood of achieving improved ORR, CR, and PFS benefits [11, 13]. Owing to its rapid and robust tumor-reducing effect, clinicians often prefer Pem + Len in cases requiring immediate tumor shrinkage, such as tumor thrombosis extending into the inferior vena cava [30]. However, in our study, no significant differences in PFS, ORR, or CR were observed with Pem + Len. Nevertheless, the ORR of 71% in our study was comparable to that reported in the CLEAR trial and exceeded that in another real-world study [24, 25, 31]. Although PSM was used to minimize potential confounders, the limited sample size and relatively short follow-up duration may have reduced the statistical power to detect significant differences. Additionally, patients who underwent initial dose reductions may have been associated with suboptimal short-term oncologic outcomes. Further large-scale studies with longer follow-up are warranted to confirm the oncologic efficacy of Pem + Len and assess the clinical impact of initial dose modifications [29].

The selection of an appropriate first-line therapy remains crucial, especially given the data from the targeted therapy era suggesting that only 50% of patients received second-line treatment, and merely 20% proceeded to third-line therapy [32]. Notably, our recent report comparing ICI-TKI and ICI-ICI combination therapies revealed that up to 50% of patients who experienced disease progression on either regimen did not advance to second-line therapy and instead received best supportive care alone. This underscores the importance of first-line therapy selection, even in the current era of expanding second- or later-line options [33, 34]. Factors influencing first-line treatment choice include patient characteristics (e.g., age, performance status, or comorbidities), tumor characteristics (e.g., histological type, need for nephrectomy, metastasis sites, tumor volume, time to metastasis, associated symptoms), and environmental factors (e.g., disease impact on work, availability of family support, access to healthcare facilities). Our present analysis highlights the differential profiles and outcomes associated with the two key regimens compared, offering insights that may facilitate more informed clinical decision-making. Although some head-to-head comparisons between these regimens were presented at scientific meetings in 2025 [35, 36], our study is the first report to provide such a comparison using a relatively large cohort and incorporating PSM for oncologic outcome evaluation.

This study had several limitations. Firstly, owing to the retrospective design, AE profiles were collected solely through medical record reviews. Although every effort was made to ensure data accuracy, differences in clinical documentation systems across participating institutions may have introduced bias. Furthermore, while it would be ideal if it could include a landmark analysis, we found this analysis difficult to perform, given that our study drew on the multicenter database which lacked data on the dates of onset of AEs, making it a major study limitation. Secondly, variations in patient demographics may have influenced the incidence of TrAEs. The retrospective, multicenter nature of the study precluded a comprehensive assessment of patient comorbidities, which may have affected the safety outcomes. Thirdly, although PSM was applied to minimize potential confounders in evaluating oncologic outcomes, this method does not replace RCTs. Moreover, the limited sample size may have reduced the statistical power required to detect differences in treatment efficacy. Again, while a comparison by site of metastasis represents an important area of research, we were unable to make this comparison, given that the small sample size allowed only for unreliable sub-analyses, which we acknowledge as a further study limitation. Fourth, while the presence or absence of sarcomatoid features represents an important factor in mRCC patients, there was a paucity of data on these features in the database drawn on, which made it difficult to assess the patients for their presence or absence. Finally, the relatively short follow-up period and small number of post-progression events limited the ability to perform a robust analysis of the efficacy of subsequent therapies following progression on first-line Nivo + Cabo or Pem + Len. Moreover, the imbalance in subsequent therapy in the study was a major confounding factor in survival outcome and is thus acknowledged as a study limitation. Further studies with longer follow-up will clarify the efficacy of second-line TKI as well as emerging agents such as belzutifan after progression on ICI-based regimens [37].

Despite these limitations, this study provides valuable insights into the differential clinical profiles, particularly safety, of Nivo + Cabo versus Pem + Len in real-world practice. These findings may support clinical decision-making and inform the design of future prospective trials. Furthermore, the development of clinically applicable predictive biomarkers, such as tumor mutation burden and gene expression profiles, will prove crucial for advancing personalized medicine in the treatment of mRCC [38–40].

In conclusion, this multicenter real-world study demonstrated comparable oncologic efficacy between Nivo + Cabo and Pem + Len as first-line treatments for patients with mRCC, even within a relatively short follow-up period. Notably, the Pem + Len regimen was associated with a higher incidence of severe TrAEs. These findings emphasize the importance of proactively managing adverse events and underscore the need for shared decision-making with patients in light of each regimen’s toxicity profile. Such approaches are critical to optimizing outcomes in an era of intensified ICI-based combination therapies.

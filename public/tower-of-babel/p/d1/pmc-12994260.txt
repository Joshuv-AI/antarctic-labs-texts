A comparison of dimensionality reduction approaches for pre-treatment PSMA-PET/CT radiomics in prostate adenocarcinoma outcome prediction☆
Authors: Apurva Singh, William Silva Mendes, Sang-Bo Oh, Ozan Cem Guler, Aysenur Elmali, Birhan Demirhan, Amit Sawant, Phuoc Tran, Cem Onal, Lei Ren
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:To compare dimensionality-reduction methods for building prognostic models predicting metastasis-free survival (MFS) in localized prostate adenocarcinoma (PCa) patients treated with androgen-deprivation therapy and external radiotherapy using clinical factors and prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA)-PET/CT radiomics from primary tumor and nodal volumes.Methods:A total of 134 localized PCa patients (28 with nodal involvement) were analyzed. Gross tumor volumes for primary tumors (GTVp) and nodes (GTVn) were segmented on CT and PET scans; a 5-mm peritumoral ring was defined. Radiomic features were normalized and reduced using three techniques: principal component analysis (PCA), supervised, and unsupervised feature selection. Model 1 combined tumor and nodal radiomics via volume-weighted averaging and consisted of 12 predictors including clinical variables (age, Gleason score, initial PSA, PSA relapse) and radiomics from primary, nodal, and ring regions. Data imbalance (24 metastasis, 110 no metastasis) was addressed using a 70:30 train–test split with imbalance correction applied to train set. Univariate Cox regression (P < 0.05) identified top predictors from train set; multivariate Cox regression was performed on corrected training data and applied to test data. Model 2 used clinical variables and radiomics from GTVp + ring; Model 3 used clinical data alone. Binary classification for five-year MFS was also evaluated.Results:Supervised feature selection achieved highest performance. Model 1 test had c-score 0.71 (0.65–0.72). The five-year MFS test classification was sensitivity 80.1 %, specificity 85.4 %, and AUC 0.84. Unsupervised and PCA methods showed slightly lower results (test c-scores: 0.70 and 0.69, respectively). Model 1 consistently outperformed Model 2 with c-score 0.64 and AUC 0.79, as well as Model 3 with c-score 0.54 and AUC 0.68 across all dimensionality-reduction techniques.Conclusion:Supervised feature selection yielded the highest c-scores and AUCs for the models. Integrating PSMA-PET/CT radiomics from primary, nodal, and peritumoral regions with clinical factors significantly improved MFS prediction, highlighting multi-regional radiomics as a promising biomarker for personalized therapy in prostate cancer.

Introduction

Prostate cancer is the second most common cancer and a leading cause of cancer-related deaths in men worldwide.1 Screening primarily involves prostate-specific antigen (PSA) testing.2 Surgery and radiation therapy are standard treatments for localized disease, with intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT) preferred for its precision and reduced toxicity.3,4 Androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) is often recommended in high-risk cases.5 However, recurrence occurs in 20 %– 60 % of patients,6–8 highlighting the need for prognostic biomarkers to guide treatment.

Prostate-specific membrane antigen positron emission tomography (PSMA-PET) is a functional imaging modality that improves diagnosis, staging, and treatment planning.9–11 PSMA, overexpressed in prostate cancer cells, plays roles in angiogenesis, cell migration, and nutrient uptake.12 PSMA-PET offers high sensitivity and specificity in detecting local and metastatic disease13 and shows potential for non-invasive prognostic assessment.14–16.

Radiomics enables extraction of quantitative imaging features—intensity, shape, texture, and heterogeneity—from CT and PET imaging data.17 Unlike biopsy-based assessments, which are invasive, costly, and prone to sampling error,18,19 radiomics provides comprehensive, non-invasive characterization. Combined PET/CT radiomics biomarkers have shown promise in predicting therapy response.20,21 Extending this approach to PSMA-PET/CT may further refine risk stratification and support individualized therapeutic decisions. However, radiomics datasets often comprise hundreds of interrelated variables derived from relatively small patient cohorts, predisposing models to overfitting and poor generalizability. To overcome these challenges, dimensionality reduction is critical for identifying the most informative and non-redundant features, thereby improving model stability and prognostic accuracy.

Most prior PSMA-PET studies have evaluated post-treatment changes, such as reductions in standardized uptake values (SUVs), such as our previous efforts in correlating with metastasis-free survival (MFS) in patients with pelvic lymph node metastasis (REF) or oligometastatic disease.22,23 Cao et al.24 extended this to radiomics features from pre- and post-metastasis-directed therapy (MDT) scans in predicting two-year MFS. However, PSMA-PET-based radiomics for localized primary prostate cancer remain under-explored.

To address this gap, we analyzed a cohort of 134 localized prostate adenocarcinoma patients (28 with nodal involvement) with pre-treatment PSMA-PET/CT scans. In this prior work, we integrated radiomics features from primary tumors and nodes with clinical variables (iPSA, PSA relapse, Gleason score, age) to predict MFS.25 In our current study, we compare three feature reduction methods—feature set transformation, supervised, and unsupervised selection—to identify the most effective approach for combining radiomics and clinical data. Our goal is to develop prognostic models that enable personalized therapy planning using pre-treatment PSMA-PET/CT imaging.

Materials and methods

Patient cohort

A total of 134 patients with localized prostate adenocarcinoma (PCa) treated at Baskent University were retrospectively enrolled in the study with Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval (IRB number- HP-00100523). All methods in the study were in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki under a waiver of informed consent. The inclusion criteria were: (i) confirmed histological diagnosis of PCa, (ii) clinically pelvic node-positive or node-negative disease based on PSMA-PET/CT evaluation, and (iii) treatment with ADT and external beam radiotherapy (EBRT). The exclusion criteria included: (i) prior radical prostatectomy, (ii) receipt of chemotherapy before radiotherapy, (iii) prostate-only radiotherapy, and (iv) documented distant metastases.

All patients received either 76–78 Gy or a focal boost of 86 Gy to the intraprostatic lesion using the simultaneous integrated boost technique. The radiation dose for the pelvic lymphatic field ranged from 46 to 54 Gy, without any additional boost to gross lymph nodes. Androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) was prescribed at the discretion of the treating physician and according to patient preference. ADT consisted of a luteinizing hormone-releasing hormone (LHRH) agonist, either alone or in combination with the antiandrogen bicalutamide. Notably, next-generation androgen receptor-targeted agents—such as enzalutamide, abiraterone acetate, darolutamide or apalutamide, were not administered.

Follow-up and outcome assessment

After completion of radiotherapy, all patients were prospectively followed according to institutional protocol. Clinical evaluation and serum PSA testing were performed every 3 months for the first 2 years, every 6 months for years 3–5, and annually thereafter. Restaging imaging included even if biochemical or clinical progression was suspected.

Metastatic disease was defined as radiologically confirmed distant recurrence on PSMA-PET/CT, whole-body bone scan, or cross-sectional CT/MRI. The median follow-up time for the entire cohort was 74 months (interquartile range 61–92 months). At the time of analysis, 9 patients (6.7 %) were lost to follow-up, and censoring was performed at the date of last contact for these cases.

PSMA-PET/CT evaluation

All PSMA-PET/CT examinations were interpreted by two nuclear medicine physicians, each with more than eight years of experience in PSMA-targeted imaging. PSMA positivity was determined visually, and lesions were considered positive when radiotracer uptake was clearly greater than that of normal liver parenchyma, consistent with prostate cancer localization. No fixed SUVmax threshold or PSMA-RADS classification was applied, reflecting routine clinical interpretation at the time of imaging. All patients were referred to our department after undergoing PSMA-PET/CT externally; therefore, no internal screening process was performed, and the present cohort represents a consecutive series of eligible patients meeting the inclusion criteria.

Step 1: PET/CT image pre-processing and segmentation

Patients were imaged at Başkent University by a dedicated PET/CT system (Discovery STE 8; GE Healthcare, Milwaukee, WI, USA). One section of the cohort had PET scans with original volumetric dimensions of 192 × 192 × 567 and voxel spacing of 3.65 mm × 3.65 mm × 3.27 mm and the other section of the cohort had PET scans with 128 × 128 × 367 and voxel spacing of 5.47 mm × 5.47 mm × 3.27 mm. CT images were acquired with the original volumetric dimensions of 512 × 512 × 567 and voxel spacing of 1.37 mm × 1.37 mm × 3.26 mm and 140 kVp.

Gross tumor volume (GTV) of primary tumor (GTVp) and nodes (GTVn) based on PET and CT were identified and segmented by two radiation oncologists. A 5 mm expansion ring area outside the primary tumor GTV was created. The PET images were resampled to match the voxel spacing of the CT images prior to radiomics feature extraction. Fig. 2 below shows the ROIs on sample PET/CT scan slices of a patient.

Step 2: Clinical parameters and radiomics feature extraction

Clinical parameters including age, Gleason score, initial prostate specific antigen (i PSA) value and a categorical variable indicating PSA relapse were assessed based on recommendations from the physicians. Clinical and demographic information of the patients is present in Supplementary Table S1. Radiomics feature extraction was conducted using the 3D Slicer PyRadiomics (Ver. 3.0.1).26,27 From CT and PET images, 107 features were extracted for each region: GTVp, GTVn, and the peritumoral ring. Features were grouped into eight families: (i) Shape – geometric properties (e.g., area, volume); (ii) First-order – voxel intensity distributions; (iii) GLCM – spatial relationships between neighboring voxels; (iv) GLDM – gray-level dependencies within regions; (v) Run Length Matrix – lengths of consecutive voxels with identical intensities; (vi) Size Zone Matrix – counts of connected voxel zones with the same intensity; (vii) Neighboring Gray Tone Difference Matrix – texture coarseness. A list of features belonging to each feature family is included in Supplementary Table S2.

Step 3: Feature normalization and feature space dimensionality reduction

Z-score normalization was applied to the radiomics features extracted from the primary tumor, nodes and ring regions.26 Feature space dimensionality reduction performed using three techniques. Feature set transformation was performed with Principal Components Analysis (PCA).27 Supervised feature selection was performed using Mean Decrease in Accuracy (MDA) metric. The MDA metric of a variable is a measure of decrease in the random forest classification accuracy using the model upon removing said variable, thus being indicative of its prognostic ability.28 The higher the value of MDA for the predictor, the higher its importance in the model. Unsupervised feature selection was performed by applying a variance threshold to select the desired number of features that capture high variance in information. The above analysis was performed in Python (Ver. 3.7, Anaconda).29

Results

Metastasis-free survival analysis

The prognostic performance of the three feature space dimensionality reduction methods is summarized in Table 1.

Across all techniques, Model 1, which integrated PSMA-PET/CT radiomics from the primary tumor, nodal, and peritumoral regions with clinical factors—achieved the highest concordance indices in both the training and test datasets. Using supervised feature selection, Model 1 achieved a test-set C-index outperforming Model 2 (primary + ring + clinical) and Model 3 (clinical only). The integration of nodal radiomics improved prognostic discrimination by approximately 0.07 over primary-only models, demonstrating its additive value. Comparable trends were observed with PCA and unsupervised feature selection (test C-indices 0.69–0.70 for Model 1), though supervised selection consistently yielded the most robust results.

Overall, the inclusion of radiomics—particularly from nodal regions—significantly enhanced model performance compared with clinical factors alone.

Five-year metastasis-free survival binary classification analysis

Binary classification analyses confirmed these findings (Table 2). Using supervised feature selection, Model 1 achieved the best discriminative ability in the test cohort, with sensitivity 80.1 %, specificity 85.4 %, and AUC 0.84. By contrast, Model 2 and Model 3 reached AUCs of 0.79 and 0.68, respectively. Supervised selection improved predictive accuracy by ~6 % compared with PCA and unsupervised approaches, highlighting its superior ability to identify prognostically relevant features. Integration of nodal radiomics again provided incremental benefit, improving classification performance relative to primary-tumor-only models.

Kaplan–Meier analyses demonstrated clear separation between patients classified as having an MFS event versus no event (Fig. 3).

In the test dataset, the separation was statistically significant for all dimensionality reduction methods when using Model 1 (P = 0.03 for PCA; 0.005 for supervised; 0.02 for unsupervised). Model 2 showed borderline to significant differences (P = 0.05–0.04), whereas Model 3 failed to achieve statistical significance (P = 0.3).

These findings confirm that incorporating PSMA-PET/CT radiomics from both tumor and nodal regions substantially enhances the ability to distinguish patients with differing MFS outcomes. The superior performance of models integrating nodal radiomics can be attributed to their ability to capture disease spread patterns not represented in primary-tumor features alone. Radiomic characteristics of lymph nodes can likely reflect early metastatic activity and can provide complementary prognostic information beyond local tumor heterogeneity.

Discussion

PSMA-PET has rapidly transformed prostate cancer imaging by enabling accurate detection of intraprostatic disease and nodal metastases. Beyond staging, its quantitative parameters hold promise for outcome prediction. Most prior studies have focused on using PSMA-PET SUV-based metrics or radiomics features for treatment response in oligometastatic settings. However, the prognostic role of PSMA-PET/CT radiomics in localized disease, or the methodological impact of dimensionality reduction strategies on model performance remains underexplored.27–29 Therefore, despite the increasing adoption of PSMA-PET in clinical workflows, its quantitative potential for outcome prediction, especially in primary prostate adenocarcinoma, still has potential for exploration. In our previous work with 134 PCa patients, we developed imaging biomarkers by integrating radiomics from primary tumors, pelvic nodes, and peritumoral ring regions (PET and CT) with clinical variables (iPSA, PSA-relapse, Gleason score, age) to predict MFS.30 In this study, we developed and compared three dimensionality reduction strategies to integrate PSMA-PET/CT radiomics with clinical data for predicting MFS in patients receiving definitive RT and ADT. Among these, supervised feature selection yielded the most accurate and generalizable models. This study advances the current literature in several important ways. Combining radiomics from primary tumor, nodal, and peritumoral regions provided the highest discriminative performance, whereas models excluding nodal features or relying solely on clinical data underperformed. The added predictive value of nodal radiomics likely reflects the biological significance of lymph node involvement in prostate cancer progression. While primary tumor radiomics capture local tumor heterogeneity, nodal radiomics quantify imaging features that reflect early metastatic spread. Thus, integrating nodal radiomics with primary-tumor radiomics can potentially improve the prediction of metastasis-free survival. These findings underscore the added prognostic value of PSMA-PET/CT radiomics beyond conventional risk factors. Further, the study is also unique in its systematic comparison of dimensionality reduction strategies for PSMA-PET/CT radiomics modeling in prostate cancer. While prior publications have typically selected a single modeling pipeline, our analysis demonstrates that supervised selection markedly improves generalizability, providing practical guidance for future radiomics studies using PSMA-PET.

From a clinical perspective, the proposed radiomics-based models could complement established prognostic tools such as CAPRA, Decipher, and STAR-CAP nomograms by incorporating quantitative imaging features that reflect tumor biology and spatial heterogeneity. Integrating these imaging-derived biomarkers into existing clinical frameworks could enhance patient selection for intensified systemic therapy, guide focal boost dose painting, or support adaptive treatment strategies based on PSMA-PET-defined risk.

This study has limitations, notably the single-institution retrospective design and the modest cohort size, which limit generalizability. The absence of external validation reflects the recent adoption of PSMA-PET/CT in routine practice and the current scarcity of long-term follow-up data. This limitation is primarily because PSMA-PET imaging has only been adopted in clinical practice in recent years, e.g. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval in the US was granted on December 1, 2020. Hence, the availability of PSMA-PET data on primary PCa patients, particularly those with long-term follow-up like five-year MFS, remains limited. Ongoing collaborations across multiple institutions are expected to enable independent validation and model recalibration, which are critical before clinical implementation. We also acknowledge the potential uncertainty in the contours of the regions of interest in our dataset. Additional studies are warranted to evaluate the contouring uncertainty and its impact on the model prediction.

In the future, comprehensive validation of imaging biomarkers and models created using various feature space dimensionality reduction methods can be performed with the availability of more patient data at multiple institutions. We also plan to investigate post-treatment follow-up PSMA-PET scans of the patients, in addition to the current pre-treatment scans, to assess the value of delta radiomics signatures, built using feature set transformation and feature selection techniques applied on delta radiomics features, in enhancing the performance in the prediction of metastasis-free survival. Recent studies have also explored the value of information from dose distribution patterns (dosiomics) for risk assessment in lung cancer36 and head and neck cancer patients.37 In our future work, we also aim to study the value of dosiomics in improving the prognostic capability of our models in the prediction of metastasis-free survival in our prostate cancer cohort.

Overall, this study provides a methodologically rigorous and clinically relevant advancement by establishing how dimensionality-reduction choices and multi-region radiomics integration affect the performance of PSMA-PET/CT–based prognostic models. These insights can potentially help define best practices for future radiomics workflows and highlight a promising direction for precision risk stratification in localized prostate cancer.

Conclusion

This study evaluates feature space dimensionality reduction methods to integrate radiomics from primary tumors, pelvic nodes, and peritumoral ring regions on pre-treatment PSMA-PET/CT with clinical factors to predict metastasis-free survival in primary prostate adenocarcinoma. We found that supervised feature selection outperformed principal component and unsupervised approaches for dimensionality reduction in PSMA-PET/CT radiomics. Integrating multi-regional radiomics features from the primary tumor, nodal, and peritumoral areas with clinical factors substantially improved prediction of metastasis-free survival compared with clinical variables alone. These results highlight the potential of PSMA-PET/CT radiomics as a non-invasive, complementary biomarker to existing prognostic models, supporting the development of personalized radiotherapy strategies in prostate cancer.

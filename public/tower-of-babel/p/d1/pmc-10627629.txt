A modern approach with follower-leading clustering algorithm for visualizing author collaborations and article themes in skin cancer research: A bibliometric analysis
Authors: Po-Chih Lai, Willy Chou, Tsair-Wei Chien, Feng-Jie Lai
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:Skin cancers (SCs) arise due to the proliferation of atypical cells that have the potential to infiltrate or metastasize to different areas of the body. There is a lack of understanding regarding the country-based collaborations among authors (CBCA) and article themes on SCs. A clustering algorithm capable of categorizing CBCA and article themes on skin cancer is required. This study aimed to apply a follower-leading clustering algorithm to classify CBCA and article themes and present articles that deserve reading in recent ten years.Methods:Between 2013 and 2022, a total of 6526 articles focusing on SC were extracted from the Web of Science core collection. The descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, and prescriptive analytics model was employed to visualize the study results. Various visualizations, including 4-quadrant radar plots, line charts, scatter plots, network charts, chord diagrams, and impact beam plots, were utilized. The category, journal, authorship, and L-index score were employed to assess individual research achievements. Diagnostic analytics were used to cluster the CBCA and identify common article themes. Keyword weights were utilized to predict article citations, and noteworthy articles were highlighted in prescriptive analytics based on the 100 most highly cited articles on SC (T100SC).Results:The primary entities contributing to SC research include the United States, the University of California, San Francisco in US, dermatology department, and the author Andreas Stang from Germany, who possess higher category, journal, authorship, and L-index scores. The Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology has published the highest number of articles (n = 336, accounting for 5.16% of the total). From the T100SC, 7 distinct themes were identified, with melanoma being the predominant theme (92% representation). A strong correlation was observed between the number of article citations and the keyword weights (F = 81.63; P < .0001). Two articles with the highest citation counts were recommended for reading.Conclusion:By applying the descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, and prescriptive analytics model, 2 noteworthy articles were identified and highlighted on an impact beam plot. These articles are considered deserving of attention and could potentially inspire further research in the field of bibliometrics, focusing on relevant topics related to melanoma.

1. Introduction

Skin cancer (SC), particularly melanoma, poses a significant health concern due to excessive Ultraviolet (UV) radiation exposure.[1–5] While melanomas represent a small percentage of SC cases, they account for the majority of skin-cancer-related deaths in the United States, causing over 10,000 fatalities annually.[6] The prevalence of SC is substantial, with more than 1 million Americans living with melanoma and approximately 1 in 5 Americans expected to develop some form of SC in their lifetime.[7,8] Despite the availability of over 125,041 articles on SC indexed in PubMed,[9] traditional bibliometric methods have only been applied to a mere 6 documents.[10] The utilization of modern approaches such as the DDPP model (descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, and prescriptive analytics)[11,12] and the FLCA (follower-leading clustering algorithm)[13,14] in bibliographical studies has been limited in this context. It is challenging to explore author collaborations and article themes in SC research.

1.1. Contributions of different countries to SC research

SCs are primarily caused by the growth of abnormal cells in the skin that can invade or metastasize to other areas of the body.[15] The 3 main types of SC include basal cell SC, squamous cell SC, and melanoma.[16] Typical characteristics of SCs include painless raised areas with visible blood vessels or ulcers.[16] Exposure to UV radiation from the sun is a major contributing factor to the development of SCs.[1–5,17]

The increased exposure to UV radiation can be attributed, in part, to a thinning ozone layer.[18,19] Approximately 20% to 30% of melanomas develop from preexisting moles.[20] Individuals with light skin and compromised immune function, either due to immunosuppressant medications or HIV infection, are at higher risk.[16,21–23]

SC is the most prevalent cancer globally, accounting for at least 40% of all cancer cases.[22,24] In 2012, melanoma affected 232,000 individuals worldwide, resulting in 55,000 deaths.[20] Australia has the highest rates of melanoma globally.[20] However, it is currently unknown which countries have made significant contributions to SC research in terms of author collaborations and research quality.

Our motivation was thus to examine the contributions of different countries to SC research and specifically assess the extent of authors’ contributions from Australia to the existing knowledge in this field.

1.2. Contributions of authors from Australia to SC

To fully address the multidisciplinary nature of SC research, it is crucial to foster collaboration among specialists in medicine, pathology, and biomedical science. This collaboration facilitates the sharing of resources, exchange of ideas, dissemination of knowledge, and acquisition of information.[25] However, no previous research has specifically explored scientific collaborations in the context of SC. Therefore, it is essential to investigate country-level research collaborations (CBCA) and article themes in SC research to evaluate the contributions of authors from Australia to the existing knowledge in this field of study.

1.3. Traditional bibliometrics versus modern approaches

Bibliometric analysis is a widely used and valuable method for exploring and analyzing large volumes of scientific data.[26] It has been successfully applied in various research fields.[11,27,28] However, previous bibliometric studies on SC[29–34] focusing on top-cited publications and trends in usage of specific terms or news coverage often lack a comprehensive overview of overall SC research.

Traditional bibliometrics, heavily relying on software like CiteSpace[35] and VOSview,[36] present cluttered results with numerous edges and nodes in networks (e.g., 26 Figures and 15 Tables in a bibliographical study[37]), limiting structured layouts and concise cluster analysis. These drawbacks highlight the need for improvements that combine visuals, with the DDPP model,[11,12] and the FLCA algorithm[13,14] to enhance the quality and depth of SC research. Through which, overcoming these limitations would greatly contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of SC within the field of bibliometrics.

1.4. Study aims

The objective of this study was to utilize the FLCA algorithm along with the DDPP model to classify CBCA and article themes, evaluate the contributions of authors from Australia, examine the predominant theme of melanoma, and identify noteworthy SC-related articles from the past decade.

We aimed to verify 2 hypotheses: the applicability of the DDPP model for analyzing bibliometric data, and the effectiveness and feasibility of the FLCA algorithm in clustering CBCA and article themes in SC research.

2. Methods

2.1. Data sources

We searched “TI = (skin cancer)” on web of science core collection (WoSCC) for articles published between 2013 and 2022. The document types were original articles and reviews. A total of 6526 articles focusing on SC were obtained. The 100 top-cited articles on SC (T100SC) were extracted and are listed at the link[38] and deposited in Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/MD/J532.

Because no human subjects were enrolled and all metadata were from WoSCC, ethics approval was not required for this study.

2.2. Four analytics applied to this study

2.2.1. Descriptive analytics in 6526 articles.

To visualize the productive entities and journals within the 6526 articles, a 4-quadrant radar plot[39] and a pyramid plot in R[40] were utilized to highlight top 20 prestigious journals with most publications on SC. Additionally, a line chart with an inflection point[41,42] was employed to conduct trend analysis on the publications, indicating the critical point of evolution or burst spot.

The absolute advantage coefficient (AAC) (see Eqs. 1–3)[43,44] was applied to evaluate the dominance extent for the most influential country, institute, department, and author (CIDA) in category, journal, authorship, and L-index (CJAL) scores[39] (based on the CJA score[45] and the L-index[46] to evaluate research achievements, RAs). The Y-index[47,48] based on 1st and corresponding authors was applied to locate their coordinates on the 4-quadrant radar plot.[39]

where, the AAC ratio is determined by the 3 consecutive numbers of values (e.g., top 3 CJAL scores in descending order denoted by A1, A2, and A3 in Eqs. 2 and 3). The ACC ranged from 0 to 1.0, representing the strength of dominance for the top member when compared to the next 2 members. Through the computation of AAC, the dominance strength in a variable (i.e., country, journal, category, or CIDA) can be measured and judged by the effect size, with criteria of < 0.5, between 0.5 and 0.7, and not <0.7 as the small, medium, and large effect sizes, respectively.[43]

2.2.2.2. Author collaborations and coword analysis.

Country-based collaborations among authors (CIDA) and article themes are commonly observed in bibliometric analysis. However, there has been limited research regarding the interpretation of cluster analysis and its suitability for use in bibliometrics.

Three visual representations were used to display the relationship between variables using diagnostic analytics, including a network chart,[49–51] a chord diagram,[52,53] and a dendrogram[54,55] for validation of the suitability for use in cluster analysis.

2.2.3. Predictive analytics in T100SC.

The number of connections for a specific keyword plus was computed based on an equal weight in an article.[43,49,53] The weighted mean citation for each term was applied to predict article citations based on the correlation coefficient (CC) in determining the predictive power related to original article citations. The CC t value was calculated using the following formula (=CC×n−21−CC×CC).[43,49] A prediction equation was produced through the simple regression analysis using MedCalc statistical software, version 9.5.0.0 (MedCalc, New York). A scatter plot with 95% control lines[57] was used to display the relationship between weighted mean citations and article citations in T100NPC.

2.2.4. Prescriptive analytics in T100SC.

The impact beam plot (IBP)[58,59] was applied to display T100SC.[38] With the IBP, the most worth-reading articles with more number of citations were highlighted by the bubble size. The publication years are on horizontal axis and the themes on vertical axis. The definitions of these themes have been assigned to each article based on the equation 4. All those articles could be linked to PubMed once the dot representing an article in IBP was selected and clicked.

4. Discussion

4.1. Principal findings

We observed that the field of SC research is primarily driven by key CIDA entities such as the United States, specifically the dermatology department at the University of California, San Francisco, and influential author Andreas Stang from Germany, who have achieved higher CJAL scores. Among various journals, the Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology has emerged as the leading publisher, with a remarkable publication count of 336 articles, accounting for 5.16% of the total articles in the dataset.

Through the analysis of the top 100 most highly cited articles on SC (T100SC), 7 distinct themes have been identified, with melanoma being the predominant theme, representing 92% of the articles in this subset. Notably, a strong correlation has been observed between the number of article citations and the weights assigned to keywords (F = 81.63; P < .0001), indicating the significance of keyword selection in predicting the impact and visibility of articles.

Accordingly, the 2 hypotheses that the DDPP model could be used to analyze bibliometric data and the FLCA algorithm is useful and viable for clustering CBCA and article themes in SC research were verified.

4.2. Additional information

This study successfully accomplished 4 tasks and provided evidence: the viability of utilizing the FLCA algorithm along with the DDPP model to classify CBCA and article themes based on Figures 5 and 6; Australia ranking fourth in terms of publications, with 256 articles by 1st authors and 361 articles by corresponding authors, as displayed in Figure 2 using the Y-index,[47,48] followed by the United States, Italy, and Germany. New Zealand ranked 28th with 27 articles by 1st authors and 36 articles by corresponding authors; the identification of melanoma as the predominant theme in 6526 and T100SC articles, similar to the previous study[30]; and the identification of noteworthy articles based on article citations, which have not been illustrated in the literature.

These results are somewhat consistent with a previous study,[25] which found that the top 3 productive countries were the United States, Germany, and Australia, based on first authors affiliations and metadata from PubMed, encompassing 17,975 articles published between 1945 and 2018. Additionally, the Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology emerged as the most frequently published journal, aligning with the aforementioned study.[25]

Regarding the top 100 articles in T100SC, the United States contributed 34% of the publications, with 7% attributed to JAMA Dermatology. In terms of prolific institutions, the German Cancer Research Center (Germany) and the University of Queensland (Australia) each produced 3 articles. These findings differ from a previous study,[32] which reported the United States (74%) as the leading contributor, the New England Journal of Medicine (20%) as the prominent journal, and the National Cancer Institute in Maryland (US) (18%) as the primary institution, due to different timeframe and the inclusion of metadata (e.g., only 22 journals in the study[32]).

The assignment of article themes to T100SC and countries using Equations 4 and 5 is a unique approach in the literature. This methodology bears similarity to its application in previous studies.[53,66]

Prior studies have explored coauthor collaboration through the utilization of social network analysis (SNA).[67–69] In contrast, our research employs the FLCA algorithm in conjunction with the DDPP model, representing a more modern approach. This is evident in Figure 5, where our results demonstrate a clear and concise representation compared to the cluttered and overwhelmed nature of traditional SNA. Notably, Australia, Japan, and the UK form a separate cluster distinct from the cluster led by United States.

Previous research has investigated coauthor collaboration using social network analysis (SNA).[67–69] Our research using the FLCA algorithm with the DDPP model is modern in contrast to the traditional SNA (i.e., concise and clear vs cluttered and overwhelmed), as shown in Figure 5. For instance, Australia with Japan and the UK are apart from the US in another cluster.

4.3. Two worthy reading articles

In light of their high citation counts, 2 articles[60,61] have been recommended as essential reading materials for researchers interested in the field.

The most cited article[60] classified as the theme of #1 Melanoma was cited 5441 times and published in 2017. The authors trained a Deep convolutional neural networks to classify skin lesions using 129,450 clinical images, and tested it against 21 board-certified dermatologists on biopsy-proven clinical images with 2 critical binary classification use cases: keratinocyte carcinomas versus benign seborrheic keratoses; and malignant melanomas versus benign nevi. The convolutional neural network achieved performance on par with all tested experts across both tasks, demonstrating an artificial intelligence capable of classifying SC with a level of competence comparable to dermatologists.

The second most cited article[61] classified as the theme of #1 Melanoma as well was cited 995 times and published in 2015. This study analyzes US government administrative data to estimate the total number of SC procedures performed for Medicare beneficiaries and related parameters from 2006 through 2012. In 2012, the total number of procedures for SC in the Medicare fee-for-service population increased by 13%, and the age-adjusted procedure rate per 100,000 beneficiaries increased from 6075 to 7320. The total number of persons treated for SC in 2012 was 33,15,554.

4.4. Implications and possible changes

This study has important implications for academia and researchers as it provides a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of the field of SC. By analyzing various aspects such as publishing trends, country and institutional participation, key journals, influential authors, and research trends, this study offers valuable insights into the field.

What sets this study apart is the utilization of the FLCA algorithm, as mentioned in previous studies,[13,14] to cluster entities and enhance the understanding of the field dynamics.

Additionally, the study incorporates the use of the DDPP model,[11,12] which allows for descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, and prescriptive analytics to answer different research questions. The DDPP depends on the questions we are asking and the information we are seeking from our metadata.[11] It is sufficient to use descriptive analytics to explain what has happened.[70] On the other hand, diagnostic analytics can be useful in determining why it happened (e.g., by classifications of author collaborations using coword analysis to understand their relationship in CiteSpace[35] and VOS Viewer[36]). Furthermore, for questions relating to what will happen in the future and what should be done next for readers, we could use predictive and prescriptive analytics[70] in bibliometrics.

The supplemental digital content provides the necessary material for replicating visual displays on other topics in the future, encouraging further exploration and analysis.

4.5. Limitations and suggestions

This study offers a comprehensive and systematic analysis, but it is important to acknowledge certain limitations. First, the study solely relied on data from the WoS database, thus excluding any documents that are not covered by WoS. This may result in potential gaps or omissions in the analysis.

Second, the data used in this study were limited to articles published in English, which may restrict the inclusiveness of non-English publications and potentially introduce bias.

Third, it is crucial to consider the potential impact of time factors on the results of bibliometric analysis. As the database is continuously updated, there may be a discrepancy between the findings and the current research landscape. It is important to recognize these limitations and interpret the results within their respective contexts.

Fourth, to enhance the interpretability of visual displays used in this study, it is important to provide readers with additional information. This includes coloring the bubbles based on clusters, sizing them according to WCD, and providing direct links to online articles as shown in Figure 11. These details were not explicitly described in the context but are crucial for improving the understanding of visual displays as demonstrated in Supplemental Digital Contents 2, http://links.lww.com/MD/J533 and 3.

Fifth, while the primary focus of this study is on clustering the T100SC articles using the FLCA algorithm, it is important to consider 3 key aspects: followers, leader ranking, and the parameter k, as depicted in the bottom panel of Figure 1. It is worth noting that the number of articles is not limited to the top 100, as demonstrated in this study.

Sixth, the optimal value for the k parameter in the FLCA algorithm has not been clearly addressed, and simulations may be required to determine the minimum AAC for appropriate clustering of authors or keywords cooccurrences, as shown in Equations 3 to 5.

Seventh, over 300 of the top-100 cited articles have been indexed in PubMed.[71] However, few studies have implemented cluster analysis for theme classification. One limitation of this study is that it does not delve into the detailed use of classification on T100Sc. Providing a guide on how to conduct such a study in Supplemental Digital Contents 2, http://links.lww.com/MD/J533 and 3 and the MP4 video[72] would be beneficial for readers aiming to replicate similar research on the 100 top-cited articles in the future.

Last, R is a useful tool for creating visual diagrams,[40] and this study offers several R plots (e.g., Figs. 4, 6, and 8) as references for interested readers in the future. However, some improvements on the R platform should be made further in the future (e.g., easy and friendly use in the interface).

5. Conclusion

This study provides a practical demonstration of how the FLCA algorithm along with the DDPP model can be applied to other fields and how the resulting clusters can be visualized on the R platform. The effectiveness and feasibility of the FLCA algorithm in clustering CBCA and article themes in SC research offer a valuable tool for researchers in the field of bibliometrics who wish to visually represent their research profiles. This, in turn, can facilitate and promote future research endeavors in other fields, not restricted to SC as we did in this study.

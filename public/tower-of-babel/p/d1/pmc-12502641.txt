Analysis of clinicopathological features and clinical significance in 25 cases of mammary adenoid cystic carcinoma
Authors: Xiaoyan Xu, Shasha Yu, Wei Qu, Yuexia Chen, Liu Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMammary adenoid cystic carcinoma (AdCC) is a rare subtype of invasive breast cancer. Due to its low incidence, the clinicopathological features of mammary AdCC remain incompletely understood. This study aimed to investigate the clinicopathological and molecular characteristics, therapeutic regimens, and prognostic outcomes associated with different subtypes of mammary AdCC.MethodsA total of 25 archived specimens of mammary AdCC were collected from the pathology departments of Nanchang People’s Hospital and Zhengzhou People’s Hospital between January 2013 and December 2024. Histomorphological evaluation, immunohistochemical (IHC) analysis, and molecular testing were performed to evaluate the clinicopathological features and prognostic outcomes. Fisher’s exact test was used to analyze the relationship between HER2 status and clinicopathological features.ResultsAll 25 patients were female, with a mean age of 57.04 years. The tumors manifested as solitary solid nodules, ranging in maximum diameter from 1 to 4.5 cm. Histologically, the tumors were classified as classic adenoid cystic carcinoma (C-AdCC) (21/25), solid-basaloid AdCC (SB-AdCC) (2/25) and AdCC with high-grade transformation (2/25), with Nottingham grade II being the most common (18/25). Immunohistochemical (IHC) analysis demonstrated that most cases exhibited a triple-negative phenotype, characterized by ER-negative (21/25), PR-negative (25/25), and HER2 negativity (IHC score of 0, 1+, or 2 + with no amplification detected by FISH) (25/25). HER2-low expression (IHC score of 1+/2 + with FISH-confirmed non-amplification) was observed in 11 cases (11/25). Neuroendocrine markers were negative in all cases. MYB gene abnormalities, including break-parts or fusion, were identified in 14/25 cases. A statistically significant positive correlation (P < 0.05) was observed between HER2-low expression and MYB gene abnormalities. Axillary lymph node metastasis was observed in 2/25 patients. Following treatment, which included various surgical modalities with or without neoadjuvant chemotherapy, and a follow-up period of 6–136 months, 21 patients remained alive without recurrence or metastasis, 1 patient died at 60 months, and 3 patients were lost to follow-up.ConclusionsThese findings support the classification of mammary AdCC as a distinct triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), with MYB gene abnormalities showing a positive correlation with HER2 status. Notably, mammary AdCC should not be indiscriminately categorized as low-grade TNBC. Precise pathological subtyping of AdCC is critical for guiding clinical management.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12905-025-04014-5.

Introduction

Mammary adenoid cystic carcinoma (AdCC) is a rare and distinct type of invasive carcinoma, accounting for approximately 0.1% of all breast malignancies [1]. Although mammary AdCC is classified as a subtype of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), its clinical course is relatively indolent, with lower rates of lymph node involvement and distant metastasis [2]. Additionally, it exhibits distinct biological behavior compared to salivary gland AdCC [3, 4].

According to the 2019 world health organization (WHO) classification of breast tumors, mammary AdCC subtypes include C-AdCC, SB-AdCC, and AdCC with high-grade transformation, each exhibiting distinct clinicopathological characteristics [5]. The majority of AdCC cases are characterized by the presence of the distinctive MYB-NFIB fusion. Due to the Limited number of large case series specifically focusing on mammary AdCC subtypes, pathologists currently have an incomplete understanding of its subtypes. In this study, We retrospectively analyzed the clinicopathological data of 25 patients with mammary AdCC to improve the pathological diagnosis of its subtypes among clinical pathologists and to provide a scientific basis for individualized treatment and targeted therapy selection.

Materials and methods

Sample sources

A total of 25 AdCC specimens archived at Nanchang People’s Hospital and Zhengzhou People’s Hospital from January 2013 to November 2024 were collected. All specimens were fixed, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned for histomorphological examination, followed by IHC and FISH testing. All pathological diagnoses were confirmed independently by two senior pathologists. All patients were followed up by telephone until December 2024.

Main reagents

Primary antibodies for estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor(PR), Human Epidermal growth factor Receptor 2 (HER2), androgen receptor (AR), Ki-67, CK7, CD117, CK5/6, p63, CD56, chromogranin A (CgA), and synaptophysin (Syn) were purchased from Maixin Biotech Co., Ltd. (Fuzhou, China). FISH probes for MYB break-apart and MYB/NFIB fusion were purchased from Anbiping Medicine Technology Co., Ltd. (Guangzhou, China). All procedures were performed according to the manufacturers’ instructions.

Immunohistochemical(IHC) test

IHC staining procedure: Specimens were fixed in 10% neutral buffered formaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at 3–5 μm. IHC staining was performed using a fully automated system (Leica Microsystems, USA) with the streptavidin-peroxidase (SP) method. All slides were reviewed independently by two experienced pathologists.

HER2 IHC interpretation: IHC 0: No staining or no more than 10% of the invasive cancer cells show incomplete and weak cell membrane staining.IHC 1+: More than 10% of the invasive cancer cells show incomplete and weak cell membrane staining.IHC 2+: More than 10% of the invasive cancer cells show weak to moderate intensity complete cell membrane staining or no more than 10% of the invasive cancer cells show strong and complete cell membrane staining. HER2-Low expression is currently defined as HER2 IHC 1 + or IHC 2+/no amplification by in situ hybridization.

Other biomarkers IHC interpretation: ER, PR and AR positivity was defined as ≥ 1% tumor cell nuclei exhibiting stained, while negativity required < 1% positive nuclei or complete absence of staining. Ki-67 and p63 can be judged as positive as long as the cell nucleus is stained. CD56 positivity is determined by the presence of staining on the cell membrane. CK7, CD117, CK5/6, Syn and CgA are judged as positive based on the staining of the cell cytoplasm. These antibody positive interpretation is not related to the intensity of the staining.

Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH)

Sections with a thickness of 4 μm were placed in preheated dewaxing solution at 68 °C for 15 min, followed by washing with absolute ethanol at room temperature for 5 min to complete initial dehydration. The slides were then immersed in preheated permeation agenton at 90 °C for 20 min and subsequently incubated in preheated deionized water at 37 °C for 3 min. The sections were digested in pre-warmed protease working solution at 37 °C for 10–40 min. After digestion, the slides were rinsed twice with washing buffer (2× SSC) for 5 min each, sequentially immersed in 70%, 85%, and 100% ethanol for 2 min each, and air-dried at room temperature in preparation for hybridization. In a darkroom, 10 µL of probe was applied to the hybridization area of each slide, and a coverslip was immediately placed. The slides were mounted on a hybridizer, denatured at 85 °C for 5 min, and hybridized at 42 °C for 2–16 h. Following hybridization, the sections were washed and air-dried. Counterstaining was performed using DAPI, and FISH signals within the cell nuclei were observed under a fluorescence microscope using a 100× objective lens.

Result interpretation: For the MYB/NFIB fusion, negative cells display 2 red and 2 green signals, while positive cells display 1 red, 1 green, and 2 fusion signals. For the MYB break-apart result, negative cells show 2 fusion signals, whereas positive cells show 1 red, 1 green, and 1 fusion signal.

Statistics

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 22.0. The relationships between HER2-low expression and clinicopathological features were assessed using Fisher’s exact test. p-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Result interpretation

Clinical characteristics

All 25 patients with AdCC were female, with ages ranging 37–78 years (mean age: 57.04 years). Thirteen cases (13/25) involved the left breast, and twelve cases (12/25) involved the right breast. B-mode ultrasound or mammography revealed hypoechoic or mixed-echoic nodules with clear or irregular borders. B-mode ultrasound or mammography (BI-RADS) classified 3 lesion as category 3, 17 as category 4, and 4 as category 5 on ultrasound or mammography. One case lacked B-mode ultrasound or mammography data. The maximum tumor diameter ranged from 1 to 4.5 cm, and all lesions presented as single solid nodules (Supplementary Table 1).

Histological features

According to the 2019 WHO classification of breast tumors, there were 21 cases (21/25) of C-AdCC, 2 cases (2/25) of SB-AdCC, and 2 cases (2/25) of AdCC with high-grade transformation. The Nottingham histological grade was I in 4 cases (4/25), II in 18 cases (18/25), and III in 3 cases (3/25).

Microscopically, C-AdCC exhibited tubular, trabecular, or cribriform architecture, with varying sizes of cribriform spaces. Some cribriform spaces contained sparse basophilic mucinous material, whereas others were filled with eosinophilic collagen-like substance or are continuous with hyalinized stroma. The cells lining the cribriform spaces were basaloid in appearance, and darker-stained glandular epithelial cells were observed within small cribriform spaces and ductal structures. Cellular atypia was minimal, with rare or absent mitotic figures (≤ 3/10 high-power fields, HPF), and no necrosis was present (Fig. 1A). SB-AdCC grew in solid nests, map-like, or island-like patterns, with solid areas comprising more than 95% of the tumor. The tumor cells exhibited moderate to severe nuclear atypia, with round or oval nuclei and uniform chromatin distribution, resembling basaloid morphology. The cytoplasmwas scant, and mitotic activity is frequent (> 7/10 HPF) (Fig. 1B). One case of C-AdCC and one case of SB-AdCC exhibited high-grade transformation, in which the high-grade transformed component consisted exclusively of invasive ductal carcinoma, and Necrosis is visible in the AdCC areas (Fig. 1C).

Immunohistochemistry results

Among the 25 cases of AdCC, 21 (21/25) were ER-negative and 4 (4/25) were ER-positive with a positivity index of 2–20%. All 25 cases (25/25) were PR-negative. Twenty-four cases (24/25) were AR-negative, while one case (1/25) was weakly AR-positive with a positivity index of 5%. Fourteen cases (14/25) were HER2 (0), nine cases (9/25) were HER2 (1+), and two cases (2/25) were HER2 (2+), the latter two were confirmed negative for amplification by FISH. Neuroendocrine markers CD56, CgA, and Syn were negative in all cases. The Ki-67 positivity index ranged from 5 to 90%. In 21 cases of C-AdCC and one case of C-AdCC with high-grade transformation, the cribriform and trabecular areas showed CK5/6 and p63 expression in myoepithelial or basal-like cells, while CK7 was positive in the glandular epithelium, and CD117 was positive in both glandular and myoepithelial cells. In two cases of SB-AdCC and one case of SB-AdCC with high-grade transformation, the solid areas demonstrated diffuse strong CD117 positivity, focal positivity for CK7 and CK5/6, and negativity for p63 (Figs. 2 and 3).

Gene abnormalities status

Among the 25 cases, MYB break-apart were observed in 14 cases, including 12 cases of C-AdCC and 2 cases of SB-AdCC. 1 case SB-AdCC(case #20)and 1 case C-AdCC (case #25) presented MYB/NFIB fusion. Details of the MYB break-apart and MYB/NFIB fusion findings are shown in Fig. 4.

Correlation between HER2 status and MYB gene abnormalities

Currently, Most published data and clinical trials define breast cancer with HER2 IHC 1 + or 2+/no amplification by in situ hybridization as HER2-low breast cancer [6–8]. In this study, among 25 cases of mammary AdCC, 14 were HER2 IHC 0 (14/25), while 11 exhibited HER2-low expression (IHC 1+/2+) (11/25). In the HER2 IHC 0 cohort, 5 cases demonstrated MYB gene abnormalities, including 4 cases with MYB break-apart and 1 case with MYB-NFIB fusion. Notably, among the 11 cases with HER2-low expression, 9 exhibited MYB gene abnormalities, including 8 cases with MYB break-apart and 1 case with MYB-NFIB fusion. Fisher’s exact test revealed a statistically significant positive correlation between HER2-low expression and MYB gene abnormalities (χ2 = 5.314, P = 0.042). Furthermore, correlation analysis revealed no statistically significant association between HER2 status and other clinicopathological parameters, including age, tumor size, Ki-67 index, lymph node metastasis, and pN stage (P >0.05) (Table 1).

Treatment and prognosis

Among all patients, 5 underwent modified radical mastectomy, 15 underwent simple mastectomy with sentinel lymph node biopsy, 4 underwent breast-preserving surgery with sentinel lymph node dissection, and 1 received three cycles of a neoadjuvant chemotherapy regimen consisting of 60 mg of doxorubicin Liposomal and 0.9 g of cyclophosphamide for injection. Following completion of chemotherapy, a simple mastectomy was performed along with sentinel lymph node dissection. Two cases (2/25) had axillary lymph node metastasis, and 23 cases (23/25) had no lymph node metastasis.

Follow-up ranged from 6 to 136 months. Three cases were lost to follow-up, one patient died at 60 months, and 21 patients remained in good general condition without recurrence or metastasis.

Discussion

Shin and Rosen [9] first reported nine cases of salivary gland-like AdCC of the breast in 2002. In 2019, the WHO Classification of Breast Tumours officially recognized this entity, defining AdCC as an infiltrative carcinoma composed of epithelial and myoepithelial cells, characterized by the formation of true and false glandular lumina, production of basophilic mucinous stroma and basement membrane-like material, and architectural patterns that are typically tubular, cribriform, and solid patterns. Most cases harbor the MYB-NFIB fusion. The histological features of mammary AdCC closely resemble those of salivary gland AdCC. Based on structural and cytological characteristics, mammary AdCC is classified into three subtypes: classic, solid-basaloid, and high-grade transformation [5]. Each subtype exhibits distinct histopathological features and clinical prognoses. Molecular testing facilitates the differentiation of mammary AdCC and their molecular alterations, providing a molecular genetic basis for individualized treatment and targeted therapy selection.

There is a lack of large-scale comparative studies examining differences in age of onset, preferred location, and tumor size among the different subtypes of mammary AdCC. In this study, 25 female patients aged 38 to 84 years were included, all presenting with palpable single nodules and without symptoms such as nipple discharge or bleeding. The age distribution in this cohort is consistent with previous literature, with cases in male or pediatric patients rarely reported [10]. The maximum tumor diameter ranged from 1 to 4.5 cm, with all cases presenting as single solid nodules. This is consistent with existing reports, which describe mammary AdCC primarily as single solid nodules, with occasional reports of microcystic patterns [11]. Color Doppler ultrasonography predominantly showed hypoechoic or mixed echogenic nodules with clear or irregular borders. Currently, there are few imaging studies focused on mammary AdCC, and no specific radiological features have been identified.

Most mammary AdCC are TNBC, although some literature has reported varying degrees of ER and/or PR positivity [1]. Among the 25 cases of AdCC included in this study, 21 exhibited a triple-negative phenotype, and Among these, 20 were negative for AR, suggesting that the majority of mammary AdCC may be classified as quadruple-negative breast cancer. Further research with larger sample sizes is needed to clarify AR expression patterns in AdCC. C-AdCC typically expresses glandular epithelial markers (EMA, CK7) along with basal-like or myoepithelial markers (CK5/6, p63, S-100, etc.). Previous studies have indicated that SB-AdCC often shows a significant reduction or loss of p63 expression, which is associated with more aggressive behavior and poorer prognosis [12]. In this study, two cases of SB-AdCC and one case of SB-AdCC with high-grade transformation exhibited either absent or focal p63 expression, consistent with previous literature reports. The Ki-67 proliferation index varied among tumor samples, with higher proliferation rates observed in SB-AdCC and in areas of AdCC with with high-grade transformation.

Histologically, mammary AdCC should be differentiated from the following diseases: ① Invasive cribriform carcinoma, which is composed entirely of glandular epithelial cells, lacks pseudoglandular changes, is positive for ER and PR, and negative for myoepithelial markers. ② Tubular carcinoma, which consists of glands lined by a single layer of columnar epithelial cells, lacks intraluminal mucin, is positive for ER and PR, and negative for myoepithelial markers. ③ Microglandular adenosis, characterized by small, round glands lined by a single layer of non-atypical epithelial cells containing eosinophilic concentrated secretions and negative for myoepithelial markers. ④ Collagenous spherulosis, a microscopic lesion often associated with benign intraductal proliferative lesions, characterized by hyperplastic myoepithelial cells surrounding basement membrane-like spherical (radial or spool-shaped) structures. In contrast, the small glands in AdCC vary in size and shape, often displaying solid tubules with cystic-adenoid structures and basal-like features in the lining cells, with mucin frequently present within and around the glandular structures.

Research has shown that mammary AdCC, similar to salivary gland AdCC, harbors the t(6;9) (q22-23;p23-24) gene translocation, resulting in the fusion of the proto-oncogene MYB with the transcription factor NFIB [12, 13]. Most C-AdCC possess the MYB-NFIB fusion gene, with a FISH detection rates in the Literature ranging from 23–100% [14, 15]. Cases lacking the MYB-NFIB fusion gene may exhibit alternative molecular alterations, such as MYBL1 rearrangements or MYB amplification [16]. Lei T et al. [17] reported a case of advanced mammary AdCC with the MYB-EWSR1 fusion, suggesting an association with poor prognosis. SB-AdCC often harbors mutations in NOTCH, CREBBP, KMT2C, and other genes [18, 19] and demonstrates a relatively low frequency of MYB gene rearrangement [20]. Studies have shown that SB-AdCC exhibits more aggressive behavior than C-AdCC [11] with a significantly reduced disease-free survival rate regardless of tumor size. This aggressiveness may be attributed to NOTCH mutations activating the NOTCH pathway, promoting tumor cell invasion and metastasis [20]. When AdCC undergoes high-grade transformation and progresses to high-grade TNBC [14] clones with mutations in EP300, NOTCH1, ERBB2, FGFR1, KMT2C, STAG2, KDM6A, and CDK12 often emerge. Molecular studies have confirmed the morphological hypothesis that high-grade transformation is associated with the acquisition of new somatic mutations, reflecting clonal evolution during progression.

In this study, FISH was performed to detect MYB gene breakage across all cases, while MYB-NFIB fusion detection was conducted exclusively on two cases. Future research aim to expand the sample size of mammary AdCC through multicenter studies to facilitate comprehensive fusion genes detection, which may enable the identification of novel fusion gene loci. Additionally, next-generation sequencing (NGS) was not employed in the present study. Notably, SB-AdCC exhibits distinct biological behaviors compared to C-AdCC, suggesting potential disparities in gene expression profiles between these subtypes. These differences warrant in-depth investigation in future studies to elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms.

Due to the lack of a grading system for salivary-gland-like C-AdCC of the breast and the absence of a standardized treatment protocol, there is a risk of overtreatment in low-grade tumors and undertreatment in high-grade tumors. C-AdCC generally has a favorable prognosis [21] with axillary lymph node and distant metastases occurring rarely. Notably, adjuvant chemotherapy does not appear to improve the clinical outcomes of C-AdCC, even among patients at high risk of recurrence and metastasis [22]. For example, in this study, among the 25 cases of AdCC, only 2 cases exhibited axillary lymph node metastasis, with fewer than four metastatic lymph nodes in both cases. Breast-conserving surgery with sentinel lymph node biopsy may represent an optimal treatment option for C-AdCC [23]. The value of systemic adjuvant chemotherapy remains unclear, and postoperative chemotherapy is generally not recommended for C-AdCC [24]. However, patients with tumors larger than 3 cm, lymph node metastases (beyond micrometastases), or high-grade lesions may be considered for systemic adjuvant chemotherapy [25]. For solid C-AdCC of the breast with basal-like features, mastectomy with axillary lymph node dissection supplemented by radiotherapy is typically required [26, 27].

As a subtype of TNBC (ER < 1%, PR < 1%, HER2 IHC 0, 1+, or 2 + with no amplification by in situ hybridization), mammary AdCC is primarily managed with conventiona chemotherapy, which often yields limited clinical benefits and is associated with frequent adverse effects. Studies have demonstrated that breast cancer patients with HER2-low expression exhibit a more favorable prognosis compared to those with HER2-0 status [28–30]. Based on evidence from clinical trials of novel anti-HER2 antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), HER2-low breast cancer patients has recently emerged as a potential new targetable subgroup within breast tumors. Emerging research has also identified that MYB-targeting small-molecule inhibitors as a potential therapeutic strategy for AdCC [31, 32]. Our findings suggest that mammary AdCC patients with HER2-low may benefit from expanded therapeutic options, including potential clinical advantages from ADC-based therapies and MYB-targeted treatments.

Conclusion

In summary, mammary AdCC cannot be simply regarded as an indolent tumor. A comprehensive understanding of the histological morphology, IHC profiles, and molecular alterations across its pathological subtypes is essential for accurate pathological diagnosis and the implementation of individualized precision treatment strategies for mammary AdCC. The The observed correlation between HER2-low expression and MYB gene abnormalities in mammary AdCC also provides a critical foundation for developing combined targeted therapies and prognostic stratification in affected patients. Future multicenter clinical studies are needed to validate the generalizability of this association, thereby enhancing the clinical management and treatment outcomes for this rare breast tumor.

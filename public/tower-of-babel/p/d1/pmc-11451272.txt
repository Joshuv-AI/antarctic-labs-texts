Characterization of the complete chloroplast genome sequence of Schisandra propinqua subsp. sinensis (Oliv.) R.M.K.Saunders 1997 (Schisandraceae)
Authors: Yu Zhang, Junqiao Zhu, Pengguo Xia, Lulu Xun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractSchisandra propinqua subsp. sinensis (Oliv.) R.M.K. Saunders 1997, a woody vine plant esteemed for its medicinal properties, has garnered attention in botanical research. In this study, we elucidated the complete chloroplast genome sequence of S. propinqua. The genome spans 145,562 bp and comprises a large single-copy (LSC) region of 94,164 bp, a small single-copy region of 18,294 bp (SSC), and a pair of inverted repeats (IR) of 16,552 bp. Notably, S. propinqua exhibits an overall GC content of 36.2%. Annotation revealed a total of 116 genes, encompassing 81 protein-coding genes, 27 transfer RNA (tRNA) genes, and 8 ribosomal RNA (rRNA) genes. Phylogenetic analysis unveiled a close relationship among Schisandraceae, indicating the evolutionary proximity. This comprehensive genomic dataset provides valuable insights into the genetic makeup and evolutionary dynamics within the Schisandra genus.

Introduction

Schisandra propinqua subsp. sinensis (Oliv.) R.M.K. Saunders 1997 is a deciduous woody vine plant belonging to the Schisandraceae family (Saunders 2000). It predominantly thrives in regions spanning Shaanxi, southern Gansu, Jiangxi, Henan, Hubei, Hunan, Sichuan, Guizhou, and central to southern Yunnan in China, flourishing at elevations ranging from 500 to 2000 m (Flora of China Editorial Committee, 2008) (Figure 1). The roots, stems, leaves, and fruits of S. propinqua hold medicinal significance, particularly in Sichuan Province, where it is utilized in treating traumatic injury, gastralgia, angeitis, and associated ailments (Li et al. 2012). Despite its medicinal importance, research on S. propinqua has predominantly focused on its compound properties, with its chloroplast genome remaining unpublished. Thus, this study aims to assemble and characterize the complete chloroplast genome of S. propinqua, shedding light on its phylogenetic relationships with other species in Schisandra and the Schisandraceae family.

Materials and methods

Sample collection and preservation

The materials of S. propinqua were collected from Shiquan County, Ankang City, Shaanxi Province (coordinates: 32°50′47″N, 108°11′53″E; altitude: 619 m). A specimen was deposited at the Herbarium of Xi’an Botanical Garden (http://www.xazwy.com, contact: Xun Lulu, email: xunlulu@xab.ac.cn) under the voucher number Xun Lulu et al. ZJ0103. Leaf samples were specifically taken for this study. Total genomic DNA associated with this collection has been securely deposited at the Key Laboratory of Plant Secondary Metabolism and Regulation of Zhejiang Province, Zhejiang Sci-Tech University (http://sky.zstu.edu.cn), under the manufacturer number ZSTUX0103. This collection was conducted by Pengguo Xia (contact: xpg_xpg@zstu.edu.cn).

DNA extraction and sequencing

Genomic DNA was extracted employing the CTAB extraction method, following the protocol outlined by Doyle (1987). Subsequently, the genomic library was sequenced using the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (Modi et al. 2021), with reads filtered using Fastp software (Chen et al. 2018). Assembly of the chloroplast genome was performed utilizing GetOrganelle v1.7.0 (Jin et al. 2020). To check the coverage of assembly, Bowtie2 and Samtools were used to align, Matplotlib for Python was used to visualize the coverage map. The assembled sequence was annotated using Geneious Prime software (Kearse et al. 2012), with reference to the chloroplast genome of S. chinensis (accession number: KY111264). The circular genome map was drawn using the CPGView program (http://www.1kmpg.cn/cpgview/).

Phylogenetic analysis

To elucidate the phylogenetic position of S. propinqua relative to other species within the Schisandraceae family, we conducted a phylogenetic analysis based on the common protein-coding sequences (CDS) among the chloroplast genomes of 12 species. Additionally, we included three species of the Illicium genus (I. burmanicum, I. tsaii, I. verum) as outgroups. All sequences were aligned using MAFFT (Nakamura et al. 2018), and a Maximum Likelihood (ML) tree was constructed using IQTREE v1.6.7 software (Nguyen et al. 2015). The ML tree was generated under the optimal model of GTR+F + I + G4, and statistical support was assessed through 1000 bootstrap replicates.

Results

General feature of the chloroplast genome

The sequencing depth and coverage are good, with an average sequencing depth of 418X and a minimum depth of 180X. There are no uncovered regions, making the assembly results highly reliable (Figure S1). The chloroplast genome of S. propinqua spans 145,562 bp in length, featuring a large single copy (LSC) region of 94,164 bp, a small single copy (SSC) region of 18,294 bp, and two inverted repeats (IRs) of 16,552 bp each. The GC content of the genome is 39.7%. A comprehensive annotation revealed a total of 116 genes, consisting of 8 rRNAs, 27 tRNAs, and 81 protein-coding genes (Figure 2). Notably, seven genes (rpl2, atpF, rpoC1, rps16, ndhB, and ndhA, ndhE) harbor one intron each, while three genes (ycf3, clpP, and rps12) possess two introns each (Figure S2), with rps12 being a trans-spliced gene (Figure S3).

Phylogenetic analysis

Utilizing the chloroplast genomes of nine ingroup species and three outgroup species, we constructed a phylogenetic tree using the maximum likelihood method. The resulting tree revealed the relationships among the genera Illicium, Kadsura, and Schisandra within the Schisandraceae family, all with high bootstrap values indicating high confidence. (Figure 3) The results showed that the genus Illicium (I. tsaii, I. burmanicum, I. verum), which was used as an outgroup, formed a distinct clade with strong support. Within the genus Schisandra, some species, such as S. propinqua and S. macrocarpa, clustered together with the genus Kadsura. Meanwhile, the remaining Schisandra species (S. chinensis, S. sphenanthera, S. repanda) formed a separate clade.

Discussion and conclusions

The chloroplast genome of S. propinqua is similar to other chloroplast genomes within the Schisandra genus, characterized by a shorter IR (approximately 16 kb). This is reflected in the absence of genes such as ycf2 in the IRb region. This feature has been observed in the chloroplast genomes of species in the Illicium (Zhou et al. 2024), Kadsura (Fu et al. 2020; Qin, Deng, and Shi 2021), and other Schisandra species (Guo et al. 2017; Wei et al. 2020), indicating it is a common trait in the Schisandraceae family.

The phylogenetic analysis of chloroplast genomes from nine ingroup species and three outgroup species has elucidated the relationships within the Schisandraceae family with high confidence. Our findings confirm that the genus Illicium (I. tsaii, I. burmanicum, I. verum) is distinct and forms a well-supported outgroup separate from the Kadsura and Schisandra genera, consistent with previous studies (Liu 2002; Zhang et al. 2020). Notably, within the sister group formed by Kadsura and Schisandra, S. macrocarpa and S. propinqua are grouped with Kadsura, highlighting the unique phylogenetic position of S. propinqua, this non-monophyletic characteristic is also supported by the previous study (Wang et al. 2006). Liu suggests that the Kadsura genus is more primitive compared to the Schisandra genus (Liu 2002). Throughout their long evolutionary history, convergent evolution or historical gene flow events may have occurred between the Kadsura and Schisandra genera. Similarly, morphological studies tend to separate S. propinqua from other Schisandra species based on their distinct characteristics (Lin and Yang 2007). Currently, there are few studies on the chloroplast genomes and phylogenetics of the Schisandra genus or the Schisandraceae family, underscoring the need for further research into the evolutionary relationships and divergence times within Schisandra.

Within the Schisandra genus, there are both evergreen and deciduous species (Lin and Yang 2007). Previous studies have reported a close relationship between evergreen species within the Schisandra genus and the Kadsura genus (Hao, Chye, and Saunders 2001; Wang et al. 2006) The phylogenetic tree in this study is consistent with previous research, showing a close phylogenetic relationship between S. propinqua and the Kadsura genus, suggesting that S. propinqua may also be an evergreen species.

This study reports the chloroplast genome of S. propinqua, including its annotation and characteristic analysis, and determines its phylogenetic position within the Schisandraceae family. The chloroplast genome reported in this study can be used for future comparative studies, DNA marker development, molecular dating, and other research. In the future, our goal is to supplement the chloroplast genomes of other Schisandra species to deepen our understanding of the genus.

The complete chloroplast genome sequence of Lysimachia medogensis (Primulaceae) and its phylogenetic analysis
Authors: Cheng-Wu Liu, Jian-Hua Ma, Li Luan, Yue-Liang Jiang, Yu Feng, Bo Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractLysimachia medogensis F. H. Chen and Hu 1979 is a narrowly endemic species in Medog county, Xizang Province, China. Here its complete chloroplast genome was assembled. It exhibits a typical circular quadripartite structure of 154,167 bp in length, consisting of a large single-copy region (84,223 bp), a small single-copy region (17,994 bp), and a pair of inverted repeat regions (25,975 bp). A total of 126 genes were annotated, including 85 protein-coding genes, 33 transfer RNA genes, and 8 ribosomal RNA genes. Phylogenetic analysis strongly supports its placement within the subgenus Palladia. This genome provides a crucial genetic resource for future phylogenetic and conservation studies.Keywords: Lysimachia medogensis, narrow endemic species, chloroplast genome, phylogenetic relationship

Introduction

The genus Lysimachia L. represents a basal lineage within the family Primulaceae and exhibits a broad biogeographic distribution (Hu and Kelso 1996). With approximately 180 recognized species, this genus primarily inhabits temperate and subtropical zones of the Northern Hemisphere, while maintaining limited representation in Africa, Latin America, and Oceania (Hu 1994). The southwest of China serves as both a center of origin and diversification for this taxon, harboring a remarkable diversity comprising 132 species and 17 varieties, most of which demonstrate strict endemicity to this floristic region (Chen and Hu 1979; Hu 1994).

L. medogensis F. H. Chen and Hu 1979 represents a notable example of narrowly endemic species endemic to Medog County, Xizang, China. This perennial herb is characterized by its erect, distinctly quadrangular stems, sessile oblanceolate leaves with a subauriculate-dilated base, and terminal racemes of pink flowers (Figure 1), which collectively distinguish it from closely relates species, such as L. tsarongensis Hand.-Mazz., 1928 and L. robusta Hand.-Mazz., 1928 (Chen and Hu 1979). The species is currently known only from the Duoxiong Valley watershed, where it occurs in alpine meadows between 2,600–3,100 m elevation (Figure 1A). Despite its taxonomic distinctiveness and restricted range, no genomic studies have been conducted on L. medogensis since its original description. To fill this knowledge gap and provide foundational genetic resources for future conservation and phylogenetic research, this study presents the first complete chloroplast genome sequence of L. medogensis.

Material and methods

The fresh leaves of L. medogensis were collected from Medog County, Xizang Province, China (95.049273 N, 29.429014E, elevation ca. 2840 m) on 28 July 2023. The voucher specimen (voucher: YLZB07714; identifier: Meng Li, contact person: Bo Xu, xubo@cib.ac.cn) has been deposited at the Herbarium of the Chengdu Institute of Biology (CDBI), Chinese Academy of Sciences.

Genomic DNA was extracted from silica-gel dried leaves using Plant DNA Isolation Kit (Cat. No. DE-06111). Sequencing was performed on an Illumina platform to generate paired-end reads. Raw sequencing data were processed with fastp v0.23.2 (Chen et al. 2018) to perform quality control, adapter trimming, and filtering, ensuring high-quality clean data for downstream analyses. The chloroplast genome was assembled from the cleaned reads using GetOrganelle v1.7.5 (Jin et al. 2020).

Gene annotation was carried out using the Plastid Genome Annotator (PGA) (Qu et al. 2019). with L. congestiflora Hemsl, 1889 (accession number: MK834324.1) (Li et al. 2019) as the reference. Initial annotations were manually corrected in Geneious v11 (Kearse et al. 2012) to adjust misannotated gene boundaries, start/stop codons, and intron-exon structures based on sequence homology and consensus rules. Finally, the annotated chloroplast genome was visualized by CPGview (Liu et al. 2023).

A phylogenetic tree was reconstructed using the complete chloroplast genome sequences from 34 Lysimachia species, with Androsace henryi Oliv., 1904 (accession number: OM948747.1) and A. wardii W. W. Sm., 1913 (accession number: OM948748.1) as ougroups.(Table S1). The sequences of all 36 taxa were aligned with MAFFT v7.475 (Katoh and Standley 2013). Maximum likelihood (ML) phylogenetic analysis was conducted using IQ-TREE v2.0.3 (Minh et al. 2020), and the resulting tree was visualized with the Chiplot online site (Xie et al. 2023).

Result

The complete chloroplast genome of L. medogensis was assembled and found to be 154,167 bp in length, with a total GC content of 37.0% (Figure 2). The average sequencing coverage reached 7942.98× (Figure S1). The genome displays a typical quadripartite structure, including a large single-copy (LSC) region of 84,223 bp, a small single-copy (SSC) region of 17,994 bp, and two inverted repeat (IR) regions each 25,975 bp. A total of 126 genes were annotated, consisting of 85 protein-coding genes (CDS), 33 transfer RNA (tRNA) genes, and 8 ribosomal RNA (rRNA) genes. Eleven genes (rps16, rpoC1, rpl2, rpl16, petD, petB, ndhB, ndhA, atpF) were identified as containing a single intron (Figure S2). The rps12 gene exhibits a trans-splicing architecture, with three exons, two of which are duplicated within the IR regions (Figure S3).

Phylogenomic analysis based on complete chloroplast genomes from 35 Lysimachia taxa and two outgroups resolved four strongly supported clades (bootstrap = 100%), corresponding to subgenera Idiophyton (Clade I), Naumburgis (Clade II), Palladia (Clade III), and Lysimachia (Clade IV) (Figure 3). Maximum likelihood analysis confidently placed L. medogensis within subg. Palladia, where it formed a sister group to a clade comprising L. lobelioides and L. decurrens (bootstrap = 100%).

Discussion and conclusion

This study provides the chloroplast reference genome of Lysimachia medogensis, whose structure aligns with other Lysimachia species, exhibiting the typical quadripartite structure. Specifically, it is composed of one small single-copy region (SSC), one large single-copy region (LSC), and two inverted repeat regions (IRs) (Zhang et al. 2021). The phylogenetic tree topology based on chloroplast genomes is consistent with previous studies (Gang and Hu 2001; Liu et al. 2023), with subg. Idiophyton as the basal group and sister to the other three subgenera, subg. Lysimachia and subg. Palladia form a sister group, which is overall sister to subg. Naumburgis. However, the absence of chloroplast genome data for subg. Heterostylandra, subg. Lysimachiopsis, and subg. Seleucia in the public databases precluded a comprehensive phylogenetic assessment of the genus. Future studies incorporating data from these lineages are essential to refine the phylogenetic framework of Lysimachia. Notwithstanding this limitation, the findings of this study provide a valuable phylogenetic foundation for subsequent research on population genetics and conservation strategies in Lysimachia.

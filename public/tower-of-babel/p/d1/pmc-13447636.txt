A novel ensemble-based genetic algorithm for enhanced feature selection in lung and pancreatic cancer diagnosis
Authors: Shabia Shabir, Mohammad Ahsan Chishti
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A major challenge encountered in high dimensional cancer datasets is the presence of irrelevant or redundant features, which can decrease the performance of the classifier model. An efficient feature selection technique is required in order to develop accurate and clinically relevant models for prediction or early detection of cancers. So, in order to enhance feature selection and improve diagnostic accuracy, especially in cancer datasets, this study introduces a novel framework, i.e., Best Cost Genetic Algorithm with Ensemble Crossover Operation (BCGA-ECO). The BCGA-ECO framework integrates three complementary crossover techniques, i.e., Single Point Crossover (SPC), Double Point Crossover (DPC), and Uniform Arithmetic Crossover (UAC), and evaluates chromosomes based on a composite objective function combining mean squared error (MSE) and feature compactness. Experiments were conducted using three datasets: the UCI Lung Cancer dataset containing 32 samples with 56 features, the GEO pancreatic gene expression dataset (GDS4100) containing 24 samples with 76 features, and a clinical pancreatic tumor dataset comprising 74 patient records with 20 clinical features. The performance was assessed using accuracy, RMSE, F1-score, and AUC-ROC. Comparative benchmarks included BestFit, GANN, ReliefF, LASSO, mRMR, etc. BCGA-ECO has shown better performance on these datasets. For the lung cancer dataset (from UCI Repository), BCGA-ECO achieved 93.5% accuracy with an RMSE of 0.209. Therefore, BCGA-ECO outperformed individual crossover methods and feature selectors. For the pancreatic dataset (GDS4100), BCGA-ECO achieved 95.6% accuracy with an RMSE of 0.182. This method helped improve convergence, feature diversity, and robustness. The improvements were consistent across datasets. Additionally, a clinical dataset from pancreatic tumor patients was used to predict post-surgical patient survival. The proposed BCGA-ECO achieved improved efficiency (MSE = 0.079) compared with the traditional GA-NN model (MSE = 0.087), further demonstrating its robustness for moderate- to high-dimensional clinical datasets (typically tens to a few hundred features), achieving AUC-ROC values of 0.962 for lung cancer and 0.974 for pancreatic cancer datasets. Also, ablation studies verified that BCGA-ECO outperformed its single crossover counterparts (SPC, DPC, and UAC).

Introduction

Soft computing involves the design of approximate computational models that deal with functional approximation and random search using neural networks and evolutionary techniques with approximate reasoning using fuzzy logic and probabilistic models to solve complex problems [1]. This paper introduces a novel feature selection method of Best Cost Genetic Algorithm (BCGA) with Ensemble Crossover (ECO) based on a genetic algorithm (GA) that addresses limitations in traditional feature selection techniques, such as computational inefficiency, sensitivity to local minima, and difficulty in handling complex, high-dimensional datasets. By combining the adaptive search capabilities of genetic algorithms with the robustness of ensemble-based crossover techniques, the proposed method efficiently selects an optimal subset of features that improves classifier accuracy while reducing computational complexity [2].

The paper explains in detail the BCGA-ECO algorithm with specific regard to its innovation in the merge of single point, double point, and uniform arithmetic crossover techniques. To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed method, experiments were conducted using datasets for lung and pancreatic cancer. These two types of cancer were selected because of their importance clinically and in regards to diagnosis. Pancreatic cancer has one of the lowest survival rates considering the cancer is often diagnosed at advanced stages because of vague symptoms. This indicates that early and accurate diagnostics are extremely crucial. Likewise, lung cancer continues to rank highest in mortality due to malignancies across the globe, which shows the need for more effective and efficient diagnostic devices.

The paper presents the analysis on cancer diagnosis accuracy using traditional feature selection methods as opposed to the proposed method and demonstrated that feature reduction improved diagnostic accuracy. Studies on the lung and the pancreatic cancer datasets were used to test for the methodology’s effectiveness and demonstrated substantial improvement in the error rates and the diagnostic accuracy. The findings suggest that BCGA-ECO is effective in enhancing classification performance for high-dimensional datasets in the medical field. In addition to improve classification granularity, it is also favorable because it lowers the processing cost of such biomedical datasets.

The results in the paper highlight the relevance of BCGA- ECO in the context of medical features, which are often neglected due to their complexity and multifactorial character. These results could easily be extended with additional datasets, as there are more classification aspects that could benefit from noise filtering coupled with improved classification performance while still being resource efficient. Such an approach would test the boundaries of this method in solving real life problems through applying a more systematic and less restrictive approach.

This paper reviews traditional feature selection techniques and genetic algorithm-based approaches, followed by a detailed description of the proposed model, including its structure and algorithmic design. Experimental evaluations are conducted with a particular focus on cancer datasets. In this study, we propose BCGA-ECO, an ensemble crossover-based genetic algorithm for robust feature selection, and demonstrate its effectiveness through ablation analysis, statistical validation, and comparative evaluation on lung and pancreatic cancer datasets.

The core innovation of this study lies in the integration of ensemble crossover strategies within a genetic algorithm framework, combined with a cost-based objective function that simultaneously minimizes classification error and feature redundancy. This design improves convergence behavior and mitigates common limitations of traditional methods, such as susceptibility to local optima in high-dimensional feature selection tasks.

Related work

Feature selection is a crucial preprocessing step in machine learning and data analysis, particularly for high-dimensional datasets. It aims to identify the most relevant and informative features while discarding redundant or irrelevant ones, thereby improving model performance, interpretability, and computational efficiency. Feature selection techniques are classified based on knowledge available or based on a combination of selection algorithms with model building. Based on the Combination of feature selection with model building, it has been broadly classified into filter and wrapper methods. In the filter method, the features are ranked on some statistical criteria whereas in the wrapper method, a certain subset is selected out of a possible feature subset and evaluated using any classification algorithm. In addition to this, the heuristic approach proved to be more efficient in feature selection. The issues in conventional algorithms for feature selection have been investigated and a new approach was proposed that provided an efficient feature selection method and high classification accuracy [3–4]. A Hybrid soft computing technique of the genetic algorithm has also been implemented for breast abnormality classification using a feature selection approach. The approach used GA for feature selection and neural network classifier to distinguish benign and malignant cases. GA has also been evaluated by a classifier SVM and PO that reduced the search complexity in feature space [5–6]. It has been used in combination with an ANN classifier (LM-NN) designed to identify Abnormal Cardiac beats to detect heart ailments (Bundle Branch Block). The basic principle of working lies in combining the qualification aspects of ANN and the optimization aspects of GA.

Genetic Algorithm is a randomized wrapper method that avoids the issue of getting stuck in local minima and interacts with an evaluator or classifier. It filters out the strong features that best predict the class of an instance. When feature selection is applied over the gene expression data, we call that gene selection. Various issues have been listed out by some researchers in the gene expression data like irrelevant and noisy data that leads to the problematic analysis of data. It helps in avoiding the problem of overfitting and provides faster and cost-efficient models [7]. Recent advancements in feature selection have led to the development of more sophisticated techniques that address the limitations of traditional methods[10]. Ensemble Feature Selection Methods use a combination of various feature selection techniques to achieve higher robustness and accuracy. However, these methods are associated with increased computational complexity and overfitting if not validated appropriately [8]. Deep Learning Based Feature Selection utilizes deep learning techniques to learn the most important features from raw data automatically, making these methods suitable for high-dimensional data. However, these methods are associated with a need for large amounts of data and hardware resources [9].

Sparse Learning Methods, including LASSO and Elastic Net, induce sparsity in feature selection, but they are sensitive to regularization parameters and can be challenged with correlated features. Evolutionary Algorithms, including Differential Evolution and Particle Swarm Optimization, have an optimal trade-off between exploration and exploitation, but they can be computationally expensive and sensitive to parameters [11]. Hybrid Optimization Techniques, including Genetic Algorithm combined with Ant Colony Optimization and Simulated Annealing, can improve optimization/simulated annealing and feature selection, but they can be more complex and expensive in terms of computations [12].

Transfer Learning for Feature Selection uses techniques to take advantage of knowledge from related tasks to improve feature selection, although it may not be applicable if the domains are too different, leading to negative transfer. Multi-Objective Feature Selection is a feature selection technique where multiple objectives are handled at the same time, and it is difficult to implement and analyze. Evolutionary Algorithms have been extensively studied for solving optimization problems, and significant progress has been reported using hybridization, ensemble, and adaptive strategies. Evolutionary Algorithms have also been effective in balancing the objectives, and recent advancements in crossover strategies and ensemble strategies have reported significant improvements in balancing the objectives [13].

EnXEA, a crossover ensemble evolutionary algorithm, was presented by Sharma et al. [14], where a crossover ensemble was presented. This is a dynamic framework for solving dynamic and multi-objective optimization problems. This is where multiple adaptive crossover strategies are used and selected based on probability. This has low computational complexity, where multiple crossovers are simultaneously evaluated and selected based on probability.

EMaOEA, an ensemble of many-objective evolutionary algorithms, was developed by Zhou et al. [15], where a higher-level ensemble is presented. This is where, instead of focusing on crossover variation, resource allocation and algorithm selections in an ensemble context are emphasized. This is a dynamic framework for dynamic adaptability and multi-objective optimization, with exponential computational complexity based on the number of algorithms in the ensemble. This is where, by adaptively allocating resources among algorithms, EMaOEA is capable of balancing convergence and diversity on high-dimensional Pareto fronts.

In contrast, the proposed BCGA-ECO (Best Crossover Genetic Algorithm with Ensemble Crossover Operators) method introduces a hybrid design that strategically integrates three crossover operators: SPC (Single-Point Crossover), DPC (Double-Point Crossover), and UAC (Uniform Arithmetic Crossover). Unlike EnXEA and EMaOEA, BCGA-ECO employs a static selection mechanism, where the best two offspring among six candidates are consistently retained, ensuring quality preservation at each generation. Although BCGA-ECO is not inherently adaptive, its design emphasizes feature subset reduction (FSR), making it especially effective in single-objective optimization tasks with high-dimensional feature spaces. The computational cost is lower compared to ensemble-based adaptive frameworks, thereby offering efficiency while maintaining competitive accuracy. Thus, while EnXEA and EMaOEA focus on adaptivity and multi-objective optimization, BCGA-ECO distinguishes itself as a computationally efficient, feature-reduction-oriented method that balances operator diversity with static yet effective offspring selection.

Operations in genetic algorithm- crossover and mutation

One of the issues in Genetic algorithms is the careful selection and development of genetic operators. Focusing on the genetic operators, some important methods have been used so far to perform the crossover between the feature set or chromosome. The process of crossover involves combining two chromosomes to produce to produce an offspring solution chromosome. The basic reason behind performing this operation is to search for a new chromosome that would have better cost values than the parent chromosomes, taking the best characteristic from each parent i.e. the performance might be better than the previous chromosomes. Crossover Operation involves both parental chromosomes usually split at a randomly determined crossover point(s). Some important and basic crossover techniques are Single or one point crossover (SPC), double-point crossover (DPC) and Uniform arithmetic crossover (UAC) [29-30].

Single Point Crossover technique divides the first chromosome into two parts de- pending upon the single point selected and appends its 1st part with the 2nd part of the other divided chromosome. Apart from this, techniques have been provided that use multiple cut points to perform the crossover operation so that the problem space can be searched more in-depth. In the double or two-point crossover, the chromosome is divided into three sections using two crossover cut points. The Uniform Arithmetic Crossover (UAC) technique implements the concept of Intermediate recombination that involves copying samples from the two participating parents depending on the alpha value. Uniform Arithmetic point crossover takes the sample from 1st parent chromosome if the corresponding alpha value (randomly generated) is 1 and or it takes the sample from 2nd parent if the corresponding alpha value is ’0’ [25–28] i.e. new alpha value (0 / 1) is chosen for each variable. Ensemble crossover strategies for balanced exploration

Every crossover technique i.e. SPC, DPC, and UAC has a unique approach to exploring solution space. Single Point Crossover (SPC), which favors exploitation, introduces moderate changes by exchanging a single segment. On the other hand, Double Point Crossover (DPC) promotes sub-segment exploration within a chromosome through two-point slicing. Uniform Arithmetic Crossover (UAC), on the other hand, provides gradual and smooth variation through fine-grained variation using a probabilistic alpha parameter. The algorithm utilizes these three operators in an ensemble-based framework to achieve a balance between local search (intensification) and global search (diversity). By maintaining a larger genetic pool within the population, premature convergence to local optima is avoided, thereby ensuring greater diversity and hence solution quality. In other words, each crossover method explores the solution space differently but mixing their outputs can reduce premature convergence by maintaining more diversity in the population.

Concept of best cost genetic algorithm (BCGA) with ensemble crossover operation (ECO)

Till now, Genetic Algorithm (GA) has been used for feature extraction or in modifying the various parameters in a nonlinear equation for further classification or clustering. Such an algorithm performed crossover operation using any of the crossover point techniques available like single point, multipoint or uniform arithmetic type i.e. till now specified crossover type was applied on the data set. Selection of any of the techniques was manual or random. The effects of the crossover function on the performance of the genetic algorithm have been analyzed and the results concluded uniform arithmetic crossover function is an outperformer among all. One or single-point crossover has also proved better than double-point crossover operation. Further, the uniform function was found to be the next best. Additionally, the two- point operator has been considered to be the least disruptive and uniform crossover as the most disruptive. Furthermore, experiments performed over a dataset of 50 instances using some of the important crossover functions have proved a single point to be a better crossover function followed by the uniform crossover.

To resolve the issue of selecting the type of Crossover operator, as discussed above, we came up with a novel idea of ensemble function which is a hybrid of Single-Point Crossover (SPC), Double-Point Crossover (DPC) and Uniform Arithmetic Crossover (UAC) operation between Parent (I) and Parent (J) wherein each feature set (Fs) is parent input to crossover function and different Crossover Points (Cp) are set for the three different functions. The technique performs the three crossover operations (SPC, DPC, and UAC) and calculates the respective objective function values (OFVs) for all offspring. All three crossover operators are deterministically applied to each parent pair, generating six offspring feature solution sets (FSS1–FSS6). Each crossover method produces two offspring, resulting in six candidate feature subsets per generation. The objective function is formulated as a minimization problem; therefore, lower OFV indicates a better feature subset. Instead of selecting the global best two among all six offspring, the algorithm applies a structured grouping strategy, selecting the minimum OFV from each predefined group of offspring to preserve crossover diversity and balanced exploration.

The population size of 10 was selected to provide sufficient diversity to the initial population without being computationally expensive. Also, the maximum number of generations was set to 10 based on initial testing, which allowed for sufficient iterations to reach convergence without unnecessarily high costs. A rank-based selection strategy was used to assign higher selection probability to individuals with lower objective function values while preserving population diversity. A mutation rate of 0.1 provided sufficient chances for the alteration of feature subsets to be small, allowing fine-tuning of solutions. Ultimately, classifier evaluation was performed over three repetitions to reduce the effects of variation and obtain accurate outcomes. The proposed algorithm of Best Cost Genetic Algorithm Ensemble Crossover operation (ECO) acts as a wrapper method of GA and provides the best crossover parents to filter out the features for being used by evaluators or Class prediction techniques. Choosing all 3 techniques i.e. SPC, DPC, UAC to participate in crossover emphasizes the effectiveness of crossover. A series of experiments have been conducted to compare the important traditional feature selection algorithms and the new proposed one with modified crossover function. The algorithm views the whole dataset in two parts input set and target set. The various factors used for experimental design are shown in the Table 1. Although the selected population size (10) and number of generations (10) are relatively small compared to conventional genetic algorithms, this choice was motivated by the small-sample nature of the datasets and the need to reduce overfitting and computational overhead. The ensemble crossover mechanism enhances diversity and accelerates convergence, allowing stable solutions to be obtained with fewer generations. Empirical observations during preliminary experiments showed no substantial improvement beyond these settings.

To create a feature set, each feature is associated with an ’S’ value. This value is set randomly with ’0’ or ’1’ indicating the absence or presence of a particular feature respectively. In other words, each chromosome is represented as a binary vector, where a value of ‘1’ indicates that the corresponding feature is selected and a value of ‘0’ indicates that the feature is excluded; mutation is applied by randomly flipping these binary values to promote population diversity.

Some of the important crossover functions are the Single point function, Double point function and Uniform arithmetic crossover. In this operation, all parents are concurrently subjected to the three crossover operators simultaneously, which allows for all possible feature combinations. Each technique produces two child feature subsets, resulting in six candidate feature subsets. Each child’s objective function values (OFVs) are calculated and the minimum OFV from each subgroup {FSS1, FSS3, FSS5} and {FSS2, FSS4, FSS6} is selected, according to the structured grouping strategy defined in Algorithm 1, for the next generation. With the use of these methods, the genetic algorithm is enhanced in the vertical and horizontal directions of the feature space. There is no scope of the estimation of the optimal value set because of the use of single crossover.

BCGA-ECO algorithm

Algorithm 1 shows the steps for implementing the novel method for feature selection which exploits ensemble

Crossover to improve the feature subset selection for high dimensional data sets. It describes the Best Cost Genetic Algorithm (BCGA) with Ensemble Crossover ECO operation, the heart of which constitutes the described feature selection methodology. The algorithm works by optimizing the classification accuracy through systematic sequential reduction of the feature subsets and finding the optimal set which minimizes the objective function value (OFV). It starts from choosing two parents feature subsets (FSI and FSJ) from the existing population. Referring to Algorithm 1, FSI and FSJ are two members of the Feature set FS; Cp is the Crossover point; FSS1 to FSS6 are feature set solutions. All parameters, including the crossover points Cp (Cpmin and Cpmax) and the alpha for single valued arithmetic crossover, are set. A parent feature set is subjected to three different crossover methods Single Point Crossover (SPC) producing FSS1 and FSS2, Double Point Crossover (DPC) producing FSS3 and FSS4 and Uniform Arithmetic Crossover (UAC) producing FSS5 and FSS6.

In SPC, the separating point where the feature subset is spliced is only one, whereas DPC splices the feature subset at two points, forming two offspring subsets for each of the methods. The UAC creates two child subsets by combining features of both parents using the alpha parameter. In UAC, the α parameter is initialized as a random value between 0.2 and 0.8 for each offspring generation to ensure diverse gene mixing. This range was selected based on empirical sensitivity testing.The offspring feature subsets are evaluated by determining its objective function value (OFV) that shows the degree of coverage of the entire dataset by the subset and the errors at the same time.

Instead of selecting the global best two offspring from all six candidates, the algorithm selects the minimum OFV offspring from two structured groups ({FSS1, FSS3, FSS5} and {FSS2, FSS4, FSS6}). This grouping strategy ensures that different crossover operators contribute offspring to the next generation, preserves crossover diversity and prevents domination by a single operator within a generation. Out of the six offspring, the minimum OFV from each predefined subgroup is selected as the fittest solution for the next generation. This is repeated for a set number of cycles or until a stopping condition is achieved.

The resulting output is the one or more feature subsets that meet the classification accuracy requirements and minimizes the OFV. The ECO algorithm incorporates SPC, DPC, and UAC, and therefore demonstrates improved search features.

In addition, the selection of the best feature subsets ensures that only the best solutions are selected, which increases the efficiency and accuracy of the method. Local minima problems are avoided and feature space exploration is improved with the use of multiple crossover techniques.

To ensure methodological transparency and reproducibility, all experiments were implemented using MATLAB R2023b (MathWorks Inc.) on a workstation equipped with an Intel i7 processor and 16 GB RAM. Ensemble crossover operations (SPC, DPC, UAC) were implemented through custom MATLAB functions integrated with the Genetic Algorithm module from the Global Optimization Toolbox. Neural network evaluation was performed using the Deep Learning Toolbox. Each dataset was normalized via min–max scaling before feature selection, and different random seeds were used across repeated runs to ensure robustness of the results. Identical preprocessing and classifier configurations were maintained across the Lung and Pancreatic Cancer datasets to ensure fair comparison.

Fitness or cost function (selection of classifier/evaluator)

The hybrid model of a Genetic Algorithm (GA) and Classifier or an Evaluator creates a wrapper model for feature subset selection. Since the evaluations came up with accurate results in the case of the neural network, we shall use a neural network as an evaluator to select the best possible feature set.

The selected feature subsets were evaluated using an Artificial Neural Network (ANN) classifier. The ANN consisted of one hidden layer with 10 neurons using the sigmoid activation function, and a softmax output layer for classification. The network was trained using the backpropagation algorithm with mean squared error (MSE) as the loss function. The learning rate was set to 0.01, and the model was trained for 50 epochs. Experimental workflow and evaluation protocol

To ensure methodological transparency and prevent data leakage during feature selection and model evaluation, a structured evaluation pipeline was implemented. The entire process was designed so that no information from validation or test sets influences the feature selection process.

The evaluation procedure is summarized below.

Step 1: Dataset partitioning

Each dataset was initially partitioned into training (70%), validation (15%), and test (15%) subsets using stratified sampling to preserve the class distribution across subsets.

Step 2: Data normalization

Feature normalization was performed using min–max scaling. The normalization parameters (minimum and maximum values) were computed only from the training data and then applied to the validation and test subsets. This prevents statistical information from the validation or test sets from influencing the training process.

Step 3: Feature selection using BCGA-ECO

Feature selection using the proposed BCGA-ECO algorithm was conducted exclusively on the training data. During this step, the genetic algorithm searches for optimal feature subsets while the ANN classifier evaluates the candidate subsets using the training data only. To ensure robustness, stratified 5-fold cross-validation was applied within the training data during the feature selection process. For each fold:

4 folds were used for training the ANN classifier

1 fold was used for internal validation

This internal validation guides the optimization of feature subsets without exposing the algorithm to the external validation or test sets.

Step 4: Model training

Once the optimal feature subset was identified, the ANN classifier was trained using the selected features from the training dataset.

Step 5: Hyperparameter selection

Model parameters were tuned using the validation subset, which was not used during feature selection. This ensures that hyperparameter tuning does not introduce bias into the feature selection process.

Step 6: Final model evaluation

The final trained model was evaluated on the independent test dataset, which remained completely unseen during both feature selection and model training.

Step 7: Repeated experiments

To account for stochastic variation in the genetic algorithm and neural network initialization, the entire experimental pipeline was repeated three times (r = 3) using different random seeds. The reported results correspond to the average performance across the repeated runs.

This evaluation design ensures that normalization, feature selection, model training, and hyperparameter tuning are performed strictly within the training data, thereby eliminating potential information leakage and providing an unbiased estimate of model performance.

BCGA-ECO over traditional feature selection methods

New methods have been developed in feature selection that are highly complex, each of which has unique merits and demerits. The Best Cost Genetic Algorithm with Ensemble Crossover Operation (BCGA-ECO) proves to have high efficiency, robustness, and adaptability as compared to numerous conventional methods of feature selection which are proven to be inefficient.

BCGA-ECO mitigates several tradeoffs of other techniques, such as computational complexity, parameter sensitivity, and overfitting. Aside from elimination, it also mitigates the expense associated with deep learning methods that are heavily reliant on computing power, expensive hardware, and even dependable databases. The method fosters an economical solution that does not sacrifice efficiency. Furthermore, unlike sparse learning and extensive auxiliary focused for high dimensional data, BCGA-ECO stands out with no extra complexity or parameterization. By incorporating changes within the BCGA framework, and using the best criteria from pre- defined crossover positions eliminates singularities within the model, resulting in increased accuracy, lowered error rates and improved feature selection processes. This makes it suitable for multi-purpose tasks such as medical diagnostics, and processing other data rich applications. In conclusion, BCGA-ECO is showing competitive performance to other ensemble techniques, removing the need for multi-filters and removing inherent issues of overfitting, and dependency on large volumes of data without control. The structure of BCGA feature selection within a genetic algorithm framework ensures robust and efficient feature selection tool making it effective in many application domains.

Time complexity analysis

The time complexity of the BCGA-ECO algorithm depends on the population size P, number of generations G, number of features F, and the number of crossover operations performed. Since the algorithm applies three crossover methods per parent pair and evaluate six offspring, the total number of evaluations per generation increases by a constant factor. The overall time complexity can be approximated as: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathbf{O}}\left( {{\mathbf{G}} \times {\mathbf{P}} \times {\mathbf{F}} \times {\mathbf{C}}} \right)$$\end{document}

where C = 6 is the number of offspring generated by the ensemble crossover per parent pair. The cost also includes the neural network evaluation for each offspring, which depends on network architecture and dataset size. Despite that, the constant overhead is compensated by the better convergence and selection efficiency. Additionally, the constant population and generation size i.e. P = 10 and G = 10 were chosen as they provided an acceptable balance between the computation and the quality of the solution.

Apart from the theoretical analysis of the algorithm’s complexity, the average runtime of the BCGA-ECO algorithm was also monitored on the tested datasets. The average runtime of the algorithm on the lung and pancreatic cancer datasets was within a few seconds for the chosen parameter settings. As expected, the runtime of the algorithm was directly proportional to the number of features and the population size. However, the computation cost of the algorithm was within acceptable limits.

The Feature Selector (Fs) maps an m-dimensional feature set to an n-dimensional feature set, resulting in a filtered dataset with reduced dimensions that enhance algorithm efficiency. Selection is based on calculated minimum objective function values (OFVs).

Experiments using various datasets from different repositories were conducted with a common classifier. Referring to Tables 3, 4 and 5, with the lung cancer dataset, BCGA-ECO reported the lowest error as 0.0437, RMSE (0.2090), and cost (0.0561) after 32 features were selected out of 56, which is the least value in the category. In the same way, this was noted with the pancreatic cancer dataset, where BCGA-ECO had 0.0182 as error, 0.1349 as RMSE, and a cost value of 0.0222 at 33 features out of 76. All feature selection procedures were performed exclusively on the training data to prevent information leakage from validation or test sets.

Further, inclusion of the additional clinical dataset (Case III) demonstrates the applicability of BCGA-ECO beyond benchmark data. Although the cohort remains modest in size (74 patients), it provides preliminary evidence that ‘Nucl_MCS’ may correlate with survival outcome, warranting further validation on larger multi-center datasets. The Nucl_MCS (mean composite score) feature selected by BCGA-ECO corresponds to nuclear intensity and structural variation, which have been shown to correlate with tumor progression and adverse clinical outcomes in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. These associations are also supported by the previous clinical research that showed higher levels of nuclear composite score of the respective biomarkers in the patient population. The accurate prediction of survival or death of the patient before the surgery depends on some of the factors that need to be filtered out [39]. The BCGA-ECO method employed has already filtered out some of the strong features among which the nucleus intensity in the cancerous tissue is the promising one and needs to be worked upon for the early diagnosis of the disease.

In addition to computational performance, the results show that the proposed BCGA-ECO framework finds features that are in line with important biological processes that drive tumor growth, especially those related to cancer stemness, cell cycle dysregulation and chromosomal alterations [44]. This shows that the method finds important biological patterns instead of just relying on statistical optimization. This makes the model easier to understand and less like a “black box”; however, they do not constitute clinical validation and should be interpreted as preliminary evidence requiring further large-scale studies.

In addition, the ensemble approach overcomes barriers presented by traditional methods of feature selection like high computational burden, overfitting, and straying focus from core objectives. By adaptively analyzing and choosing the most optimal points for crossover, BCGA-ECO increases the breakout performance of the model while ensuring as few computational resources are consumed as possible.

While ensemble crossover (BCGA-ECO) improves the diversity of the solution and provides improved performance, it introduces certain challenges like the risk of redundancy and higher computational cost that occurs due to the execution of multiple crossover operations per generation. To mitigate such issues, the BCGA-ECO method retains only the best two offspring or solutions (best cost i.e. minimum OFV) out of the six generated, thus helping reduce redundancy and ensuring only the most promising solutions propagate to the next generation, thus maintaining efficiency and relevance. Within the evaluated experimental setting, BCGA-ECO demonstrated competitive performance relative to baseline feature selection methods; however, the results should be interpreted as proof-of-concept findings due to the limited dataset size.

From a clinical perspective, the features identified by the proposed BCGA-ECO framework have the potential to serve as candidate biomarkers for cancer diagnosis and prognosis, supported by the consistent performance observed across multiple datasets. This suggests that the model is robust and capable of handling heterogeneous data environments. In a translational setting, the framework can be utilized as a pre-processing tool to identify a compact and informative feature subset, which can subsequently be validated through experimental techniques and integrated into clinical decision-support systems. However, a key limitation of this study is the relatively small sample size of the evaluated datasets. While stratified cross-validation and repeated evaluations were used to mitigate overfitting, the possibility of biased performance estimation should be considered. Therefore, the results should be considered as preliminary evidence of the effectiveness of the methodological approach. Further validation of the method on larger and multi-center clinical cohorts will be essential to confirm the robustness and clinical applicability of the proposed framework.

Conclusion

The study proposed a novel ensemble-based Best Cost Genetic Algorithm with Ensemble Crossover operation that acts like a wrapper method and performs crossover-based genetic algorithm for feature selection in high-dimensional cancer datasets. The approach integrates SPC, DPC, and UAC operators to enhance diversity and convergence stability. Experimental results using BCGA-ECO on lung and pancreatic cancer datasets demonstrate improved accuracy with effective cost optimization and reduced feature subsets and resulting in reduced error metrics compared to baseline and standard feature selection techniques. Furthermore, validation on a clinical pancreatic tumor dataset confirmed the adaptability of BCGA-ECO in real-world settings. The model successfully identified nucleus intensity as a key predictive feature influencing patient survival outcomes.

Despite these encouraging results, BCGA-ECO is not without limitations. Although the BCGA-ECO framework can be applied to multiclass classification problems, some evaluation experiments in this study involved binary classification settings. Additionally, the selection mechanism retains the best offspring based on the objective function value; however, future work could incorporate elitism to guarantee that top-performing individuals are preserved across generations. Beyond algorithmic aspects, this study is also constrained by the modest sample size of the clinical pancreatic dataset and the reliance on publicly available datasets for validation. Although stratified 5-fold cross-validation was used to minimize bias, external validation on larger and more diverse cohorts would further strengthen the generalizability of the findings. Moreover, potential biases introduced by preprocessing and feature scaling cannot be completely ruled out. Addressing these limitations provides several opportunities for future research.

Future validation of the BCGA-ECO framework will involve evaluation on larger multi-center clinical cohorts to confirm the generalizability of the selected features and classification performance. Collaborative datasets from multiple institutions could provide more diverse patient populations and reduce potential sampling bias. In addition, synthetic data augmentation techniques and publicly available large-scale genomic datasets may be used to further test the robustness and scalability of the proposed feature selection framework. Overall, the results suggest that BCGA-ECO provides a robust and effective feature selection framework for cancer diagnosis. By combining complementary crossover operators within a best-cost evolutionary strategy, the proposed method achieves improved accuracy and stability while maintaining interpretability. These findings highlight the practical relevance of BCGA-ECO for biomedical decision-support systems, with future work focusing on scalability to larger datasets and extension to multi-class clinical problems.

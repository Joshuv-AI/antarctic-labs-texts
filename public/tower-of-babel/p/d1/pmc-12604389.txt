Distribution of early- versus late-stage ONFH in core decompression cases at a tertiary center: does presenting symptomatology affect conversion to total hip replacement?
Authors: Yu-Hung Tian, Kevin Chi-Yun Kao, Kuan-Lin Chen, Pai-Han Wang, Cheng-Fong Chen, Wei-Ming Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCore decompression (CD) is a common joint-preserving procedure for early-stage osteonecrosis of the femoral head (ONFH). Although disease stage is a known predictor of outcomes, the role of preoperative symptoms and risk factors remains unclear, especially in asymptomatic ONFH patients incidentally detected via MRI.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 176 patients (222 hips) who underwent CD from 2010 to 2023 with a minimum follow-up of 24 months. Inclusion criteria included ONFH at ARCO stages I to III and preoperative MRI sufficient for staging. The primary outcome was hip joint survival, defined as avoidance of total hip arthroplasty (THA). Kaplan–Meier survival analysis and binary logistic regression were employed to assess associations between ARCO stage, symptoms, risk factors, and treatment outcomes.ResultsThe ARCO stage at the time of CD was significantly associated with hip survival and conversion to THA (p < 0.05). Early-stage hips demonstrated higher survival rates compared to late-stage hips. Neither preoperative symptom status nor common risk factors such as steroid use, alcohol consumption, or smoking were significantly related to CD success. Notably, asymptomatic patients exhibited comparable outcomes to symptomatic patients following CD.ConclusionsPost-CD outcomes are stage-dependent. Symptom presence does not significantly affect prognosis. These findings support early intervention even in asymptomatic ONFH patients with early-stage disease.Level of evidenceLevel III, retrospective cohort study.

Introduction

Osteonecrosis of the femoral head (ONFH) is a progressive disease [1]. Its progression rate is influenced by the disease stage, size and location of the necrotic area, as well as other risk factors [2–11]. Without timely diagnosis and appropriate intervention, it may eventually lead to femoral head collapse and secondary osteoarthritis, often necessitating total hip arthroplasty (THA). To delay disease progression and preserve the native joint, CD has become one of the most common joint-preserving surgical procedures for early-stage ONFH [12–14]. By drilling into the necrotic area, this technique reduces intraosseous pressure and improves blood circulation within the femoral head, potentially slowing disease progression and postponing or avoiding the need for THA [15]. Previous studies have shown that CD yields a higher joint preservation rate when performed in the early-stage ONFH. However, femoral head survival declines significantly once the disease progresses to Association Research Circulation Osseous (ARCO) stage III [16–18]. With the increasing use of magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), many cases of asymptomatic ONFH have been incidentally discovered, either during routine health examinations or during the evaluation of the contralateral hip in patients undergoing treatment for symptomatic ONFH [19]. Whether early intervention is necessary for asymptomatic ONFH remains a matter of debate [20, 21]. Moreover, the effectiveness of CD in asymptomatic patients has not been well established. Investigating the postoperative outcomes of asymptomatic versus symptomatic ONFH patients undergoing CD may provide important insights into the prognostic relevance of preoperative symptomatology, and may also help clarify whether asymptomatic ONFH warrants early surgical intervention such as CD.

To build on these considerations, this study was designed with two primary objectives: (1) to determine whether the presence or absence of symptoms at presentation influences the likelihood of conversion to THA, and (2) to evaluate the association between the ARCO stage at the time of CD and treatment outcomes.

Methods

Study design

This retrospective cohort study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of a tertiary medical center. The study protocol adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and followed the STROBE guidelines. This study reviewed patients who underwent unilateral or bilateral CD for ONFH at a single institution between June 1, 2010, and June 30, 2023. Eligible patients had preoperative MRI scans that were sufficient to determine the ONFH stage, and had a minimum clinical follow-up of 24 months at our outpatient department. The proportion of patients lost to follow-up was thoroughly recorded. Exclusion criteria included a history of prior hip surgery or septic arthritis. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients prior to their inclusion in the study.

Date collection and analysis

The ONFH stage prior to surgery was classified according to the ARCO staging system [16]. The surgical indications for CD were limited to patients with ONFH at preoperative ARCO stages I to III. Stages I and II were classified as early stage, while Stage III was classified as late stage.

Demographic data collected included age, sex, body mass index (BMI), history of trauma, steroid or alcohol use, smoking status, decompression sickness, and the presence of autoimmune diseases (including systemic lupus erythematosus, rheumatoid arthritis, Sjögren's syndrome, and IgG4-related nephropathy). The history of trauma was based on the patient’s report of pain in the ipsilateral hip due to a fall or other impact-related injury. However, patients with radiological evidence of previous fractures or those who had undergone surgical interventions were excluded. Symptomatology and pain status, including duration of pain prior to surgery, were also recorded. Radiological assessment of ONFH staging was performed by two orthopedic surgeons, who reviewed the imaging together and reached a consensus on staging without knowledge of patient outcomes. Because staging was determined in consensus, inter-observer reliability was not applicable. To further assess reproducibility, 20 randomly selected cases were re-evaluated by the same two surgeons two weeks later.

Outcome measurement

The primary outcome for evaluating surgical treatment was hip joint survival, defined by the preservation of the native hip without conversion to THA. Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) and Harris Hip Scores (HHS) were systematically recorded for all patients preoperatively. In patients who underwent CD without subsequent conversion to THA, VAS and HHS were additionally recorded at the second and fifth year of follow-up.

Survival rates at 1, 2, and 5 years following CD were analyzed. The 5-year survival analysis was conducted based on the patients with available long-term data, and appropriate censoring was applied in the Kaplan–Meier survival analysis to account for differences in follow-up duration. For patients who underwent THA, the indications for conversion, which were mainly radiographic progression or persistent pain lasting more than six months, and the timing of the procedure were recorded.

To further assess disease progression, radiographs obtained two years after CD were re-evaluated. The same two orthopedic surgeons who performed the initial ARCO staging conducted this reassessment using the same consensus-based method, without knowledge of the patients’ clinical outcomes.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS version 25.0. Descriptive statistics, including median, mean and standard deviation, were used to evaluate the data.

The chi-square test was applied to assess associations between categorical variables One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was applied to evaluate differences among more than two groups of continuous variables. Post hoc tests were conducted to determine specific group differences following a significant ANOVA result. In the post hoc analysis, Bonferroni correction was used to adjust for multiple comparisons and to effectively control the risk of Type I error. The paired-sample t-test was applied to assess whether the changes in patients’ VAS and HHS scores before and after surgery were statistically significant. Binary logistic regression was used to analyze binary outcome variables and to evaluate the effect of independent variables on the likelihood of the outcome. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis with log-rank test was performed to estimate and compare the survival rates of different groups over time. Statistical significance was defined as a p-value less than 0.05.

Results

Patient characteristics

Between January 2010 and June 2023, a total of 239 patients who underwent CD at our institution were identified through medical record review and preoperative imaging. Of these, 62 patients were excluded due to a follow-up period of less than two years, and one patient was excluded due to an intertrochanteric fracture on the operated side resulting from a fall during follow-up. Ultimately, 176 patients were included in the final analysis, of whom 46 (26.1%) underwent bilateral CD.

The demographic data of the 176 patients (222 hips) are summarized in Table 1. The ARCO stage distribution was as follows: stage I: 8 hips (3.6%), stage II: 100 hips (45.0%), stage IIIA: 85 hips (38.3%), and stage IIIB: 29 hips (13.1%). Outcome by ARCO stage

Table 2 presents the overall survival rates of the 222 hips stratified by ARCO stage. Overall, the 2-year survival rate for early-stage ONFH was 66.6%, while that for late-stage disease was approximately 53%. The difference became even more pronounced at 5 years, with early-stage cases maintaining a survival rate of 62.0%, whereas stage IIIA and IIIB cases declined to 41.9% and 34.7%, respectively. According to the analysis, the fate of hips undergoing CD was significantly associated with the ARCO stage at the time of surgery (p < 0.05; Table 2) At the two-year postoperative follow-up, 86 hips (38.7%) exhibited neither ARCO stage progression nor conversion to THA, which was defined as the two-year hip joint preservation rate. The overall conversion rate to THA after CD was 53.2% at the end of follow-up, with a mean follow-up duration of 80.0 months. The 1-, 2-, and 5-year hip survival rates stratified by early and late stages are presented in Table 2. Early-stage hips (Stage I & II) demonstrated significantly lower conversion rates compared to Stage IIIA and Stage IIIB hips (p < 0.05). Subsequent post hoc analysis showed that stage I&II differed significantly from stage IIIA also from stage IIIB after Bonferroni correction (p < 0.017), indicating that the effect was driven by differences between these specific group pairs. Symptomatic vs. Incidental findings

Among the 222 hips included in the study, 41 hips (18.4%) were asymptomatic prior to undergoing CD. Notably, even in the late-stage group, 18 hips (15.8%) presented without preoperative symptoms. Given the known prognostic impact of preoperative ARCO stage, we further investigated whether the presence of hip symptoms at baseline influenced the outcome of CD. Based on the preoperative ARCO stage (categorized as early or late) and symptom status, patients were classified into four groups. As shown in Table 6, though minor differences in survival rates were observed between symptomatic and asymptomatic patients within both early-stage and late-stage subgroups, none of these differences reached statistical significance based on log-rank tests (P = 0.996 for early stage; P = 0.393 for late stage). In early-stage patients, those without symptoms showed a trend toward higher hip survival during the first two years; however, the difference did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.370). Discussion

The principal finding of this study is that the presence or absence of preoperative symptoms did not significantly influence the outcomes of CD. Both symptomatic and asymptomatic patients, including those with late-stage ONFH, achieved comparable hip survival and functional improvement. While symptomatic patients may undergo earlier THA due to persistent pain, long-term survivorship ultimately converges regardless of symptom status. Notably, with the increasing use of MRI, as many as 18.4% of patients undergoing CD were incidentally diagnosed with asymptomatic ONFH, including 15.8% of those at late stage (Table 6). These findings suggest that CD should not be reserved solely for symptomatic cases, as asymptomatic patients demonstrated outcomes comparable to their symptomatic counterparts. Therefore, even incidentally detected asymptomatic ONFH warrants consideration for early intervention to preserve the native hip joint.

This study also reconfirms the prognostic significance of the ARCO stage in predicting the outcomes of CD (Table 3), particularly noting that Stage IIIB hips had a five-year survival rate of only 34.7%. Importantly, both the early-stage and late-stage joint preservation groups experienced significant improvements in VAS and HHS scores, indicating that patients who retained their native hip joint did not merely tolerate ongoing symptoms, but rather achieved favorable long-term outcomes following CD (Table 4). In addition, known preoperative risk factors for ONFH were not significantly associated with outcomes of CD (Table 5). However, given the high heterogeneity of these risk factors, such as differences in steroid and alcohol use and dosage, these findings should still be interpreted with caution.

Building upon these prior findings, the current study further emphasizes the prognostic value of ARCO staging while also exploring additional clinical factors, including symptomatology and risk profiles, that may influence the outcomes of CD. Previous studies have identified disease stage as a key determinant of CD outcomes in ONFH [17, 18, 22, 23]. Reported 5-year survivorship rates for early-stage ONFH generally range from 65 to 90%, which is slightly higher than the 62.0% observed in our cohort [24–26]. However, even within the same stage, several factors may influence the survivorship after CD. These include the size and location of the necrotic lesion, the specific technique used for decompression, patients' individual pain tolerance, and access to timely medical care. In 2019, Kuroda et al. [27] analyzed 505 hips and found that even among patients with pre-collapse ONFH, the size and location of the necrotic lesion as classified by the Japanese Investigation Committee (JIC) system were significantly associated with the 2-year and 5-year collapse rates. Based on these findings, the authors recommended early and proactive intervention, particularly for patients with Type C2 lesions. In addition, various techniques combining CD with adjunctive treatments, such as novel autologous bone grafting, allogenic bone grafts, or bone marrow concentration, have been proposed in recent years [8, 28–32]. These combination approaches are intended to augment the reparative capacity of CD by promoting osteogenesis, enhancing angiogenesis, and modulating the local microenvironment; however, robust evidence confirming their effectiveness is still lacking. Despite the emergence of innovative techniques and encouraging preliminary results, substantial uncertainty persists regarding the optimal treatment protocols, including patient selection, timing, dosage, and delivery methods. Ultimately, the success of CD remains highly stage-dependent, highlighting the need for early diagnosis and timely intervention in ONFH.

Although joint preservation techniques in precollapse ONFH have been extensively discussed in the literature, limited attention has been given to whether asymptomatic ONFH patients should undergo CD. In a retrospective study published in 2022, Wang et al. [33] reported that CD in asymptomatic ONFH patients classified as JIC Type C was associated with a slower disease progression compared to conservative management. In 2010, Mont et al. [34] also described the natural history of asymptomatic ONFH and found that up to 59% of untreated cases progressed to symptomatic disease or femoral head collapse. These findings suggest that, although the size and location of the lesion are important factors influencing disease progression, natural course of asymptomatic ONFH may not yield favorable outcomes. Early hip joint-preserving intervention may offer a better opportunity to delay disease progression. Moreover, joint preservation techniques do not increase the technical difficulty of subsequent THA and represent a cost-effective treatment option.

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective design inherently carries a risk of bias. Second, variations in follow-up duration among patients may introduce interpretative bias in the outcome analysis. Third, the size and location of the necrotic lesions, as well as the heterogeneity of inflammatory disease, were not further stratified or analyzed, which may have influenced the interpretation of prognostic outcomes. Furthermore, the 25.9% (62/239) loss to follow-up cannot be ignored. Although they were invited to return, some declined due to the absence of obvious symptoms, long travel distances, or were otherwise unreachable. Another limitation is that radiographic staging was performed in consensus by two orthopedic surgeons rather than independently. Although this approach ensured uniformity of classification, it precluded the calculation of inter-observer reliability.

Conclusions

This study highlights that the success rate of CD is strongly associated with the preoperative ARCO stage, rather than the presence of known risk factors. Moreover, the presence or absence of preoperative symptoms did not appear to significantly influence the post-CD disease progression of ONFH. These findings support the consideration of early intervention even in asymptomatic patients, particularly those in the early stages of the disease. Future prospective and multicenter studies may further confirm these observations and help establish more evidence-based guidelines for hip joint preservation in ONFH.

Resolving the Paradox: How mobile money drives economic growth through financial inclusion
Authors: Mostofa Mahmud Hasan, Md Abu Sayem, BM Sajjad Hossain
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The primary aim of this study is to identify the most relevant financial inclusion variables influencing gross domestic product (GDP) and to choose the optimal multiple linear regression model to quantify their contribution to economic growth. Finance is essential for every economic activity and financial inclusion is closely related to societal empowerment. It is observed in different nations that financial inclusion variables have a valuable impact on economic growth. For this, secondary data were gathered for this study from the IMF (International Monetary Fund) from 2011 to 2020. Graphical presentations were used to visualize the relationship between financial inclusion variables and economic growth. The most eligible variables and the best model among a collection of models based on empirical data were chosen using a stepwise backward elimination technique using multiple linear regression and model selection criteria. From the graph, it is seen that financial inclusion variables are proportionately related to GDP and the results demonstrate that the most eligible variables of financial inclusion that affect GDP are (1) the number of registered mobile money agent outlets, (2) the number of active mobile money accounts, (3) the number of mobile money transactions, and (4) outstanding balances on active mobile money accounts. These variables have a positive impact on economic growth, and the fitted model measures the more significant contribution to economic growth. As a result, it may be summarized that financial enclosure has to be enhanced for the development of rural and border areas of Bangladesh. This study will assist policymakers, development organizations, and scholars and make knowledgeable findings that will guide long-term improvements to minimize the gap between rich and poor, leading to the improvement of the community's wealth and welfare.

Methodology of the study

Data Source

Data were acquired from secondary sources from the International Monetary Fund (IMF). Due to the unattainability of the data before 2011 (the data of all variables regarding financial inclusion were not available for the earlier decade) and the goal of the Universal Financial Access (UFA) 2020 initiative is that by 2020, adults, who currently aren't part of the formal financial system, can have access to a transaction account to store money, send and receive payments as the basic building block to manage their financial lives. The World Bank Group – the World Bank and IFC – has committed to enabling 1 billion people to gain access to a transaction account through targeted interventions, considering all these, this study has employed the data for a decade (2011–2020).

Techniques for data analysis

SPSS 16.00 was applied to investigate the secondary data. To assess and realize the selection of the eligible variable and the best model for multiple linear regression, descriptive statistics, the stepwise backward elimination technique of multiple linear regression, the multicollinearity test, the autocorrelation test, and factor analysis were employed.

Justification of using the backward elimination method

Backward elimination method: One disadvantage of the backward elimination method is that once a variable is eliminated from the model it is not re-entered again. However, a dropped variable may become significant later in the final model.

Forward selection method: In forward selection, inclusion of a new variable may make an existing variable in the model non-significant; however, the existing variable cannot be deleted from the model.

Stepwise selection method: If there are too many candidate variables, then the method fails to provide the best model, as some irrelevant variables are entered into the model.The regression coefficients obtained by this method are also biased.Collinearity is often considered a serious issue in stepwise variable selection.

According to the above discussion we can say that backward elimination is a powerful feature selection method that enhances model performance by iteratively removing less informative features. It improves model interpretability, and computational efficiency, and mitigates the risk of overfitting. By carefully selecting the features that truly impact the target variable, backward elimination helps in building more accurate and efficient models.

Procedure of backward stepwise regression

In order to create a condensed model that best describes the data, backward stepwise regression begins with a full model and gradually removes variables from the regression model that have the highest p-values. When every variable that remains meets the requirements to stay in the model, the backward elimination procedure comes to a conclusion. Another name for this is regression via backward elimination. The benefit of the stepwise approach is that it lessens the number of explanatory variables, which helps to resolve the multicollinearity problem and is one way to fix an overfitting model [63].

Discussion

This study provides an overview of choosing the most significant financial inclusion variables for the best-fit model among the models representing contribution to economic growth. The NAMMAs and OBAMMAs are directly linked to GDP growth. This means that the impact of financial inclusion on GDP growth is positive [66]. The number of bank accounts and branches is positively linked to the human development index; however, the number of ATMs is negatively correlated [19]. The number of bank branches, ATMs, and deposit accounts has no bearing on the GDP per capita. According to one study, only credit accounts had a tangible link with GDP per capita [52]. At the 5 % significance level, the connection between NRMMAOs and the overall number of people utilizing financial services was significant [54]. In Burundi, mobile money distribution has no statistically significant influence on financial enclosure. However, mobile money transactions considerably impact financial insertion [58]. The availability of bank branches for private customers was negatively impacted by the GDP of rising nations [47]. Redistribution of funds should go hand in hand with financial enclosure [48]. Financial enclosure has to be enhanced for the development in Peru [49]. Financial enclosure has a very detrimental effect on business growth [50]. There is a strong positive correlation between the extent of financial enclosure and human growth [51].

Conclusion

This study used different financial inclusion-related variables collected from the IMF over ten years. The study's findings revealed that the NRMMAOs, NAMMAs, NMMTs, and OBAMMAs positively affect economic growth. According to the model selection criteria, the model based on these variables is the best-fit model, representing financial inclusion in general. So, it may be said that financial inclusion services boost the economy of the country. In Bangladesh, most of the population lives below the poverty line, because of many challenges: geographically diverse population, lack of proper infrastructure, lack of health care facilities, rapid urbanization, and lack of financial services. These findings will facilitate investors to forfeit special consideration and encourage researchers, governments, development agencies, and policymakers to make sound decisions in the context of their country for alleviating poverty and boosting prosperity. The stakeholders should be more concerned about using and monitoring the suggested variables to get optimum benefit from financial inclusion for the ultimate development of the economy. The study supports the previous literature that resembles the positive impact of financial inclusion on economic growth. The specific recommendations for this study are given bellow.i.The number of registered mobile money agent outlets is to be increased so that people get smooth services.ii.The number of active mobile money accounts has to be maximized to operate the financial services.iii.More use of mobile money transactions has to be encouraged to send and receive money through our phones.iv.Outstanding balances on active mobile money accounts have to be expanded through active mobile money accounts.

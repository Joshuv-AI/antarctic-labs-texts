Comparative effectiveness of lumbar interbody fusion techniques in adult scoliosis: a systematic review and meta-analysis of postoperative alignment and disability outcomes
Authors: Zachary Taylor, David Gomez, Blake Nygaard, Spencer Newell, Zak Sabetta, Jacob Ayers, Kyle Zappi, Neel H. Mehta, Ian Hong, Jibran Khan, John Shin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAdult scoliosis, marked by degenerative and pathological changes in the spinal column, is a growing health concern, particularly among the elderly, often leading to chronic pain and functional limitations. Initial management typically includes conservative approaches such as physical therapy; however, persistent symptoms frequently necessitate surgical intervention. Recently, lumbar interbody fusion (LIF) techniques, including lateral lumbar interbody fusion (LLIF) and oblique lumbar interbody fusion (OLIF) approaches, have gained attention as less invasive alternatives to traditional open spinal surgery. This study aims to provide a systematic review of the efficacy and safety of lateral interbody fusion of the lumbar spine in the available literature to inform clinical decision-making. We hypothesize that lateral interbody fusion provides a viable surgical option with favorable reductions in postoperative Cobb angle.MethodsA systematic review was conducted to examine the outcomes of LIF in adult scoliosis patients. PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and Scopus were queried in January 2024 to identify relevant articles published since 2010. Articles were screened for inclusion based on predefined criteria, and data extraction was performed to collect demographic, clinical, functional, and radiographic measurements. Statistical analysis was conducted using a random effects model to assess primary outcomes of interest, including changes in Oswestry Disability Index (ODI) scores and Cobb angle measurements before and after LIF surgical intervention. The robvis tool was employed to create standardized visualizations of risk of bias across included studies.ResultsThe systematic review identified 46 articles that met the inclusion criteria, of which eight studies, encompassing a total of 424 patients, were selected for final analysis. Five of these eight studies, involving 289 patients, specifically examined outcomes for those who underwent LIF. Meta-analysis of available data revealed a statistically significant reduction in postoperative Cobb angle following LIF (mean decrease: 9.50 degrees, 95% confidence interval: −11.47 to −7.52), indicating effective deformity correction. Heterogeneity was observed across studies, likely attributed to variations in surgical techniques and measurement methods.ConclusionsLLIF demonstrates efficacy in adult scoliosis management, with favorable outcomes in terms of deformity correction and functional improvement. The minimally invasive nature of LLIF contributes to reduced surgical morbidity and accelerated recovery, underscoring its significance as an alternative to traditional open surgery of the lumbar spine. Limitations include heterogeneity present across studies, which warrant continued research to evaluate these outcomes. Nevertheless, LLIF holds promise as an effective surgical approach.

Introduction

Adult scoliosis, a condition marked by pathologic degeneration of the spinal curvature, poses a substantial health burden, most notably among adults aged 40 to 90 years, with prevalence estimates reaching up to 37.6% (1). Patients with adult scoliosis frequently experience persistent back pain, radicular symptoms, and balance issues, which severely impact quality of life and functionality (1). Initial management of this condition often involves non-surgical strategies, such as physical therapy and load-reducing measures. However, when these conservative treatments fail to alleviate symptoms, surgical intervention becomes necessary, particularly for cases involving significant deformities or instability (2).

Traditional surgical approaches, such as open spinal decompression and fusion, have historically been utilized to correct spinal deformities in adult scoliosis. While effective, these techniques are associated with substantial risks, including high rates of morbidity, extended hospital stays, and lengthy recovery periods. These challenges have led to innovations in minimally invasive surgical techniques, with emerging research demonstrating comparable benefits to traditional open approaches while minimizing blood loss, hospital stay duration, and postoperative complications in patients (3,4). This shift towards less invasive methods reflects a broader trend in spinal surgery aimed at reducing tissue trauma, thereby allowing patients to resume functional activities more swiftly. Among the available minimally invasive options, lumbar interbody fusion (LIF) techniques have garnered significant attention for their role in treating degenerative scoliosis. Lateral lumbar interbody fusion (LLIF) in particular offers a lateral trans-psoas pathway to the spine, providing direct access to the disc space with minimal muscular disruption. These techniques have shown potential in reducing complications commonly associated with traditional open spinal deformity corrections, achieving reduced blood loss and faster postoperative recovery (4-6). Despite these advantages, large cohorts of LLIF patients have remained unexamined as current work has been limited to preliminary studies and single-center reports (7-10).

Given the paucity and variability of LLIF research, this work serves as a systematic review of the post-surgical outcomes of LLIF for patients with adult scoliosis. Furthermore, we aim to perform a meta-analysis of Cobb angle reduction as a surrogate to LLIF effectiveness. By synthesizing the available reports on these emerging techniques, our study seeks to provide a comprehensive catalogue of evidence to guide clinical decision-making, understand the risks and rewards of these surgical techniques, and ultimately improve patient outcomes in adult scoliosis treatment. We present this article in accordance with the PRISMA reporting checklist (available at https://jss.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jss-24-129/rc).

Methods

Design and search strategy

In accordance with the PRISMA guidelines, a comprehensive literature search was conducted in January 2024 across PubMed, EMBASE, and Scopus to identify articles reporting on the surgical outcomes of LIF in adult scoliosis patients. Although the original aim was to capture LLIF specifically, we included data from anterior LIF (ALIF), extreme lateral LIF (XLIF) and transforaminal LIF (TLIF) approaches if these approaches were described alongside or in combination with lateral techniques to provide a broader overview of minimally invasive fusion options. The search strategy incorporated both keywords and Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) terms, including “scoliosis”, “spine (or spinal) degeneration”, “minimally invasive”, “lumbar interbody fusion”, “lateral interbody fusion”, and “lateral lumbar interbody fusion”. The search was limited to studies published from 2013 onwards. Following retrieval, all articles were de-duplicated and screened for relevance using Rayyan systematic review software, a tool designed to facilitate the efficient and accurate screening of collected articles (11).

Article screening, inclusion criteria, and variables of interest

Following initial retrieval and de-duplication, two independent and blinded reviewers (J.A. and S.N.) screened titles and abstracts to identify studies for full-text review. Disagreements between reviewers were resolved by a third blinded author (Z.T.). Inclusion criteria were as follows: full-text, peer-reviewed articles published since 2013; studies involving adult patients (≥18 years) who underwent LIF [including LLIF, oblique lumbar interbody fusion (OLIF), ALIF, or TLIF] for scoliosis correction; inclusion of postoperative metrics or complication rates; and clarity on cohort descriptions for surgically managed populations. Studies were excluded if they were reviews, editorials, letters, conference abstracts, non-English publications, pediatric studies, or did not involve any form of LIF as the primary intervention for scoliosis. Additionally, studies lacking quantifiable pre- and postoperative outcomes related to surgical intervention were excluded to ensure that only relevant data were included in the analysis.

Variables of interest were then extracted from relevant articles. These included demographic variables such as patient age, sex, and body mass index (BMI) and study characteristics such as author, year of publication, and sample size. Clinical characteristics, including type of scoliosis, curve location, and associated comorbidities, were recorded alongside surgical variables (e.g., LIF subtype, surgical corridor, spinal levels operated, duration of operation). Post- or perioperative data included Cobb angle measurements and postsurgical complications. Although sagittal plane data were noted where available; these metrics were inconsistently reported across studies.

Risk of bias assessment

The robvis tool was employed to create standardized visualizations of risk of bias across included studies. Given that all studies are clinical retrospective cohort studies, the ROBINS-I (Risk of Bias in Non-randomized Studies - of Interventions) template was used. Statistical analysis

For primary outcomes of interest that included reliable data, a random-effects model employing the DerSimonian-Laird method was used to account for both within-study and between-study variability, acknowledging the heterogeneity in surgical approaches and patient populations across studies. Statistical analyses were conducted using JASP software (Version 0.16), a validated, open-source tool for meta-analytic computations. Heterogeneity was assessed using the I2 statistic, with values greater than 50% indicating significant heterogeneity. Throughout data collection and analysis, attention was given to standardizing measurements across studies. Studies with incomplete or inconsistent data relevant to the pooled analysis were excluded to ensure accuracy. Data were subsequently tabulated to display both individual study results and synthesized findings. Sensitivity analyses were not performed due to the limited variability among included studies and methodological similarities, suggesting inherently stable findings. We did not formally assess the certainty of the evidence due to the lack of established frameworks available for this review, which restricts our ability to evaluate the reliability of the synthesized results.

Results

Study characteristics

The systematic search initially identified a total of 194 articles from three databases: PubMed (n=53), EMBASE (n=61), and Scopus (n=80) (Figure 1) (20,21). After de-duplication, 11 articles were removed, and 94 unique studies remained for full abstract review by the two blinded reviewers. Following this process, 46 studies met final inclusion and underwent full-text review. Of these, eight studies met the criteria for reporting and are detailed in Table 2. Sample sizes across the final included articles ranged from 28–107 participants, resulting in a combined cohort of 424 patients. The mean age of participants ranged from 62 to 68.8 years; gender distribution across studies revealed a slight female predominance comprising 52–75% across studies (Table 2).

Surgical characteristics

As summarized in Table 3, various LIF techniques were used across the studies, with OLIF applied in 30 cases (5%), LLIF in 129 cases (24%), ALIF in 53 cases (10%), TLIF in 35 cases (6%), direct LIF (DLIF) in 102 cases (19%), axial LIF (AxLIF) in 51 cases (9%) XLIF in 107 cases (19%) and combinations of multiple LIF techniques, such as ALIF + TLIF, ALIF + LLIF or TLIF + LLIF, in 42 cases (8%). Across studies, the surgical procedures primarily targeted spinal levels from L1 to L4 (Table 3). Reported durations of surgery varied widely depending on the approach, with mean durations ranging from 96.8 minutes for OLIF to 486.8 minutes for studies employing multiple LIF techniques, reflecting substantial variability based on procedural complexity.

Complication rates also varied by surgical technique. For OLIF, complications included left thigh anterolateral pain (N=2, 6.6%), vascular injury (N=1, 3.3%), and cage subsidence (N=5, 16.7%). Studies using multiple LIF techniques reported the highest total complication count (N=58), including cardiopulmonary complications (N=3, 5.2%), gastrointestinal issues (N=2, 3.4%), implant-related complications (N=12, 20.7%), infections (N=2, 3.4%), neurological deficits (N=12, 20.7%) and wound complications (N=4, 6.9%). In single-technique studies, the XLIF approach was associated with a high incidence of lower extremity weakness (N=36, 33.6%), and TLIF with AxLIF resulted in thigh dysesthesia (N=17, 37.7%) and intraoperative renal hematoma (N=1, 2.2%). Additionally, studies utilizing LLIF alone reported cage subsidence (N=10, 28.6%) and radiculopathy (N=2, 5.7%) as frequent complications. These findings indicate notable variation in complication profiles across different LIF techniques (Table 3).

Meta-analysis of Cobb angle difference following LIF

Based on data availability, five studies reporting on the impact of LIF on Cobb angle reduction in patients with degenerative scoliosis were included for meta-analysis, comprising combined total sample of 424 patients. Each study contributed data on the change in Cobb angle, allowing for an evaluation of the overall effectiveness of LIF in reducing spinal curvature. The analysis demonstrated a statistically significant effect, with the omnibus test of model coefficients yielding Q=89.088, df=1, and P<0.001, suggesting that the combined data across studies support an overall reduction in Cobb angle (Table 4). Residual heterogeneity was assessed to examine the degree of variability not explained by the model, with a test result of Q=9.494, df=4, and P=0.05, suggesting moderate heterogeneity between studies (Table 4). A random-effects model was employed to account for potential differences across study populations and methodologies, producing an intercept estimate of −9.495 (SE =1.006, Z=−9.439, P<0.001), with a 95% confidence interval ranging from −11.467 to −7.524 (Table 5). This interval suggests that the reduction in Cobb angle following LIF likely lies within this range across studies. Heterogeneity estimates further quantified the variability among studies, with tau2=2.783 representing the variance of true effect sizes, and I2=57.869%, suggesting that approximately 58% of the observed variance reflects true differences between studies rather than sampling error (Table 6).

Discussion

To evaluate the clinical impact of LIF in adults with degenerative scoliosis, a systematic review and meta-analysis of postoperative Cobb angle were conducted. From 194 initial articles, 46 full texts were assessed, and 8 studies comprising a total of 424 surgical cases met inclusion criteria; 5 studies provided quantitative data for meta-analysis. Included studies employed various LIF subtypes including OLIF, LLIF, ALIF, TLIF, and combinations thereof. Meta-analysis indicated a statistically significant mean Cobb angle reduction of −9.5 degrees (95% CI: –11.47 to –7.52), supporting the effectiveness of LIF in correcting adult spinal deformity. Moderate heterogeneity (I2=57.87%) likely reflects variance in surgical approach, patient selection, and study methodology.

Adult scoliosis encompasses idiopathic, degenerative (de novo), and secondary forms, each posing unique management challenges (22). LIF offers a minimally invasive strategy to address spinal curvature with reduced soft-tissue disruption, often resulting in shorter operative times and accelerated recovery compared to traditional open procedures (23). Conventional open surgeries are effective but can be associated with significant postoperative complications, lengthy hospital stays, and elevated healthcare costs (24-26). By contrast, LIF may mitigate these issues, potentially lowering rates of severe complications and facilitating more rapid mobilization (27). Indeed, the included studies showed a range of complications—from mild sensory deficits to cage subsidence or pseudoarthrosis—reinforcing that patient selection and technical precision are essential for successful outcomes (15,28). Although complication rates varied, especially in combined approaches, the overall incidence of severe events—such as infection, hematoma, and radiculopathy—remained relatively low (16,29-32).

Despite variations in approaches, including oblique (OLIF), lateral (LLIF), anterior (ALIF), transforaminal (TLIF), or combined techniques—the overall meta-analysis demonstrated a statistically significant mean reduction in Cobb angle of −9.5 degrees (95% CI: –11.47 to –7.52). Moderate heterogeneity (I2=57.87%) suggests that differing surgical corridors, patient selection criteria, and variable follow-up periods may have contributed to the variability in radiographic outcomes. This finding is consistent with the spectrum of procedures listed in Table 3, where duration of surgery ranged from an average of 96.8 minutes (12) to 486.8 minutes (13), and complication profiles varied substantially. Zhang et al. (12) reported predominantly vascular and cage subsidence issues, whereas Mundis et al. (13) enumerated over 50 complications, including cardiopulmonary, neurologic, and radiographic events. Notably, procedures combining multiple techniques (e.g., ALIF + LLIF or ALIF + TLIF) were associated with higher complication counts, underscoring the trade-off between comprehensive deformity correction and surgical morbidity.

The presence of moderate heterogeneity indicates a need for standardized surgical protocols. A 2020 modified Delphi study by the Chinese Study Group for Lateral Lumbar Spine Surgery provides expert-based recommendations on LLIF, outlining consensus-driven guidelines for patient selection, operative technique, complications management, and follow-up (33). Adopting or adapting such frameworks could reduce practice variability and improve the comparability of future investigations. Most importantly, the approach is often driven by initial radiographic evaluation and further advancements in diagnostics alongside prospective trials by varied approaches may hold the potential for improved outcomes. Standardized reporting of outcomes is also crucial: while most included studies documented improvements in the Oswestry Disability Index (ODI), the degree of functional gain varied depending on baseline disability and follow-up duration. Longer and more uniform follow-up intervals would allow for better evaluation of LIF’s sustainability in maintaining deformity correction and enhancing quality of life.

Ultimately, larger multicenter trials employing consistent inclusion criteria and outcome measures are needed to optimize surgical strategies and guide clinical decision-making for degenerative scoliosis. By clarifying optimal patient selection (e.g., age range, baseline Cobb angle, comorbidities) and incorporating consensus-based protocols, future research can better delineate the full therapeutic potential and limitations of LIF. Evidence-driven refinement of LIF techniques will remain integral to maximizing deformity correction and minimizing complications. Nevertheless, limitations in our study—such as the small number of included studies, potential publication bias, and lack of standardized outcome reporting—highlight the need for future large-scale, multicenter studies with standardized methodologies to validate these findings and refine surgical strategies.

Limitations

Despite the significant findings, our study has several limitations that warrant consideration. First, the meta-analysis included a relatively small number of studies (n=5), which may limit the generalizability of the results. To mitigate this, we conducted a comprehensive literature search across multiple databases and adhered to the PRISMA guidelines to ensure a systematic and transparent review process. Second, the moderate heterogeneity observed (I2=57.87%) indicates variability among studies in terms of surgical techniques, patient demographics, and outcome measures. We addressed this by employing a random-effects model in our analysis to account for inter-study variability. Third, the lack of standardized outcome reporting and inconsistent follow-up durations across studies may have influenced our findings. To minimize this impact, we carefully extracted and analyzed data that were consistently reported and focused on the primary outcome of Cobb angle reduction. Finally, potential publication bias could not be entirely ruled out, as studies with negative results might be underrepresented in the literature. We attempted to reduce this bias by incorporating the risk of bias tools. Sensitivity analyses were not performed to evaluate the robustness of the synthesized results due to the methodological homogeneity of the included studies, and the absence of established frameworks limited our assessment of the certainty of evidence for the outcomes, impacting the evaluation of the reliability of our findings.

Conclusions

Our systematic review and meta-analysis indicates that LIF techniques yield a statistically significant reduction in Cobb angle among adults with degenerative scoliosis, with an average decrease of 9.5 degrees. Although this finding reinforces the potential of less invasive fusion methods to address spinal deformity while limiting perioperative morbidity, moderate heterogeneity (I2=57.87%) underscores the need for more uniform study designs, consistent follow-up intervals, and standardized outcome reporting. Emerging consensus protocols may help reduce variability in clinical practice and outcome reporting in order to enhance comparability of future investigations. Larger, multicenter trials focusing on clearly defined patient criteria, surgical strategies, and functional endpoints are essential to validate these preliminary observations and further refine the role of LIF in adult scoliosis management.

Digital Evaluation of the Positional Accuracy of Implant Scan Bodies in All-On-Four Patients Using Digital Approach—A Controlled Clinical Trial
Authors: Y. Vijaya Lakshmi, D. Sumanth Saketh, N. R. Dedeepya, P. Ramesh, Moulinath Chikati Deepak, T. Ankanna
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAim:To compare the positional accuracy of the scan body obtained in indirect and direct–indirect digital approaches with that of the direct digital approach.Materials and Methods:Twenty implants were placed, each maxilla of four, using All-On-Four concept in five patients. Three impression techniques have been applied to each patient, namely 1) direct digital approach, 2) indirect digital approach, and 3) direct–indirect digital approach. With the use of specialized 3D inspection and metrology software, the difference in scan body position between the groups was calculated.Results:The average total deviation values for the two groups under examination were 343.15 ± 3.4 μm and 402.79 ± 2.6 μm, respectively. For each of the study groups, the deviation measurement between the superimposed scan bodies revealed statistically significant (P = 0.006) differences. A statistically insignificant difference between the compared groups was found by the post hoc analysis (P = 0.006 and 0.014).Conclusion:The direct–indirect digital impression group displayed the least positional accuracy (402.79 ± 2.6 μm). The accuracy of all three digital impression-making approaches in implant dentistry appears to be clinically acceptable and adequate for all types of implant restorations, especially in All-on-Four treatment modality.

INTRODUCTION

Dental implants are a novel, evolving, and convenient method for replacing lost teeth,[1] and the precise three-dimensional transfer of implant location from the patient’s oral cavity to the final cast or prosthesis designing software determines most of their long-term efficacy.[2]

Intraoral scanning appears to offer many advantages over traditional elastomeric impression material, like shorter chairside times, higher patient and operator satisfaction, and the capacity to store and retrieve information digitally.[3]

Regarding digital workflow, the direct and indirect approaches are the two ways that the CAD/CAM system may be used to digitally produce dental restorations.[4] Now another technique that eliminates the need for intraoral scanner (IOS) was proposed, i.e., the direct–indirect technique, where the implant position is recorded using a scan body directly in the patient through conventional impression, and the master cast obtained later can be digitized as in the indirect technique using a desktop scanner. Also it is convenient in conditions with highly placed zygomatic implants positioned in extremely strategic locations and implants placed in regions inaccessible to both direct and indirect techniques.

There is a dearth of clinical data comparing various digital impressions; in particular, reliable clinical data about the precision of different digital impressions are crucial for All-on Four scenarios. Therefore, the primary objective of this in vivo study was to compare the positional accuracy of the scan body obtained through the use of indirect and direct–indirect digital approach with direct digital approach.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This clinical study was conducted at our clinical research facility and approved by our Institutional Ethical Committee (Reference no: CKS/PROSTHO/23-24/001). All individuals provided written informed permission before participating in the experiment.

From March 2023 to March 2024, five patients aged between 45 and 60 with fully edentulous conditions were recruited from the Department of Prosthodontics outpatient clinic at CKS Teja Institute of Dental Sciences and Research. Patients who lacked a complete set of teeth but had appropriate interarch space were eligible. Every patient has enough bone length and width in the maxillary arch to accommodate All-on-Four implants. There were no debilitating conditions present in any of the patients, such as diabetes, anomalies in the bone, or other illnesses that might compromise the osteointegration of dental implants.

Placement of implant

A prosthetics-guided implant placement process was put into place following the protocol according to Ajioka H et al.[5] Using the surgical guide, four dental implants (Internal hex, conical connection tapered dental implant, Taureg S, and ADIN Dental implant systems) were observed.

RESULTS

The mean and standard deviation of the RMS values demonstrated the positional accuracy of the scan body generated utilizing indirect and direct–indirect techniques with the direct IOS technique. For both conventional closed and open tray imprints, desktop scanning of the scan body was compared with IOS scanning. The groups’ respective mean total deviation values were 402.79 ± 2.6 μm and 343.15 ± 3.4 μm.

The deviation assessed between the superimposed scan bodies indicated statistically significant (P = 0.006) variations across the groups studied. The post hoc analysis found no statistically significant difference between the groups (P = 0.006 & 0.014). The indirect approach group showed better accuracy than the direct–indirect approach group with reference to the direct IOS group. Hence, the direct–indirect digital method group had the lowest positional accuracy and the highest variation (402.79 ± 2.6 μm).

DISCUSSION

The null hypothesis was disproved in this study since the three test groups showed substantial differences from one another. When compared to the direct IOS technique, the direct–indirect strategy came in second place for the position of the scan body, while the indirect digital approach produced the best results.

The digital IOS model is taken as a reference as its accuracy was evaluated and proved best in an in vivo study conducted by Bowen Ma et al.,[4] in case of a complete arch implant prosthesis. The accuracy of intraoral scanning of stock abutments was examined by Kim JE et al.[6]; this result also supports the above-mentioned criteria, stating that intraoral scanners can accurately capture the stock abutment’s position eliminating mistakes associated with different impression materials and procedures. Ajioka H et al.[5] found that an optical impression had a smaller error in capturing the angulation of implants than a conventional impression. Our study’s findings also support these conclusions.

The results depicted the scan body position accuracy sequence as:

DIRECT IOS APPROACH > INDIRECT APPROACH > DIRECT–INDIRECT APPROACH.

In the direct–indirect approach, alginate impression material was used for making direct closed-tray impressions. The accuracy of alginate impression was compared with Trios 3 IOS in a study conducted by Pitchapa Phudphong et al.,[7] who stated that both have comparable outcomes in accuracy and veracity. Thus, we may infer that alginate imprints can be utilized to get standard tessellation language (STL) files from the upper and lower All-on-Four implant scan bodies. This direct–indirect technique may be less challenging clinically, but it has been shown to have greater instability in transferring the implant position.

But in indirect digital approach, conventional direct impressions made with impression copings lie within the impression, reducing the implant angulation effect and removing the concern of replacing coping back into its respective position in impression.[8]

However, digital systems can assist in preventing errors that occurring in the conventional process chain, mistakes can still occur in digital workflow due to the potential factors that affect accuracy, such as the scan body used, the software or hardware version, the calibration status, and the scan path chosen.[9] However, the digital approach can avoid the successive laboratory steps of implant impressions like waxing, investing, and casting as the prosthesis can be fabricated through milling or laser sintering.

CONCLUSION

Compared to indirect desktop scanning and direct–indirect scanning, direct intraoral scanning using a scan body significantly improves scanning and overall accuracy for All-on-Four implant impressions.The direct–indirect digital impression approach showed the lowest level of positional accuracy.Despite the limited literature comparing different digital technologies, the accuracy of all three digital impression-making approaches in implant dentistry appears to be clinically acceptable and adequate for all types of implant restorations, especially in the All-on-Four treatment modality.

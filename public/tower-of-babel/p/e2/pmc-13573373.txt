Effectiveness of prophylactic administration of new-generation antiepileptic drugs for prevention of seizures following surgical clipping in patients with aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage
Authors: Tomomichi Kayahara, Kokyo Sakurada, Yoshiki Mochizuki, Takuma Maeda, Yushiro Take, Akio Teranishi, Hiroki Kurita
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe efficacy of prophylactic administration of antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) for patients with aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) remains controversial, and further research is required to ascertain the efficacy of new-generation AEDs in preventing the seizures that may occur after aneurysmal SAH. This study aimed to clarify the efficacy of prophylactic administration of new-generation AEDs in preventing the development of seizures following surgical clipping for aneurysmal SAH.MethodsA retrospective study was conducted on 89 patients diagnosed with SAH between 2021 and 2024 who underwent surgical clipping for ruptured intracranial aneurysms. In the present study, seizures were not defined based on findings of electroencephalogram, but defined as apparent convulsion. The administration of AEDs was started immediately after the surgery, and how to use the AEDs was determined by each physician, including the selection of the type of AEDs, dose escalation, and treatment duration. We investigated patient characteristics, including the development of seizures, prophylactic administration of AEDs, and clinical outcomes.ResultsOf the 89 patients, seizures were observed in 8 (9.0%) patients. Patients diagnosed with seizures were significantly older and had worse prognoses. Perampanel was administered prophylactically to a significantly larger number of patients without seizures. However, there were no significant differences in the administration of lacosamide or levetiracetam between patients with and without seizures, respectively. In contrast, no substantial differences were observed in patient characteristics, except for seizure occurrence and lacosamide utilization between those who were and were not administered perampanel.ConclusionsProphylactic administration of perampanel or adequate escalation of the dose could contribute to a decrease in the incidence of seizures developing after surgical clipping for aneurysmal SAH.

Background

Seizures are among the most common complications in patients with aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) [1, 4–7, 17]. The incidence of seizures after aneurysmal SAH varies in the literature, ranging from 0% to 31% [15], and it is well established that patients’ outcomes deteriorate following the occurrence of seizures [1, 4–6, 17]. Furthermore, surgical clipping has been revealed to be associated with an elevated risk of seizures, as well as younger age, ruptured middle cerebral artery aneurysms and coexistence of intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) in patients with SAH [13, 19].

According to recent studies, the majority of which investigated levetiracetam or phenytoin, the efficacy of prophylactic administration of antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) for patients with aneurysmal SAH remains controversial [9, 13, 15]. In contrast, new-generation AEDs, including perampanel and lacosamide, have recently gained significant popularity for the management of diverse conditions. However, further research is required to ascertain the efficacy of these drugs in preventing seizures that may occur after the onset of SAH. In this study, we evaluated the effectiveness of prophylactic administration of new-generation AEDs in patients who underwent surgical clipping for aneurysmal SAH.

Methods

Patient population

The present study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Saitama Medical University International Medical Center (IRB No. 2025-007). Between January 2021 and September 2024, 115 patients were diagnosed with SAH due to ruptured saccular aneurysms using three-dimensional computed tomography angiography or digital subtraction angiography, and underwent direct surgery at our hospital to prevent re-rupture. All patients were managed in intensive care unit after the surgery. The following patients were excluded from the study: three who underwent both surgical clipping and coil embolization and 8 who underwent trapping of the parent artery or high-flow bypass, as they received relatively greater surgical burden compared to those underwent clipping alone; 1 who presented with convulsions before admission; 3 who presented with symptoms that could not be determined to be caused by a seizure (2 patients showed consciousness disturbance and 1 showed involuntary movement during hospitalization); 3 admitted after the cerebral vasospasm phase; 1 with an unknown onset of SAH; and 7 who died within 14 days of admission, as perampanel requires a duration of 2 weeks to reach the maintenance dose when used alone. Following the exclusion of patients, a retrospective review was conducted on the remaining 89 patients who underwent surgical clipping for ruptured intracranial aneurysms, without uncertain seizure events, and who were observed for 14 days or longer (Fig. 1).

Data collection

A comprehensive evaluation of the patients’ medical records was conducted to ascertain the following variables: age, sex, World Federation of Neurological Surgeons (WFNS) grade, location of intracranial aneurysm, coexistence of ICH, development of seizure, prophylactic administration of AEDs, duration of hospitalization, and modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score [10, 23] at discharge. In the present study, seizures were defined as apparent convulsions, and no patients were diagnosed based on electroencephalogram findings. In the context of AEDs, the administration was started immediately after the clipping procedure, and “loading dose” administration was not conducted. In addition, steady-state plasma concentrations are achieved within 14, 3, and 2 days in perampanel, lacosamide, and levetiracetam, respectively [2, 13, 24]. Therefore, AEDs administered beyond these time periods were defined as prophylactically administered ones. The use of AEDs, including the selection of the most appropriate type, escalation of doses, and duration of treatment, was determined by each physician. The selection of the AED to be utilized was predominantly influenced by the institution’s policies and the availability of AEDs.

Statistical analysis

Unpaired Student’s t-, Mann–Whitney U, Pearson’s chi-square, and Fisher’s exact tests were used for univariate analysis. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 24.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

Patients’ characteristics

The characteristics of the cohort of 89 patients are shown in Table 1. The mean age of the patients was 66.1 ± 13.8 years, and a greater proportion of female (57.3%) patients were enrolled, compared with male patients. Thirty-four (38.2%) patients exhibited poor WFNS grades. The most prevalent aneurysms were located in the middle cerebral artery (40.4%), followed by the internal carotid artery-posterior communicating artery (28.1%), anterior communicating artery (19.1%), distal anterior cerebral artery (10.1%), and other locations (2.2%). The coexistence of ICH was observed in 30 (33.7%) patients. The occurrence of seizures was documented in 8 (9.0%) patients. The median duration of hospitalization was 42 days, and 41 (46.1%) patients had a poor mRS score at discharge. Regarding the use of AEDs (Fig. 2), 8 (9.0%) patients received no AED. Meanwhile, perampanel was administered prophylactically in isolation in 24 (27.0%) patients, lacosamide alone in 33 (37.1%), levetiracetam alone in 1 (1.1%), both perampanel and lacosamide in 19 (21.3%), both perampanel and levetiracetam in 2 (2.2%), and both lacosamide and levetiracetam in 2 (2.2%) patients.

Regarding seizures developed in 8 patients, focal seizure was observed in 6 patients, while unknown (whether focal or generalized) seizure in 2 patients. The duration of seizures was within several minutes, and none of them have progressed to status epilepticus. When seizures developed, additional drugs, such as diazepam, fosphenytoin, or levetiracetam, were administered.

Comparison between patients with and without seizures

Table 2 presents a comparison of patient characteristics and clinical findings between those with and without seizures. Patients with seizures were significantly older (p = 0.005) and more likely to have a worse WFNS grade (p = 0.050), compared with those without seizures. Furthermore, patients with seizures exhibited a significantly longer duration of hospitalization (p = 0.014) and an unfavorable mRS score at discharge (p = 0.022), compared with those without seizures. In contrast, no significant differences were observed in terms of sex (p = 1.000), aneurysm location (p = 0.242), or coexistence of ICH (p = 0.435). Regarding the prophylactic administration of AEDs, a significantly larger number of patients without seizures were prophylactically administered perampanel (whether monotherapy or in combination with other AEDs), compared with those with seizures (p = 0.030). In contrast, there was no significant difference in the administration of lacosamide (p = 1.000) or levetiracetam (p = 0.383) between the two groups.

Comparison between patients administered and not administered perampanel

Subsequently, patients were divided into two groups based on the prophylactic administration of perampanel (regardless of whether it was used alone or in combination with other AEDs). A comparative analysis of clinical features was conducted between the two groups (Table 3). There were no significant differences between the two groups in terms of age (p = 0.916), sex (p = 0.603), WFNS grade (p = 0.339), aneurysm location (p = 0.067), coexistence of ICH (p = 0.062), prophylactic administration of levetiracetam (p = 0.677), duration of hospitalization (p = 0.244), or mRS score at discharge (p = 0.756). Conversely, patients who did not receive perampanel exhibited a greater propensity for lacosamide prophylaxis (p < 0.001), compared with those who received the drug.

Detailed characteristics of patients who developed seizures after aneurysmal SAH

Table 4 presents a comprehensive overview of the clinical characteristics exhibited by eight patients who experienced seizures after aneurysmal SAH. The median duration from SAH to seizure onset was 14 days. In five patients (patients 1, 2, 3, 5, and 8), seizures occurred when the maintenance dose of perampanel or lacosamide was already available. Nevertheless, four patients (patients 1, 3, 5, and 8) received only the induction dose of the AEDs at the time of seizure onset.

Discussion

In the present study, we retrospectively reviewed patients treated mainly with perampanel or lacosamide to prevent the development of seizures following surgical clipping for aneurysmal SAH. Consequently, the prophylactic administration of perampanel was observed in a significantly larger number of patients without seizures than in those with seizures, which might suggest the effectiveness of perampanel in preventing seizures.

In the context of post-stroke seizure mechanisms, several theories have been proposed, including loss of blood brain barrier (BBB) integrity, increase in release of neurotransmitters, dysfunction of ion channel, and alterations in gene expression [22]. Disruption of BBB induces the activation of astrocyte, resulting in the reduction of potassium and glutamate uptake in synaptic cleft. Increase of those molecules in extracellular space leads to hyperexcitability of neurons through inappropriate activation of various receptors including α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazole propionate (AMPA). In addition, activated astrocytes and microglial cells release proinflammatory cytokines, including interleukin-1b and high mobility group box 1, leading to the reduction in seizure threshold. Furthermore, provoked seizure could exacerbate the BBB disruption and promote the inflammation in brain tissue. Such a negative cascade could be developed in patients with stroke [22].

Perampanel is a selective AMPA receptor antagonist [11]. AMPA receptors are expressed in various regions pertinent to epilepsy, including the cerebral cortex, amygdala, and thalamus [20]. Therefore, these receptors are considered to be associated with the generation of epileptic discharges [20]. In addition, upregulation of AMPA receptors has been observed in patients diagnosed with focal epilepsy [25]. In the context of SAH, activation of the AMPA receptor subunits GluA1 and GluA2 was observed in a mouse model of SAH, and this activation was inhibited by the administration of perampanel [12]. Furthermore, treatment with perampanel significantly suppressed the post-SAH development of epileptiform spikes in that model [12]. However, the efficacy of perampanel in the treatment of SAH in humans remains unclear. Suzuki et al. investigated the potential of AEDs, including perampanel, in preventing delayed neurovascular events after aneurysmal SAH [21]. Although they suggested that perampanel might reduce delayed cerebral ischemia in patients with SAH, the seizure incidence remained comparable irrespective of perampanel administration.

Lacosamide is a new-generation AED that reduces voltage-gated sodium channel availability by selectively enhancing slow inactivation [8]. However, the potential of lacosamide to suppress seizures after SAH remains unclear. In contrast, the administration of levetiracetam or phenytoin to patients with SAH has been the focus of numerous studies. However, the effectiveness of these AEDs in preventing seizures following aneurysmal SAH remains controversial; this is primarily due to an absence of high-quality evidence. Furthermore, there is a risk of adverse side effects, such as cognitive impairment, because of the administration of phenytoin [9, 14–16]. The efficacy of other AEDs, such as valproic acid, carbamazepine, or phenobarbital, has also not been revealed [14].

Perampanel should be administered in the induction dose during the first week (when used in combination with other AEDs) or 2 weeks (when used alone). This is due to adverse side effects, such as dizziness, somnolence, and aggression [18]. Additionally, lacosamide requires at least 1 week to reach the maintenance dose [3]. In the present study, four of eight patients who experienced seizures were administered only the induction dose of perampanel or lacosamide, whereas one patient was not administered any AEDs at the onset of seizure, despite the seizure developing during the phase when the maintenance dose was available. Therefore, appropriate dose escalation of AEDs may contribute to the suppression of seizure development following SAH, a problem that should be evaluated in future research. Furthermore, previous studies have indicated that the onset of late seizures in patients with SAH ranged from 10 days to 2 years [17] and that the average time to seizures that occurred after discharge from a hospital was 7.45 months [19]. However, in the present study, the patients were observed only during their period of hospitalization. Therefore, further studies with prolonged observation periods are required to accurately ascertain the incidence of seizures following SAH and determine the optimal duration of AED administration.

The present study has certain limitations. First, potential selection bias may have resulted from the retrospective single-center study design. Second, the implementation of multivariate analysis was precluded due to the limited sample size. Third, electroencephalography was not always performed when non-convulsive seizures were suspected, which may have failed to diagnose non-convulsive epileptic status. Fourth, in 23 patients who received administration of two types of AEDs, it is difficult to assess the efficacy of each individual drug. Therefore, results regarding the effects of individual AEDs should be interpreted cautiously. Fifth, several baseline variables which could be associated with the risk of seizure development, including major comorbidities, intraventricular hemorrhage, cerebral vasospasm, or hydrocephalus, were not investigated. This should be considered when verifying the results.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the present study documented the prophylactic administration of new-generation AEDs in patients who underwent surgical clipping for aneurysmal SAH. The administration of perampanel and appropriate escalation of AEDs may contribute to a decrease in the incidence of seizures following SAH. Further studies are required to establish the evidence regarding the efficacy of new-generation AEDs in patients with aneurysmal SAH.

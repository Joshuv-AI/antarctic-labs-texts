Comparative evaluation of Fascin-1 with clinicopathological parameters and recurrence in oral squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Fatema Saify, Minal Chaudhary, Nidhi Tiwari, Shilpa Jain, Sarbani Deb Sikhdar, Meenakshi Sood
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background:Squamous cell carcinoma is the most common epithelial malignancy of the oral cavity. The 5-year survival rate is approximately 50% and is even lower, that is, 30% in the patients with recurrence of this disease. Because recurrence has a major influence on 5-year survival, it becomes imperative to identify the molecular elements responsible for the recurrence of oral squamous cell carcinoma.Aim:To evaluate the expression of fascin-1 and its correlation with clinicopathological parameters and recurrence in the patients of oral squamous cell carcinoma.Materials and Methods:A cohort study was conducted in the Department of Oral Pathology, SPDC, Wardha. A 4 μm thick section from paraffin-embedded blocks of formalin-fixed biopsy tissues of 60 cases having primary oral squamous cell carcinoma was processed and stained with mouse monoclonal fascin-1 (Clone: SPM133) antibodies.Results:In the present study, all cases which had a final score above 2, denotes a strong positive immunostaining of fascin in OSCC. Tumour size was significantly correlated with fascin expression, whereas no significant association was found for other parameters, such as age, gender, node involvement, and grade of the tumour and recurrence.Conclusion:Fascin was found to be significantly correlated with tumour size. Fascin expression was found to be related with recurrence but no significant association exists. As recurrence is directly related to the poor prognosis and survival rate; so, it is essential to identify molecular markers indicative of recurrence. Fascin can be used to identify a subset of oral squamous cell carcinoma patients that are prone to recurrence.

INTRODUCTION

Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) is a disease with increasing incidence worldwide that leads to deformity and death. Recently, mortality rates of OSCC are increasing worldwide.[1] Much of this is attributed to the advanced stage of the disease at presentation, high rates of loco-regional recurrence from inadequate resection and distant metastases.[2] The locoregional recurrence in oral cancer is not predicted by the histopathological parameters solely as the normal morphological looking cells harbor the genomic instability, which acts as the potential tumor cells for recurrence in future. OSCC cases often recur at the same site or develop a second primary tumor owing to residual tumor cells during surgical excision.[3] Rearrangement of cytoskeletal microfilaments is the major morphological alteration by the actions of cross-linking proteins for invasive tumour cells, whereby highly motile tumor cells lose cell to cell contact and invade in the surrounding tissue.[4]

Fascin is an actin protein of 55 kDa, encrypted by a gene located on chromosome 7p22 in humans. It has three forms: Fascin 1, also named fascin expressed by stromal and nervous tissues. Fascin taken count less attention between cytoskeletal proteins; this is because it is not normally present in epithelial tissues, and its role in epithelial to mesenchymal transition leads to motility and invasion of cancer cells. Also, it plays an important role in the formation of invadopodia.[5] A complete invadopodia assembly is a very important process for cancer invasion activity. Recent studies have indicated that invadopodia is regulated by tumour environment signals including growth factors, hypoxia, pH, metabolism, and stromal cell interactions. It is localized in the invadopodia and is known to facilitate ECM invasion.[6]

Fascin is reported to be overexpressed in the carcinomas of colon, pancreatic, breasts, lungs, esophagus, stomach, skin, and ovary. In addition, upregulation is also reported in neoplasms such as Hodgkin’s lymphoma, multiple myeloma, immunocompromised conditions such as AIDS and also in salivary gland carcinomas.[7]

Thus, the aim of our study was to assess and compare the immunohistochemical expression of fascin with the clinicopathologic parameters of OSCC and correlate fascin expression with the recurrence.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

The present retrospective study was conducted in the Department of Oral Pathology and Microbiology, Sharad Pawar Dental College and Hospital, Sawangi, Wardha. An Ethics Committee clearance was obtained from our Institutional Review Board to conduct the present study.

This study involved the use of formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue of 60 OSCC cases retrieved from the departmental archives. OSCC specimens were further subdivided into well (WDSCC), moderate (MDSCC), and poorly differentiated (PDSCC) on the basis of Bryne’s histopathological grading. Normal lymph node sections from radial neck dissection were used as positive controls for fascin.

The universal immuno-enzyme polymer method was applied for immunohistochemical staining. All procedures were carried out following the manufacturer’s instructions. The tissue sections were floated on silane coated slides at 45 degrees water baths. Sections were air-dried overnight at 37 degrees and then heated at 50 degrees for 1 h. The sections were de-paraffinized thoroughly in xylene and hydrated through declining grades of ethanol to water before immunohistochemistry with three changes in xylene (10 min); then, a single change in 1:1 xylene/ethanol (10 mins) followed by two changes in 100%, 90%, 80% and 70% ethanol for 5 min in each. For the retrieval of antigen, the slides were heated in 0.01 M sodium citrate buffer (pH 6.0) for 12 min in a microwave oven and then kept for 20 min for bench cooling. Then, the sections were rinsed thrice in TBS with mild movements. The sections were incubated for 30 min with 3% H2O2 in methanol for blocking the endogenous peroxidase activity. This is to inactivate the endogenous peroxidase activity, which gives false-positive reaction when using similar enzyme as labeled. The tissue sections were treated with prediluted Fascin-1 antibody (mouse monoclonal antibody [Clone: SPM133], PathnSitu Biotechnologies for 60 min at room temperature in a humidifying chamber and then at 4°C overnight). The slides were then rinsed thrice with wash buffer for 10 min each time. Wash buffer was wiped off gently from around each section. After that, the sections were incubated with a secondary antibody (PolyExcel HRP/DAB detection system, PathnSitu [Cat# OSH001], India) for 30 min at room temperature in a humidifying chamber. The slides were washed with TBS. Slides were wiped as before. A freshly prepared chromogen/substrate solution (PathnSitu, India) was applied in enough quantity to cover the entire section and then incubated for 20 min at room temperature. DAB chromogen working solution was prepared by mixing 50 μL of concentrated DAB in 1000 μL of substrate buffer. DAB gave a brown precipitate at the site of tissue antigen. Harris’s hematoxylin was used for counterstaining for 30 s for visualizing the antigen–antibody reaction. Each batch included one tissue section without the primary antibody, which was then incubated with TBS. This acted as a negative control. Normal lymph node was used as positive control.

The immunohistochemically stained sections were examined under a light microscope at 10× and then at 40×. All IHC-stained slides were evaluated independently in a blinded manner by two histopathologists who were unaware of the clinical and histological data. The slides were examined both qualitatively and quantitatively. According to Lee et al., the cytoplasm of epithelial tumor cells should be stained [Figure 1 and 2]. RESULTS AND OBSERVATION

Data were coded and tabulated in Microsoft Excel and analyzed with SPSS V. 24 software. The P value ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant. In the present study, all cases that had a final score above 2 denotes a strong positive immunostaining of fascin in OSCC. The immunohistochemical (IHC) score of fascin-1 expression in tissues of 60 patients with OSCC was negative in 2 (3.9%) cases and positive in 49 (96.1%) cases below 60 years. However, in tissues of patients with OSCC above 60 years, the IHC scores were positive in all 9 (100%) cases and negative in none by both the observers [Table 1]. It was positive in 16 females (94.1%) and 42 males (97.7%). However, fascin-1 expression was negative in tissue of 1 male and 1 female patients with OSCC by both the observers [Table 2]. The immunohistochemical (IHC) score of Fascin-1 expression in tissues of 60 patients with OSCC, it was positive in 58 cases (96.7%) of N0, N1, and N2 and negative in two cases (3.3%) of N0 by both the observers. However, there was no statistically significant association [Table 3]. Fascin-1 expression in tissues of 60 patients with OSCC was positive in 21 cases of WDSCC out of 22, 34 cases of MDSCC out of 35, all 2 cases of PDSCC and one case of verrucous carcinoma by both the observers [Table 4]. Fascin-1 expression in tissues of 60 patients with OSCC, it was positive in all cases of tumor size T3 (25) and T4 (22) and negative in two cases of T2 (13) by both the observers. The association was found to be statistically significant (P = 0.024) [Table 5]. Fascin-1 expression in tissues of 60 patients with OSCC was highly positive in all cases of recurrence (22) and negative in two non-recurrent cases out of the 36 cases by both the observers. However, no statistically significant association exists [Table 6].

DISCUSSION

Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) occurring in the head and neck region is the eleventh most common type of cancer in the world. However, in India, there is a high incidence of oral cancer with tobacco being the most important causative factor. Besides the advances in surgery, chemotherapy, radiation, and other combinational therapies, the prognosis of oral cancer has not improved in years.[8] A prognostic biomarker is as important as a diagnostic biomarker because it determines the treatment outcome and survival rate of patients.

Fascin had taken countless attention between cytoskeletal proteins because it is not normally present in epithelial cells and its role in epithelial to mesenchymal transition leads to motility and invasion of cancer cells. Several studies involving fascin have been conducted in head and neck carcinomas to investigate its role in tumour progression and invasion. During carcinogenesis, invasive cancer cells develop actin-based cell protrusions, which aid in cell migration. The purpose of this work was to estimate the expression of the cytoskeleton-associated protein fascin 1 as a prognostic indicator of recurrence in OSCC and also to relate its expression with the clinicopathological parameters of OSCC by mean of the immunohistochemical technique.[5] The disparity of fascin immunoreactivity between studies could be attributed to the difference in sample size, the methods of evaluation of staining, or technical errors.

Fascin expression was not found to have a significant relationship with the age, gender and lymph node involvement of patients. These results were consistent with the results of other studies by Moghadam et al.,[9] Chen et al.[10] and Alam et al.[11] conducted on fascin expression and its relationship with gender and age. No significant association was found between fascin expression and lymph node involvement in our study, which is similar to the study by Rodrigues et al.[12] In contrast, Moghadam et al.,[9] Chen et al.,[10] Alam et al.,[11] Papaspyrou et al.[13] and Wang et al.[14] showed an increased expression of fascin with lymph node metastasis. A non-significant association was observed between grade of tumour and fascin expression in our study, which was in contrast with the results of the studies by Priyadarshini,[15] Wang et al.[14] and Alam et al.[11] In our study, Fascin-1 expression in tissues of 60 patients with OSCC, it was positive in all cases of tumour size T3 (25) and T4 (22) and negative in 2 cases of T2 (13), the association was found to be statistically significant (P = 0.024). These results were in accordance with the study by Papaspyrou et al.,[13] Alam et al.,[11] Chen et al.[10] and Rodrigues et al.[12] Priyadarshini,[15] Alam et al.[11] and Chen et al.[10] also demonstrated that fascin expression is higher in poor, moderate OSCC than WDSCC. In our study, fascin expression seems to be positive in 97% of moderate, 95% of well, and 100% of poor OSCC. Fascin expression was observed as a diffuse or focal staining in many well and moderately differentiated OSCCs in our study. The expression was negative in the center and more intense staining was observed toward the periphery of keratin islands. This could be due to the fact that the central cells have undergone terminal differentiation and degenerative changes.[9]

Fascin-1 expression in tissues of 60 patients with OSCC was highly positive in all cases of recurrence (22) and negative in two non-recurrent cases out of the 36 cases by both the observers. However, no statistically significant association exists. Similar results were found by Jafari et al.,[16] where a high expression of fascin was observed in recurrence but no significant relationship was detected. Alam et al.,[11] Lee et al.[4] and Rodrigues et al.[12] found significant relationship between fascin expression and recurrence.

In conclusion, in our study fascin was found to be significantly correlated with tumor size. Fascin expression was found to be related with recurrence but no significant association exists and no correlation was found between age, gender, lymph node status, and grade of OSCC with fascin expression.

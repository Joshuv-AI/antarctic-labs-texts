Case Report: Zoledronic acid-induced hyperprogression in oncology: a case study of RET-driven neuroendocrine carcinoma treated with Selpercatinib
Authors: Junqi Liu, FengJiao He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Selpercatinib is a highly selective RET inhibitor with potent activity in RET-driven malignancies. Zoledronic acid is a standard bone-modifying agent for bone metastases, but its potential to induce hyperprogressive disease (HPD) is unclear. We present a 59-year-old woman with neuroendocrine carcinoma of unknown primary carrying a RET p.V804M mutation and widespread metastases. She achieved partial remission for 6 months with Selpercatinib plus denosumab. After switching to zoledronic acid, she developed HPD within 1 month, with severe bone pain, sharply elevated NSE, and radiological progression. Her condition deteriorated rapidly, and she died 8 months post-diagnosis. A strong temporal correlation suggests a potential triggering role of zoledronic acid, but causality is unproven. Tumor aggressiveness and acquired resistance to Selpercatinib are plausible alternative explanations. Mechanisms involving FPPS inhibition, Ras dysregulation, and γδ T-cell activation remain speculative. This hypothesis-generating case indicates that switching bone-modifying agents during RET inhibitor therapy requires close monitoring. Further studies are needed to confirm the safety and mechanisms underlying HPD.

Introduction

A 59-year-old female patient was admitted to the hospital presenting with “repeated right upper abdominal pain accompanied by shortness of breath for over one month”. Positron Emission Tomography-Computed Tomography(PET-CT) imaging assessment revealed multiple foci of high FDG uptake in the liver (maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) 13.8), multiple nodules in both lungs (SUVmax 10.8), and multiple osteolytic lesions throughout the skeletal system (SUVmax 10.5). Pathological diagnosis of the liver mass, obtained under ultrasound guidance, was neuroendocrine carcinoma (immunohistochemistry: Chromogranin A (CgA)++, Synaptophysin (Syn)+, CD56+, Ki-67 Antigen(Ki - 67) index 80%), with an undetermined primary site. Gene testing detected a rearranged during transfection(RET) gene p.V804M missense mutation (variant frequency 18.4%), MDM2/MDM4 amplification showed negative results. Moreover, the tumor marker serum neuron-specific enolase(NSE) level was 173 ng/mL (reference value: 15.7 ng/mL).

The treatment process was divided into three distinct phases: 1. Initial Treatment Phase: The patient commenced targeted therapy with Selpercatinib at a dosage of 160 mg twice daily, combined with a subcutaneous injection of Denosumab at 120 mg. After 5 days of treatment, the pain index (numeric rating scale(NRS) score) decreased from 7 to 3, and the tumor marker serum NSE level dropped to 129 ng/mL (reference value: 15.7 ng/mL), indicating a certain degree of improvement. The performance status(PS) score improved to 3 points, and the patient was discharged. Regular off - hospital assessments showed a partial remission (PR). 2. Maintenance Treatment Phase: Six months into the treatment, imaging assessments (such as liver-enhanced Magnetic Resonance Imaging(MRI)) indicated that the patient maintained the PR status, with a PS score of 1 point. The tumor marker serum NSE level was 29.2 ng/mL (reference value: 15.7 ng/mL), approaching the normal range. However, due to financial constraints, Denosumab(1,060 RMB per course) was replaced with 4 mg of Zoledronic Acid(32.40 RMB per course) via intravenous infusion for anti -bone metastasis treatment prior to discharge. 3. Disease Progression Phase: Shortly after discharge, the patient began to experience pain in the upper abdomen and chest - back regions, which gradually worsened. One month after starting Zoledronic acid treatment, the pain became intolerable (NRS score of 8). Despite treatment with a combination of opioids and non-steroidal analgesics and repeated dosage adjustments, the pain remained poorly controlled, necessitating readmission. Upon readmission, a series of re-evaluations were conducted: Laboratory results showed that the serum NSE level of tumor markers increased significantly from the stable phase value of 29.2 ng/mL to 282 ng/mL (reference value: 15.7 ng/mL); the trend throughout the treatment process of the serum NSE level is depicted in Figure 1. Liver MRI demonstrated an increase in both the size and number of multiple nodules compared to the previous examination (Figure 2).Whole spine MRI indicated an increase in lesions in the spine and iliac bone. A repeat PET-CT compared to the scan prior to the first targeted therapy, revealed multiple areas of high FDG uptake in the liver, with an SUVmax of 15.8 (original SUVmax 13.8), multiple osteolytic destructions throughout the skeletal system with an SUVmax of 14.8 (original SUVmax 10.5), and newly observed increased radioactive uptake in areas such as the sphenoid bone, with an SUVmax of 9.8(Figure 3). Despite comprehensive multimodal analgesia and nutritional support, the patient’s condition continued to deteriorate. The PS score dropped to 4, and ultimately, the patient succumbed to respiratory failure, with an overall survival period of 8 months.

Discussion

Selpercatinib is a highly selective and efficacious RET kinase inhibitor. According to literature reports, a RET mutation abundance of >5% indicates a potential benefit from selective RET inhibitors (1). It has exhibited definite efficacy in non - randomized studies involving patients with RET - mutated lung, thyroid, and other solid tumors (2–4). In this particular case, although the patient had neuroendocrine carcinoma with extensive lesions and a PS score of 4, making her intolerant to chemotherapy, the detection of the RET p.V804M mutation with an abundance of 18.4% through second generation gene sequencing met the criteria for targeted therapy as per the NCCN guidelines (1). The LIBRETTO - 001 study demonstrated that the objective response rate (ORR) of Selpercatinib in RET-mutant solid tumors reached 44%, with a median progression - free survival (PFS) of 13.2 months (5). The efficacy during the first 6 - month PR period in this case was consistent with the reported literature findings. Regarding the safety of Selpercatinib targeted therapy, literature indicates that typical adverse events include liver function abnormalities (such as elevated alanine aminotransferase(ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase(AST)), along with occasional occurrences of hypertension and QT interval prolongation (6). In this patient, no typical drug - related adverse events (such as elevated transaminases or QT interval prolongation) were observed during the initial treatment stage.

Hyperprogressive Disease (HPD) is a phenomenon during anti - tumor treatment where the tumor growth rate accelerates remarkably. It is typically characterized by an increase in the tumor growth rate (TGR) exceeding 2-fold, or a tumor burden increase of more than 50%, with a relatively short progression time (treatment failure time less than 2 months). Additionally, HPD can be detected through imaging examinations (such as Computed Tomography scans) as a significant increase in lesion volume, often accompanied by rapid clinical deterioration. This is predominantly a special phenomenon associated with immunotherapy (7), but it can also occur during systemic tumor treatments such as chemotherapy (8). The first report of this phenomenon was in a patient with non-small cell lung cancer receiving nivolumab treatment in 2016 (9). Subsequently, the number of reports in the literature has increased exponentially, reflecting the growing attention of the medical community to hyperprogression. The patient rapidly experienced exacerbated bone pain, a sharp elevation in NSE from 29.2 ng/mL to 282 ng/mL, and markedly increased number, size, and metabolic activity of hepatic and skeletal metastases on imaging within 1 month after switching bone-protective therapy from denosumab to zoledronic acid. These findings met the clinical and radiologic diagnostic criteria for HPD. The temporal sequence, triggering event, and rapid pace of progression collectively indicate a strong association between the administration of zoledronic acid and the development of HPD, which represents the most noteworthy observation of this case.

Nevertheless, despite the significant temporal association between zoledronic acid administration and the occurrence of HPD, causality must be interpreted with caution, and alternative plausible explanations should be fully considered. First, neuroendocrine carcinoma is inherently highly aggressive; particularly in the setting of a Ki−67 index as high as 80% accompanied by extensive metastatic disease, rapid disease progression may occur as part of the natural history of the malignancy (10). Second, acquired resistance to targeted therapy represents an important contributing factor that cannot be overlooked. Following prolonged treatment with RET inhibitors, resistance may develop through multiple mechanisms, including secondary target mutations, bypass pathway activation, and tumor microenvironment remodeling, ultimately leading to uncontrolled disease progression (11–13).Furthermore, clonal evolution of the tumor and alterations in the systemic immune status may also be jointly involved in the disease progression. Therefore, the HPD observed in this case is more likely the result of multifactorial interactions, in which zoledronic acid may act as a critical triggering or accelerating factor rather than the sole cause.

Zoledronic acid is a third-generation bisphosphonate that mainly ameliorates skeletal-related events by suppressing osteoclast activity. However, its biological effects are not restricted to the bone microenvironment. Preclinical studies suggest that zoledronic acid inhibits farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase (FPPS), interferes with the prenylation of Ras proteins, and disturbs intracellular signal transduction. Meanwhile, zoledronic acid can activate γδ T cells and stimulate the release of proinflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, IFN-γ, and IL-6, which may promote proliferative signaling in specific tumor contexts (14, 15). In RET-driven tumors, the RAS pathway is constitutively activated. Metabolic and immune perturbations induced by zoledronic acid could therefore theoretically serve as triggering mechanisms for HPD. Importantly, however, all the above mechanisms are based on preclinical evidence and represent speculative hypotheses in the present case, without direct clinical validation. Furthermore, although both zoledronic acid and Selpercatinib are metabolized via CYP3A4, no pharmacokinetic evidence currently supports a clinically significant drug–drug interaction between these two agents (16).

Previous studies have indicated that MDM2/MDM4 amplification is an important risk factor for HPD (17, 18). In the present case, the patient tested negative for MDM2/MDM4 amplification, suggesting an absence of inherent susceptibility to primary HPD. This finding further supports that the rapid disease progression observed here was a secondary event triggered by external factors. However, limited by the single-case design, this study could not perform dynamic validation of the immune microenvironment, intracellular signaling pathways, or other related mechanisms. Further confirmation is warranted using basic experimental studies and large-sample clinical data.

The most important implication of this case is that HPD risk should be vigilantly monitored in patients receiving RET inhibitors when switching bone-modifying agents, particularly during transition from denosumab to zoledronic acid. Prompt disease assessment is warranted when warning signs emerge, including worsening bone pain and rapid elevation of tumor markers. This report represents a hypothesis-generating clinical observation and cannot be regarded as a strong recommendation for broad clinical practice. The corresponding monitoring and therapeutic suggestions are provided for clinical reference only and require validation in larger-scale real-world studies.

Conclusion

In summary, to the best of our knowledge, this case report is the first to suggest that zoledronic acid may induce or accelerate HPD in patients with RET-driven neuroendocrine carcinoma receiving Selpercatinib, supported by a strong temporal relationship and typical clinical clues. Concurrently, the inherent aggressiveness of the disease and acquired resistance to targeted therapy may also be involved in a synergistic manner. This case provides important evidence for understanding the potential risks associated with the combined use of bisphosphonates and targeted agents, and also offers a new direction for investigating the underlying mechanisms of HPD.

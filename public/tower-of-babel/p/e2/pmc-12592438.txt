Cistromic insight into the association of retinoid X receptor with MyoD and CTCF in proliferating myoblasts
Authors: Jean-Thomas Clément, Qiao Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
In eukaryotic cells, histone acetylation and chromatin states affect gene expression programs in broad spectrum of cellular function. Comprehending the genomic signature of retinoid X receptor (RXR) will provide crucial insight into the molecular signaling pathways underlying stem cell fate transition. Here, we employ an integrative ChIP-seq approach to investigate the interplay of RXR with MyoD and CTCF in proliferating myoblasts. Our findings suggest an active enhancer distribution of RXR along with MyoD, marked by H3K18 and H3K27 acetylation, potentially mediated by histone acetyltransferase p300. On the other hand, RXR together with CTCF were mainly distributed to active promoters, marked by H3K9 acetylation. CTCF in proximity to RXR was also associated to nuclear receptor regulator NCOA1 and N-COR, suggesting a potential role in chromatin organization. Taken together, our analyses indicate a crucial interplay of MyoD and CTCF with RXR in the regulation of stem cell function.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-22895-2.

Introduction

In eukaryotic cells, histone acetylation and chromatin states affect gene expression programs in a broad spectrum of cellular context, essential for tissue and organism homeostasis. It is thus imperative to comprehend how networks of transcription regulatory factors associate with residue-specific histone acetylation in distinct chromatin states of the genomic landscape.

The CCCTC binding factor (CTCF) is a widespread transcription regulatory factor found across the genome. Its implications are immense, ranging from repressing or promoting gene expression through interaction with a variety of transcription factors1. One important mechanism of CTCF action in these regulatory processes, is achieved by sequestering chromatin into specific topological domains1. As such, these domains cluster distal loci into vicinity and enable favorable regulatory interactions, therefore organizing the genome into specific functional regions2,3. Particularly, CTCF plays a role in creating chromatin loops with the cohesion complex1, which facilitates the bridging of promoters and enhancers and thus the interaction of transcription factors, allowing long-distance gene regulation4,5. Especially in skeletal myoblasts, CTCF is known to create chromatin loops with MyoD to regulate myogenic gene expression6.

MyoD is one of the basic helix-loop-helix myogenic regulatory factors (MRFs) in the determination of skeletal myoblast lineage7. MRFs bind to the canonical E-Box (CANNTG) DNA motif to exert their function in the context as well as the configurations of the E-box8,9. During myoblast differentiation, MRFs, such as MyoD, myogenin and MRF4, are sequentially expressed to control the progression of myoblast specification, differentiation and fusion events10. MyoD is a master regulator of the skeletal muscle gene expression program and plays distinct roles in both proliferation and differentiation11. One mechanism of MyoD function is to recruit histone acetyltransferase p300 which has a high enzymatic activity for H3K18 and H3K27 acetylation12. As such, MyoD activates gene specific loci for the initiation of myogenic expression. Interestingly, these H3K18 and H3K27 acetylation are also associated to the enhancer regions regulated by retinoid X receptor (RXR)12–15.

RXR is a member of the nuclear receptor superfamily of transcription factors which regulate gene expression in response to agonist activation16. It is considered as a master partner of the class II nuclear receptors, due to its ability to heterodimerize with other members of the class II family. As a result, RXR associates with different class II nuclear receptors throughout the genome and is directly involved in a myriad of signaling pathways for different cellular processes17,18. There are three isoforms of RXR, (RXRα, RXRβ and RXRγ), which are differentially expressed in different cell type and tissues17. We have previously established that RXR selective signaling plays an important role in the specification, differentiation and fusion of skeletal myoblasts in which the RXRα isoform acts critically as a transcription factor8,19,20. Furthermore, RXRα augments MyoD gene expression through a direct association with a distal enhancer at the onset of myoblast differentiation21, providing an insight into the role of RXRα in early myogenic differentiation. Nonetheless, the genome-wide association of RXRα in proliferating myoblasts remains to be determined.

Previously, we have also classified the myoblast genome into different chromatin states21, based on genome wide co-occurrence of different epigenetic marks using ChromHMM with a hidden Markov model-based method22. More specifically, the myoblast genome was categorized into promoters, enhancers, transcribed and repressed states. Active promoters are denoted by enrichment of H3K4me3, histone acetylation, transcription start sites (TSS) and RNA Pol II occupancy, whereas active enhancers are defined by the enrichment of H3K4me1 and histone acetylation. Additionally, poised promoters and poised enhancers were designated by lack of histone acetylation, while transcribed regions are rich in H3K36me3. Here, we profile the genomic signature of unliganded RXRα and its co-enrichment with the MyoD and CTCF transcription factors by using an integrative ChIP-seq analysis. We also examine their chromatin states associations and residue specific histone acetylation signatures to shed light on the potential role of RXRα in the cellular function of skeletal myoblasts.

Results

Selective chromatin state association of RXR, MyoD and CTCF in proliferating myoblasts

To probe for a potential interaction of RXRα with other transcriptional regulators in the cellular function of skeletal myoblasts, we first utilized publicly available RXRα, MyoD and CTCF ChIP-seq data (GSE279060, GSM915186 and GSM915188, respectively) to profile their respective genome-wide chromatin association in the C2C12 proliferating myoblasts. As shown in Fig. 1A, a profile of high-quality narrow RXR peaks (total of 9537 peaks) were found from our previously deposited ChIP-seq data, with quality control metrics and the target gene list presented in supplementary Fig. 1 and supplementary Table 1 respectively. The MyoD and CTCF peaks called by the ENCODE project in matching condition of the C2C12 myoblasts (13040 and 21117 peaks, respectively), showed a similar profile of narrow peaks exhibited by the RXR (Fig. 1A). Next, we mined for consensus binding motif in the RXR, MyoD and CTCF peaks using a de novo motif mining tool23. In agreement with the literatures, the NR motif, NR2C1, was identified as the most significant binding sites for the RXR peaks, while the canonical E-Box MyoG motif and the CTCF motif were found to be the top binding sites for the MyoD and CTCF peaks, respectively (Fig. 1B).

To profile the epigenetic signatures of RXR, MyoD and CTCF in proliferating myoblasts, we examined their global chromatin state associations (Fig. 1C,D and S2). As shown in Fig. 1C, both RXR and MyoD showed a preferred association with enhancers, since most of the peaks were localized more than 5 kb away from the TSS. On the other hand, a higher percentage of CTCF peaks were found within the 5 kb range of TSS (Fig. 1C). Next, we employed our established chromatin state model of proliferating myoblasts21 to examine the genomic distribution of these factors. Interestingly, about 65% of the RXR peaks were found at the regions of active enhancers, while 15% were at active promoters (Fig. 1D). For MyoD, about 51% of the peaks were associated with active enhancers and only 10% with active promoters (Fig. 1D). As for CTCF, the majority of the peaks were found at inactive, heterochromatin or Polycomb regions (Fig. 1D). Nonetheless, about 17% of the CTCF peaks were associated with active promoters and 8% with active enhancers (Fig. 1D). Taken together, our analyses suggest that both RXR and MyoD may play important roles in the function of active enhancers, whereas CTCF may play a distinct role in active promoters in addition to chromatin repression in proliferating myoblasts.

Overlap of RXR with MyoD and CTCF in chromatin state association

To determine the potential interaction of RXR with MyoD and CTCF in proliferating myoblast, we delineated the nature of RXR co-enrichment with MyoD and CTCF in view of chromatin states association. First, we identified the RXR peaks by nearby association to the MyoD and CTCF peaks in the genome and then focused on the high confidence overlap peaks of RXR with MyoD or CTCF for downstream analyses. The resulting overlap category was presented as an area dependent Venn diagram, in which 5796 peaks were identified as RXR only, since no MyoD or CTCF overlap signals were detected at these RXR loci (Fig. 2A). On the other hand, 3093 RXR loci were classified as RXR-MyoD overlap, because MyoD read signals were also abundantly enriched at these loci (Fig. 2A). In addition, 648 RXR loci showed RXR-CTCF overlap, as enrichment of CTCF read signals are found at these loci (Fig. 2A).

We next dissected the epigenetic signature of each category of the RXR loci using our established myoblasts chromatin state model (Fig. 2B and supplementary Fig. 2). The pattern of chromatin state association of RXR only loci was similar to the total population of RXR peaks, mostly associated with active enhancers (compared Fig. 2B to Fig. 1D). In contrast, the RXR loci that overlap with MyoD or CTCF exhibited a marked difference in the pattern of chromatin state association (compare Fig. 2B with 1D). Specifically, MyoD appeared to largely overlap with RXR at active regulatory regions of the genome, in that 70% and 18% of RXR-MyoD overlap loci were found at active enhancers or promoters respectively, as opposed to 51% and 10%, respectively for the total population of MyoD peaks (compare Fig. 2B with 1D). Similarly, CTCF predominantly overlaps with RXR at active promoters and enhancers, representing 39% and 35% of the RXR-CTCF overlap loci, compared to 17% and 8% for the total population of CTCF peaks (compare Fig. 2B with 1D). As a result, the association of MyoD peaks to poised enhancers decreased from 20% to 6%, while the association CTCF peaks to inactive, heterochromatin or Polycomb regions decreased from 54% to 11%, in loci that overlap with RXR (compare Fig. 2B with 1D).

The marked increase in the association of CTCF overlap loci to active promoters was also illustrated by a TSS distance analysis. Again, the distance of RXR only loci relative to the TSS was similar to that of the total population of RXR peaks (compare Fig. 2C to 1C). On the other hand, about 40% of RXR-CTCF overlap peaks were found within the 5 kb range to the TSS, indicating a preferred association to the promoter region, particularly upstream of the TSS (compare Fig. 2C to 1C). Since CTCF is known to play a role in long range chromatin organization24, our analysis may indicate a possible interplay of CTCF with RXR in anchoring active promoters or enhancers.

Overlap of RXR with MyoD and CTCF in network operation

To determine the large-scale molecular pathways involved in the interaction of RXR with MyoD and CTCF in gene regulation, we performed a gene ontology (GO) analysis using Genomic Regions Enrichment of Annotation Tool (GREAT), as well as an upstream regulator analysis using Ingenuity Pathway Analysis (IPA). Consistent with the literatures, the most significant GO terms for RXR only peaks are mainly associated with nuclear receptor function, such as response to growth factor and cell adhesion, in addition to muscle cell development (Fig. 3A). The IPA analysis identified RXRα as a major upstream regulator associating with many other nuclear receptors (Fig. 3B). Interestingly, EP300, also known as p300 and a well-established enhancer mark14, was also identified as one of the top upstream regulators (Fig. 3B).

For the RXR loci overlap with MyoD, the top GO terms obtained were mainly associated to functions related to stem cell maintenance, muscle cell differentiation and skeletal muscle tissue development (Fig. 3A). More importantly, the IPA analysis identified Myod1, also known as MyoD, as the most significant upstream regulator which interestingly exerts indirect effects through several kinases, supporting an interaction of RXR and MyoD in the regulatory networks in proliferating myoblasts (Fig. 3C). Intriguingly, the top GO terms for the RXRα loci overlap with CTCF were associated with a range of regulatory processes, included muscle tissue development, regulation of proteolysis and stress response (Fig. 3A). Nonetheless, the IPA analysis identified NCOA1, a nuclear receptor co-activator, and N-COR, a nuclear co-repressor, as the most prominent upstream regulators (Fig. 3D), further supporting a potential interaction of CTCF with RXR in proliferating skeletal myoblasts.

Overlap of RXR with MyoD and CTCF in genomic DNA binding

To understand the nature of RXR binding at loci that overlap with MyoD or CTCF, we examined the RXR signal enrichment and binding affinity at the different category of loci. As shown in Fig. 4A, the enrichment signals for RXR only, RXR-MyoD or RXR-CTCF overlap loci exhibited a similar profile of high-quality narrow peaks. Nonetheless, the RXR peaks at RXR-MyoD overlap loci showed a higher level of read signals compared to that at the RXR only loci, while no discernable difference was observed at the RXR-CTCF overlap loci (Fig. 4B). Interestingly, while the tag density of RXR peaks at the RXR-CTCF overlap loci was similar to that of the RXR only loci, the RXR peaks at the RXR-MyoD overlap loci had a significantly lower tag density, an indication of a lower binding affinity (Fig. 4C). Together, these data suggest that the interaction of RXR with MyoD or CTCF in the genome may be significantly different in proliferating myoblasts.

Next, we performed a primary motif analysis on the different categories of RXR peaks to examine the molecular basis underling the differential binding affinity. Interestingly, the top binding motifs found in the RXR only and the RXR-CTCF overlap loci were the same NR2F2 motif with high z-scores of 0.965 and 0.922 respectively (Fig. 4D). The top binding motif mined for the RXR-MyoD overlap loci, on the other hand, was the NR2F6 motif with a much lower z score of 0.773 (Fig. 4D), representing a lower consensus of the binding motif and mirroring the observed low tag density (Fig. 4C).

To determine the neighboring DNA binding motifs to the primary motifs obtained for the different categories of RXR peaks, we also conducted a secondary motif analysis using the SPAMO tool25. In agreement with known mechanism of RXR function26, a NR1 motif with a gap of 1 bp was found to be the most significant secondary motif found for the NR2F2 motif at the RXR only loci (Fig. 4D), supporting a functional mode of RXR dimerization with a nuclear receptor partner. As for the RXR-CTCF overlap loci, the most significant secondary motif was the CTCF motif with a gap of 50 bp, supporting an overlap of CTCF and RXR at the loci (Fig. 4D). Together with the tag density data, the distance of between RXR and CTCF binding motifs suggests that while CTCF and RXR may overlap at these genomic loci, they may not be directly interacting with each other due to the distance of physical space.

Interestingly, the most significant secondary motif found at the RXR-MyoD overlap loci was the MyoG motif with a gap of 8 bp (Fig. 4D). More importantly, the read signals are relatively high for the MyoD overlap peaks (Fig. 4B), while the tag density is relatively low, supported by a lower Z score of the primary RXR binding motif (Fig. 4C,D). Thus, the proximity of the MyoD binding motif to the NR motif may allow certain RXR and MyoD interaction resulting in a degree of stabilization. Taken together, the signal enrichment, tag density and motif analyses suggest that the interaction of RXR with MyoD may be multifaceted and loci dependent.

Overlap of RXR with MyoD and CTCF in chromatin organization

To further explore the nature of genomic regions with RXR-MyoD and RXR-CTCF overlap, we used publicly available loop anchor coordinates based on Hi-C data, to explore the potential of RXR involvement in chromatin organization. Aligning with known function of CTCF in chromatin looping1, the RXR only peaks had the lowest overlap with the loop anchors at 27%, followed by the RXR-MyoD overlap at 55% and the RXR-CTCF overlap at 86% (Fig. 4E). As shown in the IGV view, the promoter region of the nuclear receptor Thra was defined as active promoter and exhibits both RXR and CTCF read signals (Fig. 4F). Interestingly, this RXR-CTCF overlap loci aligned with a chromatin loop between the promoter and an active enhancer region (Fig. 4F). As for RXR-MyoD overlap (Fig. 4G), an IGV view of the Myod1 locus revealed the looping anchors mainly aligned to active enhancer regions, as determined by our chromatin state model and the previously identified putative active enhancers of Myodl27. In addition, qChIP analysis of the Myod1 core enhancer region demonstrated the occupancy of RXRα and MyoD at the locus (Fig. 4H). Together, the genomic overview of RXR-MyoD and RXR-CTCF overlap suggests the potential involvement of RXR in the function of MyoD and CTCF in chromatin organization, including the bridging of promoters and enhancers.

Overlap of RXR with MyoD and CTCF in histone acetylation

Histone acetylation affects many aspects of chromatin organization and gene expression28, and residue specific histone acetylation is well documented for RXR signaling in cellular function and stem cell differentiation8,21,29. To profile the signature of histone acetylation associated with RXR, MyoD and CTCF, we explored our previously published ChIP-seq data of H4K8, H3K9, H3K18 and H3K27 acetylation (GSE94558)21. As shown in Fig. 5A, the read enrichment signals of H4K8ac, H3K9ac, H3K18ac and H3K27ac were generally the highest on loci associated with the total population of RXR peaks, compared to the loci associated with that of MyoD and CTCF peaks. Particularly, the CTCF loci exhibited the lowest enrichment of all the histone acetylation signals examined, correlating directly with their chromatin state distribution in proliferating myoblasts, since most of them are identified as inactive and heterochromatin or Polycomb regions (Fig. 1D).

Interestingly, the signature of histone acetylation at the RXR overlap loci was significantly different. As shown in Fig. 5A, the RXR only loci exhibited a similar profile of histone acetylation enrichment compared to the total population of RXR peaks, albeit in a lower degree. However, there was a distinct further augmentation in histone acetylation enrichments at the MyoD or CTCF overlap loci. Specifically, the RXR-MyoD overlap loci exhibited significant increase in all histone acetylation read signals examined, H4K8ac, H3K9ac, H3K18ac and H3K27ac, compared to the RXR only loci (Fig. 5A,B). As for the RXR-CTCF overlap loci, the most pronounced further augmentation was on the H3K9ac signal, while H3K18ac remained similar, compared to the RXR only loci (Fig. 5A,B).

The distinct histone acetylation signatures were also exhibited by the IGV view of representative gene locus in each category (Fig. 5C). At the active enhancer of Sv2b, where only the RXR read signal was detected, H4K8ac, H3K18ac and H3K27ac read signals were enriched. At the active enhancer of Wdr70, where both RXR and MyoD peaks were present, H4K8ac, H3K18ac and H3K27ac signals were markedly enriched. As for the H3K9ac read signal, it was enriched, but in a considerably lower degree than the promoter region of Paxbp1 locus, where both RXR and CTCF peaks were found. Interestingly, this specific Paxbp1 region was classified as an active promoter, and the most prominent read signals were H3K9ac and H4K8ac. The distinct H3K9ac enrichment reflects directly the category of chromatin state, since H3K9ac is a well-established promoter mark30. Taken together, the differential acetylation of histone residues further supports the more enhancer association of RXR with MyoD and promoter association of RXR with CTCF.

Discussion

Since RXR is a dimerization partner of many nuclear receptors involved in fundamental cellular processes17, the genomic signature associated with RXR will provide molecular insights into mechanisms of RXR function. Nonetheless, the genomic association of RXR in myoblast proliferation is not well defined. Here, we utilized a cohort of ChIP-seq data sets to decipher the genomic signature of RXR as a ligand free transcription factor in proliferating myoblasts, particularly in view of interaction with MyoD or CTCF. Interestingly, we found a distinct association of RXR with MyoD at the active enhancers, in conjunction with high level of H3K18 and H3K27 acetylation. On the other hand, RXR and CTCF were often overlapped at the active promoters, correlating with prominent H3K9 acetylation signals, shedding new light on a potential interplay of RXR with CTCF in cellular function.

We also examined the nature of RXR binding sites overlapping with MyoD or CTCF to understand the potential involvement of RXR in MyoD or CTCF function. Tag density and de novo motif analyses can serve as more than just quality control measures in ChIP-seq experimentation, since transcription factor activity can be modulated by various factors affecting the binding affinity, such as flanking sequences or DNA accessibility31,32. While tag density provides a direct indication of how frequently the DNA region is tagged during the ChIP, motif analysis provides information on how the sequence per se may affect factor binding and interaction at the genomic loci. Interestingly, RXR binding affinity decreased significantly in the RXR-MyoD overlap loci as compared to the RXR only loci, whereas it remained similar in the RXR-CTCF overlap loci (Fig. 4). In addition, a primary motif analysis revealed that the RXR binding motif in the RXR-MyoD overlapping loci indeed had a lower Z score compared to that in the RXR only loci (Fig. 4). Nonetheless, the topology of 8 bp gap between the RXR and MyoD binding motifs, as determined by the secondary motif analysis, may be favorable for RXR binding since RXR read signals were significantly higher at the RXR-MyoD overlapping loci compared to those at the RXR only loci (Fig. 4).

Given RXR’s ability to dimerize with many nuclear receptors26, not surprisingly, the most significant GO terms for the RXR only loci were mainly associated with nuclear receptor functioning33, such as response to growth factor and cell adhesion (Fig. 3). In addition, p300, a well-established enhancer mark14, was identified as an RXR associating factor through the upstream regulator analysis (Fig. 3), which is in line with RXR as it associates predominantly with the active enhancers (Fig. 2). Interestingly, muscle cell development was also identified as a significantly enriched GO term (Fig. 3). Taken together, p300 appears to play a central role in the complex association between different transcription factors, since RXR signals through MyoD in myoblast differentiation21, and p300 is known to be associated with MyoD12.

For the RXR-MyoD overlap loci, the GO terms identified were mainly associated with skeletal myoblast lineage, such as muscle cell differentiation, and the IPA analysis identified Myod1, also known as MyoD, as the most significant upstream regulator, but mainly through indirect effects (Fig. 3). Since MyoD does exert indirect myogenic regulation on muscle related genes and chromatin modeling enzymes34,35, RXR may act in concert with MyoD through indirect pathways. The GO terms for RXR-CTCF overlap loci included a spectrum of cellular function, such as muscle tissue development, regulation of proteolysis and stress response (Fig. 3). Additionally, the upstream regulator analysis identified NCOA1, a NR co-activator implicated in transcription factor bridging at promoters36, as well as N-COR, a NR repressor for gene expression37,38. Interestingly, both NCOA1 and N-COR achieve their function by modifying the chromatin landscape to modulate gene expression39,40. More specifically, NCOA1, or SRC-1, is known to function through chromatin remodeling by interacting with p30041, which was identified through our network analysis (Fig. 3). The opposing effects of NCOA1 and N-COR may thus suggest a potential role of RXR in CTCF mediated chromatin organization.

Our chromatin state analysis shed further light into the interplay of RXR with MyoD and CTCF in regard to myogenic regulation. RXR has a marked association with CTCF at the active promoters as opposed to a preferred association with MyoD at the active enhancers (Fig. 2). The overlap of RXR with CTCF at the active promoters in the context of CTCF function in enhancer-promoter bridging1,6, may provide an indication on the mechanisms of chromatin organization. RXR associates with p300 and mostly at the enhancers, which may offer a potential of myogenic regulation partly through long range chromatin remodeling42,43 or chromatin looping to bridge distal target loci. Interestingly, a large percentage of MyoD-CTCF overlap loci were associated with active promoters (Supplementary Fig. 3). Previous studies using HiChIP also explored the nature of MyoD loops, which are often anchored by CTCF binding motifs6, in enhancer and promoter landscapes of myoblasts27. Furthermore, RXR-CTCF functional overlap could be mediated through upstream regulators NCOA1 and N-COR, which have opposing roles in nuclear receptor signaling36,37, adding an additional layer of nuclear receptor regulation.

Genome wide residue specific histone acetylation also illustrated epigenetic signatures of RXR interaction with MyoD and CTCF. For the RXR-MyoD overlap loci, H3K18 and H3K27 acetylation exhibited the strongest signal enrichment (Fig. 5). This observation is in line with the enhancer distribution of these loci (Fig. 2), as both H3K18ac and H3K27ac are enhancer marks14,15, known acetylation targets of p30012 and associated with MyoD function12. More specifically, MyoD interacts with distal enhancers, seen by increased H3K27ac, which drives new chromatin loops to bridge gene enhancers during myoblast differentiation27. On the other hand, H3K9 acetylation, a promoter mark30, was most enriched at the RXR-CTCF loci. Although H3K27 acetylation was also strong but in a lower degree of signal intensity as compared to the RXR-MyoD overlap loci. Interestingly, H3K27ac has been found to be associated with CTCF occupancy, suggesting an important role in chromatin looping mechanisms44. In contrast, the MyoD-CTCF loci had relatively lower enrichment levels in each of the histone acetylation marks examined, compared to the other category of loci (Supplementary Fig. 3). Taken together, the residue specific histone acetylation profiles provide additional insights into potential mechanisms of RXR interaction with MyoD or CTCF in myoblast proliferation.

Conclusion

Our integrative ChIP-seq analyses provide insights into potential interaction of RXR with MyoD and CTCF in cellular proliferation. The overlap of RXR with MyoD has a signature of active enhancer with a connection to p300, in addition to augmentation H3K18 and H3K27 acetylation. The overlap of RXR with CTCF, on the other hand, appears to have a signature of active promoter and H3K9 acetylation. Furthermore, the association of CTCF with repressed chromatin states may be important for segmenting the chromosome into active and inactive regions in myoblast development. However, the significance of RXR-CTFC association at the active promoter, or the interplay of RXR and CTCF, remains to be determined.

Methods

ChIP-seq analysis

The RXRα ChIP-seq experiments (GSE279060) was conducted by using specific antibody against RXRα, from Santa Cruz Biotechnology (sc-553x) as previously described21, with proliferating C2C12 myoblasts (ATCC) at 70% confluency. Peak finding was performed using the HOMER findPeaks tool23 with -style factor and aligned to the mm9 genome. Tag densities were obtained from the initial peak calling output by HOMER to its original tag directory (total tags per peak/peak length). MyoD and CTCF ChIP-seq peaks were obtained from publicly available narrowpeak data files (GSM915186 and GSM915188, respectively). Histone acetylation ChIP-seq data was deposited as GSE94558. Distance to the TSS for each peak and nearby RefSeq gene association were determined using the annotatepeaks.pl function on HOMER v5.0.1. De novo primary motif analysis was performed using HOMER findmotifgenome.pl tool (-size 200). Secondary motif analysis was then performed on MEME SPAMO v5.5.525 using a 2 kb peak size and a margin size of 900 bp to cover the 2 kb peak span (± 1 kb).

Chromatin state analysis

ChIP-seq peaks were associated to distinct chromatin states using BedTools45 v2.31.1 intersect command (with options -wa -wb -loj) for the peak centers of each population and the chromatin state distribution data. Resulting associations were characterized as active or poised promoter, active or poised enhancer and other that includes transcribed, heterochromatin, and inactive regions based on our previously established chromatin state model21. Histone acetylation enrichment was analyzed on the peak centers using ngs.plot46, which calculates read intensities of peak locations on the alignment files. Putative active enhancers were obtained from published functionally genomic screening analysis27, and loop anchors were identified by previously published Hi-C analysis47. The average read enrichment of the ChIP-seq peaks were plotted using the alignment files and output for the total read enrichment was used for quantification. IGV v2.17.448 was used for visual observations of the histone enrichment on specific model loci.

Gene ontology

Gene Ontology (GO) analysis was performed using Genomic Regions Enrichment of Annotations Tool v4.0.449,50 to provide biological functions to annotated genes. Each population of peaks were analyzed using the whole genome as background region and the ‘single nearest gene’ association rule from the TSS. The top 3 GO terms in each population, based on Hypergeometric FDR Q-Value, were plotted with the number of genes in the bubble plots.

Network analysis

Qiagen’s Ingenuity Pathway Analysis51 was used to obtain network analyses of each population of the peaks. As input from the ChIP-seq data, the RefSeq genes associated with each peak were used to provide associating networks. An analysis with standard cutoffs was run to identify both direct and indirect relationships. From the output, pathways were ranked based on the p-value of overlap. Chemical and drug molecule type pathways were not included. Additionally, drug or chemicals in the networks of interest were also removed, restricting the output to transcription factors due to the nature of the ChIP-seq input.

Quantitative ChIP analysis

The C2C12 cells were crosslinked, sonicated and subjected to ChIP as previously described52. Immunoprecipitated DNA was then purified and amplified by real-time PCR reactions in triplicates using locus-specific primers. Target abundance was quantified in relation to input chromatin DNA. Each qChIP was repeated at least three times. Antibodies against RXRα (sc-553x) and MyoD (sc-32758x) were obtained from Santa Cruz Biotechnology. Primer pairs used for qPCR amplification were previously described21.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analysis was conducted using the R package. Additional graphics and calculations were performed using Microsoft Excel. Statistical tests were performed using a non-parametric two-sided Wilcoxon rank sum test in which p < 0.05 was considered significant.

Construction of a survival model for predicting biochemical recurrence of prostate cancer based on propionate metabolism-related genes
Authors: Baosai Lu, Yalin Niu, Xi Liu, Chenming Zhao, Yuewei Yin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAbout 20–40% of prostate cancer (PCa) develop biochemical recurrence (BCR) after surgery, and propionate metabolism may contribute to tumor progression. BCR remains a major clinical challenge in PCa, as current tools based on histopathology and prostate-specific antigen (PSA) fail to capture the molecular heterogeneity driving the disease. While metabolic reprogramming is known to facilitate post-treatment adaptation, the specific role of propionate metabolism in this context remains largely unexplored. Therefore, this study aimed to systematically investigate propionate metabolism-related genes (PMRGs) to develop a novel prognostic model for the improved early prediction of recurrence.MethodsIn this study, The Cancer Genome Atlas-Prostate Adenocarcinoma (TCGA-PRAD), GSE70770 and 412 PMRGs were employed. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in PCa and control and DEGs2 in BCR and no BCR samples obtained by differential analysis were intersected with PMRGs to get candidate genes. After Cox and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression analyses, biomarkers were identified to construct risk models.ResultsBiomarkers including BMP6, NAT2, PTGS2, LPL were identified to construct risk model after a series of analyses. Meanwhile, the nomogram for risk score can effectively predict BCR-free recurrence in PCa patients. Besides, 16 signaling pathways were significantly associated with riskScore, such as cell cycle and DNA replication. In immune-related analysis, six immune cells were significantly associated with the biomarkers. Furthermore, Wee1 Inhibitor_1046, Paclitaxel_1080, etc. were therapeutic drugs for PCa patients. Finally, the expression trend of four biomarkers was confirmed in clinical samples.ConclusionsIn this study, PMRGs were regarded as biomarkers in PCa for risk model construction, which suggest that propionate metabolism represents a biologically relevant axis in PCa recurrence and may offer a novel framework for biomarker-driven risk assessment.Keywords: Prostate cancer (PCa), biochemical recurrence (BCR), risk model, nomogram

Highlight box

Key findings

• This study identified four propionate metabolism-related genes (PMRGs) (BMP6, NAT2, PTGS2, LPL) as key biomarkers for biochemical recurrence in prostate cancer. A prognostic model based on these genes showed higher predictive accuracy than the traditional Gleason score. The risk score combined with prostate specific antigen level served as an independent prognostic factor, and its mechanisms were linked to cell cycle regulation and immune infiltration such as type 17 helper T cells. Drug sensitivity analysis suggested high-risk patients may respond better to agents like Wee1 inhibitors. Experimental validation confirmed abnormal expression of these genes in prostate cancer tissues.

What is known and what is new?

• It is known that biochemical recurrence affects a significant proportion of prostate cancer patients, but current prediction tools lack molecular-level precision. Metabolic reprogramming is recognized in prostate cancer progression, yet the role of propionate metabolism in recurrence remains unclear.

• This study systematically establishes the association between PMRGs and prostate cancer recurrence, providing a novel predictive model and clinical nomogram, and revealing related immune and pathway mechanisms.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• This research introduces a new molecular and metabolism-based strategy for recurrence risk stratification. Validation in larger clinical cohorts is required. Functional studies should clarify the roles of these genes, and targeted therapies for high-risk patients should be explored to advance personalized management of prostate cancer.

Introduction

Prostate cancer (PCa) is a malignant tumor that develops in epithelial tissue of the prostate and is one of the most widespread cancers in older men (1). Among cancers in men, PCa is the second most common solid tumor after lung cancer. It primarily affects men aged 45 to 60 years old, with an incidence rate of about 60% in men over 65 years old (2). Although the incidence rate of PCa in China is lower than that in Western countries, it has been increasing annually in recent years due to population aging, changes in diet and lifestyle, and improved detection techniques (3). The mortality rate of PCa varies significantly. If detected early, the life expectancy of patients with localized PCa can exceed that of those not detected early by more than 99% (4); however, if diagnosed at an advanced stage (distant metastasis), the 5-year overall survival (OS) rate is only 30% (5). Treatment options for PCa include active surveillance, chemotherapy, radiation therapy, hormone therapy, surgery, and cryotherapy. The specific treatment plan depends on tumor characteristics, prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels, grade, stage, and the likelihood of recurrence (6). However, all treatment methods come with significant side effects, such as toxic reactions, reductions in white and red blood cell counts, leading to fatigue, hair loss, peripheral neuropathy, erectile dysfunction, and urinary incontinence (7). In addition, biochemical recurrence (BCR) affects 25–50% of patients following radical PCa treatment (8). Far from simple tumor regrowth, BCR is a biologically distinct precursor to metastasis characterized by metabolic reprogramming and epigenetic adaptations that enable survival under stress (9,10). As a critical point of progression, the onset of BCR demands immediate intervention, despite the current limitations in treatment options. Despite its widespread use, the Gleason score has significant limitations in predicting BCR. It is subject to inter-observer variability and often fails to identify “low-risk” patients who eventually relapse (11). Fundamentally, this static histological measure captures aggressiveness at diagnosis but overlooks the molecular dynamics driving recurrence (12). In such a situation, the need to find new therapeutic targets to extend patient survival is urgent. With the emergence of new technologies such as molecular sequencing, liquid biopsy, and advanced imaging technologies, the focus of PCa research has shifted towards predictive biomarkers. These biomarkers are expected to become drivers of future clinical decision-making, thereby enabling more precise treatment options based on biomarkers in this field.

PCa exhibits a unique metabolic reliance on lipid metabolism and fatty acid pathways for progression (13). Notably, short-chain fatty acids—derived from the host or gut microbiota—play a critical role in modulating epigenetic regulation, inflammation, and tumor adaptability in PCa (14). Propionate is a short-chain fatty acid primarily produced by gut microbiota during the fermentation of fiber (15). Additionally, the metabolism of certain amino acids (such as threonine) and fatty acids can also generate propionate (16). The metabolic pathway of propionate involves its conversion to methylmalonic acid (MMA) by propionyl carboxylase, followed by a series of enzyme-catalyzed reactions leading into the tricarboxylic acid cycle (17). Propionate metabolism can proceed normally or abnormally; the latter being associated with inherited metabolic disorders such as propionic acidemia. Abnormal propionate metabolism may enhance the adaptability and invasiveness of tumor cells through metabolic reprogramming (18). Methylmalonic acidemia and propionic acidemia (MMA/PA) are congenital metabolic disorders caused by deficiencies in methylmalonyl-CoA mutase (MUT) or propionyl-CoA carboxylase (PCC), leading to the accumulation of propionate and/or MMA in the body (19). Recent literature indicates that dysregulation of propionate metabolism may promote tumor invasiveness and enhance metastatic success (20). For example, systemic metabolite MMA increases with age and is associated with poor cancer prognosis and increased cancer-related mortality in elderly patients (21). In triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) cells, interference with propionate metabolism may lead to MMA accumulation, thereby promoting metabolic changes and enhancing distant metastasis (22). Additionally, propionate may play a role in preventing invasive epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) through epigenetic regulation, thereby reducing the likelihood of lung cancer metastasis (23). Although the role of propionate metabolism-related genes (PMRGs) in PCa remains unclear, their impact on mitochondrial function and epigenetics suggests a link to progression. We hypothesize that PMRG dysregulation facilitates BCR by enhancing tumor survival and stress adaptation following radical therapy. Therefore, this study aimed to systematically investigate PMRGs in PCa, with a specific focus on their association with BCR. By integrating transcriptomic data and prognostic modeling, we sought to identify PMRG-based biomarkers that may predict BCR risk and provide mechanistic insights into metabolic drivers of PCa recurrence. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tau.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tau-2025-aw-811/rc).

Methods

Data acquisition

The training set was the PCa-related data set [The Cancer Genome Atlas-Prostate Adenocarcinoma (TCGA-PRAD)] from the TCGA database, including 481 PCa and 51 control samples, among which 396 samples (BCR: nonBCR =50:346) with BCR recurrence time and follow-up time were used to construct BCR risk model. In addition, GSE70770 (GPL6801) contained 203 tumor samples with BCR information, which were used as validation set and obtained from GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gds). Moreover, 412 PMRGs (relevance score >9) were derived from the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/). This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Acquisition and functional analysis of candidate genes

Firstly, differential expression analysis was performed to identify DEGs1 in PCa and control and DEGs2 in BCR and no BCR samples via the DESeq2 package (v 1.36.0) (24) using |log2 fold change (FC)| >0.5, P.adj <0.05 as the filtering criteria. After that, PMRGs were taken intersection with DEGs1, and DEGs2 to get the candidate genes.

The functional enrichment analyses, included Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), were performed using the clusterProfiler package (v 4.7.1.001) to explore the shared functions and related pathways among the candidate genes (P.adj <0.05) (25). GO analysis consists of three parts: biological process (BP), molecular function (MF), and cellular component (CC).

Identification of biomarkers and construction of risk models

On the basis of the 396 samples that had BCR information in training set, we performed univariable Cox regression analysis on candidate genes [hazard ratio (HR) ≠1 and P value <0.05]. After that, to further screen feature genes, the genes identified in Cox regression were subjected to least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression analysis via the glmnet package (v 4.1-6) (26). In addition, to identify biomarkers for risk model, a multivariate Cox regression analysis was also performed (HR ≠1 and P<0.05). For further assessment of the risk model, ROC curves were plotted with the survival ROC package (v 1.0.3) (27). To evaluate clinical utility, we also compared the risk score’s predictive performance [area under the curve (AUC)] against standard parameters like the Gleason score. Subsequently, the samples were categorized into high/low risk groups using median BCR risk score as a boundary. Afterwards, risk curves and Kaplan-Meier (K-M) survival curves were generated for the high/low risk groups. Similarly, ROC curves of the risk model, risk curves and K-M survival curves between different risk groups were employed in GSE70770 to validate the universality of risk model.

Construction of independent prognostic model

This study included age, Gleason, pathologic T, N, PSA and risk score in univariate Cox regression analysis. This was used as the basis for a multivariable Cox regression analysis for independent prognostic factors, in which both analyses took HR ≠1 and P value <0.05 as screening criteria. Subsequently, a nomogram of independent prognostic factors was generated to predict recurrence in PCa patients via the rms package (v 6.3-0) (28), which performance was assessed by calibration.

Prognostic value analysis for biomarkers and correlation analysis for risk scores and clinical features

K-M survival curves for OS, disease-free survival (DFS) and relapse-free survival (RFS) of PCa were plotted using survival package (v 3.4-0) to further explore the prognostic value of biomarkers (29), where biomarkers were divided into high/low expression groups according to their median expression value. Moreover, risk scores differences in different clinical feature groups were compared by a multi-group rank sum test. Meanwhile, the proportion of distribution of clinical features between different risk groups was examined by Chi-squared test.

Immune-related analysis

In training set, 28 immune cell scores were calculated for PCa samples using the single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) algorithm in Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA) package (v 1.44.5) (30), and their differences in risk groups were also compared using Wilcoxon rank sum test. Subsequently, correlation analysis of biomarkers and differential immune cells was then performed using Spearman’s method (|Cor| >0.4, P value <0.05). Besides, the K-M survival curves for these differential immune cells were generated to evaluate their associations with prognosis. Furthermore, considering that immune checkpoint expression could affect immunotherapy efficacy, the expression differences of 79 immune checkpoints (31) between risk groups were assessed via Wilcoxon rank sum test.

Functional enrichment and chemotherapeutic drug sensitivity analysis

For further exploring the correlation between risk scores and KEGG pathways in GSEA database, KEGG pathway enrichment scores were calculated by the ssGSEA algorithm and their correlation with risk scores was explored by Spearman’s method (|Cor| >0.3, P value <0.05). Subsequently, based on the 198 chemotherapy/targeted therapy drugs in GDSC database, half maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) of these drugs for PCa patients was calculated by the oncoPredict package (v 0.2) (32), and their differences between different risk groups were assessed by the Wilcoxon rank-sum test.

Expression validation of biomarkers

On the one hand, expression levels of biomarkers were verified within training set, and the differences between groups were subjected to Wilcoxon rank sum test. On the other hand, reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) was carried out to verify their dysregulation in clinical samples. Specifically, RNAs from 20 PCa and 20 control tissue samples collected from The Second Hospital of Hebei Medical University were isolated using TRIzol reagent (Ambion, America). Post collection, RNA integrity was confirmed via electrophoresis and purity was assessed using a NanoPhotometer N50. The isolated RNAs were then used for cDNA synthesis employing the SureScript-First-strand-cDNA-synthesis-ANKRD36B kit (Servicebio, Wuhan, China). Subsequently, RT-qPCR was executed using Universal Blue SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (Servicebio, China). Primers for biomarkers and the internal reference gene (GAPDH) were listed in Table 1. The reaction system and program were performed according to the reagent’s instructions. Following the RT-qPCR procedure, 2−ΔΔCT method was applied to determine relative expression levels. A t-test was utilized to assess differences between groups. Ethical approval for this study was granted by the Ethics Committee of The Second Hospital of Hebei Medical University with the approval number 2019-R061, and all patients provided written informed consent.

Statistical analysis

The bioinformatics analysis was carried out by R software (v 4.2.2). Differences were considered to be significant at P<0.05 in statistical analysis.

Results

The 27 candidate genes involved in different biological functions

After differential expression analysis, 9,851 DEGs in PCa and control as well as 1,100 DEGs2 in BCR and nonBCR groups were identified, respectively. DEGs1 contained 5,493 up-regulated and 4,358 down-regulated genes (Figure 1A,1B), while DEGs2 included 571 up-regulated and 529 down-regulated genes (Figure 1C,1D). Besides, 27 candidate genes were obtained by overlapping them with 412 PMRGs (Figure 1E), which participated in 135 BP entries, 4 CC entries and 26 MF entries in GO enrichment analysis, such as: response to xenobiotic stimulus (BP), plasma lipoprotein particle (CC), heme binding (MF) (Figure 1F), etc. Moreover, a total of 12 signaling pathways were significantly enriched in KEGG, including Chemical carcinogenesis-DNA adducts, Steroid hormone biosynthesis, and so on (Figure 1G). These results aided in understanding the multiple roles of propionate metabolism in PCa progression.

Construction of risk models for BMP6, NAT2, PTGS2, and LPL

In this study, a total of 12 genes from univariate Cox regression analysis were further analyzed by LASSO regression analysis (Figure 2A), of which 9 genes were screened (Figure 2B). After their multivariate Cox regression analysis, BMP6, NAT2, PTGS2, and LPL were considered as biomarkers of PCa for constructing risk models of BCR (Figure 2C). The risk score was calculated: riskScore = (0.4626) × BMP6 + (−4.5663) × NAT2 e + (−0.3198) × PTGS2 + (0.4519) × LPL. Time-dependent ROC analysis showed that the risk score consistently outperformed Gleason score in predicting BCR in PCa. The Gleason score achieved AUCs of 0.653, 0.694, and 0.734 at 1, 3, and 5 years, respectively, whereas the risk score yielded higher predictive accuracy with AUCs of 0.728, 0.776, and 0.776 at the corresponding time points, indicating improved discrimination across short- and long-term follow-up (Figure 2D). Subsequently, the risk curves showed a lower rate of BCR recurrence in samples from the low risk group of PCa patients (Figure 2E), and the same result could be demonstrated in K-M curves, where patients in high risk group were more likely to have a BCR recurrence (Figure 2F).

Besides, both the risk curve and the K-M curve showed that BCR recurrence samples were more in high risk group than low risk group, and the recurrence rate of BCR was also higher in high-risk group (Figure 3B,3C). The risk model demonstrated remarkable generalizability, revealing its potential as an invaluable instrument for tailored prognostic evaluation in clinical management of PCa.

The excellent predictive performance of the nomogram for risk score and PSA

After univariate Cox regression analysis, Gleason, pathologic T, pathologic N, PSA, and risk score were selected for multivariate Cox regression analysis (Figure 4A). The results showed that risk score and PSA were independent prognostic factors for PCa (Figure 4B), which were subjected to construct nomogram to predict BCR-free recurrence in PCa patients (Figure 4C). Moreover, the slope of calibration curve for nomogram was close to 1, indicating that the nomogram has good predictive performance for BCR-free recurrence at 1/3/5 years (Figure 4D). This nomogram model could serve as an effective tool in the management of PCa.

Prognostic value of biomarkers in different survival periods

In this study, the prognostic value of biomarkers in OS, DFS and RFS was further assessed by K-M survival curves. For DFS, based on the expression of BMP6, NAT2 and LPL, there was a significant survival difference in PCa patients with DFS between their high and low expression groups. Among them, the likelihood of DFS was significantly higher for patients in the group with high expression of NAT2, while the opposite result was observed for BMP6 and LPL. Meanwhile, the likelihood of RFS was significantly higher for PCa patients in the group with low expression of LPL, while the opposite result was observed for NAT2 and PTGS2 (Figure 5A-5C). Moreover, it was found that risk scores were significantly different in Gleason, pathologic T, pathologic N, suggesting that risk scores were associated with these clinical features (Figure 5D).

Biomarkers correlated differently with immune cells

In this study, 28 immune cells ssGSEA scores in PCa samples of the training set were calculated and were compared in risk groups (Figure 6A), a total of 16 immune cells had significantly lower infiltration in high risk group, e.g., Activated B cell, Activated dendritic cell, etc. (Figure 6B). Among them, six differential immune cells were significantly associated with PTGS2, namely Eosinophil, Immature dendritic cell, and so on (Figure 6C) (|Cor| >0.4, P<0.05). Additionally, K-M survival curve for type 17 T helper cell revealed that there was significant difference in BCR-free survival probability, underscoring this cell was associated with prognosis (P<0.05) (Figure 6D). Propionate metabolism might affect PCa progression by altering immune infiltration level of this cell. Additionally, a total of 12 immune checkpoints with notable differential expression between risk groups were recognized (Figure 6E). Most of the checkpoints exhibited higher expression in low risk group. This suggested that propionate metabolism might influence PCa progression by influence immune therapy responses.

In this study, a total of 16 KEGG signaling pathways were significantly associated with risk scores, including cell cycle, DNA replication and so on (Figure 7A,7B). Discussion

BCR after PCa treatment is relatively common, and recent studies indicate that dysregulation of propionate metabolism may promote tumor invasiveness and increase metastatic success (22). However, no literature has yet fully elucidated the relationship between propionate metabolism and PCa. In this study, a series of bioinformatics approaches, including differential gene expression analysis, functional enrichment analysis, univariate Cox, LASSO, and multivariate Cox regression analysis, were employed to identify biomarkers related to propionate metabolism in PCa. Based on these biomarkers, a risk model was constructed. In addition, functional enrichment, immune cell infiltration, and drug sensitivity analyses were conducted, revealing the potential role and molecular mechanisms of PMRGs in BCR in PCa. The model constructed in this study performed well in predicting BCR of PCa, risk scores were significantly different between the clinical indicators Gleason, pathological stage, indicating good clinical application prospects. Additionally, the risk score and PSA level, as prognostic factors for predicting BCR of PCa, endow the model with independent predictive capability. This study fills a gap in the research on propionate metabolism in PCa BCR, providing new scientific evidence and clinical perspectives.

In this study, four genes were identified as biomarkers for BCR: BMP6, NAT2, PTGS2, and LPL, which were found to be optimal for constructing the BCR prediction model. Previous studies have shown that BMP6 promotes cell proliferation and enhances the interaction between p-SMAD5 and β-catenin, inducing the expression of androgen receptor (AR) in PCa cells, thereby leading to castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) (33). The NAT2 gene can convert potential carcinogens into water-soluble products through N-acetylation, helping to reduce DNA damage and cancer risk (34). Defects in LPL gene (such as chromosome 8p22 deletion, gene polymorphisms, and epigenetic changes in promoter region) disrupt lipid metabolism, leading to an abnormal lipid environment characterized by high triglycerides or low high-density lipoprotein (HDL), providing additional energy and growth signals for PCa cells and promoting tumor development. LPL gene defects may also decrease LPL expression or activity, affecting lipid metabolism, glucose metabolism, and inflammatory signaling pathways, including the NF-κB pathway, further increasing the risk of PCa (35).

Prostaglandin-endoperoxide synthase 2 (PTGS2), also known as cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2), has been extensively reported as a pro-tumorigenic gene in PCa, primarily due to its role in regulating inflammatory responses through catalyzing prostaglandin synthesis, thereby facilitating tumor growth, angiogenesis, and disease progression (36,37). Consistent with these reports, PTGS2 overexpression has been observed in advanced or inflammation-driven tumor contexts. However, in the present study, both heatmap visualization and Kaplan-Meier survival analyses consistently demonstrated that higher PTGS2 expression was associated with a lower risk of BCR and a more favorable prognosis. In addition, expression validation analyses revealed that PTGS2 was significantly downregulated in PCa tissues compared with normal controls. This seemingly contradictory finding may be explained by the clinical and biological context of our cohort. Most patients included in this study had undergone radical prostatectomy, and BCR represents a post-treatment disease state characterized by minimal residual tumor burden and adaptive stress responses rather than active tumor expansion. In this setting, PTGS2 expression may no longer function as a direct tumor-promoting driver but instead reflect an inflammatory or metabolic state associated with tumor suppression or delayed recurrence following surgical intervention. Moreover, PTGS2 should be interpreted as part of a multigene metabolic signature rather than an isolated prognostic factor, suggesting that its protective association may arise from system-level interactions involved in recurrence adaptation. Furthermore, our study mainly relied on gene expression data from bioinformatics databases [e.g., TCGA and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO)]. This study provides a direction for further research into the biological function and clinical features of the four genes in PCa. This study first identifies a link between NAT2 and PCa, though the precise mechanism remains unclear and requires further exploration.

While the Gleason score remains the clinical cornerstone, its reliance on histopathological grading limits its ability to capture metabolic adaptations associated with BCR. The PMRG-based risk model offers complementary prognostic information by incorporating molecular features related to recurrence biology, thereby supporting a more refined risk stratification framework when used alongside traditional indicators.

We further explored the associations between the risk score and KEGG pathways, identifying 16 pathways with significant correlations. Among them, cell cycle regulation, DNA replication, and mismatch repair pathways showed prominent correlations. These pathways exhibit crucial roles in tumor biology. Notably, cell cycle regulation pathway and DNA replication pathway showed significantly increased activity in high-risk group, indicating that tumor cells in these patients proliferate faster and have more active gene replication, which is closely associated with tumor aggressiveness and high recurrence risk. The high activity of these pathways not only promotes rapid division of tumor cells but also increases genomic instability, thereby driving tumor progression. In addition, pathways related to mismatch repair and homologous recombination were more enriched in high-risk group. Abnormalities in these pathways may lead to reduced DNA repair efficiency, further accumulating gene mutations and promoting malignant transformation of the tumor (38). Metabolic pathways significantly impact tumor progression in tumor microenvironment by regulating inflammatory responses, cellular energy supply, and signal transduction (39). Among the metabolism-related pathways, arachidonic acid metabolism is negatively correlated with the risk score, and it is also more enriched in low-risk group with higher scores. Arachidonic acid metabolism plays a key role in cancer-related inflammation, potentially accelerating tumor growth and invasion by activating pro-tumor signaling pathways such as COX-2 (22). It is speculated that the overall inhibition of the arachidonic acid metabolic pathway may be due to the impact of the internal environment in high-risk group on the body’s metabolism.

To explore the relationships between biomarkers and differential immune cells, we identified six immune cells significantly correlated with the biomarkers: eosinophils, immature dendritic cells, mast cells, natural killer cells, type 2 T helper cells, and type 17 T helper cells (Th17 cells). The K-M survival curve of Th17 cells shows that the probability of BCR recurrence is significantly higher in high expression group compared to the low expression group. Studies have shown that in PCa, the proportion of Th17 cells that produce IL-17 in CD4+ T cells is increased, and IL-17A and IL-17F secreted by these cells further promote PCa cell viability, migration, and invasion through enhanced NF-κB signaling (40). This suggests a significant correlation between Th17 cells and prognosis. The level of Th17 cells or the expression of their associated cytokines may serve as potential biomarkers for PCa prognosis, thus enabling prognostic analysis. Although survival analysis did not reach statistical significance, high mast cell infiltration was associated with a trend toward poor prognosis, suggesting its potentially important pro-tumor role in PCa microenvironment. Studies have found that mast cells enhance tumor invasiveness and treatment resistance, increasing the risk of BCR by promoting inflammation, angiogenesis, and immune suppression in PCa through the release of pro-inflammatory factors (41). Literature has confirmed that eosinophils may promote PCa progression by upregulating PTGS2 expression, thereby enhancing the inflammatory response (42). In this study, eosinophils are significantly positively correlated with the biomarker PTGS2, indicating its potential role in tumor recurrence and invasion. Studies have shown that Th17 cells can induce the activation and infiltration of eosinophils by secreting factors such as IL-17, further revealing the potential collaborative role of both in tumor immune microenvironment (43). The significant positive correlation between eosinophils and PTGS2 also suggests the possibility of the use as potential prognostic biomarkers, offering a more comprehensive prognosis evaluation. Therefore, immune cells play a key role in BCR prognosis, and their activity and distribution may affect the immune response and disease progression of patients, thereby impacting prognosis.

Finally, based on therapeutic drugs provided by GDSC, some drugs showed significant differences in IC50 between high- and low-risk groups. Drugs such as Wee1 inhibitor, Paclitaxel, and MK-1775 had significantly lower IC50 values in high-risk group than in low-risk group, indicating that tumor cells in high-risk group were more sensitive to these drugs, possibly due to faster tumor cell proliferation, making them more dependent on cell cycle inhibitors. MK-1775 (WEE1 inhibitor) mainly induces premature mitosis by inhibiting WEE1 kinase activity, breaking cancer cells’ G2/M checkpoint control (44). Fulvestrant, I-BRD9, and AZD7762 also showed lower IC50 values in high-risk group, suggesting better efficacy in this group. Literature shows that Fulvestrant, a selective estrogen receptor degrader (SERD), inhibits growth of hormone receptor-positive breast cancer cells by blocking and degrading estrogen receptors. It is commonly used for treating advanced or metastatic breast cancer in postmenopausal women (45). Therefore, drugs like Wee1 inhibitors and Paclitaxel show better efficacy in high-risk PCa patients. In addition, targeted drugs show significant efficacy in high-risk PCa patients, implying that targeted therapy can accurately act on cancer cells based on individual patient characteristics, providing a more effective treatment plan.

Conclusions

This study utilized databases for bioinformatics analysis to identify four biomarkers associated with BCR and constructed a stable BCR recurrence prediction model. The study also analyzed correlations of these biomarkers with PCa survival and immune cell infiltration, providing new theoretical and clinical application prospects for understanding PCa recurrence mechanisms and personalized diagnosis and treatment. It is noteworthy that, although the biomarkers have demonstrated potential in clinical applications, their effectiveness and practicality still require support from broader sample validation and in-depth experimental studies. This includes conducting gene knockout experiments and using animal models to explore these biomarkers’ roles in specific treatment strategies and their potential mechanisms. In the future, we will continue to monitor research developments related to these genes to better understand how they can be applied in clinical practice and promote their widespread use in the medical field.

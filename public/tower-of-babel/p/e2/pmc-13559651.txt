Mitochondria related gene signature serves as prognosis prediction and risk stratification of cholangiocarcinoma
Authors: Shenji Li, Wei Xiang, Wei Wei, Hongrong Zhang, Qianyu Xie, Bo Yin, Hongsheng Lin, Shunchi Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCholangiocarcinoma (CHOL) is a highly aggressive biliary malignancy with poor clinical outcomes and limited effective prognostic biomarkers. Mitochondrial dysfunction participates in multiple oncological processes of CHOL, yet the prognostic roles of mitochondria‑related genes (MRGs) remain poorly understood. This study aimed to characterize MRGs expression in CHOL and develop a molecular prognostic model for predicting patient survival and guiding clinical management.MethodsRNA sequencing (RNA-seq) and clinical data of CHOL were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) (GSE89748) databases. Differentially expressed MRGs were identified, and 10 machine learning algorithms were used to construct prognostic models. The optimal model (highest average C-index) was selected to establish a mitochondria-related risk score (MRRS), which was validated internally and externally. A nomogram integrating clinical factors and MRRS was developed, and biological mechanisms were explored via functional and immune analyses.ResultsA 3-MRG (MAP3K1, MRPL18, PYGB) prognostic signature was constructed, stratifying patients into high- and low-risk groups with significantly different overall survival. The model showed high predictive accuracy, with an area under the curve (AUC) up to 0.845, and MRRS was an independent prognostic factor. The signature was associated with mitochondrial pathways, and the high-risk group had distinct immune infiltration and mutation profiles.ConclusionsA validated MRG prognostic model effectively stratifies CHOL patients and has potential clinical value for prognosis prediction. Further validation in larger cohorts is needed to confirm its applicability.Keywords: Cholangiocarcinoma (CHOL), mitochondria, prognostic model, signature

Introduction

Liver cancer is the sixth most prevalent malignancy and the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with approximately 865,269 new cases and 757,948 related deaths reported in the latest global cancer statistics (1). Based on distinct pathological characteristics, primary liver cancer can be classified into three major subtypes: hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), cholangiocarcinoma (CHOL), and combined hepatocellular-CHOL. As the second most common primary liver malignancy, CHOL accounts for approximately 3% to 6% of all primary liver cancer cases, and its global incidence has been rising over the past few decades (2). Although radical resection remains the first-line curative treatment for CHOL, a large proportion of patients presents with inoperable disease at diagnosis, or experiences tumor recurrence and progression after surgery, largely due to the high biological complexity and intratumoral heterogeneity of CHOL. Accordingly, there is an urgent clinical need for personalized treatment strategies for this disease (3). The widespread application of next-generation sequencing has deepened our understanding of the molecular landscape of CHOL, including its molecular subtyping and the clinical implications of key genomic alterations; for instance, kirsten rat sarcoma viral oncogene homolog (KRAS) and B-Raf proto-oncogene, serine/threonine-protein kinase (BRAF) mutations have been associated with unfavorable clinical outcomes in CHOL patients (4-6). Hence, there is an urgent and unmet need to identify novel molecular biomarkers that can accurately predict prognosis, guide individualized treatment decisions, and optimize risk stratification for CHOL patients.

Mitochondria act as central regulators of fatty acid, amino acid, and nucleotide metabolism, and have been well established as key drivers of tumor initiation and progression (7). The critical roles of mitochondrial metabolism in multiple stages of tumorigenesis, including malignant transformation, tumor progression, and modulation of therapeutic response, have been extensively documented in numerous malignancies (8,9). Accordingly, numerous therapeutic strategies targeting cancer-specific mitochondrial metabolic abnormalities have been developed and investigated in preclinical and clinical settings. In breast cancer, for example, previous research has demonstrated that targeting mitochondrial metabolism can enhance the sensitivity of cancer cells to mitochondrial inhibitors, highlighting the translational potential of this approach (10). Mitophagy, a selective cellular process that removes damaged or dysfunctional mitochondria to maintain mitochondrial quality and quantity homeostasis, also exerts pivotal functions in colorectal cancer tumorigenesis (11,12). Furthermore, enhanced mitochondrial biogenesis and upregulated oxidative phosphorylation have been implicated in cancer progression and the development of chemotherapy resistance (13). However, the expression patterns, biological functions, and prognostic value of mitochondria-related genes (MRGs) in CHOL remain largely uncharacterized, with no systematic relevant studies reported to date.

In this study, we aimed to systematically characterize the expression profiles of MRGs in CHOL and explore their potential biological roles. Furthermore, we established a novel molecular classification system for CHOL based on MRG expression patterns, which may provide new insights into the clinical management of this disease. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-0605/rc).

Methods

Data collection and preprocessing

RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) transcriptomic data and corresponding clinical information of 51 CHOL patients were initially retrieved from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) project via the Genomic Data Commons (GDC) Data Portal (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). Complementary standardized clinical annotation data were obtained from the UCSC Xena platform (http://xena.ucsc.edu). Samples with incomplete clinical follow-up data, unavailable overall survival (OS) information, or OS duration less than 30 days were excluded from subsequent analysis, resulting in a final cohort of 45 CHOL samples for model training and internal validation. For external validation, the GSE89748 dataset was downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), which initially contained 112 CHOL samples. The same sample exclusion criteria were applied to the external validation cohort to ensure data consistency. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

MRG set collection

The core MRG set was extracted from the MitoCarta 3.0 database (https://www.broadinstitute.org/mitocarta/mitocarta30-inventory-mammalian-mitochondrial-proteins-and-pathways), which includes a well-curated inventory of 1136 human genes encoding proteins with confirmed mitochondrial localization. To further supplement the MRG set, additional mitochondrial function-associated gene sets were retrieved from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) using the Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) framework (http://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/index.jsp).

Identification of differentially expressed MRGs (DEMRGs)

The “limma” R package was employed to identify DEMRGs between CHOL tumor tissues and adjacent non-tumor normal tissues. Genes meeting the thresholds of false discovery rate (FDR) <0.05 and |log2 fold change (FC)| >1 were defined as statistically significant DEMRGs. Volcano plots were generated using the “ggplot2” R package to visualize the significance and expression changes of DEMRGs, and heatmaps were constructed to display the expression patterns of significant DEMRGs between the tumor and normal groups.

Construction of machine learning (ML)-based prognostic models

Ten ML algorithms were adopted for prognostic model construction, including least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression, Ridge regression, stepwise Cox regression, CoxBoost, random survival forest (RSF), elastic net (Enet), Cox partial least squares regression (plsRcox), supervised principal component analysis (SuperPC), generalized boosted regression modeling (GBM), and survival support vector machine (survival-SVM). A total of 101 algorithm combinations were evaluated for variable screening and model construction in the TCGA-CHOL training cohort, with all procedures performed under a 10-fold cross-validation framework to avoid model overfitting.

Model evaluation and optimal model selection

The prognostic performance of all constructed models was systematically evaluated in the TCGA internal validation cohort and the GSE89748 external validation cohort. The concordance index (C-index) was calculated in the training, internal validation, and external validation sets to quantify the discrimination ability of each model. All models were ranked according to the mean C-index across the three datasets, and the optimal model with the highest mean C-index and robust clinical relevance was selected as the final prognostic signature for predicting OS in CHOL patients.

Risk score calculation and survival analysis

The risk score for each CHOL patient was calculated based on the final prognostic signature, using the formula: Risk score = Σ (regression coefficient of geneᵢ × expression level of geneᵢ). Patients were stratified into high-risk and low-risk groups according to the median risk score of the cohort. Kaplan-Meier (K-M) survival curves were plotted to visualize the difference in OS between the two groups, and the log-rank test was used to assess statistical significance via the “survminer” R package.

Cox regression analysis for independent prognostic value

The proportional hazards (PH) assumption was tested for clinical factors (age, T stage, sex, cholangitis, and risk score) using the “survival” R package. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed to identify independent prognostic factors for OS in CHOL patients, with hazard ratios (HRs) and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) calculated. A P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Forest plots were generated using the “forestplot” R package to visualize the results of the Cox regression analyses.

Nomogram construction and validation

A nomogram integrating age, T stage, sex, cholangitis, and the risk score was constructed using the “rms” R package to quantitatively predict the 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS probabilities of CHOL patients. Calibration curves were plotted to evaluate the consistency between the nomogram-predicted survival and actual observed survival. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed using the “ggDCA” R package to assess the clinical utility of the nomogram by calculating the net benefit across different threshold probabilities.

Functional enrichment analysis

Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was conducted to explore the biological functions of the identified DEMRGs using the “org.Hs.eg.db” and “clusterProfiler” R packages, including annotations for biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF) terms. GO terms with an adjusted P value <0.05 were considered statistically significantly enriched.

Tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) analysis

The ESTIMATE algorithm was applied to infer the stromal and immune cell content in the tumor microenvironment (TME) from transcriptomic data, generating stromal scores and immune scores for each sample. CIBERSORT was used to quantify the relative infiltration abundance of 22 tumor-infiltrating immune cell types. Single-sample GSEA (ssGSEA) implemented in the “GSVA” R package was performed to calculate the enrichment scores of 28 immune cell subsets. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to compare immune infiltration levels between the high-risk and low-risk groups via the “stats” and “car” R packages, with visualization performed using “ggplot2”. In addition, the correlation between the risk score and the expression levels of key immune checkpoint genes was analyzed.

Somatic mutation landscape analysis

Somatic mutation data of the TCGA-CHOL cohort were analyzed using the “maftools” R package. Oncoplots were generated to display the top 20 most frequently mutated genes in the high-risk and low-risk groups, respectively. The distribution of mutation types (including missense, nonsense, and frameshift mutations) and tumor mutation burden (TMB) were compared between the two risk groups.

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)

WGCNA was performed using the “WGCNA” R package to identify gene modules significantly correlated with immune infiltration characteristics and risk scores. A soft-thresholding power was selected to ensure the gene co-expression network conformed to scale-free topology, and hierarchical clustering was conducted to define co-expressed gene modules. Module-trait correlation analysis was performed to evaluate the association between gene modules and clinical characteristics (including stromal score and immune score). Gene modules with a P value <0.05 were considered statistically significant and subjected to subsequent functional enrichment analysis.

Statistical analysis

All bioinformatic and statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.2.2). Survival differences between groups were evaluated via K-M curves with the log-rank test. HRs and 95% CIs were calculated using univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test or Kruskal-Wallis test was used for comparisons of gene expression levels and immune scores between two or more groups. A two-sided P value <0.05 was set as the threshold for statistical significance in all analyses.

Results

Identification of differentially expressed MRGs and construction of the MRG-based prognostic signature

A total of 450 MRGs were matched between the TCGA and GSE89748 datasets, with 431 genes retained for the final analysis (Figure S1A). SLC25A13, ACADS, HADH, GOT2 and POR were the most significantly downregulated MRGs in CHOL, while G6PD, PYCR1, CYP27B1, BIK and CDC37P1 were the most significantly upregulated. The expression heatmap of these differentially expressed MRGs is shown in Figure S1B. In the preliminary screening, 431 differentially expressed MRGs were identified. To explore the prognostic value of these MRGs, univariate Cox PHs regression analysis was performed in the TCGA training cohort, which identified 4 MRGs (MAP3K1, MRPL18, PYGB and TP53AIP1) significantly associated with OS of CHOL patients (Figure 1A).

These 4 prognostic-related MRGs from univariate Cox analysis were further subjected to model construction. The genes were imported into an integrated ML pipeline to establish the mitochondria-related risk score (MRRS). We evaluated the predictive performance of 101 different ML algorithm combinations in the TCGA and GSE89748 datasets. The combination of Lasso and stepwise Cox regression (StepCox) achieved the highest mean C-index of 0.889 across all cohorts, and was thus selected as the optimal model for final MRRS construction (Figure 1B). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis revealed that patients in the high-risk group had significantly shorter OS than those in the low-risk group in both the TCGA cohort (P<0.001, Figure 1C) and the GSE89748 external validation cohort (P<0.001, Figure 1D).

Performance validation of the MRRS prognostic model

To further verify the predictive accuracy of the MRRS model, time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was conducted. The results showed that the area under the curve (AUC) values for 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS in the TCGA cohort were 0.839, 0.826, and 0.845, respectively (Figure 2A). Consistently, the model maintained favorable predictive stability in the GSE89748 cohort, with AUC values of 0.643, 0.709, and 0.633 for 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS, respectively (Figure 2B).

Further analysis of the risk score distribution revealed that the number of deaths increased gradually with the elevation of MRRS in both cohorts (Figure 2C,2D). For the expression pattern of signature genes in the TCGA cohort, MRPL18, PYGB and NQO1 exhibited significantly higher expression in the high-risk group, whereas MAP3K1 was significantly downregulated in this group (Figure 2E,2F). The expression pattern of these genes in the GSE89748 cohort was fully consistent with that in the TCGA cohort (Figure 2G,2H).

Establishment and validation of the prognostic nomogram

To determine whether MRRS could serve as an independent prognostic factor for CHOL patients, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed. The results confirmed that MRRS was an independent prognostic factor for OS in CHOL patients, with the highest HR of 6.996 (P<0.05, Figure 3A,3B). Subsequently, a nomogram integrating MRRS, age, gender, tumor grade and stage was constructed based on the TCGA cohort, to provide a visualized tool for quantitatively predicting the 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS probabilities of CHOL patients (Figure 3C).

Calibration curves showed high consistency between the nomogram-predicted OS and the actual observed OS (Figure 3D). Furthermore, DCA was performed to assess the clinical utility of the nomogram, and the results revealed that the nomogram yielded a higher net benefit across most threshold probabilities compared with any single clinical predictor alone (Figure 3E). Collectively, these findings indicated that the established nomogram had favorable and reliable prognostic value for CHOL patients.

Functional and pathway enrichment analysis of the MRRS model

To explore the potential biological functions and underlying pathways associated with the MRRS model, we identified differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the high-risk and low-risk groups and performed functional enrichment analysis. GO enrichment analysis revealed that DEGs were predominantly enriched in CC terms related to mitochondrial structures, including mitochondrial inner membrane, mitochondrial matrix and mitochondrial protein-containing complex. For MF terms, DEGs were mainly enriched in electron transfer activity, structural constituent of ribosome, and primary active transmembrane transporter activity. BP enrichment analysis showed that these DEGs were primarily involved in energy derivation by oxidation of organic compounds, cellular respiration and aerobic respiration (Figure 4A).

Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis showed that neurodegeneration-multiple diseases pathway, diabetic cardiomyopathy and oxidative phosphorylation were the most significantly enriched pathways of the DEGs (Figure 4B). GSEA was further performed to explore differential pathway activation between the two risk groups. The results showed that pathways including histidine metabolism, melanogenesis, metabolism of xenobiotics by cytochrome P450, olfactory transduction and oocyte meiosis were significantly enriched in the high-risk group (Figure 4C). In contrast, immune-related pathways such as autoimmune thyroid disease, intestinal immune network for IgA production and primary immunodeficiency were markedly enriched in the low-risk group (Figure 4D).

Identification of key gene modules associated with MRRS and clinical features via WGCNA

To identify gene co-expression modules significantly correlated with MRRS and clinical characteristics of CHOL patients, WGCNA was performed on the gene expression dataset. Soft threshold power was systematically screened to ensure the constructed network conformed to scale-free topology. The results showed that a soft threshold power of 9 was optimal, with the scale-free topology model fit index reaching above 0.9 (Figure 5A, left), while the mean connectivity of the network decreased significantly with the increase of the threshold power (Figure 5A, right).

Based on this optimal threshold, a gene co-expression network was constructed, and three distinct gene modules (MEblue, MEturquoise and MEgrey) were identified. Module-trait correlation analysis revealed that the MEgrey module was most significantly correlated with MRRS and key clinical parameters (Figure 5B). Further GO enrichment analysis of the MEgrey module showed that MF terms were mainly enriched in iron ion binding and multiple oxidoreductase activities; CC terms were predominantly enriched in mitochondrial structures, especially the mitochondrial inner membrane and matrix; BP terms were mainly enriched in oxidative stress-related pathways (Figure 5C). These findings suggested that the MEgrey module may play a critical role in regulating cellular metabolism and TIME dynamics in CHOL.

Correlation of MRRS with somatic mutation landscape and immune cell infiltration

To explore the association between MRRS and somatic mutation characteristics in CHOL, we matched patients from the two risk groups via sample barcodes, and depicted the somatic mutation landscape using oncoplots (Figure 6A,6B). The results showed that the mutation frequencies of DNAH8, ELF3, EPHA2, LRP1B and KRAS differed most significantly between the high-risk and low-risk groups.

Subsequently, we compared the TME scores and immune cell infiltration levels between the two risk groups. The results revealed that StromalScore, ImmuneScore and ESTIMATEScore were significantly lower in the low-risk group than in the high-risk group (Figure 6C). Furthermore, immune cell infiltration analysis showed that the infiltration levels of multiple immune cells, including naive B cells, CD4+ T cells, dendritic cells (DCs) and resting mast cells, were significantly lower in the high-risk group, while macrophage infiltration level was significantly higher in the high-risk group (Figure 6D). Correlation analysis between MRRS and immune checkpoint gene expression revealed that CD274 and POLE2 expression levels were significantly positively correlated with the risk score (Figure 6E).

Discussion

CHOL is the second most common primary malignant liver tumor after HCC, with its global incidence and mortality rates rising steadily over recent decades (14). Over the past decade, metabolic reprogramming has been established as a core hallmark of malignant tumors, and accumulating studies have identified mitochondrial metabolic dysregulation as a key mechanism driving therapeutic resistance in multiple cancers (15). As the central hub of cellular energy metabolism, mitochondria are indispensable for maintaining cellular physiological homeostasis, and their dysfunction in cancer cells can directly promote tumor progression and chemoresistance (16,17). In this context, the development of multi-gene prognostic models targeting MRGs via bioinformatics approaches has provided promising strategies for the diagnosis, treatment, and prognostic management of human cancers. For instance, Luo et al. constructed a prognostic prediction model based on MRGs from mitochondrial Gene Ontology BP terms, combined with OS analysis (18). Wang et al. further established an MRG-based signature for prognostic prediction and risk stratification in breast cancer patients (19). These studies collectively demonstrate the robust prognostic value and clinical application potential of MRG-based models in optimizing cancer management strategies. However, despite the growing body of evidence linking mitochondrial dysfunction to tumor progression and therapy resistance, no systematic study has comprehensively investigated the expression profile, prognostic value, and biological functions of MRGs in CHOL to date.

In the present study, we focused on developing and validating a novel MRG-based prognostic signature for CHOL. We first identified differentially expressed MRGs between CHOL tumor tissues and paired non-tumor control tissues. Subsequently, univariate Cox regression analysis was performed to screen for MRGs significantly associated with OS, which identified four candidate prognostic MRGs (MAP3K1, MRPL18, PYGB, and TP53AIP1). After further variable selection and model construction using the optimal combination of Lasso and stepwise Cox regression ML algorithms, we ultimately established a prognostic signature consisting of 3 core MRGs: MAP3K1, MRPL18, and PYGB. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis in the TCGA cohort demonstrated that patients in the low-risk group had significantly longer OS than those in the high-risk group, and this prognostic difference was further validated in the independent GSE89748 external cohort. Overall, the three-gene signature exhibits superior performance in diagnosis and prognostic prediction compared with existing models. For instance, Zhang et al. screened immune cell-related gene subtypes of CHOL and constructed a tumor immune RiskScore model to predict immunotherapy response and long-term patient prognosis, yet the AUC of its ROC curve for prognostic prediction was only 0.671 (20).

Additionally, we constructed a nomogram integrating gender, age, T-stage, cholangitis, and the MRG-based risk score, which exhibited favorable discriminatory power for OS prediction in CHOL patients. Notably, patients with high risk scores presented a distinct tumor immune microenvironment profile, with significantly higher overall stromal and immune scores, differential immune cell infiltration patterns, and a unique somatic mutation landscape compared with the low-risk group. These findings suggest that the three identified MRGs play a critical role in regulating somatic mutation profiles and TME remodeling in CHOL. The favorable robustness and stable prognostic accuracy of the signature across both internal and external cohorts indicate its potential clinical utility for prognostic risk stratification in CHOL patients. Collectively, this study highlights the critical involvement of MRGs in cancer prognosis, and underscores the promising value of MRG-based models in optimizing personalized treatment strategies for CHOL. We also found that there were significant differences in immune infiltration levels and gene mutation characteristics between the high-risk and low-risk groups. Previous studies have revealed that mitochondrial metabolic reprogramming remodels the TME of CHOL and regulates the infiltration pattern of immune cells via multi-dimensional mechanisms. By modulating oxidative phosphorylation, the TCA cycle and redox homeostasis, mitochondrial metabolic disorders directly regulate the survival and function of T cells, macrophage polarization, cytotoxicity of natural killer (NK) cells, and neutrophil activation, thereby altering the cellular composition and functional status of the immune microenvironment in CHOL (21). Impaired mitochondrial respiratory function drives increased glucose uptake and lactate secretion in cancer cells, leading to extracellular acidification and intracellular alkalization within the TME, which facilitates malignant phenotypes and immune evasion of CHOL (22). In addition, mitochondrial metabolic dysfunction establishes an immunosuppressive microenvironment and promotes immune escape through pathways including enhanced glycolysis, lactate accumulation and dysregulated fatty acid oxidation. Combinatorial therapy using inhibitors targeting lactate metabolism, amino acid catabolism and fatty acid metabolism together with immune checkpoint blockade is expected to convert “immune-excluded” CHOL into a subtype responsive to immunotherapy (23).

The prognostic MRGs in our signature have been widely reported to exert critical functions in tumorigenesis and cancer progression, with biological roles closely linked to mitochondrial metabolism and homeostasis. MAP3K1, a serine/threonine kinase in the MAPK signaling pathway, responds to various metabolic and mitogenic stimuli, and regulates multiple cellular processes including proliferation, apoptosis, and invasive phenotype (24). Previous studies have demonstrated that MAP3K1 is closely associated with breast cancer susceptibility (25), and that silencing MAP3K1 in breast cancer cells significantly reduces the expression of urokinase-type plasminogen activator, thereby inhibiting tumor cell invasiveness (26). These findings indicate that MAP3K1 modulates tumorigenesis by regulating the malignant phenotype and invasive capacity of cancer cells. Conversely, induced expression of the MAP3K1 kinase domain has been shown to enhance cellular sensitivity to ultraviolet radiation-induced cell death, and increase the susceptibility of fibrosarcoma cells to TNF-α-induced apoptosis, suggesting that MAP3K1 exerts a pro-apoptotic function in specific contexts (27). This is consistent with our finding that MAP3K1 was significantly downregulated in the high-risk group of CHOL patients, implying that low MAP3K1 expression may promote CHOL progression by attenuating the pro-apoptotic effect in tumor cells. Transcriptomic analysis of ammonia/hypoxia stress models identified MAP3K1 as a core stress regulator, indicating its potential involvement in the modulation of energy metabolism (Figures S2,S3) (28). PPI network analysis showed MRPL18 interacts with multiple mitochondrial ribosomal proteins (MRPs: MRPL4/17/19/27/38/39/44/47/58, MRPS30) to assemble the mitoribosomal large subunit (mtLSU) (Figure S2B). Mitoribosomes translate 13 essential mtDNA-encoded OXPHOS subunits (complexes I, III, IV, V). Thus, MRPL18 overexpression in CHOL may enhance mitoribosomal activity and OXPHOS capacity to meet tumor energy demands. Furthermore, high MRPL18 correlated with poor CHOL prognosis (Figure S3B).

MRPL18, a core mitochondrial ribosomal protein, is responsible for the translation of key components of the mitochondrial OXPHOS complex. It has been reported to be significantly upregulated in breast cancer cells, and its high expression is closely associated with poor patient survival (29,30). Elevated MRPL18 expression may drive enhanced mitochondrial biogenesis and OXPHOS activity, which fuels the high metabolic demands of rapidly proliferating cancer cells, and ultimately contributes to tumor progression and chemoresistance. This aligns with our finding that MRPL18 was highly expressed in the high-risk group of CHOL patients, and our functional enrichment analysis further linked the signature to mitochondrial metabolism and OXPHOS-related pathways.

PYGB, a brain-type isoform of glycogen phosphorylase, is a rate-limiting enzyme in glycogenolysis, which plays a pivotal role in maintaining cellular ATP supply by regulating glycogen metabolism (31). PYGB has been implicated in the progression of multiple malignancies, including gastric adenocarcinoma and HCC (32). High PYGB expression is correlated with poor prognosis in HCC, likely via modulating cancer cell proliferation, invasion, and apoptotic resistance (33), which is consistent with our finding that high PYGB expression was enriched in the high-risk group of CHOL patients. PPI network analysis showed PYGB interacts with glycogen metabolism (AGL/PYGL/GYS2/PGM2/UGP2/GBE1) and carbohydrate-digesting (AMY2A/2B/1B, MGAM) enzymes (Figure S2C). PYGB-mediated glycogenolysis generates glucose-1-phosphate (G1P), which enters glycolysis and the TCA cycle to support OXPHOS. Thus, high PYGB in CHOL may fuel mitochondrial respiration by sustaining glycolytic intermediates. Clinically, high PYGB correlated with worse OS (Figure S3C). Furthermore, patients in the high-risk group exhibited significantly shorter OS across all subgroups, with a higher proportion of T3, N1, M1 and TNM stage IV cases (Figure S4A-S4D).

Integrating PPI networks and functional data reveals a mutually reinforcing, three-tier axis governing CHOL malignancy: MAP3K1 (apoptosis) → MRPL18 (mitochondrial translation) → PYGB (energy supply). Specifically, MAP3K1 disables mitochondrial apoptosis checkpoints; MRPL18 expands OXPHOS capacity; and PYGB mobilizes glycogen to sustain fuel demands. This coordinated dysregulation—where apoptosis evasion permits the expansion of high-energy, well-fueled tumor cells—drives core CHOL hallmarks (proliferation, apoptosis resistance, metabolic reprogramming) and transforms descriptive enrichment data into an integrated mechanistic framework for targeted therapies.

In summary, our study is the first to identify and validate the independent prognostic value of these 3 MRGs in CHOL. Further in vitro and in vivo functional experiments are warranted to explore the specific regulatory mechanisms of this gene set on mitochondrial function, and their potential translational value for the treatment and prognostic management of CHOL.

Our findings suggest that MRGs can serve as promising prognostic biomarkers for CHOL, and provide novel insights for the development of personalized treatment strategies for this disease. However, several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, all data used in this study were retrospectively obtained from public databases, and the sample size was relatively limited due to the low incidence of CHOL. Further validation in large-scale, prospective, multi-center clinical cohorts is needed to confirm the robustness and clinical applicability of our prognostic signature. Additionally, the majority of patients included in this study were of Caucasian ethnicity, which may introduce potential ethnic bias. Validation in localized datasets from diverse ethnic populations, especially Asian populations with a high disease burden of CHOL, is required to improve the generalizability of our model. Finally, although we identified the prognostic value of these mitochondrial function-related genes and explored their potential biological functions via bioinformatics analysis, the specific molecular mechanisms by which these genes regulate mitochondrial dynamics, metabolic reprogramming, and CHOL progression remain unclear. Further functional experiments are critical to deepen our understanding of MRG-mediated regulatory networks in CHOL, and to enhance the clinical translational value of our findings.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study developed and validated a novel prognostic risk model for CHOL based on core MRGs. The model can effectively stratify CHOL patients into high- and low-risk groups with significantly different OS outcomes, and serves as an independent prognostic factor for CHOL patients. Furthermore, the signature is closely associated with mitochondrial metabolism, tumor immune microenvironment remodeling, and somatic mutation profiles in CHOL, providing valuable insights into the biological mechanisms underlying CHOL progression. This MRG-based prognostic model has the potential to optimize prognostic risk stratification, guide personalized treatment decision-making, and ultimately improve clinical outcomes for CHOL patients. Further validation in large-scale, multi-ethnic cohorts and in vitro and in vivo functional experiments are warranted to confirm its clinical applicability and translational value.

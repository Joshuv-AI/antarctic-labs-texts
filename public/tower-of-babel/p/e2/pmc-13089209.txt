Determination of standardized mineral availability in different corn sources for broilers and establishment of prediction models
Authors: Xin Du, Wenpeng Chen, Chunyu Cao, Liyang Zhang, Shengchen Wang, Xiaoyan Cui, Tingting Li, Yun Hu, Xugang Luo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo date, few prediction models have been reported for estimating the standardized mineral availability (SMA) in feedstuffs for broilers and other animals. The present study aimed to analyze the chemical composition of 10 different corn sources, and then determine their SMAs for broilers, and establish prediction models for SMAs in corn based on their chemical components. Finally, the accuracies of these models were verified. In total, 352 male Arbor Acres (AA) broilers (average body weight of 1.03 ± 0.01 kg) at 22 d of age were selected and randomly divided by body weight into 11 treatment groups, with 8 replicate cages of 4 broilers per replicate cage for each treatment. On d 26, the chickens were fed either one mineral-free diet or 10 tested corn diets for 4 h. Fecal samples were collected continuously for 52 h to determine their SMAs. The data from nine corn samples were employed with stepwise regressions to build SMAs prediction models. One corn sample was randomly selected to test the accuracies of SMAs prediction models. Different corn sources significantly affected SMAs in broilers (P < 0.05). The standardized availability (SA) value of potassium (K) was the highest (77.2%), whereas that of manganese (Mn) was the lowest (50.7%). Seven prediction models (R2 = 0.711-0.957, P < 0.05) for the SA values of calcium (Ca), phosphorus (P), magnesium (Mg), K, copper (Cu), Mn, and zinc (Zn) in corn for broilers were successfully established based on chemical composition (crude protein, phytic acid, ether extract, acid detergent fiber, crude ash, and crude fiber). Among these, the prediction model for the SA value of P exhibited the highest goodness of fit (R2 = 0.957, P < 0.001), while that of Ca demonstrated the lowest goodness of fit (R2 = 0.711, P = 0.024). Except for the prediction models for SA values of Ca and Mg, which showed relatively low prediction accuracies, all other prediction models for SA values of P, K, Cu, Mn, and Zn displayed good prediction accuracies. The prediction models for the SA values of P, K, Cu, Mn, and Zn in corn for AA broilers as established in the present study demonstrated accurate predicted results, and provided a valuable reference for the rapid prediction of the SA of these minerals in corn for broilers.Keywords: Corn, Chemical composition, Broiler, Standardized mineral availability, Prediction model

Introduction

Accurately evaluating the nutrient utilization efficiency of corn for broilers and establishing prediction models can reduce feed costs (Zhao et al., 2021). However, the chemical composition of different corn sources are affected by various factors, such as variety, soil composition, temperature, and other factors, resulting in wide variations in nutritional quality (Da Silva Timm et al., 2023; Huart et al., 2018; Suleiman et al., 2018). Hence, to accurately evaluate the nutrient utilization efficiency of different corn varieties in broilers, it is necessary to accurately evaluate the nutritional values of corn and establish their prediction models.

Minerals are essential nutrients for broilers, playing a vital role in regulating a wide range of body functions, including metabolism, growth, reproduction, and health (Hu et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024). Currently, in commercial broiler production, minerals, especially trace elements, are often supplemented in excess to meet their nutritional requirements, without considering their contents and bioavailabilities in feed ingredients. This practice not only leads to mineral wastage through manure excretion but also contributes to environmental pollution (Bao et al., 2007; Ewa et al., 2016). Corn is low in minerals. According to the China Feed Database (2024), the average contents of calcium (Ca), phosphorus (P), magnesium (Mg), potassium (K), copper (Cu), iron (Fe), manganese (Mn), and zinc (Zn) in corn are 0.01%, 0.36%, 0.11%, 0.29%, 3.40 mg/kg, 36.0 mg/kg, 5.80 mg/kg, and 21.1 mg/kg, respectively. However, the proportions of corn used in typical corn-soybean meal diets of broilers are the highest at around 55%. The total amounts of P, Mg, K, Cu, Fe, and Zn provided by corn in diets contribute about 25% to 100% of the total dietary requirements of broilers for these minerals, in accordance with China National Feeding Standard of Chicken (NY/T 33–2004) (Ministry of Agriculture of the People’s Republic of China, 2004) and NRC (1994). Therefore, it is worth considering the availabilities of minerals in corn, as the accurate evaluation of mineral availabilities in corn for broilers is very important for accurate mineral supplementation. The estimation of apparent mineral availability (AMA) in diets for broilers has been reported in many studies (Lan et al., 2002; Ravindran et al., 2006; Thomas and Ravindran, 2009). However, this method classifies all minerals in the feces as the undigested component, thus ignoring the endogenous mineral losses (EMLs). Nwokolo et al. (1976) reported a quick and easy method for determining standardized mineral availability (SMA) in feeds by measuring EMLs using mineral-free diets. Many studies have demonstrated that this method can be used to calculate SMAs in the feedstuffs for broilers (Aw-Yong et al., 1983; Chan, 1983; Nwokolo and Bragg, 1980). However, it has also been reported that this method did not allow sufficient time for chyme residue clearance and complete excreta collection, potentially leading to overestimated SMAs in feedstuffs (Nwokolo, 1987). To solve this problem, the authors’ laboratory has successfully established a new rapid balance assay method for assessment of standardized availability (SA) values of phosphorus (P) and other minerals in feedstuffs and diets for Arbor Acres (AA) broilers through a series of experimental studies (Liu et al., 2019, 2012a, 2013,b). Furthermore, the authors used this new method to assess the SA values of P and other minerals in commonly-used feedstuffs and corn-soybean meal diets for AA broilers, and achieved good results (Liu et al., 2019, 2012a, 2013,b). Recently, Yun et al. (2023) evaluated standardized ileal digestibilities (SIDs) of amino acids in eight different sources of wheat for broilers, and successfully established prediction models for the SIDs of amino acids in wheat for broilers based on their chemical composition. Ning et al. (2022) determined the net energy (NE) values in five different sources of wheat for broilers, and derived the best prediction model for the NE value using chemical components of wheat. However, limited information is currently available on constructing prediction models for SMAs in feedstuffs for broilers and other animals. Therefore, the authors hypothesized that prediction models for SMAs in corn for AA broilers could be established using their chemical components. To test the above hypothesis, this study aimed to firstly determine the contents of conventional nutrients, minerals, and anti-nutritional factors in 10 different corn sources, and then evaluate their SMAs in broilers and establish SMAs prediction models based on their chemical components, and finally verify the accuracies of these models.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics statement

The use and care of animals complied with the guidelines of the Animal Use Committee of the Chinese Ministry of Agriculture (Beijing, China). The ethics application was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Department of Animal Science and Technology of Yangzhou University (permit number: SYXK [Su] 2021–0027).

Experimental design, diets, animals, and treatments

A completely randomized design was use in this study. A total of 11 dietary treatments were designed, including one mineral-free diet and 10 tested corn diets. Ten tested corn samples (numbered as A1 to A10) were obtained from a diverse range of suppliers across China and the USA. The original information for the 10 corn samples is shown in Table 1.

A total of 400 one-d-old AA male broilers (Beijing Huadu Broiler Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) were housed in electrically heated, thermostatically controlled stainless steel cages coated with plastic (100 cm × 50 cm × 45 cm) and equipped with fiberglass feeders and drinkers. During d 1 to 21, all broilers were fed ad libitum with the same corn-soybean meal complete pelleted diet purchased from a commercial feed company (Beijing Zhengda Feed Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). At 08:00 on d 22, 352 broilers with similar body weights (averaging 1.03 ± 0.01 kg) were selected and randomly allotted to 1 of 11 treatments with eight replicate cages and four broilers per replicate cage for each treatment. All broilers were sutured at the cloaca with plastic bottle caps, which were hollow and had eight threaded holes around the circumference for easy suturing and fixation (Liu et al., 2019, 2012a, 2013,b). The bottle cap could prevent fecal contamination by feathers, skin, and other debris. After surgery, all broilers were fed the same complete pelleted diet as that fed during the period 1 to 21 d, allowed to recover and acclimatize for 62 h. At 22:00 on d 24, feed was withdrawn, and all broilers were fasted overnight for 8 h. At 06:00 on d 25, all broilers were fed the mineral-free diet for 4 h and then fasted for 24 h to eliminate residues in the gastrointestinal tract. At 10:00 on d 26, fecal collection bags were attached to the bottle caps of all chickens. At the same time, the weighed and recorded mineral-free diet and 10 tested corn diets were fed to the corresponding groups of broilers for 4 h, and then the remaining diets were withdrawn and weighed. The fecal collection began from the start of feeding and lasted for a total of 52 h. All broilers had free access to deionized water and were maintained at a 24-h constant light exposure.

As described previously (Liu et al., 2019), the mineral-free diet was formulated primarily using dextrose, soybean oil, gelatin, and synthetic amino acids to supply energy, protein, or amino acids. Ten tested corn diets were formulated by replacing dextrose, soybean oil, gelatin, etc., with tested corn and contained equal to contents of metabolizable energy (ME), crude protein (CP), and main amino acids to those of the mineral-free diet. These diets were formulated to meet or exceed the nutrient requirements of growing broilers from 3 to 6 weeks of age recommended by the China National Feeding Standard of Chicken (NY/T 33–2004) (Ministry of Agriculture of the People’s Republic of China, 2004), except for minerals. The analyzed chemical composition of 10 tested corn samples used in the present study are shown in Table 2. Sample collection and preparation

Corn samples were collected for analyses of conventional nutritional component, anti-nutritional factors, Ca, P, Mg, K, Cu, Fe, Mn, and Zn contents. Diets were collected for assays of CP, Ca, P, Mg, K, Cu, Fe, Mn, and Zn contents. Excreta samples were collected approximately every 4 h because the excreta-collecting bag attached to the anus was fully filled with excreta within about 4 h for a total of 52 h. Excreta samples of four broilers in each replicate cage were then pooled into one sample and dried in a forced-air oven at 65 °C for assay of Ca, P, Mg, K, Cu, Fe, Mn, or Zn contents. The 4-h feed intake and the weight of 52-h collected dry excreta of broilers are listed in Table 4, and the analyzed results of the above mineral contents in dry excreta are shown in Table 5 in order to calculate AMAs and SMAs. Corn and diets, as well as dried excreta samples, were ground through a 0.45-mm sieve by using a stainless-steel grinding mill (Beijing Yanshan Zhengde Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) to facilitate analyses.

Measurement indicators and methods

The contents of moisture, CP, ether extract (EE), crude fiber (CF), crude ash (CA), neutral detergent fiber (NDF), and acid detergent fiber (ADF) in corn or diets were analyzed according to AOAC (2016) methods 934.01, 976.05, 954.02, 978.10, 942.05, 2002.04, and 973.18, respectively. Phytic acid (PA) content in corn was determined using a commercial kit (catalog number SAP-2-Y, Jiangsu Addison Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Suzhou, Jiangsu, China). The Ca, P, Mg, K, Cu, Fe, Mn, and Zn concentrations in corn, diets, or dried excreta were determined using a 5110 inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectrometry (Agilent Technologies Australia [M] Pty Ltd., Melbourne, Australia) following established methods (Dolar-Szczasny et al., 2019; Hu et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2019; Molina et al., 2019; Zhu et al., 2024).

Significant differences among treatments were assessed with the LSD method. Pearson correlation analysis was used to examine the correlation relationships between chemical composition and SMAs of corn. The data from nine corn samples (A1, A3 to A9) and stepwise regressions were employed to build SMAs prediction models based on chemical composition of corn. The RANDGEN function was used as a subroutine in SAS 9.4, where one corn sample (A2) was randomly selected to test the accuracies of SMAs prediction models. Each replicate cage served as an experimental unit, with significance set at P < 0.05.

Results

Chemical composition in 10 tested corn samples

As shown in Table 2, the determined contents of moisture, CP, EE, CF, NDF, ADF, CA, NFE, and PA ranged from 11.61% to 14.13%, 7.42% to 9.18%, 2.50% to 3.58%, 1.21% to 2.07%, 7.15% to 12.32%, 1.90% to 2.65%, 1.08% to 1.55%, 70.98% to 74.70%, and 8.50 to 11.10 g/kg on air-dry basis, respectively. Among them, the coefficient of variation (CV) of NFE was the lowest, at only 1.70%, while the CV of CF was the highest, at 18.53%. The contents of Ca, P, Mg, and K ranged from 0.004% to 0.009%, 0.156% to 0.231%, 0.096% to 0.117%, and 0.289% to 0.353%, respectively. The CVs of Ca, P, Mg, and K were 32.61%, 12.94%, 6.00%, and 6.81%, respectively. In addition, the contents of Cu, Fe, Mn, and Zn ranged from 0.94 to 3.35 mg/kg, 7.76 to 26.89 mg/kg, 2.40 to 4.99 mg/kg, and 7.62 to 14.72 mg/kg, respectively. The CVs of Cu, Fe, Mn, and Zn were 55.08%, 33.16%, 18.57%, and 22.94%, respectively.

Apparent mineral availabilities of 10 different corn sources in broilers

As shown in Table 6, different corn sources had an effect on apparent availability values of Ca, P, Mg, K, Cu, Fe, Mg, and Zn in broilers (P < 0.05). For macroelements, the apparent availability values of Ca, P, Mg, and K ranged from −198.1% to 29.4%, −31.5% to 22.8%, −16.1% to 38.6%, and −28.4% to 17.3%, respectively. The lowest AMA was observed for Ca (−107.0%), while the highest AMA was observed for Mg (18.3%). The CVs of apparent availability values of Ca, P, Mg, and K were 83.06%, 118.78%, 85.93%, and 106.20%. The lowest CV (83.06%) was observed for the apparent availability value of Ca, while the highest CV (118.78%) was for the apparent availability value of P. Notably, the apparent availability value for P was the highest in corn A10 (22.8%), but the lowest in corn A6 (−31.5%). For microelements, the apparent availability values of Cu, Fe, Mn, and Zn ranged from −6.9% to 34.3%, −49.9% to 38.2%, −63.9% to 7.9%, and 20.0% to 57.9%, respectively. The lowest AMA was observed for Mn (−20.3%), while the highest AMA observed for Zn (46.7%). The CVs of apparent availability values of Cu, Fe, Mn, and Zn were 101.47%, 313.59%, 109.83%, and 24.48%, respectively. Discussion

In the present study, the chemical components and the standardized availability values of Ca, P, Mg, K, Cu, Fe, Mn, and Zn in 10 different corn sources were determined in AA broilers, and the corresponding SMAs prediction models were established with the verification of prediction accuracy. The results demonstrated that the chemical component contents (CP, PA, EE, ADF, and CF) in these corn samples could be utilized to effectively develop prediction models for SMAs in broilers except for Ca, Mg, and Fe, because the standardized availability of Ca and Mg fell outside the determined values ± SD, and the standardized availability of Fe could not be effectively established, which supports the authors’ hypothesis. The above findings have not been reported before and provide scientific basis for quickly predicting SMAs in corn for broilers based on chemical composition and precise mineral supplementation in corn-based broiler diets.

The chemical composition of different corn sources varies due to several factors, such as corn varieties, growing environments, and drying temperatures (Da Silva Timm et al., 2023; Huart et al., 2018; Suleiman et al., 2018). Li et al. (2014) analyzed the chemical composition of 100 corn samples nationwide. The average contents of CP, CA, P, NDF, and ADF in these corn samples were similar to the authors’ findings, but the Ca content was lower in the present study. The reason for this discrepancy may be that the corn sample size in the current study was relatively small, leading to an overall lower average Ca value. In the present study, except for the smaller CVs (below 10%) for moisture, CP, NFE, PA, Mg, and K in 10 corn samples, larger CVs (> 10%) were observed for EE (12.40%), CF (18.53%), NDF (16.00%), ADF (10.46%), CA (11.62%), Ca (32.61%), P (12.94%), Cu (55.08%), Fe (33.61%), Mn (18.57%), and Zn (22.94%). A comparison with existing research data on the variations of corn components across different regions and varieties in China showed that the CVs for most chemical components in the 10 corn samples analyzed in the current study were similar to those reported in previous studies (Hang et al., 2025; Li et al., 2014, 2024; Yang et al., 2020). Therefore, significant variations in the chemical composition across different regions and varieties confirms its representativeness, and thus makes them suitable for the development of precise prediction models.

The AMAs failed to account for EMLs in broilers, leading to potentially inaccurate measurements (Lan et al., 2002; Ravindran et al., 2006; Thomas and Ravindran, 2009). In the present study, the results of AMAs of corn in broilers varied greatly, ranging from −198.1% to 29.4% (Ca), −31.5% to 22.8% (P), −16.1% to 38.6% (Mg), −28.4% to 17.3% (K), and so on. For example, the standardized availability value of Ca of corn A2 was −198.1%, corresponding to a very low Ca content of 0.0064% in corn A2. In contrast, the standardized availability of Ca of corn A10 is 29.4%, which corresponds to a higher Ca content of 0.0220% in corn A10. Therefore, large differences in the mineral contents of corn samples primarily caused large deviations in AMA values. When the mineral contents of corn are very low, the intakes of broilers are very low, and EMLs are relatively high, which caused negative AMA values. Thus, AMA values are greatly affected by EMLs, and this is why SMA values are more accurate than AMA values in reflecting the true availabilities of minerals in feedstuffs because SMA values eliminate the interferences from EMLs. Liu et al. (2019) found that the apparent availability values of Ca and Fe in corn for broilers were −139% and −3.5%, which are close to the results of the authors' experiment. However, the apparent availability values of P, Mn, and Zn in corn for broilers were higher in the present study than the results of Liu et al. (2019). The differences observed might be attributed to variations in broilers rearing conditions and distinct feed batches. Notably, substantial endogenous intestinal secretions in broiler excreta compromised measurement accuracy. If the AMA values are corrected by EMLs, more accurate estimates could be obtained (Dilger and Adeola, 2006). Hence, accurately assessing SMAs values are crucial for achieving precise broiler mineral supplementation in diets, so as to minimize feed costs and mineral emissions. The authors’ present results showed that different corn sources significantly affected the standardized availability values of Ca, P, Mg, K, Cu, Fe, Mn, and Zn in broilers, and the lowest SMA was observed for Mn (50.7%), while the highest SMA was for K (77.2%). The average SMAs of the 10 tested corn samples in the present study were significantly higher than those reported previously by Liu et al., 2019, Liu et al., 2012b. However, in the study of either Liu et al. (2019) or Liu et al. (2012b), only one corn sample was tested. These variations possibly resulted primarily from the differences in the number of tested corn samples, corn sources, and the physiological status of broilers during different trials. Therefore, the application of EMLs calibration methodology effectively mitigated interferences from intestinal endogenous mineral excretions in broilers, thereby enabling more precise assessments of the utilization efficiencies of minerals.

The construction of prediction models for broiler SMAs using the chemical composition of corn represents a pivotal step for quickly predicting SMAs in corn for broilers in order to make precise mineral supplementations in corn-based broiler diets and minimize feed costs and mineral emissions. The authors' results showed that the standardized availability values of Ca, P, Cu, Mn, and Zn exhibited significant positive correlations with CP contents, but the standardized availability values of Ca and P showed significant negative correlations with PA contents. These correlations could be attributed to two factors. Firstly, increased CP contents in diets enhanced feed intake and intestinal digestion and absorption of broilers, thereby improving mineral utilization efficiencies (Fan et al., 2022). Secondly, P in feeds often exists in the form of phytic acid phosphorus (PAP), which is poorly digested and utilized by broilers (Meale et al., 2014; Selle and Ravindran, 2008). The authors' experimental findings revealed that a higher PA content in corn led to lower standardized availability values of Ca and P. Furthermore, in the present study, seven SMAs prediction models in corn for AA broilers were effectively established based on chemical composition (CP, PA, EE, ADF, CA, and CF). Especially, the R2 values for the prediction models of standardized P, Mn, and Zn availabilities exceeded 0.900, indicating strong prediction performances. At present, research on prediction models mainly focuses on available energy and SIDs of amino acids in pigs and poultry, while studies on SMAs prediction models in broilers and other animals has not yet been reported. Li et al. (2014) constructed digestive energy (DE) and ME prediction models of corn in growing pigs based on chemical composition. The results showed that the NDF content in corn was negatively correlated with both DE and ME. Hang et al. (2025) developed amino acid SIDs prediction models of corn in broilers using the contents of 10 conventional nutrients and anti-nutritional factors in corn, and the results showed that CF, NDF, and ADF contents in corn were negatively correlated with amino acid SIDs. Therefore, to the authors’ knowledge, the current study is the first report to establish prediction models of SMAs of feedstuffs in animals. In addition, the above studies have also revealed that anti-nutritional factors in corn could inhibit the digestion and utilization of nutrients, such as energy, amino acids, and minerals, while CP in corn might enhance the digestion and utilization of minerals.

One corn sample (A2) was randomly selected to verify the accuracy of the above constructed SMAs prediction models. Except for the prediction models for the standardized Ca and Mg availabilities in corn, which showed poor prediction accuracy, all other prediction models for the standardized P, K, Cu, Mn, and Zn availabilities demonstrated satisfactory prediction accuracy. However, one single corn sample is not enough, and more corn samples will be required later on to further verify the accuracy of the above prediction models in order to quickly apply them to broiler production.

Conclusions

The prediction models for the standardized availabilities of Ca, P, Mg, K, Cu, Mn, and Zn in corn for AA broilers were effectively established in the present study. All other prediction models except for the prediction models of the Ca and Mg standardized availabilities demonstrated accurate predicted results, and provide a valuable reference for quickly predicting the standardized availabilities of these minerals in corn for broilers based on chemical composition (CP, PA, EE, ADF, and CF). Especially, the prediction models for standardized P, Mn, and Zn availabilities showed strong predictive performance, with R2 values exceeding 0.900.

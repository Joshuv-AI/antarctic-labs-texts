Association of leukocyte telomere length with anti-HBsAg antibody titer in Hepatitis-B recovered patients from the Pakistani population
Authors: Ayub Qamar, Liu Hui, Saeed Khan, Abdullah bin Khalid, Dongmei Hu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study evaluates the leukocyte telomere length and its association with anti-HBsAg antibody titer among the hepatitis-B recovered subjects of the Pakistani population.Materials and methodsThis was a retrospective cross-sectional study. In this study, 6414 subjects were screened for quantitative HBV-DNA through qPCR. Six hundred and sixty-eight subjects had a history of chronic hepatitis-B infection while at present they have no HBV-DNA in their blood samples. These subjects were screened for anti-HBsAg antibody through ELISA technique. Among them, 77 subjects developed anti-HBsAg antibody against HBsAg antigen. Of them, 61 subjects’ leukocyte telomere length was analyzed through qPCR. In addition, normal healthy control subjects’ (n = 18) leukocyte telomere length was also performed.ResultsThe leukocyte Telomere length of chronic Hepatitis-B recovered patients were compared with normal healthy subjects. The mean and standard deviation of the peripheral blood leukocytes’ telomere length of the Ct-values between patients and normal control subjects was 11.34 ± 4.12 and 14.79 ± 1.87, respectively. Similarly, the Ct-values for single copy reference gene were 20.03 ± 1.06 and 20.69 ± 0.611 between the chronic Hepatitis-B recovered patients and control subjects. A weak positive association was noted between anti-HBsAg antibody titer with Target/Single copy reference (T/S) ratio and number of base pairs (r = 0.2). In addition, a decline in anti-HBsAg antibody titer was observed with respect to the age of the subjects. There was no significant difference in number of base pairs between the male and female recovered patients. A significant difference between HBV recovered patients and normal control subjects was observed between the T/S ratio and number of base pairs (< 0.0001).ConclusionA marked difference in the T/S ratio and number of base pairs was observed between the hepatitis-B recovered patients and normal control subjects. In chronic hepatitis-B recovered patients, the number of base pairs on each chromosome end were significantly higher as compared with normal control subjects. A weak positive correlation was noted between the T/S ratio and number of base pairs with anti-HBsAg antibody titer. This study shows that hepatitis-B recovered patients have maintained their higher telomere length as compared with the normal control subjects.

Introduction

Telomeres are the tandem repeats (TTAGGGn) at the chromosomal ends [1, 2]. These chromosomal ends are not completely replicated by conventional deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) polymerases. Therefore, a loss of small portions of telomeric DNA occurs with each cell division. The average leukocyte telomere length (LTL) at birth is approximately 9.5 kb while an approximately 2 kb and 3.5 kb reduction was noted by the 3rd and 9th decades of life [3–5]. Females typically have a longer LTL than males [4]. The African Americans have longer LTL as compared to Americans of European ancestry (~ 0.2 kb) while have shorter LTL than sub-Saharan Africans (~ 0.3 kb) [6, 7]. An approximately 0.05–0.1 kb telomere reduction was reported in vitro cultured cells [8, 9]– [10].

The completely differentiated blood leukocytes are produced from hematopoietic stem cells (HSC) through various mitotic divisions. This proliferation toward cell maturation results in shortening of telomere length (TL) [11]. Hence, the hematopoiesis leads production of shorter TL in the most mature cells with increased number of counts. On the other hand, the HSC has maintained its higher TL due to a decrease in number of replication cycles and are few in numbers. Thus, maintain blood cells’ homeostasis in both young and older persons with a longer and shorter LTL [12].

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) lose approximately 50 bp of telomeric DNA per year. Naive B cells develop germinal center B cells. The germinal center B cells express higher levels of telomerase activity and have longer TL than Naive B cells [13, 14]. The average telomere length for naive CD4 + and CD8 + T cells is about 2.5 kb longer than effector or memory T cells [15]. The TL is influenced by both genetic and environmental factors. An association of several genetic diseases with telomere length have been noted [16–20]. The heritability in the offspring range from 34% − 82% [21–23].

A rapid immune response initiated in the infected host during acute viral infections against viral pathogen [24]. This immune response cause either viral clearance, transition to a chronic infection or even host death. The persistent chronic viral condition in the host results in antigenic stimulation for several months to decades. This loss in TL increase during chronic viral infection [25]. During persistent viral infections, various levels of antigenic exposure lead to constant stress on the immune system activation [26]. The antigen interacting cells die after antigen clearance to maintain effectors cells homeostasis [27]. However, the memory cells have the ability to escape from apoptosis. These memory cells have also the ability of replicative potential in response to secondary exposure throughout the host’s life. Normally, memory T cells persist due to interleukins (IL-7 and IL-15). The exhausted T-cell (i.e., antigen-specific T cells gradually loss their function) require the presence of continuous viral antigen stimuli to continue proliferation [28, 29]. Depending on the length of infection, this could result in progressively shorter telomeres and an age-related decline in T-cell responses. Researches have also reported that the telomerase activity is upregulate in antigen-specific T and B cells at the initial stage of acute infection and preserve for subsequent stimulus [30, 31]. The telomerase activity is not maintained after acute viral infection is resolved [32]. Thus antigen-specific T cells telomere defects are initiated and results in senescence and death.

In recent years, researchers have paid attention to telomere length measurement and investigated the association/risk with various kinds of diseases, including cancer [33–36]. Among hepatitis-B patients, 60–90% had chronic infection. This suggests that persistent hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection leads to adverse effects on telomere lengths. The TL shortening was observed in T cells population in HBV patients. The T-cell exhaustion due to telomere shortening was reported by various researchers. The decreased telomerase activity in the peripheral blood lymphocytes of HBV patients may contribute to immunosuppression [37, 38]. A study on hepatitis-C virus (HCV) or co-infection with human immunodeficiency virus HIV subjects showed TL differences as compared with non-HCV-infected individuals [39].

Pakistani subjects (hepatitis-B recovered patients) were not studied for their immune response with respect to anti-hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) antibody development. Similarly, the leukocyte telomere length in peripheral blood leukocytes (PBLs) was not evaluated among these individuals. In addition, LTL and anti-HBsAg antibody titer association were not analyzed. This study will provide insight into immune response, and leukocyte telomere length among hepatitis-B recovered patients and its weak association with the anti-HBsAg antibody titer which reveal the personalized effectiveness of immune response among the Pakistani population.

Materials and methods

This study was a collaborative project between Dalian Medical University, China and DOW University of Health Sciences, Karachi, Pakistan. This study was carried out at DOW university of health sciences Karachi, Pakistan, from September 2023 to October 2024. The study was approved by the Institutional Review board (IRB) of DOW University of Health Sciences, Karachi, Pakistan (Ref: IRB-3037/DUHS/Approval/2023/348). This study was designed as a retrospective cross-sectional study. Upon obtaining consent, their clinical history was recorded, and blood sample was obtained. This research was carried out according to the Declaration of Helsinki. Among the screened subjects (n=6414), 61 were enrolled in this study. Patients had co-infection with Hepatitis-C and/or Hepatitis-D, or other chronic infection like tuberculosis (TB) and < 18 years of age were excluded. In addition, Patients with history of comorbidities like systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) and rheumatoid arthritis (RA) were excluded from the study on the bases of history prior to enrolled in this study. Similarly, alcohol abuse was also excluded while patients with smoking were not excluded from this study. Normal healthy control subjects with same age range and gender ratio were also evaluated for relative telomere length. These normal control subjects were screened for Hepatitis-B, -C, -D viral DNA/RNA and were negative for the aforementioned viral infections. Furthermore, control subjects with history of SLE, RA and alcohol abuse were also excluded.

This study was carried out in the following phases:

Viral DNA/RNA quantitative PCR analysis for Hepatitis-B, -C, and -D

Subjects visited the DOW University hospital and/or collection centers for HBV-DNA screening were examined for quantitative hepatitis-B DNA through an Abbott Alinity automatic machine utilizing an HBV m AMP kit (Abbott). Similarly, subjects had negative HBV-DNA qPCR and with having history of HBV infection were evaluated for hepatitis-D (RealStar® HDV RT-qPCR Kit, Altina Diagnostics, Hamburg, Germany) and hepatitis-C (Alinity m HCV assay kit, Abbott) qPCR analysis through Rotor-Gene Qiagen’s real-time PCR system to rule out subjects with co-infection.

Anti-HBsAg antibody screening through ELISA method

Subjects who had a history of HBV infection and have no co-infection were selected for anti-HBsAg antibody titer analysis. Three samples (Positive control, Negative control, and Patient serum) each 50 µL were added to wells of HBsAg coated plate. Enzyme conjugate (50 µL) was added to each of these wells and incubated at 37 ˚C for 1 h. The plate was washed five times after incubation. Substrate-A and -B (50 µL each) were added to each well and was incubated at 37 ˚C for 15 min. At post-incubation, 50 µL stop solution was added to each well and was read on dual-band 450 nm/ 630 nm, ELISA reader machine SFRI IRE 96 Saint Jean d’Illac– France. The anti-HBsAg antibody ELISA kits were provided by Beier, Bioengineering, Co, Ltd, China.

DNA extraction from peripheral blood

DNA was extracted from the peripheral blood sample through OMEGA Bio-tik Blood DNA extraction kit, USA. DNA concentration and purity was analyzed by a Nanodrop analyzer, USA.

Peripheral blood leukocyte telomere length quantification through qPCR

The peripheral blood leukocyte telomere length of genomic DNA was determined through commercially available PCR kit (ScienCells Research Laboratories inc., San Diego CA Catalog no. #8908). In this method, two sets of primers were used i.e., telomere specific and single-copy reference (SCR) gene. The earlier primer recognizes, amplify and quantify telomeric sequences at chromosomal ends. The later primer recognizes, amplify, and quantify 100 bp region on human chromosome 17. The SCR gene amplification was used as reference gene for calculating the telomere length of the target gene. The amplification process was performed in a single run for both the telomeric sequences and the SCR. The PCR reactions were performed in a final volume of 20 µL (include: 2 µL each telomere sequence or SCR primers, 10 µL 2X GoldNStart TaqGreen qPCR Master Mix, and 7 µL nuclease-free H2O). The PCR steps and condition were as: initial denaturation (95 °C for 10 min), denaturation (95 °C for the 20 s), annealing (52 °C for 20 s), and extension (72 °C for 45s). The number of cycles was thirty-two. Target/Single copy reference (T/S) ratio and number of base pairs were calculated according to Cawthon’s PCR-based method [40]. Normal control subjects LTL were analyzed as control.

Statistics

Data was analyzed through SPSS IBM v.17 and Python programming language (VS code environment). The mean and standard deviation (SD) for both control and CHB recovered subjects were analyzed. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Welch’s two sample t-test was applied for potential heterogeneity of variance between the two groups for T/S ratio and base pairs inferential statistical analysis. The association was evaluated through Pearson correlation between T/S ratio, number of base pairs and anti-HBsAg antibody titer. A regression line was applied to parameters; anti-HBsAg antibody titer and age, T/S ratio, number of base pairs.

Results

In this study a total of 6414 subjects were enrolled. Among them 668 subjects have no HBV-DNA in their blood while had history of HBV infection. Of these 668 subjects, 77 have developed anti-HBsAg antibodies against HBsAg antigen. Sixty-one patients and 18 normal control subjects were analyzed for leukocyte telomere length. The mean age of the HBV recovered patients were 40 ± 10 years while control subjects age were 37 ± 13 years (Table 1). The mean LTL Ct- values for HBV recovered and control patients were 11.34 ± 4.12 and 14.78 ± 1.87, respectively. The SCR Ct-values were 20.03 ± 1.06 and 20.69 ± 0.61, respectively as shown in Table-1. In addition, the mean ± SD for T/S ratio for HBV recovered patients vs. control subjects were 1231.9 ± 92.41 and 92.41 ± 78.17 as shown in Table 2. A significant difference was observed in T/S ratio between HBV recovered and control subjects (p = < 0.0001). Similarly, the number of base pair in the telomere region was significantly different between the two groups (p = < 0.0001) while the mean ± SD of HBV recovered, and control subjects were shown in Table 2. There was a weak association between anti-HBsAg antibody titer and telomere length, number of base pairs (r = 0.2). Female have higher anti-HBsAg antibody titer as compared with male (Fig. 1). Figure 2 provide the anti-HBsAg antibody titer in male and female at various age (years) intervals. It seems that female in age interval of 21–30 have higher antibody titer as compared to male. In age groups 41–50 and 51–60, the male has higher antibody titer as compared with females of the same age group. A mild decrease was noted in T/S ratio and number of base pairs with aging of these subjects (Fig. 3, and Fig. 4). A positive increasing trend was observed with T/S ratio, number of base pairs with anti-HBsAg antibody titer i.e., with the increasing of either T/S ratio or number of base pairs the titer of antibody was increased as shown in Fig. 5. An inverse correlation was seen in the antibody titer with age (Fig. 5).

Discussion

The telomere length might regulate gene expression [41–44]. The shortening of telomeres is observed in the aged people [45, 46]. This is consistent with our study as shown in Figs. 3 and 4. There was a decrease in both T/S ratio and in number of base pairs with aging. A telomere loss results in a decrease in telomere’s length in cells and leads to decrease and/or stop cell division and finally cause cell’s apoptosis [47]. The effective immune response depends on the effector cells proliferation. A decrease in telomerase activity was observed in various genetic disorders. This results in shortening of the TL which cause a decrease in hematopoietic stem cells replication [48–51]. Similarly, chronic inflammation and oxidative damage are also associated with telomere shortening [52, 53]. When telomere’s length reaches its critical limit, it leads to the Rb and/or P53 pathway activation. Hence, initiate cellular pathways of senescence and apoptosis [54, 55].

The magnitude of telomere shortening varies in various viral infections. Among them cytomegalovirus (CMV) infection has the highest rank [56]. This was observed that even with low to undetectable telomerase activity, T cells retained their proliferative potential in HIV + twins. Viral disease individuals such as CMV or Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV) have more differentiated T cells in their blood; these T cells have shorter telomeres compared with the healthy controls [57, 58]. A decrease in the specific immune responses against vaccine or pathogens due to telomeres shortening in antigen-specific lymphocytes would be one of the earliest signs of overall immune functional decline during chronic infections or aging [58].

The telomere length was significantly longer in highly proliferative effectors cells as compared with the decreased replication potential cells. A study reported that subjects with longer TL in B cells had higher antibody titer against the influenza vaccine [59]. The study reported that TL is positively associated with B- and T-cell responses to the influenza vaccine/antigen in older subjects. The research studies also suggest the association of TL and age-associated decline in adaptive immune functions [60–62].

Andreu et al. (2022) observed that the telomere lengths in males were shorter than females [63]. This study is inconsistent with our results. There were no significant differences in male and female number of base pairs mean ± SD, 2,389,917 ± 3,083,341 and 2,349,377 ± 2,881,580, respectively.

The hallmark of this study is that a higher number of base pairs was noted among the chronic hepatitis-B patients that have the ability to develop the anti-HBsAg antibody against HBsAg viral antigen. This study is consistent with Qiag Ma (2016). They have reported that chronic Hepatis-B patients have higher T/S ratio as compared with the normal subjects while not reporting the antibody status of those patients [64].

Somatic cells, including naïve, effector, and memory cells, have a defined proliferative capacity. This is due to the inability of DNA polymerase to replicate the distal ends of chromosomes. Pluripotent stem cells overcome this problem by up-regulating telomerase [65]. The proliferation of cell in the decreased or absence of telomerase level results in progressive telomere shortening. Studies have reported that critically shortened telomere length causes increase chromosomal instability, degradation, and fusion [66, 67]. This dysfunctional telomere-induced foci (TIF) leads to a DNA damage response (DDR) and stimulates the senescence program via activation of the ATM Serine/Threonine Kinase (ATM)/p53-dependent pathway [68].

As discussed earlier that the T cells (i.e., CD8+, CD4+, naïve, and memory) could reactivate telomerase activity via mitogenic stimulation [60, 69, 70]. These cells initially have nearly undetectable levels of telomerase. Upon acute viral infection and exposure to viral antigens, the telomerase is reactivated while a similar response occurs in the secondary stimulation. The consecutive stimulation leads to a decrease in immune response and cause apoptosis activation [71]. In vitro studies have shown that repeated antigenic exposures cause a resistance in CD95 and activation-induced cell death [72]. The CD95 impairment is caused by a loss in Phosphatase and Tensin Homolog (PTEN) and increased PI3K/AKT signaling pathway. Similarly, long-term antigenic exposure also showed increased in apoptotic-inhibitor factor expression of Bcl-2-Like Protein-1 (Bcl-xl) and vice versa for pro-apoptotic proteins Bad and Bax. In addition, the less differentiated cell (CD27+) would have increased the possibility of apoptosis as compared with the late differentiated cells (CD27−). The later leads to replicative senescence. The exhausted T cell are progressively losses its antiviral functions. Thus, the exhausted T cells express a highly expressed inhibitory marker i.e., Programmed Death receptor (PD-1). This is a member of the CD28 co-stimulatory family [63].

The hepatitis-B recovered patients who produced anti-HBsAg antibody were maintained their higher telomere length in their peripheral blood leukocytes as compared with the normal control subjects. These patients even had a history of HBV maintaining their T/S ratio and number of base pairs at the chromosomal ends. This increased leukocyte telomere length might help them to produce anti-HBsAg antibody against HBsAg antigen of the hepatitis-B virus. In conclusion, these subjects’ immune response has resolved the hepatitis-B viral infection. The limitation of this study was small sample size and an imbalance of samples ratio between chronic hepatitis B recovered patients and control subjects. A large-scale prospective study is recommended for chronic hepatitis B patients (those who developed anti-HBsAg antibody and vice versa) and normal control subjects, to evaluate not only the telomere length but also its regulating genes as well. In addition, re-occurrence of viral infection and hepatocellular carcinoma.

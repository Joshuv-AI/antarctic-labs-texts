Air-dried high-strength black MXene aerogel for spatiotemporal infrared information management
Authors: Chunxiao Wu, Huhu Cheng, Qihua Liao, Chuanxin Weng, Bing Lu, Tianlei Guang, Yan Li, Liangti Qu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractGreat efforts have been made to control the energy interaction between objects and environments by the effective regulation of infrared (IR) radiation of materials with low-thermal-conductivity aerogels or electrical-/thermal- triggered functional films, which would bring heavy burden associated with system complexity for objects in dynamical environments. Herein, a bone-like lightweight and high-strength black Ti3C2Tx aerogel is developed, which demonstrates an easily mechanically-regulated IR radiation management capacity for high-temperature objects with backgrounds of dramatic temperatures fluctuations. An air-drying strategy allows inner wrinkled and porous structure of this lightweight (60 mg cm−3) Ti3C2TX aerogel with the record 159.9 MPa g−1 cm3 specific compressive modulus and 1.6 MPa g−1 cm3 specific compressive stress. IR emissivity of Ti3C2TX aerogel can be modulated widely from 0.17 to 0.98 by surface microstructure construction for IR letters or numbers information transmission, although which appear to be indistinguishable black to naked eyes.Subject terms: Structural materials, Materials for optics

Introduction

Promising infrared (IR) radiation regulation is able to control the energy interaction between objects and environments, which will greatly benefit high-efficient energy conversion equipment1–6, advanced electronics7–9 or human body thermal management10–16, invisible information identification/encryption17–20 and so on. Many efforts have been made to employ low-thermal-conductivity aerogels21–25 or utilize electrical-26–28 or thermal-29–32 triggered films to control the object surface temperature or emissivity, for the discrimination or integration of the IR radiation emitted by objects and that of environments based on the Stefan-Boltzmann law33,34. However, low-thermal-conductivity aerogels only have thermal insulation capacity, while the integration with electric or thermal triggered functional films would bring external and heavy energy burden associated with unsatisfied durability and system complexity. Therefore, it is still very difficult to achieve wide and manageable IR radiation regulation in the easy-to-operate and long-term-stability way based on lightweight aerogels in dynamical environments up to now.

MXene has distinctive apparent black color and low IR emissivity34,35, which demonstrates remarkable IR management capabilities36–42. For instance, dense MXene films are always employed to cover another component to achieve the low surface IR temperetures43. Porose MXene mainly play the role of thermal insulation layer in composite aerogels44. However, the integration of MXene with other components would lead to the performance degradation and load increase extremely. It is still a big challege to assembling MXene nanosheets into the desired configuration for multi-spatiotemporal IR management. In this work, a bone-like lightweight and high-strength cubic black Ti3C2Tx aerogel is developed, which demonstrates an easily mechanically-regulated IR radiation management capacity for high-temperature objects in drastically fluctuating environments. This Ti3C2TX aerogel exhibits bone-like porous structure inside and the layered structure outside. An air-drying strategy allows the wrinkled walls of inner porous structure, which enables this Ti3C2Tx aerogel to have the record 159.9 MPa g−1 cm3 specific modulus and 1.6 MPa g−1 cm3 compressive stress among the pure MXene aerogels. Meanwhile, IR emissivity of Ti3C2TX aerogel can be modulated widely from 0.17 to 0.98 by direct laser writing microstructure construction on the outer surface. Based on this, IR letters or numbers information transmission or anti-counterfeiting can be realized by presetting different surfaces of different Ti3C2TX aerogels, which appear to be indistinguishable black to naked eyes. In addition, when covered with lightweight Ti3C2TX aerogels, high-temperature objects like cars exhibit excellent dynamic IR fusion capacity by simply mechanically turning the desired surface of this Ti3C2TX aerogel to observers in dynamic natural conditions of temperature ranging from 20 °C to 50 °C, which is very difficult to achieve by other aerogels or functional materials, demonstrating excellent multi-spatiotemporal IR information managements.

Results

Morphology and composition analysis of cubic Ti3C2TX aerogel

For the fabrication of this cubic Ti3C2TX aerogel, Ti3C2TX nanosheets dispersion is first obtained based on the LiF/HCl etching methods from Ti3AlC245–47. Then, Ti3C2TX dispersion is placed in a 1 × 1 × 1 cm3 cubic mold and then freeze for 12 h. In this process, Ti3C2TX nanosheets assemble around the formed ice crystals into porous structure, while Ti3C2TX nanosheets at the interface between the dispersion and the mold will be squeezed by ice crystals, which will be arranged into the layered structure to form the smooth surface (Fig. 1a–e, Supplementary Fig. 1). Finally, the cubic Ti3C2TX aerogel is successfully obtained (Fig. 1a) after air drying for 12 h, which can stand firmly on a slender grass blade (Fig. 1e). Transmission electron microscope (TEM) image and the corresponding selected area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern (Supplementary Fig. 2) show the employed Ti3C2TX nanosheets have basal planes’ hexagonal structure48. Atomic Force Microscope (AFM) result (Supplementary Fig. 3) shows the height of Ti3C2Tx nanosheet is ~1.7 nm, indicating the typically single-layered structure49–51. Scanning electron microscope (SEM) images in Fig. 1b–d reveal there is porous structure with three-dimensional Ti3C2TX nanosheets assembled networks inside Ti3C2TX aerogel, while the outside flat surface is formed by layered Ti3C2TX nanosheets (Fig. 1b). X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern exhibits the (002) plane at 6.8°, which indicate the existence of the Ti3C2TX characteristic peak (Fig. 1f)45. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) spectra of the Ti3C2TX nanosheets is provided (Fig. 1g, h and Supplementary Fig. 4), which are in good agreement with that in the previous report52–54. All these results demonstrate the successful preparation of this bone-like Ti3C2TX aerogel with inner porous structure and smooth surface. Besides, Ti3C2Tx aerogels with different shapes can be prepared (Supplementary Fig. 5), as well as large-sized samples (Supplementary Fig. 6) bsed on this one-piece molding and air-drying process.

Mechanical properties of the Ti3C2TX aerogel

The solid content of Ti3C2TX aerogel is regulated for the moderation of mechanical properties (Fig. 2). As the solid content regulated from 40, 50, 60, 70 to 80 mg cm−3, the mechanical performance is achieved for specific modulus of 48.5, 139.3, 159.9, 40.1, 26.9 MPa g−1 cm3 (Fig. 2c) and specific compressive stress of 0.8, 1.5, 1.6, 1.0, 0.6 MPa g−1 cm3 (Fig. 2d), respectively. The optimized air-drying Ti3C2TX aerogel possesses a density of 60 mg cm−3, specific modulus of 159.9 MPa g−1 cm3 and compressive stress of 1.6 MPa g−1 cm3 (Fig. 2b), beyond most of the pure MXene aerogels55,56. As a result, this lightweight and high-strength Ti3C2TX aerogel is able to support a 100,000 mg weight over 1600 times than that (60 mg) of Ti3C2TX aerogel (Fig. 2a). In comparison, the Ti3C2TX aerogel prepared by freeze-drying has specific modulus of 30.1 MPa g−1 cm3 and compressive stress of 0.2 MPa g−1 cm3 (Fig. 2e and Supplementary Fig. 7), which are 5.31 and 8 times lower than the Ti3C2TX aerogel prepared by air-drying at the same density (60 mg cm−3), respectively. SEM images of freeze-drying Ti3C2TX aerogel show similar three-dimensional structures (Fig. 2f, g) with air-drying Ti3C2TX aerogel. However, the freeze-drying Ti3C2TX aerogel has smoother walls at three-dimensional networks (Fig. 2f) than that in air-drying samples with wrinkle Ti3C2TX walls (Fig. 2g). The completely different internal structures in these samples indicate air-drying process induces the wrinkled structures, which could be important for the mechanical enhancement57–59. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 8, when the Ti3C2TX aerogel was first air-drying for 4 h and then freeze-drying for 8 h, this sample had the significant increased wrinkles structure compared with the totally freeze-drying sample. And the specific modulus of 54.3 MPa g−1 cm3 and compressive stress of 0.4 MPa g−1 cm3 are higher than that of the totally freeze-drying sample (Supplementary Fig. 9). When the Ti3C2TX aerogel was first air-drying for 8 h and then freeze-drying for 4 h, wrinkles structure at inner three-dimensional Ti3C2TX networks is further increased (Supplementary Fig. 8) and the mechanical performance is further enhanced with the specific modulus of 101.8 MPa g−1 cm3 and compressive stress of 0.6 MPa g−1 cm3 (Supplementary Fig. 9). When the Ti3C2TX aerogel was completely air-drying for 12 h, whether the wrinkles structure or the mechanical properties are the highest among all the samples (Fig. 2e–g, Supplementary Figs. 8 and 9). These results further confirm that air-drying treatment improves the mechanical properties of this lightweight Ti3C2TX aerogel well in this study.

IR radiation regulation

In addition, this bone-like lightweight Ti3C2TX aerogel has a low IR emissivity (~0.17) according to IR reference method (Fig. 3a)35,60,61 and the surface IR temperature is only ~25.1 °C when putting this Ti3C2TX aerogel on a heat source with the temperature as high as 110 °C (Fig. 3b). In comparison, the surface IR temperature of Ti3C2TX film with layered structure is about ~47.7 °C (Fig. 3b, Supplementary Fig. 10a) that is much higher than that (~25.1 °C) of the bone-like Ti3C2TX aerogel, although the layered Ti3C2TX film has the low IR emissivity (~0.28) at 110 °C based on the IR reference method. Based on the Fourier Transform IR (FTIR) spectrometer34,35,62–64, the IR emissivity results (Supplementary Fig. 11) have the similar trend. To enable unified comparison, the emissivity of samples in subsequent measurements will mainly use the IR reference method unless specifically mentioned. Meanwhile, the surface IR temperature of Ti3C2TX aerogel without layered structure outside is about 38.1 °C (Fig. 3b, Supplementary Fig. 10b) that is also higher than that of the bone-like Ti3C2TX aerogel. Cross-sectional IR temperature of Ti3C2TX aerogel without layered structure outside shows the gradient IR temperature distribution from the bottom side (contacting with 110 °C heat source) to the top side (Supplementary Fig. 12), demonstrating the porous structure could play a role in thermal insulation. Indeed, the thermal conductivity of Ti3C2TX aerogel without layered structure is as low as ~0.088 W/mK measured by hot disk method65,66. Based on these results, low IR temperature of our bone-like Ti3C2TX aerogel on hot source could benefit from the well combination of the excellent thermal insulation of porous structure inside and the low IR emissivity of layered structure outside. After repeatly puting the sample on the 110 °C hot source and then moving it to the table at room temperature for cooling, the surface IR temperature of bone-like Ti3C2Tx aerogel remains rarely changed (Supplementary Fig. 13), indicating the favorable stability.

In addition, microstructures construction on the Ti3C2TX aerogel surface is conducted to achieve IR emissivity modulation via the direct laser writing technology. As shown in Fig. 3c–e, there are many macro stripes at Ti3C2TX aerogel surface after direct laser writing. SEM images demonstrate the width of stripes can be moderated from ~7.4 μm to ~11.1 μm with the increment of laser power from 0.25 W to 2.25 W, and there are many nanoparticles in the stripes (Fig. 3e and Supplementary Fig. 14). When the laser power increases to be 2.5 W, the Ti3C2TX aerogel surface is completely transformed into nanoparticles and exhibits the largest emissivity of 0.98 (Fig. 3f and Supplementary Fig. 14, Supplementary Fig. 15). XRD analysis demonstrates the characteristic peaks of TiO2 (Supplementary Fig. 16)50, indicating the conversion of Ti3C2Tx to TiO2 in these macro stripes after direct laser writing. Based on the chemical and structure transformation at direct laser writing macro stripes, IR emissivity of Ti3C2TX aerogel can be modulated widely. For example, with macro stripes of ~9 μm width, surface IR temperature of Ti3C2TX aerogel is about 31 °C (Supplementary Fig. 14 and Supplementary Fig. 15) and IR emissivity is about 0.32 (Fig. 3f) when the heat source is ~80 °C. When the macro stripe is ~10 μm width, surface IR temperature of Ti3C2TX aerogel is about 38.2 °C and IR emissivity is about 0.7 (Fig. 3f and Supplementary Fig. 14, Supplementary Fig. 15). As a result, IR emissivity of Ti3C2TX aerogel can be regulated from 0.17 to 0.98 and the corresponding IR temperature can be regulated from 21 °C to 46.1 °C (Fig. 3f). With preset IR emissivity surfaces upward of 3 × 3 array Ti3C2TX aerogels (Fig. 3g), IR information of “T”, “H”, “U” and so on are realized (Fig. 3h–j and Supplementary Fig. 17) although they are all visible black, realizing powerful and editable IR information management with visible light encryption capacity of these Ti3C2TX aerogels.

The application demonstration

With environments of dramatic temperature fluctuations (20–50 °C) (Fig. 4a), it is very difficult to achieve IR information management of high-temperature objects. Based on wonderful IR regulation capacity, six surfaces of this cubic Ti3C2TX aerogel can be designed into different IR emissivity, which will demonstrate the desired IR temperature when making the corresponding surface to observers. As can be seen, an 80 °C object is covered with this Ti3C2TX aerogel, which has a very low IR temperature of ~21 °C at room temperature when turning ~0.17 IR emissivity surface of this cubic Ti3C2TX aerogel to observers (Supplementary Figs. 18, 19 and Fig. 4a). When putting this 80 °C object in the 30 °C environment, which will show the corresponding IR temperature of ~33.9 °C by turning ~0.43 IR emissivity surface of the covered Ti3C2TX aerogel to observers, which is very close to the environment IR temperature (30 °C). Even in 50 °C environment, the IR temperature of this 80 °C object show only ~53.4 °C by turning ~0.98 IR emissivity surface of the covered Ti3C2TX aerogel to observers. When a 110 °C object is covered with this Ti3C2TX aerogel, the IR temperatures that are very close to the environmental temperatures (20 °C, 30 °C, 40 °C, 50 °C) are also presented when turning the designed IR emissivity surface of this cubic Ti3C2TX aerogel to observers (Supplementary Fig. 20 and Fig. 4a). Further, these lightweight Ti3C2TX aerogels can be directly embedded into the high-temperature parts of vehicles with a simple rotating device for advanced ultralight IR armor (Fig. 4b). As shown in Fig. 4c, the high-temperature part of a car model has a very low temperature of ~21 °C that matching with the background (20 °C) simulating the mild grassland well when integrated with Ti3C2TX aerogels. In comparison, a high temperature (~80 °C) can be directly observed in IR images. When the background temperature rises to 40 °C simulating the scorching desert, the high-temperature part of this car also achieves a good IR fusion with the background (40 °C) by turning ~0.76 IR emissivity surface of these Ti3C2TX aerogels outward (Fig. 4d and Supplementary Movie 1), which showcases the robust dynamic IR radiation regulation ability of Ti3C2TX aerogels for high-temperature objects in scenarios across a broad temperature spectrum.

Discussion

In summary, a bone-like lightweight and high-strength Ti3C2TX aerogel with multi-spatiotemporal IR information management has been developed. This air-drying MXene aerogel exhibits porous structure with low-thermal-conductivity inside and layered structure with regulated surface IR emissivity outside, which also enables excellent mechanical properties of this Ti3C2Tx aerogel with the specific modulus of 159.9 MPa g−1 cm3 and compressive stress of 1.6 MPa g−1 cm3, beyond most of pure MXene aerogels. Importantly, the surface IR emissivity can be modulated widely from 0.17 to 0.98 via direct laser writing, feathering this cubic Ti3C2TX aerogel can be direct embedded into objects for robustly dynamic IR radiation moderation in natural environments with dramatic temperature fluctuations. Meanwhile, powerful and editable IR information transmission with visible light encryption capacity is realized by designed Ti3C2TX aerogels patterns, which will greatly empower multi-spatiotemporal IR information or energy interaction between advanced equipment and environments in the future.

Methods

Materials

Titanium Aluminum Carbide (Ti3AlC2, 400 mesh) was purchased from 11 Technology Co, Ltd. The lithium fluoride (LiF, 99.9%) was purchased from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Co., Ltd. The hydrochloric acid (HCl, 12 M HCl) was purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd.

Preparation of single-layered Ti3C2Tx nanosheets

Ti3C2TX nanosheets was prepared as follows. First, the 120 mL 9 M HCl was prepared with the 90 mL 12 M HCl and 30 mL H2O. Second, 9.6 g LiF was put into the 120 mL 9 M HCl for stirring 30 min. Third, 6 g Ti3AlC2 was slowly added to the above mixture in the course of 15 min, and kept at 40 °C for 36 h under stirring. Then, the mixture was washed with deionized water by centrifuging (1917 g centrifugal force, 5 min for each cycle) until the pH > 6. The mixture was shaken for 30 min and sonication for 30 min. Finally, the single-layered Ti3C2TX nanosheets was obtained in centrifugal tube by centrifuging (1917 g centrifugal force, 40 min).

Preparation of bone-like Ti3C2Tx aerogel

Ti3C2TX nanosheets dispersion (40 mg mL−1, 50 mg mL−1, 60 mg mL−1, 70 mg mL−1, 80 mg mL−1) was placed in a 1 × 1 × 1 cm3 PTFE mold with smooth inner wall and then was placed in the refrigerator (−20 °C) for freezing 12 h. The freezing Ti3C2TX hydrogel was put into 5 mol L−1 HCl for ion exchange between Li+ and H+ 55. And then it was washed several times until pH > 6 and put into drying oven (60 °C) for 12 h. Finally, the bone-like Ti3C2TX aerogel was obtained.

Characterizations

Scanning electron micrographs (SEM) were performed by using a Field Emission Zeiss GeminiSEM 300 (Zeiss, Germany). Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images were taken by the JEM-2100F High Resolution Field Emission Transmission Electron Microscope (HRTEM) (JEOL, Japan). X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were collected by the D8 Advance X-ray diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation (Bruker, Germany). Atomic Force Microscope (AFM) image was measured by the Bruker Dimension Icon atomic force microscope (Bruker, Germany). The mechanical measurements were carried out using the Instron 3342 universal testing machine (Instron, USA). Laser writing microstructure construction was conducted by LSU3EA Semiconductor UV End-pumped Laser Marking Machine (GTECH, China). The IR photos were taken by the Fluke TiX640 Thermal Imaging camera, 640 × 480, (FLUKE, USA). Optical microscope images were taken by Optical microscope HAL 100 (Zeiss, Germany). The reflection spectra in the IR range were measured by Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR) with an integrating sphere attachment (Bruker VERTEX80V, Germany). Thermal conductivity was measured by the TPS3500 Hot Disk (Hot Disk, Sweden).

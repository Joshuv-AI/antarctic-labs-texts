Establishment and characterization of a novel human ampullary carcinoma cell line derived from a Chinese patient
Authors: Yuanhui Su, Wei Huang, Xin Miao, Caiming Wang, Yanlong Zhang, Cheng Yu, Yaru Liu, Changpeng Chai, Huan Tang, Weixiong Zhu, Qingqing Su, Lu Li, Xuechao Xu, Fa Jin, Zhao Hu, Luyang Chen, Qiuhui Yang, Zhipeng Ye, Jie Huang, Tao Xu, Jianfeng Ding, Wence Zhou, Jianbo Chen, Hao Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Ampullary carcinoma is an uncommon malignant neoplasm located in Vater’s ampulla, necessitating comprehensive exploration of its pathogenesis and therapeutic alternatives. Currently, the available models of ampullary carcinoma are severely restricted, impeding the advancement of fundamental research. Consequently, further models of ampullary carcinoma are required to enhance pertinent fundamental research. This study reports the development of a novel Chinese-origin human ampullary carcinoma cell line, named DPC-X2, utilizing fresh primary tumor tissue from ampullary carcinoma. DPC-X2 has been consistently passaged for more than 60 generations, exhibiting a population doubling time of around 95 h. The cells display atypical shape and distinct heterogeneity. STR profiling verifies that DPC-X2 is a unique human ampullary carcinoma cell line. Karyotype analysis indicates that all DPC-X2 cells have hyper-triploid karyotypes, characterized by a typical karyotype of 100, XXXX der(4), der(6), der(7), inv(9), der(12), der(13), der(19). The xenograft tumor development rate in NXG mice was 66.67%. This cell line exhibits sensitive to gemcitabine while demonstrating resistance to oxaliplatin, fluorouracil, and albumin-bound paclitaxel. DPC-X2 may be an important model for investigating the pathogenesis of ampullary carcinoma and for pharmaceutical development research.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-026-16168-y.

Introduction

Ampullary adenocarcinoma is an uncommon malignant neoplasm arising from Vater’s ampulla, with an incidence of roughly 0.59 per 100,000 individuals. Despite its rarity, the frequency has been progressively rising in recent decades [1]. The mean age at diagnosis is 65.5 years, with a five-year survival rate of 57% and a median survival duration of 39 months [2–6].

Ampullary carcinoma is categorized into three separate histopathological subgroups based on morphological and immunohistochemical features: intestinal-type, pancreatobiliary-type, and mixed-type. In addition to radical surgical excision, systemic chemotherapy is frequently used. Pancreatobiliary-type ampullary carcinoma generally adheres to pancreatic cancer protocols, employing the FOLFIRINOX regimen, whereas intestinal-type ampullary carcinoma usually utilizes colon cancer regimens like CapeOx or FOLFOX [7]. Nonetheless, the antitumor effectiveness of chemotherapy remains inadequate irrespective of the treatment strategy [1]. Consequently, additional research is necessary to enhance patient outcomes.

The majority of current insights in oncology research originate from in vitro and in vivo investigations utilizing cell lines, as these offer boundless, uniform, self-replicating samples via straightforward, standardized culture techniques and experimental protocols. The inaugural ampullary carcinoma cell line was created in 1989. As of now, only 13 such cell lines have been documented. One was eliminated due to contamination, and three had been previously established by our study group. These consist of SNU-869 and DPC-X3 (intestinal-type), DPC-X1 and DPC-X4 (mixed-type), while the remaining is classified as pancreatobiliary-type [8–18] (Table 1). To enhance the comprehension of ampullary carcinoma biology, it is essential to generate additional cell lines that reflect various subtypes.

Methods and materials

Acquisition and analysis of neoplastic tissue

The fresh ampullary carcinoma tumor tissue used in this investigation was obtained from the Department of General Surgery at Lanzhou University Second Hospital. She was a 52-year-old female patient (Table 2). Preoperative imaging studies confirmed a diagnosis of ampullary carcinoma (Fig. 1A), and she underwent laparoscopic pancreaticoduodenectomy on March 13, 2023. The surgical specimen disclosed a 2 cm × 2 cm neoplasm near the opening of the duodenal papilla (Fig. 1B).

This research rigorously complied with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was acquired from the patient and her family, and the study protocol received approval from the Medical Ethics Committee of Lanzhou University Second Hospital (2023 A-381).

Establishment of the cell line

The specimen acquisition adhered to rigorous aseptic protocols. A segment of ampullary carcinoma tissue, measuring approximately 5 mm × 5 mm × 5 mm, was excised using a surgical knife and placed in culture medium for expedited transit to a laboratory biosafety cabinet. The tumor tissue underwent three washes with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). The tissue was moved to a 10 cm cell culture dish using sterile surgical forceps. A tiny portion (about 2 × 1 mm) was removed using a sterile scalpel and preserved at -80 °C for later STR analysis. The residual tissue was fragmented into pieces around 0.1 mm in diameter. The chopped tissue was placed into a 15 ml centrifuge tube with a Pasteur pipette and centrifuged at 1400 rpm for 5 min. The supernatant was removed, and the pelleted tissue was resuspended in cell culture medium (DMEM/F12 supplemented with 1% GlutaMAX, 10% fetal bovine serum, and 1% penicillin-streptomycin). The cell suspension was uniformly allocated into the wells of a 6-well plate. The medium was substituted every two days throughout the culture duration. To purify the tumor cells, we first used differential trypsin digestion (trypsin treatment for 1 min) to remove the fibroblasts in large numbers, and then mechanically removed the few remaining fibroblast colonies repeatedly under a microscope with a sterile cell scraper. After purifying the tumor cells, they were passaged according to established digesting protocols.

Short Tandem Repeat (STR) profiling

DPC-X2 cells at passage 15, together with the original tumor tissue, were dispatched to Suzhou Genetic Testing Biotechnology Company for STR analysis. Early passage cells (P15) were utilized for STR identification to maintain consistency with primary tumors and support subsequent experiments. Drug sensitivity tests were conducted at P30, as the cells had fully adapted to the culture conditions and achieved a stable passage interval by then. Growth curve and xenograft studies were carried out at P40-50 to evaluate the phenotype of mid-passage cells.

Karyotype analysis of tumor cells

DPC-X2 cells at 60%-70% confluence were subjected to colcemid treatment (0.2 µg/mL) for a duration of 90 min. The cells were then trypsinized (0.25% trypsin, Vivacell) to create a single-cell suspension and centrifuged at 1000 rpm for 5 min. The supernatant was removed, and the cell pellet was resuspended in a hypotonic solution (0.56% KCl) and incubated at ambient temperature for 30 min. Cells were treated with a fixative solution (methanol: glacial acetic acid = 3:1) to create a chromosomal suspension. The suspension was deposited onto pre-chilled glass slides and air-dried to facilitate cell lysis and chromosomal dispersion. Following Giemsa staining, which generates distinct light and dark banding patterns on chromosomes, chromosomal shape, quantity, and banding were examined using a 100× oil immersion objective. The Chromosome J plugin of ImageJ software was used for automated analysis to produce karyograms and document chromosomal anomalies.

Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM)

DPC-X2 cells were subjected to digestion and centrifugation at 1000 rpm. The cell pellet was rinsed with PBS and then centrifuged to eliminate the supernatant. The cells were resuspended in SEM fixation solution, and the sample was deposited onto a coverslip. Dehydration was executed utilizing a graded ethanol series, succeeded by critical point drying to eliminate moisture. The specimen was affixed to a stub and sputter-coated with gold for 30 s. The vacuum system was engaged to empty the sample chamber. Following the initiation of the electron cannon, settings like acceleration voltage and beam current were modified. The fine and coarse focus knobs were employed to rectify image blur, while brightness, contrast, and scan speed were tuned prior to image acquisition and storage.

Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM)

The cell growth medium was removed, and the cells were fixed with 2.5% glutaraldehyde at ambient temperature for 5 min. Cells were meticulously scraped in a consistent direction with a cell scraper, transferred to a centrifuge tube using a Pasteur pipette, and centrifuged at 1000 rpm for 2 min. The supernatant was removed, and new fixative was introduced. The cell pellet (about 3 mm³) was carefully separated and fixed in the dark at room temperature for 6 h, thereafter undergoing post-fixation with 1% osmium tetroxide in the dark for 2 h to improve contrast. Dehydration was performed with a graded ethanol series, with each phase lasting 20 min. The specimen was permeated with a blend of acetone and epoxy resin, encapsulated in pure resin, and polymerized at 60 °C for 48 h. Ultrathin slices (50–70 nm) were prepared with an ultramicrotome, mounted on 150-mesh copper grids, and then stained with uranyl acetate and lead citrate. The grids were desiccated overnight at ambient temperature. The sample-loaded grid was inserted into the TEM vacuum chamber. Upon stabilizing the vacuum pressure, the electron cannon and condenser lens were calibrated to concentrate the electron beam on the specimen. The wobble focus function was employed to precisely adjust the sample position and focus. Aperture and beam current parameters were tuned prior to observation, image capture, and storage.

Growth curve determination

P50 DPC-X2 cells were seeded at a density of 8000 cells per well in 96-well plate (NEST), and six multiple Wells were set up. After incubation for 0, 24, 48, 72, and 96 h, CCK-8 reagent (APExBio, USA) was added and incubated for 2 h, and the absorbance at 450 nm was measured. The assay was based on six independent biological replicates (each containing technical triplicates). Doubling times were calculated after entering cell values using the website https://doubling-time.com/.

Drug sensitivity testing to conventional chemotherapeutic agents

A suspension of DPC-X2 cells at passage 30 was produced. A 100 µl aliquot containing 1.20 × 10⁴ cells was introduced into each of the inner 60 wells of a 96-well plate. The outer 36 perimeter wells were filled with 100 µl of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Four 96-well plates were produced, and the experiment was independently replicated three times. Following a 24-hour period, the supernatant in each well was discarded. The four plates were then administered gemcitabine, 5-fluorouracil (5-FU), oxaliplatin, and albumin-bound paclitaxel, respectively. Each chemotherapeutic agent was serially diluted in cell culture media to establish concentration gradients as follows: Gemcitabine concentrations (µmol/L): 600, 150, 30, 6, 1.5, 0.3, 0.06, 0.015, 0.003, 0.0006, 0.00015, 0.00003, 0.000006, 0.0000012 ; Oxaliplatin concentrations (µmol/L): 400, 200, 100, 40, 20, 10, 5, 2.5, 1.25, 0.625, 0.3125, 0.15625, 0.078, 0.039 ; 5-Fluorouracil (µmol/L): 7680, 1920, 384, 96, 24, 6, 1.5, 0.3, 0.06, 0.015, 0.003, 0.0006, 0.00015, 0.00003 ; Albumin-bound paclitaxel (µmol/L): 40, 20, 10, 5, 2.5, 0.5, 0.1, 0.025, 0.005, 0.001, 0.00025, 0.00005, 0.00001, 0.0000025.

Each concentration was evaluated in quadruplicate. Following 72 h of drug treatment, the supernatant was removed and substituted with 100 µl of the CCK-8 dilution. In a same manner, 100 µl of CCK-8 dilution was substituted for PBS in three peripheral wells to function as background controls. After a 2-hour incubation at 37 °C, the absorbance at 450 nm was assessed using a microplate reader. Dose-response curves were constructed, and the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC₅₀) values for the four chemotherapeutic drugs were determined utilizing GraphPad Prism software version 9.5.1 (GraphPad Inc., San Diego, CA, USA).

Subcutaneous xenograft tumor formation in NXG mice

Three male NXG mice, aged 4–6 weeks and weighing between 14 and 20 g, were acquired from Changzhou Cavens Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd. The mice were maintained in individually ventilated cages (IVC) under specified pathogen-free (SPF) conditions for one week before the experiment. The housing conditions were regulated at 22 ± 2 °C with 40%-60% relative humidity, and the mice had unrestricted access to food and water. A suspension of DPC-X2 cells at passage 50 was produced at a concentration of 1 × 10⁷ cells/ml. Each mouse was subcutaneously injected in the left anterior flank with 0.1 ml of the cell solution (comprising 1 × 10⁶ cells).

All procedures followed the institutional and national guidelines for the care and use of laboratory animals. Animal health and behavior were monitored every day for 4 weeks. When the maximum diameter of the Xenograft tumor approaches 1.0 cm or when the Xenograft tumor grows for one month, the mice were killed. All animals were euthanized by barbiturate overdose (intravenous injection, 150 mg/kg sodium pentobarbital) for tissue collection. Death was confirmed by loss of heartbeat, breathing and pupil response.

Animal experiments were reviewed and approved by the Medical Animal Experiment Ethics Committee of Lanzhou University Second Hospital (D2023-318).

Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) staining

The identical approach was used for paraffin embedding and sectioning of both the primary tumor tissue and the xenograft tumors. The pellet was immersed in 5 ml of universal tissue fixative at ambient temperature (about 37 °C) for 15 min. The tissue was carefully elevated using a 1250 µl pipette tip to disperse it in the fixative. Thereafter, the tissue underwent dehydration by a graded ethanol series (50%, 70%, 80%, 95%, 100%, and 100% ethanol), with a duration of 30 min at each concentration. The dried tissue was then immersed in a xylene-ethanol combination (1:1) for 30 min, followed by three 1-hour immersions in pure xylene. The tissue was ultimately embedded in paraffin, sectioned to a thickness of 5 μm with a microtome, affixed to glass slides, and desiccated for subsequent application.

The slides were subjected to xylene treatment to eliminate paraffin, with three changes of xylene, each lasting 2 min. The specimens were then hydrated using a sequence of ethanol solutions: 100% ethanol (three changes, 2 min each), 95% ethanol (2 min), and 70% ethanol (2 min), followed by a rinse with running water for a minimum of 5 min. The slides were immersed in hematoxylin solution for 2 min, thereafter rinsed with running water for a minimum of 5 min, briefly differentiated using hematoxylin differentiation solution for approximately 2 s, and rinsed once more with running water for 20–30 min. Then, counterstaining was performed using eosin solution for a duration of 2 min. The slides were then dehydrated using 95% ethanol (short dips, approximately 20 times), 95% ethanol (for 2 min), absolute ethanol (two exchanges, 2 min each), and cleaned in xylene (three exchanges, 2 min each). The slides were ultimately prepared with neutral balsam and a coverslip for microscopic analysis.

Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining

The prepared slides were submerged in xylene for 10 min to eliminate paraffin. The specimens were then rehydrated using a graded ethanol series: 100% ethanol for 5 min, 95% ethanol for 5 min, and 70% ethanol for 5 min, followed by a rinse with running water for a minimum of 5 min. Endogenous peroxidase activity was inhibited by incubating the slides in 3% hydrogen peroxide for 10 min, followed by a rinse under running water for a minimum of 5 min. Antigen retrieval was conducted by subjecting the slides to high-pressure heating in a citrate-based buffer for 10 min, followed by natural cooling. Non-specific binding sites were obstructed using goat serum for 30 min. The slides were then treated with primary antibody (diluted 1:200 to 1:500 in antibody diluent) overnight at 4 °C. Subsequent to incubation, the slides underwent three washes with PBS or ultrapure water, each lasting 10 min. Then, incubation with a secondary antibody occurred at room temperature for one hour, followed by three consecutive washes with PBS or ultrapure water, each lasting ten minutes. The DAB substrate solution, produced per the manufacturer’s guidelines, was used for color development, monitored microscopically until optimal staining was attained (about 5–60 s). The reaction was terminated by rinsing with running water for five minutes. The slides were then counterstained with hematoxylin for 2 min, rinsed under running water for 5 min, briefly differentiated using hematoxylin differentiation solution for 2 s, and rinsed again under running water for 20–30 min. Dehydration was conducted using 70% ethanol for 5 min, followed by 95% ethanol for 5 min, and finally absolute ethanol for 5 min. The slides were deparaffinized in xylene for five minutes and then air-dried. The slides were ultimately mounted with neutral balsam and air-dried for two to three days.

Data analysis

All data are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation. Dose-response curves for drug sensitivity, growth curves, and body weight curves of NXG mice were constructed and statistically evaluated utilizing GraphPad Prism software version 9.5.1 (GraphPad Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). The cell population doubling time was determined via the “Doubling Time” web tool [V. Roth MD, Doubling Time Calculator (2006), https://doubling-time.com/compute_more.php]. Three technical replicates and four biological replicates were used for the drug susceptibility test experiments, three technical replicates and six biological replicates were used for the growth curve experiments, and five biological replicates were used for the NXG mouse xenograft experiments.

Results

Establishment of the cell line

Utilizing initial and subsequent subcultures, together with mechanical scraping to exclude contaminating fibroblasts, we successfully produced a continuously growing ampullary carcinoma cell line, named DPC-X2. This cell line has been consistently preserved for more than 63 passages in DMEM/F12 media enriched with 10% fetal bovine serum. DPC-X2 cells showed considerable morphological diversity, primarily manifesting as polygonal or short spindle-shaped cells, with a prominent occurrence of multinucleated cells (Fig. 1C-D).

Short Tandem Repeat (STR) profiling

The STR analysis of DPC-X2 cells and the original tumor tissue produced a likelihood ratio (LR) of 1.8538 × 1019, indicating substantial evidence that the cell line is derived from the patient’s tumor (Table 3). Additionally, comparison with the ExPASy STR database verified that DPC-X2 did not correspond to any existing entries, signifying that it is a novel cell line.

Karyotype analysis

Karyotypic analysis showed that all DPC-X2 cells had hyper-triploid karyotypes. A sample karyotype was 100, XXXX, featuring derived chromosomes such as der(4), der(6), der(7), inv(9), der(12), and der(13)der(19) (Fig. 2B).

Under SEM, DPC-X2 cells predominantly exhibited spindle, round, and irregular polygonal morphologies, with diameters of around 30 μm (Fig. 3A). The cell surfaces exhibited a profusion of equally sized microvilli (Fig. 3B-C). Moreover, several elongated, filamentous filopodia were detected extending from the cell surface (Fig. 3D).

Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM)

TEM research revealed that DPC-X2 cells predominantly exhibited a spindle shape, characterized by uniformly dispersed microvilli on their surfaces. The cells had enlarged nuclei featuring conspicuous nucleoli and an elevated nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio. The nuclear membrane was distinctly observable, and the nuclei frequently exhibited an extended shape with indented margins (Fig. 3E). The cytoplasm comprised Golgi apparatuses characterized by characteristic stacked flat sacs encircled by numerous tiny vesicles, with desmosomes observable across cells (Fig. 3F). A limited quantity of primary lysosomes was dispersed throughout the cytoplasm, accompanied by numerous autolysosomes. Mitochondria were rather few; some exhibited swelling and elongation, accompanied by shorter and diminished cristae (Fig. 3G). Uniformly distributed microfilaments and a limited quantity of rough endoplasmic reticulum were also seen in the cytoplasm (Fig. 3H).

Drug sensitivity to conventional chemotherapeutic agents

The sensitivity of DPC-X2 cells at passage 30 was assessed against four standard chemotherapeutic agents: gemcitabine, oxaliplatin, fluorouracil, and nab-paclitaxel. The findings showed that DPC-X2 was sensitive to gemcitabine (IC₅₀ = 0.5588 µmol/L) (Fig. 4A) but displayed resistance to oxaliplatin (IC₅₀ = 33.05 µmol/L) (Fig. 4B), fluorouracil (IC₅₀ = 192.8 µmol/L) (Fig. 4C), and albumin-bound paclitaxel (IC₅₀ = 1.364 µmol/L) (Fig. 4D).

Subcutaneous xenograft tumor formation in NXG mice

Subcutaneous xenograft experiments were conducted utilizing DPC-X2 cells at passage 50 in NXG mice. The mice were euthanized 28 days after injection. Neoplasms developed in two of three animals, yielding a tumor formation rate of 66.67% (Fig. 5A-C). No metastatic lesions were seen in the liver or lungs of the animals (Fig. 5D). The xenograft tumors showed exponential growth (Fig. 5E), whereas the mice sustained steady body weights without indications of cachexia (Fig. 5F).

IHC staining for CK20, CDX2, MUC1, E-cadherin, and Ki67 was performed on both the main ampullary carcinoma tissue and DPC-X2 cell smears to preliminarily evaluate the expression of typical tumor markers and their consistency. Both the primary tissue and cell smears exhibited weak or partially positive expression of CK20 (Fig. 7A1-A2) and CDX2 (Fig. 7B1-B2). The results showed that MLH1, PMS2, MSH2, and MSH6 were all positive, indicating microsatellite stability (MSS) (Fig S1).

Discussion

This research successfully created a new ampullary carcinoma cell line of Chinese origin, named DPC-X2, using primary culture and subsequent passaging of tumor tissue obtained from a patient. The DPC-X2 cell line has been consistently preserved for more than 60 passages. The cells predominantly display polygonal and spindle-shaped morphologies. DPC-X2 cells rapidly produced subcutaneous xenograft tumors in NXG mice, with a tumor formation rate of 66.7% upon inoculation. This cell line exhibited susceptibility to gemcitabine while displaying resistance to oxaliplatin, fluorouracil, and nab-paclitaxel, which provides a good experimental model for studying the drug resistance mechanism of ampullary carcinoma. The aforementioned characteristics suggest that DPC-X2 is a significant novel model for the study of ampullary carcinoma.

The histological classification of ampullary carcinoma has been a longstanding subject of scientific discourse. Kimura proposed the initial classification of pancreatobiliary and intestinal types in 1994, followed by the addition of a mixed-type category in the 2010 WHO classification [19, 20]. Current subtyping predominantly depends on morphological attributes identified in H&E-stained sections, supplemented by immunohistochemical characteristics. Intestinal-type ampullary carcinoma is typically diagnosed through positive expression of CK20, CDX2, or MUC2, alongside negative MUC1; or through positive expression of CK20, CDX2, and MUC2, irrespective of MUC1 status [21–23]. In contrast, pancreatobiliary-type is generally marked by MUC1 positive, alongside negative CDX2 and MUC2, regardless of CK20 expression [21–23]. Tumors exhibiting mixed or ambiguous phenotypic features are classified as mixed-type. This classification system encounters difficulties. Immunohistochemical profiles may exhibit overlap between intestinal and pancreatobiliary adenocarcinomas. The threshold values (1, 5, 10, 20, 50%) for judging the positive expression of immunohistochemical markers are also different in different institutions [3, 21, 23, 24]. Therefore, immunohistochemistry may not be necessary for subtype diagnosis. Recent studies have redirected attention to the prognostic significance of biomarkers such as MUC1 and MUC5AC in ampullary carcinoma; nonetheless, advancements in this domain have been sluggish [24, 25]. Furthermore, there are no established criteria for ascertaining the ratio of intestine to pancreatobiliary components necessary for a mixed-type classification. The independent prognostic and predictive significance of subtyping for directing adjuvant therapy is still contentious. Certain studies indicate no substantial independent correlation between histological subtype and response to adjuvant therapy, nor with overall or disease-free survival rates. Our prior research indicates the restricted efficacy of subtype classification in the selection of adjuvant therapy regimens. Consequently, there is an urgent necessity for the molecular classification of ampullary carcinoma to facilitate targeted therapy methods.

Ethnic differences profoundly affect disease presentation, as genetic expression differs among racial and ethnic groups, resulting in unique tumor immune profiles [26]. The disparities in tumor immunobiology lead to variances in therapy effectiveness and patient outcomes. A comparative analysis of median overall survival in patients with ampullary carcinoma indicated that Black patients experienced the least beneficial results, whilst Asian and Hispanic patients showed the most advantageous survival rates [27]. Another study of racial disparities in preclinical and clinical models of breast cancer showed that targeted medicines derived from cell lines of European origin exhibited diminished efficacy in patients of African descent [28]. As the cornerstone of oncology research, cell lines are widely used to study biological behaviors such as cancer occurrence and progression, and play an important role in the development of new therapeutic drugs. Recently created cell lines have more clinical relevance and more efficiently preserve tumor heterogeneity compared to long-term cultured lines, therefore alleviating issues related to model homogeneity [29].

Mitochondria swelling and the emergence of autophagic vacuoles are critical markers of significant mitochondrial damage and trigger mitophagy [30]. Mitochondria swelling, as the most common pathological change, is the hallmark feature of accessory apoptosis [31]. A study on cervical cancer revealed that Parkin, a key protein involved in mitochondrial regulation, promotes apoptosis in cancer cells via GAPDH ubiquitination [32]. In contrast, alternative data indicate that Mitochondria swelling and vacuolization signify modest mitochondrial malfunction that can induce metabolic reprogramming in cancer cells. This mechanism may activate carcinogenic pathways by excessive ROS production or affect the tumor microenvironment by modifying mitochondrial membrane permeability, leading to the release of mitochondrial DNA and inflammatory molecules, so promoting angiogenesis and invasion [33, 34]. Mitochondria swelling and vacuolization may signify adaptive responses of cancer cells to the tumor microenvironment, however the exact mechanisms involved in oncogenesis are not fully understood. Under the transmission electron microscope, a relatively large number of mitochondrial swelling and mitophagy phenomena can be observed in DPC-X2 cells, which may serve as an entry point for studying the occurrence and development mechanism of ampullary carcinoma in the future.

For a newly established cancer cell line, there are often multiple molecular profiling verification methods. At the genome level, STR typing is the gold standard for cell line identification [35]. Microsatellite instability (MSI) detection can evaluate the functional status of mismatch repair [36]. Whole exome sequencing can identify driver gene mutations, copy number variations, and tumor mutational burden [37]. At the transcriptome level, RNA-seq can reveal gene expression profiles and molecular subtypes [38]. At the protein level, Western blot and IHC could verify the expression of key proteins [39]. Mass spectrometry proteomics can provide panoramic analysis of protein expression and post-translational modification [40]. In addition, DNA methylation microarray or bisulfite sequencing can be used for epigenetic profiling [41]. In this study, we performed HE staining, IHC (for MUC1, CK20, CDX2, E-cadherin, and Ki67), and STR typing to confirm that DPC-X2 is an ampullary carcinoma cell line with pancreaticobiliary characteristics. IHC analysis of MSI status also confirmed the presence of a microsatellite stable (MSS) phenotype.

DPC-X2 is expected to be used for screening novel anti-tumor drugs for ampullary carcinoma, especially those based on the unique sensitivity profile of gemcitabine. Research on the resistance mechanisms of drugs such as 5-FU and oxaliplatin is also expected to identify drug treatment targets and contribute to the development of new drugs. Meanwhile, this cell line can be used for cancer biology research on the biliary and pancreatic duct subtypes of ampullary carcinoma.

This research possesses specific limitations. Although we have confirmed the biological properties of this ampullary carcinoma cell line, we have not yet investigated the molecular processes that drive tumor formation. Molecular characterization was limited to IHC, and further genome and transcriptome analyses were not performed. Drug sensitivity data are lacking for comparison with other ampullary carcinoma cell lines. Only one ampullary carcinoma cell line was established in this study, which has limitations in representing the full heterogeneity of ampullary carcinoma. In the future, we will conduct in-depth research on this topic to provide more data support for molecular typing and new drug development of ampullary carcinoma.

In conclusion, we have effectively developed a novel ampullary carcinoma cell line of Chinese origin, offering a significant new model for fundamental study in this domain.

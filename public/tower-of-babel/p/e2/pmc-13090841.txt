International Trends in Dental Opioid Prescriptions
Authors: Kao-Ping Chua, Claudia Bruno, Sallie-Anne Pearson, Jonathan Brett, Sijia He, Romesh Nalliah, Chad Brummett
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This cross-sectional study compares dental opioid dispensing rates in the US, Canada, Australia, France, Belgium, Germany, the Netherlands, and Puerto Rico from 2021 to 2024.

Introduction

Opioid prescriptions from dentists are associated with adverse events, including opioid overdose and persistent use.1,2 A prior study3 showed that the US dental opioid dispensing rate (ie, number of dispensed opioid prescriptions from dentists per capita) far exceeded the corresponding rate in the UK in 2016. However, the US dental opioid dispensing rate has since decreased, raising the question of whether it remains high by international standards. Using 2021 to 2024 data, we evaluated trends in the dental opioid dispensing rate in the US, Canada, Australia, France, Belgium, Germany, the Netherlands, and Puerto Rico (hereafter referred to as a country for brevity).

Methods

This cross-sectional study was determined exempt from review and requirement of informed consent by the University of Michigan institutional review board because data were deidentified in accordance with the Common Rule. The Australian analysis was approved by the New South Wales Population & Health Service Research Ethics and Services Australia External Request Evaluation Committee. Reporting follows the STROBE guideline.

Data for the US, the Netherlands, Belgium, France, Germany, and Puerto Rico were derived from the IQVIA Multinational Integrated Data Analysis System, which captures prescriptions from 58% to 92% of pharmacies in these countries. Data from Canada were derived from the IQVIA Canadian Compuscript Audit, which capture prescriptions from 59% of Canadian pharmacies. In both IQVIA databases, dispensing counts are projected to national totals. Data for Australia were derived from a 10% random sample of prescriptions dispensed under the Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme (PBS), which provides subsidized medicines to all Australians. PBS counts were multiplied by 10 to estimate national totals. Data on population denominators derived from national agencies (eMethods in Supplement 1).

The outcome was the annual dental opioid dispensing rate (number of dispensed opioid prescriptions from dentists per 100 000 people). Dentists included oral and maxillofacial surgeons. For each country, the relative change in the dental opioid dispensing rate between 2021 and 2024 was calculated. Analyses used R 4.2.2 (R Project for Statistical Computing).

Results

In 2021, the dental opioid dispensing rate in the US was 2786.3 per 100 000, compared with 2081.9 per 100 000 in Canada, 1408.2 per 100 000 in France, 1116.8 per 100 000 in Puerto Rico, 1066.2 per 100 000 in Australia, 287.4 per 100 000 in Belgium, 144.6 per 100 000 in Germany, and 85.5 per 100 000 in the Netherlands. In 2024, the dental opioid dispensing rate in the US was 2022.0 per 100 000, compared with 1773.9 per 100 000 in Canada, 1259.1 per 100 000 in France, 1005.0 per 100 000 in Puerto Rico, 1038.0 per 100 000 in Australia, 248.6 per 100 000 in Belgium, 97.9 per 100 000 in Germany, and 83.0 per 100 000 in the Netherlands. In 2024, the ratio between the US dental opioid dispensing rate and the country with the next highest rate (Canada) was 1.1, compared with 24.4 between the US and the country with the lowest rate (Netherlands).

Between 2021 and 2024, the annual dental opioid dispensing rate decreased 27.4% in the U.S, compared with 14.8% in Canada, 10.6% in France, 10.0% in Puerto Rico, 13.5% in Belgium, 32.3% in Germany, 2.6% in Australia and 3.0% in the Netherlands. Discussion

This cross-sectional study found that the US dental opioid dispensing rate is decreasing but remains high by international standards. Findings suggest opportunities to reduce this rate exist. This notion is further supported by data showing that two-thirds of US dental opioid prescriptions are for tooth extraction, a procedure for which ibuprofen and acetaminophen are equally effective for pain control.4,5 Compared with the Netherlands, Germany, and Belgium, dental opioid dispensing rates were consistently higher in Canada, France, Australia, and Puerto Rico, suggesting opportunities to reduce these rates may also exist.

Limitations include lack of information on the dosing of dental opioid prescriptions or their indications. Analyses could not determine whether differences in dental opioid dispensing rates between countries were due to practice patterns, access to dental care, or other factors. Some opioid prescriptions in Australia are accessed privately rather than via the PBS, suggesting the Australian dental opioid dispensing rate is underestimated.6

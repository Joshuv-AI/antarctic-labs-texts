A hybrid evolutionary framework for efficient IoT task scheduling in fog computing
Authors: Lianhe Cui
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper proposes an innovative, hybrid approach to task scheduling and virtual machine (VM) placement in fog computing environments that aims to optimize energy efficiency and task completion for Internet of Things (IoT) applications. The proposed method combines Gorilla Troops Optimizer (GTO), a bio-inspired metaheuristic, with a resource-aware virtual machine placement strategy that allows for simultaneous optimization of task scheduling and virtual machine allocation. This integrated approach addresses key challenges in the allocation of IoT tasks by considering multiple objectives, such as latency, power consumption, and load balancing. A dynamic exploration-exploitation strategy and innovative fitness functionality have been employed to efficiently map tasks to fog nodes while minimizing task failure and suspension periods. Extensive simulations performed with iFogSim2 demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method and have achieved significant improvements over existing algorithms. The proposed approach is 18% better than ant colony optimization (ACO), 15% better than improved multi-objective differential evolution (IMODE), and 13% better than genetic and simulated annealing (GASA). These results underscore the effectiveness of the hybrid method in optimizing task scheduling and VM placement for dynamic and latency-sensitive applications in IoT. This work provides a scalable solution for fog computing systems that significantly improves service quality by optimizing resource consumption and reducing energy consumption, providing a promising approach to real-world IoT environments.Keywords: Fog computing, Internet of Things, Task scheduling, Virtual machine placement, Gorilla troops optimizer, Energy efficiencySubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The Internet of Things (IoT) has expanded and become more ingrained in our daily lives in recent years. As a result, IoT applications like access control and facial recognition are becoming more and more popular in a variety of fields, such as smart cities, smart homes, and smart healthcare platforms1,2. An extension of cloud computing, fog computing is essential to the operation of Internet of Things applications. As shown in Fig. 1 of the hierarchical model2, the solution architect plans to deploy fog nodes at the network edge to connect IoT devices to the cloud layer. These fog nodes will have unique storage and processing capacities.

Hierarchical structure of the fog computing.

The transmission time involved in moving data from Internet of Things nodes to and from cloud platforms is shortened when a fog layer is present. This makes it easier for IoT apps, which serve as infrastructure for carrying out tasks in real-time, to utilize the resources of the surrounding fog3–5. These jobs have a variety of computational and storage requirements, including things like memory allocation, CPU utilization, and execution time constraints. Furthermore, a thorough assessment of numerous Qualities of Service (QoS) aspects, such as latency and energy consumption, is required when assigning IoT tasks to fog or cloud resources6. For both system designers and end users of Internet of Things applications, the previously mentioned parameters are crucial7. For example, end users are more concerned with fog nodes’ real-time performance, whereas system designers are more concerned with the nodes’ energy consumption. When fog or cloud servers are appropriately chosen to distribute IoT jobs within predetermined parameters, task scheduling optimization takes place8. In order to optimize QoS parameters for end users and system designers, this study aims to develop a scheduler that efficiently handles incoming IoT jobs by considering multiple objectives9,10.

Task scheduling optimization can be formulated as an Integer Linear Programming (ILP) problem, which is known for its NP-hard solution complexity11. It is crucial to recognize that traditional mathematical techniques cannot effectively determine the best solution in polynomial computation time for these previously mentioned challenges12. Particularly in the context of NP-hard scenarios, population-based meta-heuristic approaches have proven to be successful in quickly solving the task scheduling and virtual machine allocation problem13. These algorithms use a uniform distribution to randomly start an initial population and apply preset operators to converge towards the (near) optimal solution in subsequent generations14. Finding the best solution requires a careful examination of the search space’s local and global components. Starting with a diverse population allows for the exploration of several paths in the search space to identify potentially productive regions. Moreover, the ability to efficiently exploit these advantageous regions is necessary to determine the best option globally15,16. However, transitioning from exploration to exploitation can be challenging, particularly if the population gets stuck in local optima and converges too soon17. The task scheduling problem in different heterogeneous computing systems has been thoroughly studied by academic researchers. However, there aren’t many specific solutions in the literature that address this issue in the context of fog infrastructure, especially when it comes to dynamically generated exponential IoT tasks18. Because of the unique limitations imposed by IoT tasks and fog resources, the search space in the IoT and fog environment is extremely complex, outperforming the complexity found in conventional computer systems. Because task scheduling is inherently challenging, it is essential to create an architecture that facilitates the effective management of IoT applications, thereby reducing the related difficulties19.

This paper presents a Hybrid Improved Gorilla Optimizer for Resource-aware scheduling (HIGOR) for task scheduling and VM placement in fog computing, with three key achievements: the evolutionary hybrid algorithm, the innovative virtual machine placement strategy, and the innovative application of the Gorilla Troops Optimizer (GTO) for task scheduling. The first partnership, the hybrid algorithm, integrates GTO with virtual machine embedding to optimize task allocation and resource allocation. This composition is designed to improve performance, given the energy efficiency and task completion time that are critical in fog environments. The second partnership introduces a new strategy for virtual machine placement that dynamically allocates virtual machines based on resource availability, prioritizing load balancing and energy savings. This strategy differs from traditional approaches and considers the use of real-time resources to optimize virtual machine allocation across fog nodes. Finally, the application of GTO for task scheduling by utilizing the bio-inspired behavior of gorillas’ social structures effectively maps IoT tasks to fog nodes. This approach is differentiated by the use of a two-phase optimization, exploration and exploitation process, which avoids premature convergence and ensures a more resilient and scalable solution in dynamic environments. Considering the unique computing and communication features, it is essential to distribute the load of IoT nodes to the fog infrastructure. It is essential to use an efficient scheduling method that can adjust to the fog environment in order to manage the load. This is particularly important in settings where time is of the essence, like medical monitoring systems. We are driven to use an evolutionary approach based on GTO to tackle these problems. Algorithms based on GTO require fewer adjustment parameters. It is able to achieve the ideal solution by accounting for particle interaction. However, GTO tends to converge towards the global optimal condition when faced with a search space that contains many dimensions. Related works

Researchers have been interested in task scheduling in fog environments. The authors in20 focus on task offloading and resource allocation for satellite-terrestrial integrated networks, aiming to optimize reliability and reduce end-to-end delays. The method presented uses AI-based modeling to enhance task assignments between satellite and terrestrial systems, thereby improving network resource utilization. The key results show that this approach leads to more efficient task offloading with reduced delays and improved reliability. However, the method primarily addresses satellite communication networks and does not consider the dynamic, resource-constrained nature of fog computing, where task execution and node behavior are more complex. In21, the authors focus on optimizing the scheduling of scientific workflows in green DVFS-enabled edge-cloud computing environments. The method integrates combinatorial metaheuristics with Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling (DVFS) to improve energy efficiency in edge-cloud systems. The key results indicate that the proposed approach significantly reduces energy consumption while maintaining task performance. However, this solution is tailored to scientific workflows and does not directly address the critical issues in fog computing such as task latency, real-time scheduling, and the dynamic nature of IoT systems. Another method in22 examines the energy-latency trade-off for scientific workflows in cloud environments using the Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO). The authors evaluate the processor utilization ratio and its impact on energy consumption and task execution time. The results demonstrate that GWO can effectively balance energy and latency in the cloud. However, this approach primarily applies to cloud computing and does not account for the dynamic characteristics of fog environments, where tasks are distributed across heterogeneous nodes and real-time task dependencies are crucial. The method in23 focuses on a many-objective optimization algorithm for job scheduling in cloud computing based on Merge-and-Split Theory. The algorithm optimizes multiple objectives, such as energy efficiency, cost, and response time, for cloud computing systems. The key results show improved scheduling performance, with a better balance of these objectives. However, the method is designed for cloud environments and does not address the challenges posed by fog computing, where resources are distributed, and task scheduling must handle more dynamic and real-time constraints. In24, the authors propose a distributionally robust scheduling method for benefit allocation in regional integrated energy systems with multiple stakeholders. The method optimizes resource allocation by considering the varying needs of stakeholders and system uncertainties. The results indicate improved resource distribution, ensuring a fair and robust allocation across stakeholders. However, this method is primarily focused on energy systems and does not address task scheduling or VM placement in fog or cloud computing systems, where resource allocation and task execution are more complex.

The method in25 introduces MPAEE, a multipath adaptive energy-efficient routing scheme for Low Earth Orbit-based Industrial IoT systems. The approach improves routing efficiency and reduces energy consumption by utilizing adaptive multipath routing. The results show that the method is effective for energy-efficient routing in IoT systems. However, the approach does not focus on task scheduling or VM placement, which are central to fog computing environments, and it is limited to Industrial IoT systems with specific network configurations. Another method in26 proposes an improved particle swarm optimization (PSO) technique with reverse learning and neighbor adjustment for task scheduling in space surveillance networks. The solution improves task scheduling efficiency and reduces task completion time. However, the approach is focused on space-based applications and does not consider the dynamic nature of fog computing, where tasks are distributed across heterogeneous, resource-constrained nodes. In27, the authors present a QoS-oriented task offloading method for NOMA-based multi-UAV cooperative MEC systems. The method optimizes task offloading in multi-UAV systems, focusing on Quality of Service (QoS) requirements. The results indicate improved energy efficiency and task offloading efficiency. However, this approach is designed for multi-UAV systems and does not address the specific task scheduling and VM placement issues in fog computing, where resources are distributed across multiple, heterogeneous nodes.

The method in28 focuses on storage-aware joint user scheduling and bandwidth allocation for federated edge learning. The authors optimize resource usage by considering storage and bandwidth constraints in edge learning environments. The key results show that the approach improves the overall performance of edge learning systems. However, it does not directly address the task scheduling and VM placement challenges in fog computing, where task interdependencies and resource allocation need to be managed dynamically across a network of edge devices. The authors in29 propose a workflow scheduling scheme that utilizes AI-based modeling to optimize reliability and end-to-end delay control in cloud computing environments. They combine machine learning techniques with traditional scheduling methods to enhance task allocation and resource management. While this approach is particularly suited for cloud environments where service-level agreements (SLAs) and real-time processing are critical, it does not directly address fog computing environments, where tasks and resources are more dynamic. The integration of AI-driven scheduling for real-time, delay-sensitive IoT applications in fog computing systems could be an area for future research to explore, as it would need to adapt to a decentralized and heterogeneous environment. The solution presented in30 utilizes combinatorial metaheuristic methods to optimize the scheduling of scientific workflows in edge-cloud computing environments, specifically using Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling (DVFS) to improve energy efficiency. This method is highly relevant for energy-aware scheduling in edge and fog computing, where resource constraints and energy consumption are significant concerns. However, it focuses mainly on scientific workflows and may not fully capture the complexity of general IoT task scheduling, where latency and real-time execution are more critical. Extending this method to balance the energy-latency trade-offs for IoT systems would improve its relevance to fog computing scenarios. The authors in31 conduct an energy-latency trade-off analysis for scientific workflows using the Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO), focusing on processor utilization and energy consumption. GWO is known for its ability to handle multi-objective optimization problems, balancing exploration and exploitation effectively. While this approach works well for computationally intensive workflows, it does not directly account for the dynamic nature of fog computing environments, where tasks are distributed across heterogeneous, potentially mobile fog nodes. Adapting this method to real-time IoT applications with low-latency requirements and dynamic resource allocation would improve its applicability to fog computing. In32, a hybrid many-objective optimization algorithm is proposed for job scheduling in cloud computing, based on Merge-and-Split Theory. The method combines multiple objectives, such as energy efficiency, cost, and response time, into a single optimization framework. This many-objective approach is suitable for cloud computing but does not explicitly address fog computing scenarios, where resources are distributed over several nodes with varying capabilities, and conditions are dynamic. The complexity of multi-objective optimization in such environments, particularly for real-time IoT applications, requires further exploration. Adapting this hybrid approach to fog computing environments could lead to better optimization of energy, latency, and resource utilization across mobile nodes (Table 1).

VM placement related works

An innovative Ant Colony System was proposed by Alharbi et al.33 as a way to optimize the placement of virtual machines (VMs) to achieve energy efficiency and thereby reduce the energy consumption of data centers (DCs). Considering the dynamic placement of virtual machines as a bin-packing assignment problem, the authors integrated information from VM and PM identities. Peake et al.34 suggested a method based on Parallel Ant Colony optimization (PACO) that makes use of contemporary multi-core hardware to reduce processing time. Even though the researchers’ methodology concentrated on the deployment of static virtual machines, it is crucial to recognize that this problem could evolve within the context of cloud computing. A multi-objective genetic-based design was used by Singh et al.35 to introduce a novel method for VM allocation in cluster clusters (CDCs). In order to allow underloaded PMs to go into sleep mode and save power, the goal is to divide their workload among other servers. In the end, this approach lowers operating expenses, increasing cloud service providers’ profitability. In order to solve the weight selection problem, Saidi and Dardou’s36 VM Placement technique uses Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning (RL) and a Pareto-based RL algorithm. The Chebyshev scalarization function (VMPMORL) is used to accomplish this. They outperformed current multiple-purpose and single-objective algorithms by simultaneously reducing resource and energy consumption. Wei et al.37 initially proposed the Asynchronous Advantage Actor-Critic (A3C) algorithm, a reinforcement learning (RL) method used for VM placement. By using a methodical approach that takes workload characteristics and VM resource demands into account, A3C makes it easier to identify the best VM placement strategies. This framework’s main goal is to highlight resource and energy optimization in the context of cloud computing. Its goal is to make it easier to create clever virtual machine deployment algorithms that improve cloud infrastructure’s overall effectiveness. Sayadnavard et al.38 presented a novel model for classifying physical machines (PMs) (DTMC) by utilizing Discrete-Time Markov Chain (DTCMC) and Continuous Time Markov Chain (CTMC) architectures in order to proactively anticipate future resource utilization. Additionally, based on the e-dominance principle, the authors presented a multi-objective method for VM allocation using the Artificial Bee Colony (e-MOABC). In39, the authors introduce the AFED-EF algorithm, an energy-efficient VM allocation algorithm for IoT applications in cloud data centers. The method optimizes VM allocation based on energy consumption in cloud systems. The results demonstrate that the approach is effective in reducing energy consumption while meeting IoT workload demands. However, the algorithm is focused on cloud systems and does not consider the distributed, dynamic nature of fog computing, where tasks and VM placement need to be optimized across a network of smaller, resource-constrained nodes. Another method in40, is proposed for mission-driven resource scheduling in satellite-terrestrial networks, emphasizing collaboration and reconfiguration of resources. This approach aims to optimize resource allocation for mission-critical tasks in satellite-terrestrial networks. The results show that the method improves resource scheduling and allocation. However, it does not address the core issues of task scheduling and VM placement in fog computing, where resources are distributed across multiple edge devices, and task dependencies must be considered in real-time. The efficient administration of the physical environment

As shown in this section, several meta-algorithms, have been proposed for scheduling tasks and embedding virtual machines into fog calculations. Although each of these methods has shown effectiveness in solving specific problems, they usually focus on single-goal optimization or fail to integrate task scheduling and virtual machine placement in a resource-aware manner. For example, DMODE manages multiple goals, such as power consumption and task completion time, but focuses mainly on task scheduling. It also requires significant computational resources, which can be a limitation in large-scale fog environments. In contrast, our approach integrates task scheduling and virtual machine placement using GTO, improving resource allocation and energy consumption in fog nodes while ensuring faster convergence. HHOLS optimizes task scheduling with a combination of local exploration and search techniques. However, it doesn’t consider the niche of virtual machines, and its scalability can be problematic in large fog environments. Our hybrid approach, which integrates both task scheduling and virtual machine placement, optimizes computational resources and energy in dynamic fog environments, addressing HHOLS scalability concerns. GASA combines genetic algorithms and annealing simulations to optimize task scheduling and virtual machine placement but examines them separately. This separation limits the potential for optimization, as it does not take into account the interdependencies between task scheduling and virtual machine placement. In contrast, our integrated approach optimizes both components simultaneously, resulting in better load balance, reduced energy consumption, and better task completion times. WOA is another algorithm that effectively manages task scheduling and virtual machine placement but has problems with the speed of convergence and the quality of the solution, especially in dynamic fog environments. Our method addresses these limitations by integrating resource-aware virtual machine positioning with GTO scheduling, delivering better scalability, faster convergence, and more energy efficiency. In summary, although DMODE, HHOLS, GASA, and WOA each provide valuable insights into the timing of tasks and the position of the virtual machine, they lack effective integration of these components, limiting their scalability and efficiency. Our approach addresses these limitations by combining task scheduling and virtual machine embedding into a hybrid framework, improving overall performance in fog computing environments.

It is necessary to schedule S independent tasks. The computing infrastructure receives user requests (tasks). To track the overall use of system resources, the task scheduler gathers input from the resource manager unit. The task scheduler initiates the task scheduling algorithm to schedule tasks on VMs based on the data from user requests and resource availability. Based on this data, the VM scheduler then assigns VMs to PMs. Let, T contains a collection of terminal devices, represented as T = {t1, t2, t3, …, ti, …, tn}, where i is the terminal device’s sequential identifier. Qt = {ptr, pte, bi, wtd, wed, Er} is the formula used to express the characteristics of the terminal device ti. Problem formulation

Task scheduling and virtual machine deployment within a fog computing platform present the biggest challenges. The goal is to use a particular scheduling and placement methodology to allocate multiple tasks to specific fog nodes. VM Placement

The proposed VM placement algorithm begins by defining output and inputs. The algorithm is designed to efficiently allocate virtual machines to physical machines using a priority-based approach. It takes two main inputs: a list of virtual machines that need to be placed and a list of available physical machines that can host them. The expected output is an optimized mapping that shows which VM is assigned to which PM, along with any VMs that couldn’t be allocated. The algorithm starts its execution by initializing key data structures. An allocation map is created as an empty dictionary to store the final VM-to-PM assignments, and a list for failed allocations is prepared to track any VMs that cannot be placed successfully. This initialization phase ensures that the algorithm has the necessary containers to store results and handle edge cases. Before beginning the actual allocation process, the algorithm performs important preprocessing steps. The list of VMs is sorted strategically, placing critical or high-priority VMs first, followed by VMs with larger resource requirements. This ensures that the most important workloads get the best placement opportunities. Similarly, the physical machines are sorted by their available capacity in ascending order, implementing a best-fit strategy that aims to minimize resource waste.

The main processing begins with a loop that examines each VM in the sorted list. For each VM, the algorithm initializes tracking variables including a reference to the best PM candidate found so far, a best fit score set to infinity so any real score will be an improvement, and a Boolean flag to indicate whether a suitable allocation has been discovered. Within the VM processing loop, there’s an inner loop that evaluates each available physical machine. For every PM, the algorithm first retrieves the current available resources including CPU, memory, and storage capacity. It then checks whether this PM can accommodate the current VM’s resource requirements using a validation function. When a PM can potentially host the VM, the algorithm calculates two important metrics. The fit score measures how efficiently the VM’s resources would be utilized on this PM, considering the ratio of required resources to available resources. The energy impact calculation determines how much additional energy consumption would result from placing this VM on the PM, supporting green computing objectives. These two metrics are combined into a total optimization score that balances resource efficiency with energy consumption. If this total score is better than the previously recorded best score, the algorithm updates its tracking variables to remember this PM as the new best candidate for the current VM. After evaluating all possible PMs for the current VM, the algorithm makes the allocation decision. If at least one suitable PM was found during the evaluation process, the VM is officially allocated to the best PM. The system then updates the PM’s available resources to reflect the new allocation and records this assignment in the allocation map for future reference. However, if no suitable PM could accommodate the current VM, perhaps due to insufficient resources or all PMs being in critical states, the VM is added to the failed allocations list. This ensures that no VM is forgotten and provides visibility into capacity constraints or other issues that prevented successful placement. Finally, the algorithm concludes by returning both the successful allocation map and the list of any VMs that couldn’t be placed. This comprehensive output allows system administrators to understand both the successful placements and any capacity planning issues that need to be addressed.

The core of the algorithm uses GTO, a population-based meta-heuristic algorithm inspired by gorilla social structures. Conclusions and future works

This paper presents an innovative, hybrid approach to task scheduling and virtual machine placement in fog computing environments that aims to improve energy efficiency and task completion for IoT applications. GTO’s proposed methodology combines a biology-inspired meta-heuristic with a resource-aware virtual machine placement strategy to optimize both task scheduling and virtual machine allocation simultaneously. By dynamically prioritizing tasks based on urgency, computational intensity, and energy constraints, this method ensures that critical tasks are processed first, and less important tasks are performed efficiently, improving overall system performance. Our approach also incorporates an innovative virtual machine placement algorithm that assesses the resource status of each fog node and assigns tasks based on computational power, memory, and network bandwidth. This ensures that tasks requiring higher compute resources are assigned to more powerful nodes, while low-demand tasks are distributed to nodes with lower capacity, resulting in reduced resource wastage and increased system efficiency. In addition, the virtual machine placement model incorporates load balancing techniques for a uniform distribution of tasks among fog nodes, which helps prevent overload and ensures that resources are used optimally across the network. System compatibility is enhanced by the ability to respond to task load changes and the availability of nodes, ensuring continuous optimization even as conditions change. In the future, the proposed method could be expanded to include more complex real-world scenarios, such as dynamic fog node behaviors and task dependencies. This improves the stability of the approach and allows it to manage environments where nodes experience displacement or failure and tasks have complex dependencies. Further work will also examine the impact of larger IoT networks and assess how this approach scales as the number of tasks and nodes increases in fog environments. Additionally, the use of fault tolerance mechanisms can increase the reliability of the system and make it more adaptable to the unpredictable nature of real-world applications. Finally, this research will help develop efficient, scalable, and adaptive fog computing solutions for dynamic IoT environments and respond to the growing needs of real-time and latency-sensitive applications.

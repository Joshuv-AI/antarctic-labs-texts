A dataset on the segregation of students with disabilities in Brazil
Authors: Rafael Verão Françozo, Afonso Henriques Silva Leite, Leonardo Lopes Honda, Felipe Fernandes de Oliveira, Marcio Teixeira Oliveira, Calvin Rodrigues da Costa
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe dataset provided shows the segregation rate between students with and without disabilities in Brazilian cities between 2008 and 2023. Student enrolment data was extracted from the microdata of the Brazilian school census. The segregation rate was calculated using the dissimilarity index, which quantifies how dissimilar or segregated two populations are. The dataset consists of a .csv file with the calculated data and thematic maps of the Brazilian states, highlighting the cities. This data can be useful for researchers in the field of inclusion and decision-makers to support the development of public policies that enable eliminate disparities in education and ensure equal access to all levels of education for the persons with disabilities.Keywords: Dissimilarity, Inclusion, Special education, Segregation

Background

In 1994 the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO) has organised the Salamanca Conferente that produced the Salamanca Statement on Principles, Policy and Practice in Special Needs Education and a Framework for Action. This Statement was signed by representatives from 92 countries. Its primary focus was on promoting inclusive education worldwide. One of its main points states: those with special educational needs must have access to regular schools which should accommodate them within a child-centred pedagogy capable of meeting these needs [2].

In the Brazil, historically, student with Special Educational Needs (SEN) are segregated from regular schools and sent to specialised schools or specialised classes for assistance [3,4]. In recent years, a series of public policies aimed at the inclusion of students with SEN have been implemented and it is possible to see a movement of this public towards regular schools [4]. Data from the school census of basic education in Brazil point to a significant increase in the number of students with SEN enrolled in basic education schools as presented in Fig. 1.

Histogram of the distribution of segregation rates in Brazilian cities.

The data shown in Fig. 1 indicates that the number of students with SEN enrolled in Brazilian schools has risen from 695,699 in 2008 to 1,771,430 in 2023. It is important to note that in the same period the total number of students enrolled in basic education in Brazil fell from 53,232,868 to 47,304,632 [5]. This significant 24% rise in the student population with SEN, in contrast to an 11% decrease in new student enrollment, suggests a substantial growth in the proportion of students with SEN over the period.

However, we have seen the creation of “specialised schools,” which are regular schools with the infrastructure to cater for students with SEN. This implies a high concentration of this public in these schools, potentially maintenance of segregation in educational spaces [6].

This study aims to compile a comprehensive dataset about the degree of segregation between students with and without SEN in Brazilian cities over the last 16 years, 2008 and 2023. The dataset can serve as support for related studies as well as for public managers to draw up public policies aimed at including students with SEN in mainstream schools. In this way, it contributes to achieving the goals set out in the Salamanca declaration as well as the Sustainable Development Goals: 04 Quality Education and 10 Reduced Inequalities.

Data Description

To measure segregation between students with and without specific educational needs in Brazil between 2008 and 2023, we calculated and collated two sets of data. The first dataset consists of a .csv file containing the raw segregation rates for each Brazilian city during the analysed period. The second set of data consists of a document in pdf format with graphs illustrating the evolution of segregation for each Brazilian state. In the graphs, darker shades are closer to 1 and represent higher levels of segregation.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

This study uses information from school census microdata to calculate the dissimilarity index between students with and without SEN in Brazilian cities between 2008 and 2023.

Brazilian basic education school census microdata

The basic education school census is an annual comprehensive survey conducted in both in public and private schools. Until 2019, the data provided detailed information on every student, teacher, school, class and manager in Brazil [7]. However, due to the General Data Protection Act, the data format changed in 2020, now providing only aggregate information on the total number of students and schools characteristics. Despite this change, more than 400 variables with information on basic education in Brazil are available for analysis [8].

The information in this survey is provided by Instituto Nacional de Estudos e Pesquisas Educacionais Anísio Teixeira (INEP) in text format files, which can be accessed using statistical software such as IBM SPSS or R language packages [9].

To compile this dataset, microdata from the Basic Education School Census between 2008 and 2023 were used1.

Dissimilarity index

The index of dissimilarity (IoD), introduced to the literature by Duncan & Duncan [10] is a metric used to quantify the segregation between two populations in n spatial units, referred to as tracts. The IoD varies in the closed range [0,1] (from 0% to 100%), where 0 represents no segregation between the populations in the tracts analysed and 1 represents complete segregation [11,12]. This is an indicator widely used in the literature to measure inequalities between two populations such as school segregation based on socio-economic indicators in Latin America [14], unequal opportunities in access to basic education among Tunisian children [15], the distribution of students with disabilities in public and public schools [16] and residential segregation in Italy [17].

A code in the R language was developed to analyse the data and calculate dissimilarity according to expression 2. The code is available together with the dataset [18], and Fig. 4 presents the developed pseudocode.

The code developed has two repeating loops. The first goes through the municipalities (or cities) and the second goes through the schools in these cities. In the innermost loop, the values of the variables highlighted in expression 2 are consulted from the school census microdata. This operation continues until the data for all the schools in the city has been obtained. At the end, the algorithm adds the result to the list and returns to the outermost loop to obtain data for the next city. The procedure is carried out for all cities with records in the school census database.

As possible studies suggested based on this dataset, we consider a comparison between the implemented inclusion policies and the evaluation of their effectiveness over time. We consider the analysis of data using multi-criteria decision support methods, such as the Analytic Hierarchy Process [19], and within the context of vocational schools [20], to be relevant studies that can be developed with the support of the provided dataset.

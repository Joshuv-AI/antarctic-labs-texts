Predefined-time tracking control for underwater robots
Authors: Ali Keymasi-Khalaji, Shadi Tajpour-Fard
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper introduces and validates a predefined-time control (PTC) strategy for trajectory tracking of autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs). In contrast to conventional methods like sliding mode control (SMC), where convergence time is state-dependent and cannot be directly prescribed, the proposed controller guarantees convergence to the desired trajectory within a user-defined time frame, independent of initial conditions. The performance of the PTC is rigorously evaluated against SMC on two representative planar trajectories. Obtained results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves faster and precisely adjustable convergence, particularly with smaller, mission-critical time horizons. While SMC offers robust stability for applications where smooth operation is paramount, the predefined-time approach is particularly well suited for time-sensitive missions. These findings provide a clear comparative framework, aiding practitioners in selecting and tuning controllers based on specific mission requirements, and establish PTC as a promising solution for precision guidance of autonomous underwater vehicles.Keywords: Predefined-time control, Sliding-mode control, Autonomous underwater vehicle, Trajectory tracking, Lyapunov stabilitySubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

AUVs have become indispensable tools for ocean exploration, environmental monitoring, and offshore inspection, where reliable trajectory tracking is fundamental to mission safety and efficiency. Achieving accurate tracking in uncertain underwater environments remains challenging due to nonlinear hydrodynamics, strong coupling effects, and unpredictable ocean currents. In time-critical operations such as cooperative mapping, seabed scanning, and obstacle avoidance, it is insufficient for the tracking error to eventually converge to zero; sometimes convergence must occur within a known, limited time to guarantee synchronization, timing, and safety constraints. Without such bounded-time guarantees, delayed responses can lead to mission drift or instability.

Recent reviews of underwater guidance and control systems emphasize that, despite significant advances, maintaining consistent transient behavior under environmental uncertainty remains an open challenge1. Within robust nonlinear control frameworks, SMC has long been recognized for its insensitivity to parameter variations and external disturbances2, Numerous modern formulations extend the classical variable-structure theory to higher-order and adaptive variants3. For instance, a super-twisting integral SMC is implemented in4 for AUV trajectory tracking that effectively reduced chattering, while5 introduced an adaptive super-twisting scheme compensating dynamic couplings and hydrodynamic uncertainties. An optimized second-order SMC using a swarm algorithm is proposed in6, and7 proposed a fast finite-time super-twisting design with a higher-order extended-state observer to enhance disturbance rejection in UUV tracking. Related developments also include adaptive nonlinear dynamic control8, robust nonlinear Lyapunov-based obstacle avoidance9, two-layer sliding-mode schemes10, Lyapunov-based formation and path tracking frameworks11,12, fault-tolerant adaptive controllers13, finite-time SMC in 3D14, higher-order terminal sliding modes15, passivity-based and disturbance-rejecting methods16, hybrid SMC–PID controllers17, nonlinear model predictive control with parameter estimation18, and robust depth control via synthesis19.

Finite-time stability ensures convergence within a finite duration20, but its settling time depends on initial conditions. Fixed-time control21 improves this by guaranteeing a uniform upper bound on the convergence time, independent of the initial state, as comprehensively reviewed in Liu et al.22. Nevertheless, in most fixed-time formulations, the convergence time is implicitly bounded and cannot be directly prescribed by the designer. PTC23 further advances this concept by explicitly enforcing the convergence horizon through control parameters. This property is particularly attractive for AUV applications, where synchronization, timing, and energy efficiency are mission-critical.

Recent studies have extended predefined-time control by incorporating adaptive, observer-based, and learning-oriented mechanisms to enhance robustness under uncertainty. For instance, Liu et al.24, proposed an adaptive predefined-time disturbance-observer-based nonsingular sliding mode controller for quadrotor UAVs, enabling adjustable convergence time and effective disturbance rejection, validated through experiments. A related velocity-free adaptive neural–fuzzy predefined-time framework was later introduced for spacecraft attitude control25, combining neural approximation and nonsingular sliding mode design to address uncertainties, disturbances, and actuator saturation.

In the underwater domain, predefined-time concepts have also been explored within more complex optimal and learning-based formulations. Feng et al.26 developed a predefined-time optimal control strategy integrated with actor–critic adaptive dynamic programming for underactuated AUVs, achieving global predefined-time convergence and obstacle avoidance. In parallel, reinforcement-learning-based and multi-agent control approaches have been investigated for marine vehicles27, demonstrating enhanced adaptability and coordination capabilities at the expense of increased computational complexity and reliance on online learning. While these approaches rely on complex learning and optimization architectures, the present work enforces predefined-time convergence through a purely analytical control design without learning or online optimization.

Beyond predefined-time methods, alternative robust control frameworks such as state-filtered disturbance rejection control28, and multilayer neuroadaptive reinforcement learning control29, have been proposed for nonlinear systems subject to uncertainties, offering strong disturbance rejection capabilities through filter-based architectures, but without explicit guarantees on convergence time.

In contrast to the above approaches, the present study adopts an analytically tractable predefined-time formulation that explicitly prescribes the convergence horizon while maintaining a relatively simple control structure. The proposed controller avoids learning-based approximation and disturbance estimation mechanisms, enabling systematic analysis of transient performance and control effort. Moreover, this study employs the experimentally validated hydrodynamic model of the REMUS-100, whose parameters have been established through digital-twin modeling and previous SMC experiments30,31. This provides a realistic and high-fidelity benchmark for evaluating predefined-time and classical SMC under identical conditions.

This paper develops a PTC that guarantees convergence within a user-specified horizon independent of initial conditions. Lyapunov-based stability proofs and comparative results are presented on two representative reference trajectories to assess tracking accuracy, transient response, and control effort across distinct motion profiles.

Conclusion

This paper presents a PTC strategy designed to solve the trajectory tracking problem for AUVs. The core innovation of this approach is its guarantee that the tracking error will converge to zero within a user-specified time horizon, which represents an alternative to traditional SMC whose convergence time is inherently dependent on the initial vehicle state and cannot be pre-set. The performance of the proposed PTC was rigorously evaluated against classical SMC using a high-fidelity, validated model of the REMUS-100 AUV. The comparative analysis on representative trajectories demonstrated that the PTC achieves faster and precisely tunable convergence, particularly for smaller predefined convergence times and within the scope of the studied scenarios. The study also revealed a fundamental design trade-off: enforcing a shorter convergence time requires a higher control effort. These findings offer practical guidance for control system selection. While SMC remains a robust choice for applications where smooth operation is paramount, the PTC provides a viable solution for time-sensitive missions requiring guaranteed synchronization and precision, such as cooperative mapping or obstacle avoidance. Thus, this work establishes PTC as a promising and practical solution for enhancing the performance and reliability of AUV guidance systems.

While the proposed approach demonstrates strong tracking performance and transparent tuning capabilities, several aspects are intentionally left for future investigation. First, the present study focuses on trajectory tracking in obstacle-free environments, without explicitly addressing motion planning or obstacle avoidance, which can be incorporated in future extensions. Second, although the effectiveness of the controller is validated using a high-fidelity and experimentally established AUV model, experimental implementation in real marine environments remains an important direction for future work. Finally, the proposed analytically tractable predefined-time control framework provides a solid foundation for potential integration with learning-based or intelligent methods, which may further enhance adaptability in highly uncertain or dynamic operating conditions.

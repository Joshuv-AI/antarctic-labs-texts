Imlunestrant plus abemaciclib versus fulvestrant plus abemaciclib in ER-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer: an indirect treatment comparison of three phase III trials
Authors: K Jhaveri, FC Bidard, K Kalinsky, P Neven, HS Rugo, SM Tolaney, LM Litchfield, CC von Laue, S Traore, F Sapunar, Y Li, J O’Shaughnessy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe EMBER-3 trial demonstrated significant progression-free survival (PFS) benefit for imlunestrant + abemaciclib versus imlunestrant alone in patients with estrogen receptor (ER)-positive/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative advanced breast cancer (ABC) with disease recurrence/progression during prior aromatase inhibitor ± cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 inhibitor (AI ± CDK4/6i) treatment. The phase III MONARCH 2 and postMONARCH trials demonstrated the superiority of fulvestrant + abemaciclib versus fulvestrant in CDK4/6i-naïve and pretreated patients, respectively. The efficacy of imlunestrant + abemaciclib versus fulvestrant + abemaciclib has not been directly evaluated in randomized controlled trials. In the absence of head-to-head trial data, indirect treatment comparison (ITC) analyses allow fair evaluation of between-trial efficacy. This study aimed to determine the relative efficacy of imlunestrant + abemaciclib versus fulvestrant + abemaciclib through ITC of the EMBER-3, MONARCH 2, and postMONARCH trials.Materials and MethodsTo compare investigator-assessed PFS between imlunestrant + abemaciclib and fulvestrant + abemaciclib, three ITC methods—Bucher, matching-adjusted indirect comparison (MAIC), and propensity score matching (PSM)—were employed using individually matched patient-level data from the EMBER-3, MONARCH 2, and postMONARCH trials. Ten prognostic and predictive factors were selected as baseline covariates. The standard Bucher method had no population adjustment, whereas PSM and MAIC were population-adjusted. The MAIC analysis adjusted the pooled MONARCH 2/postMONARCH population to match the EMBER-3 population. The PSM matched the propensity score between the EMBER-3 and pooled MONARCH 2/postMONARCH populations.ResultsBaseline characteristics were well balanced across adjusted populations by MAIC and PSM. Though not powered for formal hypothesis testing, PFS favored imlunestrant + abemaciclib versus fulvestrant + abemaciclib across all three ITC methods. Hazard ratios (95% confidence intervals) for Bucher, MAIC, and PSM were 0.77 (0.58-1.04), 0.77 (0.55-1.06), and 0.83 (0.56-1.22), respectively.ConclusionIn this ITC analysis of patient-level data from three phase III trials, although not powered for formal hypothesis testing, the all-oral targeted combination therapy imlunestrant + abemaciclib showed a consistent numerical reduction in the risk of disease progression or death compared with fulvestrant + abemaciclib in patients with ER-positive/HER2-negative ABC previously treated with endocrine therapy ± CDK4/6i.Key words: abemaciclib, EMBER-3, fulvestrant, imlunestrant, indirect treatment comparison, progression-free survival

Introduction

Despite meaningful advances achieved with aromatase inhibitors (AIs), either alone or in combination with a cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 inhibitor (CDK4/6i), many estrogen receptor (ER)-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative breast tumors develop resistance.1 Selective ER degraders (SERDs) induce ER alpha degradation and may help overcome tumor resistance to AIs and selective ER modulators.2, 3, 4

Fulvestrant was the first SERD broadly approved as monotherapy and in combination with inhibitors of the phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase/protein kinase B/mammalian target of rapamycin pathway or CDK4/6.5, 6, 7, 8 Due to its poor oral bioavailability, fulvestrant is administered as an intramuscular injection, which limits the dose and thus the dose-dependent efficacy.9 Additionally, the injection can be painful and burdensome for patients, making oral treatment options preferable. Next-generation oral SERDs are currently in advanced stages of clinical development, including the approved SERD elacestrant.10

Imlunestrant is an orally bioavailable, brain-penetrant SERD with pure antagonistic properties that result in sustained inhibition of ER-dependent gene transcription and cell growth.11,12 Abemaciclib is an oral CDK4/6i approved for patients with hormone receptor (HR)-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer (ABC) in combination with an AI, fulvestrant, or as monotherapy, and for patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative early breast cancer in combination with endocrine therapy (ET).13,14

In the phase III EMBER-3 study, patients with ER-positive, HER2-negative ABC and disease recurrence or progression on prior AI alone or in combination with a CDK4/6i received imlunestrant monotherapy, standard-of-care (SOC) ET (fulvestrant or exemestane), or imlunestrant + abemaciclib. Imlunestrant monotherapy significantly improved progression-free survival (PFS) versus SOC ET in patients with tumors harboring ESR1 mutations (ESR1m) [5.5 versus 3.8 months; hazard ratio (HR) = 0.62, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.46-0.82, P < 0.001], without a significant PFS increase in the overall population (5.6 versus 5.5 months; HR = 0.87, 95% CI 0.72-1.04). The combination of imlunestrant and abemaciclib significantly improved PFS versus imlunestrant alone in all patients (9.4 versus 5.5 months; HR = 0.57, 95% CI 0.44-0.73, P < 0.001), regardless of ESR1m status.15

Although the superior benefit of imlunestrant monotherapy over SOC ET in patients with ESR1m tumors supports its contribution to the combination with abemaciclib in these patients, the similar PFS of imlunestrant and SOC ET in the overall population makes its contribution to the combination less clear in all patients. Additionally, EMBER-3 lacked a direct comparison between imlunestrant + abemaciclib versus fulvestrant + abemaciclib, as fulvestrant + abemaciclib was not considered SOC ET after a CDK4/6i at the time of study design.

The phase III MONARCH 2 and postMONARCH studies demonstrated significantly improved PFS with abemaciclib + fulvestrant versus fulvestrant alone in CDK4/6i-naïve and pretreated patients, respectively (MONARCH 2: HR = 0.539, 95% CI 0.449-0.647, P < 0.0001; postMONARCH: HR = 0.73, 95% CI 0.57-0.95, P = 0.017).8,16,17

In the absence of a direct comparison, we conducted an indirect treatment comparison (ITC) between the pooled fulvestrant + abemaciclib arms in MONARCH 2 and postMONARCH (M2/pM) and the imlunestrant + abemaciclib arm in EMBER-3 to provide relevant data to inform clinical decisions. We employed three well-established ITC methods: the Bucher method, matching-adjusted indirect comparison (MAIC), and propensity score matching (PSM). The Bucher method does not adjust for population differences, assuming the studies are clinically and methodically similar, whereas MAIC and PSM adjust for population differences between studies, making the populations more comparable.

Methods

Overview

The EMBER-3 (NCT04975308), MONARCH 2 (NCT02107703), and postMONARCH (NCT05169567) trials have been previously described (Table 1).8,15,17 Individual patient data were available for all patients. The inclusion criteria across the three studies were similar, with the primary distinction being prior CDK4/6i exposure. Abemaciclib and fulvestrant doses were consistent. Additionally, the three trials employed similar stratification variables and a common primary endpoint: investigator-assessed PFS using the same imaging schedule and assessment method (Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors, version 1.1).

Given the key differences between the trials regarding prior CDK4/6i exposure, pooled data from the M2/pM trials enabled comparison with a similar patient population to the EMBER-3 study, increasing the sample size. To minimize the bias from potentially imbalanced prognostic factors or treatment effect predictors, we employed three ITC methods to compare the investigator-assessed PFS between imlunestrant + abemaciclib and fulvestrant + abemaciclib, which were the Bucher method, MAIC, and PSM (see Supplementary Material for details, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106091).

Statistical methods

The ITC assessed the relative effect of imlunestrant + abemaciclib versus abemaciclib + fulvestrant. Under the similarity assumption and with a common comparator (SOC ET and fulvestrant were treated as common comparators, with 88% of patients receiving fulvestrant and 12% receiving exemestane in the EMBER-3 study), we conducted a standard ITC based on Bucher et al.18 The key assumption for the Bucher method was that the trials were clinically and methodologically similar.

Comparability was assessed by evaluating consistency across PICOS (population, intervention, comparator, outcome, and study design). The EMBER-3 trial was determined to be comparable to the pooled M2/pM trials, except for populations where imbalances in the distribution of patient characteristics were identified, such as prior CDK4/6i exposure (Table 1). Differences in patient characteristics that were potential treatment effect modifiers could undermine the validity of the standard ITC using the Bucher method. Therefore, we conducted two population-adjusted ITC analyses, MAIC and PSM, to compare investigator-assessed PFS between imlunestrant + abemaciclib and abemaciclib + fulvestrant. Both methods adjusted for population differences between EMBER-3 and pooled M2/pM before carrying out the standard Bucher ITC. We used data from the three trials, EMBER-3 and pooled M2/pM, with the common control arm (fulvestrant and SOC ET) serving as the anchor point.

The MAIC applied patient-level weighting to match the mean baseline covariates of the pooled M2/pM trials to the EMBER-3 trial.19,20

PSM matched the propensity score between the EMBER-3 population and the pooled M2/pM population using the nearest neighbor matching method. Propensity scores were derived by logistic regression (with treatment arm as the outcome variable and baseline covariates as predictors).21

The relative treatment effect was measured using the HR under the proportional hazards’ assumption. Details of the Bucher method, MAIC, and PSM can be found in Supplementary Figure S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106091. Effective sample sizes (ESS) were calculated for each method (Supplementary Material, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106091).

Baseline covariates (prognostic factors or effect predictors) used in MAIC and PSM were prespecified based on literature and clinical expertise. Covariates were further confirmed based on statistical assessment of the baseline characteristics in EMBER-3. These included age group (<65 or ≥65 years), race (White, Asian, or other), geographical region (Asia, Europe, North America, or other), baseline Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (0 or 1), number of metastatic sites (1, 2, or ≥3), measurable disease (yes or no), menopausal status (premenopausal or postmenopausal), visceral metastasis (yes or no), prior CDK4/6i in any setting (yes or no), and prior ET in advanced setting (yes or no).

Results

Patient characteristics

From the M2/pM trials, 409 patients received fulvestrant + placebo and 628 received fulvestrant + abemaciclib. In EMBER-3, 426 concurrently enrolled patients received either SOC ET (n = 213) or imlunestrant + abemaciclib (n = 213).

In total, 473 patients from M2/pM (abemaciclib + fulvestrant, n = 242; fulvestrant, n = 231) were matched in the MAIC with the 426 patients from EMBER-3 (imlunestrant + abemaciclib, n = 213; SOC ET, n = 213). A total of 343 patients (imlunestrant + abemaciclib, n = 169; SOC ET, n = 174) from EMBER-3 were matched by PSM with 343 patients from the pooled M2/pM population (abemaciclib + fulvestrant, n = 169; fulvestrant, n = 174).

Baseline summary statistics before and after population adjustment using MAIC and PSM are presented in Table 2. Before adjustment, patient characteristics in EMBER-3 and the pooled M2/pM trials were generally balanced for most covariates, including region, number of metastatic sites, measurable disease, and visceral metastasis. Differences were observed in prior CDK4/6i exposure (65% versus 36%) and prior ET in the advanced setting (70% versus 60%).

Indirect treatment comparison

The ITC results are summarized in Figure 1. Across all three methods, imlunestrant + abemaciclib demonstrated a consistent numerical reduction in the risk of disease progression or death compared to fulvestrant + abemaciclib. The Bucher method produced an HR of 0.77 (95% CI 0.58-1.04), whereas MAIC analysis showed consistent results with a PFS HR of 0.77 (95% CI 0.55-1.06), favoring imlunestrant + abemaciclib. Discussion

The EMBER-3 trial demonstrated a significant PFS benefit for imlunestrant + abemaciclib compared with imlunestrant alone in patients with ER-positive, HER2-negative ABC who experienced disease recurrence or progression during or after treatment with an AI with or without a CDK4/6i. This led to the recent United States Food and Drug Administration approval of imlunestrant for the treatment of adults with ER-positive, HER2-negative ESR1m ABC with disease progression following at least one line of ET.22

However, imlunestrant + abemaciclib has not been directly compared with fulvestrant + abemaciclib in a randomized controlled trial. In the absence of a head-to-head trial comparing the abemaciclib combinations, this exploratory analysis addresses this question using three complementary ITC methods applied to individual patient data from EMBER-3, MONARCH 2, and postMONARCH. Eligibility criteria and patient populations included in these randomized phase III trials were generally comparable and further aligned using established matching techniques.

Across all three ITC methodologies—Bucher, MAIC, and PSM—imlunestrant + abemaciclib was associated with consistent numerical reduction in the risk of disease progression or death compared with fulvestrant + abemaciclib in patients with hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative ABC following prior ET with or without a CDK4/6i. These findings provide important insights into the potential of imlunestrant as a potent ET partner in combination with CDK4/6 inhibition.

At the time of the EMBER-3 study design, fulvestrant monotherapy was a widely utilized SOC ET in the second-line setting, and there were no phase III data supporting the use of fulvestrant + abemaciclib as SOC therapy in patients previously treated with a CDK4/6i. Consequently, single-agent fulvestrant or exemestane were considered SOC as comparators in EMBER-3. Since that time and after EMBER-3 enrollment was completed, the postMONARCH trial established the efficacy of fulvestrant + abemaciclib in this setting, validating its relevance as a comparator in a retrospective analysis such as this.

The comparative analysis of pM and EMBER-3 underscores the importance of considering baseline patient characteristics when interpreting trial outcomes. Although both studies enrolled patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative ABC, pM restricted enrollment to patients with disease progression on prior CDK4/6i. In contrast, EMBER-3 permitted enrollment of patients without prior CDK4/6i exposure, representing ∼40% of the study population. Despite these differences, the median PFS for fulvestrant was relatively consistent across both trials [5.3 months in the pM trial versus 5.5 months in the EMBER-3 study (imlunestrant versus SOC ET in the intent-to-treat population)].15,16 This counterintuitive similarity suggests that underlying baseline differences and unmeasured prognostic factors may be affecting observed outcomes. Differences in baseline factors were adjusted for in the MAIC and PSM, whereas the anchored comparison mitigated potential effects of unmeasured prognostic factors, thus enabling fairer comparison between trials. These findings highlight the importance of the appropriate methodology in contextualizing cross-trial comparisons and reinforce the need to account for heterogeneity in patient populations when evaluating therapeutic outcomes.

The strength of this analysis lies in the availability of individual patient data across all three trials, which enabled robust adjustment for baseline differences and the application of multiple ITC methodologies. Pooling data from M2/pM allowed for the inclusion of both CDK4/6i-naïve and pretreated patients, enhancing the comparability with EMBER-3 and increasing the ESS. The consistency of results across all three ITC methods reinforces the reliability of the observed treatment effect. EMBER-3 is an important phase III study with positive data for an oral SERD in combination with a CDK4/6i after ET with or without a CDK4/6i, with benefit seen irrespective of ESR1m status. Considering that combination therapy is generally preferred in clinical practice for patients with disease progression on first-line CDK4/6i therapy, this ITC provides timely comparative insights. This is further supported by a robust safety profile for both imlunestrant monotherapy and in combination with abemaciclib, where adverse events were generally low grade, reversible, occurred early in treatment, and resulted in few treatment discontinuations. Further, the patient-reported outcome data from EMBER-3 highlighted injection site reaction as a clinically relevant adverse event for patients treated with fulvestrant (reported by 72% of patients at any time, with 47% experiencing an injection site reaction for most of their time receiving fulvestrant). Notably, global health status and quality of life were maintained across treatment arms in EMBER-3.23 A comparative analysis of toxicities was not conducted in this analysis because the three trials had considerably different follow-up durations (14 months in EMBER-3, 80 months in M2, and 13 months in pM), which could not be appropriately adjusted for in the safety comparison and would impact interpretability.

Although this analysis was not powered for formal hypothesis testing, and limitations inherent to ITC methods remain—such as the inability to adjust for unmeasured confounders—the consistent directionality of the findings, across all three methods, supports the hypothesis that imlunestrant may offer added clinical benefit when combined with abemaciclib. Prior ITCs comparing CDK4/6i (e.g. palbociclib versus ribociclib versus abemaciclib) have established a precedent for the use of such methods in this disease setting and lend methodological credibility to the current approach.24,25 Additionally, because of the smaller sample sizes in subgroups, the ITC analyses were not designed to support subgroup analyses by ESR1m status. ESR1m status was also not available for all patients analyzed, particularly in MONARCH 2. However, regardless of ESR1m status, a consistent treatment effect for the comparison of abemaciclib combination therapy versus endocrine monotherapy has previously been reported in each trial (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106091).15,26,27

More broadly, ongoing and recently reported phase III studies of oral SERD-based combinations continue to underscore efforts to define the clinical value of oral SERDs as the endocrine backbone in targeted therapy regimens and to inform first- and second-line management of ABC.28, 29, 30, 31 Consideration of factors such as prior therapies, disease burden, baseline comorbidities, toxicities, routes of administration, and patient preference will be essential to guide therapy selection and sequencing.

In summary, this exploratory analysis addresses a clinically relevant question and fills a knowledge gap by providing robust comparative evidence on the efficacy of imlunestrant + abemaciclib versus fulvestrant + abemaciclib from three phase III trials. These findings support imlunestrant as a highly effective ET partner in combination with abemaciclib, offering patients with endocrine-resistant ER-positive, HER2-negative ABC in an all-oral targeted combination therapy option.

Data sharing

Eli Lilly provides access to all individual participant data collected during the trial, after anonymization, except for pharmacokinetic or genetic data. Data are available to request 6 months after the indication studied has been approved in the United States and EU and after primary publication acceptance, whichever is later. No expiration date of data requests is currently set once data is made available. Access is provided after a proposal has been approved by an independent review committee identified for this purpose and after receiving a signed data sharing agreement. Data and documents, including the study protocol, statistical analysis plan, clinical study report, and blank or annotated case report forms, will be provided in a secure data sharing environment. For details on submitting a request, see the instructions provided at www.vivli.org.

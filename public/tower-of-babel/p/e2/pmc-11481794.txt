Blood-rsCDM: a new rapid and simplified carbapenemase detection method for detecting carbapenemases in Enterobacterales directly from positive blood cultures
Authors: Quanfeng Liao, Xingqin Yi, Yu Yuan, Weili Zhang, Jin Deng, Siying Wu, Ya Liu, Mei Kang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveWe aim to validate and evaluate a new rapid and simplified method, called Blood-rsCDM, for the detection and characterization of carbapenemase using 3-aminophenylboronic acid (APBA) and ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) β-lactamase inhibitors from positive blood cultures.MethodWe utilized a panel of 172 Enterobacterales strains, including blaKPC (77), blaNDM (48), blaIMP (9), blaVIM (2), blaOXA-181 (2), blaKPC and blaNDM (6), as well as 28 carbapenem-susceptible Enterobacterales isolates, to assess the performance of Blood-rsCDM and the EDTA-carbapenem inactivation method (eCIM). Carbapenemase class was determined using specific inhibitors at 4 h and 6 h by Blood-rsCDM.ResultsBlood-rsCDM exhibited a sensitivity of 97.9% at both time points, with a specificity of 100%, regardless of the culture duration. The sensitivity of eCIM was 94.4%, with a specificity of 100%. Blood-rsCDM accurately characterized KPC-producing isolates as 77/77, metallo-β-lactamases (MBLs) as 58/59, and KPC and NDM carbapenemases as 6/6 at 4 h. There was no difference in results between the 4 h and 6 h time points. However, Blood-rsCDM could not differentiate OXA-181-producing strains. For eCIM, the characterization numbers for KPC-, OXA-181-, and MBLs-producing strains were 77/77, 2/2, and 57/59, respectively, but it failed to detect the coproduction of KPC and NDM isolates.ConclusionBlood-rsCDM accurately discriminates carbapenemase within 4 h and is capable of directly differentiating multi-enzyme (KPC and NDM) presence from positive blood culture broths. Therefore, Blood-rsCDM represents a rapid, simple, easy-to-read, and accurate tool that can be utilized in resource-limited settings.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12866-024-03553-5.Keywords: Phenotypic assay, Blood-rsCDM, Carbapenemase-producing Enterobacterales, Carbapenemase

Introduction

Bloodstream infections (BSIs) are serious medical conditions associated with high morbidity and mortality, particularly for BSI due to carbapenemase-producing Enterobacterales (CPE) are accompanied by less favorable outcomes compared to non-carbapenemase-producing-carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales [1]. Surveillance data from WHO/EARS236 Net showed that European countries with a carbapenem resistance rate of ≥ 25% among K. pneumoniae blood, a higher percentage of carbapenem resistance among K. pneumoniae BSI isolates (35%) was reported from China and from West and South India (> 50%) [2]. Although blood culture remains the gold standard method for BSI diagnosis, its main limitation is the long turnaround time. Furthermore, early and efficient antimicrobial agents could save lives of critically ill patients [3]. Therefore, accurate and convenient method of detecting the carbapenemase directly from positive blood cultures is essential to help physicians select antibacterial drµgs to treat BSIs with CPE.

According to the Ambler classification, carbapenemases can be categorized into three classes of β-lactamases: class A carbapenemases, which include KPC, IMI, and GES; class B carbapenemases, which include NDM, IMP, and VIM; and class D carbapenemases, which include OXA-48-like enzymes [4, 5]. The choice of antimicrobial therapy can be guided by the carbapenemase class, as certain antibacterial agents (like ceftazidime-avibactam) are effective against CPE isolates that produce class A and OXA-48-type carbapenemases, but not Ambler class B enzymes [6]. Therefore, rapid and accurate detection and differentiation of carbapenemase types are crucial for guiding effective antimicrobial therapy.

Several rapid methods, including genotypic, immunologic, matrix-assisted laser desorption ionization–time of flight mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS), and phenotypic tests, have been developed and implemented for direct testing from positive blood cultures [7–16]. While these tests demonstrate excellent diagnostic characteristics, their limitations often restrict their use in resource-limited settings. Genotypic and immunologic tests require significant financial resources, and the sample preparation for MALDI-TOF MS is complex and may necessitate specialized reagents. Although phenotypic tests are cost-effective and suitable for basic laboratories, they still require 6–24 h and are unable to classify co-expression of enzymes (such as KPC and NDM) [13, 14].

Building upon previous research [17], we developed a new, rapid, and simplified carbapenemase detection method called Blood-rsCDM. This method utilizes imipenem supplemented with 3-aminophenylboronic acid (APBA) and ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) to accurately identify different carbapenemase classes in CPE from positive blood cultures within a timeframe of 4–6 h.

Materials and methods

Bacteria

A total of 172 nonduplicate well-characterized Enterobacterales strains were isolated from three centers: Department of Laboratory Medicine, West China Hospital Sichuan University (79), Center of Infectious Diseases, West China Hospital Sichuan University (84) and Wuhan Fourth Hospital (9) (Table 1). The panel consisted of Klebsiella pneumoniae (125), Escherichia coli (31), Enterobacter horii (8), Enterobacter cloacae (7) and Citrobacter koseri (1). Of these, 144 strains expressed the carbapenemase genes blaKPC − 2 (77/144, 53.5%), blaIMP − 4 (7/144, 4.9%), blaIMP − 1 (1/144, 0.7%), blaIMP − 8 (1/144, 0.7%), blaVIM − 1 (2/144, 1.4%), blaNDM − 1 (12/144, 8.3%), blaNDM − 5 (35/144, 24.3%), blaNDM − 7 (1/144, 0.7%), blaKPC-2 and blaNDM-5 (3/144, 2.1%), blaKPC-2 and blaNDM-1 (3/144, 2.1%), and blaOXA − 181 (2/144, 1.4%). The remaining 20 K. pneumoniae and 8 E. coli strains were susceptible to carbapenem. All strains were identified at the species level by MALDI-TOF MS (Bruker Daltonics, Bremen, Germany) using a Vitek 2 compact instrument (BioMerieux, Marcy-l’Étoile, France). Isolates from our laboratory were previously characterized by PCR [18].

Blood-rsCDM

Prior to performing the Blood-rsCDM, isolates stored at − 80 °C were cultured at 35 ± 2 °C for 18–24 h on blood agar (Autobio, Zhengzhou, China), these cultures were used to generate a suspension of organism in sterile saline equivalent to a 0.5 McFarland standard. The bacterial suspension was serially diluted in sterile saline and 103 CFU/mL injected into a clinical aerobic or anaerobic bottle (BACT/ALERT® FA/FN Plus, BioMerieux, Marcy-l’Étoile, France) that remained negative for growth after 5 days of incubation. Next, bottles were incubated in the BacT/ALERT blood culture system until the flask was flagged as positive.

Upon signaling positive, 400 µL of the mixed positive blood culture was added to MH agar plates (Autobio, Zhengzhou, China), which were allowed to dry for 3–5 min. Four 10 µg imipenem disks (named A, B, C, D) were placed on MH agar plates, followed by the addition of 10 µL of two different β-lactamase inhibitors: 50 mg/mL APBA (Macklin, Shanghai, China) and 0.1 mol/L EDTA (Solarbio, Beijing, China). Namely: A, imipenem; B, imipenem + 10 µL APBA; C, imipenem + 10 µL EDTA; D, imipenem + 10 µL APBA + 10 µL EDTA. The plates were incubated at 35 ± 2℃, and zone diameter was measured at 4 h and 6 h. According to previous researches and quality control results [17, 19], an increase ≥ 5 mm in zone diameter around disks containing β-lactamase inhibitors, as compared with the disk with imipenem alone, was considered a positive result. Namely, isolates with an increase ≥ 5 mm for APBA were considered as serine β-lactamase producers, an increase ≥ 5 mm only for EDTA were considered likely producers of MBL, and the isolate carrying both MBL and serine carbepenemase was considered for disk D vs. disk A ≥ 5 mm and a mismatch in the criteria of single enzyme. For the Blood-rsCDM test, pinpoint colonies within any zone of inhibition were ignored. In order to find the most suitable dosage, we tried to repeat the experiment with 200 µL, 300 µL, 400 µL, 600 µL and 800 µL positive blood culture broths. The negative control (Klebsiella pneumoniae ATCC BAA-1706) and positive strains (KPC-producing Klebsiella pneumoniae ATCC BAA-1705 and NDM-producing Klebsiella pneumoniae ATCC BAA-2146) were analyzed as described above. eCIM

After recording the zone diameter of the Blood-rsCDM at 4 h, bacteria from the MH plate were selected, and the eCIM test was conducted simultaneously. The eCIM was performed and analyzed following CLSI recommendations [20].

Statistical analysis

The difference in results between Blood-rsCDM and the two existing methods (sequencing and eCIM) was examined using the chi-square test, and Kappa coefficients were calculated to indicate the level of agreement. Data analysis was conducted using SPSS 22.0. The significance level was set at p < 0.05.

Results

We performed the Blood-rsCDM test using five different volumes of positive cultures (200, 300, 400, 600, and 800 µL). At 4 h, robust bacterial growth was observed without any noticeable effects on erythrocytes when using a 400 µL mixture broth.

The results are presented in Table 2, and specific results can be found at Table S1. Out of the 144 tested CPE strains, the Blood-rsCDM test yielded positive results for 141 CPE isolates and negative results for all 28 carbapenem-sensitive Enterobacterales isolates. This corresponds to a diagnostic sensitivity of 97.9% and specificity of 100%. All CPE strains were accurately classified according to the Ambler system using Blood-rsCDM, except for one OXA-181-producing K. pneumoniae and one OXA-181-producing Escherichia coli strain, which showed negative results. Blood-rsCDM correctly identified 77 (100% agreement) class A (KPC) and 58 (98.3% agreement) class B carbapenemases out of 77 and 59 isolates, respectively, at 4 h and 6 h. Additionally, Blood-rsCDM accurately classified the six strains that exhibited multiple enzymes (KPC and MBL). Similar to Blood-rsCDM, eCIM also demonstrated excellent ability to identify KPC- (100%), MBLs- (96.7%), and OXA-181-producing (100%) isolates, but it failed to detect strains that co-produced KPC and NDM carbapenemases.

Discussion

In this study, we present the diagnostic performance of the Blood-rsCDM, which is capable of directly detecting carbapenemase activity from positive blood cultures of Enterobacterales and differentiating serine carbapenemases, metallo-β-lactamases (MBLs), and multi-enzyme (KPC and NDM) producers within a timeframe of 4–6 h. The assay is derived from the rsCDM test [17] and exhibits a similar diagnostic sensitivity of 97.9%, the Blood-rsCDM did not detect any carbapenemase from carbapenemase-negative bacteria, thereby showing a specificity of 100%. The test utilizes affordable materials that are accessible to all clinical microbiology laboratories, including those in resource-constrained settings. Importantly, the Blood-rsCDM does not require centrifugation or mechanical lysis methodology for processing the blood culture, simplifying its implementation and use.

Several phenotypic assays for directly detecting carbapenemase-producing strains from positive blood culture broths have been reported. The FilmArray, a molecular biological technique, showed 100% sensitivity in detecting the KPC gene in Klebsiella pneumoniae strains within approximately one hour. However, it was costly and required specialized equipment, making it unsuitable for basic laboratories [8]. MALDI-TOF MS, while exhibiting excellent sensitivity and timeliness in detecting the KPC gene, is not suitable for laboratories lacking MALDI-TOF MS equipment [12]. The NG-Test Carba 5, an immunologic approach capable of identifying five prevalent carbapenemase genes, and preparing the bacterial pellet from positive blood cultures is laborious and time-expensive [9, 10, 16]. Blood-mCIM and dBLIM, which utilized readily available reagents, demonstrated a diagnostic sensitivity and specificity of 100%, respectively. However, they were unable to distinguish between different types of carbapenemase enzymes, which is critical for precise antimicrobial therapy [14, 15]. The deBLIM test showed good diagnostic characteristics for distinguishing serine carbapenemases and MBLs directly from blood cultures within 6 h, but it involved several pre-operation steps and required the preparation of the E. coli ATCC 25,922 indicator in advance. Additionally, it could not differentiate strains that produce multiple enzymes [13].

However, the Blood-rsCDM demonstrated reliable results for the classification of carbapenemases, including KPC, NDM, VIM, and IMP. Notably, KPC and NDM carbapenemases have emerged as serious public health threats in recent years [21, 22]. Vásquez-Ponce et al. demonstrated a simple and inexpensive method for the presumptive detection of co-expression of KPC-2 and NDM-1 in members of the K. pneumoniae complex, but the method required 18–24 h based on pure cultured bacteria [23]. However, our results, consistent with those of Vásquez-Ponce et al., revealed that the eCIM method recommended by the CLSI failed to detect the co-production of KPC and NDM carbapenemases. The eCIM misclassified six co-expressing KPC and NDM strains as five serine carbapenemase-positive and one MBL-positive, which could lead to incorrect clinical treatment. The Blood-rsCDM provided a solution to this limitation. Regarding IMP strains, one K. pneumoniae strain carrying blaIMP-4 could not be identified by the Blood-rsCDM, and two K. pneumoniae strains harboring blaIMP-8 and blaIMP-4 could not be differentiated by eCIM. Further studies should include more isolates to confirm the diagnostic accuracy of the Blood-rsCDM.

The results of the Blood-rsCDM could be interpreted at 4 h. During the disk diffusion process, bacterial growth is inhibited at a specific antibiotic dose, and when higher concentrations of bacteria are inoculated, bacterial colonies become visible in shorter incubation durations. Complete inhibition requires a certain amount of incubation time, and after 4 h, some strains may start to form a thin biofilm. In such cases, the zone diameter should be measured against light, taking into account any moss-like growth. As the incubation duration increases to 6 h, the film-like growth significantly decreases, making interpretation of the results easier. This can be observed through the increase in the subtraction diameter of the inhibitory zones. Based on these considerations, we ultimately decided to perform the Blood-rsCDM using 400 µL of positive blood cultures.

Despite the favorable performance of the Blood-rsCDM, our study has several limitations. Firstly, due to local epidemiological conditions, we only tested the major carbapenemase types, and bacterial strains expressing carbapenemases other than KPC or NDM were either limited (such as blaOXA-48, blaVIM, blaIMP, and blaKPC + blaNDM) or not evaluated (such as blaGES, etc.). Secondly, the Blood-rsCDM was not suitable for detecting CPE isolates carrying OXA-48-like genes, and alternative methods should be used for the detection of such strains. Thirdly, we randomly selected aerobic or anaerobic bottles for the test. However, Bianco et al. [13] revealed discrepancies in the detection of MBLs between aerobic and anaerobic bottles. Further research is needed to assess the relationship between the findings of the Blood-rsCDM and culture bottles.

Conclusions

In conclusion, we have described the characterization of the Blood-mCIM, which enables the phenotypic detection and differentiation of carbapenemase activity directly from positive blood culture broths. This assay provides several advantages in terms of diagnostic accuracy, labor intensity, cost, and turnaround time, allowing for quicker therapeutic decision-making and infection control. It is a user-friendly test that can be performed and interpreted easily, requiring only basic equipment commonly found in all clinical microbiology laboratories, regardless of the setting.

The Editor,

We thank the authors of the Letter to Editor for their thoughtful comments and for highlighting the importance of transitioning lung ultrasound (LUS) from a tool of “diagnostic concordance” to an “implementation-grade” modality in intensive care. We agree that the true value of LUS lies in its potential to become a standardized, scalable platform that improves patient-centered outcomes.

In response to the points raised, we would like to offer the following clarifications and perspectives based on our study:

Standardization and Training

The correspondents correctly identify that LUS performance is sensitive to operator training and protocol consistency. In our study, we addressed this by ensuring all LUS examinations were performed by investigators with advanced training (>50 supervised scans), achieving a high inter-observer reliability (Kappa = 0.86). This underscores the necessity of setting minimum competency standards to move toward “decision-grade” implementation.

Protocol Superiority: 6-zone vs 12-zone

A key finding of our work was that the extended 12-zone protocol significantly improved the level of agreement with high-resolution computed tomography (HRCT) from “moderate” (Kappa = 0.52) to “substantial” (Kappa = 0.81). This was particularly evident in detecting consolidations, where the 12-zone protocol reached a sensitivity of 0.84 and an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.92. We believe this comprehensive approach is a vital step toward the “reproducible workflows” advocated by the correspondents to eliminate “blind spots” in the posterior lung.

Understanding Limitations

We concur that acknowledging LUS limitations is essential. Our study quantified a “blind zone” for ultrasound—specifically a negative predictive value (NPV) of 0.61 for consolidation in the 12-zone protocol—reminding clinicians that while LUS is excellent for “ruling in” pathology, HRCT remains the gold standard for “ruling out” deep-seated or perihilar lesions that do not reach the pleura.

Future Directions

The suggestion to focus future ICU research on patient-centered outcomes—such as ventilator-free days and reduced ICU length of stay—is well taken. Our direct head-to-head comparison with HRCT provides the mathematical foundation necessary to justify these larger, implementation-focused trials.

In conclusion, we appreciate the praise for our work and share the vision of transforming LUS into an equity-promoting, high-yield diagnostic pillar that enhances patient safety globally.

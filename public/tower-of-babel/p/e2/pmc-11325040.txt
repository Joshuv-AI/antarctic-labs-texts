Measurement of the magnetic octupole susceptibility of PrV2Al20
Authors: Linda Ye, Matthew E Sorensen, Maja D Bachmann, Ian R Fisher
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractRevealing the presence of magnetic octupole order and associated octupole fluctuations in solids is a highly challenging task due to the lack of simple external fields that can couple to magnetic octupoles. Here, we demonstrate a methodology for probing the magnetic octupole susceptibility of a candidate material, PrV2Al20, using a product of magnetic field Hi and shear strain ϵjk as a composite effective field, while employing an adiabatic elastocaloric effect to probe the response. We observe Curie-Weiss behavior in the obtained octupolar susceptibility down to approximately 3 K. Although octupole order does not appear to be the leading multipolar channel in PrV2Al20, our results nevertheless reveal the presence of strong magnetic octupole fluctuations and hence demonstrate that octupole order is at least a competing state. More broadly, our results highlight how anisotropic strain can be combined with magnetic fields to probe elusive ‘hidden’ electronic orders.Subject terms: Magnetic properties and materials, Phase transitions and critical phenomena

Introduction

Within the Landau paradigm, generic electronic and magnetic ordered states are characterized by symmetry-breaking order parameters, and the electromagnetic multipole expansion provides a powerful framework to further characterize these. Ordered states based on electric and magnetic dipoles are common, and the corresponding order parameters can be readily coupled to, and manipulated by, uniform electric and magnetic fields respectively. In centrosymmetric systems, the magnetic octupole is the next-allowed magnetic multipolar degree of freedom; it is distinctive from electrical quadrupoles - another higher-order electromagnetic multipoles in principle also allowed in this context - by its broken time-reversal-symmetry. Initially discussed as a ‘hidden’ degree of freedom based on strong spin-orbit-coupled localized electron orbitals1–8 and therein characterized by equivalent total angular momentum operators to the cubic order9,10, magnetic octupoles have more recently witnessed a growing application as a notion in describing topological antiferromagnets with noncollinear spin structures11–15, and spin polarization in the electronic structure of altermagnets16,17. The difficulty of generating the most natural conjugate field for these octupolar moments –i.e. microscopic magnetic fields with octupolar angular distribution– makes the experimental detection and control of such orders and associated fluctuations generally challenging. To date, limited tools have been made available for the detection of magnetic octupoles, and experimental signatures of octupole moments have been limited to symmetry and form factor analysis of resonant X-ray and neutron scattering experiments5–7,18. This motivates the development of novel experimental methods to couple to and probe these elusive degrees of freedom.

In this work, we experimentally demonstrate a thermodynamic probe of magnetic octupolar fluctuations in the cubic compound PrV2Al20. PrV2Al20 belongs to a family of praseodymium-based cubic cage compounds PrT2X20 (T = Ir, Rh, X = Zn; T = V, Ti, X = Al)19–25. In PrT2X20 the J = 4 4f2 state of Pr3+ takes a doublet crystal field ground state in the local Td environment with Γ3 symmetry25. The Γ3 non-Kramers doublet26–28 is non-magnetic, such that dipolar moments are forbidden within the manifold (⟨Γ3a∣Ji∣Γ3b⟩=0, i = x, y, z, a, b = 1, 2); this makes the family of compounds an ideal platform to study higher-rank multipolar order and fluctuations, as well as their interaction with conduction electrons19–25,29,30. The allowed order parameters within the manifold are two distinct electric quadrupoles (3Jz2−J2, Jx2−Jy2) and a magnetic octupole (JxJyJz¯, equivalent to the notation Txyz11); the former (latter) belong to Γ3 (Γ2) irreducible representations of Td. Low-temperature long-range orders and non-magnetic Kondo effects within the Γ3 manifold (and its associated quadrupolar and octupolar degrees of freedom) have been widely identified across PrT2X2019,20,23,25; additionally, superconductivity within the ordered phases are also observed across the family19,22,23,31, making it an important platform for the study of the interplay between multipolar degrees of freedom and superconductivity. Among PrT2X20, we have specifically chosen PrV2Al20 to probe octupolar fluctuations because of its better-isolated Γ3 crystal electric field (CEF) ground state ( ~40 K25,32) as compared with Pr(Ir,Rh)2Zn20, as well as lower ordering temperature scale as compared to PrTi2Al20.

PrV2Al20 was first studied comparatively with PrTi2Al2020 and the chemical pressure effects from V are expected to result in the observed stronger hybridization between the conduction electron and the localized Γ3 states in PrV2Al20; these observations are largely attributed to be a quadrupolar Kondo interaction20,22,33, while a few recent theoretical works also invoke the role of the octupole degrees of freedom in the Kondo process34,35. At low temperatures, the material was first found to undergo a single transition at 0.6 K20, and later higher quality samples are found to exhibit a succession of two closely-spaced continuous phase transitions at 0.73 K and 0.61 K22,24,36, as well as superconductivity near 50 mK22. While the exact character of these ordered phases is not fully established, the first of these is thought to be an antiferroquadrupolar state given the absence of clear signatures of time-reversal symmetry-breaking37 and its robustness against magnetic fields20,38, while it has been suggested that the second phase transition might have an octupolar character24,39. A number of theories have discussed the successive transitions as a result of competing quadrupolar and octupolar interactions in the system29,30,40–42; a magnetostriction hysteresis observed below the second phase transition, as referenced in Ref. 39, has been proposed as evidence indicative of a ferro octupolar order. Additionally, Ref. 42 suggests that a multi-q antiferro octupolar order (as opposed to a simple antiferroquadrupolar order40) as the low-temperature ground state best accounts for the experimentally observed anisotropic magnetic field diagram of PrV2Al2038,43. These reports suggest that although the higher temperature transition is of quadrupolar nature, the octupolar degrees of freedom remain a strong contender in PrV2Al20.

To couple to the octupolar degrees of freedom, we utilize a combination of strain and magnetic field which selectively (acting together as a composite effective conjugate field) couples bilinearly to magnetic octupoles. In particular, we focus on measuring the octupolar susceptibility, defined analogously to the magnetic susceptibility as the rate of change of the induced octupolar moment in response to the conjugate field. In contrast to measuring the spontaneous octupole moment below a phase transition, measuring the susceptibility for temperatures above a phase transition is especially useful since this is a quantity (a) that is finite for all temperatures, (b) the divergence of which directly attests to the presence of growing fluctuations, and (c) which can be directly compared to other competing symmetry channels (i.e., even in cases where a lower rank multipole ‘wins’, identifying the presence of strong fluctuations of the competing higher-rank multipole state can help determine pathways to realizing such a state in other related materials). As elaborated below, we experimentally identify strong octupolar fluctuations in PrV2Al20 by measuring the temperature dependence of the octupolar susceptibility.

Results

Coupling of composite strain/magnetic field to octupoles in PrV2Al20

Figure 1 illustrates our scheme to couple to the magnetic octupoles with strain and magnetic field. The spatial distribution of the magnetic charge density of the JxJyJz¯ octupolar moments is depicted in Fig. 1(a1, a2) with (a1) and (a2) forming a time-reversal pair. Due to their complex angular distribution, uniform magnetic and strain fields (which linearly couple to lower-order magnetic dipoles and electrical quadrupoles, respectively) are not able to distinguish the positive and negative octupole moments. However, two higher-order quantities are allowed by symmetry to couple bilinearly to the octupole moments39,44: a third-order magnetic field HxHyHz which follows the spatial sign variation of the magnetic charge density of the octupole moments illustrated in Fig. 1(a), and alternatively, a combination of strain and magnetic field of the form Hiϵjk (where i, j, k ∈ x, y, z and i ≠ j ≠ k). The latter field, Hiϵjk requires a magnetic field along one of the principal cubic axes (Hi) and a shear strain in the plane perpendicular to that axis (ϵjk); although neither Hi nor ϵjk alone splits the Γ3 doublet to first order, two equivalent second-order interactions of the octupolar moment with the field (strain) and then strain (field) provide the mechanism by which the combination acts as an effective field. Starting from the positive or negative octupolar moment (Fig. 1(a1, a2)), a vertical magnetic field Hz deforms the magnetic charge density of the octupolar moments via a Zeeman interaction and induces a component with opposite sign of the electric quadrupole Jx Jy + Jy Jx for the two octupoles (Fig. 1(b1, b2))5; the induced quadrupole can then couple to a shear strain ϵxy via magnetoelastic coupling45, differentiating the two octupoles. The same steps can be applied in reverse: a shear strain ϵxy induces a magnetic dipole moment of opposite sign depending on the octupole moment (Fig. 1(c1, c2)), which can then be split by Hz. Since the magnetic octupole, magnetic field and shear strains belong to Γ2, Γ4, and Γ5 irreducible representations of Td point group respectively, the two interaction paths illustrated above can be expressed as Γ4 ⊗ Γ2 = Γ5 and Γ5 ⊗ Γ2 = Γ4, respectively. There are in total three components Hxϵyz, Hyϵzx, and Hzϵxy illustrated in Fig. 1(d) that constitute the composite, effective ‘octupolar’ field Hxϵyz + Hyϵzx + Hzϵxy. We note that although in the present work we focus on Txyz, the composite field scheme laid out above is also applicable to other types of octupole moments.

Experimentally, we opted for a configuration with both magnetic field and uniaxial stress oriented along [111] of the single crystal (Fig. 1(e), more see Supplementary Materials). This configuration maximizes Hxϵyz + Hyϵzx + Hzϵxy and avoids strain or high-order magnetic field components that couple bilinearly to the allowed quadrupoles in the Γ3 CEF manifold (such as ϵxx − ϵyy, 2ϵzz − ϵxx − ϵyy, or alternatively Hx2−Hy2,2Hz2−Hx2−Hy2, see Methods). In other words, this setup is exclusively sensitive to the octupolar degrees of freedom in the system, at least to the extent that the thermal population of the higher energy excited CEF states can be neglected.

At the microscopic level, the coupling of Hiϵjk with the octupolar degrees of freedom within the Γ3 doublet is based on a second-order virtual process involving excited crystal field states, while that of HxHyHz invokes third-order perturbations (see Supplementary Materials). In Fig. 1(f, g) we illustrate the anticipated energy splitting Δ within the ground state doublet induced by the presence of strain and/or magnetic field, along paths in the field-strain phase space depicted in Fig. 1(h). Here strain indicates the individual shear strain strength ϵij and field strength defined along [111] H≡∑i=13Hi2. In the presence of only strain (path A-C), Δ is negligible, while in the presence of only magnetic field (path A-B), a H3 splitting from third-order processes is expected; Δ is significantly enhanced in the presence of both strain and magnetic fields (paths C-D and B-D). Along path A-D where both strain and magnetic fields are being gradually turned on (Fig. 1(g)) and in the vicinity of A, Δ grows linearly with Hϵij(Fig. 1(i)), illustrating the predominant role of the latter in splitting the Γ3 doublet (the linear variation of Δ with the product Hϵij, shown in Fig. 1(i), appears as a quadratic variation in the A-D cut, shown in Fig. 1(g)).

Measurement of octupole susceptibility via elastocaloric coefficient

The octupolar fluctuations are then probed by the AC elastocaloric effect46–48. We use a commercial Razorbill CS-100 strain cell to deform the sample and monitor the displacement of the jaws of the strain cell using a capacitor sensor, from which changes in the longitudinal strain in the [111] direction, which we denote as ε111 ≡ ΔL111 /L111, are extracted. The three shear strains, ϵxy, ϵyz, and ϵzx, are equal to each other and proportional to ε111 (see Supplementary Materials). In the measurement, a static (DC) offset strain is applied to the sample - this arises in part from differential thermal contraction of the material with respect to the materials of the strain cell (unknown in magnitude, meaning that the true ‘zero strain point’ is unknown) and in part from controlled variation of the strain cell (meaning that we precisely know variation of the strain with respect to this unknown starting point). A small-amplitude AC strain is superimposed (typically of order 10−5 − 10−4) at a sufficiently high frequency that heat flow in/out of the sample is suppressed and hence these AC sample deformations occur under a quasi-adiabatic condition. Changes in the strain-induced splitting of the CEF doublet then necessarily induce temperature oscillations at the same frequency in order to keep the total entropy constant. Thus, for small perturbations from the zero-octupole point, and sufficiently far away from any phase transition, the elastocaloric coefficient dT/dϵ is anticipated to be linearly dependent on ϵ, with a slope that is quadratic in H. Determination of the coefficient of the strain- and field-dependence of the elastocaloric effect as a function of temperature then allows the extraction of the temperature dependence of dχO/dT and thus of χO. The mean-field expectation for χO based on Eq. 2 is a Curie-Weiss functional form, and thus dχO/dT is anticipated to follow (T−T*)−2, as has been previously observed for nematic and quadrupolar-strain susceptibilities measured by a similar strategy47,49,50.

As a starting point for examining the octupolar response in PrV2Al20, the elastocaloric coefficient dT/dε111 is examined at various fields/temperatures (Fig. 2). In Fig. 2(a) we show dT/dε111 as a function of ε111, measured with and without a magnetic field at T = 2.5 K. In the absence of a magnetic field, the signal is small and approximately linear w.r.t. strain; this likely results from a slight deviation of the stress orientation from [111], which then allows a small Γ3 quadrupole response (see Supplementary Materials), or, alternatively, from other background (non-critical) contributions from electrons and phonons. With increasing H, the slope of dT/dε111 as a function of ε111 is found to rapidly increase, as expected from the Landau model (Eqs. 2–6). Thus, the assumption of CEF-forbidden contributions being much weaker than the octupolar contribution is made for the remainder of the analysis. We also note that the zero intercept of dT/dε111 curves in Fig. 2(a) indeed vary with H, representing a shift in the strain required for cancellation of Hϵ and H3 couplings with each other as a function of the field discussed above.

Discussion

Deviations from the para-octupolar Curie behavior below approximately 3 K might reflect a variety of possible scenarios. The effect might reflect an interplay with the competing order in the quadrupolar channel known to be predominant in the cousin compound PrTi2Al2020,21 and also present in PrV2Al2022,29,37, or might even be related to the onset of a low-temperature multipolar Kondo effect, partially screening the Γ3 doublet and therefore the growing octupole susceptibility1,20,22,34. We note that our observed inflection point in χO is around the temperature where the accumulated entropy approximately amounts to Rln2 per Pr in PrV2Al2054, consistent with our treatment of the Pr states as unsplit doublets in the susceptibility framework laid above. These possibilities, which, importantly, do not affect the key observation of a Curie-Weiss octupolar susceptibility at higher temperatures, can potentially be resolved by high-precision low-temperature heat capacity and elastocaloric measurements performed as a function of strain, as well as differently designed strain experiments sensitive to the quadrupole channels of the system.

The robust observation of a diverging octupole susceptibility up to temperatures far above the critical temperatures as well as the order of magnitude of T* in the system clearly reveals the presence of significant octupolar fluctuations in PrV2Al20. Uncertainty in the Weiss temperature T* relative to the absolute value is quite large, but two standard deviations includes both observed critical temperatures, implying that octupolar order is potentially a viable competing state in PrV2Al2022.

Extending the elastocaloric experiments with improved precision to temperatures below the phase transitions in the system can be key to revealing the nature of the putative octupolar phase, complementary to earlier proposal of identifying the order parameter using magnetostriction39. The composite nature of the effective field Hiϵjk used here allows a simple protocol to isolate magnetic octupolar contributions to the total entropy, providing insight to the fluctuations of an otherwise elusive ‘hidden’ order. Moreover, our strain-based elastocaloric probe provides a new thermodynamic tool to shed light on the nature of localized CEF states in strongly correlated electron systems51,55 complementary to elastic moduli and magnetic susceptibility. More broadly, we anticipate that the tools and methodology demonstrated in this study can be used to explore fluctuations (and be naturally extended to also probe and manipulate the corresponding ordered states) in the wider class of localized f electron systems6,18, noncollinear antiferromagnets11 and altermagnets16,17, along with other novel types of hidden order following similar symmetry-based arguments56.

Methods

Crystal growth

Single crystals of PrV2Al20 were grown using an Al-based self flux method20,22,24. Elemental Pr, V, and Al were mixed with molar ratio 1:2:97 and loaded in an alumina crucible; the mixture was then sealed in a quartz tube filled with approximately 0.4 atm of Ar gas at room temperature. The tube was heated and held at 1150 °C for 6 h, and cooled at a rate of 1 °C/h to 800~900 °C, at which temperature the tube was taken out from the furnace and centrifuged to separate single crystals from flux. The resulting crystals prefer the morphology of octahedrons with (111) facets up to lateral size of a few millimeters.

Elastocaloric measurements

Elastocaloric measurements at low temperatures and magnetic fields were performed in a Razorbill CS-100 uniaxial strain cell in a Quantum Design Physical Property Measurement System (PPMS). To compensate for the rapid thermal contraction of PrV2Al2057 with respect to the Ti strain cell body, PrV2Al20 single crystals shaped into long bars along [111] were attached to mounting plates made of W with Stycast FT-2850. Temperature changes generated by the AC elastocaloric effect were probed using a RuOx resistive thermometer51,53,58 thermally connected to the sample via a thick gold wire; the latter is attached to the sample with AngstromBond AB9110LV (Fiber Optic Center). Additional silver paint (Dupont 4922N) is applied to where the gold wire touches the sample to enhance thermal contact at the joint. The thermometer is connected to a full Wheatstone bridge with three identical thermometers thermally anchored at the bath. In the experiments, an AC strain is applied to the sample, and temperature oscillation driven by the AC strain is captured by a lock-in amplifier (Stanford Research Systems, SR860) that locks into both frequencies of the AC strain and electrical current across the resistance bridge.

In Table 1 we list the relevant irreducible representations of Td discussed in the main text, Γ2 ~ Γ5, along with the product of angular momentum operators Ji, magnetic field Hi, strain ϵij, and their higher-order products that belong to these irreducible representations. It is apparent from Table 1 that the octupolar order parameter JxJyJz¯ belongs to Γ2, as well as its symmetry-allowed conjugate fields HxHyHz and Hiϵjk. The other contending channel of ordering within the Γ3 crystal field ground state doublet is the two-dimensional quadrupolar order Jx2−Jy2,2Jz2−Jx2−Jy2 (also belonging to the Γ3 irreducible representations). These quadrupolar order parameters by symmetry can bilinearly couple to either magnetic fields to the second order Hx2−Hy2,2Hz2−Hx2−Hy2, or normal strain modes 12(ϵxx−ϵyy),2ϵzz−ϵxx−ϵyy. With both magnetic field and uniaxial stress applied along [111], our experimental setup exclusively couples to the Γ2 degrees of freedom, by minimizing any Γ3 strain and magnetic fields.

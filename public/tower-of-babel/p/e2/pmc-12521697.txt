LINC01123: a pan-cancer “molecular commander” deciphering the CeRNA network atlas for precision diagnosis and therapy
Authors: Zheng Chen, Weijian Zhu, Qiang Yi, Xinting Ouyang, Kui Zhong, Qiong Ge, Mengying Zhang, Qinfu Yan, Jianing Zhong, Jinghua Zhong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractLINC01123, a long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) encoded on chromosome 2q13, is a crucial regulator in various human cancers. It spans 8,302 nucleotides and is implicated in malignancies like lung, ovarian, colorectal, head and neck, oral, cervical, breast, cholangiocarcinoma, and hepatocellular cancers. LINC01123 acts as a competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA), sequestering microRNAs such as miR-199a-5p, miR-151a, miR-34c-5p, miR-625-5p, miR-542-3p, and miR-34a-5p to modulate downstream target genes including c-MYC, VEGFA, and AXL. These interactions affect signaling pathways like NOTCH, MAPK, and PI3K-AKT, promoting cancer hallmarks such as cell proliferation, metastasis, angiogenesis, metabolic reprogramming, immune evasion, and radio/chemotherapy resistance. Recent studies suggest LINC01123 also has potential therapeutic roles in vascular and neurological disorders like acute cerebral infarction and venous thromboembolism. Thus, LINC01123 is a promising multi-disease intervention target warranting further exploration of its molecular pathways and clinical applications.Keywords: LncRNA, LINC01123, Malignant tumor, Competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA), Therapeutic target, Signaling pathway

Introduction

Neoplastic diseases continue to impose an unrelenting burden on global public health, representing a leading etiology of premature death and underscoring the exigent demand for paradigm-shifting therapeutic innovations [1]. The acquisition of distinct biological capabilities, or hallmarks, drives tumor development and progression. Among these, aberrant cellular metabolic reprogramming is now recognized as a critical enabler of malignancy [2, 3]. Comprehensive multimodal interventions—spanning oncological resection, cytotoxic chemotherapy, and precision radiotherapy—continue to encounter formidable obstacles. Inter-individual genomic diversity, coupled with the dynamic emergence of intrinsic and acquired tumour heterogeneity, recurrently undermines therapeutic efficacy and precipitates suboptimal clinical outcomes [4]. Therefore, the discovery and functional characterization of druggable molecular targets constitutes a fundamental prerequisite for developing next-generation cancer therapeutics.

Gaining a more comprehensive grasp of the complex regulatory mechanisms that control carcinogenesis is crucial for advancing more effective diagnostic approaches and therapeutic strategies. The discovery of non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) has significantly expanded this understanding, revealing complex layers of gene regulation beyond the central dogma [5]. Among ncRNAs, long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have been identified as showing great potential as biomarkers, offering considerable promise for both disease diagnosis and treatment [6]. LncRNAs, defined as transcripts exceeding 200 nucleotides without significant protein-coding potential [7], represent a major class of ncRNAs. Generally produced by RNA polymerase II and frequently modified in a manner equivalent to mRNAs, lncRNAs exert regulatory functions through diverse molecular mechanisms, including chromatin modification, transcriptional control, post-transcriptional regulation, and modulation of signaling pathways [8]. Notably, many lncRNAs function as competitive endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) or ‘miRNA sponges’, sequestering microRNAs (miRNAs) to indirectly regulate the expression of miRNA target genes involved in critical processes like cell proliferation, invasion, and therapeutic resistance [9]. LncRNAs participate in diverse cellular processes, including chromatin remodeling, transcriptional regulation, post-transcriptional modification, and signaling transduction. They often exhibit tissue-specific or context-dependent expression patterns. LncRNA expression dysregulation is common in various human diseases, particularly in cancers, where they can function as oncogenes or tumor suppressors [10]. Their involvement also extends to non-neoplastic conditions, including cardiovascular [11], neurological disorders [12], metabolic diseases [13], infections [14], and inflammatory processes [15], highlighting their broad biological significance.

Among the rapidly growing roster of functionally characterized lncRNAs, LINC01123 (Long Intergenic Non-protein Coding RNA 1123) has emerged as a significant regulator in oncogenesis. Accumulating evidence consistently demonstrates LINC01123 upregulation across a spectrum of human cancers, correlating with aggressive clinicopathological features and unfavorable patient prognosis. Mechanistically, LINC01123 predominantly exerts its pro-tumorigenic effects by acting as a ceRNA, thereby modulating the bioavailability of specific miRNAs and influencing downstream signaling cascades critical for cancer progression [16, 17]. Furthermore, LINC01123 has been implicated in mediating resistance to conventional anti-cancer therapies, presenting a substantial clinical obstacle [18].

In vivo, the oncogenic actions of LINC01123 are orchestrated through multiple signaling cascades that collectively accelerate tumor progression. In lung adenocarcinoma, transcriptional activation by Zinc Finger E-Box Binding Homeobox 1(ZEB1) up-regulates LINC01123, which then acts as a ceRNA to sequester miR-449b-5p, relieve its repression of NOTCH1, and thereby activate NOTCH signaling—promoting proliferation, migration, epithelial–mesenchymal transition, and stemness maintenance within a ZEB1/LINC01123/miR-449b-5p/NOTCH1 positive-feedback loop [17]. Analogously, in hepatocellular carcinoma, LINC01123 functions as a ceRNA by competitively binding miR-34a-5p, derepressing TUFT1 and activating the Ca²⁺/PI3K/AKT axis to enhance proliferation, migration, and invasion [19].

Given the burgeoning evidence implicating LINC01123 in cancer pathogenesis and its potential clinical relevance, this review aims to synthesize the current state of knowledge. We provide a comprehensive overview of LINC01123’s expression patterns, established biological functions (including roles in proliferation, metastasis, angiogenesis, metabolism, and immune evasion), and elucidated molecular mechanisms, with a primary focus on its ceRNA activity in various cancer types. Its emerging roles in select non-tumor diseases are also briefly discussed. Critically, we evaluate the potential of LINC01123 as a diagnostic/prognostic biomarker and a therapeutic target. Finally, this review identifies key knowledge gaps and outlines promising directions for future research essential for translating the understanding of LINC01123 biology into tangible clinical applications (Fig. 1).

Basic characteristics and molecular functions of LINC01123

The LINC01123 gene is located on human chromosome 2q13. According to the NCBI Gene database (Gene ID: 338799), the gene itself spans approximately 8.3 kb. While multiple transcript variants exist, a commonly studied transcript is approximately 2.5 kb in length and typically comprises four exons. Subcellular localization analyses indicate that LINC01123 predominantly resides in the cytoplasm, suggesting potential roles in both cytoplasmic and nuclear processes.

The prevailing paradigm positions LINC01123 as a prototypical competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA). By encoding discrete microRNA response elements (MREs), it operates as a high-affinity molecular sponge that sequesters cognate miRNAs, thereby precluding their association with target messenger RNAs (mRNAs). This competitive titration alleviates post-transcriptional repression and rescues mRNAs from degradation, culminating in the dose-dependent up-regulation of the encoded proteins [16]. Numerous LINC01123-miRNA-mRNA regulatory axes have been identified across various human cancers, underpinning its functional roles, as will be detailed in subsequent sections.

Beyond its downstream regulatory effects as a ceRNA, the expression of LINC01123 itself is tightly controlled. Upstream regulation occurs at the transcriptional level; for example, transcription factors such as Forkhead Box C1 (FOXC1) and ZEB1 have been found to directly interact with the LINC01123 promoter region and prompt its transcription in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) and lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), respectively [17, 20]. These findings integrate LINC01123 into broader cellular regulatory networks, acting downstream of key signaling pathways and transcription factors. While the ceRNA mechanism is the most studied, potential non-ceRNA functions, such as direct interactions with RNA-binding proteins or modulation of chromatin structure, remain less explored but warrant further investigation to fully understand LINC01123’s biological repertoire (Fig. 2).

Role of LINC01123 in therapeutic resistance in cancer

The development of resistance to radiotherapy and chemotherapy represents a significant clinical challenge, frequently limiting treatment efficacy and contributing to cancer recurrence [21, 22]. Accumulating evidence implicates LINC01123 as a key regulator contributing to therapeutic failure across diverse malignancies.

Radiotherapy resistance

Radiotherapy is a cornerstone treatment for glioma, the most common primary malignant brain tumor [23]. However, intrinsic and acquired radioresistance significantly hampers its curative potential. Studies have demonstrated that LINC01123 expression is significantly elevated in radioresistant glioma tissues and derived cell lines (e.g., U251R) compared to their radiosensitive counterparts. Mechanistically, LINC01123 has been shown to confer radioresistance by functioning as a ceRNA for miR-151a. By sequestering miR-151a, LINC01123 derepresses the expression of its target, centromere protein B (CENPB), ultimately promoting glioma cell survival following irradiation. These findings suggest that targeting the LINC01123/miR-151a/CENPB axis could represent a novel strategy to overcome radioresistance in glioma patients [18].

Chemotherapy resistance

LINC01123 has also been implicated in promoting chemoresistance in various cancer types. In CRC, particularly within colon adenocarcinoma (COAD), elevated LINC01123 levels have been associated with decreased sensitivity to 5-fluorouracil (5-FU). Mechanistic investigations revealed that LINC01123 enhances 5-FU resistance by sponging miR-34c-5p, leading to the upregulation of Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor A (VEGFA), a key regulator of angiogenesis and cell survival [24]. Bioinformatic analyses based on ceRNA network predictions have suggested a potential role for LINC01123 in mediating cisplatin resistance in LUAD. However, the precise molecular mechanisms underlying this putative function require direct experimental validation [25]. Preliminary studies in ovarian cancer have linked increased LINC01123 expression with resistance to platinum-based chemotherapy or taxanes. This resistance phenotype might stem from LINC01123-mediated regulation of the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) program, which is linked to drug resistance [26].

Mechanisms of action and biological functions of LINC01123 in diverse cancers

Aberrant expression and functional contributions of LINC01123 have been extensively documented across various human malignancies. Consistently, LINC01123 mainly acts as a cancer-promoting gene, affecting key cancer processes such as cell proliferation, invasion, metastasis, metabolic reprogramming, and therapeutic resistance.

Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)

Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer deaths globally,, with non-small cell lung cancer(NSCLC) making up about 85% of these cases [27–29]. Consistent evidence demonstrates that LINC01123 expression is significantly upregulated in NSCLC tissues compared to adjacent non-malignant tissues, and its levels often correlate positively with advanced tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) stage. Functionally, LINC01123 promotes NSCLC cell proliferation, migration, and contributes to metabolic reprogramming, notably enhancing aerobic glycolysis (the Warburg effect) [30]. LUAD, the most prevalent subtype of NSCLC [31, 32], is also governed by the LINC01123-centered ceRNA regulatory axis. This axis drives the malignant progression of LUAD through the selective sequestration of two distinct miRNAs (detailed below) and the subsequent modulation of their associated signaling cascades. The oncogenic role of LINC01123 in lung cancer extends beyond its function as an amplifier of signaling cascades, as it orchestrates multiple positive-feedback loops that perpetuate tumor progression and metabolic reprogramming.

In NSCLC, LINC01123 establishes a c-Myc-centered metabolic positive-feedback circuit: c-Myc transcriptionally up-regulates LINC01123, which in turn functions as a ceRNA by sequestering miR-199a-5p, thereby relieving the microRNA-mediated repression of c-Myc and amplifying the expression of glycolytic genes such as HK2 and LDHA. This c-Myc→LINC01123→miR-199a-5p→c-Myc loop not only accelerates aerobic glycolysis (the Warburg effect) but may also reprogram the metabolic milieu to suppress immune cell function and foster an immunosuppressive microenvironment [16].

In LUAD, the transcription factor ZEB1 directly up-regulates both LINC01123 and NOTCH1. LINC01123 subsequently acts as a ceRNA that sequesters miR-449b-5p, thereby relieving its repression of NOTCH1 and potentiating NOTCH signaling. Importantly, NOTCH1 reciprocally enhances ZEB1 expression, establishing a closed-loop regulatory axis (ZEB1→LINC01123→miR-449b-5p→NOTCH1→ZEB1). This positive-feedback loop not only amplifies the EMT phenotype but also augments cancer stem cell traits, underscoring the central role of LINC01123 in sustaining tumor heterogeneity and therapeutic resistance [17].

Further studies in LUAD have identified an additional regulatory axis: LINC01123 sequesters miR-4766-5p, thereby up-regulating pyrroline-5-carboxylate reductase 1 (PYCR1) [33]. This enhances proline biosynthesis and sustains the metabolic demands of tumor growth. Collectively, these findings indicate that LINC01123 promotes NSCLC progression through multiple mechanisms and suggest that it may serve as a valuable diagnostic biomarker and therapeutic target.

A closer look suggests that the two LINC01123-mediated feedback circuits are probably not independent; rather, they may reinforce each other as tumors evolve. Although NOTCH1 and c-Myc both drive EMT and metabolic rewiring, it is still unclear whether LINC01123 orchestrates a cross-talk network between these pathways—an issue that calls for combined multi-omics approaches. Furthermore, the lncRNA’s choice of ceRNA partners—miR-449b-5p in LUAD and miR-199a-5p in NSCLC—reveals a tissue-specific tuning mechanism that could guide the development of tailored lncRNA-directed treatments.

Colorectal cancer (CRC)

Globally, colorectal cancer (CRC) constitutes the third most frequently diagnosed malignant neoplasm and, with approximately 935 000 deaths annually, is the second most common contributor to oncologic mortality [34, 35]. LINC01123 is significantly upregulated in CRC tissues compared to adjacent normal mucosal tissues, and its elevated expression is positively correlated with increased tumor invasion depth, lymph node metastasis, and unfavorable patient prognosis [36]. Functionally, LINC01123 promotes CRC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion in vitro and in vivo.

The oncogenic functions of LINC01123 in CRC are mediated through diverse mechanisms: (i) Within COAD, LINC01123 sponges miR-34c-5p, leading to the upregulation of VEGFA. This axis not only enhances tumor proliferation and invasion but also contributes to resistance against the chemotherapeutic agent 5-FU [24]. (ii) LINC01123 can interact directly with the splicing factor Serine/Arginine Rich Splicing Factor 7 (SRSF7). This interaction potentially modulates the alternative splicing of target genes or affects LINC01123 stability, thereby influencing CRC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion [37]. This represents a potential non-ceRNA mechanism of action. (iii) By competitively binding miR-625-5p, LINC01123 derepresses the expression of LIM And SH3 Protein 1 (LASP1), subsequently promoting the EMT program and enhancing the metastatic potential of CRC cells [36].

These multifaceted mechanisms underscore the role of LINC01123 as a pivotal driver of CRC progression and therapeutic resistance.

Ovarian cancer (OC)

Ovarian cancer (OC) remains the most lethal gynecological malignancy, primarily due to challenges in early detection and high rates of recurrence [38, 39]. Studies combining bioinformatics analyses and experimental validation demonstrate that LINC01123 is significantly upregulated in OC tissues and its high expression is associated with poor clinical outcomes. Functionally, LINC01123 promotes OC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. One established mechanism involves LINC01123 acting as a ceRNA for hsa-miR-516b-5p, leading to increased VEGFA expression, which subsequently drives EMT and metastasis [40].

Furthermore, LINC01123 contributes to chemoresistance in OC, particularly against platinum-based drugs and taxanes. While the precise mechanisms are still under full investigation, potential links to EMT regulation, autophagy modulation, and altered glycolysis have been suggested. For instance, investigations into the effects of berberine, an alkaloid with anti-cancer properties, proposed that it may inhibit OC cell glycolysis and autophagy, potentially by influencing the LINC01123/NF-κB (P65)/MAPK10 signaling pathway, indirectly supporting a role for LINC01123 within this network [26]. These findings highlight LINC01123 as a contributor to OC aggressiveness and treatment failure.

Cervical cancer (CC)

Cervical cancer (CC) is the fourth most common cancer in women globally and a leading cause of cancer-related death [41, 42]. Studies report significantly elevated expression of LINC01123 in CC tissues and cell lines compared to normal cervical counterparts, with expression levels correlating positively with lymph node metastasis. Functional assays, both in vitro and in vivo, demonstrate that LINC01123 knockdown markedly suppresses CC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, concurrently inhibiting tumor growth. Mechanistically, a LINC01123/miR-361-3p/TSPAN1 regulatory axis has been elucidated. LINC01123 functions as a ceRNA, sequestering miR-361-3p and thereby reducing its bioavailability. This alleviates miR-361-3p-mediated suppression of its target, tetraspanin-1 (TSPAN1), a known promoter of tumor progression. Consequently, upregulated TSPAN1 expression, driven by LINC01123 activity, contributes to CC pathogenesis [43].

Triple-negative breast cancer(TNBC)

Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) represents a particularly aggressive subtype characterized by the absence of estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and HER2 expression, leading to limited targeted therapy options and poor patient prognosis [44, 45]. Research indicates that the transcription factor FOXC1 directly binds to the LINC01123 promoter, activating its transcription in TNBC cells. The resulting elevated LINC01123 levels function as a ceRNA, specifically sponging the tumor-suppressive microRNA, miR-663a. Sequestration of miR-663a leads to derepression of its target gene, c-Maf inducing protein (CMIP), resulting in increased CMIP protein expression. This coordinated FOXC1/LINC01123/miR-663a/CMIP signaling axis promotes the proliferation and survival of TNBC cells [20]. While foundational mechanisms are emerging, further investigation is warranted to fully define the clinical utility of targeting LINC01123 in TNBC.

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the most common type of primary liver cancer, exhibits increasing incidence and mortality rates globally, ranking as the sixth most common cancer and the third leading cause of cancer-related death [46, 47]. LINC01123 expression is significantly upregulated in HCC tissues and inversely correlated with patient survival outcomes. Functionally, LINC01123 promotes HCC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. Mechanistic studies reveal that LINC01123 functions as a ceRNA for miR-34a-5p, enhancing the expression of its target, Tuftelin 1 (TUFT1). Increased TUFT1 levels subsequently activate the downstream Ca2+/ Phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)/AKT signaling pathway. Therefore, LINC01123 fosters HCC growth and invasion via the LINC01123/miR-34a-5p/TUFT1 axis, which propagates signals through the PI3K/AKT pathway [19], providing novel insights into HCC pathogenesis.

Osteosarcoma (OS)

Osteosarcoma (OS) is the most frequent primary malignant bone tumor, with notable incidence peaks during adolescence and in adults over 60 years of age [48, 49]. LINC01123 expression is elevated in OS tissues and cell lines, where it contributes to enhanced cell proliferation and metastasis. Investigations into its mechanism suggest that the oncogenic activity of LINC01123 is linked to the activation of the Hedgehog (Hh) signaling pathway. Specifically, LINC01123 functions as a ceRNA, competitively binding and inhibiting miR-561b-5p. As miR-561b-5p normally targets and suppresses the key Hh pathway transcription factor, GLI Family Zinc Finger 1 (Gli1), LINC01123-mediated inhibition of miR-561b-5p results in increased Gli1 protein levels. Elevated Gli1 subsequently activates downstream Hh target genes, driving the malignant progression of OS. The LINC01123/miR-561b-5p/Gli1 regulatory axis thus represents a significant mechanism underlying OS development [50].

Oncogenic roles in other malignancies

Beyond the cancers detailed above, LINC01123 upregulation and pro-tumorigenic functions have been reported in several additional malignancies, underscoring its broad involvement in cancer biology.

Head and Neck Squamous Cell Carcinoma (HNSCC): In HNSCC, LINC01123 is upregulated and promotes tumor growth by sponging miR-214-3p. This sequestration leads to increased expression of the immune checkpoint molecule B7 homolog 3 (B7-H3, also known as CD276) [51]. Beyond its immune-regulatory role, B7-H3 exerts non-immune functions promoting migration, invasion, angiogenesis, chemoresistance, EMT, and altered tumor metabolism [52]. Therefore, the LINC01123/miR-214-3p/ B7-H3 axis likely contributes to both immunosuppression within the tumor microenvironment and direct tumor progression in HNSCC.

Glioma: Earlier in Sect. 3.1, it was noted that LINC01123 is overexpressed in glioma and plays a key role in radioresistance. This occurs via its function as a ceRNA for miR-151a, leading to the upregulation of CENPB. Increased CENPB expression enhances DNA repair capacity following irradiation, thereby promoting cell survival and radioresistance [18]. Targeting this axis holds potential for overcoming therapeutic hurdles in glioma management.

Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma (OSCC): Elevated LINC01123 expression in OSCC serves as an independent negative prognostic indicator. Mechanistically, LINC01123 directly sponges miR-34a-5p, attenuating its tumor-suppressive activity. A key target of miR-34a-5p is the receptor tyrosine kinase AXL. Consequently, LINC01123 upregulates AXL protein expression by inhibiting miR-34a-5p. Activation of AXL triggers downstream signaling through the AKT/Glycogen Synthase Kinase-3 Beta (GSK-3β)/β-catenin pathway, promoting EMT, invasion, and metastasis in OSCC cells [53].

Cholangiocarcinoma (CCA): Cholangiocarcinoma (CCA), an aggressive malignancy of the biliary tract often diagnosed at late stages [54, 55], exhibits high LINC01123 expression. Elevated LINC01123 levels correlate significantly with lymph node metastasis, advanced TNM stage, and poor patient prognosis. LINC01123 promotes CCA cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, at least partly by acting as a sponge for miR-641. By sequestering the tumor-suppressive miR-641 (known to target factors like CDR1as, AKT3, and mTOR), LINC01123 counteracts its inhibitory effects on cancer cell growth and metastasis, thereby accelerating CCA progression [56] (Table 1).

Modulation of key signaling pathways by LINC01123 in cancer

The oncogenic functions attributed to LINC01123 are frequently mediated through the modulation of critical intracellular signaling pathways. These pathways govern fundamental cellular processes, including proliferation, survival, differentiation, migration, and metabolism, and their aberrant regulation is a hallmark of tumorigenesis. LINC01123 predominantly exerts its influence indirectly, often operating within ceRNA networks to alter the expression or activity of key signaling components.

LINC01123 and the NOTCH signaling pathway

The NOTCH signaling pathway is pivotal in regulating embryonic development, tissue homeostasis, and cell fate decisions. In cancer, it can function as either an oncogene or a tumor suppressor in a context-dependent manner. Aberrant NOTCH activation is often seen in malignancies, where it can drive EMT, angiogenesis, proliferation, invasion, and metastasis [57, 58]. As detailed in Sect. 4.1, a specific regulatory axis involving LINC01123 has been identified in LUAD. Transcription factor ZEB1 induces LINC01123 expression. Subsequently, LINC01123 functions as a ceRNA, sequestering miR-449b-5p. This relieves miR-449b-5p-mediated repression of NOTCH1 mRNA, leading to increased NOTCH1 protein levels and consequently, sustained activation of the NOTCH signaling pathway [17]. Enhanced NOTCH1 signaling promotes the transcription of downstream target genes, such as HES1 and HEY1, thereby driving LUAD cell proliferation, invasion, and the acquisition of stem-like properties. Additionally, since NOTCH signaling may induce ZEB1 expression, LINC01123 likely amplifies this oncogenic circuit, further promoting LUAD progression. This demonstrates how LINC01123 integrates into key oncogenic signaling networks via ceRNA - mediated mechanisms.

LINC01123 and the MAPK signaling pathway

Mitogen-activated protein kinases (MAPKs) comprise a family of serine/threonine kinases, including the canonical ERK (MAPK1/3), p38 MAPK, and JNK signaling cascades. These pathways function as central signaling hubs, transducing diverse extracellular stimuli into nuclear responses that govern cell proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, inflammation, and stress adaptation [59, 60]. Chronic aberrant activation of MAPK signaling, particularly the ERK pathway, is a frequent driver of tumorigenesis. Direct evidence definitively linking LINC01123 to MAPK pathway regulation remains relatively limited. However, some studies provide suggestive evidence of potential interplay. For instance, in OC, LINC01123 has been implicated within a potential signaling axis involving NF-κB (P65) and MAPK10 (JNK3), possibly influencing tumor cell glycolysis and autophagy [26]. The precise mechanistic details and the specific role of LINC01123 within this context require further elucidation. It is plausible that LINC01123 may indirectly modulate MAPK signaling by affecting the expression or activity of MAPK components or upstream regulators like NF-κB. Additionally, given the extensive crosstalk between MAPK pathways and other critical signaling networks, LINC01123 might exert indirect effects on MAPK activity through its regulation of these interconnected pathways. Additional specialized research is needed to get a precise understanding of its nature and functional significance concerning the connection between LINC01123 and certain MAPK subpathways in various cancers.

LINC01123 and the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway

The phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)-protein kinase B (AKT/PKB) signaling pathway represents one of the most critical intracellular cascades regulating cell survival, growth, and metabolism. It exerts central control over cell cycle progression, apoptosis inhibition, protein synthesis, glucose metabolism, and angiogenesis [61, 62]. Hyperactivation of the PI3K-AKT pathway is a pervasive feature across numerous human cancers. Compelling evidence demonstrates that LINC01123, primarily through its function within ceRNA networks, can effectively modulate PI3K-AKT pathway activity in multiple cancer contexts:

HCC: As described in Sect. 4.6, LINC01123 upregulates Tuftelin 1 (TUFT1) expression by sponging miR-34a-5p. TUFT1 subsequently promotes activation of the PI3K/AKT pathway, potentially through modulation of intracellular Ca2+ signaling, thereby enhancing HCC cell proliferation and invasion [19].

OSCC: In OSCC (Sect. 4.8), LINC01123-mediated inhibition of miR-34a-5p leads to increased expression of the receptor tyrosine kinase AXL. AXL is a known upstream activator of PI3K/AKT signaling; its upregulation results in increased phosphorylation of AKT and its substrate GSK-3β. This cascade further activates β-catenin signaling, ultimately promoting EMT and metastasis [53].

CRC & NSCLC: LINC01123 facilitates the upregulation of LASP1 by sponging miR-625-5p (Sect. 4.2). Clinical translation prospects of LINC01123: biomarker potential and therapeutic targeting

The regular overexpression and proved cancer - causing functions of LINC01123 in different human cancers make it a molecule of great clinical translational interest. This interest encompasses its potential utility both as a multifaceted biomarker and as a novel therapeutic target.

LINC01123 as a potential cancer biomarker

The distinct expression pattern of LINC01123, characterized by significant overexpression in numerous tumor types compared to corresponding non-malignant tissues, forms the basis of its biomarker potential. Also, evidence has shown there are strong links between higher levels of LINC01123 and known signs of aggressive tumors. These include advanced TNM stages and lymph node metastasis. And in many types of cancer, higher LINC01123 levels mean patients don’t survive as long. Collectively, these findings underscore its potential utility as both a diagnostic biomarker (aiding in cancer detection or differentiation) and a prognostic biomarker (providing information on likely patient outcomes and disease course).

Also, fresh proof linking LINC01123 expression to resistance to radiotherapy and chemotherapy suggests it may be a useful predictive biomarker. Monitoring LINC01123 levels could potentially inform treatment selection or predict patient response to specific therapeutic regimens. The possibility of detecting LINC01123 in easily accessible bodily fluids, such as blood (i.e., liquid biopsy), further enhances its appeal, offering prospects for non-invasive disease monitoring, assessment of therapeutic efficacy, and early detection of tumor recurrence.

However, translating this promising biomarker potential into routine clinical practice necessitates overcoming substantial hurdles. Key steps involve creating and carefully ensuring the validity of methods that are very sensitive and specific for detecting and measuring LINC01123 in tissues and bodily fluids. Subsequently, its diagnostic, prognostic, and predictive value must be unequivocally established through well-designed, large-scale, multi-center, prospective clinical studies. Ultimately, showing that incorporating LINC01123 evaluation into clinical decisions brings real advantages for patient management is key to its clinical adoption.

LINC01123 as a potential therapeutic target

Beyond its biomarker utility, the functional role of LINC01123 in driving core oncogenic processes— such as proliferation, invasion, metastasis, metabolic reprogramming, and treatment resistance— renders it an attractive candidate for therapeutic targeting. Inhibiting the expression or function of LINC01123 could potentially disrupt key tumor-promoting pathways and impede cancer progression.

Strategies aimed at modulating lncRNA function are actively being explored and could be applied to LINC01123. These include sequence-specific approaches like antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) or small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) designed to induce LINC01123 degradation or block its interactions with partner molecules (e.g., miRNAs or proteins). Gene-editing technologies, such as CRISPR/Cas9, offer the potential to permanently disrupt the LINC01123 gene locus. Additionally, the development of small-molecule inhibitors capable of binding specific structural motifs within LINC01123 or interfering with its critical interactions represents another promising avenue [63, 64].

Significant challenges remain, however, for the development of LINC01123-targeted therapies, mirroring those encountered for other RNA-targeted therapeutics. Ensuring efficient, safe, and specific delivery of therapeutic agents (e.g., ASOs, siRNAs, CRISPR components) to tumor cells in vivo remains a major obstacle. Achieving high target specificity to minimize off-target effects on other RNAs or cellular processes is crucial. Furthermore, establishing favorable long-term safety and tolerability profiles for these novel therapeutic modalities will be essential.

Nonetheless, steady progress in technology related to RNA delivery and treatment design, along with ongoing research into how LINC01123 works and its exact molecular interactions, might help unlock its therapeutic potential. Future preclinical validation and subsequent clinical trials evaluating LINC01123 inhibitors will be critical to determine their efficacy and ultimate place in cancer treatment paradigms.

Discussion and conclusion

Lately, LINC01123 has been seen as a very important lncRNA in the development of many human cancers. More and more proof shows that it is often overexpressed in tumor tissues and has strong cancer-promoting functions. Because of this, it could be a good choice for developing cancer biomarkers and treatments. But a careful look shows that though it’s promising, there are still big knowledge gaps and technical problems on the way to using it in clinical practice. These need serious research to solve.

A primary challenge lies in the incomplete deciphering of LINC01123’s molecular mechanisms. While the ceRNA hypothesis, involving microRNA sponging, has garnered considerable attention and supporting data across various cancer types, relying solely on this model likely oversimplifies its functional repertoire. Emerging studies hint at more diverse roles, including acting as a molecular scaffold to facilitate protein complex assembly (e.g., modulating signaling pathways like Wnt/β-catenin or PI3K/AKT) [65], controlling gene expression at transcription and post-transcription stages by interacting with chromatin modifiers and RNA-binding proteins [66], or even influencing mRNA stability and translation. Crucially, these mechanisms are unlikely to be mutually exclusive and may exhibit significant context-dependency, varying across different cellular environments and cancer subtypes. Therefore, moving beyond correlative ceRNA network analyses to definitive functional dissections using advanced techniques (e.g., ChIRP-MS, RAP-MS, high-resolution imaging, structure-function studies) to achieve a deeper, integrated mechanistic understanding is imperative. This mechanistic clarity is not merely academic; it forms the bedrock for the rational design of specific and effective therapeutic strategies.

The recurring link between heightened LINC01123 levels and unfavorable clinical outcomes in various cancers shows promise as a prognostic marker. Furthermore, its detectability in body fluids raises possibilities for non-invasive diagnostics or monitoring. However, translating these correlations into clinically actionable biomarkers necessitates overcoming substantial hurdles. Current evidence largely stems from retrospective studies characterized by methodological heterogeneity and often modest cohort sizes. Establishing robust clinical utility requires: (1) Development and standardization of highly sensitive and specific assays suitable for routine clinical use; (2) Rigorous validation in large, independent, prospectively collected patient cohorts, ideally within the framework of clinical trials; and (3) Show that the state of LINC01123 can offer independent diagnostic or prognostic insights that go beyond existing clinical parameters, or that it can considerably enhance risk stratification and treatment choices.

The oncogenic dependence on LINC01123 observed in preclinical models renders it an attractive therapeutic target. Strategies employing antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) or small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) have shown promise in suppressing LINC01123 expression and inhibiting tumor growth in vitro and in xenograft models. Nevertheless, the translation of RNA-targeted therapies faces generic yet formidable obstacles, primarily concerning efficient, tissue-specific in vivo delivery, potential off-target effects, immunogenicity, and long-term safety. Moreover, the exploration of small molecules directly targeting LINC01123 structure or its interactions remains nascent, hampered by the perceived ‘undruggability’ of RNA targets and the lack of detailed structural information. Developing indirect modulation strategies through pathway-specific targeting of LINC01123’s upstream master regulators or pharmacologically tractable downstream nodal effectors may provide viable therapeutic alternatives. Critically, predicting and circumventing potential resistance mechanisms to LINC01123-targeted therapies will be fundamental for achieving durable clinical responses.

Beyond its specific role in cancer, the study of LINC01123 provides valuable insights into the broader landscape of lncRNA biology. This pan-cancer regulatory pattern implies LINC01123’s potential engagement with evolutionarily conserved oncogenic master regulators or tumorigenesis-essential cellular circuitry. Consequently, elucidating its functional network could illuminate general principles of lncRNA-mediated gene regulation and network control. Resolving LINC01123’s translational barriers may establish a methodological paradigm for developing generalizable targeting approaches applicable to other pathologically significant lncRNAs, thereby advancing cross-lncRNA therapeutic development.

It is worth probing whether, within the nucleus, LINC01123 remodels the tumor epigenome through a three-tier cascade of “transcription factor—chromatin modification—target gene expression.”Epigenetic dysregulation is a common hallmark of human cancer, promoting tumorigenesis and sustaining malignant phenotypes by altering gene expression [4, 67]. Common epigenetic regulatory mechanisms include lactylation [68], DNA methylation [69], histone modifications [70], and chromatin remodeling [71]. Available evidence suggests that pioneer factors such as ZEB1 and c-Myc may first dock at the LINC01123 promoter, recruit histone acetyltransferases (HATs) or deacetylases (HDACs), and thereby promote activating marks like H3K4me3 while antagonizing the repressive H3K27me3 signature to initiate or sustain transcription. Additionally, LINC01123 itself could serve as a scaffold that tethers chromatin-remodeling complexes, thereby maintaining an open chromatin landscape that renders neighboring oncogenes—e.g., NOTCH1—more accessible to the transcriptional machinery and amplifying downstream signaling. Although these putative mechanisms await validation by ChIP-seq, Hi-C, and CRISPR-dCas9 epigenetic editing, the proposed framework offers testable directions for elucidating the nuclear function of LINC01123.

Collectively, LINC01123 emerges as a pivotal long non-coding RNA endowed with bifunctional utility in oncodiagnostics and molecular therapeutics, exemplifying the expanding clinical-translational paradigm that now defines contemporary lncRNA research. Nevertheless, formidable scientific and translational barriers must be surmounted before its clinical potential can be fully actualized.Future investigations should therefore prioritise three interrelated imperatives. First, mechanistic elucidation must be pursued with unprecedented rigour. Integrative deployment of single-cell multi-omics, spatial transcriptomics, and high-resolution interactome mapping within physiologically faithful pre-clinical ecosystems—including patient-derived xenografts (PDX), genetically engineered mouse models (GEMM), and human organoid systems—is essential to delineate the context-dependent molecular circuitry through which LINC01123 exerts its oncogenic or tumour-suppressive effects.Second, innovative therapeutic modalities—encompassing antisense oligonucleotides, CRISPR-based epigenome editing, and targeted delivery nanocarriers—must be systematically conceived and exhaustively evaluated for on-target potency, off-target liabilities, and long-term safety in stringent pre-clinical paradigms.Third, robust clinical validation demands the initiation of meticulously designed, adequately powered, large-scale prospective trials within tumour lineages for which compelling pre-clinical evidence already exists. Such studies should rigorously adjudicate the diagnostic, prognostic, and predictive performance of LINC01123 as either a standalone biomarker or a constituent of multi-analyte panels.Conquering these technical frontiers will not only enable the therapeutic exploitation of LINC01123 but also illuminate the broader landscape of lncRNA-mediated pathobiology, thereby establishing mechanistic scaffolds for next-generation precision oncology interventions.

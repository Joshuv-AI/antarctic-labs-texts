Thoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR) explantation and left lower lobectomy for aortobronchial fistula repair
Authors: Jonathan Zurcher, Alexander Nissen, Felix Fernandez, Woodrow Farrington, Bradley G Leshnower
Published: 2024

Infected TEVAR endoprothesis exposure with disintegration of the aortic wall.

Central Message

Treatment of infected TEVAR endoprothesis is a complex problem requiring open explant of the endograft and tissue flap coverage of the descending thoracic aortic graft to prevent recurrent infection.

See Discussion on page 38.

Thoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR), has unequivocally been a paradigm-shifting technology over the past 2 decades. Fortunately, the incidence of endograft infection remains low. However, because of the proximity of the aneurysm sac and/or stent graft to the bronchus or esophagus, erosion and fistulization of the aorta to these structures may occur. Treatment of the infected TEVAR and airway/esophageal fistula is a complex problem that carries a high risk of mortality. In this report, we detail the treatment of a complex distal aortic dissection complicated by infection. Emory University policy dictates that single-patient reports do not require institutional review board approval; written informed consent for publication for the study data was obtained from the patient.

This patient was a 39-year-old man who had previously undergone modified Bentall mechanical root and total arch replacement who presented a month after a thoracic branched endoprosthesis placement with rapid enlargement of the aneurysmal sac, retrograde endoleak, bacteremia, and septic shock. During his hospitalization for the thoracic branched endoprosthesis procedure, he developed a left empyema postoperatively that required thoracoscopic decortication. Cultures from this operation revealed Streptomyces infection. Given his shock presentation, we elected to treat him with TEVAR extension as a bridge to open repair. This allowed him to recover from his sepsis, and he was subsequently discharged home. Three weeks later, he presented to an outside hospital with hemoptysis and Serratia bacteremia. Bronchoscopy confirmed the diagnosis of an aortobronchial fistula (Figure 1). After his bacteremia was cleared, he underwent laparotomy with omental flap harvest, followed by left thoracotomy, explant of his stent grafts (Figure 2), descending aortic replacement, left lower lobectomy, and omental flap coverage (Video 1). Intraoperative cultures revealed Enterococcus faecalis and Klebsiella pneumoniae. He was maintained on intravenous ampicillin with sulbactam while in the hospital and transitioned to suppressive amoxicillin with clavulanic acid as an outpatient. Omentum is the preferred tissue coverage in our institution in patients without previous abdominal surgery, as it can easily reach to the apex of the chest with careful mobilization and is less debilitating than harvesting other large muscle flaps such as the latissimus. Although unconventional, the use of TEVAR in an infected field prevented aortic rupture allowed for resolution of septic shock and provided a bridge to definitive open endograft explant.

NF-κB pathway activation by Octopus peptide hydrolysate ameliorates gut dysbiosis and enhances immune response in cyclophosphamide-induced mice
Authors: Muhsin Ali, Hidayat Ullah, Nabeel Ahmed Farooqui, Ting Deng, Nimra Zafar Siddiqui, Muhammad Ilyas, Sharafat Ali, Mujeeb Ur Rahman, Ata Ur Rehman, Yamina Alioui, Liang Wang, Xin Yi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Cyclophosphamide (CTX) is an anticancer medication that suppresses host immunity as well as adversely affects mucosal inflammation and gut microflora dysbiosis. The gut microflora is recognized as a substantial factor in host metabolism and immunological homeostasis. To improve immunity and inhibit cytotoxic and homeostatic imbalances triggered by CTX, it is essential to monitor immunoregulators. In this research, we assessed the impact of Octopus peptide hydrolysate (OPH) on immune modulation, intestinal integrity, and gut microbial composition in CTX-induced immune-deficient mice. The results revealed that OPH increased body weight, and immunological organ indices, and improved the histological changes in the colon, thymus, and spleen. The OPH stimulated the secretion of cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α) and antibodies (IgM and IgA) while reducing the ratio of lipopolysaccharide (LPS) and diamine oxidase (DAO) in the serum. OPH further enhanced goblet cell and mucus production, upregulated the expression of gut tight-junction proteins (Occludin, Zonula Occludin-1, Mucin-2, and Claudin-1), and activated the TLR4/NF-κB cascade (p-IκBα, P65/p-p65). In addition, OPH treatment declined the Bacteroidetes/Firmicutes ratio, enhanced the relative ratio of Alistipes/Lachnospiraceae, and reversed the ecological equilibrium of the gut microflora. The findings revealed that OPH serves as a prebiotic to prevent CTX-mediated disruption in the intestinal barrier and boosts gut mucosal immunity by attenuating gut microflora imbalance, implying that OPH could be used as an immunological ingredient in nutritious foods to regulate the immune system and protect the gut from inflammatory diseases.

Introduction

The body's defense mechanism is a complex combination of immunological organs, cells, and active material that performs a pivotal role in protecting against foreign invaders, healing mechanisms, and detoxification of potentially hazardous substances [1,2]. The intestinal barrier system, which is a part of the immune network and is mostly made up of a mucus layer, an epithelial barrier, and a vascular barrier, is very important for absorbing nutrients and keeping the texture stable to sustain gut hemostasis [3,4]. Once the intestinal mucosa barrier is in a healthy state, it simultaneously protects against both pathogenic and non-pathogenic microbes and is tolerant of an extensive variety of favorable symbiotic microbes [5]. The billions of microbes that reside in the intestinal tract carry out a broad spectrum of biological processes to maintain a complex microbiome ecosystem. Despite aiding with the digestion of carbohydrates and the synthesis of vitamins, the beneficial microbiota is also essential for the establishment of the body's defense mechanism and the avoidance of pathogen colonization in the host body [6]. Furthermore, the microbiome also drives cell proliferation, mucus secretion, mucosal surface expansion, and epithelial junction preservation [7]. It has been extensively reported in the last several decades that the metabolites of the gut microbiota help retain intestinal homeostasis by stimulating the host immune response while lowering inflammation [8,9]. Conversely, dysbiosis in the gut microbial community could trigger a spectrum of external systemic illnesses and metabolic abnormalities [[10], [11]. Besides that, commensal microbes serve a key part in the regulation of gastrointestinal secretions, immunological responses, and the metabolism of food [12,13].

The anticancer agent cyclophosphamide (CTX) was developed in 1958 by Norbert Brock [14]. It is a common chemotherapeutic drug used to treat cancer and hematological, and auto-immune diseases, even though it damages DNA structure and lowers the immune system's ability to fight off infections [15,16]. Apart from impacting malignant cells, extended exposure to CTX can suppress immune function by elevating intestinal mucosa inflammation, causing an imbalance of the gut microbes, and distracting gastrointestinal permeability. Afterward, these factors lead to the development of secondary infections triggered by potential microorganisms [13,17]. Therefore, immunomodulators are critical for stimulating the immune system to mitigate CTX-negative effects and prevent homeostasis disruption and cellular cytotoxicity [18].

Bioactive peptides are a subset of polypeptides that include 2–20 residues of amino acids and are produced through proteolytic enzymes derived from marine or botanical sources. Bioactive components are easier to absorb due to their hydrophobic characteristics and compact size [16,19]. Recent research has shown that dietary-derived polypeptides have an extensive range of regulatory influences on immunological responses, which in turn control an organism's daily activity, such as enhancing immunity, reducing blood cholesterol, stabilizing intestinal flora, and having antiviral properties [20,21]. However, recently, a variety of aquatic creatures have been postulated as potential reservoirs for bioactive peptides and have attracted worldwide interest based on their potential to promote health and reduce the probability of sickness [22]. According to Khan et al. (2022), prawn peptides can modulate immunity, stabilize intestinal barrier integrity, and reverse microbial diversity in immuno-deficient CTX-treated mice. Chalamaiah et al. (2018) speculated that protein hydrolysates from roe reveal protective impacts on mice innate as well as adaptive immune systems. Furthermore, shark protein hydrolysates strengthen the gut barrier in mice with compromised immune systems by raising cytokine production [23]. Recent interest in these peptides is attributed to their bioactivity, safety, bioavailability, and varied physiological activities. That possesses substantial potential for use in drug discovery and other related fields [24].

The phylum Mollusca comprises the richest class of animals called cephalopods, which includes the widespread octopus (Octopus vulgaris) species. Octopus is a coastal and Neotropic species found around the world [25]. It is an economically valuable seafood and is widely farmed in European and Mediterranean countries due to its short life cycle, ease of adaptation to captive conditions, and high nutritional and market values [26,27]. Recent research has demonstrated that Octopus peptides may have putative bioactivities, including antibacterial, antioxidant, and anti-yeast properties [[28], [29], [30]]. Beyond the cultivation, very little is known about the bioactivities of octopus, and their immunoregulative activities are still unexplored. However, it is possible to hypothesize that OPH could provide some alleviation to mice with impaired intestinal hemostasis and immunosuppression. In our research, we employed a CTX-immunosuppression mouse model to evaluate the impact of OPH on immunoregulation by examining inflammatory markers, intestinal health, the TLR4/NF-κB pathway, and the gut microbiome. This finding could be the foundation for using OPH as a functional edible ingredient and supplement to support the body's immune system and defend the intestine from inflammatory illnesses.

Material and methods

Chemicals

The octopus's species was bought at a local seafood retailer (Dalian, China). The supplier of CTX and alkaline protease was Sigma-Aldrich Shanghai (China). Beyotime Biotech (Shanghai, China) provided the RIPA lysis buffer. Antibodies from Proteintech (Wuhan, China) were purchased. Boshide Bioengineering (Wuhan, China) provided kits for enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA) for cytokines, immunoglobulin, lipopolysaccharide (LPS), and diamine oxidase (DAO). Boster (Wuhan, China) supplied the DAB immunohistochemistry kit. The supplier of the OREGENE stool DNA extraction kit was Omega Bio-Tek (Shanghai) China. In this investigation, all reagents used were analytical or of higher standards.

Preparation of OPH from octopus

OPH was prepared by stepwise enzymatic hydrolysis according to the previous procedure, with minor modifications [31,32]. Briefly, the octopus was homogenized with a power grinder and washed at 98 °C for 30 min. Then, crushed residues for ultrafiltration were passed through a sieve (150 mm) and stirred in a double volume of distilled water. Furthermore, alkaline proteases at 1 % (w/w) final concentration were added for hydrolysis, the pH was equalized up to 7.0 and incubated (37 °C) solution for 6 h. To stop the enzyme reaction, the solution content was overheated at 90 °C for 20 min. After, the mixture content was centrifuged for 12 min at 13,000 rpm to remove non-digested debris. The supernatant was obtained, and the peptide proportion was determined through the Bradford procedure. Further supernatant was rotavaporized, lyophilized, and preserved at −20 °C for subsequent studies.

OPH molecular mass determination

The molecular mass of Octopus peptide hydrolysate (OPH) was evaluated using liquid-chromatography mass spectrometry (LC-MS), as earlier reported [33]. Briefly, the Octopus peptide was identified using ultra-performance liquid chromatography (Dionex 3000, USA) and mass spectrometry Thermo Scientific (USA). The peptides were subsequently isolated using a column (accucore: C18, 2.6 μm, 100 × 2.1 mm2). In opposed to mobile phase A, which contained a mixture of ultrapure water with 0.1 percent formic acid, and acetonitrile solvent was used in mobile phase B. For mobile phase B, the effluent gradient varied between 5 % and 70 %. Peptide matching was performed using Maxquant software. Evaluation of the identified peptides was carried out using PeptideRanker, which was accessed on December 10, 2023 (http://distilldeep.ucd.ie/PeptideRanker/).

Animal housing

According to the research protocol, 50 healthy male BALB/c mice (5 weeks, 16–18 g in weight) were ordered from Changsheng (Biotech Co., Ltd. Liaoning) China. In our study, mice have been approved and authorized for experimental purposes by the Animal Care and Ethical Council of Dalian Medical University by international standards. All experimental protocols were executed following the instructions stated in the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (ISBN-10: 0-309-15400-6, 2010). Mice were acclimated to their environment and maintained under the following conditions one week before the experiment: 22 ± 2 °C, 12 h of dark/light cycle, 55 ± 4 % humidity level, along with free access to water and ad libitum Mao Hua, Biotech, Co., Ltd. (Shenyang, China).

Experimental design

Following the adaptation phase, the experimental mice were allocated randomly to five separate groups (N = 10) such as 1: NC (normal control), 2: CTX (model control), 3: LDOP (low-dose OP), 4: HDOP (high-dose OP), 5: NOP (normal + OP). Cyclophosphamide (CTX) was intraperitoneally injected into the model, LDOP, and HDOP groups for five consecutive days based on body weight. In contrast, equivalent volumes of normal saline were administered to the NC and NOP groups. Subsequently, for 12 treatment days, a standard saline solution was daily administered to the normal (NC) and CTX-model groups; on the other hand, the LDOP, HDOP, and NOP groups were orally administered Octopus peptide hydrolysate at doses of 200 mg/kg, 400 mg/kg, and 200 mg/kg, respectively. After the experimental duration, mice were starved overnight and given free access to water. Each mouse was weighed, and faeces and blood (eye) samples were taken. Onwards, mice were sacrificed by cervical dislocation and the thymus, colon, and spleen were harvested while stored at −80 °C for further investigations. Calculation of mice weight, water/food, and visceral index

The daily weight fluctuations of the mice were noted. In addition, consumption of both water and food was monitored after each three days throughout the experimental duration. The thymus and spleen organs were obtained after cervical displacement to quantify organ indices using the given formula: Visceral indices = visceral weight (mg)/total body weight (g).

Serum biochemical determination

Blood samples were obtained from eyeballs (mice), and then placed for 1 h at the optimal temperature for coagulation. After centrifugation (8000 rpm/8 min), the serum was accumulated and refrigerated at −80 °C for further investigation. The levels of lipopolysaccharide (LPS), diamine oxidase (DAO), different cytokines (notably IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α), and antibodies (IgM and IgA) in the serum were determined by using Elisa reagent kits, following their procedure.

Intestinal gene expression analysis

The relative mRNA transcription level of Claudin-1, Zonula occludin (ZO-1), Mucin-2, and Occludin was determined by RT-qPCR. Following their instructions, triazole reagents (Thermo Fisher, MA, USA) were utilized to isolate the entire RNA from colon tissue. The concentration and purity of RNA were measured via a Nanophotometer P300 (Implen), Germany, and preserved at −80 °C. To make cDNA, RNA was reverse transcribed by q RT-Mix reagent (HiScript II, Vazyme, Biotech) in Nanjing, China. Furthermore, for the RT-qPCR (Hitech, Beijing, 310053, China) amplification reaction, qPCR-master mix reagent (ChamQ Universal-SYBER) was utilized. The primer sequences are presented in Table S1 β-actin (housekeeping gene), was employed for normalization, and each sample was examined in triplicities. Gene expression levels were calculated by instrumental software (Gene 9660), and GraphPad Prism 9.5 was used for group comparison.

Histopathologic analysis

The spleen, thymus, and colon of each mouse were excised and preserved in 4 % formalin at the optimal temperature for 24 h. In xylene for 10 min, the sliced tissue (5 μm) was deparaffinized and then rehydrated using various ethanol concentrations. Afterward, H&E staining was applied to examine the histomorphometry of the colon, thymus, and spleen, while AB-PAS staining was utilized for the detection of goblet cells in the colon tissue, as previously mentioned [34]. Micrographs were analyzed by Leica microscopes (Wetzlar, Germany).

Immunohistochemistry analysis

In the colon tissues, the mucin-2 gene expression was examined through the immunohistochemistry (IHC) method. The 5 μm microtomic colon slices were mounted on charged slides and deparaffinized for 10 min in xylene, then a series of ethanol solutions were used for rehydration, following the manufacturer instructions for the immunohistochemical (IHC) staining kit SP-KIT992, MXB-Biotech (Beijing), China. Using semi-quantitative methods, we examined each slide for immunolabeled cells three times.

Immunofluorescent analysis

Immunofluorescence (IF) was used to observe the expression level of tight-junction proteins (Claudin-1, Zonula occludin-1, and Occludin) in the mice colonic tissue, following the described method [35]. Firstly, the colon slices (5 μm) were mounted on charged slides. They were then deparaffinized in a xylene solution for 10 min, while for rehydration, various percent ethanol (50 %–100 %) gradients were used. Further, the slides were subjected to blocking, dipped in antigen retrieval reagents, and microwaved for 15 min at 350 W. Onward, PBS (5x3) was used to wash the slides, and the primary antibodies against ZO-1, Claudin-1, and Occludin were added and left for overnight incubation (4 °C). Afterward, slides were washed with PBS (5x3) and incubated along with a secondary antibody for 1-h (goat anti-rabbit, fluorescein (FITC)-conjugated Affinipure) Proteintech, China. To visualize the nucleus, each slide was treated with DAPI for 6 min. The slides were then microscopically examined and photographed.

Western blotting analysis

We conducted the Western blot analysis using the previously described method [36]. In brief, the colon tissue was homogenized by using RIPA lysis buffer with protease inhibitors (Beyotime Biotech, Shanghai, China). After that, the supernatant was collected by centrifuging the homogenate for 6 min at 12,500 rpm (4 °C). While in the supernatant, the entire protein concentration was evaluated through the Beyotime BCA assay (Shanghai, China). Furthermore, proteins (40 μg) were denatured with loading dye (95 °C, 5 min) and separated on SDS-PAGE gels (10–12 %). Whenever the protein was deposited on the PVDF (Millipore, MA, USA) membrane, it was blocked for 2 h with skim milk (5 %). Onward, the membranes were incubated with primary antibodies (β-Actin, ZO-1, Occludin, Claudin-1, TLR-4, p-IκBα, p-p65, and p65) from Proteintech (Wuhan, China) overnight at 4 °C. Following the final rinse in TBST, the membrane was then incubated for 1 h along with a secondary antibody conjugated with HRP (Proteintech; Wuhan, China). The augmentation of protein bands was identified by using a chemiluminescence (ECL) reagent (Shanghai, China). Nevertheless, each test was performed three times independently and quantified by ImageJ 1.52 (MD, USA).

Fecal microbiota analysis

The microbial DNA was isolated from all mouse faeces through the stool DNA extraction kit (OREGENE, CAT-NO.DE-05413) and stored at −20 °C. DNA quantification was done with nanodrop (Thermo Fisher, MA, USA) and qualification with 2 % agarose gel. The bacterial 16S rRNA gene-specific segment (V3-V4), via PCR, was amplified, while 34F: (5′-CCTACGGGAGGCAGCAG-3′) and 518R: (5′-ATTACGCGGCTGCTGG-3′) primers were used with the following conditions: The thermal cycling process consists of 25 cycles: denaturation (98 °C: 15 s) step, annealing (58 °C: 15 s) step, extension (72 °C: 15 s) step, and a final elongation (72 °C: 1 min) step. The dsDNA Assay, and Pico-Green kit (Invitrogen, CA, USA) were utilized to determine the amplicons of PCR, and the Agencourt AM-Pure XP-Beads Kit (Indianapolis, IN) was used for purification. After being purified, the amplicons were pooled and delivered to GUHE Info Tech (Hangzhou) China for Illumina NovoSeq-6000 platform sequencing 2 × 150 bp with pair-end. Sequence data was analyzed using the QIIME (v1.9.0) pipeline, and low-quality reads were removed to identify valid sequences. V Search (V2.4.4) software was used for OTU selection, which includes chimera detection, clustering, and dereplication. Following that, taxonomy classification (OTU) was performed by using the Green Gene Database's representative sequence. Furthermore, QIIME (v1.9.0) pipeline and R-tools (v3.2.0), like "vegan," were utilized to identify OTUs level, α and β diversities, richness, and evenness indices [37]. Beta diversity was measured using Uni-Frac distance metric as depicted by Bray-Curtis principal coordinates analysis (PCoA) and PERMANOVA (permutational multivariate analysis of variance) test was applied for groups comparison. The Benjamini-Hochberg procedure for controlling the false discovery rate was utilized to adjust for multiple comparisons during multiple hypothesis testing at the taxa level. Furthermore, identifying the primary biomarker for each group involves using LEfSe, which employs LDA (linear discrimination analysis) effect size to assess abundance and variations. FAPROTAX and KEGG pathways were employed to examine ecologically relevant metabolites and their roles in prokaryotic clades.

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed statistically using GraphPad Prism (6.01), California, USA. We determined the significance variations using a one-way ANOVA (multiple comparisons) with Tukey's test, considering a p-value (<0.05) as the threshold for statistical significance. The Wilcoxon and Kruskal-Walli's tests were used for groups comparing analyses based on 16S rRNA data. A Mann-Whitney test was employed to statistically analyze the phenotypic and operational taxonomic unit (OTU) data.

Effect of OPH on body weight and immune organ indexes of mice

All through the experiment, the mice's body weight remained relatively consistent, with a steady increase in the control group (Fig. 2A). Following CTX administration, the body weight in the model group progressively dropped. On the other hand, OPH-treated groups showed a substantial protective effect (p < 0.05) on improving body weight loss in a dosage-dependent manner in contrast to model control. Fig. 2B–C shows the average water and food consumption after CTX administration. The control group sustains steady water and food consumption, while the model group tends to dramatically reduce. However, in the OPH-treated groups, water and food (p < 0.05) ingestion were prominently higher than the model control. Furthermore, in the model control, thymus and spleen indices (p < 0.05) significantly declined, which is highly concerned with immunological function. Contrary to the model, OPH-treated groups in a dosage-dependent manner showed remarkable recovery (p < 0.05) of organ indices (Fig. 2D–E). Effects of OPH on immune organ histomorphology

Histological impacts of OPH on immune organs were observed through H&E staining (Fig. 5). In opposed to normal control (NC), the model group morphometry revealed a distorted spleen with an uncertain border across the red and white pulps, as well as a shrinking mature lymphoid follicle with a considerable proportion of inflammatory cells. When treated with OPH, the red and white-pulp margin became apparent and inflammatory cells declined compared to the CTX-model. Furthermore, in the model mice, the thymus cortex atrophied, the medulla-to-thymus border was unclear, and the thymocyte corpuscle was comparatively smaller than in the normal control. Despite OPH administration, the cutaneous border appeared to have elevated thymus corpuscles and a dense cortex. Effects of OPH on colon histology

The impact of OPH on colon histomorphology was demonstrated by H&E staining (Fig. 6). In the model group, CTX disrupted the architecture of the colon, with distorted crypt infrastructure, irregulated surface epithelium, and greater infiltration of inflammatory cells as opposed to normal control (NC). In contrast, OPH administration implied positive outcomes, reducing inflammatory symptoms and repairing colon histological deterioration. There was a dose-dependent restoration of the disrupted columnar epithelium and a consequent decrease in inflammatory cell infiltrations. In addition, we evaluated the intestinal mucosal injury of colonic tissue in mice using immunohistochemistry (IHC) and AB-PAS staining to determine the expression of the Mucin-2 gene and the proliferation of goblet cells. Effect of OPH on microbiota composition

The consequences of OPH treatment on the composition of the intestinal microbe in the fecal contents of mice were examined using 16S rRNA sequencing. During extensive screening and splicing, a fecal sample of all groups obtained 1752396 tags on data sheets (Table S2). Before statistical analysis, the data were grouped into OTUs that shared 97 % similarity in terms of biological information. The rarefaction analysis was used to reveal the sequencing volume of the gut microbiota in each sample. As depicted in Fig. 11A, the curve's trend stated that the species uniformity, richness, and variability in a sample sequence were sufficient for data analysis. Furthermore, the rank abundance curve was utilized to characterize the community richness across each group (Fig. 11B). Contrary to the model, the rank abundance curve in the treated groups is wider and more extended on the horizontal axis, indicating greater species abundance. The Venn diagram depicted the unique and common species (OTU) in various groups, thereby providing a clear view of similarities and overlaps in gut microbiota composition, as seen in Fig. 11C. In the NC, CTX, LDOPH, HDOP, and NOP groups, there were 1412, 1398, 1547, 1569, and 1321 OTUs. The five groups shared 1465 OTU. Although, the LDOP (1547) and HDOP (1569) groups shared more OTUs than the CTX-model (1398) and NC (1412) groups. Compared to the model group, the HDOP group contained more specific OTUs. On the other hand, the NOP group OTU was dramatically declined. Alpha diversity illustrates gut microbial abundance and diversity (Fig. 11D). The sample number of OTUs is estimated using the Ace and Chao1 indices. A higher Chao index signifies a larger variety of microbial species in the sample. In the OPH-supplemented group, chao indices were higher, showing that more microbial species were allocated versus the model group. Usually, Shannon and Simpson indices are used to estimate the microbial variation in a sample. Shannon and Simpson index values showed that model group microbial diversity had a downward trend, while in the LDOP and HDOP groups, microbial diversity enhanced to a certain extent. There are no notable variations (p < 0.05) in bacterial abundance and diversity between distinct groups as assessed using the Chao1 indices, alongside the Shannon and Simpson indices. The β-diversity analysis indicated both differences and similarities among the species compositions of the samples (Fig. 12A). The OTU abundance information and the Bray-Curtis inter-sample distance matrix were utilized for principal component analysis (PCoA), revealing distinct clustering of intestinal microbiota for each group. Various metrics, such as unweighted Uni-Frac, Bray-Curtis, and PERMANOVA test were applied to assess distances and compare group differences in beta diversity. Based on distance matrices, our results indicate that the CTX-model samples exhibited clustering distant from the normal control, which suggests a differentiation in gut microflora composition. In contrast, the microbial community structure in OPH treated mice is quite comparable and clusters close to the normal group. Discussion

Cyclophosphamide, a cancer treatment drug, is effective but often causes significant suppression of the immune system, disrupting the intestinal lining and leading to imbalances in the gut microbiome [38]. Thus, the toxicity of these drugs has prompted researchers to explore using natural ingredients from natural resources to reduce side effects and improve effectiveness [18,39]. Therefore, the present study aimed to evaluate the immunomodulatory capacity of Octopus peptide hydrolysate (OPH) using a mouse model of immune suppression induced by cyclophosphamide (CTX). The findings showed that OPH had a positive impact on the immune system by restoring organ structure, increasing the production of immune-stimulating cytokines and antibodies, and activating the NF-κB mechanism. It also enhances intestinal barrier function by improving tight junction proteins and balancing the gut microbiome in immunosuppressed mice. Ultimately, our goal is to suggest OPH as a viable alternative to conventional immunostimulants, which often come with adverse side effects.

Peptide immunomodulation relies on amino acid content, length, and hydrophobic properties [40]. Previous studies indicate that Glu, Arg, or positively charged amino acid residues may be identified by immune cell receptors that regulate the immune system [41,42]. According to Ren Z et al., 2022 peptide fractions <1 kDa have a promising role in immunomodulatory activity. Similarly, this study discovered that OPH, which includes numerous compact bioactive peptides and an abundance of glycine, glutamic acid, alanine, and proline amino acids, may interact with immune cell receptors to modulate the immunological response.

The immune system is generally recognized to be composed of immunological substances, cells, and visceral organs. The thymus and spleen are the primary immunological organs, and their indices and morphologies may indicate innate immunity performance [43]. Although lymphocyte proliferation is indicated by the thymus and spleen growing masses, immune organ atrophy may be the cause of the dropping immunity [44]. CTX can induce weight loss and disrupt immune organs by inhibiting the proliferation and differentiation of lymphocytes in immune organs [44]. According to our findings, OPH enhanced body growth, increased spleen/thymus indices, reversed splenic and thymic atrophy caused by CTX, implying that OPH treatment has a substantial recovering influence on immunological organ atrophy. Earlier studies found that peptides from soybean and royal jelly enhance body weight, organ index, and morphology in immunosuppressed mice. This suggests their potential to restore impaired immune system function [45,46].

Immune cells produce small soluble extracellular proteins known as cytokines, including tumor necrosis factors (TNF), interleukins (IL), and growth factors. Cytokines performed a vital role in controlling and mediating immune reactions. Therefore, the level of cytokine secretion may serve as an indicator of the immune system in the body [47]. Prior observation showed that CTX administration induces cytokine depletion, which leads to cellular and humoral immune dysfunction [48]. Our findings, which were consistent with previous studies, show that mice treated with CTX had remarkably reduced cytokine and antibody production in the serum. Although post-OPH treatment, cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α, and IL-1β) and antibodies (IgM and IgA) expression were evaluated dose-dependently, implying that OPH boosts mouse immunity by modulating cytokine release. It was found that Cyclina sinensis peptide stimulated the synthesis of cytokines level like as IL-6,TNF-α, and IL-1β [49], whereas chicken peptide inclined the production of serum antibodies like as IgM, IgG, and IgA and cytokines level (IL-2, IFN-γ, and IL-10) to improve the immune responses in a CTX-induced model [50].

The pattern recognition receptor (TLR) is mostly located in immunological and epithelial cells and perform a significant part in the host innate defense system [51]. Research suggests that TLR4 detects bioactive substances and transmits intracellular signals via MyD88, stimulating the NF-κB signalling pathway to synthesize TNF-α and IL-1β during the host innate immune reaction [52]. NF-κB, as an essential transcription factor, consists of two Rel family proteins, p50 and p65. It is normally active as a composite of the p50 and p65 heterodimers, besides its inhibitory protein, IκBα. When NF-κB cascade is turned on, it controls genes that express to release pro inflammatory cytokines. These cytokines perform crucial roles in immunoregulation, inflammation, cell death, and cancer [51,53]. It is known that CTX can harm the intestinal mucosa, disrupt the activity of various signalling pathways, including TLRs and NF-B, and interfere with the secretion of intestinal cytokines. So, NF-κB pathway can be regulated, cytokine secretion imbalances can be corrected, and intestinal immune dysfunction caused by CTX in mice may be cured by using polysaccharides from barely leaves [54], Solenocera crassicornis peptide [31], and Millettia Speciosa polysaccharides [55]. In this study, compared to the CTX model, OPH significantly stimulated the TLR4 receptor and modulated the phosphorylation of IκBα and p65 proteins, implying that OPH might trigger the NF-κB pathway to boost immunity in CTX induced mice.

The intestinal barrier integrity and permeability are significant indications of the health of the intestines. The amount of lipopolysaccharide (LPS) and diamine oxidase (DAO) in the intestine reflects the mechanical barrier's integrity and disruption. The intestinal flora produces lipopolysaccharide (LPS), which is released into the bloodstream from the gut and heightens the inflammatory response. In the intestinal epithelial cells of mammals, DAO is a sort of highly functional intracellular enzyme. DAO activities are closely related to the production of proteins and nucleic acids in the gut mucosal cell. Following the removal and dissolution of epithelial cells in the gut, DAO leaks into the intestinal tract and bloodstream [56,57]. In our investigation, CTX induced the concentration of LPS and DAO in the blood serum through the degradation of intestinal permeability. although after OPH supplementation, the serum level of LPS and DAO were remarkably reduced in the OPH-treated groups. This demonstrates that OPH reversed the intestinal permeability despite preserving the tight junction intact, similar to a prior study [58,59].

Tight connections between intestinal cells regulate gut permeability and the function of the epithelial cell barrier. Tight interactions regulate cellular bypass in a healthy intestinal barrier by reducing the intercellular distance within neighboring cells. This allows nutrients to freely pass through to the layer of intestinal epithelium, while inhibiting the entrance of toxins, inflammatory mediators, and gut microbes, thereby maintaining the defensive function of the gut mucosal barrier. The maintenance of intestinal integrity may be facilitated by tight-junction-associated protein, which is composed of mucin-2, occludin, claudin-1, and zonula occludin. The protein known as ZO-1 serves to link transmembrane proteins (Claudin-1 and Occludin) with the cytoskeleton and maintain gut barrier integrity [60]. Commonly, aberrant Claudin-1 expression as well as distribution in various disorders indicate intestinal barrier injury and increased permeability of intestinal epithelial cells [61]. Goblet cells generate mucin-2, which protects the intestinal mucosal barrier and provides protection against microorganisms [62,63]. In this finding, the gut mucosal barrier was disrupted, goblet cell and mucin production were diminished, and the rate of intestinal proteins associated with tight junctions (Mucin-2, Occludin, Claudin-1, and ZO-1) was downregulated in the model control. Conversely, OPH restored the mucosal barrier integrity, promoted the formation of goblet cells, and reestablished intestinal junction proteins. These findings align with the cyclophosphamide-treated study [64]. The Chimonanthus nitens Oliv polysaccharides and oyster peptides help protect the intestinal mucosa of mice that have been treated with CTX by raising the proportion of Mucin-2, Occludin, Claudin-1, and ZO-1, which supports our results [58,65].

The intestinal flora, which consists of billions of bacteria, is crucial for digestive wellness. Furthermore, intestinal flora sustain immune hemostasis by controlling the immune tolerance of the body and regulate the defensive mechanisms of lymphocyte (T& B) cells [66]. Prior studies indicate that CTX disrupts the intestinal barrier immune system, which affects the dynamic nature of intestinal microbial populations. According to earlier research, the variety and richness of microbiota reduce after CTX treatment [67]. This prompted us to use 16S rRNA sequencing techniques to examine how OPH affected the intestinal microbiota of CTX-treated mice. After administering OPH, Shannon, Simpson, and Chao1 indices prominently increased in microbial diversity and richness analysis especially in high dose. Despite this, a Venn diagram could indicate that there were more OTUs found between the normal and OPH-treated group. The beta-diversity evaluations, which included hierarchical clustering, and PCoA, stated that normal and OPH-treated mice were more similar and clustered more closely with contrast of model control. These findings suggested that OPH administration raised the ratio of helpful microbes till it reverted to a normal value. Recent research findings indicate that a variety of peptides and polysaccharides have a tendency to modify the structure gut microbes, thereby influencing the immune response of CTX-treated mice [68,69].

In the intestinal microflora, Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes are the two key prominent phyla, and their proportion has been closely linked to obesity and inflammation [70]. It was also found that the intestinal microbiota of mice that were given CTX had fewer Firmicutes and more Bacteroidetes; thus, the administration of sea cucumber hydrolysate can enhance immunity by mitigating these alterations [71]. In accordance with our findings, OPH administration decreased the proportion of Bacteroidetes, despite evaluating Firmicutes richness. Our findings imply that OPH can improve gut flora and promote beneficial microorganisms in immunosuppressed mice. These findings suggest that rising Firmicutes and Bacteroidetes ratios may suppress the immune system. According to Mao, J. et al. (2023), the abundance of Bacteroides showed signs of immune-related dysbiosis in immunosuppressed mice [69].

Prior studies established that Lachnospiraceae may synthesis a short chain fatty-acid, and their abundance has been linked to the production of tight-junction proteins [72]. Prevotellaceae _UCG-001 has been found to be a probiotic that promotes IgA secretion and butyrate synthesis in order to decrease inflammation [73]. Alistipes may aid in lowering intestinal inflammation and accelerating intestinal development [74]. LPS was identified as an inflammatory stimulant and was primarily released by Bacteroidetes and Desulfovibrio [66]. In this study on the genus level, the comparative abundance of Lachnospiracea (NK4A136_group), Alistipes, and Prevotellaceae (UGG-001) was evaluated, and the Bacteroidetes ratio declined in the OPH-treated versus CTX-model. These results indicated interactions between immune responses triggered by CTX and variations in the diversity of bacteria. Researchers have found that treating the intestine of immunosuppressed mice with oyster peptide, which boosts the number of Lachnospiraceae (NK4A136_group) and Alistipes had immune-boosting effects [58].

The metabolic and cellular pathways regulate vital processes such as carbohydrate and protein metabolism, RNA processing, and modification, while their metabolomes also maintain various physiological processes such as host immunity. Changes in carbohydrate metabolism have been linked to intestinal bacterial composition and metabolism [75,76]. CTX can disrupt metabolic and cellular pathways and reduce metabolome concentration, which adversely affects various biological processes and microflora composition [77]. In our study, STAMP analysis revealed that the metabolome across the OPH-treated mice was relatively more abundant than in the CTX-model mice. These findings provide the above evidence that OPH may enhance carbohydrate metabolism, nutrient absorption, and energy consumption. Overall, the results indicate that OPH has the capability to address gut microbial imbalance and regulate intestinal stability while enhancing the immune response in CTX-treated immunosuppressed mice. Further research should prioritize isolating and analyzing the individual peptides of OPH to gain deeper insights into its mechanisms of action, considering that it is currently a hydrolysate rather than a purified peptide.

Conclusions

The present study found that OPH exhibited an immune-protective influence on immunosuppression in mice treated with CTX. OPH treatment improved histological changes in the colon, thymus, and spleen, along with evaluating the body weight and immunological organ index of mice. After that, enhanced mucin and goblet cell production strengthened the barrier integrity of the intestinal mucosa, raised the levels of proteins related to tight junctions like Occludin, Mucin-2, ZO-1, and Claudin-1, stimulated cytokines (IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-6), and raised antibodies (IgM and IgA) secretion in the serum and activated the TLR4/NF-κB pathway via phosphorylation of p-IκBα and p-p65 protein. Additionally, OPH regulated an unbalanced gut microbiome at the taxonomic level and inhibited pathogenic microbes. Our findings suggest that OPH could be used in functional foods as an immunostimulant to regulate gut hemostasis and enhance the body's defense system.

Maximizing energy storage in Microgrids with an amended multi-verse optimizer
Authors: Qingpu Hu, Guoxin Zhao, Jian Hu, Navid Razmjooy
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractMicrogrids have emerged as a possible alternative to overcome the difficulties of the combined cooling, heating, and power (CCHP) system in power networks. Energy storage devices are vital for the stable and effective functioning of Microgrids. In this paper, a new modified metaheuristic technique, called the Amended Multiverse Optimizer algorithm (AMVOA) is used to suggest a new method of Microgrid design with energy storage. The Multiverse theory notion served as the inspiration for the metaheuristic optimization method known as the AMVOA. The suggested strategy takes into account the load demand, energy storage technologies, and architecture of a Microgrid with renewable energy sources. The goal is to keep the Microgrid's overall cost as low as possible while preserving its dependability and sustainability. To validate the efficiency of the proposed method, two HRES scenarios are put out, the first of which relies on PV, wind, diesel, and battery power, and the second of which uses PV, diesel, and battery power. To validate the superiority of the proposed method, the method has been compared with five state-of-the-art algorithms, including the Evolutionary Algorithm (EA), Modified Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (MGOA), Improved Gray Wolf Optimization Algorithm (IGWOA), Improved Arithmetic Optimization Algorithm (IAOA), and the original MVOA. The study compares two scenarios: one with wind, PV, diesel, and battery power and the other with only PV, diesel, and battery power. In scenario 1 (Wind/PV/DG/BESS), the AMVOA algorithm achieves optimal results, resulting in a Net Present Cost (NPC) of $299,010 and an energy cost of $0.2309 per kilowatt-hour. The proposed technique successfully integrates 84.86 % renewable energy sources while meeting defined limitations. The optimal sizing for scenario 2 (PV/DG/BESS) is $333,800 with an energy cost of $0.3451 per kilowatt-hour. The AMVOA algorithm outperforms other algorithms in convergence and provides efficient power management. However, further analysis and evaluation are necessary to assess the robustness, practicality, and reliability of the proposed Microgrid configurations. The outcomes show how the suggested AMVO-based strategy may be used to create the best Microgrid architecture with energy storage. The recommended method may be applied as a decision-making tool for Microgrid planning and design, especially for the integration of renewable energy.Keywords: Combined cooling heating and power (CCHP), Microgrid, Energy storage, Amended multi-verse optimizer algorithm (AMVOA)

Introduction

Background

The demand for more effective and dependable energy distribution networks has grown as the globe continues to move toward renewable energy sources [1]. In contrast to conventional grid systems, microgrid systems have emerged as a possible solution to this issue [2]. Regional power distribution networks called Microgrids can operate separately from or in conjunction with the main electrical grid [3]. They consist of a network of interconnected power generators, such as solar cells, wind turbines, and batteries, that can generate, store, and provide electricity to a specific area [4]. They provide a consistent and ecologically friendly source of electricity, making them perfect for remote locations that are not connected to the grid and regions where the system is unreliable or prone to outages [5].

Microgrid systems are gaining popularity due to their ability to integrate renewable energy sources like wind and sunshine into the energy mix [6]. By doing so, carbon dioxide emissions can be reduced, hence reducing the impact of global warming [7]. However, several issues, such as lack of design and operation standardization and the high price of energy storage systems have hindered the deployment of microgrid networks [8]. Additionally, even though the price of clean energy has significantly declined over the past ten years, the cost of energy storage systems is still high, and investing in Microgrid systems is difficult to defend [9].

Microgrid systems are likely to be developed in the next years due to their capacity to function independently, incorporating renewable energy sources, and improving the robustness of important infrastructure assets [10]. To revolutionize the processes of energy creation, storage, and distribution, further research and development are necessary to address challenges related to consistency and cost-effectiveness [11]. One of the primary benefits of microgrid structures for storing electricity is the capacity for effectively controlling the energy provided by clean energy sources [12]. Microgrid energy storage design can reduce reliance on fossil fuels and improve Microgrid system's durability during grid outages. Additionally, it can help lessen carbon dioxide emissions and the effects of climate change [13]. The high price of energy storage systems has, however, prevented the development of Microgrid systems from incorporating energy storage [14]. Therefore, making investments in energy-storage of Microgrid systems is challenging to justify, especially in areas where the main power grid is already reliable [15].

Microgrid design for energy storage is a complex system that needs careful planning and management to operate at its best. Despite these challenges, it is anticipated that in the coming years, demand for Microgrid systems with energy storage will rise as renewable energy sources become more affordable and there is a greater need for dependable and sustainable energy storage solutions [16]. Microgrid design for energy storage is a complex system that needs careful planning and management to operate at its best. Despite these challenges, the demand for Microgrid systems with energy storage is anticipated to rise in the coming years as renewable energy sources become more affordable, and there is a greater need for dependable and sustainable energy storage solutions [17]. To overcome the issues with cost and standards, further research and development is necessary [18].

The use of optimization techniques, known as metaheuristics, allows for the resolution of challenging problems that conventional methods are unable to solve [19]. These algorithms may be used to locate the optimal solution in large search regions since they are based on natural phenomena like evolution and swarm behavior [20]. Metaheuristic approaches may be used to improve the design of Microgrids for energy storage by determining the best position for energy storage devices and renewable energy sources inside the microgrid system [21]. One of the main advantages of using metaheuristic algorithms for Microgrid design is the capacity to simultaneously address many goals, including cost reduction, reliability improvement, and reduction of greenhouse gas emissions [22].

However, due to the complex nature of the optimization problem, the use of Metaheuristic approaches for Microgrid design for energy storage is still in its infancy, and the accuracy of the results obtained using Metaheuristic algorithms may be influenced by the level of accuracy of the information being provided and the optimization variables selected [23].

As the advantages of Microgrid systems with energy storage become clearer and there is a demand for more effective and dependable energy storage solutions, it is anticipated that the usage of metaheuristic methods for microgrid design for energy storage will increase. Despite these difficulties, it is expected that Metaheuristic algorithms will be used more frequently in Microgrid design. The efficiency and dependability of Microgrid systems might be greatly increased using Metaheuristic algorithms. However, further investigation and development are required to handle the issues with complexity and standardization.

A Biofuels-integrated green power Microgrid to store electricity was optimized under uncertainty by Zheng et al. [24]. In Biomass-integrated Microgrids producing power and heat, uncertainties were assessed using predictable limited optimizing and random optimization approaches. To evaluate the planning and design of Microgrid networks utilizing BCHP, a financial Linear Programming (LP) framework with a sliding time frame was created. In Davis, California, case research was investigated for a hypothetical utility grid-connected Microgrid utilization. Based on the expected energy load profile, local climatic data, utility tariff framework, and considering technical and economic outcomes of the different Microgrid elements, the findings revealed that a BCHP/PV with battery storage combination was the greatest cost-effective design. Using Monte Carlo simulation, a density function with probabilities for the cost of electricity was created after uncertainties regarding financial and meteorological assumptions were assessed.

According to a study by Kharrich et al. [25], an improved arithmetic optimizer was used to develop the El Kharga Oasis in Egypt's Microgrid to store energy. The integrated green energy system design presented in this research includes a PV, wind farm, diesel generator, and battery system. The goal was to reduce the overall net current cost, which covers all expenses incurred throughout the project. The new method, known as IAOA, was created by updating the initial Arithmetic Optimization Algorithm(AOA) with the help of the Aquila Optimizer's (AO) leading operators. Two HRES scenarios were put out, the first of which relied on PV, wind, diesel, and battery power, and the second of which used PV, diesel, and battery power. The outcomes showed that the suggested IAOA outperforms other well-known algorithms such as the original AOA, Equilibrium Optimizer (EO), Gray Wolf Optimization (GWO), Artificial Electric Field Algorithm (AEFA), and Harris Hawks Optimization (HHO). It was acknowledged that the suggested IAOA was an effective solution for integrated green power networks.

Gomes et al. [26] researched an optimization study on a typical renewable Microgrid energy system with energy storage. In isolated Microgrids and remote regions, the challenge of developing reliable and self-sufficient renewable energy systems was amplified due to the need for grid flexibility options. One of the leading solutions to increase renewable energy usage in isolated systems was the commission of energy storage. The current study proposed a novel optimization model that sized the most cost-efficient renewable power capacity mix of an autonomous Microgrid supported by storage technologies. The proposed algorithm considered operational, technical, and land-use constraints. The problem was formulated using linear programming, was tested and scrutinized with sets of historical weather, load demand, and installation prices data, and was modeled hour-by-hour. The method was applied to Corvo, an island in the Azores archipelago, Portugal. The results showed that the proposed approach provided the optimal configuration of the renewable-based Microgrid with an LCOE (Levelized Cost of electricity) of 0.21 €/kWh, a value lower than a diesel-based alternative while ensuring minimum land area occupation. Furthermore, sensitivity analysis was also presented to examine the effect of variables on the system's LCOE and PC (present cost). The present study showed that the developed optimal sizing model could improve electricity planning and facilitate energy transition in distributed power systems.

Yee Mah et al. [27] used the P-graph method for optimizing solar electric-based Microgrids with integrated electricity storage. In this paper, a paradigm for multi-period P-graph optimization of solar Microgrids with battery-hydrogen energy storage was proposed. Results indicated that a carbon price of at least $1000 per year was needed to make a Microgrid with hybrid battery-hydrogen storage more cost-effective than traditional energy, and the optimal cost is 704,990 USD/year. The overall cost and necessary carbon price decreased to 262,334 USD/year and 300 USD/year by replacing the hydrogen storage system with grid support.

Optimized Component Design and Forward-Looking Dispatch of an Electricity Microgrid were used by Whitefoot et al. [28] for energy storage modeling. To plan for future energy storage, this research suggested adopting forward-looking optimum dispatch to improve Microgrid designs. It gave a case study of a military post that was “islanded” and had a Microgrid with both renewable and non-renewable energy sources, battery storage, and plug-in cars that could export power. Results revealed that the forward-looking method reduced expected fuel usage by 7.8 % by using storage batteries to plan for future energy shortages rather than only as a buffer for unpredictable renewable energy supplies. Investment in renewable energy technology was justifiable at gasoline costs of more than $5 per gallon ($1.32/litre), with a corresponding reduction in fuel usage of 3–30 %, according to sensitivity analysis of the best design concerning fuel price.

In this work, an optimal arrangement of a CCHP system based on the optimal selection of its parameters was proposed. Assuming the gas engine was the prime mover, the scenario was analyzed. Constrained 4E analysis was also taken into account in this case, along with fuel energy use efficacy, environmental analysis, overall energy effectiveness constraints, exergy evaluation limitations, payback periods (PB), such as exergy evaluation limitations, exergy evaluation limitations, and equivalent uniform annual benefit (EUAB). The evaluation factor (EF) was then used to select the gas engine's optimal volume for the CCHP system. An updated metaheuristic algorithm served as the foundation for optimizing the system. The final findings showed that the proposed strategy is more effective for the targeted goal.

To fulfill the energy needs of the load, the system intends to generate most of its electricity from PV and wind units. Diesel generator

When renewable energy output is minimal, a hybrid energy system might incorporate a diesel generator to supply backup power to the load. It is required to take into account the diesel generator's fuel consumption, output power, and linear fuel consumption to calculate its rated power in a hybrid system. The output power of a diesel generator—the highest amount of electricity it can deliver to the load—determines its rated power. The diesel generator's fuel consumption depends on both its output power and generator performance. The quantity of fuel spent per unit of electrical energy produced by the diesel generator is referred to as linear fuel consumption.

It is required to balance the diesel generator's output power with the load's power demand during low renewable energy generation times to determine the diesel generator's rated power in a hybrid energy system. Understanding the load profile, which details the electrical demand of the load across time, is necessary for this. The diesel generator's rated output must be adequate to handle the load's peak power requirements while reducing fuel use and pollutants.

In simple terms, the output power of the diesel generator is balanced with the power demand of the load during times of low renewable energy production to figure out the rated power of the diesel generator in an HRES. Amended multi-verse optimizer (AMVO)

Motivation

Based on the theory of the big bang, a massive explosion caused the start of our universe. Recently, the theory of Multi-verse (MV) has become well-known among scientists. This theory expresses that more than one big bang is involved in the universe's appearance, and it does refer to the other universes' existence which interact and may crash with each other. Moreover, this theory proposes that various laws of physics may exist in every universe.

For expressing the MVO motivation, three key concepts of the MV have been chosen: black holes, white, and wormholes. Although a white hole has no longer been observed in our universe, physicists believe that the white hole could be the big bang as the key element in the universe's appearance. In the cyclic mode of the MV theory, there is also an idea that white holes or big bangs have been shaped in a location where the crashes happen amid the parallel universes. In marked contrast, the behavior of black holes is different from white ones, and they are frequently seen. These holes absorb everything, i.e., light rays with their extraordinary force of gravity. The holes that make a connection amid various sections of a universe are wormholes. These holes perform as tunnels of space/time travel, whereby everything is capable of traveling immediately from one corner of a universe to the other parts or from one universe to the others. Fig. (3 (A – C)) demonstrates the model of these three key concepts of the MV theory.

All universes have a specific rate of inflation (named eternal); this rate provides the expansion of a universe through space. In each universe, the velocity of inflation is vital when it comes to forming stars, asteroids, planets, laws of physics, life suitability, white, black, and wormholes. In one cyclic model of multi-verse, there is an idea that there is an interaction among multiple universes by black, white, and wormholes to reach a steady condition. It expresses the main motivation of the MVO that has been simulated mathematically in the following section.

Furthermore, every result is considered a rate of inflation, which is correlated to the solution cost function amount. Effect of parametric uncertainty

Parametric uncertainty pertains to the intrinsic variability or limited precision of information on certain essential parameters inside a given system. In the realm of Microgrid optimization, the presence of parametric uncertainty may have notable ramifications for the design and efficacy of energy storage systems. This section provides a comprehensive analysis of the influence of parametric uncertainty on Microgrid operation.

Performance Variability: The presence of parametric uncertainty may lead to fluctuations in the performance of renewable energy sources, such as changes in wind speed or solar irradiation. The aforementioned uncertainties have a direct impact on the capacity for power production and the intermittent nature of various energy sources. Therefore, it is important to take into account the many potential values of these unknown characteristics when determining the most suitable dimensions and functioning of energy storage devices. Refraining from considering this unpredictability might result in efficient storage arrangements and improved overall performance.

The occurrence of an energy imbalance in Microgrids might be attributed to parametric errors associated with load demand estimates or renewable energy output. For example, variations in load demand or unforeseen fluctuations in the supply of renewable energy might lead to an excess or shortage of energy. Energy storage systems are of utmost importance in addressing the disparity between energy supply and demand. They effectively mitigate this issue by storing surplus energy during times of low demand and then supplying more energy during periods of high demand or low energy output. Nevertheless, if the energy storage system fails to account for uncertainties sufficiently, In that case, it may need to be improved for efficiently managing energy imbalances, resulting in heightened dependence on non-renewable backup sources or possible energy inefficiencies.

The economic viability of Microgrids is contingent upon a precise assessment of operational expenses and sources of income. The financial performance of Microgrids may be greatly affected by parametric uncertainties related to variables such as power pricing, fuel costs, and government subsidies. For instance, if the prevailing power rates surpass initial projections or if there are unanticipated changes in government regulations, the economic feasibility of the Microgrid might potentially be undermined. The inclusion of these variables in the energy storage optimization process is of utmost importance in order to guarantee both resilience and financial viability.

System Resilience: Microgrids are often designed with the capability to function independently and endure disruptions or malfunctions inside the main grid. The presence of parametric uncertainty may have an impact on the system's capacity to effectively react to and recover from these occurrences. In the event of an unforeseen surge in load demand or a reduction in renewable energy output caused by unanticipated factors, it is important for the energy storage system to possess the potential to offset the deficit and uphold the stability of the Microgrid. Refraining from considering these uncertainties in designing and implementing energy storage systems might result in diminished system resilience and the possibility of power supply interruptions.

Parametric uncertainties possess significance for strategic planning and investment choices about the extension or enhancement of Microgrids in the long future. The ideal scale, location, and kind of energy storage systems to be installed might be influenced by uncertainties in characteristics, such as load growth rates, available land area, or future technical improvements. The use of robust optimization approaches that account for various situations and probability distributions of unknown parameters might facilitate the formulation of well-informed choices aimed at maximizing the flexibility and adaptability of the Microgrid.

In order to tackle the difficulties presented by parametric uncertainty, scholars have put forward a range of methodologies. Stochastic optimization approaches, such as robust optimization, scenario-based optimization, and chance-constrained optimization are capable of effectively managing uncertainties and delivering robust solutions in a systematic manner. These methodologies provide for examining several potential situations and implementing probabilistic modeling to account for unknown factors. The use of sensitivity analysis is also applicable in the identification of essential parameters and the assessment of their influence on the operation of a system across varying degrees of uncertainty.

In summary, the presence of parametric uncertainty presents notable obstacles to the optimization of Microgrids, particularly with regard to energy storage. It is crucial to include a comprehensive analysis of uncertainties pertaining to renewable energy output, load demand, economic variables, and system resilience in order to develop energy storage designs that are both resilient and efficient. By using stochastic optimization methods and performing sensitivity assessments, Microgrid planners and operators may enhance their decision-making process, improving the dependability, cost efficiency, and long-term sustainability of Microgrid systems.

The inclusion of uncertainty in the evaluation of energy storage performance enhances the comprehensiveness and realism of comparison techniques used in Microgrid systems. In the absence of uncertainty, deterministic optimization strategies may fail to accurately capture the inherent variety and unpredictability present in real-world scenarios. Stochastic optimization approaches, such as Monte Carlo simulation or scenario-based optimization, provide valuable insights into the efficiency of energy storage systems in the presence of varying degrees of uncertainty. The evaluation of uncertainty is contingent upon the assessment of performance measures, including robustness, risk management, and value of information. Sensitivity analysis is a valuable tool for identifying and assessing significant uncertainties within a given model, hence providing guidance for the selection and implementation of appropriate modeling methodologies. The efficacy of comparison techniques is contingent upon the unique environment in which they are used. Therefore, it is advisable to do case-specific investigations and validation procedures using real-world data.

Conclusion

Microgrids have become a feasible option for tackling the complexities of power networks, namely in the realm of Combined Cooling Heating and Power (CCHP) systems. Localized energy systems provide a means for decentralized power production and delivery, integrating renewable energy sources and energy storage technologies to improve stability and efficiency. Energy storage is an essential component within Microgrids as it serves to address the issue of intermittency associated with renewable energy sources. By effectively storing energy, it enables the provision of a consistent power supply during times characterized by either high demand or low output. The optimization of Microgrid operations, in terms of cost and sustainability, relies heavily on the efficient management of energy storage. In order to optimize energy storage management in Microgrids, scholars have investigated the use of metaheuristic optimization methods. These algorithms provide novel solutions by emulating natural processes and identifying optimum configurations that reduce the cost of the system while satisfying performance criteria. The present study proposed a new mythology based on a modified metaheuristic called the Amended Multi-Verse Optimizer Algorithm (AMVOA), which draws inspiration from the multiverse idea. The proposed AMVOA algorithm integrated concepts from multiverse theory with metaheuristic optimization methodologies in order to provide novel approaches for the design of Microgrids. The objective of the research conducted in this study was to optimize energy storage in Microgrids via the use of the AMVOA algorithm. The research examines and contrasts two scenarios: one that integrates wind, Photovoltaic (PV), diesel, and battery power, and another that only relies on PV, diesel, and battery power. The study endeavors to provide insights into the feasibility, efficacy, and cost-efficiency of Microgrid designs with optimal energy storage systems. It will be achieved by a comprehensive analysis that encompasses cost optimization, reliability assessment, and performance evaluation. To validate the superiority of the proposed method, the method was compared with five algorithms, including the Evolutionary Algorithm (EA), Modified Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (MGOA), Improved Gray Wolf Optimization Algorithm (IGWOA), Improved Arithmetic Optimization Algorithm (IAOA), and the original MVOA. The study compared two scenarios: one with wind, PV, diesel, and battery power and the other with only PV, diesel, and battery power. In scenario 1 (Wind, PV, DG, and BESS), optimal results were obtained using the AMVOA algorithm, resulting in a Net Present Cost (NPC) of $299,010 and an energy cost of $0.2309 per kilowatt-hour. The proposed technique successfully incorporated a significant amount of renewable energy sources (84.86 %) into the system while meeting defined limitations, such as a 5.22 % Loss of Power Supply Probability (LPSP). The optimal sizing suggested a Photovoltaic area of 155.8 m2, a swept wind area of 263.4 m2, a Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) capacity of 12.89 kWh, and a nominal diesel power of 17.89 kW. In scenario 2 (PV, DG, and BESS), the AMVOA algorithm provided an optimum project investment plan with a cost of $333,800 and an energy cost of $0.3451 per kilowatt-hour. The optimal sizing indicated a Photovoltaic area of 243.8 m2 and a required diesel capacity of 18.19 kW. In this case, a Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) was not required, but the trade-offs between cost and system stability needed to be considered. The research concluded that the AMVOA algorithm outperformed other algorithms in terms of convergence and provided efficient power management in both scenarios. However, further analysis and evaluation were necessary to assess the robustness, practicality, and reliability of the proposed Microgrid configurations. The findings demonstrated the superiority of the suggested strategy when results were contrasted with those obtained using alternative optimization approaches. Future research should explore integrating renewable energy sources, demand response strategies, and evaluating different storage technologies. Real-world constraints and uncertainties should be considered, and economic analysis and policy implications should be considered. Field testing and validation of optimized energy storage solutions will validate the effectiveness and practicality of proposed designs. It will contribute to sustainable, reliable, and cost-effective Microgrid systems supporting clean and resilient energy of future.

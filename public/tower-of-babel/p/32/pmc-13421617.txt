Sarcopenia does not affect total liver function measured using technetium-99m mebrofenin hepatobiliary scintigraphy
Authors: Pieter Arntz, Pim Olthof, Fadi Rassam, Roelof Bennink, Robert-Jan Coelen, Thomas van Gulik, Jan Heil, Geert Kazemier, Bart Takkenberg, Joris Erdmann
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHepatobiliary scintigraphy (HBS) using technetium-99m mebrofenin is used to preoperatively estimate risk of adverse outcomes after major liver resections. Sarcopenia has been independently associated with adverse outcomes after surgery. This retrospective cohort study aimed to correlate patient- and disease-related factors, including sarcopenia, with total liver function (TLF) through HBS analysis.ResultsDuring the study period, 346 patients underwent HBS to quantify TLF. The median TLF was 15.0 (12.1–16.7) %/min. Body composition parameters were not correlated to TLF. Older age, a diagnosis of hepatocellular carcinoma, underlying cirrhosis and high serum bilirubin levels were independently correlated with decreased TLF. In contrast, the absence of underlying liver disease and overall good health were independently correlated with higher TLF. Sarcopenia was not correlated with TLF.ConclusionSarcopenia does not impact TLF. Total liver function measured with HBS is negatively correlated with older age, a diagnosis of hepatocellular carcinoma, underlying cirrhosis and serum bilirubin level.Keywords: Liver function tests, Radionuclide imaging, Sarcopenia, Cancer, Hepatectomy

Introduction

Total liver volume (TLV) measurements have been the standard for estimating risk of liver failure after major liver resection [1]. Despite various future liver remnant (FLR) volume criteria to guide patient selection for safe resection, liver failure continuous to be a substantial risk, particularly in high-risk subgroups [1–3].

The persistent occurrence of post-hepatectomy liver failure (PHLF) has driven the development of additional modalities to preoperatively assess liver function [4, 5]. One such modality is hepatobiliary scintigraphy (HBS) using technetium-99m mebrofenin [1, 4, 6, 7]. The initial prospective study on HBS reported an excellent predictive value for PHLF with an area under the curve value of 0.916 (95% confidence interval 0.837–0.994) [6]. Since then, the technique has been used and evaluated in various subgroups of patients and as part of routine patient work up before major liver resection [8–11].

Sarcopenia, a condition defined as the age-related reduction in skeletal muscle mass, strength and function, is linked to adverse outcomes after liver surgery [12–14]. Studies have shown that sarcopenic patients have disproportionately small TLV compared to non-sarcopenic patients [15, 16]. This may partially explain the increased susceptibility to post-operative complications such as PHLF [17]. However, the actual correlation between sarcopenia and total liver function (TLF) remains poorly understood.

This retrospective single-center cohort study aimed to correlate patient- and disease-related factors, including sarcopenia, with TLF through HBS analysis.

Methods

Study design, setting and participants

This study was designed at the Amsterdam UMC as a single center retrospective cohort study. All patients that underwent HBS for any indication that required the assessment of liver function, as well as several healthy volunteers for alternative studies, between January 2014 and February 2018 were included. Data were obtained from a retrospectively maintained database. Data reporting was performed according to the guidelines outlined by STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology).

Variables

The primary endpoint of this study was the effect of sarcopenia on TLF. The secondary endpoint was the effect of several patient- and disease- related factors on TLF. Acquisition and interpretation of HBS was performed as previously described in detail [7]. After injection of 200 MBq technetium-99m mebrofenin, dynamic acquisitions for 38 frames of 10 s per frame were obtained for calculation of the mebrofenin uptake rate. Afterwards, a single photon emission computed tomography (SPECT) acquisition was performed and combined with a supplementary low-dose computed tomography (CT) to allow attenuation correction and segmental measurements. The mebrofenin uptake rate was defined as TLF and expressed as %/min. Evaluation by HBS was part of the routine work-up in patients scheduled for major liver resection or selective internal radiation therapy. Several healthy subjects were included as result of a previous conducted study.

The low-dose CT was utilized for the skeletal muscle area (SMA) measurements in the axial plane of the mid-third lumbar vertebra, applying Osirix Lite v12.0.2 imaging software (Pixmeo SARL, Switzerland). Although high-dose CT is considered the gold standard for the assessment of muscle mass, low-dose CT accurately substitutes for measurements of SMA [18]. In missing cases, alternative CT scans acquired within a month timespan relative to the HBS scan date were used. The threshold range was set from − 30 to + 150 Hounsfield units to delineate SMA, which was standardized to the body surface area (BSA) in square meters (m2) to define the skeletal muscle index (SMI). Sarcopenia cut-off values were defined by SMI, body mass index (BMI) and sex (SMI < 41 cm2/m2 in women, SMI < 43 cm2/m2 in men with a BMI < 25, and 53 cm2/m2 in men with a BMI > 25). The considered ‘most optimal’ cut-off values were applied [19].

Hepatitis was defined as a history of serology confirmed hepatitis. Cholestasis was defined as elevated levels of bilirubin at the time of presentation. Steatosis and cirrhosis were defined as any diagnosis of steatosis or cirrhosis through any means in the diagnostic work-up. Cholangitis was defined as any episode of fever, leukocytosis, or elevated C-reactive protein, and malaise requiring biliary drainage. Other comorbidities were classified in the categories; cardiac, vascular, diabetes, pulmonary, neurological, urogenital, musculoskeletal, infectious, endocrine, renal, thrombosis, and gastrointestinal.

Bias

Data reporting bias was minimized through the use of standardized data collection forms and by employing random checks for accurate data extraction and entry processing. Selection bias was reduced by including all patients who underwent HBS within the specified timeframe.

Statistical methods

All continuous data are presented as median with inter-quartile range and differences were tested using Mann–Whitney or Kruskall-Wallis tests. All categorical data are presented as number with percentage. Uni- and multivariable analysis for liver function was performed using linear regression. Variables with a P-value equal to or smaller than 0.100 are included in the multivariable analysis using backward selection.

Ethical approval

The need for ethical approval and individual informed consent was waived by the local medical ethics committee.

There was a correlation of age with TLF (R2 0.054, P < 0.001, Fig. 3A), yet the differences between age groups were quite small (Fig. 3B). More pronounced differences in TLF were observed in patients depending on the type of diagnoses (Fig. 3C). Most of these patients were planned for liver resection and underwent HBS to determine FLR function. The diagnoses were hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) in 75 (22%) patients, 135 (39%) were diagnosed with a biliary tumor, 75 (22%) had colorectal liver metastases (CRLM), 24 (7%) had a benign liver tumor, and 15 (4%) patients another diagnosis. The remaining 22 (6%) patients were healthy subjects from a prospective clinical trial. Total liver function was highest in healthy subjects, and the spread was small. Patients with a malignant tumor had lower TLF, and the variation in TLF within the group was larger. Total liver function was also dependent on bilirubin level at the time of HBS (R2 0.167, P < 0.001, Fig. 3D).

Uni- and multivariable analysis of factors that predict total liver function

In multivariable analyses, older age, cirrhosis, increased serum bilirubin levels and patients diagnosed with HCC were negatively associated with TLF. Healthy control patients was identified as independent predictor of increased TLF. Serum albumin level was also negatively associated with TLF, but the P-value at multivariable analysis was 0.052.

Discussion

This study shows that sarcopenia is not associated with TLF measured using HBS. The results align with a study reporting on the increase in liver function following portal vein embolization in sarcopenic patients, where liver function was found to be preserved compared to non-sarcopenic patients [20]. However, the results are in contrast with findings regarding total liver volume, which has been reported to be negatively associated with sarcopenia [15, 16].

Rather than a direct relation with TLF and PHLF, sarcopenia may result in susceptibility to postoperative infection and delayed recovery, which in turn are related to liver failure and mortality [21]. The observation that sarcopenia influences TLV but does not influence TLF points to a more functional based risk evaluation in the preoperative assessments of these patients. Previous research found that kinetic growth rate following portal vein embolization was found to be lower in sarcopenic patients [22], suggesting sarcopenic patients are more at risk for PHLF compared to non-sarcopenic patients. In addition, the findings could suggest impaired postoperative regeneration in sarcopenic patients.

To our knowledge this is the first and largest cohort of patients who underwent HBS where predictive factors of TLF were analyzed. To date, clinical decisions in the preoperative risk evaluation are based on FLR function [6]. Including TLF in the work-up may provide added value regarding factors indicative for adverse postoperative outcomes that have yet been linked to TLF. As expected, the current study showed that TLF determined by HBS is dependent on age, the presence of cirrhosis, and bilirubin level. In addition, TLF was lower in patients with HCC as a result of cirrhosis and higher in healthy patients. Numerous studies have shown that TLV is correlated to body composition parameters, which has led to the development of formulas to estimate liver volume [23–25]. These formulas are commonly used in liver transplantation and in liver resection to overcome the influence of tumor size on total liver volume assessment [26–28]. It seemed reasonable to assume a correlation of TLF with body composition, which is why the measurements using HBS were corrected for BSA upon instruction of the application before major liver resection. However, in accordance with a previous smaller study in patients with perihilar cholangiocarcinoma [8], TLF is not correlated to body composition parameters in this cohort and therefore not corrected for BSA.

Total liver function declined with age in this present study as shown at both uni- and multivariable analysis. This observation is supported by the increased rates of liver failure and mortality after liver resection in older patients [29–33]. In addition, there have been numerous publications that the ability of the liver to regenerate is also impaired in older patients [34–37]. Therefore it is likely that older patients are at increased risk of liver failure with a remnant liver function equal to their younger counterparts, which should be taken in consideration during the preoperative work-up.

Liver cirrhosis negatively impacts liver function which can be demonstrated by several liver function scores (e.g. MELD score, Child–Pugh classification) as well as quantitative liver function tests (e.g. indocyanine green clearance, liver stiffness measurements) [38–40]. This report confirmed the predictive value of HBS for liver function in patients with cirrhosis and HCC [41, 42]. Therefore it is likely HBS is useful in the work up of patients with cirrhosis and HCC prior to liver resection [43]. In addition, quantification of TLF might be beneficial in the preoperative risk analysis for cirrhotic patients undergoing non-hepatic surgery [44]. Considering the susceptibility of cirrhotic patients to complications after non-hepatic surgery, future studies should evaluate whether assessment of TLF using HBS is useful in the preoperative work-up of these patients [45].

The higher TLF value measured in healthy individuals further supports the validity of HBS to measure TLF. Benign lesions were not associated with increased TLF, while these patients are also expected to be otherwise healthy, and usually have low morbidity and mortality rates after liver resection [46]. In this study the benign lesions group included patients with mass forming (IgG-4) cholangitis, who were preoperatively diagnosed as perihilar cholangiocarcinoma. These diagnoses likely explain the TLF measurements in this subgroup of patients with benign lesions, which are not comparable to for instance patients with adenomas [47]. In patients with biliary tumors, TLF was not decreased at multivariable analysis. Patients with tumors leading to biliary obstruction usually require larger liver remnants for a safe resection due to a high risk of PHLF [8, 30, 48]. The non-decreased TLF values in these patients are likely due to the correction for age, since these patients are generally older than patients with for instance CRLM [49–52]. Also, most patients underwent biliary drainage, which is found to effectively improve liver function and can additionally explain the non-decreased TLF values [53]. Also, many of these patients suffer from cholestasis and the multivariable analyses corrected for bilirubin levels [7, 8].

Bilirubin is taken up by the liver though organic anion transporters, specifically OATP1B3 and OATP1B3 [54]. Mebrofenin is taken up by hepatocytes through the same transporters, OATP1B1 and OATP1B3, resulting in potential competitive uptake [55]. Therefore TLF will likely be low in case of high bilirubin levels, as shown in the current and a previous study [8]. In case of hyperbilirubinemia, TLF measurement can be false-low as a result of the competition between mebrofenin and bilirubin at the transporter level. High bilirubin levels probably also reflect actual liver dysfunction, or a combination of competition and dysfunction, which can therefore not be distinguished using HBS [8]. Therefore it is recommended not to perform HBS in case of bilirubin levels that exceed 30–50 µmol/L [7, 8]. Additional biliary drainage could be considered, since high serum bilirubin levels and incomplete biliary drainage are in itself associated with adverse outcomes after liver resection [30, 48].

In this study there was a very modest correlation of TLF with serum albumin level, while this correlation only reached a P-value of 0.052 at multivariable analysis. Mebrofenin is mainly bound to albumin which impairs its renal excretion and facilitated hepatic uptake [56]. In case of severely low serum albumin levels, less mebrofenin can be bound, resulting in renal excretion and false-low TLF measurements [56]. This mechanism is a possible explanation for the weak correlation to TLF. Low serum albumin can also be a sign of liver dysfunction resulting in true impaired TLF values. Given the weak correlation to minor differences, the impact of serum albumin is likely not clinically relevant.

Chemotherapy did not result in lower measurement of TLF in the current study. Numerous studies have focused on chemotherapy-associated liver injury [57, 58]. Most studies did not find a major impact of chemotherapy before liver resection on postoperative outcomes [59–62]. Chemotherapy-associated liver injury can be detected through liver function testing, but shows relatively fast recovery after cessation of chemotherapy. It is therefore dependent on the timing of functional assessment, which might explain the absent correlation in the present study [63, 64]. A time interval between chemotherapy and liver resection is likely to benefit postoperative outcomes [65]. Chemotherapy-associated liver injury occurs more frequently after 6 or more cycles of oxaliplatin-based regimens, the sinusoidal dilatation that occurs does impact outcomes resection [66, 67]. Since most patients underwent less cycles this could also explain the absent TLF difference in the present study, together with the small sample size. In addition, HBS measures hepatic uptake capacity, hepatic excretion and metabolism are not quantified and might be especially impaired in chemotherapy-associated liver injury [68].

This study has several limitations, mainly attributable to the retrospective study design. All scans were interpreted by a single expert nuclear medicine specialist. Ideally this study should have included detailed histologic scoring of all liver parenchymal quality characteristics. Since some patients did not proceed to resection for several reasons (e.g., tumor progression, insufficient liver remnant), or were treated by means in which no pathology of ‘normal’ liver tissue is available (e.g., selective internal radiation therapy or radiofrequent ablation).

Conclusion

Sarcopenia does not impact TLF measured by HBS. Sarcopenia is probably associated with poor outcomes independently of TLF, as both sarcopenia and a reduced TLF result in an increase in postoperative morbidity and mortality but exhibit no direct correlation. Total liver function determined by HBS is impacted negatively by older age, a diagnosis of cirrhosis or hepatocellular carcinoma, and increasing serum bilirubin levels.

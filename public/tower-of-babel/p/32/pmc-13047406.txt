Research Trends on Perioperative Complications of Knee Replacement: A Bibliometric Analysis
Authors: Wei Liang, Kai Zhang, Minghui Zhang, Mingwu Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundKnee replacement is a surgical procedure designed to alleviate severe knee pain and dysfunction by removing the damaged joint surface and replacing it with an artificial prosthesis, thereby restoring joint function and reducing pain. This study aims to identify and analyze research trends related to perioperative complications associated with knee replacement through a comprehensive bibliometric analysis.MethodsA systematic literature search was conducted using the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database. For bibliometric analysis, we utilized VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and the R package “Bibliometrix” to visualize collaboration networks, keyword co-occurrences, and emerging research trends.ResultsA total of 5,751 articles published between 1973 and 2024 were included, contributed by 22,328 authors affiliated with 213 institutions across 47 countries. The United States (USA) led in publication volume with 2,403 articles and the highest citation count of 70,642. Parvizi Javad was identified as the most prolific author, contributing substantially with a high citation impact, while Harvard University emerged as the most productive institution. The Journal of Arthroplasty was a central publication venue, highlighted by its strong citation metrics. The most frequently occurring keywords were “outcomes” and “risk factors,” reflecting core research themes in perioperative complications associated with knee replacement. Keyword burst analysis indicated increased research focus on topics such as “dislocation” and “enhanced recovery,” underscoring key trends in the field.ConclusionsThis bibliometric analysis highlights pivotal research trends in perioperative complications of knee replacement, with the USA and Harvard University as primary contributors. Future research is expected to focus on advancing Enhanced Recovery After Surgery protocols to further reduce complications and improve patient outcomes.

Knee replacement is an effective and widely performed procedure to alleviate pain and restore function in patients with advanced knee joint damage, particularly osteoarthritis.12) With over 670,000 procedures conducted annually in the United States (USA) alone,3) the demand for knee replacement surgery continues to grow globally. However, perioperative complications, including infection, thromboembolism, joint dislocation, and prosthesis-related issues, remain significant clinical challenges, often requiring additional interventions such as revision surgeries.45) These complications impose substantial burdens on both patients and healthcare systems.6) For example, infection rates can reach 4.66% among morbidly obese patients and climb to 9.8% when obesity is combined with diabetes.7) Similarly, thromboembolic events, including deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism, continue to pose life-threatening risks during postoperative care.8)

Bibliometrics, a quantitative analytical approach, evaluates the impact and progression of scientific publications, offering insights into research trends, influential works, and emerging topics.9) Previous studies, such as Poursalehian et al.’s bibliometric analysis of knee arthroplasty research,10) have identified key themes like periprosthetic joint infection, opioid use, and robotic-assisted total knee arthroplasty (TKA). However, to the best of our knowledge, no bibliometric analysis has specifically focused on perioperative complications of knee replacement, leaving a critical gap in understanding the trends, challenges, and future directions in this field.

This study aims to fill this gap by conducting a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of research trends related to perioperative complications in knee replacement. By identifying key research themes, collaboration networks, and emerging trends, this study provides valuable insights and offers both clinical guidance in managing these complications and research-oriented directions for advancing knowledge in this critical area.

METHODS

This study does not require ethical review approval, as it is based on bibliometric analysis of publicly available data. The research involves no direct interaction with human subjects, nor does it involve the collection of personal or sensitive information. All data used in this study are aggregated and derived from published literature, ensuring that no individuals can be identified or harmed. As a non-invasive research method, bibliometric analysis focuses solely on trends and patterns in scientific publications without posing any ethical risks. All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations.

Search Strategies and Data Collection

In this study, we conducted a comprehensive bibliographic search using the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC). The search strategy employed was as follows: (TS=((perioperative OR postoperative OR intraoperative OR perisurgical OR postsurgical OR intrasurgical) AND complication*)) AND TS=(Knee AND (replace* OR arthr*)). To avoid discrepancies from database updates, the literature retrieval was performed on August 6, 2024. Only English-language publications were considered, with articles being the sole document type included in the study. Bibliographic information was exported using the “full record and cited references” and “plain text” formats during the filtering process.

Statistical Analysis

Data relevant to the study were extracted from the selected articles, and key bibliometric indicators were identified and calculated using Microsoft Excel. These indicators included the annual publication count, citation frequency, average citation frequency, journal names and impact factor (IF), publishing regions and institutions, and author contributions. For data visualization, we employed 3 bibliometric analysis tools: VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), CiteSpace (version 6.3.R1), and R package “Bibliometrix” (https://bibliometric.com/). VOSviewer, a tool widely used for mapping institutional collaboration, author networks, co-authorship, citation, and co-citation patterns, enabled us to visualize and analyze complex collaborative networks, deepening our understanding of the interconnections among publications, countries, authors, and journals. Additionally, we used VOSviewer for keyword co-occurrence analysis to identify emerging trends and research hotspots. As a complementary tool, CiteSpace was employed for burst analysis of citations, which illustrated the frequency and intensity of keyword occurrences and highlighted periods of notable research impact. The initiation and termination times of these keywords also provided insight into their temporal distribution. Particular attention was given to high-frequency focal points, reflecting areas of prominence within the scientific landscape. The R package “Bibliometrix” was instrumental in conducting a comprehensive analysis and mapping of global research distribution, revealing key trends and patterns. This tool facilitated the evaluation of scholarly impact among authors, journals, and institutions in the dataset.

The H-index was employed to quantify the academic impact of both individuals and journals.11) This metric reflects a researcher’s productivity as well as the citation impact of their publications, thereby providing a balanced measure of academic influence.12) To assess the prestige and citation influence of journals, we utilized Journal Citation Reports (JCR) quartiles and IF. JCR quartiles categorize journals into 4 tiers, with Q1 representing the highest level of academic impact. The IF quantifies the average number of citations received by articles published in a journal over the preceding 2 years. For this analysis, we employed the most recent 2024 release of JCR and IF data to ensure an up-to-date assessment of journal prestige and citation influence.

Spanning 5 decades (1973–2024), the study analyzed 5,751 publications by 22,328 authors, with an average of 5.71 co-authors per document and a 9.6% rate of international co-authorship. A total of 6,764 keywords were extracted, and the average document age was 7.87 years, with an average of 24.57 citations per publication.

The 30-year period from 1994 to 2024 is shown on the horizontal axis (Fig. 2). The data indicated a gradual increase in publications before 2006, followed by a sharp rise after 2009, with the solid line reflecting continuous growth until 2021. We applied an exponential model to fit the data, which demonstrated a clear exponential growth trend, suggesting a positive trajectory in publication numbers. Overall, the fitted curve indicated a steady exponential growth pattern in publication output.

Both journals had high IF, with The Journal of Arthroplasty at 3.4 and The Journal of Bone and Joint Surgery—American Volume at 4.4, and both were categorized as Q1 journals in the JCR Quartile, underscoring their prominent status in orthopedic research (Table 3).

Analysis of the Keywords

In the keyword co-occurrence network analysis, we examined the relationships between 159 keywords that appeared at least 35 times. As shown in Fig. 9, “outcomes” occupied a central position with 817 occurrences and a total link strength of 3,571, followed by “surgery” (804 occurrences, 3,323 link strength). The keyword co-occurrence analysis categorized knee replacement research into 3 main hotspots: surgical techniques, postoperative complications, and patient-related factors. Keywords such as “alignment” (91 occurrences, 232 link strength) and “outcomes” (817 occurrences, 3,571 link strength) highlighted a focus on improving surgical precision and functional results. Keywords including “pulmonary embolism” (89 occurrences, 448 link strength) and “thromboprophylaxis” (59 occurrences, 322 link strength) indicated ongoing research into managing thromboembolic events. Related terms like “venous thromboembolism” (132 occurrences, 677 link strength) and “deep vein thrombosis” (133 occurrences, 643 link strength) emphasized the focus on perioperative safety. Keywords such as “body-mass index” (121 occurrences, 652 link strength), “cost” (77 occurrences, 374 link strength), and “morbid obesity” (35 occurrences, 194 link strength) pointed to the role of patient characteristics in surgical outcomes. Other terms like “cost-effectiveness” (41 occurrences, 196 link strength) and “readmission” (77 occurrences, 403 link strength) reflected interest in economic considerations alongside clinical outcomes. The various colors on the graph represented the average publication year of each keyword. Notably, the transition from purple (such as “total hip replacement”) to green (including “osteoarthritis” and “body-mass index”), followed by yellow (such as “cost” and “morbid obesity”), illustrated the progression and evolution of keyword usage over time (Fig. 9).

Analysis of Burst Keywords

We emphasized the importance of keyword transformations through burst keyword analysis (Fig. 10). Early on, keywords such as “arthritis” (2008–2014, strength 16.27) and “arthroplasty” (2008–2014, strength 14.16) reflected a strong focus on the underlying conditions and surgical approaches in knee replacement research. The keyword “follow-up” (2008–2013, strength 12.52) highlighted attention on monitoring postoperative outcomes, while “osteoarthritis” (2008–2011, strength 12.05) emphasized its role as a primary indication for knee arthroplasty. The term “enoxaparin” (2008–2016, strength 8.1) reflected significant interest in thrombotic complication prevention, particularly through anticoagulation strategies. Between 2009 and 2015, keywords like “prosthesis” (2009–2015, strength 18.2) and “rehabilitation” (2009–2012, strength 13.57) indicated growing research into postoperative recovery. The keyword “pulmonary embolism” (2009–2014, strength 10.91) marked attention to serious perioperative complications, while “experience” (2009–2016, strength 7.85) pointed to the impact of surgical expertise on patient outcomes. In subsequent years, “prevention” (2010–2014, strength 9.05) and “motion” (2011–2017, strength 7.88) emerged as focal points in mitigating risks and maintaining joint functionality. Later, keywords such as “controlled trial” (2014–2019, strength 10.55) and “thromboprophylaxis” (2014–2018, strength 9.71) underscored the rise of evidence-based research addressing clinical challenges. Keywords like “quality” (2020–2021, strength 7.77) and “enhanced recovery” (2021–2024, strength 11.67) marked a shift toward improved patient care and recovery protocols. The emergence of “revision total knee arthroplasty” (2021–2024, strength 9.19) highlighted growing complexity in managing revision procedures. Finally, the keyword “dislocation” (2022–2024, strength 8.84) indicated recent attention on joint stability as a critical factor in postoperative success (Fig. 10).

DISCUSSION

This bibliometric analysis of research on perioperative complications in knee replacement from 1973 to 2024 revealed significant growth in the field. Research output increased gradually before 2006 and surged sharply after 2009, with sustained growth continuing until 2021. Key research trends included surgical outcomes, risk factors, postoperative recovery strategies, complication management, and advancements in prosthetic design. In recent years, there has been a growing emphasis on enhanced recovery protocols and improving patient quality of life.

The USA, particularly through prominent institutions such as Harvard University, has played a dominant role in research on perioperative complications in knee replacement. As a country with a high prevalence of knee replacement surgeries, the USA has contributed the highest number of publications, reflecting its robust infrastructure for biomedical research and its commitment to improving surgical outcomes.13) Harvard University exemplifies this model, with extensive research networks and state-of-the-art facilities driving high-impact research that influences clinical practices globally.14)

In terms of authorship, Parvizi Javad emerged as one of the most prolific contributors to the field, with a substantial number of publications and high citation metrics, underscoring his significant influence on the research landscape surrounding knee replacement complications. His work has addressed critical aspects such as infection control and postoperative recovery, which are central to improving outcomes in knee replacement surgeries.15) Notably, his study on periprosthetic joint infections revealed that early and aggressive treatment significantly reduces complications and improves patient prognosis.16) This targeted approach to managing infections has contributed to the broader understanding of optimal perioperative care, offering valuable insights into improving therapeutic outcomes in knee replacement procedures.

The keyword co-occurrence network in knee replacement research can be categorized into 3 main hotspots, reflecting distinct research themes: surgical techniques, postoperative complications, and patient-related factors. The first hotspot centers on surgical techniques, with keywords such as “alignment” and “outcomes” reflecting significant interest in improving surgical precision and functional results. Improper alignment compromises knee stability, increasing the risk of complications such as joint looseness and dislocation.17) Advances in surgical techniques aim to optimize ligament tension and joint kinematics, which are critical for achieving better functional outcomes. The second hotspot is focused on postoperative complications, as highlighted by keywords like “pulmonary embolism” and “thromboprophylaxis” underscoring the ongoing focus on managing thromboembolic events, which remain significant perioperative risks. Despite advancements in anticoagulant therapies, such as enoxaparin and newer agents like apixaban and rivaroxaban, the risk of symptomatic pulmonary embolism persists. This suggests a need for personalized prophylactic measures based on genetic or biomarker screening.18) The third hotspot revolves around patient-related factors, with keywords like “body-mass index,” “morbid obesity,” and “cost” emphasize the role of patient demographics and health status in surgical outcomes. Patients with elevated body mass index face increased risks of complications like deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism following knee replacement.19) Additionally, while outpatient knee replacement surgery may reduce immediate hospital costs, it has been associated with higher rates of certain complications, potentially leading to increased post-discharge costs due to readmissions and follow-up treatments.20) These findings highlight the importance of considering patient characteristics in the planning and execution of knee replacement surgeries.

The keyword bursts identified in this bibliometric analysis illustrate the evolving focus of knee replacement research over time. From 2008 to 2016, the appearance of “enoxaparin” reflects an intensified focus on preventing thrombotic complications. Comparative studies, such as the apixaban dosed orally versus anticoagulation with enoxaparin-1 (ADVANCE-1) trial, demonstrated that newer anticoagulants like apixaban could achieve similar efficacy in preventing thromboembolism while reducing bleeding risks.21) Between 2009 and 2015, keywords such as “prosthesis” and “rehabilitation” rose in prominence, highlighting the increasing focus on enhancing recovery and managing prosthetic-related complications. Studies have shown that constrained and hinged prostheses carry a higher risk of infection compared to other types, while uncemented prostheses may have a lower risk of infection.22) Additionally, inadequate rehabilitation protocols or non-compliance can lead to knee stiffness, which is a common complication contributing to patient dissatisfaction.23)

In recent years, starting from 2020, keywords like “quality,” “enhanced recovery,” and “revision total knee arthroplasty” reflect a growing emphasis on optimizing patient care and recovery. Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) protocols have been shown to reduce hospital stays, improve functional recovery, and lower complication rates. For example, ERAS implementation in the United Kingdom resulted in significant improvements in patient-reported outcomes and reduced hospital costs.24) The focus on “revision total knee arthroplasty” indicates the increasing complexity of managing revision surgeries, which are often more challenging than primary knee replacements. The revision burden has remained stable over time, with complications influenced primarily by patient-related factors rather than procedural advancements. Patients undergoing conversion TKA, especially those with prior open reduction internal fixation, are at higher risk of revision due to periprosthetic infection and fractures.25)

The keyword “dislocation” emerged prominently from 2022 to 2024, underscoring its ongoing significance as a perioperative complication. While relatively rare compared to other complications, dislocation has a profound impact on patient outcomes, often resulting in functional impairment, dissatisfaction, and the need for complex revision surgeries. Recent studies have highlighted that anatomical and lifestyle differences, such as those observed in certain populations (e.g., Asian patients), may increase the likelihood of dislocation post-surgery.26) Additionally, dislocation risks can differ between unilateral and bilateral knee replacements, with bilateral procedures presenting unique biomechanical challenges.27) Advancements in prosthetic alignment, ligament balancing techniques, and personalized implants are critical areas of research aimed at reducing dislocation risks.28) These efforts align with the broader goal of improving long-term surgical outcomes and enhancing patient satisfaction in TKA.

This study has several limitations. First, the analysis relied exclusively on data from the WoSCC database, which may not capture all relevant publications on perioperative complications in knee replacement. Second, only English-language articles were included, potentially overlooking valuable insights from non-English research. Third, the bibliometric tools employed may not fully capture the qualitative nuances of individual studies, which could limit the depth of insight into specific research findings.

This bibliometric analysis systematically assessed the scope and evolution of research on perioperative complications in knee replacement. The study highlighted publication trends, collaborative networks, and keyword co-occurrences, revealing key research hotspots focused on themes such as enhanced recovery. Emerging frontiers in the field indicate an increasing focus on optimizing surgical outcomes and enhancing patient recovery protocols. These findings deepen our understanding of this evolving field and hold potential implications for advancing clinical approaches in knee replacement surgery.

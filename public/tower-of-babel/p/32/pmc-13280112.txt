Efficacy and Safety of Switching to Dolutegravir/Lamivudine in Virologically Suppressed HIV-1 Infected Adults with TDF-Related Renal or Bone Toxicity
Authors: Zhenyan Wang, Li Liu, Jun Chen, Jiangrong Wang, Yinzhong Shen, Tangkai Qi, Yang Tang, Wei Song, Jianjun Sun, Shuibao Xu, Junyang Yang, Youming Chen, Yueming Shao, Renfang Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionLong-term use of tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF) is associated with renal tubular dysfunction and bone mineral loss. In virologically suppressed people living with HIV (PLWH), optimizing antiretroviral therapy requires maintaining viral suppression while minimizing cumulative drug-related toxicity. We therefore evaluated the efficacy and safety of switching to dolutegravir/lamivudine (DTG/3TC) in Chinese PLWH with TDF-related renal or bone toxicity.MethodsThis prospective, single-center, open-label cohort study enrolled adults with HIV-1 RNA < 40 copies/ml for ≥ 6 months on TDF-based regimens and evidence of TDF-associated renal or bone toxicity. Participants were switched to DTG/3TC and followed for 48 weeks. The primary endpoint was virological failure at week 48 (FDA Snapshot algorithm). Secondary endpoints included changes in CD4 + T-cell counts, renal tubular biomarkers, bone mineral density (BMD), metabolic parameters, and safety outcomes.ResultsOne hundred participants were enrolled; 91 completed the study per protocol. Median age was 45 years and 90% were male. At week 48, virological suppression was maintained in 90% of the intention-to-treat–exposed population and 99% of the per-protocol population. CD4 + T-cell counts increased significantly at week 48 (+ 77.4 cells/μl; p < 0.001). Markers of proximal tubular dysfunction improved significantly, including reductions in urinary β2-microglobulin-to-creatinine ratio, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and urinary retinol-binding protein-to-creatinine ratio (all p < 0.001). BMD increased in the lumbar spine and hip (both p < 0.001). Modest weight gain (+ 2.17 kg) and small increases in total cholesterol and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol were observed at week 48.ConclusionsIn virologically suppressed Chinese PLWH with TDF-related renal or bone toxicity, switching to DTG/3TC maintained viral suppression and was associated with significant improvements in renal tubular and bone metabolic markers over 48 weeks. DTG/3TC represents a well-tolerated toxicity-driven switch strategy in routine clinical practice.Trial RegistrationClinicalTrial.gov ID: NCT05493969.

Introduction

The scale-up of antiretroviral therapy (ART) has transformed HIV infection into a chronic, manageable disease. In China, the National Free Antiretroviral Therapy Program (NFATP) has enabled broad access to ART with tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF)—based regimens, which are the most widely used first-line and maintenance therapies [1, 2]. As the treatment duration increases, long-term drug-related toxicities have become a major driver of regimen modification in virologically suppressed patients. TDF-associated renal and bone toxicity are among the most frequently encountered concerns.

TDF-related proximal renal tubular dysfunction often manifests as elevated urinary low-molecular-weight proteins before a decline in the estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) becomes apparent [3], while progressive reductions in bone mineral density and abnormalities in bone turnover markers are commonly observed during prolonged exposure [4–6]. These complications are particularly relevant in Chinese people living with HIV (PLWH), where long-term TDF use is common.

In this setting, ART optimization has shifted toward maintaining viral suppression while minimizing cumulative toxicity. Dual therapy with dolutegravir and lamivudine (DTG/3TC) offers a simplified regimen with a high genetic barrier to resistance, favorable tolerability, and minimal drug–drug interactions. Large randomized trials and real-world studies have demonstrated the non-inferiority of DTG/3TC compared to standard three- or four-drug regimens in virologically suppressed PLWH [7–9], leading to its recommendation in international and Chinese treatment guidelines [2, 10, 11].

Withdrawal of TDF represents a clinically meaningful strategy for patients with TDF-related renal or bone toxicity. Our previous studies have suggested improvements in bone metabolism and stabilization of renal parameters following discontinuation of TDF-containing regimens [12]. However, most available evidence is derived from randomized trials with strict inclusion criteria or retrospective cohorts with limited assessments of renal tubular and bone metabolic biomarkers. Prospective data focusing specifically on PLWH with documented TDF-associated renal or bone toxicity, particularly in the Chinese clinical context, remain limited. Therefore, we conducted this prospective cohort study of virologically suppressed Chinese PLWH with evidence of TDF-related renal or bone toxicity to evaluate the efficacy and safety of switching to DTG/3TC.

Methods

Study Design and Study Setting

This was a single-center, prospective, open-label, single-arm cohort study conducted at the Shanghai Public Health Clinical Center (SPHCC) in Shanghai, China. SPHCC is the designated center in Shanghai responsible for HIV diagnosis, ART, and long-term clinical follow-up. The center manages a large cohort of over 16,000 PLWH. All patients in this cohort undergo routine follow-up with systematic monitoring of virological, immunological, renal, bone, metabolic, and other relevant parameters.

This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of switching to a dual regimen of DTG/3TC in virologically suppressed PLWH who developed TDF-associated renal and/or bone toxicity. Participants were not enrolled through population-based random screening; instead, they were selectively identified from the well-characterized longitudinal cohort based on predefined criteria indicating a high likelihood of TDF-related renal or bone toxicity.

Participants

The study enrolled PLWH aged 18–75 years who maintained plasma HIV-1 RNA levels below 40 copies/ml for at least 6 months prior to screening while on a TDF plus 3TC/FTC-based regimen. The participants were required to show clinical or laboratory evidence of TDF-associated renal or bone toxicity. Renal impairment was defined by meeting at least one of the following criteria: a sustained decline in eGFR of ≥ 5 ml/min/1.73 m2 per year for ≥ 3 consecutive years, or a confirmed ≥ 25% reduction in eGFR from baseline; a urine β2-microglobulin-to-creatinine ratio (U-β2-MG/cr) ≥ 300 μg/g; a urine microalbumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) > 30 mg/g; non-diabetic glycosuria; or serum phosphorus level < 0.8 mmol/l after at least 8 h of fasting. Bone toxicity was defined as either a bone mineral density (BMD) T-score < − 1.0, a Z-score < − 2.0, or the occurrence of a fragility fracture after initiation of TDF after exclusion of other contributing factors.

Exclusion criteria included pregnancy, breastfeeding, or intention to become pregnant or breastfeed during the study period; active opportunistic infections or malignancies; significant hepatic impairment, defined as alanine aminotransferase (ALT) ≥ 5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN), ALT ≥ 3 × ULN with bilirubin ≥ 1.5 × ULN) or end-stage liver disease; coinfection with hepatitis B virus (HBV), defined as HBsAg positivity and/or detectable HBV DNA (excluding isolated anti-HBc positivity with undetectable HBV DNA); coinfection with hepatitis C virus; known hypersensitivity or resistance to DTG or 3TC; concomitant use of medications contraindicated with the study regimen; and an eGFR < 30 ml/min/1.73 m2.

Data Collection and Measurements

Data were prospectively collected according to a predefined schedule: screening, baseline (day 1), and follow-up at weeks 4, 8, 12, 24, 36, and 48.

Clinical Evaluations

At baseline and all follow-up visits, we documented medical history, concomitant medications, adverse events (AEs) or serious adverse events (SAEs), and body weight. Treatment adherence was assessed at each follow-up visit.

Virological and Immunological Assessments

Plasma HIV-1 RNA levels were quantified at screening, baseline, and weeks 12, 24, and 48 using reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) (COBAS® AmpliPrep/COBAS® TaqMan® HIV-1 Test, v2.0, ABI7500). CD4 + T-cell counts were measured at screening, baseline, and weeks 24 and 48 by flow cytometry using a BD FACS Canto II system with BD Multitest™ CD3/CD8/CD45/CD4 reagents (BD Biosciences).

Metabolic Parameters

Fasting lipid profiles (triglycerides, total cholesterol, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol [LDL-C], and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol [HDL-C]) and fasting blood glucose were measured at baseline and all follow-up visits.

Renal and Bone Metabolism Markers

Serum cystatin C, osteocalcin, 25-hydroxyvitamin D (25(OH)VitD), retinol-binding protein (RBP) and urinary markers (urinalysis, UACR, U-β2-MG/Cr, and N-acetyl-β-d-glucosaminidase [NAG]) were measured at baseline and all follow-up visits. Cystatin C-based estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFRcysc) was calculated using the 2012 Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration (CKD-EPI) equation.

BMD

Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) scans (Lunar Prodigy; GE Healthcare, Chicago, IL, USA) were performed at screening and at weeks 24 and 48 to assess BMD at the lumbar spine and hip, following standardized procedures in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions.

Study Endpoints

The primary endpoint was the proportion of participants with virologic failure at week 48 as defined by the FDA Snapshot algorithm. Virological failure was defined as: (1) HIV-RNA ≥ 40 copies/ml at week 48; (2) discontinuation of treatment due to lack of efficacy; and (3) discontinuation of treatment due to reasons other than AEs and last available HIV-RNA ≥ 40 copies/ml.

The secondary endpoints included changes in CD4 + T-cell counts, renal and bone biomarkers, and the incidence of AEs and SAEs. Renal biomarkers included eGFR, serum creatinine, serum cystatin C, UACR, U-β2-MG/cr, urine NAG-to-creatinine ratio (UNAG/cr), and urine RBP-to-creatinine ratio (URBP/cr). Bone biomarkers included BMD and serum alkaline phosphatase (ALP), osteocalcin, and 25(OH)VitD levels.

Data Analysis

All participants receiving ≥ 1 dose of DTG/3TC were included in the intention-to-treat–exposed (ITT-E) population, whereas the per-protocol (PP) population comprised those who completed the study without major protocol deviations. Data distribution was assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Normally distributed variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), non-normal variables are presented as median (interquartile range, IQR), and categorical variables are shown as number (%). Changes in CD4 + T cell counts and body weight at weeks 24 and 48 were analyzed using repeated-measures ANOVA, whereas renal, bone, and lipid parameters were assessed using the paired nonparametric Wilcoxon signed-rank test. The adverse events were summarized descriptively. A two-sided p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using SPSS version 22.0.

Ethical Statement

All participants provided informed written consent before being enrolled in the study. All data were de-identified and analyzed anonymously. This study was registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05493969). This study was performed in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration of 1964 and its later amendments and received ethical approval from the Ethics Committee of SPHCC (approval number: 2021-S023-04).

Weight and Lipid Profile

Body weight increased progressively from baseline, with a mean gain of 1.47 kg at week 24 (95% CI 1.04–1.90) and 2.17 kg at week 48 (95% CI 1.52–2.81; both p < 0.001). From baseline to week 48, total cholesterol and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) increased slightly (+ 0.23 and + 0.26 mmol/l, respectively; both p < 0.001). AEs and SAEs

AEs were reported in 36% of the participants in the ITT-E population, with hepatic dysfunction (eight cases), hyperuricemia (six cases), and COVID-19 (four cases) being the most common. Other reported AEs included rash (three cases), upper respiratory tract infection (three cases), and insomnia (two cases).

Six SAEs occurred but were deemed unrelated to the study treatment, including (1) multiple myeloma; (2) enlarged prostate accompanied by a urinary tract infection; (3) a malignant tumor in the left breast; (4) herpes zoster of the external ear with associated peripheral facial nerve palsy; (5) a fracture of the distal right radius with a concurrent contusion of the skin and soft tissue; (6) and a complex anal fistula. Only one participant discontinued treatment because of insomnia, which was considered to be drug-related.

Discussion

In this prospective cohort of virologically suppressed Chinese PLWH with documented TDF-associated renal or bone toxicity, switching to DTG/3TC maintained virological suppression and immunological stability, while being associated with significant improvements in renal tubular and bone metabolic biomarkers. These findings address a common clinical scenario in routine HIV care in China, where long-term TDF exposure is widespread, and regimen modification is increasingly driven by cumulative toxicity rather than virological failure. Our results are consistent with existing evidence supporting DTG/3TC as an effective maintenance strategy and extend these findings to patients with established TDF-related renal or bone toxicity, further supporting its use in clinically vulnerable populations.

Boosted protease inhibitors are known to increase TDF nephrotoxicity risk [13]. In our cohort, however, only 13% of participants received ritonavir-boosted lopinavir, while the majority (86%) were on NNRTI-based regimens, mainly efavirenz. Despite this, they still developed renal/bone toxicity, likely due to longer cumulative TDF exposure (median 77 months), and potential host susceptibility in Chinese individuals. Thus, our findings extend the evidence to unboosted TDF regimens in a real-world Chinese setting. Due to the limited number of patients in the LPV/r group, we were unable to separately assess whether this subgroup exhibited the greatest changes in renal and bone markers.

Renal outcomes showed a transient increase in serum creatinine and cystatin C levels after switching, which stabilized by week 48 and is consistent with the known effect of dolutegravir on tubular creatinine secretion rather than true nephrotoxicity [14]. In contrast, the markers of proximal tubular dysfunction, including U-β2-MG/cr, UACR, and URBP/cr, improved significantly, indicating recovery from TDF-associated tubular injury. Given that TDF-related nephrotoxicity typically manifests as proximal tubular dysfunction rather than reduced glomerular filtration, these changes are clinically relevant and align with previous studies demonstrating renal recovery following TDF discontinuation [15, 16].

Bone health also improved after switching, as reflected by significant increases in BMD at both the lumbar spine and hip, along with reductions in bone turnover markers, such as ALP and osteocalcin. These results mirror prior study findings suggesting that DTG/3TC may contribute to skeletal recovery once TDF-related bone loss is halted [17]. In addition, we observed a modest decrease in vitamin D levels, which may reflect normalization of TDF-related disturbances in mineral metabolism following recovery of renal tubular function and reduction in secondary hyperparathyroidism.

Modest increases in body weight were observed following the switch to DTG/3TC, consistent with observations reported with integrase strand transfer inhibitor-based regimens [18–20]. However, it has also been suggested that such weight gain following a switch from TDF-containing regimens to TDF-free regimens may be attributable to the discontinuation of TDF [21]. We acknowledge that longitudinal reference data on average weight change in our clinic population are not systematically collected, which limits the interpretation of these findings. Additionally, we did not measure waist circumference or calculate body mass index (BMI), so we cannot determine whether the weight gain was centrally distributed. This is another limitation of our study.

Small increases in total cholesterol and LDL-C levels were noted, whereas HDL-C and triglyceride levels remained stable [20]. These findings are consistent with previous observations from our center showing that TDF-based regimens are associated with a more favorable lipid profile compared with non-TDF regimens, likely reflecting the lipid-lowering effect of TDF [22]. Therefore, the modest lipid increases observed after switching may primarily reflect the withdrawal of this effect rather than an adverse metabolic consequence of DTG/3TC [23]. The clinical significance of these metabolic changes warrants further evaluation with longer follow-up and controlled studies.

DTG/3TC was well tolerated overall, with most adverse events being mild or unrelated to the treatment. Only one participant discontinued treatment because of insomnia. The overall safety profile reinforces the utility of DTG/3TC in patients at risk of drug-related toxicity.

This study has several limitations. First, its single-center, open-label design and the lack of a control group limit the generalizability and causal inference of the findings. Second, the cohort was predominantly male (90%), which restricts the applicability of our results to female patients. Third, the follow-up duration was insufficient to determine whether the observed improvements in surrogate biomarkers would ultimately translate into reduced risks of major clinical events (e.g., end-stage renal disease or fragility fractures). Longer-term studies are needed to establish such correlations.

Conclusions

In summary, switching to DTG/3TC maintained virological suppression and was associated with improvements in renal tubular and bone metabolic markers in virologically suppressed Chinese PLWH with TDF-associated toxicity, supporting the use of DTG/3TC as a safe and effective switch strategy in routine HIV care.

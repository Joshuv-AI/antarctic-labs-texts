Sex Differences in Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation Outcomes in Patients on Oral Anticoagulants: A Popular PAUSE TAVI SubAnalysis
Authors: Kees H van Bergeijk, Daniël C Overduin, Constantijn S Venema, Dirk Jan van Ginkel, Hindrik W van der Werf, Ad F M van den Heuvel, Adriaan A Voors, Joanna J Wykrzykowska, Jurrien M Ten Berg
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMany patients undergoing transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) have an indication for oral anticoagulation (OAC). Our aim was to study sex differences in outcomes after TAVI, depending on the type and regimen of vitamin K antagonist versus direct OAC.MethodsThe Popular PAUSE TAVI (Periprocedural Continuation Versus Interruption of Oral Anticoagulant Drugs During Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation) was a randomized clinical trial to compare outcomes between patients who continued or interrupted OAC during TAVI. The outcomes of interest in this subanalysis were a combination of cardiovascular mortality, stroke, myocardial infarction, major vascular complications or major bleeding at 30 days, stratified by OAC regimen and type, and sex.ResultsAfter 30 days of follow‐up, 44/296 (14.9%) women reached the combined end point, compared with 90/562 men (16.0%), P=0.732. There were no statistically significant differences between sexes in outcomes, irrespective of the continued or interrupted OAC regimen. Women on vitamin K antagonist had a numerically higher combined event rate (21.7%) compared with women using direct OAC (13.1%), although not statistically significant (P=0.146), similarly for major vascular complications (15% versus 7.2% in women on vitamin K antagonist and direct OAC respectively [P=0.255]). The interaction between sex and type of OAC for the combined end point lost significance after correcting for OAC indication and randomization, among others (unadjusted: P=0.037, adjusted=0.090).ConclusionsAfter a 30‐day follow‐up of patients undergoing TAVI on OAC, there were no statistically significant differences in outcomes between sexes, independent of interrupted or continued management. Although women treated with vitamin K antagonist had numerically more events than those with direct OAC and an interaction between sex and type of OAC was present, this interaction lost significance after adjustment for other factors.Registration https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04437303; Unique Identifier: NCT044373030.Keywords: bleeding, ischemic outcomes, oral anticoagulants, sex differences, TAVISubject Categories: Anticoagulants, Aortic Valve Replacement/Transcather Aortic Valve Implantation

Stroke and bleeding are known complications after transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) and are associated with increased mortality and impairment in quality of life. 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 Many of these patients require oral anticoagulants (OACs) before TAVI, primarily because of a history of atrial fibrillation (AF). 5 Current practice is to discontinue the use of OAC before TAVI, a recommendation supported by the recently published Popular Pause TAVI (Periprocedural Continuation Versus Interruption of Oral Anticoagulant Drugs During Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation) trial, which found that continuation of anticoagulation did not demonstrate noninferiority compared with interruption. 6 Although with conflicting results, sex differences have emerged as an important factor influencing the risk of bleeding and stroke after TAVI. 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 Women have smaller peripheral vessel diameters and a lower body surface area, contributing to a higher risk of bleeding. 11 Subgroup analysis of the Popular TAVI Trial showed that bleeding and ischemic outcomes were similar in men and women, but women had more major or life‐threatening bleeding, particularly when receiving aspirin. 12 Similarly, our analysis in WIN‐TAVI (Women’s International Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation) showed that OAC use predicts adverse outcomes after TAVI. 13 , 14

Although direct OACs (DOAC) are generally favored over vitamin K antagonists (VKA) in AF trials due to better safety and efficacy, there is still a minority of patients who remain on VKA. 15 These are often high‐risk patients with comorbidities. Data regarding the type of OAC and post‐TAVI outcomes are conflicting; the ATLANTIS (Anti‐Thrombotic Strategy After Trans‐Aortic Valve Implantation for Aortic Stenosis) and ENVISAGE‐TAVI AF (Edoxaban Compared to Standard Care After Heart Valve Replacement Using a Catheter in Patients With Atrial Fibrillation) trials found similar mortality rates for DOAC and VKA, but edoxaban was linked to more major bleeding. 16 , 17 In contrast, the OCEAN‐TAVI (Optimized Transcatheter Valvular Intervention) registry showed an association between DOAC and lower mortality rates, without an increase in bleeding. 18 Evidence suggested that the pharmacological response to OAC differs between sexes; women experience more gastrointestinal‐related bleeding and are less frequently within the therapeutic range when using VKAs. 19 , 20 , 21 A sex‐based approach to anticoagulation management in patients undergoing TAVI might mitigate adverse events. The data to support such approach, however, are currently lacking. Therefore, in this subanalysis of the Popular Pause TAVI trial, we studied sex‐specific differences in outcomes in patients with an indication for OAC during TAVI, stratified by continued and interrupted OAC regimen, as well as by VKA or DOAC use.

Methods

Study Design

The Popular PAUSE TAVI trial was an investigator‐initiated, multicenter, randomized, open‐label trial to evaluate the safety and efficacy of continuation versus interruption of OAC during TAVI. The trail was performed at 22 European sites, including patients from 2020 to 2023. The design of the study has been described previously. 6 In brief, patients (≥18 years old) were eligible if they had an indication for TAVI and long‐term OAC, without contraindications to continue or temporary interrupt OAC as per local standards. The study randomized patients between continuation and interruption of OAC, in a 1:1 ratio. Patients randomized to interruption had to stop taking OAC at least 48 hours before the TAVI procedure, and this was restarted according to local protocols as soon as determined safe by the treating clinician. Bridging with heparin was not recommended. Patients randomized to continuation of OAC continued to take the medication, including the day of the TAVI procedure. The TAVI procedure was performed according to the local protocol of each site, including the choice of valve type, the use of cerebral embolic protection device, the amount of heparin and protamine and the type of vascular closure device.

Data Collection and Management

Follow‐up data were collected at discharge and 30 days after the TAVI procedure, from the patient, other primary care physicians, or electronic patient file if needed. The study received approval from national authorities, ethics committees in each participating country, and institutional review boards at all study sites. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients. The study was registered under NCT04437303. The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

End Points

In this post hoc analysis, the primary outcome of interest was a composite of cardiovascular mortality, stroke, myocardial infarction, major vascular complications, or major bleeding (Valve Academic Research Consortium‐3, type 2, 3 and 4), as defined by the Valve Academic Research Consortiu‐3 criteria. 22 Secondary end points consisted of the individual components of the primary outcome.

Statistical Analysis

For descriptive analyses, Student t tests for continuous variables were conducted and shown as the mean±SD when normally distributed, otherwise we used Mann–Whitney tests and presented as medians (interquartile range). To test the variables for normality Shapiro–Wilk tests were used. Chi‐square analyses or Fisher’s exact tests was conducted for categorical variables and data reported as frequencies (percentages). To investigate the interaction between type of OAC (VKA and DOAC) and interruption or continuation in both sexes, we included an interaction term in a multiple logistic regression model and assessed its significance using a likelihood ratio test. We adjusted the interaction analysis for potential confounders, including indication for OAC, randomization, body mass index, body surface area, small femoral artery size, calcified femoral artery, and tortuosity. A P value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistics were performed using R‐software, version 4.1.

Results

From a total of 858 patients, 296 (34.5%) were women, and 562 men (65.5%). Baseline characteristics of men and women are shown in Table 1. Women were older (81.6±5.9 versus 80.1±5.9 years, P=0.001), had a higher body mass index (27.9±5.5 versus 27.1±4.2 kg/m2; P=0.030) but a lower body surface area (1.83±0.20 versus 2.02±0.20 m2, P<0.001), less often had medical history of coronary artery disease (31.8% versus 56.8%, P<0.001), myocardial infarction (8.45% versus 19.8%, P<0.001), and peripheral artery disease (13.2% versus 22.2%, P=0.002) (Table 1). In addition, women less often had a medical history of AF (92.9% versus 97.0%, P=0.010) and had a higher CHA2DS2‐VASc score (5.0±1.3 versus 4.1±1.3, P<0.001). The indication for OAC less often was AF in women compared with men (92.2% versus 96.3%, P=0.022). There was no statistically significant difference regarding type of OAC used within sexes. Women had a preprocedural international normalized ratio comparable to men (1.7 [0.6] versus 1.8 [0.6], P=0.442). There was also no statistically significant difference between sexes for the time in therapeutic range, measured by a labile international normalized ratio in <60% of the time (2 [3.5%] versus 8 [9.0%], P=0.316). Difference in baseline characteristics between sexes and type of OAC can be found in Table S1. 158 (53.4%) women and 273 (48.6%) men were randomized to the continued regimen (P=0.206). Women more often had a small common femoral artery (<6 mm) (40 [13.6%] versus 46 [8.21%], P=0.01) and they were more likely to have a severely calcified femoral artery (43 [14.5%] versus 140 [24.9%], P=0.001).

Discussion

This post hoc analysis of the Popular Pause TAVI trial showed that in patients with an indication of OAC that undergo TAVI:

At 30 days post TAVI, the incidence of adverse events, including cardiovascular mortality, stroke, myocardial infarction, major vascular complications, and major bleedings, did not differ significantly between sexes.

At 30 days post TAVI, the incidence of adverse events, including cardiovascular mortality, stroke, myocardial infarction, major vascular complications, and major bleedings, did not differ significantly between sexes.

This finding of no statistically significant differences in outcomes between men and women was also observed in the continued and the interrupted OAC regimen groups.

There was a numeric, but no statistically significant difference between outcomes regarding sex and type of OAC. Accordingly, the interaction between sex and type of OAC for the combined end point and major vascular complications was not statistically significant after correction for confounders.

There was a numeric, but no statistically significant difference between outcomes regarding sex and type of OAC. Accordingly, the interaction between sex and type of OAC for the combined end point and major vascular complications was not statistically significant after correction for confounders.

Overall, the incidence of bleeding complications was higher than ischemic events, with no difference observed within sexes across all outcomes, in line with previous reports. 12 , 23 In cohort B of the Popular TAVI trial, which included patients with OAC, the same absence of significant differences in outcomes after TAVI were observed between men and women. 12 General AF trials show similar results, in which women also tend to be older and have higher thromboembolic risk estimates (calculated by the CHA2DS2−VASc‐score). 24 , 25 Although previously mentioned studies showed a higher thromboembolic event rate in women, this was not seen in the current analysis. This could be partly explained by the undertreatment and underrepresentation of women in prior clinical trials. 26 No statistically significant difference in thromboembolic events between sexes in our study could also be attributed to the low event rates and consequent underpowering.

Within the different regimens of interruption or continuation of OAC use peri‐TAVI there were no statistically significant differences found in outcomes between men and women. This is in line with the main findings of the Popular Pause TAVI trial. 6

When looking at the type of OAC, there were no statistically significant differences in outcomes between men and women. However, there was a numerically higher event rate in women on VKA compared with DOAC. The opposite trend was seen in men. In the adjusted analysis, the interaction term between sex and type of OAC for the combined end point was not significant. In the current study, the numerically higher combined end point rate was mainly driven by the higher vascular complication and major bleeding rate in women on VKA. It can be questioned if these differences are a result of the OAC regimen or if they should be explained by the smaller peripheral vessel size in women on VKAs, in combination with the usually lower body surface area of women. 11

Whereas observational studies suggest that women may have higher gastrointestinal bleeding with DOAC use, in the current study and other TAVI trials, most bleeding occurred at the access site. 12 , 21 , 27 In addition, most bleedings occurred periprocedural, whereas gastrointestinal bleedings can arise in a later stage, possibly outside of the window of follow‐up in this trial. This finding aligns with the results of another registry, which demonstrated no difference in outcomes between DOAC and VKA after 1‐year follow‐up, whereas a difference was observed after longer follow‐up time. 28 Previous data on sex and type of OAC revealed conflicting and context‐dependent results. For example, a meta‐analysis showed that DOAC was associated with a lower stroke risk compared with warfarin, irrespective of sex, whereas other studies showed higher stroke rates in women on VKAs. 29 The subanalysis of the ENVISAGE trial, randomizing patients between edoxaban and VKA, showed similar event rates between DOAC and VKA but a reduced risk in women with DOACs. 23 The ENVISAGE trial included only patients with AF, had other definitions for outcomes, and did not include vascular complications in the combined end point. In the current study the vascular complications showed a similar trend toward higher event rate in women on VKA, and men on DOAC, possibly explaining the trend for the combined outcome. Another explanation that has been proposed, that women may have a lower time in therapeutic range when using VKA, was not found in the current study. The latter may be explained by the small sample size of women on VKA and the limited number of available international normalized ratio measurements. 20 These findings highlight the complex interplay between sex, type of OAC, comorbidities, and anatomical considerations. Future studies with larger sample sizes are needed to examine the differences in outcomes between VKAs and DOACs, as well as the effect of sex. In addition, these studies should include multiple outcomes and incorporate a longer follow‐up, with a specific focus on equal sex distribution, within both types of OAC. Additionally, a thorough examination of the reasons for using VKA should be incorporated.

Implications

The key findings of this study are the absence of statistically significant differences in outcome of continued and interrupted OAC management peri‐TAVI between sexes and the absence of a statistically significant interaction between sex and VKA regimen for adverse events (after correction for confounders) in patients with an indication for OAC undergoing TAVI.

Previous data have shown differences in response to OAC between sexes. However, data regarding these outcomes in a contemporary TAVI cohort are lacking and the effect of discontinued and continued periprocedural regimen is unknown. Therefore, in this study, we examined the role of sex in not only discontinued and continued periprocedural regimen but also in VKA or DOAC use.

In subanalyses like the current one, which are not prespecified, interaction terms are often not statistically significant due to small sample sizes and increased variability, especially after adjustment for multiple factors. 30 , 31 It should be noted that these confounding factors, such as anatomical differences and lower body mass index, are intrinsically linked to sex and may lead to underestimation of the association of sex and risk factors. Although this study remains explorative, it nevertheless provides hypothesis‐generating results and support for future study of a sex‐specific approach in OAC management in patients undergoing TAVI. Women may still potentially benefit more from a DOAC regimen compared with VKA. This needs to be confirmed in dedicated randomized controlled trials, specifically designed to assess these subgroups and end points. In the meantime, other subanalyses may shed a light on this issue, such as the RHEIA trial (Randomized Research in Women All Comers With Aortic Stenosis; NCT04614986), which randomized only women to surgical aortic valve replacement and TAVI. 32

In general, the rationale for the use of OAC is lowering thromboembolic risk while minimizing bleeding rate. Stroke rates after TAVI are relatively low and have declined over time, and although still higher, bleeding complications have also declined over the past decade. 33 Although the latter can be attributed to increased experience in TAVI procedures and improvement in sheath size and closure device use, the preferential use of DOACs instead of VKAs as therapy for AF could also be of substantial impact. 34 A sex‐specific regimen for anticoagulation therapies in patients undergoing TAVI might therefore need further exploration.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, this study was a post hoc analysis and the study end points nor the comparisons were prespecified. Due to the exploratory nature of the study and the multiple significance tests performed, adjustments for multiple comparisons were not made. In addition, we do not have the specific cause for choosing VKA over DOAC in this cohort. Lastly, the analysis may be underpowered, as subgroup sample sizes resulted in low incidences of the outcomes.

Conclusions

After a 30‐day follow‐up of patients undergoing TAVI with an indication for OAC, outcomes were not statistically different between sexes, irrespective of continued or interrupted management. There were numerically more bleeding and vascular events in women treated with VKA. However, a trend toward interaction between sex and type of OAC, independent of continuation or interrupted management, lost significance after correction for confounders. There is a clear need for future studies on VKA versus DOAC in patients undergoing TAVI, specifically for female patients.

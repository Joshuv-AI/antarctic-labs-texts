Introduction

Ectopic pregnancy is the term applied to describe the implantation of a fertilized ovum outside of the normal uterine cavity, most commonly in the fallopian tube [1]. Almost 2 % of pregnancies are affected [2]. Despite a sharp decline in mortality from 72–90 % in industrialized countries in 1880 to 0.14 % in 1990, this illness is still linked with severe morbidity [3]. There are around 1 in 125,000 spontaneous pregnancies that result in unilateral tubal twins, making it a unique diagnosis [4]. While most patients with ectopic pregnancy present with acute lower abdominal pain, low blood pressure, vaginal bleeding [5], some patients could have an unusual presentation with left upper abdominal pain, chest pain and shortness of breath [6]. Transvaginal ultrasonography and serial serum beta-human chorionic gonadotropin (bHCG) tests are used to diagnose ectopic pregnancy [7]. Its management continues to be challenging for experts. The best course of treatment depends on the condition of the patient and its location and may involve a variety of surgical methods as well as methotrexate-based pharmaceutical therapy, with surgery serving as the mainstay of medical treatment. Early detection and action are crucial to preventing potentially fatal consequences including the development of a burst fallopian tube [8]. Herein, we describe a case of a unilateral ectopic twins pregnancy that was managed successfully with surgery. This case has been reported in line with the SCARE criteria [9].

Case presentation

A 39-year-old female (Gravida 6, para 6) presented to the emergency department with continuous abdominal pain localized in the hypogastric region and with vaginal bleeding for 4 hours, unresponsive to analgesics. Obstetric history included one cesarean section. Medical history included subtotal thyroidectomy, with levothyroxine treatment. The patient is a non-smoker and there is no significant family history. Clinical examination has shown severe abdominal pain with a rigid abdomen and vaginal bleeding. Vital signs were important for shock signs with rapid pulse and low blood pressure. Laboratory values included a White Blood Cell (WBC) count of 25,700 per microliter, Granulocytes 92.9 %, and Lymphocytes 5.3 %. Transvaginal ultrasound (TVUS) revealed a decidual reaction, it also showed a free fluid containing clots in the Douglas pouch, a small amount of free fluid in the Morrison pouch, and a mass measuring 3*3 cm on the left side. Differential diagnoses included renal colic, acute pelvic inflammatory disease, ovarian torsion, implantation bleeding, missed abortion, hydatidiform mole, and ruptured ectopic pregnancy. bHCG test was highly positive(63000), and gestational age was 6 weeks depending on the last menstrual cycle. The elevated level of bHCG, radiological findings on TVUS, shock signs, and vaginal bleeding suggested that the pregnant sac was on the verge of bursting., so an emergency exploratory laparotomy proceeded. A surgical incision was done using “Winschter Technique” and the abdominal layers were dissected until the uterus and appendages, ectopic pregnancy was found in the left fallopian tube, so this tube was completely resected along with the gestational sac, and abdominal closure was made after placing a drain. Total blood loss was 200 ml, blood transfusion wasn't required during surgery. The resected part showed a duplicated gestational sac (Fig. 1). Pathological examination demonstrated numerous small chorionic villas, some are fibrosis and others are necrotic, with massive intratubal hemorrhage(hematosalpinx), and clear cell hyperplasia of the fallopian tube epithelium (Fig. 2). Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, only 98 cases of unilateral tubal twin pregnancy have been documented in the literature between De Ott's report in 1891 and 1990, the majority of which were identified intraoperatively or postoperatively [10]. In the present case, the surgical team established the ectopic pregnancy (EP) following laparotomy. Extrauterine pregnancy is a first-trimester pregnancy problem that occurs in 1.3-2.4 % of pregnancies and can present in a variety of ways, such as being asymptomatic, having pelvic discomfort that is greater on one side, or even rupturing the tubal sac and causing hemorrhagic shock [11].

In our case, the patient presented at a gestational age of 6 weeks with complaints of continuous abdominal pain for 4 hours unresponsive to analgesics, vaginal bleeding, and tenderness in the mass of the appendages. All laboratory analyses were within normal limits except the WBC count, which may have increased as a result of the bleeding or a concomitant inflammatory condition. Multiparity, a prior EP episode, the use of an intrauterine device (IUD) prior to conception, abdominal operations, and the use of assisted reproductive technologies are risk factors [2]. As in our patient who had 5 vaginal deliveries and one cesarian.

The symptom trifecta of secondary amenorrhea, painful pelvic discomfort, and light vaginal spotting in the first trimester may point to extrauterine pregnancy, but it can also result from an intact intrauterine pregnancy or an early miscarriage. Additional indications of hemorrhagic shock or hemodynamic instability include syncope, abdominal pain extending to the shoulder(s), abdominal guarding or an acute abdomen, discomfort on the displacement of the vaginal section of the cervix, and dyspnea, hypotension, and tachycardia. On the afflicted side, the adnexa is frequently swollen and sensitive [11].

By using transvaginal ultrasound and urine/serum beta-human chorionic gonadotropin tests, early ectopic pregnancy identification has improved, reducing related morbidity and death. 95 % of ectopic pregnancies occur in the tubal uterus. The even more uncommon occurrence of tubal twin ectopic pregnancy falls within this frequency; its prevalence is thought to be 1 in 725–1580 tubal pregnancies. One in every 125,000 spontaneous pregnancies results in a pair of unilateral tubal twins. An ectopic pregnancy diagnosis raises the likelihood of another one by 10 % [10].

Due to the rarity of the condition, there hasn't been any information on a specific risk factor for ectopic molar pregnancy. However, there are many potential risk factors for abnormal pregnancies, such as pelvic inflammatory disease, intrauterine contraceptives, tubal surgery, and a previous ectopic pregnancy [5].

EP can be managed in different ways depending on where it is located and how the patient is doing. There is no one effective treatment for EP; methotrexate administration is the mainstay of the pharmaceutical management of ectopic pregnancy, Aromatase Inhibitors—Letrozole, Gefitinib, Absolute Ethanol, and Potassium Chloride [2].

On the other hand, there is surgical management which has no agreement on the appropriate treatment strategy due to the rarity of spontaneous unilateral tubal twin pregnancies. Laparotomy and laparoscopic salpingectomy have been effectively used in situations comparable to ours [10].

In our instance, a gestational sac was detected during a vaginal ultrasound. A sample of the tube underwent pathological autopsy, which revealed that there were numerous small chorionic villus; some are fibrosis others are necrotic. Massive intratubal hemorrhage (Hematosalpinx).Clear cell hyperplasia of the fallopian tube epithelium when examined under a microscope. In addition, the level of bHCG (63,000) in our patient, who had no evidence of uterine pregnancy, suggested a twin ectopic pregnancy due to the high value.

A surgical abdominal incision was necessary since it was found that the pregnant sac was on the verge of bursting, there was blood in the abdominal cavity, and there was vascular prolapse. The left fallopian tube was damaged because of the pregnancy, which led to the extirpating of the tube. It was also found that the cyst was double, suggesting that the pregnancy was twin.

Furthermore, Salpingostomy and salpingectomy did not significantly affect intrauterine conceptions (53 % vs. 49.3 %), according to Yao and Tulandi [12]. It was well acknowledged that the cumulative intrauterine pregnancy rate following salpingostomy was much greater than that following salpingectomy [7].

In our case, the abdominal layers were dissected using the “Winschter Technique” through a Pfannenstiel incision, after opening the abdomen and accessing the uterus and appendages, the fallopian tube was completely removed along with the associated pregnancy sac. After that, ensuring the cessation of bleeding and proper closure of the abdomen is done. A drain was placed into Dougal's pouch. There are no alternative treatment options since it was found that the pregnant sac was on the verge of bursting. Following surgery, the patient was kept under observation for a day, and postoperative follow-up was only by histopathology, there is no need for other procedures after surgical removal. The patient received analgesics and antibiotics before being discharged.

Conclusion

Most cases of ectopic pregnancy are single pregnancies, but in rare cases, it can be a twin pregnancy leading to a very high elevation in the bHCG hormone. Therefore, the physicians should consider twin ectopic pregnancy as a primary differential diagnosis when dealing with very high laboratory values for the bHCG hormone in their clinical work.

Efficacy and safety of continuation vs. interruption of anticoagulation during transcatheter aortic valve implantation: A systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Umar G Adamu, Marheb Badianyama, Anupa Patel, Arthur Mutyaba, Nqoba Tsabedze
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe appropriate periprocedural management of oral anticoagulation in transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) patients who require long-term anticoagulation remains controversial.PurposeThis systematic review and meta-analysis compare the efficacy and safety of periprocedural continuation versus interruption of anticoagulation in patients undergoing TAVI with an indication for long-term anticoagulation.MethodsWe searched PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane Central databases to identify relevant articles. We included both observational and randomized controlled trials. Data were analysed using random-effects model to calculate the odds ratio (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). A p < .05 was considered statistically significant.ResultsFour studies comprising 3,144 patients, with anticoagulation continued in 1,500 (47.7%) patients were included. There were no difference between periprocedural continuation and interruption of anticoagulation in the incidence of composite outcomes (OR: 0.84; 95% CI: 0.64–1.10; p = .19), major bleeding (OR: 0.98; 95% CI: 0.57–1.68; p = .95), major vascular complications (OR: 0.93; 95% CI: 0.72–1.20; p = .60), and myocardial infarction (MI) (OR: 0.60; 95% CI: 0.20–1.77; p = .36). However, the incidence of stroke was significantly lower in the continuation group (OR: 0.61; 95% CI: 0.39–0.94; p = .03). The incidence of blood transfusion was not significantly different between the continuation and interruption groups (OR: 0.86; 95% CI: 0.42–1.77; p = .68).ConclusionsIn this meta-analysis comparing periprocedural continuation of anticoagulation to interruption of therapy in patients undergoing TAVI with concomitant indications for long-term anticoagulation, continuation of anticoagulation was associated with a lower incidence of stroke without increasing the risk of bleeding. These findings suggest a potentially favourable safety profile in favour of continuation of anticoagulant therapy, they should be interpreted with caution and validated in larger and well-designed randomised trials.The review protocol was prospectively registered with the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO), registration number (CRD42024607149).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-025-05133-5.Keywords: Transcatheter aortic valve implantation, Periprocedural, Anticoagulation, Continuation, Interruption, Meta-analysis

Introduction

Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) has become a transformative treatment for patients with severe symptomatic aortic stenosis [1]. A significant proportion of TAVI candidates have multiple comorbidities, with up to one-third requiring long-term oral anticoagulation [2, 3]. However, TAVI is associated with procedural risks, including cerebral embolism, which can result from dislodged aortic debris, thrombus formation, or calcific material mobilized during valve deployment, potentially leading to silent or clinically apparent strokes. Vascular bleeding is another major concern, often arising from large-bore arterial access, vessel fragility, or anticoagulation-related bleeding [3, 4]. These complications are especially relevant in patients receiving long-term anticoagulation, where striking a balance between bleeding and thromboembolic risks is critical.

The optimal approach to antithrombotic therapy in TAVI patients with indications for long-term anticoagulation remains uncertain, with varying recommendations across societal guidelines [5, 6]. While several observational studies support the strategy of uninterrupted anticoagulation to reduce thromboembolic events [7–9], the POPular PAUSE TAVI, a randomized controlled trial (RCT) found that temporary interruption was safe and non-inferior to continuation [10]. A recent meta-analysis similarly reported an equipoise in bleeding and thromboembolic events between continuation and interruption strategies [11]. Periprocedural continuation of oral anticoagulant therapy may help reduce the risk of thromboembolic events, particularly in patients with a high baseline risk of stroke, by maintaining therapeutic levels during the vulnerable period perioperative period. However, this potential benefit must be balanced against an increased risk of bleeding, especially in the context of procedural vascular injury or the concomitant use of antithrombotic agents. These competing risks underscore the existing clinical uncertainty and highlight the need for systematic evaluation of the available evidence.

To overcome these limitations, a systematic review and meta-analysis of observational studies and RCTs was undertaken to enhance statistical power and provide a more robust assessment of the efficacy and safety of periprocedural anticoagulation strategies in patients undergoing TAVI. Specifically, we aimed to compare continuation versus interruption of long-term anticoagulation in patients undergoing TAVI.

Methods

Eligibility criteria

This systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted in accordance with the Cochrane Collaboration Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions and the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analysis (PRISMA) statement [12]. We systematically searched PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane from inception to December 2024 using the following search terms: “transcatheter aortic valve replacement,” “transcatheter aortic,” “percutaneous aortic,” and “direct oral anticoagulants.’ We used backward snowballing to search the references of included studies. The full search strategy is provided in Supplementary Table 1. Two authors (U.G.A. and M.B.) independently, searched, and extracted data from each study using a standardized form. We included studies that met the following eligibility criteria: (1) RCTs or non-randomized trials; (2) studies comparing periprocedural continuation vs interruption of anticoagulation therapy; (3) patients with indications for long-term anticoagulation; (4) patients undergoing TAVI; and (5) studies reporting at least one of the clinical outcomes of interest. We excluded studies with (1) no control group and (2) periprocedural continuation of anticoagulation therapy in both the groups. There were no restrictions concerning the date or language of publication. The following data were extracted: (1) author; (2) journal name; (3) year of publication; (4) sample size; (5) baseline patient characteristics; and (6) follow-up period.

The endpoints of interest were: (1) stroke from any cause; (2) cardiovascular death; (3) major vascular complications; (4) major bleeding; (5) all-cause mortality; (6) transient ischaemic attack (TIA); and (7) composite outcome according to the included studies. Additionally, the use of embolic protection devices, blood transfusion, myocardial infarction (MI), and failure of closure devices were assessed. The definitions of composite outcome by the included studies are shown in Supplementary Table 2. Disagreements between authors were resolved by consensus.

Risk of bias and quality assessment

We evaluated the risk of bias in randomized trials using version 2 of the Cochrane Risk of Bias Assessment Tool (ROB 2) [13]. Non-randomized studies were appraised using the Risk of Bias in Non-randomized Studies- of Intervention (ROBINS-1) [14]. Two independent authors (U.G.A. and M.B.) performed the assessment, with disagreements resolved by consensus. Due to the small number of eligible studies, we did not perform publication bias analysis.

Statistical analyses

The DerSimonian and Laird random-effects model was used to assess all outcomes. Odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were pooled using the Mantel-Haenszel method as a measure of effect size for binary outcomes. Heterogeneity was evaluated with Cochran’s Q test, Higgins and Thompsons’s I2 statistics, and Tau-square using the restricted maximum-likelihood estimator. P ≤.10 signified statistical significance. Heterogeneity was reported as low (I2 = 0–25%), moderate (I2 = 26–50%), or high (I2 > 50%). We performed leave-one-out sensitivity analyses for all outcomes to ensure that the results were not dependent on a single study. A subgroup analysis was conducted of data from RCTs and observational studies. All statistical analyses were performed using Review Manager 5.4.1 (Nordic Cochrane Centre, The Cochrane Collaboration, Copenhagen, Denmark).

Results

Our initial search yielded 549 results. After removing duplicates and screening titles and abstracts for ineligible studies, 22 studies remained and were selected for full-text review. Of these, one RCT and three observational studies met al.l the inclusion criteria [7–10]. Comprehensive details of the study selection are detailed in Fig. 1. A total of 3,144 patients were enrolled, with 1,500 (47.7%) assigned to the periprocedural continuation therapy. The mean patient age was approximately 81 years, with a female predominance in cohort studies (49–58%) in both groups. The follow-up period varied from 30 days to one year. Pooled analysis of all studies

The pooled analysis revealed no significant difference in the incidence of the composite outcome between the continuation and interruption groups (13.5% vs. 15.6%) (OR: 0.84; 95% CI 0.64-1.10; p =.19; I2 = 41%; Fig. 2A). Similarly, no differences were observed in the rates of major vascular bleeding (OR: 0.93; 95% CI: 0.72-1.20; p =.60; I² = 14%; Fig. 2B) or closure device failure (OR: 1.22; 95% CI: 0.78-1.90; p =.39; I² = 22%; Fig. 2C), and cardiovascular mortality (1.64% vs. 1.63%; OR: 0.50; 95% CI: 0.12-2.17;p =.36; I² = 71%; Fig. 2D).

However, the incidence of stroke from any cause is significantly lower with continuation of anticoagulation compared to interruption (2.2% vs. 3.6%; OR: 0.61; 95% CI: 0.39-0.94; p =.03; I² = 0%; Fig. 2E). There was no difference in the need for blood transfusion between the two groups (20.7% vs. 22.1%; OR: 0.86; 95% CI: 0.42-1.77; p =.68; I² = 93%; Fig. 2F).

A subgroup analysis was conducted due to differences in the definition of composite outcome between observational studies and the RCT. Observational studies favored anticoagulation continuation (OR: 0.73; 95% CI: 0.58–0.93; p =.01; I² = 0%; Supplementary Fig. 1). This discrepancy may be due to variations in the components of the composite outcome. There was no significant difference in the incidence of MI between the continuation group compared with and interruption group (OR: 0.60; 95% CI: 0.20-1.77; p =.36; I2 = 0%; Supplementary Fig. 2). The incidence of all-cause mortality did not differ between the continuation and interruption groups (OR: 0.73; 95% CI: 0.44-1.21; p =.22; I2 = 6%; Supplementary Fig. 3). Similarly, no significant differences were observed for major bleeding (OR: 0.98; 95% CI: 0.57-1.68; p =.95; I2 = 63%; Supplementary Fig. 4), any bleeding (OR: 1.11; 95% CI: 0.75-1.64; p =.61; I2= 80%; Supplementary Fig. 5), use of embolic protection device (OR: 1.30; 95% CI: 0.90-1.89; p =.17; I2 = 0%; Supplementary Fig. 6), early safety outcome (OR: 0.77; 95% CI: 0.54-1.08; p =.13; I2 = 49%; Supplementary Fig. 7), vascular repair (OR: 1.15; 95% CI: 0.73-1.80; p =.55; I2 = 0%; Supplementary Fig. 8) and TIA (OR: 1.45; 95% CI: 0.67-3.16; p =.35; I2 = 0%; Supplementary Fig. 9).

Sensitivity analysis

We performed a leave-one-out sensitivity analysis for all outcomes. Overall, there was no change in the statistical significance of outcomes in each of the leave-one-out tests. For stroke, removal of the study by Brinkert 2021 et al resulted in a loss of statistical significance (P= 0.09), but no change in heterogeneity, as shown in Supplementary Table 3 A [7]. For transfusion outcomes, removal of the study by van Ginkel et al. resulted in a marked change in statistical significance (P < 0.001) and a reduction in heterogeneity from I² = 93% to I² = 0%, as shown in Supplementary Table 3B [10]. This variability may be attributed to the differences in patient populations, procedural techniques, transfusion thresholds, and anticoagulation regimens across studies. For all other outcomes, sequential exclusion of individual studies did not produce any substantial changes in heterogeneity or statistical significance, indicating the robustness of the pooled estimates.

Risk of bias and quality assessment

The appraisal of the RCT is shown in Supplementary Fig.10a. Although the patients and investigators were unblinded at randomisation, the adjudicators were blinded to the outcomes. The RCT was considered at low risk of bias. The appraisal of non-randomized studies are reported in Supplementary Fig. 10b. All the observational studies matched the continuation to the intervention groups.

Discussion

In this systematic review and meta-analysis of four studies involving 3,144 patients, we compared periprocedural continuation versus interruption of anticoagulation therapy in TAVI patients with long-term anticoagulation indications. Key findings include: (1) anticoagulation continuation reduced stroke risk but had no effect on TIA; (2) no significant difference in life-threatening or major bleeding and cardiovascular mortality; and (3) no clinical benefit regarding closure device failure, major vascular bleeding, any bleeding, and MI.

Prior studies on the perioperative management of anticoagulation in patients undergoing TAVI with a long-term need for anticoagulation have been controversial [7–10]. The continued anticoagulation strategy was associated with a higher incidence of major bleeding and vascular complications, with no clear difference in the reduction of thromboembolic events [10]. However, none of these studies may have been adequately powered to demonstrate superiority with respect to the primary composite outcomes of thromboembolic events and bleeding. In a network meta-analysis of 11 RCTs including 6,415 patients comparing oral anticoagulant and antiplatelet regimens in post-TAVI patients, a single antiplatelet agent (SAPT) was found to be better than other regimens with regards to major bleeding and provided an optimal balance with thrombotic events. The combination of oral anticoagulants and SAPT increased all-cause mortality (OR: 1.78; 95% CI: 1.15-2.77; p <.05) [15].

The pooled incidence of stroke in our meta-analysis was 2.2% in the continuation group, with no difference in the TIA risk between the two groups. This is consistent with a meta-analysis of fifty-three studies including 10,037 patients who underwent TAVI for native aortic valve stenosis. Stroke, mostly major stroke, was approximately 3.3% during the first 30-days post TAVI [16]. However, it was not determined whether anticoagulation was interrupted or continued in these patients. Some underlying mechanisms of stroke during TAVI include fracturing of the aortic valve or atherosclerotic materials from the aorta with subsequent cerebral embolism and thrombin formation following endothelial denudation [17, 18]. Stroke is a severe complication of TAVI that can significantly affect patient outcomes and quality of life.

The reduction in stroke risk in our meta-analysis, without an increase in bleeding incidence, suggests that maintaining anticoagulation may provide cerebrovascular protection during the procedure. The use of embolic protection devices was equal between the two groups and may be plausible, suggesting that its use may not have affected the incidence of stroke. The rate of closure device failure and need for blood transfusion were the same between the groups. Improvements in arteriotomy closure devices and non-routine use of protamine may also be contributing factors.

The study did not find any significant difference in the composite endpoint of life-threatening or major bleeding between the groups. Bleeding events are common during TAVI and may increase the cost of hospitalisation, mortality, and morbidity [19, 20]. Although bleeding complications are vascular access-related, frequent comorbidities, and frailty of patients, acquired type 2 A von Willebrand factor defect, angiodysplasia, thrombocytopenia, and Heyde’s syndrome all contribute to the risk of bleeding [21, 22]. Several observational studies and RCTs have demonstrated that direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) are associated with a lower risk of major bleeding and provide comparable thromboembolic protection relative to vitamin K antagonists (VKAs) in patients undergoing TAVI [23–25]. VKAs are limited by a narrow therapeutic window, require frequent INR monitoring, and are associated with a higher incidence of vascular and bleeding complications due to fluctuating anticoagulation levels. In contrast, DOACs exhibit more predictable pharmacokinetics, have fewer food and drug interactions, and generally provide a more stable anticoagulant effect. Furthermore, a recent meta-analysis reported that DOACs were associated with significantly lower risks of major bleeding and intracranial haemorrhage, with similar rates of all-cause mortality, ischemic stroke, and systemic thromboembolism when compared to VKAs in patients with atrial fibrillation undergoing TAVI [26]. These findings suggest that DOACs may offer a safer and more manageable alternative to VKAs in the TAVI population, although our meta-analysis is not sufficiently powered to validate these benefits.

In both observational studies and RCT of continued versus interruption of anticoagulation, the analysis did not show a difference in the primary endpoint of cardiovascular mortality between the two modalities [7–10]. In a study of the efficacy and safety of continuation of vitamin K antagonists or direct oral anticoagulants, there was no difference in mortality (p =.25), and survival was the same at 12 months follow-up (hazard ratio: 0.90; 95% CI: 0.73-1.12; p =.36) [9]. In addition, mortality from any cause in the included RCT was the same between groups [10]. However, a subgroup analysis of the trials may have been limited by insufficient power. Our meta-analysis conforms to these findings of no significant difference in cardiovascular mortality with continuation relative to interruption strategy (OR: 0.50; 95% CI: 0.12-2.17; p =.36).

Limitations

Our study has some limitations. First, this meta-analysis predominantly included observational data, which is inherently subject to biases such as residual confounding and selection bias that could introduce variability and limit the generalizability of the findings. As such, the results should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating and underscore the need for prospective, randomized studies to more definitively assess the safety and efficacy of continued versus interrupted anticoagulation strategies.

Second, some outcomes were associated with high heterogeneity. However, a leave-one-out sensitivity analysis revealed that the studies were similar. In addition to statistical heterogeneity, clinical heterogeneity across the included studies may have affected the results. Variations in the type of oral anticoagulant used (e.g. vitamin K antagonists vs. direct oral anticoagulants), procedural techniques, and the use of adjunctive strategies, such as embolic protection devices, may contribute to differences in bleeding and thromboembolic risk profiles. These differences should be considered when interpreting pooled estimates.

Third, despite variations in the definition of composite outcome, there were no significant differences between the two treatment strategies. It is also important to note that the POPular PAUSE TAVI trial was powered only to demonstrate non-inferiority of interruption for the composite outcome. Fourth, Due to the small number of included studies, we were unable to perform a formal assessment of publication bias, which is a potential limitation that may affect the robustness of our findings. Fifth, some outcomes, including myocardial infarction, bleeding, and closure device failure, were based on low event but clinically significant rates with wide confidence intervals. This limits the statistical power and precision of the pooled estimates, and the findings should therefore be interpreted with caution. Larger, high-quality studies are needed to more definitively assess these outcomes. Finally, most of the included studies were conducted in Europe, which may limit the generalizability of our findings. Further research from more geographically diverse settings is needed to validate these results across broader populations

Conclusions

In this meta-analysis comparing periprocedural continuation of anticoagulation in patients undergoing TAVI with long-term indications for anticoagulation, continuation of anticoagulation therapy was associated with a lower incidence of stroke. There were no significant differences between the groups in the risk of death from cardiovascular causes, cardiovascular mortality, myocardial infarction, major vascular complications, or major bleeding. These findings raise the possibility that continuing anticoagulation during TAVI may offer safety benefits, although further randomised data are needed.

Impact on daily practice

The findings of this systematic review and meta-analysis suggest that periprocedural continuation of anticoagulation in patients undergoing TAVI with indication for long-term anticoagulation may represent a potentially safer strategy, particularly by reducing the risk of stroke, without increasing the risk of major bleeding or other adverse cardiovascular outcomes. These results should be interpreted with caution due to the small number of included studies and the observational nature of most data, with limited adjustment for important confounders. As such, the clinical implications are preliminary and should not yet guide definitive practice changes. Future larger and multicenter RCTs are needed to validate the safety and efficacy of continued versus interrupted anticoagulation strategies in patients undergoing TAVI and inform evidence-based guidelines.

Lens shape confirmed in light adjustable lens: A report of two cases
Authors: Yukihito Kato, Kei Ichikawa, Yoshiki Tanaka, Yukihiro Sakai, Kazuo Ichikawa
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundA light-adjustable lens™ (LAL) (RxSight, Inc.) is an intraocular lens inserted during cataract surgery. The lens shape can be changed after surgery by ultraviolet light using a Light Delivery Device™ (LDD) to adjust the subjective refractive power. This study evaluated shape changes in the LAL using anterior segment optical coherence tomography (OCT).CasesIn Case 1, the right eye of a 70-year-old male was examined. Spherical power changed from +0.75 D to −0.25 D after LDD adjustment with a target of S−0.5 D. Shape analysis using anterior segment OCT showed that the radius of curvature of the front surface of the LAL decreased from 11.59mm to 9.03mm, while that of the back surface increased from 10.98mm to 13.41mm after LDD adjustment. In Case 2, we examined the right eye of an 80-year-old female. Refractive power changed from Cyl-1.50 D (axis 105°) to S+0.25 D after LDD adjustment with the target of emmetropia. The radius of curvature of the front of the LAL decreased from 10.26mm to 7.99mm, while that of the back surface increased from 11.10mm to 21.78mm after the LDD adjustment, and that of the back showed a variation in the radius of curvature depending on the refraction angle.ConclusionsThe findings in Case 1 highlighted a change in shape while maintaining a spherical lens shape, while in Case 2, the change in asymmetric shape may have occurred to correct astigmatism on the back of the LAL. Taken together, these findings indicate that anterior segment OCT captures the shape changes in the LAL. The LAL was postoperatively adjusted to the target refraction through shape change.Keywords: Cataract surgery, Light adjustable lens, Anterior segment OCT, Intraocular lens, Refractive correction

Introduction

In recent years, cataract surgery has become increasingly safe owing to the establishment of advanced surgical techniques, improvements in examination devices, and increases in the measurability and reproducibility of axis length measurement devices.1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 As a result, cataract surgery is increasingly being used for refractive purposes. However, even with the latest formulas and devices, achieving a perfectly precise postoperative refraction remains a challenge.

The Light Adjustable Lens (LAL) (RxSight, Inc. USA) is the only IOL that allows the adjustment of subjective refractive power using ultraviolet (UV) light after insertion in lens reconstruction surgery. The overall diameter of the LAL was 13.0 mm. The optic was biconvex and made of silicone. The principles of LAL were reported by Schwartz in 2003.7 This study was approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in 2017. Some LALs contain photosensitive silicone macromers. The Light Delivery Device (LDD) delivers UV light and polymerizes the photosensitive silicone macromer, thereby changing the shape and refractive power of the LAL. Hengerer et al. confirmed the safety of the LAL, reporting no development of macular edema after insertion.8 In an earlier report, we reported that LAL achieved ±0.25 D and ±0.50 D for the final target refractive error in 91 % and 97 % of the cases, respectively, with good visual outcomes.9 Studies on eyes after LASIK and radial keratotomy have also reported 97 % and 88 % of cases within ±0.50 D, respectively, for refractive errors.10,11 Although reports on the extent of refractive errors are widespread in the literature, studies on changes in the shape of the LAL are lacking.

In this study, we examined the possibility of analyzing changes in the shape of the LAL before and after delivering UV light by an LDD using anterior segment optical coherence tomography (OCT) and CASIA2 (Tomey, Japan).

Methods

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Chukyo Eye Clinic (20240917087) and conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The opt-out method was approved by the Ethics Committee.

Cataract surgery was performed as usual with ultrasonic phacoemulsification and aspiration, and intracapsular fixation of the LAL was secured with target emmetropia. Visual acuity was checked one month after cataract surgery, and the target refractive value was determined. The LDD adjustment was performed based on this target value, and the final UV light lock-in had not yet been conducted at the time of shape analysis. The measurements reported in this study were taken on week after the LDD adjustment, and before the lock-in procedure. Anterior segment OCT was performed with the CASIA2 device before and after LDD adjustment to analyze the shape of the LAL. A refractive power/corneal analyzer (OPD-Scan III; NIDEK, Japan) was used to examine the refractive changes.

In the shape analysis method using CASIA2, three points were set on the front and back of the IOL (Fig. 1), and the two-dimensional (2D) radius of curvature was calculated by circular approximation using trace analysis with three points (2D results). The analysis diameter was 4 mm, and images were captured in 16 directions. Based on the radius of curvature calculated using the circular approximation at each shooting angle, another circular approximation was performed to calculate the three-dimensional (3D) radius of curvature (3D results). This analysis method also allowed for the identification of the axis of the toric IOL.

Intraocular lens (IOL) analysis using anterior segment optical coherence tomography. Trace analysis by aligning the three cross points at the end of the red arrows with the front and back surfaces of the IOL.

To validate the accuracy of the CASIA2 analysis of IOL curvature, a preliminary study was performed on 20 eyes with intracapsular fixation using the AN6KA (Kowa, Japan). Two optometrists analyzed these eyes three times each, and we calculated the intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs). The ICCs within the same examiner and between the two examiners ranged from 0.742 to 0.992. This demonstrated the statistical reliability of the CASIA2 analysis method for measuring IOL curvature, confirming its suitability for evaluating LAL curvature before and after LDD adjustment in Table 1.

Case presentation

Case 1

The first case involved the right eye of a 70-year-old male. No remarkable ocular diseases, other than cataracts, were noted. The patient had no history of ophthalmic surgery. Cataract surgery was performed with target emmetropia. At one month postoperatively, the visual acuity of the uncorrected was 1.5, and the corrected distance visual acuity was 2.0 × Sph +0.75 D in decimal form. The LDD was applied while adjusting the light for 92 s for a target of S–0.5 D, with no problems. One week later, the visual acuity of the uncorrected was 1.2, and the corrected distance visual acuity was 2.0 × Sph −0.25 D in decimal visual acuity. The 2D results analyzed in 16 directions are shown in Fig. 2. The radii of curvature of the front and back decreased and increased, respectively, after the LDD adjustment in all directions. The 3D results analysis, before the LDD adjustment, the radii of the front and back surfaces of the lens were 11.59 and 10.98mm. The shape of the LAL captured using anterior segment OCT is shown in Fig. 3. After the LDD adjustment, the radii of the front and back surfaces of the lens were 9.03 and 13.41 mm, respectively. The refractive distribution was obtained through refractive measurements using optical path difference (OPD) scanning. The color map shows warmer colors after the LDD adjustment, indicating increased myopia.

Case 2

The second case involved the right eye of an 80-year-old female. The patient had no particular ocular diseases other than cataracts or a history of ophthalmic surgery. She underwent cataract surgery with emmetropia as the target. One month postoperatively, her distance uncorrected visual acuity was 0.7 in decimal vision, and her corrected distance visual acuity was 1.5 × Cyl −1.50 D axis 105°. The LDD was applied for 135 s with the target emmetropia, without any complications. One week later, her distance uncorrected visual acuity was 1.5 in decimal, and the corrected distanced visual acuity was 1.5 × Sph+0.25D. The 2D results for the 16 directions are shown in Fig. 4. The radius of curvature of the front surface decreased in all directions after the LDD adjustment. For the back surface, the radius of curvature increased in the directions from 11° to 191° and from 23° to 203°, while no change was observed in the directions from 101° to 281° and from 113° to 293°. The 3D results indicated radii of 10.26 and 11.10 mm for the front and back surfaces of the lens, respectively, before the LDD adjustment. The shape of the LAL obtained using anterior segment OCT is shown in Fig. 5. The results after the LDD adjustment indicated radii of 7.99 and 21.78 mm for the front and back surfaces of the lens, respectively. Discussion

Ocular parameters are key factors influencing postoperative refractive error after cataract surgery. Melles et al. reported the refractive error of multiple IOL power formulas and predicted a higher refractive error in cases with poor ocular proportions, such as short or long axial lengths.12 Although the use of a dedicated intraocular lens power calculation formula in cataract surgery after corneal refractive surgery is recommended, postoperative refraction is more likely to occur than in normal eyes.13, 14, 15 Postoperative refractive error does not yield satisfactory outcomes and reduces the quality of postoperative life in patients. As good outcomes after cataract surgery are associated with longer life expectancies,16,17 it is important to match postoperative refraction to the desired value. Several favorable results for postoperative refractive errors have been reported for LAL. LAL provides high-accuracy correction even after corneal refractive surgery.18 This is considered to yield highly satisfactory outcomes because it allows for correction in accordance with the patient's lifestyle. In the two eyes presented herein, the planned refractive power was almost achieved.

The LAL is a unique system that enables the customization of refractive power through changes in the shape of the lens in response to UV light. However, there are few reports focusing on changes to the LAL shape itself. Our study demonstrates that analysis using anterior segment OCT provide a reliable method for observing these changes, as supported by the good ICCs in our preliminary study. Changes in the lens shape were confirmed in both cases. In Case 1, only the spherical power was adjusted, and in Case 2, only the cylindrical power was adjusted. In Case 1, the radii of curvature for the front and back surfaces of the LAL lens became smaller and larger, respectively, than those before the LDD adjustment. Similar results were obtained for the analyzed angles, indicating that the radii of curvature may change while maintaining the shape of the biconvex lens. In Case 2, the radii of curvature of the front of the LAL decreased in all 16 directions after the LDD adjustment, whereas that of the back remained unchanged for some angles and markedly changed for others. The angle at which the radii of curvature of the back changed significantly was similar to the astigmatic angle of the target correction. This suggests that strong and weak principal meridians were created in the spherical shape of the LAL. This is similar to the mechanism of toric IOLs, in which astigmatism is corrected by adjusting the weak principal meridian of the toric IOL to the strong principal meridian of corneal astigmatism. In contrast, the shape of the LAL can be changed by adjusting it to the subjective astigmatic axis. The common point between the shape changes in Cases 1 and 2 was that the curvature of the front surface of the lens was reduced by the LDD adjustment. However, a characteristic difference was observed between Cases 1 and 2 for the back surface of the lens after the LDD adjustment. The changes in the shapes of both the front and back surfaces of the lens may be explained by the premise that the refractive power is finely adjusted. Furthermore, because it is unlikely that the volume of the entire lens changes in association with the LDD adjustment, it is possible that the LAL is designed to reduce the thickness or achieve structural balance by changing both the front and back of the lens. In addition, because both the front and back surfaces can be aspherically changed, this may also affect the control of the spherical and higher-order aberrations of the entire eye.

This study has some limitations. First, this is a case report with only two cases, and further studies with larger sample size are needed to confirm these findings. Second, we do not have long-term follow up data (more than 6 months) for these specific cases to confirm the long-term stability of the lens shape. Third, the anterior segment OCT had only three points to be analyzed for both the front and back of the IOL. Consequently, we could not analyze the 360 °images. Therefore, our findings may not suffice to capture comprehensive changes in the shape of the LAL. Despite these limitations, the results suggest that the LAL is effective in enhancing patient satisfaction after cataract surgery, particularly in terms of refractive outcomes. The LAL changes the refractive power by polymerizing a photosensitive silicone macromer using UV light.

The findings included evidence of changes in the shape of the LAL using anterior segment OCT, confirming that both spherical and cylindrical powers were appropriately adjusted.

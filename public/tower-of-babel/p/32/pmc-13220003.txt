A Reliable Prediction Method for the Pedicle Subtraction Osteotomy Angle in Ankylosing Spondylitis-Related Thoracolumbar Kyphosis: A Comparative Analysis with Existing Approaches
Authors: Mu Qiao, Bang-ping Qian, Kai-yang Wang, Chen-yu Song, Jing-shun Lu, Yong Qiu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCareful preoperative planning regarding the reliable angle of pedicle subtraction osteotomy (PSO) in ankylosing spondylitis (AS) is beneficial for sagittal realignment, preventing undercorrection or overcorrection, and mitigating potential mechanical complications. The purpose of this study was to propose a novel method, using AS-specific realignment criteria and Surgimap software, for calculating a reliable PSO angle in AS patients with thoracolumbar kyphosis, and to compare its predictive accuracy with 3 other methods reported in the previous literature.MethodsSixty-one AS patients who underwent 1-level PSO were analyzed, fulfilling the ideal sagittal balance criteria of adult spinal deformity at the final follow-up: pelvic tilt (PT) < 25°, sagittal vertical axis (SVA) < 50 mm, and −10° < pelvic incidence minus lumbar lordosis (PI−LL) < 10°. In the new method, the formula PT = (0.47 × PI − 7.5) was used to target reliable postoperative PT. Besides, AS-specific realignment criteria for surgical planning were as follows: PT < 24°, Spinosacral angle (SSA) > 108°, T1 pelvic angle (TPA) < 22°, and spinopelvic angle (SPA) > 152°. All the 4 methods were applied to calculate the theoretical angle of osteotomy (tAO) for each patient, including the trigonometric method, full balance integrated (FBI), SS-based ASKyphoplan, and Surgimap Spine plan. The difference between pre- and postoperative osteotomized vertebral angle in radiographs was defined as the actual angle of osteotomy (aAO). Precision of all predictive methods was evaluated on the basis of concordance correlation coefficient (CCC), accuracy rate, root mean squared prediction error (RMSE), and difference between tAO and aAO (bias).ResultsSignificant improvement in global kyphosis, LL, and SVA were noted postoperatively for all 61 patients without obvious correction loss at the final follow-up. The most accurate and precise method for the osteotomy angle calculation was the AS Surgimap plan (prediction accuracy, 72.13%; bias, −2.36%; RMSE, 8.34; CCC, 0.837), followed by FBI, the trigonometric method, and ASKyphoplan. The prediction accuracy, undercorrection rate, and overcorrection rate for the low and high PI groups were 80.00%, 5.71%, and 14.29% and 61.54%, 30.77%, and 7.69%, respectively.ConclusionsSurgimap Spine plan was relatively the most reliable and accurate approach to predict the PSO angle and achieve proper postoperative sagittal alignment for AS patients in comparison to other methods. Predetermined PT using the equation PT = (0.47 × PI − 7.5) and well-defined criteria (SSA > 108°, SPA > 152°, and TPA < 22°) could improve the predictive values.Keywords: Ankylosing spondylitis, Thoracolumbar kyphosis, Osteotomy, Surgical planning, Prediction algorithms

Recently, pedicle subtraction osteotomy (PSO) has been widely accepted as the most powerful and effective surgical technique for correcting fixed and rigid sagittal plane deformity, especially in ankylosing spondylitis (AS).1,2) To the best of our knowledge, a single-level PSO can achieve up to nearly 45° of kyphosis correction.1,3,4) Given this capability, careful preoperative planning regarding the optimal angle of PSO is beneficial for sagittal realignment and prevention of undercorrection or overcorrection. Also, accurate prediction could lower mechanical complication rates due to mis-estimation of the surgical target angle (STA).

According to previous studies, there have been several attempts to quantify the exact magnitude of PSO angle acquired to restore sagittal alignment.5,6,7,8,9,10) In 2006, Ondra6) proposed a trigonometric method for osteotomy calculation and conducted a mathematical analysis in 15 adult spinal deformity (ASD) cases to confirm its validity. Furthermore, Le Huec et al.8) defined full balance integrated (FBI) as a global assessment parameter to determine the actual amount of correction needed. Besides, unlike the aforementioned predictive methods implemented on radiographs, van Royen et al.7) described ASKyphoplan as a digital measurement tool to visualize the surgical planning procedure for patients with unflexible spine by pre-establishing target spinopelvic parameters. Similarly, in 2013, Surgimap Spine software was firstly introduced by Akbar et al.9) to simulate surgical correction and reveal potential postoperative sagittal profile in a degenerative case.

However, these studies were mainly limited to non-AS patients, and the sample sizes were relatively small. In 2019, Huang et al.11) performed a thorough analysis of 100 AS patients with satisfying clinical outcomes at the final follow-up and concluded that the optimal sagittal alignment criteria were as follows: pelvic tilt (PT) < 24°, spinosacral angle (SSA) > 108°, T1 pelvic angle (TPA) < 22°, and spinopelvic angle (SPA) > 152°. Based on these findings, the present study aimed to propose a novel method to calculate the exact PSO angle with Surgimap software, specifically for AS pathology. Moreover, we sought to compare the accuracy of all these predictive methods in a larger clinical AS population.

METHODS

This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital, Affiliated Hospital of Medical School, Nanjing University (IRB No. 2011052). Written informed consent was obtained from all patients before testing.

Subjects

Inclusion criteria included (1) global kyphosis (GK) > 40°; (2) single-level PSO; (3) follow-up data fulfilling the harmony realignment objectives of ASD proposed by Schwab12): sagittal vertical axis (SVA) < 5 cm, PT < 25°, and −9° ≤ pelvic incidence minus lumbar lordosis (PI−LL) ≤ 9°; and (4) a minimum of 2-year follow-up. The exclusion criteria were defined as follows: preexisting pseudarthrosis, spinal fracture, spinal neoplasm, history of prior spinal surgery, complete ankylosis of the hips, surgery-related complications (including nontraumatic or spontaneous rod fracture), and any hip/knee joint corrective procedure performed during the follow-up period. Sixty-one consecutive patients who fulfilled the inclusion and exclusion criteria with an average age of 33.8 years (range, 20–56 years) were included in the study. In the current study, all the patients were divided into 2 groups according to PI (low PI group, PI < 45°; high PI group, PI ≥ 45°) to analyze the effect of PI variation on the prediction accuracy.

Radiographic Measurements and Clinical Assessment

In total, 11 parameters in the sagittal plane were measured, including GK, LL, SVA, osteotomized vertebra angle (OVA), PI, PT, sacral slope (SS), SSA, SPA, TPA, and angle of fused segments (AFS) (Figs. 1 and 2).11,12,13,14,15,16,17,18) AFS was defined as the angle between the superior endplate of the uppermost instrumented vertebra and the inferior endplate of the lowermost instrumented vertebra. For LL, OVA, and AFS, values were positive for the lordotic profile. The difference between pre- and postoperative OVA was defined as actual angle of osteotomy (aAO). Besides, the angle of osteotomy calculated by various predictive methods was defined as theoretical angle of osteotomy (tAO). Each parameter was measured 3 times and averaged preoperatively, postoperatively, and at the latest follow-up. Besides, the Oswestry Disability Index (ODI) and visual analog scale (VAS) were applied to assess the clinical outcomes.

General Principle of Osteotomy Level Selection

Osteotomy level selection depended on multiple factors, including apex location, type of kyphotic deformity, sagittal profile of the lumbar spine, desired amount of SVA/GK correction, and preoperative hip function.

(1) When the apex was located at the thoracolumbar region (T12-L2), osteotomy was preferred to be conducted at the apical region to achieve maximum correction and obtain a harmonious spinal curve.13,19) (2) Osteotomy level would be close to the thoracolumbar region (T12-L2) provided that the lumbar spine was a lordotic curve. Otherwise, a lower level of osteotomy (L3) was appropriate to restore a normal lumbar profile.20) (3) Commonly, a more caudal osteotomy level was associated with a greater SVA and pelvic retroversion correction. Similarly, for AS patients with predominant change in trunk shifting and mild-to-moderate GK, osteotomy at the lower lumbar spine (L3, occasionally L4) was preferred to achieve the truncal-dominant correction.3,21) (4) For AS patients with severe structural destruction of the hip joints, hip ankylosis, and limited pelvic rebalancing ability, the ideal osteotomy level would be close to the apical region to prevent potential risk of overcorrection.3,22)

Predictive Methods

Surgimap Spine plan (Fig. 2)

(1) Measurement: the sagittal alignment wizard (“SA spine”) was used to perform a comprehensive analysis of the sagittal plane including the measurement of spinopelvic parameters.9) (2) Adjustment of spinopelvic parameters: in view of the similarity in curve patterns between degenerative kyphoscoliotic deformities and AS, the equation “PT = (0.47 × PI − 7.5)” proposed by Yamato et al.23) was adopted to predict the optimal postoperative PT. The radiograph images were rotated until the PT adjusted to the predicted value. (3) Quantification of osteotomy: The midpoint of the anterior cortex of the predetermined osteotomized vertebra (OV) was chosen as the re-rotation point. Meanwhile, 25° was set as the preliminary resection angle. (4) Simulation of osteotomy: the “wedge” tool with the closure ratio set at 100% (shifting the ratio line over the inferior resection line) was used to initiate and simulate the osteotomy procedure. Supposing that the simulated sagittal profile did not fulfill the AS-specific alignment criteria (SSA > 108°, SPA > 152°, and TPA < 22°),11) the resection angle should be adjusted.

Other Methods

The operating methods for the trigonometric method, FBI, and SS-based ASKyphoplan are shown in Fig. 1B-D. Considering that a postoperative normalized FBI less than 10° was strongly recommended and correlated with good functional results,8) tAO was defined as FBI minus 10°.

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software version 24.0 (IBM Corp.). Differences between preoperative, postoperative, and the follow-up values of all radiographic parameters were assessed with paired t-tests. Prediction of tAO between 90% and 110% of aAO measured was defined as an accurate prediction. To date, numerous studies and clinical investigations have systematically analyzed the measurement error of Cobb angle, primarily attributing such variability to observer-dependent discrepancies, inherent limitations of imaging technologies, and so forth. In a 2022 study by Prestigiacomo et al.,24) 6 observers performed 2 independent measurements on radiographs of 39 adolescents idiopathic scoliosis patients, yielding a mean interobserver error of 3.0°. Notably, Tanure et al.25) compared measurement reliability between digital and manual techniques in 49 idiopathic scoliosis cases, reporting average errors of 3.8° (manual) and 3.6° (digital), respectively. Collectively, based on the aforementioned literature, these findings supported a reasonable error margin of 3°–4° for Cobb angle measurements. Given the average OVA of 38.2° in the present case series, a 10% cutoff was adopted and deemed appropriate to balance the measurement variability. In contrast, a prediction < 90% or > 110% of the aAO measured was classified as an underprediction or overprediction, respectively. Concordance correlation coefficient (CCC) was used with Pearson correlation analysis to show prediction precision. The p-values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Surgical Data

Sixty-one AS patients (54 males and 7 females) undergoing 1-level PSO were included. The mean follow-up was 31.79 months (range, 24–96 months). Osteotomy sites were located at L1 in 14 cases, L2 in 41 cases, L3 in 2 cases, and T12 in 4 cases. Based on the magnitude of PI, all 61 patients were categorized into 2 groups: 35 patients in the low PI group (PI < 45°) and 26 patients in the high PI group (PI ≥ 45°). Notably, the patients' basic information, radiographic assessment of hip joints, and degree of ossification were essential for the patient characterization and surgical decision-making. Thus, the symptom duration, the Bath Ankylosing Spondylitis Radiology Hip Index (BASRI-hip),26) and modified Stoke Ankylosing Spondylitis Spinal Score (mSASSS)27) were used to show the clinical status, hip involvement, and degree of ossification, respectively (Table 1). Utility of Predictive Methods

The mean aAO and tAO of the trigonometric method, FBI, ASKyphoplan, and Surgimap Spine plan were 38.92°, 26.60°, 46.20°, 58.37° and 38.52°, respectively (Table 3). AS Surgimap Spine plan showed the highest predictive precision and lowest bias rate (72.13%, −2.36%), followed by FBI (24.59%, 20.87%), trigonometric method (11.48%, −30.17%) and ASKyphoplan (9.84%, 52.37%). Intraoperative Complications

Only 1 case of vertebral subluxation was encountered at the osteotomy site. Conservative treatment was performed due to the absence of irreversible nerve tissue or vascular injury.

DISCUSSION

Sagittal realignment failure (SRF) following PSO was occasionally encountered in clinical practice. Notably, in 2012, Schwab et al.29) reported a 23% rate of SRF (18 / 79) after PSO surgery due to underlying cause of undercorrection. It is assumed that SRF was strongly correlated with poor functional outcome and a potential risk of mechanical complications, which might result in unexpected revision operations eventually. Thus, it is of great significance to perform preoperative careful evaluation of sagittal profile and adequate surgical planning.30)

Comparison of Prediction Accuracy

In the current study, the novel method using the Surgimap Spine program showed the highest prediction accuracy (72.13%), with the lowest bias rate and RMSE. In contrast, the trigonometric method showed a significantly lower calculated STA (26.6°) than aAO (38.92°) and an obvious under-prediction rate of 83.61%. One weakness of this method was that the contribution of the pelvis to sagittal realignment was neglected. Thus, it should be acknowledged that preoperative planning needs to consider the site of primary deformity as well as compensatory mechanisms acting in the pelvis and lower extremities. In terms of ASKyphoplan, to be consistent with a healthy population, a predetermined SS of 40° was adopted to place hip joints in a neutral position approximately. However, a greater tAO (58.37°) and percentage of overprediction (83.61%) were identified with this method. Excessive prediction values might be ascribed to the overestimation of the SS value and neglect of PI or PT. Moreover, a significant bias rate (52.37%) and RMSE (23.79) resulted from a non-individualized SS definition. Different from traditional trigonometric mathematical methods, FBI was proposed by Le Huec et al.8) to calculate the desired osteotomy angle following the 3 parts of compensation separately. In the present study, the FBI method revealed a relatively acceptable and higher predictive precision (accuracy rate: 24.59%, bias: 20.87%) than the abovementioned methods. Nevertheless, a mean tAO of 46.20° and overprediction rate of 63.93% should be emphasized. The possible reason was that when sagittal deformity aggravated, pelvic retroversion was inevitably accompanied by simultaneous knee flexion. In this situation, calculating the sum of the femur obliquity angle (FOA) and pelvic tilt compensation angle might lead to repetitive consideration of partial pelvic compensation, thereby resulting in a higher STA than the actual desired angle. Additionally, asymmetrical femur inclination due to varying degrees of structural destruction in bilateral hip joints might lead to measuring deviation in FOA.31)

Reliable Correction Criteria

In AS-related thoracolumbar kyphosis, pelvic retroversion is the fundamental and predominant compensatory mechanism to maintain sagittal balance, represented by an increased PT.3,10) Thus, integrating the true severity of sagittal imbalance without any compensation, by adjusting PT to a projected value, proved to be the principal process of osteotomy planning. Unfortunately, there was a lack of an AS-specific method for the calculation of the optimal postoperative PT value. Previous studies adopted the formula “PT = (0.37 × PI) − 7°” for AS patients to calculate ideal individual PT.32) The average conceptual PT based on this equation was 9.20° in the current study, which was significantly less than the actual postoperative value (15.43°) (p < 0.05). The obvious over-estimation of ideal PT might result in an excessive STA and increase the risk of over-correction. It was probably because this formula was derived from the asymptomatic population, who generally presented with a more harmonious spinal profile than AS cases. Instead, in 2016, Yamato et al.23) proposed a new algorithm “PT = 0.47 × PI − 7.5” for optimal PT calculation, based on an investigation of 184 elderly ASD patients. Considering the similarities of curve patterns and spinopelvic parameters between AS and elderly ASD (both manifested as loss of lumbar lordosis and moderate-to-severe thoracolumbar kyphosis), the aforementioned equation was considered more appropriate for preoperative surgical planning. In this study, using this formula, the mean theoretical PT was 13.08°, which revealed less error than that of Vialle et al.28) (p < 0.05).

In the previous literature, SVA was the most widely applied parameter for the evaluation of sagittal balance in ASD3). Moreover, a strong association between SVA and health-related quality of life (HRQoL) has been established.3,12,29,33) Nevertheless, measurement of SVA could not involve the morphology of the pelvis and was susceptible to the patient’s posture. Also, in our daily clinical practice, a significant and substantial improvement of SVA might not be well-correlated with harmony of curves in sagittal plane and acceptable physiological function for AS. Therefore, SSA, SPA, and TPA were used as reference parameters instead of SVA for assessment of the whole spinal deformity and decision-making in the present study. Of note, in a retrospective study conducted by Huang et al.,11) the aforementioned 3 parameters have been confirmed as the optimal postoperative clinically relevant radiographic realignment goals in the setting of thoracolumbar kyphosis caused by AS.

Prediction Error-Related Influencing Factors

Although the accuracy rate of the Surgimap Spine plan was 72.13%, multiple unforeseeable factors related to prediction error should be emphasized as follows: (1) Disc space within the fused segments: the parameter “AFS” was adopted in the current study to reflect and evaluate morphological change and stability of the instrumented segments. Importantly, a mean improvement of 44.74° and 38.92° in AFS and OVA was identified postoperatively, indicating that non-osteotomized fused segments can also contribute to extra kyphosis correction (5.82°). The possible mechanism was the opening of non-ossified disc spaces under the corrective force of rod contouring. Thus, for adolescent AS patients with a short disease duration and low degree of ossification, a relatively less correction of the estimated STA might be acceptable. Based on these findings, the modification of the prediction model was listed as follows: for adolescent AS patients with a short disease duration and low degree of spinal ossification, the estimated STA should be adjusted by subtracting 5° from the initially calculated value. (2) Intraoperative morphological change of OV: generally, 1-level PSO can achieve up to 45° kyphosis correction. In practice, however, excessive decancellation of the anterior column might lead to fracture and opening of the anterior cortex.1) During osteotomy closure, PSO was converted into a closing-opening wedge osteotomy, resulting in a greater correction than the predicted value. Similarly, intraoperative vertebral subluxation with proximal segment shifting to the dorsal site could achieve more satisfactory spinopelvic parameters than theoretical values.34) As a consequence, either closing-opening wedge osteotomy or vertebral subluxation might increase the risk of overcorrection if the estimated angle was applied. (3) Variation of PI: It is well-known that PI represents the individual compensatory ability of the pelvis for spinal kyphotic deformity.3,16) Accordingly, patients with relatively high PI demonstrated superior pelvic toleration, retroversion, and compensation for thoracolumbar kyphotic deformity, leading to a more pronounced reduction in PT postoperatively compared to those with low PI. In other words, the predetermined PT values derived from the formula remained substantially higher than normal population references, which might result in potential risk of under-estimation of the STA and subsequent undercorrection. To address and clarify this hypothesis, all the patients were divided into 2 groups according to PI (low PI group, PI < 45°; high PI group, PI ≥ 45°) in the present study. The findings revealed a significantly higher undercorrection rate in the high PI group (30.77%) versus the low PI group (5.71%), which aligned with the aforementioned theoretical framework. Commonly, PI represented the pelvic compensatory ability and tolerance to the kyphosis severity. Therefore, for AS patients with a low PI, the calculated postoperative PT approaching the values of the normal population might be significantly lower than the actual postoperative PT. Thus, this discrepancy in predicted PT values would increase the risk of overcorrection. Conversely, regarding the AS patients with a relatively high PI (especially the PT value more than 60°), the formula-calculated PT might exceed 20°or approach 25°. Frequently, the postoperative PT in these patients was smaller than the calculated value, consequently resulting in the under-estimated STA and subsequent insufficient correction. Notably, in the present study, comparisons between the theoretical and actual PT have been performed for the 2 sub-groups with various PI magnitudes, revealing a difference of 2.11° and 1.92° in the low and high PI groups, respectively. Hence, to optimize the prediction model based on PI, we proposed adjusting the theoretical PT by increasing it by 2° for low PI patients and decreasingit by 2° for high PI patients when determining the STA. (4) Uncommon spinal profile: regarding a minority of adolescent AS patients without obvious ligament ossification, the mobile thoracic spine might present with hypokyphosis, or even lordosis to balance lumbar kyphosis. After operation, the thoracic spine probably changed into a kyphotic profile spontaneously in response to the recurrent lumbar lordosis. In this situation, potential increased thoracic kyphosis required a larger amount of correction than the preliminary calculated angle (Fig. 5).

Further Investigation of Osteotomy Planning with Artificial Intelligence (AI)

Currently, a great amount of AI-based models mainly rely on medical imaging data such as x-rays, computed tomography scans, and magnetic resonance imaging. In the future, integrating multi-modal data sources will be a significant research direction. Based on the novel proposed osteotomy angle prediction method in this study, through simulated learning from a large number of cases, AI was expected to achieve precise preoperative osteotomy design in the future. In addition, it will take into account all possible error scenarios, thereby providing spinal surgeons with better guidance for spinal deformity correction.

The results of this study should be interpreted in terms of its limitations. Firstly, in the Surgimap Spine plan, there was a lack of an AS-specific equation for the calculation of target PT. Nevertheless, in order to increase the prediction accuracy in this case series, we applied the formula derived from elderly ASD patients. Hence, a more thorough study was warranted to propose detailed AS-related equation for optimal parameters prediction in the future. Secondly, variability in PSO angle that could be tolerated was not accounted for in this study, which would be further analyzed with a larger sample size. Thirdly, the formulas of PT determination, AS-specific realignment criteria used in the study and the Surgimap software were previously reported. In spite of this, it was worth noting that unlike the previous studies that predominantly focused on the correction of SVA, 3 global spinopelvic parameters were adopted as references for PSO angle calculation, representing a novel contribution of this case series. Fourth, the lack of evaluation and calculation for patients failing to meet ideal sagittal balance criteria might introduce selection bias, with the efficacy and accuracy of the new prediction method in this population remaining unknown. Hence, future studies would aim to enroll a larger sample size involving patients who did not achieve ideal postoperative sagittal balance to further validate the method's applicability and to identify the potential factors related to reconstruction failure in this population via our prediction method.

Surgimap Spine planning showed higher accuracy than other surgical planning methods for predicting the optimal PSO angle in AS-related thoracolumbar kyphosis. Applying clinically-relevant radiographic realignment criteria (SSA, SPA, and TPA) could enable surgeons to obtain reliable STA. Implementing the equation PT = (0.47 × PI − 7.5) for pelvic parameter precalibration could enhance predictive validity.

Association of adverse pregnancy outcomes with coronary microvascular dysfunction: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Syeda Beenish Bareeqa, Syed Hasham Humayun, Sourbha Satish Dani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAdverse pregnancy outcomes (APOs) are recognized cardiovascular risk enhancers, yet their association with coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) has not been systematically quantified.ObjectiveTo evaluate the association between APOs and CMD and to provide pooled estimates of CMD prevalence and severity in women with APO history.MethodsPubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science were searched through December 2025. Observational studies evaluating coronary microvascular function using PET, Doppler echocardiography, stress CMR, or invasive physiology in women with APOs versus controls were included. Multivariate random-effects meta-analysis was performed to account for non-independent effect estimates arising from shared control groups. Risk of bias was assessed using NOS; certainty of evidence using GRADE.ResultsFive studies (1,183 participants; 583 APO, 600 controls) were included. Women with APO history demonstrated significantly reduced CFR (SMD −0.94; 95% CI, −1.43 to −0.45; p<0.0001; I²=88.2%) and elevated CMD prevalence (OR 6.74; 95% CI, 1.55–29.42; p0.011; I²=92.2%). An exploratory graded pattern was observed, with combined preeclampsia and gestational diabetes associated with the largest CFR reduction (SMD −1.27); however, this was driven primarily by a single study with shared controls. Publication bias assessment was non-significant for CFR (p0.312) but borderline for CMD prevalence (p0.078).ConclusionsAPOs are associated with reduced CFR and elevated CMD prevalence, suggesting CMD may represent a plausible intermediate phenotype linking pregnancy complications to long-term cardiovascular disease. Given low to very low certainty evidence and substantial heterogeneity, these findings are hypothesis-generating and support further prospective research.Keywords: Adverse pregnancy outcomes (APOs), Coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD), Pre-eclampsia, Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM)

Introduction

Adverse pregnancy outcomes (APOs), including preeclampsia, eclampsia, gestational hypertension, gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM), preterm delivery, placental abruption, small-for-gestational-age (SGA) birth, and stillbirth, complicate an estimated 10% to 20% of pregnancies worldwide [1,2]. The American Heart Association (AHA) has formally recognized APOs as sex-specific risk-enhancing factors for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD), recommending their systematic incorporation into cardiovascular risk assessment for all women [3,4].

Coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) encompasses both endothelial-dependent and endothelial-independent microvascular dysfunction and is believed to account for at least half of ANOCA/INOCA cases. However, ANOCA/INOCA also includes epicardial vasospasm as a distinct mechanism. CMD is typically defined by impaired coronary flow reserve (CFR <2.0 or <2.5, depending on modality), reduced coronary flow velocity reserve (CFVR <2.0 or <2.5), or elevated index of microcirculatory resistance (IMR ≥25) [4]. CMD carries a nearly 4-fold increase in mortality (OR 3.93; 95% CI, 2.91–5.30) and a 5-fold increase in major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE; OR 5.16; 95% CI, 2.81–9.47) compared with patients without CMD [5].

Despite the established link between APOs and long-term cardiovascular disease, the specific association between APOs and CMD has not been systematically quantified. This systematic review and meta-analysis were conducted to evaluate the association between APOs and CMD, assessed by validated modalities, and to provide pooled quantitative estimates of CMD prevalence and severity in women with APO history.

Importantly, the relationship between APOs and CMD may not be unidirectional. Pre-existing endothelial or microvascular dysfunction may predispose women both to APOs and to later CMD, such that APOs and CMD reflect shared vascular vulnerability rather than a direct causal sequence. Endothelial dysfunction has been demonstrated in women with prior preeclampsia years after delivery, independent of traditional cardiovascular risk factors, suggesting that novel maternal factors may contribute to both conditions. This concept of shared vascular susceptibility has important implications for interpreting the association between APOs and CMD and is considered further in the Discussion section.

Methods

Protocol and registration

This systematic review and meta-analysis were conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines. The protocol was registered prospectively in the PROSPERO database (CRD420261347645).

Eligibility criteria

The population comprised adult women (≥18 years) with a history of pregnancy. The exposure was adverse pregnancy outcomes (APOs), including preeclampsia, eclampsia, gestational hypertension, gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM), preterm birth, placental abruption, small for gestational age birth, or stillbirth. The comparison group consisted of women with uncomplicated pregnancies. Primary outcomes included coronary flow reserve (CFR), coronary flow velocity reserve (CFVR), myocardial flow reserve (MFR), and CMD prevalence. Secondary outcomes included the index of microcirculatory resistance, diastolic dysfunction, and major adverse cardiovascular events.

Studies were included if they were observational designs (cohort, case-control, or cross-sectional) evaluating coronary microvascular function using validated methods (PET, Doppler echocardiography, stress CMR, or invasive physiology) in women with APOs compared to controls, with quantitative outcome data. CMD was defined according to the diagnostic criteria used in each included study, with the specific threshold and modality detailed in Table 1. Studies were excluded if they were case reports, case series with <10 patients, reviews, conference abstracts without full data, animal studies, or studies assessing only peripheral microvascular function.

Search strategy

PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science were searched from inception through December 2025. English language restrictions were applied. Details of the search combined terms are provided in the supplemental file. Reference lists of included studies were manually searched. PRISMA flow diagram details are provided in Fig. 1.

PRISMA flow diagram detailing study selection process.

Study selection and data extraction

Two reviewers independently screened titles/abstracts and full texts. Data extracted included study characteristics (author, year, country, design), patient characteristics (age, BMI, time since pregnancy, cardiovascular risk factors), APO type, CMD assessment method (modality, stress agent, threshold), and outcomes (CFR/CFVR/MFR values with standard deviations, CMD prevalence). Disagreements were resolved by consensus. Inter-rater reliability for study selection was assessed using Cohen's kappa statistic.

Risk of bias and certainty of evidence

Risk of bias was assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS), evaluating selection, comparability, and outcome domains. Studies scoring 7-9 stars were considered low risk, 4-6 moderate risk, and 0-3 high risk. Certainty of evidence was evaluated using the GRADE approach across five domains: risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias.

Statistical analysis

Multivariate random-effects meta-analysis was performed to account for non-independent effect estimates arising from shared control groups (Kul 2021 contributed three subgroups sharing a single control arm of 80 women). This approach models the covariance between correlated effect estimates and provides valid standard errors and confidence intervals, as recommended in the statistical literature. The multivariate analysis was conducted using the metafor package in R (version 4.5.3).

For continuous outcomes, standardized mean differences (SMD) with 95% confidence intervals were calculated using Hedges' g. For dichotomous outcomes, odds ratios (OR) with 95% CI were calculated. Heterogeneity was assessed using I² statistics, with values of 25%, 50%, and 75% representing low, moderate, and high heterogeneity. Subgroup analyses were performed by APO type and CMD assessment modality. Publication bias was assessed using Egger's regression test (p <0.10 indicating significant asymmetry). However, Egger's test has limited statistical power with fewer than 10 studies, and results should be interpreted with caution. Sensitivity analyses included leave-one-out analysis and comparison of univariate versus multivariate models to assess the impact of accounting for shared control groups. All analyses were performed using RevMan (version 5.4) for primary forest plots and the metafor package in R for multivariate meta-analysis. Statistical significance was defined as p <0.05 for all analyses. All reported p-values were two-sided. To further characterize the uncertainty associated with high between-study heterogeneity, 95% prediction intervals were calculated for the primary meta-analyses using the formula described by IntHout et al. Prediction intervals present the expected range of true effects in similar future studies and provide a more clinically interpretable measure of heterogeneity than I² alone. Prediction intervals were calculated only when at least four comparisons were available.

For Ozyildirim 2023, which reported CFR as a continuous variable without a dichotomous CMD threshold, CMD prevalence was estimated by applying a CFR ≤2.5 cutoff under the assumption of normally distributed CFR values. This approach introduces synthetic prevalence data that may not fully reflect observed clinical outcomes, and the resulting estimates should be interpreted with this methodological limitation in mind. A sensitivity analysis excluding this derived estimate was considered to assess the robustness of the CMD prevalence findings.

Differences between protocol and review

The registered PROSPERO protocol (CRD420261347645) specified DerSimonian-Laird univariate random-effects meta-analysis as the primary statistical method. During the review process, it was identified that Kul 2021 contributed three subgroups (pPE, pGDM, pPE+pGDM) sharing a single control group of 80 women, generating non-independent effect estimates. To address this methodological concern, the primary analysis was changed to a multivariate random-effects meta-analysis using the metafor package in R, which models the covariance between correlated effect estimates and provides valid standard errors and confidence intervals. This decision was made on methodological grounds before conducting the final pooled analysis and was not influenced by knowledge of the direction or magnitude of results. The originally planned univariate analysis was retained as a sensitivity analysis for comparison. The PROSPERO record has been updated to reflect this change. No other deviations from the registered protocol occurred.

Results

Study selection

A total of 847 records were identified, with no additional records from trial registries. After removal of 198 duplicates, 649 records were screened, of which 589 were excluded based on title and abstract (primarily due to irrelevance to the PICO question, review articles, animal studies, and conference abstracts). Sixty full-text articles were assessed for eligibility; 3 reports were not retrieved due to unavailable full text. Of the 57 studies reviewed in full, 52 were excluded for reasons including lack of CMD assessment, absence of a control group, peripheral microvascular focus only, insufficient data, or duplicate cohorts. Ultimately, 5 studies were included in the final analysis, all contributing to the CFR meta-analysis, with 3 additionally included in the CMD prevalence analysis. The details of the search strategy are provided in Fig. 1. Inter-rater reliability for study selection was substantial (Cohen's κ =0.72).

Study characteristics

The five included studies comprised a total of 1,183 participants (583 women with APO history and 600 controls). Studies were published between 2015 and 2024. Study designs included prospective cohorts (n=2) and cross-sectional studies (n=3). CMD assessment modalities varied across studies: two studies used positron emission tomography (PET) to measure CFR or myocardial flow reserve (MFR), while three studies used transthoracic Doppler echocardiography to measure coronary flow velocity reserve (CFVR) or CFR. Stress agents included adenosine, regadenoson, and dipyridamole. The CMD threshold was defined as CFR/CFVR/MFR ≤2.5 in studies reporting prevalence data. Mean age ranged from 34.1 to 55 years in APO groups, and time since pregnancy ranged from during pregnancy (Ozyildirim 2023) to postmenopausal status (Park 2020). Park 2020 used WISE-CVD data (1996–2012, multicenter US), and Countouris 2024 is also a US-based PET study. While these studies differ in enrollment periods, geographic sites, and inclusion criteria, the possibility of minimal participant overlap cannot be entirely excluded, though it is considered unlikely given the distinct study designs and populations. Detailed study characteristics are presented in Table 1. The covariates adjusted for in each study's primary analysis are detailed in Table S8 (Supplemental data); variability in confounder adjustment across studies represents a potential source of residual confounding.

Risk of bias assessment

All five included studies were assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale and demonstrated low risk of bias, with scores ranging from 7 to 9 out of 9 stars (Table S4). The main limitations were the cross-sectional design of three studies, which precluded assessment of temporality, and the relatively small sample size in one study (Countouris 2024, n=30).

Coronary flow reserve

Five studies with eight independent comparisons (accounting for Kul 2021 subgroups) provided data on continuous CFR/CFVR/MFR outcomes. Multivariate meta-analysis accounting for the shared control group in Kul 2021 yielded the following pooled estimates. The pooled analysis demonstrated that women with APO history had significantly lower coronary flow reserve compared to controls (SMD −0.94; 95% CI, −1.43 to −0.45; p<0.0001; I²=88.2%) (Fig. 2). The multivariate model yielded wider confidence intervals and greater heterogeneity than the univariate sensitivity analysis (SMD −0.95, 95% CI −1.34 to −0.55), reflecting the reduced effective sample size after accounting for shared controls in Kul 2021. Heterogeneity was high (I² = 88.2%), reflecting not only statistical variability but also conceptual and biological differences across studies. The included studies combined PET-derived myocardial flow reserve and echocardiographic CFVR/CFR, assessments during active pregnancy (Ozyildirim 2023), early postpartum evaluations (Countouris 2024, 3.5 years), and assessments years to decades after pregnancy (Kul 2021, Caliskan 2015, Park 2020). These populations likely represent fundamentally different physiologic states, from acute hemodynamic and metabolic effects of pregnancy itself to persistent long-term microvascular injury. The pooled estimate should therefore be interpreted as an average effect across heterogeneous populations and methodologies rather than a precise estimate applicable to any specific clinical scenario (Table S1). The 95% prediction interval for the pooled SMD ranged from −2.42 to 0.54, indicating that while the average effect favors reduced CFR in women with APO history, the true effect in a future similar study could potentially include the null or even a small positive value. This wide prediction interval underscores the substantial clinical and methodological heterogeneity across included studies and suggests that the consistency of effect direction (all studies favoring reduced CFR in APO groups) may be more informative than the precise pooled magnitude.

Forest plot demonstrating pooled analysis of coronary flow reserve.

Individual study effect sizes ranged from SMD −0.30 (Park 2020) to SMD −1.57 (Ozyildirim 2023). The largest reductions in CFR were observed in studies assessing women during or shortly after pregnancy with GDM (Ozyildirim 2023: SMD −1.57, 95% CI −2.10 to −1.04; Caliskan 2015: SMD −1.42, 95% CI −1.76 to −1.08). Among the Kul 2021 subgroups, a pattern consistent with graded association was observed: women with combined past preeclampsia and GDM showed the greatest reduction in CFR (SMD −1.27, 95% CI −1.62 to −0.91), followed by GDM alone (SMD −0.91, 95% CI −1.25 to −0.58) and preeclampsia alone (SMD −0.71, 95% CI −1.04 to −0.38). However, this gradient derives from a single study with shared controls, and therefore should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory of a graded association (Table S1).

Subgroup analysis by CMD assessment modality

Subgroup analysis by imaging modality revealed that studies using echocardiography-based assessment (Kul 2021, Caliskan 2015, Ozyildirim 2023) demonstrated a larger pooled effect size (SMD −1.05, 95% CI −1.35 to −0.75, I² = 58.2%) compared to studies using PET-based assessment (Park 2020, Countouris 2024), which showed a more modest effect (SMD −0.42, 95% CI −0.68 to −0.16, I² = 12.4%). This difference may reflect methodological variations in flow reserve measurement or differences in patient populations and timing of assessment relative to pregnancy. Notably, PET-derived myocardial flow reserve and echocardiographic CFVR are not interchangeable measurements. PET is considered the noninvasive reference standard for CMD assessment, providing quantitative global myocardial blood flow measurements across all coronary territories simultaneously. In contrast, transthoracic Doppler echocardiography measures coronary flow velocity (not absolute blood flow) and is typically limited to the left anterior descending artery territory, assuming this is representative of global microvascular function. The iPOWER study demonstrated only modest agreement between CFVR and PET-derived myocardial blood flow reserve (Pearson's r = 0.36), with systematically higher values obtained by PET [6]. These methodological differences in diagnostic performance, physiological interpretation, and clinical applicability may contribute to the observed divergence in effect sizes between modality subgroups and represent an important source of heterogeneity in this meta-analysis.

Subgroup analysis by APO type

Subgroup analysis by APO type demonstrated consistent reductions in CFR across all APO categories. Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) showed the largest pooled effect (SMD −1.27, 95% CI −1.66 to −0.87), followed by combined preeclampsia and GDM (SMD −1.27, 95% CI −1.62 to −0.91). Preeclampsia alone demonstrated a moderate reduction in CFR (SMD −0.67, 95% CI −0.97 to −0.37). Mixed APO populations showed the smallest effect (SMD −0.30, 95% CI −0.50 to −0.09). Overall, these findings suggest an exploratory pattern in which metabolic and combined APO phenotypes may be associated with greater impairment in coronary microvascular function compared to hypertensive disorders alone. However, this apparent gradient is largely driven by a single study (Kul 2021) that shares controls and overlaps with other subgroups. These findings should therefore remain clearly framed as exploratory and hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory of a dose-response relationship, and require validation in independent cohorts. Details of the analysis are provided in the supplementary Table S8 and Fig. S1.

CMD prevalence

Three studies with five independent comparisons provided dichotomous data on CMD prevalence using a threshold of CFR/CFVR ≤2.5. Multivariate meta-analysis accounting for the shared control group in Kul 2021 yielded the following pooled estimates. The pooled analysis demonstrated that women with APO history had significantly higher odds of CMD compared to controls (OR 6.74; 95% CI, 1.55–29.42; p0.011; I²=92.2%) (Fig. 3). At this level of heterogeneity (I² = 92.2%), the pooled estimate should be interpreted with substantial caution. The 95% prediction interval for the pooled OR ranged from 0.18 to 252.4, reflecting extreme uncertainty in the expected effect in any individual future study and further underscoring the limitations of the small number of contributing studies and high heterogeneity.

Forest plot demonstrating pooled CMD prevalence.

CMD prevalence in APO groups ranged from 41.3% to 68.8%, compared with 5.3% to 31.7% in control groups. The highest prevalence was observed in women with combined prior preeclampsia and GDM (Kul 2021 pPE+pGDM: 68.8% vs. 8.8% in controls; OR 22.94, 95% CI 9.19–57.28). Elevated CMD prevalence was also observed in women with prior GDM alone (Kul 2021 pGDM: 47.5% vs. 8.8%; OR 9.43; Caliskan 2015: 49.5% vs. 5.3%; OR 17.62) and prior preeclampsia alone (Kul 2021 pPE: 41.3% vs. 8.8%; OR 7.32). In contrast, the Park 2020 study, which included a mixed APO population in a postmenopausal cohort, demonstrated a more modest association (44.1% vs. 31.7%; OR 1.70, 95% CI 1.14–2.54) (Table S2). Park 2020 differs substantially from the other included studies because it involves a symptomatic WISE-CVD referral cohort with high baseline CMD prevalence in controls (31.7%). This elevated control event rate reflects the symptomatic referral population rather than community-based prevalence and biases the OR toward the null, partially explaining the more modest association observed in this study compared with the other included studies. Overall, these findings suggest an association between APO history and higher CMD prevalence, although estimates varied across studies and populations.

Subgroup analysis by APO type for CMD prevalence

Among studies reporting CMD prevalence, subgroup analysis demonstrated that the magnitude of association varied by APO type. Combined preeclampsia and GDM was associated with the highest odds of CMD (OR 22.94, 95% CI 9.25–56.9), followed by gestational diabetes mellitus alone (OR 12.92, 95% CI 6.58–25.34). Preeclampsia alone was associated with a more moderate increase in CMD prevalence (OR 7.32, 95% CI 2.99–17.91). In contrast, mixed APO populations demonstrated a comparatively smaller association (OR 1.70, 95% CI 1.20–2.42). Overall, these findings suggest a pattern in which metabolic and combined APO phenotypes are associated with greater odds of CMD compared with hypertensive disorders alone. However, these subgroup estimates are derived from a limited number of studies, with some categories represented by single comparisons, and should therefore be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory of a graded relationship. Details of the analysis are provided in the supplementary Table S10 and Fig. S2.

Publication bias assessment

Publication bias was assessed using Egger's regression test for both meta-analyses. For the CFR continuous outcome meta-analysis, Egger's test yielded an intercept of −2.14 (SE 1.87) with t = −1.14 and p = 0.312, indicating no significant funnel plot asymmetry and low concern for publication bias. Visual inspection of the funnel plot demonstrated a symmetric distribution of studies around the pooled effect estimate.

For the CMD prevalence meta-analysis, Egger's test yielded an intercept of −3.42 (SE 1.56) with t = −2.19 and p = 0.078. While this did not reach conventional statistical significance (p < 0.10), the borderline result suggests potential publication bias or small-study effects. Visual inspection of the funnel plot revealed mild asymmetry, with smaller studies tending to report larger effect sizes. However, interpretation is limited by the small number of studies (n = 5 comparisons), as Egger's test has reduced power with fewer than 10 studies. Details are provided in the supplemental document (Table S5).

Sensitivity analysis

Univariate meta-analysis showed a significant association between APO history and coronary microvascular dysfunction. For CFR, the pooled standardized mean difference was −0.95 (95% CI −1.34 to −0.55; I² = 88.2%), while CMD prevalence showed a pooled odds ratio of 8.41 (95% CI 2.54–27.83; I² = 92.2%) (Table S7). Sensitivity analyses demonstrated a consistent direction of effect for CFR, with pooled SMD estimates ranging from −0.81 to −1.14 across leave-one-out analyses, all remaining statistically significant. For CMD prevalence, the pooled OR varied depending on the study excluded, ranging from 4.23 to 13.96. Notably, exclusion of Caliskan 2015 yielded a pooled OR of 4.23 (95% CI 0.66–27.10), with the confidence interval crossing 1.0 and the association no longer reaching statistical significance. This indicates that the CMD prevalence finding is fragile and dependent on the inclusion of all contributing studies.

Certainty of evidence

The certainty of evidence was assessed using the GRADE framework (Table S3). For the CFR continuous outcome, the evidence was rated as LOW certainty. For the CMD prevalence outcome, the evidence was rated as VERY LOW certainty.

Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis is the first to comprehensively examine the association between adverse pregnancy outcomes and coronary microvascular dysfunction. Our findings demonstrate that women with APO history have significantly reduced coronary flow reserve (SMD −0.94) and markedly elevated CMD prevalence (OR 6.74) compared to controls.

Comparison with existing evidence

Our findings extend the established literature linking APO to cardiovascular risk. Prior meta-analyses demonstrated that preeclampsia confers approximately 2-fold increased risk of ischemic heart disease (RR 2.16, 95% CI 1.86–2.52) [7], with similar associations reported for gestational diabetes mellitus (RR 1.98, 95% CI 1.57–2.50) [8]. However, these studies focused predominantly on macrovascular outcomes such as myocardial infarction and stroke.

Our analysis reveals that CMD, increasingly recognized as a precursor to overt cardiovascular disease, is substantially more prevalent in women with APO history, with effect sizes exceeding those reported for macrovascular endpoints. The odds ratio of 6.74 for CMD prevalence in our analysis is notably higher than the 2-fold risk increases typically reported for hard cardiovascular outcomes, suggesting microvascular dysfunction may represent an early and sensitive marker of cardiovascular risk in this population [9].

The 2021 American Heart Association scientific statement formally recognized adverse pregnancy outcomes as sex-specific cardiovascular risk enhancers, recommending their incorporation into risk assessment algorithms [1]. Our findings provide mechanistic support for this recommendation by demonstrating that APOs are associated with measurable abnormalities in coronary microvascular function years to decades after the index pregnancy.

The concept of pregnancy as a cardiovascular "stress test" has been previously proposed, suggesting that pregnancy complications unmask underlying vascular vulnerability [10]. Our data support this hypothesis, demonstrating persistent microvascular abnormalities that may represent the pathophysiological link between APO and subsequent cardiovascular events.

The echocardiography subgroup (Kul 2021, Caliskan 2015, Ozyildirim 2023) comprised younger women assessed during or shortly after pregnancy, whereas the PET subgroup (Park 2020, Countouris 2024) included older women assessed years to decades postpartum. These groups may be answering fundamentally different biological questions: the former may capture acute hemodynamic and metabolic effects of pregnancy itself (including increased cardiac output and altered vascular resistance), while the latter may reflect lasting microvascular injury. The divergence in effect sizes between modality subgroups (SMD −1.05 vs. −0.42) likely reflects this population-level difference more than methodological variation alone. Accordingly, the pooled estimate across all studies should be interpreted as an average across distinct clinical scenarios rather than a single generalizable effect.

Graded association across APO types

The exploratory pattern observed across APO types provides hypothesis-generating insight but must be interpreted with important caveats. Combined preeclampsia and GDM was associated with the highest odds of CMD (OR 22.94, 95% CI 9.25–56.9), followed by gestational diabetes mellitus alone (OR 12.92, 95% CI 6.58–25.34). This gradient is consistent with possible additive effects of metabolic and hypertensive pregnancy complications on coronary microvascular function. Critically, this gradient derives primarily from a single study (Kul 2021) with shared controls and overlapping subgroups. The apparent dose-response pattern, therefore, cannot be considered confirmatory and requires replication in independent cohorts with adequate sample sizes and distinct control groups before a true graded relationship can be established.

Preeclampsia is characterized by abnormal placentation, angiogenic imbalance, and widespread endothelial dysfunction. The elevated soluble fms-like tyrosine kinase-1 (sFlt-1) and reduced placental growth factor cause vascular injury that may persist beyond pregnancy [11,12]. Gestational diabetes mellitus involves insulin resistance, beta-cell dysfunction, and chronic low-grade inflammation, which are associated with endothelial dysfunction and oxidative stress. Women with previous GDM demonstrate persistent markers of cardiovascular risk, including endothelial dysfunction and increased arterial stiffness [13]. The observed association with combined preeclampsia and GDM may reflect convergent pathways of endothelial injury, metabolic dysregulation, and inflammation affecting the coronary microvasculature through distinct but overlapping mechanisms.

Clinical implications

CMD represents a potentially modifiable intermediate phenotype amenable to early intervention. Studies have demonstrated that reduced coronary flow reserve predicts adverse cardiovascular outcomes in women with suspected ischemia, with impaired CFR associated with 2.5-fold increased risk of cardiovascular events. CMD is highly prevalent among patients with chest pain and non-obstructive coronary arteries, affecting 50-65% of cases with higher prevalence in women [14].

While the effect sizes in this analysis are notable, the low to very low certainty of evidence, observational study designs, and substantial heterogeneity preclude definitive recommendations regarding targeted CMD screening at this time. Rather, these findings are primarily hypothesis-generating and intended to support future mechanistic and longitudinal research that could ultimately inform screening strategies. Current guidelines recommend recognition of APO as a cardiovascular risk factor, and the ESC guidelines recommend regular cardiovascular risk assessment in women with a history of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy or gestational diabetes [15,16].

Should future prospective studies confirm these associations and demonstrate that CMD mediates the pathway between APOs and cardiovascular events, integration of APO history with CMD assessment could potentially enable more precise risk stratification in women currently underserved by traditional risk prediction tools. However, such implementation would require evidence from longitudinal studies demonstrating clinical benefit and cost-effectiveness analyses to inform healthcare policy. Women demonstrate a higher prevalence of CMD compared with men, and impaired CFR is associated with worse outcomes in women, highlighting the importance of sex-specific approaches to cardiovascular risk assessment [17].

Shared vascular vulnerability versus direct causal pathway

A key unresolved question is whether APOs independently contribute to CMD development or whether APOs simply identify women with underlying cardiometabolic risk profiles predisposed to CMD. Evidence suggests that pre-existing endothelial or microvascular dysfunction may predispose women both to APOs and to later CMD. Chambers et al. demonstrated that flow-mediated dilation is reduced in women with prior preeclampsia years after delivery, independent of traditional risk factors, suggesting that novel maternal factors contribute to endothelial dysfunction [18]. Stuart et al. reported that endothelial dysfunction may serve as a shared risk factor for both preeclampsia and coronary artery disease via inadequate vascularization of the uterus during pregnancy and of the myocardium later in life [19]. Lane-Cordova et al. described vascular endothelial dysfunction during APOs similar to that occurring in cardiac microvascular dysfunction and HFpEF [20]. Khan et al. noted that it remains unclear whether APOs reflect latent cardiovascular risk unmasked in pregnancy or are themselves independent causal risk factors [21]. APOs and CMD may therefore reflect shared vascular vulnerability rather than a direct causal sequence, and the observational design of all included studies precludes distinguishing between these possibilities. Future studies incorporating pre-pregnancy vascular assessments and longitudinal follow-up are needed to clarify the directionality of this association.

Heterogeneity

A range of demographic, cardiometabolic, reproductive, lifestyle, and treatment-related factors were identified as potential confounders in the association between adverse pregnancy outcomes and coronary microvascular dysfunction. Key variables such as maternal age, BMI, hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia were consistently associated with increased CMD risk, while factors like smoking and physical activity influenced coronary flow reserve. Temporal factors (time since delivery), APO severity, and medication use may further modify observed associations. However, reporting of several confounders, particularly socioeconomic status, physical activity, and medication use, was inconsistent across studies, highlighting potential residual confounding and the need for more standardized adjustment in future research. The details of confounder adjustment are provided in the supplemental file (Table S9).

Limitations

Several limitations warrant consideration. All included studies are observational, predominantly cross-sectional, which limits causal inference and temporality assessment. Residual confounding remains a major concern, particularly given differences in BMI, hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, and smoking prevalence between APO and control groups. It cannot be determined from the available evidence whether APOs cause CMD or whether both conditions share common underlying risk factors. First, the observational design and variable adjustment across studies limit causal inference, and residual confounding—particularly from cardiometabolic risk factors such as BMI, hypertension, and diabetes—cannot be excluded. Second, substantial heterogeneity (I² >70%) likely reflects differences in populations, timing of assessment, and methodologies; thus, pooled estimates represent average effects rather than precise clinical estimates, although the consistent direction of association is notable. Third, the small number of studies limited power for subgroup and publication bias analyses, with borderline evidence of small-study effects for CMD prevalence (Egger’s p0.078). Fourth, variability in CMD assessment methods (PET vs echocardiographic CFVR) and inconsistent diagnostic thresholds introduce additional heterogeneity. Fifth, the observed gradient across APO subtypes is driven largely by a single study with shared controls and requires independent confirmation. Finally, potential minimal cohort overlap and the predominance of cross-sectional designs limit assessment of temporality, precluding conclusions on the sequence of APO and CMD development.

Park 2020 draws from the WISE-CVD cohort, comprising women with signs and symptoms of ischemia referred for angiography rather than a general APO population. The 31.7% CMD prevalence in their control group reflects this symptomatic referral cohort and is substantially higher than expected community-based prevalence, which inflates the control event rate and biases the OR toward the null. Additionally, the APO group in this study had high rates of hypertension (42%) and diabetes (12%), confounders that independently cause CMD and may partially account for the observed association.

Additionally, the very small number of included studies (n = 5, with only 1,183 total participants and only three studies contributing to the CMD prevalence analysis) substantially limits statistical power, precision of pooled estimates, robustness of subgroup analyses, and reliability of publication bias assessment. The sensitivity analysis demonstrating loss of statistical significance for CMD prevalence upon exclusion of a single study (Caliskan 2015) further highlights the fragility of these findings.

Future directions

Longitudinal studies with serial CMD assessment are needed to characterize the trajectory of microvascular dysfunction following APO and determine whether abnormalities are present immediately postpartum or develop over time. Clinical trials evaluating targeted interventions, including intensive lifestyle modification, statins, ACE inhibitors, and novel therapies in women with APO history and documented CMD, are warranted.

Studies should evaluate whether CMD mediates the association between APO and subsequent cardiovascular events, and whether improvement in CMD translates to reduced cardiovascular risk. Development of standardized protocols for CMD screening in women with APO history would facilitate clinical implementation. Finally, cost-effectiveness analyses are needed to inform healthcare policy regarding CMD screening in this population.

Conclusion

Women with a history of adverse pregnancy outcomes demonstrate significantly impaired coronary microvascular function, evidenced by reduced coronary flow reserve and markedly increased odds of coronary microvascular dysfunction. These findings, based on low to very low certainty evidence from a small number of observational studies, support the concept that APOs may reflect early vascular vulnerability rather than isolated obstetric events, although a shared underlying predisposition cannot be excluded. The observed associations were consistent across studies, although substantial heterogeneity and limited sample size warrant cautious interpretation. A potential graded pattern, with higher risk in combined metabolic and hypertensive APO phenotypes, remains hypothesis-generating. Collectively, these results suggest that coronary microvascular dysfunction may serve as an intermediate phenotype linking pregnancy complications to future cardiovascular disease. These results are primarily hypothesis-generating and intended to support future mechanistic and longitudinal research. Further prospective studies with serial CMD assessment, pre-pregnancy vascular phenotyping, and standardized assessment protocols are needed to clarify causality, determine the temporal trajectory of microvascular dysfunction following APOs, and evaluate whether targeted CMD screening and early intervention improve cardiovascular outcomes in this population.

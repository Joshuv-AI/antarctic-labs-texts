Enhancing Mycoplasma synoviae control: A novel quantitative real-time PCR approach to differentiate MS-H vaccine strain and field strains
Authors: Mengyao Chen, Mengjiao Guo, Chengcheng Zhang, Zongyi Bo, Yantao Wu, Xiaorong Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMycoplasma synoviae (MS) is a prevalent pathogen in poultry, causing substantial economic losses. Vaccination is a crucial strategy for controlling MS infections. In China, a temperature-sensitive live vaccine, Vaxsafe®MS (MS-H strain), was introduced to address the rising prevalence of MS since 2017. Accurate differentiation between the MS-H vaccine strain and field strains is essential for the effective application of the vaccine and the eradication of MS in poultry. In this study, a duplex TaqMan real-time quantitative PCR (qPCR) method was first developed based on a single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) at position 367 of the obg gene. However, some reisolated strains of the MS-H vaccine strain may exhibit reverse mutations at this site, potentially leading to false-positive results field strains detection. To address this issue, a comprehensive comparison of 24 MS genomes was conducted, identifying 18 SNPs unique to the MS-H lineage. Subsequently, an (A/G) SNP at position 131 of the MSH_02330 gene was selected through sequencing. Based on this SNP, a novel duplex qPCR method was established, characterized by high sensitivity, specificity, and repeatability. This method eliminates the influence of obg revertant strains, enabling accurate differentiation of the MS-H vaccine strain from MS field strains. It can be applied to both pure cultures and clinical samples, serving as a valuable tool for evaluating the immunogenicity of the MS-H vaccine and advancing the prevention and control of MS infections.Keywords: Mycoplasma synoviae, Single nucleotide polymorphism, MS-H live vaccine, DIVA, qPCR

Introduction

Mycoplasma synoviae (MS) is a significant pathogen in poultry, responsible for infections that result in substantial economic losses due to decreased productivity and increased mortality. It primarily affects chickens and turkeys, leading to conditions such as airsacculitis, infectious synovitis, and eggshell apex abnormalities (Cisneros-Tamayo et al., 2020a; Liu et al., 2021). MS can be transmitted vertically through hatching eggs or horizontally via aerosols, complicating its elimination once established within a flock (Ter Veen et al., 2020). The infection typically results in diminished feed efficiency, stunted growth, elevated culling rates, and reduced egg production (Feberwee et al., 2022). Furthermore, MS frequently co-infects with other pathogens, including the avian influenza virus and the infectious bronchitis virus, exacerbating symptoms and increasing mortality rates (Bolha et al., 2013; Landman and Feberwee, 2004; Sid et al., 2015).

A 2022 study indicated that the global pooled prevalence of MS is 38.4 % (Chaidez-Ibarra et al., 2022). In China, the incidence of MS infection has significantly increased since 2010, affecting various types of chickens (Sun et al., 2017a, 2017b; Xue et al., 2017). The primary strategies for controlling MS infection include medication, vaccination and eradication. The cost of eradication is relatively high and difficult to achieve in the short term. Due to the intensification of food safety concerns and bacterial resistance (Zhang et al., 2022), the use of antibiotics has been strictly restricted, making vaccination a crucial approach (Gong et al., 2020). The approved MS vaccines in China include the temperature-sensitive live vaccine Vaxsafe®MS (MS-H strain) and an inactivated vaccine.

The MS-H vaccine, derived from the Australian field strain 86079/7NS (Morrow et al., 1998), is widely used globally due to its ability to outcompete field strains and elicit a strong mucosal immune response (Zhu et al., 2018). The effect of the vaccine strain is predicated on its successful colonization of the respiratory mucosa and its competitive exclusion of field strains, a process that usually occurs alongside the coexistence of both field and vaccine strains. Therefore, developing a discriminatory detection method to differentiate between vaccine and field strains is essential for evaluating vaccine efficacy and eliminating MS (Moronato et al., 2018). This method can assess the colonization effect of vaccines, accurately monitor the pressure of field strains of infection, and identify the currently dominant strains, providing critical evidence for the formulation of subsequent immunization and control plans.

Traditional methods for differentiating the MS-H vaccine strain from field strains include bacterial isolation, sequencing of target genes, PCR-restriction fragment length polymorphism (PCR-RFLP), and multi-locus sequence typing (MLST) (El-Gazzar et al., 2017; Ghanem and El-Gazzar, 2018; Limpavithayakul et al., 2024; Ogino et al., 2011). However, these methods are often time-consuming and labor-intensive. Newer methods based on specific genetic markers, such as single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), have been developed to address these limitations. In this study, we screened for SNP sites between the MS-H vaccine strain and field strains, and developed a novel duplex qPCR method to accurately differentiate between them.

Materials and methods

Mycoplasma strains

The MS strain WVU 1853 (Accession Number: CVCC385) and Mycoplasma gallisepticum (MG) R strain (Accession Number: CVCC1652) were procured from the China Veterinary Microbial Culture Collection and Management Center. The temperature-sensitive live vaccine Vaxsafe®MS (MS-H strain) was obtained from Australian BioResources Pty Ltd. The MS isolates Shandong/2017-2, NingXia/2017-1, and Hebei/2018-1 have been maintained in our laboratory (Zhang et al., 2021).

Samples and genomic DNA extraction

A total of 143 cleft palate swab samples were randomly collected from three breeder farms located in Hebei and Jiangsu provinces of China, where the chickens had been administered the MS-H vaccine at three weeks of age. At the time of sampling, the chickens exhibited no clinical signs of respiratory disease or synovitis, and detailed flock information is provided in Supplementary Table 4. DNA was extracted from the swabs and strains using the boiling method. Specifically, the samples were centrifuged, and the supernatants were discarded. The precipitates were then resuspended, boiled, and the supernatants containing DNA were collected.

Establishment of the duplex qPCR assay based on obg gene

The genomes of the MS-H vaccine strain (CP021129.1) and its parental strain 86079/7NS (CP012624.1) were obtained from GenBank to develop probes MS367-PW/PV and primers MS-uni-F312/R432 for differentiating the SNP at position 367 of the obg gene between the MS-H vaccine strain and field strains (Table 1). Primers and probes were synthesized by Nanjing GenScript Biotechnology Co., Ltd. The genomic DNA of the WVU 1853 strain and the MS-H vaccine strain served as templates to amplify the obg gene using the obg-comF and obg-comR2 primers (Fig. 1) (Shahid et al., 2013). The amplification product was recovered, ligated into the pEASY-Blunt Simple vector (TransGene, Beijing, China), and transformed into Trans1-T1 competent Escherichia coli (TransGene, Beijing, China). Positive transformants were identified by PCR and then sequenced at Shanghai Sangon Biotechnology. The copy number of the two plasmids was calculated using the formula: N = (C × 10-9)/(M × 660) × 6.02 × 1023, where C represents the concentration of the recombinant plasmid and M represents the number of bases in the recombinant plasmid. The optimal concentrations of the two probes and primers were determined using the matrix method. The total reaction volume was 20 μL, comprising of 2 μL of genomic DNA or standard, 10 μL of 2 × qPCR Probe Master Mix (Vazyme, Nanjing, China), 200 nM of MS-uni-F312/MS-uni-R432 primers, 200 nM of probe MS367-PW, and 100 nM of probe MS367-PV. The reaction was conducted in a Bioer 9600 PLUS system (Bioer Technology, Hangzhou, China) under the following conditions: 95 °C for 5 min, followed by 45 cycles of 95 °C for 10 s and 60 °C for 30 s, and then held at 60 °C for 30 s to collect the signal.

Sensitivity, reproducibility and specificity of the duplex qPCR assay based on obg gene

To determine the detection limit and establish a standard curve, serial dilutions of two recombinant plasmids were detected, ranging from 1 × 100 copies/μL to 1 × 108 copies/μL. Intra-assay and inter-assay evaluations were performed using concentrations of 1 × 105 copies/μL, 1 × 106 copies/μL, and 1 × 107 copies/μL, with each sample analyzed in triplicate to assess repeatability. The coefficient of variation (CV) for Cq values was calculated for both intra-assays and inter-assays. Mixed templates of WVU 1853 and MS-H genomic DNA were prepared in varying ratios (Table 3) to evaluate the interference of these mixed templates on detection. Genomic DNA from the MS-H vaccine strain, WVU 1853 strain, MG R strain, MS Shandong/2017-2 strain, MS Ningxia/2017-1 strain, and MS Hebei/2018-1 strain was utilized to verify specificity.

Screening for SNP sites other than the obg gene

In order to avoid false positives of field strains caused by obg gene recovery mutations, a comparison was conducted between the 30 SNPs present in 23 MS genomes and the MS-H reference strain (Supplementary Tables 1 and 2). This analysis revealed 18 unique SNPs specific to the MS-H lineage. Four specific SNP sites, located at positions 14181 nt, 481287 nt, 498421 nt, and 522899 nt in the MS-H reference genome (CP021129.1), were selected based on their high GC content and conservation within the sequence. Primers 14181-F/R, 481287-F/R, 498421-F/R, and 522899-F/R were designed for the amplification of the conserved regions encompassing these four SNP sites (Table 1). The SNP at position 522899, corresponding to position 131 of the MSH_02330 gene, was selected based on sequencing results.

Establishment of the duplex qPCR assay based on MSH_02330 gene

The TaqMan MGB probes (522899-PW/PV) and primers (522899-F2/R3) were designed to differentiate between field strains and the MS-H vaccine strain based on the (A/G) SNP at position 131 of the MSH_02330 gene (Fig. 2). The specific sequences of the primers and probes are detailed in Table 1. Genomic DNA from the WVU 1853 strain and the MS-H strain was used as templates for PCR amplification with primers 522899-F2 and 522899-R2. The resultant 161 bp fragment, which contains the SNP site was ligated into the pEASY-Blunt Simple vector, and the recombinant plasmid was extracted and quantified. Recombinant plasmids were diluted in a 10-fold gradient to serve as standards. The optimal concentrations of probes and primers were determined using the square matrix method in a 20 μL reaction volume, which consisted of 2 μL of genomic DNA or standard, 10 μL of 2 × qPCR Probe Master Mix, 100 nM of 522899-F2/R3 primers, 200 nM of probe 522899-PW, and 300 nM of probe 522899-PV. The thermal cycling parameters were set to 95 °C for 5 min; followed by 45 cycles of 95 °C for 10 s and 60 °C for 30 s, with signal collection during a 30 s hold at 60 °C.

Alignment of the designed primers and probe with the sequences of the MS strain.

Sensitivity, reproducibility and specificity of the duplex qPCR assay based on MSH_02330 gene

Sensitivity, reproducibility, and specificity analyses were conducted as previously described. Mixed templates of WVU 1853 and MS-H genomic DNA were detected. Reproducibility testing included three plasmid concentrations (1 × 105 to 1 × 107 copies/μL) across three experimental replicates, encompassing both intra-assay and inter-assay evaluations. The specificity of the assay was confirmed using MS and MG strains.

Clinical samples testing

The cleft palate swab samples were initially tested using the qPCR method which targeted the vlhA gene and could detect both field strains and the vaccine strain (Sprygin et al., 2010). Subsequently, further analysis was conducted to differentiate between field strains and the MS-H vaccine strain, employing methods based on SNP sites in the obg gene and the MSH_02330 gene, respectively. To validate the accuracy of these two qPCR methods, PCR amplification was carried out using vlhA genotyping primers Link and MSConsR (Jeffery et al., 2007), and the resulting products were sequenced. The sequences were classified based on the length of the PRR region (Limpavithayakul et al., 2016).

Results

The duplex qPCR assay based on obg gene

The detection limit of the obg qPCR was determined to be 10 copies/μL (Fig. 3A and B). The linear correlation standard curves for detecting the MS-H vaccine strain and MS field strains were represented by the equations y = −3.37x + 39.59 and y = −3.4x + 40.22, respectively. The amplification efficiencies were 97.99 % and 94.45 %, with correlation coefficients (R²) of 0.999 for both assays, indicating a strong linear relationship across the concentration range of 10 copies/μL to 108 copies/μL (Fig. 3C). The four MS field strains were exclusively detected in the FAM channel, while the MS-H vaccine strain was only detected in the VIC channel. No signal was detected in the MG R strain template, confirming the specificity of the assay (Fig. 3D). The repeatability test showed that the CV of Cq values for different plasmid concentrations in both inter-assays and intra-assays was below 1.5 % (Table 2), indicating a high level of repeatability for the method. When the concentration of the MS-H vaccine strain was 8 × 102 copies/μL and the MS field strain was 3 × 105 copies/μL in the mixed sample, this method was still able to accurately quantify both simultaneously. Selection of the SNP

The size of the amplified candidate SNP fragment product was consistent with expectations, and sequencing results indicated that the SNPs at positions 14181, 522899, and 481287 were specific to the MS-H lineage. The nucleotide at position 498421 was a C in both the MS-H vaccine strain and Shandong/2017-2, which was not unique to MS-H. Additionally, the presence of an (A/C) SNP at position 481286 suggested a lack of sequence conservation near the SNP at position 481287 (Fig. 4). The SNP at position 14181 did not alter the amino acid polarity (Val→Ile), whereas the SNP at position 522899 resulted in a transition from a non-polar to an acidic amino acid (Gly→Glu). This transition could potentially impact the secondary structure of the protein, making the SNP at position 522899, located within the MSH_02330 gene (position 131), a candidate for the development of the duplex qPCR detection method.

The duplex qPCR assay based on MSH_02330 gene

The detection limit of the MSH_02330 gene duplex qPCR method was established at 10 copies/μL for both the MS-H vaccine strain and MS field strains (Fig. 5A and B). The standard curve for the MS-H vaccine strain, generated within a plasmid concentration range of 10 copies/μL to 108 copies/μL, exhibited an amplification efficiency of 99.44 % and a correlation coefficient of 0.999, described by the equation y = −3.34x + 39.72. Similarly, the standard curve for the MS field strains was represented by the equation y = −3.34x + 38.45, demonstrating an amplification efficiency of 99.22 % and a correlation coefficient of 0.999 (Fig. 5C). Specificity tests confirmed the anticipated results, with genomic DNA from the MS field strains yielding positive signals in the FAM channel and negative signals in the VIC channel, and vice versa. No signal was detected in the MG R strain sample (Fig. 5D). Repeatability tests revealed that the CV of Cq values for both inter-assay and intra-assay detection of varying concentrations of recombinant plasmids using this method was below 2.5 % (Table 4). Moreover, experiments involving single and mixed templates indicated that high-concentration plasmids in a mixed template could inhibit the amplification of low-concentration plasmids, suggesting competition for the low-concentration template (Table 5).

Comparison of two duplex qPCR methods

Out of 143 clinical samples, 93 tested positive for MS, with 79 samples successfully sequenced (Fig. 6). The obg gene duplex qPCR method revealed that 32 samples contained MS field strains, while 54 samples contained the MS-H vaccine strain. Another method, based on the MSH_02330 gene, identified 59 samples as the MS-H vaccine strain and 20 samples as MS field strains. Sequencing of the vlhA gene classified 59 samples as type C (with sequences completely identical to the MS-H vaccine strain) and 20 samples as type L (corresponding to MS field strains). Notably, the vlhA genotyping results matched the detection results from the SNP of the MSH_02330 gene with 100 % accuracy (Supplementary Table 5). The method based on the MSH_02330 gene demonstrated 100 % accuracy in identifying both the MS field strains and the MS-H vaccine strain, surpassing the 93.67 % accuracy of the method based on the obg gene.

Discussion

MS is classified as a reportable pathogen by the World Organisation for Animal Health, holding significant economic value. In recent years, MS has been prevalent in several countries and regions across Asia, Africa, and Europe, with no significant decline in incidence rates (Fagrach et al., 2023; Galluzzo et al., 2022; Giram et al., 2022; Kursa et al., 2024; Rufai et al., 2025; Yin et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025). The MS-H live vaccine has been introduced in many countries for the prevention and control of MS infections. Accurate differentiation between the MS-H vaccine strain and field strains is essential for the effective utilization of the vaccine and the eradication of MS in poultry. However, MS infections in chicken flocks are often subclinical, presenting no obvious clinical symptoms. Therefore, it is more reasonable to assess the immunoprotective effects of MS-H by detecting the colonization of vaccine strains and field strains in the upper respiratory tract of chickens.

Current diagnostic methods for MS-H vaccine strains and field strains primarily focus on the obg and oppF genes, employing techniques such as duplex qPCR, high-resolution melting (HRM) curve analysis, mismatch amplification mutation assays (MAMA), and indirect ELISA (Kordafshari et al., 2019; Kreizinger et al., 2015; Shahid et al., 2014; Zhu et al., 2017). Among these, duplex qPCR targeting the SNP at position 367 of the obg gene is predominant in China. This SNP is associated with the temperature sensitivity of the MS-H vaccine, and duplex qPCR facilitates rapid detection. In 2019, we established the relevant methods, and after testing tens of thousands of clinical samples, we found that clinical applications of the MS-H vaccine have identified revertant mutations in the obg gene. The reversion of the obg gene results in the loss of temperature sensitivity (Klose et al., 2023b), which in turn enhances antibody production and transmissibility (Klose et al., 2023a; Noormohammadi et al., 2003). Although these reversions do not make the MS-H vaccine cause more severe symptoms (Klose et al., 2022), they significantly undermine the accuracy of field strains monitoring.

The MGB probe qPCR method presents several advantages over HRM analysis for genotyping known single SNP sites, including simpler operation, faster turnaround times, and lower technical requirements for personnel. Although cgMLST or NGS-based methods have been reported (Cisneros-Tamayo et al., 2020b; Ghanem and El-Gazzar, 2018) for MS typing, offering high throughput and specificity, they remain cost-prohibitive for large-scale clinical screening. In clinical settings, the duplex MGB probe qPCR method serves as a rapid and economical solution for accurately distinguishing the MS-H vaccine strain from field strains in high-volume testing. In this study, we developed a novel duplex qPCR method based on a SNP at position 131 of the MSH_02330 gene to differentiate the MS-H vaccine strain from field strains. This method demonstrated high sensitivity and repeatability, showed no cross-reaction with MG, with a detection limit of 10 copies/μL. The method effectively eliminated the influence of revertant strains, which has been a challenge in the obg gene duplex qPCR method. The accuracy was validated through comparison with sequencing results of the vlhA gene, showing a 100 % match in identifying the MS-H vaccine strain and field strains. The evaluation of mixed templates indicates that when the ratio of high-concentration target substances to low-concentration target substances exceeds 10:1, the amplification of low-concentration target substances is inhibited. This suggests that precise quantification is not feasible for mixed templates with significant concentration differences; however, this does not undermine the qualitative diagnostic value of the detection method. In a clinical setting, such significant differences in concentration can already reflect the competitive exclusion results between strains. Our method can reliably identify dominant strains, providing evidence for the formulation of subsequent management interventions.

The genomes utilized for SNP screening were obtained from six countries across Asia, North America, South America, Europe, and Australia. This diversity indicates that the SNP of the MSH_02330 gene serves as a stable marker that distinguishes the MS-H vaccine from numerous internationally circulating strains, thereby demonstrating its potential for global applicability. We are actively collaborating with international laboratories to collect relevant clinical samples from different geographical regions, various strains, diverse farms, and different vaccination programs, as well as cases involving multiple infections. This effort aims to comprehensively assess the reliability and applicability of the method across various environments.

The findings of this study hold important implications for the poultry industry. The novel duplex qPCR method developed herein offers a rapid, sensitive, and specific tool for differentiating the MS-H vaccine strain from field strains. In regions with a high reversion rate of the obg gene, this method can replace obg-based qPCR assays. The core technology of this detection method, the SNP genotyping technology based on MGB probes, has become the foundation for successful and standardized commercial kits worldwide, such as TaqMan® SNP Genotyping Assays (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, USA). Given that our detection method based on the MSH_02330 gene exhibits high stability and high specificity, it also has the potential to be developed into a high-throughput platform and commercial kits. These commercial kits can be used to monitor the colonization of both vaccine and field strains in MS-H vaccinated chickens, providing valuable data for optimizing vaccination strategies and eliminating MS infections, ultimately contributing to reduced economic losses and enhanced biosecurity in the poultry industry.

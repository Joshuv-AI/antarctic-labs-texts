TGF-β1/miR-30d-5p axis regulating the expression of EMT key factors to induce apoptosis of lung cancer cells
Authors: Qigang Zeng, Wenjie Shi, Longyun Xie, Yong Dai, Chenggong Wei
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposePrevious studies have reported that miR-30d-5p gene expression can inhibit tumor proliferation, but its mechanism has not been fully elucidated.MethodsBased on previous findings, TGF-β1 was used to induce epithelial mesenchymal transformation in A549 cells. The expression levels of DUSP-1, E-cadherin and Vimentin were detected by Western blot. mRNA expression levels of DUSP-1, E-cadherin and Vimentin were determined by RT-qPCR. The expression level of miR-30d-5p was determined by RT-qPCR.ResultsThe induction effect of TGF-β1 could be reversed by the intervention of miR-30d-5p in A549 cells. miRNA inhibition experiment showed that miR-30d-5p inhibitor could effectively inhibit the expression of miR-30d-5p in A549 cells. After miR-30d-5p intervention, TGF-β1 was reversed on EMT-related genes (Dusp-1, E-cadherin, Vimentin).ConclusionTGF-β1 can induce epithelial mesenchymal transformation of A549 cells, and miR-30d-5p may be a key regulatory mechanism in regulating gene and protein expression of TGF-β1-mediated DUSP-1, E-cadherin and Vimentin. This provides a new perspective for understanding the anti-tumor effect of miR-30d-5p gene.Keywords: miR-30d-5p gene, Non-small cell lung cancer, Dusp-1, e-cadherin, Vimentin, Proliferation, Apoptosis

Introduction

Lung cancer is currently the second most prevalent cancer in the world [1], and about 1.8 million people are diagnosed with lung cancer every year. As the cancer with the highest lethality, its diagnostic technology and treatment methods have made remarkable progress. Still, most patients are in the middle to late stage at diagnosis, when the tumor may have already undergone peripheral tissue infiltration and lymph node metastasis. The five-year survival rate is less than 20 % [2,3]. Currently, the treatment of lung cancer patients mainly relies on surgical resection, but the limited opportunity for early diagnosis leads to a generally poor prognosis [4].

Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) is critical in tumor invasion and metastasis. It promotes cell adhesion loss, increased migration, and enhanced ability to fight apoptosis [5]. Studies have shown that Vimentin [6], Dusp-1, and E-cadherin genes and proteins, as tumor stromal cell marker factors [7,8], undergo expression changes marking the occurrence of the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) process [9]. Lung cancer cells and tissues can promote tumor cells' proliferation, metastasis, and drug resistance under the EMT process [10].

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small, non-coding endogenous RNAs that regulate gene expression at the post-transcriptional level [11], which regulates gene expression and affects cell proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis. In lung cancer, abnormally expressed microRNAs (miRNAs) play an essential role in tumourigenesis, progression, and invasion and serve as potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets [12]. Numerous studies have demonstrated that miRNAs show significant expression differences in tumor tissues and blood of lung cancer patients. This finding provides important clues for early diagnosis and targeted therapy of lung cancer [13,14].

Nowadays, exploring the targeted therapy of lung cancer, which involves regulating a variety of genes and cytokines constituting an intricate signal transduction network between each other and exerting biological effects, is gaining increasing attention [[15], [16], [17]]. To improve the clinical outcomes of lung cancer patients at an early stage and to increase their survival rate, individualized and targeted therapy is one of the hotspots and priorities in the future therapeutic research of lung cancer.

Our previous studies have demonstrated that microRNA-30d-5p is abnormally expressed in a variety of tumors and can act as a tumor suppressor gene to inhibit tumors, especially in normal lung tissues of mice. This gene is highly expressed in normal lung tissues but down-regulated in lung squamous cell carcinoma and other types of non-small cell lung cancer tissues [18].

In this study, we aimed to investigate whether microRNA-30d-5p is involved in inhibiting lung cancer cell proliferation by regulating the expression of Vimentin, Dusp-1, and E-cadherin. Using miRNA inhibiting technology, we detected differences in the expression of key EMT factors in A549 cells with or without microRNA-30d-5p expression and explored the potential anti-tumor effects of microRNA-30d-5p. Our data offer essential insights into understanding the antitumor mechanisms of microRNA-30d-5p.

Experimental methods and materials

Cells and cell cultures

The A549 cells were cultured at 37 °C in a cell incubator with a volume fraction of 5 % CO2 and saturated humidity after being inoculated into DMEM complete medium containing 10 % fetal bovine serum, 100,000 U/L penicillin, and 100 mg/L streptomycin. The medium was replaced every 2 days. Once the cells reached about 80 % growth, they were digested and counted. The cells in the logarithmic growth phase were selected for subsequent experiments.

Preparation of TGF-β1

TGF-β1 was initially prepared as a stock solution (20 μg/mL) in a reconstitution solution containing 8 mm hydrochloric acid and BSA. Subsequently, it was diluted to the required concentration for dosing experiments.

Cell modeling, grouping and administration

The original culture medium was discarded, and the cells were divided into a control group and four treatment groups at different concentrations. The control group received an equal volume of serum-free DMEM medium, while the four treatment groups were individually treated with TGF-β1 serum-free DMEM medium containing concentrations of 1.25 ng/mL, 2.5 ng/mL, 5 ng/mL, and 10 ng/mL for 24 h. Triplicate wells were used in each group. Subsequently, all cells were cultured in a cell incubator set at 37 °C, 5 % CO2, and saturated humidity for 24 h to facilitate the extraction of total RNA and protein.

CCK8 assay for A549 cell survival

Cells in logarithmic growth phase were inoculated into 96-well plates, and then TGF-β1 solution was diluted to four concentrations (1.25 ng/mL, 2.5 ng/mL, 5 ng/mL, and 10 ng/mL) with DMEM medium. Subsequently, considering the necessity of several steps including replacing the cell culture medium, establishing the control group, and introducing varying concentrations of TGF-β1 solution, the culture was continued for 24 h. Following this, CCK8 reagent (5 μL/well) was added, and after a 30-min incubation period, the OD values of each well were detected at a wavelength of 562 nm using a microplate reader. This data was used to calculate the survival rate by dividing the mean OD values of the experimental group by the mean OD values of the control group, and multiplying the result by 100 %.

Etraction of cellular proteins and expression of target proteins

After treatment with TGF-β1 for 24 h, A549 cells in logarithmic growth phase were collected and scraped into 1 ml of RIPA protein lysis buffer. The cell lysate was then incubated at a low temperature for 30 min and subsequently centrifuged at 4 °C, 10,000 rpm/min for 5 min to collect the supernatant. The protein concentration of the supernatant was determined using a BCA protein kit. Following the protein quantification, the samples in all groups were adjusted to the same concentration by adding an appropriate amount of lysate. Subsequently, a 45 μg total protein stock solution was prepared by adding 5x loading buffer, and 20 μl of this solution was loaded onto the gel for the detection of antibodies including Dusp-1, E-cadherin, vimentin, and α-tubulin.

RT-PCR was used to detect the expression of target gene mRNA

A549 cells in logarithmic growth phase were treated with TGF-β1 for 24 h. Following treatment, the cells were collected and 1 mL Trigol reagent was added. Subsequently, the cells were scraped, evenly mixed, and then transferred to a 1.5 mL centrifuge tube. The tube was left at room temperature for 5 min before being subjected to centrifugation at 12,000 rpm/min for 10 min at 4 °C to separate the cell components. The resulting supernatant was carefully removed and transferred to a new 1.5 mL centrifuge tube, to which 1/5 volume of chloroform was added. The mixture was gently shaken for 5 min and then centrifuged at 12,000 rpm/min for an additional 10 min at 4 °C to further isolate the RNA. The supernatant from this step was again transferred to a new 1.5 mL centrifuge tube. Simultaneously, an equal volume of isopropanol was introduced into the tube, and the entire solution was allowed to incubate at 4 °C for 2 h to promote RNA precipitation. If the precipitation was not readily observable, the tube was placed at −20 °C overnight for enhanced RNA precipitation. The next day, the tubes were subjected to high-speed refrigerated centrifugation at 12,000 rpm/min for 10 min. The resulting supernatant was discarded, and the RNA pellet was washed 2–3 times with 500 μL of 75 % ethanol, ensuring thorough removal of cellular debris. After complete volatilization of residual ethanol at room temperature, the precipitate was reconstituted in 200 μL of sterilized ultrapure water. Subsequently, the OD260/OD280 ratio was determined using a nucleic acid protein tester to ascertain the concentration and purity of the extracted RNA.

Reverse transcription was conducted following the experimental protocol included in Takara M-MLV RT Master Mix (Table 1), resulting in the synthesis of first-strand cDNA using the extracted total RNA mentioned above.

MicroRNA detection method

Total RNA was extracted as previously described. Following the manufacturer's instructions, RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA. The next step involved mixing 2 μL cDNA, 9 μL ddH2O, 0.5 μL specific primer, 0.5 μL universal primer Mrq3, 12.5 μL SYBR Green, and 0.5 μL ROX. Subsequently, amplification was carried out using the following conditions: 95 °C for 10s, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 5s, 60 °C for 20s, and 95 °C for 60s, and finally 55 °C for 30s. Gene expression was determined using the 2-ΔΔCt method during the amplification step at 60 °C. The primers used in this study were synthesized by Takara Company, and their sequences can be found in Table 3. The internal reference for gene expression analysis was U6, and the primer for U6 was also provided by Takara Company.

Statistical analysis

The measurement data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (x ± s). ANOVA or Kruskal-Wallis tests were conducted using SPSS 22.0 software to compare the mean values between the two groups.

Results

Effect of TGF-β1 solution on A549 cell growth

Compared with the normal control group, TGF-β1 solution at each concentration did not impact the cell morphology and number of A549 cells. Cell survival rates, as determined by the CCK8 assay, revealed no significant differences between the 1.25–10 ng/ml TGF-β1 solution group and the control group (P > 0.05)(Fig. 1).

The effect of different concentrations of TGF-β1 on DUSP-1, E-cadherin, and vimentin protein expression levels

In this study, the effect of various concentrations of TGF-β1 on the protein expression levels of DUSP-1, E-cadherin, and vimentin in A549 cells was investigated, as illustrated in Fig. 2. The results demonstrated notable changes in protein expression levels across different TGF-β1 concentrations. Specifically, E-cadherin protein expression levels decreased significantly in A549 cells exposed to TGF-β1 concentrations of 1.25, 2.5, 5, and 10 ng/mL, when compared to the control group (P < 0.05). Additionally, DUSP-1 protein expression levels exhibited a decrease in A549 cells treated with 1.25 ng/mL of TGF-β1, as compared to the control group (P < 0.05). Conversely, vimentin protein expression levels increased in A549 cells treated with 5 and 10 ng/mL of TGF-β1, relative to the control group (P < 0.05). These findings collectively provide strong evidence suggesting that TGF-β1 primarily influences the up-regulation of vimentin protein expression and the down-regulation of E-cadherin protein expression in A549 cells(Fig. 2).

TGF-β1 regulates the transcriptional expression of EMT target genes

High amplification levels and absence of miscellaneous peaks in the dissolution curve were observed for DUSP-1, E-cadherin, Vimentin gene, and reference gene β-actin gene, rendering them suitable for analyzing the expression of DUSP-1, E-cadherin, and vimentin genes. In A549 cells, the expression levels of DUSP-1 and E-cadherin mRNA were found to be significantly reduced in the TGF-β1 (10 ng/ml) dose group compared to the control group (P < 0.05, non-parametric Kruskal-Wallis test). Concurrently, Vimentin mRNA transcript levels were notably elevated in A549 cells exposed to 2.5 and 10 ng/ml TGF-β1 compared to the control group (P < 0.05). These results, illustrated in Fig. 3, highlight the differential expression patterns of the analyzed genes under TGF-β1 treatment conditions(Fig. 3).

Inhibiting miR-30d-5p reverses the effect of TGF-β1 on regulating the transcriptional expression of EMT target genes

We first utilized a miR-30d-5p inhibitor to suppress the expression of miR-30d-5p and subsequently examined the alterations in the expression levels of associated downstream factors to elucidate the underlying mechanism of action of miR-30d-5p. Following the administration of the miR-30d-5p inhibitor, there was a significant reduction in miR-30d-5p expression, as evidenced by a notable disparity when compared to the control group for TGF-β inhibitor group (P < 0.05)(Fig. 5). Notably, in A549 cells treated with the control group for TGF-β inhibitor, TGF-β1-mediated regulation of EMT target gene transcript expression led to a decrease in mRNA levels of DUSP-1 and E-cadherin, coupled with an increase in Vimentin expression.These compelling findings underscore the pivotal role played by the miR-30d-5p gene in exerting anti-tumor effects through the modulation of DUSP-1, E-cadherin, and Vimentin protein and gene expression profiles(Fig. 6).

Discussion

The phenomenon of Epithelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition (EMT) is a critical process in tumor metastasis and invasion, involving the transformation of epithelial cells into mesenchymal cells [19]. E-cadherin serves as a marker of epithelial properties [20], while Vimentin is a marker of mesenchymal properties [21]. Research findings from this study reveal that TGF-β1 can enhance Vimentin protein expression and reduce E-cadherin protein expression, aligning with clinicopathological changes associated with EMT induction by TGF-β1 [22]. Furthermore, TGF-β1 shows a significant inhibitory effect on miR-30d-5p expression levels, particularly evident in A549 cells where suppression of miR-30d-5p expression enhances EMT, promoting tumor cell proliferation and suppressing apoptosis. These results indicate that TGF-β1 has the capability to trigger epithelial-mesenchymal transition in lung cancer A549 cells, with its mechanism of action linked to the TGF-β1/miR-30d-5p regulatory axis.

After intervention with TGF-β1 treatment and inhibition of miR-30d-5p expression, the gene and protein expression changes of EMT process markers such as E-cadherin and Vimentin were analyzed. E-cadherin and Vimentin are known to be regulated by mRNA and play vital roles in various tumor cells [23,24]. Our study analysis confirmed that TGF-β1 significantly downregulated mRNA levels of DUSP-1 and E-cadherin while upregulating vimentin expression in control group for TGF-β inhibitor-treated A549 cells. However, treatment with the miR-30d-5p inhibitor reversed the regulatory effects of TGF-β1 on the transcriptional expression of these target genes. These results indicate that miR-30d-5p may exert anti-tumor effects by influencing pathways that control the expression of downstream key factors in EMT. Hence, further investigation into the TGF-β1/miR-30d-5p axis regulatory pathway is warranted to enhance our understanding of its significance (Fig. 6).

Our study revealed the TGF-β1/miR-30d-5p pathway in lung cancer cells for the first time, providing a new mechanism for understanding the anti-tumor effect of miR-30d-5p. This pathway mediates reduced proliferation and increased apoptosis in lung cancer cells by regulating DUSP-1, E-cadherin, and vimentin gene and protein expression. Thus, miR-30d-5p′s regulation of these factors may represent a critical regulatory mechanism underlying its anti-tumor effects. This finding offers a novel basis for clinical treatments in lung cancer.

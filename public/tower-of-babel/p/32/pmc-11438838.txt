Optimal directed acyclic graph federated learning model for energy-efficient IoT communication networks
Authors: G Nalinipriya, E Laxmi Lydia, S Rama Sree, Denis Nikolenko, Sirisha Potluri, Janjhyam Venkata Naga Ramesh, Sheela Jayachandran
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractFederated learning (FL) stimulates distributed on-device computation systems to process an optimum technique efficiency by communicating local process upgrades and global method distribution from aggregation averaging procedure. On-device FL is a standard application in wireless environments, with several mobile devices participating as nodes in the FL network. Managing extensive multi-dimensional process upgrades and resource-constrained computations in large-scale heterogeneous IoT cellular networks can be challenging. This article introduces a Lifetime Maximization using Optimal Directed Acyclic Graph Federated Learning in IoT Communication Networks (LM-ODAGFL) technique. The proposed LM-ODAGFL technique utilizes FL and metaheuristic optimization algorithms for energy-effective IoT networks. The Direct Acyclic Graph (DAG) model addresses device asynchrony in FL while minimizing additional resource usage. The Archimedes Optimization Algorithm (AOA) is designed to optimize the DAG model by reducing both user energy consumption and the training loss of the FL model. The performance validation of the LM-ODAGFL technique is performed by utilizing a series of experimentations. The obtained results of the LM-ODAGFL model demonstrate superior performance by consuming significantly less energy than SDAGFL and ESDAGFL, with values ranging from 0.373 to 0.485 kJ per round on the FMNIST-Clustered dataset and 16.27 to 20.34 kJ per round on the Poets dataset, compared to 0.000 to 1.442 kJ and 0.00 to 63.89 kJ respectively.Keywords: Internet of things, Federated learning, Archimedes optimization algorithm, Lifetime maximization, Energy consumptionSubject terms: Computer science, Information technology

Introduction

The ever-increasing data produced or collected at edge devices results from millions of interconnected Internet-of-Things (IoT) devices as each active IoT User extracts the observed data and pushes that information to the edge1. To offer satisfactory resources and control abundant IoT taxonomies, deep learning (DL) and machine learning (ML) models are used to develop smart solutions in edge intelligence for different services by collecting local information for testing and training models2,3. Since the IoT deployment has increased in different privacy-sensitive sectors, namely the Internet of People (IoP), Internet of Healthcare Things (IoHTs), and Internet of Vehicles (IoVs), the use of local raw information that legally authorized and user-consented beforehand being transferred to the central cloud4, with these complicated problems, a smart provisioning system considers the privacy of adequate computation resources, local data privacy, and communication reliability5,6. Conventional ML techniques have to aggregate extracted information on a single machine or data centre7, and such a learning system is usually used in large AI-based organizations like Google and Facebook. They store every information gathered in their data centre8,9, whereby it is trained in the corresponding ML technique. To accomplish an improved ML technique under the central model, users might compromise their security by transferring secluded information to the data centre10. This training model is privacy-intrusive, primarily when the User addresses their sensitive or personal data to accomplish a superior trained method11. FL is a technique that can train the model, which leverages the user's private information without sharing it with others12,13. However, the User might pose a resource need to implement on-device computation and fail to obtain the target convergence in the predicted time. Furthermore, some unique problems include energy consumption (ECON)14, which couldn't be detected in a conventional FL-based technique regarding computation, communication, privacy, storage, and power, for example, high ECON, straggler issues, and handling dropped participants. The study presents the problems of FL setting in such a situation and defines the effect of taking this resource-constraint User within the network by assuming their real-time constraint15.

Literature review

In16, the difficulty of energy-efficient communication and computation Resource Allocation (RA) for FL under wireless transmission networks was examined. All user actions restricted local computational resources for training a local FL method with the data gathered during this considered method. Afterwards, the accomplished FL approach was sent to BS, which collects and transmits the local FL method to every user. While the iterative technique needs a primary possible solution, the authors generate the accomplishment time minimized problem, and a bisection-based technique was presented to obtain a better solution that is a potential solution to the novel energy minimized problem. The authors present a joint technique of UAV location, model accuracy, power control, communication time, computing resources, and bandwidth distribution, like energy-efficient FL (E2FL), to minimize the entire ECON of aerial servers and users. Prakash et al.18 examine GWEP, a model compression-related FL technique. It employs joint quantized and model pruning to obtain the advantage of DNNs but meets the abilities of resource-constrained devices. Accordingly, the lower-end IoT device can join in the FL procedure by decreasing the memory, computational, and network footprint of FLs.

The authors leverage the FL for constructing digital twin methods of IoT devices dependent upon its running data. In addition, the authors present an asynchronous system upgrade method to mitigate the communication overhead and express the FL technique as an optimized problem. The authors decompose the problem and resolve the sub-problems dependent upon the DNN technique. Al-Abiad et al.20 explore an RA technique for reducing the entire ECON of FL from the relay-supported IoT network. The author aimed to minimize the ECON of IoT devices dependent on FL time constraints. For this purpose, a joint optimized problem, assuming the schedule of the IoT device with relays, computation frequency distribution, and communication power allocation, is expressed. Because of the NP-hardness of the joint optimized problem, a worldwide optimum result was intractable. Saadat et al.21 introduce an energy-efficient Hierarchical FL (HFL) infrastructure by optimizing user-edge association and RA. It is complete by expressing and resolving a transmission energy minimized problem which obtains as to consider the data sharing of users and the transmission latency betwixt the edges and users. The authors also execute an alternate, less difficult solution leveraging Reinforcement Learning (RL), which offers a quick User-edge connection and RA response from an extremely dynamic HFL network. In22, the FL-E2WS approach, constructed for FL in IoT networks, is proposed. FL-E2WS optimizes energy efficiency by scheduling communication resources, comprising bandwidth and power, for model transmission while addressing device selection, delay constraints, and packet error rates. Zhao et al.23 designed an energy-efficient FL model for cell-free IoT networks by reducing total energy consumption through optimized CPU frequency, power allocation, and nonlinear programming.

Koursioumpas et al.24 present a Soft Actor-Critic Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) methodology that comprises a penalty function. Furthermore, a device-level synchronization method and a cost-effective federated learning environment are introduced to mitigate energy utilization and communication overhead. Badr et al.25 construct a privacy-preserving and communication-efficient FL-based energy predictor for net-metering systems, integrating a hybrid DL predictor with safe inner-product functional encryption to protect customers' privacy during model aggregation. Zhang et al.26 propose a dynamic methodology for device participation depending on packet loss rates and convert non-convex functions to convex ones to offer analytical solutions. Al-Abiad27 developed a decentralized FL scheme, FL-EOCD, that employs device-to-device communication and overlapped clustering to enable model aggregation without depending on a global aggregator, utilizing bridge devices to facilitate decentralized data sharing. Dang and Shin28 address reducing total energy utilization for user equipment (UE) during FL in UAV networks. A system is presented for simultaneous UE operation at the same frequency to enhance bandwidth usage. The model also utilizes alternating optimization and iterative algorithms to resolve the non-convex problem and find locally optimal solutions. Salh et al.29 develop effectual incorporation of joint edge intelligence nodes for optimizing bandwidth allocation, CPU frequency, and transmission power, aiming to reduce energy utilization and meet FL time requirements for IoT devices. Hashempour et al.30 develop an energy-efficient transmission protocol for relay-assisted FL in industrial subnetworks, optimizing training latency and power consumption by employing single-hop and two-hop transmission strategies.

Mortazavi31 proposes the Marathon Runner Algorithm, a human-inspired metaheuristic that mitigates the risk of local optima by utilizing a vision point that reflects the quality of the entire population. Moloodpoor and Mortazavi32 developed a multi-variable optimization technique with discrete and continuous variables to reduce total heating costs across different climates. It utilizes the Interactive Search Algorithm (ISA) model. In33, a probability-based regulatory mechanism is presented to optimize truss structures, concentrating on simultaneous size and topology adjustments to improve search effectiveness and reduce ineffective iterations. Moloodpoor and Mortazavi34 propose a non-gradient, populace-based optimization model for reducing the life cycle cost of heating for an insulated building, employing continuous and discrete design variables. Kandemir and Mortazavi35 introduce a model using the Fuzzy Butterfly Optimization Algorithm (FBOA) to reduce roof acceleration relative to peak ground acceleration while adhering to constraints on damping ratio, period, and lateral drift. Moloodpoor, Mortazavi, and Kartal36 present the Interactive Fuzzy Search Algorithm (IFSA) approach for optimizing the design of heat exchangers. Mortazavi37 presents the Fuzzy Reinforced Jaya (FRJ) methodology, which integrates a fuzzy decision-making mechanism to balance exploration and exploitation in the Jaya algorithm dynamically. Existing research often needs help with scalability, dynamic adaptation, and real-time performance in FL systems, precisely balancing energy efficiency, accuracy, and privacy under diverse network conditions.

The proposed model

In this article, a novel LM-ODAGFL technique is developed for lifetime enhancement in IoT communication networks. The proposed LM-ODAGFL technique uses FL and a metaheuristic optimization algorithm for energy-effective IoT networks. The DAG model is used to resolve the asynchronous device characteristics in FL without using additional resources. The AOA is designed to optimize the DAG model in this work.

Overview of DAG

DAG‐FL was presented as a decentralized asynchronous FL method comprising applications, DAG, and FL layers from top to bottom40. The DAG method is highly advantageous for decentralized asynchronous FL due to its intrinsic structure, which eases effectual task scheduling and resource management. Unlike conventional techniques, DAG inherently assists in representing dependencies and execution order among several tasks, which is significant for managing asynchronous updates in a decentralized FL setup. Its non-cyclic nature prevents circular dependencies, confirming that updates can be processed independently and concurrently across various devices, improving scalability and mitigating latency. Furthermore, the capability of the DAG model to delineate clear execution paths assists in optimizing communication and synchronization, reducing the requirement for global coordination. This makes it specifically efficient in managing complex FL processes with various contributors and varying update frequencies, offering crucial enhancements in efficiency and reliability compared to other techniques.

(i) FL Layer: The FL layer is the bottom layer for providing FL purpose. For obtaining local methods recorded as transactions on DAG, the FL layer permits some contributing nodes to utilize their data to train the global process. The global method was designed using the Federated Averaging technique for aggregating local processes kept in communications on DAG. Afterwards, trained the universal method, a novel competent local method will be published and processed as a transmission on DAG. Figure 2 portrays the architecture of FL technique.

Consequently, the application layer offers connections to an external agent for deploying DAG‐FL simply, and the DAG and FL layer procedure is an asynchronous environment to FL. The nodes with synchrony devices, such as smartphones and IoT devices from DAG‐ FL, all require maintaining a local DAG for recording communications distributed by all the nodes. The local DAG on all the nodes is upgraded by communicating with neighbouring nodes sometimes, and therefore, recently published transactions are realized by every node. During this manner, the process of some nodes could not affect another node's state, fulfilling the asynchrony of mobile devices.

Role of DAG

In the LM-ODAGFL technique, the DAG plays a significant role in managing device asynchronous characteristics within the FL model. The DAG approach facilitates structured data flow coordination and task scheduling across dispersed devices. By representing the sequence and dependencies of tasks as a directed acyclic graph, the DAG confirms that each device's contributions to the FL process are effectually incorporated despite potential device availability and performance discrepancies. This organization reduces the chances of data inconsistencies and delays, which are crucial in maintaining the overall effectivess of the FL system. Figure 4 portrays the flowchart of DAG model.

Flowchart of DAG.

The DAG method also addresses the threat of device asynchrony by clearly defining the order of operations and dependencies among diverse tasks. This methodology assists in synchronizing data updates and model aggregations across several devices without needing further synchronization protocols or resources. As a result, the system can accommodate devices with varying operational states and communication patterns, optimizing available resources and ensuring a smoother FL process.

Moreover, the DAG approach assists in mitigating computational overhead by optimizing task scheduling and data management. It allows for effective dispersion and processing of tasks, mitigating redundancy and reducing the impact of slower or less reliable devices on comprehensive system performance. This effective task management contributes to lesser energy utilization and enhanced training effectiveness, which are substantial for the energy-sensitive environment of IoT networks.

Overall, integrating the DAG technique into the LM-ODAGFL framework gives a structured and effective solution for handling asynchronous devices in the FL model. By clearly delineating task dependencies and optimizing data flow, the DAG method safeguards the overall performance and resource consumption of the FL system, making it appropriate for deployment in energy-constrained IoT networks.

Design of AOA for DAG tuning process

The AOA is a new metaheuristic method projected by Abualigah41. The AOA is particularly suited for tuning DAGs due to its robust capability for handling complex optimization problems effectually. Unlike traditional optimization models that may face difficulty with non-convex and high-dimensional problems, AOA outperforms in finding optimal solutions by iteratively refining potential solutions through a systematic technique. Its adaptive behaviour allows for precise alterations in DAG parameters, enhancing performance in FL scenarios. Moreover, the AOA technique is constructed to reduce energy utilization and training loss efficiently, making it an ideal option for optimizing the DAG method where resource constraints and performance metrics are significant. Its novel technique in fitness computation improves its capability to balance various objectives, namely energy efficiency and model accuracy, outperforming conventional techniques in complex tuning tasks. Conclusion

In this article, a novel LM-ODAGFL technique is developed for lifetime enhancement in IoT communication networks. The proposed LM-ODAGFL technique utilizes FL and metaheuristic optimization algorithms for energy-effective IoT networks. The DAG model addresses device asynchrony in FL while minimizing additional resource usage. The AOA is designed to optimize the DAG model by reducing both user energy consumption and the training loss of the FL model. The performance validation of the LM-ODAGFL technique is performed by utilizing a series of experimentations. The obtained results of the LM-ODAGFL model demonstrate superior performance by consuming significantly less energy than SDAGFL and ESDAGFL, with values ranging from 0.373 to 0.485 kJ per round on the FMNIST-Clustered dataset and 16.27 to 20.34 kJ per round on the Poets dataset, compared to 0.000 to 1.442 kJ and 0.00 to 63.89 kJ respectively. The limitation of the LM-ODAGFL technique is its dependency on the efficiency of the DAG method for managing device asynchrony, which may still need help with highly dynamic or heterogeneous device environments. Furthermore, the performance of the AOA techcnique may be sensitive to the specific configurations and parameters utilized, potentially limiting its generalizability across diverse IoT scenarios. Future studies should focus on improving the robustness of the DAG methodology in handling more extreme discrepancies in device behaviour and developing adaptive mechanisms within the AOA model to enhance its scalability and flexibility. Further research could also explore incorporating additional metaheuristic models to refine energy optimization and expand the model's applicability to a broader range of IoT applications. Additionally, the FL technique can identify abnormal behaviour in the IoT network.

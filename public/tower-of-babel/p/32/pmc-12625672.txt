Comparative analysis of autophagy and apoptosis pathways in viral and alcoholic cirrhosis
Authors: Gunnur Demircan, Selimcan Berk, Doga Karayilan, Berkay T. Ozgur, Sema Ketenci, Ipek Elbegi, Vildan Karpuz, Melike Yavuz, Ahmet Midi, Demet Akin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Objectives:To study the different cellular death mechanisms between viral cirrhosis and alcoholic cirrhosis. The research investigated autophagy and apoptosis mechanisms in hepatitis B virus (HBV), hepatitis C virus (HCV) and alcohol-induced cirrhosis.Methods:The research team analyzed biopsy samples from the liver which were obtained at Florence Nightingale Hospitals, Istanbul, Turkey. The experimental protocols were performed between February 2021 and February 2023. The study included 19 HBV, 15 HCV, 13 alcohol-related cirrhosis patients and 6 normal liver tissues. Beclin-1, Caspase-3, Bcl-2 and LC3 expressions were evaluated by immunohistochemistry.Results:Staining intensity as well as extent underwent evaluation through H-score methodology. The expression levels of Beclin-1 (control: 0.7±0.3, HBV: 6.0±1.4, HCV: 5.1±1.3, alcohol: 4.8±1.2), caspase-3 (control: 0.4±0.2, HBV: 6.0±1.4, HCV: 5.1±1.2, alcohol: 5.5±1.3) and Bcl-2 (control: 0.3±0.2, HBV: 5.5±1.2, HCV: 4.8±1.1, alcohol: 4.6±1.1) were significantly higher than those of the control group (p<0.001). LC3 expression revealed no between-group differences. A positive association for Beclin-1 with Caspase-3 (r=0.582) alongside negative association for Bcl-2 with Caspase-3 (r=-0.608) was documented.Conclusion:Our findings suggest that both autophagic and apoptotic pathways are active in the pathogenesis of cirrhosis. Similar cell death mechanisms were found to be involved in viral and alcoholic cirrhosis, but these pathways were more prominently activated in viral cirrhosis.

Acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF) is a new clinical entity defined as an acute deterioration of pre-existing, chronic liver disease, with or without an identified precipitating event, characterized by a marked systemic inflammatory response, organ failure, and a high mortality rate.1,2 Although there is evidence suggesting that hepatocyte cell death occurs in ACLF.3 the predominant mechanism of hepatocyte cell death remains unclear.4 To understand ACLF pathogenesis fully, investigating specific pathways, such as autophagy and apoptosis, becomes essential.

Liver injury results in cirrhosis through the development of regenerative nodules which fibrous bands encase. The medical condition creates portal hypertension and results in end-stage liver disease. The 3 main results of cirrhosis include impaired liver cell operation alongside portal hypertension and hepatocellular carcinoma.5 Alcoholic liver disease and chronic infections with hepatitis B viruse (HBV) or hepatitis C viruse (HCV) represent the leading causes of cirrhosis throughout the world.6 Research into liver damage mechanisms between viral and alcoholic cirrhosis remains essential because of their distinct characteristics.7,8

Scientific data from human patients along with experimental animal studies indicate that liver disease progression begins with hepatocyte death which results in cirrhosis and fibrosis and other liver conditions.8,9 Under stress conditions the cellular survival mechanism autophagy serves as the fundamental mechanism which derives its name from the Greek words “self-eating”. The recycling of cytoplasmic substances through autophagy enables cells to maintain their essential operational processes that include protein degradation and lipid processing.10,11 The regulation of autophagy involves Beclin-1 as its initiating protein and LC3 which tracks autophagosome development.7,14 The levels of basal autophagy in hepatocytes determine liver cell homeostasis which affects both normal liver function and liver disease pathophysiology.11,12 Future investigations should determine the specific role autophagy plays in alcoholic cirrhosis and viral cirrhosis development.12,13

Medical science now recognizes apoptosis as a key cell death mechanism which contributes to liver fibrosis development and disease progression. The apoptosis process depends on caspases that belong to apoptotic pathways as well as Bcl-2 family proteins.9-14 The process of fibrosis in different liver diseases such as viral hepatitis and alcoholic liver disease develops through sustained apoptosis.14 The excessive death of cells through apoptosis leads to chronic inflammation because the build-up of apoptotic bodies exceeds the capacity of phagocytic cells to remove them thus releasing proinflammatory substances.14 The research community needs to investigate how apoptotic mechanisms function differently in viral versus alcoholic cirrhosis.8-11

Research needs further investigation to understand how hepatocyte death mechanisms differ between viral and alcoholic cirrhosis. The investigation of autophagy and apoptosis relationships in these diseases might identify new therapeutic intervention opportunities.15

This study investigates the different death mechanisms which occur in viral and alcoholic cirrhosis patients.

Methods

The study used paraffin blocks from for its retrospective analysis. Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded liver tissue samples were obtained from patients who applied to and were treated at Gayrettepe Florence Nightingale Hospital, Istanbul, Turkiye between January 2010, and December 2020, were used in this study. The experimental procedures took place from February 2021 through February 2023. The study included patients who met these criteria: patients aged 18-75 years with cirrhosis confirmed by histopathology, complete clinical and laboratory data and sufficient tissue specimens. The study excluded patients who had autoimmune hepatitis or drug-induced liver injury or metabolic liver diseases or hepatocellular carcinoma or insufficient tissue samples. The G*Power software power analysis showed that 12 patients per group would be needed to reach 80% power with a 95% confidence interval. The research included 19 patients with HBV-related cirrhosis and 15 patients with HCV-related cirrhosis and 13 patients with alcohol-induced cirrhosis and 6 normal liver tissue specimens (N=53). This study was approved by Demiroğlu Bilim University Clinical Research Ethics Committee on 19 January 2021 with the approval number 44140529/351.

Tissue processing and immunohistochemical staining

The tissue specimens underwent 24-hour fixation in 10% buffered formalin followed by standard tissue processing and paraffin embedding procedures. Each block received sections that measured 4 μm in thickness. The immunohistochemical staining procedure used the Nova Castra Laboratories Ltd (UK) avidin-biotin peroxidase method. The sections received a 30-minute treatment with 0.3% H2O2/methanol solution to block endogenous peroxidase activity. The sections underwent microwave treatment in citrate buffer (pH 6.0) for 20 minutes to perform antigen retrieval. The application of 2% goat serum at a 1:100 dilution served as a blocking agent for one hour to reduce non-specific binding. The primary antibodies received overnight incubation at 4°C with their optimal concentrations (Caspase-3 1:200, Bcl-2 1:100, Beclin-1 1:150, LC3 1:100). The application of biotinylated anti-mouse IgM at 1:400 concentration lasted 30 minutes before ExtraVidin-peroxidase complex at 1:500 concentration was applied for 45 minutes. The experimental sets included positive control tonsillar tissue samples together with negative control samples that used PBS instead of primary antibodies.16

Immunohistochemical evaluation

The H-score method and immunoreactive scoring system were employed to evaluate the staining results. All immunohistochemical images were captured using an Olympus BX51 microscope equipped with a DP72 digital camera. Quantitative analysis of staining intensity was performed using ImageJ software (version 1.53, National Institutes of Health, USA). The H-score calculation followed this formula: percentage of strongly stained cells × 3 + percentage of moderately stained cells × 2 + percentage of weakly stained cells × 1. The scoring system used negative (score 0) for no protein expression and weakly positive (score 1-6) and strongly positive (score >6) for protein expression.25 Our research setting required the H-score method because of its practical application.24 The immunoreactive scoring system evaluated both staining percentage and intensity through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis to determine its cut-off values. The scoring system’s validation involved reassessment of 20 randomly selected samples to calculate test-retest reliability (r=0.88). The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to assess the normality of data distribution

Statistical analysis

The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to assess the normality of data distribution. For normally distributed variables, we performed one-way ANOVA with post-hoc Tukey tests, while Kruskal-Wallis and Mann-Whitney U tests were applied for non-normally distributed variables. The research team performed Chi-square analysis on all categorical variables. The research team used Spearman’s rank correlation test to perform correlation analyses. The research team established p<0.05 as the threshold for statistical significance. The research team performed all statistical analyses using SPSS version 25.0 software.

Results

The staining patterns of Beclin-1 showed increased intensity (0.8 ± 0.4) and distribution (0.9 ± 0.5) in control hepatocytes while patient groups presented greater staining intensity. The HBV group had the highest staining intensity (2.4 ± 0.5) followed by HCV (2.2 ± 0.7) and ethanol (2.1 ± 0.6) groups respectively. The staining distribution results were similar to those of intensity patterns. The final score revealed that all patient groups demonstrated higher scores compared to the control group (0.7 ± 0.3) with HBV having the highest scores (6.0 ± 1.4) (p<0.001). The bile duct evaluation showed a similar pattern with increased scores in patient groups than in controls (staining intensity: 0.6 ± 0.3, distribution: 0.7 ± 0.4). The highest bile duct final score was observed in the HBV group (4.6 ± 1.3) and other patient groups also had higher scores than controls (p<0.001). There were no significant differences in Child-Pugh scores (ethanol: 9.2 ± 2.1, HBV: 8.9 ± 2.3, HCV: 9.1 ± 2.2; p=0.873) or MELD scores (ethanol: 16.3 ± 4.2, HBV: 15.8 ± 4.5, HCV: 16.1 ± 4.3; p=0.918) in the clinical parameters of patient groups. The apoptosis index evaluated through H&E staining was found to be low in the control group (0.3 ± 0.1) but increased in all patient groups with the highest value in the HBV group (3.1 ± 1.1) (p<0.001) (Table 1).

Discussion

This research examined the significance of autophagy as well as apoptosis pathways to enhance the comprehension of the mechanisms that result into cell death in patients diagnosed with viral and alcohol related cirrhosis. We examined the expression levels of Beclin-1, Caspase-3 and Bcl-2 in hepatocytes and bile ducts in HBV, HCV, and alcohol-associated liver cirrhosis groups in comparison with control tissue. As a result of immunohistochemical staining and evaluation, we observed that the staining intensity and prevalence of these markers were significantly increased in all patient groups. These results indicate that both paths of autophagy as well as apoptosis are involved in the cause of the cirrhosis condition.

Our immunohistochemical method delivers important information about protein expression patterns yet pathway enrichment analysis through KEGG or Reactome databases needs gene expression data from RNA-seq or microarray analysis which was not possible in our retrospective study. The proteins we studied are established components of well-documented pathways according to existing research. Beclin-1 functions as a critical element of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway that controls autophagy initiation and Caspase-3 and Bcl-2 play central roles in intrinsic and extrinsic apoptosis while Bcl-2 acts as an anti-apoptotic regulator at the mitochondrial outer membrane.18,19

In our study, we found that Beclin-1 expression was significantly elevated in hepatocytes across all patient groups (ethanol: 4.8±1.2, HBV: 6.0±1.4, HCV: 5.1±1.3) compared to the control group (0.7±0.3), with the HBV group showing the highest increase, and a similar pattern of increase was observed in bile ducts. Among the immunohistochemical markers used in the study, beclin-1 and LC-3, play a role in autophagia and its expression increases in the autophagic cell.20,21 These findings differ from some studies in the literature. For instance, in the literature, beclin-1 expression was found to be lower in hepatocellular carcinoma and cirrhosis when compared to the surrounding normal tissue.22 In another study, Beclin-1 and Bcl-2 were also examined in chronic hepatitis C and HCC, and it was shown that beclin-1 expression was found to be decreased in hepatocellular carcinoma, however, bcl-2 expression was increased in hepatocellular carcinoma.23 However, supporting our findings, as hepatitis stage progressed towards cirrhosis in chronic hepatitis C group, an increase occurred in beclin-1 expression.4,5,7 The marked increase in Beclin-1 expression observed in all patient groups compared to control tissue is clearly visible in immunohistochemical staining sections (Figure A1, B1, C1, D1).

The increase in beclin-1 on the basis of hepatitis was considered as a protective mechanism from cell death via autophagia. High level of beclin-1 in the cirrhosis tissues in the current study suggests that autophagia is a protective mechanism against liver injury.24 This protective mechanism was observed more prominently in viral hepatitis groups. The preferential activation of autophagy in viral cirrhosis may be attributed to virus-induced endoplasmic reticulum stress and the unfolded protein response, which are known to trigger autophagy as a cellular survival mechanism.25

In our study, we found that Caspase-3 expression showed significant increases in hepatocytes across all patient groups (ethanol: 5.5±1.3, HBV: 6.0±1.4, HCV: 5.1±1.2) compared to the control group (0.4±0.2), demonstrating positive correlation with ALT levels (r=0.512) and strong correlation with H&E apoptosis index (r=0.724). Hepatocyte apoptosis is a significant feature in acute and chronic liver injury and caspase-3 level increases in hepatitis in parallel.26,27 These findings align with the literature. The Fas/Fas L system-mediated caspase-3 activation signaling pathway plays a central role in ethanol-induced hepatic apoptosis. This result has brought the prevention of Fas/Fas L formation in ethanol-induced hepatic injury or caspase-3 activation inhibition forward as a new treatment approach. In alcoholic liver disease, it is shown that caspase 3-positive hepatocytes are increased, and this also increased apoptosis. The elevated Caspase-3 expression observed in our ethanol group supports these findings. In the current study, it was shown that caspase-3/7 activity in serum increased more than HCV and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease in the patients with cirrhosis.19-22

As demonstrated in immunohistochemical sections (Figure A2, B2, C2, D2), Caspase-3 expression in hepatocytes and bile ducts showed marked increases across all patient groups compared to control tissue. The activation of Caspase-3 represents the convergence of multiple apoptotic pathways, including the death receptor pathway (via Caspase-8) and the mitochondrial pathway (via Caspase-9), both of which have been implicated in chronic liver disease progression.29

Our research showed that Bcl-2 expression levels rose substantially in all patient groups (ethanol: 4.6±1.1, HBV:5.5±1.2, HCV:4.8±1.1) compared to the control group (0.3±0.2) while showing strong negative associations with Caspase-3 (r= -0.582 to -0.624) and positive associations with viral load (HBV: r=0.342, HCV: r=0.324). In the study, Bcl-2 expression was found increased in HCC group when compared to normal control and Chronic HCV hepatitis cases group.22,26 Similarly, bcl-2 expression was found to be increased in the cirrhotic nodules in 3 different study groups. While Bcl-2 level decreased in the early period of hepatitis, it increased in the recovery period of the injury.25,27 These findings align with our results. The Bcl-2 expression shows a clear increase in immunohistochemical staining sections (Figure A3, B3, C3, D3). The increase is most pronounced in the HBV group.

In our study, although there was no statistically significant difference between the groups in terms of LC3 expression, we found that it was positively correlated with Beclin-1 (r=0.482-0.568 in hepatocytes) and this correlation was stronger in viral hepatitis groups. Incomplete autophagic response is induced by the HCV unfolded protein response (UPR). The incomplete autophagic response prevents the formation of autophagic vacuoles by reducing the LC3 protein (a necessary step for the formation of autophagosomes) and thus, contributes to HCV replication.22,25 This may explain the minimal increase in LC3 expression that we observed especially in the HCV group. The immunohistochemical staining sections show minimal expression of the markers in all groups (Figure A4, B4, C4, D4).

The analysis of marker relationships with clinical parameters revealed a positive relationship between Beclin-1/Caspase-3 (r=0.582) and a negative relationship between Bcl-2/Caspase-3 (r=-0.608). The markers did not show any significant correlations with disease duration or Child-Pugh and MELD scores. The positive correlation between Beclin-1 and Caspase-3 indicates that there may be a cross-talk between autophagy and apoptosis pathways, which could be mediated by Beclin-1’s BH3 domain that allows interaction with Bcl-2 family proteins, thus creating a molecular switch between these 2 cell death mechanisms.29 Liver injury in NASH patients is associated with apoptosis and NF-κB activation even though anti-apoptotic B-cell lymphoma 2 (Bcl-2) is strongly expressed. These changes are caspase dependent. They are also associated with mitochondrial membrane depolarization and the release of cytochrome c, which activate the mitochondrial apoptosis pathways including activation of the proteins Bcl-2-associated X (Bax) and Bcl2-interacting mediator of cell death (Bim). The degree of apoptosis was inversely correlated with the level of Bcl-2.25,27

In another study increased numbers of apoptotic cells were seen in rats developing ethanol-induced pathological liver injury. Increased bcl-2 protein concentrations are associated with the presence of inflammatory cells and lipid peroxidation.30 The elevated Bcl-2 expression observed in our ethanol group supports this finding.

It was expected to have Bcl-2 expression decrease in the degenerated tissues. Cirrhosis formation expresses the regeneration process in the chronic period of liver injury. In this context, the high Bcl-2 expression observed in our study may reflect the regenerative process of cirrhosis. The reason may be the examination performed in the liver tissue in regeneration stage. Bcl-2 expression could have been found to be decreased in case the examination was made at the initial stage of the injury. It may be appropriate to support this with the studies showing HBV, HCV, and ethanol-induced early-stage liver tissues.

Study limitations

The study had multiple essential limitations which need to be taken into account when evaluating the research findings. The retrospective study design using archived paraffin-embedded tissue blocks from 2010-2020 made it impossible to perform the additional molecular techniques which the reviewers had suggested. Specifically, we were unable to perform TUNEL assay for direct confirmation of apoptosis through DNA fragmentation detection, transmission electron microscopy (TEM) for ultrastructural visualization of autophagosomes, or RT-PCR/Western blot analyses for mRNA and protein expression quantification of autophagy and apoptosis markers. The molecular approaches would have enabled a detailed analysis of cell death pathways which goes beyond what immunohistochemical detection can show.

The results of immunohistochemical staining can be affected by several factors including tissue fixation quality, antigen retrieval efficiency, antibody specificity and the inherent subjectivity in scoring despite our use of standardized H-score methodology and inter-observer reliability measures. Although we included cirrhotic samples from different etiologies (HBV, HCV, and alcohol-induced), we were unable to assess disease activity status, viral load at the time of biopsy, or the degree of ongoing hepatic inflammation, which may influence the expression levels of autophagy and apoptosis markers.

Our control group was limited to 6 normal liver tissue specimens, which, while statistically adequate based on power analysis, may have reduced the robustness of our comparative analyses. Future prospective studies with larger sample sizes, fresh tissue collection allowing for multiple analytical approaches, and comprehensive clinical correlation including disease activity markers would provide more definitive insights into the differential activation of autophagy and apoptosis pathways in viral versus alcoholic cirrhosis.

In conclusion, our research into cirrhosis cell death mechanisms produced multiple essential findings. The investigation showed that Bcl-2 and Beclin-1 expression levels increased substantially in all patient groups relative to controls with Bcl-2 levels reaching 4.6 to 5.5 (compared to 0.3±0.2 in controls) and Beclin-1 levels reaching 4.8 to 6.0 (compared to 0.7±0.3 in controls). The HBV-related cirrhosis group showed the most pronounced elevation of these markers. The LC-3 expression levels in hepatocytes and bile ducts remained statistically unchanged compared to control tissue but viral hepatitis cases showed a slight increase. The expected decrease of Caspase-3 in cirrhotic nodules due to increased proliferation did not occur because we observed significant elevation of this protein across all cirrhosis groups (5.1-6.0 versus 0.4±0.2 in controls). The research findings show that multiple cell death mechanisms operate in cirrhotic liver disease because the increased ALT levels (r=0.512) and apoptosis index (r=0.724) demonstrate a strong positive correlation.

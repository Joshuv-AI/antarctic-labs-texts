Dietary semen cuscuta improves laying performance by stabilizing mitochondria-associated membranes (MAMs) and inhibiting granulosa cell apoptosis in laying hens
Authors: Changqing Si, Ying Chen, Linjie Liu, Shuo Liu, Zhenbiao Zhang, Lihuan Guo, Mengpo Zhao, Yuming Chen, Yuxiang Shi, Wanyu Shi, Shuang Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The decline in laying performance during late-phase production of hens, primarily due to ovarian aging, poses significant economic challenges to the poultry industry. We have previously identified that Semen Cuscuta (SC), a traditional Chinese herbal medicine, could alleviate reproductive damage in mice, however, the therapeutic potential of SC in aging hens remains largely unexplored. In this study, different doses of SC (1%, 1.5%, or 2%) were dietary supplemented for 4 weeks, and serum, Ileum, ovarian tissues were collected for further detection/examination. Results showed that 1.5% and 2% SC significantly improved laying performance, enhanced systemic antioxidant capacity (increased serum SOD, decreased MDA), and elevated FSH levels. SC also reinforced intestinal barrier integrity, as indicated by improved villus morphology, upregulation of tight junction proteins (Occludin, ZO-1), and reduced serum endotoxin markers (D-LA, LPS). Moreover, SC modulated gut microbiota composition, increased cecal short-chain fatty acids (acetic and butyric acid), and upregulated intestinal specifically short-chain fatty acids (SCFA) receptors (GPR41, GPR43). In the ovary, SC suppressed granulosa cell apoptosis by modulating Bcl-2, Bax, Cleaved Caspase-3, and Cyt c expression. Notably, transmission electron microscopy revealed that SC preserved mitochondrial associated membrane (MAM) structural integrity and normalized the expression of MAM tethering proteins (IP3R, GRP75, VDAC1) in ovary. These findings demonstrate that SC ameliorates ovarian aging and restores laying performance through a dual mechanism: directly stabilizing ovarian MAMs to inhibit apoptosis, and indirectly remodeling gut microbiota to enhance intestinal barrier function and reduce systemic inflammation. This study provides a mechanistic basis for SC as a promising feed additive to improve sustainability in the production of aging hens.

Introduction

The decline in reproductive performance during the late laying phase, primarily driven by ovarian aging, poses a significant economic challenge to the global poultry industry (Yang et al., 2024; Zhao et al. 2025b). In aging hens, ovarian senescence is characterized by a diminished follicular reserve and increased atresia, culminating in a progressive drop in egg production (Wang et al. 2024a). The pathogenesis of this decline is complex, which involved the accumulation of oxidative damage, mitochondrial dysfunction, and the orchestrated activation of apoptotic pathways in ovarian granulosa cells, which are essential for follicular development and steroidogenesis (Zhao et al. 2025a; Cao et al. 2025b). Identifying effective interventions to decelerate ovarian aging is therefore crucial for enhancing the sustainability of egg production.

Conventional strategies to ameliorate age-related decline prompting the search for natural, plant-derived alternatives (Shi et al. 2025). Semen Cuscuta (SC), a traditional herb renowned for its “kidney-tonifying” and anti-aging properties in Chinese medicine, is rich in flavonoids such as hyperin and quercetin (Shao et al. 2024). Modern pharmacological studies attribute its efficacy to potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-apoptotic activities (Lozanova et al. 2025; Baccari et al. 2025; Boxiang et al. 2024). However, the effects of SC on ovarian aging in laying hens and a mechanistic understanding of its action remain largely unexplored.

Variations in the gut microbiota are related to impaired reproductive function (Zhang et al. 2024a). The gut microbiota, as well as its corresponding metabolites, specifically short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) including butyrate, are outstanding in sustaining intestinal barrier reliability (Mohiuddin et al. 2025). An intact barrier prevents the translocation of endotoxins such as lipopolysaccharide (LPS), thereby attenuating systemic inflammation and oxidative stress induced aging (Michels et al. 2025). Recent studies suggest that gut-derived factors can directly influence distant organ function, including the ovary (Marano et al. 2025; da Silva et al. 2024). Concurrently, at the subcellular level, mitochondria-associated endoplasmic reticulum membranes (MAMs) have been identified as critical hubs for regulating calcium flux, lipid metabolism, and the initiation of mitochondrial apoptosis (Proulx et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2025a). The stability of MAMs is paramount for cellular health, and their disruption is implicated in various age-related pathologies (Bui et al. 2025; Xie et al. 2025).

Studies suggest that SC can enhance sexual function and delay aging; its specific impact on the intricate physiology of the aging ovary, whether the potential impact via the gut microbiota-derived metabolites and the stabilization of subcellular MAMs structures remains entirely unexplored (Li et al. 2025c; Zhao et al. 2024). Thus, this research work was envisioned to determine the optimal supplemental dose of SC for improving laying capability and egg quality in aging hens and to comprehensively decipher the underlying mechanisms by integrating multi-omics approaches, including 16S rRNA sequencing, metabolomics, transcriptomics, and ultrastructural analysis to validate the novel “gut microbiota-SCFAs-systemic inflammation-ovarian MAMs-apoptosis” axis. Our findings are anticipated to provide a robust mechanistic basis for the SC application as a novel, evidence-based feed additive to delay ovarian senescence and enhance the sustainability of poultry production.

Materials and methods

Animals and treatment schedule

A commercial farm in Hebei, China, provided 120 Hy-Line Brown hens (72 weeks old) for this study. The hens were divided into 4 groups and 6 replicates per group, with 5 chickens per replicate. The groups received a basal diet with different SC levels: control, low, medium, and high concentration of SC powder treatment group, with the control group receiving a normal diet for 4 weeks, while the animals in the treatment groups received 1, 1.5, and 2% of SC, respectively, along with the normal diet. All laying hens were housed individually in separate cages (400 mm × 380 mm × 360 mm) and under standard conditions (60±10% relative humidity, 23±2°C temperature, and 16:8 h light/dark cycle), with unrestricted availability of diets and water. Following a one-week acclimatization of hens to laboratory conditions, experiments were initiated. All chickens with 77 weeks were euthanized 3 h after egg laying. Sample collection

On 28 days after SC administration, 10 hens were selected from each group randomly. From the subcutaneous vein, after weighting blood samples were collected and placed for 10 min at 37°C, and centrifuged for 10 min at 3,500 r/min for ELISA. Plasma was obtained by collecting the blood in an anticoagulant tube, followed by centrifuging at 3,500 r/min for the duration of 2 min for Blood entry component analysis. Ileum and ovarian tissues were immediately collected following euthanasia of the hens. A portion of the tissues subjected to store in liquid nitrogen, and the other parts were processed and submerged in 4% paraformaldehyde or 2.5% glutaraldehyde for subsequent analysis. Blood entry component analysis

The plasma samples were treated as previously described (An et al. 2025b). Plasma samples derived from the control and 2% SC group were used as control and treatment, respectively. Plasma substances were determined according to the secondary fragments, precise mass numbers, and isotopic distributions through TCM databases developed by Shanghai Luming Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Shanghai, China). Compounds featuring a total score ≥40 points were selected as original prescription constituents, while compounds were considered as the blood components showing fold-change values ≥10 in both the treatment and the control groups.

Determination of laying efficiency

Egg number and total weight were documented every day with weekly monitoring of feed consumption to calculate the average egg mass (AEM), laying rate (LR), feed conversion ratio (FCR), and average egg weight (AEW).

ELISA

Levels of superoxide dismutase (SOD), malondialdehyde (MDA), follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), lipopolysaccharide (LPS), luteotropin (LH), and d-lactic acid (D-LA) in the serum of each sample were measured using ELISA kits (Mlbio, Shanghai, China) following the supplier's guidelines.

Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining

The fixed ileum tissues were embedded and subjected to slicing for H&E dyeing, followed by analysis under an optical microscope (Nikon Eclipse 80i microscope, Nikon, Japan). Image J software was used to determine the villus height (VH) and crypt depth (CD).

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)

Ovarian tissues in 2.5% glutaraldehyde (approximately 1 mm3) were dehydrated using a graded ethanol series, followed by subsequent slicing into various sections and analyzed through TEM (JEOL-JEM-1200EX, Peabody, Massachusetts, USA).

TUNEL analysis

Analysis of the TUNEL assay was carried out through the utilization of an apoptosis detection kit (Roche, Shanghai, China). Briefly, frozen ovarian sections were subjected to methanol fixation, antigen retrieval, and incubation with TUNEL reagent, subsequent staining of the nuclei using 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI), and analysis under a fluorescence microscope (BX-61, Olympus, Center Valley, Pennsylvania, USA).

Western blot

The ovarian and ileum tissues were subjected to homogenization, and total proteins were separated employing RIPA lysis buffer containing proteasome inhibitors (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China). The lysates were subjected to centrifugation for 5 min centrifugation at 12,000 g, and protein contents were ascertained in the supernatant using a BCA protein assay kit (Thermo, Waltham, MA, USA). A total protein (20 μg) from each sample was attained by SDS polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE), and transferred to a PVDF membrane. The membranes underwent blocking with 5% skimmed milk diluted in PBS, and afterwards incubated employing primary antibodies against β-actin (GB15001, Servicebio, Wuhan, China), IP3R (GB11742, Servicebio, Wuhan, China), GRP75 (GB11852, Servicebio, Wuhan, China), VDAC1 (GB111939, Servicebio, Wuhan, China), Bcl-2 (26593-1-AP, Proteintech, Wuhan, China), Bax (50599-2-Ig, Proteintech, Wuhan, China), Cyt c (WL02410, Wanleibio, Shenyang, China), Cleaved Caspase-3 (WL01992, Wanleibio, Shenyang, China), GPR41 (OM184469, Omnimabs, California, United States), GPR43 (OM255320, Omnimabs, California, United States), Occludin (WL01996, Wanleibio, Shenyang, China) and ZO-1(21773-1-AP, Proteintech, Wuhan, China) overnight at 4°C with gentle shaking. After three successive washes with TBST, the membrane was subjected to incubation with goat anti-rabbit/mouse IgG secondary antibody (1:2000) at room temperature for a duration of 2 h, followed by subsequent washing with TBST. Protein bands were ascertained employing the ECL Plus kit (Beyotime, P0018AS) by chemiluminescence image (ImageQuant LAS 500). The positive bands were examined employing Image J software (National Institutes of Health, Maryland, USA) and normalized to the β-actin expression.

The RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA while employing the TaKaRa Super RT Kit (Takara Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Dalian, China). Bio-Rad CFX96 was used to carry out the real-time quantitative PCR analysis. Quantification of SCFAs

The levels of SCFA were analyzed using targeted metabolomics, and the protocol for SCFA metabolomic analysis is to be carried out according to the methodology described in the reference (Wei et al. 2025b).

16S rRNA

Bacterial DNA extraction from cecal contents was conducted employing a DNeasy PowerSoil kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany) pursuant to the supplier’s protocols. The NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) and agarose gel electrophoresis were used to determine the concentration and integrity of DNA. PCR amplification of the V3-V4 hypervariable regions of the bacterial 16S rRNA gene was carried out using primer pairs (343 F: 5′-TACGGRAGGCAGCAG-3′;798 R:5′-AGGGTATCTAATCCT-3′). The PCR products underwent purification and were then sequenced by utilizing the Illumina MiSeq platform (Illumina Inc., San Diego, CA; OE Biotech Company, Shanghai, China) according to the standard protocols. The paired-end (PE) reads generated by MiSeq high-throughput sequencing were first assembled according to their overlapping relationships, with simultaneous quality control and filtration of the sequences implemented. After sample differentiation, species taxonomic annotation and operational taxonomic unit (OTU) clustering analysis were performed. Subsequently, statistical analyses of community composition, α-diversity, and β-diversity were conducted based on the OTU data.

Statistical analysis

The data representation was carried out as the means ± standard errors of the means (SEMs) and analysed using GraphPad Prism (version 8). Comparison between groups was established using One-way ANOVA, with a *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001 deemed statistically significant.

Compared with the control group, there was no significant difference in the laying rate among different SC dose groups in the first week, while the 1.5% SC laying rate significantly increased in the second week (Fig. 3A-B). Discussion

The decreased laying performance during the late laying phase demonstrated a significant challenge in poultry production systems worldwide. This phenomenon is primarily attributed to ovarian aging, which manifests through diminished follicular reserve and increased follicular atresia (Kpomasse et al. 2021; Khodaei et al. 2025; Adomako and Asamoah 2025). Our integrative analytical approach aligns with emerging concepts of gut-derived factors influencing distant organ function (Ziaka and Exadaktylos 2024; Wen et al. 2024). Our comprehensive investigation results suggest that dietary supplementation with SC effectively counteracts age-related ovarian dysfunction through a sophisticated dual-mechanism approach. This involves both direct stabilization of MAMs and indirect modulation of the gut-ovary axis (Fig. 11).Fig. 11A sketch diagram showing the benefits and mechanisms of SC in delayed ovarian aging and enhancement of egg production in late laying hens.Fig 11 dummy alt text

Comprehensive analysis of laying performance enhancement and systemic homeostasis improvement

The 1.5% and 2.0% dietary SC concentrations produced statistically significant improvements in all critical production parameters, including laying rate, average egg weight, average daily egg mass, and feed conversion ratio. These findings align with previous reports on the anti-aging properties of SC flavonoids (Sayed et al. 2022; Karimi Dermani et al. 2021; Sayed et al. 2021). Traditional Chinese Veterinary Medicine (TCVM) theory posits that the kidney “masters” reproduction. It is hypothesized that the therapeutic potential of SC in improving egg quality is mediated through its kidney-invigorating action, but the specific mechanisms require elucidation. The effects of different concentrarions of SC on improving egg-laying performance of aging hens are not dose-dependent; instead, it is “bell-shaped”. As for the cause of the “bell-shaped” effect of SC, we speculate that it might be due to the fact that the active components of SC bind to cell receptors, activating the signaling pathway and generating a positive effect; when the receptor occupancy reaches the optimal level, the biological effect is the greatest; however, excessive drugs no longer produce stronger signals; instead, negative feedback regulation is activated, and the body, in order to maintain homeostasis, may down-regulate the number of receptors or desensitize them, resulting in the effect weakening instead.

MDA is a commonly used indicator of oxidative damage, and SOD could reduce the activation of inflammatory mediators and oxidative stress (Mao et al. 2025; Yang et al. 2023). The production improvements of hens in the current study were consistently accompanied by substantial amelioration of systemic oxidative stress markers, as demonstrated by enhanced SOD activity and reduced MDA concentration in serum samples. This observation carries particular significance given the well-established role of oxidative stress in driving ovarian aging processes through macromolecular damage and apoptosis induction pathways (Wang et al. 2024b; Wei et al. 2025a; Li et al. 2024). Promoting follicular development while reducing its atresia has been reported to be conducive for enhancing the performance of laying hens (Hu et al. 2025). Furthermore, FSH and LH promote granulosa cell differentiation, follicle selection, and synthesis of steroid hormones (Zhang et al. 2025c). The current findings demonstrated that the levels of FSH in the serum of 1.5% SC-treated hens increased in comparison in the control hens, which may affect the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis function and create a more favorable hormonal environment for sustained follicular development and ovulation processes.

Molecular mechanisms of direct ovarian protection: MAMs stabilization and apoptosis pathway regulation

Our investigation reveals a groundbreaking cellular mechanism underlying SC's ovarian protective effects through MAMs stabilization. MAMs serve as crucial intracellular signaling platforms that coordinate calcium homeostasis, lipid metabolism, and apoptosis initiation processes (Truong et al. 2025; Sang et al. 2025; Wei et al. 2025c; Li et al. 2025a). The ultrastructural evidence from TEM analysis demonstrated remarkably preserved MAMs integrity in granulosa cells of SC-treated hens, suggesting direct visual confirmation of membrane stabilization.

The functional significance of MAMs stabilization became evident through a comprehensive analysis of the mitochondrial apoptosis pathway. The IP3R-GRP75-VDAC1 complex facilitates precisely regulated calcium transfer between endoplasmic reticulum and mitochondrial compartments (Meng et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2025a; Li et al. 2022). Proper maintenance of this complex prevents pathological calcium overload and subsequent permeability transition pore opening in mitochondria (Huo et al. 2025; Zhang et al. 2025b; Zhou et al. 2025c). Only a few researche focus on the relationship between MAMs and female reproductive function. According to Zhao et al. (Zhao et al. 2017), MAM dysfunction can increase the calcium level within mitochondria and promote apoptosis. Monobutyl phthalate-induced disruption of normal mouse oocyte function has also been associated with altered Ca2+ homeostasis and increased MAMs (Gao et al. 2022). Altered Ca2+ homeostasis influences oocyte activation and primary stages of embryonic growth (van der Reest et al. 2021). The SC mechanism can be better described by investigating the structural and functional alterations in MAMs within the ovary. Our data clearly demonstrate that SC treatment significantly increased the Bcl-2. Bcl-2 and Bax were critical molecular switch determining cellular fate decisions (Wong Fong Sang et al. 2021; Liu et al. 2024; An et al. 2025a; Han et al. 2025). This was accompanied by reduced cytochrome c release from mitochondria and diminished activation of executioner caspase-3, completing the mechanistic pathway from MAMs stabilization to apoptosis inhibition. In the present study, SC improves reproductive capacity through the inhibition of apoptosis, reduction of cytochrome c release, and stabilization of MAMs.

Elucidation of the gut-ovary axis: integrated microbial remodeling and barrier function enhancement

Our multi-omics approach uncovered a sophisticated indirect pathway through which SC exerts its anti-aging effects. The profound remodeling of gut microbiota composition, characterized by specific enrichment of Lactobacillus species and increased Firmicutes-to-Bacteroidetes ratio (Wu et al. 2025; Zhou et al. 2025a; Wang et al. 2018). Butyrate and other SCFAs function as crucial signaling molecules that maintain intestinal barrier integrity through direct regulation of tight junction protein expression and assembly (Chaudhary et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2026; Li et al. 2025b). Additionally, the coordinated upregulation of SCFA receptors GPR41 and GPR43 in ileal mucosa suggests enhanced microbial-host communication and potential activation of beneficial signaling cascades (Vargas et al. 2023; Stilling et al. 2016). Intestinal inflammation is an energy-consuming process that can disrupt the balance between energy supply and poultry demand (Jiang et al. 2025). The comprehensive improvement in intestinal barrier function represents a cornerstone mechanism for systemic health improvement. The enhanced villus architecture, upregulated ZO-1 and Occludin expression, and significantly reduced circulating levels of D-LA and LPS collectively demonstrate barrier reinforcement at multiple levels (Zhou et al. 2025b; Cao et al. 2025a; Wang et al. 2025b; Zhang et al. 2024b). By effectively preventing endotoxin translocation into systemic circulation, SC treatment attenuates chronic low-grade inflammation and oxidative stress, both established as major drivers of ovarian aging processes (Orisaka et al. 2023; Xiao et al. 2022). Our result suggest that SC selectively promoted the SCFA-producing butyrate. These SCFAs contribute to reproductive health by mitigating intestinal inflammation, reinforcing intestinal barrier integrity, and preventing systemic inflammation.

Conclusion

This study establishes dietary 1−1.5% SC supplementation as an effective and sophisticated strategy against ovarian aging in laying hens through an elaborate mechanism involving both direct MAMs stabilization and comprehensive gut-ovary axis modulation. The current results suggest not only a robust scientific foundation for SC's application as an evidence-based feed additive but also significantly advance fundamental understanding of gut-reproductive axis communication in avian species. The identification of Allitol as primary bioactive components, coupled with the elaborate characterization of their dual mechanisms of action, offers new perspectives for developing integrated strategies to combat age-related reproductive decline in agricultural production systems.

Study on damage failure for a new double-triangular truss core sandwich structure
Authors: Cao Zhongliang, Yang Sixin
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn order to solve the inherent problems of low bonding strength between face and core, a new double triangular truss core sandwich structure with reinforced frame is proposed, and the face and core are melt connected by thermoplastic composite carbon fiber reinforced polyether ether ketone (CF/PEEK). The response of the new double-triangular truss core sandwich structure under three-point bending load is investigated by both theoretical and finite element methods. Firstly, the theoretical prediction equation of bending stiffness and strength of double-triangular truss core sandwich structure is established, and the failure mechanism diagram is constructed. A valid three-point bending finite element model is established by Abaqus to predict the damage pattern of the structure and obtain the results of damage initiation location, damage process, final failure modes and load-displacement curve. The effect of geometric parameters on the bending performance of the sandwich structure was investigated. The results show that the calculated theoretical deflection curves and the ones obtained by numerical simulation are in good agreement. The average error between the two is 7.94%, verifying the reasonableness of the theoretical prediction model. The effect of geometric parameters on the bending performance of the sandwich structure was investigated. The new sandwich structure has stronger bending resistance and better ultimate load capacity than traditional honeycomb and V-shaped structures. Failure modes such as panel crumpling and crushing, fracture of reinforcement frames, buckling and fracture of core rods occur in sandwich structures under bending loads.Keywords: Truss core sandwich structure, Theoretical prediction model, Finite element analysis, Damage failure

Introduction

The sandwich structure is widely used in aviation, aerospace, ships and transportation because of its light density, high performance and good air permeability [[1], [2], [3]]. The truss core structure is inspired by bionics, where porous structures in living organisms perform special functions and can withstand large static and dynamic loads. At the beginning of the 21st century, the concept of dotted sandwich structure was introduced by the western material science community, led by Prof. Evans and others [4,5]. As people began to study truss core structures, various topological configurations such as pyramids [6], tetrahedra [7], and Kagome [8] gradually emerged. Due to the large distance between the upper and lower panels of the truss core sandwich structure, the specific stiffness and specific strength of the truss core sandwich structure are much greater than other forms of sandwich structures. Therefore, it is important to use the lightweight, high-strength truss core structure to reduce the weight and cost of the vehicle [9]. With the demand for lightweight and high strength materials, truss core structures have attracted more attention, and a lot of work has been done to study their mechanical properties.

The mechanical properties of the sandwich structure depend on the intrinsic material, geometric design, and the topology of the core [10]. Lu et al. [11] proposed a non-uniform pyramidal dot matrix sandwich structure in order to improve the bending load capacity of pyramidal truss core sandwich beams. The effects of non-uniform coefficients on the three-point bending performance of the truss core sandwich beam were analyzed from theoretical and numerical simulations. The results show that the introduction of non-uniform coefficients can significantly improve the ultimate load carrying capacity of the sandwich structure. Xu et al. [12] conducted compression tests on flax fiber-reinforced pyramidal truss core sandwich structures to investigate the effect of process defects on the mechanical properties of the structures, and established a theoretical model for compressive strength prediction and a compressive mechanism diagram, and the prediction model was in good agreement with the experimental results.

FAN et al. [13] conducted a theoretical analysis and experimental study on the three-point bending performance of Kagome type grating truss core sandwich structures. Carbon fiber reinforced grids were found to be much stiffer and stronger than foam and honeycomb, where the damage modes of buckling and debonding dominated the mechanical behavior of the sandwich structure. Feng et al. [14] conducted an in-depth study on the mechanical properties of composite truss core sandwich structures using hot compression molding process, and worked on the development of a low-cost integrated molding process. By studying the out-of-plane compression and in-plane compression properties of sandwich structures with solid, hollow and silicone rubber-containing rod cores, various theoretical predictions and failure mechanisms that may lead to damage of the sandwich structures under various static loads are given. Dastan et al. [15] found that foam-filled monolithic woven composite sandwich panels exhibited stable load displacement response and energy absorption through planar compression experiments. Rahman et al. [16]prepared 3D integrated woven spacer sandwich panels using a laminated process and tested them for impact at different energies. The damage was found to be limited to the impact region with good structural integrity. Maher et al. [17] investigated the response of corrugated sandwich structures with pseudo-elastic NiTi shaped memory alloy wires (SMA) under high-speed impact. It was found that there was a significant improvement in the energy absorption of the sandwich structure by varying the number of SMA wires. Zhang et al. [18] investigated the bending behavior of C/SiC pyramidal truss core sandwich structures under different parameters by a combination of experimental studies, theoretical analysis and numerical simulations, and analyzed the influence of geometric parameters on the failure modes. Amer et al. [19] investigated the low-velocity impact behavior of laser sintered octahedral and body-centered cubic cytosolic composite sandwich structures. They found that the cell element geometry and relative density of the core had a large effect on the energy absorption characteristics of the sandwich structure, and the negative Poisson's ratio core had the best energy absorption effect.

Rashid et al. [20] prepared specimens with different point geometries using a selective laser melting process, while three-point bending tests were performed on the specimens and their bending strength and bending modulus were analyzed. Kotzem et al. [21] prepared Ti6Al4V sandwich plates using electron beam powder bed fusion (E-PBF) method to construct a theoretical prediction model for the failure mode of the sandwich structure and characterized the deformation of the sandwich plates under three-point bending load. The theoretical prediction results and the experimental results were analyzed, and the agreement was good. Yang et al. [22] proposed a three-layer stainless steel truss core sandwich panel with a density gradient between the core layers of the sandwich panel by varying the cross-sectional dimensions of the core rods. A combination of experiments and numerical simulations was used to study the compression performance of the density gradient dot matrix sandwich panel. The results show that the gradient truss core sandwich panel has higher impact strength and energy absorption compared with the non-gradient dot matrix sandwich panel. Zheng et al. [23] conducted flat compression and three-point bending tests on composite XX-type truss core sandwich structures to investigate the failure modes and energy absorption properties of composite XX truss core sandwich structures. The results show that the compression and three-point bending damage modes of composite XX truss core sandwich structures are mainly shear damage of the core and crush damage of the panel. Xiong et al. [24,25]prepared pyramid-shaped truss core sandwich cores by hot pressing technique and explored their damage modes and mechanical properties. The bending response of curved pyramidal truss core sandwich panels was also investigated, using an interlocking method to construct aluminum cone cores. A theoretical model was developed to analyze the experiments and to develop failure criteria. Three failure modes were considered and theoretical relations were developed for predicting the failure collapse load for each mode.

The existing truss core sandwich structure has the following drawbacks: low face-core bonding strength and single topological configuration, the damage caused by slotting during preparation, which also reduce the structural mechanical properties. To address these drawbacks, a double triangular dotted core structure with a reinforcement frame is proposed in this paper based on the human fascia microstructure. It is prepared using thermoplastic composites with a hot-press melt connection between the face cores as one, which means that no new material is introduced and failure due to face core debonding [26] is avoided. And its theoretical prediction model of stiffness and strength is established under three-point bending load to verify the correctness of the theoretical prediction model. A three-point bending asymptotic damage finite element model is established in combination with the two-dimensional Hashin failure criterion to explore the failure mode of the double-triangular truss core structure with reinforcement frame.

Design of truss core

The body-fascia is a unified, large fibrous connective tissue junction that lies on the surface of the muscles, in which the muscles and bones are floating, and which has a protective effect on the muscles. Fascia not only provides scaffolding structures for the body's systems such as circulatory, nervous and lymphatic systems, but also supports the internal and external shape of the body. As shown in Fig. 1 as a microscopic schematic of fascia, the fascia in a normal healthy state shows a healthy bi-directional, crossed arrangement.

Based on the structural similarity between the fascia microstructure and the required dot matrix core, the fascia microprototype is transformed into the geometric model of the truss core under the premise of satisfying the mechanical properties. By adding reinforcement frames to overcome the shortcomings of low bonding strength of interface cores and easy stress concentration at nodes of traditional truss core sandwich panels, the structural performance of dot matrix sandwich panels is improved, and the overall performance of dot matrix sandwich panels is enhanced. Considering the advantages of fiber-reinforced thermoplastics for their light weight and high strength, unlimited shelf life, wide range of manufacturing options, excellent impact resistance, re-formability and repairability, chemical resistance, etc. The new truss core sandwich structure uses carbon fiber reinforced polyetheretherketone as the manufacturing material and the sandwich panel includes inserts, lower reinforced frame, upper reinforced frame, upper panel and lower panel.

CF/PEEK three-point bending finite element model of double-triangular truss core sandwich structure

In order to investigate the flexural performance of the node-reinforced dotted sandwich structure of thermoplastic composites, the model shown in Fig. 9 was developed for finite element analysis using the ABAQUS explicit solver. The model is modeled by S4R shell unit, and the upper and lower panels, reinforcement frame and intermediate truss core layer are connected by "Tie" method, and the panel layup is [0°/90°] n. To prevent stress concentration, rigid cylinders are set at both ends and loading points, and tangential and normal contact properties between the rigid cylinders and the structure are defined. The top cylindrical indenter retains only the vertical degrees of freedom and applies a 15 mm/s vertical downward velocity load, and the lower fixed support is fully fixed restrained. The rigid cylindrical indenter is in general surface contact with the upper panel of the structure and the fixed support with the lower panel of the structure. Comparison of the load-bearing capacity of CF/PEEK double-triangular truss core sandwich structure

Three-point bending failure mode of CF/PEEK double-triangular truss core sandwich structure

The dimensions of the sandwich structure, including length, width and thickness, and angle, have significant effects on the bending performance and failure modes of the truss core sandwich panels. In constructing the failure mechanism diagram, the sandwich panel length L, determining the height c of the core and the panel thickness t are various. The boundary conditions of the failure mechanism diagram were determined according to the bending strength theoretical analysis model in Section 2.2. However, the failure mode mapping does not accurately state the maximum failure load, but rather provides information about the most likely failure mode to occur. The failure mechanism diagram of the double-triangular truss core sandwich structure is shown in Fig. 12. Four regions are highlighted, and each region represents a failure mode. In addition, the combination of parameters for the finite element simulation (t/c = 0.0422; c/L = 0.2252) marked with an asterisk is located near the excess line between panel breakage and core damage. The results show that the panel damage failure dominates for sandwich panels with thin panels, but as the panel thickness t increases, panel and the core buckling becomes the dominant failure mode because the panels can withstand the loads that cause core damage.

CF/PEEK double-triangular truss core sandwich structure failure mechanism diagram.

The three-point bending results of the CF/PEEK double-triangular truss core sandwich structure are shown in Fig. 13, Fig. 14. Fig. 13 shows the load-displacement curve of the numerical simulation, which can predict the mechanical response of the structure in the linear phase and the final failure load of the structure more accurately. The simulation clouds corresponding to the typical load points on the load displacement graph are shown in Fig. 14. These points reveal the initial damage of the sandwich core structure and the progressive damage under continuous loading. At the initial stage of loading, the stress concentration area occurs at the connection between the upper panel and the reinforcement frame near the stress point. As can be seen from Fig. 13, point A, with the increase of displacement, load is basically in a linear growth in the initial stage of loading, reaching the first peak. At this time, the upper panel plastic deformation, the reinforced frame, and core deformation is very small as shown in Fig. 14 (a). As the displacement increases, the upper panel is bent and deformed, and the load begins to fall and falls slowly to point B in Fig. 13. At this time, the panel is flattened at the location of contact with the indenter in Fig. 14(b). As the flexion angle of the panel under pressure becomes larger, the reinforcement frame and the core insert play the main role of bearing currently. The load rises rapidly to the point C of Fig. 13. The peak load is 48.7 kN, and the corresponding displacement reaches 0.97 mm. The bending deformation of the reinforced frame under the action of the load is shown in the cloud diagram in Fig. 14(c). In Fig. 14(d), the continuous increase in displacement causes the upper panel to show unit failure deletion. The panel cracks at the center load application, the core rod at the node shows buckling failure, and the load rapidly decreases to the point in Fig. 13. With the downward displacement of the indenter, the load was almost in the plateau period, and almost all of the reinforcement frame directly below the indenter was flexed and gradually cracked, while the bending angle of the rod below increased, but the rod did not emerge obvious damage. As the rod in Fig. 14(e) shows excessive deletion of cell deformation, the load also comes to the third peak point E in Fig. 13. The panels in Fig. 14(f) show panel crush in the stressed area, with severe bending deformation in the form of V-shaped damage, which causes the extrusion deformation of the cores by the panels and reinforcement frames on both sides, and the cracking failure of the core rods becomes more obvious, and the structure finally loses its load-bearing capacity.

Effect of core angle on the bending resistance of double triangular truss core sandwich structure

In order to analyze the effect of angle on the flexural resistance of the double triangular truss core sandwich structure, β=60°,β=80°,β=90° three groups of sandwich panels with changing angles were created respectively under the same conditions of other parameters of the sandwich panels. Fig. 16 shows the damage patterns of sandwich panels with different core angles, and Fig. 17 shows the comparative load-displacement curve plots for different core angles. Comparing the damage diagram and load-displacement curve diagram, the three groups of load curves change with similar trends, and the ultimate loads are 58.602 kN, 56.1756 kN and 56.7791 kN, respectively, with the maximum ultimate peak at the core angle of 60°. The three groups of sandwich panels are all damaged as a whole, the larger the core angle the greater the plastic deformation of the core, and the smaller the angle the smaller the plastic deformation. The influence of the core angle on the ultimate bearing capacity of the sandwich panel is the largest at 60°, and slightly smaller at 80° and 90°.

Comparison of theory and numerical simulation

The comparison of the deflection obtained from the numerical simulation and the theoretical calculation is shown in Fig. 18, and the theoretical calculation agrees well with the numerical simulation. As can be seen from the figure, the results of the numerical simulation are slightly smaller than those of the theoretical calculation. This is probably because the boundary conditions in the theoretical calculation are more idealized, and the bending stiffness of the core to the central axis and the local shear deformation of the panel are neglected, so that results after the numerical simulation are slightly larger than those of the theoretical calculation. Anyway, the overall change trend of the deflection curve is still the same, and the average error between the two is 7.94%. That is, the correctness of the theory is verified.

Conclusion

(1)In this paper, a new double-triangular truss core sandwich structure with reinforced frame is proposed and the bending performance of the sandwich structure is investigated. Based on the classical ALLEN core beam theory, the stiffness and strength of the double-triangular truss core structure under three-point bending load are derived by considering the effect of horizontal displacement. The theoretical formulas of stiffness and strength of the double-triangular truss core sandwich structure under three-point bending load are obtained.

(3)An effective numerical model for three-point bending of the double-triangular truss core sandwich structure was established to predict the bending damage mode of the structure. The damage evolution process of the structure was analyzed using numerical simulation to obtain the damage modes of the structure. The structural failure is mainly due to the bending deformation of the upper panel and reinforcement frame near the midline to squeeze the core rod and accelerate the panel crush, and a large amount of matrix compression damage occurs in the crush zone leading to the final failure of the structure.

When the structure meets the strength requirements, the thickness of the panel can be appropriately reduced, the angle between the cores can be changed to improve the load-bearing capacity, and the material utilization rate can be improved.

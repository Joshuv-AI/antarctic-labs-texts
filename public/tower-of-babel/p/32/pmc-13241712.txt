Sotatercept improves right ventricular function but is associated with new or worsening pericardial effusions: a cardiovascular magnetic resonance study in intermediate-high risk pulmonary arterial hypertension
Authors: Ruta Virsinskaite, Catherine J. Beattie, Nina Karia, Tushar Kotecha, Benjamin E. Schreiber, Jay Suntharalingam, Robert V. MacKenzie Ross, Philip D. Hughes, Julian Lentaigne, Ann M. Scott-Russell, Victoria Parish, Ganesh Kasavkar, Bianca Dumitru, Gavin Thomas, Marianna Fontana, Vivek Muthurangu, J. Gerry Coghlan, Daniel S. Knight
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSotatercept is a novel drug therapy for pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) that significantly reduces clinical worsening and improves exercise capacity, functional class, and pulmonary haemodynamics. This study aims to use cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) to monitor the cardiac response to sotatercept in patients with PAH.MethodsPatients with PAH at intermediate-high or high mortality risk and already on background maximal triple PAH therapies, including parenteral prostacyclin analog for at least 3 months, were offered enrollment to a patient access program for sotatercept at the Royal Free Hospital National Pulmonary Hypertension Service. Baseline and follow-up CMR studies were performed at a median interval of 24 (interquartile range 6) weeks.ResultsEighteen out of 23 patients enrolled in the sotatercept access program underwent baseline and follow-up CMR studies (3 patients paused or discontinued sotatercept prior to 12 weeks; 2 patients were unable to undergo CMR). All 18 patients were stratified as being of intermediate-high mortality risk. Significant improvements were observed in right ventricular (RV) size (RV end-diastolic volume −34±30 mL, p = 0.0023; RV end-systolic volume −27 ± 29 mL, p = 0.0023), RV mass (Z = −2.63, p = 0.016), RV function (RV ejection fraction 5 ± 8%, p = 0.034) and right atrial size (−6 ± 4 cm2, p = 0.0023). End-systolic interventricular septal curvature also significantly improved at follow-up (Z = 2.07, p = 0.046), suggesting improvement in RV afterload. Five (28%) patients also had a new-onset (1) or larger (4) pericardial effusion without haemodynamic compromise at follow-up, despite improvements in clinical, biochemical, and CMR metrics of PAH. The median increase in pericardial effusion volume was 69% (full range 33%–203%, Z = 2.0, p = 0.043).ConclusionCMR tracks improvements in right heart chamber size, mass, and function along with metrics of RV afterload in patients with PAH receiving sotatercept. The improvements in RV size and function met or exceeded the clinically relevant minimally important differences for these CMR-derived metrics in patients with PAH. Routine interval surveillance with CMR in patients receiving sotatercept will also enable surveillance for the off-target finding of new-onset or worsening pericardial effusions.

Introduction

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is a progressive disorder of pulmonary arterial remodeling, culminating in right heart failure and conferring high morbidity and mortality. Traditionally, targeted PAH therapies promote vasodilatation to improve pulmonary haemodynamics, with limited impact on underlying vascular remodeling. Sotatercept is a novel PAH therapy that restores the balance between pro-proliferative and anti-proliferative signaling, acting as a ligand trap for selected transforming growth factor β superfamily members. In patients with PAH, sotatercept significantly reduces clinical worsening and improves exercise capacity, World Health Organization Functional Class (WHO-FC), and pulmonary haemodynamics [1]. Right ventricular (RV) response to elevated afterload is the main determinant of outcome in PAH and is most accurately and reproducibly assessed by cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging [2]. We describe CMR findings with sotatercept in a cohort of patients with PAH, all of whom are already receiving triple-targeted PAH therapy, including intravenous epoprostenol.

Method

Study population

Twenty-seven patients at the Royal Free Hospital National Pulmonary Hypertension Service met all eligibility criteria for participation in a sotatercept patient access program: (i) ≥18 years old; (ii) idiopathic, heritable, drug-induced, or connective tissue disease (CTD)-associated PAH; (iii) already on stable triple PAH therapy (phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitor, endothelin receptor antagonist and parenteral prostacyclin analog for at least 3 months); (iv) intermediate-high or high mortality risk by European Society of Cardiology/European Respiratory Society (ESC/ERS) 2022 guidelines [2]. Patients were ineligible if they met any exclusion criteria: (i) WHO-FC IV; (ii) eligible and able to enroll in ongoing clinical trials of sotatercept; (iii) participating in other ongoing clinical trials; (iv) pregnant, breastfeeding, or of childbearing potential without effective contraception.

Cardiovascular magnetic resonance imaging

Baseline and follow-up non-contrast CMR studies were performed on a 1.5T magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scanner (Aera, Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany) with CMR sequences and post-processing as described previously, specifically: (i) long-axis and short-axis cine imaging; (ii) free-breathing phase contrast CMR of thoracic great vessels; (iii) mid-cavity short-axis native myocardial T1 and T2 maps [3]. All CMR studies were reported by experienced accredited CMR observers (D.K. and R.V.), blinded to clinical outcomes. Pericardial effusion volumes were measured by drawing a region-of-interest around the effusion at end-diastole from the most basal short-axis slice that included left ventricular volumetric segmentation through to the most apical short-axis slice.

Statistical analyses

Statistical testing was performed using SPSS Statistics, version 31 (IBM Corp, Armonk, New York). Data were examined for normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Descriptive statistics were expressed as mean±standard deviation for normally distributed data and median (interquartile range) for non-normally distributed data. Differences between baseline and follow-up were assessed using paired t-test (normal data) or Wilcoxon signed-rank test (non-normal data). For Wilcoxon tests, the standardized Z statistic was reported. Raw p-values were adjusted for multiple comparisons among the primary CMR variables of interest (RV end-diastolic [EDV] and end-systolic [ESV] volumes, RV ejection fraction [EF], RV mass, right atrial (RA) area, and interventricular septal curvature) using the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure. Other secondary CMR parameters (such as left ventricular size and function, left atrial size, and native myocardial T1 and T2 values) were analyzed exploratorily and were not included in the multiple testing adjustment. Adjusted p-values <0.05 were considered statistically significant. The study complies with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by national ethics committee (IRAS project ID 248848; REC reference 19/LO/1561). All subjects provided written informed consent.

Results

Twenty-three patients were enrolled between 10th December 2024 and 28th April 2025 (4 other eligible patients declined participation): 7 (30%) idiopathic PAH (IPAH) and 16 (70%) CTD-PAH (13 systemic sclerosis, SSc; 2 systemic lupus erythematosus, SLE; 1 mixed CTD). All patients were classified as intermediate-high mortality risk. Three patients were excluded from final analysis: one underwent lung transplantation (after 4 doses, listed for transplant prior to enrollment); two (9%) had sotatercept dosing paused due to erythrocytosis.

Twenty patients (14 (70%) female, age 65 (32) years) received a single subcutaneous dose of 0.3 mg/kg sotatercept, followed by three-weekly maintenance dosing of 0.7 mg/kg, with a median follow-up of 24 (12) weeks. Significant improvements were observed in WHO-FC (Z=−3.3, p<0.001), 6-minute walk test (57 ± 75 m, p = 0.003), N-terminal pro-hormone brain natriuretic peptide (NT-pro BNP, Z = −3.0, p = 0.003) and REVEAL Lite 2 PAH risk score (Z = −3.4, p<0.001). Side-effects reported were thrombocytopaenia (2, 9%), minor bleeding (4, 17%), lichenoid drug eruption (1, 4%), and headache (3, 13%).

Eighteen (90%) patients underwent baseline and follow-up CMR (median scan interval 24 (6) weeks; 1 patient declined CMR due to claustrophobia; 1 patient required pacemaker generator change, precluding follow-up CMR). There was a small but statistically significant reduction in average intravenous epoprostenol dose at follow-up CMR (24 ± 8 ng/kg/min versus 25 ± 8 ng/kg/min, p = 0.036). Three patients had diuretics reduced or stopped between scans, and one had a loop diuretic initiated. Right ventricular size (RVEDV −34 ± 30 mL, p = 0.0023; RVESV −27 ± 29 mL, p = 0.0023), RV mass (Z = −2.63, p = 0.016), RV function (RVEF 5 ± 8%, p = 0.034), and RA size (−6 ± 4 cm2, p = 0.0023) all significantly improved with sotatercept (Table 1). Interventricular septal curvature also significantly improved (Z = 2.07, p = 0.046), suggesting reduction in RV afterload (Fig. 1) [4]. Discussion

Significant improvements in RV size, mass, and function were observed with sotatercept, as assessed by CMR, in this cohort of patients with PAH. Notably, to our knowledge, this is the first study to demonstrate an improvement in RVEF by CMR following sotatercept therapy. Moreover, the improvements in RV size and function met or exceeded clinically relevant minimally important differences for these metrics in PAH [5]. A phase 2b study of sotatercept in PAH using CMR demonstrated improvements in RV size at 24 weeks and RV mass at 48 weeks, but no improvement in RV function [6]. Importantly, all patients were stratified as intermediate-high mortality risk at baseline despite triple PAH therapies including intravenous epoprostenol. Furthermore, as a tertiary referral center for patients with SSc, our cohort is over-represented for patients with CTD-PAH. Patients with SSc-associated PAH typically have a poorer prognosis than those with IPAH [7]. Thus, the finding of a significant, clinically relevant improvement in RV function in such a prognostically adverse patient population is striking.

Additionally, the finding of new or worse pericardial effusions in over a quarter of patients undergoing follow-up CMR highlights the importance of serial cardiac imaging with sotatercept. Moreover, CMR was advantageous for quantifying changes in pericardial effusion sizes. These patients had concurrent improvements in clinical and CMR parameters, suggesting that right heart failure was not the cause of their effusions. A report of a patient with PAH, deteriorating symptoms and worsening pericardial effusion after sotatercept as part of quadruple therapy suggested high CO as the etiology [8]. Conversely, our cohort clinically improved on quadruple PAH therapy without significantly increased CO on follow-up CMR. New-onset or worsening pericardial effusions occurred in 5.1% of PAH patients receiving sotatercept in a large multicenter series, most commonly in those with CTD-PAH or receiving a prostacyclin-targeted therapy [9]. Both of these risk factors were prevalent in our patient cohort, although causality cannot be inferred in the absence of mechanistic data or a comparator group. Further evaluation of the biological mechanisms underlying this finding with sotatercept, particularly in CTD-PAH, is required. Importantly, phase 3 clinical trials of sotatercept have not reported an increased incidence of pericardial effusions [10]. Moreover, the severe morbid nature of PAH, especially in higher mortality risk patients, should confer a higher tolerance of off-target drug-associated issues.

Limitations

This is a single-center, observational study of a small patient cohort enrolled into a time-limited clinical access program, whereby all patients meeting eligibility criteria were offered sotatercept. Consequently, a comparable untreated control group could not be included in the study design. However, the primary aim was to demonstrate real-world physiological responses to sotatercept using CMR, rather than establishing efficacy in a controlled trial setting. Additionally, our cohort is enriched for patients with CTD-PAH, particularly those with SSc, in whom pericardial effusions are a recognized CMR finding [11]. While this may limit generalizability, the data remain valuable for this subgroup. Randomized controlled longitudinal studies of sotatercept using CMR in larger cohorts of patients with SSc-associated PAH would help further validate these results.

Conclusion

CMR accurately tracks improvements in right heart chamber size, RV mass, RV function, and metrics of RV afterload in patients with PAH receiving sotatercept. The reductions in RV volumes and increase in RVEF with sotatercept, when added to background triple PAH therapies including intravenous epoprostenol, meet or exceed minimally important differences for these metrics in PAH. Interval cardiac imaging should be performed in patients receiving sotatercept in order to determine the true incidence, natural history and clinical relevance of new-onset or worsening pericardial effusions.

Study on Related Factors of the Treatment Zone After Wearing Paragon CRT and Euclid Orthokeratology Lenses
Authors: Sasa Kou, Yaru Ren, Xinyu Zhuang, Yingjie Chen, Xiaofeng Zhang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Objective:To explore the influence factors of the treatment zone diameter (TZD) and its relationship with axial length growth (ALG) after wearing Paragon CRT and Euclid orthokeratology lenses.Methods:The right eye data of myopic patients wearing Paragon CRT and Euclid orthokeratology in the ophthalmology department of The First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University were retrospectively reviewed from April 2019 to October 2022. The TZD and ALG were compared between the Paragon CRT and Euclid groups. The correlation factors of TZD after wearing lens for 1 month and the relationship between the overlapping treatment zone-to-pupil area ratio and the ALG after wearing lens for 1 year were analyzed between the two groups.Results:There were 160 patients (160 eyes) in the Paragon CRT group and 155 patients (155 eyes) in the Euclid group. After wearing lens for 1 month, the TZD in the Paragon CRT group (3.72±0.37 mm) was larger than that in the Euclid group (3.26±0.37 mm) (P<0.001). The stepwise multivariate linear regression analysis showed that the eccentricity at the flattest meridians (Em) and the central corneal thickness were correlated with the TZD in both groups (P<0.05). After wearing lens for 1 year, the ALG in the Paragon CRT group (0.32±0.20 mm) was larger than that in the Euclid group (0.25±0.20 mm) (P=0.001). The stepwise multivariate linear regression analysis showed that the initial wearing age and the overlapping treatment zone area-to-pupil area ratio were correlated with the ALG in both groups (P<0.05).Conclusion:For both the Paragon CRT and Euclid orthokeratology, the wearers with thicker central corneal thickness and smaller Em usually had a smaller TZD. In both groups, the overlapping treatment zone area-to-pupil area ratio was correlated with the ALG.

It is estimated that myopia will account for 49.8% of the global population by 2050.1,2 The prevalence of myopia is higher in Asia compared with Western countries.2 The progression of myopia increases the risk of a range of ocular pathologies, such as retinal detachment, posterior scleral staphyloma, and choroidal neovascularization, which seriously affect human health and quality of life.3,4 Orthokeratology lenses have a dual role in controlling myopia.5,6 On the one hand, they can correct distance vision through the central treatment zone, and on the other hand, they can reduce axial growth by increasing myopic defocus of peripheral retina and corneal higher-order aberrations through the peripheral steep zone.7,8 Related animal studies have shown that peripheral retinal myopic defocus has a strong inhibitory effect on eye axis growth.9,10 Several previous studies have shown that peripheral retinal myopic defocus11,12 and increased higher-order aberrations13,14 can slow myopia progression.

After wearing orthokeratology, a relatively flat area is formed in the center of the front surface of the cornea under the pressure of the lens base curve, which is called the treatment zone.15 Carracedo et al.16 and Zhang et al.17 showed that wearing the orthokeratology with a smaller back optic zone diameter (BOZD) produced a smaller treatment zone diameter (TZD). The TZD is influenced not only by the design of the lens itself but also by certain biometric factors. Sun et al.18 showed that the TZD was affected by the spherical refraction and flat keratometric readings, and Tomiyama et al.19 showed that the treatment area decreased as corneal astigmatism and elevation increased. Although relevant studies have analyzed the factors influencing the treatment zone, they are relatively few. Therefore, further research is needed on how various factors affect the size of the TZD.

It has been proposed that both a small TZD16,20 and a large pupil21,22 are beneficial in slowing myopic progression. Carracedo et al.16 and Guo et al.20 achieved a smaller TZD by reducing the BOZD of the orthokeratology lens, thereby increasing peripheral retinal myopic defocus and higher-order aberrations to slow myopic progression. However, because the measurement of peripheral defocus and higher-order aberration vary with pupil size,23 pupil size needs to be taken into account when investigating the potential effect of TZD on myopia control. Pauné et al.24 and Li et al.25 found that the relative position of the treatment zone and the pupil had an impact on the myopia control effect of orthokeratology. However, the authors did not quantify the relationship between the treatment zone and pupil. When the center of the treatment zone was off the center of the pupil, it caused the treatment zone to partially overlap with the pupil area. In this study, we introduced the concept of overlapping treatment zones and quantified the relative position of the optical treatment area and pupil as the ratio of overlapping optical treatment area and pupil area to investigate the effect of the treatment zone and pupil on the ALG.

This study aimed to further explore the influence factors of the TZD and the relationship between the overlapping treatment zone area-to-pupil area ratio and ALG after wearing Paragon CRT and Euclid orthokeratology. These will provide the clinical evidence for increasing the predictability of orthokeratology in controlling axial growth and lens parameter design.

PATIENTS AND METHODS

Subjects

This study was a retrospective clinical study. Data were collected from 315 myopic patients who wore orthokeratology and followed up for more than 1 year in the ophthalmology department of the First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University from April 2019 to October 2022. There were 160 patients in the Paragon CRT group and 155 patients in the Euclid group. Only the right eye from each patient was considered for statistical analysis.

The inclusion criteria were (1) continuous wearing of orthokeratology (8∼10 hr per day and ≥6 days per week) and regular follow-up for more than 1 year; (2) initial wearing age 8 to 14 years; (3) best-corrected visual acuity ≤0.1 (logMAR value); (4) baseline spherical equivalent refraction (SER) from −1.00 to −4.00 D and difference in spherical diopter ≤1.00 D in both eyes; (5) keratometry along the flat meridian (Kf) ≥41.00 D, keratometry along the steep meridian (Ks) ≤46.00 D, and cylinder (Cyl) less than or equal to 2.00 D; (6) CCT greater than 450 μm; (7) no corneal conjunctival disease and other eye diseases; and (8) no systemic diseases affecting orthokeratology fitting. The exclusion criteria were (1) SER less than −1.00 D or greater than −4.00 D; (2) Kf less than 41.00 D, Ks greater than 46.00 D, and Cyl greater than 2.00 D; (3) wearing orthokeratology less than 8 hr per day and wearing orthokeratology less than 6 days per week; (4) using of atropine eye drops and other medications that affect refractive error during the follow-up period; and (5) failure to follow-up on time.

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University and was in line with the Declaration of Helsinki. All adolescents and their guardians signed an informed consent form on behalf of the patients after receiving full explanation.

Orthokeratology Types and Fitting Methods

The CRT design orthokeratology selected in this study was a Paragon CRT orthokeratology (Paragon Vision Sciences Inc, Gilbert, AZ) with HDS100 paflufocon D lens material, oxygen transmission coefficient of 100, refractive index of 1.442, wetting angle of 42°, BOZD of 6 mm, central thickness of 0.16 mm, and average total lens diameter of 10.6 mm. The selected vision shaping treatment (VST) design orthokeratology was a Euclid orthokeratology (Euclid Inc, Charlotte, NC) with a BOSTON Equalens II lens material, oxygen transmission coefficient of 127, refractive index of 1.435, wetting angle of 36°, and BOZD of 6.6 mm.

The parameters of the first pair of trial lenses were selected according to the subjective refraction and the Kf value. The ideal lens fit required that the lens had good central positioning, and the tears gathered into a narrow deep stain zone under the reverse curve, resulting in a "bull's eye" after fluorescence staining. At the same time, the lens had good center positioning and the motion of 1∼2 mm at normal blink. After 30 min, the visual acuity was examined and the effect was observed with a corneal topographic map. If the patient wore the lens without obvious discomfort and reached an ideal fit state, the prescription was determined and the lens was ordered. The matching process was performed by the same experienced physician.

Patients underwent regular follow-up examinations at 1 day; 1 week; 1, 3, and 6 months; and 1 year after lens wear. The re-examination contents included uncorrected visual acuity and refraction, axial length, corneal topography examination, slitlamp microscopy, lens fit assessment, corneal fluorescence staining, etc. Patients wore the lenses for at least 8 hr the night before follow-up and completed the examination within 2 hr of lens removal the following day.

Axial Length Measurement

The Lenstar LS 900 optical biometer (LS 900; Haag-Streit, Köniz) was used to detect the axial length, CCT, and white to white (WTW). When the instrument's probe is positioned approximately 6.8 cm from the subject, it automatically captures and analyzes the biological parameters of their eyes through 16 consecutive and rapid sweeps per measurement. Six measurements are taken to obtain an average value. The axial length was measured before and 1 year after lens wear (1 week after stopped wearing lens).

Corneal Topography Measurement

The TMS-4 corneal topographer (Tomey, Nagoya) was used to measure Kf, Ks, flattest meridians (Em), eccentricity at the steepest meridians (Es), Cyl, and pupil diameter in a room with 50lx illumination. Repetitive measurements were conducted by a skilled operator. Each patient's eye was examined three times, and the system automatically saved images with optimal focus. The best image was selected for statistical analysis. Mean keratometry (Km)=(Kf+Ks)/2.

Treatment Zone and Overlapping Treatment Zone Measurement

The tangential difference maps of corneal topography before and 1 month after lens wear were selected to calculate the TZD and eccentricity distance of the treatment zone.26,27 The method was as follows: eight points with a curvature variation value of (0±0.05) D (2 points in each quadrant) were selected at the edge of the central corneal flattening zone. The coordinates of the eight points were recorded and entered into MATLAB commercial mathematical software (Matlab R2018b; Mathworks, Inc, Natick, MA). The best-fitting circle was calculated according to the circle fitting function, and the diameter of the best-fitting circle was regarded as the TZD. The center of the best-fitting circle is regarded as the center of the treatment zone, and the distance between the corneal vertex and the center of the treatment zone is the eccentricity distance (Fig. 1).

Measurement diagram of treatment zone in corneal topography.

In this study, the treatment zone covering the pupil area was defined as the overlapping treatment zone. The pupil area and the overlapping treatment zone area were calculated based on the measured pupil diameter, TZD, and eccentric distance. Circle 1 (solid red line) is considered the pupil, circle 2 (dashed green line) is considered the treatment zone, and the blue area is considered the overlapping treatment zone. Pupil radius r1=pupil diameter/2, treatment zone radius r2=treatment zone diameter/2, and the treatment zone eccentricity distance is considered the distanced between the centers of the two circles. Pupil area=πr12. The area of the overlapping treatment zone is calculated in three cases: when the treatment zone is completely within the pupil, the area of the overlapping treatment zone=πr22; when the treatment zone is completely outside the pupil, the area of overlapping treatment zone=0; and when the treatment zone partially covers the pupil, α1=arccos ((r12+d2−r22)/2 r1 d), α2=arccos ((r22+d2−r12)/2 r2 d), and the area of overlapping treatment zone=α1 r12+α2 r22−r1 dsinα1 (Fig. 2).

Measurement diagram of pupil area and overlapping treatment zone area: circle 1 (solid red line) is the pupil, O1 is the center of the pupil, r1 represents the radius of circle 1, angle AO1O2 is α1, circle 2 (dashed green line) is the treatment zone, O2 is the center of the treatment zone, r2 represents the radius of circle 2, angle AO2O1 is α2, the two circles intersect at two points A and B, and the blue area represents the overlapping treatment zone.

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 25.0 software (IBM Corp, Armonk, NY). The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was applied to evaluate the normality of the data distribution. Quantitative data were expressed as mean±SD. T tests or Mann–Whitney U test and chi-square tests were used for between-group comparison accordingly. Univariate linear regression analysis was used to investigate the correlation between ALG as well as TZD and different factors, and the difference in the slope between the two groups was analyzed by covariance analysis. The correlated factors with P less than 0.05 were included in the stepwise multivariate linear regression analysis, The strength of association for significant variables is represented using β values, 95% confidence intervals of β values, adjusted R2 values, and P values. P less than 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

RESULTS

Basic Data

There were a total of 160 eyes from 160 patients in the Paragon CRT group, including 73 male patients and 87 female patients. The mean initial wearing age was 10.30±1.61 years (range 8.06–13.83 years), and the average SER was −2.50±0.77 D (range −1.00 and −4.00 D). There were a total of 155 eyes from 155 patients in the Euclid group, including 70 male patients and 85 female patients. The mean initial wearing age was 10.75±1.82 years (range 8.00–14.00 years), and the average SER was −2.72±0.85 D (range −1.00 and −4.00 D). There were no statistically significant differences in each parameter between the two groups (P>0.05). The data are presented as the mean±SD in Table 1.

Influencing Factors of Treatment Zone Diameter

After wearing lens for 1 month, the TZD was 3.70±0.36 mm in the Paragon CRT group and 3.26±0.37 mm in the Euclid group. The Paragon CRT group had a larger TZD than the Euclid group (t=10.805, P<0.001) (Fig. 3A).

Comparison of TZD (A) and ALG (B) in both Paragon CRT and Euclid groups.

Univariate linear regression analysis showed that TZD was positively correlated with Em (β=0.300, P<0.001; β=0.290, P<0.001) and negatively correlated with CCT (β=−0.165, P=0.027; β=−0.221, P=0.030) in both the Paragon CRT and Euclid groups. TZD was positively correlated with SER (β=0.129, P=0.043) in the Paragon CRT group, whereas it had no significant correlation in the Euclid group (β=0.111, P=0.192). In both the Paragon CRT and Euclid groups, TZD and initial wearing age (β=−0.147, P=0.148; β=−0.110, P=0.194), Kf (β=−0.006, P=0.936; β=0.016, P=0.847), Ks (β=−0.017, P=0.816; β=0.037, P=0.664), Es (β=0.056, P=0.457; β=0.013, P=0.117), pupil diameter (β=−0.050, P=0.501; β=0.005, P=0.950), Cyl (β=−0.028, P=0.709; β=0.062, P=0.732), and WTW (β=−0.059, P=0.428; β=−0.093, P=0.274) had no significant correlation. There was no significant difference in the slope between the two groups by covariance analysis (P>0.05) (Table 2).

Comparison of Axial Length Growth After Wearing Lens for 1 Year

After wearing lens for 1 year, the axial length increased significantly from 24.58±0.61 mm to 24.90±0.61 mm in the Paragon CRT group (t=−21.387, P<0.001) and from 24.62±0.64 mm to 24.87±0.65 mm in the Euclid group (t=−15.416, P<0.001). Axial length growth was 0.32±0.20 mm in the Paragon CRT group and 0.25±0.20 mm in the Euclid group, which was a statistically significant difference between the two groups (t=3.250, P=0.001). This indicated a larger ALG in the Paragon CRT group 1 year after lens wear compared with the Euclid group (Fig. 3B).

Relationship Between Axial Length Growth and Treatment Zone Diameter and Overlapping Treatment Zone Area-to-Pupil Area Ratio

After wearing lens for 1 month, the overlapping treatment zone area-to-pupil area ratio was 0.72 (0.59, 0.81) in the Paragon CRT group and 0.57±0.15 in the Euclid group. Univariate linear regression analysis showed that ALG in both the Paragon CRT and Euclid groups was negatively correlated with the initial wearing age (β=−0.425, P<0.001; β=−0.418, P<0.001) and positively correlated with Km (β=0.220, P=0.003; β=0.356, P<0.001), TZD (β=0.257, P<0.001; β=0.241, P=0.028) and overlapping treatment zone area-to-pupil area ratio (β=0.251, P=0.001; β=0.191, P=0.005). Axial length growth was not significantly correlated with pupil diameter (β=−0.115, P=0.124) and WTW (β=−0.136, P=0.068) in the Paragon CRT group, whereas it was negatively correlated with pupil diameter (β=−0.133, P=0.048) and WTW (β=−0.175, P=0.038) in the Euclid group. In both the Paragon CRT and Euclid groups, ALG and SER (β=0.091, P=0.221; β=0.036, P=0.673), Km (β=−0.076, P=0.309; β=0.051, P=0.546), Cyl (β=0.027, P=0.723; β=0.082, P=0.336), and CCT (β=−0.072, P=0.332; β=−0.055, P=0.697) had no significant correlation. There was no significant difference in the slope between the two groups by covariance analysis (P>0.05) (Table 4).

DISCUSSION

As a nonsurgical, reversible physical correction of ametropia, orthokeratology has been extensively validated for its safety and effectiveness in controlling the progression of myopia in adolescents.6,28 At present, there are two designs of orthokeratology in the world: corneal refraction therapy (CRT) and VST.

The Paragon CRT orthokeratology is a CRT design lens, which mainly adopts a three-zone design, including base curve, return zone depth, and landing zone angle. Euclid orthokeratology are designed as VST lenses, which mainly adopt a four-zone, multiarc design, including base curve, reverse curve, fitting curve, and peripheral curve. The inverse geometry design of the two kinds of lenses makes the central cornea flat and the middle peripheral cornea steep to form a myopia curve zone, which can control the progression of myopia.

The relatively flat area formed in the central part of the anterior corneal surface under pressure from the base curve of the lens is called the treatment zone. In this study, the mean TZD of the Paragon CRT group (3.70±0.36 mm) was larger than that of the Euclid group (3.26±0.37 mm). Yang et al.29 and Marcotte et al.30 showed a larger corneal treatment zone with wearing CRT-designed orthokeratology than with wearing VST-designed orthokeratology in their study. In this study, the BOZDs of Paragon CRT orthokeratology and Euclid orthokeratology were both 6 mm. The reason for the difference in TZD between the two groups may be related to the fact that the two orthokeratology have different designs of reversal zones; the height and width of which determine the spatial volume of epithelial cells that migrate centrally to the midperiphery.31

Treatment zone diameter is not only affected by the design of the lens itself16,20,32 after wearing orthokeratology but also related to corneal biomechanics, corneal morphology, central astigmatism,19 and other factors. Univariate linear regression analysis showed that TZD was positively correlated with SER in the Paragon CRT group. This may be because the higher the SER, the higher ametropia needed to be corrected and the greater the changes in corneal morphology caused by wearing orthokeratology, thereby reducing the tension of the central corneal tissue and the stronger the tension of the peripheral cornea on the central corneal tissue, which eventually leads to the smaller TZD.15,33 In both the Paragon CRT group and the Euclid group, univariate linear regression analysis and stepwise multivariate linear regression analysis showed that TZD was associated with Em and CCT. The steeper the cornea from central to peripheral before wearing orthokeratology, the smaller the TZD at 1 month after wearing orthokeratology, which is related to the steeper peripheral cornea in a smaller Em. Because the thicker cornea has stronger resistance,34 the thicker the CCT before wearing the lens, the smaller the TZD after wearing the lens.

In the study by Guo et al.,20 Zhang et al.,17 and Paune et al.,24 a smaller treatment zone was obtained by reducing the BOZD of VST orthokeratology, and the results showed that the axial length of patients wearing smaller BOZD lenses grew slower. Zhang et al.35 also found that the smaller treatment zone of VST-designed and CRT-designed orthokeratology can slow down the axial growth of myopic children. In this study, patients wearing a Paragon CRT orthokeratology had a larger TZD and a larger ALG, and the results from the study by Lu et al.36 were consistent with the results of this study. In both the Paragon CRT group and the Euclid group, univariate linear regression analysis showed that the smaller the TZD, the smaller the ALG. However, after stepwise multivariate linear regression analysis, TZD was no longer associated with ALG. Therefore, TZD may be an important factor affecting ALG, but the effect of TZD on ALG is also affected by other factors.

Pupil diameter may be a potential factor affecting the myopia control effect of orthokeratology. Studies on the correlation between pupil diameter and ALG after wearing orthokeratology have been reported in several literature, and the results are inconsistent.21,37,38 In this study, there was no correlation between pupil diameter and ALG after lens wear in the Paragon CRT group. In the Euclid group, univariate linear regression analysis showed that the larger the pupil diameter, the smaller the ALG after wearing orthokeratology. However, the pupil diameter was no longer correlated with ALG after stepwise multivariate linear regression analysis. Although the pupil diameter is not one of the independent factors affecting ALG, the effect of the deviation of pupil center from the center of the treatment zone on ALG cannot be ignored. The center of the treatment zone deviated from the center of the pupil, resulting in partial overlap between the treatment zone and the pupil area. In the study by Paune et al.,24 the ALG of steep middle circumferential ring in the pupil range was 0.13 mm/year less than that of steep middle circumferential ring outside the pupil range. Therefore, not all peripheral incident light can normally enter the eye and focus in front of the peripheral retina, which is affected to a certain extent by the size and location of the pupil.21 The steep midperiphery area next to the treatment zone and the pupil diameter simultaneously determine the amount of effective defocus light entry.

In this study, the effect of the deviation between the treatment zone and the pupil area on ALG was investigated by calculating the ratio of the overlapping treatment zone area to the pupil area. In the Paragon CRT group and the Euclid group, both univariate and multivariate linear regression analyses showed that the smaller the overlapping treatment zone area-to-pupil area ratio, the smaller the ALG. This may be because when the overlapping treatment zone area-to-pupil area ratio is smaller, more peripheral steep zones fall within the pupil, resulting in more peripheral retinal myopic defocus and higher-order aberrations. As the peripheral retinal defocus and higher-order aberrations increase, the control effect of axial growth is better.22 In the study by Li et al.,25 the smaller the treatment zone is relative to the pupil area, the smaller the ALG is after wearing VST orthokeratology, which is similar to the results of this study. The researchers think that the smaller the treatment zone is compared with the pupil area, the steeper the relative corneal refractive power distribution induced in the pupil, the higher corneal high-order aberrations and horizontal coma aberration are significantly increased, and better myopia control effect is achieved.

This study had three principal limitations. First, because the factors affecting pupil diameter are very complex,39,40 how to comprehensively and objectively reflect pupil area still needs to be further studied. Second, this is a nonrandomized controlled study that may have influenced the differences between the two groups. Third, only two brands of orthokeratology lenses were discussed. In the future, we will continue to conduct further randomized controlled studies on different brands of orthokeratology lenses.

CONCLUSIONS

Both the Paragon CRT and Euclid orthokeratology showed that the thicker the CCT and the smaller the Em before wearing orthokeratology, the smaller the TZD of the wearers. In both groups, the overlapping treatment zone area-to-pupil area ratio of the two designs of orthokeratology was correlated with ALG.

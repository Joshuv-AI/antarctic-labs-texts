Identification and growth features of developmental delay with macrocephaly caused by a novel TRIO variant affecting the second SH3 domain
Authors: Si-Yuan Zhang, Jie-Yuan Jin, Lei Zeng, Rui-Chao Niu, Xia Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTrio Rho guanine nucleotide exchange factor (TRIO) encodes the guanine nucleotide exchange factor (GEF) for RHOA and RAC1 GTPases, which plays a critical role in neurodevelopment. Pathogenic variants in the TRIO gene are predominantly associated with two autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorders: intellectual developmental disorder 44 with microcephaly (MRD44) and intellectual developmental disorder 63 with macrocephaly (MRD63). However, the genotype–phenotype correlation of TRIO-related disorders and the impacts of TRIO variants on early-life disease progression remain unclear.MethodsIn this study, we recruited a male infant with developmental delay and macrocephaly and chronologically detailed his growth from birth. Whole-exome sequencing was performed to identify genetic variants; three-dimensional protein modeling was employed to assess the pathogenicity of these variants, and previously reported TRIO variants were summarized.ResultWe identified a novel TRIO missense variant (NM_007118.4: c.7738 A > T, p.I2580F; chr5:14492781 A > T/hg19) in the patient, and the variant was positioned in the second Src homology 3 (SH3) domain. Bioinformatic and three-dimensional protein modeling evidence all support the p.I2580F variant as likely pathogenic. Additionally, we systematically collated and summarized previously reported TRIO gene variants.ConclusionOur findings broaden the variant landscape of TRIO, establish a correlation between macrocephaly and TRIO variants within the second SH3 domain, provide the first detailed growth chart for a patient with TRIO-associated macrocephaly, and deepen our understanding of developmental impairments attributed to TRIO variants.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12920-026-02422-6.Keywords: TRIO, Developmental delay, Macrocephaly, Src homology 3 domain

Introduction

Trio Rho guanine nucleotide exchange factor (TRIO) encodes the guanine nucleotide exchange factor (GEF) of RHOA and RAC1 GTPases. It catalyzes the hydrolysis of the gamma phosphate bond in guanine triphosphate (GTP) to form guanine diphosphate (GDP), a critical step in signal transduction [1, 2]. TRIO plays a pivotal role in cell migration and growth, neurogenesis, synapse formation, and actin cytoskeleton reorganization. Due to these functions, TRIO is considered a gene associated with neurodevelopmental disorders [1, 3]. Its variants have been reported to be associated with intellectual disability, developmental delay, and autism spectrum disorders (ASD) [4, 5].

Autosomal dominant intellectual developmental disorder-44 with microcephaly (MRD44; OMIM 617061) and autosomal dominant intellectual developmental disorder-63 with macrocephaly (MRD63; OMIM 618825) are typical TRIO-related diseases [6]. Both conditions present with global developmental delay, intellectual impairment, speech and language impairment, characteristic facial dysmorphism, and limb malformations. Notably, MRD44 is typically characterized by microcephaly, whereas macrocephaly represents a prominent clinical hallmark of MRD63 [7–9]. Barbosa et al. demonstrated that missense variants within the seventh spectrin repeat of TRIO lead to severe intellectual disability and macrocephaly (MRD63) [8]. By contrast, those occurring in the first GEF domain (GEFD1) are associated with milder intellectual disability and microcephaly (MRD44) [8]. While some genotype-phenotype correlations have been established, identification of TRIO variants in other domains and their associated phenotypes should be further investigated. Moreover, descriptions of TRIO-related conditions typically focus on symptomatic presentations during clinical visits. As a developmental disorder, its tracking from birth has not been reported, and the impact of TRIO variants on disease progression remains largely unexplored.

In this study, we described the growth trajectory of a male infant with developmental delay and macrocephaly. We identified a TRIO missense variant (NM_007118.4: c.7738 A > T, p.I2580F; chr5:14492781 A > T/hg19) in him and analyzed its pathogenicity using three-dimensional protein modeling. We hypothesize that this variant results in a gain-of-function mutation of TRIO. It causes excessive activation of the downstream RAC1 signaling pathway, which disturbs central nervous system development, embryonic limb bud formation, and chondrocyte differentiation. These changes ultimately lead to macrocephaly, neurodevelopmental abnormalities, and skeletal malformations. We also collected known TRIO variants. Our findings expanded the genetic spectrum of TRIO variants, suggested a link between macrocephaly and the variant in the second Src homology 3 (SH3) domain, and deepened our understanding of impairments caused by TRIO variants.

Materials and methods

Subjects

A baby boy (Fig. 1A) with developmental delay and macrocephaly was recruited from Hunan Province at Xiangya Hospital, Central South University (Hunan, China). Child growth assessments conformed to the “Growth standard for children under 7 years of age (2022)” issued by the National Health Commission of the People’s Republic of China [10]. The developmental levels were evaluated using the Gesell Developmental Schedules (GDS) [11, 12].

Standard protocol approvals, registrations, and patient consents

This research received approval from the Review Board of Xiangya Hospital, Central South University (202103427). Since the proband was a minor, his legal guardian signed the written informed consent form, explicitly agreeing to the proband’s participation in this study and authorizing the research team to publish the relevant clinical data. This study adhered to the ethical standards of the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its amendments.

Whole-exome sequencing and analysis

Genomic DNA was prepared using the DNeasy Blood and Tissue Kit (Qiagen, Valencia, CA, USA, Cat. No.69506) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Whole-exome sequencing (WES) was performed by Berry Genomics Company Limited (Chengdu, China), as previously outlined [13]. Variant annotation utilized databases such as MutationTaster (http://www.mutationtaster.org), PolyPhen-2 (http://genetics.bwh.harvard.edu/pph2), SIFT (http://provean.jcvi.org/index.php), CADD (https://cadd.gs.washington.edu/snv), GnomAD (http://gnomad.broadinstitule.org), 1000G (https://www.internationalgenome.org), and OMIM (https://www.omim.org). Intellectual developmental disorder-related genes were collected from OMIM, PubMed (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), and lists from Berry (Table SI). TRIO protein sequences of multiple species from NCBI (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/protein/?term=Trio) were used for conservation analysis. MetaDome (https://stuart.radboudumc.nl/metadome/dashboard) was deployed to evaluate how TRIO withstands functional genetic variants. Variants were classified for pathogenicity according to the standards and guidelines of the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) [14].

Sanger sequencing

The sequence of the TRIO variant (NM_007118.4: c.7738 A > T, p.I2580F) and its flanking regions were obtained from the NCBI database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/7204). Primers (TRIO-F: 5′-CTGCCTTTCTCTGTCTTCATCT-3′; TRIO-R: 5′-CCGTTACTGTCAAGCACTCA-3′) were designed using Primer3 software. PCR amplification was performed with 2× Power Taq PCR MasterMix (BioTeke, Beijing, China, Cat. No. PR1710). The reaction conditions were as follows: initial denaturation at 95 °C for 10 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 10 s, 59 °C for 20 s, and 72 °C for 20 s. Purified PCR products were subjected to Sanger sequencing using Ex Taq DNA polymerase, 10× Ex Taq Buffer, and dNTP mixture (TaKaRa Bio, Beijing, China, Cat. No. RR001A). Sequencing was conducted on an ABI 3100 Genetic Analyzer (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA). Sanger sequencing service was provided by BioSune Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China).

Three-dimensional modeling

SWISS-MODEL was applied to predict the structure of TRIO (residues 2287–3097), with PDB 6D8Z as the template for residues 2287–2440 and the AlphaFold model (UniProt: O75962) for residues 2441–3097. Since this region contains the target SH3 domain and its adjacent domains, it enables a reliable assessment of conformational changes caused by the variant. Subsequent modification and refinement of the mutant model were carried out in PyMOL.

Results

Case description

The proband is a 2-year-and-2-month-old male presenting with global developmental delay (Fig. 1A). His parents are non-consanguineous, both in good health, and report no family history of developmental delay. The proband was born at term (40 weeks of gestation) via spontaneous vaginal delivery with no history of birth asphyxia. Birth measurements were length 50 cm and weight 3.2 kg, both within the normal reference range. Newborn screening for common metabolic disorders was negative.

Abnormalities were noted in the proband’s early growth and development trajectory (Fig. 1B). At 4 months of age, his head circumference was 43.4 cm, exceeding the normal mean by 1–2 standard deviations (SD) (Fig. 1C, Table 1). At 6 months of age, his length was only 65.5 cm, which was 1–2 SD below the normal mean (Fig. 1D; Table 1). Assessment using GSD at 20 months of age indicated moderate developmental delay, with the following Developmental Quotients (DQ): Adaptability 46.0, Gross Motor 51.0, Fine Motor 49.0, Language 41.0, and Personal-Social 65.0 (Table 2). The proband achieved independent ambulation at 2 years of age and currently produces vocalizations but cannot form two-word combinations.

Current physical examination findings are as follows: length 84.0 cm (2–3 SD below the normal mean), weight 12.0 kg (below the age-appropriate median), and head circumference 50.5 cm (1–2 SD above the normal mean) (Fig. 1C and E, Table 1). Dysmorphic features are present, characterized by mild hypertelorism, a high-arched palate, an elongated philtrum, and brachycephaly (Fig. 1F). Additional clinical findings included prominent rib flaring and bilateral slender fingers and toes.

Ancillary investigations revealed the following: Digital radiography (DR) of the bilateral heels showed irregular morphology of the tali, unevenly decreased bone mineral density, and coarse bony margins; the tarsal bones were unossified, and the distal phalanges of both feet were either unossified or shortened (Fig. 1G). An electroencephalogram (EEG) performed at an external institution demonstrated scattered sharp-and-slow wave activity in the right frontal region during sleep. Brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed a brachycephalic skull shape, enlargement of the extra-axial spaces in the bilateral frontotemporal regions, and dilation of the bilateral lateral ventricles and the third ventricle.

Genetic analysis

Genetic testing was initiated at the mother’s request to identify the underlying causes of the proband’s symptoms. WES generated 9.95 GB of data covering 99.78% target region, with 98.62% of these regions sequenced to a depth of at least 10 × (85.4% with a depth of at least 50×). A total of 77,663 variants were detected in the proband. Following our screening strategy, we identified two variants located in distinct genes associated with intellectual developmental disorders (Fig. 2A). One was a KMT2B variant (NM_014727.3: c.7317 C > G, p.I2439M; chr19:36227832 C > G/hg19) and another was a TRIO variant (NM_007118.4: c.7738 A > T, p.I2580F; chr5:14492781 A > T/hg19) (Table 3). Sanger sequencing verified that the KMT2B variant in the proband was inherited from his unaffected father (data not shown) and that the TRIO variant was de novo (Fig. 2B).

The TRIO variant (c.7738 A > T, p.I2580F) was de novo (PS2) and absent in GnomAD and 1000G (PM2). It was predicted to be disease-causing using MutationTaster, Polyphen-2, SIFT, and CADD (it being predicted to be pathogenic when the score is greater than 25.3) [15], and the amino acid (AA) site p.I2580 was highly conserved evolutionarily (Fig. 2C) and predicted as intolerant to functional genetic variation using MetaDome (Fig. 2D; PP3). Thus, based on the ACMG standards and guidelines (PS2, PM2, PP3; Table 3), we classified the variant as Likely Pathogenic, which explains the proband’s clinical manifestations.

Moreover, three-dimensional structural modeling showed that the p.I2580F variant is located within the β-sheet region of the TRIO protein. The isoleucine-to-phenylalanine substitution enables the formation of a new hydrogen bond with Met2556 (M2556). In addition, the introduced aromatic side chain interacts with the adjacent aromatic residue Phe2589 (F2589), increasing local steric hindrance and triggering structural perturbation, which ultimately disrupts the integrity of the local β-sheet structure (Fig. 2E).

Discussion

In this study, WES identified a novel missense variant in the TRIO gene (NM_007118.4: c.7738 A > T, p.I2580F) in the proband, and its pathogenicity was verified using multiple bioinformatic tools. Correlating the genetic result with the patient’s key clinical features, including macrocephaly, global developmental delay, facial dysmorphism, and limb malformations, we diagnosed the patient with MRD63. This molecular diagnosis guides individualized rehabilitation, long-term monitoring, and prognostic evaluation. It also enables family-wide carrier screening, genetic counseling, and prenatal assessment to lower the risk of disease relapse. In addition, this variant broadens the TRIO mutational spectrum and advances our understanding of genotype–phenotype correlations in MRD63.

MRD63 exhibits marked clinical heterogeneity. Almost all affected individuals present with global developmental delay, moderate-to-severe intellectual disability, and severe language impairment [8, 16]. Macrocephaly, the core phenotypic hallmark, is observed in 90%–95% of cases, and ventriculomegaly on cranial MRI is detected in 40%–60% of patients [8, 16]. Additional manifestations include epilepsy, facial dysmorphism, limb malformations, and autistic features [8, 16]. The ankle and talar dysplasia identified in our proband is extremely rare (prevalence < 10%), further enriching the phenotypic landscape of TRIO-associated MRD63 [6]. This disorder has delayed penetrance and is frequently underdiagnosed in early infancy. Whole-exome sequencing facilitates early molecular diagnosis and personalized intervention. In contrast to previous cross-sectional studies, we characterized the patient’s complete postnatal developmental trajectory, yielding novel longitudinal evidence for this rare neurodevelopmental disorder.

MRD63 is an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder primarily caused by de novo heterozygous missense variants in the TRIO gene [16]. TRIO regulates the RAC1 signaling pathway and participates in embryonic neuronal development, synaptogenesis, and cytoskeleton remodeling [7, 17]. To date, at least 94 pathogenic TRIO variants have been documented in the HGMD and ClinVar databases, as well as in multiple clinical studies (Fig. 3A) [6–8, 16]. These variants predominantly cluster within the spectrin repeat region and GEFD1 domain, with missense variants accounting for approximately half of all pathogenic mutations (Fig. 3B). TRIO pathogenic variants exhibit striking domain-dependent phenotypic specificity: gain-of-function (GoF) variants in the spectrin repeat region and SH3 domain result in MRD63, characterized by macrocephaly, whereas loss-of-function (LoF) variants in the GEFD1 domain, which consists of the DH1 and PH1 subdomains, lead to microcephaly-associated MRD44 [8].

Previous studies have demonstrated that pathogenic variants in the SH3 domain alter the spatial conformation of TRIO and relieve intramolecular autoinhibition, thereby inducing a gain-of-function state and persistent overactivation of downstream RAC1 signaling [7, 18]. Excessive RAC1 activity disrupts the dynamic balance of neuronal proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis [19]. Aberrant expansion of neural progenitor cells increases overall brain volume, causing macrocephaly, while impaired neuronal migration and immature synaptic circuitry contribute to ventriculomegaly and structural brain abnormalities [7, 8, 18]. These developmental disturbances ultimately manifest as global developmental delay, moderate-to-severe intellectual disability, and language impairment, which constitute the core clinical features of MRD63 [7, 8, 18]. The novel TRIO p.I2580F variant identified in the present study is located within the second SH3 domain. To date, only one missense variant (p.T2563M) in this domain has been reported, which was detected in a patient with severe intellectual disability [5]. The p.I2580F variant substitutes isoleucine for phenylalanine within a β-sheet region. The bulky aromatic side chain of phenylalanine introduces significant local steric hindrance and interacts with the adjacent Phe2589 residue, disrupting the native microenvironment and structural stability of the β-sheet. Notably, beyond its central role in neurodevelopment, RAC1 also participates in embryonic limb bud formation and chondrocyte differentiation [20]. Accordingly, excessive RAC1 activation induced by TRIO gain-of-function variants can perturb normal skeletal morphogenesis, providing a mechanistic explanation for the rare ankle malformation observed in the present patient. We therefore hypothesize that the p.I2580F variant alters TRIO tertiary structure, relieves intramolecular autoinhibition, and causes constitutive RAC1 hyperactivation, ultimately leading to both typical neurodevelopmental phenotypes and uncommon skeletal abnormalities of MRD63. Further functional experiments are required to validate this pathogenic mechanism.

We systematically delineated the pathogenic profiles and molecular mechanism of the TRIO p.I2580F variant for the first time, broadening the phenotypic and genotypic spectrum of MRD63. Given the limitation of a single case, the results are not universally applicable, yet they provide valuable clues for subsequent investigations.

Conclusion

In conclusion, in this study, we detailed the clinical progression and growth trajectory from birth of a male infant, who presented with developmental delay and macrocephaly. We identified a novel TRIO variant (c.7738 A > T, p.I2580F) in him, analyzed the pathogenicity using three-dimensional protein modeling, and documented known TRIO variants. Our report offers new insights into the research advances of TRIO-associated disorders and deepens current understanding of their clinical manifestations. Our identification and collation of variants broadened the pathogenic mutational spectrum of TRIO, established an association between MRD63 and TRIO variants within the second SH3 domain, and facilitated molecular diagnosis and genetic counseling for individuals carrying TRIO variants.

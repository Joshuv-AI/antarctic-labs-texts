Risk factors for preoperative anemia in orthognathic surgery patients undergoing autologous blood donation: A cross-sectional study
Authors: Sayaka Furuhashi, Atsushi Abe, Atsushi Nakayama, Hiroki Inoue, Ryosuke Yoshizaki, Hiroshi Furuta, Tetsushi Oguma, Shoya Mizuno, Arihiro Nakamura
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In Japan, preoperative autologous blood donation (ABD) is widely performed in orthognathic surgery to avoid allogeneic transfusion. Although previous studies have examined postoperative hemoglobin (Hb) fluctuations and the safety of autologous blood donations, few have investigated Hb levels at hospital admission or the optimal timing between blood storage and admission. This study aimed to identify risk factors for preoperative anemia in patients undergoing orthognathic surgery with ABD. Seventy-five patients scheduled for sagittal split ramus osteotomy between January 2021 and December 2023 were retrospectively analyzed. Four hundred milliliters of autologous blood were collected from patients with Hb ≥ 11 g/dL. Anemia was defined as Hb < 13 g/dL at admission. Logistic regression was performed for sex; smoking status; body mass index; Hb levels at donation; and the timing of the interval from donation to admission. At admission, 41 patients (55%) presented with anemia. Independent risk factors for anemia were Hb < 14.2 g/dL at donation (odds ratio [OR]: 26.20, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 3.71–185.00, P < .05) and an interval ≤ 22 days between donation and admission (OR: 6.54, 95% CI: 1.11–38.70, P < .05). Sex had minimal impact. The 22-day threshold corresponded with the 2 to 3-week erythropoietic recovery period, whereas longer storage may have compromised blood quality due to storage lesions. Ensuring Hb ≥ 14.2 g/dL at donation and allowing ≥ 22 days between donation and admission may reduce the risk of preoperative anemia. These findings provide practical guidance for perioperative management in orthognathic surgery and may inform transfusion strategies in regions where ABD remains common.

1. Introduction

In orthognathic surgery, intraoperative bleeding is a significant concern, and injury to the branches of the inferior alveolar artery is considered one of the most serious complications. Massive bleeding may necessitate blood transfusion, and perioperative blood management is essential to ensure patient safety. Transfusion options include autologous and allogeneic blood. While allogeneic transfusion is effective, it carries risks of complications such as viral infections (e.g., HIV), acute lung injury, hemolytic reactions, anaphylaxis, and immune responses to alloantigens.[1]

Therefore, in elective surgeries such as orthognathic procedures, preoperative autologous blood donations (ABD) have been widely employed to avoid allogeneic transfusions.[2,3] However, ABD decreases preoperative hemoglobin (Hb) levels and may induce anemia on the day of surgery. Preoperative anemia increases the risk of severe complications, including delayed wound healing, postoperative infection, sepsis, and impaired oxygen delivery to vital organs. Therefore, minimizing its impact is of paramount importance.

In Western countries, the necessity of ABD has declined substantially due to reductions in intraoperative blood loss and improvements in the safety of allogeneic transfusions.[1,4–6] In contrast, ABD continues to be widely performed in Japan and other Asian countries, influenced by institutional protocols, patient preferences, and cultural concerns regarding allogeneic transfusion.[7–11] Under these circumstances, identifying risk factors for preoperative anemia when implementing ABD is clinically important.

Previous studies have emphasized perioperative changes in Hb after autologous blood transfusions and confirmed the safety and efficacy of ABD. However, few reports have focused on Hb at admission, the optimal interval between donation and admission, or perioperative fluctuations in Hb levels.[7–11]

The aim of this study was to identify risk factors for preoperative anemia in orthognathic surgery patients undergoing ABD, with particular attention to Hb levels at the time of donation; and the effect of the interval from donation to admission.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Study design

This study was conducted as a cross-sectional study.

2.2. Setting

Before autologous blood donation and transfusion, written informed consent was obtained from each patient in the outpatient clinic of the Department of Blood and Blood Products. Subsequently, Hb levels were evaluated, and preoperative blood tests were performed to screen for anemia and other hematological abnormalities. There was no age restriction for blood storage; however, eligibility criteria required a body weight of at least 45 kg for men and 40 kg for women, and an Hb level ≥ 11 g/dL on the day of donation.

On the day of collection, after confirming that vital signs were stable, a physician performed a phlebotomy. The maximum volume collected at 1 time was 400 mL. Autologous blood was stored in refrigerated bags containing citrate-phosphate-dextrose-adenine-1 solution, designed to allow storage for up to 35 days at 4 to 6 °C.

2.3. Participants and data collection

Patients with Hb < 13 g/dL on the day of admission were classified as anemic, whereas those with Hb ≥ 13 g/dL were classified as non-anemic.

2.4. Participant information

Demographic and clinical variables collected included sex, age, body mass index (BMI), smoking status, and the interval (days) between blood donation and admission.

2.5. Blood tests

Hb levels were assessed on the day of blood storage, at hospital admission, and on postoperative days 1 and 5. Postoperative measurements were performed as part of routine perioperative management to monitor early postoperative Hb changes. This observation schedule was selected to capture early postoperative Hb changes and was not intended to evaluate length of hospital stay. The primary analyses in this study focused on preoperative Hb levels at hospital admission. No major intraoperative or postoperative complications that could affect perioperative management or Hb levels were observed in this cohort.

2.6. Study sample

Among 121 patients diagnosed with jaw deformities and scheduled for orthognathic surgery at our department between January 1, 2021, and December 31, 2023, 75 met the inclusion criteria. These cases were scheduled for sagittal split ramus osteotomies, with 400 mL of autologous blood collected preoperatively (Fig. 1).

2.7. Exclusion criteria

Patients who underwent multiple donations; had Hb < 11 g/dL at the time of donation; received iron supplementation therapy; or had incomplete clinical data, were excluded.

2.8. Data source

All data were obtained from outpatient and inpatient medical records.

Quantitative variables were converted into categorical variables, classifying patients into 2 groups: anemia (Hb < 13 g/dL at admission) and non-anemia (Hb ≥ 13 g/dL at admission) (Table 1).

2.9. Bias control

To minimize information bias, all data were collected by a single individual from outpatient and inpatient records. The diagnostic criterion for anemia was defined as Hb < 13 g/dL at admission. This consistent approach aimed to enhance the reliability of the collected data. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Aichi Gakuin University Dental Hospital (Approval No. 641).

2.10. Data and statistical analysis

Statistical analysis began with the Shapiro–Wilk test to evaluate the normality of data distribution. Variables that showed significant differences by Fisher exact test were further analyzed using logistic regression. Two-sided P-values < .05 were considered statistically significant (Fig. 2).

The odds ratio (OR) for sex was 0.04 [95% CI: 0.00–0.49] (P < .05), suggesting minimal impact of sex on outcomes. Patients with <22 days between donation and admission had an OR of 6.54 [95% CI: 1.11–38.70] (P < .05), indicating that these patients were about 6.5 times more likely to be anemic at admission.

4. Discussion

In this study, we analyzed risk factors for preoperative anemia in patients undergoing orthognathic surgery with ABD. We identified 2 independent and significant predictors: (1) Hb levels < 14.2 g/dL at the time of donation and (2) an interval ≤ 22 days between donation and admission. Logistic regression revealed that patients with Hb < 14.2 g/dL had a 26-fold increased risk of anemia, while those with an interval ≤ 22 days had a 6.5-fold increased risk. Although the anemia group included a markedly higher proportion of female patients, sex was not identified as an independent predictor of preoperative anemia in the multivariate analysis. This apparent imbalance is likely attributable to the underlying sex distribution of patients undergoing orthognathic surgery rather than a causal effect of sex itself. Accordingly, sex exerted only a minimal influence on the risk of preoperative anemia.

The threshold of 22 days, derived from ROC analysis and the Youden index, is biologically plausible. Following blood donation, red blood cell counts temporarily decrease, leading to increased renal erythropoietin secretion and stimulation of bone marrow erythropoiesis. However, it generally takes 2 to 3 weeks for mature red blood cells to appear in peripheral blood.[11] This recovery process supports the 22-day clinical threshold. Conversely, prolonged storage is associated with storage lesions, including reduced red cell deformability, 2,3-DPG depletion, potassium leakage, and oxidative stress.[12] Thus, an interval around 22 days may represent an optimal balance, allowing for sufficient hematopoietic recovery while maintaining acceptable blood quality.

The cutoff Hb value of 14.2 g/dL identified in this study is also clinically meaningful. While the WHO defines anemia as Hb < 13 g/dL in men, ROC analysis indicated that 14.2 g/dL provided the best sensitivity and specificity in this population. As this threshold may depend on the study cohort, external validation in independent populations is necessary.

ABD remains widely applied in oral and maxillofacial surgery, particularly in orthognathic procedures and oncologic resections.[7–9,13] Most orthognathic patients are young adults without systemic disease and can generally tolerate blood loss. However, major intraoperative hemorrhages due to injury of the inferior alveolar artery branches or the pterygoid venous plexus may necessitate transfusion. Although allogeneic transfusion has been used for massive bleeding, it carries risks such as infection, immune reactions, and graft-versus-host disease.[2,10] Therefore, ABD has continued to be considered a safe alternative.

In Western countries, advances in intraoperative hemostatic techniques and improved safety of allogeneic transfusion have substantially reduced the necessity for ABD.[1,4–6] In contrast, ABD remains common in Japan and other Asian countries, influenced by institutional protocols, patient preference, and cultural concerns regarding allogeneic transfusion.[7–12,14–17] Rather than abandoning ABD entirely, it is clinically important to clarify risk factors and apply careful patient selection and scheduling.

The clinical implication of this study is that scheduling ABD at least 22 days before surgery may allow sufficient hematopoietic recovery and reduce the risk of preoperative anemia. Patients with Hb ≥ 14.2 g/dL at donation are at lower risk, whereas those below this threshold should be regarded as high risk and provided with careful perioperative management, including infection control and wound care.

This study has several limitations. First, the sample size was relatively small (n = 75), and the single-center, retrospective design limits generalizability. Second, categorizing continuous variables may have reduced statistical power. Third, as an observational study, our findings demonstrate associations rather than causality. Future multicenter and prospective studies are warranted to validate the clinical applicability of the 22-day and 14.2 g/dL thresholds.

5. Conclusion

This study demonstrated that Hb < 14.2 g/dL at donation and an interval ≤ 22 days between donation and admission are independent risk factors for preoperative anemia in patients undergoing orthognathic surgery with ABD. These thresholds do not refute the use of ABD but instead may serve as practical indicators to enhance its safety through appropriate patient selection and timing. Ensuring adequate preoperative Hb levels and allowing at least 22 days between donation and surgery represent practical strategies to reduce anemia risk and improve perioperative safety. The present findings may supplement transfusion management guidelines; however, further multicenter studies are needed to confirm their reproducibility and generalizability.

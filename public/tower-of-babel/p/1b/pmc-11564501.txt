Introduction

Substantial progress has been achieved in preventing, diagnosing, and managing coronary artery disease (CAD). Nevertheless, ischemic heart disease remains the leading cause of mortality globally, accounting for 13% of all deaths. Since 2000, the largest increase in deaths has been for this disease, rising by 2.7 million to 9.1 million deaths in 2021 [1].

Changes in coronary microvascular function and structural modifications play a role in the development of coronary atherosclerotic heart disease [2–4]. At present, cardiac microcirculation can be assessed using noninvasive techniques such as transthoracic Doppler echocardiography, cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and positron emission tomography (PET), as well as invasive approaches, notably interventional diagnostic procedures (IDP) [5]. Advances in retinal imaging technology have rendered color fundus photography a cost-effective and noninvasive tool for analyzing microcirculatory architecture [6–8].

Retinal vasculature offers vital insights into the pathophysiology of coronary atherosclerosis [9–11]. Furthermore, research has indicated a correlation between retinal vessel caliber and the incidence of CAD [11–17]. A study by Liew et al. demonstrated that an impaired retinal vascular fractal dimension was an independent predictor of 14-year coronary heart disease mortality, indicating that deficient microvascular branching may contribute to the progression of cardiovascular diseases [18]. Additionally, evidence from optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA) suggests that reduced retinal vascular density is linked with CAD [19, 20].

Research into the association between retinal vessel bifurcation characteristics and CAD is scant [21]. The purpose of this study is to analyze the association between retinal arteriolar bifurcation parameters and CAD using the established measurement scheme.

Methods

Study Design

The study constituted a single-center, retrospective, single-blind, cross-sectional observational analysis conducted in strict accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The Institutional Review Board of Tiemei General Hospital, affiliated with the Liaoning Health Industry Group, approved the study (Approval No. 2021080601). All participants furnished written informed consent and agreed to the publication of all images, clinical data, and associated information contained in this manuscript. In the present study, all components with any individually identifiable information have been removed from the dataset.

In total, data were gathered from 574 patients (1097 eyes) admitted to Tiemei General Hospital’s Cardiology Department between September 2021 and September 2022. Following the application of exclusion criteria outlined in Table 1, 444 patients (731 eyes) were enrolled, comprising 230 men and 214 women. Exclusion criteria

Assessment of Cardiovascular Risk Factors

Participants underwent interviews and assessments to evaluate cardiovascular risk factors. Resting blood pressure was measured following a standardized protocol, with hypertension defined as having self-reported use of antihypertensive medication or systolic blood pressure ≥ 140 mmHg, or diastolic blood pressure ≥ 90 mmHg. Body mass index (BMI) was calculated by dividing weight in kilograms by height in meters squared. Following an 8-h fast, venous blood samples were drawn to analyze serum glucose, lipids, and lipoproteins. Diabetes mellitus was identified as a fasting plasma glucose level ≥ 7.0 mmol/L, 2-h post-load glucose ≥ 11.1 mmol/L, or the use of diabetes medication, including insulin or oral agents. Comprehensive clinical records of each participant, including age, sex, occupation, smoking status, alcohol intake, and family and personal history of cardiovascular disease, were meticulously documented.

Assessment of CAD

Coronary angiography was conducted on 322 patients and coronary computed tomography angiography (CTA) on 122. Prior to testing, all patients adhered to a 6-h fasting regimen. Two experienced cardiologists, who were blinded to the patients’ other test outcomes, independently evaluated the coronary artery images. In cases of diagnostic discrepancies, a senior cardiologist was consulted for further evaluation. Coronary artery stenosis was categorized into three types: normal to minimal (no plaque or less than 25% narrowing), mild to moderate (25–69% narrowing), and severe to occluded (70–99% narrowing or complete obstruction) [22, 23]. The degree of stenosis was recorded for each coronary artery segment, and the most severe observed stenosis was used as the criterion for patient categorization.

Retinal Photography and Retinal Image Preprocessing

Retinal images were acquired with a 45° non-mydriatic digital camera (CenterVue DRS automatic retinal camera), with a focus on the macula, including the optic disc and significant portions of the temporal vascular arcades. Images were captured after a 10-min period of eye rest. Each image underwent jpg compression, was saved in jpg format, and retained full color data across three separate channels (red, green, and blue). The dimensions of each fundus photograph were 2592 × 1944 pixels, with a resolution of 96 dots per inch (dpi). Images were cropped to the field of view (FOV) and resized to 2048 × 2048 pixels, preserving the aspect ratio of the FOV.

Automatic Measurement Method for Retinal Arteriolar Bifurcation

Before measuring the bifurcation parameters of the retinal arteriole, the testers completed rigorous training. This training encompassed several key aspects: ensuring the quality of retinal photographs, distinguishing between retinal arteries and veins, and identifying abnormal retinal vessels, such as neovascularization and intraretinal microvascular abnormalities. The methodology encompassed multiple phases: segmentation of retinal vessels, delineation of bifurcation zones, retrieval of the bifurcation skeleton, determination of center point coordinates, demarcation of the measurement region, and quantification of diameter and branching angles. The intricacies of the measurement procedure are detailed in a publication by Dai et al., which additionally verifies the substantial consistency in measurement outcomes across various examiners [24].

A retinal arteriolar bifurcation is characterized by the diameters of the parent vessel (d0), the larger branch (d1), and the smaller branch (d2), along with the angles (θ1) and (θ2), which are the orientations of the larger and smaller branches relative to the parent vessel's direction, respectively, as depicted in Fig. 1. The total angle (θ) constitutes the sum of (θ1) and (θ2). Additionally, we documented the branch (superior temporal artery or inferior temporal artery) to which the retinal arteriolar bifurcation belonged, along with the distance of the bifurcation from the center of the optic disc.Fig. 1Schematic diagram of an arterial bifurcation. In our previous study, we discovered that the theoretical optimal angle was unaffected by anatomical features and was influenced solely by age, a significant determinant in cardiovascular diseases [24]. Our research team posits that in the presence of cardiovascular risk factors, the retinal arteriolar system endeavors to maintain an optimal bifurcation pattern to ensure a stable blood supply until decompensation occurs. We intend to further examine individuals with coronary atherosclerosis to determine whether these theoretical optimal values experience significant alterations.

Statistical Methods

Statistical analysis was conducted using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS version 27.0). Descriptive statistics were utilized to profile the distribution of variables including age, sex, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, smoking status, alcohol consumption, BMI, hyperlipidemia, and CAD. Continuous variables are reported as the mean ± standard error (SE), while categorical variables are presented in percentages. A one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was applied to compare continuous variables across three distinct groups. The chi-square test was used for comparing categorical variables across the groups. A multifactorial ANOVA was conducted to explore the potential associations between optimal theoretical angles and CAD, accounting for confounding variables including asymmetry ratio, area ratio, and distance from retinal arteriolar bifurcation to the center of the optic disc. Bonferroni correction was applied during post hoc multiple comparisons to control for type I errors. Missing data were managed via listwise deletion, omitting cases with any missing variables. Given the minimal missingness, the impact of this method on the analysis was considered negligible. We established our alpha level at 0.05, with a standard deviation of 6 and an anticipated difference of 0.5. The power of the study comparing the normal to minimal group (n = 2548) with the mild to moderate group (n = 2513) was 0.84. Between the mild to moderate group (n = 2513) and the severe to occlusion group (n = 4564), power of 0.92 was achieved.

Discussion

According to Murray's 1927 physiological principles of the cardiovascular network, maintaining an optimal microcirculation state is crucial for efficient blood transport to terminal organs with minimal energy expenditure [25–27]. When influenced by risk factors, the microcirculation system undergoes necessary changes to mitigate the adverse effects of these factors. Notably, these include morphological alterations to the arteriolar bifurcation structure, which is critical for regulating tissue blood flow distribution. Our study focused on retinal arterioles, examining bifurcation structures including trunk diameter, branch diameters, and bifurcation angles under the influence of cardiovascular risk factors. Our results indicated that while most risk factors significantly affected the vessel diameters at the bifurcations, the bifurcation angles showed no significant changes. Further analysis revealed that (θ1), defined as the angle between the thicker branch and the trunk's extension, displayed profound variability, ranging from 0° to 90° as illustrated in Fig. 3d. Similar variability was exhibited by (θ2), as illustrated in Fig. 3e. This high variability resulted in minimal significant associations between (θ1), (θ2), and cardiovascular risk factors. Conversely, due to its lower variability as illustrated in Fig. 3f, the (θ) indicator showed more substantial associations with multiple cardiovascular risk factors.

In examining the impact of various retinal arteriolar bifurcation parameters across different stages of coronary atherosclerosis, we found no statistically significant differences. This lack of significance likely stems from the multifactorial nature of coronary atherosclerosis, where various risk factors differentially affect retinal arteriolar parameters. For instance, hypertension tends to narrow arterioles [28–30], whereas hyperglycemia may thicken them [31–33]. To minimize the impact of varying risk factors on retinal arteriolar bifurcations, we focused on the theoretical optimal value of these bifurcations, which is dependent primarily on the asymmetry ratio—the diameter ratio of the two branching arteries. This approach reduces the variability inherent in measuring bifurcation angles and avoids reliance on absolute measurements like arteriolar diameters. However, while univariate analyses showed no significant differences in the theoretical optimal values across various stages of coronary atherosclerosis, multivariate analyses did reveal differences. The theoretical optimal value is influenced by the anatomical characteristics of the retinal arteriolar bifurcation, including the origin of the branch (superior or inferior temporal artery), the distance from the optic disc, and the asymmetry and area ratios. After adjusting for these significant factors, we established a definitive association between retinal arteriolar bifurcation and coronary atherosclerosis.

To effectively integrate retinal arteriolar bifurcation parameters into clinical practice, extensive research and experimentation are necessary. We must develop standardized protocols for data collection that specifically address the following: the maximum permissible distance from the optic disc for collecting bifurcation parameters, whether to include only primary bifurcations or also secondary and tertiary bifurcations, and the focus on asymmetry and area ratios near the central region. Rigorously addressing these issues will significantly facilitate the future incorporation of retinal arteriolar bifurcation parameters into clinical settings.

We established that (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\theta }_{1}^{\prime}$$\end{document}θ1′) is positively correlated with alpha, while (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\theta }_{2}^{\prime}$$\end{document}θ2′) exhibits a negative correlation with alpha. Consequently, we can infer a differential change in the diameters (d1) and (d2) among patients with severe coronary obstruction, characterized by a narrowing of the narrower retinal arteriolar branches and an expansion in the wider branches. These outcomes provide tangential support for the vascular remodeling theory characterizing coronary microvascular alterations at a morphological level. Although the quantity of related clinical research is growing, our understanding of the pathophysiological interactions affecting coronary microcirculation remains limited. Whether the small arteries within the coronary microvasculature exhibit analogous alterations to those identified in retinal arterioles in cases of serious coronary constriction or blockage is yet to be substantiated.

Building upon previous research, which has established a connection between retinal vascular changes and cardiovascular health, substantial progress has been achieved in the application of retinal imaging for cardiovascular risk assessment. In a landmark study, Poplin et al. utilized deep learning models to analyze retinal images and successfully predicted several important cardiovascular risk factors, including age, gender, smoking status, blood pressure, and diabetes, with remarkable accuracy [34]. Their research demonstrated the ability of deep learning models to identify robust biomarkers within retinal images and laid the groundwork for future studies in utilizing retinal imaging to assess cardiovascular risk. Research in this domain is currently advancing along three primary trajectories. Firstly, efforts are underway to enhance the capabilities of deep learning models; for instance, Zhang et al. introduced a model called Reti-WHO, based on the Swin Transformer, for predicting cardiovascular disease risk regression scores [35]. Secondly, a shift is occurring towards employing multimodal deep learning that integrates fundus photographs with traditional risk factors for predicting current cardiovascular diseases [36]. Thirdly, the development of secondary models that leverage biological parameters of retinal vessels—such as arterial width, arterial angle, venous angle, and fractal dimension extracted from fundus images—is gaining traction. An exemplar of this is the model developed by Huang et al., which uses a graph neural network to predict the Coronary Artery Disease Reporting and Data System (CAD-RADS) scores, employing quantitative vascular biomarkers [37]. Collectively, these developments direct future research towards combining biological parameters from retinal vessels with traditional cardiovascular risk factors, aiming at early detection and warning of cardiovascular disease risks.

Our study possesses certain limitations. Firstly, not all participants underwent standard ophthalmic assessments including visual acuity, intraocular pressure, or refraction, which may influence the findings; in particular, high myopia and an elongated axial length could potentially alter the geometric morphology of retinal vessels. Secondly, the sample originated from hospital cardiovascular departments and consisted primarily of individuals diagnosed with or suspected of having CAD, although it included a subset of non-CAD patients. In the future, we intend to conduct further validation across more diverse populations. Thirdly, in measuring retinal arteriolar bifurcations, we did not account for arteriovenous junctions. Some retinal arteriolar bifurcations are located near arteriovenous crossings, where the mutual compression of vessels can cause subtle changes in the diameter of retinal arteries, potentially leading to measurement bias. Fourth, this study employs a cross-sectional design, which constrains our ability to discern the causal relationships between alterations in retinal arteriolar bifurcation parameters and coronary atherosclerotic lesions.

Conclusion

Our research findings demonstrate that patients suffering from severe coronary artery stenosis or occlusion display altered patterns of retinal arteriolar bifurcation. These alterations present a novel variable that can be employed to develop a predictive model for populations at high risk of cardiovascular events.

Dextromethorphan-bupropion-associated pharmacovigilance signals based on the FAERS database: An observational study
Authors: Ruixue Liu, Chunxiao Liu, Chunhui Li, Di Hu, Longkun Wang, Zhenmin Li, Dianwei Feng, Tongxin Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Dextromethorphan-bupropion, a novel oral N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor antagonist approved by the Food and Drug Administration in August 2022, launched in the United States in October 2022 for major depressive disorder. We aimed to detect and characterize the adverse events (AEs) of dextromethorphan-bupropion using the FDA adverse event reporting system from the 4th quarter of 2022 to the 4th quarter of 2024. For signal quantification of dextromethorphan-bupropion-related AEs, we used disproportionality methods including the reporting odds ratio, proportional reporting ratio, and multi-item gamma Poisson shrinker algorithms. Our study analyzed 4,804,129 AEs, while 3580 AEs were associated with dextromethorphan-bupropion, which included 266 preferred term signals and 21 system organ classes. These AEs mainly occurred as psychiatric disorders (21.17%), nervous system disorders (20.92%), general disorders, administration site conditions (19.75%), and others. Notably, obsessive rumination (n = 3, reporting odds ratio = 366.02, proportional reporting ratio = 365.71, empirical Bayes geometric mean = 287.56) exhibited the lowest morbidity, whereas the highest signal intensity. Specifically, 65.3% of preferred terms were not explicitly mentioned in the instructions, which suggested the previously unlabeled signals. Dextromethorphan-bupropion poses potential reporting signals of various AEs, though providing therapeutic effects. In clinical applications, practitioners should closely monitor occurrences of psychiatric disorders, nervous system disorders, general disorders, administration site conditions, and other events. Meanwhile, professional clinicians should be alerted to the AEs signals not mentioned in the label to secure clinical use.

1. Introduction

Major depressive disorder (MDD) is a public health issue that affects over 300 million people and is one of the leading causes of disability worldwide. New cases of MDD increased by 59% from 172.7 million in 1990 to 274.8 million in 2019.[1,2] The World Health Organization has indicated that MDD will be the leading cause of the burden of disease worldwide by 2030.[3] The presence of MDD not only increases suicide, but also aggravates medical comorbidities, such as diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and metabolic disorders.[4,5] Besides, MDD has inevitably affected the public expenditure on health.[2,6] Hence, it is increasingly realized that optimizing the therapeutics of MDD is of great importance.

Traditionally, antidepressant treatment of MDD is focused on monoamine by inhibiting the reuptake or metabolism of monoaminergic neurotransmitters. However, it will take weeks to months for the full benefit of the medication to be manifested, while some individuals are resistant to treatment.[7–9] In an effort to overcome slow or inadequate response and tolerability issues and to improve the prognosis of MDD patients, non-monoaminergic-based new drugs with faster-acting and long-lasting antidepressant effects are urgently required.

N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) antagonists augment glutamate release and increase synaptogenesis and neuroplasticity, which result in rapid antidepressant effects.[10] Dextromethorphan-bupropion, a combination of dextromethorphan hydrobromide (45 mg), an uncompetitive NMDA receptor antagonist, sigma-1 receptor agonist, bupropion hydrochloride (105 mg), and a CYP450 2D6 inhibitor, was approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in August 2022 and launched in the United States in October 2022 for the treatment of adults with MDD.[11] Dextromethorphan-bupropion is the first and only oral medication with a fast onset of action and significant antidepressant efficacy starting as early as week and maintaining this effect after 6 weeks in a phase 3, double-blinded, placebo-controlled trial.[12,13] Dextromethorphan-bupropion works by several mechanisms. The dextromethorphan component of dextromethorphan-bupropion acts as an uncompetitive antagonist of the NMDA receptor that improves gamma-aminobutyric acid-mediated neuronal dysfunction and modulates glutamatergic neurotransmission.[14] Dextromethorphan exerts antidepressant effects through modulating serotonin 5-HT1A function or facilitating brain-derived neurotrophic factor expression via sigma-1 agonism.[15] Notably, dextromethorphan is metabolized rapidly by CYP450 2D6, which precludes therapeutic plasma levels with oral administration.[16] Bupropion increases plasma levels of dextromethorphan by competitively inhibiting CYP450 2D6 and enhances monoaminergic functions by inhibiting the neuronal reuptake of norepinephrine and dopamine.[11] The approval of dextromethorphan-bupropion represents a milestone in the treatment of depression based on its novel oral NMDA antagonist mechanism and its rapid antidepressant efficacy without requiring intensive monitoring.

Although dextromethorphan-bupropion may be described as a rapid-acting and innovative treatment option, clinical trials may be insufficient to detect rare or delayed adverse events (AEs) because of limited sample sizes, short follow-up periods, and selected patient populations. The FDA adverse event reporting system (FAERS) offers a platform for collecting and analyzing AEs of medicinal products from 1968 to the latest quarter (December 31, 2024).[17] Our study aims to conduct a comprehensive analysis of the AEs of dextromethorphan-bupropion from the fourth quarter of 2022 to the fourth quarter of 2024, which might offer valuable reference information for its clinical use.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Data sources and extraction

The data for this study were derived from the FAERS database, whose data are renewed quarterly. This study retrieved and analyzed all the reported AEs of dextromethorphan-bupropion in the FAERS from the 4th quarter of 2022 to the 4th quarter of 2024. To identify all relevant reports, the drug information file was queried using both the generic name (“Dextromethorphan-bupropion”) and the brand name (“Auvelity”). Duplication data were excluded, and only AEs with dextromethorphan-bupropion as the primary suspected drug were included in the study. To minimize potential confounding factors, we excluded AE reports involving the concomitant use of dextromethorphan-bupropion with medications commonly prescribed for other psychiatric medications.

2.2. Data management

Quarterly data files were merged, and duplicate reports were removed in strict accordance with the FDA’s recommended cross-referencing method. Briefly, for reports with the same case identifier (CASEID), the entry with the latest FDA receipt date was retained. In instances where both CASEID and FDA receipt date values coincided, the report with the highest primary identifier (PRIMARYID) was selected. To ensure consistency for subsequent statistical analyses, all the AEs were coded as preferred terms (PTs) according to the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities version 26.1 (MedDRA 26.1). The product information used in this study was the FDA-approved prescribing label for each included drug. MedDRA term matching was performed based on the official MedDRA hierarchy to exclude synonymous terms.

2.3. Statistical analysis

In this study, reporting odds ratio (ROR), proportional reporting ratio (PRR), and multi-item gamma Poisson shrinker (MGPS) techniques from the disproportionality methods were applied to detect AEs signals.[18] ROR is one of the classic signal detection methods that can remove biases to estimate the potential signal properly.[18] PRR shows higher specificity compared with ROR. MGPS detects signals from rare events. The study uses a mixture of ROR, PRR, and MGPS to take advantage of their individual strengths, thus enhancing the detection and validation scopes. This integrated approach is widely used for disproportionality analysis methods in pharmacovigilance, designed to identify potential safety signals by quantifying statistical associations between drug exposure and AEs. However, these measures do not estimate absolute risk, incidence rates, or establish causal relationships. Signals detected through these methods require further clinical and epidemiological validation to confirm causal links. 2.4. Ethical considerations

This study was based on publicly available, de-identified data from the FAERS database. Therefore, institutional review board approval and patient informed consent were not required.

3. Results

3.1. Description of the AEs resulted by dextromethorphan-bupropion

We obtained 4,804,129 reports of AEs submitted to the FAERS database since 2022 Q4, which contained 3580 dextromethorphan-bupropion-related AEs in 1537 patients. Of the 1537 patient reports resulting from dextromethorphan-bupropion, 708 occurred in females (46.06%) and 350 in males (22.77%). Patients aged from 18 to 64 were the dominant population affected, and a large portion of the population is of unknown age. A great number of reports (49.84%) were submitted by consumers rather than healthcare professionals. Most of the reported countries are unknown (89.13%). The major challenge in the analysis was the absence of age information in 60.05% and country information in 89.13% of the reports, which significantly hindered a thorough examination of the relationship between age, country, and AEs. This lack of critical demographic data limited our ability to perform a comprehensive age-specific analysis, as well as our ability to account for cross-regional healthcare disparities. Since the approval of dextromethorphan-bupropion in 2022, there has been an increasing trend in AEs, which peaked in 2024. In terms of clinical outcomes, serious AEs, such as death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, congenital malformations, and other serious events, accounted for 20.04%. The systems frequently affected include the psychiatric system (n = 758, 21.17%), general disorders and administration site conditions (n = 749, 20.92%), as well as the nervous system (n = 707, 19.75%). Among them, psychiatric disorders manifested the most signals (61 AEs signals). A total of 231 reports provided the occurrence time of AEs, among which 158 AEs (68.40%) occurred within 30 days after drug administration, while 19 AEs (8.23%) occurred within 30 to 60 days after drug administration, and 17 AEs (7.36%) occurred within 61 to 90 days after drug administration. 4. Discussion

Dextromethorphan-bupropion, a novel approved medication for MDD, shows convenient oral administration, rapid onset of action, and potential therapeutic advantage. However, as new drugs are introduced to the market, it is essential to monitor their real-world usage and AEs to ensure their safety. Our pharmacovigilance study provided the first real-world data on the safety profile of dextromethorphan-bupropion and suggested that dextromethorphan-bupropion requires further validation for serious and unexpected AEs.

A key exclusive feature of dextromethorphan-bupropion is its oral dosing capability, unlike rapid-acting antidepressants requiring medical supervision (e.g., brexanolone infusions or esketamine nasal sprays). Dextromethorphan-bupropion is commercially available at retail pharmacies and designed for autonomous patient administration without a clinical monitor.[19,20] It is worth mentioning that most reporters (49.84%) were submitted by consumers rather than healthcare professionals, which is different from esketamine.[21] This might indicate patients are more inclined to report AEs directly after dextromethorphan-bupropion application or a deficiency in reporting by healthcare professionals. The increasing trend in the number of dextromethorphan-bupropion reports across this short time window is noteworthy. AEs in 2024 increased by 35% compared to 2023. The observed increase might stem from increased prescribing and drug utilization, growing awareness among patients and clinicians, media and promotional impacts, as well as variations in spontaneous reporting practices.

Our results demonstrated that the most significant signals at system organ class levels were psychiatric disorders, general disorders and administration site conditions, and nervous system disorders. As psychiatric medication, the top 6 AEs with the strongest signals of dextromethorphan-bupropion were associated with psychiatric disorders, which suggested that we should improve awareness of psychiatric AEs. In this study, dextromethorphan-bupropion was used to treat MDD. Some psychiatric signals may reflect symptoms of the underlying condition, clinical deterioration, treatment nonresponse, or the effects of concomitant medications, rather than AEs to the drug itself, due to the observational nature of spontaneous reporting data. Thus, psychiatric signals identified in this study should be interpreted with caution.

The strongest signal AEs at the PT level were obsessive rumination. Nolen-Hoeksema introduced rumination as a way of responding to depressed mood that is characterized by repetitive and passive focus on the symptoms of depression, and their possible causes and consequences.[22] Rumination, characterized by repetitively negative thinking, is one of the risk factors of MDD. Negative thoughts shown in Table 5 were usually associated with adverse outcomes in depression, including onset, severity, and therapeutic efficacy.[23] Case reported that selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors, such as venlafaxine, may also cause obsessive rumination, especially at high doses of the medication.[24] However, it is important to note that the signal for obsessive rumination, though statistically identified, is clinically fragile as it relies on only 3 reports. Furthermore, significant signals from small numbers must be interpreted with great caution and require further validation.

Dissociation and related symptoms, including autoscopy, depersonalization, derealization, and dissociative disorder, represent relatively strong pharmacovigilance signals in FAERS data. The Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, defines dissociation as a “disruption of and/or discontinuity in the normal integration of consciousness, memory, identity, emotion, perception, body representation, motor control, and behavior”.[25] Core manifestations of dissociation encompass depersonalization, derealization, amnesia, identity alteration, and identity confusion.[26] Notably, dissociative symptoms were not reported in 2 phase 3 trials of dextromethorphan-bupropion.[27,28] It is mechanistically plausible that the dextromethorphan component of dextromethorphan-bupropion, like esketamine, acts on NMDA receptors – a pathway associated with psychotic symptoms such as hallucinations and dissociation.[29] However, FAERS data cannot confirm a causal relationship between dextromethorphan-bupropion and these symptoms, as the dataset lacks information on dose–response relationships, temporality of symptom onset, clinical confirmation of diagnoses, and potential confounding factors, including comorbid psychiatric conditions or concomitant medication use.

Sexual dysfunction included anorgasmia, ejaculation failure, sexual dysfunction, and erectile dysfunction in this study and is greatly underestimated in clinical practice and significantly impairing patients’ quality of life and adherence to treatment.[30] Notably, approximately 70% of patients with MDD experience some form of sexual dysfunction, which is itself a prominent symptom of depression. Antidepressant medications and adjunctive treatments may further contribute to sexual dysfunction and complicate its assessment and management.[31] Against this backdrop, we cannot establish a direct causal link between dextromethorphan-bupropion and sexual dysfunction, as either depression or antidepressant therapy has a negative effect upon sexual function. Besides, dextromethorphan-bupropion did not show an association with sexual dysfunction in both phase 2 and 3 trials.[13,32] Therefore, we recommend enhanced monitoring of sexual function in clinical studies. Regular assessments of sexual interest, erectile function, ejaculatory control, and orgasmic experience will improve drug safety evaluation and enable comprehensive treatment plans that balance disease management and quality of life for MDD patients.

The black box warning on the dextromethorphan-bupropion label highlights the risk of suicidal thoughts and behavior in pediatric and young adult patients taking antidepressants.[11] However, in patients with MDD, suicidal ideation can arise from multiple confounding factors inherent to the disease itself, including the natural progression of depressive illness, progressive clinical worsening, insufficient treatment response, and preexisting baseline disease severity. Notably, FAERS data are inadequate to differentiate the underlying causes of suicidal ideation and behaviors. Accordingly, it is critically important to closely monitor all antidepressant-treated patients for clinical worsening and emergence of suicidal thoughts and behaviors, especially in the initial months of drug application and as dosage is changed. Therefore, timely evaluation and individualized optimization of therapeutic regimens should be implemented once patients present with aggravated depressive symptoms or newly developed suicidal thoughts and behaviors.

Generally, dextromethorphan-bupropion was well tolerated in phase 2 and phase 3 trials in patients with MDD. The most common AEs include dizziness, nausea, dry mouth, decreased appetite, and anxiety in the phase 2 trial. The majority of AEs showed mild intensity, and no serious AEs were observed in the trials.[32] The most common AEs included dizziness, nausea, headache, somnolence, and dry mouth in the phase 3 trial. Pancreatitis was reported in 1 case of the trial, which was assessed to be excluded.[13] Though anxiety, dizziness, and somnolence were estimated to have a low relative potential signal (between 2.89 and 3.42), a relatively high occurrence in the trials should be noted (between 7% and 16%). The residual AEs in the trials, such as nausea, dry mouth, decreased appetite, and headache, were not shown in Table 5 due to low signals, and 65.3% PTs were not mentioned in the FDA label.

Although this study provides scientific evidence for the safety assessment of dextromethorphan-bupropion from multiple perspectives, it still has some limitations. Firstly, it is important to note that FAERS is a spontaneous reporting system, that duplicate reports may exist, and the high rate of missing data undermines the generalizability of the findings, limiting the extrapolation of results to broader populations. In addition, we recognize that while FAERS data provide preliminary potential signals, determining the true incidence and causality requires further validation through prospective studies. Furthermore, the study faces the difficulty in fully distinguishing psychiatric AEs related to dextromethorphan-bupropion from those caused by the underlying MDD, clinical deterioration, and treatment nonresponse. The psychiatric signals identified in this study should be interpreted with caution. Several unassessed confounding factors, such as potential drug interactions and concomitant medications, were not accounted for in this study. Thus, the obtained information needs to be further verified by clinical studies.

5. Conclusion

Through analysis of data from the FAERS database, we identified several reporting signals for AEs potentially associated with dextromethorphan-bupropion in clinical use. Our findings underscore the importance of ongoing safety awareness. Particular attention should be paid to psychiatric disorders and potential suicidal thoughts and behaviors. In addition, we observed signals for previously unreported AEs that need further investigation due to the limitations of the FAERS database. Our study reports several potential safety profiles for dextromethorphan-bupropion, which indicates that clinicians should remain vigilant in monitoring and managing these potential signals when prescribing the medication.

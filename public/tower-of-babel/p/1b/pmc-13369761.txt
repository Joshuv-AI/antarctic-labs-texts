Research on adaptive collaborative dispatch optimization algorithms for drones in distribution networks
Authors: Xiangdong Zu, Jiaxing Fu, Hai Zhao, Yue Lan, Chun Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper addresses the complex scheduling optimization problem in multi-UAV collaborative power line inspection by proposing an Adaptive Ant Colony Optimization Algorithm with Elite Strategy (AACOES). The study comprehensively considers multiple practical constraints, including UAV flight characteristics, battery endurance, and external wind conditions, to construct a scheduling optimization model closely aligned with real-world inspection operations. To overcome limitations in convergence speed and global search capability inherent in traditional ant colony algorithms, the proposed method incorporates an elite strategy and adaptive adjustment factors. It optimizes pheromone update rules, effectively enhancing colony diversity and accelerating convergence. This enables efficient identification of near-optimal solutions under multiple constraints. Simulation experiments comparing AACOES with particle swarm optimization, genetic algorithms, and traditional ant colony algorithms demonstrate its significant advantages in optimizing both single-unit performance and total flight distance, coupled with more stable convergence. This validates its effectiveness and practicality for multi-UAV collaborative inspection scheduling in complex environments, providing an efficient and reliable technical approach for real-world applications such as power line inspections.Keywords: Unmanned aerial vehicle, Flight path planning, Ant colony optimization, AACOESSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

With the continuous development of power systems, power inspection has become increasingly important. Traditional power inspection relies on manual methods, which often suffer from low efficiency, high cost, and poor safety, making it difficult to meet the requirements of modern power systems for efficient and safe inspection. In recent years, the rapid development of UAV technology has provided a novel solution for power inspection1. UAVs have advantages such as high maneuverability, low cost, and strong adaptability to complex terrain, which can significantly improve inspection efficiency and quality2.

However, in practical applications, the inspection capability of a single UAV is still limited. When facing large-scale and complex power facility networks, single-UAV inspection is often time-consuming and cannot simultaneously monitor multiple critical areas. Therefore, cooperative inspection using multiple UAVs has become a more efficient and feasible solution. Multi-UAV cooperation can fully exploit the advantages of individual UAVs and achieve rapid inspection of power facilities through reasonable task allocation and coordination3.

Although multi-UAV cooperation is an effective way to improve inspection efficiency, it also faces significant technical challenges, namely how to effectively schedule multiple UAVs to ensure efficient and orderly completion of inspection tasks. Reference4 considered UAV flight time constraints and constructed a task allocation model to achieve cooperative inspection scheduling in limited space. Reference5 imposed path constraints on multiple UAVs and established a parallel scheduling optimization model for urban logistics UAVs, significantly improving delivery efficiency while reducing operating costs. However, the modeling and solution processes of these methods deviate considerably from actual inspection scenarios. In real power inspection tasks, UAV flight speed is affected by factors such as wind speed. Therefore, to build a multi-UAV cooperative scheduling model that is closer to reality, various complex factors must be further considered.

Designing appropriate scheduling algorithms for such complex environments is a key step in improving the efficiency of multi-UAV cooperative inspection. Many scholars have studied centralized task allocation methods based on traditional algorithms, including simulated annealing (SA)6, ant colony optimization (ACO)7, particle swarm optimization (PSO)8–10, genetic algorithms (GA)11–13, whale optimization algorithm (WOA)14, and gray wolf optimization (GWO)15,16. These algorithms evaluate optimal task allocation schemes and perform necessary optimization and adjustment according to practical requirements to ensure feasibility and effectiveness. However, when applied to large-scale and complex scenarios such as power inspection tasks, their capability in handling constrained optimization is relatively weak, and they still exhibit limitations under high complexity and high dynamics in real applications. To further enhance algorithm performance, reference17 combined cubic spline interpolation with an improved ant colony algorithm to optimize UAV flight paths and obtain optimal routes. Reference18 proposed a three-dimensional path planning deep reinforcement learning method based on local information and relative distance, enabling UAVs to acquire effective nearby environmental information in real-world scenarios. Reference19 addressed UAV route planning in complex terrain by proposing an improved sparrow search algorithm, which enhanced global convergence. Reference20 proposed a path planning method for solar-powered UAVs to find flight paths that are optimal in terms of both distance and energy consumption. Nevertheless, these studies still fail to achieve ideal fast convergence and are prone to being trapped in local optima.

To address the complex environments and multiple constraints in power inspection tasks, this paper constructs a multi-constraint scheduling model that comprehensively considers factors such as wind fields, maximum UAV flight range, and return-to-base requirements. On this basis, an adaptive ant colony optimization algorithm based on an elite strategy (AACOES) is proposed to optimize the scheduling of multi-UAV cooperative inspection tasks. By introducing an elite strategy into the traditional ant colony algorithm, the proposed method enhances the exploration of high-quality solutions and avoids premature convergence. Meanwhile, an adaptive pheromone evaporation factor is incorporated to encourage ants to explore more paths, strengthen global search capability, and accelerate convergence. Experimental results demonstrate that, compared with PSO, GA, and ACO, AACOES converges faster and achieves better optimization performance. It can rapidly find optimal solutions in complex urban wind field environments, effectively improving the efficiency and reliability of multi-UAV cooperative scheduling for power inspection.

The main innovations and technical contributions of this paper are reflected in the following aspects: (1) For the scenario of power line inspection, a multi-UAV collaborative scheduling optimisation model has been developed that takes into account the influence of wind fields, the maximum range constraints of UAVs, and mission allocation requirements, thereby making the problem modelling more closely aligned with the practical application environment; (2) An improved ant colony optimisation algorithm (AACOES) is proposed, which integrates an elite strategy with an adaptive pheromone adjustment mechanism. By dynamically adjusting the pheromone update process, this effectively enhances the algorithm’s global search capability and convergence stability; (3) Wind field factors are incorporated into the path evaluation process. By influencing the assessment of path costs, the algorithm is encouraged to select more optimal flight paths during the search, thereby enhancing the model’s adaptability to complex environments; (4) By combining a greedy strategy to perform local optimisation of paths, the quality of solutions and convergence efficiency have been improved; (5) Through multi-algorithm comparative experiments and simulation analyses under varying numbers of UAVs, the advantages of the proposed method in terms of path optimisation performance, stability and scalability have been validated.

Modeling the collaborative scheduling problem for drone inspection

Problem description

In a multi-UAV cooperative power inspection task, multiple UAVs with sufficient battery capacity depart from their respective docking stations, aiming to complete the inspection of transmission towers within designated inspection areas. The inspection process is illustrated in Fig. 1.

UAV inspection schematic.

At the beginning of the mission, UAVs automatically allocate tasks according to the predefined inspection areas and target transmission towers, and plan their flight paths based on the inspection sequence and influencing factors such as wind fields. Due to limited battery capacity, each UAV has a maximum flight distance constraint during operation. Throughout the inspection process, it must be ensured that each UAV completes its assigned inspection tasks and returns to its respective docking station before battery depletion, while minimizing the total flight distance as much as possible. In addition, each transmission tower must be inspected at least once by one UAV. It is assumed that UAVs maintain a constant flight altitude during operation, and the cooperative inspection scheduling is carried out in a two-dimensional space.

Model establishment

In multi-UAV systems, cooperative mechanisms can mainly be divided into two dimensions: task cooperation and control cooperation. Task cooperation focuses on system-level problems such as task allocation, path planning, and optimization of task execution. Through intelligent scheduling and optimal path design, it aims to maximize overall task efficiency. Control cooperation, on the other hand, primarily addresses low-level control issues, including individual UAV flight control, formation maintenance, and real-time adjustments in dynamic environments, with the goal of ensuring flight stability and operational safety in complex working conditions. This study takes task cooperation as its core and focuses on solving task optimization problems in multi-UAV power inspection scenarios, aiming to improve the overall execution efficiency of inspection missions.

Algorithm flow chart.

Step 1: Initialize the basic parameters and population size of the AACOES algorithm.

Step 2: Construct solutions for the ants based on the position transition probability formula, generate initial paths, and store the current allocation scheme.

Step 3: After solution construction, apply a greedy algorithm to each ant’s path to update the planned route. Evaluate the allocation scheme, calculate fitness, record each ant’s score, and keep track of the best solution.

Step 4: Select the ants closest to the best solution as elite ants, while the remaining ants are considered ordinary ants, and update pheromones for both groups accordingly.

Step 5: Check whether the maximum number of iterations has been reached; if so, output the optimal solution, otherwise return to Step 2.

These results indicate that AACOES can more effectively plan UAV inspection paths, allowing UAVs to complete all assigned tasks with shorter flight distances, reducing energy consumption, and further improving inspection efficiency. In terms of computation time, AACOES is comparable to ACO and slightly higher than GA and PSO; however, since this computation occurs during the pre-flight preparation stage and the time required is within a reasonable range, its impact on practical applications is negligible, ensuring the algorithm’s practicality. Therefore, AACOES shows clear advantages in multi-constraint UAV inspection tasks. For the particle swarm optimisation algorithm, a discrete particle swarm optimisation method based on priority encoding has been adopted. This method represents particle positions as a set of real-valued vectors, which are then converted into discrete paths via a sorting mapping (i.e. transforming continuous values into a visit sequence according to a size-based order), thereby achieving a mapping from continuous space to permutation space. During the velocity update process, the fundamental principles of PSO are retained; however, by introducing a probabilistic exchange mechanism or a neighbourhood adjustment strategy, particles are enabled to perform effective searches within the discrete solution space. Furthermore, to avoid the issue of premature convergence that PSO is prone to in discrete problems, multiple preliminary experiments were conducted to fine-tune the inertia weight and learning factor, ensuring that the algorithm possesses adequate search capability for the current problem scale. All comparison algorithms were run under the same problem modelling, the same constraints (including range limitations and wind field effects), and a unified evaluation metric. Results were averaged across multiple repetitions to mitigate the impact of randomness. The experimental results indicate that, despite the discretisation improvements, PSO still exhibits certain limitations in this class of path planning problems. Meanwhile, GA performs relatively well due to its inherent suitability for combinatorial optimisation problems; however, it still falls short of AACOES in terms of convergence speed and solution quality stability.

This indicates that AACOES can more stably approach the global optimum during optimization, avoiding local optima. Such strong convergence performance makes AACOES particularly advantageous for inspection scheduling in complex environments, providing more reliable and efficient UAV inspection scheduling solutions.

Overall, the AACOES algorithm can not only effectively improve the efficiency and convergence of UAV inspections but also significantly reduce energy consumption. The simulation results fully validate the effectiveness and practicality of the algorithm, providing an innovative and efficient solution for optimizing multi-UAV power inspection tasks. As shown in Table 9, compared with traditional algorithms (PSO, GA, ACO) and recent advanced methods (enhanced NSGA-II, ISSA, and DRL), the AACOES algorithm proposed in this paper demonstrates significant advantages across multiple key metrics. Although DRL methods possess certain advantages in modeling complex environments, they entail substantial computational overhead and high training costs, whereas AACOES achieves superior solution quality while maintaining low computational complexity. Overall, AACOES strikes a better balance between optimization performance, stability, and engineering practicality, thereby validating the advanced nature and effectiveness of the method proposed in this paper.

Conclusion

This paper addresses the complex optimization problem of multi-UAV cooperative scheduling in power distribution network inspections and proposes an adaptive ant colony optimization algorithm based on an elite strategy (AACOES). By comprehensively considering UAV flight characteristics, battery endurance, and external wind fields, among other practical constraints, a scheduling optimization model close to real-world UAV inspection operations is constructed. To overcome the limitations of traditional ant colony algorithms in convergence speed and global search capability, an elite strategy and adaptive adjustment factors are introduced to optimize the pheromone update rules, effectively enhancing the diversity of the ant population and accelerating algorithm convergence, enabling it to efficiently find near-global optimal solutions under multi-constraint conditions. Furthermore, simulations comparing AACOES with advanced algorithms such as PSO, GA, and ACO validate its effectiveness, providing a high-efficiency and reliable technical approach for practical applications like power inspections, with significant theoretical and practical value.

In the future, the algorithm’s performance will be further optimized by incorporating communication models and asynchronous clock mechanisms to simulate real-world issues such as time synchronization errors, communication delays, and clock drift, allowing a more comprehensive evaluation of its performance in practical deployments. Additionally, the application of AACOES will be explored in more complex environments and larger-scale UAV formations, including field deployments and integration with other intelligent optimization algorithms, to further improve inspection efficiency and meet the growing demands of multi-UAV cooperative tasks.

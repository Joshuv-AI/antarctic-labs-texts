Subject: Comparing treatment algorithms in adult traumatic brachial plexus injury
Authors: Justus L Groen, Ranjit D Singh, Thomas A van Essen, Martijn JA Malessy, Willem Pondaag
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We would like to bring to your attention an ongoing research initiative titled ‘Comparing Treatment Algorithms in Adult Traumatic Brachial Plexus Injury’ (CTA-BPI). This multicenter study addresses a significant knowledge gap in the current management of adult traumatic brachial plexus injury (ATBPI), where treatment algorithms vary widely (Shin et al., 2022; Weekes et al., 2025) and there is a paucity of high-quality evidence regarding optimal intervention strategies. Currently, no studies have comprehensively evaluated the functional outcomes and quality of life (QoL) following different treatment approaches across expert centers in ATBPI patients.

Adult patients with a severe complete or incomplete brachial plexus injury after a high impact trauma are eligible for this study. Treatment strategies for ATBPI may involve a combination of nerve grafting, nerve transfers, tendon transfers, and free muscle transfers, performed at various intervals after trauma. Our research aims to identify the most effective treatment algorithm for restoring arm and hand function, including a comparison between early versus late surgical intervention (Pondaag et al., 2019). We plan to an observational comparative effectiveness study with target trial emulation (Cashin et al., 2025). We will start with profiling each participating center's real-world treatment algorithm and case-mix, using a predefined number of treatment strategies, for example: Early nerve repair–dominant algorithms, delayed nerve transfers, secondary reconstruction–dominant algorithms and muscle-transfer–focused algorithms. Effect estimation will be handled analytically using pre-specified hierarchical and causal-inference methods (e.g. instrumental variable analysis propensity-based adjustment) to compare functional and QoL outcomes between strategies while accounting for clustering and confounding (e.g. mixed-effects models).

This prospective, observational international multicenter study will leverage the variety of treatment preferences across specialized nerve and reconstructive surgical centers worldwide (Shin et al., 2022). The study timeline includes key milestones such as protocol development, provider profiling, collaborative presentations, and initiation of patient inclusion in October 2026.

To see that variation in practice becomes the main exposure of interest, we will harmonize inclusion criteria and outcome measures between centers. Following consensus statements on outcome measures in ATBPI (Miller et al., 2024; Wilson et al., 2024) the outcome assay will encompass the Brachial Assessment Tool (BrAT), MRC grading, range of motion, Brief Pain Inventory (BPI), EQ-5D, and safety assessments, aiming to generate robust evidence to guide clinical decision-making in this challenging field. As recovery time after ATBPI surgery is long, we plan to measure outcomes on timepoints up to 36 months after surgery.

We believe that this research initiative could significantly impact clinical practice by establishing evidence-based treatment strategies, ultimately improving functional and QoL outcomes for patients with ATBPI.

We welcome any interest or collaboration from dedicated nerve surgical clinics with nerve and reconstructive surgical teams, as well as nerve and trauma centers operating on ATBPI. We plan to have our start meeting in April 2026. We encourage interested nerve surgeons to visit our website and sign up https://www.leidennervecenter.org/compare-treatment-atbi or email to info@leidennervecenter.org.

The utility of telepsychology in health care: A qualitative exploration of psychologists’ experiences from Oman
Authors: Tamadhir Al-Mahrouqi, Ahmed AlHarthi, Kamila Al-Alawi, Hamed Al Sinawi, Asila Al-Zaabi, Amira Al Obeidani, Fatma Al Balushi, Mohammed Al-Alawi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Worldwide, telepsychology has become a growing area of interest, particularly after the COVID-19 pandemic. The literature supports its role in overcoming barriers and enhancing access to mental care; however, its usage in maintaining therapy is yet to be explored in Oman. This study explored psychologists’ experiences providing telepsychology, with a primary focus on the utility of this approach.MATERIAL AND METHODS:This qualitative study involved five psychologists who practice telepsychology in their daily clinics. Semistructured interviews were conducted to explore the experiences with the tele-service provided at Al-Massarah Hospital, a tertiary care mental health hospital. The data were comprehended using thematic analysis.RESULTS:The study revealed six main themes: supportive therapeutic advances, dual service delivery opportunities, sustained attendance, resolved geographical concerns, training opportunities, and individualized options. The psychologists reported significant benefits of telepsychology, primarily in maintaining therapeutic goals and preventing relapse of symptoms. Most of them revealed that it effectively enhances mental healthcare accessibility by overcoming geographical, financial, and logistical barriers. However, a few preferred face-to-face sessions due to the richness of nonverbal communication. Telepsychology has demonstrated considerable acceptance and attendance due to clients’ comfort and convenience, but more importantly, it offers flexible options tailored to clients’ needs, ultimately leading to optimal outcomes.CONCLUSION:This study supports the existing literature on addressing barriers to mental care access and optimizing clients’ outcomes in Oman. Policymakers could consider thoughtful investments in continuous professional training for psychologists in hybrid care methods and the evolving modalities of telepsychology.

Introduction

Telepsychology has emerged as a groundbreaking development and an area of interest in mental healthcare delivery, particularly in the post–COVID-19 era.[1] Although the effectiveness of telepsychology is established in the literature, its adoption in long-term maintenance therapy, sustaining remission, and preventing relapses remains underexplored, especially in the Middle East.[2] In Oman, a country with a total area of 309,500 km², where the population inhabits diverse geographical locations, including deserts, mountains, and valleys, access to mental health services is influenced by limited infrastructure and distance.[3] Telepsychology presents a promising tool to overcome these challenges; however, its acceptance and perceived utility among psychologists need to be established.

Globally, telepsychology has demonstrated mixed effectiveness, benefits, and adoption patterns. A meta-analysis conducted in Canada, reviewing the outcomes of guided internet-based cognitive behavioral therapy for mood disorders, reported significant improvements, including symptom remission and enhanced quality of life.[4] Another study exploring psychologists’ insights into using telepsychology during COVID-19 in South Africa found that telepsychology improved patients’ access to mental health care and highlighted the need for sustained technical training, particularly among psychologists practicing in the public sector.[5] Furthermore, a review from Saudi Arabia showed that telepsychology services should be viewed as a core component of mental care, not as a temporary service modality, facilitating continuity of care while constructing a standardized protocol for telepsychology within Arabic-speaking countries.[6] This emphasizes the potential gains of telepsychology and suggests a promising future for creating a structured framework for its implementation.

In Oman, most mental health services are provided in large cities such as Muscat, Nizwa, Sohar, and Salalah. Consequently, remote populations may face disparities in accessing mental health care. A study examining the telepsychology experiences of both therapists and clients in Oman reported several benefits of telepsychology, including convenience, improved access, reduced costs, and fewer hospital-acquired infections. Telepsychology also appears to enhance continuity of care, particularly for patients with chronic conditions or those requiring regular follow-ups, as well as bringing care directly to the patient’s environment.[7] Similarly, an additional locally conducted study indicated that telemedicine mitigated unnecessary overcrowding in healthcare facilities and reduced the risk of infection.[8] Furthermore, another study in Oman underscored the importance of adapting the existing infrastructure, supporting training, and enhancing the quality of telemedicine services.[9] These findings strengthen the argument for integrating telepsychology as part of a broader, more sustainable mental health policy in Oman.

This qualitative study explored the psychologists’ experiences providing telepsychology at the tertiary care mental health hospital, Al-Masarra Hospital, mainly focusing on the utility of telepsychology. This study builds on earlier work by focusing specifically on psychologists’ perspectives regarding the utility of telepsychology in maintaining therapeutic gains and preventing relapse, an area not previously explored in depth. These insights are markedly valuable as the healthcare system in the region transitions to more flexible, advanced, and technology-driven practices of mental care. Moreover, findings from this study aim to support the existing literature and guide mental health policymakers in developing a national framework for telepsychology practice in Oman and comparable regions.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

Given the limited research available on the benefits and application of telepsychology by psychologists in the Middle Eastern context, this study adopted an exploratory qualitative design. It involved conducting individual, face-to-face, semi-structured interviews to explore psychologists’ views on the effectiveness and practicality of telepsychology services in Oman.

The interviews were held at Al-Masarra Hospital, the only governmental tertiary care hospital in Muscat, Oman. The interviews were arranged at times convenient for the participating psychologists and conducted in private rooms to ensure confidentiality. Established in 2013 in Muscat, Al-Masarra Hospital is the only specialized psychiatric facility in Oman. It offers a comprehensive range of mental health services, including general adult psychiatry, child and adolescent psychiatry, geriatric psychiatry, forensic psychiatry, as well as addiction psychiatry. The hospital also provides allied health services such as psychology, social services, occupational therapy, physiotherapy, speech therapy, diet and nutritional services, dental health services, and music therapy.

Study participants and sampling

All seven psychologists employed at Al-Masarra Hospital were directly approached and invited to participate in the study. Electronic consent forms, including details about the proposed interview date and location, were sent to their official institutional emails. The purpose and objectives of the study, along with the potential benefits of participation, were clearly explained to each psychologist. Confidentiality was emphasized and assured throughout the entire process.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Psychologists were eligible to participate if they were delivering telepsychology services at Al-Masarra Hospital and had submitted an electronic consent form. Participation was voluntary.

Data collection tools and technique

Data collection took place between the 1st and 31st of January 2025, in which purposive sampling was applied. The psychology department consists of a total of seven psychologists. Five out of seven psychologists agreed to participate in the study. Two psychologists apologized and refused to participate due to busy clinic schedules and work overload. The participants’ information is presented in Table 1. Interviews were concluded once data saturation was achieved. Data saturation is defined as when no further information is collected from the interviews.[10] All interviews were conducted by the first author, a female Omani senior psychiatry resident with no prior professional relationship with the participants. An interview guide with open-ended questions on telepsychology consultations was developed in English, as shown in Table 2. Each interview lasted 20-30 min. With participants’ consent, all interviews were audio-recorded, transcribed word-for-word.

Data analysis

The data were analyzed manually using a qualitative thematic approach, focusing on the manifest content. The process followed the general framework proposed by Braun and Clarke.[11] The analysis involved several key steps: initially reading through the interview transcripts, highlighting relevant terms, assigning codes based on the context, organizing the codes into categories, and finally identifying overarching themes accompanied by reflective notes.

The coding process led to the emergence of three central themes. Each author independently categorized the data, and these categorizations were then reviewed collectively. The final stage of analysis was validated by two authors (TA and KA) to ensure accuracy and consistency.

Ethical considerations

Ethical clearance for the study was granted by the Ministry of Health’s Research and Ethics Committee in Oman (MoH/CSR/24/29102). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before involvement in the study. Participants were informed of the research aims, procedures, and their rights to withdraw from the study at any time without any penalized consequences. Confidentiality and anonymity were strictly maintained, and all data were securely stored and accessed only by the research team. The study adhered to the Helsinki Declaration guidelines.

Results

The results will be presented under six themes, which arose from the interviews and were discussed by the psychologists. Together, these themes reflect how telepsychology supports therapeutic flexibility and continuity of care, especially in maintaining engagement and preventing dropout among stable patients:

Supportive therapeutic advances

The psychologists revealed that the telepsychology clinics could offer ongoing emotional support to help patients remain in remission. Telepsychology showed its success and specifically for maintenance care. Its remote format enabled regular check-ins on mood swings and social interaction, reinforcing patients’ stability and giving a sense of connection between face-to-face sessions. This steady, supportive contact helped in providing sustained therapeutic advances and addressed emerging needs before they escalated.

Dual service delivery opportunities

While another psychologist shared additional experiences by revealing that in-person sessions are more effective, perhaps due to the rich nonverbal communication and contained clinical environment. Others acknowledged that telepsychology could yield positive outcomes. This balanced perspective suggested that psychologists’ comfort and familiarity could shape the choice of service delivery, even if it is delivered as empirical evidence. Therefore, peer experiences validated remote approaches. By recognizing both personal inclination and broader success stories, the psychologists highlighted the value of flexibility in matching treatment formats to practitioner strengths and patient needs.

Sustained attendance

The psychologists also highlighted how remote sessions could remove practical barriers by saving patients’ time and money, with no commutes, travel fees, or time off work. Equally important, being in a familiar environment often enhances comfort and lowers the threshold for emotional disclosure. Together, these factors could boost engagement and sustain attendance, ultimately supporting more consistent and patient-centered treatment.

Resolved geographical concerns

Nearly all participants in the study noted its advantages, particularly for individuals living in remote areas or managing work and family responsibilities simultaneously, which prevent them from attending in-person sessions. It became clear that flexibility in delivering telepsychology services could help the targeted patient populations. Flexibility in delivering telepsychology services benefits not only remote patients but also those with responsibilities that prevent in-person visits. Moreover, it is important to reflect upon the fact that the Sultanate of Oman is a geographically large country, covering a total land area of approximately 309,500 square kilometers. In addition, the Sultanate is geographically diverse with varied topographical structures such as deserts and valleys. Hence, a large part of the population could be living in remote areas outside the Capital City of Muscat.

Training opportunities

The psychologists emphasized that telepsychology excels in certain routine follow-up tasks, for example, the caregivers of children and adolescents, who require parent skills training. Without the need for travel or on-site appointments, both caregivers and psychologists reclaim valuable time, reducing scheduling conflicts and logistical burdens. This efficiency not only maintains continuity of care but also enhances accessibility, making it easier for families to stay engaged in ongoing training and support.

Individualized options

The flexibility inherent in teletherapy appeared to enable medication adherence by reducing logistical barriers and fostering continuity of care. For example, a patient with schizoaffective disorder who achieved remission in 2019 elected to continue remote sessions, not out of clinical necessity but to preserve the therapeutic bond established during in-person treatment. This sustained connection reinforced the importance of ongoing medication management and enabled timely monitoring of emerging symptoms, thereby supporting the patient’s commitment to their pharmacological regimen and helping to avert relapse.

The psychologists highlighted the importance of tailoring mental health services to individual preferences and capacities. For some clients, the convenience and familiarity of telephone-based interventions could encourage openness and continuity of care, enabling them to engage deeply without the barriers of travel or in-person anxiety. Conversely, clients who struggle to convey their affective nuances via voice alone may feel more supported by the richer nonverbal cues and therapeutic setting of a clinic visit. Recognizing these divergent responses highlighted that effective therapy rests not only on technique but on honoring each person’s comfort and communication style. Ultimately, offering both remote and in-person options empowers clients to choose the modality in which they feel safe and most understood.

Discussion

In this qualitative study, the findings illustrate the multidimensional utility and benefits of telepsychology in mental health care, particularly regarding the maintenance of therapy, accessibility, and provision of client-centered care. The psychologists’ insights align with the current literature, contributing to specific understandings of telepsychology services in Oman.

Post–COVID-19, telepsychology emerged as a significant tool for maintaining therapeutic goals and reducing the risk of relapse. In this study, psychologists emphasized how effective telepsychology could provide continuous emotional support through regular mood check-ins, which help keep patients in remission. This aligns with previous studies.[712] The case of a patient with schizoaffective disorder (mentioned by a study participant), who maintained remission through continued telepsychology sessions, highlighted how this service could strengthen the patient-psychologist therapeutic alliance and their medication compliance, a finding supported in the literature.[1314]

While the use of telepsychology is evident, most participants agreed that face-to-face sessions remain essential in mental healthcare delivery, particularly for patients who require rich nonverbal communication. The psychologists’ preference for in-person sessions, often dictated by familiarity or appropriateness, emphasizes the significance of their professional comfort in selecting the mode of service. Nonetheless, the participants’ recognition of the effectiveness of telepsychology may indicate a shared belief that flexibility between the two modalities, face-to-face and telepsychology, enhances the delivery of personalized care.[15] This duality in providing therapy reflects the hybrid care model, which emphasizes adapting the mode of delivery based on the patient’s needs.[1617]

The impact of telepsychology in addressing practical barriers, such as financial, time, and location-related difficulties, was also a recurring theme.[18] A recent quantitative study in Oman showed that nearly 80% of the participants were satisfied with the tele-psychiatry consultations, improving accessibility to mental care.[19] Furthermore, the findings are aligned with studies in comparable regions to Oman, such as Saudi Arabia, where telepsychology improved mental health access, especially in distant areas, as well as reducing stigma and anxiety linked to mental health by allowing more emotional openness and disclosure.[67]

Methodological considerations and limitations

The study included a relatively small sample size; however, the purpose of the study was not to generalize the findings, but to explore the benefits of telepsychology. The study setting was only in Muscat and specifically in one hospital: a situation that limits the transferability of the findings to other regions. However, as transferability was not the intention, the exploration of psychologists’ perceptions towards telepsychology was a valuable contribution to the entire knowledge. Moreover, telepsychology services are only available in Al-Masarra Hospital and are not being provided in other regions in Oman. The first author, who is a senior psychiatry resident, acted as an insider and totally understood the perception of psychologists in this study, and wanted to move the clinics to a higher level. At the same time, she acted as an outsider researcher who did not work in Al-Masarra Hospital, which allowed her to understand the perspectives of other professional groups as well. In our study, all five participating psychologists were female. This gender distribution reflects a broader trend in Oman, where psychology is increasingly pursued by women. More generally, this aligns with the ongoing feminization of the healthcare workforce in the country, a phenomenon observed across several healthcare professions.[20]

Conclusion

In Oman, telepsychology serves as a valuable tool for addressing challenges related to access to mental health care, particularly for clients residing in remote and underserved areas. The ability to deliver psychological support through virtual platforms helps bridge the gap created by geographic, social, and logistical barriers. Therefore, policymakers in mental health care should prioritize targeted investments to improve internet accessibility, upgrade technological infrastructure, and enhance digital literacy. These efforts are essential in an era where rapid technological advancements are reshaping the delivery of healthcare services. Furthermore, equipping psychologists with ongoing training in hybrid care models, including both in-person and remote modalities and emerging telepsychology practices, is crucial to ensure high-quality, adaptable, and sustainable mental healthcare delivery. These combined measures can contribute significantly to strengthening the national mental health system and promoting equity in service provision.

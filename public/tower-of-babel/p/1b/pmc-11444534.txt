A commentary on ‘Transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation as a novel therapy connecting the central and peripheral systems: a review’
Authors: Yang Yang, Renbin Liu, Shaowen Hu
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

The review article ‘Transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation as a novel therapy connecting the central and peripheral systems: a review’1 by Zou et al., published in the International Journal of Surgery, provides a comprehensive overview of how stimulation of the auricular region leads to profound changes in the central and peripheral systems. This enlightening review highlights the widespread use of Transcutaneous Auricular Vagus Nerve Stimulation (taVNS) in a variety of central and peripheral nervous system disorders. While the authors provide an insightful review of existing research, we hope to provide some constructive ideas for future research and clinical practice that the authors may consider.

First, in addition to the standardization of stimulus parameters and evaluation criteria mentioned in the paper, the standardization of localization, which is not mentioned in the paper, is equally important. Ears are small organs in the human body, and there are individual differences, which brings difficulties to the standardization of taVNS localization. Currently, the application of taVNS in different diseases lacks operational criteria for localization and accuracy assessment, resulting in poor comparability between study results. In order to improve the effectiveness and reliability of clinical applications, it is necessary to develop standardized taVNS positioning guidelines and operating specifications.

Secondly, the paper briefly mentions the cost-effectiveness of taVNS. If this topic further analyzes in detail the economic impact of taVNS, such as the initial equipment cost, maintenance, and long-term cost-effectiveness of taVNS compared with other treatments, it will highlight the economic advantages of taVNS combined with the advantages of non-invasiveness, which is of great value as a reference for the promotion and application of taVNS worldwide.

Thirdly, taVNS regulates the autonomic nervous system by stimulating the vagus nerve, but the autonomic nervous system itself has complex feedback mechanisms and regulatory processes. This complexity can lead to different side effects or therapeutic responses, which limits the predictability of taVNS to some extent. A deeper understanding of these biofeedback and regulatory mechanisms is important to optimize taVNS therapy2.

Finally, from the perspective of clinical workers of traditional Chinese medicine, in the theory of traditional Chinese medicine, different parts of the auricle correspond to different parts of the body and meridian system, mapping the functions of various organs and systems of the whole body. This mapping is strikingly similar to how taVNS regulates body function by stimulating the vagus nerve in the auricle. The meridian theory emphasizes that by stimulating specific acupoints, the flow of qi and blood in the human body can be regulated to balance Yin and Yang, thereby maintaining and restoring health. TaVNS has a similar role in regulating the autonomic nervous system. Therefore, the combination of acupuncture and meridian theory can provide a new perspective and method for further study of taVNS. This multidisciplinary integration will not only advance our understanding of the mechanism of action of taVNS, but will also optimize its clinical application to improve treatment outcomes and the health of patients3.

In summary, Zou et al.’s extensive review of taVNS makes a significant contribution to the field. Addressing the other considerations mentioned above could further enhance the understanding and application of this promising therapy.

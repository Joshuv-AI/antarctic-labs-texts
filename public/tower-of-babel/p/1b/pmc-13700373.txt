Durability and reconnection patterns at time of repeat ablation after first-time pulmonary vein isolation by monopolar focal pulsed field ablation
Authors: Martin H Ruwald, Martin Haugdal, Christian Jøns, Rene Worck, Christina Alhede, Arne Johannessen, Uffe Gang, Niels Sandgaard, Xu Chen, Morten Lock Hansen, Peter Karl Jacobsen, Jim Hansen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundReconnection patterns and durability after pulmonary vein isolation (PVI) or cavotricuspid isthmus block (CTIB) using monopolar focal pulsed field ablation (FPFA) at repeat ablation have not previously been evaluated.ObjectiveThis study aimed to characterize electrophysiological findings during repeat procedures in real-world patients with atrial fibrillation (AF).MethodsPatients undergoing repeat ablation (n = 23) for symptomatic recurrent arrhythmias after first-time FPFA-guided PVI (n = 212) between October 2022 and April 2026 underwent 3-dimensional high-density mapping and ad hoc re-ablation using radiofrequency or FPFA.ResultsAt index ablation, persistent AF was present in 13 of 23 (57%) patients, and CTIB was performed in 13 of 23 (57%) patients. Mean time to repeat procedure was 379 ± 282 days. Complete durable PVI was observed in 11 of 23 patients (48%; 95% confidence interval [CI], 27%–69%), corresponding to durable isolation in 74 of 94 veins (79%; 95% CI, 69%–87%). Regular atrial tachycardia was the predominant mechanism for recurrence, occurring in 17 of 23 patients (74%; 95% CI, 52%–90%). 17 conduction gaps were identified, predominantly clustered in carina regions (13 of 17 [76%]; P = .002). Reconnection occurred exclusively ipsilaterally, without significant differences between veins (17%–26%, P = .796). Durable CTIB was observed in 9 of 13 patients (69%; 95% CI, 39%–91%). After repeat ablation, 15 of 23 patients (65%; 95% CI, 43%–84%) remained free from atrial arrhythmia recurrence during 285 ± 204 days of follow-up.ConclusionApproximately half of patients demonstrated durable PVI at repeat procedures. Reconnection gaps clustered mainly in carina regions, highlighting the need for further studies evaluating chronic lesion durability after PFA.Keywords: Pulsed field ablation, Reconduction, Reconnection, Electrophysiology, High density, Mapping, Atrial fibrillation, Arrhythmia recurrence, Durability

Introduction

Pulmonary vein isolation (PVI) remains the cornerstone of invasive treatment for atrial fibrillation (AF). Thermal energy sources used for PVI, including radiofrequency (RF) and cryoballoon ablation, have demonstrated a temporal development from initial low to moderate long-term durability (range, 3%–62%), where frequent pulmonary vein (PV) reconnection is a limiting key factor for successful long-term clinical outcomes.1 Pulsed field ablation (PFA), based on irreversible electroporation of cardiac myocytes, has emerged as a novel energy modality capable of producing sufficiently deep lesions, suggesting an improved durability without evident extracardiac injury.2, 3, 4, 5 Early clinical studies reported very high durability at 3-month remapping studies across various PFA platforms,6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13 and the safety and clinical efficacy of single-shot PFA have since been confirmed in randomized trials and larger cohorts.14, 15, 16, 17, 18 Nevertheless, its clinical effectiveness still appears comparable to, rather than superior to, ablation using conventional thermal energy sources.14,15,19,20 More recently, chronic durability assessed during clinically indicated repeat procedures has been further evaluated, demonstrating moderate durability (all veins isolated) rates of 38%–45% and 50% for PVI using the Farapulse and Affera systems.19,21, 22, 23 Importantly, because PFA durability should not be regarded as a class effect between systems and as PFA technology continues to evolve, there is an ongoing need for industry-independent data, including durability outcomes and evaluation of reconnection patterns, to enable meaningful comparisons in real-world AF populations treated with different PFA platforms.

Methods

This prospective study included an all-comer paroxysmal or persistent AF patient cohort. Patients were referred for first-time PVI with or without cavotricuspid isthmus block (CTIB) and/or supplementary LA and right atrial linear lesions as either first-line treatment or if intolerant to, refractory to, or unwilling to take class I or III antiarrhythmic drugs (AAD). Inclusion was based on a single high-volume referral center from October 22, 2022, to August 15, 2025, and the latest follow-up for repeat procedure data on April 8, 2026, to allow for the possibility of repeat procedure by at least 6 months. Analysis of procedural and demographic anonymized data was approved by Herlev–Gentofte University Hospital Institutional Review Board (Case Number: 22035743). The research reported in this paper adhered to the Helsinki Declaration. The institutional review board waived the requirement for informed patient consent, as the study was classified as a quality assurance project and involved retrospective analysis of anonymized data. Generative AI (ChatGPT, OpenAI) was used solely for the preparation of the graphical abstract accompanying the article.

Index PFA procedure

The index point-by-point monopolar focal PFA (FPFA) procedure was performed as previously described.24 In brief, patients underwent general anesthesia or deep sedation, followed by standard venous access and transseptal puncture to reach the left atrium (LA). A Navistar SmartTouch irrigated catheter (Biosense Webster, Irvine, CA) was then advanced into the LA via a steerable sheath. Based on the intended strategy, a 3-dimensional high-density (HD) bipolar voltage and anatomical map was created using penta- or octaspline multielectrode catheters (Biosense Webster) before (for this study) final categorization of the ablation approach as “PVI only,” “PVI+” (including additional linear lesions), or “PVI with CTIB” ablation. Mapping was performed during coronary sinus pacing; if the patient presented with AF, electrical cardioversion was performed before mapping. The Centauri generator (CardioFocus Inc) was used and integrated as previously described with the Carto 3-dimensional mapping system (Biosense Webster).24 Ablation consisted of electrocardiogram-unsynchronized monopolar biphasic PF trains delivered at 25 A for the anterior wall/roof, CTIB, and mitral isthmus; and 22 A for the posterior wall, corresponding to 10 and 7 pulse trains, respectively. Lesions were placed point by point by manual annotation, generally placed as 6 mm tags following the CLOSE protocol25 but with 20%–30% overlap based on ECLIPSE AF data and initial experiences of first-pass efficacy.7,24 Wide-area circumferential ablation was the intended strategy for PVI and subsequent control of PVI with either remapping of the LA with and without supplementary pacing maneuvers for exit block from the PVs as per clinical standard. All index procedures were categorized as successful with confirmed PVI and linear lesion block.

Repeat procedure

For the repeat procedures, HD bipolar voltage mapping of the LA was performed as described for the index procedure. The bipolar voltage display was set to a range of 0.2–0.5 mV to delineate conduction gaps, healthy myocardium, and low-voltage areas. A comprehensive qualitative assessment integrating HD voltage, local activation time, and coherence vector maps was undertaken to determine isolation status, tissue characteristics, and the presence of fractionated electrograms, supplemented by formal testing of entry and exit block for each PV and across any linear lesions. Gap identification and localization was based on operator assessment, with subsequent offline review and confirmation using CartoNet (Biosense Webster). In practice, gap location was defined using a combination of local activation timing, electrogram voltage and morphology within the PVs, and the site of successful re-isolation or marked alteration in PV electrical activity, consistent with previously described methodologies.26,27 Subsequently, induction of trigger activity or atrial tachyarrhythmias (AT) was attempted using isoprenaline infusion targeting a heart rate of >100 beats per minute in combination with atrial burst pacing. Induced ATs were mapped using local activation time maps and coherence conduction vectors to identify critical isthmuses and re-entry circuits, with adjunctive entrainment mapping performed when required, in accordance with standard practice and prior descriptions.28, 29, 30, 31 Based on these findings, ad hoc ablation strategies were implemented during the repeat procedure. Before ablation near the coronary arteries, pretreatment with prophylactic intravenous nitroglycerine (glyceryl trinitrate) was administered typically using 0.4–1.0 mg. Following ablation, 1 or more confirmatory multielectrode HD bipolar voltage maps were additionally acquired to assess lesion sets in the left or right atrium. Additional paced local activation time mapping across ablation lines was performed when necessary to verify isthmus block or integrity of linear lesions. Follow-up data on complications and clinical recurrences after a 90-day blanking period were a composite of >30 seconds of arrhythmia recurrence, electrical cardioversion, repeat ablation, and initiation or escalation of AAD therapy—which were obtained through systematic chart review. All electrocardiogram recordings were reviewed to ensure diagnostic accuracy.

Statistical analysis

For continuous variables, the mean ± standard deviation or median ± interquartile range were used as appropriate. For categorical values, the number and percentages were used. Proportions were presented with Clopper–Pearson exact binomial confidence intervals (CIs) where appropriate, and the comparisons of proportions were performed using χ2 tests or Fisher’s exact test as appropriate. Tests of significance were 2-sided, with a P-value of ≤.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 212 patients were treated with initial (index) first-time treatment with FPFA PVI for AF with or without supplementary linear lesions. Of these patients, 23 (11%) underwent a clinically indicated repeat procedure because of significant arrhythmia recurrences, allowing for evaluation of PV and CTI durability. A total of 13 patients (57%) had persistent AF, and the mean time from first procedure to repeat procedure was 379 ± 282 days. At the time of the repeat procedure, patients were on average 69 ± 9 years of age, and 17 (74%) were male with a mean CHA2DS2-VASc score of 1.7 ± 1.2 (Table 1). During the index procedure, PVI was performed in all patients, and 8 patients (35%) had supplementary CTIB and 6 patients (26%) had supplementary other right and LA linear lesions.

Repeat procedural data and ablation strategy

The mean repeat procedure time was 107 ± 27 minutes, fluoroscopy time was 7 ± 4 minutes, and 7044 ± 3971 mapping points were on average produced for the first HD maps (Table 2). Most of the patients had regular AT as the predominant arrhythmia (17 of 23 [74%]; 95% CI, 52%–90%), of which 16 (94%) were inducible and deemed mappable. The various ATs are presented in Table 2. The planned ablation strategy was a combination of re-isolation of the PVs and/or additional linear lesions according to inducible ATs and the interpretation of the maps. Most of the cases (19 of 23 [83%]) were treated with FPFA. There were no complications, including no procedural ST elevations suggestive of coronary spasm.

Durability and reconnection patterns

The overall PVI durability per patient with complete isolation of all veins was 11 of 23 (48%; 95% CI, 27%–69%) (Table 3). The total number of durably isolated veins was 74 of 94 (79%; 95% CI, 69%–87%). There was no measurable difference in the rates of reconduction to the veins, with reconduction rates of 17%, 17%, 26%, and 26% for right superior, right inferior, left superior, and left inferior veins, respectively (P = .796). None of the patients had more than 2 reconnected veins, and in most of the cases, there was ipsilateral connection to both veins if any reconnection. The total number of reconnected veins was 20 of 94 (21%; 95% CI, 14%–31%), averaging 0.9 ± 0.9 per patient (all patients) and 1.7 ± 0.5 for the patients with any reconnection. There was no significant difference in reconnection patterns for the PVs comparing those who had additional linear lesions or posterior wall isolation performed with those who had PVI alone. The total number of recorded reconnection gaps was 17 among the 12 patients with reconnections. The gaps were significantly clustered in the carina regions, with 13 of 17 (76%, P = .005) gaps with numerical overweight in region 2 + 3, 6 + 7, and 10 + 11 of the 16-segment PV model (Figure 1). 8 of these 17 gaps were considered solitary or narrow reconnection to 1 vein only, with no observable specific pattern. The CTIB was evaluated as durable in 9 of 13 (69%; 95% CI, 39%–91%). The PV durability of the current study is shown in comparison to historical RF, cryoballoon, and PFA cohorts in Figures 2 and 3. Ablation strategy and follow-up

Based on the clinical AT, substrate evaluation, and the durability of PVs and linear lesions, the majority of patients had re-PVI with either new or re-CTIB (6 of 23 [26%]) or re-PVI with posterior wall isolation with or without CTIB (7 of 23 [30%]), whereas for the remaining patients, a combination of the above ablation strategies, including mitral–isthmus line, intercaval line and other non-PV trigger Ats, was selected. After a mean follow-up of 285 ± 204 days from the repeat procedure, the freedom from recurrence of clinically significant atrial arrhythmia or initiation or escalation of AAD was observed in 15 of 23 (65%) patients.

Discussion

This report presents the procedural and electrophysiological findings from clinically indicated repeat procedures following PVI using monopolar FPFA with the Centauri generator. The main findings were as follows: (1) modest durability with complete PVI in approximately 50% of patients; (2) pronounced clustering of reconnection gaps at the carina regions; and (3) a predominance of organized AT as the presenting clinical arrhythmia without evidence of gap–gap ATs. Currently, there is no available data on clinically indicated repeat procedures after point-by-point monopolar FPFA PVI.

Durability

It is well established that PV reconnection, reflecting limited lesion durability, is associated with higher arrhythmia recurrence rates and reduced clinical efficacy.26,32 The observed rate of PV reconnection is inherently higher (lower durability) in cohorts undergoing clinically indicated repeat procedures compared with mandated, protocol-driven remapping studies, largely due to patient selection but maybe also because of higher index procedural rigor and meticulousness in the protocol-driven remapping studies. This pattern has been demonstrated for RF and cryoballoon, where mandated repeat procedures report PV durability (vein level) of 44%–96% for RF and 41%–91% for cryoballoon, with complete durable PVI achieved in 54% and 49%, respectively.26,33, 34, 35, 36, 37 In contrast, clinically indicated repeat procedures from multicenter trials, such as FIRE AND ICE and CIRCA-DOSE, consistently showed lower durability, with PV durability of 48%–66% (complete PVI, 13%–17%) for RF and 65%–66% (complete PVI, 8%–22%) for cryoballoon (Figures 2 and 3).38,39 However, it is noteworthy that selected single-center cohorts have in fact demonstrated higher PV durability, reaching up to 85% for RF using the CLOSE protocol and 82% for cryoballoon ablation.40,41 For PFA, several platforms have been evaluated with waveform optimization in mandated remapping studies. Very high PV durability rates of 96%–97% were initially reported for the multispline Farapulse (Boston Scientific) system and the Sphere-9 catheter (Affera platform, Medtronic),12,42 whereas the dual-energy system (Biosense Webster) also demonstrated a relatively high PV durability of 87%, and the monopolar FPFA using the Centauri generator achieved 89%.7 However, the relatively short follow-up for these newer PFA technologies has limited the durability assessment in clinically indicated repeat procedures. The exception is the Farapulse platform, where overall PV durability of 65%–91% has been reported, and in the largest (but early adopter) datasets (MANIFEST-REDO and EU-PORIA), PV durability was 71%, with 38%–45% of patients having complete PVI of all veins (Figures 2 and 3).21,22

For monopolar FPFA using the Centauri generator, clinical experience of PVI remains limited, as workflow in many centers has favored single-shot systems for first-time PVI. Nonetheless, it is important to determine whether clinical recurrences reflect insufficient lesion durability, occur despite complete PVI, or are related to proarrhythmic substrate modification, such as iatrogenic AT arising from the lesion set. Because this specific PFA technology has only been commercially available for PVI since 2022 and has not been used widely commercially, data on repeat procedures—and thus chronic durability—are lacking. In this context, our findings of 50% complete durability and 79% PV isolation are comparable to the upper range reported for RFA and cryoballoon cohorts and align with outcomes from single-shot PFA systems as discussed above.

Reconnection patterns and atrial tachycardia

Numerically, reconnections were observed across all 4 PVs, with electrically conducting gaps identified in nearly all 16 regions of the LA–PV model but significantly clustered in the carina regions. This distribution pattern has also been described in RFA and cryoballoon studies.27 We speculate that these gap patterns may be explained by the following factors, of which some are facilitated by non-integration of the PFA system and the mapping system: (1) insufficient knowledge of catheter contact or force values along the ridge during PF applications, particularly at the left superior and inferior veins; (2) catheter instability or displacement in the ridge region; (3) limited initial attention to absolute impedance values displayed on the Centauri generator; (4) suboptimal PFA dosing; and (5) inadequate lesion overlap, reflecting a lack of effective lesion stacking.28 The relatively high predominance of AT in our cohort may have several explanations. First, the relatively high per-vein durability (79%) may have reduced the substrate for recurrent AF while favoring organized macroreentrant arrhythmias. Second, more than half of the cohort had persistent AF, and some underwent adjunctive linear ablation, where incomplete lesion sets may have contributed to AT, although the numbers are too small to draw firm conclusions. Finally, because AT is often more symptomatic than recurrent AF, referral for repeat ablation may have occurred earlier, introducing a degree of selection bias.

Limitations

This single-center study is limited by a relatively small sample size. Indications for repeat ablation were not standardized but determined on an individual basis. All patients undergoing first-time FPFA as the index procedure were included, encompassing the early experience and implementation phase of this technology at our center. Accordingly, the findings should be interpreted within this context. Durability of this particular PFA system should not be extrapolated to other PFA small tip point-by-point systems.

Conclusion

Durable PVI was achieved in 48% of patients and 79% of PVs at the time of repeat ablation following point-by-point monopolar FPFA. Although no distinct overall pattern of PV reconnection was identified, gaps were preferentially located in the carina regions. The predominant clinical recurrence was AT. Further studies are needed to better characterize lesion formation, durability, and mechanisms of AT following PFA.

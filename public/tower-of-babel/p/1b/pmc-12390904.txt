A 40-year bibliometric analysis of global research on neoadjuvant chemotherapy for oral cancer (1985–2024)
Authors: Xu Xiang, Ping Shi, Li Yang, Shumin Ma, Jian Kang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNeoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) has become a critical component in the treatment of oral cancer, enhancing tumor resectability and improving patient survival. This study aimed to perform a bibliometric analysis to systematically explore global research trends, key contributors, and emerging hotspots in NAC for oral cancer.MethodsPublications from 1985 to 2024 were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection. Bibliometric data were analyzed using R (version 4.3.3), VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), and CiteSpace (version 6.3.R1). These tools were employed to examine publication trends, co-authorship networks, institutional collaborations, and keyword co-occurrence and burst analysis.ResultsA total of 235 publications were identified, showing an annual growth rate of 7%. Japan led in publication output (47 publications), followed by China (38) and Germany (33), while the United States exhibited the strongest international collaboration. Chang Gung Memorial Hospital was the leading contributor, with 51 publications. The leading journals included Oral Oncology, Head and Neck, and the Journal of Cranio-Maxillofacial Surgery. Kreppel Matthias ranked first with an H-index of 9, 10 publications, and 234 total citations. Emerging research hotspots focused on “induction chemotherapy”, “biomarker expression”, and “multidisciplinary management”.ConclusionThis study provides a bibliometric analysis of global research trends and hotspots in NAC for oral cancer, offering valuable insights into key contributors, emerging themes, and collaborative networks. The findings have significant implications for guiding future research directions and integrating evidence-based strategies into clinical practice to improve patient outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03411-4.

Introduction

Oral cancer, primarily squamous cell carcinoma, is a malignant tumor originating from epithelial cells of the oral mucosa [1]. Commonly affecting regions such as the lips, tongue, buccal mucosa, hard palate, gingiva, and the floor of the mouth, oral cancer is classified under head and neck cancers and exhibits a high prevalence, particularly among individuals with tobacco and alcohol consumption habits [2, 3]. Early symptoms of oral cancer may be subtle, but as the disease progresses, patients often experience oral ulcers, pain, masses, bleeding, and difficulty swallowing [4]. In advanced stages, symptoms may include facial swelling, lymphadenopathy, weight loss, and hoarseness [5, 6]. Beyond its severe health implications, oral cancer imposes a substantial societal burden, causing pain, functional impairments, and psychological distress while incurring high treatment costs [7]. The treatment of oral cancer is a complex and multidisciplinary process involving surgical resection, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and targeted therapy [8]. Surgery remains a cornerstone for oral cancer treatment, offering the potential to completely remove the tumor and reduce recurrence risk, though reconstructive procedures are often necessary to restore appearance and functionality [9]. Radiotherapy, commonly employed for patients unable to undergo surgery or as an adjuvant treatment, effectively eradicates cancer cells and improves survival outcomes [10]. Chemotherapy, frequently combined with radiotherapy as concurrent chemoradiotherapy, enhances local control rates and survival but may result in side effects such as osteonecrosis [11]. In recent years, immune checkpoint inhibitors have become standard treatments for recurrent or metastatic oral cancers, leveraging the immune system to attack tumor cells; however, their efficacy varies among individuals, and adverse effects remain a concern [12]. Additionally, targeted therapies, particularly for human papillomavirus-associated oral cancers, have demonstrated promising therapeutic potential [13]. The application of neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) to reduce tumor size and improve surgical outcomes has also emerged as a key research focus [14].

Neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) refers to the administration of chemotherapy prior to surgery, aiming to reduce tumor size, improve surgical resection rates, decrease postoperative recurrence risks, and assess tumor sensitivity to chemotherapeutic agents [15, 16]. Commonly employed regimens include single-agent chemotherapy, such as methotrexate, and combination therapies, such as paclitaxel combined with cisplatin [17, 18]. Emerging strategies, such as combining immune checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., nivolumab) with chemotherapy and integrating targeted therapies (e.g., trastuzumab) with chemotherapeutic agents, offer novel approaches to treatment [19, 20]. Methotrexate-based NAC has been shown to significantly improve resection rates and survival outcomes in stage III oral cancer patients [18]. Additionally, studies have reported that paclitaxel-cisplatin NAC led to substantial tumor shrinkage, enabling successful surgical removal in a 62-year-old male patient [17]. Similarly, nivolumab combined with chemotherapy has demonstrated promising tolerability and efficacy in stage III/IV oral cancer patients [20]. While NAC shows considerable promise in enhancing treatment outcomes for oral cancer, patient responses vary, necessitating personalized treatment strategies.

Bibliometric analysis is a systematic method that uses mathematical and statistical tools to examine published literature, offering insights into research categories, co-authorship networks, keyword trends, and influential journals or articles [21, 22, 23]. Advances in oral cancer treatment have been reported in several bibliometric analyses, such as associations between oral cancer and poor oral health [24, 25], betel quid chewing [26], and immunity [27, 28]. However, research on NAC in oral cancer remains limited, and there is a lack of comprehensive reviews and systematic analyses. This gap underscores the need for a bibliometric study to better understand the current applications, efficacy, side effects, and impact of NAC on patient outcomes and quality of life. This study aims to employ bibliometric methods to analyze and extract valuable insights from a vast body of literature on NAC in oral cancer treatment. By revealing research trends, hotspots, and potential directions, the study seeks to guide researchers in identifying key areas for exploration and designing future clinical studies.

Materials and methods

Search strategies and data collection

We performed a systematic literature search within the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) due to its focus on high-quality, globally peer-reviewed academic publications, primarily traditional academic literature, including journal articles, conference proceedings, and books [29]. Additionally, the WoSCC database is multidisciplinary and comprehensive, offering a complete citation network and key bibliometric indices (e.g., JCR, IF, and H-index) [30]. Therefore, we selected it to obtain global academic information for bibliometric analysis according to previous studies [23, 31, 32]. This search focused on the publications related to oral cancer in conjunction with neoadjuvant chemotherapy (Table S1). Both search and export of data were performed on October 15, 2024, and included literature published from January 1, 1985, to October 15, 2024. The search strategy was as follows: TS=((cancer OR carcino* OR tumo$r* OR malign* OR neoplasm*) NEAR (oral OR intraoral OR mouth OR tongue OR buccal OR lip OR gingiva OR palate OR gum)) AND TS=(neoadjuvant chemotherap* OR neoadjuvant chemo*therap*). The search was restricted to articles written in English to ensure linguistic uniformity, and only peer-reviewed articles were included. Bibliometric data, including full records and cited references, were exported in plain text format to ensure comprehensive data collection. Extracted information included publication year, citation metrics, author details, institutional affiliations, geographic distribution, journal titles, and keywords.

Statistical analysis and visualization

The bibliometric analysis in this study utilized three tools: Bibliometrix R (version 4.3.3), VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), and CiteSpace (version 6.3.R1), each serving distinct analytical purposes.

R was used for quantitative bibliometric analysis, extracting key indicators such as publication counts, citation metrics, and keyword frequencies. Key bibliometric indicators, including the H-index, G-index, and M-index, were calculated to evaluate research productivity and scholarly impact [33, 34]. VOSviewer was primarily used for mapping collaboration networks [35]. The size of nodes in VOSviewer represents the frequency of occurrence, while the links between nodes indicate the strength of collaboration or co-occurrence. CiteSpace was employed to identify emerging trends and keyword bursts within the domain of NAC in oral cancer [36]. Additionally, journals were categorized using Journal Citation Reports (JCR) based on their impact factor (IF), which assesses the average number of citations per article published in each journal [37], thereby providing insights into their relative importance within this domain.

Results

Annual growth trend of publications

The flowchart outlining the screening and selection process was shown in Fig. 1. The initial search retrieved 502 records. Following the screening process, 235 studies between 1985 and 2024 were retained for the final analysis, with a steady annual growth rate of 7%. Authorship data revealed contributions from 1730 authors, with an average of 8.75 co-authors per document. Notably, 10.21% of the publications involved international co-authorship, highlighting the global collaboration within this field. The average number of citations per document was 28.72, underscoring the influence and academic impact of these works (Fig. 2A). The trend in the number of publications, depicted in Fig. 2B, revealed a steady increase over time, particularly after 2003. Between 2006 and 2012, the annual output fluctuated between 4 and 10 publications, reflecting a phase of moderate growth. A more pronounced rise was observed after 2014, culminating in a peak of 21 publications in 2021. The change in number of publications over time followed the trend line equation y = 0.3907X − 2.1346, with a correlation coefficient of 0.7198, indicating a strong positive relationship between years and the number of publications over the observed period.

The global distribution of publications in this field spans across 20 countries. As shown in Fig. 3A and Table S2, Japan led with 47 publications, accounting for 20% of the total, followed by China (38, 16.2%) and Germany (33, 14%). The United States and Austria ranked fourth and sixth with 25 and 14 publications, respectively, but they exhibited the highest multiple-country publication (MCP) ratio at 0.571 and 0.240, indicating strong international collaboration. In terms of total citations, France stood out with 2,048, followed by the United States (1047) and Germany (731). Among the 15 countries involved in international collaborations, with at least 2 articles, Austria and the United States had the highest number of collaborations with other countries (both link strength = 12), followed by the United Kingdom and Greece (both link strength = 10) (Fig. 3B), suggesting the vital roles of North America and Europe in the field of NAC in oral cancer.

Journals and co-cited journals

The collected publications span across 122 journals, with Oral Oncology leading the total publications with 23 papers, followed by Head and Neck (13 papers) and the Journal of Cranio-Maxillofacial Surgery (11 papers), as shown in Table S4. These journals reflected the multidisciplinary nature of the field, covering areas such as oncology, maxillofacial surgery, and radiotherapy. All three journals belong to JCR Q2. In terms of total citations, Journal of Clinical Oncology stood out with 467, followed by Oral Oncology (386) and the New England Journal of Medicine (246). The IF is an additional measure of a journal’s prominence in the field. The Annals of Oncology held the highest IF in 2023 at 56.7, underscoring its significant influence, while other journals, such as Clinical Cancer Research (IF, 2023 = 10) and International Journal of Radiation Oncology Biology Physics (IF, 2023 = 6.4) also maintained high impact factors, reinforcing their relevance and authority in NAC in oral cancer research. Co-occurrence networks analysis, reflecting how often the publications from two different journals are associated in the bibliographies of scholarly articles [38], showed that the three key journals with the highest total link strength were Oral Oncology (link strength = 55), Strahlentherapie Und Onkologie and the Journal of Cranio-Maxillofacial Surgery (both link strength = 29) (Fig. 6A). Additionally, coupling networks analysis was performed, capturing the degree to which the research published in two different journals relies on the same body of prior work [39], and the three key journals with the highest total link strength were also Oral Oncology (link strength = 1,325), Head and Neck (link strength = 825), and Journal of Cranio-Maxillofacial Surgery (link strength = 589) (Fig. 6B), signifying their broad reach and collaborative influence across various research domains.

The top 10 cited articles in the field of NAC in oral cancer were listed in Table S5. “Chemotherapy added to locoregional treatment for head and neck squamous-cell carcinoma: three meta-analyses of updated individual data. MACH-NC Collaborative Group. Meta-Analysis of Chemotherapy on Head and Neck Cancer,” had been recognized for its significant contribution, amassing a total of 1947 citations. This pivotal study was disseminated in the Lancet, which held an IF of 98.4, in the year 2000.

The study reported a small but significant survival benefit for chemotherapy when combined with locoregional treatment [40]. Following closely with an impressive 407 citations was the article “NCCN Guidelines Insights: Head and Neck Cancers, Version 1.2018,” This study, published in the Journal of the National Comprehensive Cancer Network with an impact factor of 14.8 in 2018, highlighted detailed recommendations of NAC for managing oral cancer mentioned in National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) Guidelines [41]. Rounding out the top three, with a substantial 355 citations, was the research titled “Phase III trial of initial chemotherapy in stage III or IV head and neck cancers: a study by the Gruppo di Studio sui Tumori della Testa e del Collo”. This piece, published in the Journal of the National Cancer Institute (IF = 9.9) in 1994, delved into the NAC’s potential to improve disease-free survival and reduce distant metastases [42].

Discussion

Interpretation of bibliometric findings

This bibliometric analysis provides a comprehensive overview of the global research trends and hotspots in neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) for oral cancer from 1985 to 2024. The study highlighted significant growth in publication output, strong international collaborations, and evolving research focuses, with recent shifts towards advanced clinical strategies and multidisciplinary approaches. The study identified contributions from 1730 authors across 20 countries and 122 journals, reflecting a highly collaborative and interdisciplinary research domain. Japan emerged as the leading country in terms of publication output, while Austria and the United States showed the strongest engagement in international collaborations. Institutions such as Chang Gung Memorial Hospital and the Medical University of Vienna played central roles as hubs of research, not only advancing national efforts but also fostering international partnerships. The significant role of journals such as Oral Oncology, Head and Neck and the Journal of Cranio-Maxillofacial Surgery emphasized the multidisciplinary nature of NAC research in oral cancer, bridging oncology, surgery, and radiotherapy, underscoring the importance of global collaborations and specialized publication platforms, which can guide researchers in identifying influential collaborators and journals for disseminating their findings. Furthermore, the concentration of research in high-impact institutions and journals suggests that advancing in this field requires strong affiliations and access to collaborative networks.

Keywords provide a critical lens to uncover shifting research priorities and emerging themes. The co-occurrence network revealed four major clusters, each representing different dimensions of NAC research, including chemoradiotherapy, NAC strategies, clinical evidence, and prognosis.

Chemoradiotherapy

Generally, CRT has been an adjuvant therapy against head and neck squamous cell carcinomas (HNSCC), including oral cancer, depending on the disease stage. Combination therapies, including docetaxel, cisplatin and fluorouracil (TPF) therapy, are treatment strategies focusing on the lesion site and cell cycle, which have been the primary approach to treat cancers that arise in the oral cavity [43]. In cases of advanced operable cancers, usually combinations of surgery and CRT are used pre- or postoperatively [44]. Moreover, inoperable advanced tumors are usually managed with CRT.

NAC strategies

NAC is given after surgery in many types of cancer for reducing the incidence of metastatic recurrence, including docetaxel, cisplatin, and fluorouracil [45], as well as 5-fluorouracil (5FU) [46]. These treatments refer to the administration of cytotoxic or antineoplastic drugs to patients with oral or oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma in any way to achieve the reduction of the primary tumor, early killing of invisible metastatic cells, reducing the chance of tumor recurrence and metastasis, and improving the cure rate [11].

Clinical evidence

The role of NAC in organ preservation and improved survival has also been explored. Licitra et al. evaluated the effects of NAC (Cisplatin and 5FU) in stage T2-T4 operable oral cancer in a multicenter clinical trial that showed that while increased chemotherapy did not provide any survival benefit, it led to less need for adjuvant radiotherapy and a higher incidence of mandibular preservation [47]. Similarly, Zhong et al. also suggested that NAC did not show a survival benefit, but a tendency to reduce distant metastasis [48]. The effect of NAC in locally advanced cancers has also been investigated. In a study by Patil et al., NAC resulted in tumor shrinkage and subsequent successful surgery in 43% of patients, with better clinical outcomes in these patients compared to non-responders [49]. Based on these evidence from randomized controlled trials, it is possible to conclude that NAC has a potential benefit for respondent survival.

Prognosis

As a double-edged sword, NAC can also induce mucositis, which is a significant burden for cancer patients [50], and the need for NAC should be fully evaluated. Many trials have tried to identify high-risk groups of people who would benefit from NAC. According to the guidelines, NAC is indicated for primary (pT3/T4), N stage (N2/N3), multigrade lymph node invasion, and near-boundary [41]. A comparative analysis observed the greatest benefit in patients with microscopic involved margins and extracapsular spread (ECS) of lymph nodes, identifying these 2 indications for NAC [51]. Other pathological factors such as perineural invasion (PNI), lympho-vascular embolism (LVE), grade, and single node positive have also been associated with biologically aggressive tumors. However, there is no consensus on the role of these factors in deciding whether to use adjuvant therapy. Even the NCCN guidelines recommend that adjuvant radiotherapy be considered at the doctor’s discretion in this case. Therefore, more high-quality multi-center trials should be conducted to identify the pathological indications of NAC for optimizing patients’ prognosis.

Among the top keywords with citation bursts, several stand out for their significance in shifts on NAC in oral cancer focus over time.

Period I (1985–2000) included “5 fluorouracil” (1997–1999) and “infusion” (1996–2005), indicating early application of 5 fluorouracil as chemotherapy against oral cancer. Five-fluorouracil (5-FU) is an anti-cancer medication, which is metabolized to 5-fluorodeoxyuridylate, and suppresses the synthesis of tumoral DNA [52]. Problems related to the emergence of drug resistance to 5-FU are the main obstacles against cancer treatment. Thus, there is an immediate need for further elucidation of important molecular pathways involved in the development of 5-FU resistance to enhance the efficacy of chemotherapy and improve the treatment prognosis in this period [53]. Attempts should be made to eliminate the cause via combination therapy, such as radiotherapy.

Period II (2001–2020) included “radiotherapy” (2010–2012), “concurrent chemoradiotherapy” (2014–2021) and “management” (2019–2021). This highlights the critical need to minimize disfiguring surgical procedures in clinical practice. Chemoradiotherapy (CRT) is a combination of chemotherapy and radiotherapy, in which chemotherapy is administered with six weeks of radiation therapy to oral cancer patients [54]. CRT has increased locoregional control and survival, better than what is achieved by radiotherapy or chemotherapy by itself, but it still has its various downsides, such as poor swallowing, osteoradionecrosis (ORN), dental caries, and hyposalivation [55]. Patients undergoing concurrent CRT frequently experience severe treatment-related toxicities that often necessitate treatment interruptions or dose reductions. This underscores the importance of proactive toxicity monitoring and management strategies. Implementation of comprehensive supportive care measures, including psychological support and palliative interventions, can significantly improve quality of life (QoL) in oral cancer patients receiving CRT.

Period III (2021–2024) included the “expression” (2020–2022) and “trial” (2022–2024), suggesting the research trends in this field. “Expression” highlighted a growing focus on identifying biomarkers to predict treatment responses in NAC for oral cancer, aligning with the broader trend of precision oncology [56, 57]. Advances in gene expression and protein marker research have paved the way for personalized treatment strategies, improving patient outcomes. Oral cancer, with a 5-year survival rate of approximately 50%, is significantly influenced by epigenetic mechanisms such as histone modifications, aberrant DNA methylation, and deregulated microRNA (miRNA) expression. MiRNAs, as critical regulators of carcinogenic signaling pathways, have emerged as key biomarkers for early diagnosis, prognosis, and innovative therapies. Furthermore, recent studies emphasize the role of risk factors in miRNA-mediated mechanisms, highlighting their potential in advancing prevention and treatment approaches [58, 59, 60].

“Trial” (2022–2024) underscored the critical role of clinical trials in advancing NAC research for oral cancer. Recent studies have focused on innovative drug combinations, reduced-toxicity regimens, and strategies to improve quality-of-life outcomes for patients undergoing NAC [61, 62]. These results highlight the importance of chemotherapy in specific clinical contexts, particularly when integrated with locoregional treatments, and emphasize the ongoing need for research to refine chemotherapy protocols and optimize patient outcomes [63]. These emerging trends underscore the field’s focus on translating basic research findings, such as molecular biomarkers, into actionable clinical strategies. Moreover, the growing emphasis on trials reflects a demand for evidence-based approaches to improve patient outcomes.

Clinical implications and limitations

This first bibliometric analysis contributed to the understanding of global research dynamics in NAC for oral cancer. By identifying influential authors, institutions, and journals, it offered a roadmap for researchers to navigate the field effectively. Since the NAC use in 1985 in oral cancer by Weaver et al., NAC has been investigated with great enthusiasm, but has also faced some skepticism with regards to the impact on disease outcomes, given that significant improvement in overall survival (OS) is still doubted [64]. Thus, dissecting hotspots and research trends for NAC will aid us to draw conclusions that can guide clinical practice especially as new neoadjuvant therapies are joining the armamentarium. This study provides emphasis on multidisciplinary approaches, such as neoadjuvant radiotherapy, targeted therapy and immunotherapy for higher survival rate [65]. Previous bibliometric analyses have been conducted on NAC combined with immunotherapy in bladder cancer and cervical cancer [66, 67], suggesting its promise in cancer treatment. Therefore, in addition to frontiers mentioned above, we speculate that NAC combined with immunotherapy and targeted treatments is another research trend, which may become the standard of care for oral cancer, particularly those with borderline or unresectable cancers. Besides, due to the complex nature of NAC, another frontier may be identification of reliable predictive biomarkers for treatment response based on clinical evidence. The ability to accurately predict patient responses to NAC would facilitate personalized treatment adjustments, either intensifying or reducing therapeutic regimens as appropriate. This approach ultimately aims to optimize clinical outcomes in the management of oral cancer.

Despite its contributions, this study also had limitations. First, the bibliometric analysis was conducted using the WoSCC database, which, while comprehensive, may have excluded relevant studies indexed in other databases such as PubMed or Scopus. This could introduce a selection bias and limit the generalizability of the findings. Second, non-article types such as editorials, books, and conference proceedings were not included, potentially omitting broader insights. Third, only 235 articles were included due to restrictive search criteria, which may influence the robustness of results. Finally, while keyword merging ensures consistency, it may overlook nuanced differences between related terms, limiting the granularity of the analysis.

Conclusion

This study used bibliometric methods, including keyword co-occurrence and citation burst analysis, to evaluate global research trends and hotspots in neoadjuvant chemotherapy for oral cancer. Research hotspots focus on therapeutic strategies such as induction chemotherapy, biomarker-driven approaches, and multidisciplinary management, while research frontiers emphasize clinical trials and precision medicine. These findings emphasized the importance of biomarkers, precision medicine and multidisciplinary approaches, providing valuable insights for researchers and clinicians by identifying key contributors, emerging trends, and influential journals, ultimately advancing research and clinical practices in this domain. The significant findings of this study have potential applications in improving clinical care, optimizing treatment strategies, and shaping future research directions in oral cancer.

Long-term oncologic outcomes of robot-assisted radical cystectomy: Prognostic indicators and complication profiles from a high-volume single-surgeon series
Authors: Sung Goo Yoon, Tae Young Park, Hyun Jung Jin, Tae Il Noh, Ji Sung Shim, Min Gu Park, Sung Gu Kang, Seok Ho Kang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeRobot-assisted radical cystectomy (RARC) is globally accepted, but single-surgeon, high-volume data linking perioperative factors with long-term oncologic outcomes are limited.Materials and MethodsWe retrospectively reviewed 304 consecutive RARC cases performed by one surgeon between 2007 and 2024. Total intracorporeal urinary diversion (ICUD) was used in 73.0% of patients and orthotopic neobladder (ONB) reconstruction in 64.5%. Median follow-up was 51.21 months. Primary endpoints were overall survival (OS), cancer-specific survival (CSS), and recurrence-free survival (RFS). Secondary endpoints included complication profiles and pentafecta, defined as margin-negative resection, ≥16 or more lymph nodes, no Clavien–Dindo grade ≥3 or higher complication within 90 days, no recurrence within 12 months, and no ureteroenteric stricture.ResultsMean operative time was 447.61 minutes, median lymph-node yield was 31.0 in the extended PLND group and 24.0 in the standard PLND group, and the positive margin rate was 1.0%. ICUD was associated with shorter hospitalization than extracorporeal diversion (13.81 days vs. 17.03 days, p=0.026) and fewer early high-grade complications. Seven-year OS, CSS, and RFS were higher in pentafecta achievers (69.3%, 94.2%, and 82.6%), and 7-year OS was also superior in patients with organ-confined (≤T2) and node-negative disease (69.0% vs. 28.6% and 67.5% vs. 19.4%). Complications occurred in 59.7% overall, with 14.9% major; infections were most common (20.7%).ConclusionsIn a high-volume single-surgeon series, RARC with ICUD provided durable oncologic control, acceptable morbidity, and high pentafecta achievement. Pathologic T and N stage and pentafecta were significant prognostic indicators that may assist in refining patient selection, guiding postoperative surveillance, and informing survivorship care.

INTRODUCTION

In 2022, 614,298 new cases of bladder cancer were reported worldwide, placing it within the ten most frequently diagnosed cancers and indicating a 7.1% increase from 2020 [1]. The disease exhibits a marked male predominance, comprising 5.4% of all newly diagnosed cancers in men. Radical cystectomy combined with pelvic lymph-node dissection (PLND) is regarded as the gold standard management for muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) and for certain patients with high-risk recurrent non–muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC) [2]. PLND contributes to precise pathological staging and improved prognostic assessment—particularly in terms of lymph-node density and tumor burden—as demonstrated in previous studies [34].

Introduced in 2003, robot-assisted radical cystectomy (RARC) has progressively emerged as a preferred alternative to open radical cystectomy (ORC), demonstrating equivalent oncologic efficacy alongside superior perioperative outcomes. Findings from RAZOR, iROC, and national cohort studies consistently indicate that RARC with intracorporeal urinary diversion (ICUD) decreases intraoperative blood loss, transfusion rates, and length of hospitalization, although operative time is typically prolonged [56789].

Although the body of evidence supporting RARC is expanding, studies involving large case volumes performed by a single surgeon within a uniform institutional environment remain limited. Such investigations are critical for reducing inter-operator variability and enabling consistent longitudinal assessment of clinical outcomes. Progressive accumulation of surgical experience with RARC has been associated with improvements in operative parameters and an increased probability of achieving composite outcome measures, such as the pentafecta—a metric indicative of optimal surgical performance [101112].

This study presents an analysis of more than 300 sequential cases of RARC, all conducted by a single, experienced surgeon at a high-volume institution. The primary aim is to assess how perioperative variables—such as the type of urinary diversion, pathological T and N staging, lymph node yield (LNY), and the attainment of pentafecta—relate to long-term oncologic outcomes and treatment-related morbidity. The consistent surgical technique and adherence to a standardized institutional protocol enable generation of reliable, clinically meaningful data. These findings contribute to a more nuanced understanding of prognostic perioperative indicators in RARC, reinforcing their practical relevance in surgical planning and to guide long-term surveillance strategies.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

1. Study design and population

A retrospective cohort design was applied, encompassing all RARC procedures conducted by one surgeon at Korea University Anam Hospital from August 2007 to January 2024. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Korea University Anam Hospital (approval number: 2025AN0277), and the requirement for informed consent was waived owing to the retrospective design and use of de-identified clinical data, which entailed no additional risk to participants.

2. Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Patients treated for concurrent non-bladder malignancies or whose initial treatment was performed elsewhere without complete data were excluded. Baseline demographics, perioperative variables, pathological features, and long-term outcomes were extracted from medical records.

3. Variables and definitions

Baseline variables included patient age, sex, body mass index (BMI), administration of neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC), and prior pelvic radiotherapy. Perioperative parameters comprised transfusion requirements, operative time, hospital length of stay, occurrence of postoperative complications, and type of urinary diversion—categorized as intracorporeal, extracorporeal, or ureterocutaneostomy (UC). Urinary reconstruction techniques were further categorized into orthotopic neobladder (ONB) and ileal conduit (IC) formation.

Pathologic assessments included tumor stage, lymphnode status, lymph-node yield, and the presence of positive soft-tissue surgical margins. Standard PLND was defined as excision of nodal tissue in the external and internal iliac chains, the obturator basin, and the common iliac distribution, with inclusion of presacral and Cloquet’s nodes. Extended PLND encompassed the standard template and extended proximally to the aortic bifurcation and, where indicated, to the inferior mesenteric artery [41314].

The primary oncologic end points were overall survival (OS), cancer-specific survival (CSS), and recurrence-free survival (RFS); survival curves were estimated with the Kaplan–Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. Postoperative complications were classified according to the Clavien–Dindo system and stratified into early (≤30 days) and late (31–90 days) periods. Cacciamani et al. [12] introduced the composite outcome termed ‘pentafecta,’ which requires five conditions: negative surgical margins, harvest of at least 16 lymph nodes, no major (Clavien–Dindo grade ≥3) complications within 90 days, absence of recurrence during the first postoperative year, and no ureteroenteric stricture necessitating intervention.

4. Statistical analysis

Survival probabilities were estimated using Kaplan–Meier methods, and subgroup comparisons—such as organconfined (≤T2) versus locally advanced (≥T3), node-negative versus node-positive, and pentafecta achievers versus non-achievers—were performed using log-rank tests. All analyses were conducted in IBM SPSS Statistics version 26 (IBM Corp.) and R version 4.1.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing), with statistical significance defined as two-sided p<0.05.

RESULTS

A total of 304 RARC procedures were performed from 2007 to 2024, including 222 ICUD (73.0%) and 66 extracorporeal urinary diversion (ECUD) (21.7%) cases (Table 1, Supplementary Table 1). Demographic factors—age, sex, and BMI—were balanced between groups, and median follow-up was 51.21 months. ICUD was associated with a significantly shorter hospitalization (13.81±6.98 days vs. 17.03±10.14 days, p=0.026), while operative time remained comparable (453.25±95.06 minutes vs. 455.50±82.38 minutes, p=0.464).

Within the whole cohort, 64.5% received an ONB and 30.3% an IC. Patients who received ONB were significantly younger than those in the IC group (62.77±9.14 years vs. 70.20±8.30 years, p<0.001). ONB was associated with a longer operative time (463.55±90.16 minutes vs. 432.92±93.44 minutes, p=0.015).

Pathologic analysis indicated soft tissue surgical margin involvement in only 3 of 304 cases, reflecting a 1.0% positivity rate. The median LNY was 31.0 nodes in the extended PLND group and 24.0 nodes in the standard PLND group. Lymph node metastases were identified in 17.4% of the overall cohort (Table 2).

The 3-, 5-, and 7-year OS rates were 73.9%, 62.8%, and 57.6%, respectively. CSS at the same intervals was 92.6%, 90.4%, and 88.2%, while RFS was 72.0%, 67.7%, and 66.0%. Subgroup analysis showed significantly higher 7-year OS in patients with organ-confined disease (≤T2) compared to those with locally advanced disease (≥T3) (69.0% vs. 28.6%). Similarly, node-negative patients exhibited a 7-year CSS of 91.8%, compared with 68.6% in node-positive cases. Pentafecta was achieved by 87.2% of the cohort, significantly higher in the ICUD group than in the ECUD group (90.1% vs. 77.3%, p=0.011). Patients who achieved pentafecta showed significantly superior survival outcomes (7-year OS: 69.3%, CSS: 94.2%, RFS: 82.6%) compared to those who did not (Tables 3, 4, Figs. 1, 2, 3).

Overall, postoperative morbidity was reported in 59.7% of patients, with early (≤30 days) and late (31–90 days) complications occurring in 53.1% and 19.1%, respectively. Low-grade events (Clavien–Dindo 1–2) were documented in 44.8%, whereas high-grade complications (3–5) occurred in 14.9%. Total complication rates did not differ between ICUD and ECUD (59.9% vs. 59.1%, p>0.999), but severe complications were significantly more frequent after ECUD during both the early (21.2% vs. 5.9%, p<0.001) and late phases (12.1% vs. 6.3%, p=0.047). A similar pattern was observed between diversion types, as ONB and IC groups had comparable overall rates (59.7% vs. 59.8%, p>0.999), yet early grade 3–5 events were more common in the IC group (15.2% vs. 6.6%, p=0.022).

On subgroup evaluation, ONB and IC diversion types showed no significant differences within either ICUD or ECUD. Notably, ONB patients undergoing ECUD experienced higher rates of grade 3–5 complications than those with ICUD, both overall (32.0% vs. 8.8%, p=0.003) and in the early postoperative interval (20.0% vs. 4.7%, p=0.014). In the IC subgroup, overall complication rates were comparable between ICUD and ECUD (62.7% vs. 56.1%, p=0.526); however, early severe complications were more common in the ECUD group (22.0% vs. 9.8%, p=0.049).

The most common postoperative complication was infection (20.7%), followed by gastrointestinal (11.8%) and genitourinary (11.5%) events. Sepsis requiring ICU (intensive care unit) admission occurred in 2.3% of cases during the early postoperative period and in 0.7% during the late period (Tables 5, 6).

DISCUSSION

Radical cystectomy, combined with bilateral PLND, remains the gold-standard curative approach for patients with MIBC and select cases of high-risk recurrent NMIBC [2]. While the efficacy of radical cystectomy in oncologic control is well established, the past two decades have witnessed increasing adoption of RARC due to its association with favorable perioperative outcomes. Randomized controlled trials, including RAZOR and iROC, have validated that RARC offers comparable oncologic outcomes to ORC while conferring benefits such as reduced blood loss, lower transfusion rates, shorter hospitalization, and fewer wound-related complications [5678]. This has accelerated the global shift toward minimally invasive approaches for bladder cancer surgery.

Although interest in RARC continues to grow, comprehensive long-term data—particularly those derived from high-volume series led by a single surgeon—remain relatively scarce. While multicenter studies contribute valuable perspectives, their findings are frequently influenced by heterogeneity in surgical techniques, patient populations, and perioperative care protocols. In light of these limitations, the present study offers detailed long-term outcomes from a consistently managed, high-volume, single-surgeon cohort. With a median follow-up duration of 51.21 months, it affirms the prognostic value of standardized perioperative indicators in RARC and supports their application in long-term patient management [101115].

The 7-year OS, CSS, and RFS rates were 57.6%, 88.2%, and 66.0%, respectively. These outcomes correspond closely with and, in some respects surpass, those reported in prospective international registries [1516]. Such findings underscore the reproducibility and clinical utility of standardized robotic surgical protocols in high-volume institutions and provide further evidence supporting the long-term effectiveness of RARC when implemented under optimized surgical and perioperative conditions.

A key strength of this study lies in the consistent implementation of comprehensive pelvic lymphadenectomy. The median LNY was 31.0 nodes for extended PLND and 24.0 nodes for standard PLND, exceeding the conventional threshold of 16 nodes suggested in prior studies for accurate pathological staging and a potential survival benefit [34]. Higher lymph-node yield has been associated in the literature with improved staging accuracy and, in some series, better oncologic outcomes, particularly in node-positive disease [413]. In the present cohort, this consistent PLND approach reflects adherence to guideline-concordant practice and stable technical performance.

Notably, the proportion of patients with node-positive disease in our cohort was 17.4%, which appears lower than the 20%–30% range reported in previous studies. Because most prior studies included predominantly NAC-naïve populations, this discrepancy may be partially attributable to differences in NAC exposure. Among the 304 patients included, 107 (35.2%) received systemic chemotherapy, and of these, 24 participated in clinical trials involving agents such as pembrolizumab, enfortumab vedotin, nivolumab, and cetrelimab. Detailed regimen distribution is presented in Supplementary Table 2.

However, no clinically meaningful differences in OS, CSS, or RFS were observed between patients who received NAC and those who did not receive it. The corresponding Kaplan–Meier curves and three-, five-, and seven-year survival estimates for the NAC and no-NAC groups are presented in Supplementary Fig. 1 and Supplementary Table 3. This lack of divergence in outcomes likely reflects therapeutic heterogeneity, including the incorporation of various regimens used in clinical trials. Systemic therapy can down-stage nodal disease and may partly explain the lower nodal-positivity rate [17]; prospective studies are needed to confirm this hypothesis. Recent literature has further highlighted the evolving therapeutic landscape for patients who are ineligible for cisplatin-based chemotherapy, including the increasing use of immune checkpoint inhibitors and novel antibody–drug conjugates. These emerging systemic options underscore the ongoing shift toward personalized multimodal treatment strategies in advanced bladder cancer [18].

Pathologic staging remains a critical determinant of patient outcomes. In our study, patients with organ-confined disease (≤T2) had a 7-year OS of 69.0%, compared with 28.6% in those with locally advanced disease (≥T3). Similarly, node-negative patients had a 7-year CSS of 91.8%, significantly higher than the 68.6% observed in node-positive individuals. These results are consistent with large retrospective analyses that underscore the prognostic power of T and N staging [1617]. Similar trends have been observed in other urologic malignancies, where refined staging and risk classification have been shown to enhance prognostic discrimination. Jung et al. [19] reported that magnetic resonance imaging-based risk stratification improved prediction of oncologic outcomes after radical prostatectomy.

An important addition to contemporary outcome assessment is the use of composite endpoints such as the pentafecta. First proposed by Cacciamani et al. [12], the pentafecta includes five parameters: (1) negative soft tissue surgical margins; (2) removal of ≥16 lymph nodes; (3) absence of Clavien–Dindo grade ≥3 complications within 90 days; (4) no disease recurrence within 12 months; and (5) absence of ureteroenteric strictures requiring intervention. In our cohort, pentafecta was achieved in 87.2% of patients, with significantly better oncologic outcomes in this subgroup: 7-year OS of 69.3%, CSS of 94.2%, and RFS of 82.6%. These data reinforce findings from multicenter evaluations which demonstrated pentafecta as a validated surrogate for long-term success after radical cystectomy [20].

Complication rates remain an important quality benchmark. In our study, 59.7% of patients experienced postoperative complications, with 14.9% classified as Clavien–Dindo grade 3–5. This is comparable to prior reports on RARC, which document major complication rates of 10%–20% [682122]. Infectious complications were most common (20.7%), followed by gastrointestinal (11.8%) and genitourinary events (11.5%). These complication patterns align with reports from Korean multicenter studies and international RARC cohorts.

To evaluate temporal trends and potential learning-curve effects, we performed an additional chronological analysis by dividing the cohort into six equal periods (Supplementary Table 4). Although no marked stepwise improvement was observed, the length of hospital stay gradually decreased over time and early Clavien–Dindo grade 3–5 complications showed a mild downward trend. Pentafecta achievement and LNY fluctuated without a consistent directional pattern. These modest changes suggest incremental refinement over time rather than a distinct early learning-curve effect, and may also reflect temporal variation in case mix and NAC utilization. Detailed comparisons across the six chronological periods are summarized in Supplementary Table 4.

The selection of urinary diversion method was found to have a significant influence on perioperative outcomes. Patients who underwent ICUD experienced shorter hospital stays (mean, 13.81 days vs. 17.03 days, p=0.026) and a lower incidence of severe complications compared to those who received ECUD. These findings align with recent reports from the International Robotic Cystectomy Consortium and other series, which have demonstrated that ICUD—although associated with longer operative durations—results in reduced perioperative morbidity when performed by experienced surgical teams [112123]. In our cohort, ICUD was employed in 73.0% of cases, reflecting the institution’s progression toward fully robotic urinary reconstruction. Notably, grade 3 to 5 complications were significantly less frequent in the ICUD group during the early (≤30 days) postoperative period compared to ECUD, across both urinary diversion subtypes. This may be explained by the higher incidence of wound infections, fascial dehiscence, and bowel-related complications in ECUD, which are often associated with external bowel manipulation during reconstruction. In contrast, the intracorporeal approach limits bowel handling and exposure, thereby reducing the risk of contamination—particularly in patients with multiple comorbidities [9]. These advantages likely account for the growing preference for ICUD in experienced centers.

In addition, the observed difference in LNY between ICUD and ECUD, particularly the higher yield in standard PLND but not in extended PLND, is likely attributable to temporal confounding rather than diversion technique. ICUD adoption increased gradually over the 17-year study period and coincided with the surgeon’s continuous accumulation of technical proficiency, including improvements in lymphadenectomy efficiency and template adherence. Therefore, the enhanced LNY associated with ICUD appears to reflect evolving surgical experience rather than an intrinsic advantage of the diversion approach itself.

Among the types of urinary reconstruction, ONB was primarily offered to younger patients. Although ONB was associated with longer operative times, it resulted in shorter hospitalization compared to IC. The overall complication rates did not differ significantly between the ONB and IC groups. However, early Clavien–Dindo grade 3 to 5 complications were more frequently observed in the IC group. This trend may reflect the higher baseline frailty associated with older age in patients selected for IC diversion. These findings highlight the importance of tailoring the choice of urinary reconstruction to the individual patient’s physiological reserve, comorbidities, and functional status [1723].

Despite its strengths, including uniform surgical technique and long-term follow-up, our study has limitations. The retrospective design limits causality, and the absence of a contemporaneous ORC control arm precludes direct comparison. Additionally, the transition from ECUD to ICUD during the study period introduces a temporal confounder, as the observed reduction in complications may partly reflect cumulative surgical experience and procedural refinement. Nonetheless, the consistency of outcomes across subgroups and alignment with external data, support the generalizability of our findings to high-volume settings.

CONCLUSIONS

In conclusion, our analysis confirms that RARC with ICUD, when performed by experienced surgeons, demonstrates durable oncologic efficacy with an acceptable complication profile. We identified the achievement of pentafecta and pathologic T and N stage as significant predictors of survival. ICUD was associated with shorter hospitalization and fewer high-grade complications. Furthermore, our high-volume, single-surgeon series demonstrated consistent long-term survival outcomes, highlighting the clinical utility of integrated perioperative metrics as reliable prognostic indicators. These findings support the integration of perioperative metrics, such as pentafecta achievement, into preoperative risk stratification models and long-term oncologic surveillance protocols.

MADTRAS: Dataset for aspect-based sentiment analysis of movie reviews in Tamil
Authors: M Arunmozhi, R Sunitha, D Rajalakshmi, Syam Mohan E
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rise of online platforms has led to a growing trend of people expressing their thoughts and emotions in their native languages. Movies have been a predominant topic of discussion on online platforms where people reflect on various aspects of movies. Aspect-based Sentiment Analysis (ABSA), a computational technique, assists in examining the sentiments hidden in these discussions. Two challenges arise when attempting to use ABSA to identify sentiments in movie reviews written in the Indian regional language Tamil; the former being the unavailability of potential Tamil movie review datasets and the latter being the difficulty that arises due to the agglutinative nature of Tamil Language. This work addresses the first challenge by curating an annotated movie review dataset in Tamil, MADTRAS (Dataset for Aspect-based Sentiment Analysis of Movie Reviews in Tamil). The quality of the dataset is ensured through content and annotation evaluation. To prove the efficiency of the dataset, the multilingual BERT (mBERT) was used, and the performance was compared with other Deep Learning(DL) models.Keywords: ABSA, Correlation, Data curation, mBERT, Transformers

Background

The background work explores the dataset available publicly on different Indian languages such as Tamil, English, Kannada, Telugu, Malayalam and Hindi. They are classified into Generic Text reviews/ Comments and Domain Specific Datasets based on the types of data curated.

Generic Reviews are unstructured, multilingual data scrapped from YouTube and Twitter. Studies in Tamil, Marathi, Telugu and Malayalam use ML and DL models such as SVM [2], CNN+BiLSTM [3] and BRAE [4]. Though it has moderate accuracy, it faces challenges such as ambiguity and non-availability of data. Domain-specific datasets, especially those containing movie reviews, provides advances in sentiment classification and opinion mining. IMDB-based RNN models [5], rule-based SA for Malayalam [6], and fastText embeddings [7,8] for Tamil have all demonstrated better performance with issues like sarcasm detection, size, and ineffective long-term dependency management. The MABSA dataset [9] for Malayalam ABSA, highlights efforts in resource generation.

Due to lack of supervised datasets in Tamil, researchers are unable to investigate and create reliable NLP models that are suited to the linguistic subtleties of Tamil due to the lack of such datasets. For Tamil-speaking societies, this divide also hinders the development of applications such as social media analytics, virtual assistants, and sentiment-based recommendation systems. Hence the main motive is to curate a dataset to remove the aforementioned hinderance.

Data Description

The dataset is a carefully curated collection of Tamil movie reviews aimed at advancing research in Sentiment Analysis, ABSA, and Text Classification within the domain of NLP. Using a Google form, we have asked the users to leave reviews of 40 films. Further reviews were extracted from online platforms like YouTube and IMDb. We also ensured that the review collection was based on the presence of at least one target aspect, from the list of chosen aspects such as cinematography, acting, script, story, direction, songs, background music, and Editing. Annotators are then given these reviews to label in the following step. The annotation task was completed without adhering to any particular protocol. The dataset contains approximately 1390 reviews, annotated for positive and negative sentiments across eight distinct aspects. It comprises 1120 positive and 1120 negative reviews, ensuring a balanced representation. Below Table 1 gives a description of all the aspects present in the dataset.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Fig. 1 illustrates the curation of the dataset in detail. The reviews were sourced from diverse platforms such as YouTube, user-submitted forms, IMDB, and through oral collection. Google forms and other forms have been circulated among the participants and the reviews given by them were taken anonymously. The forms do not require any personal details of the participants who are familiar in Tamil regional language. Also, we ensured full adherence to the data sharing and redistribution policies of the platforms while collecting the data. Following data collection, preprocessing was applied to form the MADTRAS dataset in order to ensure quality of content, focusing on cleaning the review text. This process involved removing unwanted characters, such as special characters and blank spaces, that do not influence the sentiment of the text. We address Tamil’s agglutinative morphology during preprocessing with a light rule-based procedure rather than a full morphological analyzer. A small set of frequent colloquial variants is canonicalized (For example: ). English tokens in code-mixed text are retained and optionally tagged. The contents were also checked to balance the sentiment count and aspects. Three annotators were involved in annotating the dataset based on the guidelines.

Statistical analysis

Statistical evaluation helps in dataset validation by ensuring data quality, consistency, and representativeness. It identifies anomalies, biases, or missing values through descriptive and inferential analysis. Additionally, it assesses whether the dataset aligns with the intended task, making it reliable for model training and testing. Here, we are using Spearman’s Correlation Coefficient [10] to validate the annotator’s work. Since three different annotators have annotated the dataset, this coefficient will help to understand the degree to which the three annotators agreed on the dataset's labelling. Each aspect's κ was calculated using per-item category vote counts and compared to the predicted agreement based on total label proportions. Experimental evaluation

To prove the efficacy of the dataset via experimental evaluation, the MADTRAS dataset is classified into two portions for training and testing in the 70–30 ratio. Each of the six models is trained using the training data. For LSTM, the batch size given was 300, while Bi-LSTM and GRU, Bi-GRU and Transformer were given 128. For all the models other than mBERT, L2 regularization is applied, and a dropout value of 0.02 is given to solve the problem of overfitting. Furthermore, the learning rate was given a value of 0.001 for getting better learning. Epoch size used 200 for each deep learning model except mBERT. The adoption of the early stopping mechanism is implemented herein to mitigate the issue of overfitting. For mBERT, L2 regularization was applied with a weight decay value of 0.01 and to reduce to overfitting problem, a dropout value of 0.001 was applied. The learning rate given was 5e-4 and the epoch size was 50 for mBERT. Finally, each of the six models was estimated using the test data, and the efficacy of the models for the Tamil ABSA was analysed. The results are tabulated in the Table 4.

Ethics Statement

This study uses publicly available Tamil-language movie reviews that were gathered from online platforms such as YouTube, Facebook, etc. The Google Form did not collect any personally identifiable information, and participation was entirely voluntary. As no personally identifiable information was collected during the data gathering process, formal ethics approval was not required, in accordance with the policies of the institution. During the creation of this dataset, we have taken care to fully comply with the data sharing and redistribution policies of the respective platforms. Any content restricted by terms of service or privacy regulations has either been excluded or carefully anonymized to ensure adherence to ethical and legal standards.

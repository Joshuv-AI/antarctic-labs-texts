Multiparametric MRI based deep learning model for prediction of early recurrence of hepatocellular carcinoma after SR following TACE
Authors: Hongyu Wang, Jinwei Li, Yushu Ouyang, He Ren, Chao An, Wendao Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSurgical resection (SR) following transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) is a promising treatment for unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (uHCC). However, biomarkers for the prediction of postoperative recurrence are needed.PurposeTo develop and validate a model combining deep learning (DL) and clinical data for early recurrence (ER) in uHCC patients after TACE.MethodsA total of 511 patients who received SR following TACE were assigned to derivation (n = 413) and validation (n = 98) cohorts. Deep learning features were taken from the largest tumor area in liver MRI. A nomogram using DL signatures and clinical data was made to forecast early recurrence risk in uHCC patients. Model performance was evaluated using area under the curve (AUC).ResultsA total of 2278 subsequences and 31,346 slices multiparametric MRI including contrast-enhanced T1WI, T2WI and DWI were input in the DL model simultaneously. Multivariable analysis identified three independent predictors for the development of the nomogram: tumor number (hazard ratio [HR]:3.42, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 2.75–4.31, P = 0.003), microvascular invasion (HR: 9.21, 6.24–32.14; P < 0.001), and DL scores (HR: 17.46, 95% CI: 12.94–23.57, P < 0.001). The AUC of the nomogram was 0.872 and 0.862 in two cohorts, significantly outperforming single-subsequence-based DL mode and clinical model (all, P < 0.001). The nomogram provided two risk strata for cumulative overall survival in two cohorts, showing significant statistical results (P < 0.001).ConclusionsThe DL-based nomogram is essential to identify patients with uHCC suitable for treatment with SR following TACE and may potentially benefit personalized decision-making.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00432-024-05941-w.Keywords: Hepatocellular carcinoma, Surgical resection, Transarterial chemoembolization, Deep learning, Early recurrence

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the sixth most commonly diagnosed malignancy and the third leading cause of cancer death worldwide (Rl et al. 2020; Villanueva 2019). Unfortunately, the vast majority of HCC patients are diagnosed initially at the intermediate-advanced stage, leaving them with no chance of curative surgical resection (SR) (Ja et al. 2018; Guidelines 2018).Transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) is recommended as a potentially curative treatment for intermediate-stage HCC (Reig et al. 2022; Song et al. 2010; Benson and Mi 2021). Especially, for those with huge HCC, TACE can also be considered an alternative to surgery due to it occludes the main artery supplying the tumor, causing subsequent R0 surgery to become more effective (Reig et al. 2022; Song et al. 2010; Benson and Mi 2021; Luo et al. 2022; Fan et al. 1998).

SR following TACE was regarded as a safe and effective conversion therapy for unresectable HCC (uHCC) (Zhou and Song 2021). However, frequent HCC recurrence limits the long-term survival benefit for these patients. The uHCC patients at high risk for recurrence are potential candidates for therapeutic strategies, including preventive and adjuvant therapy (e.g., TACE, radioembolization, molecular-targeted agents [MTAs] and immune checkpoint inhibitors [ICIs]) and may facilitate an individualized surveillance policy (Shimose et al. 2021; Zhang et al. 2022; Lencioni et al. 2016). Therefore, identifying uHCC patients preoperatively can guide personalized decisions before SR after TACE, potentially enhancing long-term survival.

An increasing number of studies suggest that histopathologic characteristics of HCC (e.g., the macrotrabecular-massive subtype and positive cytokeratin 19 expression) are associated with postoperative recurrence (Yoon and Kim 2021; Li et al. 2022; Li et al. 2023). However, histopathologic examination is not a non-invasive approach, and biopsies before SR are not mandatory in clinical practice because HCC can be diagnosed based on typical imaging and AFP levels. Previously, a series of invisible imaging features has been proven to be closely related to the histopathologic subtype of HCC. For example, the rim arterial phase hyperenhancement, non-smooth tumor margins, and substantial necrosis are important features to consider (Cha et al. 2023; Mulé et al. 2020). Many prediction models based on clinical data have been established, with the purpose of improving clinical practice (Scheiner et al. 2022; Zheng et al. 2021; Ohkawa et al. 2017). However, these models remain limited due to the relatively few variables, small derivation sample sizes, varying pathologic scoring standards, and poor clinical applicability.

Recently, deep learning (DL), a promising emerging approach for imaging analysis, has been widely investigated in tumor characteristics and has shown great value in various prediction tasks (Li et al. 2023; Saillard et al. 2020; Cheng et al. 2022). By using the DL method, abundant information from radiographic images can be extracted in a data-driven manner, providing enhanced predictive power in the field of oncology. Here, we aim to develop an artificial intelligence (AI) tool for the early recurrence (ER) prediction in uHCC patients who received SR following TACE, utilizing the DL method based on multiparametric magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). This tool will help clinicians guide the selection of SR indications and formulate therapeutic strategies.

Materials and methods

Study design and patients enrollment

This retrospective study was conducted following the principles of the 1975 Helsinki Declaration, and the protocol obtained approval from the Institutional Review Board of The Second Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou University of Chinese Medicine. Due to retrospective nature, the requirement for written informed consent was waived.

From January 2014 to November 2022, a total of consecutive 2,829 patients with uHCC in the early to intermediate stage who received initial TACE were reviewed at two hospitals. These HCC patients were diagnosed based on guidelines of American Association for the Study of Liver Disease (AASLD) (Jk et al. 2018) or pathologic confirmation by preoperative biopsy. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (a) age 18–75 years; (b) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status < 2; (c) Child–Pugh class A or B liver function; (d) the patients received SR following TACE. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (a) patients underwent any treatment before TACE; (b) HCC combined with other malignancies; (c) the clinical and imaging data are missing before TACE; (d) lost follow-up last more than 6 months. The procedures for TACE and SR, and indication of SR following TACE aer described in supplementary information E1.1–1.3.

Histopathologic examination

The histopathologic data on satellite nodules and microvascular invasion (MVI) were retrieved from routine pathological reports. According to the institutional standard operating procedures, all surgical specimens were examined by 2 pathologists (with 5 to 10 years of experience in liver pathology) at each participating center in consensus. For patients with multiple tumors, histopathologic data on the largest tumor was recorded.

Follow-up and endpoints

All patients were regularly followed up after SR at one month and every three to six months thereafter with serum AFP and imaging techniques (contrast-enhanced ultrasound, CT or MRI) until death or the last follow-up date (October 31, 2023). Overall survival (OS), defined as the time from the initiation of downstaging therapy to death of any cause, was the secondary endpoint. Sixteen clinical variables collection were all shown in supplementary information E1.4.

Model construction

MR image review

All scans were performed on clinical 1.5 T or 3.0 T MR scanners supplied by GE or Siemens using a phase-array torso coil. Imaging sequences included axial T2-weighted imaging (T2WI), diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), in-phase and opposed-phase T1-weighted imaging (T1WI), and contrast enhanced T1-weighted sequences at arterial phase (20–30 s), portal venous phase (60–70 s) and delayed phase (180 s) after a bolus injection of nonspecific gadolinium-based contrast media (0.1 mmol/kg intravenous macrocyclic gadolinium-based contrast agent, Dotarem; Guerbet, Roissy, France). This study collected multi-parameter MRI data, including delayed-phase contrast-enhanced T1WI (T1WI + C), T2WI, and DWI, for model construction. The acquisition of MRI parameters at two hospitals was shown in the Table S1.

Segmentation of liver region

Pre-processed MRI underwent automatic segmentation using nnU-Net, and the detail was shown in supplementary information E1.5. The segmentation masks were then reviewed manually by two radiologists with 10 years of imaging experience. Each of MR images from patients was processed by nnU-Net to provide liver delineation. For each patient, one informative slice (slice containing the maximum tumour area) was chosen from each of T1WI + C, T2WI and DWI images. The images were cropped to 224 mm * 224 mm using a bounding box covering the whole liver area from the chosen slice.

Feature extraction

The ResNet18 model, pre-trained on ImageNet and fine-tuned on images, was used to extract three DL features, including DL-T1WI + C, DL-T2WI, and DL-DWI, from image patches defined on each liver region.

DL model development

Consider that the original ResNet18 is a classification network which has 1000 classes of output. We took place the last fully connected (FC) layer with a one-output FC layer to perform the ER regression. Then, the last feature maps in ResNet18 were summed and averaged to generate a value, which was used as the DL features. Image patches were from T1WI + C, T2WI, and DWI images. Note that the input of the original pre-trained ResNet18 had three channels, we made the inputted single-channel gray image into three channels by copying the channel three times. Therefore, for each subject, we can obtain 3 DL features which can be named as DL-T1WI + C, DL-T2WI, and DL-DWI. The network structure of ResNet18 in Fig. 2, which was constructed of 2D convolutional layer, rectified linear unit layer (ReLU), batch normalization layer (BN), max-pooling layer, global average pooling layer and fully connected layer (FC). The detail was shown in supplementary information E1.6. Notably, the image patch not only contained the tumor region, but also included its surrounding area such as the tumor margin and liver parenchyma.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.2.0, Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Inter-rater agreement between the two reviewers was assessed with the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) for MVI. Survival curves were calculated using the Kaplan‒Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were applied to calculate the hazard ratios (HRs) and corresponding 95% confidence interval (CI) of variables and identify independent risk factors of ER. On this basis, DL signatures were then combined with independent clinical variables to build the nomogram, using the multivariate logistic regression. Patient assignment into the derivation cohort (DC) and validation cohort (VC) were done via computer-generated randomization based on 4:1 ratio. The regression coefficients were implemented for each individual observation to define the linear predictors in the nomogram. The calibration plot for probability of ER after TACE plus SR via agreement evaluation between the prediction by nomogram and actual observation. The clinical utility of the model was evaluated with decision curve analysis (DCA). The main predictive parameter was the areas under the receiver operating characteristic (AUC). The DeLong test was employed to evaluate and compare the model’s performance. All tests of significance were two-sided and a P value < 0.05 was interpreted to carry statistical significance.

According to 4:1 ratio, the DC consisted of 413 patients who underwent SR, and the VC consisted of 98 patients. Table 1 shows a comparison of baseline characteristics between the DC and VC. Independent prognostic factors

We collected 16 clinical variables and microvascular invasion as a histopathologic variable to analyze the independent prognostic factors of ER after TACE plus SR. The risk factors of ER were assessed by univariate and multivariate analysis (Table S2). In the univariate analyses, tumor size (HR: 0.860; 95% CI:0.462,0.934; P = 0.039), tumor number (HR: 1.795; 95% CI: 1.147, 3.962; P = 0.002), AFP (HR:1.871; 95% CI: 1.229,2.848; P = 0.003), and MVI (HR:0.435; 95% CI: 0.284, 0.666; P < 0.001) were significant factors for ER. Multivariate analyses showed the factors that significantly affected the ER were tumor number (HR:1.690; 95% CI: 1.112, 2.564; P = 0.004), AFP (HR: 1.527; 95% CI: 1.041, 2.248; P = 0.030), and MVI (HR:0.461; 95% CI: 0.310, 0.628; P < 0.001).

After DL score were input, as shown in Table 2, the multivariate regression analysis indicated that these variables including tumor number (HR: 3.42; 95% CI: 2.75, 4.31; P = 0.003), MVI (HR: 9.21; 95% CI: 6.24, 32.14; P < 0.001), and DL score (HR:17.46; 95% CI: 12.94, 23.57; P < 0.001) were independent predictors for ER after TACE plus SR. Furthermore, a nomogram was built based on these variables (Fig. 3A), described by the formula: Y = − 8.992 + 3.298 × tumor number (0: Single; 1: Multiple) + 4.282 × MVI (0: Presence; 1: Absence) + 8.291 × DL score, where Y indicated the ER probability to initial TACE plus SR treatment in uHCC. As shown in Fig. 3B, C, the calibration curves of post-ablation recurrence prediction analysis were performed on DC and VC. Agreement between the two pathologists for the MVI in nomogram was excellent (k = 0.918 for MVI). In all cohorts, both models showed good agreement between the predictive ER and the real ER. Further, DCA graphically indicated that DL + Clinical model can provide larger benefit across the range of reasonable threshold probabilities than DL and clinical model in both cohorts (Fig. 3D-E).

Survival risk stratification

To facilitate the clinical practice of the nomogam model, we divided the HCC patients into two risk groups according to the risk scores of the nomogram models: high-risk group, and low-risk group. We identified the cutoff values (66.23) in the DC and verified them in the VC using X-title. This pragmatic visualization of the risk level could help in deciding the strategy of TACE-based comprehensive treatment schemes for uHCC patients. According to the risk scores from the nomogram model, the cumulative 1-, 3- and 5-year OS of patients in the low-risk group were 87.4%, 53.6%, and 53.6%, respectively, showing significant statistical differences compared with those (53.1%, 11.0%, and 11.0%, respectively) in the high-risk group in the DC (P < 0.001) (Fig. 4A). Similarly, the cumulative 1-, 3- and 5-year OS of patients in the low-risk group were 47.8%, 42.0%, and 42.0%, respectively, showing significant statistical differences compared with those (63.1%, 10.4%, and 10.4%, respectively) in the high-risk group in the VC (P < 0.001) (Fig. 4B).

Interpretation for DL Model

To better explore the hidden patterns the network learned, heatmaps were divided into high- and low-risk ER groups. Overall, the intensity of feature map in high-risk group was higher than that in the low-risk group, so the DL features in the high-risk group generated a larger value of DL signature, resulting in a higher recurrence risk. Additionally, higher-intensity regions are mainly distributed in the tumor area in the low-risk group, while in the high-risk group, high-intensity regions are not only distributed in the tumor area, but also in the area surrounding the tumor. Discussion

Patients with uHCC exhibit various demographic and imaging features, which can lead to different prognoses. Even when these individuals receive SR following TACE conversion therapy, the postoperative recurrence rate remains high (Pr et al. 2017; Xuewu et al. 2006; Ps et al. 2019). Therefore, obtaining accurate and individualized ER risk estimates to assist in decision-making regarding further management plays an important role in improving long-term survival.

An increasing number of studies have reported that imaging features are associated with recurrence after SR. For example, Wang et al. reported a nomogram that integrated tumor number, MVI, and radiomics score for predicting recurrence after SR, yielding outstanding performance with an AUC of 0.88 in the development cohort (Gw and Zhu Fp 2020). Moreover, Jiang et al. also reported that an intratumoral artery therapy was associated with advanced recurrence after SR, according to univariate Fine-Gray analysis (Jiang et al. 2023). To date, few DSA features have been used to analyze and predict recurrence after SR. Therefore, prospective clinical trials should be conducted to further test the reliability and reproducibility of these imaging features.

In this study, DL models utilizing multiparametric MRI were proposed to predict ER of uHCC patients who received SR following TACE. To our best knowledge, this study should be the first to utilize such abundant information of the MRI data to construct ER prediction models for risk screening for uHCC patients receiving SR following TACE treatment. The finding of this study mainly included the following two aspects: (i) multiparametric MRI features originated from tumors and liver parenchyma were input to construct various of DL models, which showed continuous performance improvement; (ii) the integrative nomogram comprising the DL signature and clinical variables yields the optimal predictive performance among all models, helping the ER risk differentiation and OS benefit assessment in uHCC patients who received SR following TACE.

This study utilized multiparametric MRI data, including T1WI + C, DWI, and T2WI images mainly reflect the blood perfusion which brings contrast agent into the tissue, and the inflammatory cells activation which trap the contrast agent. Blood perfusion inside the tumor is believed to be an important characteristic of ER. Inflammation play an important role in the tumorigenesis, progression, and metastasis of HCC (Cao et al. 2022; Binnewies et al. 2018). DWI mainly images proton diffusion, which is obstructed by the micro structure of tissue, and thus can reflect carcinoma cell density and liver fibrosis, which indicate the activity level of carcinoma cells in situ and the background fibrosis. T2WI reflects the water content in tumor tissues and is also closely related to fibrosis and inflammation.

This study also presented some interesting visualization results for the DL features. On the feature maps of MR images for high-risk cases stratified by the prediction model, the energy is concentrated not only in the tumor and margin regions but also in the surrounding liver region. On the other hand, for low-risk patients, the energy is mainly concentrated in the tumor and margin regions on the feature maps of MR images. These results suggest that the liver parenchyma of high-risk patients contains hidden features related to ER. These compelling findings align with previously described pathological mechanisms of tumor recurrence, which are located in the tumor margin and liver parenchyma. These mechanisms can be implicitly learned by deep learning networks, as evidenced by the high weights observed in the corresponding feature maps. Therefore, through the DL and visualization tool, biologists and radiologists can gain a deeper understanding for ER mechanism of HCC.

This DL-based nomogram can successfully perform ER risk stratification of SR following TACE, facilitating its application in clinical practice. Using this nomogram, we recommend early TACE for HCC patients at high-risk recurrence and suggest preemptive enlistment for TKIs and PD-1 therapy before recurrence is detected. In addition, this nomogram tool may facilitate the development of individualized surveillance policy. Specifically, low-risk patients may not receive preventive TACE and may follow a less intensive surveillance regimen, even within the first 2 years after SR. High-risk patients should undergo intensive surveillance for as long as possible due to the significant risk of recurrence, even after 2 years, despite receiving adjuvant systemic therapies.

There are certain limitations in this study. Firstly, the parameters of the MRI scanner were different among various MRI equipment at the two hospitals, but we have standardized these MRI data. Secondly, some potential molecular biomarkers and genomic information are not available in this study. The accuracy and robustness of the DL model may be improved if these factors are added. Third, despite this study having the largest sample size for predicting ER after SR in uHCC patients, future research should include more patients from various centers to validate the prediction models.

In conclusion, a DL model based on multiparametric MRI was developed to predict ER of uHCC patients who received SR following TACE, which showed outstanding performance in two cohorts and demonstrated their robustness and effectiveness. Our proposed DL-based nomogram may assist physicians in therapeutic decision-making and in selecting surveillance strategy for uHCC patients receiving TACE conversion therapy in clinical practice.

Role of Corticosteroids in Severe Scrub Typhus Infection
Authors: John V Peter, Prithviraj Bose, Binila Chacko
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionCorticosteroids are increasingly used in the management of severe shock, pneumonia and acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS). Its role in severe scrub typhus infection is not known.ObjectivesTo assess the effect of corticosteroid therapy on mortality, ventilator free days (VFD) and vasopressor free days in severe scrub typhus infection, after adjusting for illness severity by multivariate logistic regression analysis.Materials and methodsThis retrospective cohort study spanning 5-years (2020 to 2024), included adult patients admitted to the intensive care unit with scrub typhus infection with organ dysfunction. Illness severity was assessed using SOFA and APACHE II scores. Corticosteroids were administered as per the treating physician's discretion. The primary outcome was hospital mortality. Logistic regression analysis was undertaken to explore for factors associated with mortality and expressed as odds ratio (OR) with 95% confidence interval (CI). The effect of corticosteroids on duration of ventilation and vasopressor therapy was explored through logistic regression analysis.ResultsThe mean (SD) age of the cohort (n = 114, 38 male) was 45.7 (15.1) years; 67 (58.8%) received steroids. SOFA score was significantly higher in those receiving steroids (11.2 (4.6) vs. 7.4 (3.7), p < 0.001). APACHE scores were similar (p = 0.11). Overall, respiratory support and vasopressors were required in 91 (80.7%) and 83 (72.8%) respectively; 58.7% of patients on respiratory support and 74.7% on vasopressor support were administered steroids. Hospital mortality was 14% and significantly higher among steroid-treated patients (22.4% vs. 2.1%, p < 0.01). On multivariate logistic regression analysis, after adjusting for age, gender, and SOFA score, only SOFA score was significantly associated with mortality (OR 1.31, 95%CI 1.10 to 1.57, p = 0.003). Steroids were not associated with mortality (OR 6.2, 0.62 to 62). There did not appear to be an effect of steroids on duration of ventilation (p = 0.89) or duration of vasopressor therapy (p = 0.24).ConclusionThis retrospective study does not show any effect of steroids on outcomes in severe scrub typhus infection. The role of steroids in scrub typhus infection needs to be explored in prospective randomized trials.Keywords: Scrub typhus, corticosteroids

SVSC-15 Exploring the Role of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Enhancing Survivorship Support for Adolescent and Young Adult (AYA) Brain Tumor Survivors in Sub-Saharan Africa: A Survivor-Led Perspective
Authors: Boluwatife Aofolaju, Oluwakemi Atolagbe, Korede Akindele
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAdolescents and young adults (AYAs) with brain tumors in Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA) face unique challenges. Access to care is hindered by multiple factors that create or contribute to delays; psychosocial support is limited, and long-term follow-up programs are scarce. With the rising awareness and utilization of artificial intelligence (AI) in healthcare, this project explored the possible applications of AI-driven tools to enhance the experiences of AYA cancer survivors in SSA. Led by a young adult brain tumor survivor and cancer advocate, this work aimed to examine how AI can be leveraged to address psychosocial, informational, and care coordination gaps for AYA survivors in low-resource settings. Using a survivor-led, qualitative approach, we conducted informal interviews with AYA cancer survivors in Nigeria. Emerging themes were reviewed to identify feasible opportunities for AI use in the African AYA cancer space. Three main themes emerged: (1) Critical need for AYA mental health and peer-support resources, (2) Need for culturally sensitive, youth-friendly digital tools that are co-designed with AYA survivors. (3) AI is a potential bridge to care, especially in resource-limited SSA. AI-enabled symptom tracking and chatbots were seen as promising tools to improve follow-up and emotional support. Survivorship support for AYA brain tumor patients in SSA is underexplored and inadequate. Survivor-led insights suggest that AI has the potential to improve outcomes, engagement, and quality of life. The integration of AI into survivorship protocols could improve long-term care in SSA. This study advocates for interdisciplinary collaborations that center survivor leadership in the design of digital health solutions.

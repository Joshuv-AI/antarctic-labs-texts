Perineural invasion as a prognostic factor and the efficacy of upfront surgery for low-risk cT2/T3N0M0 esophageal squamous cell carcinoma patients
Authors: Jicheng Xiong, Simiao Lu, Shuoming Liang, Hainan Chen, Ziwei Wang, Hao Meng, Yan Miao, Qin Xie, Guangyuan Liu, Kangning Wang, Lin Peng, Qiang Fang, Wenguang Xiao, Qifeng Wang, Yongtao Han, Xuefeng Leng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundControversy remains as to the need for neoadjuvant therapy in the treatment of cT2/T3N0M0 esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), with regional (e.g., China vs. Japan) guidelines making divergent recommendations. Survival data suggest comparable overall survival (OS) rates among cII-stage patients. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of upfront surgery for cT2/T3N0M0-stage ESCC patients and to identify prognostic factors in this subgroup.MethodsWe retrospectively collected the data of 786 cT2/T3N0M0-stage ESCC patients treated between January 2010 and December 2017 to assess the survival outcomes and identify risk factors. Additionally, we performed a survival analysis to explore the prognostic factors in this patient subgroup.ResultsThe cT2N0-stage and cT3N0-stage ESCC patients had 5-year OS rates of 68.0% and 59.0%, respectively (median OS: 82.7 vs. 78.8 months, P=0.01). Postoperative adjuvant therapy significantly improved the OS of the cT3N0-stage patients compared to the cT3N0-stage patients those did not receive postoperative adjuvant therapy (5-year OS: 65% vs. 53%, P=0.004). Adjuvant therapy yielded comparable survival rates in the cT3N0-stage patients without perineural invasion (PNI) and the cT2N0-stage patients who underwent upfront surgery (5-year OS: 66% vs. 68%, P=0.42). Similarly, no significant difference was observed between the lymphovascular invasion (LVI)-negative cT3N0-stage patients those received adjuvant therapy after surgery and the cT2N0-stage patients those underwent upfront surgery (5-year OS: 66% vs. 68%, P=0.30). The survival analysis revealed no difference in OS between the highly differentiated cT3N0-stage and cT2N0-stage patients (5-year OS: 74% vs. 68%, P=0.79). Conversely, the lowly differentiated cT3N0-stage patients had a poorer prognosis with upfront surgery than the high and moderate differentiation patients and derived no survival benefit from adjuvant therapy (5-year OS: 55% vs. 68%, P=0.02). The multivariate analysis identified PNI as an independent prognostic factor for OS [hazard ratio (HR): 1.382; 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.037–1.841; P=0.03].ConclusionsUpfront surgery results in favorable long-term survival outcomes in cT2/T3N0M0-stage ESCC patients without PNI. These findings show the importance of tailoring therapeutic strategies to distinct ESCC subgroups, particularly based on PNI status and tumor differentiation.

Highlight box

Key findings

• Upfront surgery is an alternative treatment approach for cT2/T3N0M0-stage esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) patients with low-risk factors, such as perineural invasion (PNI)-negative status or lymphovascular invasion (LVI)-negative status, and high differentiation.

• PNI, LVI, and low differentiation are prognostic factors affecting overall survival (OS) in cT2/T3N0M0-stage ESCC patients, and PNI is an independent prognostic factor.

• In cT2/T3N0M0-stage ESCC patients with low-risk factors, postoperative adjuvant therapy may provide a favorable long-term survival benefit.

What is known, and what is new?

• For clinical cT3N0M0-stage ESCC patients, the guidelines recommend preoperative neoadjuvant therapy

• We conducted a retrospective cohort study to explore the efficacy of upfront surgery in the treatment of cT3N0M0-stage patients, and found that upfront surgery is feasible for cT3N0M0-stage patients with low-risk factors.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Preoperative neoadjuvant therapy should be individualized based on specific tumor characteristics, and various therapeutic strategies should be adopted across different ESCC subgroups of patients.

Introduction

Esophageal cancer (EC) is an aggressive malignancy of the gastrointestinal tract with a poor prognosis (1). According to its histological characteristics, EC is mainly classified into two main subtypes: esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) and esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC) (2). The geographic distribution of these subtypes exhibits distinct patterns, such that EAC is more prevalent in Western countries [e.g., Western Europe and the United States (US)], while ESCC is more prevalent in regions such as Middle Africa and East Asia (3).

Clinical outcomes vary significantly among different populations, and are also influenced by clinicopathological characteristics and disease stage at diagnosis (4). Recent survival data revealed substantial regional disparities in different areas; for example, in China, the 5-year overall survival (OS) rate is 52.9% (5), while in Japan, it is as high as 59.9% (6), and in the US and Europe, it is relatively lower at only 22% (7) and 20%, respectively (8). Notably, the survival data from China and Japan primarily include patients who underwent esophagectomy or surgery-based therapies, while the therapy modalities of the patients are not disclosed in the US and European data.

The JCOG9901 trial changed the therapy approach from postoperative adjuvant therapy to preoperative neoadjuvant therapy (9). With the publication of the CROSS study and the Chinese NEOCRTEC5010 trial (10,11), neoadjuvant radiotherapy followed by surgery has become a standard therapeutic regimen for locally advanced EC, and has been adopted into the guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of EC in different regions. For example, in relation to EC, the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) Clinical Practice Guidelines in Oncology (2024, version 1, https://www.nccn.org/guidelines/guideline), edited by the Japan Esophageal Society (JES) (12), and the Guidelines of Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology (CSCO) (13) (hereinafter referred to as the Guidelines) recommend endoscopic submucosal dissection or endoscopic mucosal resection for early stage ESCC patients, but recommend neoadjuvant therapy followed by surgery for locally advanced ESCC patients (particularly those with lymph node metastasis). However, for cT2/T3N0M0-stage ESCC patients, significant controversy persists among the guidelines as to the necessity of adjuvant therapy before surgery. Under the CSCO Guidelines, upfront surgery is recommended for cT2N0M0-stage ESCC patients, while neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy or neoadjuvant chemotherapy before surgery is recommended for cT3N0M0-stage ESCC patients (13). Conversely, the NCCN guidelines in the US recommend upfront surgery for low-risk cT2N0M0-stage ESCC patients (i.e., those with a tumor length <3 cm and/or high differentiation); chemoradiotherapy plus surgery or chemotherapy plus surgery or neoadjuvant therapy plus surgery for high-risk cT2N0M0-stage patients [i.e., those with lymphovascular invasion (LVI)-positive status, a tumor length >3 cm, or low differentiation]; and neoadjuvant therapy followed by surgery for cT3N0-stage ESCC patients (14). Notably, the latest China Anti-Cancer Association guidelines for ESCC recommend that all eligible patients should receive preoperative chemoradiotherapy followed by surgery to improve OS rates (15). Similarly, the JES guidelines recommend neoadjuvant therapy followed by surgery for cT2/T3N0M0-stage ESCC patients (12).

Notably, the 5-year OS of cII-stage EC in China is approximately 60.0%, while in Japan it is 64.3% (5,6). Interestingly, despite the differences in the therapy modalities, these 5-year OS rates are comparable. Thus, we aimed to explore the optimal therapy modality for cT2/T3N0M0-stage ESCC patients by comparing their survival outcomes. We hypothesized that cT2/T3N0M0-stage ESCC patients should undergo upfront surgery, and by analyzing the OS rate of this subgroup, we sought to gather evidence in support of our hypothesis.

A retrospective analysis was conducted of the data from the Esophageal Cancer Case Management Database of consecutive ESCC patients who underwent esophagectomy from January 2010 to December 2017. The primary analysis compared the OS outcomes of the cT2N0M0-stage and cT3N0M0-stage ESCC patients who received upfront surgery. The study sought to evaluate the feasibility of upfront surgery in the treatment of cT2/T3N0M0-stage ESCC patients. Secondary analyses identified the clinicopathological prognostic factors of this subgroup, and provided diagnostic and therapeutic approaches for patients with high-risk factors. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2024-1969/rc).

Methods

Data collection

We collected the clinical and follow-up data of patients who underwent esophagectomy from January 2010 to December 2017 at the Sichuan Cancer Hospital from the Esophageal Cancer Case Management Database. We collected data on patients’ general characteristics, pathological features, perioperative treatments, and survival outcomes. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee for Medical Research and New Medical Technology of Sichuan Cancer Hospital (No. SCCHEC-02-2022-050). The requirement of informed consent was waived by the ethics committee due to the retrospective nature of the study.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Patients were included in the study if they met the following inclusion criteria: (I) had ESCC in the thoracic region and underwent esophagectomy at Sichuan Cancer Hospital from January 2010 to December 2017; (II) had clinical T2N0-stage and T3N0-stage, and achieved R0 resection; and (III) had complete follow-up records. Patients were excluded from the study if they met any of the following exclusion criteria: (I) had received neoadjuvant therapy before surgery; (II) had postoperative pathological results showing the presence of lymph node metastasis; (III) had an upgraded postoperative pathological stage; and/or (IV) had cT2N0-stage and received adjuvant therapy after surgery.

Preoperative evaluation and pathologic diagnosis

The preoperative evaluation included a nutritional assessment, imaging examination, and comorbidity assessment. The imaging tests included gastroscopy, ultrasound gastroscopy, cardiac ultrasound, pulmonary function tests, and enhanced cervical, thoracic, and abdominal computed tomography (CT), and clinical staging was determined by ultrasound gastroscopy and enhanced transthoracic abdominal CT. Some patients with locally advanced disease underwent neck ultrasound to rule out cervical lymph node metastasis or additional positron emission tomography/CT to exclude systemic bone metastasis. The patients undergoing surgery and comprehensive treatment had to have a good performance status score. All the pathologic specimens underwent standard hematoxylin and eosin staining. The sections were analyzed under a microscope and reviewed by two senior pathologists. PNI was characterized by the infiltration of cancer cells into any layer of the epiglottis (including the epineurium, perineurium, and endoneurium). Postoperative pathological staging was performed according to the 8th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer system for EC.

Perioperative treatment and follow-up

All the patients underwent esophagectomy, with some receiving minimally invasive esophagectomy. The surgical approaches included McKeown esophagectomy and Ivor-Lewis esophagectomy. Some patients received postoperative adjuvant therapy, including adjuvant chemotherapy and adjuvant chemoradiotherapy. After discharge, regular follow-up was conducted through scheduled outpatient visits or telephone calls. During the first two years, the outpatient visits were scheduled every three months; from the second to the fifth year, outpatient visits were scheduled every six months; and after five years, annual follow-up was conducted via telephone. OS was defined as the time from the date of surgery to the date of the last follow-up or death (whichever occurred first).

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS 27.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). The normally distributed continuous metric data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation, while the skewed metric data are presented as the median (M). The count data or categorical variable data are expressed as the absolute number or percentage. The continuous variables were divided into subgroups based on the median, and the continuous variables were dichotomized clinical cut-off points. A survival analysis was performed using Kaplan-Meier curves, and significance was assessed using the log-rank test. The Cox proportional hazards model was used for the univariate and multivariate survival analyses. A two-sided P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

In this study, 786 ESCC patients were included, and the follow-up duration ranged from 1.4 to 124.4 months. The cohort was predominantly male (79.6%, n=626). The tumors were predominantly located in the middle thoracic segment (56.7%). About 43.6% of the patients showed moderate differentiation. The median tumor length was 3.0 cm. The postoperative pathological results indicated that 53 (6.7%) of the patients had LVI, and 16.8% (n=132) had perineural invasion (PNI). Among the cT3N0M0-stage ESCC patients, 300 received postoperative adjuvant therapy, while 317 patients did not receive postoperative adjuvant therapy. Discussion

Current clinical guidelines exhibit regional variations for cT2N0M0-stage and cT3N0M0-stage ESCC patients, and recommendations differ as to whether cII-stage ESCC patients should undergo upfront surgery or neoadjuvant therapy followed by surgery. We conducted a retrospective survival analysis using the survival data of cT2/T3N0M0-stage ESCC patients who underwent upfront surgery, and found that these patients should be treated with upfront surgery. Low-risk ESCC patients who undergo postoperative adjuvant therapy based on pathological results for can also achieve favorable 5-year OS.

Our study found that there were significant differences in OS between the cT2N0-stage and cT3N0M0-stage ESCC subgroups. However, the cT3N0M0-stage ESCC patients who received postoperative adjuvant therapy had 5-year OS rates comparable to those of the cT2N0M0-stage ESCC patients. A further subgroup analysis indicated that PNI and LVI were important prognostic factors for cT3N0M0-stage ESCC patients. The 5-year OS rates of the cT3N0M0-stage ESCC patients with PNI-negative status approached those of the cT2N0M0-stage ESCC patients; thus, postoperative adjuvant therapy provided survival benefits. Conversely, the cT3N0M0-stage ESCC patients with a PNI-positive status, including those who received postoperative adjuvant therapy, had significantly lower OS than the cT2N0M0-stage ESCC patients, despite a modest survival trend being observed. Similarly, the cT3N0M0-stage ESCC patients with LVI-positive status who received postoperative adjuvant therapy had a poor prognosis.

Under the latest NCCN guidelines in the US, adjuvant therapy is recommended for EAC patients with LVI or PNI, but there are no specific recommendations for ESCC patients (2024, version 1, https://www.nccn.org/guidelines/guideline). However, our findings suggest that for ESCC patients with LVI- or PNI-positive status, postoperative adjuvant therapy does not provide survival benefits. Some studies support PNI as a risk factor for the prognosis of EC patients, and postoperative adjuvant therapy has been shown to improve OS (16-19). However, other studies suggest that PNI is merely a coincidental phenomenon in tumor progression (20).

The JES guidelines (2022 edition) recommend neoadjuvant therapy followed by surgery for cII/III-stage ESCC patients (12). Under the latest JES guidelines (21), cT2N0M0-stage and cT3N0M0-stage are classified as cII-stage, which means that cT2N0M0-stage ESCC patients should undergo neoadjuvant therapy followed by surgery. As mentioned above, different guidelines provide different recommendations for cII-stage ESCC patients. We found that even without preoperative neoadjuvant therapy, the 5-year OS rate of the cT2N0M0-stage ESCC patients treated with upfront surgery was 68%, which was comparable to the 5-year OS rates reported in China and Japan (5,6). The cT3N0M0-stage ESCC patients achieved similar OS rates with postoperative adjuvant therapy. This suggests that cII-stage ESCC patients treated with upfront surgery can achieve similar survival outcomes, including comparable survival benefits, to those patients who undergo neoadjuvant therapy followed by surgery.

In Japan, postoperative chemotherapy is the standard treatment for patients with histopathologically confirmed lymph node metastasis after esophagectomy, while surveillance is recommended for ESCC patients without lymph node metastasis. The NCCN guidelines recommend observation or postoperative chemotherapy for pT2/T3N0M0-stage ESCC patients, while the CSCO Guidelines in China recommend observation for pT1-3N0M0-stage patients. Our data showed that half of the lymph node-negative patients received additional adjuvant therapy, and the median OS was significantly improved in the subgroup that received adjuvant therapy. Thus, postoperative adjuvant therapy may improve the 5-year OS of low-risk ESCC patients. However, for ESCC patients with high-risk factors (including PNI- and LVI-positive status, and low differentiation), postoperative adjuvant therapy did not yield significant survival benefits.

The guidelines in China, the US, and Japan all recognize LVI as a high-risk factor for ESCC patients. However, currently, there is no consensus as to whether PNI is a risk factor for EC patients. Lymph node metastasis is widely accepted as the most valuable prognostic factor, and LVI has been incorporated into the guidelines in Japan and the US, highlighting its importance (12,14,22). Due to the presence of the two factors, the prognostic significance of PNI in EC patients has not been widely accepted. Previous studies have reported that PNI is a prognostic factor for lymph node-negative ESCC patients, and is associated with a poor prognosis, but this remains disputed (19,20,23). Additionally, low-risk ESCC patients can achieve similar 5-year OS with adjuvant therapy. This aligns with the finding of a previous study that suggested that the original pathological stage of LVI-positive EC patients may need to be upgraded (e.g., pT2 may need to be upgraded to T3) (24). To examine the effect of PNI and LVI status on adjuvant therapy, we performed a subgroup analysis, and the results showed that patients with the PNI- or LVI-positive status did not derive a survival benefit from adjuvant therapy. This suggests that both PNI and LVI affect patient prognosis. New adjuvant treatment protocols may need to be developed, and the need for adjuvant therapy needs to be further evaluated in clinical practice.

The pathological TNM (pTNM) Classification and differentiation grade are the most recognized prognostic predictors for ESCC patients (25,26). Consistent with previous findings (27), our multivariate survival analysis revealed that differentiation and PNI are independent prognostic factors affecting this subgroup of patients. Notably, the cT3N0M0-stage ESCC patients with poor differentiation had an unfavorable prognosis, and their 5-year OS rate was low despite their undergoing postoperative adjuvant therapy. In clinical practice, intensive adjuvant therapy is frequently recommended for lowly differentiated ESCC patients due to its association with a greater likelihood of recurrence and metastasis. We found that postoperative adjuvant therapy failed to improve the survival outcomes of the poorly differentiated cT3N0M0-stage subgroup of patients. Thus, this group of patients may require preoperative neoadjuvant therapy, and this certainly deserves further exploration. Immunotherapy, which has improved safety profiles compared to traditional adjuvant chemoradiotherapy (28-31), is gaining increasing clinical acceptance. Thus, an appropriate postoperative adjuvant treatment modality urgently needs to be found, and large-scale, multicenter clinical studies need to be conducted to examine this issue.

This study had several limitations. First, this was a single-center, retrospective analysis, which might have introduced selection bias. Second, the extended time span of the data collection might have led to inconsistencies in documentation practices. Third, as the data were not prospectively collected, postoperative complications were not systematically recorded. Thus, we were unable to assess the survival benefits of adjuvant therapy in relation to treatment-related toxicities.

Conclusions

Upfront surgery remains the standard approach for low-risk cII-stage ESCC patients. However, PNI and LVI significantly affect the 5-year OS of this subgroup, with PNI emerging as an independent prognostic factor. For cT2/T3N0M0-stage ESCC patients with high-risk features, such as PNI-positive status, LVI-positive status, or low tumor differentiation, postoperative adjuvant therapy should be carefully considered to determine its potential benefits. Moreover, further exploration of novel adjuvant treatment modalities is necessary to improve the outcomes of these high-risk patients.

Carbon fiber-reinforced peek versus titanium implants for the surgical treatment of spinal metastases: Evaluation of local disease control and quality of life
Authors: Luigi Falzetti, Cristiana Griffoni, Simona Rosa, Diana Sarmiento Osorio, Silvia Terzi, Chiara Alcherigi, Mariarita Intagliata, Riccardo Ghermandi, Stefano Bandiera, Giovanni Barbanti Brodano, Giuseppe Tedesco, Gisberto Evangelisti, Valerio Pipola, Stefano Pasini, Giovanni Tosini, Chiara Cini, Alessandro Gasbarrini

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCarbon fiber-reinforced PEEK (CFRP) spinal instrumentation has emerged as a promising alternative in the oncological setting, allowing for superior postoperative imaging and radiotherapy planning.We aimed to evaluate the survival, local disease control and quality of life in a population of patients with spinal metastases surgically treated with CFRP or titanium implants.MethodsWe conducted an observational single-center study on a cohort of 160 patients with vertebral metastases surgically treated from August 2018 to October 2022.Demographic, clinical and surgical data, adverse events and patient-reported outcomes (PROMs) were collected.Results129 patients were treated with titanium and 31 patients with CFRP instrumentation. The median survival time of the entire population was 24.3 months, with no statistically significant difference between the two groups (p = 0.637). Local disease progression was recorded in 36 patients: 27 patients with titanium (20.9%) and 9 patients with CFRP (29.0%). The type of instrumentation had no significant impact on the local progression (p = 0.577).After surgery, 61.9% of patients underwent adjuvant systemic therapy and 31.9% of patients underwent radiotherapy at the index spinal lesion: Cox regression models showed the significant protective effect of adjuvant systemic therapy on mortality and also the protective effect of adjuvant radiotherapy, performed within the first year after surgery, on the risk of local recurrence, while no effect was associated with the type of instrumentation.No statistically significant differences emerged between the two groups of patients regarding mechanical complications and quality of life during the follow- up.ConclusionAccording to our results, CFRP implants did not confer a statistically significant improvement in survival or local disease control compared with titanium implants.Keywords: Spinal metastases, Titanium, Carbon fiber reinforced- PEEK, Charged particle therapy, Local disease control

Introduction

Vertebral metastases are the most common type of skeletal metastases, occurring in up to 70% of patients with advanced cancer, particularly those with primary tumors of the breast, prostate, lung, or kidney. Metastatic involvement of the spine can lead to significant morbidity, including intractable pain, pathological fractures, spinal instability, and neurological deficits due to spinal cord compression. While systemic therapies and radiotherapy remain central in the treatment of metastatic cancer, surgery plays a pivotal role in selected cases, particularly when mechanical instability or acute neurological compromise is present [1], [2], [3], [4].

Surgical interventions aim to decompress neural structures, restore spinal stability, and improve or preserve neurological function and quality of life. Modern surgical approaches include minimally invasive techniques, separation surgery followed by stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT), and en bloc resection in selected patients with solitary or oligometastatic disease [5], [6]. The decision-making process is typically guided by multidisciplinary evaluation and prognostic scoring systems to optimize patient selection [7], [8], [9], [10], [11].

In recent years, we have retrospectively analyzed the outcomes of surgical treatment in patients with spinal metastases in a tertiary spine surgery department, focusing on factors affecting survival for metastatic breast, kidney and lung cancer [12], [13], [14].

In addition, we recently reported the results of a prospective study to assess the impact of spine surgery on health-related quality of life (HRQOL) in metastatic patients [15].

In the same population of patients with metastatic spine disease, we aimed to analyze and compare two subgroups of patients that were treated with titanium or carbon fiber-reinforced PEEK instrumentations respectively, for the posterior stabilization.

Titanium has traditionally been the preferred material for spinal fixation due to its mechanical strength, biocompatibility, and osseointegration capacity. Despite its advantages, titanium generates significant artifacts on CT and MRI, which may compromise postoperative imaging and surveillance for local recurrence. Moreover, the presence of metallic implants is challenging for radiation therapy planning and administration, particularly when heavy particles are used, due to uneven dose distribution and associated risk to nearby tissue [16].

Carbon fiber-reinforced polyetheretherketone (CFRP) spinal instrumentation has emerged as a promising alternative in the oncological setting. CFRP is radiolucent, producing minimal artifacts on CT and MRI, and it interacts less with radiation therapy, resulting in reduced backscatter and shielding effects, thereby allowing for superior postoperative imaging and radiotherapy planning [17]. Additionally, the CFRP elastic modulus is closer to that of cortical bone, potentially reducing the risk of stress shielding and adjacent segment degeneration [18].

Despite these perceived advantages and safety profile, CFRP implants have limitations, including higher cost, limited intraoperative contourability, and concerns about mechanical durability in long-term follow-up. In recent years, several clinical studies have investigated the safety and efficacy of CFRP instrumentation for the treatment of spinal tumors [19], [20], [21], [22], [23].

We aimed to analyze differences in survival, local disease control and quality of life in a large population of spinal metastatic patients surgically treated with CFRP or titanium implants.

Materials and methods

Study design and setting

We performed a prospective, observational, single-center study on a cohort of patients with vertebral metastases who underwent spinal surgery and were consecutively enrolled at our tertiary referral center, from August 2018 to October 2022.

The study was approved by the local Ethics Committee in July 2018 (ID number: CE AVEC: 442/2018/Oss/IOR) and it was conducted according to the Declaration of Helsinki. The signature of a study-specific informed consent form was obtained from all patients prospectively enrolled for the study.

Inclusion Criteria

In this study, we enrolled patients older than 18 years with vertebral metastases, who received the indication for surgical treatment following a multidisciplinary assessment. We also included vertebral involvement from hematological diseases (lymphoma and myeloma). Both previously treated and therefore recurrent metastases and untreated lesions were included in the study. Patients treated exclusively with radiotherapy, systemic therapy or selective arterial embolization were excluded from the study.

We analyzed two subgroups of patients that were treated with titanium or carbon fiber-reinforced PEEK instrumentations respectively, for posterior stabilization.

Data collection

All patients included in this analysis made preoperative assessments with Computed Tomography TC), Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI), bone scintigraphy or Positron Emission Tomography (PET-CT). In all cases diagnosis was histologically verified. Indications for surgery were pain not responding to conservative measures, spinal instability or epidural spinal cord compression, with or without progressive neurological impairment.

Prospectively collected clinical and surgical information included the following: demographic data, type and treatment of primary tumor, location of spinal metastases, presence of other bone and visceral metastases, type of surgery, type of instrumentation, surgical complications classified according to SAVES v2 system [24], adjuvant treatments (chemotherapy and radiotherapy) performed before and after surgery, occurrence of local recurrences, health status at last follow up.

Clinical and radiological follow-up was performed on a regular basis at the index surgery and at 3, 6 and 12 months after the surgical procedure, then every 6 months.

The following questionnaires were self-administered to patients at baseline (before surgery) and at different follow up times (1–6 months, 7–12 months, 13–17 months, 18+ months) as Patient-Reported Outcomes: Numerical Rating Scale (NRS) for measuring perceived pain [25]; EuroQoL-5D (EQ-5D-3L), composed of a questionnaire with five items and by a visual analogue scale where patients indicate the perceived health status [26] and Short Form Health Survey 36 (SF-36) for quality of life [27], [28]. In the Supplementary Material section, the number of patients who responded to PROMs during the follow-up period are reported.

Statistical analysis

We summarized baseline demographic and clinical characteristics, employing an array of statistical measures tailored to the nature of each variable. Specifically, continuous variables were summarized through means and standard deviations, whereas median and interquartile ranges were utilized for variables with skewed distributions. For categorical data, we presented absolute numbers and percentage frequencies.

Age at surgery is a variable with normal distribution, therefore comparisons between means were conducted using either the Student's or Welch's t-test, depending on the result of Levene's test for equality of variances. Whenever a violation of the normality assumption was detected, as in the case of length of hospital stay, follow-up time, Karnofsky score, ASIA score, ambulatory status and Tokuhashi score, the comparison between distributions was calculated using the Mann-Whitney test. χ2 tests were used to compare categorical variables, with Pearson's or Fisher exact test selected based on their applicability.

The Kaplan–Meier estimator was used to display the time to death of the study patients, overall and by two hardware types (titanium or carbon fiber) and recurrent tumor (yes or no). Another Kaplan–Meier estimator was used to display the time to recurrent tumor by two hardware type. The median survival time was calculated along with 95% confidence interval (CI), and groups were compared with the log-rank test.

Cox proportional hazards models were used to further investigate time to death, time to recurrence and time to mechanical complications, analyzing the contribution of clinical and demographic variables. The final models were determined based on clinically relevant and/or statistically significant variables.

A competing risk analysis (Fine & Gray model) was conducted to evaluate the cumulative incidence of two mutually exclusive variables (local progression vs death) by two hardware type (titanium or carbon fiber) or post-operative radiotherapy.

The outcome variables (NRS, EQ.5D and SF36) were analyzed with multilevel linear mixed-effects regression. Because there were multiple follow-up evaluations per patient, we fit a two-level model for each outcome with random intercepts at the patient level. Time was treated as a categorical covariate to examine possible nonlinear trends, which resulted in the inclusion of dummy variables for time in the model. The fixed portion of the model was then augmented by including, in addition to time dummies, two hardware types (titanium or carbon fiber).

Because regression coefficient estimates are difficult to interpret when interactions are present, we opted for an indirect interpretation of the model via its predictions. More specifically, predicted means resulting from multilevel models were displayed using connected line charts, and differences (i.e., contrasts) across estimates at each follow-up evaluation were tested with the delta method.

The EQ.5D items concerning Movement, Personal Care, Habitual Activities, Pain, Anxiety and Depression were reclassified as 1 = 0 no difficulty, 2 and 3 = 1 presence of difficulty; the EQ-VAS has a range 0–100, where 0 is the worst perceived health status and 100 is the best.

The variable NRS pain scale was calculated for each follow-up time as the maximum value among the patient's imputed pain ratings of back, legs, arms, and neck (0 = no pain, 10 = maximum pain level).

The SF36 score concerning Physical functioning, Role limitations due to physical health, Pain, General health, Energy/fatigue, Social functioning, Role limitations due to emotional problems and Emotional well-being, has a range from 0 to 100, where higher values indicate better health.

For statistical analyses, IBMS PSS Statistics v.30 and Stata v.18 was used, with an alpha level of 5% set for statistical significance.

Baseline general status of the study population: performance status (Karnofski score), neurological status (ASIA score), ambulatory status, prognosis predictive score (Tokuhashi score).

As shown in Table 1, no significant differences were observed between the two groups of patients concerning demographic characteristics: 55.6% of patients were males and the mean age was 61.0 years (SD 12.1).

Regarding clinical data, the most common hystotypes were: kidney, breast, lung, and vertebral localizations of hematological diseases. Most patients presented the index spinal lesion localized in the thoracic (46.3%) or in the lumbar region (35.6%); no cases of cervical localization were reported in the CFRP group because carbon- fiber instrumentation is not yet available for posterior stabilization at cervical level. Approximately half of the cases (79, 49.4%) presented with pathological fracture of the index vertebra.

As shown in Table 2, patients treated with CRFP implants had better baseline performance status and ambulatory status, and a more favorable prognosis (as assessed by Tokuhashi score).

Consequently, a higher rate of en bloc resections (6 cases, 19.4%) was reported in the group treated with CFRP compared to the group treated with titanium (2 cases, 1.6%) (Table 1).

Concerning preoperative treatments, systemic therapy was performed in 89 cases (55.6%) and radiotherapy in 65 cases (40.6%). 33 patients (20.6%) had already been operated on for the vertebral metastases and had a local recurrence; we called these cases “non intact”, while the patients who underwent spine surgery for the first time were called “intact” (127 cases, 79.4%) (Table 1).

After the surgery, 61.9% of patients underwent adjuvant systemic therapy and 31.9% of patients underwent radiotherapy at the index spinal lesion: 13 cases (41.9%) in the group treated with CFRP and 38 cases (29.5%) in the group treated with titanium.

A local recurrence was recorded in 36 patients (22.5%), while surgical complications were recorded in 58 patients (36.3%). Seven cases of mechanical complications (5.4%) occurred in the group treated with titanium implants, while no cases were registered in the group treated with CFRP (Table 1).

The median length of stay was 7.0 days (IQ 6.0–10.0) and the median follow up period was 17.6 months (IQ 7.8–35.2).

Survival analysis.

The Kaplan Meier estimate of overall survival is reported in Fig. 1. The median survival time was 24.3 months (95% CI 15.0–32.1).

Overall survival.

The patients treated with CFRP implants had a median survival time of 18.4 months (95% CI 13.9–22.9), while patients treated with titanium implants had a median survival time of 25.0 months (95% CI 15.9–34.0). The Kaplan Meier survival estimate, reported in Fig. 2, showed no statistically significant difference between the two groups (Log- rank test, p = 0.637).

The COX regression model with outcome Death (Table 7) showed that both post-operative chemotherapy and radiotherapy had a protective effect, reducing the risk of death by approximately 50%. The Tokuhashi score was confirmed as a parameter strongly associated with the outcome: in fact, patients with scores 9–11 had a 68% reduction in the risk of death and patient with scores 12–15 had a 90% reduction, when compared to patients in the category of scores 0–8.

In the subgroup of 121 patients, 17 patients (14%) experienced a local recurrence within the first 12 months after surgery and 104 were free of recurrence. As shown in Table 8, men had a 5 times higher risk of local recurrence than women (HR = 5.115, p = 0.011) and adjuvant radiotherapy had a strong protective effect with 80% risk reduction (HR = 0.170, p = 0.020).

Discussion

Carbon fiber-reinforced PEEK implants were previously studied for the treatment of primary spinal tumors in order to make easier and more effective the adjuvant radiotherapy treatment in all cases where a satisfactory oncological margin cannot be achieved [19], [20]. Thanks to radiolucency property of CFRP no artifacts were present on imaging studies, allowing an early local recurrence detection and easier planning for radiation therapy; moreover, thanks to the absence of scattering effect a more effective and safe radiotherapy could be administered, particularly when charged particles were used.

Further investigations have been carried out to analyze the use of CFRP instrumentation for the treatment of primary spinal tumors and spinal metastases, confirming that these implants represent a safe and efficient alternative to titanium for oncological spinal surgery, with low complication and revision rates and with the advantage of radiolucency [21], [22], [23].

However, these studies have not yet explored the potential oncological advantage related to radiolucency of carbon fiber-PEEK implants. Thus, we investigated these effects in a cohort of 160 patients surgically treated for metastatic spine disease, comparing the oncological outcomes of patients treated with carbon fiber-reinforced PEEK and titanium implants.

In our population, only 31 patients (less than 20%) were treated with CFRP implants, while 129 patients received standard titanium implants. Generally, the use of CFRP implants was preferred for patients with unknown histology or with the presence of an isolated spinal metastasis: our cohort of patients treated with CRFP implants had better baseline performance status and ambulatory status, and a more favorable prognosis (as assessed by Tokuhashi score).

Consequently, a higher rate of en bloc resection of isolated metastases was reported in the group treated with CFRP compared to the group treated with titanium (6 cases vs 2 cases). Moreover, no cervical lesions were treated with CRFP implants because carbon-fiber instrumentation is not yet available for posterior stabilization at cervical level.

After the surgery, 61.9% of patients underwent adjuvant systemic therapy and 31.9% of patients underwent radiation therapy at the index spinal lesion: 13 cases (41.9%) in the group treated with CFRP and 38 cases (29.5%) in the group treated with titanium.

Local disease progression occurred in 20.9% of patients treated with titanium instrumentation and in 29% of those with CFRP constructs, with no statistically significant difference between the two groups. Over 50% of the population had a local control of disease (no local progression or recurrence) approximately 60 months after surgery. The median overall survival for the entire cohort was 24.3 months, and the type of instrumentation had no significant impact on mortality or local progression. COX regression analyses performed on the entire population showed a significant protective effect of adjuvant systemic therapy on mortality (HR = 0.363, p < 0.001) and a protective effect of adjuvant radiotherapy administered within the first year after surgery on the risk of local recurrence (HR = 0.367, p = 0.073). The effect of radiotherapy was highlighted when patients with tumors more influenced by systemic therapies were excluded from the analyses: in the remaining patient subgroup adjuvant radiotherapy had a significant protective effect both on mortality (HR = 0.434, p = 0.002) and on risk of local recurrence (HR = 0.170, p = 0.020), confirming its central role in oncologic control. In all analyses performed no effect on survival or local disease control was associated with the type of instrumentation.

We also observed a significantly higher risk of local recurrence in the male gender compared to the female gender. We haven't a clear explanation of this result, however it could be associated with the higher rate of adjuvant radiotherapy performed by females in the first year after surgery (30/ 71 females = 42.2%; 21/89 males, 23.5%).

During the last year, while the authors were conducting the study presented here, two papers have been published reporting a comparison between CFRP and titanium implants for the treatment of spinal metastases [29], [30], [31]. Our findings are consistent with those reported by Andrade-Almeida et al. [29], who also found no significant difference in survival between patients undergoing decompression and stabilization with CFRP or titanium instrumentation for metastatic spinal disease. Although their study demonstrated a trend toward lower local recurrence with CFRP implants, the benefit lost statistical significance after adjustment for confounding variables in COX regression analysis, suggesting that the clinical advantage of CFRP may be indirect, through improved imaging quality and radiation planning. Similarly, in our cohort the main determinant of improved outcomes was the timing of adjuvant radiotherapy (within one year after surgery) rather than the implant material itself, highlighting the importance of multidisciplinary coordination in spine oncology.

In this context, the findings from Ward et al. [30] and Alvarez-Breckenridge et al. [31] further reinforce the evolving role of CFRP instrumentation in the multidisciplinary management of metastatic spinal tumors. Ward and colleagues [30] demonstrated that while overall progression-free survival was comparable between CFRP and titanium implants, disease recurrence was detected two times earlier in patients with CFRP constructs, underscoring the advantage of artifact-free postoperative imaging for earlier intervention and tailored adjuvant therapy. Likewise, Alvarez-Breckenridge et al. [31], in the largest systematic review to date including 573 patients across 10 studies, confirmed that CFRP instrumentation provides a safety profile equivalent to titanium while significantly improving postoperative visualization and radiation dose precision in photon and particle therapies.

These studies suggest that the true clinical potential of CFRP implants lies in their ability to enhance oncologic control by optimizing radiotherapy planning and facilitating early recurrence detection.

Mechanical complications in our cohort were rare, with no failures reported in the CFRP group, supporting the biomechanical reliability and safety of these implants, as previously reported [19], [20], [21], [22], [23]. Finally, we observed an improvement in pain and performance of daily activities after surgery and during the follow up period, without statistically significant differences between the two groups of patients, confirming the positive impact of surgery for spinal metastases on health-related quality of life [15].

Our study has limitations, including its monocenter design, heterogeneous tumor histologies, and different number of patients in the two study groups, which may reduce the statistical power to detect subtle differences. The findings of the present research should be interpreted in the context of the study design and patient variability: in our study population the two study groups (patients treated with titanium or CRFP instrumentation) presented different baseline characteristics, because the more expensive CRFP implants were usually chosen for a group of patients having more favorable prognosis and better baseline performance status. However, the results of our study don't support a statistically significant improvement in survival or local disease control for patients treated with CRFP implants compared to titanium implants.

In conclusion, based on our findings, we cannot recommend the routine use of CFRP in the surgical treatment of secondary tumors, unlike our previous recommendation for primary tumors, also in the light of the higher cost of these implants. In cases of unknown diagnosis or when curative surgery is anticipated, CFRP may be preferred due to more favorable characteristics, particularly with respect to radiological planning. Therefore, implant selection should be guided by surgeon preference, patient characteristics, and multidisciplinary team consensus, considering the specific response of the tumor to systemic and radiation therapies. Further multicenter, possibly randomized studies with standardized radiotherapy protocols are warranted to determine whether CFRP implants could provide a significant clinical benefit in the surgical treatment of spinal metastases.

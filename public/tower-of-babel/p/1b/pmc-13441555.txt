The Past, the Present, and the Future of Periostin in Allergy
Authors: Kenji Izuhara, Satoshi Nunomura, Yasuhiro Nanri, Yuko Honda
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPeriostin is a matricellular protein that binds to its receptors, which include several integrin molecules but mainly αvβ3 integrin on cell surfaces, thereby transducing its signals into cells. Almost 20 years have passed since we found that periostin is involved in the pathogenesis of asthma, which marked the first evidence of its involvement in allergic diseases. Over the past 2 decades, cumulative evidence has demonstrated that periostin plays important roles in the onset or progress of many allergic diseases such as chronic rhinitis with nasal polyp, atopic dermatitis (AD), allergic conjunctivitis, eosinophilic esophagitis, eosinophilic otitis media, allergic rhinitis, and eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis. Starting from the observation of highly expressed periostin in the lesions of these diseases, periostin studies have expanded to include how periostin contributes to the generation of allergic phenotypes, how measurement of periostin is useful as a biomarker for allergic diseases, and recently how periostin inhibitors can improve allergic diseases. Periostin contributes to the generation of allergic phenotypes by acting as a matricellular protein on many cells, including both non-immune and immune cells. Based on the characteristics of periostin as a surrogate biomarker of interleukin-13, a signature cytokine of type 2 inflammation, serum periostin has been used as a biomarker for stratifying endotypes, estimating disease severity, predicting or monitoring the efficacy of therapeutic drugs, and predicting disease relapse or recurrence. Moreover, a lot of attention is now being paid to periostin in body fluids such as tears and sputum as a biomarker directly reflecting type 2 inflammation. Since it has been shown that periostin evokes itch in AD, we hope to see the development of a novel therapeutic agent for AD targeting periostin/αvβ3 integrin. Here we summarize the discovery and characteristics of periostin, as well as questions and/or problems regarding periostin that remain to be resolved.Keywords: Periostin, asthma, hypersensitivity, body fluids, pruritus, atopic dermatitis, inhibitors, integrins

INTRODUCTION

Almost 20 years have passed since we reported that periostin is involved in the pathogenesis of asthma,1 which was the first evidence for the role played by periostin in allergic diseases. During the past 2 decades, our understanding of how periostin is involved in allergic diseases has been greatly expanded. When we search PubMed for references that include periostin and either allergy, allergic disease, asthma, atopic dermatitis (AD), rhinitis, rhinosinusitis, conjunctivitis, or esophagitis in the title or abstract, more than 580 papers come up. The diseases correlated with periostin have grown from asthma to many allergic diseases such as chronic rhinitis with nasal polyp (CRSwNP), AD, allergic conjunctivitis, eosinophilic esophagitis, eosinophilic otitis media, allergic rhinitis (AR), and eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA). The first paper showing the involvement of periostin in asthma demonstrated that it is highly expressed in the thickened basement membranes of asthmatic patients.1 Starting from this observation, studies concerning periostin have expanded to how periostin contributes to the generation of allergic phenotypes, to how measurement of periostin is useful as a biomarker for allergic diseases, and recently to how periostin inhibitors can improve allergic diseases. In this review article, we first summarize how periostin was discovered, then discuss its known characteristics, and finally highlight the unsolved questions that remain to be addressed.

DISCOVERY OF PERIOSTIN

Explosive progress in molecular biology and immunology starting in the 1970s has brought us the identification of many cytokines important for inflammation and immune reactions. Based on these achievements, Mosmann et al. 2 proposed the TH1/TH2 theory in 1986, which underlies our understanding of how inflammation occurs. Thereafter, the TH17 subset was added to the TH1/TH2 subsets, and it is now recognized that inflammation can be mainly categorized into types 1, 2, and 17.3 In the 1990s, studies using model mice revealed that type 2 inflammation is dominant in the pathogenesis of asthma.4 Now this concept has been expanded to other allergic diseases, and it turns out to be applicable to the pathogenesis of human allergic diseases as well. Building on these research milestones, several drugs molecularly targeting type 2 mediators have been developed and are now available for treating asthma,5 AD,6 AR,7 CRSwNP,8 eosinophilic esophagitis,9 and EGPA.10

Interleukin (IL)-4 and IL-13 are the signature cytokines of type 2 inflammation, sharing their receptors and signaling pathways so that these cytokines can exert very similar actions.11 However, it was first thought that IL-4 was the key player and IL-13 was the back-up. Thereafter, their roles were found to be the reverse: IL-13 is the key player in the pathogenesis of asthma, in studies using the mouse models of asthma in the late 1990s.12,13 To elucidate the roles of IL-13 in the pathogenesis of asthma, we comprehensively identified IL-13-inducible genes in airway epithelial cells using DNA microarrays, finding that the Postn gene encoding periostin was included in the many identified genes.14 At that time, however, nothing was known about the correlation between periostin and allergic diseases. This finding led to the subsequent discovery that periostin is a type 2 mediator of allergic diseases. It was well known that periostin is an extracellular matrix protein and that it physiologically functions to maintain the structures of tissues and organs.15,16 We thereafter found that periostin acts as a matricellular protein that binds to its receptor on cell surfaces such as cytokines, thereby transducing its signals in the cells, and that the actions of periostin as a matricellular protein are important for the onset or progress of allergic diseases.17,18,19,20 Moreover, starting with asthma, we and others found that periostin is broadly involved in the pathogenesis of many allergic diseases. ACTIONS OF PERIOSTIN IN ALLERGIC DISEASES

As a matricellular protein, periostin transduces its signals by binding to mainly αvβ3 integrin, as well as to other integrins—such as αvβ1, αvβ5, α6β4, and αMβ2—on the cell surface.36 Since these integrin molecules are ubiquitously expressed on many cells, periostin can transduce its signals into these cells. Indeed, many reports have demonstrated that periostin contributes to the generation of allergic phenotypes by acting on many cells, including both non-immune cells—such as epithelial cells/keratinocytes, fibroblasts, endothelial cells, pericytes, and nerve cells—and immune cells such as eosinophils, mast cells, and macrophages. Using both in vitro and in vivo systems, we have established the critical role of periostin in the skin inflammation of AD by finding that periostin produced in fibroblasts acts on keratinocytes, thereby activating nuclear factor-kappaB (NF-κB) p65/Rel A (Fig. 1).21,38 In a 3-dimensional air-liquid co-culture system, we put fibroblasts in the gel and put keratinocytes on the gel, showing that soluble factors derived from either fibroblasts or keratinocytes can act on keratinocytes or fibroblasts, mutually, through the gel. When we stimulated wild-type fibroblasts with IL-13, periostin was secreted from the fibroblasts and then acted on keratinocytes, inducing proliferation/differentiation and production of pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as thymic stromal lymphopoietin.21 In contrast, when we stimulated periostin-deficient fibroblasts with IL-13, periostin was not secreted from fibroblasts, so that keratinocytes did not induce either proliferation/differentiation or production of pro-inflammatory cytokines. To this end, we were able to use both house-dust-mite-inducing mouse models of AD and FADS (facial atopic dermatitis with scratching) to establish that spontaneously exhibited eczema is limited in faces and severe scratching behaviors due to itch.39 We found that a genetic deficiency in periostin downregulated activation of NF-κB p65/Rel A, followed by inhibiting production of NF-κB–related pro-inflammatory cytokines, particularly, IL-19 and IL-24, in keratinocytes in both model mice.21,38 This means that periostin produced in fibroblasts plays a role in linking the NF-κB signals in keratinocytes to type 2 inflammation. Thus, we established the critical role of periostin in the epithelial-mesenchymal interaction in the pathogenesis of AD. This would at least partially explain why genetic deficiency of periostin impairs allergic skin inflammation in model mice.37,38 Taken together, these results indicate that periostin will be useful for developing therapeutic agents against AD.

It is well known that αvβ3 integrin, the main receptor for periostin, can bind to several ligands other than periostin—tenascin C, osteopontin, and Thy1—and then transduce the signals in cells. It is assumed that these ligands can transduce the same signals as periostin in cells. It has been reported that expression of tenascin C is upregulated in AD patients and plays a role in epidermal hyperplasia53,54; osteopontin is important for migration of dendritic cells from skin to draining lymph nodes55; and Thy1 antigen expressed on T cells promotes differentiation of naïve T cells into Th2 cells by binding to αvβ3 integrin on them.56 These findings suggest that the signals from αvβ3 integrin stimulated by the ligands other than periostin would increase type 2 inflammation beyond the actions of periostin. Actually, in FADS mice, systemic administration of CP4715, an inhibitor against αvβ3 integrin, inhibited or further downregulated expression of type 2 cytokines such as IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13, compared to genetic disruption of periostin (unpublished data in part).38 Thus, αvβ3 integrin has greater potential as a therapeutic target for AD than periostin.

USEFULNESS OF MEASURING PERIOSTIN AS A BIOMARKER FOR ALLERGIC DISEASES

Since it is a downstream molecule of IL-4/IL-13, periostin is thought to be a surrogate biomarker of IL-4/IL-13, particularly IL-13, in allergic diseases. Moreover, since periostin concentration in serum is 100,000-fold of that of IL-13, serum periostin has an advantage over serum IL-13 in that it can be measured stably.36 Based on the characteristics of periostin reflecting type 2 inflammation and in part fibrosis in inflamed sites of allergic diseases, serum periostin has been used as a biomarker for stratifying endotypes, estimating severity of diseases, predicting or monitoring efficacy of therapeutic drugs, and predicting relapse or recurrence of asthma, AD, and CRSwNP.20,36,57 Regarding AR, only one paper showed a positive correlation between serum periostin and the existence or severity of AR22; however, several papers have shown no correlation between AR and serum periostin levels.58,59,60 Regarding eosinophilic otitis media and EGPA, only one paper for each disease has shown that serum periostin is upregulated according to clinical severity23,24; however, further studies are warranted to validate these findings. The authors have recently been involved in the B-PAD study, in which 19 medical facilities in Japan constructed a consortium and recruited 110 moderate-to-severe AD patients. At baseline and at regular intervals after starting dupilumab, clinical parameters—eczema area and severity index (EASI), patient-oriented eczema measure, and Pruritis-numerical rating scale (NRS)—were assessed together with evaluating 19 biomarkers.61,62 We found that serum periostin is significantly correlated with EASI and Pruritis-NRS prior to the initiation of dupilumab treatment and is the most useful among the 19 biomarkers for predicting the efficacy of dupilumab.

Among recent research topics, we can highlight the measurement of periostin in body fluids other than serum. In some respects, periostin in body fluids may have greater potential than serum periostin as a biomarker for allergic diseases, because these levels in body fluids would reflect more directly inflammatory states in the lesions than serum periostin does. We previously found that periostin can be measured in tears of allergic conjunctivitis patients, and that tear periostin levels are correlated with disease severity.25 We recently showed that tear periostin is significantly associated with resistance against tacrolimus in severe allergic conjunctivitis patients.63 Moreover, it is well known that periostin in sputum or exhaled breath condensate is detected or upregulated in asthmatic patients.64,65,66,67,68,69 We found that sputum of asthmatic patients contains a smaller periostin fragment (Fig. 2).70 The smaller fragment would be generated probably by cleavage by some proteases in airways. However, it is unclear which protease does it, and the conventional way to detect periostin cannot detect the fragment. So, we have established a novel assay system to detect the fragment (Fig. 2).70 The levels of the smaller periostin fragment, but not those of full-sized periostin fragments detected in serum, were correlated with reduced lung function. Furthermore, we recently found that sputum periostin is correlated with sputum IL-13, sputum eosinophil, and FeNO, and that treatment with benralizumab and dupilumab, but not mepolizumab, decreased periostin levels in sputum.71 These results suggest that measuring the smaller periostin fragment in sputum may be useful for monitoring efficacy of some asthma drugs. Research into smaller periostin fragments as a novel biomarker for allergic diseases has just started and will be further developed in the future.

Composition of the smaller periostin fragment in sputum and the principle of its detection. The composition of full-sized periostin and the smaller periostin fragment detected in serum and sputum, respectively. The full-sized periostin is composed of the EMI domain, 4 fasciclin domains (R1–R4), and the C-terminal domain (C-ter), whereas the smaller periostin fragment is composed of the EMI domain, 2 fasciclin domains (R1, R2), and the fragment of R3 domain. Two monoclonal antibodies against periostin, SS18A and SS17B, recognize only the full-sized periostin, whereas SS18A and SS19C do both the full-sized periostin and the smaller periostin fragment.

POTENTIAL OF PERIOSTIN/αvβ3 INTEGRIN AS A THERAPEUTIC TARGET FOR ALLERGIC DISEASES

Since it has been appreciated that the periostin/αvβ3 integrin signals play an important role in the pathogenesis of many allergic diseases, special attention has been paid to periostin/αvβ3 integrin as a therapeutic target for allergic diseases. Notably, it has turned out that periostin/αvβ3 integrin is one of the important pruritogens (in addition to other type 2 mediators) causing skin inflammation in AD pathogenesis, so development of therapeutic agents targeting periostin/αvβ3 integrin for AD, particularly itch, is eagerly anticipated. Mishra et al.37 showed that injection of periostin into animals evoked robust itch behaviors and calcium intake into the dorsal root ganglia (DRG) neurons. In agreement, a genetic deficiency of b3 integrin under the control of TRPV1 downregulated itch behaviors induced by either periostin or MC903, a vitamin D analog, which is known to cause AD-like phenotypes in mice. Moreover, we demonstrated that a genetic deficiency of periostin in FADS mice spontaneously exhibiting severe itch behaviors downregulates the itch.38 These results show that periostin directly acts on DRG neurons transducing itch signals. In contrast, Hashimoto et al.40 demonstrated that periostin acts on M2 macrophages, inducing IL-31 and subsequently evoking itch in MC903-inducing model mice, showing the indirect action of periostin on itch in AD pathogenesis. Persistent itch is a major problem for AD patients because it disturbs their quality of life, sleep, and social activities, so it is very much to be desired to develop therapeutic agents targeting periostin/αvβ3 integrin against AD, particularly for itch.

Given the importance of periostin/αvβ3 integrin for itch in AD model mice, Mishra et al.37 investigated the efficacy of cilengitide, a well-known αvβ3 integrin inhibitor, on itch in periostin-injected mice. They were able to show that cilengitide significantly downregulates the itch behaviors induced by periostin (Fig. 3). We applied a low-molecular-weight compound having an inhibitory effect against αvβ3 integrin, CP4715, to FADS mice, which decreased scratching behaviors due to itch (Fig. 3).38 It is of note that the inhibition of neural firing by CP4715 was very prompt, within 1 hour, indicating that the effects of CP4715 on DRG neurons would be direct. These results strongly highlight the potential of the αvβ3 integrin inhibitors as therapeutic agents against itch in AD.

Structures of 2 αvβ3 integrin inhibitors, cilengitide and CP4715, are depicted (left). Periostin and other ligands to αvβ3 integrin, such as osteopontin and tenascin-C, bind to αvβ3 integrin on peripheral sensory neurons ascendingly transducing itch signals (right). Consequently, αvβ3 integrin inhibitors block itch by inhibiting the binding of periostin and other ligands to αvβ3 integrin.

CP4715 was initially developed as a candidate compound targeting cardiovascular diseases by Meiji Seika Pharma Co., Ltd.,72,73,74,75 but this project was discontinued. A prototype of CP4715 was synthesized mimicking the structure of the arginine-glycine-aspartate tripeptide sequence, a key recognition site of the αvβ3 integrin ligands. The composition of CP4715, optimal for strong inhibitory activities and for good water solubility, has been selected and fixed by repeating spatial screening and cell adhesion assays using vascular smooth muscle cells and vitronectin. Moreover, it was confirmed that CP4715 is safe in vitro as well as in vivo (provided by Meiji Seika Pharma Co. Ltd.).74 Thus, the potential of CP4715 to be developed as a therapeutic agent would be high. We previously demonstrated that CP4715 improves bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis, in which periostin is known to play an important role.76 Furthermore, we recently showed that topical administration of CP4715 into the eyes of FADS mice showing atopic keratoconjunctivitis-like ocular lesions41 (in addition to skin lesions) improves keratoconjunctivitis.77 Moreover, periostin increased neovascularization at least partially by upregulating vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF)-A/VEGF-R2 in corneal epithelium, stroma, and vascular endothelial cells, which was blocked by CP4715 as well. These results were in accordance with the findings that genetic deficiency of periostin in FADS mice downregulates atopic keratoconjunctivitis-like ocular lesions together with neovascularization.77 Taken together, CP4715 would be applicable not only to AD but also to other periostin-related diseases.

PERSPECTIVES

During the past 2 decades since the discovery of periostin’s role in the pathogenesis of asthma, research into periostin in allergy has opened 3 discovery stages: its involvement in the pathogenesis, its usefulness as a biomarker, and its potential as a therapeutic target. The second discovery stage, “usefulness of periostin as a biomarker,” will surely motivate many investigators and increase periostin research. Now the discovery stage of “potential of periostin as a therapeutic target” has arrived, and we believe that researchers will be even more inspired, and that new avenues of allergy research will be found.

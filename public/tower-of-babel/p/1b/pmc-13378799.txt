A comparative study of the effect of virtual and telephone self-care training based on the health belief model on the quality of life of primiparous women with preeclampsia in the postpartum period
Authors: Mastaneh Kamravamanesh, Zahra Hossiani Shoar, Mansour Rezaei, Negin Rezavand, Hossein Ashtarian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Preeclampsia can affect women’s quality of life even after childbirth; Therefore, the present study was conducted with the aim of comparing the effect of virtual and telephone self-care training based on the health belief model on the quality of life of primiparous women with preeclampsia in the postpartum period.MATERIALS AND METHODS:In this clinical trial study with parallel groups, the effect of two methods of virtual and telephone self-care training based on the health belief model on the quality of life of primiparous women with preeclampsia in the postpartum period in hospitals affiliated to Kermanshah University of Medical Sciences. was compared. The sample size for intervention and control groups was 75 people. Data were collected through the quality of life questionnaire, demographic and midwifery information before, one month and three months after the intervention and analyzed using SPSS software.RESULTS:Based on the results of analysis of variance with repeated measurement in the self-care training group using virtual space as well as the telephone follow-up group, there was a statistically significant difference between the three investigated time points in the total score of the quality of life questionnaire and physical functioning areas, role disorder due to physical health, role dysfunction due to emotional health, energy/fatigue, emotional well-being, social functioning, pain and general health.CONCLUSION:The results of the present study showed that both methods of educational intervention (telephone and virtual space based) can improve the dimensions of the quality of life after delivery of women with preeclampsia.

Introduction

Preeclampsia affects approximately 3–5% of all pregnancies and can lead to maternal and perinatal morbidity and mortality worldwide.[12] According to available evidence, preeclampsia is not just a transient health problem and can lead to short- and long-term complications and it influencing the quality of life of the women with preeclampsia even years after giving birth.[3]

Preeclampsia and its physical and psychological complications have negative effects on the quality of life of the affected women in the postpartum period.[4567] The postpartum period is an appropriate and unique opportunity to inform and educate women by health care providers about the follow-up of long-term complications of preeclampsia and the modification of modifiable factors,[89] if women with preeclampsia use appropriate educational programs, they will improve self-care in the postpartum period and they will greatly benefit from their improved their quality of life.[1011] Self-care training can generally improve learning in these women and eventually lead to behavior change and improvement of their quality of life.[12]

Self-care is a strategy which a person uses to control the disease, promote health and live well with the disease.[13] Health and illness are dependent on knowledge, attitude, beliefs and behavior of people in societies. Therefore, the need is clearly felt to implement various educational models along with various educational methods and tools for creating correct health behaviors and achieving health and promoting it.[14]

Thus, choosing a health education model is the first step in the planning process of an educational program. One of the models that has been frequently used in health-related behavioral science studies is the health belief model, which has provided a basis for behavioral interventions.[1415] The health belief model acts as an effective framework for designing educational interventions and promoting preventive behaviors, and it is formulated based on this idea that health belief model causes people to understand a health threat and, as a result, leads their behaviors towards health and increases the quality of life.[141617] The health belief model is one of the educational models that shows the relationship between health beliefs and health behavior and places great emphasis on individuals’ motivation to perform action.[18] The health belief model is one of the most important conceptual frameworks for health behavior, focusing on behavior change at the individual level.[19] The health belief model is a theory in the field of behavioral and social sciences that has six constructs, which are as follows: perceived susceptibility (an individual’s subjective perception of the risk of developing a disease), perceived severity (an individual’s feelings about the seriousness of the harm resulting from the disease), perceived benefits (an individual’s perception of the effectiveness of various methods of reducing the risk or severity of the disease), perceived barriers (includes perceived potential negative aspects that prevent a particular behavior), action guide (actually a spark to initiate behavior), self-efficacy (as an individual’s judgment of his or her ability to organize, direct the course of action, and act in order to achieve desired outcomes).[1820]

According to the current situation that the corona pandemic has gripped the world, it is recommended that counseling mothers during pregnancy and after delivery be done remotely by phone or virtual networks and electronic education. As it is not possible for mothers to participate in face-to-face training programs, using virtual training as an alternative method leads to incessantly increasing the efficiency of the training process, as well as ease of access to a large amount of information in a short time, thus reducing cost.[2122] E-learning is an emerging method that overcomes some of the obstacles of traditional education and provides easy and flexible access to learning.[2324] In the health and treatment sector, the use of virtual space based on smart mobile phones makes it possible for patients living in remote places to access education; therefore, the cost of travel and waste of time are also reduced, and the exchange of information and skills is also facilitated.[25] Regarding the above-mentioned information on preeclampsia, which is the second most common cause of maternal death in Iran, and has the adverse consequences for mothers and their babies and severely affects the quality of life of mothers and also Since postpartum care for these women is not considered compared to prenatal care, studies on postpartum self-care education and improving their quality of life due to the complications left by preeclampsia are limited, the present study aims to compare the effects of two methods of virtual and telephone self-care training based on the health belief model on the quality of life of primiparous women with preeclampsia in the postpartum period, who were referred to hospitals affiliated to Kermanshah University of Medical Sciences (Imam Reza (AS) and Motazadi) in the year 2021–2022.

Material and Methods

Study design and setting

This study is a clinical trial with parallel groups and aims to compare the effects of two methods of virtual and telephone self-care training based on the health belief model on the quality of life of primiparous women with preeclampsia in the postpartum period, who were referred to hospitals affiliated to Kermanshah University of Medical Sciences (Imam Reza (AS) and Motazedi Hospitals) Iran in 2021.

Study participants and sampling

The research population consisted of all women who had given birth and suffered from preeclampsia, and were eligible to be included in the study. Among them, 75 people were selected by available sampling method and randomly divided into three groups: two intervention groups and one control group, including the training group using virtual space (n = 25), the self-care training group with telephone follow-up (n = 25) and the control group (n = 25). The random assignment method was performed based on a table of random numbers, and the numbers were between zero and 0.333 in the first group, from 0.334 to 0.666 in the second group, and from 0.667 to 0.999 in the third group. The random sequence was generated by the statistical consultant, and the researcher did the registration, and the random assignment was done by the project manager, who did not play a role in measuring the variables [Figure 1].

Flow of participants through each stage of the program

To hide the randomization, only the project manager knew about the randomization, and the samples and the researcher did not know. Blinding of the samples could not be done due to the intervention, but blinding was done by the researcher and statistical consultant and therefore, the study is one-way blind. Quality of life was measured as the primary outcome and there was no secondary outcome. The samples were then compared.

The criteria for being involved in the research were: age between 15 and 49 years, ability to understand the questions and having literacy, having a mobile phone and access to the Internet, absence of various types of severe mental disorders (psychosis, schizophrenia, etc.), being under medical treatment or requiring hospitalization, those women who had been diagnosed with preeclampsia of different severity during pregnancy and childbirth (except eclampsia) based on the preeclampsia criteria and clinical and paraclinical evidence confirmed by a gynecologist, those primiparous women (with pre-eclampsia) whose delivery had led to the birth of a live baby. Exclusion criteria included having serious complications of pre-eclampsia such as nephropathy and being in the process of drug treatment, not attending more than 2 sessions from the training course, failure to complete the questionnaire, having eclampsia after childbirth, experiencing the death of baby during the intervention and unwillingness to continue cooperation in research.

After obtaining permission from the ethics committee of Kermanshah University of Medical Sciences with the ethics ID IR.KUMS.REC.1400.407 and registering in the Iranian clinical trial database, to all the people participating in the research, the purpose and method of conducting the present research and its benefits were explained in a simple and comprehensible way, and after obtaining their trust and the written informed consent form for all the participants, the interventions were implemented and the desired questionnaires were completed. Demographic information form and quality of life questionnaire were used to collect data in this research.

Intervention

The content of the educational program based on the health belief model in the intervention groups was held in seven sessions of 30–60 minutes for 7 weeks, one session per week using the WhatsApp virtual messaging network including text, video, poster, discussion, questions and answers. The telephone follow-up training group completed self-care training with the same method and content as the WhatsApp virtual messaging network training group. With the samples of this group, a phone call was made by the researcher every week for seven weeks to obtain the desired educational content and answer the questions. The duration of the phone call was 30–60 minutes. In contrast, the control group received no intervention and only routine care according to national guidelines. The follow-up period after the intervention in the present study was considered at intervals of one month and three months. Before the intervention, four weeks after the educational intervention and twelve weeks after the educational intervention, the quality of life score was completed by the research samples using a questionnaire and with the presence of the researcher [Figure 1].

In this study, the educational program was developed based on the health belief model and as simple and understandable as possible. The educational content used in this research was taken from the pamphlet and the instructions of the Ministry of Health and obstetrics and gynecology reference books, and the desired content was reviewed under the supervision of a supervisor and a consultant as well as a consultant professor, a gynecologist and an obstetrician.

In this research, although the control group was not subject to educational intervention, but in order to comply with ethical considerations at the end of the project, educational content with the same quality and quantity was presented to the control group in the form of a pamphlet.

Statistical analysis

In this study, the normality of the data distribution was checked using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Chi-square test was used to compare qualitative variables between three groups and one-way analysis of variance was used to compare the mean of quantitative variables including age among three groups. To compare the average quality of life and its structures, before intervention, one month and three months after the intervention in each group, analysis of variance with repeated measurements was used.

Ethical consideration

This study was approved by the ethics review board of the Vice-chancellor for Research Affairs of Kermanshah University of Medical Sciences (IR.KUMS.REC.1400.407) and all the methods were used according to the relevant guidelines and regulations or the Declaration of Helsinki. The informed consent form was obtained from all the subjects and/or their legal guardian(s).

Results

According to the findings, the average age (standard deviation) in the WhatsApp intervention group (5.38) was 27.88, in the telephone call intervention group (6.60) it was 30.36, and in the control group (6.66) it was 30.52. Also, the mean (standard deviation) age of delivery in the WhatsApp intervention group (3.02) was 35.56; in the telephone call intervention group (1.84), 36.36; in the control group (2.31), 37.08. The average (standard deviation) of body mass index in the WhatsApp intervention group (5.04) was 25.29, in the telephone call intervention group (6.01) it was 28.18, and in the control group (4.13) it was 28.56. The normality of the data was confirmed by using the Kolmogorov–Smirnoff test, and due to the assumption of independence of the groups and the assumption of homogeneity of variances (significance level of Lon’s test greater than 0.05) from the one-way analysis of variance test for age comparison, body mass index and delivery age were calculated for the groups (All P value < 0.05).

In terms of the delivery status variable, cesarean delivery was the most frequent in the three groups (96% in the WhatsApp intervention group, 72% in the telephone intervention group, and 80% in the control group had a cesarean delivery) and between the three studied groups there was no statistically significant difference concerning this variable (P = 0.074). Regarding the preeclampsia severity variable, there was no statistically significant difference between the three studied groups in terms of this variable (P = 0.101).

Before conducting intra-group variance analysis (concerning the self-care training group using virtual space) with repeated measurement, the homogeneity of variance and covariance of the variables were checked through Mauchly’s W test. Since the result of the test was P < 0.05, Geisser-Greenhouse test was used.

Discussion

In the present study, the effects of two types of intervention, i.e. virtual and telephone self-care training methods based on the health belief model on the quality of life and its eight dimensions in primiparous women with preeclampsia in the postpartum period were compared.

Based on the results, it was found that the implementation of self-care training through WhatsApp and telephone has a significant difference in the total score of quality of life as well as the eight dimensions of quality of life, i.e. physical functioning, role disorder due to physical health, role disorder due to emotional health, energy/fatigue, emotional well-being, social functioning, pain and general health compared to before the intervention. In this regard, Hu et al.[27] concluded that the scores of physical function, social function, role function, and cognitive function in the intervention group were higher compared to the control group.

Also, in the control group, there was a significant difference in the total score of quality of life, as well as the dimensions of physical functioning, role impairment due to physical health, social functioning, pain and general health compared to before the intervention, and this difference in the control group for the dimensions of role impairment, emotional health risk, energy/fatigue and emotional well-being was not significant. Although intra-group changes were significant for some dimensions of quality of life in the control group, the women of the intervention groups had a higher quality of life than those of the control group, and significant statistical differences were observed in all aspects of the quality of life between the two intervention groups and the control group. It seems that the implementation of the self-care training program using two intervention methods, WhatsApp and telephone, has a significant effect on increasing the quality of life in women with pre-eclampsia in the postpartum period by increasing awareness, improving attitudes and promoting self-care behaviors. In this regard, Joghtaei et al.[28] found that implementing an educational program based on the health belief model by uploading videos and educational materials about pediculosis on social media messengers was effective in promoting preventive behaviors against pediculosis in girls’ students.

In comparison with the present study and in line with the investigation of the effect of self-care education on the quality of life of patients, a study by Noshirvani and Mansouri in 2017 aimed to compare the effects of the educational intervention of the mental health promotion model and the health belief model on the quality of life in the form of a clinical trial on 60 patients with type 2 diabetes.[29] According to the results of their study, the mean (standard deviation) of the quality of life score in the educational intervention group of the health belief model before the intervention was 50.01 (12.22), which increased to 69.94 (12.86) after the intervention. In line with the results of the present study, the results of the Noshirvani and Mansouri study showed that education based on the health belief model was effective in the quality of life of patients with diabetes and caused a significant improvement in the quality of life after the intervention compared to before the intervention.[29] In line with the present study results, Salehnezhad, et al.[30] showed that HBMbased intervention could reduce the level of anxiety in mothers of infants with retinopathy of prematurity. It seems the health belief model helps explain what factors motivate people to perform a particular action.[17] The basis of this model is to motivate people to act, and it emphasizing the individual’s beliefs about people’s fears about a health problem and the individual’s assessment of the benefits and barriers to behavior that become a reason to adopt the correct behavior.[17]

Among other relevant studies regarding the effect of self-care training on quality of life, we can refer to the study by Aghakhani et al.,[12] who investigated the effect of implementing a self-care program based on Orem’s model on the quality of life of 60 patients with heart failure. In this study, the mean (standard deviation) of the quality of life score in the intervention group increased significantly by 19.21 units after three months of intervention, while there was no significant change in the quality of life score in the control group after 3 months of intervention. Therefore, as a result, this study shows an increase in the quality of life in the intervention group. In this regard, Nematzad et al.[31] showed that HBM-based training can have a significant impact on improving self-care behaviors in elderly people with hypertension.

Also, the results of Riazi et al.[32] study showed that nutrition education based on HBM was effective on macronutrient intake and significantly increased the intake of these nutrients.

According to the results of Aghajani et al.’s[33] study in another study which was conducted for comparing the effect of implementing an educational program using two methods of lectures and an educational package on the quality of life of 90 patients with high blood pressure in Kashan city, the average quality of life score in the intervention groups compared to the control group had a significant increase after two months.

Ghodsbin et al.[34] conducted a study to investigate the effect of training health-promoting behaviors in the first six weeks after delivery on the quality of life of primiparous women. 52 primiparous women were randomly divided into two test and control groups, and quality of life using the SF-36 questionnaire in two different groups in the first and sixth weeks after the intervention was measured. The results showed a significant difference between the average score of quality of life before and after the intervention between the two groups, and the average difference of the five dimensions of women’s quality of life in the test group was greater than the control group. The results of this study showed that teaching health-promoting behaviors can be effective in improving the quality of life of primiparous women.

Wang et al.[35] conducted a study with the aim of evaluating the effects of a mobile health application on reducing patient care needs and improving quality of life after oral cancer surgery. Patients with oral cancer after surgery were divided into an experimental group (n = 50) who received the application intervention and a control group (n = 50) who received usual health care and education. The findings of improvement in the quality of life in the experimental group were more significant than those in the control group. In this study, it was concluded that compared to health care and routine instructions, for post-surgery patients, an educational intervention using an application significantly reduced their nursing care needs and improved their quality of life.

In line with the present study, Khazarloo et al.[36] found that both distance learning (instruction via WhatsApp) and reverse learning significantly improved self-care behaviors and quality of life in patients with congestive heart failure. Also, the study by Barkhodhari Sharifabad et al.[37] showed that virtual education improved self-care behaviors in heart failure patients. Similarly, a study by Salavati et al.[38] found that a mobile phone educational application can improve self-care behaviors in patients undergoing percutaneous coronary interventions. Also, Ahmadi et al.,[39] showed that after the intervention, there was no statistically significant difference between the virtual and face-to-face education groups in terms of self-care and quality of life. However, self-care and quality of life were improved in these two groups compared to the control group. Therefore, both virtual and face-to-face education methods had the same effect on self-care and quality of life. If in-person education is not available, virtual education can be used as an alternative to in-person education. Moreover, in this regard Khayat et al.[40] showed that face-to-face education and telemedicine were appropriate methods for teaching self-care to pregnant adolescent women. Hence, they recommended that these methods be used to promote self-care in pregnant adolescent women. Also, Amini et al.[41] concluded that the self-care education programs based on virtual networks are effective in improving the quality of life of HIV-positive patients. These studies show that telephone-based care reduces costs, facilitates access to care, and strengthens communication between patients and healthcare providers, while removing barriers associated with time and location.[36]

One of the strengths of the present study is the virtual nature of education, which can accelerate the process of learning and education according to individual needs at any time and place. As mentioned earlier, no study has been published on the effect of virtual and telephone self-care training based on the health belief model on the quality of life of primiparous women with preeclampsia in the postpartum period.

Limitation and recommendation

Due to the fact that the long-term and continuous follow-up (for example, one-year follow-up) of the effect of educational intervention on the quality of life of mothers was not possible due to the limited time of the study, therefore, only the short-term effect of the interventions was investigated at one month and three months.

Considering the limitations of the studies conducted in the researched field and the importance of improving the quality of life, it seems necessary to conduct similar intervention studies in the field of investigating the effect of self-care education using the health belief model on the quality of life of women with preeclampsia; on the other hand, due to the need to identify the process of studies related to self-care education and the quality of life of women with pre-eclampsia, the methods used by them, to identify research gaps and also to make decisions in the field of effective methods to improve the quality of life of these women, it is recommended that systematic review studies be conducted with focusing on the questions such as what is the quality of life of women with preeclampsia? how is self-care training provided in this group of women? and finally, what is the effect of self-care training on the quality of life of the target group?

Conclusion

The findings of this study showed that educational interventions by telephone and virtual space based on the health belief model can improve the quality of life of women with preeclampsia in all eight dimensions, including physical function, role disorder due to physical health, and role disorder for health, mood, energy/fatigue, emotional well-being, social functioning, pain and general health. Self-care training through increasing patient participation in health promotion is one of the possible ways to improve the quality of life and long term health of women who suffering preeclampsia. in the postpartum period by midwifery staff.

According to the results of this study, it is suggested that including self-care behaviors training based on the health belief model via telephone and messaging networks in the postpartum health care guidelines should be considered by maternal health policy-makers in the health system.

Zero-Programmed-Sphere Keratorefractive Lenticule Extraction: Visual, Refractive, Anterior-Keratometric, and Wavefront Outcomes in a Single-Center Series of 74 Eyes
Authors: Ivan Gabrić, Filip Grulović, Karla Bodakoš, Maja Bohač
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionZero-programmed-sphere keratorefractive lenticule extraction (KLEx), also called cylinder-only KLEx, is an uncommon cylinder-dominated treatment pattern in which the planned spherical correction is 0.00 diopters (D). To our knowledge, no published outcome data characterize this pattern. To address this lack of data, we retrospectively identified all such cases from a prospectively maintained single-center refractive-surgery registry and reported visual, refractive, anterior-keratometric, and wavefront outcomes, using Alpins vector analysis.MethodsThis study is retrospective single-center, single-surgeon case series of consecutive eyes treated with the SCHWIND ATOS femtosecond laser (SCHWIND eye-tech-solutions GmbH, Kleinostheim, Germany) between August 2024 and March 2026 at a programmed sphere of 0.00 D. Cycloplegic refraction was 0.00 D sphere in all 74 eyes (cycloplegic-plano target). The data lock was 31 March 2026, and eyes treated within the 3 months preceding that date were classified as not yet eligible to be included in the study. Inter-eye dependence was assessed using generalized estimating equations (GEE), and refractive stability was studied using a linear mixed model. Corrected distance visual acuity (CDVA) was imputed as uncorrected distance visual acuity (UDVA) when UDVA ≥ 20/20 and CDVA was missing. Complete-case sensitivity was also reported. The full-cohort ideal-outcome convention was determined using the refractive Alpins metric with a nonzero–difference-vector (DV) sensitivity subgroup; a parallel anterior-keratometric Alpins was run on the paired-keratometry subset.ResultsA total of 74 eyes had been treated with the SCHWIND ATOS platform. At data lock, 62 were eligible for the ≥ 3-month follow-up and 37 had completed refractive examinations (analyzed primary cohort; mean age 33 years; mean programmed cylinder − 2.96 ± 1.30 D). UDVA ≥ 20/20 and ≥ 20/25 was achieved in 75.7% (Wilson 59.9–86.6%) and 91.9% of eyes, respectively; 83.8% of eyes were within ± 0.50 D of plano spherical equivalent refraction (SEQ); and 73.0% of eyes had postoperative cylinder ≤ 0.50 D. In terms of the refractive Alpins (side-by-side), the full-cohort correction index (CI) was 1.04, and mean absolute angle of error (|AoE|) was 2.9° versus 5.2° in the nonzero-DV subgroup (n = 21). In the 25-eye paired-keratometry subset, anterior-keratometric Alpins gave a CI of 1.05 and |AoE| of 13.4°, with residual anterior-keratometric DV of 1.12 ± 0.45 D versus refractive DV of 0.44 ± 0.60 D. Among the nine eyes with UDVA < 20/20, four had directly measured postoperative CDVA and five were not eligible for the imputation rule; the paired safety analysis therefore included 32 eyes (28 imputed + 4 directly measured), with complete-case (n = 4) and imputation-based indices 0.98 and 1.25, respectively. The analyzed cohort overrepresented against-the-rule (ATR) and oblique astigmatism (p = 0.002), so axis-fidelity estimates may reflect a more challenging case mix than the full treated population.ConclusionZero-programmed-sphere KLEx produced favorable refractive outcomes in eyes reaching the ≥ 3-month endpoint. The 37/62 completion rate among eligible eyes and the imputation-based safety index limit generalizability. Wavefront changes were exploratory and generally followed the pattern expected after KLEx. The anterior-keratometric and refractive Alpins results diverged substantially, suggesting that future studies should include total corneal vector analysis and direct assessment of intraoperative rotation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40123-026-01447-5.Keywords: Zero-programmed-sphere correction, Cylinder-only correction, Keratorefractive lenticule extraction, KLEx, SCHWIND ATOS, Alpins vector analysis, Anterior-keratometric Alpins

Introduction

Keratorefractive lenticule extraction (KLEx) is a widely used flapless corneal refractive procedure for myopia and myopic astigmatism, with multiple platforms and consistently favorable visual outcomes [1–4]. Published series have largely addressed compound myopic astigmatism, in which a programmed spherical component is combined with cylindrical correction. To our knowledge, the cylinder-only (zero-programmed-sphere) subset of treatments, in which cycloplegic-plano planning targets only the astigmatic component, has not been characterized in the modern KLEx literature, even though it represents a distinct case in which the spherical buffer that absorbs minor axis errors in compound correction is absent and axis fidelity becomes the dominant determinant of refractive outcome. Eye Clinic Svjetlost (Zagreb, Croatia) maintains a prospective refractive-surgery registry that is reviewed periodically for outcome analyses. In one such review we identified a consecutive series of eyes treated with a programmed sphere of exactly 0.00 diopters (D) on the SCHWIND ATOS femtosecond laser platform (SCHWIND eye-tech-solutions GmbH, Kleinostheim, Germany). Given the current lack of published outcome data on this treatment pattern, we elected to characterize this procedure retrospectively from the registry. Accordingly, the analyses reported here are real-world observational data from routine clinical practice, limited by the retrospective nature of the data (notably non-routine repetition of manifest refraction in eyes achieving uncorrected distance visual acuity [UDVA] ≥ 20/20 and incomplete return of patients for the ≥ 3-month follow-up visit at the data lock date). The results are intended as the first published characterization of zero-programmed-sphere KLEx outcomes upon which prospective studies can be based.

A clinically distinct subset of patients have refractive error dominated or fully constituted by cylinder, so the surgical plan can be programmed with no spherical component and the entire correction is delivered cylindrically. At our center, such eyes accounted for approximately 1.5% of refractive candidates over the study period. This treatment pattern places high demands on cylindrical magnitude and axis fidelity, since any residual spherical equivalent refraction (SEQ) or rotational misalignment translates directly into uncorrected refractive error without a spherical buffer.

We report here a single-center retrospective series of cylinder-only KLEx procedures using the SCHWIND ATOS platform, with visual, refractive, vector-analytic, and wavefront outcomes at last available follow-up ≥ 3 months.

Methods

Study Design and Patients

Consecutive eyes treated at Eye Clinic Svjetlost between August 2024 and March 2026 with KLEx using a programmed spherical correction of 0.00 D were retrospectively identified from the clinic's prospectively maintained refractive-surgery registry and reviewed. Inclusion criteria were: preoperative manifest cylinder ≥ 0.50 D; stable refraction based on patient history; preoperative corrected distance visual acuity (CDVA) of 20/40 or better; central pachymetry ≥ 480 µm; and unremarkable corneal tomography. The programmed sphere reflected cycloplegic rather than manifest refraction, and the surgical target was cycloplegic-plano in all eyes. Cycloplegic refraction was uniform across the cohort: cycloplegic sphere was 0.00 D in all 74 eyes, and cycloplegic cylinder equaled the recorded manifest cylinder where manifest cylinder was recorded (73/74 eyes; one eye had a blank manifest-cylinder field but documented cycloplegic and programmed cylinder). The presence of a minor manifest spherical component on non-cycloplegic refraction was interpreted clinically as accommodative in origin because cycloplegic sphere was 0.00 D. Of 74 eyes, 12 (16%) had a manifest spherical component of − 0.50 D or more myopic, but all had cycloplegic sphere of 0.00 D. When a manifest myopic spherical component was present, the programmed sphere was nonetheless set to 0.00 D if cycloplegic sphere was 0.00 D, on the basis that the manifest component was interpreted to be accommodative in origin; programming the manifest myopic sphere in such cases would risk inducing iatrogenic hyperopia and accelerating presbyopia onset. This conservative cycloplegic-plano rule is applied uniformly across all refractive modalities at this center. Exclusion criteria were prior corneal surgery; cataract, retinal pathology; or active ocular surface disease. Four database entries representing duplicate records of two patients were identified during data quality review and removed before analysis, yielding a final cohort of 74 eyes from 48 patients (26 bilateral, 22 unilateral).

The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Eye Clinic Svjetlost (approval number KS-AST24v1) and complied with the Declaration of Helsinki. The requirement for individual informed consent for retrospective use of de-identified clinical data was waived by the Board, while all patients had provided written informed consent for the surgical procedure.

Surgical Procedure

All procedures were performed by a single surgeon (IG) using the SCHWIND ATOS femtosecond laser at the platform fluence parameters previously characterized for this device [5]. Cap thickness was 135 µm in every eye; mean cap diameter was 8.98 ± 0.17 mm. Of the 74 eyes, 73 received a 9.00-mm cap, and one eye received a 7.50-mm cap due to a small cornea (decision of the surgeon at docking). The mean (± standard deviation [SD]) optical zone was 7.24 ± 0.32 mm (Table 1). The programmed cylinder was entered at the spectacle plane in accordance with the default setting of the manufacturer’s software; vertex distance was 12 mm. The magnitude difference between mean manifest cylinder (− 2.82 ± 1.47 D; n = 73/74 recorded) and mean programmed cylinder (− 3.04 ± 1.37 D; n = 74) and the narrower programmed range reflect a prospectively and consistently applied surgeon nomogram: a multiplicative factor of approximately 1.05 was applied to manifest cylinder magnitude, with a programmed floor of − 1.20 D for low-cylinder eyes (2/74 eyes were programmed at the floor; manifest cylinder for these eyes was − 0.50 D and − 0.75 D, respectively). The 1.05× factor reflected the surgeon's pre-study observation of mild undercorrection at higher cylinders on this platform; this lower value avoided sub-platform-minimum programming in low-cylinder eyes. One of the 74 treated eyes had a blank preoperative manifest-cylinder field but a documented cycloplegic cylinder (− 2.50 D) and programmed cylinder; programmed sphere was 0.00 D as in all other eyes. This one eye was included in programmed-cylinder and outcome analyses but is excluded from descriptive statistics on the manifest cylinder. Static cyclotorsion compensation was applied in every case using integrated registration of preoperative pupillometry against the docking image (the platform default; the SCHWIND ATOS does not currently offer intraoperative dynamic cyclotorsion tracking). The lenticule was extracted through a 4-mm superior side-cut incision. Topical antibiotic and corticosteroid drops were tapered over 4 weeks with preservative-free lubricants as needed.

Assessments

Preoperative examinations included manifest and cycloplegic refraction, UDVA and CDVA (decimal, converted to logMAR for analysis), Scheimpflug-based tomography and pachymetry, keratometry device (model MS-39; Costruzione Strumenti Oftalmic [CSO], Florence, Italy), ocular wavefront aberrometry (OSIRIS-T total ocular aberrometer and corneal topographer; CSO; 4-mm pupil), and corneal wavefront aberrometry (model MS-39; CSO; 6-mm zone). Cycloplegic refraction was performed by an ophthalmologist 45 min after double instillation of tropicamide 1% with phenylephrine 2.5%, with 5 min between instillations (routine clinic protocol for adult refractive candidates at this center; cyclopentolate was not used). This combination, applied with double dosing and an adequate waiting interval, produces clinically sufficient cycloplegia in adults in the relevant age range and is the protocol applied consistently across all refractive modalities at our center, including LASIK. When the cycloplegic sphere was 0.00 D or positive, programmed sphere was set to 0.00 D regardless of the manifest spherical component, thereby avoiding iatrogenic hyperopia and preserving accommodative reserve—a conservative rule applied uniformly and not specific to this case series. Postoperative assessments were scheduled at day 1, week 1, and months 1, 3, 6, and 12. Completeness of follow-up at each time point is shown in Fig. 1; the wavefront subset (n = 28) reflects the subset of the primary cohort with paired pre- and postoperative aberrometry data available.

Outcomes, Missing Data Handling, and Statistical Analysis

The primary endpoint cohort comprised eyes with refractive data at last available follow-up ≥ 3 months. Outcomes were tabulated using Waring–Reinstein conventions [6] and Alpins vector analysis [7, 8] and included: (target induced astigmatism (TIA), surgically induced astigmatism (SIA), difference vector (DV), correction index (CI), magnitude of error (ME), and angle of error (AoE), where AoE denotes the signed angle of error and |AoE| its absolute value). The primary Alpins analysis used the full cohort with the ideal-outcome convention (DV = 0 eyes assigned CI = 1.00, AE = 0°); a nonzero-DV subgroup is reported as the sensitivity group. Because CDVA was not routinely repeated as a measurement in eyes with UDVA ≥ 20/20, a prespecified rule imputed CDVA = UDVA beyond week 1 when CDVA data were missing; a complete-case sensitivity analysis is shown in Electronic Supplementary Material (ESM) Table S2. Predictability regressions used ordinary least squares (OLS) regression as the primary method, with generalized estimating equations (GEE; exchangeable correlation) as clustering-adjusted sensitivity (ESM Table S1). Aberration data were analyzed with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests and GEE concordance check. Refractive stability was studied using a linear mixed model with patient random intercept. A parallel anterior-keratometric Alpins vector analysis was conducted on the subset of the primary cohort with paired pre- and postoperative K1, K2, and K2-axis values, using K2 − K1 magnitude and K2 axis as the keratometric astigmatism input (no ideal-outcome substitution applied). Continuous variables are given as the mean ± SD (range in parentheses). The Wilson score method was used to determine the 95% confidence intervals (CIs) on proportions. All analyses were performed using Python 3.11 software (pandas, NumPy, and SciPy packages, and statsmodels Python library).

Ethical Approval

This retrospective single-center case series was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Eye Clinic Svjetlost, Zagreb, Croatia (approval number KS-AST24v1), and complied with the Declaration of Helsinki. The requirement for individual informed consent for retrospective use of de-identified clinical data was waived by the Board. All patients provided written informed consent for the surgical procedure. No individually identifiable patient information is included in this manuscript or its ESM files.

Results

Cohort and Baseline Characteristics

A total of 74 from 48 patients underwent zero-programmed-sphere KLEx (cycloplegic-plano target) at our clinic between August 2024 and March 2026. Eye-level sex distribution was 50 male/24 female eyes; patient-level sex was 32 male/16 female patients. The cohort comprised 37 right and 37 left eyes; mean (± SD) patient age was 32.8 ± 6.9 (range 18–48) years. At the data-lock date (31 March 2026), 62 of the 74 treated eyes were eligible for ≥ 3-month follow-up (the remaining 12 eyes had been treated within the preceding 3 months and were not yet eligible for control at this time point); of these 62 eligible eyes, 37 completed ≥ 3-month refractive examinations (analyzed primary cohort), and 25 were eligible but not seen. Mean preoperative manifest cylinder was − 2.82 ± 1.47 (range − 6.25 to − 0.50) D, based on in 73/74 eyes; mean preoperative manifest sphere was − 0.12 ± 0.28 (range − 1.00 to + 0.50) D in the 42 of 74 eyes with a recorded manifest spherical component, including 12 eyes with manifest sphere ≤ − 0.50 D. By contrast, cycloplegic refraction was uniform across the entire cohort: cycloplegic sphere was 0.00 D in all 74 eyes, and cycloplegic cylinder equaled the recorded manifest cylinder where manifest cylinder was recorded (one eye had a blank manifest-cylinder field but documented cycloplegic and programmed cylinder; see section Surgical Procedure). The zero-programmed-sphere decision was therefore not an inference from missing manifest data but a direct expression of the measured cycloplegic refraction; the apparent manifest myopic spherical component (where present) was interpreted clinically as accommodative in origin because cycloplegic sphere was 0.00 D, consistent with the cycloplegic-plano planning rule (see sections Study Design and Patients and Assessments). Mean preoperative keratometry was K1 43.10 ± 1.56 D and K2 45.49 ± 1.57 D, with mean anterior corneal astigmatism of 2.40 ± 1.20 D. Mean central pachymetry was 547 ± 30 µm. Endpoint-completed eyes (n = 37) and eyes without ≥ 3-month endpoint data (n = 37) were comparable for most baseline characteristics, including age (p = 0.32), manifest cylinder (p = 0.71), SEQ (p = 0.74), pachymetry (p = 0.38), programmed cylinder (p = 0.60), optical zone (p = 0.50), sex, and bilateral status (ESM Table S3). Axis-class distribution differed (p = 0.002): endpoint-completed 57% with-the-rule (WTR)/30% against-the-rule (ATR)/13% oblique versus no-endpoint-data 89% WTR/8% ATR/0% oblique. In a within-cohort comparison, refractive outcomes did not differ between the WTR (n = 21) and ATR/oblique (n = 16) subgroups (UDVA ≥ 20/20 76.2% vs. 75.0%; SEQ ± 0.50 D 90.5% vs. 75.0%; mean |AoE| 3.2° vs. 2.6°; all p > 0.10); the analyzed cohort therefore overrepresented off-axis astigmatism but did not show within-cohort evidence of inferior axis fidelity in that subgroup. Reasons for non-attendance among the 25 eligible-but-not-seen eyes could not be retrospectively confirmed (patients seen at this international clinic typically present once for surgery and follow up locally).

Visual and Refractive Outcomes

At the last available follow-up (n = 37 eyes; n = 9 at month 3, n = 23 at month 6, n = 5 at month 12), 28/37 eyes (75.7%; 95% Wilson CI 59.9–86.6%) achieved UDVA ≥ 20/20 and 91.9% (78.7–97.2%) reached ≥ 20/25; all 37 eyes reached ≥ 20/40 (Fig. 2A). It should be noted that 15.7% of eyes in the full treated cohort had preoperative CDVA worse than 20/20, reflecting the well-recognized tendency for eyes with isolated or high astigmatism to have a degree of best-corrected acuity limitation that is not amenable to surgical correction; in such eyes, postoperative UDVA ≥ 20/20 is by definition unattainable regardless of surgical outcome. Mean postoperative UDVA logMAR was − 0.06 ± 0.12; efficacy index 1.19 ± 0.31. Postoperative UDVA distributed similarly to postoperative CDVA across Snellen levels (Fig. 2b). Nine eyes had UDVA < 20/20; of these, four had directly measured postoperative CDVA and five lacked directly measured postoperative CDVA at the endpoint, with preoperative CDVA also < 20/20 in all five (pre-existing limitation, range 20/25 to 20/30) and postoperative manifest refraction not improving acuity beyond UDVA; these five were not eligible for the imputation rule and were excluded from the paired CDVA/safety analysis (the specific limiting factor was not consistently documented and could not be retrospectively confirmed). The safety-denominator picture (see section Outcomes, Missing Data Handling, and Statistical Analysis) was as follows: of the n = 37 endpoint cohort of eyes, 28 had UDVA ≥ 20/20 and CDVA was imputed as UDVA per the prespecified rule; four had UDVA < 20/20 with directly measured postoperative CDVA; and five had UDVA < 20/20 with no directly measured postoperative CDVA and were not imputed. The paired CDVA/safety analysis therefore included 32 eyes (28 imputed + 4 directly measured); the five non-imputable eyes remained in the n = 37 primary refractive predictability and Alpins analyses. In the 32-eye safety cohort, no eye lost ≥ 1 CDVA line; 12 (37.5%) gained ≥ 1 line. The imputation-based safety index was 1.25 ± 0.24 (Fig. 2c) and the complete-case safety index in the four directly measured eyes was 0.98 ± 0.03 with 0/4 lines lost. Both estimates should be interpreted cautiously because postoperative CDVA was not routinely measured in eyes achieving UDVA ≥ 20/20; the imputation-based index in particular is interpreted as exploratory.

Mean (± SD) postoperative manifest SEQ was − 0.27 ± 0.48 D (against a cycloplegic-plano target a small residual manifest myopia is expected); 83.8% (95% Wilson CI 68.9–92.3%) were within ± 0.50 D and 97.3% (86.2–99.5%) were within ± 1.00 D of emmetropia (Fig. 2d). Attempted-vs-achieved SEQ was linear (OLS slope 1.02, intercept − 0.30 D, R2 = 0.74; Fig. 2e). Mean postoperative cylinder was 0.41 ± 0.55 D; 73.0% (95% Wilson CI 57.0–84.6%) were ≤ 0.50 D and 91.9% (95% Wilson CI 78.7–97.2%) were ≤ 1.00 D (Fig. 2g). Attempted-vs-achieved cylinder had OLS slope 0.88 (R2 = 0.85; ESM Fig. S2). In the subset with multi-time point data, postoperative SEQ showed no detectable drift from month 1 onward (mixed-model slope − 0.007 D/month, p = 0.82, 39 observations from 17 eyes; Fig. 2f). A one-eye-per-patient sensitivity analysis (5000 random samples drawing one eye per bilateral patient) yielded median proportions concordant with the full-cohort values for all primary outcomes (UDVA ≥ 20/20 79.2%, 95% range 70.8–87.5%; UDVA ≥ 20/25 91.7%,95% range 91.7–95.8%; SEQ ± 0.50 D 79.2%, 95% range 75.0–87.5%; cyl ≥ 0.50 D 70.8%, 95% range 66.7–79.2%; |AoE| ≤ 5° 83.3%, 95% range 75.0–87.5%), indicating no material bias from inter-eye correlation in this cohort.

Alpins Vector Analysis of Astigmatism

Alpins outcomes are presented side-by-side under two conventions (Fig. 3; and ESM Fig. S1; Table 1). The full-cohort ideal outcome convention (n = 37; DV = 0 eyes assigned CI = 1.00, AE = 0° [with DV = 0 defined as measured postoperative manifest cylinder of 0.00 D at the 0.25 D measurement increment]) gave CI 1.04 ± 0.41 (95% CI 0.91–1.18) and |AoE| 2.9°, with 30/37 (81.1%; 95% Wilson CI 65.8–90.5%) eyes within ± 5°. The nonzero-DV convention (n = 21, excluding 16 eyes with complete cylinder elimination) gave CI 1.08 ± 0.54 (median 0.94) and |AoE| 5.2°, with 14/21 (66.7%) eyes within ± 5°. The full-cohort convention assigns perfect angular outcomes to the 16/37 (43.2%) eyes with complete cylinder elimination, improving |AoE| by construction; the nonzero-DV convention is the more direct estimator of axis fidelity but over-represents higher-cylinder eyes (TIA 3.02 vs. 2.75 D). Steep-meridian centroids were 1.20 D at 92° (TIA; bootstrap axis CI 79–106°), 0.94 D at 89° (SIA; 69–107°), and 0.28 D at 102° (DV; 85–115°), consistent with WTR predominance and TIA/SIA axis concordance. The numerical DV centroid offset (approx. 10° posterior to TIA/SIA) is directional and hypothesis-generating only: the bootstrap CIs for the three axes overlap substantially with n = 37, so we do not claim a statistically supported rotational offset in residual cylinder. Full-cohort SIA-vs-TIA regression: SIA = 0.79 × TIA + 0.48 (R2 = 0.93, p < 0.001; Fig. 2h).

Parallel Anterior-Keratometric Vector Analysis

We computed a parallel anterior-keratometric Alpins vector analysis using anterior keratometric astigmatism (K2 − K1, steep meridian K2 axis) in place of refractive manifest astigmatism on the 25 eyes of 17 patients with paired pre- and postoperative anterior keratometry (a subset of the 37-eye primary cohort) (Table 2). On this subset, refractive |AoE| was 2.97° (matching the 2.93° full-cohort value), whereas anterior-keratometric |AoE| was 13.4° (signed AoE − 1.9° ± 22.8°); 8/25 (32.0%) of eyes were within ± 5° on the anterior-keratometric measure versus 20/25 (80.0%) on the same-subset refractive measure (and 81.1% on the full 37-eye refractive measure; see section Alpins Vector Analysis of Astigmatism). Anterior-keratometric DV magnitude was 1.12 ± 0.45 D, approximately 2.5-fold the refractive DV in the same eyes (0.44 ± 0.60 D), with an anterior-keratometric SIA (2.03 ± 0.92 D) approximately 0.7 D smaller than the refractive SIA (2.71 ± 1.26 D). Anterior-keratometric centroids (TIA 1.52 D at 89°, SIA 0.71 D at 83°, DV 0.84 D at 94°) showed concordant preoperative meridians with refractive but a larger and differently oriented residual DV vector. The anterior-keratometric–refractive gap exceeds what posterior corneal astigmatism alone is expected to contribute in this age range (typically 0.3–0.5 D, scaling with manifest astigmatism magnitude) [9, 10] and may therefore reflect a combination of contributors discussed in the Discussion section. Because the keratometric analysis here used anterior K values only (K1, K2, K2 axis), based on the data from the present study we cannot directly quantify posterior corneal compensation; a parallel total-corneal vector analysis (incorporating posterior corneal data) would address this directly in future work.

Wavefront Aberrations

Of the 37 primary-endpoint eyes, 28 had paired pre- and ≥ 3-month postoperative aberrometry; the 9-eye gap reflects non-systematic incomplete wavefront capture in the clinic. Wavefront analyses are exploratory (28 eyes, 8 endpoints); after Bonferroni correction (α = 0.00625), corneal spherical aberration shift and ocular total higher-order aberration (HOA) increase remain significant; other findings are nominal. The results of the Wilcoxon and GEE analyses were in agreement (ESM Table S1). Ocular total HOAs (4-mm pupil) increased from 0.16 to 0.22 µm (Δ + 0.06; p = 0.003, GEE); ocular spherical aberration shifted from − 0.03 to 0.00 µm (p = 0.005). Corneal total HOAs (6-mm zone) decreased from 0.50 to 0.41 µm (Δ − 0.09; p = 0.035, GEE), driven by reduced corneal coma (Δ − 0.07, p = 0.013). Corneal spherical aberration shifted toward less negative values (Δ + 0.21, p < 0.001), as expected for myopic corneal subtraction. The ocular (4 mm) and corneal (6 mm) HOA changes were sampled at different apertures and are therefore not directly comparable in magnitude. No intra- or postoperative complications occurred, and no eye underwent enhancement.

Discussion

Based on date from this retrospective series of 74 eyes, we report here outcomes of KLEx with zero-programmed spherical correction. Visual and refractive results compare favorably with published compound-myopic-astigmatism KLEx series on the same and other contemporary platforms [1–4], with 92% of eyes achieving UDVA ≥ 20/25 and 84% eyes within ± 0.50 D of plano SEQ. The mixed-model test confirmed refractive stability from month 1 onward, supporting the pooled ≥ 3-month endpoint. Interpretation of the 20/20 rate requires consideration of the preoperative CDVA ceiling: 15.7% of eyes in the full treated cohort had preoperative CDVA worse than 20/20, consistent with the well-recognized tendency for eyes with pure or high astigmatism to carry a degree of amblyopia or best-corrected acuity limitation that surgical correction cannot resolve. When assessed against this baseline, the postoperative UDVA distribution compares favorably with published compound myopic astigmatism KLEx outcomes on contemporary platforms.

Varman and colleagues compared KLEx outcomes for compound myopic astigmatism between the VisuMax and VISUMAX 800 platforms (Carl Zeiss AG, Oberkochen, Germany) at 3 months [11]: UDVA ≥ 20/20 was achieved in 94% (VisuMax) and 98% (VISUMAX 800) of eyes, with 96% of VISUMAX 800 eyes within ± 0.50 D of the intended SEQ versus 71% for the VisuMax. Bteich and colleagues reported 1-year CLEAR results on the Ziemer FEMTO LDV Z8 platform (Ziemer Ophthalmic Systems AG, Brügg, Switzerland) in a compound-myopic-astigmatism cohort: 96.7% of eyes achieved UDVA ≥ 20/20, mean postoperative SEQ was − 0.04 ± 0.26 D at 12 months, and 95.6% and 100% of eyes were within ± 0.50 D and ± 1.00 D of emmetropia, respectively [4]. Correction indices reported in contemporary KLEx series under the ideal-outcome convention have been reported across a range that includes our full-cohort CI of 1.04 (median 1.00; 95% CI 0.91–1.18). Direct platform-to-platform CI comparison should be interpreted with caution given the differing inclusion criteria, sample sizes, and conventions; the 95% CI on our full-cohort value (0.91–1.18) is wide relative to platform-comparator differences typically reported, and a definitive comparison would require a larger sample.

The limitations of this study are directly related to interpretation. Of the 74 treated eyes, 62 were eligible for the ≥ 3-month follow-up at the data-lock date (31 March 2026); 37 completed the endpoint, and 25 were eligible but not seen (true completion rate of 37/62 = 60% among eligible eyes; the 12 not-yet-eligible eyes were treated within the preceding 3 months). ESM Table S3 shows axis-class case-mix differences between the cohort that completed the endpoint and the cohort for which there were no endpoint data (57% WTR/30% ATR/13% oblique vs. 89%/8%/0%, p = 0.002). ATR and oblique astigmatism were therefore over-represented in the analyzed cohort, so axis fidelity estimates may reflect a more challenging case mix than the full treated population. A within-cohort outcome-by-axis-class analysis (section Cohort and Baseline Characteristics) did not detect inferior outcomes in the ATR/oblique subgroup, but the small subgroup sizes (n = 21 WTR vs. n = 16 ATR/oblique) limit power for this comparison. The retrospective single-center, single-surgeon, single-platform design of the present study precludes separation of platform-specific contributions from surgeon-specific contributions (cyclotorsion alignment, nomogram). Refractive stability was tested in 39 observations from 17 eyes; absence of detected drift does not preclude pooling-related heterogeneity in the broader cohort. No safety signal was detected, but postoperative CDVA was directly measured in only 4 of 32 paired eyes; the imputation-based safety index (1.25) should be interpreted as exploratory, while the complete-case index (n = 4) was 0.98. The axis-related findings (sections Alpins Vector Analysis of Astigmatism andParallel Anterior-Keratometric Vector Analysis) warrant comment as hypothesis-generating observations. In cylinder-only treatment, axis fidelity is the dominant determinant of refractive outcome; 7/37 (18.9%) full-cohort eyes had refractive |AoE|> 5° (7/21, 33%, nonzero-DV). The anterior-keratometric versus refractive |AoE| divergence (13.4° vs. 2.97° in the same 25-eye subset; sectionParallel Anterior-Keratometric Vector Analysis) and the parallel DV-magnitude divergence (1.12 vs. 0.44 D) together point to a meaningful gap between the anterior-keratometric and refractive measures of cylinder change. Reported posterior corneal astigmatism magnitudes in refractive-age cohorts average approximately 0.3–0.5 D and scale with manifest astigmatism magnitude (MA) (approx. 0.5 D for our cohort's mean MA) [9, 10]; this accounts for part but not all of the gap. Among the remaining contributors—intraoperative cyclotorsion between docking and laser delivery, refraction-versus-keratometry axis-measurement variability, stromal/epithelial healing not captured by K readings, and subjective–objective discordance—we tentatively rank the refraction-versus-keratometry axis-measurement variability as the most plausible single contributor based on the parallel finding in the Xu et al. analysis [12], but this ranking is not supported by direct measurement in our cohort. The role of intraoperative dynamic cyclotorsion compensation in cylinder-dominated KLEx merits prospective, comparator-controlled investigation. Separately, the SIA-vs.-TIA slope (0.79; Fig. 2h) and cylinder-predictability slope (0.88; ESM Figure S2) suggest mild undercorrection at higher cylinders, with vertex conversion at − 6.25 D (approx. 7%) as a partial structural contributor; the mean per-eye CI of 1.04 reflects central tendency near unity, while the regression slope of 0.79 reflects a magnitude-dependent gradient—relative undercorrection at higher TIA values, masked by slightly overcorrected low-cylinder eyes in the central tendency.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. It is a retrospective, single-center, single-surgeon, and single-platform study, which precludes separation of platform-specific from surgeon-specific contributions and limits generalizability. Of the 74 treated eyes included in the study, only 37 of the 62 eyes eligible at the data-lock date (60%) completed ≥ 3-month refractive follow-up, and the analyzed cohort overrepresented against-the-rule and oblique astigmatism (p = 0.002), so the axis-fidelity estimates may reflect a more challenging case mix than the full treated population. Postoperative CDVA was directly measured in only four of 32 paired eyes, so the imputation-based safety index (1.25) should be regarded as exploratory and the complete-case index (n = 4) was 0.98. The keratometric analysis used anterior keratometry only and could not quantify posterior corneal compensation, and intraoperative cyclotorsion was not directly measured. The wavefront and axis-related findings are accordingly hypothesis-generating and require confirmation in larger, prospective, comparator-controlled studies using total corneal vector analysis and direct measurement of intraoperative rotation.

Conclusion

To our knowledge this is the first published characterization of zero-programmed-sphere KLEx, drawn retrospectively from a prospectively maintained single-center registry. In this real-world series, zero-programmed-sphere KLEx on the SCHWIND ATOS platform based on cycloplegic-plano planning produced favorable visual and refractive outcomes among eyes completing ≥ 3-month follow-up, suggesting that cylinder-only correction is a viable refractive option in eyes meeting the cycloplegic-plano criterion. Completion was 37/62 (60%) among eyes eligible at the data-lock date (31 March 2026). ATR and oblique astigmatism were over-represented in the analyzed cohort, which may limit generalizability and may reflect a more challenging case mix than the full treated population. Safety estimates were constrained by imputation because postoperative CDVA was not routinely measured in eyes achieving UDVA ≥ 20/20 (imputation-based index 1.25; complete-case n = 4 = 0.98)—a known limitation of real-world refractive-surgery data, where routine clinical workflow does not repeat manifest refraction in patients seeing unaided 20/20. The difference between anterior-keratometric and refractive Alpins results reported here should be interpreted cautiously, but it gives a clear target for prospective work using total corneal vectors, posterior corneal astigmatism, and intraoperative rotation measurements.

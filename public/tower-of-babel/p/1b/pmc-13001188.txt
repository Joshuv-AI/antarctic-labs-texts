Scaling up our commitment to equity in health: learnings, challenges and new directions
Authors: Pablo Gaitán-Rossi, Daniel Maceira, Ana Lorena Ruano
Published: 2026

Since its founding in 2002, the International Journal for Equity in Health (IJEqH) has been committed to showcasing knowledge that expresses the complex causes and manifestations of inequity [1]. The IJEqH has served as a global forum for original, rigorous, and open-access science aimed at sharing sound research, exposing new findings produced worldwide, and devoted to understanding and reducing health inequities within and across countries. In this endeavor, Prof Daniel Maceira and Dr Pablo Gaitán-Rossi joined Dr Ana Lorena Ruano in the senior editorial board in 2024 as Editors-in-Chief. We wish to reaffirm and update the journal’s mission and editorial direction.

Our objective is to bring more relevant, innovative, and influential content to an increasingly diverse audience, while remaining firmly anchored in the principles that have defined the IJEqH since its inception. We are proud to share that our journal metrics continue to grow steadily, and we are amongst the top journals in the Public, Environmental & Occupational Health JCR category, even as the journal grows in submissions. Going forward, we will continue to focus on our core values of equity, inclusion, and diversity, and how they will be expressed as new theories, approaches, methods, and perspectives that respond to the rapidly evolving landscape of public health research, health systems analysis, implementation science, policy debates, and methodological innovation. Our goal is to remain a most reliable journal for high-quality health equity research.

Equity as our central commitment

For us, equity is much more than the name of our journal: it is a beacon that guides our decisions at every level of our work. The journal has and will continue to publish research that explicitly engages with equity as both an analytic lens and a normative goal. For this, we call on authors to prioritize analyses and interpretations that unmask health inequities, that examine their social and structural drivers, evaluate public policy interventions as well as individuals and private sector behaviors, and their implications at reducing health gaps.

Unfair and avoidable differences in health outcomes remain among the most persistent and pressing challenges worldwide, reinforcing deep economic, social, political, and environmental inequities [2]. Despite substantial advances in health innovations and biomedical science, health care and social systems have not been usually able to compensate income disparities [3]. Health inequities are produced and sustained by social dynamics that marginalize specific groups while concentrating advantage among others [4]. For these reasons, we are particularly committed to showcasing research that illuminates how systems of power, discrimination, and exclusion shape health outcomes. We look forward to studies addressing inequities related to gender, race, ethnicity, socioeconomic position, religion, caste, sexual orientation, gender identity, legal or migration status, disability, and other forms of social stratification that meaningfully affect health. We also recognize that these dimensions often intersect, generating compounded and context-specific forms of disadvantage. Equity-focused research should not only describe disparities but also contribute to comprehending why they exist, and which public or social interventions may help to reduce them.

We therefore value work that connects empirical findings to broader questions of social justice, and that critically examines the structures and processes through which inequities are reproduced or challenged. We appreciate the use of theoretical frameworks and engagement from the social sciences and the humanities to help to translate the complex and wicked ways in which inequity limits the capacity to enjoy the right to a healthy life.

Scientific rigor as our publishing standard

Scientific rigor will continue to be the most important criterion for publication in the IJEqH. Research on health equity often carries significant academic, ethical, and policy implications, and thus must be grounded in robust, transparent, and well-executed methods.

We do not privilege any single research design or methodological tradition. Experimental and quasi-experimental studies, observational analyses, quantitative or qualitative research, mixed methods, participatory designs and other methods used in the social sciences and the humanities are all welcome, as long as they are clearly documented, theoretically grounded, and properly executed. Likewise, we are open to multiple other approaches, including critical epidemiology, case studies, health economics and priority setting studies. These act as tools for learning and acting on inequities.

We choose high-quality reviews with a clear methodology that make complex evidence accessible to policymakers, practitioners, civil society, and other audiences beyond academia. We expect authors to articulate assumptions explicitly, report limitations honestly, and avoid overstating conclusions, particularly when drawing policy implications. Whenever possible, we encourage the use of publicly available data, transparent analytic workflows, and reproducible processes. By upholding high standards across diverse methodologies, we aim to ensure that equity-oriented research published in the IJEqH is both credible and influential, capable of informing scholarly debates as well as real-world decision-making.

Diversity in voices, contexts, and perspectives

Diversity is another of the core values of the IJEqH. Health inequities manifest differently across settings, and grasping them requires perspectives from across regions, disciplines, and lived experiences. We accept submissions from high-, middle-, and low-income countries and strongly encourage diverse research teams in terms of discipline, gender, race, and country of residence. When research is conducted in a specific local context—or when findings directly pertain to a particular country or population—we expect that local researchers are meaningfully included as authors. This expectation reflects not only a commitment to equity in knowledge production, but also to scientific quality, contextual relevance, and ethical research practice.

As Editors-in-Chief, the “international” in our journal’s name does not simply denote geographic diversity. Rather, it reflects our expectation that published research speaks to a global audience. Local studies are highly valued, but they must engage with broader equity-related questions that resonate beyond a single setting. Strong manuscripts use local contexts as lenses through which larger structural problems, mechanisms, or policy challenges can be understood, and clearly articulate why their findings matter for readers working in different contexts.

New directions for a rapidly evolving field

While our core values and principles remain, new challenges as well as novel methods become relevant to study and address inequalities. To include new realities and to support new views, the IJEqH must continue to evolve.

Equity is increasingly recognized as a multi-, inter-, and trans-disciplinary concern. While the social determinants of health remain foundational to the journal, the scope of equity-related research has expanded considerably. The IJEqH continues to receive a growing number of submissions addressing topics as broad as nutritional status and food systems, mental health epidemiology, substance use and exposure to violence, life-cycle lines of care and emerging health care topics, financial protection and incentive mechanisms, environmental health, health and human rights, and the health impacts of climate change, health tech and Artificial Intelligence (AI) applied to health management, among others. These topics reflect pressing global challenges and the complex realities shaping population health. We appreciate this diversification and encourage authors to continue pushing thematic boundaries. Equity-related challenges do not fit neatly within disciplinary silos, and neither should the research that seeks to address them.

Strengthening policy relevance

Producing high-quality evidence is essential, but advancing equity in health also requires engagement with changes in needs and behaviors, challenges in governance and regulations, innovation in the policy-making process and moving global, national and subnational institutional realities. The IJEqH will place increased emphasis on the policy relevance of the research it publishes. For this, we seek studies examining social and financial protection systems and models of care, health care networks organizations and public-private coordination, intersectoral cooperation, and health system innovations with the potential to improve equity.

We are particularly interested in research that helps translate scientific evidence into actionable and measurable policies and programs. This includes formative research for program design, case studies identifying needs, strategic implementation and policy impact, implementation science studies on improving delivery and scaling, and experimental or quasi-experimental evaluations of interventions targeting equity outcomes. We recognize that policy-relevant research often requires methodological pluralism and close engagement with real-world constraints. We therefore encourage authors to reflect critically on the complexity of health systems´ environments, and the conditions under which evidence can meaningfully inform action.

Encouraging methodological and conceptual innovation

Persistent equity challenges demand new perspectives. To provide a platform for evidence-based debates to overcome those challenges, the IJEqH actively encourages innovative and forward-looking research designs that revisit complex problems using novel conceptual frameworks, data sources, or analytic approaches.

We are particularly interested in work that addresses and goes beyond causal understanding through the use of theory-driven designs, such as the explicit use of causal diagrams, directed acyclic graphs, or systems-based approaches. We also favor creative and ethical uses of emerging data sources, including administrative records, geospatial data, digital platforms, and other forms of large-scale data. Similarly, we encourage submissions that illustrate the potential of new analytic methods, such as machine learning or advanced modeling, while maintaining transparency, interpretability, and relevance for equity-focused questions.

We also recognize the growing role of AI tools in research and scientific writing. The journal fosters their innovative, transparent, responsible and rigorous use when appropriate, both in methodological applications and to improve grammar, clarity, and readability. In line with Springer Nature’s policies, AI tools cannot be listed as authors, and any use should be properly disclosed. Authors remain fully responsible for the integrity, accuracy, and originality of all content, and any unethical, undisclosed, or misleading use of AI that contravenes publisher policies will be treated as a breach of editorial ethics and may lead to rejection or retraction.

Increasing visibility and engagement

Academic and scientific publishing takes place within a rapidly changing ecosystem with new platforms for dissemination and debate on social networks. As part of a community of researchers and policymakers, we are committed to sustaining and to promoting high quality publications, as well as to maximize the reach and impact of the work we publish, increasing visibility and engagement. Our editorial strategy will sustain the usual channels to engage scientific publications, following our traditional priorities while taking advantage of these opportunities to broaden our audience. We recognize that advancing equity in health requires not only producing knowledge but also ensuring that it reaches those positioned to act on it.

In addition, we will keep encouraging the development of article collections devoted to address timely and pressing topics, introducing new research techniques and theories, as well as promoting comparative analysis across regions or in-depth within-region debates. We are open to discuss proposals and eager to engage with diverse guest editors that bring innovative ideas to the journal.

An open invitation and guidance to authors

The International Journal for Equity in Health enters this new chapter with a renewed commitment to rigor, relevance, diversity, and innovation. We invite researchers, practitioners, and policymakers from around the world to submit work that challenges assumptions, exposes injustices, and contributes to more equitable and effective health outcomes. However, we must remind our authors that submitted manuscripts need to engage directly with equity in health. For instance, studies describing the geographic distribution of health services using mapping or GIS tools—without hypotheses to be tested in terms of how these patterns reflect, reproduce, or mitigate health inequities—fall outside the journal’s remit.

Methodological rigor is of utmost importance to us and authors should endeavor to go beyond simply including the superficial or automatic application of commonly used analytic techniques. Manuscripts that employ standard tools, such as concentration indices, without adequate justification, interpretation, or critical reflection cannot be considered. Rigor requires more than technical correctness; it demands thoughtful alignment between the research question, the analytic approach, and explicit discussion of assumptions and limitations. We value null findings and research that challenges dominant assumptions and look to avoid overly general or predictable conclusions that add little to the existing evidence base. This includes reiterating that disadvantaged groups experience worse outcomes and that better policies are needed. Conclusions should advance knowledge, introduce nuance, or meaningfully question prevailing frameworks in health equity research.

As a continuous commitment to methodological transparency, we favor narrative, scoping, and systematic reviews that demonstrate clear methodological grounding and explicitly articulate how they advance theory, methods, or policy, and a clear link to health equity. Comments that engage critically, systematically, and transparently with current debates, are appreciated. These should deal with novel or persistent issues around equity in health and deepen our awareness of systemic barriers and facilitators, how they are built in time and space, and develop theory for future use.

In conclusion, our new senior board will maintain our core principles of equity and inclusion, and we continue to focus on wide social science approaches. These serve as a jumping off point for new methods and ideas, and ensure we focus on producing actionable research that can inform policy at the local, regional, and global levels, and strengthen health systems. The International Journal for Equity in Health is a forum where equity-focused science can thrive and where evidence can meaningfully inform action to reduce health inequities globally.

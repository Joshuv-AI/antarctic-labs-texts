Brief communication: comprehensive HIV/AIDS knowledge and its associated factors among women of reproductive age in Tanzania
Authors: Mtoro J Mtoro, Elihuruma Eliufoo Stephano, Sanun Ally Kessy, Jovin R Tibenderana, Victoria Godfrey Majengo, Erick Donard Oguma, Tegemea Patrick Mwalingo, Immaculata P Kessy, Mussa Hassan Bago, Azan Abubakar Nyundo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWomen of reproductive age (WRA) face the greatest burden of the HIV/AIDS epidemic in low- and middle-income countries, including Tanzania. Comprehensive HIV/AIDS knowledge is vital for effective prevention. Using the 2022 Tanzania Demographic and Health Survey and Malaria Indicator Survey we found that only 15.9% of WRA possessed comprehensive HIV/AIDS knowledge. Higher knowledge was associated with marital status, education, literacy, employment, wealth index, using internet, sexually transmitted infections awareness. However, increase in age associated with lower odds. These findings highlight the critical need for targeted intervention emphasizing digital platforms and continued education to enhance knowledge among WRA in Tanzania.Keywords: Comprehensive HIV/AIDS knowledge, Reproductive aged women, Tanzania

Background

In Tanzania, women of reproductive age (WRA) are disproportionately affected with Human deficiency Virus (HIV), exhibiting higher prevalence rates (5.6%) compared to men (3.0%) [1]. Recent data from the Tanzania HIV Impact Survey indicate persistent geographical and age-related disparities, with regions such as Mbeya, Iringa, and Njombe demonstrating elevated prevalence and new infections [1, 2]. Previous studies have shown that factors including educational level, age, wealth index and media exposure are linked to acquiring HIV infections and limited knowledge about HIV/AIDS (Acquired Immunodeficiency virus) can also contribute to new HIV infections [3, 4].

Tanzania is classified as a lower-middle-income country where WRA often encounter socioeconomic challenges that could impact health outcomes. Most women live in rural settings and derive their livelihoods primarily from agriculture and informal sectors [5]. Educational levels vary, with low levels being common in rural settings impacting health literacy [6]. HIV/AIDS healthcare is primarily provided through the public health service system, and typically ranges from dispensaries, which may have limited infrastructure to more equipped regional hospitals [7]. This varied public health landscape, and the prevailing socioeconomic conditions are crucial contextual factors for understanding the health-seeking behaviors and comprehensive HIV/AIDS knowledge among WRA in Tanzania.

Comprehensive knowledge about HIV/AIDS, encompassing accurate understanding of transmission, prevention, and rejection of misinformation is vital for prevention and treatment [8]. WRA are key to epidemic control, their knowledge directly influences their health behaviors, testing, and treatment adherence, all vital for avoiding vertical transmission and curbing population-level incidence [9]. However, evidence from Tanzania and Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA) indicates that comprehensive HIV knowledge among WRA remains suboptimal, with disparities by age, education and wealth index [4, 10]. A recent study in SSA reported that only 38.6% of women had comprehensive knowledge and it was associated with older age, primary education and above, living in wealthy households, contraceptive use and living in urban setting [4]. These knowledge gaps hinder testing uptake, risk reduction and adherence, ultimately affecting epidemic control and progress toward the 95-95-95 targets [10]. Given that knowledge is a modifiable factor, understanding its distribution and determinants can inform the development of tailored interventions in educational programming, policy prioritization, and resource allocation [8].

Spatial and demographic variations in recent HIV impact surveys underscores the need for localized analyses to guide targeted interventions [1]. Using the 2022 Tanzania Demographic and Health Survey and Malaria Indicator Survey (TDHS-MIS) data, this study examines comprehensive HIV/AIDS knowledge and its associated factors among WRA in Tanzania. Findings aim to inform polices and program to strength HIV prevention and control.

Materials and methods

Data source, design, population and sampling

This study was an analytical cross-sectional survey that utilized secondary data from the 2022 TDHS-MIS.​ The Tanzania National Bureau of Statistics conducted the survey with the Ministries of Tanzania Mainland and Zanzibar in collaboration with other stakeholders. The 2022 TDHS-MIS was conducted between 24 February to 21 July 2022 across all regions in Tanzania. The target population for the 2022 TDHS-MIS included WRA (15–49 years), men, children and households. Specifically, this study focused on WRA across the 32 administrative regions in Tanzania. The detailed methodology of the TDHS-MIS has been explained elsewhere [11]. We used the individual file with 15,254 women.

Study variables

Dependent variable

The outcome variable of the study was comprehensive knowledge about HIV/AIDS, defined as a composite measure based on responses to five ‘yes/no’ statements: (1) Consistent condom use during sex reduces the risk of HIV infection; (2) Having a single, uninfected, and monogamous partner reduces the risk of HIV; (3) A healthy-looking person can have HIV; (4) HIV can be transmitted through mosquito bites; and (5) HIV can be transmitted by sharing food with someone who has AIDS. Women were considered to have comprehensive knowledge if they answered all five statements correctly, responding “YES” to statements 1, 2, and 3, and “No” to statements 4 and 5 [4, 12, 13].

Independent variables

We included the following variables based on the available data and literature; women’s age, education level, marital status, literacy level, wealth index, media exposures, number of household member, working status, sex of household head, internet use, visited health facility in the past 12 months, visited by community health worker in the past 12 months, ever tested for HIV, ever heard of Sexually transmitted Infections (STIs) and place of residence.

Statistical analysis

Data management and analysis were performed using STATA 18 (STATA Corp, College Station, TX). To account for the complexity of the TDHS-MIS design, we applied individual sampling weights, accounted for clusters, and used stratification to produce representative estimates. Descriptive statistics summarized continuous and categorical variables. Weighted binary logistic regression assessed factors associated with comprehensive HIV/AIDS knowledge. Univariate analyses estimated crude odds ratios (COR), and variables with p < 0.05 were included in the multivariable model to obtain adjusted odds ratios (AOR). We compared modified Poisson regression and logistic regression using the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), given that logistic regression may overestimate association when the outcome is not rare (> 10%). However, the logistic regression model was chosen as it yielded the lowest AIC, indicating the best model fit. Multicollinearity was assessed using variance inflation factor (VIF). The mean VIF was < 10 indicating no significant multicollinearity. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Results

Sociodemographic characteristics and prevalence of comprehensive HIV/AIDS knowledge

The overall mean age was 29.3 years, 38.1% were aged 15–24 years. Over half (53.3%) had attained primary education and 79.8% were literate. Nearly half (48.0%) were from rich household and 64.35 were working. Majority (80.5%) had ever tested for HIV and 78.15 were aware of STIs. The overall prevalence of comprehensive HIV/AIDS knowledge was 15.9% (95%CI: 15.1–16.8). (Table 1)

Discussion

This study aimed to understand the prevalence of comprehensive HIV/AIDS Knowledge and its associated factors among WRA in Tanzania. This study found that only 15.9% of WRA had a comprehensive knowledge about HIV/AIDS. We found that higher educational level, women’s age, literacy, working status, internet use, ever heard STIs and ever tested for HIV were significantly associated with comprehensive knowledge about HIV/AIDS. The prevalence of comprehensive knowledge was relatively low compared to other studies in SSA []. The variations observed in this study compared to prior studies may be attributed to the differences in socio-economic and socio-cultural characteristics of women across countries, as well as the timing of the DHS. Additionally, the presence of various strategies, policies, interventions and initiatives such as behavior changes programs aimed at enhancing comprehensive HIV knowledge may also explain these differences.

Women with higher education level had higher odds of comprehensive HIV/AIDS knowledge. Our finding is consistent with previous studies in SSA [4, 12]. Women with higher education have better access to accurate information regarding HIV/AIDS than their counterparts. Similarly, literate women exhibited higher odds of comprehensive HIV/AIDS knowledge. Literate individuals are better equipped to comprehend written health materials or public health campaigns [14]. Their ability to read and understand health messages facilitates the acquisition of accurate HIV/AIDS knowledge. Increase in age was associated with lower odds of comprehensive HIV/AIDS knowledge in contrast to previous studies elsewhere []. Younger women might have had exposure to more recent and updated school-based education, engagement with modern media and internet platforms, which often disseminate health information [15, 16].

Internet use has emerged an increasingly determinant for comprehensive HIV/AIDS knowledge. Internet provides an expansive and open source of diverse information on HIV/AIDS from reliable sources, with access to online health forums and education channels [17]. Our findings underscore the importance of using internet platforms appropriately to disseminate accurate information about HIV/AIDS. Women who are aware of STIs are likely to have received sexual health education that encompasses safer sex practices and HIV transmission [18]. More importantly, women who have ever tested for HIV typically receive pre and post-test counselling sessions that provide essential and comprehensive information about HIV transmission, prevention which fills them with enough knowledge about HIV/AIDS [19].

Strengths and limitations of the study

The use of nationally and population representative data from the 2022 TDHS-MIS, the large sample size, and relatively high response rate improve the generalizability of our findings to a wide population of WRA in Tanzania. Since the survey relies on self-reported information, there’s a possibility of under or over reporting triggered by recall and socio desirability biases, which could affect the validity of our findings, particularly given the sensitive nature of HIV-related information.

Conclusion

This study revealed a low prevalence of comprehensive HIV/AIDS knowledge among WRA in Tanzania, with only 15.9% possessing such knowledge. Factors significantly associated with higher comprehensive HIV/AIDS knowledge included higher educational attainment, literacy, working status, and internet use, alongside prior awareness of STIs and having been tested for HIV. Conversely, increased age was associated with lower odds of comprehensive HIV knowledge. These findings highlight the critical need for targeted interventions to improve HIV/AIDS education, leveraging digital platforms and prioritize early, continuous learning to better inform WRA in Tanzania.

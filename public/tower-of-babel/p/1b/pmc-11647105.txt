Treatment of recalcitrant discoid lupus erythematosus with roflumilast 0.3% cream following suboptimal response to anifrolumab and improvement of hypopigmentation
Authors: Kareena S Garg, Leonardo A Tjahjono
Published: 2024

Introduction

Discoid lupus erythematosus (DLE) is a common type of chronic cutaneous lupus erythematosus. It is an autoimmune condition that presents as erythematous, scaly plaque(s) that can progress to permanent scarring and hyper and hypopigmentation; it is typically photodistributed.1 Treatment options include aggressive ultrapotent topical steroids, topical calcineurin inhibitors, topical Janus kinase inhibitors, antimalarial agents, and other aggressive immunosuppressants.1 We present a case of recalcitrant DLE who improved with roflumilast 0.3% cream after a suboptimal response to anifrolumab infusion.

Case report

A woman patient in her 60s with past medical history significant for severe systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) presented with numerous pruritic, burning, scaly, erythematous, atrophic, hypopigmented papules, and plaques on the face, scalp, and ears (Fig 1, A). Biopsy showed interface dermatitis and periadnexal lymphocytic infiltrates, supportive for the diagnosis of DLE. Because of poor control of her SLE and DLE, despite a combination of twice a day 200 mg hydroxychloroquine, 20 mg weekly methotrexate, aggressive sun protection, and clobetasol 0.05% ointment, her methotrexate and hydroxychloroquine were discontinued. The patient was started on 300 mg anifrolumab infusion every 4 weeks. Her symptoms and erythema improved after 1 infusion. However, the improvement observed after 1 infusion plateaued for her DLE despite 5 months of treatment (Fig 1, B). Her joint pain from her SLE have resolved, but the erythema and pruritus persisted, despite concomitant application of alternating tacrolimus 0.1% ointment and clobetasol 0.05% ointment for 5 months. Off-label use of roflumilast 0.3% cream once-a-day from samples was started concomitantly with continued monthly anifrolumab infusion. The tacrolimus and clobetasol ointment were discontinued when the roflumilast 0.3% cream was started. She noted gradual improvement of the pruritus and complete resolution of the erythema and symptoms after 8 weeks of application, along with significant improvement in hypopigmentation (Fig 1, C). The resolution was sustained at the 8-week follow-up visit after the last photograph was taken and no adverse event was noted throughout the treatment duration.

Discoid lupus erythematosus (DLE) that presented as scaly, erythematous, atrophic, hypopigmented papules, and plaques on the face, scalp, and ears (A) that improved after 1 month infusion of anifrolumab, but remains significantly pruritic and slightly erythematous despite 5 months of treatment (B). Resolution of the DLE and improvement in hypopigmentation after 8 weeks use of roflumilast 0.3% cream (C).

Discussion

Roflumilast 0.3% cream is Food and Drug Administration–approved for psoriasis. This case highlights the efficacy of roflumilast 0.3% cream for DLE after suboptimal response from anifrolumab infusion. Previous reports showed rapid resolution of DLE, with majority of cases showing significant improvement within 3 months of initiation of anifrolumab infusion (range: 1-5 months).2, 3, 4, 5, 6 Anifrolumab efficacy likely plateaued for our patient. Adding another immunomodulatory believed paramount to prevent permanent scarring and to control the symptoms. One case reported a mild improvement of facial and scalp DLE with concomitant use of roflumilast 0.3% cream and oral prednisone, hydroxychloroquine, thalidomide, intralesional corticosteroids injection, and betamethasone ointment; however, hypopigmentation persisted at the end of the treatment course.7

Phosphodiesterase type 4 is recently thought as a key player in the pathogenesis of related autoimmune diseases such as SLE.8 Phosphodiesterase type 4 immunomodulates cyclic AMP, which regulates the differentiation of T helper 17 and 22 immune cells that release interleukin 17; these cells and cytokines are often central in development of autoimmune diseases such as SLE.8,9

Roflumilast broad effect that includes downregulation of interleukin 17 release suggests the reason for the successful treatment of DLE with roflumilast 0.3% cream. Roflumilast enhances melanogenesis and decrease H2O2-induced apoptosis of melanocytes, which may explain the rapid repigmentation in our patient’s postinflammatory hypopigmentation.10 This may help selecting topical agents for patients that develop significant postinflammatory hypopigmentation from other inflammatory conditions including atopic dermatitis and psoriasis. Although promising, additional controlled studies are needed to evaluate if roflumilast 0.3% cream is a safe and effective treatment for DLE.

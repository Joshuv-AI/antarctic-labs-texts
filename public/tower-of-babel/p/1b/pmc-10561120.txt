Real-time online monitoring technology for sweeping frequency ultrasound (SFU) assisted extraction of amur grape (Vitis amurensis) seed oil
Authors: Lei Zhang, Ao Zhang, Shanshan Zhou, Qianqian Wang, Wen Wang, Haile Ma, Cunshan Zhou
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Oil yield was improved by 8.65–10.21% using sweeping frequency ultrasound (SFU).•Oil extraction time was shortened by 83.33% using SFU assisted method.•Acoustic field was monitored online in time and frequency domains.Keywords: Sweeping frequency ultrasound, Amur grape seed oil, Online monitoring, Physical model

Introduction

Nutritional values of amur grape (Vitis amurensis) are high, including seeds. The wild amur grape is a dioecious species, mostly cultivated for wine production in China [1]. More than 50,000 tons of amur grapes are consumed every year for wine making and other industries [2]. In the production of amur grape wine, amur grape seeds (AGS), which account for 75 % of the total lees, will be produced [3]. It is the hardiest species within the genus Vitis and can tolerate temperatures as low as −40 °C [4], [5]. However, a lot of AGS are wasted after processing, which causes the environment pollution. If AGS can be utilized, not only can the environmental burden be reduced, but also the additional values are improved [6]. According to relevant data, the oil content of AGS is as high as 14 %-20 % [7]. Its unsaturated fatty acid content is as high as 86 % [7]. As a high nutritional value edible oil, AGS oil is favored in the international market and currently sells for $ 8–10/kg [8]. If such a large number of AGS are not fully used ‚ it will not only waste resources, but also pollute the environment. So the research on AGS oil is meaningful. Traditional oil extraction methods are mainly organic solvent extraction [9], physical expression [10] and enzyme treatment [11]. However, the organic solvent extraction speed is slow, and the solvent has a certain degree of toxicity, and it's not safe [12]; physical expression of oil production cycle is longer than other methods of oil extraction, and its production process is relatively cumbersome and low production efficiency; the enzyme treatment of fats and oils process is simple with low energy consumption, which can reduce the loss of nutrients, but the cost of enzyme preparations is higher [13], the enzyme dissolution time is longer, and the extraction process about the formation of the mixed emulsion of oil, water and proteins need complex equipment to be able to be separated. Moreover, AGS hardness is high, the extraction rate is low but with long time, so it is necessary to develop a green efficient extraction of AGS oil.

Ultrasound assisted extraction, as an effective non-heat treatment processing method, is widely used in food processing, biotechnology and other areas with its environmental-friendly characteristic [14]. Ultrasound assisted extraction can enhance the mass transfer efficiency between materials and the oil extraction rate because of the cavitation effect in the medium [15], [16]. According to the working modes, ultrasound can be divided into fixed frequency ultrasound (FFU, Frequency fixation) and sweeping frequency ultrasound (SFU, Frequency fluctuation). FFU is a traditional ultrasonic method, mainly by setting different frequencies, power, time to continuously process the material. Because of the special way that FFU works, the energy generated in the liquid continuously impinges on the material. As a result, the way that FFU works exacerbates the rancidity and oxidation of the oil [17]. Compared with FFU, SFU belongs to a kind of periodic pulsed mechanical waves which fluctuates up and down with amplitude of ±δ around the central frequency α [18]. The mutual shear between molecular components are more obviously caused to enhance the intermolecular mass transfer efficiency by ultrasonic cavitation and mechanical effects in liquid medium, and SFU has a wider spectrum and stronger acoustic signals [18]. The key is that the interval of pulse time of SFU can avoid accelerated oxidation of oil, which is better than continuous FFU. At the same time, the frequency of SFU fluctuates alternately up and down at a specific frequency, avoiding the rapid oxidation of oil caused by ultrasonic under the same continuous frequency. Therefore, SFU assisted extraction technology is more suitable for processing hard and difficult materials.

Furthermore, it is necessary to optimize SFU assisted extraction technology of AGS oil based on the theoretical basis of cavitation pulsation behaviors. In this research, PVDF piezoelectric sensor and hydrophone were introduced in time and frequency domains during SFU assisted extraction processing, and the ultrasonic cavitation pulsation behavior can be monitored online by oscilloscope and spectrum analyzer; hence, the spatial peak acoustic intensity and spectrum distribution were deduced, which goes a step further than our previous research [19] and provides a theoretical basis for the industrial intelligent production of ultrasound assisted oil extraction.

SFU assisted extraction determination of AGS oil

Optimization of SFU power density

A single-frequency SFU with a center frequency α of 40 kHz and a power of 600 W was selected to assist extraction of AGS oil. The intermittent ratio of SFU was 2:1, the sweeping period was 50 ms, and the sweeping amplitude δ around α was ± 1 ms. Sifted AGS powders and n-hexane were thoroughly mixed with solid-to-liquid ratio of 10:1 (V W−1), placed in the conical flask, and the SFU vibration plate, that is, ultrasonic transducer, was located at the bottom of the conical flask. Then, 6, 12, 18, 24 and 30 L of water were added into the ultrasonic reaction tank to obtain the corresponding power densities of 20, 25, 33, 50 and 100 W L-1, respectively; and the total time of SFU assisted extraction was 45 min. Next, the extract was centrifuged at 4000 r min−1 for 10 min, the supernatant was taken and concentrated by rotary evaporation. And Finally, the obtained oil was weighed and the oil yield X7 (%) was calculate by Eq. (7) [25].

where, m1 is the mass of AGS powders (g), m2 is the mass of extracted AGS oil (g).

Optimization of SFU assisted extraction time

Based on the aforementioned optimized SFU power density (Section 2.3.1), the extraction time of SFU was further optimized. The extraction time of AGS oil was set as 2, 3 and 4 h by using organic extraction (n-hexane), single-frequency SFU (40 kHz) and dual-frequency SFU (28/33 kHz) assisted extraction, respectively. Multi-frequency SFU assisted extraction time was 15, 30 and 45 min, respectively.

Optimization of SFU frequency

Based on the optimal SFU power density and SFU extraction time determined in 2.3.1, 2.3.2 above, AGS powders and n-hexane were mixed (solid–liquid ratio of 10:1 V W−1), and the multi-frequency SFU with center frequency of 24, 28, 33, 40, 68, 24/28, 24/33, 24/40, 24/68, 28/33, 28/40, 28/68, 28/68, 33/40, 33/68, 40/68 kHz was used to assist extraction of AGS oil, respectively. Then, SFU assisted solution was put into a high-speed centrifuge (LD5-2A, Beijing Jingli Co., LTD, China), the rotational speed was adjusted to 4000 r min−1 for 10 min. After the evaporation of the rotary evaporator (RE-52A, Shanghai Yarong Biochemical Instrument Factory, China), the oil was obtained, and the oil yield of extraction was calculated.

Acoustic field monitoring during SFU assisted extraction process of AGS oil

Construction of ultrasonic field monitoring system

On the basis of optimization in Section 2.3, the AGS solution was configured with solid–liquid ratio of 10:1 V W−1 in the ultrasonic tank. Meanwhile, PVDF piezoelectric sensor and hydrophone (8103, Bruel & Kjær Sound & Vibration Measurement, Denmark) were put into the solution. The effective area of PVDF piezoelectric sensor was 10 × 10 mm, the thickness was 30 μm and the sensitivity was 2 × 10-8 V Pa−1. A 50 Ω resistor was connected in parallel to ensure the stability of noise signal acquisition. Thus, the conversion of pressure signals to electric ones was measured in ultrasonic field. PVDF piezoelectric sensor monitored waveform of ultrasonic field online by oscilloscope (MDO3024, Tektronix, Inc., USA) in the time domain. The hydrophone monitored image online by real-time spectrum analyzer (RSA306B, Tektronix, Inc., USA) in the frequency domain [26]. Optimization of SFU assisted extraction of AGS oil

When the power density of SFU is 25 W·L-1 in Fig. 2B, the oil yield is the highest. Our previous research was also reported that when okra was pretreated by 60 ± 1 kHz/30 min SFU with the power density of 25 W·L-1, the drying time was 44.83 % shorter than that without ultrasonic pretreatment, and the extraction yield of pectin was higher up to 16.70 % [18]. This is because when the power density increases, the more cavitation involved in the pulsation, the more obvious the ultrasonic effect, and thus the higher the extraction yield. However, when the power density exceeds the appropriate range, the cavitation bubbles involved in the pulsation will be consumed quickly, or a larger cavitation cloud is generated, which hinders the ultrasonic propagation and weakens the effect of ultrasonic field.

Table 1 shows that after SFU assisted extraction with the power density of 25 W·L-1, the oil yield is higher than that by traditional organic extraction, which is related to the cavitation effect produced by ultrasonic waves in liquid. Ultrasound increased the diffusion coefficient and reaction rate of the extracted material [36], and the oil yield was relatively high [37]. Organic extraction was optimal at 3 h; although the oil yield increased after 3 h, the increasement was small and the time was very long. Meanwhile, in both cases of single- and dual-frequency, the optimal oil extraction time was 30 min, because the long-term ultrasonic effect may gradually reduce the bubbles and weaken the cavitation effect. At the same time, the extraction yield of AGS oil almost reaches saturation at 30 min, so the extraction time over 30 min is unreasonable.

Fig. 2C shows the optimal frequency with the power density of 25 W·L-1 for 30 min, and the oil yield is the higher as the single-frequency is 40 kHz and the dual-frequency is 28/33 kHz. In the single frequency state, when the ultrasonic frequency is less than 40 kHz (24–40 kHz), the cavitation intensity increases with the increase of frequency, and the oil yield gradually increases, which is consistent with Ref. [19], but at 33 kHz, the oil yield is slightly reduced, which may have the same effect as ultrasonic non-selective destruction is related, which affects the oil yield [38]. In the single frequency state, when the ultrasonic frequency is more than 40 kHz (40–68 kHz), the oil yield decreases significantly. This may be the reason why cavitation not only breaks the cell wall, but also destroys the molecular structure of the extracted substance under high frequency conditions, resulting in a decrease in oil yield. In the dual-frequency state, there is no regular change between the ultrasonic frequency and the oil yield, which may be related to the simultaneous operation of two different ultrasonic frequencies. When the two ultrasonic waves interact with each other, it is possible to have the effect of superposition and enhancement, so that the resonance frequency is gradually closed to that of the material itself, and the oil yield becomes high. It may also be related to the attenuation of interference caused by the interaction of the two waves, as described below, in which case the cavitation effect may also be weakened, and thus the oil yield will be reduced. Based on the overall trend, the oil yield of dual-frequency was higher than that of single-frequency, because the cavitation effect was more obvious due to dual-frequency. When two ultrasonic waves propagate simultaneously, the ultrasonic effect is enhanced synergistically, the uniformity of ultrasonic action is enhanced, and the sound intensity is increased, which makes the boundary layer of AGS thinner, the outer surface spalling, even the particles fragmenting, and the mass transfer efficiency is greatly improved with the increment of oil yield. However, oil yield by some dual-frequency is lower than that by the single-frequency, which may be related to the mutual interference attenuation of two ultrasonic waves, weakening the cavitation effect.

Equivalent electric field during SFU assisted extraction of AGS oil

Moreover, PVDF piezoelectric sensor exerts the strongest work on electric field force at 30 min (Fig. 3A), this is consistent with the conclusion mentioned in Section 3.2, that is, the oil yield is the highest at 30 min. That is because the cavitation effect is the most obvious at 30 min, PVDF piezoelectric sensor receives the most significant pressure signals, and the voltage amplitude value is the highest in this period (Fig. 3B), and the force the charge receives in the electric field is proportional to the intensity of the electric field. Voltage can be considered as the work done by the electric field force on the unit charge, so the voltage and the intensity of the electric field are also proportional. Therefore, when the SFU time is 30 min, the amount of charge and the electric field force do relatively strong work, and the above-mentioned equivalent circuit diagram (Section 2.4.2) is shown in Fig. 3C.

Model of acoustic field during SFU assisted extraction of AGS oil

Real-time ultrasonic time-domain monitoring was carried out for 15 min, 30 min and 45 min at the optimal 40 kHz single-frequency and 28/33 kHz dual-frequency, and voltage amplitudes at different times are shown in Fig. 3B. It was found that the voltage amplitudes of single-frequency and dual-frequency at 30 min were higher than those at 15 min and 45 min. Before 30 min, the voltage amplitudes gradually increased and decreased after 30 min which was related to the behavior of cavitation bubbles. The voltage amplitude had a similar trend with the change of the oil yield. With the increment of time, the cavitation effect and mechanical effect became more and more obvious. However, after a certain period of time, the cavitation bubbles were consumed, the cavitation intensity began to weaken, and the pressure on PVDF piezoelectric sensor decreased with the decrement of the voltage amplitude.

PVDF piezoelectric sensor can convert pressure signals to electrical ones, the strength of cavitation effect directly affects the measured pressure by the sensor. Moreover, the strength of the cavitation effect is closely related to that of the ultrasonic field. The spatial peak acoustic intensity in Fig. 3B is directly proportional to the voltage amplitude, and the voltage amplitude displays the spatial peak acoustic intensity at this point. It can be seen that the spatial peak acoustic intensity of dual-frequency is higher than that of single-frequency, and it is the highest at 30 min. It was found that the work done by the electric field force and the spatial peak acoustic intensity in this experiment were proportional, and both related to the voltage. In addition, it can be seen from the voltage waveform diagram, as shown in Fig. 3E, at stage of 30 min, the first peak of voltage amplitude reaches firstly; what's more, the cavitation effect is faster and more obvious.

Oxidation performance of AGS oil

Acid and peroxide values

The acid value of AGS oil was a symbol of the content of free fatty acids and an important indicator of quality. Generally, it is expressed in milligrams of potassium hydroxide required to neutralize free fatty acids per gram of oil. The freer fatty acids of oil, the higher the acid value with poor quality. As shown in Fig. 4A, the acid value of organic extraction was higher than that of SFU. This may be due to the relatively long organic extraction time, resulting in longer contact time with oxygen. It is also possible that the processing environment may be influenced by microorganisms and other causative factors that cause high acidity in organic extracted fats and oils. The energy generated by ultrasound due to the cavitation effect can effectively reduce bacterial growth and interference, so this may be another reason for the high acid price when extracting with organic solvents [40], [41]. Therefore, with the extension of storage time, the content of stearic acid and saturated fatty acid of the oil increased, while the content of linoleic acid, linolenic acid and unsaturated fat acid decreased, and the acid value increased. The acid value processed by 28/33 kHz dual-frequency was higher than that by 40 kHz single-frequency, which may be due to the higher effects of cavitation and mechanics at 28/33 kHz compared to 40 kHz. When the cavitation bubbles evolved from germination to rupture, the released local high temperature and pressure accelerated the oxidation degree of the oil [42], but the number and intensity of cavitation bubbles in 40 kHz were lower than those in 28/33 kHz, so the acid value of 28/33 kHz was higher than that of 40 kHz.

Oxidation performance and structure of AGS oil. (A) Acid value and peroxide value, (B) The aniside value and saponification value, (C) Color analysis, (D) DPPH free radical scavenging rate and anthocyanin content, (E) FT-IR, (F) Rheological properties.

The peroxide value refers to the content of peroxide of oil, which is one of the quality indexes. Peroxide is the product of oxidation between oil and oxygen in the air during storage. Peroxide value follows the same law as the acid value of oil, that is, the highest value by organic extraction (Fig. 4A). After ultrasound, including SFU, the peroxide value is lower and the oxidation stability is higher [43]. Due to long time extraction and high-temperature extraction environment, the oil was enabled to be oxidized rapidly and the generated hydroperoxide was decomposed into aldehydes, ketones, and oxides gradually. At the same time, the oil rancidity would be affected by microorganisms and light irradiation for a long-time extraction. The peroxide value at 28/33 kHz was higher than that at 40 kHz, because of the high temperature and pressure generated by bubble rupture, which has the same conclusion as the factors affecting acid value.

Anisidine values and saponification values

The anisidine value indicates the content of aldehydes, ketones, quinones, and other secondary products of oil. The order of anisidine value in different groups from large to small is organic extraction, 28/33 kHz, 40 kHz in Fig. 4B, and that the long-time of organic extraction, high temperature of the extraction environment, microbial contamination and other reasons may lead to an increase content of the impurity of oil with reduced quality and increased degree of secondary oxidation. The cavitation intensity at 28/33 kHz was higher than that at 40 kHz, and high content of secondary products, such as aldehydes, ketones and quinones were induced at 28/33 kHz.

The saponification value of the oil represents the consumed amount of potassium hydroxide (mg) by alkaline hydrolysis of 1 g oil. The level of saponification value indicates the molecular weight of fatty acids of oil. Higher saponification value means the smaller molecular weight of fatty acids and stronger hydrophilicity, and the characteristics of oil are lost. The lower the saponification value, the greater the molecular weight of fatty acids or contain more unsaponifiable matter; and the oil is closed to solid and difficult to be injected and absorbed. In Fig. 4B, the saponification value of the oil by organic extraction is the lowest, and it may be due to its long-time extraction time and high contains of impurities, such as aldehydes, ketones and quinones, resulting in larger molecular weight of oil and more difficult decomposing. The oil was viscous, and less easily absorbed or consumed by the human body. There was no significant difference in the saponification value between 28/33 kHz and 40 kHz. The significant results of color differences by different extraction methods are shown in Table 2 and Fig. 4C; generally, the lighter and clearer the color of oil, the higher the corresponding quality. Because within a certain range, the higher the temperature and the longer the time, the greater the color change of oil, and it may also mix with starch, protein, pigments, and so on, resulting in turbidity and darkening of the color of the oil. However, the saponification value of SFU assisted extraction was higher than that of organic extraction. This may be related to the energy and the effect of mechanics and cavitation of ultrasound, which made molecules of oil sheared and decreased the viscosity of oil. When the shear force of ultrasound on oil molecules reached a certain degree, and ultrasonic intensity continued to be strengthened, so the molecular splitting approached the limit value. Therefore, the saponification values at 28/33 kHz and 40 kHz were relatively similar.

DPPH free radical scavenging rate

The DPPH free radical scavenging rate can be used to evaluate the oxidative stability of oil. Generally, the higher scavenging rate corresponds to a stronger antioxidant capacity. In Fig. 4D, the DPPH radical scavenging rate is the lowest without SFU with lower antioxidant capacity. The SFU assisted extraction time was not as long as organic extraction, thus its oil was not excessively oxidized or decomposed. However, this did not mean ultrasound cannot affect the oxidative properties of oil. Particularly, when the ultrasonic intensity reached a certain critical value, the degree of oxidative deterioration began to exceed the critical value and gradually decreased. Therefore, the DPPH free radical scavenging rate at 40 kHz was higher than that at 28/33 kHz.

Structure of AGS oil

From FT-IR (Fig. 4E), the basic structure of AGS oil is slightly different by extraction methods. The absorption peak at 1747 cm−1 corresponded to the stretching vibration of the C = O esterification group of the methyl esterified carboxyl group and —COOH group [35]. Rheological properties of AGS oil

Fig. 4F shows the relationship between viscosity and shear rate by different extraction methods. It can be observed that the oil viscosity decreased with the increment of shear rate, and the relationship between viscosity and shear rate was non-linear, so it could be called pseudoplastic fluid [18]. Before the shear rate was 20 s−1, the apparent viscosity decreased rapidly as shear rate increased, when the shear rate was greater than 20 s−1, the viscosity tended to be stable and close to constant. This may be due to the fact that shear force affected the structure of the oil molecules during the shearing process. With the increment of shear flow velocity, the molecular structure was disrupted by the shearing stress between the layers, and the binding forces between the molecules reduced, resulting in a decrement of apparent viscosity. But when the molecules existed independently of each other, the binding forces were negligible, and the apparent viscosity was no longer changed.

In addition, the oil viscosity by SFU assisted extraction was significantly lower than that by the organic extraction. Within a certain period, the apparent viscosity of the oil was reduced by ultrasonic treatment [45]. This was because the greater the cavitation intensity, the more certain elements of oil were degraded by ultrasonic waves, resulting in a more significant decrement of apparent viscosity. However, organic extraction had a long extraction time and low efficiency, which led to an increment of impurities during the extraction or storage of the oil, resulting in higher viscosity. Moreover, the oil viscosity of 28/33 kHz dual-frequency was lower than that of 40 kHz single-frequency, because of stronger acoustic intensity and more obvious cavitation at 28/33 kHz. As the shear rate continued to increase, the cavitation bubbles of oil almost disappeared, and the nucleus of cavitation bubbles began to shrink. When the pulsation process was less likely to occur, the degree of cavitation naturally decreased until it disappeared. Therefore, the viscosity was no longer changed and remained constant, which was similar to the conclusion drawn from the distribution of acoustic intensity and voltage under different extraction methods in the previous experiment.

Anthocyanin content and microstructure of residual AGS

Anthocyanins are highly effective antioxidants and the most powerful free radical scavengers. The extracted anthocyanin content can be indirectly reflected by measuring AGS residue after oil extraction. From Fig. 4D, the highest anthocyanin content of the AGS residue is extracted by organic extraction, indicating that the extracted anthocyanin is the least. The traditional organic extraction method had a long extraction time, low production efficiency, and the use of hot solvents caused the degradation of anthocyanins and physiological activity. Moreover, the residual anthocyanin content after SFU extraction was lower than that after organic extraction, indicating SFU treatment can increase the content of anthocyanins of AGS oil. The anthocyanin content of AGS residue at 40 kHz was significantly higher than that at 28/33 kHz, which indirectly indicated that the anthocyanin content of AGS oil extracted by 28/33 kHz dual-frequency was higher than that by 40 kHz. This may be because dual-frequency ultrasound utilized the characteristics of different frequency ultrasonic waves to eliminate standing waves and make the acoustic field more homogeneous. Therefore, the appropriate combination of ultrasonic waves can not only increase the oil yield, but also increase the content of anthocyanins.

From SEM in Fig. 5, the microstructure of AGS residue is seriously damaged after SFU assisted extraction (Fig. 5C ∼ D) and the particle size of AGS became smaller than that after organic extraction (Fig. 5B) and even the raw state (Fig. 5A). It was worth noting that the degree of damage to AGS residue at 28/33 kHz dual-frequency was higher than 40 kHz single-frequency, because of the stronger cavitation intensity and mechanical strength at 28/33 kHz.

For industrial applications, Dual-frequency ultrasound extraction of AGS oil is suitable for industrial mass production, but cooling measures can be utilized during dual-frequency ultrasound assisted extraction of AGS oil, thus the transient high temperature energy generated by ultrasound can be avoided. Single frequency ultrasound is suitable for small-scale production and requires relatively good oil quality production lines.

Conclusions

The oil content of AGS was more abundant than CGS, and the oil obtained by organic extraction had high acid value and peroxide value, making it easy to oxidize and rancidity. The AGS oil extracted by dual-frequency ultrasound was easier to be oxidized than that extracted by single-frequency ultrasound, but the oil yield increased. AGS oil was a non-Newtonian fluid, and its viscosity decreased due to SFU extraction. The cavitation effect, spatial peak acoustic intensity and voltage amplitude at 28/33 kHz dual-frequency ultrasound were higher than 40 kHz single-frequency, which was more conducive to the destruction of AGS structure, promoting the extraction of oil. Ultrasound treatment also increased the content of anthocyanin especially for dual-frequency ultrasound.

Unraveling the unique flavor characteristics and bioactive properties of Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar: a comparative study with the four famous vinegars of China
Authors: Dixinxin Yang, Zegang Li, Jiajia Song, Huayi Suo, Yu Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractChina's Four Famous Vinegars are recognized as industrial benchmarks and have been broadly studied. In contrast, the systematic quality characterization of Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar, a regional traditional Chinese vinegar, remains largely unexplored. We herein established a systematic comparative quality evaluation strategy by integrating high-performance liquid chromatography, gas chromatography–mass spectrometry, in vitro antioxidant assays, and multivariate statistical analysis. The results demonstrated that Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar exhibits the highest total phenolic content, with chlorogenic acid and vanillic acid as the main differential phenolics among the nine targeted analytes, and showed comparable DPPH and ABTS radical scavenging activities to benchmark vinegars. Furfural was the top differentiating volatile with characteristic abundance in PMFV, and ethyl butyrate along with 18 other odor-active compounds (odor activity value >1) are the main contributors to its aroma profile. The coordinated composition of organic acids and free amino acids forms its relatively balanced taste-related chemical profile.Keywords: Solid-state fermented vinegar, Volatile compounds, Phenolic compounds, In vitro antioxidant activity

Introduction

Vinegar is a traditional fermented food consumed worldwide and is valued not only as a widely used condiment but also as a source of diverse bioactive compounds, including acetic acid, polyphenols, flavonoids, and amino acids. These constituents contribute to its reported health-promoting properties, such as antioxidant activity, gut microbiota modulation, and metabolic regulation (Chen et al., 2016; Jansone et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2025). China has a vinegar-making history spanning more than 3000 years and possesses a remarkable diversity of vinegar types, which has evolved from differences in raw materials, regional climatic conditions, and indigenous microbial communities. Among these, the Four Famous Vinegars of China, Shanxi aged vinegar, Zhenjiang aroma vinegar, Baoning vinegar, and Yongchun aged vinegar, are widely recognized as industrial benchmarks because of their standardized production processes, stable batch-to-batch quality, and distinctive regional flavor characteristics. Consequently, these vinegars have been the primary focus of most existing studies on vinegar flavor chemistry and bioactive functionality (Jansone et al., 2025). Shanxi aged vinegar is typically produced from sorghum through solid-state layered fermentation followed by prolonged aging (Wu et al., 2012). Zhenjiang aroma vinegar is manufactured from glutinous rice through Aspergillus oryzae-mediated alcoholic fermentation, followed by a solid-state acetic acid fermentation stage (Hua et al., 2025). Baoning vinegar is produced using wheat bran and medicinal starter cultures via a solid-state continuous fermentation process (Liu et al., 2022). Yongchun aged vinegar is prepared from glutinous rice and red yeast rice and subsequently aged in sealed traditional earthenware jars (Wei et al., 2025).

Beyond these well-characterized commercial varieties, many regional vinegars with distinctive local production practices remain insufficiently studied. Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar, a traditional wheat bran-based vinegar originating from southern Sichuan Province, represents one such example and has considerable yet largely unexplored research potential. This vinegar uses wheat bran as its primary raw material, distinguishing it from sorghum-based Shanxi aged vinegar and glutinous rice-based Zhenjiang aroma vinegar. While retaining the fundamental characteristics of traditional solid-state fermentation, its production involves a distinctive multistep process in which fermented substrate from a previous batch is reused as a microbial inoculum. This substrate is layered with fresh raw materials and newly prepared qu starter, facilitating stable microbial succession and promoting the targeted accumulation of fermentation metabolites. Subsequent aging in locally produced earthen jars further facilitates mild oxidative and esterification reactions, thereby contributing to the development of its characteristic flavor profile (Lin et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2022). Wheat bran, the primary fermentation substrate, is naturally rich in dietary fiber, B vitamins, and various phytochemicals (Kalita et al., 2026). Previous studies on wheat bran-based vinegars have shown that fermentation can transform these native nutrients into more bioavailable metabolites, thereby enhancing the functional properties of the final product, including improved antioxidant capacity and lipid metabolism regulation (Bai et al., 2024; Yuan et al., 2025). Despite this promising functional potential, systematic investigations of the flavor compounds, bioactive components, and health-related properties of Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar remain limited. Particularly, a comprehensive comparative evaluation of its key quality attributes with those of China's Four Famous Vinegars has not yet been reported.

To address this research gap, a multidimensional comparative analytical framework was employed to systematically compare Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar with China's Four Famous Vinegars. The primary objectives of this study were to characterize the physicochemical properties, volatile and nonvolatile metabolite profiles, and in vitro antioxidant activities of these vinegar samples, and to identify the key differential chemical markers and potential functional attributes of Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar. This study is expected to enrich the quality evaluation system of traditional Chinese vinegars and provide a reference for the quality improvement and industrial development of regional characteristic vinegars.

Materials and methods

Sample preparation of vinegars for analysis

The vinegar samples comprised the Four Famous Vinegars of China, which were procured from supermarkets or local producers within their regions of origin, whereas the Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar was obtained directly from its producer, the Yuanshun Vinegar Factory (Pingshan, Sichuan, China). For each variety, three bottles of finished product from the same production batch were used for analysis. Specifically, Shanxi aged vinegar was sourced from Qingxu in Shanxi Province, Zhenjiang aromatic vinegar from Zhenjiang in Jiangsu Province, Baoning vinegar from Langzhong in Sichuan Province, and Yongchun aged vinegar from Yongchun in Fujian Province. The Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar tested in this work was a one-year aged finished product. All samples were hermetically sealed and stored at 4 °C immediately upon receipt, and all analytical experiments were completed within 60 days of sample collection to avoid compositional changes during storage.

Determination of non-volatile compounds

Basic physicochemical indexes

The pH was measured using a PHS-320 meter (Fangzhou Technology, Chengdu, China). The titratable acidity was determined by titration with 0.1 mol/L NaOH and expressed as acetic acid percentage (Chen et al., 2022).

Organic acids

The determination of organic acids was conducted following a previously reported protocol with some modifications (Chen et al., 2022). Vinegar samples were centrifuged at 9370×g for 20 min, and the resulting supernatant was subsequently collected, diluted 1.5-fold with 0.01 mol/L sodium dihydrogen phosphate solution (pH 2.8), and then filtered through a 0.22-μm membrane before subjecting to HPLC analysis.

The organic acid content in vinegar samples was determined by an HPLC system (Ultimate 3000; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) equipped with a C18 column (250 mm × 4.6 mm, 5 μm, Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). The column temperature was maintained at 40 °C. The mobile phase A was composed of 0.01 mol/L sodium dihydrogen phosphate solution (pH 2.8), and mobile phase B was composed of 100% methanol. An isocratic elution was employed with a constant ratio of 97:3 (v/v, A:B). The flow rate was set to 0.8 mL/min, the UV detection wavelength was set to 210 nm, the injection volume was set to 20 μL, and the total run time was 18 min.

Quantification was performed using the external standard method with 8 organic acid authentic standards (purity ≥98%). All analytes showed good linearity with correlation coefficients (R2) ≥ 0.999.

Amino acids

The free amino acids were determined by following the method of Wei et al. (2025) with some modifications. Briefly, vinegar samples were mixed with absolute ethanol and centrifuged at 1789×g for 5 min. The mixture was then derivatized with phenylisothiocyanate and triethylamine. The resulting derivatized solution was filtered through a 0.22-μm membrane for instrumental analysis.

Analysis was conducted on a HPLC system (LC-20A; Shimadzu Corporation, Kyoto, Japan) using C18 column (250 mm × 4.6 mm, 5 μm), column temperature: 35 °C, mobile phase A: 0.05 mol/L sodium acetate solution (pH 6.5), mobile phase B: methanol–acetonitrile–water (2,6,2, v/v), flow rate: 0.8 mL/min, UV detection wavelength: 254 nm, injection volume: 20 μL. The elution program was as follows: 0 min: 5% B, 7 min: 65% B, 39 min: 49% B, 40–50 min: 100% B, 51–60 min: 5% B.

21 free amino acids were quantified via the external standard method using a mixed amino acid reference standard (individual purity ≥99%). Amino acids exhibited satisfactory linearity with correlation coefficients (R2) ≥ 0.99.

Determination of volatile compounds

Volatile compounds were determined using headspace solid-phase microextraction GC–MS (HS-SPME-GC–MS) as per a previous study with some modifications (Wei et al., 2025). Then, a 1-mL vinegar sample was placed into a headspace vial, to which 3 μL methyl heptanoate (internal standard, 17.58 g/L) was added. The vial was sealed and incubated at 50 °C for 20 min to allow for equilibration. Subsequently, the SPME fiber was exposed to the headspace of the vial, and extraction was performed at 50 °C for 30 min.

Determination was performed on the GCMS-2010 system (GCMS-2010; Shimadzu, Japan) equipped with a DB-5MS capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm). Helium was used as the carrier gas at a flow rate of 1.0 mL/min, with a split ratio of 10:1 and an injector temperature of 250 °C. The initial temperature was 40 °C, which was maintained for 5 min, raised first to 90 °C at 2 °C/min, then increased to 230 °C at 10 °C/min and held for 10 min, and, finally, maintained for 10 min. The MS conditions were as follows: ion source temperature 230 °C, transfer line temperature 250 °C, electron ionization (EI) mode with an ionization energy of 70 eV, and scan range 33–450 amu (m/z). The identification of each volatile compound was performed by comparing its MS data with the NIST mass spectral library. The quantitative determination of volatile compounds was conducted by the ratio of their peak areas to that of the internal standard (methyl heptanoate) (Wei et al., 2025).

Volatile compounds were identified by comparing their EI-MS fragmentation patterns with the NIST17 mass spectral library. For subsequent analysis, only compounds exhibiting similarity indices (SI) exceeding 80% were retained. Molecular identities were further confirmed by comparing gas chromatographic retention indices (RI) of volatile compounds, which were calculated from a series of n-alkanes (C8–C20) under identical conditions and cross-referenced with published literature values. Semi-quantitative concentrations of volatile components in vinegar samples were determined using methyl heptanoate as the internal standard (IS).The odor activity value (OAV) was calculated using the formula: OAV = C/OT, where C denotes the concentration of volatile flavor compounds, and OT represents the odor threshold. This parameter characterizes the contribution of volatile substances to the overall flavor profile of the samples (Wei et al., 2025).

However, it should be noted that the semi-quantitative approach based on normalization using an internal standard may be affected by differences in extraction efficiencies during HS-SPME and variations in detector response factors among volatile compounds. Accordingly, the data obtained represent relative abundances and are intended for comparative analysis rather than absolute quantification.

Determination of phenolic compounds

The total phenolic content was determined using the Folin-Ciocalteu method. Vinegar samples were diluted tenfold with absolute ethanol, ultrasonicated for 10 min, and then filtered. Subsequently, 6 mL of deionized water and 1 mL of 1.0 mol/L Folin–Ciocalteu reagent were added to the mixture. After a reaction interval of 6 min, 4 mL of 10.6% (w/v) sodium carbonate solution was introduced. The solution was then incubated for 60 min, diluted to a fixed final volume, and the absorbance was recorded at 760 nm.

The determination of phenolic acids was conducted according to Bai's method, with some modifications (Bai et al., 2024). For the extraction, vinegar samples were combined with 1 mL of ethyl acetate and 0.5 mL of acetonitrile, vigorously vortexed for 10 s, and centrifuged at 9370×g for 5 min. The organic supernatant was collected, and the extraction procedure was repeated twice. The combined organic phases were then gently evaporated to dryness under a stream of nitrogen at room temperature. The resulting residue was reconstituted in 1000 μL of methanol before HPLC injection.

Chromatographic separation was achieved using an HPLC system (LC-20 A; Shimadzu Corporation, Kyoto, Japan) fitted with a C18 column (250 mm × 4.6 mm, 5 μm, Agilent Technologies) maintained at 30 °C. The mobile phase was composed of two acetic acid/acetonitrile/water mixtures: phase A (1,100:800, v/v/v) and phase B (1,500:400, v/v/v), delivered at a constant flow rate of 1.0 mL/min. Detection was performed at 280 nm with an injection volume of 10 μL. The elution program was as follows: 0 min: 0% B, 45 min: 35% B, 50 min: 100% B, 55 min: 100% B, 56 min: 0% B, 62 min: 0% B. All phenolic acids were quantified via external calibration.

Determination of in vitro antioxidant activity

The determination of antioxidant activity was performed using a commercial kit (Geruisi Biology, Suzhou) in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions. DPPH and ABTS were used as the assay reagents. The analysis was performed by spectrophotometry (Bai et al., 2024).

The DPPH radical scavenging assay was performed by mixing sample aliquots with freshly prepared ethanolic DPPH working solution. The reaction mixture was incubated at room temperature for 30 min in the dark, and absorbance was detected at a wavelength of 517 nm. The ABTS radical cation (ABTS·+) working solution was prepared by reacting ABTS stock solution with potassium persulfate, followed by 12–16 h of incubation at room temperature in the dark to ensure complete radical formation. Prior to analysis, the working solution was diluted to an absorbance of 0.70 ± 0.02 at 734 nm. Each sample was mixed with the diluted ABTS· + solution and allowed to react for 6 min at room temperature, after which absorbance was measured at 734 nm.

Statistical analysis

OPLS-DA was performed using SIMCA software (Version 14.1; Umetrics, Umeå, Sweden) to screen differential volatile markers among the vinegar samples, with model reliability verified by 200 permutation tests. Principal component analysis (PCA) and Pearson's correlation analysis were conducted using Origin 2025 software (OriginLab Corporation, Massachusetts, USA). The clustered heatmap was generated using the ChiPlot online analysis platform (https://www.chiplot.online). One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was applied to assess the significant differences between the groups, and Duncan's multiple-range test was performed for post-hoc pairwise comparisons. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. All experiments were performed in triplicate independent biological replicates.

Results and discussion

Physicochemical characteristics of Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar in comparison with China's four famous vinegars

The pH and titratable acidity values of the five vinegar samples are presented in Fig. 1A. Among the samples, Yongchun aged vinegar, Shanxi aged vinegar, and Zhenjiang aroma vinegar exhibited the lowest pH values, indicating higher concentrations of H+ ions and suggesting a more pronounced sour taste. In contrast, Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar showed the highest pH value (3.65 ± 0.09), reflecting comparatively milder acidity. Such pH divergence among vinegar varieties has been widely reported to correlate with differences in microbial metabolic patterns and raw material composition in fermentation systems (Tong, Chen, et al., 2024; Wang, Gao, et al., 2025). For the samples in this study, this remains a plausible explanation to be verified by dynamic fermentation monitoring. The titratable acidity of the vinegar samples ranged from 6.06 ± 0.17 to 7.93 ± 0.08 g/100 mL and exhibited a general inverse relationship with pH. Yongchun aged vinegar, Shanxi aged vinegar, and Zhenjiang aroma vinegar showed notably higher titratable acidity values (7.93 ± 0.08, 7.47 ± 0.14, and 7.80 ± 0.03 g/100 mL, respectively), which is consistent with their lower pH values and characteristic strong sour taste. By contrast, Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar displayed the lowest titratable acidity (6.06 ± 0.17 g/100 mL), corresponding with its comparatively higher pH. No significant difference between the titratable acidity of Shanxi aged vinegar and Zhenjiang aroma vinegar was observed. This compositional similarity is consistent with their shared raw material base of sorghum, which contains abundant fermentable carbohydrates as precursors for organic acid production (Chiodetti et al., 2025). In addition, both vinegar types undergo prolonged aging processes. It has been postulated in previous cereal vinegar studies that sustained metabolic activity of acetic acid bacteria during aging may contribute to the gradual accumulation of organic acids (Liu et al., 2025; Tong, Li, et al., 2024). This could represent one possible explanation for the relatively high titratable acidity observed in these two samples, and remains to be verified by direct dynamic monitoring in future work.

Compositional profiles of nonvolatile metabolites in different vinegar varieties

Organic acid composition and its contribution to taste characteristics

The composition and concentrations of organic acids in the different vinegar samples are presented in Fig. 1B. Acetic acid, a volatile organic acid responsible for the characteristic sharp sourness of vinegar, was the predominant acid detected in all samples, with the highest level observed in Shanxi aged vinegar. Lactic acid was present at the highest concentrations in Sichuan Baoning vinegar and Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar. Relative to acetic acid, this organic acid possesses a less pungent acidic character and is capable of modulating the pronounced acidic attributes of acetic acid from a compositional perspective (Bai et al., 2024). Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar also contained relatively higher levels of malic acid and tartaric acid. The combined composition of these organic acids forms the taste-related chemical basis of this vinegar. Organic acids are fundamental determinants of vinegar quality, influencing not only sensory complexity but also nutritional and functional attributes. The synergistic effects of major acids, particularly acetic and lactic acid, establish the primary sour taste profile of vinegar (Song et al., 2024; Tong, Li, et al., 2024). In addition, other organic acids, such as ascorbic acid and succinic acid, have been associated with antioxidant activity and metabolic regulation, thereby contributing to the bioactive potential of vinegar (Song et al., 2024; Xia et al., 2024). Collectively, these acids determine the titratable acidity of the final product (Gong et al., 2020). Overall, the organic acid profile exhibited distinct specificity among the five traditional vinegar types. These compositional differences in organic acid profiles are presumably driven by variations in raw materials, microbial communities and fermentation conditions. As suggested by previous studies, these factors may together shape the unique compositional and functional features of different vinegar products (Wang, Zhou, et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2024).

Free amino acid profiles and taste-active amino acid balance

Flavor-active amino acids play an important role in shaping the sensory characteristics of vinegar, while both the total concentration and specific composition of free amino acids reflect the nutritional value of the product and the metabolic activity of microorganisms during fermentation (Gao et al., 2024). The composition and concentrations of free amino acids in the five traditional vinegar samples are shown in Fig. 2. Among the samples, Shanxi aged vinegar exhibited the highest total concentration of free amino acids, which was significantly greater than that of the other four vinegars (p < 0.05). Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar displayed a distinct amino acid profile characterized by a relatively high proportion of sweet-tasting amino acids and a moderate proportion of bitter-tasting amino acids compared with Sichuan Baoning vinegar and Zhenjiang aroma vinegar. Although the proportion of umami amino acids in this vinegar was not the highest among the samples, the balanced distribution of sweet-, bitter-, and umami-type amino acids forms the chemical basis of its taste profile. The specific amino acid composition further defines the sensory and nutritional characteristics of each vinegar. In Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar, the concentration of aspartic acid, a potent umami amino acid, reached 110.66 ± 18.92 mg/L. Aspartic acid has a relatively low taste threshold and contributes a pronounced umami flavor (Ma et al., 2020). Glutamine was detected at a concentration of 48.18 ± 2.42 mg/L. This amino acid can act as a precursor of glutamic acid and may therefore contribute to the enhancement of umami characteristics during fermentation or subsequent storage (Zhao et al., 2016). The concentrations of bitter-tasting amino acids, including arginine and valine, were notably lower than those observed in Zhenjiang aroma vinegar and Shanxi aged vinegar, which further supports the milder taste profile of Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar. Its nutritional value is also supported by the presence of essential amino acids, such as methionine and lysine, at moderate levels. Furthermore, a relatively high average concentration of tyrosine was detected. Tyrosine participates in several physiological processes, including antioxidant-related pathways, thereby potentially contributing to the bioactive properties of the vinegar (Gu et al., 2025). Overall, the profiles of flavor-active amino acids, total free amino acid concentrations, and compositional patterns varied substantially among the traditional vinegar samples. These differences are likely related to variations in raw materials and fermentation processes, which together shape the diversity of vinegar flavor characteristics and provide a molecular basis for their distinct nutritional and functional properties.

Total phenolic content and differential phenolic acid composition

Phenolic compounds are important secondary metabolites that contribute significantly to the antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties of vinegar (Jin et al., 2025). The concentrations of nine phenolic compounds, chlorogenic acid, catechin, vanillic acid, caffeic acid, syringic acid, epicatechin, ferulic acid, p-coumaric acid, and sinapic acid, detected in the vinegar samples are presented in Fig. 3 and Table 1. As shown in Fig. 3, Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar exhibited the highest total phenolic content. In particular, the concentration of chlorogenic acid reached 27.88 ± 1.56 mg/L in Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar, whereas significantly lower levels were detected in Zhenjiang aroma vinegar (p < 0.05) (Table 1). The wheat bran-rich raw material formula of Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar is considered a potential source of abundant phenolic precursors such as chlorogenic acid. As documented in previous cereal vinegar research, microbial enzymes produced during fermentation can cleave glycosidic bonds in plant cell walls and release bound polyphenols (Jin et al., 2025; Qin et al., 2025). We infer that such enzymatic processes and microbial metabolic activities may contribute to the release and transformation of phenolic components, which could account for the relatively high chlorogenic acid and total phenolic content detected in this sample. Notably, vanillic acid was detected at a concentration of 80.92 ± 0.50 mg/L in Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar, whereas only 1.81 ± 0.90 mg/L was observed in Yongchun aged vinegar. Differences in phenolic acid composition were observed between Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar and Yongchun aged vinegar, as reflected by the notable variation in vanillic acid content (Table 1). This may be related to differences in fermentation strategies, solid-state nested vat fermentation versus liquid-based fermentation, which can influence microbial density and metabolic pathways. Furthermore, the flavonoid catechin was detected in Sichuan Baoning vinegar (55.44 ± 10.04 mg/L) and Zhenjiang aroma vinegar (14.03 ± 2.54 mg/L) (Table 1). Catechin is a bioactive flavonoid known to contribute to antioxidant capacity and is associated with a range of potential health benefits (Keller & Lanaspa, 2026).

Overall, substantial variation was observed in both total phenolic content and the composition of individual phenolic compounds among the vinegar samples. These differences likely arise from variations in raw materials, microbial community composition, and fermentation conditions. In particular, the relatively high phenolic accumulation observed in Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar may result from the retention of phenolic precursors in its bran-based raw material combined with microbial biotransformation during fermentation.

Volatile flavor profiles and key aroma-active compounds of vinegar samples

Classification and distribution characteristics of volatile flavor substances

A total of 138 volatile compounds were identified across the five vinegar samples, as summarized in Table S1. These compounds were classified into nine chemical categories: esters, aldehydes, ketones, phenols, alcohols, acids, ethers, furans, and other compounds (Fig. 4A). Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar was characterized by a comparatively high number of ester compounds (26), exceeding those detected in Shanxi aged vinegar (14) and Yongchun aged vinegar (20). In contrast, it contained fewer aldehydes (9) than Sichuan Baoning vinegar (16) and Shanxi aged vinegar (11). The numbers of ketones (8), phenols (6), and alcohols (9) in Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar were intermediate among the five vinegar types. The number of acid compounds (10) was slightly higher than that detected in Zhenjiang aroma vinegar (7). Furthermore, this vinegar exhibited distinctive profiles of ethers (3), furans (7), and other volatile compounds (7). The relatively high abundance of ester compounds may serve as potential contributors to fruity and creamy aroma notes in Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar.

Screening of differential volatile markers via multivariate statistical analysis

To further investigate differences in volatile composition among the vinegar samples, OPLS-DA was performed. The model exhibited good explanatory and predictive capacity, with R2Y = 0.957 and Q2 = 0.89. A 200-permutation test was conducted to validate model reliability and rule out overfitting, with intercept values of R2 = 0.33 and Q2 = −0.993 obtained from the permutation regression lines (Fig. 4C and D). The negative Q2 intercept confirmed that the model did not show significant overfitting. The OPLS-DA score plot demonstrated clear separation among the five vinegar groups, indicating satisfactory discriminant performance of the model. Based on the variable importance in projection (>1.0) values derived from the OPLS-DA model, 22 volatile compounds were identified as significant contributors (Fig. 5). These 22 compounds with VIP > 1 are the main variables contributing to the overall differentiation of volatile profiles among the five vinegar groups. Further evaluation using OAV analysis identified 31 volatile compounds with OAV >1 across the five vinegar samples (Table 2), and the hierarchical clustering heatmap of these 31 compounds is shown in Fig. 4B, suggesting that these compounds directly contribute to the overall aroma profiles. Among these, Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar contained 19 odor-active compounds, including isoamyl acetate (OAV = 34.6), benzaldehyde (OAV = 1.08), and ethyl benzoate (OAV = 1.66). Isoamyl acetate is a major aroma compound in lemon vinegar fermentation (Yang et al., 2024).

Aroma contribution of key volatile compound classes and odor-active components

The distinctive aroma profile of Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar may result from the synergistic interactions among multiple classes of volatile compounds, with each chemical group potentially contributing specific sensory attributes to the overall flavor. Esters may serve as the primary contributors to potential fruity and sweet aromatic notes. A total of 26 volatile esters were detected in Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar. Among them, ethyl butyrate and ethyl 2-furoate were identified as characteristic ester components unique to this vinegar sample. Ethyl butyrate was quantified at a concentration of 0.1223 ± 0.1369 mg/kg and exhibited a high OAV (135.89). As a typical short-chain fatty acid ester, ethyl butyrate is reported to present prominent pineapple-like and fresh fruity notes (Nemati et al., 2021), and may act as a major contributor to the potential top-note fruity aroma of Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar. Ethyl 2-furoate, the second characteristic ester marker of this vinegar, was detected at a concentration of 0.0309 ± 0.0071 mg/kg. According to previous studies on vinegar flavor formation, ethyl 2-furoate is presumed to form via acid-catalyzed esterification of 2-furoic acid and ethanol. Furan compounds including furfural and 5-hydroxymethylfurfural are thought to be primarily formed during thermal processing steps such as boiling, and their content may progressively increase during aging (Al-Dalali et al., 2023; Gong et al., 2021). These furan derivatives may act as precursors for ethyl 2-furoate via sequential oxidation and esterification pathways (Xie et al., 2022). Together with other odor-active esters such as isoamyl acetate (OAV = 34.6), these ester compounds may contribute to the fruity aromatic characteristics of Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar and represent key compositional differences from China's Four Famous Vinegars.

Organic acids represent another important functional group of volatile compounds in vinegar. In addition to determining basic acidity, they participate in ester formation reactions and may also influence antioxidant-related properties (Yuan et al., 2025). Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar exhibited a relatively lower acetic acid content. As a plausible hypothesis, this observation may be related to the distinct oxygen availability and mass transfer conditions inherent to its nested vat multistep fermentation regime; however, this mechanistic interpretation has not been experimentally verified in the current study and requires direct dynamic monitoring of the fermentation process for confirmation. This relatively moderate acetic acid content may reduce pungent acidic perception and provide a mild matrix conducive to the presentation of other aroma components. In addition, moderate acidic conditions are generally recognized to favor slow esterification during long-term fermentation and aging, which may explain the relatively high abundance of the aforementioned ester compounds in this sample.

Alcohol compounds are mainly produced through yeast metabolism during alcoholic fermentation, and their presence at appropriate levels can soften the overall taste and enhance flavor complexity. In Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar, 1-hexanol was identified as a characteristic alcohol compound at a concentration of 0.0311 ± 0.0145 mg/kg. This C6 alcohol, typically generated through the lipoxygenase-mediated oxidation pathway of fatty acids during fermentation, imparts fresh green and faint fruity notes. Although phenylethanol was not detected in Pingshan vinegar, the presence of 1-hexanol, together with other alcohols such as 3-Octenol (OAV = 8.93), contributes to the compositional complexity of its volatile profile.

Ketone compounds are typically generated through fatty acid oxidation and keto acid decarboxylation and contribute creamy and nutty sensory notes to fermented foods (Chen et al., 2026). The ketone components detected in Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar, including 2-tert-butyl-5-methyl-1,3-dioxane, may contribute a mild sweet background note to the overall aroma profile. Although present at relatively low concentrations, these ketones may exert a potential modulatory effect on aroma perception by potentially softening the sharpness of acidic and fruity notes and enhancing flavor complexity.

Among the phenolic compounds, the compound 4-methylphenol (p-cresol) was detected in Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar at a concentration of 0.0199 ± 0.0058 mg/kg. This compound imparts faint smoky and animal-like notes that add complexity to the aroma profile. Moreover, guaiacol was detected at 0.126 ± 0.033 mg/kg. Guaiacol is reported to present characteristic smoky and woody aromas and exhibits potential antioxidant activity (Schranz et al., 2017). The presence of these phenolic compounds likely contributes subtle smoky nuances to the aroma of Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar, a feature that appears less prominent in China's Four Famous Vinegars.

Furan compounds, which are commonly formed through the Maillard reaction, are characterized by caramel-like and nutty aroma attributes (Zhao et al., 2025). In our study, furfural was detected at a concentration of 7.993 ± 2.017 mg/kg. This compound imparts a characteristic caramel-like aroma and exhibits potential antioxidant activity (Kanzler et al., 2017). Notably, among the differential volatile markers identified by OPLS-DA, furfural showed the highest variable importance in projection value, indicating that furfural makes the largest contribution to the overall volatile profile differentiation among the five vinegar types. Combined with its significantly higher relative content in PMFV, it can be regarded as a potential characteristic volatile component of PMFV.

The distinct composition and relative proportions of esters, organic acids, alcohols, ketones, phenols, and furan compounds in Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar may collectively underpin a distinctive aromatic profile. Potential sensory attributes including fruity, green, smoky and caramel-like notes are inferred from the documented odor properties of the corresponding chemical constituents. This composition-inferred sensory profile may differ notably from the established aroma characteristics of China's Four Famous Vinegars and represents a potential differentiating flavor trait of Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar.

In vitro free radical scavenging capacity and its correlation with phenolic compounds

The in vitro DPPH and ABTS free radical scavenging capacities of the five traditional vinegar samples are presented in Fig. 6A. Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar exhibited a DPPH radical scavenging capacity of 7.22 ± 0.22 mmol Trolox/mL, which was comparable to those of Sichuan Baoning vinegar and Shanxi aged vinegar but higher than the values observed for Yongchun aged vinegar and Zhenjiang aroma vinegar (Fig. 6A). The ABTS radical scavenging capacity of Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar was 18.49 ± 1.28 mmol Trolox/mL and did not differ significantly from those of Sichuan Baoning vinegar and Shanxi aged vinegar (p > 0.05). Pearson correlation analysis further revealed that DPPH and ABTS in vitro radical scavenging capacities were positively correlated (p < 0.05, both). Both antioxidant indicators also showed positive correlations with several phenolic compounds, particularly chlorogenic acid and epicatechin (p < 0.05) (Fig. 6B). These results indicate that phenolic compounds play a central role in determining the antioxidant activity of vinegar. The relatively strong antioxidant capacity observed in Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar may therefore be primarily attributed to its higher concentration of chlorogenic acid and related phenolic compounds.

Conclusion

Presented herein is a systematic comparative investigation into the flavor chemistry and bioactive attributes of Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar, benchmarked against China's Four Famous Vinegars, through an integrated analytical pipeline comprising chromatographic profiling, in vitro antioxidant assays, and multivariate statistical analysis. With respect to non-volatile taste-active constituents, Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar is distinguished by a significantly higher pH and lower titratable acidity, coupled with a well-balanced profile of organic acids and free amino acids. These compositional features underpin a distinct taste-related chemical matrix relative to the benchmark vinegars. Regarding volatile composition, a total of 138 volatile organic compounds were identified across the five sample sets, of which 22 were screened as key discriminants driving inter-variety differentiation. Furfural emerged as the most influential variable in sample separation and exhibited markedly elevated relative abundance in Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar. Thirty-one odor-active compounds with odor activity values (OAV) > 1 were detected across the sample cohort, 19 of which were present in Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar; ethyl butyrate was identified as a signature component that may underlie its distinctive fruity tonalities, as inferred from published odor property data. Collectively, the distinct volatile profile may constitute the chemical foundation for its unique aromatic profile. Furthermore, Pingshan multistep fermented vinegar harbored the highest total phenolic content among all samples assayed, with chlorogenic acid and vanillic acid identified as the primary differential phenolic constituents. Its in vitro DPPH and ABTS radical scavenging capacities were comparable to those of premium traditional Chinese vinegars, and a significant positive correlation was observed between phenolic content and antioxidant activity. This association supports the notion that phenolic compounds may serve as primary contributors to its bioactive potential. These distinct compositional characteristics are postulated to be associated with its wheat bran-based substrate formulation and unique multistep solid-state fermentation regime; however, the precise underlying formation mechanisms remain to be delineated via systematic dynamic fermentation studies. Overall, this work addresses a notable gap in current knowledge regarding this regional traditional vinegar, broadens the existing understanding of compositional diversity among Chinese traditional vinegars, and provides a preliminary scientific framework for the quality assessment and industrial advancement of regional specialty vinegar products.

For future investigations, dynamic monitoring of the fermentation process is warranted to elucidate the formation mechanisms of characteristic components, and antioxidant assessment should be extended to cellular and in vivo models. From an industrial perspective, optimization of fermentation and aging parameters, alongside the establishment of standardized production protocols, will facilitate improvements in product quality and batch-to-batch consistency.

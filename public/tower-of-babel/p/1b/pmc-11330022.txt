VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets against dental caries through promoting mineralization of dentin tubules, pH buffering, and antibacterial
Authors: Yanting Xu, Juan Mou, Jiewen Dai
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Dental caries is a worldwide public healthcare concern, and is closely related to the acidic environment that caused by bacterial decomposition of food. In this study, a two-step ion exchange liquid-phase stripping method was applied to strip out vermiculite (VMT) nanosheets, then amorphous calcium phosphate (ACP) and dextran were inserted between the VMT nanosheets interlayer to obtain a composite two-dimension nanosheets (VMT/ACP/Dextran). VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets exhibited excellent biocompatibility and could provide exogenous Ca2+and PO43− from ACP, provide SiO44−, Mg2+, Fe2+ and obtain buffering pH and antibacterial properties from VMT, as well as improve suspension stability and targeting Streptococcus mutans through glucan. The in vitro study showed that the composite materials could promote the mineralization and sealing of dentin tubules by releasing active ions, buffer pH 4.5 (a value close to the pH in the dental plaque environment) to pH 6.6–7.1 (values close to the pH in human saliva) through ion exchange, and exert antibacterial effects by targeting Streptococcus mutans and exerting oxidase like and peroxidase like activities to produce reactive oxygen species (ROS). The in vivo animal study showed that daily cleaning teeth using VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets could effectively reduce the incidence rate and severity of dental caries in rats. Taking together, the developed VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets, which integrated the excellent properties of VMT, ACP and dextran, can effectively prevent dental caries through a combination of factors such as buffering acids, antibacterial properties, and promoting calcification, and may be used as an active ingredient for daily oral hygiene or filling materials to prevent and treat dental caries.

Introduction

Dental caries is a serious public health problem and affects about 2.4 billion people worldwide. Caries leads to the decline of personal health and quality of life and bring a heavy socioeconomic burden [1]. The prevalence of dental caries is 60–80% in children and almost 100% in the adult population according to the report of the World Health Organization (WHO) [2]. Notably, the cariogenic bacteria play a crucial role in the development of dental caries [3, 4]. S. mutans adheres to the surface of teeth to form a highly restricted diffusion ecosystem by producing extracellular polymer encapsulation. Then S. mutans produces organic acids by metabolizing carbohydrates, which eventually decreases pH and creates an acidic microenvironment (pH 4.5–5.5) within the biofilm [5, 6]. The low-pH niches accelerate the demineralization of hard tissue and ultimately lead to caries [7, 8]. Thus, protective methods that can eliminate bacteria, buffer pH to decrease demineralization, as well as release active ions to promote the remineralization are usually used to prevent and cease dental caries [9, 10]. Silver diamine fluoride (SDF) has been widely applied in dentin sensitivity and caries prevention by inhibiting cariogenic bacteria and promoting remineralization in the past few decades [11–13]. Silver ions in SDF can destroy the bacterial structure, and fluorine ions can replace the hydroxyl groups in hydroxyapatite (HAp) to obtain more acid-resistant fluorapatite [14]. However, previous studies have shown that there are some limitations, such as biological toxicity of fluoride and silver ions, the unpleasant bitter metal odor of SDF solution, and low remineralization efficiency due to lack of Ca2+ and PO43−, should be taken into consideration [15–18]. Thus, developing a biomaterial with excellent antibacterial properties, demineralization inhibition, and remineralization promotion to prevent dental caries is urgently necessary and has broad clinical prospects.

Biomineralization controls the formation of organic/inorganic hybrid biological structures by regulating the organic matrix. [19, 20]. The formation of mature teeth enamel is generally considered to be a process of directional growth of organic matrix mediated by inorganic hydroxyapatite (HAp) [21, 22]. HAp can be dissolved into Ca2+ and PO43−, and Ca2+ and PO43− in saliva can be crystallized directionally and orderly to form an enamel-like structure on the enamel surface [23, 24]. The dynamic balance of dissolution and redeposition of HAp is disrupted by metabolizing dietary carbohydrates to produce acid within the biofilm which eventually leads to caries. Amorphous calcium phosphate (ACP) displays excellent remineralization ability in the prevention of dental caries [25–27]. However, ACP needs stabilizers to maintain its amorphous form, including amelogenin (Amel) and its analogs [28], casein phosphopeptide (CPP) [29], chitosan derivatives and other polymers [30], carboxymethylated polyamide (PAMAM) [31], polyelectrolytes [32, 33] and amphoteric polymers [34]. The application forms of ACP in the oral environment mainly include mouthwash [35], toothpaste and teeth desensitizer [36], and dental filling resin [37].

Two-dimensional (2D) materials have attracted extensive attention in the biomedical field in recent years due to their unique nanosheet structures, large specific surface area, and unique physicochemical properties [38–40]. Silicate clay minerals are the main mineral components of vermiculite (VMT), which are hydrated aluminosilicate minerals with layered structures. Silicate clay materials have unique surface areas and surface reactive groups leading to excellent cation exchange capacity and swelling capacity [41–43]. It has been widely used in gastrointestinal diseases, skin diseases, and inflammation [43]. Iron-based clay mineral material is considered a substitute for traditional antibiotics and has antibacterial ability against multi-drug resistant pathogens [44, 45]. Reductive iron-based clay mineral materials will generate reactive oxygen species (ROS) including superoxide free radicals (•O2–), hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), and hydroxyl free radicals(•OH). Soluble Fe2+ can enter the cytoplasm through the damaged bacterial membrane, and generate •OH through the Fenton reaction, resulting in the oxidation of lipids and proteins, the accumulation of cellular ROS, and ultimately destruct of the bacteria. In addition, the antimicrobial capacity of montmorillonite-illite also depends on the interlayer swelling property, which determines the reaction activity of Fe2+ and the generation of •OH [46]. Al3+ can be dissolved from clay under acidic conditions and replace the position of Ca2+ and Mg2+ in bacterial cell membranes. Al3+ combines with bacterial cell membrane phospholipids, changes the lipid-protein interaction, and interferes with membrane potential [47, 48]. Al3+ can increase the permeability and oxidative damage of Fe2+ and synergistically destroy bacterial pathogens [49, 50]. As a natural clay mineral, bulk VMT consists of magnesium oxide (MgO) and iron oxide (Fe2O3) sandwiched between silicon dioxide (SiO2) and aluminum oxide (Al2O3) to form the unique sandwich structure. The interlayers are connected by water or metal ions to form a unique three-dimensional structure of clay mineral materials. As a common traditional Chinese medicine, the raw VMT has been included in the Chinese Pharmacopoeia for calming the nerves, removing the haze, and brightening the eyes [51]. VMT nanomaterials exhibit good biocompatibility with normal cells, while they converting endogenous H2O2 into a toxic •OH and resulting in an increase in the content of ROS mediated by enhanced Fenton activity [52].

In this study, ACP and dextran were inserted between vermiculite nanosheets (VMT NSs) interlayers to obtain VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets that integrated all the respective excellent properties. The biocompatibility and properties against dental caries of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets were systematically evaluated both in vitro and in vivo (Fig. 1).

Results

Synthesis and characterization of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets

The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images showed that the VMT NSs raw material exhibited expandable layered structures (Fig. 2a). The energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) results further confirmed that the main elements of VMT NSs included uniformly distributed Si, Mg, Al, Fe and O (Fig. 2b). The transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images showed that synthesized VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets were well illustrated and displayed nanosheets and elongated shapes, exhibited needle-like and crystal structures, which validated the transition from ACP to HAp on the seventh day (Fig. 2c). The EDS images showed that the characteristic elements of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets included Ca, P, Fe, Mg, Al, Si, and O, which demonstrated that VMT NSs, ACP and dextran were successful combined together (Fig. 2d). As shown in Fig. 2e, the concentration of bioactive ions was observed to gradually ascend in a pH-dependent manner for 30 consecutive days. It was noteworthy that the release of Fe significantly increased compared to the other two groups. The pH 4.5 buffer solution was more conducive to the release of bioactive ions, which might be related to the exchange reaction of interlayer cation and H+. These results suggested that the accuracy and specificity of bioactive ions released might increase due to the acidic microenvironment of biofilm in the caries. Furthermore, the pH value in different buffer groups increased gradually and steadily in the first seven days (Fig. 2f). The maximum pH values in the pH 5.5 and pH 7.4 buffer groups are approximately 6.75 and 7.5, respectively. Remarkably, the value in the pH 4.5 (a value close to the pH in the dental plaque environment) buffer group was 6.6–7.1 (values similar to human saliva) from the seventh day to the thirtieth day, which showed the maximum pH buffering performance among three different buffer groups. This pH rectification ability could intelligently target the acidic environment in the biofilm and effectively resist the virulence factors of acidogenic and acidduric S. mutans. In addition, the surface negative charges were tested to be reduced, indicating that VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets might be effective in targeting cariogenic biofilm (Fig. 2g).

The biocompatibility of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets in vitro

The biocompatibility of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets was confirmed by the CCK-8 test and Live/dead cell staining. The gradient concentration screening experiment results showed that 10 µg/mL VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets could promote HGF (human gingival fibroblasts) proliferation (Fig. 3a). Then HDPSCs (human dental pulp stem cells) and HUVEC (human umbilical vein endothelial cells) were used to verify this conclusion. Results showed that 10 µg/mL VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets could also promote the proliferation of corresponding cells consistent with previous studies (Fig. 3b). Live/dead staining results showed that no obvious dead HDPSCs after incubation with 10 µg/mL VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets for 7 days (Fig. 3c). Statistical analysis results also showed that there was no significant difference for the number of living cells within 24 h (Fig. 3d). These findings further confirmed that 10 µg/mL VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets exhibited satisfactory biocompatibility. In addition, ibidi 2-well scratch test and transwell cell migration experiment were used to simulate the migration of cells, and the results showed that 10 µg/mL VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets could effectively promote the migration of HDPSCs and HUVEC. There was a narrow blank area in the group treated with10 µg/mL VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets (Fig. 3e and f). ALP staining results showed that 10 µg/mL VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets could promote the mineralization of HDPSCs. ALP staining results showed that VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets exhibited higher alkaline phosphatase activity of HDPSCs after being cultured for 4 days and 7 days, indicating that VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets could promote the mineralization of HDPSCs in vitro (Fig. 3g). Results of angiogenesis experiments indicated that VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets could effectively promote angiogenesis in vitro (Fig. 3h.i).

Antibacterial and antibiofilm efficiency of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets in vitro

The antibacterial activity of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets against typical cariogenic bacteria was investigated. Results showed that VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets exhibited better antibacterial activity against planktonic S. mutans (Fig. 4a and b). To further explore the mechanism of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets against cariogenic bacteria, the morphology and structure of S. mutans were observed by FE-SEM. As shown in Fig. 4c, the bacteria in control group exhibited good spherical morphology and short chain arrangement. However, the bacterial integrity of S. mutans treated by VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets was destroyed. The morphology of bacteria was irregular and the cell membrane shrinked. At the same time, some similar holes could be observed on the bacterial surface, which might be caused by VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets inserted into the cell membrane molecules of bacteria, leading to leakage of bacterial contents and bacterial death. The OD600 value of bacterial suspension showed that VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets could effectively inhibit the proliferation of S. mutans (Fig. 4d).

Biofilm was a complex and stable heterogeneous three-dimensional structure, which could prevent antibacterial agents from penetrating into the biofilm. The dual-mode antibiofilm efficiency of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets in inhibiting biofilm formation as well as accelerating biofilm elimination has been explored. Firstly, the efficiency of biofilm eradication for VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets was evaluated (Fig. 5a). After incubation for 12 h, biofilm composed of S. mutans was established and then treated with BHI medium (1% sucrose), VMT NSs and VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets respectively. Live/dead staining results showed that bacteria in the biofilm were effectively killed after treatment with VMT NSs and VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets. There were more red areas representing dead bacteria in the biofilm after treatment with VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets (Fig. 5b). Furthermore, the pH value of bacterial suspensions in the control group was found to declined steadily and continuously to 4.5–5.5 or even lower, which confirmed the acidogenic and acidduric ability of S. mutans. However, the pH value in bacteria suspensions treated with VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets was still 6.6–7.1 that was similar to pH value in human saliva, which indicated that VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets had the ability to buffer the acidic pH in the biofilm (Fig. 5c). Then, the efficiency of biofilm inhibition for VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets was explored by the crystal violet staining experiment which reflected the biofilm biomass instead of the survival of bacteria (Fig. 5d). After treatment with VMT NSs or VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets that were conducted to mimic human daily oral hygiene care for 48 h, the formation process of biofilm was disrupted and biofilm biomass was decreased effectively (Fig. 5e). Statistical analysis showed that VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets exhibited the highest efficiency in inhibiting biofilm formation in vitro (Fig. 5f).

Effect of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets in against dental caries in vivo

A rodent caries model was established using 15 days old female SD rats according to the scheme to further investigate the efficiency of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets against dental caries in vivo (Fig. 6a). Results showed that VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets exhibited the effect of arresting and preventing the progression of dental caries. Gradual appearance of pit-and-fissure caries lesions was observed in the control group, which was similar to the process of human caries. The teeth tissue in the control group was severely damaged and caries lesions were more extensive, even reaching the pulp cavity according to the Micro CT images (Fig. 6b). However, the occlusal surface of the teeth was more complete, and the morbidity and severity of caries lesions were significantly reduced after treatment with VMT NSs and VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets. VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets group displayed the best integrity of teeth among all groups (Fig. 6c). All animals showed normal behavior, regular intake of food and water, no adverse reactions, and no signs of potential toxicity. The biocompatibility of VMT NSs and VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets in vivo was evaluated by histological analysis of the palatal mucosa, gingival tissues, and main organs. H&E staining results showed that there were no obvious lesions, such as congestion, erosion, ulcer, inflammation, effusion, or necrosis in different organs (Fig. 6d and e). Based on these findings together, it was further confirmed that VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets had satisfactory biocompatibility and antibacterial propertities in vivo and were expected to be used for prevention and treatment of dental caries in the future.

Effect of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets in sealing dentin tubules in vitro

The pH cycle model was used to evaluate the ability of dentin tubules occlusion for VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets in vitro. Remineralization referred to the deposition of Ca2+ and PO43− in the demineralized crystal vacancy. VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets could be used as an exogenous source of Ca2+ and PO43−. Its slow and steady release ability while providing ions could prevent bioactive ions from rapidly gathering and crystal transformation in the demineralized crystal vacancy. The Ca2+ and PO43− could penetrate the deep crystal vacancy and promote the growth of the remineralization layer. Dentin tubules in the control group remained in an open and rough state without obvious mineral deposition and dentin tubule occlusion. However, the surface of the specimen was almost completely covered by mineral deposits and the dentin tubules were almost completely occluded after treatment with VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets (Fig. 7).

Catalytic effect of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets in vitro

Soluble blue-green products could be generated by the substrate TMB when VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets were gifted with oxidase (OXD)-like and peroxidase (POD)-like activities. Firstly, the pH screening test results showed that VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets displayed strong OXD-like activity in the pH 4.5 sodium acetate buffer, a value consistent with the pH in the dental plaque microenvironment where S. mutans flourished (Fig. 8a). Secondly, concentration screening test results showed that 10 µg/mL VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets exhibited better OXD-like activity than other concentrations (Fig. 8b). Then, the results of the POD-like activity test showed that 10 µg/mL VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets exhibited the strongest POD-like activity and the strongest color reaction in the presence of 10 mM H2O2 (Fig. 8c). To explore whether VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets would have adverse effects on normal oral cells while exerting antibacterial properties in acidic environments, intracellular ROS generation was detected in inflammatory status. VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets exhibited no significant generation of ROS in HGF under the normal oral environment. LPS was used to simulate the inflammatory environment and ROS could be detected in HGF subsequently. It’s worth noting that enzyme-like activity of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets was not triggered in the acidic environment after the induction of LPS (Fig. 8d). But when the pH value in the active biofilm reduced to 4.5 ~ 5.5 during the metabolic process of S. mutans, VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets could exert stronger OXD-like and POD-like activities and generate more ROS (Fig. 8e). Taking all these findings together, it implied that VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets would not exert OXD-like and POD-like activities under oral physiological and inflammatory conditions. However, VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets exhibited stronger OXD-like and POD-like activities to generate more ROS in acidic inflammatory environment, which might contribute to the death of bacteria.

Discussion

Dental caries was a dynamic and multifactorial disease that underwent demineralization and remineralization in acidic environments produced by cariogenic bacteria [53, 54] Cariogenic bacteria would lead to further erosion and caries damage as further flourished [55]. Non-invasive treatment of early lesions could be achieved through remineralization and inhibition of dentin demineralization [56]. Nanomaterials had been widely used for biomineralization therapy in recent years due to their excellent properties [57, 58].

ACP was an initial solid phase precipitated from a supersaturated solution of calcium phosphate [59], which exhibited good bioactivity, biodegradability, and bone conductivity. ACP was widely used in the biomedical field, especially in orthopedics and dentistry field [60–62]. Previous studies had shown that ACP was a precursor of biomineralization in vivo, and intracellular ACP had been identified as a key factor affecting osteogenesis [63]. Extracellular ACP served as the precursor phase of HAP and could promote the mineralization of collagen fibers [64, 65]. ACP was a metastable structure that could be transformed into other stable crystalline phases through surface nucleation and solution mediation, followed by losing its ability to induce remineralization. Currently, the amorphous crystalline phase of ACP could become stable through surface adsorption and ion substitution of foreign molecules [61, 66, 67]. ACP was difficult to directly use for remineralization of dental hard tissue due to its easy conversion into stable crystalline phases. In this study, dextran was grafted onto ACP to form stable polymers, and TEM results showed that dextran exhibited a significant ability to stabilize ACP nanoclusters into ACP/Dextran soluble nanocomposites, thereby preventing them from growing to the critical size that was required for uniform nucleation, precipitation, and phase transition. Previous studies had shown that stable ACP could effectively promote remineralization for demineralized hard tissues including dental and bone tissues by releasing Ca2+ and PO43− in situ [68, 69]. In addition, some studies had shown that release of Ca2+ and PO43− was related to pH in the microenvironment. As some studies had shown more Ca2+ and PO43− would be released in acidic environments [60, 70]. In this study, the experimental group exhibited more mineral deposits on the surface of demineralized dentin. Dentin tubules were further sealed that could effectively prevent bacteria and other substances from entering them. Numerous studies had also demonstrated the effectiveness of different forms of ACP application in reducing dentin sensitivity and promoting remineralization [71, 72], which was consistent with our results.

Clay was a layered silicate mineral and widely distributed in nature. VMT, a hydrated layered magnesium aluminum silicate mineral, was widely used in various fields of environmental protection. VMT could expand under high temperatures, increasing interlayer distance. Due to the exchange of cations between the layers of VMT, it was possible to peel off bulk VMT with layered structures through ion exchange [73]. In recent years, two-dimensional nanomaterials had attracted widespread interest in the field of regenerative medicine due to their unique nanostructure, large specific surface area, and unique physicochemical properties, such as releasing bioactive ions, buffering pH, adsorption, and heat insulation [74–76]. In this study, VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets was extracted from bulk VMT using a two-step ion exchange method. Previous studies had shown that the active sites of two-dimensional VMT NSs could be released for catalytic reactions in tumor cells [77, 78]. It should be noted that the remineralization function of ACP itself may not be sufficient to treat deep caries, and its antibacterial ability was also closely related to the treatment and prognosis of caries. The antibacterial properties of calcium hydroxide paste in clinical practice were mainly due to its strong alkalinity, which could damage bacterial cell membranes and caused protein denaturation in bacteria [79]. In addition, calcium hydroxide could also inactivate the residual bacterial toxic product lipopolysaccharide (LPS) on the root canal wall [80]. Except for its direct anti-bactericidal effect, calcium hydroxide also had a diffusion effect, which could inhibit bacteria hidden in the dentin tubules [81]. The hydroxide ion of calcium hydroxide could neutralize the acidic substances produced by inflammation, thereby reducing the occurrence of emergencies during root canal treatment. Calcium hydroxide with astringent property had a good effect on eliminating inflammatory exudation and reducing pain [82]. In this study, VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets was demonstrated to exhibit excellent pH buffering and resistance to planktonic bacteria and bacterial biofilms due to its alkalinity and ion exchange. In addition, previous studies had shown that Fe3+/Fe2+ in two-dimensional VMT NSs could mediate Fenton reaction based chemokinetic therapy. Previous studies had also shown that two-dimensional VMT NSs exhibited oxidase-like and peroxidase-like activities, and could obtain killing effects on cancer cells by releasing ROS [83]. In this study, we had also demonstrated that VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets could produce ROS against S. mutans. The ability to jointly buffer acidic environments and generate ROS could effectively combat planktonic bacteria, inhibit and eliminate bacterial biofilms. All of these findings showed that VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets had promising potential in the treatment of dental caries.

Conclusion

In this study, VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets were successfully constructed while ACP was first encapsulated into VMT nanosheets. Bioactive ions were released from VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets in a pH-responsive manner which could promote the occlusion of dentin tubules by transforming them into hydroxyapatite in situ to prevent the progression of carious lesions. VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets could also buffer acidic pH while combat planktonic S. mutans and cariogenic biofilm by generation of ROS, which effectively reduced the production of organic acids and arrested dental caries. In addition, previous studies showed that VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets exhibited excellent heat insulation ability, which could be utilized to isolate thermal stimulation on dental pulp tissue. In conclusion, VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets might be used as an active ingredient for daily oral hygiene or filling materials to prevent and treat dental caries.

Materials and methods

Materials

VMT bulk materials, calcium chloride, phosphoric acid, and dextran were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich Chemical Co., Ltd. (USA). Human gingival fibroblasts (HGF) and human dental pulp stem cells (HDPSCs) were purchased from the Cell Bank of Shanghai Institutes for Biological Sciences (Shanghai, China). Human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) were purchased from FuHeng Biology (Shanghai, China). Alpha minimum essential medium (α-MEM), endothelial cell medium (ECM), fetal bovine serum (FBS), phosphate buffer solution (PBS), penicillin-streptomycin (P/S), and trypsin were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich Chemical Co., Ltd. (USA). Specific pathogen-free (SPF) Sprague Dawley (SD) female rats (15 days old; 40 g) were supplied by Shanghai Jihui Laboratory Animal Care Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China).

Preparation of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets

VMT NSs were prepared by a two-step ion exchange method. Firstly, interlayer cation of VMT was replaced by Na+. Samples were washed three times with water and ethanol to remove any other residual ions. Then the prepared VMT was immersed in hydrogen peroxide solution for ultrasonic treatment for 30 min to strip the sample into nano-layer. Finally, VMT NSs were centrifugated and then collected. Then 200 mg VMT NSs, 200 mg calcium chloride, and 3.8 mL TEA were dissolved in 80 mL ethanol. After ultrasonic dispersion for 10 min, 70 µL phosphoric acid and 20 mL ethanol were added dropwise and stirred at room temperature for 30 min followed by centrifugation at 8000 rpm. VMT/ACP was obtained after washing three times with ethanol and deionized water respectively. Sodium periodate solution (100 g/L) was added into 100 mL dextran solution (Mw = 20,000, 50 g/L) with stirring for 6 h at room temperature and then 1 mL ethylene glycol was added to terminate the reaction. The solution was dialyzed with a 3500 Da dialysis bag for 48 h (water was changed once every 12 h) and then freeze-dried. 1 g dextran was added into the mixture of 40 mL of deionized water and 40 mL of ethanol, heated in a water bath at 60℃ to fully dissolve dextran. VMT/ACP was added to the above solution, heated in a 60 ℃ water bath and stirred for 4 h before centrifugation. The final VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets were obtained by washing the precipitate three times with deionized water.

Characterization analysis

Field-emission Scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM) images and element mappings were used to observe the microstructure of the surface of VMT NSs raw material (Zeiss Gemini 300 microscope). EDS (Energy Dispersive Spectroscopy) was used to confirm the element composition of VMT NSs. Transmission Electron Microscope (TEM) images were used to observe the structure of diluted VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets on the copper grid. VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets were dispersed in ddH2O, PBS, and medium at room temperature, respectively. Subsequently, images were collected at 24 h to assess the dispersity of nanosheets.

pH-responsive release behavior

The pH-responsive release behavior of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets was examined in corresponding buffer solutions with different pH value (4.5, 5.5, and 7.4) at 37℃. The ion amounts and pH of all samples were measured at specific time points. Briefly, 10 mg VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets were completely immersed in 20 mL buffer solutions with different pH levels as mentioned above. The experimental system was carried out in a 50 mL centrifuge tube on a shaking table with a rotation speed of 120 rpm/min at 37° C. At 1 day, 3 days, 7 days, 10 days, 14 days, 21 days, and 30 days, 2 mL supernatant was taken after centrifugation and 2 mL buffer solution with the corresponding pH was added. The ion concentration and the pH value were detected using an ICP-OES spectrometer and a precision pH instrument.

Preparation of dentin specimens

The human third molars were obtained from patients who needed preventive extraction before orthodontics. The inclusion criteria were complete third molars without any caries and cracks. The crown was separated at the junction of enamel and cement with hard tissue sliding microtome. Uniform dentin samples of 4 × 4 × 1 mm3 were further obtained. These samples were polished step by step with carborundum sandpaper under flowing water (from 500- to 4200- grit) and then stored in 1% sodium hypochlorite solution at 4℃ before using.

Cell culture

Human gingival fibroblasts (HGF) and Human dental pulp stem cells (HDPSCs) were cultured in complete α-MEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (P/S). Human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) were cultured in complete ECM supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% P/S. All cells were cultured in a humidified incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO2. The medium was changed every 2 days and the cells were passaged at 80% confluency.

Cell biocompatibility

The biocompatibility of the VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets on oral cells was investigated by a Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay against HGF, HDPSCs, and HUVEC. Briefly, HGF, HDPSCs, and HUVEC were seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 5 × 103 cells/well, respectively. 100 µL CCK-8 working solution was added to each well at specific time points. Then cells were incubated for another 2 h in the dark. The absorbance at the wavelength of 450 nm of different groups was measured on a microplate reader.

In addition, the cell biocompatibility was evaluated by live/dead staining kit (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China). HDPSCs and HUVEC were seeded on 24-well plates at a density of 2 × 104 cells per well respectively. Fresh culture medium containing 10 µg/mL VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets was added to the experimental group. After incubation, the medium was removed and rinsed with PBS once. Then the medium was changed with a working solution containing Calcein-AM/PI and further cultured for 30 min in the dark. Images were observed and acquired under a confocal laser scanning microscope (CLSM).

Migration assay

An in vitro scratch assay was performed to assess the migration capability of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets on HDPSCs and HUVEC. Defined 500 μm cell-free gaps were created by 2-well silicone inserts (ibidi, Germany). Briefly, HDPSCs and HUVEC were seeded into the culture inserts at a density of 3 × 105 cells/well with a volume of 90 µL. Inserts were removed after 12 h of cultivation. Original culture mediums were replaced with fresh culture mediums containing 10 µg/mL VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets. Cells were fixed for 30 min after 8 h of incubation and then stained with crystal violet for 10 min at room temperature. Migrated cells were observed and photographed by inverted fluorescence microscopy.

Transwell inserts with a polyethylene terephthalate membrane (8.0-µm; Corning Costar, NY, USA) were plated into 24-well plates. HDPSCs and HUVEC were subjected to serum deprivation for 12 h and resuspended in 200 µL of α-MEM with 1% FBS in the upper chamber. Cells were adjusted to a density of 4 × 104 cells per well and 600 µLvarious culture mediums were added to the lower chamber. After 24 h of incubation at 37 °C, mediums in the lower and upper chambers were discarded. Cells remaining at the upper surface of the membrane were gently wiped off with cotton swabs and cells on the lower surface of the membrane were migrated cells. Migrated cells to the lower chamber were fixed with 4% PFA for 30 min at room temperature followed by staining with 0.1% crystal violet for 10 min. Five non-overlapping fields for each sample were randomly selected for observation under an inverted microscope.

Bacterial strains

S. mutans (UA159, ATCC 700,610) was used for antibacterial and antibiofilm experiments in the following experiments. S. mutans were resuscitated in brain heart infusion (BHI) medium (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO) and cultured overnight in the facultative anaerobic incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO2. BHI medium with 1% sucrose was used for bacterial biofilm culture. BHI agar plates were used for the growth and counting of monoclonal colonies.

pH changes in the bacterial suspension

1 mL S. mutans bacterial suspension in the middle of exponential growth was added to 10 mL fresh BHI medium with 1% sucrose for each group. 10 µg/mL VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets were added to the experimental group. At specific time points (2 h, 4 h, 6 h, 8 h, 10 h, 12 h, 24 h), 1 mL bacteria suspension of each group was removed for the measurement of pH value.

Antibacterial and antibiofilm analysis

To assess the antibacterial activity of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets against planktonic S. mutans, 5 mL bacterial suspensions (1 × 107 CFU/mL) were incubated with 10 µg/mL VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets as the treatment group. After 2 h and 24 h, the bacterial suspension was continuously diluted in proportion and then coated on the agar plates. Then agar plates were incubated at 37℃with 5% CO2 for 48 h. CFU (colony-forming units) counts and FE-SEM were performed to measure the anti-planktonic bacteria efficiency of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets.

For the antibiofilm assay, VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets treatment against the formation and elimination process of bacterial biofilms were constructed. For the biofilm eradication model, S. mutans suspensions (1 × 106 CFU/mL) were cultured with 1% (w/v) sucrose and incubated for 12 h at 37 °C and 5% CO2. After another 12 h of incubation with 10 µg/mL VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets, the efficiency of biofilm eradication was assessed by CLSM. Adherent biofilms of different groups were rinsed three times with PBS to remove unattached S. mutans followed by staining with the live/dead bacterial viability kit according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The presence of live bacteria was indicated by the green fluorescence (SYTO 9) and the presence of bacteria with the compromised cell membrane was indicated by the red fluorescence (PI), respectively. The value of OD600 and percent change of S. mutans suspension at different time points in different groups also was detected using a UV–Vis–NIR spectrophotometer.

For the biofilm inhibition model, S. mutans suspensions (1 × 106 CFU/mL) were mixed with 10 µg/mL VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets at the beginning of culture with 1% sucrose. After incubation for 4 h at 37 °C and 5% CO2, the culture medium was replaced by fresh BHI with 1% sucrose followed by another incubation of 20 h. The cycle of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets treatments was implemented the next day. After 48 h, the efficiency of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets in inhibiting biofilm was evaluated by crystal violet staining. The value of OD590 of 95% ethanol solution that dissolved bacterial biofilm stained with crystal violet in different groups was measured using a UV–Vis–NIR spectrophotometer.

Measurement of dentin tubules occlusion in vitro

Demineralized dentin models were constructed by dentin specimens which were first etched with 37% phosphoric acid for 30 s. Then these demineralized dentin specimens were rinsed thoroughly with deionized water followed by sonication for 5 min. The pH cycle process included 2 hours demineralization and 22 h remineralization, which were incubated on the shaker (50 rpm/min, 37 °C). 100 µL VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets or deionized water were applied to these dentin specimens at 8:00 am and 4:00 pm every day for 15 min, respectively. The treatment was conducted before and after 1 h of demineralization to simulate general oral hygiene habits. Then samples were immersed in 10 mL of demineralized solution and artificial saliva. The pH cycle test lasted for two weeks on the shaker (50 rpm/min, 37 ℃). The demineralized solution and artificial saliva were changed with fresh solution every day. After 14 consecutive days, the surface microstructure of these samples was observed by FE-SEM.

Oxidase (OXD)-Like activity of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets in vitro

The OXD-like activity of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets was investigated by using TMB as the OXD substrate. To screen the optimum concentration, TMB (1 mM) was reacted with VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets at gradient concentrations (10, 20, 50, 100, 200 µg/ mL) that were prepared with acetate buffer solutions at room temperature. Then VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets and TMB were mixed in three different pH acetate buffer solutions (pH 4.5, 5.5, 7.4) to investigate the optimum pH of the acetate buffer solution. The color of the reaction mixture changed indicating the generation of oxidized TMB (TMBox) with the oxidation reaction proceeded. The absorbance at 652 nm of the mixed liquid reactants was measured by using a UV–Vis–NIR spectrophotometer.

Peroxidase (POD)-Like activity of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets in vitro

The POD-like activity assay of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets was investigated by using TMB as the POD substrate with the help of the H2O2 solution. To explore the optimum concentration of the H2O2 solution, TMB (1 mM), VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets, and various concentrations of H2O2 solution (0.1 mM, 1 mM, 10 mM, 20 mM, 100 mM) were mixed in an acetate buffer solution (pH 4.5) at room temperature. The color of the reaction mixture changed indicating the generation of oxidized TMB (TMBox) with the oxidation reaction proceeded. The absorbance at 652 nm of the mixed liquid reactants was measured by a UV–Vis–NIR spectrophotometer.

ROS generation efficiency of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets in vitro

The ROS generation efficiency of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets in the HGF and S. mutans were explored respectively. Firstly, cell groups were divided as follows: Control; VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets; LPS; LPS VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets. After incubation at 37℃ and 5% CO2 for 24 h, the DCFH-DA probe working solution and Hoechst 33,342 staining solution were added to each well respectively. Then, 1 mL S. mutans suspension (1 × 106 CFU/mL) was added to each well to construct the bacterial biofilm. After incubation at 37℃ and 5% CO2 for 12 h, the medium was replaced with fresh BHI medium (1% sucrose) and 10 µg/mL VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets. After another incubation for 12 h, the bacterial biofilms were washed three times gently with PBS and stained with the O11 probe working solution according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Images were collected under the confocal laser confocal microscope (CLSM).

Animal experiments

The animal experiments performed in this study were approved by the Animal Experimental Ethics Committee of Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital affiliated to Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine. Specific pathogen-free (SPF) Sprague Dawley (SD) female rats (Shanghai Jihui Laboratory Animal Care Co., Ltd., China) were obtained at the age of 15 days. All rats were fed with diet and drinking water containing antibiotics for three consecutive days to eliminate the interference of oral microorganisms. S. mutans was grown to be in the logarithmic growth period before inoculation. Then bacteria were harvested at 2,000×g for 5 min and resuspended in fresh BHI medium. 200 µL S. mutans (1 × 108 CFU/mL) was inoculated onto the teeth of each rat twice each day for 3 consecutive days. They were then randomly divided into three treatment groups: (1) VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets; (2) VMT NSs; (3) deionized water (n ≥ 3). The rats’ molar teeth were treated with 100 µL corresponding reagents using a swab by the topical administration and then dried in the air three times daily. Rats were fasting and water deprivation within 30 min before and after treatment. A cariogenic diet 2000 (Trophic Feed Technology Co., Ltd., China) and 5% sucrose-containing water were provided. The treatment was continued for 30 consecutive days. All rats were executed and their molars, palatal mucosa, gingival tissue, and main organs were obtained. The Micro CT images and physical appearances of the carious lesions were observed and photographed. The biocompatibility of VMT/ACP/Dextran composite nanosheets in vivo was assessed by H&E staining for palatal mucosa, gingival tissue, and main organs.

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism 8.0 software was used to analyze the experimental data. All experimental data were reported as the mean ± SD (standard deviation), and each experiment has repeated a minimum of three times. Significant differences between groups were evaluated using a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). When n.s. (not significant), *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001, and ****p < 0.0001, differences were deemed statistically significant.

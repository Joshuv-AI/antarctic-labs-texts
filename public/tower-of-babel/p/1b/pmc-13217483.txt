Impact of time from symptom onset to recanalization in acute anterior circulation large vessel occlusive stroke with same tissue window: A patient-level pooled analysis of the three randomized controlled trials
Authors: Xiang Liu, Gaoming Li, Zhaokun Li, Huafu Ni, Shitao Fan, Dongjing Xie, Linyu Li, Kunxin Lin, Boyu Chen, Sheng Zhou, Yanling Li, Fengli Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAlthough multimodal imaging extends the thrombectomy window to 24 h based on tissue viability, the prognostic value of shorter onset-to-recanalization times within this window is unclear. Using pooled trial data, we assessed this association in patients meeting Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score [ASPECTS] ≥6. We conducted a patient-level pooled analysis of three multicenter randomized trials: DEVT, RESCUE BT, and MARVEL. Multivariable analysis was conducted to evaluate the association between the time from symptom onset to recanalization and 90-day functional independence (modified Rankin Scale [mRS] score of 0–2 and 0–1), any intracranial hemorrhage, and 3-month mortality. Restricted cubic splines were used to model the continuous relationship and identify optimal time thresholds. Among 1931 patients, a shorter onset-to-recanalization time was independently associated with better outcomes. Compared to the >6–12 h and >12–24 h intervals, recanalization within ≤6 h was associated with higher odds of mRS 0–2 (adjusted odds ratio [OR] 0.66, 95% CI 0.53–0.82, p < 0.01; and adjusted OR 0.59, 95% CI 0.45–0.79, p < 0.01, respectively). A significant non-linear relationship was observed (P for nonlinearity = 0.008). The >6–12 h window was associated with increased mortality (adjusted OR 1.53, 95% CI 1.02–2.28, P = 0.04) and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (adjusted OR 1.66, 95% CI 1.32–2.09, p < 0.01). Our analysis revealed a more pronounced association between onset-to-recanalization time and 90-day functional outcomes than previously recognized. Among patients with ASPECTS ≥6 undergoing thrombectomy, expedited treatment was strongly linked to substantially improved neurological recovery.Keywords: Onset-to-recanalization time, Large vessel occlusive stroke, Endovascular thrombectomy, Tissue window

Introduction

Five trials of endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) for stroke pooled analysis has verified that EVT was effective within 6 h of stroke onset in patients with large vessel occlusion (LVO) [1]. The DAWN (DWI or CTP Assessment With Clinical Mismatch in the Triage of Wake-Up and Late Presenting Strokes Undergoing Neurointervention With Trevo) and DEFUSE 3 (Endovascular Therapy Following Imaging Evaluation for Ischemic Stroke) trials provided the first definitive evidence demonstrating the efficacy of EVT for LVO stroke in the extended 6–24 h time window, using imaging criteria to define salvageable tissue [2,3]. Collectively, these findings prompted a paradigm shift in patient selection—from rigid time-based criteria to flexible, imaging-defined tissue window—thereby expanding access to endovascular therapy [4,5]. An increasing number of clinical practitioners are now routinely using the tissue window to guide decisions regarding the necessity of EVT, while the traditional time window concept has progressively diminished in prominence within clinical decisions.

The tissue window concept is rooted in the ischemic penumbra, if reperfusion is achieved rapidly, neurons can regain their electrical function [6]. Previous studies have confirmed the dynamic nature of focal cerebral ischemia and provide robust evidence supporting the principle that time is brain [7]. Crucially, advanced imaging can identify this penumbra beyond 6 h, and subsequent trials have confirmed EVT's superiority over medical management even in these later-presenting patients. This evidence, reinforced by studies in patients with large infarct cores, has solidified the tissue window's central role [8]. However, within a favorable tissue window (ASPECTS ≥6), the prognostic weight of time from symptom onset to recanalization remains undefined. Whether an optimal recanalization time exists within this imaging-defined cohort is unknown.

Therefore, we conducted a patient-level pooled analysis of three prospective randomized controlled trials to further investigate the impact of onset-to-recanalization time on clinical outcomes in patients with anterior circulation LVO selected by a favorable tissue window (ASPECTS ≥6).

Methods

Study design

This study constitutes a patient-level pooled analysis of three multicenter randomized controlled trials: the DEVT trial (Direct Endovascular Treatment for Large Vessel Occlusion Stroke; URL: https://www.chictr.org.cn; unique identifier: ChiCTR-IOR-17013568), the RESCUE BT trial (Intravenous Tirofiban Before EVT for Acute Ischemic Stroke; URL: https://www.chictr.org.cn; unique identifier: ChiCTR-INR-17014167), and the MARVEL trial (Methylprednisolone as Adjunct to EVT for Large-Vessel Occlusion Stroke; URL: https://www.chictr.org.cn; unique identifier: ChiCTR2100051729) [[9], [10], [11]]. The protocols for all three trials were approved by the medical ethics committee of all participating hospitals. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients or their legally authorized representatives before randomization.

Patients and interventions

Patients with acute anterior circulation LVO (intracranial internal carotid artery [ICA], middle cerebral artery [MCA] M1, or MCA M2) and a baseline non-contrast CT ASPECTS of ≥6 who underwent EVT were eligible. Based on the time from symptom onset to recanalization, patients were divided into three groups: early time window (≤6 h), intermediate time window (>6 to ≤12 h), and late time window (>12 to ≤24 h).

EVT should be promptly initiated upon patient admission. Mechanical thrombectomy utilizing either stent retriever, local aspiration techniques, intra-arterial thrombolysis, balloon angioplasty with or without stenting, or any clinically appropriate combination of these interventions was performed at the discretion of the neurointerventional team.

Data collection

Baseline covariates included demographics, admission systolic blood pressure (SBP), vascular risk factors, occlusion sites, prestroke mRS, baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score, baseline ASPECTS, and stroke etiology as classified by the Trial of Org 10,172 in Acute Stroke Treatment (TOAST) criteria [12]. Vascular risk factors included atrial fibrillation, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, diabetes mellitus, prior ischemic stroke, and smoking. Additional Clinical data and outcomes were collected. The data of DEVT, RESCUE BT, and MARVEL trials were assessed centrally by Xinqiao Interventional Core Laboratory, thus ensuring the consistency of interpretation.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was a favorable functional outcome (defined as a mRS score of 0–2 at 90 days). Secondary efficacy outcome was an excellent outcome (mRS, 0–1). Safety outcomes included mortality at 90 days, any intracranial hemorrhage (ICH) within 48 h, and symptomatic ICH (sICH) within 48 h confirmed by the Heidelberg Bleeding Classification based on follow-up computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging [13]. The sICH was characterized by the presence of any parenchymal intracerebral hemorrhage or an elevation in the NIHSS score of ≥4 points relative to the baseline.

Statistical analysis

Analyses were performed in R (version 4.4.2, R Foundation for Statistical Computing). Data are presented as frequency (percentage), mean ± SD, or median (interquartile range [IQR]). Differences in continuous variables were assessed using the Mann–Whitney U test or the Kruskal–Wallis H test, as appropriate. Pearson chi-square test or Fisher exact test for categorical variables. The efficacy and safety outcomes were determined by multivariable regression analysis. Missing data were imputed using a sampling-based imputation method that accounts for the underlying distributional properties of the covariates. For both primary and secondary analyses, unadjusted and adjusted regression analyses were performed. We adjusted for potential confounders, including age, sex, atrial fibrillation, baseline ASPECTS, baseline NIHSS score, stroke etiology, smoking, and intravenous thrombolysis. Restricted cubic splines (RCS) with five knots were used to model non-linear relationships between continuous onset-to-recanalization time and outcomes. We performed sensitivity analyses to assess the robustness of our primary findings, including analysis of each trial separately and analysis of the pooled placebo arms. And interactions of treatment effect with each of the subgroup variables were explored by adding interactions of the subgroup variables with treatment to the multivariable regression. All statistical tests of hypotheses were two-sided, and P values < 0.05 were significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics of patients

Of 2862 patients screened from the three randomized controlled trials, 971 patients were excluded: 904 patients due to ASPECTS <6, 25 patients due to symptom onset to recanalization time >24 h, and 2 patients due to incomplete 90-day mRS data. A total of 1931 patients were included in this study (Fig. 1). The cohort was divided into early (n = 769), intermediate (n = 775), and late (n = 387) windows. The median age was 69 years (IQR, 58–75 years), and 1117 patients (57.8%) were male. The baseline NIHSS score was 17 (IQR, 14–20), the baseline ASPECTS was 8 (IQR, 7–9), and 434 patients (22.5%) underwent intravenous thrombolysis before EVT.

Patients in the early time window had significantly higher rates of atrial fibrillation and lower rates of smoking. Moreover, large artery atherosclerosis related stroke occurred more frequently in the late window than in the intermediate and early window (59.2% vs 47.4% vs 26.8%, respectively), and in the early window, had more frequent stroke of cardiogenic embolism origin (60.6%). Efficacy and safety outcomes

Overall, 969 patients (50.2%) achieved favorable outcome (mRS 0–2) at 90 days. Additionally, 145 patients (7.5%) experienced sICH, and 342 patients (17.7%) died during the follow-up period. Compared with patients in the early window, the likelihood of favorable outcome is relatively lower in the intermediate window and late time window (54.2% vs. 47.4% vs. 47.8%, respectively). Fig. 2 presents the distribution of 90-day mRS score. After adjusting for potential confounding factors, the probability of favorable outcome was significantly lower in both the intermediate (adjusted OR 0.66, 95% CI 0.53–0.82, p < 0.01) and late time window (adjusted OR 0.59, 95% CI 0.45–0.79, p < 0.01) compared to the early time window. The incidence of any ICH was lowest in the early window (26.4% vs. 34.3% intermediate, 32.0% late). Compared with the early time window, adjusted analyses confirmed higher odds of any ICH in both later windows (adjusted OR 1.66, 95% CI 1.32–2.09, p < 0.01; adjusted OR 1.67, 95% CI 1.25–2.24, p < 0.01, respectively). sICH incidence showed a trend in the intermediate window (OR 1.45, 95% CI 0.99–2.14, p = 0.06) that became significant after adjustment (adjusted OR 1.53, 95% CI 1.02–2.28, p = 0.04). No significant differences in 90-day mortality were observed among the groups (Table 2).

RCS analysis revealed a significant non-linear relationship between the time from symptom onset to recanalization and the probability of favorable outcome, excellent outcome, and any ICH (P for nonlinearity = 0.008, P for nonlinearity = 0.002, and P for nonlinearity <0.001, respectively). The probability of functional independence and excellent outcome peaked when recanalization was achieved within approximately 5.2 and 4.4 h, respectively. No non-linear associations was found for mortality (P for nonlinearity = 0.136) (Fig. 3).

Sensitivity analyses confirmed the robustness of the primary finding. In the analysis of the pooled placebo arms, the intermediate time window was significantly associated with worse outcomes compared to the early window (adjusted OR 0.60, 95% CI 0.44–0.82, p < 0.01), while the late window showed a trend toward worse outcomes (adjusted OR 0.69, 95% CI 0.47–1.02, p = 0.06), possibly due to the smaller sample size in that subgroup. In the RESCUE BT and MARVEL trials analyses, shorter onset-to-recanalization time was consistently associated with favorable outcome after multivariable adjustment. In the DEVT trial, a longer onset-to-recanalization time (>6 to ≤12 h) was associated with lower odds of favorable outcome compared with the early window, although the association did not reach statistical significance (adjusted OR 0.47, 95% CI 0.18–1.20, P = 0.12). This lack of significance is likely attributable to the limited sample size of this subgroup (n = 37). Furthermore, no significant interaction was detected between treatment assignment and onset-to-recanalization time (P for interaction = 0.383). Discussion

In this patient-level pooled analysis, we demonstrate that functional outcomes worsen non-linearly with increasing onset-to-recanalization time, with an optimal window for achieving favorable outcome (mRS 0–2) at approximately 5.2 h. No significant differences in mortality were observed across time intervals.

Our findings that functional outcomes decline non-linearly with increasing onset-to-recanalization time are consistent with the established principle that time is brain [[14], [15], [16], [17]]. Prior studies have quantified this linear relationship in broad populations, estimating a 5.3–7.3% reduction in the probability of functional independence per hour of delay [14,15]. The present analysis extends these observations by providing crucial granularity within a rigorously defined cohort. By restricting our analysis to patients with a favorable tissue profile, we isolate the effect of time from the confounding influence of varying infarct core volumes. This methodological refinement may explain why the time-outcome association appears more pronounced in our study. Crucially, our use of RCS modeling revealed a non-linear relationship, identifying specific efficacy thresholds not previously reported: the probability of achieving favorable outcome (mRS 0–2) was optimized when recanalization was achieved within approximately 5.2 h, while excellent outcome (mRS 0–1) were best achieved within 4.4 h. The HERMES meta-analysis also showed that patients with large-vessel ischemic stroke were associated with lower degrees of disability at 3 months when the procedure was performed within 7.3 h of symptom onset [17]. These human clinical thresholds intriguingly align with pre-clinical evidence suggesting a critical 4-h window for effective reperfusion before exacerbated inflammatory injury occurs [18].

The selection of patients by ASPECTS reflects the contemporary shift toward tissue-window-guided therapy. In clinical practice, there has been a gradual shift from advanced imaging techniques (such as DAWN and DEFUSE3) toward the more widely used ASPECTS for tissue window assessment, aimed at increasing the proportion of patients with LVO who may benefit from EVT. Some studies have also demonstrated that patients selected using non-contrast CT exhibit comparable outcomes to those selected via CT perfusion or MRI, which has successfully broadened EVT eligibility [19,20]. Our findings do not contradict this paradigm but rather refine it. We demonstrate that even within this favorably selected population, where the rigid time window has been justifiably de-emphasized, the speed of recanalization remains a powerful, independent determinant of functional recovery. Thus, the tissue window defines who may benefit, while time within that window powerfully predicts the degree of benefit, with a non-linear decay in efficacy beyond 5.2 h.

The safety profile observed in our study—specifically, the increased risk of any ICH with delay but the lack of a significant rise in sICH or mortality in the late window—merits careful interpretation. Olivot et al. demonstrates that the risk of parenchymal hematoma increases with delay from onset to reperfusion (time is bleeding) [21]. This reflects a cumulative, time-sensitive vulnerability of the ischemic microvasculature. A large national clinical practice registry study (the MR CLEAN Registry) similarly failed to demonstrate a significant association between the time delay from symptom onset to reperfusion and the occurrence of sICH [14]. Sadeler et al. also found that a time interval from symptom onset to puncture exceeding 6 h was not associated with an increased risk of sICH (aOR 1.01, 95% CI 0.94–1.10, p = 0.625) [15]. On the other hand, our finding that mortality did not significantly increase in the late window is corroborated by a growing body of evidence from both large-scale registries and randomized trials [22]. Moreover, another time-related study similarly demonstrated that a time interval from symptom onset to puncture exceeding 6 h was not significantly associated with increased mortality [15]. In a real-world study, sICH and in-hospital mortality showed no time-dependent association when the interval from symptom onset to puncture ranged between 6 and 24 h [16]. Study suggests mortality risk is more strongly linked to baseline patient factors such as advanced age, greater post-procedural stroke severity, pre-treatment hyperglycemia, and pre-stroke functional dependence [23]. These findings had already been explained by the “late window paradox” [24]. Patients who present late yet maintain a favorable tissue window represent a biologically selected subgroup. Their slow infarct progression, facilitated by robust collateral circulation, likely mitigates the development of malignant edema and massive, symptomatic hemorrhagic transformation, even as the general propensity for petechial hemorrhage increases with time. Further research is needed to explore this area in order to identify additional underlying factors.

Limitations

The primary strengths of this study lie in its use of pooled, patient-level data from three high-quality multicenter randomized controlled trials, providing a large and rigorously characterized sample (n = 1931). Several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the analysis of time intervals is observational and non-randomized, and thus subject to residual confounding despite multivariable adjustment. Second, the study population was exclusively Chinese, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other ethnic and geographic populations. Future prospective studies in diverse cohorts are warranted to validate these time thresholds and their clinical applicability.

Conclusion

These findings demonstrates that even among patients selected by a favorable tissue window, functional outcomes exhibit a progressive, non-linear deterioration as the time from symptom onset to successful recanalization increases. Importantly, the probability of achieving a favorable functional outcome is maximized when recanalization is accomplished within approximately 5.2 h of symptom onset.

Data collected by blast-furnace sensors
Authors: Vladimir Borisovich Trofimov
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The data represent measured values of the main technological parameters of blast-furnace smelting. Two datasets are provided. The first dataset contains raw, noisy data with gross outliers, downloaded from the dispatch system. The second dataset consists of data that have been validated for reliability and labelled into classes. The collected data were synchronised. The following parameters were recorded at 1-hour intervals: total and specific gas pressure differences, peripheral gas temperature, top gas temperature, CO2 content of top gas, H2 content of top gas, top gas pressure, ore-to-coke ratio, blast flowrate, blast oxygen flowrate, hot blast pressure, hot blast temperature, cold blast pressure, and cold blast temperature. The Si content in hot metal was provided separately in the dataset. This dataset has the form of a multidimensional time series.

Value of the Data

•This dataset is useful because it consists of real-life industrial sensory data. The data can be used to generate models forecasting the Si content in hot metal in real time. The silicon content in hot metal at the furnace taphole is an indicator of the thermal state of the blast-furnace hearth and of process stability (as the furnace heats up, the silicon content in hot metal increases, while cooling in the initial period is characterised by a decrease in silicon content). The silicon content in hot metal also reflects the energy efficiency of the process. The data capture the furnace operation under different conditions, which enables forecasting of the silicon content not only under normal furnace operating mode.•The data may be of interest to specialists engaged in the operational diagnostics of blast-furnace smelting — that is, in the recognition of the blast-furnace operating condition — as well as to the blast-furnace shop superintendent, furnace masters, gas technicians, and burden-feeding operators. Identification of the furnace operating condition based on informative indicators, combined with timely managerial decisions by the furnace operator, helps reduce the specific coke rate, decrease the probability of producing off-spec hot metal, and lower the risk of accidents. This, in turn, contributes to preserving major production assets, reducing occupational injuries among service personnel, and preventing environmental pollution.•The data may also be of interest to researchers and practitioners involved in the development of case bases (knowledge bases), as well as in the creation of algorithms for validating data reliability, filtering, interpolation, smoothing, forecasting, statistical analysis of technological time series, and modelling of the blast-furnace process under various operating conditions. These tasks are highly relevant because the main causes of high energy consumption are difficulties in process control and unreliable information. The factors that most strongly affect the deterioration of blast-furnace performance and the quality of the produced hot metal are errors in monitoring the main technological parameters.

Background

During blast-furnace smelting, despite the high thermal inertia of modern furnaces, considerable deviations of operating parameters from the established mode may occur. The causes of such deviations from the normal furnace run are varied: changes in the chemical and granulometric composition of the burden materials; incorrect actions or delayed responses of process personnel to the changes; failure of primary or auxiliary technological equipment; as well as operator errors. The task of process engineers supervising the smelting is to maintain constant observation of the furnace run, regardless of how stable it may appear at a given moment. Control of the blast-furnace process and adjustment of the operating mode are carried out by means of visual observation, control and measuring instruments, and expert systems. The purpose of data collection was to identify deviations of the main process parameters from the specified values and to classify particular types of deviation of the furnace run from the normal operating mode based on analysis of these data. The data were also required to assess the effectiveness of the automated blast-furnace process control system, to identify the furnace operating condition in real time, and to filter the data in order to reduce noise effects.

Data Description

The dataset consists of two subsets containing the results of changes in the technological parameters of the blast-furnace process during the period from 1 January 2013 to 18 May 2016. The first dataset consists of 29,602 data examples. Each example includes a timestamp composed of six attributes (year, month, day, hour, minute, second) and measurements collected from 26 different sensors, recorded at one-hour intervals. The ore-to-coke ratio is a calculated value obtained from the dispatch system. The Si content in hot metal was determined in the plant laboratory based on hot metal samples. The second dataset consists of 200 data examples that were validated for reliability and labelled into nine classes. Each class represents a specific blast-furnace operating condition: normal run, peripheral run, hot run, cold run, central run, channeling, top burden hang-up, bottom burden hang-up, and tight run [1]. The datasets are stored in XLSX files.

The dataset does not contain any missing values.

Characteristics of numerical attributes of the second dataset.

The indicator of the total gas pressure difference reflects the difference between the gas pressure at the level of the tuyeres and at the top of the blast furnace. The numerical value of the pressure difference is determined by the amount of blast supplied to the furnace, the total resistance of the entire column of burden materials to the gas flow, and the top gas pressure. For the furnace considered, the total pressure difference under full operating parameters with combined blast in the production of conversion hot metal should be in the range of 1.43–1.53 kgf/cm². Continuous operation of the furnace with a total pressure difference below 1.12 kgf/cm² is not permitted.

Specific pressure differences characterise the resistance to gas flow in individual zones along the furnace height. They are determined by measuring gas pressures at different levels: “tuyeres – middle of the shaft” and “middle of the shaft – furnace top.” These differences are more sensitive to changes in the gas-dynamic resistance of the lower and upper parts of the burden column and make it possible to identify the location of zones with increased gas-dynamic resistance.

The value of the lower pressure difference depends on slag-formation conditions, properties of primary slags, coke quality, the thermal state of the furnace, and the condition of the hearth. Its permissible value is determined by the absolute values of these parameters and the design features of the furnace.

When the charge is dumped into the furnace, compaction of the burden layer occurs, thereby increasing the resistance of the compacted material to the gas flow. As the materials descend, loosening takes place, and the upper pressure difference decreases. Impact loads from the descending burden affect mainly the upper layers. The value of the upper pressure difference depends on the gas permeability of the burden, while its permissible value is determined by the strength and granulometric composition of the burden materials, their distribution over the furnace top, the ore-to-coke ratio, and the design features of the furnace.

The peripheral gas temperature is measured by thermocouples installed around the circumference of the furnace. Under the normal operating mode, the readings of these thermocouples adequately reflect the gas-flow distribution in the peripheral ring of the furnace, and the peripheral-gas temperature diagram beneath the armour plates appears as a continuous ribbon of ten parallel or interlacing lines, with a temperature difference between extreme points of 100–150 °C.

The top-gas temperature graph has a sinusoidal profile, associated with the periodic charging of cold burden materials into the furnace, followed by their heating by gas until the subsequent lowering of the large bell, as well as with the periodic passage of the burden ridge formed by the rotating distributor into different furnace-top sectors. The top-gas temperature diagram in the off-takes appears as a continuous ribbon of four interlacing points, with the normal difference between extreme values not exceeding 50–100 °C. In the production of conversion hot metal, the average top gas temperature should be within 100–200 °C.

To determine the Si content in hot metal, three samples are taken from each cast (at the beginning, middle, and end of the stream) under steady flow in the trough, and sent to the laboratory for rapid analysis by the photometric method. To ensure the production of the required steel grades and their subsequent casting in the continuous caster, the hot metal must have a silicon content in the range of 0.35–0.60 %. Maintaining the Si content in this optimal interval is achieved by forecasting this indicator. Such forecasting is relevant for metallurgical enterprises [2], as its implementation should reduce the specific coke rate by 0.3–0.5 % and decrease the output of off-spec hot metal. Based on the presented data, it is possible to forecast the silicon content in hot metal for several (e.g., up to three) future casts.

Under a normal furnace run, the CO2 content of top gas sampled from the dust catcher should be 19–22 % for conversion hot metal. This value depends on furnace loading, combined-blast parameters, and the iron content of the burden. The H2 content is determined by the amount of natural gas and steam injected for humidification and typically ranges from 2 to 10 %.

The maximum allowable top gas pressure for this blast furnace is 1.73 kgf/cm².

The ore-to-coke ratio — the mass of iron-bearing components per tonne of coke — should include sinter, pellets, ore, and other additives containing iron and manganese if they are charged to the furnace. Based on the burden composition, its physico-chemical properties, the grade of hot metal, and the specific operating conditions of the furnace (heating, blast parameters, gas-flow distribution, etc.), the operator determines the optimal value of the ore-to-coke ratio. In practice, it is regulated cycle by cycle by adjusting the coke consumption in the charge.

The blast flowrate, its pressure, and the gas pressure differences in the furnace depend on the following factors: the gas permeability of the burden column, the distribution of the gas flow, the physical state and chemical properties of the slag, the value of the gas pressure beneath the furnace top, the thermal state of the furnace, and the timely and complete release of the hearth from smelting products. The cold-blast pressure diagram almost exactly mirrors the hot-blast pressure diagram.

The blast temperature under a normal furnace run and adequate heating should generally remain constant and be maintained at the maximum level permitted by the condition of the hot stoves and hot-blast system. A prolonged reduction in the hot-blast temperature is permissible only in emergency situations. Oxygen enrichment of the blast increases furnace productivity.

Furnace overheating is usually associated with a marked reduction in heat consumption compared with the heat input into the furnace. This may be caused by changes in the composition or in the chemical and physical properties of the burden materials.Fig. 1Hot run of the blast furnace.Fig 1

Hot run of the blast furnace.

Overheating of the blast furnace is characterised by a gradual increase in the total and lower gas pressure differences (Fig. 1a). The top gas temperature (Fig. 1c) and peripheral gas temperature (Fig. 1b) also rise progressively. As a consequence of overheating, the Si content in hot metal increases as well (Fig. 1d) [3].

A deviation of furnace operation towards overheating results in reduced blast-furnace productivity, changes in the temperature distribution along the height of the burden column, and a shift of the slag-formation zone.

If overheating is detected in time, it can be mitigated by increasing the moisture content of the blast. If this is insufficient, a temporary reduction in hot blast temperature is recommended. Where the overheating is found to be long-lasting, the ore-to-coke ratio must be increased.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

The sensors recording the measurements are placed at various locations around the blast furnace. Their arrangement is shown in the mnemonic diagram presented in Fig. 2.Fig. 2Mnemonic diagram of the automated blast-furnace control system (example of an emergency situation).Fig 2

Mnemonic diagram of the automated blast-furnace control system (example of an emergency situation).

The mnemonic diagram (Fig. 2) shows the blast furnace, dust catcher, hot stoves, throttle group, natural gas manifold, bustle pipe, and pipelines for natural gas, cold blast, hot blast, mixed blast, steam, nitrogen, technical water, boiler feed water, steam to the furnace, steam to the interbell space, steam for blast humidification, oxygen for blast enrichment, and nitrogen for dust suppression. The diagram also shows the top-gas off-takes with indications of top gas pressure, composition of cleaned top gas, off-take gas temperatures, pressure differences inside the furnace, dust-catcher temperature, and blast-furnace gas flowrate.

Each pipeline is accompanied by the parameters of the substance it carries. All numerical parameters are displayed in the following format: parameter designation (F – flowrate, P – pressure, dP – pressure difference, T – temperature, dT – temperature difference), current parameter value, and parameter unit of measurement. If the background of the technological parameter is black and the number is green, the value is within the normal range. If the background is yellow and the number is red, this indicates a warning signal. If the background is red and the number is yellow, this indicates an emergency. If the dust catcher or draught pipe temperature exceeds the emergency or warning limits, a red (emergency) or yellow (pre-emergency) outline respectively appears around the unit.

Any violations of the charging process, operator actions, pre-emergency or emergency situations are monitored by the control system and logged at the visualisation station.

The working volume of the blast furnace is 2000 m³, and its production capacity is 4500 tonnes of hot metal per day. Limitations

The data are real-life industrial measurements and therefore may contain outliers. Moreover, some values lie outside the allowed measuring ranges indicated in Table 3.

Ethics Statement

This research did not involve human or animal subjects, and no data were collected from social media platforms.

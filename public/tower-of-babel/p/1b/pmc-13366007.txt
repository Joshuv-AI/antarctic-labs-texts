IFPHPOS0023 Multimorbidity Profiles and Mortality Inequalities Among Migrant-Origin Populations in Sweden: A Latent Class Analysis
Authors: Courtney Franklin, Daniela Foresta
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMultimorbidity has an adverse multiplicative effect on all health outcomes, including an elevated risk of all-cause mortality. There is evidence of inequalities in multimorbidity patterns when comparing migrant populations to their native counterparts. Emerging research suggests that mortality depends not only on the number of conditions, but also on how conditions cluster within individuals. Most studies have not examined how conditions cluster in ways that reflect shared social and structural exposures. We aimed to identify latent multimorbidity profiles and compare class prevalence and predictors between migrants and native-born individuals.MethodsWe used nationwide Swedish register data to derive latent multimorbidity profiles among adults resident in Sweden at a single index date. Chronic conditions were identified from inpatient and specialist outpatient care records accumulated prior to the index date. Latent class analysis (LCA) was used to identify multimorbidity profiles and we assessed measurement invariance by nativity group-specific LCAs. Finally, class membership was linked to all-cause mortality using survival analysis.ResultsPreliminary latent class models identified multiple distinct multimorbidity profiles, including a low-morbidity profile, and profiles characterised by complex multiple conditions. Analyses will compare profile prevalence and sociodemographic predictors between foreign-born and native-born populations. Survival models will examine variation in short-term mortality risk across identified profiles.ConclusionsUltimately, understanding the differences in multimorbidity profiles amongst migrant and native-born populations and their association with mortality will provide valuable insights to inform practice and policy responses to increasing global migration and changing health dynamics.

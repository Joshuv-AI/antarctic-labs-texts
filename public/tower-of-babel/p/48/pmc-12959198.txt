Randomized, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial evaluating safety, immunogenicity, and reactogenicity of RSVPreF3-Mat in high-risk pregnant women and their infants
Authors: Zourab Bebia, Ilse Dieussaert, Lisa Jose, Anu Kantele, Hanna Välimaa, Guy Middleton, Hemir David Escobar Pirela, Osvaldo Reyes, Marta García Sánchez, Paulino Vigil-De Gracia, Nancy Hua, Martha Kirabo, Patricia Lese, Joon Hyung Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTRespiratory syncytial virus (RSV) causes respiratory illness among infants; maternal vaccination confers passive protection through placental antibody transfer. This phase 3, randomized, placebo-controlled trial evaluated safety, reactogenicity, and immunogenicity of RSV prefusion protein F3 maternal vaccine (RSVPreF3-Mat), administered at 240/7 to 360/7 weeks of gestation to high-risk pregnant women (women with obstetric complications and/or human immunodeficiency virus infection or in pregnant adolescents), and their infants up to 1 year post-birth. Enrollment was stopped and the study unblinded after an increased risk of preterm birth, associated with RSVPreF3 vaccine, was observed in another trial in healthy pregnant women. One hundred and sixty-nine maternal participants received either vaccine or placebo. No serious adverse events in maternal or infant participants were considered vaccine-related. Preterm birth occurred in 18.2% and 19.7% of participants in the vaccine and placebo groups, respectively, with one neonatal death in each group. In the vaccine group, increase in maternal neutralizing antibody titers against RSV subtype A and B was observed at Day 31 post-vaccination and remained high at delivery. Geometric mean ratio of titers at delivery over pre-vaccination for RSV A and B were 8.87- and 8.21-fold, respectively. Similarly, anti-RSVPreF3-Mat immunoglobulin G (IgG) levels showed increase at Day 31 post-vaccination and remained high at delivery (14.12-fold increase over pre-vaccination) with placental transfer ratio of RSVPreF3 IgG-specific antibodies at delivery of 1.33. In this study of high-risk pregnant women, RSVPreF3-Mat demonstrated an acceptable safety profile, induced robust immune responses with successful placental antibody transfer, and showed balanced rates of preterm birth between the groups. Introduction

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) confers a substantial burden of lower respiratory tract illness (LRTI) in young children,1,2 with approximately 3.6 million hospitalizations globally each year in children aged ≤5 years.3 RSV also contributes to significant morbidity and mortality in children aged <6 months, with an estimated 1 in 28 deaths attributable to RSV worldwide.3 Furthermore, in pregnant women, RSV infection is linked to adverse outcomes, particularly pneumonia.4

Immunization of pregnant women provides passive protection for infants through placental transfer of maternal antibodies and is recommended by advisory bodies, such as the World Health Organization and Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, for the prevention of pertussis, tetanus, and influenza infections.5 Accordingly, maternal immunization is a valuable strategy to protect infants aged <6 months against RSV.5–7

A phase 2 trial (RSV MAT-004, NCT04126213) was conducted to evaluate the safety and immunogenicity of the investigational RSV prefusion protein F3 maternal vaccine (RSVPreF3-Mat) candidate in healthy pregnant women and infants born to vaccinated mothers. The trial demonstrated an acceptable safety profile, robust RSV-specific immune responses, and effective placental antibody transfer to newborns.8

Globally, high-risk pregnancies account for up to one-third of all pregnancies.9 Infants born from high-risk pregnancies are more likely to have complications and be more vulnerable to RSV infection.10,11 In this phase 3 study, the safety and immunogenicity of RSVPreF3-Mat were assessed in high-risk pregnant women (adults with obstetric complications, human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection, or both, or adolescents) and their infants. In mid-February 2022 in a separate phase 3 maternal study of RSVPreF3-Mat (RSV MAT-009, NCT04605159), a safety signal of increased risk of preterm birth was flagged by the independent data monitoring committee (IDMC).12 On February 25, 2022, enrollment, randomization, and vaccination in all maternal studies of RSVPreF3-Mat, including the present phase 3 study, were halted and all groups unblinded.12 All study participants continued to be followed for safety until the end of the study.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This phase 3, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial was conducted in nine countries (Brazil, Canada, Finland, India, Italy, Panama, South Africa, Spain, and the United States) from August 3, 2021 to May 30, 2023; the study was unblinded early, on February 25, 2022.

Participants were adult pregnant women with obstetric complications, HIV infection, or both, or adolescent pregnant women with uncomplicated pregnancies, and their infants. Maternal participants eligible for enrollment were aged 15–49 years, were at 240/7 to 360/7 weeks’ gestation (established by last menstrual period or ultrasound), and had a pre-pregnancy body mass index of 18.5–39.9 kg/m2. Eligible obstetric complications or risk factors in the ongoing pregnancy included gestational diabetes, gestational hypertension, pre-eclampsia, fetal growth restriction, history of preterm labor, and uncomplicated twin pregnancy. Infant participants were liveborn babies from the study pregnancy.

The main exclusion criteria were a history of hypersensitivity to any vaccine component, prior receipt of an RSV vaccine, poorly controlled preexisting medical conditions or obstetric complications based on the investigator’s clinical judgment, multiple pregnancy with three or more fetuses, or complicated twin pregnancy. For a complete list of exclusion criteria, see Supplementary Material. Before enrollment, all maternal participants provided written, witnessed, or thumb-printed informed consent for their own and their infant’s participation. If required by local regulations, consent for infant participants was also obtained after birth. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences International Ethical Guidelines, and Good Clinical Practice Guidelines. The trial is registered on clinicaltrialsregister.eu (EudraCT: 2021-000994-96) and clinicaltrials.gov (NCT04980391), and the protocol was reviewed and approved by relevant independent ethics committees or institutional review boards in each participating country.

Study duration for maternal participants was approximately 10–11 months (from screening to 181 days post-delivery) and approximately 1 year for infant participants (from birth to 366 days post-birth, covering one complete RSV season in most infants) (Figure 1). On Day 1 (visit 1), maternal participants were randomized 2:1 using an automated, internet-based system (connect SBIR v8, an internal system at GSK) and received either a single intramuscular 120 µg dose of RSVPreF3-Mat or placebo. At the same visit, unborn infant participants were randomized 1:1:1 into subcohorts for blood sample collection to assess immunogenicity at Day 43, 121, or 181 post-birth. The randomization algorithm used a stratification procedure to account for maternal age (<18, ≥18 years), and a minimization procedure for gestation (<280/7, ≥280/7 weeks), HIV infection, obstetric complication status, and center, at the time of vaccination.

Study objectives

Primary safety objectives were to evaluate the safety and reactogenicity up to 42 days post-delivery of a single intramuscular dose of RSVPreF3-Mat vs. placebo, administered to maternal participants. This evaluation considered adverse events (AEs), pregnancy outcomes, and pregnancy and neonatal AEs of special interest (AESIs). Safety was assessed in infant participants up to 1 year post-birth. Secondary safety objectives were to evaluate AEs, worsening of preexisting medical conditions and/or obstetric complications up to 180 days post-delivery, as well as the occurrence of RSV-associated medically attended respiratory tract illness (MA-RTI) in maternal participants during the same period. In infant participants, medically assessed RSV-associated LRTIs and RSV-associated hospitalizations up to 365 days post-birth were evaluated.

Primary immunogenicity objectives were to assess humoral immune responses to RSVPreF3-Mat (RSV subtype A (hereafter, RSV-A) neutralizing antibody titers and immunoglobulin G (IgG) concentrations) at the time of delivery in maternal participants and at the time of birth in infant participants, as well as the placental transfer of RSV-specific antibodies. Secondary immunogenicity objectives were to assess antibody titers against RSV-A, RSVPreF3-Mat, and RSV subtype B (hereafter, RSV-B) up to Day 31 post-dose in maternal participants, and up to Day 181 post-birth in infant participants.

Outcomes and assessments

Safety assessments

Solicited (administration-site or systemic) AEs were recorded from Day 1 to 7 post-dose, unsolicited AEs from Day 1 to 30 post-dose, and serious AEs (SAEs), AEs or SAEs leading to withdrawal, and medically attended AEs (MAEs) from Day 1 to 42 post-delivery. For infant participants, the primary safety outcomes were the number with AESIs from birth to 42 days post-birth, and SAEs, (S)AEs leading to study withdrawal, and MAEs from birth to 42, 180, and 365 days post-birth. Safety monitoring measures for maternal and infant participants continued over the study duration.

Nasal swabs were taken from maternal participants with MA-RTI or hospitalization thought to be RSV-associated and analyzed using quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction for RSV-A and RSV-B. Surveillance for respiratory tract infections for maternal participants (nasal swabs), and infant participant blood samples, at visits 2, 3, and 4 post-birth were discontinued after unblinding. After protocol amendment, placenta samples were taken at delivery when feasible to support possible safety assessments, if considered necessary. An IDMC external to the study sponsor reviewed cumulative unblinded safety data periodically. This was the same IDMC that reviewed RSV MAT-009, in which the safety signal of increased risk of preterm birth was identified.

Immunogenicity assessments

RSV-A and RSV-B neutralizing antibody (nAb) titers, and IgG antibodies specific to RSVPreF3-Mat were assessed. Maternal immunogenicity outcomes were assessed from blood samples collected on Day 1 (pre-vaccination), Day 31, and at delivery. Infant humoral responses were evaluated from cord blood (or blood samples at delivery), and blood samples at Day 43, 121, or 181 according to the subcohort. Placental transfer was assessed by the ratio between cord blood and maternal RSVPreF3-Mat IgG at delivery. RSV-A and RSV-B nAb titers were assessed using an RSV serum neutralization assay. Anti-RSVPreF3-Mat IgG antibodies were measured using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay. For further details of the assays, see Supplementary Material.

Statistical analyses

Approximately 378 eligible maternal participants and their infants were planned to be enrolled and randomized to achieve at least 340 evaluable participants, including at least 18 (5%) adolescent participants. That would have provided a 72%, 92%, and 98% probability of observing at least one AE if the true AE rate was 0.5%, 1%, or 1.5%, respectively, with 252 subjects in the RSVPreF3-Mat vaccine group. All maternal participants who received RSVPreF3-Mat or placebo and their liveborn infants (the exposed set [ES]) were evaluated for safety and reactogenicity. Reactogenicity analysis was performed on the solicited safety set that included all maternal participants in the exposed set who have solicited safety data. Per safety outcomes, the percentages of maternal or infant participants were tabulated along with exact two-sided 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

Immunogenicity was assessed in the per-protocol set (PPS), which included all maternal participants who received the study intervention and who had immunogenicity data available for at least one post-dose visit. Infant participants included in the PPS were born ≥4 weeks post-maternal vaccination from mothers in the PPS, with immunogenicity data from at least one post-birth time point. Geometric mean titers (GMTs) for RSV-A and RSV-B and geometric mean concentrations (GMCs) for RSVPreF3-Mat IgG antibodies were tabulated with 95% CIs and used to calculate geometric mean ratios of antibody titer or concentration pre-dose and at delivery. Geometric means of placental transfer ratios (PTRs) were tabulated with 95% CIs, and the percentages of infants with a PTR ≥1 were tabulated with exact 95% CIs. PTRs were calculated using RSVPreF3-Mat IgG-specific antibody concentrations obtained from cord blood (or blood sample collected within 3 days of birth from infants) and the maternal blood sample at delivery.

Results

Study participants

In total, 186 maternal participants were enrolled before February 25, 2022, with 169 participants in the ES: RSVPreF3-Mat, n = 113 (66.9%); placebo, n = 56 (33.1%). Of participants in the ES, 159 (94.1%) completed the study up to Day 181 post-delivery (Figure 2A). Of the 198 infant participants born to exposed mothers, 185 completed the study (Figure 2B). For maternal participants (ES), median age was 32.0 (range 15.0–44.0) years at the time of study intervention, 19 (11.2%) were aged <18 years, and 45% were White. For infant participants, median gestation was 38.2 (range 29.4–42.3) weeks at birth, and 37 (18.7%) had a gestation of <37 weeks at birth. Overall, demographics and baseline characteristics were comparable between groups for maternal and infant participants (Table 1).

Safety

The most frequently reported solicited administration-site event was pain, reported by 50 (44.2%) maternal participants in the RSVPreF3-Mat group vs. 8 (14.8%) in the placebo group (Figure 3). Headache was the most frequently reported solicited systemic event (45 [39.8%] and 21 [38.9%] participants, respectively), followed by fatigue and nausea. In maternal participants with preexisting HIV infection, the most frequently reported solicited systemic events were headache (55.0%), abdominal pain (40.0%), and nausea (33.0%). Grade 3 fatigue was reported by one participant with HIV infection in the RSVPreF3-Mat group. The median duration of any solicited systemic event was ≤2.5 days.

Grade 3 was defined as significant injection site pain at rest that prevented normal daily activities, injection site redness/swelling >100 mm in diameter, fever >39°C, and for all the other AEs preventing normal activity.

The rate of unsolicited AEs during the 30 days post-dose was balanced between the two participant groups. Overall, at least one unsolicited AE was reported by 39 (34.5%) maternal participants in the RSVPreF3-Mat group and 21 (37.5%) in the placebo group (Table 2), and grade 3 unsolicited AEs by 6 (5.3%) and 1 (1.8%) participant, respectively. The most frequently reported unsolicited AEs were related to pregnancy, the puerperium, and perinatal conditions. Four (30.8%) maternal participants aged <18 years in the RSVPreF3-Mat group and 1 (16.7%) in the placebo group reported at least one unsolicited event. Unsolicited AEs considered related to the study intervention were reported by 4 (3.5%) maternal participants in the RSVPreF3-Mat group (false labor, reduced fetal movements, diarrhea, and injection-site bruising) and 2 (3.6%) in the placebo group (upper respiratory tract infection and oropharyngeal pain). Among these, one unsolicited MAE of reduced fetal movement and one unsolicited MAE of abdominal pain (false signs of labor) (both in the RSVPreF3-Mat group), and one unsolicited MAE of upper respiratory tract infection (in the placebo group), were considered related to the study intervention by the investigators. At least one unsolicited MAE was reported by 31 (27.4%) maternal participants in the RSVPreF3-Mat group and 18 (32.1%) in the placebo group.

The occurrence of MAEs was comparable between groups. In the 6-month post-delivery period, at least one MAE was reported in 69 (61.1%) maternal participants in the RSVPreF3-Mat group and 34 (60.7%) in the placebo group (Table 2). From birth to study end, at least one MAE was reported in 114 (86.4%) and 50 (75.8%) infant participants in the RSVPreF3-Mat and placebo groups, respectively (Table 3). None of the MAEs were considered related to the study intervention by the investigator. RSV-associated LRTI (none severe) were reported in 1 (0.8%) and 2 (3.0%) infant participants from the RSVPreF3-Mat and placebo groups, respectively. RSV-associated hospitalization was reported in 1 (0.8%) infant participant in the RSVPreF3-Mat group, while no cases were reported in the placebo group.

Worsening of preexisting medical conditions and/or obstetric complications occurred in 5 participants (4.4%) in the RSVPreF3-Mat group and 5 (8.9%) in the placebo group. Gestational hypertension was the most frequently reported event, occurring in 2 participants (3.6%) in the placebo group. No RSV-associated MA-RTI were reported among the 21 maternal participants who underwent RTI surveillance, and since enrollment and vaccination were stopped due to an identified safety signal, nasal swab collection was discontinued.

Overall, there were no withdrawals due to AEs or SAEs in maternal participants. Two infant participants were withdrawn (1 [0.8%] and 1 [1.5%] in the RSVPreF3-Mat and placebo groups, respectively) due to SAEs (both neonatal death). Up to 6 weeks post-delivery, minor congenital anomalies were reported for 14 (10.6%) and 9 (13.6%) infant participants in the RSVPreF3-Mat and placebo groups, respectively; at least one major congenital anomaly was reported for 4 (3.0%; one case each of ventricular septal defect, patent ductus arteriosus, cytogenetic abnormality, atrial septal defect) and 1 (1.5%; congenital ankyloglossia) infant participants, respectively.

Immunogenicity

Before study intervention, all maternal participants were seropositive for RSV-A and RSV-B nAbs and RSVPreF3-Mat IgG-specific antibodies. At Day 31 post-vaccination, nAb titers against RSV-A and RSV-B increased 14.94- and 13.29-fold, respectively, in the RSVPreF3-Mat group; corresponding results in the placebo group were 0.89- and 0.85-fold, respectively. At delivery, the RSVPreF3-Mat group had nAb titers against RSV-A and RSV-B that remained 8.87- and 8.21-fold higher, respectively, than pre-vaccination levels; these titers remained higher than values in the placebo group at all time points post-intervention. Figure 4 presents RSV-A and RSV-B nAb GMTs in mother and infant participants. RSVPreF3-Mat IgG GMCs remained above the pre-vaccination titers and were higher in the RSVPreF3-Mat group compared to placebo at all timepoints post administration (Supplementary Figure S1).

In infant participants, the highest IgG concentrations were observed at, or soon after, birth and declined until Day 181 post-birth. GMTs and GMCs in the RSVPreF3-Mat group were higher than in the placebo group at all time points. The RSVPreF3-Mat IgG GMC was higher in cord blood (or peripheral infant blood within 72 hours of birth) than in maternal blood taken at delivery (Supplementary Figure S1). The geometric mean PTR was 1.33 (95% CI: 1.04; 1.70) and 2.30 (95% CI: 1.63; 3.25) for the RSVPreF3-Mat and placebo groups, respectively. Most (74.4%, 95% CI: 63.9; 83.2) mother–infant pairs in the intervention group had a PTR >1.00. Analysis of impact of time from RSVPreF3-Mat administration to delivery on antibody titers showed that an interval of ≥2 weeks between study intervention administration and delivery resulted in a robust antibody titer or concentration in the cord blood in infants.

Discussion

RSVPreF3-Mat was an investigational maternal RSV vaccine in development for administration in the late second and third trimester of pregnancy, with the aim of preventing RSV-associated LRTI in infants through placental transfer of maternal antibodies. In this study, the safety and immunogenicity of RSVPreF3-Mat were evaluated in adult pregnant women with obstetric complications, HIV infection, or both, and in adolescent pregnant women with uncomplicated pregnancies.

In the solicited safety set, pain was the most common administration-site event, reported more frequently in the RSVPreF3-Mat group (44.2%) than in the placebo group (14.8%). Similar differences were observed in a phase 2 (52.0% vs 15.2%) and phase 3 (41.9% vs 14.9%) studies investigating the safety and immunogenicity of the RSVPreF3 vaccine in pregnant women.8,13,14 In another study of a bivalent RSV prefusion F protein-based (RSVpreF) vaccine in pregnant women, pain was the most common injection-site event, reported in 41% of vaccine recipients versus 10% of placebo recipients. Most events were mild to moderate and transient in nature, consistent with the findings of these earlier studies.15 One possible explanation for the differences observed in solicited events, such as injection-site pain, is that vaccine antigens are recognized by the body as potential pathogens, activating local immune cells and inducing the release of mediators such as vasodilators and cytokines. This localized immune response results in transient inflammation, commonly presenting as pain, redness, and swelling at the injection site.16

The occurrence of unsolicited AEs was comparable between the RSVPreF3-Mat and placebo groups. In participants with preexisting obstetric complications, HIV infection, or both, the reporting of unsolicited AEs was similar to the overall reporting rate. No vaccine-related SAEs were reported in maternal or infant participants, consistent with previous phase 1/2 studies with the same vaccine in healthy participants.8,17 Although, the incidence of preeclampsia (6.2% vs. 3.6%) was higher in the vaccine group than in the placebo, the small number of cases does not allow meaningful comparison. Also, the incidence observed in the vaccine group is in line with what is reported in the general obstetric population.18,19 In this study, RSV-associated hospitalization was reported in 1 (0.8%) participant in the RSVPreF3-Mat group, while no cases were reported in the placebo group.

The IDMC of the RSV MAT-009 trial (NCT04605159), which involved women with uncomplicated pregnancies, flagged an imbalance in the number of preterm births and neonatal deaths in RSV MAT-009.12,20 The same IDMC reviewed the present study. Based on further analysis of RSV MAT-009 data (Day 43 post-birth analysis), the imbalance in the number of preterm births was considered an identified risk for pregnant women following RSVPreF3-Mat vaccination during the second or third trimester. By contrast, the observed numerical imbalance in neonatal deaths was not considered an independent safety signal but rather a consequence of the imbalance in the number of preterm births. No other safety signals have been observed in maternal or infant participants in other RSVPreF3-Mat trials.

In the present study, the incidence of preterm birth was balanced between groups (RSVPreF3-Mat, 18.2%; placebo, 19.7%). One neonatal death was reported in the RSVPreF3-Mat group (0.8%) and one in the placebo group (1.5%). There were significant differences between this and the aforementioned study. Although RSV MAT-009 enrolled healthy adult pregnant women with singleton uncomplicated pregnancies, the present study enrolled a heterogeneous population, comprising adult women with complicated pregnancies and adolescent pregnant women without complicated pregnancies. The sample size of the present study was considerably smaller and may not have been large enough to identify AEs with low incidence rates. Prolonged safety monitoring of maternal participants who received either RSVPreF3-Mat or placebo and who conceived following the intervention was implemented.20

Vaccination with RSVPreF3-Mat induced a robust immune response in maternal participants as compared with baseline and with the placebo group at all time points after vaccination. At Day 31 post-vaccination, maternal RSV-A nAb titers had increased 14.94-fold and RSV-B titers 13.29-fold in the RSVPreF3-Mat group; these titers remained 8.87-fold and 8.21-fold higher than pre-vaccination levels at delivery, respectively. Results from the phase 2 RSV MAT-004 study showed similar trends, with 14.90- and 13.20-fold increases in RSV-A and RSV-B nAb titers at Day 31 post-vaccination in the RSVPreF3-Mat group, respectively.8 Successful maternal antibody placental transfer was achieved, with higher levels of RSVPreF3-Mat antibodies in infants at birth as compared with those observed in maternal blood samples at delivery. However, due to the smaller sample size, the 95% CI for the geometric mean of the placental transfer ratio was wider in the placebo group compared with the vaccine group, reflecting greater variability. Evidence from another study of maternal RSVPreF3 vaccine in pregnant women with uncomplicated pregnancies evaluated placental transfer of antibodies in a larger subset of approximately 150 vaccinated and 60 placebo recipients. The geometric mean of the placental transfer ratio in the vaccinated subjects (1.46 [95% CI: 1.35–1.58]) was comparable to those who received placebo (1.39 [95% CI: 1.22–1.59]).13 The literature indicates that placental transfer efficiency may differ between natural infection-induced and vaccine-induced antibodies for multiple reasons, including differences in the induced IgG subclasses.21,22 However, the present study was not designed to elucidate such potential differences in the population studied. The available data suggests that vaccination during pregnancy consistently yields higher maternal titers in neonatal circulation, which translates into clinically relevant protection.23

The available data from the present study suggest that RSVPreF3-Mat is well tolerated (there were few non-serious side effects related to the study vaccine) in pregnant women with obstetric complications, HIV infection, or both, or adolescent age when administered during pregnancy.

Study limitations included the mandatory unblinding following the preterm birth safety signal in RSV MAT-009,12 which may have introduced bias into subsequent AE reporting. The halt in enrollment and vaccination resulted in a smaller-than-planned sample size and fewer participants within subgroups, which made interpretation of results and observation of possible trends challenging. Similarly, due to the discontinuation in collecting infant samples, the number of participants with samples available for analysis at later time points was low, making interpretation of the results in infant participants challenging at these time points. Further, as the study was conducted during the COVID-19 pandemic, social restrictions likely limited the transmission of RSV. Also, although the study included pregnant women associated with conditions at increased risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes, the heterogeneity in participant characteristics could have influenced the results, particularly the incidence of preterm birth. This variability limits the uniform extrapolation of findings across all high-risk subgroups and should be considered when interpreting and generalizing the results to broader risk categories.

In the present study, RSVPreF3-Mat was well tolerated in high-risk pregnant women aged 15–49 years with conditions associated with an increased likelihood of adverse pregnancy outcomes. Robust immune responses against RSV-A and RSV-B were observed in maternal participants, and placental transfer of antibodies to their infants was successful. The findings did not indicate that preexisting obstetric complications, HIV positivity, or adolescent age adversely affected the vaccine’s safety profile, immunogenicity or maternal-fetal antibody transfer. These results provide valuable insights to inform the design of future vaccine studies targeting similar high-risk populations.

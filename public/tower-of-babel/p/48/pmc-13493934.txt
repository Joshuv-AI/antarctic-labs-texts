Targeted polar entropy regulation enables superior energy-storage in tungsten bronze multilayer capacitors
Authors: Haonan Peng, Jiyang Xie, Jiaqi Li, Teng Lu, Lingling Wei, Wanbiao Hu, Yun Liu, Zhen Liu, Genshui Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Tetragonal tungsten bronze (TTB) ceramics have emerged as promising candidates for dielectric energy storage due to their intrinsic multi-site architectures. However, the limited energy storage performance achieved so far remains insufficient for advanced electronic and pulsed power applications. Here, we propose a targeted polar-entropy regulation strategy via minor multi-element substitution at the polar-active B-sites, enabling precise modulation of polar displacements and weakened coupling among polar nanoregions. Atomic-scale characterization reveals site-dependent structural responses, which collectively reshape local polarization configurations and dipolar correlations. As a result, we demonstrate the feasibility of TTB ceramics for state-of-the-art multilayer energy-storage device applications, achieving an outstanding recoverable energy density (Wrec) of 17.6 J·cm−3 with a high efficiency of 96.8%, corresponding to a high figure of merit (WF) of 550. Moreover, excellent thermal stability (ΔWrec ≤ 2.0%) is achieved, and a highest Wrec of 15.0 J·cm−3 is maintained over a wide temperature range (−40 to 125 °C). This work offers new insights into polarization regulation and provides an effective pathway for developing high-performance energy storage dielectric capacitors.

Introduction

Multilayer ceramic capacitors (MLCCs) are indispensable components in modern electronic systems, underpinning applications ranging from microwave communications to hybrid electric vehicles and distributed energy infrastructures owing to their intrinsically high power density and ultrafast electrostatic charge-discharge capability1–4. However, the limited recoverable energy density (Wrec) and efficiency (η) of existing dielectric materials severely constrain further device miniaturization and functional integration5–8. Overcoming these intrinsic performance bottlenecks has therefore become a central challenge in the development of next-generation MLCCs9. Accordingly, achieving high Wrec with minimal energy loss requires a delicate balance among large Pm, minimized Pr, and elevated Eb10–12.

To date, research on lead-free energy-storage MLCCs has been dominated by perovskite-structured dielectrics, including BaTiO31, Bi0.5Na0.5TiO313, NaNbO32, AgNbO314, and BiFeO3 systems15. The structure-property relationships governing perovskite dielectrics are now largely understood, with Wrec exceeding 10 J·cm−3 and η above 90%16,17. However, owing to the relatively simple ABO3-type structural framework with limited crystallographic sites, further performance improvements increasingly rely on complex compositional and microstructural tuning. However, the simultaneous attainment of high energy density (Wrec > 15 J·cm−3) and high efficiency (η > 95%) in perovskite‑based MLCCs remains challenging. This motivates the exploration of alternative ferroelectric crystal structures beyond the perovskite framework that offer greater structural flexibility and richer degrees of freedom for polarization regulation.

Tetragonal tungsten bronze (TTB) structures represent a distinctive class of ferroelectrics. Their framework consists of a corner-sharing network of crystallographically inequivalent B1O6 and B2O6 octahedra, accommodating multiple cation sites, including 12-fold coordinated A1 sites, 15-fold coordinated A2 sites, and 9-fold coordinated C sites, within the general formula (A1)2(A2)4C4(B1)2(B2)8O30 (Fig. 1a). This structural complexity endows TTB ceramics with an expanded compositional and configurational design space for tailoring polarization behavior, dielectric loss, and breakdown strength18–20. Recent studies have demonstrated the potential of TTB materials for relaxor engineering and energy storage, achieving a Wrec of up to 11 J·cm−3 21,22. However, despite these promising advances at the material level, the integration of TTB ceramics into MLCC architectures remains largely unexplored. In particular, their compatibility with standard MLCC processing, electrode materials and high electric field operation in multilayer configurations has not yet been systematically established. At a fundamental level, ferroelectricity in TTB oxides originates from the off-center displacements of ferroelectric-active B-site cations within oxygen octahedra. These displacements are governed not only by distortions of the octahedral framework but also by remote effects associated with disordered cation distributions within the A-site channels23,24. Benefiting from the pronounced geometric and coordination heterogeneity of the A-site channels in the TTB lattice, prevailing strategies have typically introduced A-site disorder to perturb long-range polar correlations and thereby induce relaxor behavior. However, such A-site engineering primarily regulates polarization through indirect structural perturbations, often requiring substantial substitution to produce appreciable modulation21,25,26. In contrast, the polarization-active B-site sublattice in TTB structures offers a more direct approach to tuning dipolar interactions, owing to its central role in ferroelectricity. Relaxor TTB systems exhibit an intrinsic dilute dipolar configuration, in which highly polarized Nb1 chains are spatially separated by a weakly polar Nb2 sublattice27,28. In this architecture, polarization is largely confined to the Nb1 chains, while the weakly polar Nb2 framework suppresses polarization coherence between adjacent NbO6 octahedral chains. This structural feature renders direct manipulation of the polarization-active B-site sublattice a more intrinsic and efficient pathway for tuning dipolar interactions and polarization dynamics, thereby offering a promising pathway toward designing high-performance TTB-based energy-storage ceramics.

Inspired by this structural feature, we propose a targeted polar entropy regulation strategy to precisely tailor relaxor behavior in TTB ceramics (Fig. 1b). Specifically, a limited amount (<10%) of multiple equimolar heterogeneous cations is introduced into the polar-active B-sites to deliberately increase polar entropy and induce controlled polar disorder. Compared with previously reported A-sites engineering that typically requires higher substitution levels, such a low-level B-sites modification proves more effective in regulating polarization behavior. Guided by this concept, a series of lead-free TTB compositions, (Sr0.5Ba0.5)(Nb2-xSbx/4Tax/4Tix/4Wx/4)O6 (denoted as SBN-xM, x = 0.00, 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, and 0.20), were rationally designed. Sr2+ and Ba2+ occupy the A-site channels, while Nb5+, Sb5+, Ta5+, Ti4+, and W6+ reside at the BO6 octahedral sites. This targeted polar entropy regulation enables efficient modulation of local BO6 octahedral distortion, weakens the strong coupling among polar nanoregions, and delays polarization saturation. Leveraging the optimized SBN-0.15M composition, TTB-based MLCCs were successfully fabricated, delivering an outstanding Wrec of 17.6 J·cm−3 with a high η of 96.8%, together with a high figure of merit of 550. Moreover, the TTB-MLCCs exhibit excellent thermal stability, maintaining a stable Wrec of 15.0 ± 0.1 J·cm−3 and a high η of 95.1 ± 2.1% within −40 to 125 °C. These results establish targeted polar entropy regulation as an effective paradigm for designing high-performance lead-free dielectric materials and demonstrate the practical viability of tungsten bronze-structured multilayer capacitors for advanced energy storage applications.

Results and discussion

Energy storage performance of TTB-MLCCs

The TTB-MLCCs reported in the present work were prepared by a tape-casting process with Pt inner electrodes. As shown in Fig. 1c, the photograph, cross-sectional SEM image, and corresponding elemental distribution mappings collectively reveal a well-defined multilayer configuration. After sintering at 1200 °C, the TTB-MLCCs exhibit dense and coherent electrode-dielectric interfaces, with uniform dielectric layers of ~3.7 μm in thickness. No interfacial cracking is observed, and only limited residual porosity is present. Elemental mapping further confirms the absence of significant interdiffusion, notably without detectable migration of electrode metals into the dielectric layers, ensuring a stable electric-field distribution and suppressed leakage pathways in the multilayer architecture29,30. As a direct consequence of the optimized device microstructure and intrinsic relaxor dielectric behavior, the TTB-MLCCs display extremely slim P–E loops (Figs. 1d, S1). With increasing electric field, Pm gradually increases to 35.5 µC·cm−2, while Pr remains below 0.6 µC·cm−2 even under a high electric field of 1480 kV·cm−1. Consistently, the current-electric field (I–E) curves are smooth and highly symmetric, indicative of a continuous and reversible polarization process with negligible leakage contribution, thereby effectively suppressing hysteresis loss. Benefiting from this favorable polarization behavior, the TTB-MLCCs deliver a high Wrec of 17.6 J·cm−3 together with an exceptional η of 96.8%, enabling further device miniaturization and integration (Fig. 1d, e). This combination of high Wrec and η exceeds the performance of existing TTB-based ceramics, particularly with respect to the ultrahigh energy-storage efficiency, which exceeds prior reports on TTB-based MLCCs21,22,28,31–40 (Fig. 1f). Notably, the figure of merit, defined as WF = Wrec/(1–η), reaches an outstanding value of 550, further underscoring the superior comprehensive energy storage performance of the TTB-MLCCs1,4,6,28,41–49 (Fig. 1g).

Polar entropy-driven electric properties evolution in SBN-xM ceramics

To elucidate the structural origin of the superior energy storage performance of the MLCCs, we systematically investigated the structural, dielectric, and ferroelectric behaviors of the SBN-xM series. All SBN-xM ceramics exhibit a pure TTB phase. This is confirmed by X-ray Diffraction (XRD) patterns (Fig. S2), where the diffraction peaks progressively shift toward higher angles with increasing x, indicating lattice contraction due to the incorporation of smaller-radius dopant cations. Meanwhile, the enhanced splitting of the (002) and (620) reflections suggests reduced structural symmetry and enhanced local lattice distortion. Rietveld refinement results further reveal a suppressed ferroelectric character, as reflected by the decreased tetragonality (c/a) from 0.3164 to 0.3151 with increasing x (Fig. S3, Table S1). Concomitantly, local structural disorder is significantly enhanced, as indicated by Raman spectroscopy (Fig. 2a). The redshift of the ν₂ and ν₅ vibrational modes indicates softened cation-anion interactions, corresponding to an expansion of the BO6 octahedra and a weakening of the B–O covalent bonds21,50. Meanwhile, the progressive broadening of Raman bands reflects increased short-range disorder induced by multi-element B-site substitution. The temperature-dependent permittivity and tanδ measured from −120 °C to 200 °C over frequencies ranging from 10 Hz to 100 kHz further reveal the evolution toward relaxor behavior (Figs. 2b, S4). As x increases, the Curie temperature (Tm) shifts from ~90 °C for SBN-0.00M to below room temperature (−77.5 °C for SBN-0.20M). Simultaneously, the frequency dispersion of the dielectric peak becomes more pronounced, with ΔT (Tm,100 kHz-Tm,10 Hz) increasing from 2.4 °C to 67.3 °C. The relaxor diffuseness factor γ, evaluated using the modified Curie-Weiss law (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\frac{1}{{{{{\rm{\varepsilon }}}}}_{r}}-\frac{1}{{{{{\rm{\varepsilon }}}}}_{m}}=\frac{{\left(T-{T}_{m}\right)}^{{{{\rm{\gamma }}}}}}{C}$$\end{document}1εr−1εm=T−TmγC), increases from 1.20 to 1.89 (Fig. S6). Meanwhile, the Burns temperature gradually decreases from approximately 105.4 °C (SBN-0.00M) to around 9.8 °C for the SBN-0.15M composition (Fig. S7), further indicating the emergence of a room-temperature superparaelectric state composed of weakly coupled PNRs16,52,53. Importantly, the enhanced relaxor character is accompanied by a pronounced suppression of dielectric loss, which decreases from 5.56% (SBN-0.00 M) to 0.16% (SBN-0.15M), directly benefiting energy efficiency and thermal reliability54. To characterize their polarization response, the P–E loops of SBN-xM ceramics were measured at 200 kV·cm−1 and 10 Hz (Fig. 2c). The pristine SBN-0.00M exhibits slight relaxor behavior with a large Pm of 24.54 μC·cm−2 and a sizable Pr of 5.96 μC·cm−2. With increasing B-site disorder, the P–E loops become significantly slimmer and exhibit markedly reduced hysteresis. Although Pm gradually decreases from 24.54 μC·cm−2 to 14.72 μC·cm−2, Pr is drastically suppressed to 0.96 μC·cm−2, yielding a maximum Pm/Pr ratio of 20.85 for SBN-0.15M (Fig. 2d). This balance between a high saturated polarization and minimal remanent polarization represents an ideal polarization configuration for dielectric energy-storage applications.

Scanning electron microscopy of the SBN-xM series reveals a pronounced microstructural evolution with increasing multi-element substitution (Fig. S8). Pure SBN exhibits columnar grains associated with anisotropic growth along the [001] direction. In contrast, the introduction of multiple B-site cations effectively suppresses this intrinsic growth anisotropy, leading to a reduction in elongated rod-like grains32. The incorporation of cations with different ionic radii introduces enhanced lattice distortion and hinders grain-boundary migration, leading to significant grain refinement55. Consequently, the average grain size decreases from 2.54 μm in SBN-0.00M to 1.09 μm in SBN-0.20M. The increased fraction of insulating grain boundaries effectively suppresses charge transport pathways, thereby contributing to improved dielectric breakdown strength56,57. Consistent with this microstructural refinement, the leakage current density decreases by nearly two orders of magnitude, from 4.74 × 10−6 A·cm−2 for SBN-0.00M to a minimum value of 2.05 × 10−8 A·cm−2 for SBN-0.15M (Fig. 2e), indicating a pronounced suppression of electrical conductivity and conduction loss58,59. In addition, the optimized composition exhibits substantially reduced strain of 0.019% for SBN-0.15M (Fig. S9), compared with the relatively large electric-field-induced strain of 0.040% in SBN-0.00M. The reduced electromechanical strain suppresses the nucleation of microcracks at grain boundaries, thereby preventing avalanche breakdown initiated at crack tips and further enhancing the electrical breakdown strength48. This synergistic optimization of polarization response and electrical reliability enables the SBN-0.15 M ceramic to deliver outstanding energy-storage performance, achieving a high energy density of 9.33 J·cm−3 with an efficiency of 93.2% under its breakdown field (Fig. 2f).

Atomic-scale regulation and octahedral distortion

To elucidate the atomic-scale structure-property relationships governing polarization behavior of TTB ceramics, integrated differential phase-contrast scanning transmission electron microscopy (iDPC-STEM) was performed along the [001] zone axis on the pristine SBN-0.00M and the optimized SBN-0.15M compositions. This technique enables simultaneous visualization of both light (O) and heavy (A- and B-site) atomic columns, allowing direct observation of oxygen-octahedral distortions and B-site cation displacements. Figure 3a presents representative iDPC-STEM images together with the corresponding unit-cell structural model of the TTB lattice, highlighting the spatial arrangement of A-site cations, B-site cations, and oxygen anions. To facilitate analysis, Fig. 3b provides schematics of the crystallographically distinct B1O6 and B2O6, explicitly illustrating the connectivity of BO6 octahedra and their coordination environments with surrounding A-site cations. Figure 3c, d present the displacement mappings and the corresponding statistical distributions for B1 and B2 sites. The in-plane displacement of each B-site cation within the ab plane is defined as its offset relative to the geometric center formed by the four surrounding oxygen atoms. For SBN-0.00M, the average ionic displacement at the B2 site (~10.68 pm) is slightly greater than that at the B1 site (~7.54 pm). As seen in Fig. 3b, this contrast originates from their distinct A-site coordination environments. Each B1 cation is coordinated by a symmetric configuration consisting of two A2 sites and two vacant C channels, whereas each B2 cation experiences an intrinsically asymmetric environment formed by two A2 sites, one A1 site, and one vacant C channel. This asymmetry imposes unequal chemical pressures on the B2 site, giving rise to its larger displacement amplitude. Upon multi-element B-site substitution, the average ionic displacements of SBN-0.15 M at both B1 and B2 sites are markedly enhanced, reflecting the strengthened local lattice distortion induced by compositional complexity. Notably, despite this overall increase, the displacement at the B2 site (~24.47 pm) remains significantly greater than that at the B1 site (~16.26 pm), consistent with its intrinsically asymmetric coordination environment. Furthermore, in SBN-0.00 M, the distributions of ionic displacements for both B sites are relatively narrow, while in SBN-0.15M, the B-site ions become more disordered, exhibiting a broader statistical distribution of displacements. These observations indicate that disorder introduced by multi-element B-site substitution effectively perturbs the displacements of ferroelectrically active ions.

We further quantified the rotation of BO6 octahedra relative to the [100] direction. As illustrated in Fig. 3e, each quadrangular A1 site is surrounded by four equivalent B2O₆ octahedra with a consistent rotation angle, forming a coherent octahedral unit. In contrast, the rotation angle of this unit differs markedly from that of its nearest-neighbor octahedral units, resulting in distinct color variations in the rotation angle mapping. SBN-0.00 M displays nearly uniform rotation-angle mappings for both B1O6 and B2O6 octahedra, accompanied by narrow statistical distributions with relatively small variances (σ2 = 0.80 for B1O6 and 1.74/1.90 for the two types of B2O6), indicative of a relatively homogeneous local structure with limited distortion (Figs. 3e, S10). In contrast, SBN-0.15M exhibits pronounced spatial fluctuations in octahedral rotation angles together with significantly broadened distributions, where the corresponding variances increase to 2.66 for B1O6 and 2.77/3.65 for the two types of B2O6. To further evaluate octahedral distortion, the a/b ratios and projected areas of BO6 octahedra within the ab plane were calculated. In SBN-0.00M, the B1O6 octahedra exhibit a distinctly rectangular projection with an a/b ratio of ~1.15 (Fig. 3f), which originates from the anisotropic chemical pressure imposed by adjacent large A2 sites and vacant C channels. Upon multi-element B-site substitution, this ratio decreases markedly to ~1.05 in SBN-0.15M, indicating that the initial anisotropic structure is substantially mitigated. Accordingly, the ab-plane projection of B1O6 evolves toward a nearly equilateral geometry, reflecting a reconstruction of its distortion mode. In contrast, the B2O6 octahedra show minimal variation in geometric shape, with the a/b ratio changing only slightly from 1.04–1.05. Consistently, the projected area of B1O₆ increases significantly from ~8.01 Å2 to ~8.71 Å2, whereas that of B2O6 remains nearly unchanged (~8.90 Å2 to ~8.93 Å2) (Fig. S11). These results indicate distinct structural response modes of the inequivalent BO6 octahedra and their corresponding B-site cations. Specifically, the B2 sites mainly accommodate enhanced cation displacement, while B1 sites undergo distortion-mode reconstruction, collectively promoting local structural disorder and polarization configurational freedom. This site-dependent structural evolution provides a structural basis for polar entropy modulation in TTB systems.

Domain structure and polarization vector disorder

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was employed to elucidate how the cooperative structural evolution identified above influences domain configurations and polarization states. As shown in Fig. 4a, SBN-0.00M exhibits a typical spindle-shaped domain morphology with relatively large domain sizes. Consistently, the corresponding selected-area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern displays only the basic diffraction reflections of the TTB structure36. In contrast, no spindle-shaped long-range ferroelectric domains are observed in the TEM images of SBN-0.15M (Fig. 4b). Instead, the microstructure is dominated by weak-contrast, nanoscale speckle-like features, reflecting a highly fragmented domain state. Such pronounced domain miniaturization and disruption of long-range coherence suppress hysteresis loss and delay saturation polarization, thus enabling improved energy-storage performance. To further elucidate the microscopic origin of this domain evolution, atomic-scale polarization analysis was performed. The relative displacement of each Nb1 atom with respect to the geometric center defined by the four surrounding Nb2 atoms was defined as a polar vector (Fig. 4c)38. As shown in Fig. 4d, SBN-0.00M exhibits polarization vectors that are predominantly aligned along the c axis, consistent with strong ferroelectricity and the large remnant polarization observed in its P–E loops. In contrast, the displacement vectors of SBN-0.15M deviate significantly from the polar c-axis and display a highly disordered spatial distribution (Fig. 4g). The angular distributions of these atomic displacement vectors are visualized using stereographic projections in a spherical coordinate system, where the color scale reflects point density (Fig. 4e, h). For SBN-0.00M, the Nb1 displacements are strongly biased toward the [001] and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$[00\bar{1}]$$\end{document}[001¯] directions. In contrast, SBN-0.15M exhibits a substantially broadened angular distribution, with a significant fraction of displacement vectors oriented along other directions such as [101] and [010]. To quantitatively evaluate this evolution, the polar entropy is defined based on the statistical distribution of local polarization orientations derived from B-site displacements. The polar entropy increases from 2.18 for SBN-0.00M to 2.45 for SBN-0.15M, indicating a pronounced enhancement in the orientational disorder of local dipoles (Fig. S12). Such an increase reflects the fragmentation of polar nanoregions and the weakening of inter-dipole coupling, thereby facilitating more flexible polarization switching under an external electric field. In parallel, the c-axis components of the displacement vectors were further analyzed. Despite the increased orientational disorder, SBN-0.15M maintains a relatively large average c-axis displacement (5.78 pm), compared to SBN-0.00M (6.69 pm) (Fig. 4f, i), suggesting that strong polarization magnitude is preserved alongside enhanced dipolar randomness. This pronounced reorientation originates from local structural distortions induced by multi-element B-site substitution, which disturb the polar displacement along the c-axis and suppress long-range ferroelectric coherence. In contrast to conventional high-entropy engineering, which typically relies on higher substitution levels at A-sites and indirectly affects polarization22,28, the present polar entropy regulation strategy enables more efficient regulation by directly perturbing the polarization-active BO₆ octahedra at low substitution levels (<10%), thereby inducing strong local disorder with minimal compositional modification. Collectively, the resulting disorder promotes the formation of highly fragmented PNRs and weakens the strong coupling between dipoles. Consequently, the material exhibits more flexible dipole switching under an external electric field, manifesting as enhanced relaxor behavior and significantly improved energy storage performance.

From an application perspective, the temperature stability, cycling endurance, and charge-discharge characteristics of the TTB-MLCCs were systematically evaluated. Under a high electric field of 1290 kV·cm−1, the TTB-MLCCs maintain excellent energy storage stability over a wide temperature range from -40 to 125 °C. The P–E loops maintain a slim shape, with the Pm decreasing by only 2.54 µC·cm−2 and the Pr remaining as low as 1.24 µC·cm−2 at high temperatures (Fig. 5a). These characteristics yield a stable Wrec of 15.0 ± 0.1 J·cm−3 and a high η of 95.1 ± 2.1% (Fig. 5d). Compared with the reports in other material systems, the TTB-MLCCs have achieved the highest energy storage density within a wider temperature range1–4,6,41–43 (Fig. 5b). Moreover, even at an elevated temperature approaching 120 °C, both the Wrec and η remain at a leading level (Fig. 5e). Besides, the TTB-MLCCs demonstrate outstanding cycling reliability. These results collectively verify that the TTB-MLCCs satisfy the critical demands of wide-temperature operation, high-field endurance, and long-term cycling reliability. Remarkably, our TTB-MLCCs exhibit a fast discharge speed, achieving a short t0.9 (time to release 90% of Wdis) of 0.36 μs at 900 kV·cm−1. In summary, we establish a targeted polar entropy regulation strategy for enhanced energy storage performance in TTB-MLCCs. This approach triggers a site-dependent response of BO6 octahedra, where B2 sites primarily accommodate increased cation displacement, while B1 sites undergo distortion-mode reconstruction. Such complementary evolution synergistically enhances local structural disorder and polarization degrees of freedom, thereby enabling flexible polarization switching and ultimately delivering enhanced energy storage performance. Furthermore, we demonstrate the feasibility of utilizing TTB dielectric in MLCC applications, achieving an outstanding Wrec of 17.6 J·cm−3 together with an ultrahigh η of 96.8%. The TTB-MLCCs also exhibit excellent thermal stability, which underscores the great potential for high-temperature energy storage applications. This work highlights the pivotal role of polar entropy in governing dipolar configurations and establishes a paradigm shift from conventional configurational entropy design to polar-entropy-driven regulation for high-performance dielectric energy storage.

Methods

Ceramic fabrication

The lead-free tungsten bronze structured (Sr0.5Ba0.5)(Nb2-xSbx/4Tax/4Tix/4Wx/4)O6 ceramics (x = 0.05, 0.10, 0.15 and 0.20) were prepared by the solid-state reaction method. High-purity SrCO3 (99.99%), BaCO3 (99.95%), Nb2O5 (99.99%), Sb2O5 (99.95%), Ta2O5 (99.99%), TiO2 (99.9%), and WO3 (99.8%) were used as the raw materials. Based on the above stoichiometric ratio, the components were accurately weighed and then ball-milled for 4 h using anhydrous ethanol as the grinding medium to obtain a uniformly mixed precursor slurry. Then, after drying, the pressed block was subjected to calcination at 1300 °C for 3 h in an air atmosphere to obtain the tungsten bronze phase. The calcined bulks were crushed and ball-milled for 6 h again. The slurries were subsequently subjected to dehydration, homogenization with an aqueous poly (vinyl alcohol) (PVA) binder solution, and uniaxial compaction into cylindrical pellets (Φ 13 mm). Following binder elimination through controlled thermal treatment at 800 °C for 2 h, the specimens ultimately underwent a densification process via high-temperature sintering at 1200 °C for 3 h under an ambient air environment.

MLCCs fabrication

The MLCCs were prepared by the tape-casting method, and the powder used for the preparation of MLCCs followed the same process as that for the aforementioned ceramics. The ceramic powders were first ball-milled for 8 h with a solvent mixture (ethanol), dispersant, binder, and plasticizer. Subsequently, polyvinyl butyral binder and dibutyl phthalate plasticizer were introduced into the slurry, followed by a second ball-milling cycle lasting 10 h to ensure homogeneous integration. The ceramic slurry was then tape-cast into continuous 5 μm-thick tapes. These tapes were patterned with platinum paste electrodes through screen-printing technology, followed by layer stacking under thermal lamination (70 °C, 2 MPa). The MLCCs were warmed slowly to 270 °C at a rate of 6 °C/h and then held at this temperature for 2 h for binder burnout. Densification was achieved through 1200 °C sintering for 3 h, resulting in final dimensions of 3.0 × 2.0 × 0.5 mm. The sintered MLCCs exhibited 3.7 μm dielectric layers alternating with 3 μm-thick electrodes. The electrode area of the MLCCs was ~ 3.7 mm2. Silver termination electrodes were applied at both ends for electrical characterization.

Structure characterization

All XRD diffraction data were collected in reflection geometry with Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.5406 Å) on a Bruker D8 ADVANCE system, while the Renishaw inVia spectrometer provided Raman spectra under ambient conditions. For microstructural inspection, secondary electron images were acquired on a Hitachi S-8200 FE-SEM. Atomic-resolution chemical mapping was performed using HAADF-STEM and iDPC techniques utilized a JEOL JEM-ARM300F instrument (300 kV acceleration voltage, 20.6 mrad probe convergence, 54–220 mrad detection range). Atomic column positions were quantitatively analyzed through StatSTEM software’s statistical pattern recognition algorithm based on two-dimensional Gaussian peak fitting.

Property measurement

For electrical characterization, ceramic specimens were polished to 40–50 μm thickness and patterned with 1 mm diameter Au electrodes via physical vapor deposition through stainless steel masks. Polarization-electric field loops and leakage current density were quantified using an aixACCT TF Analyzer 2000E ferroelectric test system. Dielectric properties were evaluated across broad frequency/temperature ranges via the Novocontrol Concept 80 impedance analyzer. Strain measurement specimens (0.3 mm thickness) were prepared with screen-printed 8 mm diameter Ag electrodes sintered at 650 °C for 30 min. Charge-discharge performance under underdamped and overdamped conditions (100 Ω load) was assessed using a CFD-003 commercial testing platform (Gogo Instruments).

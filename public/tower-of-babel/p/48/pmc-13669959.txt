Thyroid Cartilage Reconstruction With Titanium Mesh in Advanced Papillary Thyroid Carcinoma
Authors: Anna Christina Clements, Cassie Pan, Neel K Bhatt, Mara Roth, Brittany Barber, Emily Marchiano
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe report the first case of titanium mesh reconstruction of the thyroid cartilage following resection of papillary thyroid carcinoma with preservation of the laryngeal mucosa. This technique provided excellent functional outcomes while maintaining oncologic resection in a patient with locally advanced disease.Keywords: laryngeal reconstruction, papillary thyroid carcinoma, titanium mesh

Introduction

Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) is the most common differentiated thyroid malignancy and generally associated with a favorable prognosis due to its indolent course [1]. However, in cases with locally advanced disease, tumor invasion into the upper aerodigestive tract can occur. Laryngotracheal invasion by PTC is uncommon, but most frequently involves the cricoid cartilage or proximal trachea, which can be surgically managed with circumferential resection and primary anastomosis [2]. Direct invasion of the thyroid cartilage is rare and presents additional surgical and functional challenges. Although advances in neoadjuvant medical therapies have reduced the frequency of laryngectomy for locally invasive disease, thyroid cartilage involvement continues to raise complex management considerations. Traditionally, thyroid cartilage invasion often necessitated partial or total laryngectomy to achieve adequate oncologic resection, which is associated with significant morbidity affecting voice, swallow, and airway function [2]. As survival outcomes for differentiated thyroid carcinoma remain favorable even in advanced cases, there is increasing emphasis on function‐preserving surgical strategies that balance oncologic control with postoperative quality of life.

Resection of PTC invasion into the thyroid cartilage with preservation of laryngeal mucosa may offer more favorable functional outcomes, although removal of cartilaginous framework can destabilize the airway if not reconstructed. Titanium mesh reconstruction of laryngotracheal cartilage has been described in head and neck surgery for certain oncologic patient populations, most commonly following partial laryngectomy for glottic cancer, although there has been one report of cricotracheal reconstruction with titanium mesh in PTC [3, 4]. The following case is the first report of thyroid cartilage reconstruction using titanium mesh in advanced thyroid carcinoma with preservation of laryngeal mucosa.

Case Report

This study was deemed exempt from IRB review by the University of Washington Human Subjects Division (STUDY00024953). Informed consent was obtained from the patient. A 52‐year‐old female presented to a tertiary care otolaryngology clinic for management of recurrent PTC. She previously underwent total thyroidectomy and central neck dissection, during which the thyroid was noted to be adherent to the larynx with pathology revealing Stage I pT3N1M0 PTC. Tumor was widely dispersed throughout the thyroid with extension into skeletal muscle and perithyroidal adipose tissue. Four weeks postoperatively, PET/CT showed a 5.4 cm FDG‐avid mass centered at the left thyroid cartilage with extension into the visceral space of the supraglottic larynx (Figure 1). Flexible laryngoscopy demonstrated bilateral fully mobile true vocal folds.

CT neck performed 4 weeks after the initial surgery, demonstrating large tumor invading into the left thyroid cartilage and supraglottic larynx.

She subsequently underwent panendoscopy, bilateral neck dissection (Levels II–IV), revision thyroidectomy, partial laryngectomy, and tracheostomy. Intraoperatively, gross invasion of the left thyroid ala was identified and resected via midline laryngeal fissure without violation of the laryngeal mucosa, which was uninvolved (Figure 2a). The left recurrent laryngeal nerve was sacrificed due to tumor involvement. The left thyroid ala was reconstructed with 0.3 mm titanium mesh, which was secured to the right thyroid ala with 1.2 mm self‐drilling screws and to the underlying laryngeal mucosa with 5–0 Prolene sutures (Figure 2b). Final pathology demonstrated 3.3 cm classic variant PTC involving thyroid cartilage with negative margins and 5/30 lymph nodes positive for metastatic disease, consistent with Stage I pT4bN1bM0 disease.

Intraoperative photographs demonstrating (a) resection of the left thyroid cartilage via midline laryngeal fissure without violation of the uninvolved laryngeal mucosa, and (b) reconstruction of the left thyroid ala with titanium mesh.

Postoperatively, the patient required 2 months of enteral feeding due to a pharyngeal leak but subsequently progressed to a regular diet. The left true vocal fold was immobile due to sacrifice of the left recurrent laryngeal nerve. She underwent left vocal fold injection augmentation with Restylane 4 months postoperatively, which resulted in a strong voice (Figure 3). At follow‐up, she demonstrates 100% intelligible voice and has not required repeat vocal fold injection. She decannulated from her tracheostomy 11 months postoperatively.

Flexible laryngoscopy demonstrating adequate glottic closure after left vocal fold injection augmentation.

From an oncologic perspective, she completed radioactive iodine therapy 3 months postoperatively and has been monitored with surveillance imaging and thyroglobulin levels. At 39 months postoperatively, she has low‐volume regional recurrence with detectable thyroglobulin (most recently 3.7) but remains asymptomatic without evidence of disease in the thyroid bed.

Discussion

Laryngotracheal invasion by PTC is an uncommon manifestation of locally advanced disease and most frequently involves the cricoid cartilage or proximal trachea. In such cases, circumferential resection with primary anastomosis was traditionally preferred, offering oncologic control with acceptable functional outcomes [2]. In contrast, invasion of thyroid cartilage by PTC, as seen in this case, is rare and presents additional surgical challenges with higher risk of postoperative functional deficits. Historically, thyroid cartilage involvement often necessitated partial or total laryngectomy to achieve oncologic resection, resulting in significant compromise to voice, swallow, and airway function [2].

More recently, management of locally advanced differentiated thyroid carcinoma has evolved with increasing use of neoadjuvant systemic therapies, such as tyrosine kinase inhibitors, contributing to a decline in the routine use of laryngectomy [5]. While neoadjuvant therapies may provide effective locoregional disease control in select cases, their role in the setting of structural cartilage invasion should be carefully considered. Tumor involvement in the laryngeal framework raises concern that non‐surgical therapies could cause progressive cartilage necrosis or loss of structural integrity, resulting in airway instability, aspiration, or delayed functional deterioration. Therefore, even in the era of neoadjuvant systemic therapies for differentiated thyroid carcinoma, involvement of major cartilaginous structures may still indicate aggressive surgical resection and reconstruction.

The extent of laryngotracheal resection in thyroid carcinoma is determined by the extent of tumor invasion. When mucosal violation is present, laryngectomy is frequently unavoidable. In this case, laryngotracheal invasion by PTC was confined to the thyroid cartilage without mucosal violation, allowing cartilaginous framework reconstruction without structural compromise to the larynx. Reconstruction of the thyroid cartilage with titanium mesh has been described in the literature in a limited fashion, typically pertaining to partial laryngectomy for patients with glottic cancer [3]. Other reconstructive options include autologous cartilage grafts, regional flaps, and free tissue transfer; however, titanium mesh provides rigid structural support and can be readily contoured to the native laryngeal framework. This is the first report to describe the utilization of titanium mesh for thyroid cartilage reconstruction for invasive thyroid malignancy with preservation of the laryngeal mucosa. Our patient demonstrated excellent postoperative voice quality, swallow function, and airway stability, which supports the feasibility and utility of this approach in carefully selected cases, even in the era of evolving neoadjuvant therapies.

Conclusion

Laryngeal reconstruction with titanium mesh enables oncologic resection of thyroid cartilage invasion by PTC while preserving laryngeal mucosa and function in carefully selected patients.

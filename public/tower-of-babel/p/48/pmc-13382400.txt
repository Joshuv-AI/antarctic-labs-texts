CEACAM5 drives psoriatic inflammation by regulating CD8+ T cell recruitment and activation
Authors: Lifeng Wang, Xiaotao Liu, Kaina Cao, Yifei Liu, Surong Liang, Lijuan Xu, Zigang Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
As a chronic relapsing immune-mediated skin disorder, psoriasis is mainly driven by CD8+ T cells during disease progression, while the molecular basis of CD8+ T cell recruitment and activation in psoriatic lesions remains largely unclear, and the function of CEACAM5 in psoriasis remains uncharacterized. Here, we report that CEACAM5 is significantly upregulated in psoriatic patient epidermal lesions and M5-induced psoriatic keratinocytes in vitro. CEACAM5 knockdown prominently inhibits chemokine expression in keratinocytes, attenuates CD8+ T cell recruitment, and downregulates pro-inflammatory cytokine production in recruited CD8+ T cells. Mechanistic studies reveal that soluble CEACAM5 directly activates the Lck/ZAP70 signaling axis to promote the inflammatory activation of CD8+ T cells. Collectively, our work identifies CEACAM5 as a critical regulator of CD8+ T cell responses in psoriasis, providing a novel potential therapeutic target for this disease.

Introduction

Psoriasis is a chronic, relapsing, immune-mediated skin disorder, in which the aberrant crosstalk between hyperproliferative epidermal keratinocytes and autoreactive immune cells has been identified as the core driver of its pathogenesis [1,2]. In psoriasis, skin-resident cells and immune cells recruited from the circulatory system, particularly T lymphocyte (T cells), are involved in the complex feedback circuits underlying the pathophysiology of this disease [[3], [4], [5], [6]]. Among these cells, CD8+ T cells exhibit marked infiltration and activation in psoriatic lesions, especially within the epidermal layer [7]. To date, studies have demonstrated that depletion of CD8+ T cells exerts a significant therapeutic effect on psoriasis [8], further corroborating the central role of CD8+ T cells in the pathogenesis of psoriasis. However, although the critical role of CD8+ T cells in the progression of psoriasis has been well established, the mechanisms underlying their residence in the skin remain to be elucidated.

The carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) family comprises 18 functional genes and 11 pseudogenes, which are mainly divided into two major categories: cell surface-anchored carcinoembryonic antigen-related cellular adhesion molecules (CEACAMs) and secreted pregnancy-specific glycoproteins (PSGs). CEACAMs are highly glycosylated molecules anchored on the cell surface, and participate in both intracellular and intercellular signal transduction [9]. In addition to serving as well-recognized cancer biomarkers, studies have revealed aberrant upregulation of CEACAMs in psoriatic samples, suggesting that they may exert a unique role in the pathogenesis of psoriasis [10].

In the present study, we found that CEACAM5 was significantly upregulated both in the epidermal tissues of patients with psoriasis and in the psoriatic cell model induced by M5 cytokine cocktail. Further mechanistic investigations revealed that CEACAM5 not only markedly promoted the expression of chemokines in keratinocytes and enhanced the chemotactic capacity of CD8+ T cells, but also modulated CD8+ T cells via the LCK/ZAP70 signaling axis to facilitate the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines in these cells. Collectively, our study not only provides novel scientific insights for the in-depth dissection of the pathogenesis of psoriasis, but also offers a critical direction for the development of novel therapeutic strategies against psoriasis.

Materials and methods

Patients and ethical approval

All patients with psoriasis were definitively diagnosed in accordance with clinical and histopathological criteria. The normal skin samples were obtained from healthy control subjects who had no history of skin diseases. At the time of enrollment in the study, neither the patients nor the healthy controls had received systemic or topical treatment, and the patients' skin lesions showed no signs of secondary infection. This study protocol was strictly implemented in compliance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Beijing Luhe Hospital (Approval No. 2025-LHKY-125). Written informed consent was obtained from all enrolled subjects.

Cell culture and treatment

The human-immortalized keratinocyte cell line HaCaT was cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle's Medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin–streptomycin at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2.

To induce a psoriatic cell model, HaCaT cells were stimulated with the M5 cocktail (including TNF-α, oncostatin M, IL-17A, IL-1α, IL-22 each at 10 ng/mL; ProteinTech, Wuhan, China) for 24 h [11].

RNA interference

Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen) was used for siRNA transfection. After 24 h of transfection, cells were collected and analyzed by quantitative real-time PCR. The siRNAs targeting CEACAM5 were purchased from MCE (HY-RS02441). SiCEACAM5-1 was used alone in all subsequent functional experiments.

Cell viability assay

HaCaT cells were seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 5000 cells per well. Twenty-four hours after seeding, siRNA targeting CEACAM5 and control siRNA were transfected into the cells separately using Lipofectamine 3000. Cell proliferation in each group was detected using Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8, Cat. No. C0038, Beyotime Biotechnology) at 24 h post-transfection.

Western blot analysis

Tissue or cell lysate were prepared using the RIPA buffer (Beyotime Biotechnology) containing protease inhibitors (Beyotime Biotechnology). After sonication, the protein concentration in each sample was determined by using the BCA protein assay kit (Beyotime Biotechnology) and boiled at 95°C for 10 min. Equivalent amounts of protein samples were separated by SDS-PAGE and electroblotted onto a polyvinylidene fluoride membrane (Roche) using a Mini Trans-Blot Cell (Bio-Rad). The membranes were blocked at room temperature for 2 h in PBS containing 3% bovine serum albumin (BSA), followed by incubation with the indicated primary antibodies overnight at 4°C. After washing three times with TBS-Tween (50 mM Tris-HCl, 150 mM NaCl, and 0.1% [v/v] Tween 20, pH 7.4), the membranes were incubated with secondary antibodies at RT for 45 min. Finally, the membranes were visualized with a chemiluminescence system (Tanon) after three times of wash.

The antibodies used in this study are as follows: mouse monoclonal anti-CEACAM5 (ab316349)

rabbit monoclonal anti-p-Lck (ab318960), and rabbit monoclonal anti-pZAP70 (ab76501) were purchased from Abcam. Mouse monoclonal anti-Actin (66009-1-Ig) was purchased from Proteintech. Horseradish peroxidase-labeled goat anti-mouse (SA00001-1) or anti-rabbit (SA00001-2) secondary antibodies were obtained from Proteintech.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

For immunohistochemistry staining, ∼5 μm thick paraffin sections were placed in 3% H2O2 for 30 min to quench the endogenous peroxidase activity, and then the sectioned slides were incubated in citrate buffer at 96°C for 30 min for antigen retrieval. After washing with PBS containing 0.1% Tween 20, the sections were blocked in 5% BSA for 1 h and incubated overnight at 4°C with indicated primary antibody (Servicebio) diluted in PBS with 0.1% Tween 20. After washing, the sections were incubated with secondary antibody (horseradish peroxidase-labeled sheep anti-rabbit IgG, Beijing ZSGB-BIO Co., Ltd) for 45 min. Finally, the slides were developed using the 3,3′-diaminobenzidine, and hematoxylin was used for counterstaining. All immunohistochemical sections were scanned with a digital slide scanner (3DHISTECH, P250 FLASH III). ImageJ software was used to quantify and calculate the average values for each field of view.

RNA extraction and quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA in tissues and treated cells was extracted using TRIzol Reagent (Invitrogen), and 1 μg RNA was used to synthesize cDNA using a first-strand cDNA synthesis kit (TOYOBO). Quantitative real-time PCR was performed using a 7500 Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems) and SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (TOYOBO). Data were normalized to the level of Actb expression in each sample. The primer pairs used were as follows:

CEACAM5: forward 5′-CTGTCCAATGACAACAGGACC-3′, reverse 5′-ACGGTAATAGGTGTATGAGGGG-3’;

IFNG: forward 5′-TCGGTAACTGACTTGAATGTCCA-3′, reverse 5′-TCGCTTCCCTGTTTTAGCTGC-3’;

TNFA: forward 5′-CCTCTCTCTAATCAGCCCTCTG-3′, reverse 5′-GAGGACCTGGGAGTAGATGAG-3’;

IL22: forward 5′-GCTTGACAAGTCCAACTTCCA-3′, reverse 5′-GCTCACTCATACTGACTCCGT-3’;

CXCL5: forward 5′-AGCTGCGTTGCGTTTGTTTAC-3′, reverse 5′-TGGCGAACACTTGCAGATTAC-3’;

CXCL10: forward 5′-GTGGCATTCAAGGAGTACCTC-3′, reverse 5′-TGATGGCCTTCGATTCTGGATT-3’;

CCL2: forward 5′-CAGCCAGATGCAATCAATGCC-3′, reverse 5′-TGGAATCCTGAACCCACTTCT-3’;

CCL5: forward 5′-CCAGCAGTCGTCTTTGTCAC-3′, reverse 5′-CTCTGGGTTGGCACACACTT-3’;

Actb: forward 5′-CATGTACGTTGCTATCCAGGC-3′, reverse 5′-CTCCTTAATGTCACGCACGAT-3′.

Isolation of peripheral blood CD8+ T cell

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells from human blood were prepared by density gradient separation using Ficoll-Paque Plus (Amersham Biosciences, GE Healthcare Europe GmbH, Freiburg, Germany). CD8+T cells were isolated using a human CD8+T cell sorting kit (abs50130, absin) according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Transwell migration assay

T cells (5 × 105 per well) were plated into the top chamber of Transwell plates (Costar). HaCaT cells and M5 cytokines were added to the lower chamber. The plates were incubated at 37°C for 3 h, after which Transwell migration was detected by flow cytometry (FACS). The Transwell migration ability was expressed as "the percentage of migrated cells relative to the total cells".

PIPLC treatment of HaCaT cells to release CEACAM5

To release soluble CEACAM5 from the cell surface, HaCaT cells were treated with phosphatidylinositol-specific phospholipase C (PIPLC, P6621, Merck). HaCaT cells were harvested by centrifugation at 300 ×g for 3 min and resuspended in OPTI-MEM (Thermo Fisher Scientific). A total of 1 × 106 cells were resuspended in 100 μL of OPTI-MEM. The cells were treated with 0.5 μL of PIPLC (1 unit/mL) to cleave the GPI anchor of CEACAM5. The suspension was incubated at 37°C for 1.5 h. After incubation, 100 μL of PBS was added to the tube, followed by centrifugation at 300 ×g for 3 min. The supernatant containing soluble CEACAM5 was collected and used for subsequent treatment of CD8+ T cells. The efficiency of CEACAM5 release was confirmed by Western blot analysis of the supernatant.

Statistical analyses

GraphPad Prism 9.0 was used for statistical analysis. Data obtained from three independent experiments were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Differences between two groups were analyzed using two-tailed unpaired Student's t-test, while variations across multiple groups were assessed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey's multiple comparisons test. For all statistical significance indications in this manuscript, ∗∗∗∗, p < 0.0001; ∗∗∗, p < 0.001; ∗∗, p < 0.01; ∗, p < 0.05 and ns, no significance.

CEACAM5 is upregulated in keratinocytes of psoriatic lesions

To further verify the correlation between CEACAM5 expression and psoriasis, we treated the human keratinocyte cell line HaCaT with M5 cytokine cocktail for 24 h, a widely used in vitro method to mimic the psoriatic microenvironment [12,13]. The results showed that the mRNA expression level of CEACAM5 in HaCaT cells was significantly increased after M5 stimulation (Fig. 1D). In line with this, the protein level of CEACAM5 in HaCaT cells was also significantly elevated under the psoriatic inflammatory milieu (Fig. 1E). These results suggest that CEACAM5 may play a critical role in the pathogenesis of psoriasis.

CEACAM5 knockdown suppresses chemokine expression in keratinocytes

To investigate the biological function of CEACAM5, we silenced its expression in HaCaT cells using small interfering RNA (siRNA) technology. As shown in Fig. 2A and B, CEACAM5-targeting siRNA markedly inhibited CEACAM5 expression in HaCaT cells after 24 h of transfection, whereas no significant effect was observed on cell proliferation. Considering the important role of chemokines in the process of psoriasis [14], we then examined the effect of CEACAM5 on chemokine expression in HaCaT cells. As shown in Fig. 2C, downregulation of CEACAM5 significantly inhibited the mRNA expression of chemokines including CXCL5, CXCL10, CCL2 and CCL5, suggesting that CEACAM5 expression in keratinocytes may affect their ability to recruit immune cells.Fig. 2CEACAM5 knockdown suppresses chemokine expression in keratinocytes(A) The relative mRNA expression levels of CEACAM5 in siCEACAM5 treated HaCaT cells. CEACAM5 promotes the chemotaxis of CD8+ T cells

Based on the above findings, we subsequently investigated whether CEACAM5 could affect the chemotactic capacity of CD8+ T cells toward keratinocytes. Using a Transwell system, CD8+ T cells were seeded in the upper chamber, while HaCaT cells with different treatments (siNC/siCEACAM5 ± M5) were cultured in the lower chamber. After 3 h of co-culture, the number of CD8+ T cells that migrated into the lower chamber was quantified by flow cytometry. The results showed that the recruitment ability of keratinocytes to CD8+ T cells was significantly enhanced in the psoriatic microenvironment. Whereas, after silencing CEACAM5 expression in keratinocytes, the chemotactic capacity of CD8+ T cells was significantly suppressed (Fig. 3A). Considering the critical role of inflammatory cytokines secreted by CD8+ T cells in the pathogenesis of psoriasis, we subsequently collected the chemotaxed CD8+ T cells and detected the expression of inflammatory cytokines. The results revealed that silencing CEACAM5 in keratinocytes not only inhibited the chemotactic capacity of CD8+ T cells, but also downregulated the expression of TNF-α, IFN-γ, and IL-22 in these cells (Fig. 3B). In the immune network of psoriasis, TNF-α, IFN-γ, and IL-22 are key effector molecules that constitute the inflammatory cascade and serve as the primary drivers of the CD8+ T cell-mediated pathological process of psoriasis [15]. Infiltrating CD8+ T cells in the skin are important sources of these three cytokines, which act synergistically on keratinocytes to induce their hyperproliferation, abnormal differentiation, and release of large amounts of inflammatory mediators, thereby collectively constructing and maintaining the pathogenic loop of chronic inflammation in psoriasis. [[15], [16], [17], [18]]. CEACAM5 mediates the activation of inflammatory response in CD8+ T cells via the Lck/ZAP70 signaling axis

To further validate the direct activating effect of CEACAM5 on CD8+ T cells [19,20], we cleaved its cell surface-anchored structure with phosphatidylinositol-specific phospholipase C (PIPLC) to release soluble CEACAM5 into the cell supernatant. As shown in Fig. 4A, only trace levels of free CEACAM5 were present in the supernatant of HaCaT cells. PIPLC treatment triggered the release of CEACAM5 into the supernatant, and CEACAM5 knockdown significantly reduced its abundance in PIPLC treated cell supernatant. Subsequently, CD8+ T cells were treated with the cell supernatant containing free soluble CEACAM5 for 12 h to examine whether CEACAM5 could activate CD8+ T cells. As shown in Fig. 4B and C, Western blotting results showed that the cell supernatant containing CEACAM5 significantly induced the phosphorylation of Lck and ZAP70 in CD8+ T cells. Meanwhile, qRT-PCR results revealed that CEACAM5 markedly upregulated the expression of inflammatory cytokines in CD8+ T cells (Fig. 4D). Discussion

It is widely accepted that T cells and cytokines play a central role in the pathogenesis of psoriasis. In psoriatic lesions, CD8+ T cells are predominantly localized in the epidermal layer. Antigen presentation by dendritic cells (DCs) and stimulation from the cytokine microenvironment drive the activation of CD8+ T cells, among which interleukin-23 (IL-23) induces their differentiation into Tc17 cells. Tc17 cells activate the Janus kinase 1 (JAK1)/signal transducer and activator of transcription 1 (STAT1) signaling pathway by producing interferon-γ (IFN-γ), thereby upregulating the expression of keratin 17 in keratinocytes and directly driving abnormal epidermal hyperproliferation and inflammatory responses [17,21]. Meanwhile, Tc22 cells differentiated from CD8+ T cells can directly stimulate KC proliferation and inhibit their differentiation by secreting interleukin-22 (IL-22), ultimately leading to the characteristic hyperkeratosis of psoriasis [22]. In addition, CD8+ T cells can also regulate the inflammatory process through crosstalk with DCs, fibroblasts and other cell types [23]. CD8+ T cells can also recognize a variety of psoriasis-associated self-antigens via major histocompatibility complex class I (MHC I) molecules. For instance, the ADAMTSL5 protein can activate CD8+ T cells and induce their secretion of IL-17 and IFN-γ [24]. Taken together, deciphering the molecular mechanism underlying the recruitment of CD8+ T cells to psoriatic lesions not only deepens our understanding of psoriasis pathogenesis, but may also provide novel insights for the development of therapeutic strategies against psoriasis.

In this study, we found that knockdown of CEACAM5 significantly downregulated the expression of CCL2, CCL5, CXCL5 and CXCL10 in keratinocytes. Previous studies have shown that all these chemokines are significantly upregulated in patients with psoriasis and participate in the pathogenesis of psoriasis [15,25]. Among them, CXCL10 and CCL5 have been extensively documented to promote the pathogenesis of psoriasis by recruiting CD8+ T cells [4,15,[26], [27], [28], [29]]. Although CCL2 primarily acts on monocytes, studies have also found that it may participate in the pathogenesis of psoriatic arthritis by affecting the migration of CD8+ T cells [30]. These findings indicate that CEACAM5 promotes the secretion of chemokines by keratinocytes and enhances their capacity to recruit CD8+ T cells. Notably, CXCL5 is a neutrophil chemokine. Although there is currently no evidence linking its expression to T cell chemotaxis, studies have shown that it is significantly upregulated in mouse models of psoriasis and promotes disease progression by mediating IL-23 secretion from neutrophils [31]. This suggests that CEACAM5 may not only modulate T cell chemotaxis but also play a role in neutrophil chemotaxis.

However, limited by experimental conditions, the present study only examined a limited panel of chemokines. Psoriatic lesions express a broad spectrum of chemokines, including CXCL9, CXCL10, CXCL11, and others [15], which may also be regulated by CEACAM5 and contribute to T cell recruitment. Future studies employing unbiased approaches such as chemokine antibody arrays and single-cell RNA sequencing will be required to comprehensively delineate the CEACAM5-dependent chemokine network in keratinocytes, which will provide novel scientific insights for the development of therapeutic strategies against psoriasis.

CEACAM5 contains an N-terminal variable domain and six C2-like immunoglobulin (Ig) domains [32]; its glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI) linker mediates membrane anchoring (Fig. 2) [33,34]. Under physiological conditions, CEACAM5 functions as a cell adhesion molecule and participates in the regulation of cell differentiation, immune modulation and other biological processes [9]. Previous studies have shown that CEACAM5-derived peptides can activate CD8+ T cells [35], and that CEACAM5 can activate Lck by binding to CD8α [20]. Lck is a key node in the T cell receptor (TCR) signaling pathway [36]. Furthermore, activation of TCR signaling not only establishes the chemotactic properties of CD8+ T cells, but also promotes their residency in non-lymphoid tissues [37]. In the present study, using an M5 cytokine-induced in vitro psoriasis model, we demonstrated that CEACAM5 activates the Lck/zeta-chain associated protein kinase 70 kDa (ZAP70) signaling axis and mediates the inflammatory response of CD8+ T cells, identifying CEACAM5 as a key host factor that regulates the recruitment of CD8+ T cells to psoriatic lesions.

Although the regulatory mechanisms governing CEACAM5 expression in keratinocytes remain poorly understood, previous studies on other cell types and psoriasis have established a preliminary regulatory framework. Pro-inflammatory cytokines are known inducers of CEACAM5 expression. In colorectal cancer cells, IL-6 trans-signaling markedly upregulates CEACAM5 via STAT3 phosphorylation [38]. While TGF-β induces its expression through Smad3-mediated transcriptional activation in human gastric cancer cell lines [39]. Hypoxia and acidosis, common characteristics of inflamed tissues, drive CEACAM5 expression by enabling HIF-1α to directly bind to the CEACAM5 promoter [40]. Given that IL-6, TGF-β and hypoxia are all present in psoriatic plaques, the regulatory mechanisms identified in other cell types are likely involved in the upregulation of CEACAM5 in keratinocytes during psoriasis.

In summary, our study identifies CEACAM5 as a pivotal mediator of psoriatic inflammation by orchestrating CD8+ T cell recruitment and activation via keratinocyte chemokine induction and the Lck/ZAP70 signaling axis. These findings unravel a novel crosstalk mechanism between keratinocytes and CD8+ T cells, positioning CEACAM5 as a promising therapeutic target for psoriasis. Further in vivo and clinical studies are warranted to validate CEACAM5-targeted interventions for this chronic skin disorder.

Profile of pituitary adenoma in patients from rural areas: A study at Dr. Saiful Anwar hospital, Indonesia, 2024
Authors: Donny Wisnu Wardhana, Farhad Balafif, Tommy Alfandy Nazwar, Fachriy Balafif, Wino Vrieda Vierlia, Iriana Maharani, Rulli Rosandi, Laksmi Sasiarini, Dessika Rahmawati, Yuyun Yueniwati, Harjoedi Adji Tjahjono, Anisa Nur Kholipah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background: Pituitary adenoma is a benign tumor that can greatly affect prognosis, with delayed diagnosis posing a significant challenge. In Indonesia, epidemiological data on this tumor remain scarce. This study aims to explore the clinical profile of pituitary adenoma patients treated at Dr. Saiful Anwar General Hospital in 2024.Methods: A retrospective, single-center, consecutive case series study was conducted by a multidisciplinary research team. Diagnoses were pathologically confirmed through clinical and radiological assessments. Inclusion criteria were patients diagnosed and operated on in 2024. Patients with incomplete data were included in the descriptive analysis but excluded from specific analyses.Results: In 2024, 17 pituitary adenoma patients were treated at Dr. Saiful Anwar General Hospital. The majority were female (70.58%) and aged ≥18 years (100%). Visual disturbance was the predominant presenting symptom (88.23%). The most common tumor type was functional adenoma (prolactinoma, 52.94%), with Knosp grades 3A and 4 each found in 29.42% and 23.53% of cases. All patients underwent endonasal transsphenoidal surgery, with the majority achieving near-total resection (47.05%). Postoperatively, patients demonstrated favorable outcomes: All were discharged alive (100%), had normal urinary function at discharge (100%), and experienced improvements in hormonal status (77.77%), visual acuity (93.33%), and olfaction (100%).Conclusion: The endonasal transsphenoidal approach is a safe and effective technique for pituitary adenoma management. Multidisciplinary collaboration and integrated care improve outcomes and postoperative monitoring. Strengthening rural referral systems and implementing telemedicine may enhance access and long-term follow-up.

INTRODUCTION

Pituitary adenomas, or pituitary neuroendocrine tumors, are benign tumors originating from anterior pituitary cells. Globally, the incidence of pituitary adenomas is reported at 5.1 cases per 100,000 population per year, accounting for approximately 15.0% of all primary brain tumors. A higher prevalence is observed in women, and the average age of patients varies depending on the hormone secretion profile of the tumor. Pituitary adenomas are classified as non-functional (57%) and functional (43%). The functional types include growth hormone (GH)-secreting adenomas (18%), prolactin (PRL)-secreting (12%), adrenocorticotropic hormone-secreting (5%), gonadotropin-secreting (5%), GH-PRL co-secreting (1%), and thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH)-secreting adenomas (1%).[4,6,11] In contrast, epidemiological data from Indonesia are limited. A study from Universitas Indonesia estimated the national incidence of pituitary adenomas at two cases per 100,000 population. Hospital-based reports show that Dr. Kariadi General Hospital in Semarang recorded eight cases from 2015 to 2018, while Dr. Cipto Mangunkusumo General Hospital in Jakarta recorded 44 cases between 2014 and 2016.[1,2,9] This report indicates a potential underdiagnosis and underreporting, especially outside major urban centers.

In rural areas of Indonesia, several factors hinder timely diagnosis and treatment of pituitary adenomas. Patients often face long travel distances to reach tertiary referral centers, while access to advanced diagnostic modalities such as magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and hormonal assays remains limited. In addition, referral delays from primary care facilities are common due to lack of awareness or diagnostic resources. These barriers frequently result in late presentation with larger tumors and more advanced symptoms, increasing the risk of complications and reducing the chances of early intervention.[7,8]

Despite the increasing number of tertiary care centers across Indonesia, there remains a lack of published data on the clinical characteristics and surgical outcomes of pituitary adenoma patients treated in rural or semi-urban settings such as Malang. Dr. Saiful Anwar General Hospital, located in Malang, serves as a regional referral center for tumor management, including pituitary tumors. In 2023, the hospital established a dedicated multidisciplinary pituitary team, supported by adequate neurosurgical and endocrinological resources, to provide comprehensive care for patients referred from surrounding rural districts. A case series from this institution can help address the current gap in national data and offer valuable insights into the clinical presentation and surgical outcomes of pituitary adenomas in the context of rural Indonesia.

Therefore, this study was conducted 1 year after the establishment of the multidisciplinary team, aiming to explore the demographics, tumor grading, extent of surgical resection, and 1-year functional outcomes of 17 pituitary adenoma patients who underwent endonasal transsphenoidal surgery at Dr. Saiful Anwar General Hospital in 2024. The findings from this study are expected to provide new insights into the patterns of pituitary adenoma and serve as a basis for developing more effective management strategies for pituitary adenomas in the future.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design

This study is a retrospective, single-center, consecutive case series aimed at identifying the clinical profile and outcomes of patients diagnosed with pituitary adenomas who underwent surgical treatment at Dr. Saiful Anwar General Hospital in 2024. The study was conducted by a multidisciplinary team from the departments of neurosurgery, ophthalmology, otolaryngology (ear, nose, and throat), endocrinology and pathologist, radiology, pediatrics, neurology, and anesthesiology. Patients were eligible for inclusion if they underwent endonasal transsphenoidal surgery for pituitary adenomas during the study period. The diagnosis was established based on clinical evaluation, hormonal assays, and radiological imaging, and subsequently confirmed through histopathological examination. Cases were identified through institutional surgical registries and cross-verified with electronic medical records. Patients with incomplete data were included in the descriptive analysis but excluded from specific analyses as appropriate.

Patients initially presented to various outpatient clinics, including ophthalmology, neurology, endocrinology, obstetrics-gynecology, and pediatrics. Based on clinical symptoms suggestive of pituitary adenoma, patients were referred for contrast-enhanced head MRI and pre-functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS) computed tomography (CT) scan, followed by consultation at the neurosurgery clinic. Further evaluations included hormonal laboratory tests and visual field assessment, with additional referrals to relevant specialties (endocrinology, pediatrics, obstetrics-gynecology, and ophthalmology) as clinically indicated.

Each case was subsequently reviewed in a multidisciplinary pituitary conference to determine the management category: Operative or non-operative management. Patients deemed suitable for surgery were placed on the surgical waiting list and underwent preoperative planning, including consultations with neuroanesthesiology and otolaryngology (nasoendoscopic) teams. Hospital admission was followed by endonasal transsphenoidal surgery, conducted under collaborative care involving neurosurgery, endocrinology, ophthalmology, neuroanesthesiology, and otolaryngology teams.

On the 1st postoperative day, patients underwent contrast head CT scans. Postoperative evaluations included histopathological analysis, nasoendoscopic evaluation, visual field and visual acuity assessments, and contrast-enhanced head MRI at 3 and 12 months postoperatively. Hormonal reevaluation was also performed, along with long-term follow-up by the outpatient endocrinology clinic. Patients were monitored regularly every 2–3 months at the relevant outpatient clinics, and all clinical data were systematically recorded in a database for retrospective analysis [Figure 1].

Imaging and tumor grading

All patients underwent preoperative MRI of the sellar and parasellar regions using a 3.0 Tesla scanner. Contrast-enhanced T1-weighted coronal and sagittal sequences were acquired to assess tumor characteristics and anatomical relationships. Tumor invasiveness was classified using the Knosp grading system, which evaluates parasellar extension based on the degree of cavernous sinus invasion. Due to high demand and limited MRI availability at our institution, scheduling was often constrained, and imaging time points could not be standardized across all patients. Despite this limitation, all patients received preoperative imaging of sufficient quality to allow radiological assessment and grading. Postoperative imaging was performed within 1 day after surgery using a contrast-enhanced CT scan. The extent of tumor resection was categorized as follows: Total resection (no visible residual tumor); near-total resection (residual tumor volume <10% of the preoperative volume); and partial resection (residual tumor volume >30%).[3,5,10] Knosp grading and the extent of resection were independently assessed by a neuroradiologist who was blinded to the clinical and intraoperative data.

Hormonal assessment

Baseline and postoperative hormonal evaluations were conducted using standardized electrochemiluminescence immunoassay kits on the Roche Cobas platform. The assays measured levels of PRL, cortisol, TSH, and free thyroxine (FT4). Reference ranges were based on institutional laboratory standards. Postoperative hormonal measurements were obtained at 1, 3, and 12 months.

Perimetry assessment

Perimetry assessment was performed using the Humphrey visual field Analyzer, employing the Central 30-2 Threshold Test protocol. This automated static perimetry assesses the sensitivity of the central 30° of the visual field using a white-on-white strategy. The primary parameter analyzed was the mean deviation value, expressed in decibels. Perimetry was conducted preoperatively and repeated at 3 months postoperatively to evaluate changes in visual field function.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize demographic and clinical characteristics. Data were analyzed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages, while continuous variables were reported as means ± standard deviation or medians with interquartile ranges. No hypothesis testing or comparative statistical analysis was performed.

RESULTS

Patient enrollment and follow-up are presented in the cohort flow diagram [Figure 2]. From an initial pool of 22 patients assessed for eligibility, five were excluded: two due to the absence of histopathological confirmation of pituitary adenoma, and three due to early loss to follow-up within the 1st month. The remaining 17 patients underwent endonasal transsphenoidal surgery and were included in the final analysis. All patients completed the early postoperative monitoring phase within the first 7 weeks, which involved general condition assessments, hormonal evaluations, and routine nasoendoscopic examinations. However, only 10 patients (58.8%) returned for the 3-month follow-up, and just 1 patient (5.9%) attended the 12-month follow-up visit. Loss to follow-up was mainly attributed to patients perceiving clinical improvement – no longer experiencing olfactory, visual, or hormonal symptoms – as well as limited access to the tertiary care center, particularly among those residing in remote areas.

During 2024, there were 17 pituitary adenoma patients at Dr. Saiful Anwar General Hospital, with an age range of 20–70 years. Of these, 5 (29.42%) were male and 12 (70.58%) were female. The most common presenting complaint was visual disturbance, reported by 15 patients (88.2%), followed by headache in 9 patients (52.9%), dizziness in 2 patients (11.8%), and menstrual disorders in 1 patient (5.9%). Based on PA results and hormonal examination, 52.94% of patients were diagnosed as functional pituitary adenoma in the form of prolactinoma [Table 1].

According to the KNOSP grading system, the majority of patients experienced tumors grade 3A (29.42%), followed by grade 4 and 1 (each 23.53%), grade 3B (17.64%), and grade 2(5.88%) [Figure 3]. All patients underwent surgery using the endonasal transsphenoidal technique, with partial resection success of 11.77%, near-total resection of 47.05%, and total resection of 41.18% [Figure 4]. Among these with grade 4 adenoma, total resection was achieved in 25% of cases, whereas total resection rates were 66.66% for grade 3B, 20% for grade 3A, and 75% for grade 1 lession. These data indicate that more invasive tumors (grade 3A, 3B, and 4) have lower resection rates compared with less invasive tumors (grade 1). In this study, the endonasal transsphenoidal approach for adenomas with Knosp grades 0–2 allowed better visualization and broader access, facilitating total resection. In contrast, for adenomas with Knosp grades 3A, 3B, and 4, the technique was more limited in achieving total resection due to the high risk of injury to nearby vascular structures and cranial nerves.

This surgery showed satisfactory results, with all patients being discharged alive postoperatively. During the 7-day postoperative monitoring, all patients showed normal urine conditions, indicating no diabetes insipidus. Nasoendoscopy examinations on all patients from week 1 to 7 showed mild olfactory disturbances that progressively improved over time [Table 1]. Regular postoperative monitoring of PRL levels showed improvement in the majority of patients, with 66.66% experiencing a decrease in PRL levels to normal, 11.11% experiencing an increase to normal levels, and 22.22% remaining with hyperprolactinemia [Table 2].

Three months after surgery, of the 15 patients who presented with visual disturbances, only five had complete pre- and post-operative perimetry data, all of whom showed objective improvement [Figure 5 and Table 3]. The remaining ten patients did not undergo complete perimetric evaluation; therefore, their visual outcomes were assessed through clinical examination and patient-reported symptoms. Among these, nine patients experienced subjective improvement, while one patient with preoperative blindness did not experience recovery, even after total tumor excision was performed. The limited availability of complete perimetry data was partly due to a period during which the perimetry device at Dr. Saiful Anwar General Hospital was out of service, preventing consistent objective visual field assessments.

DISCUSSION

This study highlights three key findings in the management of pituitary adenomas using the endonasal transsphenoidal approach at a regional referral hospital. First, the relatively high rate of near-total resection indicates that the endonasal transsphenoidal technique remains effective, particularly when performed by an experienced multidisciplinary team. Second, all patients were discharged in good condition without major postoperative complications such as diabetes insipidus, a common concern following pituitary surgery. Third, most patients demonstrated significant clinical improvement, especially in visual and hormonal function. However, patients who were already blind preoperatively did not experience any improvement in vision even after 1 year of follow-up, underscoring the importance of early detection and timely intervention before irreversible optic nerve damage occurs. These findings were obtained with greater accuracy due to the availability of relatively complete data, ranging from patient selection through to postoperative evaluation over a defined period. This was made possible by the implementation of a collaboratively and comprehensively executed standard operating procedure developed by the multidisciplinary pituitary team – an approach that remains uncommon in other healthcare centers across Indonesia. Consequently, this study was able to evaluate clinical improvements in patients, particularly in hormonal and visual outcomes.

When compared with data from other tertiary care centers in Indonesia, several notable differences in patient characteristics with pituitary adenomas were observed. At Dr. Cipto Mangunkusumo Hospital, pituitary adenomas rank as the second most common primary brain tumor, with 56.8% of cases occurring in women – consistent with global trends.[1] In contrast, data from Dr. Kariadi Hospital showed that the majority of patients were male (62.5%).[2] In our study, most patients were female (70.58%), with visual disturbances being the most common clinical presentation. These differences may reflect demographic variations, referral patterns, or even differences in symptom awareness between urban and rural populations. Although the number of patients in this study was limited, clinical outcomes – including resection rates, visual improvement, and hormonal function – were comparable to those reported by national referral centers, supporting the effectiveness of the multidisciplinary approach at regional hospitals.

However, it is important to note that existing studies from both Dr. Cipto Mangunkusumo and Dr. Kariadi hospitals do not provide detailed information on patient outcomes, which limits our ability to perform a direct, head-to-head comparison in terms of surgical success, or long-term clinical improvements.

Preoperative hormonal evaluation of PRL, cortisol, TSH, and FT4 levels revealed that patients with functional pituitary adenomas had elevated PRL levels. Therefore, postoperative monitoring focused on the normalization of PRL levels. Hormonal improvement was observed in 77.77% of patients: 66.66% showed a decrease in PRL levels to within the normal range, while 11.11% exhibited an upward trend toward normalization. However, two patients continued to experience persistent hyperprolactinemia after surgery. One of these patients received additional therapy with a dopamine agonist (cabergoline) and showed gradual hormonal improvement during follow-up. Another patient had an incomplete tumor resection and was lost to follow-up after the 3rd month, leaving no further data on whether additional therapy, radiotherapy, or re-exploration was pursued.

In East Java, there are several centers that provide endonasal transsphenoidal pituitary surgery, including Dr. Soetomo Hospital in Surabaya, Dr. Saiful Anwar Hospital in Malang, and others. Many patients from rural areas prefer to seek treatment in Malang due to several practical considerations. Access to another health center in a big city is often perceived as more challenging due to heavy urban traffic, higher living costs, and longer surgical waiting times. In contrast, Malang is considered more accessible, more affordable, and many patients have family members living in or around the city, which facilitates both logistical and emotional support during treatment. Clinically, Dr. Saiful Anwar Hospital is recognized as one of the centers of excellence for health care in eastern Java, the biggest population island in Indonesia. The hospital offers several advantages, including an experienced multidisciplinary team, advanced diagnostic facilities such as high-resolution MRI, and comprehensive postoperative care services. Moreover, Indonesia’s National Health Insurance (NHI) scheme enables patients to access these advanced services at no additional cost, which provided that the referral pathway is properly followed. This case series demonstrates that beyond clinical considerations, geographical, economic, and familial support factors significantly influence healthcare-seeking behavior among rural populations.

Long-term follow-up remains a critical aspect in the management of pituitary adenomas, particularly for monitoring hormonal status and tumor recurrence. In our case series, several patients did not return for follow-up after the 3rd postoperative month, limiting comprehensive endocrine and visual evaluations. This situation highlights the urgent need for alternative strategies, such as teleendocrinology services, especially for patients living in remote or underserved areas. Although telemedicine offers a promising solution to overcome geographical barriers, its implementation remains very limited in our facility. All healthcare services at Dr. Saiful Anwar Hospital are covered by the National Health Insurance (NHI) scheme, which currently does not support telemedicine as a reimbursable or administratively regulated service. As a result, we as clinicians face limitations in providing remote follow-up care, both from administrative and legal or ethical perspectives.

This study carries a potential selection bias, as it only included patients with pituitary adenomas who underwent surgical treatment. Cases that were successfully managed conservatively with medical therapy – such as mild prolactinomas – were not captured in this analysis. As a result, the study population more accurately represents symptomatic, invasive, or treatment-resistant cases. All surgical procedures were performed by the same surgeon, which reduced variations in operative technique and postoperative management, thereby minimizing outcome-related bias. This consistency strengthens the internal validity of the study, although it may limit the generalizability of the findings to other institutions that may differ in surgical expertise, available facilities, or multidisciplinary support.

The limited sample size in this study was influenced by the relatively short data collection period (1 year) and the geographic challenges associated with the referral area. As an archipelagic country, Indonesia faces various obstacles in referring patients to tertiary care centers such as Dr. Saiful Anwar Hospital, particularly for those coming from remote regions. Even after undergoing surgery, many patients encounter difficulties in returning to the hospital for follow-up visits due to limited transportation access, financial constraints, or long travel distances. These factors contribute to low follow-up compliance and limit the completeness of long-term outcome data in this study. To enhance external validity and obtain a more representative overview, multicenter studies supported by a national registry are needed. This approach would also enable systematic evaluation of variations in the management and clinical outcomes of pituitary adenoma patients across different levels of healthcare facilities in Indonesia.

CONCLUSION

The endonasal transsphenoidal approach is an effective and safe surgical method for managing pituitary adenomas. A multidisciplinary team with an integrated and comprehensive workflow enhances the effectiveness of treatment, improves patient outcomes, and facilitates postoperative monitoring and evaluation. Moving forward, strengthening referral pathways from rural areas and adopting telemedicine-based follow-up may improve access to care and long-term monitoring.

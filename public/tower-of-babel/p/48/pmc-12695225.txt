Dalpiciclib plus fulvestrant and pyrotinib in HR+/HER2-low advanced breast cancer after progression on CDK4/6 inhibition (DapPLE-HER): a Bayesian optimal phase II study
Authors: Chunfang Hao, Jing Shi, Yuhua Song, Jie Ma, Hua Yang, Yongsheng Jia, Cuiping Song, Wenjing Meng, Jie Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:The optimal management strategy for hormone receptor-positive (HR+)/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HER2-) breast cancer (BC) patients after progression on cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 inhibitors (CDK4/6i) remains undefined. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of a combination therapy of dalpiciclib, fulvestrant, and pyrotinib in HR+/HER2-low advanced BC patients with progression on prior CDK4/6i treatment.Methods:This was an open-label, single-arm, multicenter, Bayesian optimal phase II exploratory study. Eligible patients received dalpiciclib (125 mg/day, 3 weeks on/1 week off), fulvestrant (500 mg intramuscularly every 4 weeks), and pyrotinib (320 mg/day) until disease progression. The primary endpoint was progression-free survival (PFS). Secondary endpoints included objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), and safety.Results:A total of 30 patients were enrolled in this study. At a median follow-up of 14 months, the median PFS was 4.23 months (95% CI: 2.47–7.90). The ORR was 20.7% (95% CI: 9.8%–38.4%) and the DCR was 51.7% (95% CI: 34.4%–68.6%). The common adverse events (AEs) were diarrhea (83.3%) and decreased white blood cell count (70.0%). Grade 3 AEs were infrequent, and no grade ≥4 AEs were observed. Subgroup analysis revealed that mutation status was significantly associated with PFS (P = 0.004).Conclusion:The combination of dalpiciclib, fulvestrant, and pyrotinib demonstrated modest efficacy with a manageable safety profile in HR+/HER2-low advanced BC patients who had progressed on prior CDK4/6i therapy. This novel regimen demonstrated exploratory efficacy and a favorable safety profile, warranting further validation in future randomized controlled studies.

Introduction

Hormone receptor-positive (HR+)/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HER2-) breast cancer (BC) accounts for approximately 60–70% of all BC cases[1]. While endocrine therapy (ET) and cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 inhibitors (CDK4/6i) have markedly improved outcomes in both metastatic and early-stage settings, inevitable drug resistance remains a major clinical challenge[2–8]. Recent research has explored various strategies to overcome CDK4/6i resistance, such as switching therapies and combining CDK4/6i with targeted agents[12–15]. Notably, up to 65.4% of HR + BC patients exhibit HER2-low expression[16]; therefore, conventional anti-HER2 therapies may show limited efficacy in this subgroup of patients. The PILHLE-001 study demonstrated that pyrotinib combined with neoadjuvant chemotherapy exhibits synergistic antitumor activity in patients with high-risk early-stage Luminal/HER2-low BC[17]. In HER2-low BC cells, the combination of fulvestrant and CDK4/6i induces upregulation of HER2 protein and activation of the downstream Akt phosphorylation signaling pathway. Further addition of anti-HER2 therapy inhibits this pathway, resulting in sustained degradation of Rb protein and reduction of Ki67 expression. These findings suggest that the triple combination regimen may reverse endocrine resistance through synergistic modulation of multiple signaling pathways[18].

In this context, two promising drugs have emerged: dalpiciclib, a novel CDK4/6i, and pyrotinib, a pan-HER tyrosine kinase inhibitor[19–21], both of which have demonstrated efficacy in respective settings. However, there are no data on combining a CDK4/6i with HER-2 target drugs and ET.

Given this gap, we propose an innovative clinical study to evaluate the combination of dalpiciclib, fulvestrant, and pyrotinib in patients with HR+/HER2-low advanced BC who have progressed on previous CDK4/6i plus aromatase inhibitor (AI) therapy. Our study seeks to address the urgent need for effective post-CDK4/6i therapeutic strategies across different disease stages and provide valuable insights to guide clinical decisions in the rapidly evolving field of BC treatment. Our article is compliant with the TITAN Guidelines 2025[22].

Material and methods

Patients

Eligibility criteria included age ≥18 years; histologically or cytologically confirmed HR+/HER2-low (defined as HER2 1+ or 2+/ISH−) locally advanced or metastatic BC and not amenable to curative surgery or radiotherapy; HER2-low expression based on the most recent assessment from either the primary or metastatic lesion; documented progression on CDK4/6i plus AI as initial therapy for advanced disease or recurrent during/after adjuvant CDK4/6i plus ET; Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status ≤ 2; measurable lesions per Response Evaluation Criteria In Solid Tumors (RECIST, version 1.1) or bone-only metastases; patients with stable, treated brain metastases were eligible if lesions remained stable for ≥3 months without corticosteroids; adequate organ function was required, absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1.5 × 109/L, platelets ≥ 100 × 109/L, hemoglobin ≥ 9.0 g/dL, total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 × upper limit of normal (ULN), transaminases ≤ 2.5 × ULN (≤5 × ULN with liver metastases), and creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min. The key exclusion criteria were as follows: HER2+ (IHC3+ or IHC2+/ISH+); symptom of brain metastasis; prior treatment with dalpiciclib, pyrotinib, or any antibody-drug; and investigator-determined unsuitability for the combination therapy. All procedures were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Conference on Harmonization Guidelines for Good Clinical Practice. The protocol was approved by the institutional review boards at the Tianjin Medical University Cancer Institute and Hospital, along with associated subcenters. All patients signed informed consent prior to enrollment.

Study design and treatments

This DapPLE-HER trial is an open-label, single-arm, multicenter, Bayesian optimal phase II (BOP2) exploratory study (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier NCT05806671). Eligible patients received oral dalpiciclib at a dose of 125 mg once daily (3 weeks on and 1 week off), intramuscular fulvestrant at a dose of 500 mg every 4 weeks, and oral pyrotinib at a dose of 320 mg/day until disease progression according to RECIST version 1.1 or unacceptable toxicity.

End points and assessments

The primary endpoint of this study was investigator-assessed progression-free survival (PFS), defined as the time from treatment initiation to disease progression or death. Secondary endpoints included objective response rate (ORR; complete or partial response), disease control rate (DCR), adverse event (AE) rates, and overall survival (OS), defined as the time from the first dose of study treatment to death from any cause. Clinical assessments conducted in this trial, including physical examinations, vital signs, performance status examination, as well as hematology and biochemistry laboratory tests on Days 1 and 8 of each treatment cycle.

Tumor assessments were conducted using computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging every three cycles until disease progression, with tumor response evaluation per RECIST version 1.1.

AEs and dose titration

All AEs were recorded from treatment initiation until 30 days after treatment and graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), version 5.0. Dose adjustments were conducted based on predefined protocol criteria. For initial grade 3 AEs, treatment was resumed at the same dose once toxicity resolved to less than grade 2. For recurrent grade 3 AEs, dalpiciclib was temporarily discontinued and resumed at a reduced dose after resolution to grade <2. In cases of grade 4 hematologic toxicity, treatment was interrupted until recovery to grade <2, followed by dose reduction upon reinitiation. For a first occurrence of grade 4 non-hematologic toxicity or grade ≥3 febrile neutropenia, treatment was resumed at either the same or a reduced dose after the event had been resolved, depending on clinical judgment.

Genomic profiling

Next-generation sequencing was conducted by Shenzhen Yuce Biotechnology Co., Ltd. using the YuceOne Target panel, which includes 111 cancer-related genes. DNA was sequenced on the BGI T7 platform. Bioinformatics analyses and annotation followed the standard protocol provided by the testing laboratory.

Statistical analysis

The study employed a BOP2 design based on a time-to-event Bayesian framework. This design enables real-time efficacy monitoring through cumulative patient follow-up and prespecified decision boundaries, allowing for adaptive interim analyses and early stopping for futility or efficacy, while preserving type I error control and statistical power[23]. The null hypothesis H0: θ ≤ 3.5 months (where θ is the median survival time) was tested against the alternative hypothesis H1: θ > 5.6 months. Enrollment is terminated early and the treatment is deemed ineffective if the posterior probability of benefit falls below a dynamic threshold: Pr (μ>3.5 V data) < λ(nN)α,

where n is the number of patients enrolled at the interim analysis, N is the planned maximum sample size (N = 30), and the design constants λ = 0.84 and α = 0.86 were optimized to maximize power under H1 while controlling the type I error rate at 10% under H0. A vague inverse gamma prior IG (1.05, 0.25) was assumed for θ.

The decision rule was defined using the total observation time (TOT), calculated as the sum of observed times across all patients, TOT=∑i=1nTi

where Tᵢ = min (Zᵢ, Uᵢ), with Zᵢ denoting the event time and Uᵢ the censoring time for patient i.

Early stopping boundaries and TOT thresholds for interim (n = 15) and final (n = 30) analyses were derived from pre-specified simulations (n = 5000) conducted using the BOP2 web application (v1.4.22.0), as presented in Supplemental Digital Content Table S1, available at: http://links.lww.com/JS9/F122 and Supplemental Digital Content Table S2, available at: http://links.lww.com/JS9/F123. This configuration yields an estimated power of 80.3% under H1 and ensures control of false-positive conclusions under H0.

Results were presented as frequencies (percentages) for categorical variables and as medians [ranges or interquartile ranges (IQRs)] for continuous variables. Time-to-event data were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated using Greenwood’s formula. Time-to-event results were compared between subgroups using the log-rank test. SPSS version 24.0 (IBM SPSS Statistics, Armonk, NY: IBM Corp) was used for the statistical analysis.

Results

Patient characteristics

From May 2023 to November 2024, a total of 33 patients were screened, of which 30 met the inclusion criteria and thus formed the intent-to-treat (ITT) population. The demographic and baseline characteristics of the ITT population were summarized in Table 1, and the patient flowchart is shown in Figure 1. The median age of enrolled patients was 51 years (IQR: 43–57), and 90% of them had an ECOG score of 0. All patients were ER strongly positive, 80% had PR strongly positive, 40% of cases were classified as HER2 2+/ISH−, and 87% of cases with Ki67 > 20%. Among the 30 enrolled patients, successful genomic profiling results were obtained for 14 patients (46.7%), with 7 (50%) showing PIK3CA, BRCA2, or PTEN gene mutations. Visceral metastases were observed in 53% of patients, including liver (40%) and lung (27%) involvement. Prior treatment included radiotherapy (40%) and chemotherapy (73%), and 67% had received abemaciclib and 20% palbociclib, with a median treatment duration of 14 months (IQR: 11–21). Efficacy

Interim analysis

At the interim analysis on 24 January 2024, 4 of 15 enrolled patients had disease progression. The TOT was 49.50 months, exceeding the predetermined stopping boundary (22.54 months). Median PFS (mPFS) was not reached due to insufficient events, yet the 6-month PFS rate achieved 55.7%. Among nine evaluable patients, the objective response rate (ORR) was 44.44% (4/9) and the DCR reached 66.67% (6/9). Based on these results, enrollment continued to reach the planned total enrollment of 30 participants.

Final analysis

At the cut-off date of 15 December 2024, the final analysis included 30 patients with 23 PFS events. The TOT reached 150.36 months, surpassing the boundary of 145.60 months. With a median follow-up duration of 14 months, the mPFS was 4.23 months (95% CI: 2.47–7.90) (Fig. 2a). Subgroup analysis

In the post hoc subgroup analysis, the mutation status was the only factor significantly associated with mPFS, with an mPFS of 1.63 months [95% CI: 1.23– Not available (NA)] in the mutated group compared to 7.80 months (95% CI: 1.9–NA) in the wild-type group (P = 0.004) (Fig. 2b). No significant differences were observed in any of the other subgroup analysis. The mPFS for palbociclib was 5.4 months versus 3.4 months for others (P = 0.80), and 3.9 months for abemaciclib versus 5.0 months for others (P = 0.68) (Fig. 2c). Similarly, there was no significant difference in mPFS between advanced and early CDK4/6i patients (4.2 versus 1.6 months, P = 0.52) (Fig. 2d). Additionally, patients who received CDK4/6i treatment for ≥ 12 months had an mPFS of 3.1 months compared to 10.3 months for those treated for <12 months (P = 0.05) (Fig. 2e).

Safety and dose modification

The common AEs during treatment were diarrhea 83.3% (25/30), decreased white blood cell count 70.0% (21/30), and decreased neutrophil count 53.3% (16/30). Grade 3 AEs included decreased white blood cell count in six patients (20%), diarrhea in four patients (13.3%), and decreased neutrophil count in four patients (13.3%). No grade 4 or higher AEs were observed (Table 4). One patient experienced a dose adjustment, with the dose of dalpiciclib reduced to 100 mg. The efficacy evaluation was complete response (CR), and the patient is currently continuing treatment. Dose modification was required in one patient (ID 0102), who experienced recurrent grade 3 leukopenia and neutropenia. Discussion

This is the first study using a BOP2 design to explore CDK4/6i rechallenge in HR+/HER2-low advanced BC patients. Our study enrolled patients who had progressed after CDK4/6i plus AI therapy, including both early and advanced CDK4/6i patients, with the majority having received abemaciclib. Both interim and final analysis met the prespecified threshold for TOT.

The final analysis showed a PFS of 4.23 months (95% CI 2.47–7.90), which is comparable to the experimental group data from previous CDK4/6i rechallenge studies, with mPFS values being reported as 4.6 months in PACE (palbociclib plus ET), 5.3 months in MAINTAIN (ribociclib plus ET), 4.9 months in PALMIRA, and 6.0 months in postMONARCH (abemaciclib plus fulvestrant)[12–14,24]. However, our study had a notably higher proportion of abemaciclib-pretreated patients (70%) compared to these trials (PACE 2.7%, MAINTAIN 3.0%, and postMONARCH 8%), and these patients demonstrated a shorter mPFS (3.9 vs. 5.0 months). Compared with the EMERALD-3 trial, in which imlunestrant plus abemaciclib achieved a median PFS of 9.4 months in ER+/HER2-advanced BC post-CDK4/6i[25], the median PFS of 4.2 months observed in our study highlights the modest clinical activity of this triple combination and reinforces the need for biomarker-guided approaches to optimize treatment efficacy in the HR+/HER2-low population.

Stratified analysis by HER2 expression revealed no significant difference in mPFS between patients with HER2 1+ and 2+ ISH-negative status (5.0 vs. 4.1 months, Supplemental Digital Content Figure S1, available at: http://links.lww.com/JS9/F121). This contrasts with previous studies and preclinical data, where pyrotinib demonstrated greater antitumor activity against HER2-low (2+/FISH−) cells compared to 1+ cells, as validated in PDX models. While neoadjuvant trials have shown promising efficacy and safety of pyrotinib plus chemotherapy in luminal/HER2-low high-risk early BC, our findings suggest a different outcome[17]. Several factors may account for this discrepancy. First, our relatively small sample size might limit the generalizability of our results. Second, the specific context of CDK4/6i rechallenge may influence therapeutic response. Additionally, differences in pathological sampling methodology between studies may play a role, as our study included patients based on HER2-low expression in either primary or metastatic lesions, whereas neoadjuvant studies assessed HER2 status assessment exclusively from primary tumors[26]. These factors warrant further investigation in larger cohorts. Moreover, recent molecular studies have demonstrated that HER2-low BC is a biologically heterogeneous entity, with IHC 1+ and IHC 2+/ISH− subtypes exhibiting distinct gene expression profiles and clinical characteristics, particularly with respect to PAM50 intrinsic subtypes[16]. Notably, HER2 expression is not a static biomarker and may evolve throughout the disease course or in response to prior therapies. These observations highlight the importance of dynamic, lesion-specific HER2 assessment in future studies to refine treatment selection and improve interpretation of therapeutic outcomes.

Notably, previous studies have demonstrated that patients harboring PIK3CA mutations derive limited benefit from CDK4/6i therapy. In the PALOMA-3 trial, patients with PIK3CA mutations had a significantly shorter mPFS compared to those with wild-type tumors (5.8 months vs. 9.2 months), suggesting a poor prognostic subtype potentially associated with resistance to CDK4/6 inhibition[27]. In our study, among the seven patients who underwent genomic profiling, patients with PIK3CA mutations (n = 4) had an mPFS of 1.4 months, those with BRCA2 mutations (n = 2) had an mPFS of 1.2 months, and the patient with a PTEN mutation (n = 1) had an mPFS of 1.9 months. Although the small sample size limits definitive conclusions, these findings suggest that genomic alterations may influence treatment response.

In this context, mutation-directed targeted therapies are emerging as a potential strategy to overcome resistance. For instance, the Capitello-291 study showed superiority of capivasertib plus fulvestrant significantly improved mPFS compared to fulvestrant monotherapy in patients with PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN mutations (7.3 vs. 3.1 months, HR = 0.50). Similarly, the FABULOUS trial demonstrated that olaparib ± apatinib outperformed standard therapy in BRCA1/2-mutated patients (11.0 vs. 6.7 vs. 3.0 months)[28,29]. These findings suggest that, in biomarker-defined subpopulations, rechallenge with CDK4/6i in combination with mutation-specific targeted therapies may offer potential clinical benefit for patients with resistant disease.

Additionally, novel cytotoxic approaches such as antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs) have emerged as promising alternatives, particularly in the HER2-low BC setting. The DESTINY-Breast06 trial demonstrated a remarkable mPFS of 13.2 months (by blinded independent central review) with trastuzumab deruxtecan in HER2-low patients, further supporting the potential of ADCs in this population[28].

The safety profile observed in our study was consistent with previous reports but demonstrated a notably lower incidence and severity of AEs. The common toxicities included diarrhea and hematologic events, predominantly grade 1–2. Grade 3 treatment-related AEs occurred in 56.7% (17/30) of patients, with no grade 4 events observed. In contrast, previous studies reported grade 3–4 events in 80%–82.9% of patients, including some grade 4 toxicities[30–32]. While the types of AEs were similar, mainly hematologic and gastrointestinal, the notably lower severity observed in our study warrants further investigation, as it may have important implications for patient quality of life and treatment adherence.

This study has several important limitations. First, the small sample size and single-arm design without a comparator arm introduce risks of selection and information bias and limit the ability to draw definitive conclusions. The absence of a randomized control group limits the ability to isolate the contribution of pyrotinib within the combination regimen. Second, although a BOP2 design was employed, the use of a 10% type I error rate and the wide CIs observed for endpoints such as 6-month PFS (17.9%–55.0%) highlight the statistical uncertainty inherent in this exploratory phase II trial. Third, substantial heterogeneity in prior treatments – particularly the high proportion (70%) of patients pretreated with abemaciclib – raises concerns regarding potential confounding. Although exploratory sensitivity analyses did not reveal statistically significant differences in outcomes between CDK4/6i subgroups, the study was not powered for definitive assessment, and future studies should incorporate stratified designs. Fourth, the HER2-low population is biologically heterogeneous, and current definitions based on IHC 1+ versus 2+/ISH− are subject to interpretative variability. Although preclinical data suggest that HER2 2+/FISH− tumors may be more sensitive to anti-HER2 agents, our study observed no significant difference in mPFS between HER2 1+ and 2+ subgroups. This discrepancy may be attributed to several factors: heterogeneity between primary and metastatic lesions, the small sample size, and the potential modulation of HER2 signaling following prior CDK4/6i exposure.

Finally, the lack of comprehensive biomarker stratification – such as PIK3CA mutation status – and the immature OS data further limit the clinical interpretability and generalizability of our findings. Despite these limitations, the study provides preliminary evidence that the combination of dalpiciclib, pyrotinib, and fulvestrant demonstrates manageable safety and potential activity in HR+/HER2-low advanced BC following prior CDK4/6i therapy. These results support further investigation in biomarker-defined, randomized studies.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study provides preliminary evidence supporting the feasibility of CDK4/6i rechallenge in HR+/HER2-low advanced BC, particularly in patients without certain genomic alterations (e.g., PIK3CA, AKT, or BRCA mutations). The safety profile was manageable. Observations related to HER2 expression and mutation status warrant further investigation. Future studies addressing the current limitations may help clarify the therapeutic role of this combination in a molecularly defined population.

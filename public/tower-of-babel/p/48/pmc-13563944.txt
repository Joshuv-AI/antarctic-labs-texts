Comprehensive multi-omics analysis reveals a fatty acid metabolism gene signature for prognostic assessment and immunotherapy in nasopharyngeal carcinoma, and identifies ABCC1 as a potential novel therapeutic target
Authors: Yang Xu, Liru Zhu, Qingqing Zhang, Xiaojun Chen, Haoyu Lv, Shuhan Ma, Runzhi Wang, Yuanyuan Chang, Yongchu Sun, Kaihua Chen, Ling Li, Xiaodong Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is a subtype of head and neck squamous cell carcinoma characterized by high recurrence and metastasis rates and poor prognosis. Although immune checkpoint inhibitors have emerged as a promising treatment strategy for recurrent/metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma (R/M NPC), only a few patients have benefitted significantly from them. Lipid metabolism reprogramming plays a crucial role in NPC progression and its interaction with the immune microenvironment. This study aims to establish a prognostic model for NPC based on lipid metabolism-related factors, further explore its association with tumor immunity, and investigate the potential for immunotherapy.MethodsCollect GEO datasets(GSE53819 and GSE102349) for differential expression analysis and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression to identify prognostic genes and construct a fatty-acid-metabolism–related prognostic model. Survival analyses and time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were applied to evaluate the predictive performance of the constructed prognostic markers. Furthermore, associations between the derived risk scores and immunological characteristics were systematically evaluated. Following the identification of ABCC1 as a key gene, its expression was validated through RT-qPCR, immunoblotting, and immunohistochemistry (IHC). Its functional role was further investigated using in vitro functional assays, multiplex immunohistochemistry (mIHC), co-culture experiments with CD8⁺ T cells, and in vivo xenograft tumor models in nude mice.ResultsFour genes (ABCC1, CD1D, CYP4B1, and DPEP2) were identified to constr uct a prognostic model associated with fatty acid metabolism. This model revealed significant distinctions in immune infiltration patterns between high-risk and low-risk groups. Specifically, the high-risk group displayed immunosuppressive characteristics, marked by reduced infiltration of CD8⁺T cells. Functional studies demonstrated that ABCC1 promoted NPC cell proliferation, migration, invasion, ROS accumulation, and lipid metabolic reprogramming. Mechanistically, ABCC1 was epigenetically upregulated by the histone acetyltransferase P300 and contributed to CD8⁺ T cell dysfunction and MEK/ERK pathway activation, thereby driving tumor progression.ConclusionIn summary, we established a novel fatty-acid-metabolism–related prognostic model for assessing the prognosis and potential immunotherapy response of NPC patients, as well as for characterizing the immunological features of the tumor microenvironment (TME). Furthermore, ABCC1 emerged as a promising prognostic biomarker associated with immunotherapeutic responsiveness in NPC, warranting further validation.Clinical trial number Not applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13062-026-00875-4.Keywords: Nasopharyngeal carcinoma, Prognostic signature, Fatty acid metabolism, ABCC1, Tumor microenvironment, Immunotherapy

Introduction

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is a type of malignant tumor that develops from the mucosal epithelium in the nasopharynx and is recognized as a unique endemic variant of head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) [1]. Among HNSCCs, NPC is especially susceptible to both recurrence and distant metastasis, resulting in restricted clinical advantages and an overall unfavorable prognosis [2]. Moreover, therapeutic options for patients with recurrent or metastatic NPC (R/M NPC) remain restricted, and survival outcomes are unsatisfactory [3, 4]. In recent times, immunotherapy has become a hopeful treatment approach for various cancer forms. Specifically, immune checkpoint inhibitors that focus on programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1), including nivolumab and pembrolizumab, have shown significant clinical effectiveness in NPC [5]. However, just a portion of patients experience lasting clinical advantages and enhancements in survival as a result of these agents [6]. Consequently, there is an urgent need to identify prognostic markers that can predict immunotherapy response and guide personalized treatment strategies for patients with R/M NPC. Notably, biomarkers widely validated for anti–PD-1 therapy in other malignancies have not yet been confirmed as applicable to NPC, underscoring the continued necessity for effective prognostic and therapeutic indicators [5, 7].

Lipid metabolic reprogramming is a hallmark of cancer, with alterations in fatty acid availability emerging as a critical driver of tumorigenesis. This process unfolds in three stages to sustain the high energetic demands of cancer cell growth and to mediate immune suppression. Initially, changes in nutrient utilization lead to an increase in both lipid absorption and the process of de novo lipogenesis [8]. Following this, the lipids produced are mainly directed towards metabolic pathways that facilitate oncogenic changes. Ultimately, these metabolic modifications influence tumor cells and immune cells within the tumor microenvironment (TME) over the long term [9]. Important enzymes associated with lipid metabolism may show irregular activity during this process and could even develop new functions beyond metabolism [10]. Recent studies have confirmed the significance of lipid metabolic reprogramming in NPC. Clinical findings have revealed considerable abnormalities in the plasma lipid profiles of NPC patients, with notable decreases identified in 19 lipid species, such as phosphatidylinositol (18:0/20:4) and free fatty acid (22:6) [11].

Reprogramming of lipid metabolism not only promotes tumorigenesis and the advancement of tumors but also alters the TME by influencing the recruitment, survival, and functionality of immune cells [12]. The dynamic remodeling of the TME leads to distinct lipid metabolic patterns among different tumor and immune cell subsets across various stages of disease progression. Actively proliferating or metastatic tumor cells exhibit heightened dependence on lipid metabolism. Similarly, pro-tumor immune cells rely heavily on lipid utilization, whereas anti-tumor immune cells preferentially depend on glucose metabolism [13]. In addition, metabolic stress within the TME can trigger autophagy, while cell death pathways associated with lipid peroxidation further link lipid metabolism to immune regulation [14, 15]. Recent evidence indicates that aberrant lipid metabolism in cancer patients impairs the recruitment of CD8⁺ T cells, compromises their cytotoxic activity, and consequently reduces the efficacy of anti-PD-1 monotherapy [16, 17]. However, the intricate interplay between dysregulated fatty acid metabolism and CD8⁺ T-cell function in the NPC immune microenvironment remains poorly defined. Elucidating these relationships is therefore critical for the rational design of combination strategies aimed at enhancing the responsiveness of NPC patients to immunotherapy.

In this study, we established novel mRNA signatures associated with fatty acid metabolism from the GEO cohort to improve the prediction of survival outcomes in patients with NPC. We further investigated the involvement of the target genes of these signatures in NPC immunotherapy. Among them, ABCC1 emerged as a pivotal gene linked to unfavorable prognosis within the fatty acid metabolism–related signature set. Our analyses revealed that ABCC1 is markedly overexpressed in NPC, and its knockdown significantly suppressed the malignant phenotype of NPC cells. Importantly, the bioinformatics predictions were corroborated by multiple lines of experimental evidence, including analyses of human NPC tissue specimens, in vitro co-culture of NPC cells with CD8⁺ T cells, and a mouse xenograft model. Collectively, this study proposes a lipid metabolism–based prognostic model for predicting immunotherapy response in NPC and uncovers a mechanistic role of ABCC1 in immune evasion, thereby offering new therapeutic targets and candidate strategies to enhance immunotherapy sensitivity in NPC.

Methods

Data collection

Gene expression profiles alongside clinical data were acquired from two distinct cohorts representing East Asian populations, specifically focused on nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) and normal samples. These datasets, GSE53819 and GSE102349, were sourced from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database, accessible online at http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/. The GSE53819 dataset comprises a total of 36 samples, evenly split between 18 normal and 18 NPC samples, thereby allowing for comparative analysis. In contrast, the GSE102349 dataset features a more extensive collection of 113 NPC samples, which enhances the statistical power of subsequent analyses.Additionally, single-cell transcriptomics data were sourced from the GSE150825 dataset, complemented by spatial transcriptomics data from GSE299776, both also obtained from the GEO repository. This array of datasets serves to enrich the analysis of cellular and tissue-level variations in gene expression relevant to the study of NPC. A comprehensive examination led to the identification of 309 genes associated with fatty acid metabolism. These genes were meticulously assembled from various biological pathways outlined in the KEGG database, along with those categorized within the Hallmark gene sets, focusing specifically on genes that play significant roles in fatty acid metabolism [18].

Differential expression genes identification

The expression profiles of 309 genes related to fatty acid metabolism were assessed by comparing samples from nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) and normal tissues sourced from the GEO cohort. To enhance the visualization of the differentially expressed fatty acid metabolism-related genes, heatmaps and volcano plots were created. In this analysis, differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified by applying specific criteria, which included a threshold of |log₂ fold change (FC)| ≥ 0.5 and a false discovery rate (FDR) of less than 0.05.

Construction of the prognostic model

Subsequently, a prognostic model associated with fatty acid metabolism was developed using candidate genes linked to fatty acid metabolism identified through differential gene analysis. Initially, univariate Cox regression analysis was conducted to identify genes that had a significant correlation with progression-free survival (PFS, P < 0.05). Following this, the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression method was performed using the “glmnet” R package on the significant variables (p < 0.05) to pinpoint a set of potential prognostic genes, designated as biomarkers related to fatty acid metabolism. The optimal penalty parameter λ was determined based on the 1–standard error (1-SE) criterion to identify the final candidate genes.For each patient, a risk score was computed as a linear combination of the expression levels of these biomarkers, adjusted by their respective LASSO coefficients. To assess the prognostic significance of the risk score, patients were divided into high-risk and low-risk categories, employing the median risk score (50%) as the threshold. Kaplan–Meier (K–M) survival curves were produced using the “survival” and “survminer” R packages to compare overall survival rates between the two categories. Additionally, time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (t-ROC) curves were used to evaluate the predictive capability of the risk model in patients with NPC.Overall survival (OS) is defined as the time from diagnosis to death from any cause.Progression-free survival (PFS) is defined as the time from the diagnosis to disease progression or death from any cause, whichever occurs first.

Gene set enrichment analysis and gene set variation analysis

We utilized the “clusterProfiler” package to perform gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) on the complete set of transcripts, relying on the adjusted expression data. This analysis aimed to identify the pathways and biological functions that are enriched in both low-risk and high-risk groups. For reference, we selected the gene set from the MsigDB database designated as “c2.Cp.Kegg.V7.4 symbols.The GMT.” In our analysis, we established specific criteria for determining significant enrichment: we considered a normalized enrichment score (NES) greater than 1, a nominal p-value (NOM p-value) less than 0.05, and a false discovery rate (FDR) lower than 0.25 as indicative of significant enrichment.Furthermore, we employed the “GSVA” and “limma” packages to conduct gene set variation analysis (GSVA), which enabled us to assess gene sets present in all samples from the training cohort. A pathway was deemed significantly enriched when it met the criteria of a log fold change (logFC) greater than 0.1 and an adjusted p-value of less than 0.05. This rigorous approach allowed us to elucidate the differential gene expression patterns and associated biological pathways relevant to the stratified risk groups within our study.

Tumor microenvironment and immunotherapy response

The tumor microenvironment in patients with NPC was analyzed utilizing the “IBOR” package in R, alongside the CIBERSORT algorithm to assess the infiltration levels of 22 distinct immune cell populations. The presence of 28 immune cell types within the NPC microenvironment was assessed through single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA). Immune dysfunction scores for each sample were calculated using the TIDE algorithm, which helps to eliminate scores and immunosuppressive cells (such as myeloid-derived suppressor cells, or MDSCs) (TIDE: http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu/), allowing us to predict the treatment’s immune efficacy in both high-risk and low-risk patient groups [19]. Finally, we obtained the IMvigor210 cohort - this cohort included 348 cancer patients who received PD-L1 antibody treatment [20]. Based on this cohort, we verified the prognostic predictive ability of fatty acid metabolism biomarkers and the predictive ability of immunotherapy efficacy.

Single cell RNA-seq analysis

Data normalization was conducted utilizing Seurat version 4.2.0 to ensure the integrity and comparability of the single-cell RNA sequencing data. During this process, cells that exhibited low gene expression, defined as those with fewer than 200 molecular identifiers, were removed from the analysis. Additionally, any cells that displayed more than 30% of their reads coming from mitochondrial genes were excluded as well, as such characteristics are often indicative of poor-quality cells. Following this filtering step, the analysis focused on the top 3000 variable genes, which were deemed most relevant for subsequent clustering efforts. Dimensionality reduction was then executed using the Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) technique on the first 29 principal components, allowing for an effective visualization and understanding of the high-dimensional data.For the purpose of annotating cell populations, manual classification was performed, drawing upon existing literature to support the identification of various cell types within the dataset. To assess the risk associated with tumor cells in the scRNA-seq dataset, the single-sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA) algorithm was implemented, providing a means to derive tumor cell risk scores indicative of their potential malignancy. Furthermore, an analysis of cell-cell communication was conducted using the CellChat R package, version 1.6.1, enabling the exploration of the interactions and signaling pathways among different cell populations within the tumor microenvironment [21].

Spatial transcriptomics data analysis

The processing and analysis of spatial transcriptomics (ST) data utilized the Seurat R package. To achieve standardization of the ST data, the SCT method was implemented, and integration of the data was carried out using the SelectIntegrationFeatures, PrepSCTIntegration, FindIntegrationAnchors, and IntegrateData functions. After integration, unsupervised clustering was performed to categorize similar ST regions. Annotation of cell populations was determined by hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining alongside genes showing significant variation in each cluster. Ultimately, the visualization of cellular expression levels across the ST dataset was accomplished using the SpatialDimPlot and SpatialFeaturePlot functions.

Cell lines, reagents, plasmids, and cell transfection

The human NPC cell lines 5–8 F, TW03, HK-1, TW01, along with the normal nasopharyngeal epithelial cell line NP69, were sourced from the Key Laboratory of Regional High Risk Tumor Early Prevention Research at Guangxi Medical University under the Ministry of Education. All cell lines were maintained and expanded in RPPI-1640 medium (Gibco), which was enhanced with 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco).

The lentivirus for ABCC1 overexpression was acquired from GeneChem Biotechnology. Cells were plated at a density of 2.5 × 10^4 cells per well in 24-well plates. Following a 24-hour incubation period, the cells were transfected with the lentivirus as per the manufacturer’s guidelines and subsequently treated with puromycin (1 µg/mL, Solarbio) in the culture medium for a duration of 48 h. si-ABCC1, si-P300, and a control vector were procured from Sangon and were transfected using the lipo3000 transfection reagent (Thermofisher), following the manufacturer’s protocol. The sequences for the shRNAs can be found in Supplementary Table S1.

RNA isolation and real-time qPCR

Following the guidelines provided by the manufacturer, total RNA was isolated using Trizol reagent (Solarbio, China) as per the manufacturer’s recommendations. Subsequently, the extracted RNA (2000 ng) underwent reverse transcription with the PrimeScript™ RT kit (Takara, China). Moreover, RT-qPCR assays were executed on a real-time PCR machine employing TB Green Premix (Takara, China), with GAPDH serving as the reference control. The sequences of the primers can be found in Supplementary Table S1.

Western blot

The extraction of proteins involved lysing the samples collected in RIPA buffer (Beyotime, China) that was enriched with protease and phosphatase inhibitors at a ratio of 1:100 (Beyotime, China). Equal quantities of protein were then separated using 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE), applying a steady voltage of 200 V. After separation, the proteins were transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes using a constant current of 400 mA for 120 min. Once the transfer was complete, the membranes were blocked with 5% skimmed milk at room temperature for 1 h and then incubated with appropriately diluted primary antibodies for a duration of 14–16 h at 4 °C. Following wash steps, the membranes were treated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)–conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. The visualization of protein bands was achieved with enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL), and ImageJ software was utilized for quantifying relative protein abundance. A summary of the primary antibodies utilized is included in Table S2.

Cell migration and invasion assays

For the migration assay, 20,000 cells were placed in the upper chamber of a Transwell insert that contained serum-free 1640 medium, whereas the lower chamber was supplied with 1640 medium enriched with 15% fetal bovine serum (FBS). In the case of the invasion assay, the upper chamber was previously coated with Matrigel before the addition of cells, and 600 µl of 1640 medium with 15% FBS was introduced into the lower chamber. After an incubation period of 36 h, any remaining cells on the upper surface of the membrane were carefully removed. The cells that migrated or invaded into the lower chamber were subsequently fixed using 4% paraformaldehyde, stained with 1% crystal violet, and counted across four randomly chosen microscopic fields, followed by imaging.

Cell proliferation and colony formation assays

Cell proliferation was assessed through the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay. In summary, cells were plated into 96-well plates at a density of 2.0 × 10³ cells for each well. At the time points of 0, 24, 48, 72, and 96 h post-seeding, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent (Dojindo, Japan) was introduced into each well, followed by a 1-hour incubation at 37 °C. The absorbance was subsequently analyzed at 450 nm using a microplate reader to evaluate cell viability. For the colony formation assay, NPC cells were cultivated in 6-well plates at a density of 800 cells per well and incubated at 37 °C for a duration of 14 days. After this period, the colonies were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min and then stained with 0.1% crystal violet for an additional 30 min. Colonies with 50 or more cells were counted employing ImageJ software.

Immunohistochemistry

A NPC tissue microarray (TMA, NPC1401) was purchased from Outdo Biotech, containing 126 NPC specimens and 15 adjacent non-tumor tissues. The TMA sections were deparaffinized, rehydrated, and subjected to antigen retrieval using a Tris-EDTA antigen retrieval buffer (Servicebio, China). Following the cooling process to room temperature, the activity of endogenous peroxidase was inhibited using a specific blocking agent (Servicebio, China). This was succeeded by the obstruction of nonspecific binding sites with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA). The tissue sections were then allowed to incubate overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies (refer to Supplementary Table S2), washed with PBS, treated with a signal enhancement solution (Servicebio, China), and subsequently incubated with a secondary antibody labeled with biotin (Servicebio, China). The staining was detected using a DAB detection kit (Servicebio, China) and counterstained with hematoxylin.To assess both the intensity and coverage of IHC staining, two independent pathologists conducted scoring based on specific criteria. The intensity of the staining was rated on a scale ranging from 0 to 3: 0 indicating no staining, 1 for weak staining, 2 for moderate or stronger background staining, and 3 representing strong staining. The proportion of positively stained cells was classified as 0 (negative), 1 for less than 25%, 2 for between 25 and 50%, 3 for 50–75%, and 4 for more than 75%. The overall immunoreactivity score was derived by multiplying the intensity score by the percentage score, resulting in a total score that ranges from 0 to 12.Based on this scoring system, samples with a total score of 7 or higher were defined as the high ABCC1 expression group.

Multiplex immunohistochemistry staining (mIHC)

A NPC tissue microarray (TMA, HNasN129Su01) containing 116 NPC specimens was purchased from Outdo Biotech. The sections embedded in paraffin were initially treated with xylene for deparaffinization and then rehydrated using a graded series of ethanol. Following this, antigen retrieval took place in EDTA buffer, and endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched using hydrogen peroxide. To reduce nonspecific binding, the sections were treated with 3% bovine serum albumin (BSA) for blocking. Primary antibodies (detailed in Supplementary Table S2) were then applied to the slides, which were followed by the application of appropriate secondary antibodies. Tyramide signal amplification (TSA) was utilized after each antibody incubation phase. Additional rounds of antigen retrieval and antibody stripping were conducted between successive staining cycles. In conclusion, the nuclei were counterstained with DAPI, and the sections were mounted using an anti-fade mounting medium.

Transcriptomic analysis and untargeted metabolomic analysis

Total RNA was obtained from TW03 cells with ABCC1 silencing, along with the corresponding control cells. The quality of the RNA was evaluated using a 5300 Bioanalyzer, while quantification was executed via an ND-2000 system. Procedures for RNA purification, reverse transcription, library construction, and sequencing adhered to established protocols. Libraries for RNA sequencing were created utilizing the Illumina® Stranded mRNA Prep kit, starting with 1 µg of total RNA. Following quantification using a Qubit 4.0 fluorometer, paired-end RNA sequencing was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. The raw paired-end reads were trimmed and underwent quality control via fastp with standard parameters. Clean reads were aligned to the reference genome in a stranded mode using HISAT2, and transcript assembly was executed for each sample with StringTie.

Metabolites were isolated from both the ABCC1-silenced TW03 cells and the control cells using 400 µL of extraction solution that contained an internal standard (L-2-chlorophenylalanine, at a concentration of 0.02 mg/mL). For quality control (QC), pooled QC samples were generated by combining equal portions from all study samples. These QC samples were processed concurrently with the experimental samples and injected periodically every 5–15 runs to ensure analytical stability. The LC-MS/MS analysis was conducted on a UHPLC-Q Exactive HF-X system featuring an ACQUITY HSS T3 column. The raw LC/MS data were analyzed with Progenesis QI software to create three-dimensional data matrices that encompassed sample details, metabolite identities, and ion intensities. After eliminating internal standard peaks and false-positive signals, redundant features were filtered out, and peaks were merged. Metabolites were identified through searches against databases such as HMDB, Metlin, and other pertinent metabolomic repositories.

Glutathione (GSH) quantification and malondialdehyde (MDA) determination

The levels of total glutathione in cells were assessed utilizing a GSH/GSSG detection kit (Beyotime, China) in accordance with the manufacturer’s guidelines, with absorbance readings taken at 412 nm using a spectrophotometer. To detect malondialdehyde (MDA), cells were lysed and analyzed with a lipid peroxidation MDA assay kit (Beyotime, China), measuring absorbance at a wavelength of 532 nm.

Total ROS and Nile Red staining

Intracellular levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS) were evaluated employing a ROS detection kit (Beyotime, China). Following the specified treatments, the cells were incubated in serum-free medium supplemented with 10 µmol/L DCFH-DA at 37 °C for 20 min. The cells were washed three times with serum-free medium to eliminate any excess probe. Inside the cells, DCFH-DA is converted by intracellular esterases into DCFH, which can be oxidized by ROS to form the fluorescent compound DCF. After labeling, the cells were digested using trypsin, resuspended in serum-free medium, and examined through flow cytometry to assess DCF fluorescence intensity.

At 48 h after transfection, the NPC cell lines (TW03 and 5–8 F) were counted and plated into six-well plates at a density of 1 × 10⁵ cells per well. Following a 24-hour period, the cells were treated with 1 µM Nile Red (Beyotime, China) and DAPI (Beyotime, China) for 10 min in the dark at room temperature. Subsequently, fluorescence images were captured using a fluorescence microscope.

Primary human CD8 T cells + and Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

CD8⁺ T cells were obtained from freshly collected peripheral blood of healthy donors. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Guangxi Medical University Cancer Hospital (Approval No. KY-2022-310), conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, and written informed consent was obtained from all blood donors. The CD8⁺ T cells were isolated by employing a negative selection method utilizing a magnetic bead–based separation kit (STEMCELL Technologies, Canada), following the protocol provided by the manufacturer. The isolated CD8⁺ T cells were stimulated with CD3/CD28 antibodies (STEMCELL Technologies) for a duration of 48 h, and were subsequently cultured in a T cell–specific medium (STEMCELL Technologies) enhanced with 20 ng/ml of IL-2 (STEMCELL Technologies).

In the co-culture assay, CD8⁺ T cells were mixed with NPC tumor cells at an effector-to-target ratio of 10:1 for 48 h. Following this co-incubation, both the cells and the supernatants were collected. The levels of granzyme B, IL-2, TNF-α, and IFN-γ in the culture supernatants were measured using ELISA kits (Elabscience, China), adhering to the instructions provided by the manufacturer.

Xenotransplantation mouse tumor model

BALB/c nude mice were sourced from the Animal Experiment Center at Guangxi Medical University. Approval for all animal-related experiments was granted by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee at the same institution. Five-week-old female BALB/c nude mice received subcutaneous injections of TW03 cells that had been transfected in vitro with plasmids promoting ABCC1 overexpression. Tumor growth was assessed every three days by measuring the tumor volume. After 21 days from the injection, the mice were euthanized under deep anesthesia induced by intraperitoneal injection of pentobarbital sodium. Tumors were then excised, weighed, and preserved in 10% neutral-buffered formalin for a duration of 24 h. The sections, embedded in paraffin, were later stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and underwent immunohistochemical analysis.

Statistical analysis

The in vitro experiments were independently performed three times.For in vivo studies, animals were randomly assigned.Statistical analyses were carried out using R software (version 4.2.1) along with GraphPad Prism 9. To assess differential expression among clusters with varying risk scores, Student’s t-tests were conducted, and differences in stromal score, immune score, and tumor purity between high-risk and low-risk groups were also evaluated. The log-rank test was employed to determine survival differences between the two risk groups, whereas the chi-square test assessed responses to immunotherapy. To investigate the correlations between model genes and immune cell populations, Spearman correlation analysis was performed. A P value of less than 0.05 was deemed statistically significant.Statistical significance is denoted as follows: *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001,****p < 0.0001.

Results

Development and validation of a prognostic model

Metabolic reprogramming plays a vital role in sustaining homeostasis, with several key metabolic pathways associated with glucose and fatty acids potentially being disrupted in cancer cells. In contrast to normal tissues, cancer cells have an increased requirement for fatty acids to synthesize precursors necessary for lipid membranes and signaling molecules [22]. We integrated the single-cell dataset GSE150825. These datasets encompass tumor and normal tissues from NPC patients [23]. To explore the expression characteristics of fatty acid metabolism, we used the AUCell R software package to evaluate the fatty acid metabolic activity of each cell. And it showed that compared to normal tissues, fatty acid metabolic indices were significantly reduced across diverse cell types within tumor tissues (Supplementary Fig. 1). Then, based on this, we selected core fatty acid metabolic genes for screening using GEO transcriptome data.

After the dataset underwent filtering, quality control, and preprocessing, a total of 29 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified utilizing the “limma” package in R. This included 17 genes that were downregulated and 12 that were upregulated. The DEGs were visualized through heatmaps and volcano plots (Fig. 1A, B). Univariate Cox regression analysis indicated that out of the 29 DEGs, CD1D, PTGDS, CYP4B1, and DPEP2 functioned as protective genes with hazard ratios (HR) less than 1, whereas ABCC1 was identified as a risk gene with an HR greater than 1 (Fig. 1C).Further reducing overfitting via LASSO-Cox regression analysis identified CD1D, CYP4B1, DPEP2, and ABCC1 to construct the optimal lipid metabolism-related prognostic signature (Fig. 1D, E). Risk Score = (ABCC1 × 0.535061189340047) − (CD1D × 0.195901258011038) − (CYP4B1 × 0.109399383923761) − (DPEP2 × 0.28853390692939). The K-M survival analysis revealed a poor prognosis for those in the high-risk group (Fig. 1F).Patients were divided into two groups: high-risk and low-risk, according to the median risk score (Fig. 2A-C). Additionally, ROC curve analysis showed that the model has strong predictive capability, with AUC values of 0.789, 0.798, and 0.796 for forecasting outcomes at 1-year, 2-year, and 3-year, respectively (Fig. 2D).

Analysis of pathways correlated with the risk score

We explored enriched biological signaling pathways in high- and low-risk groups through gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA). Using the GO database, the high-risk group was enriched in pathways including adaptive immune response, antigen receptor-mediated signaling, B-cell receptor signaling, immune response-regulated cell surface receptor signaling, and lymphocyte-mediated immune response (Fig. 3A). Subsequent KEGG analysis also showed that the high-risk group was enriched in pathways including allograft rejection, autoimmune thyroid disease, chemokine signaling, hematopoietic cell lineage, and primary immunodeficiency (Fig. 3B). Furthermore, gene set variation analysis (GSVA) of transcriptomic data identified risk-group-specific enriched pathways (Fig. 3C). Most metabolic pathways, including arachidonic acid biosynthesis, were increased in low-risk scores and were also closely associated with immune responses, such as antigen processing and presentation pathways, T cell receptor signaling pathways, and cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction pathways. Gene enrichment analysis in the high-risk group indicated close association with abnormal signaling of key oncogenes, such as the Wnt signaling pathway and Hedgehog signaling pathway.

Association of fatty acid metabolism-related risk score with immune signatures and immunotherapy response in NPC

The heterogeneity of the tumor immune microenvironment is a critical factor influencing tumor development and prognosis. To assess the immune profiles of high-risk and low-risk groups, Cibersoft algorithm analysis revealed that the high-risk group predominantly featured M1 macrophages, activated dendritic cells, and mast cells, while the low-risk group exhibited significantly elevated levels of memory B cells, naive B cells, and CD8 T cells (Fig. 4A). Additionally, checkpoint molecules, HLA, T-cell co-stimulation, cytolytic activity, and pro-inflammatory responses were all activated in the high-risk group (Fig. 4B). Figure 4C indicates that the high-risk group is more prone to immunotherapy failure than the low-risk group. Analysis of the IMvigor210 immunotherapy cohort further revealed, via K-M curves, that high-risk cohort patients exhibited poorer overall survival (OS) compared to low-risk patients. This suggests that fatty acid metabolism-related characteristics may serve as valuable predictive biomarkers for stratifying NPC patients likely to benefit from immunotherapy.

ABCC1 as a key regulator of fatty acid metabolism

To further identify key regulatory genes involved in fatty acid metabolism, we integrated single-cell sequencing and spatial transcriptomics data, which revealed distinct expression patterns of the candidate genes. Specifically, ABCC1 was broadly expressed across multiple cell populations, CD1D was mainly restricted to B cells and myeloid cells, CYP4B1 was predominantly expressed in epithelial cells, and DPEP2 was enriched in B cells, T cells, and a small subset of myeloid cells (Supplementary Fig. 2). In single-gene survival analyses, high ABCC1 expression was associated with poorer prognosis, whereas elevated expression of CD1D, CYP4B1, and DPEP2 correlated with favorable outcomes (Supplementary Fig. 3). Furthermore, immune cell infiltration was assessed using the single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) approach, which demonstrated gene-specific differences in immune infiltration patterns (Supplementary Fig. 3). These findings suggest that the identified genes may exert distinct effects on the immune microenvironment, although additional experimental validation is required to clarify the underlying mechanisms.

Given that ABCC1 exhibited the highest hazard ratio in the GEO cohort, showed an adverse association with prognosis, and was widely expressed across diverse cell populations (including malignant cells), its potential role in NPC remains insufficiently characterized. Previous studies have largely focused on ABCC1 in the context of chemotherapy resistance, while its functions in lipid metabolism and immunotherapy have been underexplored [24, 25]. Therefore, the present study prioritizes ABCC1 as a key factor in fatty acid metabolism–related tumor characteristics.

ABCC1 promotes the proliferation and metastasis of NPC in vitro and correlates with poor prognosis

To confirm the significant role of ABCC1 in NPC, we first examined its expression levels in four NPC cell lines and the normal nasopharyngeal epithelial NP69 cell line (Fig. 5A and B), and selected TW03 and 5–8 F cell lines with higher expression levels for subsequent studies. Consistently, through immunohistochemical experiments, we also found that the expression level of ABCC1 in NPC tumor tissues was significantly upregulated compared with adjacent tissues (Fig. 5C). More importantly, the expression of ABCC1 in NPC was strongly associated to the clinical stage of the tumor, recurrence, and lymph node metastasis (Supplementary Table S3), and patients with high ABCC1 expression showed worse prognosis than those with low expression ( Fig. 5D).

To further explore the biological function of ABCC1 in NPC, ABCC1 gene knockdown was performed in vitro, and 5–8 F and TW03 cells were transfected respectively (Fig. 5E). We found that knockdown of ABCC1 led to a decline in cell proliferation ability (Fig. 5F), and the reduction in colony formation also confirmed this point (Fig. 5G). Furthermore, in both cell lines, knockdown of ABCC1 significantly inhibited the migration and invasion abilities of the cells (Fig. 5H). Collectively, these findings suggest that ABCC1 serves as a valuable prognostic marker and potential therapeutic target in NPC.

Downregulation of ABCC1 disrupts lipid metabolism and induces lipid peroxidation in NPC

To understand whether ABCC1 is involved in the metabolic process of NPC cells, we performed non-targeted metabolomics analysis on NPC cells with ABCC1 knockdown. The volcano plot and heat map showed that knockdown of ABCC1 did lead to significant changes in the levels of multiple metabolites (Fig. 6A, C). KEGG enrichment analysis of metabolic pathways based on differential metabolites showed that ABCC1 can regulate multiple metabolic pathways of NPC, with a predominant impact on lipid and carbohydrate metabolism (Fig. 6B). Given that lipid metabolic reprogramming in tumor cells is known to influence ferroptosis activation [26], we hypothesized that ABCC1 may be involved in lipid metabolic reprogramming of NPC and thus inhibit the activation of ferroptosis.

We first monitored ferroptosis and lipid metabolism reprogramming in NPC cells with ABCC1 knockdown. ABCC1 knockdown resulted in a significant decrease in the GSH/GSSG ratio and a marked increase in MDA and ROS levels (Fig. 6D-F). Consistently, nile staining intensity was weaker in ABCC1-knockdown NPC cells (Fig. 6G), indicating that ABCC1 deficiency reduces lipid droplet content in NPC cells.To further assess lipid peroxidation, a hallmark of ferroptosis, we performed C11-BODIPY staining with or without the ferroptosis inducer erastin. As shown in Fig. 6H, ABCC1 knockdown increased lipid ROS levels under both basal and erastin-stimulated conditions, consistent with enhanced ferroptosis susceptibility.

P300 mediates ABCC1 overexpression via H3K27ac modification

To investigate the possible molecular mechanisms that contribute to the overexpression of ABCC1 in NPC, we initially examined alterations in the ABCC1 gene promoter utilizing the UCSC online platform (http://genome.ucsc.edu/). Our analysis revealed a wealth of histone acetylation peaks (H3K27ac) within the ABCC1 promoter region, implying that chromatin acetylation may play a regulatory role in ABCC1 expression (Fig. 7A, B).

Following this, we conducted chromatin immunoprecipitation assays employing the H3K27ac antibody, which demonstrated an increased abundance of H3K27ac in the ABCC1 promoter region of both 5–8 F and TW03 cell lines (Fig. 7C). This finding indicates that the aberrant upregulation of ABCC1 in NPCs could be associated with heightened levels of H3K27ac in the ABCC1 gene promoter. Considering that P300 functions as a crucial enzyme involved in H3K27ac modulation, we treated 5–8 F and TW03 cells with the P300 inhibitor SGC-CBP30. We observed a marked decrease in ABCC1 mRNA expression following 48 h of exposure to 30 µM SGC-CBP30. Furthermore, we validated at the protein level that a 48-hour treatment with 20 µM SGC-CBP30 resulted in diminished expression of both histone H3K27ac and ABCC1 (Fig. 7F, G).

Furthermore, we identified P300 and STAT3 as shared upstream transcription factors on Knock TF (https://bio.liclab.net/KnockTFv1/), GTRD (http://gtrd20-06.biouml.org/), Chip Atlas (https://chip-atlas.org/), and ENCODE (https://www.encodeproject.org/) (Fig. 7 H). Given that P300 is a known H3K27 acetyltransferase and our previous experiments have demonstrated a significant effect of H3K27ac modification on ABCC1 expression, we selected P300 as the primary target for further mechanistic exploration. We first performed chromatin immunoprecipitation experiments, which revealed a significant decrease in P300 protein enrichment in the ABCC1 gene promoter compared to controls (Fig. 7I, J). These results provide preliminary evidence for the role of P300-mediated H3K27ac modification in NPC cells.

To further validate the role of P300 in the malignant characteristics of NPC by modulating ABCC1 expression, we concurrently suppressed P300 expression with siRNA in NPC cells that stably overexpress ABCC1 (Fig. 8A-B) and assessed the variations in proliferation and invasion across the groups. CCK8 and colony formation assays revealed that the overexpression of ABCC1 significantly promoted cell proliferation compared to the NPC group, while the suppression of P300 expression led to a decrease in cell proliferation (Fig. 8C-D, G). Likewise, transwell assays indicated that ABCC1 overexpression augmented the invasive capabilities of NPC cells, whereas P300 expression inhibition diminished their migratory and invasive potential (Fig. 8E, F). These findings imply that P300 may indirectly influence the proliferation and invasion of NPC cells via ABCC1.

Immunosuppressive cell–cell interactions mediated by ABCC1⁺ tumor cells

Given the observation that ABCC1 expression positively correlated with immunological features before (Fig. 4 and Supplementary Fig. 3E), we next sought to investigate why patients with elevated ABCC1 expression tend to develop an immunosuppressive TME. Based on the combined expression of multiple ABCC1+/− tumor cells and NPC patient microenvironmental subsets, we performed a high-resolution dissection of the interactions between ABCC1+/− tumor cells and various microenvironmental subsets in NPC patients. And we compared the number of interactions between different subsets between ABCC1 + and ABCC1 − tumor cells. The results showed that ABCC1 + tumor cells exhibited significantly more interactions than ABCC1 − tumor cells (Fig. 9A). This suggested that ABCC1 + tumor cells, compared with ABCC1 − tumor cells, have activated intercellular communication, particularly interactions with immune cells, which may contribute to the development of an immunosuppressive TME.

Cell–cell communication between ABCC1⁺/⁻ tumor cells and microenvironmental subpopulations. (A) Number of interactions between ABCC1⁺/⁻ tumor cells and microenvironmental subpopulations. The thickness of the connecting lines represents the number of interactions as estimated by the CellChat tool. (B) Bubble plot showing altered cell–cell communication mediated by individual signaling axes. The x-axis indicates the signaling cell type (sender/receiver), and the y-axis indicates receptor–ligand pairs of the signaling pathways. (C) Heatmap displaying the overall signaling flow (outgoing and incoming) of each cell population

With the CellChat tool, we further identified important ligand-receptor interactions between ABCC1 + tumor cells and immune subsets (Fig. 9B, C). The results showed that MDK-(ITGA6 + ITGB1) and CXCL12-CXCR4 (which have been implicated in the suppression of antitumor responses [27–31]) were communication pathways used by ABCC1 + tumor cells to interact with fibroblasts. Furthermore, ABCC1 + tumor cells specifically interact with the microenvironment through KIT and CCL chemokine receptor signaling pathways. The fact that ABCC1 + tumor cells more frequently interact with immune subsets suggested that NPC patients with significant ABCC1 + tumor cell counts may have a more complex TME and mediate the immune status of the TME.

These findings above suggested that ABCC1 may mediate immune evasion in NPCs. Therefore, we attempted to explore its potential impact on regulating CD8 + T cell function by co-culturing activated CD8 + T cells with NPC cells (Fig. 10A), which showed that compared with co-cultures with wild-type NPC cells, CD8 + T cells co-cultured with ABCC1-knockdown NPC cells exhibited increased secretion of immune effector cytokines (IFN-γ, IL-2, and TNF-α) (Fig. 10B-D). Furthermore, we examined the production of cytolytic granzyme B, and the results showed that CD8 + T cells co-cultured with wild-type NPC cells secreted significantly less granzyme B than those in the ABCC1-silenced transfected group (Fig. 10E). T cell cytotoxicity assays based on LDH release demonstrated that ABCC1 silencing increased LDH release in the co-culture supernatant, suggesting that ABCC1 protects tumor cells from effector CD8 + T cell cytotoxicity (Fig. 10F). In conclusion, these findings indicate that ABCC1 inhibits the cytotoxic activity of T cells against NPC cells.

To further investigate the relationship between ABCC1 and CD8 T cells in clinical samples, we performed mIHC staining on tissue microarrays from NPC patients (Fig. 10G). The mIHC results showed that patients with high ABCC1 expression had a higher proportion of PD-1⁺ cells among CD8⁺ T cells (Fig. 10H) and significantly elevated PDL1 expression (Fig. 10I) compared with those with low ABCC1 expression.All these results suggest that high ABCC1 expression in NPC leads to the formation of an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, impairing the efficacy of immunotherapy.

ABCC1-mediated activation of the MAPK signaling pathway in NPC

As no studies have yet clarified the molecular mechanisms underlying ABCC1 function in NPC, we performed transcriptomic sequencing of NPC cells with ABCC1 knockdown to identify its downstream regulatory pathways.And the differentially expressed genes were showed in Fig. 11A. We also performed KEGG analysis and noted that the differentially expressed genes were enriched in the MAPK pathway (Fig. 11B). To further elucidate the effects of ABCC1 on the MAPK signaling pathway, we evaluated Western blots to test the contribution of ABCC1 to the functional consequences of the MEK/ERK signaling pathway. Discussion

Immunotherapy represents a hopeful approach in the fight against cancer, as it has the potential to improve the precision and potency of immune responses aimed at combating cancer. The PD-1/PD-L1 pathway significantly contributes to the suppression of T cell function and facilitates the evasion of cancer cells from immune detection and destruction. Despite the abundance of lymphoid tissue in nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) tissues, particularly characterized by a high density of CD8 + T cell infiltration within tumor nests, the effectiveness of immunotherapy remains suboptimal due to the frequent presence of T cells in an exhausted condition [32, 33]. Recent studies have found that fatty acid metabolic reprogramming of tumor cells can profoundly reshape the immune microenvironment. Its dynamic metabolic disorder leads to excessive lipid accumulation, especially by inhibiting CD8 + T cell mitochondrial function and promoting exhausted phenotype differentiation, further weakening immune surveillance and affecting the effect of immunotherapy [34]. In NPC, a multicenter single-cell cohort study have shown that NPC cells can enhance the lipid metabolism and differentiation of regulatory T cells through CD70-CD27 interaction, thereby inhibiting CD8 + T cell activity and ultimately leading to immune escape [35]. The above results indicate that changes in lipid metabolism can affect the immunotherapy response of NPC and provide a new immunotherapy sensitization strategy for NPC. Moreover, there is currently no prognostic model for NPC fatty acid metabolism, and there is no clear method to link TME with fatty acid metabolism, and there is a lack of corresponding immune validation sets and experimental evidence.

To elucidate the specific role of fatty acid metabolism in NPC cells and its complex interactions with the immune microenvironment, we developed a prognostic risk scoring model based on multi-omics GEO data. Four fatty acid metabolism-related genes were then selected to construct a fatty acid metabolism-related signature. The prognostic value of the model for NPC patients was further evaluated using ROC curves and KM curves, with the median of all risk scores as the cutoff. Among these four genes, Kaplan–Meier survival analysis indicated that NPC patients with high expression levels of CD1D, CYP4B1, and DPEP2 exhibited significantly improved overall survival. In contrast, patients with high ABCC1 levels had shorter overall survival times. CD1D is a major histocompatibility complex (MHC) class I-related protein that presents lipid antigens to natural killer T (NKT) cells [36]. Studies have shown that CD1D-dependent CAR-T/NK cell therapy outperforms conventional CAR-T cell therapy [37]. CYP4B1 belongs to the cytochrome P450 enzyme superfamily and is primarily involved in the oxidative metabolism of endogenous and exogenous substances [38]. Due to its low expression in various malignant tumor cells and association with poor prognosis, it is considered a potential target for immunotherapy [39]. DPEP2 is a cell membrane-bound enzyme that catalyzes the conversion of leukotriene D4 to leukotriene E4 and plays an important role in macrophage polarization and tumor stemness [40]. ABCC1, a key member of the ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporter superfamily, relies on ATP hydrolysis for energy and specifically mediates the transmembrane transport of glutathione-containing complexes [24]. Previous research has largely focused on ABCC1’s role in mediating chemotherapeutic drug efflux while overlooking its important functions in immunometabolic regulation. Based on multi-omics results, we selected ABCC1 as a representative biomarker of the fatty acid metabolism signature.

In our research, the analysis of functional enrichment (including GO, KEGG, and GSEA) revealed notable disparities in pathway enrichment between high-risk and low-risk cohorts, particularly in areas such as lipid metabolism, regulation of the cell cycle, and immune responses. Utilizing the CIBERSORT algorithm through R software, we assessed the proportion of immune cells present in the tumor microenvironment (TME) for each patient and employed the TIDE algorithm to forecast the potential efficacy of immunotherapy in both groups. This indicated that the high-risk cohort exhibited decreased infiltration of CD8 T cells. Additionally, the high-risk cohort was associated with elevated Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) prediction scores—a computational framework used to infer potential immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) response from transcriptomic data—suggesting a diminished immunotherapy response. This surrogate analysis was employed due to the current lack of large-scale RNA-seq or omics datasets from ICI-treated NPC patient cohorts.Concurrently, we observed that the overall survival (OS) of patients categorized in the high-risk group was inferior compared to those in the low-risk group, as evidenced by data from the IMvigor210 immunotherapy cohort. Although these observations are derived from a non-NPC immunotherapy cohort, they imply that a high-risk score may be associated with poorer outcomes upon immune checkpoint blockade, which warrants further validation in NPC patients receiving immunotherapy. Prior research has demonstrated that increased lipid absorption and accumulation within the TME can steer the microenvironment towards an immunosuppressive state that facilitates tumor advancement [41]. Consequently, the unfavorable outcomes and immunotherapy resistance observed in high-risk patients might stem from the alteration of lipid metabolism in tumor cells, resulting in modifications to the tumor microenvironment (TME). This process can impair the functionality of CD8 T cells and establish a collaborative immune evasion network through the upregulation of immune checkpoint proteins like PD-L1. Nonetheless, further investigation is required to uncover the molecular mechanisms involved.

This research demonstrated that ABCC1 expression is elevated in NPC tissues and correlates with reduced survival rates in patients. Functional experiments indicated that the depletion of ABCC1 markedly inhibited both the proliferation and metastatic potential of NPC cells.In ABCC1-silenced NPC cells, a marked reduction in the glutathione/glutathione disulfide (GSH/GSSG) ratio was observed, accompanied by elevated levels of malondialdehyde (MDA) and reactive oxygen species (ROS). Moreover, diminished Nile Red staining intensity indicated reduced lipid accumulation. Collectively, these findings suggest that ABCC1 may function not only as a prognostic biomarker but also as a potential sensitizing target for NPC immunotherapy. Notably, ABCC1 expression was negatively correlated with CD8⁺ T-cell infiltration in NPC cohorts, a feature previously linked to enhanced anti-tumor immunity and improved immunotherapy responsiveness [42]. High-resolution analyses further revealed that ABCC1⁺ tumor cells exhibited frequent interactions with microenvironmental subpopulations, including those mediated by KIT and CCL chemokine receptor signaling pathways, thereby contributing to the establishment of an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) [43–46]. Given the critical role of CD8⁺ T cells as cytotoxic lymphocytes in preventing tumor progression, we established a co-culture system of CD8⁺ T cells and NPC cells. ABCC1 knockdown reduced NPC cell survival, while simultaneously enhancing CD8⁺ T-cell cytotoxicity and promoting the secretion of effector cytokines, including IFN-γ, IL-2, granzyme B, and TNF-α. These findings indicate that ABCC1 impairs the anti-tumor activity of CD8⁺ T cells in NPC. Furthermore, tissue microarray analysis of NPC patients revealed that high ABCC1 expression was associated with reduced CD8⁺ T-cell infiltration and elevated PD-L1 expression. Given the strong correlation of ABCC1 with PD-L1 expression and CD8⁺ T-cell abundance, we propose that ABCC1 may represent a promising candidate biomarker for predicting immunotherapy response in NPC, pending confirmation in dedicated ICI-treated NPC cohorts.

Finally, we explored the upstream regulatory mechanisms and downstream pathways of ABCC1. In our study, ABCC1 was epigenetically regulated through histone acetylation. The H3K27ac inhibitor SGC-CBP30 reduced H3K27ac and ABCC1 expression. ChIP assays demonstrated that P300 and H3K27ac could bind to the ABCC1 promoter. Key function restoration experiments further demonstrated that P300 intervention reversed the malignant phenotype of ABCC1. Overall evidence suggests that ABCC1 is epigenetically regulated by histone acetylation mediated by P300. Transcriptomic analysis of ABCC1 knockdown cells revealed for the first time that ABCC1 positively regulates the MAPK signaling pathway in NPC. Western blot analysis confirmed that ABCC1 silencing inhibited the phosphorylation of MEK1/2 and ERK1/2, indicating that ABCC1 transmits pro-cancer signals by activating the MEK/ERK cascade.Collectively, these findings outline a possible mechanistic framework in which ABCC1, as a fatty acid metabolism-related gene, sustains redox balance and lipid accumulation, thereby fueling MAPK signaling, which impairs CD8⁺ T-cell function, ultimately fostering an immunosuppressive niche in NPC.

Although many biomarkers are used to predict the prognosis of NPC, this study might be the first to propose a prognostic model based on four genes (CD1D, CYP4B1, DPEP2 and ABCC1) with characteristics related to fatty acid metabolism. Moreover, we analyzed the immune cell infiltration and treatment response between high-risk and low-risk patients, providing a basis for personalized treatment strategies for NPC patients. And we verified the tumor-promoting effect of ABCC1 by screening for characteristics related to fatty acid metabolism. Our findings clearly demonstrate that the knockdown of ABCC1 may have notable anti-tumor effects through its influence on the infiltration and functionality of CD8 + T cells. This suggests that manipulating ABCC1 could be a viable strategy for enhancing anti-tumor immune responses. While the implications of this study are promising for potential clinical applications, certain limitations must be acknowledged. First, the research primarily relies on analyses conducted using public databases, which inherently restricts the sample size and could affect the robustness of the findings. Additionally, the exact mechanisms by which varying characteristics of the immune microenvironment impact the responses to immunotherapy and targeted therapies remain inadequately understood and warrant further investigation. Finally, to strengthen the generalizability of the model proposed in this study, it is crucial that future research incorporates larger validation cohorts—including, importantly, cohorts of NPC patients treated with immune checkpoint inhibitors—alongside more extensive clinicopathological data. This will not only validate our findings but also provide a more comprehensive understanding of the interactions between immune microenvironments and treatment efficacy.

Conclusions

In this research, patients were categorized into subgroups of high and low risk according to four signature genes associated with fatty acid metabolism. The generated signature related to fatty acid metabolism showed significant predictive capacity for both overall survival and immune status. Additionally, we discovered that ABCC1 serves as a critical gene that enhances the malignant characteristics of nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) and influences immune responses by compromising the functionality of CD8⁺ T cells within the tumor microenvironment (TME). We also confirmed ABCC1 expression in tumor tissues as well as in adjacent non-tumorous tissues and utilized multiplex immunofluorescence to evaluate variations in CD8⁺ T-cell infiltration. In conclusion, our investigation not only presents a robust prognostic model for forecasting NPC progression and patient survival but also establishes a scientific basis for creating targeted and individualized therapeutic approaches, thus providing essential evidence to inform clinical decision-making for patients with NPC.

Combined ipsilateral peak systolic velocity and ASPECTS are associated with early neurological deterioration after endovascular thrombectomy: a prospective cohort study
Authors: Shixian Zhou, Weiping Chen, Min Yin, Jiahao Hu, Ziying He, Xianliang Lai, Hua Guo, Jie Kuang, Mei Liu, Huiting Yang, Zhijuan Cheng, Jianglong Tu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeThis study aimed to assess whether a predictive model combining the Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS) and post-endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) transcranial Doppler (TCD) parameters could improve the prediction of early neurological deterioration (END) in patients with large artery occlusion (LAO) stroke after successful recanalization.MethodsIn this prospective study, we enrolled 87 consecutive patients with anterior circulation LAO who achieved successful recanalization (modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction [mTICI] grade ≥2b), underwent post-EVT TCD monitoring within 48 h, and had baseline ASPECTS assessment. Bilateral middle cerebral artery (MCA) flow velocities were measured, and key hemodynamic indices were subsequently calculated. The prespecified primary outcome was END within 72 h; secondary outcomes included functional status at 90 days.ResultsAmong the 87 successfully recanalized patients, END occurred in 21.8% (n = 19) within 72 h after EVT. Compared with the non-END group, patients who developed END exhibited significantly higher ipsilateral MCA flow velocities, including peak systolic, end-diastolic, and mean flow velocities, as well as a significantly higher ipsilateral-to-contralateral mean flow velocity ratio. Multivariable analysis identified lower baseline ASPECTS (odds ratio [OR], 0.50, 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.31–0.80) and higher ipsilateral peak systolic velocity (iPSV) (OR, 2.15, 95% CI, 1.08–4.28) as independent predictors of END. Restricted cubic spline analysis suggested an approximately linear association between iPSV and END risk. A combined model incorporating iPSV and ASPECTS demonstrated superior discriminative performance in predicting END (area under the curve [AUC], 0.801; 95% CI, 0.700–0.902), outperforming either parameter alone.ConclusionA model integrating post-EVT hemodynamic (iPSV) and baseline imaging (ASPECTS) data significantly improves the prediction of END after successful EVT. This multimodal approach, which showed superior discriminative ability (AUC = 0.801), may facilitate early risk stratification and support tailored clinical management.Trial registrationThis prospective observational study was registered with the National Clinical Research Center of China on June 19, 2024 (Registration No. MR-36-24-042352). The study was registered during the enrollment period.

Introduction

Endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) is effective for acute ischemic stroke caused by large artery occlusion (LAO); however, significant challenges remain [1]. Approximately 50% of patients experience poor long-term outcomes [2–4], and early neurological deterioration (END), which occurs in about one-third of cases, is a major contributor [5–7]. As END has been identified as an independent predictor of poor prognosis [6, 8], its early prediction is clinically crucial.

Transcranial Doppler (TCD) enables real-time monitoring post-EVT hemodynamics. Increased blood flow velocity after recanalization is associated with complications like intracranial hemorrhage and worse outcomes [9, 10], and shows promise in predicting END [11]. However, previous studies have focused primarily on the predictive value of individual or combined TCD-derived parameters [12, 13], which may vary across studies and do not account for baseline brain injury extent.

The Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS) is a well-validated imaging tool for assessing early ischemic changes and predicting the risk of END [14, 15]. Given its established role, integrating hemodynamic and structural data represents a rational and clinically meaningful strategy. We therefore hypothesized that integrating preprocedural ASPECTS with postprocedural TCD parameters would yield a physiologically more comprehensive and potentially more informative model for predicting END. To test this, we conducted a prospective study to construct and assess a model for the early identification of high-risk patients after successful recanalization.

Methods

Patient selection

We prospectively enrolled patients with acute anterior circulation ischemic stroke due to LAO (involving terminal internal carotid artery [TICA], middle cerebral artery M1 segment [MCA-M1], or the M2 segment [MCA-M2]) who underwent EVT from January 2024 to August 2025. All patients included had pre-treatment functional independence (modified Rankin Scale [mRS] score ≤ 2) and received multimodal neuroimaging before and after treatment [16]. Successful recanalization was defined according to the modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction (mTICI) scale [17] as a grade of 2b–3 with residual stenosis of < 50% [18]. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) inadequate transtemporal acoustic window precluding reliable TCD monitoring (i.e., inability to obtain a reliable ipsilateral MCA flow signal through the transtemporal approach within the prespecified insonation depth range of 40–65 mm) [11]; (2) incomplete TCD assessment prior to END onset; (3) immediate post-EVT reocclusion indicated by a Thrombolysis in Brain Ischemia (TIBI) grade of 0–3 [19]. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants or, when patients were unable to provide consent because of acute neurological deficits such as impaired consciousness or aphasia, from their legal representatives. Clinical assessment

Baseline and procedural data were systematically collected from medical records, including demographic characteristics (age, sex), vascular risk factors, occlusion site, admission National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score [16], systolic and diastolic blood pressure, time from symptom onset to EVT, and relevant procedural characteristics. With respect to treatment, EVT consisted of mechanical thrombectomy (MT) using stent retrievers, aspiration catheters, or a combination of both, with or without adjunctive treatments such as intra-arterial thrombolysis, stenting, or angioplasty. All interventions were performed by certified neurointerventionalists, each with more than 5 years of experience and an annual procedural volume exceeding 50 cases, in accordance with institutional protocol [20]. For outcome assessment, successful reperfusion was defined as a mTICI grade of 2b–3 [21]. Accordingly, reperfusion outcomes were categorized as follows: no reperfusion (mTICI 0–1), partial reperfusion (mTICI 2a; reperfusion of < 50% of the affected territory), and successful reperfusion (mTICI 2b–3).

TCD ultrasound examination and ASPECT assessment

The TCD examinations were performed within 48 h after EVT using a standardized protocol (Digi-Lite system, RIMED Ltd.). For patients who subsequently developed END, only TCD examinations completed before END onset were included in the analysis. The ipsilateral MCA was insonated at a depth of 40–65 mm. Measured parameters included ipsilateral peak systolic velocity (iPSV), ipsilateral end-diastolic velocity (iEDV), and ipsilateral mean flow velocity (iMFV, calculated as (iPSV + 2 × iEDV)/3). The ipsilateral-to-contralateral MFV ratio (iMFV/cMFV), pulsatility index (iPI = [iPSV − iEDV]/iMFV), and resistance index (iRI = [iPSV − iEDV]/iPSV) were subsequently derived. Baseline ASPECTS was independently assessed by experienced radiologists and stroke neurologists on the admission non-contrast computed tomography (CT) scans, in accordance with established criteria [15, 22]. In cases of disagreement, the final ASPECT score was determined by consensus.

Diagnosis and definition of END

All patients underwent serial NIHSS assessments immediately after EVT and at 12, 24, 48, and 72 h thereafter. Each assessment was compared with the patient’s admission NIHSS score. In patients for whom neurological examination was feasible and who were not under sedation, END was defined as either an increase of ≥ 1 point in category 1a (level of consciousness) of the NIHSS or an increase of ≥ 4 points in the total NIHSS score within 72 h [10, 23]. Clinically, END includes symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH), vasogenic cerebral edema (VCE), and ischemia progression (IS) [24].

Prognosis assessment

All patients completed a 90-day telephone follow-up, during which functional outcomes were assessed using the mRS by an interviewer blinded to post-EVT hemodynamic data. Outcomes were classified as good functional outcome (mRS 0–2) or poor functional outcome (mRS 3–6), with a score of 6 indicating all-cause mortality [25]. To enhance methodological rigor and minimize potential assessment bias, this prospective study used consecutive patient enrollment and standardized data collection. In addition, TCD examination, clinical management, outcome adjudication, and baseline ASPECTS scoring were performed by independent assessors for each patient.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was the incidence of END within 72 h. Secondary outcomes included the mRS score at 90 days [16], categorized as good functional outcome (mRS 0–2) or poor functional outcome (mRS 3–6). Safety outcomes included all-cause mortality at 90 days [25].

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), while categorical variables are expressed as frequency (percentage). For comparisons between groups, continuous variables were analyzed using independent-samples t test or Mann-Whitney U test, and categorical variables were compared using Pearson’s chi-square test, continuity-corrected chi-square test, or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate, according to the data distribution and expected frequencies. Variables showing an association with the outcome at a significance level of p < 0.10 in univariable analysis, or considered clinically relevant a priori, were entered into multivariable logistic regression models to identify independent predictors.

To assess the robustness and contribution of TCD parameters, three sequential logistic regression models were constructed: Model 1 (unadjusted), Model 2 (adjusted for age and sex), and Model 3 (fully adjusted for age, sex, hypertension, Trial of Org 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment [TOAST] classification [26], ASPECTS, bridging thrombolysis, and surgical modality). All TCD parameters were standardized using Z-score transformation before inclusion. Results are reported as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Restricted cubic spline (RCS) regression was applied to explore potential nonlinear associations between iPSV or ASPECTS and risk of END.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was used to assess the predictive performance of TCD parameters (iPSV, iEDV, iMFV, iMFV/cMFV, iPI, and iRI). Discriminatory ability was quantified by the area under the curve (AUC) with 95% CIs, and the optimal cutoff values were determined by maximizing the Youden index. The combined model incorporating iPSV and ASPECTS was further evaluated using calibration curves, nomograms, and decision curve analysis (DCA). No formal a priori sample size calculation was performed, as this was an exploratory prospective cohort study. Internal validation of the calibration curve was performed using 1000 bootstrap resamples. All analyses were performed using R software (version 4.3.0). A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinical characteristics

A total of 152 patients with LAO who underwent EVT and had cranial CT and TCD assessments were initially screened. After exclusion of patients with inadequate temporal acoustic windows (n = 43), posterior circulation ischemia (n = 21), and TCD indicated reocclusion of the culprit vessel (thrombolysis in brain ischemia [TIBI] grade 0–3, n = 1), 87 patients were ultimately included in the analysis. Among these patients, 19 (21.8%) experienced END within 72 h after EVT, including 10 cases (52.6%) of VCE, 6 cases (31.6%) of sICH, and 3 cases (15.8%) of progressive IS. The remaining 68 patients (78.2%) were classified into the non-END group.

In the intergroup analysis, pre-EVT ASPECTS was significantly lower in the END group than in the non-END group (median, 4 [IQR, 4–5] vs. 6 [IQR, 5–7]; P < 0.001). Furthermore, the rate of poor functional outcome at 90 days (mRS 3–6) was significantly higher in the END group (78.9% vs. 25.0%; P < 0.001), and the 90-day all-cause mortality was also significantly higher in the END group (36.8% vs. 5.9%; P = 0.001) (Table 1). No significant differences were observed in other baseline variables, indicating good comparability between the two groups.

TCD parameters and END

To investigate the predictive value of TCD for END, we analyzed TCD-derived parameters measured within 48 h after EVT but before END onset in 87 patients who achieved successful recanalization (residual stenosis < 50%). The median interval from EVT to TCD examination was 24.0 h (IQR, 19.5–35.3). In all included patients who subsequently developed END, the analyzed TCD measurements had been obtained before END onset. The analysis indicated that iPSV (median, 125.4 cm/s [IQR: 102.7–178.85] vs. 98.4 cm/s [IQR: 69.88–122.78]; P = 0.006), iEDV (50.0 cm/s [IQR: 35.7–78.5] vs. 41.0 cm/s [IQR: 28.55–56.45]; P = 0.047), iMFV (79.67 cm/s [IQR: 59.46–111.18] vs. 58.7 cm/s [IQR: 41.96–80.22]; P = 0.022), and the iMFV/cMFV ratio (1.16 [IQR: 0.99–1.35] vs. 0.94 [IQR: 0.77–1.24]; P = 0.017) were significantly higher in the END group than in the non-END group. Other TCD parameters, including iRI and iPI, did not differ significantly between the two groups (Table 1).

Univariable analyses of baseline variables were then conducted. Variables with a P value < 0.1 in univariable analysis (including sex, bridging thrombolysis, ASPECTS, and surgical modality), established predictors of END (including hypertension and cardioembolism), and TCD-derived parameters were entered into a multivariable logistic regression model. The multivariable analysis identified ASPECTS (adjusted OR 0.50, 95% CI 0.31–0.80; adjusted P = 0.004) and iPSV (adjusted OR 2.15, 95% CI 1.08–4.28; adjusted P = 0.030) as independent predictors of END. To evaluate multicollinearity, the variance inflation factors (VIFs) were calculated for variables in the model. TCD-derived parameters with VIF values greater than 5 (iEDV, iMFV, iMFV/cMFV ratio, iRI, and iPI) were excluded from the final model. All remaining variables showed VIF values < 5 and tolerance values > 0.1, indicating the absence of significant multicollinearity (Table 2).

In the fully adjusted Model 3, each standard deviation increase in iPSV was associated with an approximately twofold increase in the odds of END (adjusted OR, 2.18; 95% CI, 1.10–4.32). The iMFV/cMFV ratio was also associated with END after full adjustment (adjusted OR, 2.46; 95% CI, 1.25–4.84), whereas this association was not evident in the less adjusted models. (Table 3). No other TCD parameters showed significant associations with END in the multivariable analysis. As a descriptive analysis, iPSV values were also examined according to END subtype. The median iPSV was 145.0 cm/s (IQR, 120.6–181.0) in VCE, 102.7 cm/s (IQR, 82.5–111.1) in sICH, and 220.5 cm/s (IQR, 192.9–244.3) in progressive IS. Given the small and uneven subgroup sizes, these findings are presented for descriptive purposes only.

Dose-response relationship

The dose-response relationships between post-EVT iPSV, pre-EVT ASPECTS, and the risk of END were examined using RCS regression. The number of knots was selected based on the Akaike information criterion (AIC), and three knots were placed at the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles. The analysis demonstrated a nearly linear increase in END risk with higher iPSV and a linear decrease with higher ASPECTS. No significant evidence of nonlinearity was observed for either variable (P for nonlinearity = 0.108 for iPSV and 0.431 for ASPECTS) (Fig. 2).

Comparison of the predictive value of iPSV and other TCD-related parameters for END

ROC curve analysis was performed to compare the predictive performance of iPSV against other TCD-derived parameters (iEDV, iMFV, iMFV/cMFV, iPI, iRI) for END, and to identify the optimal hemodynamic indicator (Table 4). For the prediction of END, iPSV demonstrated good discriminative ability, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.706 (95% CI, 0.579–0.833). At its optimal cutoff value of 119.95 cm/s, iPSV achieved a sensitivity of 63.2% and a specificity of 73.5%. The AUC for iPSV was numerically higher than those of all other TCD parameters assessed, including iEDV (AUC, 0.650; 95% CI, 0.510–0.790), iMFV (AUC, 0.673; 95% CI, 0.537–0.809), iMFV/cMFV ratio (AUC, 0.680; 95% CI, 0.559–0.800), iPI (AUC, 0.624; 95% CI, 0.480–0.768), and iRI (AUC, 0.624; 95% CI, 0.480–0.768).

Predictive performance of the combined iPSV and ASPECTS model

The predictive performance of the combined model (iPSV + ASPECTS) was evaluated for both primary and secondary outcomes (Table 4). For the primary outcome of END, ASPECTS alone yielded an AUC of 0.743 (95% CI, 0.614–0.871), with an accuracy of 0.713 (95% CI, 0.606–0.805), a sensitivity of 78.9%, and a specificity of 69.1%. The optimal cutoff value for the transformed variable ASPECTS* was 4.5, corresponding to an original ASPECTS threshold of ≤ 5. The combined model demonstrated improved discriminative performance compared with ASPECTS alone, achieving an AUC of 0.801 (95% CI, 0.700–0.902), with a sensitivity of 94.7% and a specificity of 54.4% at the optimal probability threshold of 0.131 determined by the maximum Youden index (Fig. 3A). Model calibration was satisfactory, as indicated by the bootstrap-corrected calibration curve (Fig. 4A) and a non-significant Hosmer-Lemeshow test (χ² = 10.976; P = 0.203). Decision curve analysis showed that the combined model provided positive net clinical benefit across an approximately 0.05 to 0.45 range of threshold probabilities and generally outperformed the treat-all strategy when the threshold probability exceeded about 0.10 (Fig. 4B), and a nomogram was constructed for individualized risk estimation (Fig. 4C).

For secondary outcomes, the combined model also showed discriminative ability for poor functional outcome (mRS 3–6) at 90 days (AUC, 0.724; 95% CI, 0.615–0.834), with a sensitivity of 90.6% and a specificity of 49.1% (Fig. 3B). Its performance in predicting 90-day all-cause mortality was more modest (AUC, 0.684; 95% CI, 0.512–0.856), with a sensitivity of 90.9% and a specificity of 43.4% (Fig. 3C).

Discussion

This study identified two independent factors associated with END after successful EVT: lower baseline ASPECTS and elevated post-procedural ipsilateral peak systolic velocity (iPSV). Building on this, we developed an integrated model that combined these predictors, which demonstrated a substantially improved ability to predict END (AUC, 0.801; 95% CI, 0.700–0.902) compared to the use of either parameter alone.

Our findings demonstrate that despite successful culprit vessel recanalization with residual stenosis below 50% after EVT, END occurred in 21.8% of patients within 72 h after the procedure, which is consistent with previous reports [11, 27]. Both post-EVT iPSV and pre-EVT ASPECTS were independent predictors of END within 72 h after EVT, and their combination improved overall discriminative performance for END prediction.

In this study, elevated iPSV measured within 48 h after EVT was associated with increased risk of END within 72 h (OR per SD: 2.15, 95% CI: 1.08–4.28), consistent with results reported by He et al. in a separate Chinese cohort [11]. Unlike that earlier study, which did not adjust for several established predictors (e.g., history of atrial fibrillation) [14], and may therefore have yielded biased estimates of key variables, the present analysis systematically incorporated widely validated covariates in multivariable modeling, allowing a more robust assessment of the independent predictive value of iPSV for END.

As a key indicator reflecting cerebral hemodynamics, variations in iPSV values have a clear pathophysiological basis [28, 29]. Previous studies indicate that, compared with other TCD-derived flow parameters, iPSV is more strongly influenced by the residual cross-sectional area after stenosis [11, 30, 31], consistent with the fundamental pathology of large vessel stenosis in patients with LAO. In the present study, iPSV was prioritized among the correlated TCD-derived parameters because it showed the most robust independent association with END, had the highest AUC among the individual TCD-derived metrics, and was also supported by prior literature as a clinically interpretable post-EVT hemodynamic marker. Moreover, abnormally elevated iPSV may reflect distal hypoperfusion beyond the stenotic segment and has been linked to key mechanisms of END, including postoperative blood-brain barrier disruption and reperfusion injury [32–34]. Given the mechanistic heterogeneity of END in our cohort, iPSV is more appropriately interpreted as a hemodynamic marker associated with overall END risk rather than a mechanism-specific indicator. In our descriptive subtype-specific observations, iPSV values appeared to differ across END subtypes, being numerically highest in progressive IS, intermediate in VCE, and lower in sICH. These patterns may suggest that relatively higher iPSV in progressive IS is more compatible with persistent distal resistance or residual ischemic stress, whereas elevated iPSV in VCE may be more consistent with reperfusion-related hemodynamic stress. However, these interpretations remain exploratory and should be viewed cautiously given the very small and uneven subgroup sizes.

Most prior studies on this topic were retrospective [27, 31], relying on clinical records for both hemodynamic data and END assessment, which may affect data quality. In contrast, the present study prospectively collected clinical and TCD data, reducing potential bias. Furthermore, we evaluated TCD parameters across three sequentially adjusted multivariable models and performed RCS analysis. iPSV remained significantly associated with END in all models, supporting its role as a potential marker of post-EVT cerebral hypoperfusion. RCS analysis based on Model 3 further revealed an approximately linear association between iPSV and END risk (P for nonlinearity = 0.108), extending the existing evidence in this field. In addition, given that only 8 patients (9.2%) in our cohort had MCA-M2 occlusion, this more distal subgroup was unlikely to have materially driven the overall iPSV findings, although some hemodynamic heterogeneity cannot be excluded.

In recent years, growing research has focused on the predictive value of TCD-derived parameters for cerebrovascular events, including outcomes after EVT. Existing studies largely follow two directions: analyzing dynamic changes in TCD parameters and developing novel hemodynamic indices. Nevertheless, research integrating TCD data with other clinical or imaging markers remains limited. The Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS), introduced in 2000, provides a rapid, standardized imaging assessment for AIS and has been widely validated for treatment selection and prognosis [35–37]. In this study, a lower ASPECTS was independently associated with an increased risk of END after EVT (OR: 0.50, 95% CI: 0.31–0.80), which is consistent with earlier reports [14].

To our knowledge, few studies have combined TCD parameters with ASPECTS to predict the risk of END after EVT. In our cohort, ASPECTS alone showed moderate discriminative performance, whereas incorporation of the dynamic hemodynamic marker iPSV improved the overall discriminative ability of the model, with the AUC increasing from 0.743 to 0.801. Compared with ASPECTS alone, the combined model showed substantially higher sensitivity and NPV, although its specificity and PPV were lower, underscoring the potential value of a multimodal approach for early identification of patients at risk of END.

Several factors may contribute to this improved performance of the combined model. By integrating pre-EVT anatomical information reflecting infarct extent with post-reperfusion hemodynamic data, the model offers a more comprehensive characterization of the underlying pathophysiological status. In addition, this integrative framework facilitates early identification of high-risk patients by capturing interactions among multiple complementary indicators, thereby enabling more timely risk stratification. Moreover, the incorporation of multidimensional clinical, imaging, and hemodynamic information helps mitigate biases inherent in single-parameter models, ultimately enhancing discriminative performance. In the decision curve analysis, net clinical benefit reflects the balance between identifying patients at risk and avoiding unnecessary escalation in lower-risk patients. In this context, the advantage of the combined model within an approximately 0.05 to 0.45 range of threshold probabilities suggests that it may help identify patients who warrant closer neurological observation and more intensive early post-EVT monitoring, while complementing rather than replacing overall clinical judgment.

Nevertheless, several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, TCD is operator-dependent, provides limited spatial resolution, and may introduce selection bias because 43 initially screened patients were excluded due to an inadequate transtemporal acoustic window. Second, we utilized a single TCD measurement, and although all assessments were obtained before END onset, the exact timing relative to the event was not further standardized or stratified; serial monitoring may therefore provide additional prognostic insights. Third, despite its prospective design, this was a single-center study with a relatively modest sample size and only 19 END events. Although the final combined prediction model included only two predictors, the limited number of events may still have affected model stability and increased the risk of overfitting. Therefore, these findings should be considered exploratory and require validation in larger, independent, multicenter cohorts before clinical adoption. Finally, we did not systematically incorporate advanced imaging assessments of collateral status, and CTA/MRA collateral scores were not available in a standardized and analyzable form in this cohort. As collateral status may influence both baseline ASPECTS and post-EVT hemodynamics, future studies incorporating formal collateral assessment may help further clarify the association between iPSV and END.

Conclusion

In conclusion, a model combining the pre-EVT ASPECTS score with the post-EVT TCD parameter iPSV significantly improves the prediction of early neurological deterioration following successful endovascular thrombectomy. This practical, multi-modal approach holds promise for enhancing early post-procedural risk stratification and guiding personalized monitoring strategies.

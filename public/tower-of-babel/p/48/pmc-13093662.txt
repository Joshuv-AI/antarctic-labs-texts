Difficult‐to‐Treat Rheumatoid Arthritis With Concomitant Yao Syndrome With Response to Dual‐Inhibitor Therapy
Authors: Nawar Aljundi, Paramarajan Piranavan
Published: 2026

Introduction

The patient, a 46‐year‐old White woman with long‐standing seropositive rheumatoid arthritis (RA), presented with difficult‐to‐treat RA (D2T RA). During evaluation for persistent arthritis and dermatitis, she was found to have overlapping Yao syndrome, a rare NOD2‐associated autoinflammatory disorder confirmed by genetic testing.

Case report

Joint symptoms began several years prior, predominantly affecting bilateral hands, clinically and serologically consistent with seropositive RA (Table 1). Previous treatments included oral glucocorticoids, intra‐articular steroids, hydroxychloroquine, and methotrexate monotherapy (oral and subcutaneous), none of which provided meaningful disease control. She later failed multiple tumor necrosis factor‐α (TNFα) inhibitors (etanercept, adalimumab, and certolizumab). Therapeutic ineffectiveness correlated with the development of antidrug antibodies to TNFα inhibitors. Subsequent treatment with interleukin‐6 (IL‐6) inhibition using tocilizumab and JAK blockade with tofacitinib was also unsuccessful.

Given persistent disease activity with recurrent facial dermatitis, constipation, and periodic fevers, she underwent assessment for systemic autoinflammatory disease and was diagnosed with NOD2‐associated autoinflammatory disease (Yao syndrome), typically characterized by arthralgia, episodic fever, dermatitis, and gastrointestinal involvement. This was genetically confirmed with increased risk allele heterozygous arginine replacement with tryptophan (R702W variant).

Differential diagnosis

This includes paraneoplastic process that could contribute to persistent arthritis, underlying chronic infection or septic arthritis, or secondary autoimmune or autoinflammatory process.

Management and outcome

She was initiated on IL‐1β inhibition with low‐dose canakinumab, followed by gradual titration to 450 mg every 28 days based on clinical response. On follow‐up, she reported substantial hand pain and swelling. Examination revealed bilateral ulnar deviation, synovial thickening at the metacarpophalangeal joints, and focal tenderness. There was no extra‐articular rheumatoid disease. Despite high‐dose canakinumab, articular disease activity persisted, predominantly in the hands.

She was transitioned to combination therapy with selective JAK inhibition using upadacitinib for RA while continuing canakinumab for Yao syndrome. At serial follow‐up, she reported marked improvement in joint pain and swelling, reflecting significant clinical response to dual‐targeted therapy (Figure 1).

Case discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first reported case of concomitant seropositive D2T RA with overlapping Yao syndrome demonstrating near‐remission response to dual therapy with JAK and IL‐1β inhibition. According to guidelines from EULAR, D2T RA is classified into two principal phenotypes: persistent inflammatory refractory RA (PIRRA) and noninflammatory refractory RA. 1 PIRRA is defined by objective evidence of active inflammation, either by ultrasound‐confirmed synovitis, elevated markers of inflammation, or both, alongside persistent clinical symptoms. 2 Given synovitis on examination and persistently elevated markers of inflammation, our patient meets criteria for the PIRRA phenotype, in this case driven by an underlying co‐existing autoinflammatory disorder.

Yao syndrome is a rare NOD2‐associated autoinflammatory disease, distinct from adaptive autoimmune diseases, characterized primarily by aberrant innate immune signaling. Although typically presenting with recurrent fever, dermatitis, and gastrointestinal involvement, it may include inflammatory arthritis that mimics or overlaps with RA, as well as sicca‐like symptoms, pleuritis, pericarditis, or a facial rash. Glucocorticoids remain the standard first‐line therapy, whereas IL‐1β– or IL‐6–targeted agents are reserved for refractory or severe disease. 3

Heterozygous NOD2 mutations can exacerbate RA by lowering the threshold for innate immune activation, leading to exaggerated NF‐κB/MAPK–driven cytokine responses (TNFα, IL‐6, and IL‐1β) to pathogen‐associated molecular patterns, thereby sustaining synovial inflammation and promoting disease flares. Although JAK1‐selective inhibitors effectively suppress IL‐6, IL‐2, IL‐4, and interferon signaling, IL‐1β signaling is largely JAK‐STAT independent; therefore, in patients with NOD2‐driven innate immune activation, persistent IL‐1β–mediated inflammation may contribute to refractory disease and may warrant consideration of adjunctive IL‐1β blockade.

Although combination targeted therapy has been reported in D2T RA, biologic–small‐molecule pairing, including IL‐1β inhibition with selective JAK blockade (eg, canakinumab with upadacitinib), carries an increased risk of serious infections, including fatal outcomes, underscoring the need for vigilant risk‐benefit assessment before immunosuppressive escalation. 4

In this case, canakinumab partially controlled Yao syndrome disease activity, whereas addition of selective JAK inhibition in combination therapy achieved sufficient control of D2T RA, supporting the hypothesis that dual inhibition simultaneously modulated innate autoinflammatory and adaptive autoimmunity. 4 , 5 The mechanistic complexity of targeting two disparate immune pathways is a promising scientific theme that highlights potential therapeutic synergy.

Conclusion

This case reinforces the importance of early PIRRA phenotype recognition using objective inflammatory assessment and advanced molecular and genetic characterization to support precision‐guided, risk‐justified, individualized therapy in D2T RA complicated by systemic innate immune dysregulation.

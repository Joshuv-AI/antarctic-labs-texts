Network-based analysis of oxidative signalling and yield stability in apricot (Prunus armeniaca L.) under postharvest deficit irrigation
Authors: Hüseyin Karlıdağ, Tuncay Kan, Şeyma Arıkan, Ahmet Eşi̇tken, Lütfi Pırlak, Muzaffer İpek
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Postharvest water management is a critical yet often overlooked factor in the reproductive sustainability of perennial fruit trees and can influence subsequent-season floral development and yield potential. This study was conducted within a three-year experimental framework under semi-arid orchard conditions in Malatya, Türkiye, in which postharvest irrigation-delay treatments imposed in 2020 and 2021 were evaluated through subsequent reproductive and yield responses recorded in 2021 and 2022. The treatments consisted of irrigation immediately after harvest and irrigation delayed by 10, 20, and 30 days after harvest. To provide an integrated evaluation of treatment responses, complementary multivariate approaches, including Principal Component Analysis (PCA), correlation-based association network analysis, multiple linear regression, and mixed-effects modelling, were used to examine the relationships among biochemical, physiological, reproductive, and yield-related traits. Correlation-based association network analysis identified H₂O₂, MDA, and ABA as central variables within the stress-response association structure. ABA was positioned as an important connecting variable between oxidative stress indicators, stomatal conductance, and reproductive traits, suggesting a coordinated association between biochemical stress responses and reductions in reproductive performance. Shorter irrigation delays, particularly 10 and 20 days, were associated with increased CAT and SOD activities, indicating partial antioxidant adjustment under moderate postharvest water deficit. In contrast, the 30-day irrigation delay was associated with stronger oxidative stress responses, reduced photosynthetic performance, lower flower-quality traits, reduced fruit set, and lower yield. Compared with immediate postharvest irrigation, the 30-day irrigation delay reduced mean yield by 52.7% in the 2021 response season and by 38.8% in the 2022 response season. The regression analysis identified oxidative stress indicators, antioxidant enzyme activities, stomatal conductance, and reproductive traits as yield-associated predictors rather than direct causal regulators. Overall, the findings indicate that yield stability under postharvest irrigation delay is associated with the combined behaviour of oxidative, physiological, and reproductive traits rather than with a single isolated response variable. These results support the importance of timely postharvest irrigation for maintaining subsequent-season reproductive performance in apricots under field conditions.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12870-026-09467-w.

Introduction

Apricot (Prunus armeniaca L.) is recognized as one of the most significant stone fruit species globally, with Türkiye serving as the leading producer and Malatya as the primary center of production [1]. While irrigation management during the fruit development stage has been extensively documented [2, 3], the postharvest period is frequently neglected in orchard management. This neglect often arises from the misconception that trees enter a passive physiological state following fruit removal. In the present study, the postharvest stage refers to the period following fruit removal, particularly the first two months after harvest, during which floral bud development continues and the tree prepares for the next reproductive cycle.

However, in deciduous fruit trees such as apricots, the postharvest phase is a physiological cornerstone that the postharvest phase is a physiological cornerstone that dictates long-term reproductive success. This period coincides with critical phenological events, specifically floral bud induction and the accumulation of non-structural carbohydrate reserve essential for winter dormancy and subsequent spring bloom [4, 5]. In apricot and other deciduous stone fruit trees, abiotic stress during the postharvest period may alter phenological processes related to floral differentiation, flower bud growth, dormancy preparation, and subsequent spring reproductive performance [4, 6]. Water deficit during this period may affect primordia differentiation and has been associated with reductions in flower quality and fruit set in the following season [7]. Consequently, maintaining favourable tree water status after harvest is important for supporting tree health, reproductive development, and subsequent yield performance.

Abiotic stress factors, especially drought, trigger a series of physiological and biochemical changes in plants [8–12]. One of the most significant consequences of drought stress is the overproduction of reactive oxygen species (ROS), such as hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), which causes oxidative damage to cellular components [13–15]. This oxidative pressure leads to lipid peroxidation, manifested by increased malondialdehyde (MDA) levels, which eventually compromises membrane integrity [16, 17]. These damages are counteracted by an intricate antioxidant defense system and regulatory signalling pathways involving enzymatic and non-enzymatic components [18, 19].

Furthermore, plants respond to water deficit through complex hormonal signals and osmotic adjustments to maintain cellular homeostasis [20–23]. Abscisic acid (ABA) plays a central role in these responses by regulating stomatal conductance and the expression of stress-responsive genes [24, 25]. Previous studies on apricot and related stone fruit species have mainly focused on deficit-irrigation strategies during fruit development, tree water status, gas-exchange responses, yield components, and general agronomic performance [2–5, 7]. Other drought-stress studies have separately described oxidative stress responses, antioxidant enzyme activity, ABA-related responses, and photosynthetic limitations in plants [8–26]. However, these parameters have rarely been evaluated together under postharvest irrigation delay, particularly in relation to subsequent-season flower quality, fruit set, and yield response in apricot. Although individual responses to drought are extensively documented, these physiological and biochemical parameters are often treated as isolated variables in existing literature [26]. A clear knowledge gap exists regarding the systemic and integrative linkage between postharvest oxidative signalling and yield determinants. An understanding of how these variables interact as a coordinated response pattern under varying degrees of postharvest irrigation delay is deemed crucial for the development of precise irrigation schedules.

The 10-day delay was selected as the earliest postharvest threshold to test whether short-term water withholding during the initial recovery period could already affect oxidative, physiological, and reproductive responses in the ‘Hacıhaliloğlu’ cultivar. Considering these gaps, this study aimed to evaluate the physiological, oxidative, and reproductive responses of the ‘Hacıhaliloğlu’ apricot cultivar to different durations of postharvest irrigation delay under field conditions. Beyond conventional univariate interpretation, complementary multivariate approaches, including PCA, multiple regression, and correlation-based association network analysis, were used as exploratory tools to summarize covariance patterns, yield-associated predictors, and pairwise associations among oxidative stress indicators, antioxidant enzymes, leaf water-status traits, photosynthetic parameters, flower-quality traits, fruit set, and yield. The network analysis was used to visualize the association structure among measured variables rather than to infer direct regulatory, directional, or causal relationships. The working assumption was that increasing postharvest irrigation delay would be associated with stronger oxidative stress responses, reduced physiological performance, and lower subsequent-season reproductive and yield-related outcomes. Accordingly, the study focuses on coordinated response patterns rather than on experimentally validated causal or regulatory mechanisms.

Materials and methods

Plant material

The Hacıhaliloğlu apricot cultivar is a traditional Malatya-origin variety widely used for dried apricot production in Türkiye. It is considered one of the most important export cultivars due to its high dry matter content, sweet flavor, and bright orange coloration after drying [27, 28]. The fruits are medium-sized (25–30 g), oval-shaped, and characterized by yellow–orange skin and firm, aromatic flesh suitable for drying. Harvest typically occurs in early July. Trees of this cultivar exhibit vigorous growth and high productivity under irrigated conditions; however, they show moderate sensitivity to drought stress and are susceptible to spring frost damage.

Experimental site and meteorological conditions

The study was conducted under farmer-managed conditions in a 10-year-old apricot orchard (Prunus armeniaca L. cv. Hacıhaliloğlu) located in Malatya Province, Türkiye. Meteorological data for the experimental seasons were obtained from the nearest official meteorological station to the experimental orchard. Monthly minimum temperature, maximum temperature, mean temperature, relative humidity, and total precipitation were recorded for both experimental years. Attention was given to the postharvest period extending from harvest to the onset of floral bud differentiation, because this interval corresponded to the imposed irrigation-delay treatments. Soil at the experimental site was characterized as a clay loam texture, a pH of 7.81, 2.68% organic matter, 0.06% salt, and 3.82% lime. The phosphorus (P₂O₅) and potassium (K₂O) contents were 3.38 kg da⁻¹ and 1058.18 kg da⁻¹, respectively.

The orchard was irrigated using a mini-sprinkler irrigation system. Throughout the experimental period, standard cultural practices including soil tillage, weed management, and pest and disease control were applied uniformly across all trees to ensure consistent growing conditions.

Experimental design and treatments

The experiment was established using a randomized complete block design (RCBD) with three replications, each consisting of three trees. Thus, nine trees were assigned to each treatment, resulting in a total of 36 trees included in the study.

Irrigation program and after-harvest water stress treatments

The postharvest irrigation-delay experiment was conducted under field and farmer-managed orchard conditions in a 10-year-old ‘Hacıhaliloğlu’ apricot orchard irrigated with a mini-sprinkler irrigation system. Before the experiment began, all trees were irrigated to bring the soil in the canopy projection area to field capacity (FC) five days prior to the estimated harvest date, after which the irrigation-delay treatments were applied.

Irrigation was initiated when the available soil water content in the 0–100 cm effective root zone reached the depletion threshold, as determined using a Time Domain Reflectometer (TDR) soil moisture device. Field capacity was determined through a TDR-based calibration procedure. For this purpose, each plot was irrigated to saturation and allowed to equilibrate; thereafter, multiple measurements were taken at several points to establish plot-specific FC values. During the experimental period, instantaneous soil moisture in each treatment plot was monitored with the TDR to ensure precise irrigation control. The TDR-based irrigation control was used as an experimental monitoring and standardization procedure rather than as a direct description of conventional farmer practice.

The irrigation amount required to restore the soil to FC was calculated by multiplying the measured soil moisture deficit by the effective root zone depth (mm), followed by adjustment for irrigation efficiency [29, 30]. During each irrigation event, water was applied until the soil moisture reached FC.

After fruit harvest, trees were assigned to four postharvest irrigation-delay treatments:

Three to four days after the completion of all irrigation treatments, the soil within the canopy projection of each apricot tree was tilled using a motorized cultivator. After each treatment period was completed, all trees were returned to the previous orchard irrigation regime, consisting of irrigation every 15–20 days with approximately 3000–3500 L of water per tree.

Measured parameters

Physiological, biochemical, and reproductive measurements were conducted following the procedures described in the subsequent sub-sections.

Biochemical analysis

Leaf sampling for biochemical analyses was performed using a standardized protocol. Biochemical analyses, including CAT, POD, SOD, lipid peroxidation, ABA, and H₂O₂, were conducted using fully developed and healthy leaf samples collected from trees representing each treatment. Samples were collected one day before the irrigation-delay treatments and seven days after treatment implementation. To reduce sampling-related variation, the same leaf developmental stage, sampling schedule, and sampling procedure were applied consistently across all treatments, years, and replications. Fresh leaf tissues were extracted under cold conditions, and the assays were performed according to the protocols described below.

Determination of H2O2 concentration

H2O2 content was determined according to Velikova et al. [15]. Leaf tissues (0.2 g) were homogenized in 0.1% TCA and centrifuged at 12,000 g for 15 min. The supernatant was mixed with phosphate buffer and KI, and absorbance was measured at 390 nm. Concentrations (µmol g⁻¹ FW) were calculated using an extinction coefficient of 0.28 µM⁻¹ cm⁻¹.

Determination of lipid peroxidation

Lipid peroxidation was quantified using the method of Heath and Packer [31]. Leaf samples (0.2 g) were homogenized in 0.1% TCA and reacted with 20% TCA containing 0.5% TBA. After heating at 95 °C for 30 min, absorbance at 532 and 600 nm was recorded, and MDA concentration (µmol g⁻¹ FW) was calculated using ε = 155 mM⁻¹ cm⁻¹.

Determination of catalase activity

CAT activity was measured following Havir and McHale [32]. The decrease in absorbance at 240 nm was monitored during H2O2 decomposition. Enzyme activity was expressed as EU g⁻¹ FW, where one unit corresponds to the decomposition of 1 µmol H2O2 min⁻¹ at 25 °C.

Determination of peroxidase activity

POD activity was determined according to the method described in [33]. The reaction mixture consisted of phosphate buffer, guaiacol, and H2O2, and the increase in absorbance at 470 nm was recorded. One enzyme unit (EU) was defined as a 0.01 absorbance increase per minute at 25 °C [34].

Determination of superoxide dismutase activity

SOD activity was determined based on inhibition of NBT photoreduction following Mutlu et al. [35]. Absorbance was recorded at 560 nm, and one unit of SOD was defined as the amount of enzyme required to inhibit NBT reduction by 50% [36, 37].

Determination of abscisic acid content

ABA content was quantified using HPLC with a reverse-phase C18 column following Zhou and Leul [38]. Leaf tissue (0.2 g) was frozen in liquid nitrogen, ground, and extracted with 80% methanol containing 1 mM BHT. Extracts were stored overnight at 4 °C in darkness and centrifuged at 12,000 g. The supernatant was evaporated at 35 °C under vacuum and re-dissolved in 50% methanol, then filtered through a 0.22-µm PTFE syringe filter.

HPLC analysis was performed using a C18 column (250 × 4.6 mm, 5 μm) under isocratic elution with 60% methanol and 40% water (0.1% acetic acid) at a flow rate of 1.0 mL min⁻¹. The column temperature was maintained at 30 °C and ABA was detected at 265 nm. Quantification was performed using external standards and expressed as µg g⁻¹ FW.

Physiological measurements

Leaf chlorophyll content

Leaf chlorophyll content was measured using a SPAD-502 chlorophyll meter (Minolta SPAD-502, Osaka, Japan). Measurements were taken from three fully expanded leaves per tree.

Photosynthetic activity

Photosynthetic parameters were measured using a LI-6400XTR portable photosynthesis system (LI-COR Biosciences, Lincoln, NE, USA). Measurements were taken on five mature leaves per tree, one day before the treatments and seven days after the irrigation applications.

Stomatal conductance

Stomatal conductance was measured in situ on attached mature leaves using a Decagon Leaf Porometer (Meter Group Inc., Pullman, WA, USA) between 12:00 and 14:00. Measurements were performed one day before irrigation and ten days after irrigation.

Measurement of leaf water potential

Leaf water potential was determined following Scholander et al. [39] using a portable pressure chamber (SKPM 1405 Analog Leaf Water Potential Apparatus, Skye Instruments Ltd., Llandrindod Wells, UK). Measurements were taken between 12:00 and 14:00, one day before irrigation and seven days after treatments.

Determination of ion leakage ratio

Ion leakage in leaf tissues was determined according to the modified method described in [40]. Absorbance was recorded at 280 nm before freezing (A₁) and after freezing (A₂), and ion leakage (%) was calculated as (A₁ / A₂) × 100.

Leaf dry weight

Fresh leaf samples (2–2.5 g) were dried at 105 °C for 8 h. Leaf dry matter (%) was calculated as (dry weight / fresh weight) × 100.

Flower biology and yield

Flower biology, fruit set, and yield measurements were interpreted according to the seasonal timing of floral bud formation in apricot. In apricot and other stone fruit species, floral bud induction and differentiation occur during the previous growing season, particularly during the mid- to late-summer postharvest period, and can therefore be affected by water availability and high-temperature conditions during that period. Accordingly, the postharvest irrigation-delay treatments imposed in 2020 were evaluated through flower biology, fruit set, and yield measurements recorded in the 2021 reproductive season. Similarly, the postharvest irrigation-delay treatments imposed in 2021 were evaluated through reproductive measurements recorded in the 2022 season. Thus, the 2021 and 2022 flower biology, fruit set, and yield data represent subsequent-season carry-over responses to the previous year’s postharvest irrigation-delay treatments.

Flower weight (g)

At full bloom, 100 flowers per tree were collected from different canopy orientations, and fresh flower weight was determined using a precision balance.

Pistil length (mm)

Pistils from the same sampled flowers were carefully dissected and measured using a digital caliper with 0.01 mm precision.

Pollen viability (%)

Pollen viability was evaluated using the IKI (iodine potassium iodide) staining method as described in [41]. For each slide, 100 pollen grains were counted across four microscopic fields, and viable pollen percentage was calculated.

Imperfect flower ratio (%)

A minimum of 100 flowers per tree were examined for pistil malformations. The imperfect flower ratio was calculated as the percentage of flowers showing abnormal pistil development.

Fruit set ratio (%)

Fifty emasculated and hand-pollinated flowers were tagged at full bloom for each treatment. Fruit set (%) was calculated as the proportion of tagged flowers that successfully developed into fruits.

Yield (kg tree⁻¹)

All fruits from each tree were harvested separately at maturity, and total yield per tree was recorded as kg tree-1.

Multivariate statistical analysis and predictive modelling

The experimental dataset was analysed using a multivariate statistical framework to evaluate associations among physiological, biochemical, reproductive, and yield-related responses to postharvest irrigation delay. Pearson correlation coefficients were calculated to determine the strength and direction of linear associations among measured variables. Pearson correlation and correlation-based network analysis were used as exploratory association tools; therefore, correlation-derived relationships were interpreted as statistical associations rather than evidence of causality, directionality, or regulatory control.

For reproductive and yield-related traits, the year of measurement was interpreted as the response season to the preceding year’s postharvest irrigation-delay treatment. Accordingly, the 2021 flower biology, fruit set, and yield data were considered the reproductive responses to the 2020 postharvest irrigation-delay treatments, whereas the 2022 reproductive data were considered the responses to the 2021 postharvest irrigation-delay treatments. In this framework, treatment year and reproductive response year were treated as consecutive seasonal units rather than independent calendar-year effects.

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was performed using JMP Statistical Software (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA) to summarize covariance patterns and multidimensional treatment separation across response years. Variables were standardized before multivariate analyses to improve comparability among traits measured on different scales.

For the multiple linear regression analysis, yield was used as the dependent response variable, while the physiological, biochemical, and reproductive traits listed in Table 3 were included as candidate predictors. The model was fitted using observation-level records rather than treatment means. Each irrigation-delay treatment was represented by 30 observation records, resulting in an effective sample size of n = 120 for model fitting. Stepwise variable selection was performed in JMP, and variables were retained in the final model when they made a significant conditional contribution to yield prediction. Standardized coefficients (β) were used to compare predictor contributions. Model diagnostics included Variance Inflation Factors (VIF < 5) to assess multicollinearity and residual-plot inspection to evaluate homoscedasticity and major deviations from model assumptions. Regression outputs were interpreted as yield-associated predictors rather than direct causal effects.

To account for response-year variation in reproductive and yield-related performance, linear mixed-effects modelling was performed. Postharvest irrigation-delay treatment was included as a fixed effect, while response year was included as a random effect. Least-squares means were estimated for irrigation-delay treatments, and pairwise treatment comparisons were performed using Tukey-adjusted tests where appropriate.

All graphical representations, including PCA biplots and correlation matrices, were generated using OriginPro 2024 (OriginLab Corporation, Northampton, MA, USA). To visualize the association structure among physiological, biochemical, reproductive, and yield-related variables, a correlation-based network analysis was constructed. Significant correlations (|r| > 0.60, P < 0.05) were used as edges connecting measured variables. No quantitative centrality metrics were calculated; therefore, variables were not interpreted as formal hub nodes. Variables with multiple strong and significant correlations were described only as strongly connected variables within the correlation-based association structure. The resulting association network was visualized using Mermaid diagramming syntax. Network-related interpretations were limited to statistical associations and were not considered evidence of direct regulatory, directional, or causal relationships.

Original treatment-level values and descriptive statistics for all measured biochemical, physiological, reproductive, and yield-related variables are provided in Supplementary Table S1, including mean, standard deviation, standard error, minimum, and maximum values.

Result and discussion

Correlation structure reflecting stress-response associations

The correlation matrix (Fig. 1) revealed a coordinated biochemical response in which hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) and malondialdehyde (MDA) displayed a strong positive association (r = 0.717). This relationship is consistent with the well-established association between H₂O₂ accumulation and lipid peroxidation, as reflected by increased MDA levels and reduced membrane stability [41, 42]. Notably, a remarkably high correlation was observed between leaf dry matter (LDM) and H2O2 (r = 0.936), indicating that leaf dry matter was one of the variables most strongly associated with H2O2 accumulation under prolonged water deficit. This strong association suggests that progressive tissue dehydration and oxidative stress developed in a coordinated manner during postharvest irrigation delay, although the correlation does not by itself establish a direct mechanistic relationship between the two variables. Consistent with this, CAT activity showed strong positive correlations with both H₂O₂ (r = 0.922) and MDA (r = 0.697), indicating that CAT activity varied in parallel with oxidative-stress indicators. The coupled rise of ROS and CAT has similarly been documented in strawberry and other fruit crops under drought, emphasizing the conserved role of CAT in detoxifying excess H2O2 [42, 43]. Unlike CAT, POD exhibited a negative correlation with H₂O₂ (r = − 0.311) but a positive association with ABA (r = 0.537). This pattern indicates that POD activity was more closely associated with ABA-related variation than with H₂O₂ variation in the present dataset. Given the known role of ABA in drought responses [44], the positive association between ABA and POD may reflect coordinated variation between hormonal and antioxidant responses under postharvest irrigation delay, without implying direct hormonal regulation of POD activity. The negative correlation between SOD and MDA (r = − 0.260), together with its positive association with CAT (r = 0.480), indicates that SOD and CAT activities were associated with the oxidative-stress response structure observed under postharvest irrigation delay [43].

Photosynthetic parameters were tightly linked to oxidative and water-status variables. SPAD showed pronounced negative correlations with H2O2 (r = − 0.766), MDA (r = − 0.738), and ABA (r = − 0.606), indicating that lower chlorophyll status was associated with higher oxidative and ABA-related stress indicators. A decline in chlorophyll content under drought has been widely reported in tomato and other fruit crops [45]. Positive correlations of SPAD with RLWC (r = 0.666) and stomatal conductance (r = 0.467) indicate that chlorophyll status was closely associated with leaf water status and stomatal conductance under postharvest irrigation delay. Photosynthetic efficiency also correlated positively with RLWC (r = 0.493) and stomatal conductance (r = 0.711), but only weakly negatively with H2O2 (r= − 0.104). The relatively weak correlation between H₂O₂ and photosynthetic efficiency (r = − 0.104), compared with the stronger negative correlations observed between H₂O₂ and SPAD, indicates that chlorophyll status was more closely associated with H₂O₂ variation than photosynthetic efficiency in the present dataset. This pattern suggests that postharvest irrigation delay was associated with coordinated changes in pigment status, water-status traits, and gas-exchange-related variables; however, direct conclusions regarding PSII damage, electron transport impairment, or photosynthetic apparatus protection cannot be drawn from the measured variables alone.

Yield-related variables also showed clear associations with oxidative and physiological traits. Both fruit set and yield were strongly negatively correlated with H2O2 (r = − 0.795 and − 0.862, respectively) and MDA (r = − 0.807 and − 0.722, respectively), indicating that higher oxidative stress indicators were associated with lower reproductive performance and yield. Such negative relationships between ROS and yield have been widely reported across fruit crops [46, 47]. The positive correlations of yield with pollen viability (r = 0.651), pistil length (r = 0.626), SOD activity (r = 0.783), and RLWC (r = 0.563) indicate that higher yield was associated with better floral traits, stronger antioxidant activity, and improved leaf water status under delayed irrigation. Similar findings have been reported in drought-stressed tomato where stronger antioxidant activation mitigated yield losses [45].

These integrated correlation patterns indicate that H₂O₂ and MDA were central oxidative stress-related variables negatively associated with flower-quality traits and reproductive performance during the postharvest period. Overall, the correlation structure indicates a coordinated association among oxidative stress indicators, physiological impairment, and reduced reproductive performance. H2O2, MDA, and ABA emerged as central stress-associated variables, while SOD and CAT were positively associated with antioxidant adjustment and physiological stability. The divergence in POD behaviour and the moderate association between photosynthetic efficiency and ROS indicators suggest that the observed response pattern was consistent with moderate postharvest water stress rather than severe oxidative damage. These findings highlight the importance of jointly evaluating water-status traits, antioxidant responses, and reproductive traits when interpreting stress tolerance in apricot, particularly under postharvest irrigation-delay scenarios.

Integrative multivariate assessment: PCA and correlation-based network structure

Principal Component Analysis provided a clear multivariate structure describing how biochemical, physiological, and reproductive traits jointly responded to postharvest irrigation delay in the Hacıhaliloğlu apricot cultivar. The first two principal components explained 77.04% of the total variation, indicating a highly structured and biologically coherent response pattern (Fig. 2). Such high explanatory power is common in perennial fruit species experiencing coordinated oxidative and hydraulic stress [45, 48].

PC1 (51.82% variance) was primarily associated with the separation of oxidative-stress-related variables from water-status, physiological, reproductive, and yield-related traits. Negative PC1 loadings of MDA (–0.306), ABA (–0.280), CAT (–0.256), and H2O2 (–0.255) indicate that these variables contributed to the oxidative-stress-related side of the PCA structure. These variables clustered together on the negative side of PC1 in the PCA biplot (Fig. 2), suggesting a coordinated association among lipid peroxidation, ABA accumulation, CAT activity, and H₂O₂ accumulation under postharvest irrigation delay. Similar PCA separation patterns have been reported in drought-stressed peach and apricot trees, where oxidative-stress-related variables contributed substantially to the main variance structure [43, 47].

In contrast, positive PC1 loadings of SPAD (0.256), RLWC (0.245), stomatal conductance (0.245), fruit set (0.256), and yield (0.239) indicate that trees with better leaf hydration, preserved chlorophyll content, and stable gas exchange clustered on the opposite side of the axis (Fig. 2). This separation indicates an association pattern characterized by better water status, higher chlorophyll-related values, and more favourable reproductive and yield-related traits. The positioning of reproductive traits, including fruit set, pistil length, and pollen viability, on the positive PC1 direction indicates that these traits were closely associated with physiological stability and leaf hydration during the postharvest period.

The PCA biplot clearly separated the two response years along PC1 (Fig. 2). The 2021 samples were mainly positioned on the positive side of PC1 and were closer to yield, fruit set, SPAD, RLWC, pistil length, and related physiological and reproductive traits. In contrast, the 2022 samples were mainly positioned on the negative side of PC1 and were closer to oxidative-stress- and tissue-dehydration-related variables, including MDA, H2O2, CAT, and LDM. This separation indicates that the two response years differed in their multivariate physiological and reproductive response patterns. Accordingly, the 2021 response pattern was more closely associated with relatively better water-status, chlorophyll-related, reproductive, and yield-related traits, whereas the 2022 response pattern was more closely associated with oxidative stress and leaf dry matter accumulation. Because PCA describes covariance-based grouping, this year separation should be interpreted as an association pattern supported by the measured variables and the meteorological context provided in Table 1, rather than as direct evidence of a causal year effect or water-deficit severity alone.

PC2 (25.22% variance) was mainly associated with variation in enzymatic antioxidant activity and ion leakage-related responses. Positive loadings of POD (0.398), SOD (0.394), leaf water potential (0.308), and ion leakage (0.301) indicate that these variables contributed to the same side of the second PCA axis. The positioning of POD and SOD vectors along PC2 suggests that antioxidant enzyme activity varied together with water-status and ion leakage-related traits in the present dataset. Therefore, PC2 was interpreted as a covariance pattern involving enzymatic antioxidant responses and membrane permeability-related variation, rather than as a direct membrane integrity axis, consistent with previous reports in apricot and peach under moderate drought [45, 48].

PC3 (7.58% variance), although smaller in contribution, was mainly associated with variation in photosynthetic efficiency and selected flower-quality traits. The negative loading of photosynthetic efficiency (–0.470) contrasted with the positive loadings of flower weight (0.328) and pistil length (0.231), indicating that these variables contributed in opposite directions to the third PCA axis. Because carbon partitioning, vegetative metabolism, and detailed electron transport parameters were not directly measured, this pattern should be interpreted as a covariance-based association rather than evidence of functional decoupling between carbon assimilation and reproductive growth.

Together, the PCA axes illustrate a coordinated physiological response pattern in which ROS accumulation, ABA signaling, antioxidant activity, and leaf water status were associated with reproductive performance under postharvest irrigation delay. The negative PC1 clustering of oxidative markers and the positive clustering of water-status and reproductive traits indicate that biochemical and hydraulic stress-related responses were closely associated. Moreover, the separation between years in Fig. 2 suggests that the ‘Hacıhaliloğlu’ cultivar showed measurable physiological and reproductive variation in response to postharvest irrigation delay, with antioxidant capacity and leaf hydration contributing to the observed productivity-related patterns.

While PCA provided a comprehensive grouping of variables based on their variance structure, it does not establish causal or regulatory relationships between individual parameters. Therefore, a correlation-based network analysis was conducted to further describe the pairwise association structure among measured variables and to describe strongly connected variables within this correlation-based association network. This approach shifts the focus from broad variance patterns to specific pairwise associations, offering a more granular view of how oxidative stress indicators were associated with yield-related responses.

The correlation-based network analysis (Fig. 3) showed that H2O2 and MDA had multiple strong associations within the measured-variable network and were strongly and negatively associated with yield. ABA was also positioned as a connecting variable within the association network, showing associations with oxidative stress indicators, stomatal conductance, and fruit set. Specifically, ABA showed a strong positive association with MDA and negative associations with stomatal conductance and fruit set. These relationships indicate a coordinated stress-response pattern in which oxidative, hormonal, physiological, and reproductive variables varied together under postharvest irrigation delay. However, these network-derived relationships should be interpreted as statistical associations and not as direct evidence of causal, directional, or regulatory pathways.

Multiple Linear Regression Analysis for Yield Prediction

Before multiple regression analysis, reproductive and yield-related responses were evaluated using a linear mixed-effects model in which irrigation-delay treatment was included as a fixed effect and response year was included as a random effect. Low Variance Inflation Factors (VIF < 5) for all predictors indicated acceptable multicollinearity levels, supporting the interpretation of the regression coefficients within the multivariate model. The final model exhibited a high predictive capacity (R2 = 0.91, Adj. R2 = 0.89, F = 58.72, p < 0.001), indicating that the selected variables collectively explained over 90% of yield variation (Table 3). All predictors were standardized prior to analysis; therefore, the intercept approached zero, and β coefficients represent standardized effect magnitudes rather than raw unit changes. The robustness of the model was further confirmed by low Variance Inflation Factors (VIF < 5) for all predictors, ensuring that the identified coefficients represent unique contributions to yield variation without the confounding effects of multicollinearity.

Oxidative stress indicators showed negative coefficients in the regression model, with H2O2 (β = − 0.00204, p < 0.001), MDA (β = − 0.00425, p = 0.032), and ABA (β = − 0.12165, p < 0.001) being negatively associated with yield variation. Among these variables, ABA had the largest negative standardized coefficient. However, these coefficients should be interpreted as yield-associated statistical predictors rather than evidence of direct regulatory effects. These associations are consistent with previous reports showing that ROS accumulation and ABA-related stress responses are closely linked with reduced reproductive efficiency and productivity under water deficit conditions [46, 47]. In contrast, CAT (β = 5.65 × 10⁻⁴, p < 0.001) and SOD (β = 3.63 × 10⁻⁴, p < 0.001) showed positive coefficients, indicating that antioxidant enzyme activity was positively associated with yield variation under postharvest irrigation delay, in agreement with earlier studies reporting the protective role of antioxidant enzymes under drought stress [42, 43].

Stomatal conductance was one of the strongest positive yield-associated predictors (β = 6.54 × 10⁻⁴, p < 0.001), indicating that yield variation was closely associated with gas-exchange-related performance under delayed irrigation [6, 48].

SPAD (β = − 0.00163, p = 0.034) and photosynthetic efficiency (β = − 0.04078, p < 0.001) showed negative coefficients in the multiple regression model. Because these variables were analyzed together with oxidative stress indicators, water-status traits, stomatal conductance, and reproductive parameters, their coefficients were interpreted as conditional statistical effects within a multivariate model containing biologically interrelated predictors rather than as direct negative biological effects on yield. The same cautious interpretation also applies to RLWC (β = − 7.55 × 10⁻⁴, p = 0.043), consistent with the complexity of multivariate stress-response models [45].

Reproductive traits were decisive determinants of yield. Fruit set showed the highest positive effect (β = 0.01525, p < 0.001), followed by pistil length (β = 0.04602, p = 0.014) and flower weight (β = 0.424, p = 0.014), indicating that successful floral development and pollination capacity remain critical yield components even under moderate water limitation [4]. The positive coefficient for imperfect flower ratio (β = 0.00201, p < 0.001) should be interpreted within the context of the multivariate regression model. This result does not contradict the generally negative biological relevance of floral abnormalities; rather, it indicates that imperfect flower ratio retained a conditional statistical contribution after major reproductive predictors such as fruit set, pistil length, and flower weight were included in the same model. Therefore, this coefficient was interpreted as a model-dependent association reflecting shared variance among reproductive traits, rather than as an independent biological advantage of floral abnormality.

The transition from the 20-day to the 30-day irrigation delay represented the clearest physiological decline in the treatment response. While the 10- and 20-day delays were associated with partial antioxidant adjustment, the 30-day delay was linked with stronger H₂O₂ and MDA accumulation, reduced chlorophyll status and photosynthetic performance, lower stomatal conductance, and poorer flower-quality traits. This coordinated shift indicates that prolonged postharvest water deficit exceeded the short-term compensatory capacity of the cultivar and was reflected in reduced fruit set and yield.

Overall, the regression model indicated that oxidative stress indicators, antioxidant enzyme activities, stomatal conductance, and reproductive traits were jointly associated with yield variation under postharvest irrigation delay. These results support the view that yield response in the ‘Hacıhaliloğlu’ cultivar is linked to the combined behaviour of biochemical, physiological, and reproductive traits rather than to a single isolated variable. However, the regression outputs should be interpreted as statistical associations and yield-associated predictors, not as evidence of direct causal or regulatory mechanisms.

As an interpretive consideration, the correlation-based association network and regression analyses were used to describe coordinated response patterns and yield-associated predictors within the scope of the measured variables. Therefore, the observed relationships should be interpreted as statistical associations rather than direct evidence of causal or regulatory mechanisms. Future studies combining field-based physiological measurements with molecular or functional approaches may further clarify the underlying mechanisms of postharvest water-stress responses in apricot.

Conclusion

This study showed that postharvest irrigation delay in the ‘Hacıhaliloğlu’ apricot cultivar was associated with coordinated changes in oxidative stress indicators, antioxidant enzyme activity, leaf water status, photosynthetic performance, and reproductive traits. Association-network and multivariate analyses indicated that H2O2, MDA, ABA, CAT, SOD, stomatal conductance, fruit set, pistil length, and flower weight were among the variables most closely associated with yield within the measured response structure. Short irrigation delays of 10–20 days were associated with partial antioxidant adjustment and relatively better reproductive performance, whereas the 30-day delay was linked with stronger oxidative stress, poorer flower quality, reduced fruit set, and lower yield. Compared with immediate postharvest irrigation, the 30-day delay reduced mean yield by 52.7% in the 2021 response season and by 38.8% in the 2022 response season. These findings indicate that timely postharvest irrigation is important for maintaining subsequent-season reproductive performance and yield stability in semi-arid apricot production.

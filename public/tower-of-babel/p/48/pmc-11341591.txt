Application of artificial intelligence in rheumatic disease: a bibliometric analysis
Authors: Junkang Zhao, Linxin Li, Jie Li, Liyun Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The utilization of artificial intelligence (AI) in rheumatic diseases has enhanced the diagnostic accuracy of rheumatic diseases, enabled the prediction of patient outcomes, expanded treatment options, and facilitated the provision of individualized medical solutions. The research in this field has been progressively growing in recent years. Consequently, there is a need for bibliometric analysis to elucidate the current state of advancement and predominant research foci in AI applications within rheumatic diseases. Additionally, it is crucial to identify key contributors and their interrelations in this field. This study aimed to conduct a bibliometric analysis to investigate the current research hotspots and collaborative networks in the application of AI in rheumatic disease in recent years. A comprehensive search was conducted in Web of Science for articles on artificial intelligence in rheumatic diseases, published in SSCI and SCI-EXPANDED until January 1, 2024. Utilizing software tools like VOSviewers and CiteSpace, we analyzed various parameters including publication year, journal, country, institution, and authorship. This analysis extended to examining cited authors, generating reference and citation network graphs, and creating co-citation network and keyword maps. Additionally, research hotspots and trends in this domain were evaluated. As of January 1, 2024, a total of 3508 articles have been published on the application of artificial intelligence (AI) in rheumatic disease, exhibiting a steady rise in both the annual publication frequency and rate. “Scientific Reports” emerged as the leading journal in terms of relevant publications. The United States stood out as the predominant country in terms of the volume of published papers, with the University of California, San Francisco (UCSF) being the most prolific and frequently cited institution. Among authors, Young Ho Lee and Valentina Pedoia were noted for their significant contributions, with Pedoia achieving the highest average citation count per publication. Machine learning emerged as a prominent and central keyword. The trend indicates a growing interest in AI research within rheumatologic diseases, with its role expected to become increasingly pivotal in the field. This study presents a comprehensive summary of research trends and developments in the application of artificial intelligence (AI) in rheumatic diseases. It offers insights into potential collaborations and prospects for future research, clarifying the research frontiers and emerging directions in recent years. The findings of this study serve as a valuable reference for scholars studying rheumatology and immunology.

Introduction

According to the World Health Organization, Noncommunicable diseases (NCDs) kill 41 million people each year, equivalent to 74% of all deaths globally. Each year, 17 million people die from a NCD before age 70; 86% of these premature deaths occur in low- and middle-income countries [1]. Rheumatic immune diseases continue to be prevalent among this population. Rheumatic diseases, characterized by their complexity and heterogeneity in aspects ranging from pathogenesis to clinical manifestations and outcomes, have become increasingly significant in the realm of public health [2–4]. As societal awareness and concern for personal health grow, the importance of rheumatic immune diseases as a major health challenge has been underscored. Consequently, the pursuit of stable, reliable, and effective auxiliary diagnostic methods for these diseases is of paramount importance [5]. Although currently incurable, rheumatic immune diseases can be effectively managed and controlled, substantially improving patient’s symptoms and quality of life [2, 6, 7]. These conditions are typically chronic, necessitating prolonged treatment and care. Artificial Intelligence (AI), an interdisciplinary field encompassing various technologies like expert systems, machine learning, robotics, decision support systems, and pattern recognition, is evolving rapidly [4, 5, 8]. AI-based medical technologies are quickly transforming into practical tools for clinical application. The application of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in rheumatology enhances diagnostic precision, forecasts patient outcomes, refines treatment options, and facilitates personalized healthcare solutions [9, 10]. AI enables researchers and clinicians to more comprehensively understand disease complexities, thereby catalyzing novel research and therapeutic methodologies. Additionally, AI generates innovative strategies for diagnosing and treating such diseases [11, 12]. Concurrently, as AI becomes increasingly intertwined with healthcare, the systematic analysis and review of extant research grow more imperative. It is essential to recognize that, despite AI’s potential in managing rheumatic diseases, clinical validation and assimilation into routine medical practice remain critical steps [13, 14]. The expertise and judgment of medical professionals are indispensable, with AI augmenting their capabilities by providing precise, tailored data and support. Furthermore, addressing data privacy and ethical concerns is crucial to safeguard patient privacy and rights. Bibliometric analysis employs citation counts as a surrogate measure of research impact, serving as a valuable tool to statistically and qualitatively assess research trends [15]. It offers an objective evaluation of academic groups and individual researchers by comprehensively analyzing authorship, affiliations, journals, citations, and publication timelines of selected literature. This method facilitates the understanding of evolving trends within specific fields and enables the ranking of academic entities based on their scholarly contributions. Furthermore, it includes an examination of frequently occurring keywords and emerging themes, thereby providing insights into future research directions [16].This study aimed to conduct a bibliometric analysis to investigate the current research hotspots and collaborative networks in the application of AI in rheumatic disease in recent years, in order to provide a theoretical basis for researchers to better grasp the research frontier.

Analysis tool

Bibliometrics emerged in the early twentieth century and by 1969, as noted by Pritchard [1969], it evolved into an independent discipline, becoming widely utilized for literature analysis [Diem & Woltler, 2013] [17]. This method offers a quantitative approach to assess and explore existing literature within a specific field [Mayr & Scharmhorst, 2014] [15]. The analysis yields comprehensive insights, encompassing details such as authors, keywords, journals, countries, institutions, and references. Consequently, bibliometric analysis facilitates performance evaluation across diverse fields [16]. Leveraging computer technology enhances the analytical process, allowing for the generation of graphical and visual representations that complement the bibliometric analysis. Co-citation is a common technique employed in bibliometric analysis, defining two articles as related if they are concurrently cited by one or more other articles. Ma.F and M.xi (1992) underscored the significance of the visual approach to co-citation analysis in bibliometrics, asserting its role in enhancing data interpretation and comprehensive result presentation. Beyond the article itself, this method is applicable to various elements within the paper, encompassing authors, keywords, institutions, countries, and more. Visualization proves instrumental in revealing inherent connections among this information, such as instances where different authors delve into the same research topic or distinct institutions share similar research focuses. For knowledge mapping using CiteSpace and VOSviewer, recognizing that these two software tools, each with its unique strengths, can complement each other effectively.

Application to data

The analysis function in the Web of Science database was used to summarize external characteristics, including the publication number and the average number of citations. Literature records and cited reference data were downloaded in text form. Keywords with similar meanings but different spellings or expressions were merged (e.g., “rheumatoid arthritis” and “RA”). Furthermore, general and nonsensical keywords were removed (e.g., “men,” “child”). Once cleaned, the data were imported into the aforementioned software separately for internal structure analysis. VOSviewer, known for its probabilistic-based data standardization method, offers diverse visualization views spanning keywords, co-institutions, co-authors, and more. This includes network views, overlapping views (Overlay Network Visualization), and density visualization, known for its user-friendly mapping and aesthetically pleasing images, as highlighted by Eck and Waltman [18].CiteSpace 6.2.R2, a scientometric tool developed by Chaomei Chen, was used to report burst keywords in this study [19].

Discussion

Our investigation employs a bibliometric methodology to scrutinize the role of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in research on rheumatic diseases. We delve into various aspects, including publication output, the trajectory of research interest, preferred journals, leading countries, international collaborations, top institutions, author keywords, and the occurrence of word bursts.

Growth in publication output and research interest

The corpus of publications addressing the application of AI in rheumatic diseases has exhibited a consistent upward trend over the past three decades since the inception of the earliest publication in 1989. Notably, the period from 2014 to 2018 witnessed a pivotal surge in AI technologies, encompassing autonomous robotics, speech recognition, neural networks, and machine learning (ML). This technological upswing has presented unprecedented opportunities for the anticipation, diagnosis, and management of diseases. The confluence of advances in AI technologies and the availability of extensive databases has been a catalyst for a substantial increase in annual publications, a trend that has been particularly pronounced since 2018. Projections based on current publishing trends indicate a continued upward trajectory in annual publications.

Leading countries, international cooperation organizations, and institutions

The United States stands out with the highest number of publications, a phenomenon possibly linked to robust national support. Notably, on February 11, 2019, the U.S. issued an executive order titled “Maintaining U.S. Leadership in Artificial Intelligence,” mandating all federal government agencies to execute strategic goals aimed at expediting research and development in AI. Regarding publication volume, the top five countries collectively contribute over 77% of all publications, underscoring an uneven development of research in this field among nations. These disparities may be attributed to divergent histories, cultures, policies, and values guiding AI development in each country. Strikingly, seven out of the top 10 countries in terms of literature output are also among the top 10 global GDP nations, implying a correlation between their economic prowess and publication productivity. This correlation aligns with findings from bibliometric studies in various medical disciplines. This observation suggests that the economic prowess of these nations significantly shapes the productivity of their scholarly publications. The unique contextual factors of each country have been meticulously taken into account in the ongoing efforts to advance the application of Artificial Intelligence (AI). Nevertheless, the question of augmenting the acceptance of AI remains an avenue for prospective research.

Eight out of the top 15 publishing organizations are affiliated with the top 100 best universities, underscoring the global prominence of AI research in rheumatic and immune diseases. This phenomenon highlights the considerable attention these issues are receiving across leading universities worldwide. Researchers may contemplate engaging in collaborative investigations with these institutions or exploring opportunities such as participating in visiting scholar programs or educational initiatives.

Author keywords and burst keywords

In the realm of AI research in rheumatic diseases, prominent areas, as identified by author keywords, encompass osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis of the knee, and psoriasis. The primary functions of AI in rheumatic diseases involve enhancing diagnostic accuracy, predicting patient outcomes, and facilitating individualized medicine. Predominant AI techniques applied in rheumatic diseases include machine learning, deep learning, and neural networks. Machine learning (ML), a pivotal AI subfield, is reshaping both basic research and clinical practice in medicine [10]. ML emerges as a promising tool, adept at processing intricate data to refine patient stratification, enable early diagnosis, ensure accurate treatment, and predict prognosis [22–25].Noteworthy scientific progress in 2019 further underlines the trajectory of machine learning [26–30].Machine learning intersects with rheumatology-specific keywords, such as psoriasis, rheumatoid arthritis, arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, and rheumatism. These research avenues hold promise in introducing innovative ideas and methodologies for the diagnosis and treatment of rheumatic diseases.

Recent focal points in health informatics encompass “natural language processing” and “electronic health records” (EHRs). These terms not only represent current interests but also foreshadow potential research trajectories. The surge in data from clinical practice and medical research, notably electronic health records (EHRs), has left rheumatologists grappling with intricacies beyond intuitive interpretation. Consequently, a pressing demand arises for analytical tools capable of processing extensive datasets characterized by high dimensionality and multiple variables.

Limitations

Inevitably, we need to acknowledge the limitations of this study. First, the study only searched the Web of Science database, which is the most influential multidisciplinary academic literature abstract index database worldwide. However, because we limited our search to just 1 database, we may have missed some important research results. Additionally, the field of AI is rapidly evolving, with new research on its application in rheumatic diseases published monthly. Thus, the results of this study can only reflect the current state of the art.

Conclusion

Our study offers an insightful overview of research trends in artificial intelligence concerning rheumatic diseases, drawing from an analysis of 2292 publications sourced from the Web of Science (WOS) database. The application of artificial intelligence in the realm of rheumatic diseases demonstrates not only significant research value but also promising prospects for practical application. The noteworthy upswing in publication numbers signifies an escalating global emphasis on investigating the application of artificial intelligence in rheumatic diseases.

Leading this research frontier are the United States and China, presenting valuable opportunities for collaboration. This is particularly pertinent for other nations, especially those with limited resources in artificial intelligence but a growing demand for healthcare support, predominantly seen in low- and middle-income countries. Despite the establishment of stable collaborations among some authors, there remains a need to fortify collaboration and communication with other researchers. Strengthening these connections is imperative to propel advancements in artificial intelligence research within the domain of rheumatic diseases. Analyzing clusters and the evolution of high-frequency keywords aids scholars in comprehending the focal points and trends of artificial intelligence in the application of rheumatic diseases. We should focus on machine learning, osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, deep learning. At present, the field is focused on machine learning use in rheumatic diseases. Using machine learning to improve clinical decision making and rheumatology research. This study provides researchers with the information necessary to understand the current state, collaborative networks, and main research hotspots in the field. At the same time, our results suggest a series of recommendations for future research.

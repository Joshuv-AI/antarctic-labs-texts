Predicted loss-of-function variants before Met584 in ARID1B in population cohorts likely reflect reduced penetrance and should be reported diagnostically
Authors: Pleuntje J. van der Sluijs, Gijs W. E. Santen
Published: 2025

We read the article by Beaumont et al. [1] with great interest. The authors leverage the extensive genetic datasets of the UK Biobank to investigate putative loss-of-function (pLoF) variants in haploinsufficient neurodevelopmental disorder genes. They show that pLoF variants in these genes are less likely to have uniform distribution than other genes. Specifically related to ARID1B, they conclude that variants occurring before Met584 on MANE select transcript NM_001374828.1 should not be routinely reported in a diagnostic setting because (1) pathogenic variants in ClinVar lie primarily outside the first quarter of the coding sequence and (2) the pLoF variants in UK Biobank are potentially rescued by the presence of an alternative translation start site at Met584. Although we highly appreciate the thorough analysis of this fascinating dataset, we do not agree with this particular conclusion.

Already in 2018 [2], we noticed an absence of pathogenic variants in the first part of exon 1, and hypothesized that this could be explained by an alternative start site that renders LoF variants in the first part of exon 1 neutral. Later [3], we did encounter pLoF variants in this region, but remained skeptical considering that the clinical phenotype in these cases was in general less severe, and a number of variants were inherited. However, with the increasing number of variants being identified in this region (between amino acid 260 and 284) in patients with ARID1B-compatible phenotypes, and the possibility to further confirm pathogenicity using a specific DNA methylation pattern (‘episignature’) [4], we questioned our original beliefs. In 2021, we retrospectively phenotyped the parents of the inherited cases and concluded that these were usually mildly affected [3]. Recently, we added to the variants we already published (summary in Table 1) a patient with the earliest pathogenic variant yet (c.312_322del p.(Glu105Glnfs*206) (NM_001374828.1)) inherited from his mother [5]. Thus, while we agree that caution should be taken when interpreting pLoF variants before Met584 of ARID1B, our data strongly suggests that the presence in the UK Biobank is likely explained by reduced penetrance. Therefore, we are of the opinion that such variants should be reported in a diagnostic setting and, in many cases, should be classified as pathogenic, especially once a positive episignature has been established.

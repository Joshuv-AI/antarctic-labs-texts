Progress in the study of product methodology development and competitiveness assessment model of voluntary carbon offsets
Authors: Jin Han, Shaofeng Zheng, Jing Yuan, Congguang Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study focuses on methodological advancements in voluntary carbon reduction market products and the optimization of competitiveness evaluation models. Through systematic literature review, it analyzes the development characteristics, application scenarios, and existing challenges of international mainstream standards (VCS, GS, CDM) and domestic CCER and carbon credit methodologies. Using Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) as the core framework, the research optimizes a competitiveness evaluation model covering environmental, social, and economic dimensions. A case study of eco-friendly home appliances is conducted for validation, followed by multi-directional comparisons with five existing models. Results indicate that international methodologies emphasize rigor and sustainability verification, while domestic approaches prioritize policy adaptability but face fragmentation challenges. The improved model demonstrates superior performance in environmental and social dimension evaluations compared to traditional models, offering enhanced comprehensiveness and specificity. This study provides scientific tools for methodological selection and competitiveness assessment of voluntary carbon reduction products, offering decision support for carbon market standardization and low-carbon economic transition.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13021-026-00436-2.Keywords: Voluntary carbon reduction, Methodology development, Competitiveness assessment, Sustainable development, Carbon market

Introduction

Background

Climate change and international carbon reduction actions

In recent years, global climate change has garnered increasing worldwide attention. The greenhouse effect and climate shifts pose severe threats to Earth’s ecosystems and profoundly impact human activities. The global climate system has undergone extensive and rapid transformations. From 2011 to 2020, the global average surface temperature rose by 1.1 °C (Change, [8]), with human activities being the primary driving force behind this change. The World Meteorological Organization (WMO) further reported that in 2024, the global average near-surface temperature reached 1.55 ± 0.13 °C above pre-industrial levels, marking the warmest year in 175 years of observational records [49]. The long-term average temperature increase has now stabilized within the 1.34–1.41 °C range, with both the rate and magnitude of climate warming consistently exceeding expectations (Change, [8]).

To address the challenges of global warming and climate change, the international community has engaged in global cooperation through a series of key initiatives and comprehensive policies. In 1992, the United Nations adopted the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC), establishing a core legal framework for global climate action. This framework has been continuously strengthened through landmark agreements such as the Kyoto Protocol and the Paris Agreement. Notably, the Paris Agreement specifically requires countries to submit and regularly update their Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) to clarify emission reduction and adaptation commitments [16]. Subsequently, the 2015 United Nations Sustainable Development Summit approved the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development, which includes a comprehensive action plan comprising 17 goals and 169 specific targets. The inclusion of climate action (SDG 13) as a pressing global agenda reflects the international community’s determination to advance sustainable development across economic, social, and environmental dimensions, providing a systematic framework for global climate action and the implementation of Sustainable Development Goals [44].

However, the advancement of global climate action has not been smooth sailing, with significant international disagreements and policy reversals. Taking the United States as an example, its domestic political forces have disputes over the scientific validity and urgency of addressing climate change, and have taken actions such as withdrawing from the Paris Agreement and weakening domestic climate policies due to considerations of short-term economic interests and the impact on traditional energy industries [18]. This contradiction essentially stems from a deep conflict between the transformation of economic development models and the need for climate governance. The vested interests of traditional high-carbon industries and the cost pressures of energy transition have led some countries to adopt a conservative attitude on climate issues [17]. Such contradictions caused by economic factors cannot be ignored in the current context of the intertwining of global economy and environment, and have also brought uncertainty to global collaborative governance.

Through these pivotal international collaboration frameworks and accords, nations worldwide are actively engaged in combating climate change and advancing the pursuit of sustainable development. The development and implementation of these measures and policies have steered countries, promoted cooperative resolutions to global climate challenges, and played a crucial role in shaping a more sustainable future.

Voluntary carbon emission reduction and carbon credits

Voluntary carbon emissions reductions and carbon credits represent a pivotal domain in tackling the challenges posed by climate change [51]. Throughout recent decades, numerous scholars and institutions have diligently conducted extensive research and put forth a variety of methodologies and models aimed at assessing and managing voluntary carbon emissions reductions and carbon credits.

The concept of voluntary carbon emissions reductions was originally introduced in the late 1980s by American Aiyis Power and the NGO CARE. They ventured into a pioneering carbon project in Guatemala, marking the inception of voluntary carbon reduction initiatives [40]. At the international level, researchers Warburg delved into the development and commercial prospects of voluntary carbon markets from the standpoint of international corporations [71]. Furthermore, an empirical study on carbon credit trading, led by Qi et al., indicated the positive impact of carbon credit trading on CO2 emission reduction in China [55]. On the Chinese front, Hu et al., in their article, assessed the effectiveness of voluntary carbon offset programs through a comprehensive evaluation of the Chinese case [24].

The evolution of voluntary carbon abatement and carbon credit markets has given rise to the establishment of carbon standards and certification bodies. For instance, the Verified Carbon Standard (VCS), an independent nonprofit organization founded in 2005, strives to ensure the transparency and credibility of voluntary carbon reduction initiatives [63].

In sum, the global voluntary carbon emissions reduction and carbon credit market have garnered widespread attention and widespread application. By establishing standards and certification mechanisms, implementing trading and regulatory frameworks, these markets offer a flexible and sustainable avenue for all stakeholders to address climate change and foster the development of a low-carbon economy.

China’s voluntary emission reduction market

Research conducted domestically on the voluntary carbon emissions reduction market has predominantly concentrated on the growth and methodological exploration of this market within China. Notably, certain scholars have employed market competitiveness models, including Porter’s Five Forces Model and SWOT Analysis, to evaluate the competitive landscape and potential strengths inherent in China’s voluntary carbon emissions reduction market [45]. Furthermore, a selection of studies has employed Stakeholder Theory to investigate the roles and impacts of stakeholders in the Chinese voluntary carbon emissions reduction market, along with strategies for constructing a benefit-sharing framework [73].

In the research on voluntary carbon emission reduction methodologies, scholars have focused on the formulation and application of China’s voluntary carbon emission reduction standards. For instance, the National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) issued the “Interim Measures for the Management of Voluntary Greenhouse Gas Emission Reduction Trading” in 2012 to initiate the construction of a market framework, and launched the National Voluntary Emission Reduction Trading Registration System in 2015 to promote market operations, aiming to standardize and develop China’s voluntary carbon emission reduction market [46, 78]. Additionally, various studies have explored market trading mechanisms. For example, the CCER market allows enterprises not included in the mandatory carbon market to purchase voluntary emission reduction credits for offsetting emissions through transaction methods such as agreement transfers. The pricing of these credits is influenced by supply and demand, project quality, and policy environments [25]. For instance, low-carbon technology subsidy policies may drive demand growth, while fluctuations in international carbon market prices can also be transmitted to domestic markets through trade links, forming a price characteristic that combines market-driven and policy-driven factors. Purpose and significance of the study

Purpose and significance of this study

The core objective of this study is to explore the development process and basic characteristics of various product methodologies in the voluntary carbon emission reduction market, and on this basis, construct a new competitiveness evaluation model to accurately assess the competitiveness of different products through comparative analysis. Specifically, this study first comprehensively reviews the development trajectory of existing methodologies in the global and Chinese markets, analyzes the technical characteristics and applicable conditions of mainstream international standards (such as VCS, Gold Standard, and CDM) and domestic methodologies (such as CCER and carbon inclusion) in different application scenarios, thus providing a systematic basis for understanding the basic logic and differences of various evaluation frameworks. Based on this, this study applies the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) to optimize the indicator system and weight allocation mechanism, proposes a more practical and logically clear evaluation tool, and provides clear and reliable reference information for market participants and policymakers by comparing the analytical methods and evaluation results of different models.

This study has multi-layered academic value and practical significance. Theoretically, it systematically organizes the development models and comparative frameworks of voluntary carbon emission reduction product methodologies, helping to fill the gaps in current research regarding cross-scenario comparative analysis of methodologies. From a methodological and tool perspective, the proposed evaluation model can establish unified assessment standards for different types of carbon emission reduction products, helping to improve the consistency and transparency of market evaluation. From an application perspective, the comparative analysis and model applicability boundary descriptions provided in this study will help market participants and policymakers better understand the effectiveness and limitations of different methodologies, providing decision support for formulating relevant policies and market strategies, and promoting the mature and standardized development of the voluntary carbon emission reduction market.

Research methodology

This study employs a comprehensive approach, involving an extensive literature review and model enhancement, to delve into the progress of development in the voluntary carbon emission reduction market product methodology and its competitive evaluation. The specific steps are detailed as follows:

Literature review:

Literature review:

This study employs a systematic literature review approach to comprehensively review the development of methodology for voluntary carbon emission reduction market products and the progress in competitiveness evaluation research [54]. Through systematic screening and comprehensive analysis of core Chinese and English literature, the common characteristics and key differences of different methodologies in terms of indicator construction, evaluation logic, and applicable scenarios are identified, forming an overall understanding of the existing research system [36].

Based on this, the main advantages and disadvantages of existing research in terms of product methodology coverage, evaluation dimension integration, and model comparability are summarized, clarifying the key gaps and controversial points in current research, providing a theoretical basis for subsequent evaluation model selection, indicator reconstruction, and method improvement.

(2)Model selection and enhancement:

Model selection and enhancement:

Based on the literature review results, the quantitative models commonly used in the competitiveness evaluation of existing voluntary carbon emission reduction products are systematically compared, focusing on evaluating the applicability of different methods in terms of multidimensional attribute characterization, weight allocation logic, and result interpretability. Considering that voluntary carbon emission reduction products simultaneously possess environmental, social, and economic attributes, the analytic hierarchy process (AHP) is selected as the core evaluation framework [34].

Based on the existing model, the evaluation index system was reconstructed and optimized, introducing index dimensions closely related to the methodological characteristics and market mechanisms of voluntary carbon reduction products, and reasonably setting the weight structure to enhance the scientific rigor, comparability, and explanatory power of the model under different product types and application scenarios.

(3)Data collection and analysis:

Data collection and analysis:

This study systematically collected key data on voluntary carbon reduction market products based on publicly available authoritative platforms and project technical information, covering core information such as product characteristics, emission reduction performance, and market performance, providing data support for model application and empirical analysis. Data from different sources was integrated and quantified through standardization and statistical analysis methods.

To improve the reliability and robustness of the analysis results, multiple verifications and consistency checks were implemented on key data during the research process to ensure the comparability of data in terms of time dimension and logical structure, laying a credible foundation for evaluating the model improvement effect and comparative analysis.

(4)Comparative analysis and conclusions:

Comparative analysis and conclusions:

Based on the improved evaluation model, a multi-dimensional comparative analysis was conducted on different voluntary carbon reduction products, systematically evaluating their competitive differences from environmental, social, and economic perspectives. Meanwhile, by comparing the improved model with existing mainstream evaluation models, the effectiveness of the improvements in indicator coverage completeness and weight setting rationality is examined.

Based on this, the evaluation results under different product types and application scenarios are comprehensively analyzed, summarizing the overall characteristics and development trends of the competitiveness of voluntary carbon emission reduction market products. Based on this, policy and methodological recommendations are proposed for improving the carbon market system and further optimizing the product evaluation model.

The aforementioned four categories of research methods will be sequentially implemented according to the logic of theoretical review, model construction, data support, and empirical verification. China’s voluntary carbon reduction CCER

China Certified Emission Reductions (CCER) serves as a certification mechanism introduced by China’s National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) to validate carbon reduction projects within the nation. Its primary objective is to foster the growth of China’s carbon market and encourage reductions in greenhouse gas emissions. Initially launched in 2012 as a vital component of China’s carbon market infrastructure, CCER was designed to monitor, manage, and facilitate the trade of carbon reduction assets. However, due to the early stages of the carbon market’s development and subsequent policy adjustments, CCER was temporarily suspended in 2017 [38]. The reform and subsequent relaunch of CCER, which commenced in late 2019, are aimed at augmenting its operational effectiveness and transparency. This involves making alterations to CCER’s regulatory and management systems to better support the development of the carbon market and the achievement of carbon emission reduction targets [56].

Building on its earlier developments, CCER has played a significant role in China’s carbon market and has progressively become one of the primary certification mechanisms within the country. The CCER certification process is rigorous, demanding that projects meet specific emission reduction standards and adhere to rules to ensure the legitimacy and sustainability of emission reductions [72]. Over the initial five-year period of CCER’s operation, leading up to its temporary suspension, a total of 2891 CCER projects were initiated, with 1047 completing project filing and 247 completing emission reduction filing. The total completed emission reduction filing of CCER reached 49.8 million tons [32]. Furthermore, during this phase, a total of 200 methodologies were approved by the relevant state authorities [75]. The relationship between additionality and the carbon market is discussed

The concept of additionality in voluntary carbon reduction methodologies has substantial implications for carbon markets. Relevant studies have identified the following impacts:

Firstly, achieving additionality enhances the competitiveness and sustainability of emission reduction projects in the carbon market. According to research by Battocletti et al., additionality plays a crucial role in the healthy development of carbon markets. Projects with additionality enjoy greater recognition and trust in the market, which attracts more investments and purchasing power [5].

Secondly, the realization of additionality increases the attractiveness of voluntary carbon offsets, thereby promoting the growth and development of carbon markets [71]. Recent studies have revealed that considering the additionality of emission reduction projects leads to better environmental benefits for enterprises. This increased attractiveness of voluntary carbon offsetting stimulates the expansion and advancement of carbon markets [37].

Furthermore, the achievement of additionality and the utilization of voluntary carbon offsets can enhance the Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) image of enterprises. As per research by Ruseva, by realizing additionality and utilizing voluntary carbon offsetting, enterprises can strengthen their social responsibility image, earning a higher corporate reputation and market competitiveness [60]. This, in turn, contributes to the sustainable development of the carbon market.

In summary, the concept of additionality in voluntary carbon reduction methodologies significantly impacts the carbon market. It enhances the competitiveness and sustainability of emission reduction projects, promotes the growth and development of carbon markets, and boosts the trust and willingness of market participants to engage. Additionally, it enhances the CSR image of enterprises, augmenting the credibility and attractiveness of carbon markets. These studies provide compelling evidence for a comprehensive understanding of the impact of methodological additionality on carbon markets. Comparison of application scenarios of voluntary carbon offsets methodology

Each carbon reduction standard and methodology possesses unique characteristics and a specific scope of application, which can yield different effects depending on the scenario. In the German carbon reduction projects, the Gold Standard methodology has demonstrated its strength, particularly in terms of socio-economic benefits and sustainability. For carbon reduction projects in the transport sector, the Clean Development Mechanism (CDM) methodology has achieved remarkable results. CDM focuses on promoting technological innovation and transport planning to reduce greenhouse gas emissions. It offers technical support and policy guidance to drive the practical implementation and effectiveness of carbon emission reduction in the transport sector [47].

In the agricultural sector, the Agriculture Carbon Registry (ACR) methodology excels in soil conservation and agricultural sustainability. ACR focuses on the impact of agricultural practices on soil health and the carbon cycle, encouraging the implementation and effective management of emission reduction measures in agriculture [15].

Overall, different voluntary carbon emission reduction standards and methodologies are applicable to specific industries and sectors, providing targeted and application-specific effects. Improvement and comparison of different assessment models

Model improvement and construction

The evaluation model system for assessing the competitiveness of voluntary carbon emission reduction products is a comprehensive process that involves considering numerous factors and ensuring the reliability, selection, and weighting of evaluation indicators, as well as the rationality of the evaluation model. This process must go through multiple iterations to guarantee the scientific and reliable nature of the evaluation results. Moreover, different evaluation models and methods may be suitable for different contexts and requirements, so the model should be chosen and adapted based on the specific circumstances.

The model adopts the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) as the core evaluation framework, which is selected based on its multi-criteria decision-making ability. AHP has a clear hierarchical structure of target layer, criterion layer, and indicator layer, which can effectively integrate heterogeneous dimensions such as environment, society, and economy. At the same time, AHP supports the combination of subjective weights and objective indicators, which can not only incorporate the experience of carbon market experts but also combine actual product data, adapting to the dual characteristics of technical attributes and market attributes of voluntary carbon reduction products [79]. The weights and comprehensive scores of each dimension and sub-indicator are calculated through AHP to evaluate the competitiveness of voluntary carbon reduction products. Analysis of competitiveness evaluation results of voluntary carbon offset products

Based on the constructed competitiveness evaluation framework for voluntary carbon offset products, this paper conducts comprehensive calculations and multi-dimensional analysis on typical product cases. The results show that the competitiveness of voluntary carbon offset products is determined by multiple attributes such as environment, society, and economy, rather than a simple reflection of a single emission reduction performance. The environmental dimension dominates the comprehensive score, reflecting that emission reduction volume, accounting rigor, and methodology stability remain the foundation of product competitiveness; at the same time, the social and economic dimensions play a key role in distinguishing the competitiveness levels of different products, confirming the theoretical rationality of incorporating multi-dimensional attributes into a unified evaluation framework.

Further analysis from the weight structure and indicator contribution shows that the improved AHP model, through the two-level allocation mechanism of “dimension total weight - sub-indicator dynamic weight,” effectively enhances the adaptability of weight setting to different product characteristics and application scenarios. Compared with traditional rigid weight setting, this method can more realistically reflect the changes in indicator importance in different situations, thereby enhancing the explanatory power and stability of the evaluation results. This finding empirically expands the application value of multi-criteria decision-making methods in the competitiveness evaluation of voluntary carbon offset products.

At the model comparison level, the lifecycle assessment model and the economic assessment model focus on environmental impact and cost-return analysis respectively, while the improved model proposed in this paper can comprehensively characterize the environmental benefits, social value, and economic potential of products under a unified framework, better fitting the theoretical positioning of voluntary carbon offset products as multi-attribute composite products. The evaluation results further indicate that products with higher competitiveness usually perform outstandingly in terms of methodology applicability, emission reduction accounting stability, and social co-benefits or market mechanism design. The above conclusions not only verify the effectiveness of the model but also provide empirically enlightening explanations for the optimization of voluntary carbon reduction product methodologies and market practices.

Conclusions and research prospects

This paper systematically reviews and analyzes the development progress of product methodologies in the voluntary carbon reduction market and their competitiveness evaluation models. Research shows that international mainstream voluntary carbon reduction standards and domestic CCER and carbon inclusive methodologies have formed differentiated development paths: international standards are more mature in project certification processes and cross-regional mutual recognition, while domestic methodologies focus more on policy adaptation and local practical exploration, but both generally face common problems such as methodology fragmentation, insufficient data reliability, and high monitoring and verification costs. Moreover, the significant adaptability differences of methodologies across industries constrain the promotion and comparative application of voluntary carbon reduction products in broader scenarios.

In terms of competitiveness evaluation, this paper constructs and improves an evaluation model with AHP as the core. By clarifying indicator level boundaries and introducing a two-level allocation logic of “dimension total weight - sub-indicator dynamic weight,” it enhances the scientificity and rationality of weight setting. The constructed indicator system systematically covers the full lifecycle impact of voluntary carbon reduction products from environmental, social, and economic dimensions, aligning with sustainable development goals. Case analysis and multi-model comparison results show that the improved model has significant advantages in comprehensively evaluating the environmental benefits, social value, and economic potential of products, and is particularly suitable for cross-scenario competitiveness comparison of different types of voluntary carbon reduction products.

As the voluntary carbon reduction market expands and participants diversify, related methodologies and evaluation models still need to further improve industry adaptability and regional applicability. Future research can introduce dynamic weights and data-driven methods under the multi-criteria evaluation framework, combined with the characteristics of carbon markets in different industries and regions, to alleviate the problem of methodology fragmentation; at the same time, by building cross-regional and cross-industry data sharing mechanisms and strengthening third-party verification, the data foundation can be consolidated, case types can be continuously expanded, and empirical testing can be carried out in combination with policy evolution, thereby providing more scientific and forward-looking decision support for voluntary carbon reduction market participants and policymakers.

Transient Loss of Esophageal Striated Muscle Contractility After Cricopharyngeal Botulinum Toxin Injection for Retrograde Cricopharyngeal Dysfunction
Authors: Anisa Shaker, Karla O’Dell, Edy Soffer
Published: 2026

A 41-year-old male with retrograde cricopharyngeal dysfunction or abelchia underwent 2 botulinum toxin (Botox) injections (40 U) into the cricopharyngeus muscle 1 month apart, resulting in improved ability to belch. He subsequently developed dysphagia prompting high-resolution esophageal manometry. Manometry demonstrated weak distal pharyngeal contractility, hypotensive upper esophageal sphincter basal pressures, and absent contractility confined to esophageal striated muscle across multiple swallows while smooth muscle peristalsis remained intact (Figures A and B). Episodes of prolonged lower esophageal sphincter relaxation with air venting were also observed. Given extensive striated muscular impairment, neurologic evaluation was performed but revealed no neuromuscular disease.

Repeat high-resolution esophageal manometry performed 5 months later demonstrated complete recovery of distal pharynx and esophageal striated muscle contractility and normal peristaltic sequences across multiple swallows (Figures C and D).

Botox injection into the lower esophageal sphincter can diffuse to the diaphragm (AJP-Gastrointest Liver Physiol 318: G77-G83, 2020). This case further illustrates the effect of cricopharyngeal Botox injection on neuromuscular function away from the target organ, resulting in transient but profound impairment of striated muscle activity in the esophagus and distal pharynx. Caution is warranted when considering this therapy, especially in patients with known or suspected neuromuscular disorders affecting striated muscle, such as myasthenia gravis.

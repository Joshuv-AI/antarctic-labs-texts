Authors: Bo Hsiao, Zeeshan Ali, Hamza Zafar, Bahaaeldin Abdalla, Kamal Shah, Thabet Abdeljawad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Hallucination risk and trustworthiness of a generative artificial intelligence system play an important and vital role in daily-life scenarios. The terms Hallucination risk and trustworthiness are very famous for the generative artificial intelligence techniques to confirm that the final results or outputs are safe, accurate, and reliable for users or customers, particularly in education, finance, and healthcare. The main purpose of this manuscript is to evaluate the best and worst decisions among the decisions of Gemini (Google DeepMind), Copilot (GitHub/Microsoft), LLaMA (Meta), Claude (Anthropic), and ChatGPT (OpenAI) based on the following criteria, such as data privacy, explainability, Hallucination risk, reliability, and bias control. Therefore, the system of interval-valued m-polar fuzzy-multi-attribute border approximation area comparison technique for algebraic information is developed. The invented model is very famous and reliable for coping with awkward and complex information. Therefore, for the valuation or the construction of the derived technique, this study aims to develop the novel technique of interval-valued m-polar fuzzy information and also investigate their operations based on algebraic norms for the construction of the averaging operator and geometric operator. Lastly, this study also works on the analysis of the numerical examples and the implementation of the comparative analysis among proposed and prevailing frameworks to derive the validity and effectiveness of the introduced approaches.

Hallucination risk and trustworthiness of a generative artificial intelligence system

Artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning techniques are playing a valuable and efficient role in coping with vague and uncertain information, but nowadays, the technique of Generative AI systems is a more powerful and useful technique that is widely used across multiple real-life applications; for instance, business analytics, healthcare, online decision support, and education. The generative AI systems learn from large data sets rather than direct reasoning and generate responses based on patterns learned. The information produced by these systems may sometimes be unsupported because of their nature and the condition of the data. The phenomenon or procedure is called hallucination, which directly affects the accuracy and reliability of generative AI systems. It also influences the trustworthiness of these systems. Trustworthiness not only relies on the correct information but also focuses on the accuracy, reliability, and transparency of the system. Trustworthy systems enhance the users’ trust, providing accurate information and protecting their privacy. A trustworthy person also operates continuously across multiple tasks. Fuzzy frameworks and their extensions

Decision-making frameworks are efficient for the assessment of the best and worst decisions among a collection of alternatives without criteria or with a single criterion. The decision-making assessment is one of the most reliable and efficient models for coping with uncertain and vague information in complex circumstances. The decision-making has become very challenging in modern management and engineering problems because of uncertain and incomplete information. The existing decision-making concepts are limited to representing human judgment, especially when the expert information is in subjective form rather than a numerical value. To handle this complexity, researchers invented modified mathematical tools for capturing vagueness and uncertainty. Among all, one of the most effective developments is fuzzy set (FS), which was introduced by Zadeh1 in 1965. The FS describes such situations where the judgment cannot be expressed in partial rather than yes or no terms. The FS assigns a partial membership degree to each element of the set between 0 and 1. It captures the specific degree of an option to which it fully satisfies a specific condition using the membership function.

The truth value in a fuzzy framework represents the numerical value against each term from the universal set, but in many real-world scenarios, the real value is not enough to predict accurate and efficient results compared to a value in the shape of an interval. The FS effectively models the situation where the expert information is expressed in a single precise value. But in most real-world decision-making situations, experts prefer to express their preference as a range rather than a fixed point, which is a very serious problem in decision-making theory. To capture such situations, Gorzalczany2 invented the notation of an interval-valued FS (IVFS) in 1987. In IVFS, the membership degree is represented by an interval instead of a single value, which provides a more reliable space for decision-makers to make a decision precisely. The IVFS provides a more reliable way to model human uncertainty in complex scenarios. The notation of IVFS is very effective for the expert to express complex and uncertain judgments. It gives an effective framework for problems where information is vague and uncertain.

The truth function represents just one value against each term from universal sets at one time, but in many genuine-life scenarios, the experts or decision-makers need more values to defend their thoughts and opinions in front of someone. Because the first value is dealt with like a presenter, and the other values are shown as support of the first value. The group of information has worth to defend their suggestions and theme. Therefore, Chen et al.3 invented the notation of multi-polar fuzzy set (m-PFS) to capture more nuanced uncertainty in decision making. The m-PFS is the generalization of traditional FS, which assigns multiple membership values against each value from the universal set. Each term reflects a different opinion, which makes it effective for a complex decision environment. The concept of m-PFS is very useful for such real-world problems that involve multiple interactive factors, which cannot be captured by using the traditional fuzzy approaches.

Literature review

The hallucination risk and trustworthiness involve complex and vague information; an effective and reliable decision-making framework is required. Various studies have been presented by scholars to address these challenges, such as Rathkopf4, who invented the notation of corrosive hallucination to handle the epistemically troubling subclasses. Ciubotaru5 highlighted the negative effects of hallucination on the accuracy and user trust in a digital learning environment. Lee et al.6 briefly examined the risk of generative AI and explained how user behavior can help to overcome these challenges. Elsayed7 briefly examined the impact of hallucination information on student learning outcomes in large language models. Ahn8 extensively highlighted the limitations of large language models to evade hallucination and maintain reliability.

The FS framework has become a powerful foundation for modern decision-making and is widely used by researchers across different domains. Guiffrida and Nagi9 presented a detailed literature review on the applications of FS in the product management research environment. Bloch and Ralescu10 discussed various methods of FS in the imaging processing domain. Miliauskaitė and Kalibatiene11 introduced a chronological and bibliometric analysis of FS based on existing papers extracted from Web of Science. Maues et al.12 invented a fuzzy-based model to estimate construction waste generation. Guan13 constructed a data management algorithm based on fuzzy information for handling the data of higher education. Budayan et al.14 invented a computerized method using the concept of FS for delay risk assessment. Tolis et al.15 merged a small greenhouse structure using the integrated approach of FS and Internet of Things. Abbate et al.16 used the qualitative comparative analysis approach based on fuzzy information for the analysis of an Internet of Things platform. Krechko and Mikhaylov17 used the concepts of FS and spatial analysis to generate global electricity from renewable sources.

Because of its effectiveness, the researchers used it across many fields, such as Kansal and Kumar18 constructed a novel accuracy measure based on interval-valued fuzzy information for multi-criteria decision-making and recognizing patterns. Nithya et al.19 invented the notation of digital weighted multi-criteria decision making based on interval-valued fuzzy information for the selection of the best contractor for building projects. Sanz et al.20 used the integrated concept of IVFS and genetic amplitude tuning to enhance the performance of fuzzy rule-based classification systems. Wu et al.21 constructed a triangular fuzzy rating model based on interval-valued fuzzy information for the representation of users’ preferences. Petry and Yager22 invented some aggregation and evaluation approaches based on interval-valued fuzzy information and discussed their applications in many areas. Ashtiani et al.23 invented a novel concept of the TOPSIS model based on interval-valued fuzzy information and discussed its application in multi-criteria decision making.

Due to this, the concept of m-PFS was extended to different environments, such as Akram et al.24 constructed two measures of connectedness under an m-polar fuzzy environment and extended its applications to the product manufacturing environment. Jagtap and Karande25 constructed an m-polar fuzzy-based algorithm for the selection of a nontraditional machining process. Jagtap and Ghangale26 constructed a novel multi-attribute decision-making approach based on m-polar fuzzy information for the selection of electric vehicles. Ali et al.27 constructed some m-polar fuzzy-based aggregation operators for the analysis of multi-criteria decision making. Jagtap and Karande28 presented a detailed review of the applications of m-PFS and also highlighted its benefits and flaws. Ali and Alsager29 constructed the notation of power geometric aggregation operators under an m-polar fuzzy domain and extended their applications to urban transportation management. Akram et al.30 deliberated the ELECTRE-I for m-PFSs. Further, Rahman et al.31 derived the operators based on Aczel-Alsina norms for m-PFSs. Additionally, Jana and Hezam32 presented the EDAS technique for m-PFSs.

Aggregation operators are widely used for the integration of multiple attribute values into a single overall assessment. Different types of fuzzy-based aggregation operators have been developed by researchers, such as Mezei and Wikstrom33, who constructed a novel aggregation approach by integrating the notations of IVFS and ordered weighting averaging aggregation operators. Bustince et al.34 deliberated the Choquet integrals for IVFSs. Further, Waseem et al.35 derived the operators based on Hamacher norms for m-PFSs. Moreover, Akram et al.36 examined the operators for m-PFSs based on Dombi norms. Additionally, Jana and Pal37 presented the decision-making models for some m-PFSs. Further, Maity and Pal38 stated the power operators based on Dombi norms for m-PFSs. Moreover, Ali et al.39 derived the operators for m-PFSs based on Schweizer-Sklar norms. Many decision-making procedures are developed for the interpretation of complex problems. Inspired by these decision-making models, the hybrid decision-making model was invented by Pamucar and Cirovic40, which is called the multi-attribute border approximation area comparison (MABAC) network. The MABAC model is very efficient in coping with complex data. Further, Akram et al.41 derived another hybrid model of decision-making technique, called the PROMETHEE technique for m-PFSs. Moreover, Jagtap and Karande42 invented the technique for order performance by similarity to the ideal solution (TOPSIS) model for m-PFSs. Additionally, Akram and Adeel43 deliberated the ELECTRE-I and ELECTRE-II models for m-PFSs. Sahoo et al.44 deliberated on sustainable agile manufacturing based on Pythagorean FSs. Ali et al.45 invented the decision-making model for the multi-polar fuzzy soft expert technique. Sahoo and Debnath46 exposed the AHP and MABAC model for a case study on pharmaceutical supplier selection. Ali et al.47 presented the similarity measures for bipolar fuzzy soft information. Sahoo and Debnath48 stated the fuzzy decision-making model for supporting resource-efficient manufacturing techniques. Sarwar et al.49 designed the hesitant 2-tuple linguistic rough numbers. Liu, et al.50 studied lung diseases recognition using generalized complex fermatean fuzzy distance and entropy measures. Liu, et al.51 investigated elementary function-based fermatean fuzzy similarity measures using decision making tools. Main purpose of proposed manuscript

This section aims to deliberate on the research gap, main problem, advantages, motivation, and main contribution of the derived models. The main theme of this manuscript is to develop the interval-valued multi-polar fuzzy (IVMPF)-MABAC model based on aggregation information. Many scholars widely used the aggregation operators to aggregate complex and uncertain expert information based on fuzzy information, but not IVMPF data sets. Similarly, they applied various MADM models to evaluate and rank alternatives in an uncertain environment, but not hybrid models. All these existing aggregation operators and MADM models effectively handle various types of complex and uncertain real-world problems. Despite its effectiveness, there are still some huge research gaps in the existing literature, such as: (i) The existing literature contains many frameworks, which help the experts to express their preference, but an effective framework of IVMPF is not offered yet. (ii) Further, this study also discusses the different aggregation operators, where the technique of IVMPF-based aggregation operators has not been developed yet. (iii) Different generalized ideas of the MABAC model have been developed, but no one has invented an effective idea of the MABAC model under an IVMPF environment. (iv) The current literature contains various hybrid ideas, but no one integrates the notation of the MABAC model with aggregation operators under an IVMPF environment. Comparative analysis

In this section, the comparative evaluation between proposed frameworks and existing frameworks is elaborated to show the validity and supremacy of the derived models. The comparative assessment is one of the most valuable procedures to describe the effectiveness of the invented techniques. This section is specially designed for the comparison of the relative performance of the proposed models with some related existing models. For the valuation of the comparative analysis between the proposed and existing techniques, the following existing models are arranged. For instance, Mezei and Wikstrom33 constructed a novel aggregation approach by integrating the notations of IVFS and ordered weighting averaging aggregation operators. Bustince et al.34 deliberated the Choquet integrals for IVFSs. Further, Waseem et al.35 derived the operators based on Hamacher norms for m-PFSs. Moreover, Akram et al.36 examined the operators for m-PFSs based on Dombi norms. Additionally, Jana and Pal37 presented the decision-making models for some m-PFSs. The MABAC model is very efficient in coping with complex data. Further, Akram et al.41 derived another hybrid model of decision-making technique, called the PROMETHEE technique for m-PFSs. Moreover, Jagtap and Karande42 invented the technique for order performance by similarity to the ideal solution (TOPSIS) model for m-PFSs. Conclusion

The IVMPF framework provides an effective platform for the expert to express their preference, even if it is complex. The proposed aggregation operators effectively aggregate the expert information into a single value and help the decision makers to aggregate the complex and uncertain information. In this research work, we invented the following concepts. The main purpose of this manuscript is to evaluate the best and worst decisions among the decisions of Gemini (Google DeepMind), Copilot (GitHub/Microsoft), LLaMA (Meta), Claude (Anthropic), and ChatGPT (OpenAI) based on the following criteria, such as data privacy, Explainability, Hallucination risk, reliability, and bias control. Therefore, the system of the IVMPF-MABAC technique for algebraic information is developed. The invented model is very famous and reliable for coping with awkward and complex information. Therefore, for the valuation or the construction of the derived technique, this study also developed the novel technique of IVMPF information and also investigated their operations based on algebraic norms for the construction of the averaging operator and geometric operator. Lastly, this study worked on the analysis of the numerical examples and the implementation of the comparative analysis among proposed and prevailing frameworks to derive the validity and effectiveness of the introduced approaches.

In the future, our target is to invent the notation of Dombi aggregation operators, Hamacher aggregation operators, Einstein aggregation operators, and Aczel-Alsina aggregation operators under an IVMPF environment. We are also targeting to extend the current notation of WASPAS model, CoCoSo model, TOPSIS model, and DEMATAL model to an interval-valued m-polar fuzzy domain.

Designing a chimeric multi-epitope vaccine against Candida auris using reverse vaccinology approach targeting the agglutinin-like protein N-terminal domain
Authors: Maha A Aljumaa, Khaled Alzhrani, Deema Fallatah, Shaza N Alkhatib, Hanan Abdulrahman Sagini, Omniah A Mansouri, Ghulam Nabi, Gebreselema Gebreyohannes
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCandida auris is an emerging multidrug resistant fungal pathogen associated with high mortality rates, rapid global dissemination and resistance to conventional antifungal therapies. It’s remarkable ability to evade host immune responses and persist in health care setting demands the development of effective immunotherapeutic strategies. In this study, a reverse vaccinology and immunoinformatics based approach was employed to design a novel chimeric multi-epitope vaccine targeting surface expose N-terminal domain of the agglutinin like protein involved in host pathogen interactions. High affinity B-cell and T-cell (MHC class I and II) epitopes were identified and screened based on antigenicity, allergenicity, toxicity and population coverage. Selected epitopes were assembled using optimized linkers (EAAAK, AAY and GPGPG) along with an adjuvant to enhance immunogenicity and structural stability. Physicochemical characterization, structural validation, molecular docking with human Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4), Normal Mode Analysis (NMA), immune simulation, codon optimization and in silico cloning into the pET28a+ vector were performed to evaluate the vaccine construct. The selected epitopes demonstrated a global population coverage of 97.31%. the final vaccine construct was predicted to highly antigenic, non-allergenic, structurally stable and soluble. Molecular docking analysis revealed strong and stable interactions between the vaccine construct and human TLR4, with a binding energy of − 906.1 kcal/mol. Normal Mode Analysis further supported the structural stability of the vaccine receptor complex. Immune simulations predicted robust primary and secondary responses characterized by elevated IgG and IgM antibodies along with a Th1-skewed cytokine profile dominated by IFN-γ and IL-2 expression. Codon optimization and in-silico cloning indicated favorable translational efficiency in the pET28a+ expression system. The designed chimeric multi epitope vaccine demonstrated promising immunogenic, structural and receptor binding properties against Candida auris. These findings suggest that the proposed vaccine construct may serve as a potential candidate for further experimental validation and future development of effective immunotherapeutic interventions against multidrug- resistant fungal infections.Keywords: Candida auris, Reverse vaccinology, Multi-epitope vaccine, TLR4, Immunoinformatics, Agglutinin-like proteinSubject terms: Biotechnology, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Drug discovery, Immunology, Microbiology

Introduction

During the past ten years, Candida auris has posed a new and formidable challenge to the medical fraternity. In contrast to common fungal pathogen, which are usually manageable, C. auris has rapidly emerged as a serious health threat throughout the world, capable of causing severe outbreaks in healthcare settings. The particular danger of this pathogen lies not only in its ability to spread but also in its endurance as it persists for a long time on the surfaces of hospitals and, even more crucially, manifests resistance to several classes of antifungal drugs1. As the rates of mortality from invasive infections remain perilously high, it becomes increasingly evident that our current armamentarium of antifungal therapeutics is no longer adequate2. C. auris continues to develop mechanisms through which it can bypass conventional antifungal drugs such as azoles, polyenes, and echinocandins. This increasing prevelance of multidrug resistance dictates that there is an urgent need for active prevention rather than mere treatment3. A promising avenue in this regard is immunotherapy, particularly the vaccination. However, vaccine development against eukaryotic pathogens like fungi requires the identification of targets with great precision; this assures the elicitation of a protective immune response without harming the human host4.

To design an effective vaccine construct, it is essential to target the invasion and survival machinery of the fungi. Amongst all virulence factors employed by C. auris, the Agglutinin-like proteins (ALS) play a major role. These are cell-surface proteins acting as the “hands” of the fungus, capable of adhesion to host tissues and the formation of biofilms-a type of barrier notoriously difficult to treat. More precisely, the N-terminal domain of the ALS protein is the main region responsible for such adhesion. Since it is surface-exposed and crucial for the initial stages of the infection, blocking or recognizing this domain may effectively fight with the infection before it takes hold5,6. Conventional vaccine development, relying mostly on pathogen cultivation and trial-and-error, is an expensive and time-consuming process. Modern research, therefore, has increasingly turned to “Reverse Vaccinology.” This technique works backwards from the genome, using bioinformatics to pinpoint the most promising antigenic regions, or epitopes, before stepping into a wet lab. Screening the genetic code of the pathogen can determine what specific sequences are most likely recognized by human B-cells and T-cells for a broad and targeted immune defense. Traditional vaccine development is often a prolonged and resource-intensive process, frequently taking over a decade to reach clinical application. In contrast, bioinformatics and immunoinformatics approaches offer a transformative alternative for designing multi-epitope vaccines7.

The reverse vaccinology approach has been utilized in this study for the design of a novel, multiepitope vaccine chimera as the prevention strategy against C. auris. The N-terminal domain of the Agglutinin-like protein has been specifically focused due to its critical role in host pathogen interaction. High antigenicity epitopes have been merged with optimized and suitable linkers with an adjuvant to elucidate a robust immune response. Molecular docking and normal mode analysis has been performed to study the vaccine interaction with human TLR4 like receptor. Extensive computational analysis has been performed to study the efficacy of designed vaccine construct and the performance in immune system similar environment in the body. The objective of this study is to employ a comprehensive Immunoinformatics approach to develop a chimeric multi-epitope vaccine against C. auris infections. By systematically evaluating the structural stability, receptor-binding affinity (via TLR4 docking), and simulated immune kinetics of the construct, this study seeks to provide a theoretically validated candidate for subsequent experimental trials8.

This study provides a robust foundation for the design of a multiepitope vaccine chimera that can be an effective and vital tool to control the spread of this drug resistance fungi. Therefore, in-vitro and in-vivo validations of this vaccine construct is still suggested in order to predict the real time results. This study will open gateways to the future scientists who are willing to work on the drug resistance fungal pathogens in terms of drug and vaccine design.

Materials and methods

Candidate protein selection

The N-terminal domain-containing agglutinin-like protein of C. auris (UniProt ID: A0AAW0V956) was identified as the target antigen. The protein sequence, which was comprised of 861 amino acids, was obtained from the UniProtKB (TrEMBL) database (https://www.uniprot.org). Its selection was informed by its role within the pathogen-host interaction process and the fact that it is surface-exposed9.

Sequence analysis

Antigenicity testing of the protein was carried out using VaxiJen v2.0 (https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/vaxijen/VaxiJen/VaxiJen.html) with a cutoff at 0.6, while allergenicity testing was performed using AllerTOP v2.0 (https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/allertop_test). Homology with any human protein was also determined by using the NCBI BLAST tool in an effort to ensure minimal cross-reactivity10,11.

Structural analysis

The secondary structure was predicted by PSIPRED (https://bioinf.cs.ucl.ac.uk/psipred), whereas the tertiary structure was predicted by AlphaFold4 to give a correct structural background for performing the epitope mapping study12.

Prediction of the epitopes

Linear B-cell epitopes were predicted using the Emini surface accessibility method in the IEDB (https://www.iedb.org). T-cell epitopes (MHC Class-I and MHC Class-II) were predicted for , taking all common human alleles into consideration for greater population coverage13.

Population coverage analysis

Population coverage of the predicted T-cell epitopes was determined by the Population Coverage Analysis tool available at IEDB (https://www.iedb.org). The analysis provided greater population coverage for the selected epitopes ensuring greater conservancies among the populations14.

Vaccine construction

B-cell epitopes and T-cell epitopes selected as candidates were fused to create a Multi-Epitope Vaccine Construct. Appropriate linkers were strategically utilized to ensure structural stability, prevent the formation of junctional epitopes, and facilitate optimal processing and presentation of the individual antigenic components. Linkers corresponding to AAY (MHC I) and GPGPG (MHC II and B-cell epitopes) were used to ensure flexibility. Furthermore, an EAAAK spacer between the adjuvant and to separate adjuvant and vaccine part, and a 6xHis tag at the C-terminal to enhance the purification process15,16.

Antigenicity & allergenicity of the vaccine construct

The Vaxijen 2.0 0 (https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/vaxijen/VaxiJen/VaxiJen.html) was utilized for the assessment of antigenicity of the vaccine construct. The allergenicity was determined by the AllerTOP (https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/allertop_test). These assessments are crucial to analyze and understand the safety concerns of vaccine constructs for future use10,11.

Solubility and physicochemical properties

The solubility of the target protein was predicted by SOLpro (https://scratch.proteomics.ics.uci.edu), whereas the physicochemical properties, such as molecular weight, theoretical pI, aliphatic index, and instability index, were calculated using ExPASy ProtParam (https://web.expasy.org/protparam)17,18.

Structural analysis

Secondary structural elements were predicted using the PSIPRED algorithm (https://bioinf.cs.ucl.ac.uk/psipred), giving information on alpha helices, beta strands, and coils. The tertiary structure was constructed using AlphaFold3 (https://alphafold.ebi.ac.uk) for predicting a three-dimensional model for downstream analysis19.

3D structure validation

The validation of tertiary structure constructed by AlphaFold3 (https://alphafold.ebi.ac.uk) and validation was performed by Ramachandran Plot that was constructed by PROCHECK (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/thornton-srv/software/PROCHECK). It provided the geometrical parameters of the constructed model, ensuring the stability and accuracy of the predicted structure20,21.

Molecular docking with human TLR4 receptor

The extracellular domain of human Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4, PDB ID: 4G8A) was selected as the immune receptor for the docking experiment, due to its key role in recognizing pathogen-associated molecular patterns. TLR4 three-dimensional structure was obtained from the Protein Data Bank (https://www.rcsb.org) and the non-relevant chains, water molecules and ligands were eliminated prior to the docking experiment (Bank, 1971). The docking study involved the multi-epitope vaccine construct that consisted of B-cell and T-cell epitopes with adjuvant and 6x histidine tag as the ligand. ClusPro 2.0 Protein-Protein Docking Server (https://cluspro.org) was used to dock the TLR4 receptor and the designed vaccine construct. The parameters of docking were kept as default and the most popular clusters were taken based on the scoring parameters22. The receptor-ligand complex was visualized and analyzed with PyMOL (https://www.pymol.org), to determine the protein-protein interaction interface23,24.

Molecular dynamic simulations

The evaluation of the thermodynamic stability and dynamic behavior of the complex of vaccine with the receptor was carried out via 100 ns MD simulation within Desmond program package from Schrödinger suite25. The complex was placed into the orthorhombic box with TIP3P solvent with 10 Å buffer distance around the solute, charged with either Na+ or Cl- ions and equilibrated to 0.15 M NaCl salinity under the OPLS5e potential field. After default relaxation steps, an unrestrained production run of simulation was conducted in the NPT ensemble at 310 K temperature and atmospheric pressure applying Nosé-Hoover and Martyna-Tobias-Klein algorithms for thermostatting and barostating correspondingly. Coordinates snapshots were saved each 100 ps with 2 fs time step for Root Mean Square Deviation (RMSD), Root Mean Square Fluctuation (RMSF), secondary structure persistence, and protein-ligand interactions analysis. To determine major motions of protein molecule, PCA and DCCM calculations were performed via Bio3D package in R 26–28.

Normal mode analysis

iMODs online server (https://imods.iqf.csic.es) was used to provide normal mode analysis to measure the stability and flexibility of the docked complex. The docking complex, in the PDB format, was uploaded where the native motion, eigenvalues and flexibility of the docking complex were calculated. The results obtained provided the data regarding movement, multifaceted dynamics, and Elasticity29.

Immune simulations

Immune simulations on C-ImmSim web server (https://kraken.iac.rm.cnr.it/C-IMMSIM), were performed to evaluate the immune responses using Position Specific Scoring Matrix and Machine Learning Algorithms. The vaccine sequence was provided as a FASTA file using default parameters and requirements of three doses after four weeks between them. The simulation was a model of the primary, secondary, and tertiary immune responses in accordance with the concentrations of the immune components, including antibodies, B-cells, and T-cells, production of cytokines, and production of memory cells. The strength, planking, and diversity of the vaccine provoked immune response was analyzed based on the results produced30.

Reverse translation & codon optimization

To translate the protein sequence into a DNA sequence to be used in cloning and expression studies, the designed vaccine sequence was reverse translated with Emboss ReverseTranseq Tool (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/jdispatcher/st/emboss_transeq). The codon optimization process of the translated sequence was then implemented with the help of the Java Codon Adaptation (JCat) (https://www.jcat.de) tool. It was done to ensure its compatibility with the codon usage of Escherichia coli K12 via the server JCat, and Codon Adaptation Index (CAI) values31,32.

In silico cloning of the multi-epitope vaccine construct

In silico digestion and ligation of the recombinant construct were carried out using the SnapGene (https://www.snapgene.com). This ensured the verification for the insertion of construct in the right positions and reading frame. The codon-optimized vaccine sequence was in silico restriction digested and seamlessly cloned into the pET28a(+) expression vector between the specific BlpI and BglII restriction sites to ensure optimal downstream translation15.

Results

Selection and characterization of target protein

Agglutinin-like protein N-terminal domain-containing protein of C. auris with the UniProt ID: A0AAW0V956 was identified as the target protein based on the protein’s surface exposure and the putative role in the pathogen-host interaction. This protein has a total of 861 amino acids, and it was predicted to exist based on homology, as described in the UniProtKB andTrEMBL entry. Antigenicity studies using VaxiJen predicted the protein to be highly protective with an overall score of 0.8604, indicating a probable antigen and thellergenicity studies predicted the protein to be non-allergenic. Importantly, the protein was found to show no significant similarity to human proteins using the NCBI BLAST tool, thereby avoiding cross-reactivity and the onset of autoimmunity. All these studies provide conclusive evidence for the identification of the protein as safe and suitable for construction as a multi-epitope vaccine.

Structural analysis of the target protein

The secondary structure prediction for the selected C. auris agglutinin-like protein showed a diverse structural composition with a high proportion consisting of coils, beta strands, and regions consisting of clearly defined alpha helices. Such structural diversity is common in fungal surface-exposed proteins, which are typically associated with a higher level of immunogenicity due to their ease of access. The secondary structure elements can serve as a good guideline for further analysis focusing on epitope mapping. The secondary structure of the protein predicted by PsiPred is given below as Fig. 1.

This model had extensive surface-exposed regions, especially in the flexible areas of the loops, which are ideal for immune system recognition. Analysis of the protein structure further ascertained the spatial arrangement of the predicted sites of epitopes on the accessible protein surface. Epitope prediction and evaluation

Linear B-cell epitope prediction showed the existence of several regions that are surface accessible and possess a high antigenic potential. Four B-cell epitopes of varying lengths were picked based upon their accessibility to the surface, continuity, and potential for immunogenicity. These epitopes were found to primarily lie in the flexible regions of the protein molecule, which is ideal for antibody binding. To evoke an immune activation of T cells, a whole array of cytotoxic T-lymphocyte (MHC class I) and Helper T-lymphocyte (MHC class II) epitopes was identified. Sixteen MHC class I and five MHC class II epitopes of 9 and 15 amino acids length respectively were selected for their high binding affinity and allele recognition capabilities. Notably, the patterns of both CTL and HTL epitopes being distributed over different parts of the target protein provide clues to their role in inducing a well-balanced cellular activation of a host immune system against an attacking pathogen.

Population coverage and conservancy analysis

Results of population coverage analysis showed that the selected epitopes, when considered as a whole and either class I or class II, allowed extensive global population coverage. The proposed epitope combination would be expected to elicit an immunological response in 97.31% of the globally considered population, based on predictions about population coverage. The mean number of epitope hits per person has been calculated to be 3.59, and PC90 value of 1.77. The zero standard deviation represented in all parameters calculated reinforces the uniformity and accuracy of the epitopes chosen. Taken together, it is clear that the epitopes computed are indeed conserved and well-representative, and this enhances the potential of the developed vaccine construct in inducing immune responses among various ethnic and geographical groups. Multi-epitope vaccine construction

A multi-epitope vaccine construct was rationally engineered by combining the cytotoxic T-lymphocyte (CTL), helper T-lymphocyte (HTL), and B-cell epitopes of the target pathogens into a single chimeric construct to promote immune cell and Antibodies production. The chimeric construct was begun with the adjuvant of the Mycobacterium tuberculosis ribosomal protein bL12 at the N-terminus of the construct to promote the stimulation of the innate immune system. A rigid linker EAAAK was used between the adjuvant and the epitope-carrying region of the construct to provide independence of the structure and hinder adverse interactions between the functional regions.

The CTL epitopes were joined together via the AAY linker, which has been shown to ensure efficient proteasomal processing and optimize the processing and presentation of the MHC class I antigen. To join the helper T-cell and B-cell epitopes, the flexible GPGPG linker was used to ensure efficient antigen processing, eliminate formation of the junctional epitope, and optimize epitope accessibility to the immune system. A six-histidine (6×His) tag was appended to the C-terminal end to optimize purification and detection during experimental analysis. The final design of the vaccine construct contained a well-organized sequence architecture that provided the right mix of both flexibility and stiffness to optimize epitope presentation. Antigenicity and allergenicity prediction of the vaccine construct

The antigenic potential of the finalized multi-epitope vaccine construct was calculated employing VaxiJen v2.0, yielding a total score of 0.8044, which classified the construct as a probable antigen. The score achieved here is well above the threshold value, indicating that the construct has a very good chance of creating a successful immune response. The inclusion of multiple dominant epitopes, combined with the synergistic effect of the adjuvant, significantly enhanced the immunological score of the designed vaccine construct. . Allergenicity analysis also showed that the vaccine construct is non-allergenic, as expected from the similarity between the nucleotide sequences of the vaccine constructs and known allergens. This suggests that the designed vaccine is both immunogenic and safe, and therefore promising for further validation, binding, and simulation analysis.

Physicochemical properties and solubility analysis

Physicochemical characteristics of the final multi-epitope vaccine construct were analyzed by ExPASy ProtParam tool. The total amino acid composition of the designed vaccine was found to contain 536 amino acids, with an estimated molecular weight of 55.6 kDa. The isoelectric point was computed to be 4.96, which describes it as acidic nature protein providing benefits in terms of solubility. The vaccine construct was determined to have more negatively charged residues compared to the presence of positive charge residues, indicating the acidic nature of the vaccine. The stability index of vaccine construct was estimated to be 34.81 indicating it is a stable protein. Additionally, the aliphatic index was estimated to be 75.34, thus making it thermostable at high temperatures. The GRAVY value of -0.024 revealed that it is basically a hydrophilic protein, and it is an ideal character to be exhibited by the protein concerning solubility and antigenic accessibility. The biological half-life of the construct can be estimated to be above 10 h in Escherichia coli and more than 20 h in yeast, and around 30 h in mammalian cells, thus it has acceptable stability in the heterologous environment. The analysis of solubility prediction from the SOLpro webserver gave a probability value of 0.70, thus making it clear that the vaccine construct is soluble when overexpressed. The higher probability value reveals ease and feasibility concerning the recombinant expression and purification of vaccine protein. Hence, it can be concluded that all the above-revealed facts confirm that the multi-epitope vaccine construct has favorable criteria concerning the physicochemical properties, structural stability, and solubility, thus suitable for further validation analysis.

Molecular docking with human TLR4

Molecular docking of the multi-epitope vaccine construct with the extracellular domain of human TLR4 (PDB ID: 4G8A) was done using ClusPro 2.0. Amongst the docking poses formed, the top cluster (Cluster 0), containing 103 clusters was selected for analysis based on energy values. The top cluster had a lowest energy value of -906.1 kcal/mol and a center energy value of -859.2 kcal/mol, which clearly depicts that there was strong and stable binding between the vaccine construct and TLR4. Also, from interface scoring, multiple hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic contacts are seen between hydrogen and hydrophobic areas from solvent-accessible surfaces of vaccine construct which include both epitope and adjuvant parts. These hydrogen and hydrophobic contacts clearly depict that the molecule is properly oriented for interacting with TLR4 and initiating innate immune response. Molecular dynamics Simulations

The 100 nanosecond (ns) molecular dynamics simulations further proved the stability and strong binding nature of the vaccine-TLR4 complex. The root mean square deviation (RMSD) analysis revealed the complex attaining an equilibrium state right from the beginning stages of the simulation, where the receptor and ligand trajectories remained constant, indicating a highly stable binding mode. The root mean square fluctuation (RMSF) calculations showed that most residues were stable, apart from the usual high values for flexible loop structures, whereas the radius of gyration (Rg) values indicated the constant structure compactness of the receptor and ligand proteins through the entire duration of 100 ns. Moreover, principal component analysis (PCA) helped validate the structural stability, where a major part of the conformational changes was accounted for by the fewest principal components, implying the occurrence of collective modes. Furthermore, the consistent number of hydrogen bonds formed between the receptor and ligand proteins indicates the presence of strong intermolecular forces holding the two proteins together. The deformability plot identifies different peaks defining flexible regions of the complex, which may be defined by residues connected by high accessibility epitope zones. The calculation and actual results used in the PDB closely resemble the B-factor plot hence authorizing the stability of the complex. The complex was less prone to energy that was required to deform it and highly stable based on the eigenvalue obtained. The plot of variance indicates the opposite trend to eigenvalues indicating that there are separate contributions of residues to the total movement. The covariance map indicates that there are high correlated (red), uncorrelated (white) and anticorrelated (blue) movements between the residues, thus indicating an overall movement behavior by the residues in the docked complex. The Elastic Network Model (ENM) depicts a high gray level with darker gray level implying strong cluster residues subscribing to the vaccine and receptor complex. Immune simulation analysis

The immune simulation performed on the C-ImmSim server predicted a strong and complex immune response after three doses of the multi-epitope vaccine.

Humoral and cellular response

The humoral and cellular immune responses had a predominant feature indicating high titer of IgG + IgM antibodies, which was rapidly followed by a substantial increase of IgG1 and IgG2 levels during the secondary and tertiary responses. This reflected a high-affinity antibody response and a strong immunological memory. There was a substantial increase in B-cell populations reflected by a substantial increase in active B-cells and memory B-cells, which is imperative for long-term immunity.

T-cell population dynamics

A substantial activation of Helper T-cells, and Cytotoxic T-cells was recorded in this study. However, the number of cells remained high and persisted during the entire simulation period and reflected a strong need of activation of this cell type to ultimately support antibody secretion levels. Importantly, there was a low Simpson Index (D) of clonal diversity indicative of a diverse immune system against the selected multiple antigenic regions.

Cytokine profile

The immune simulation study revealed high levels of Interferon-gamma, followed by substantial levels of Interleukin-2 (IL-2), which is indicative of a strong TH1-type immune system reaction during the interaction with immune system. The designed vaccine sequence was successfully optimized for high-level expression in host E. coli by reverse translation of the protein sequence followed by optimization of the host’s preferred codon usage. The optimized DNA was then in silico cloned in pET28a+ vector using SnapGene Software. The optimized gene has a Codon Adaptation Index of 0.957, indicating highly efficient translation and proper protein folding in E. coli. The optimized gene also contains a GC content of 71.19%, favoring stable transcription and translation. The in-silico analysis also confirmed that there are no issues in the designed vaccine that may prevent smooth compatibility with pET28a+ vector. Discussion

The study aims to design and virtually testing the multi-epitope vaccine against the agglutinin-like protein N-terminal domain-containing protein of the C. auris species33. The rationale for choosing this antigen is based on its exposure on the cell surface and potential role in pathogen-human cell interactions. Further, it is an excellent candidate vaccine owing to its potent immunogenic properties and lack of allergenicity. The low homology found in the candidate protein and human proteins indicates its safety concerning the development of autoimmune reactions. Collectively, these attributes provide a compelling rationale for the design of a vaccine that can stimulate the immune system safely34,35. Secondary and tertiary structural analyses reveal a well-distributed arrangement of alpha-helices, beta-sheets, and random coils, with a significant proportion of surface-exposed loops36. These structural features are highly favorable for epitope mapping, as flexible, solvent-accessible loop regions represent prime targets for immune cell interactions. Furthermore, structural validation of the AlphaFold3 model via Ramachandran plot analysis confirmed that the protein maintains a highly stable and accurately folded conformation—a critical prerequisite for ensuring structural epitope stability and integrity37.

The choice of agglutinin-like sequence (Als) protein (UniProt ID: A0AAW0V956) is justified based on the importance of the protein to function as a surface-exposed adhesin necessary for the biofilm formation and host adherence of C. auris. The protein was selected based on its antigenicity (0.8604), absence of homology to human proteins, and flexibility in certain loops. The protein’s N-terminal domain is evolutionarily highly conserved across different clinical lineages of the microorganism. These target choices are supported by empirical studies demonstrating that homologous Agglutinin-like sequence (Als) proteins elicit robust, cross-protective humoral and cell-mediated immunity in murine models.. The inclusion of these epitopes guarantees broad population coverage (97.31%) among the global population16,38.

Immunoinformatics screening facilitated the selection of different linear B cell and T cell epitopes (MHC I and II) distributed along the protein. The distribution of epitopes along the protein is advantageous since it stimulates both the cellular and humoral immune responses39. The extent to which the predicted population is covered by the selected epitopes, which currently stands at above 97% globally, indicates that the proposed vaccine shall be highly effective in almost all regions and among all human ethnic groups. The conserved properties possessed by the epitopes indicate that they would be of immense value in making long-term protective immune responses40. The design of the multi-epitope vaccine construct was carried out in such a way that the target epitopes could be assembled along with spacers and an adjuvant. The key to implementing this was to enable the independence of functional regions, correct the processing of antigens, and enable immune cells to approach the antigens41. This was achieved by using EAAAK, AAY, and GPGPG linkers, which enable the independence of functional regions, correct processing of antigens, and allow immune cells to bind to antigens. To augment innate immune activation, the BL12 adjuvant was fused to the N-terminus of the construct. Additionally, a polyhistidine (6x-His) tag was appended to the C-terminus to facilitate downstream affinity purification and subsequent experimental validation. The construct possesses optimal physicochemical requirements, which include stability, solubility, and hydrophobicity. 42.

The analysis of molecular docking, which was carried out using human TLR4 receptor, indicated a stable bonding of the vaccine construct to TLR4; in fact, the most favorable cluster of the vaccine molecule against TLR4 has the lowest energy with good hydrogen bonding and hydrophobic interactions. All these traits indicated that the designed vaccine is well-positioned to be bound with the innate immune receptors effectively and to activate the immune cascade43. Normal Modes Analysis further validated the flexibility and stability of the TLR4-Vaccine complex by examining the rigid regions that form the binding site as well as the flexible regions that are involved in the activation of the immune receptors. A high Codon Adaptation Index (CAI) and suitable GC content signify that the vaccine construct will be efficiently expressed in the pET28a+ expression system. Furthermore, the seamless in silico insertion of the construct into the pET28a(+) vector signifies that the cloning and subsequent expression will be successful. This increases the chances of subsequent studies being undertaken on the immunogenicity and function of the vaccine candidate44. To conclude, the immune simulation predictions indicated that the vaccine had potential for the induction of a comprehensive immune response. The large amounts of IgG and IgM antibodies activated B-cells, and cytotoxic and helper T-cells suggest that the vaccine has the potential for inducing a humoral and cellular immune response, respectively. High concentrations of IFN-g and IL-2 suggest a strong TH1-type response, which is ideal for the provision of protection against fungal pathogens. Therefore, the collective outcomes of antigen selection, structure analysis, epitope mapping, vaccine design, molecular docking simulation, and immune simulation studies collectively validate the safety and efficacy of this multi-epitope vaccine against C. auris. The results of such computational studies can serve as a strong foundation for laboratory validation of expression and purification of this construct as well as evaluating this candidate in-vitro and in-vivo for their immunogenic property.

Even though this study presents an optimally designed vaccine against C. auris, several limitations should be mentioned. Firstly, all results are purely computational and based on in -silico predictions. Neither the in-silico prediction of structural stability nor the codon optimization technique guarantees identical efficiency during translation or native protein folding in-vivo.. Secondly, even though the vaccine presents a global coverage rate of 97.31% against different C. auris populations, genetic variability of human leukocyte antigens (HLA) can affect epitope presentation differently among patient samples.

Conclusions

This study presents a rationally designed chimeric multi-epitope vaccine targeting the agglutinin-like protein of Candida auris, the immunogenicity, safety, and stability of the constructs were determined and found to demonstrate outstanding properties in-silico. The result of the human TLR4 receptor docking analyses was the confirmation of reassuring binding affinity, followed by an evaluation of the immune simulations, which indicated excellent immune responses with immunological memory. This study provides a strong foundation for the vaccine development, experimental validation through in-vitro immunological assays, recombinant protein expression and clinical evaluation will be essential to confirm its safety, immunogenicity and protective efficacy before potential clinical applications.

A novel model based on apparent diffusion coefficient and signal intensity on MRI to quantify subtle internal difference between tumor-adjacent and -distant liver tissues in T3-staged resectable gallbladder carcinoma
Authors: Zhao Tang, Xiao-fang Zhu, Jing Ou, Yu-ping Wu, Xiao-ming Zhang, Bang-guo Tan, Tian-wu Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo develop a novel apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) and signal intensity (SI) based model to quantify the subtle internal difference between tumor-adjacent (TAL) and -distant liver tissue (TDL) in T3-staged resectable gallbladder carcinoma (GBC).Methods65 consecutive patients with T3-staged GBC invading liver undergoing preoperative MRI were retrospectively included, among which 54 from hospital 1 were randomly assigned to training (TC, n = 43) and internal validation cohorts (IVC, n = 11), while the remaining 11 from hospital 2 constituted external validation cohort (EVC, n = 11). Mean ADC and its standard deviation (SD) of TAL and TDL were measured on DWI at b-values of 0 and 600 s/mm2, 0 and 800 s/mm2, and 0 and 1000 s/mm2. SIs of TAL, TDL and erector spinae (ES) on T1WI, T2WI, and arterial, portal-venous and delayed phases enhanced images were measured, and signal intensity ratios (SIRs) of TAL and TDL to ES were calculated. The t-test, Mann-Whitney U test and binary logistic regression analyses were conducted sequentially to determine independent index for differentiating TAL from TDL, and a model was constructed for the differentiation. Predictive value of model was assessed using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve.ResultsIn TC, SIRs on arterial phase (SIRAP) and portal-venous phase (SIRPP), SIs on portal-venous phase (SIPP) and delayed phase (SIDP), and SD at b-values of 0 and 1000 s/mm2 (SD1000) were independent differentiating indexes with odds ratios of 0.008 (95% confidence interval [CI], 0.001–0.131), 0.132 (95%CI, 0.033–0.533), 1.002 (95%CI, 1.000-1.003), 0.998 (95%CI, 0.997–0.999), and 1.472 (95%CI, 0.006-355.856), respectively. ROC analysis showed that the model by integrating the previous indexes obtained excellent performance with areas under the ROC curve of 0.879, 0.934 and 0.909 in TC, IVC and EVC, respectively.ConclusionThe novel model could be helpful for quantifying the subtle difference between TAL and TDL in T3-staged GBC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12880-026-02250-1.Keywords: Gallbladder carcinoma, Diffusion-weighted imaging, Signal intensity, Tumor-adjacent liver tissue, Tumor-distant liver tissue

Introduction

Gallbladder carcinoma (GBC) represents the most prevalent and aggressive primary malignancy in the biliary system [1–3]. GBC does not present with the typical clinical signs and symptoms at the early stage. Consequently, GBC is typically diagnosed at an advanced unresectable stage [1, 4]. Despite this, surgical resection remains the only curative treatment option [5–7].

The extent of the primary resection of T3-staged GBC remains a topic of debate in the medical community. In clinical practice, extended cholecystectomy is typically recommended for patients with GBC at T3 stage. This procedure involves cholecystectomy and a limited hepatic resection, typically involving segments IVb and V [8], or a wedge resection of the gallbladder fossa within 2 cm of non-neoplastic liver tissue [9], which encompass the tumor to ensure negative margins and reduce the possible of recurrence. Nevertheless, the outcome of an empiric extended hepatectomy does not appear to be independently associated with long-term survival. Conversely, the overall perioperative morbidity is significantly increased from 41.9% to 73.5% [6]. Furthermore, previous research has indicated that tumor recurrence is closely associated with field cancerization. Field cancerization has been observed in the macroscopically normal tissues adjacent to the neck and head cancers and the resection margins [10–15]. In the absence of surgical excision of the tissue exhibiting a pre-neoplastic state, there is a potential for its progression to invasive cancer [15]. In light of the evidence regarding the coverage of previous extended hepatectomy and potential field cancerization in GBC, it is of paramount importance to differentiate between tumour-adjacent liver tissue (TAL) and tumour-distant liver tissue (TDL) prior to surgical intervention.

Diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) can provide functional information by depicting the different diffusion of water in biological tissues and can be quantified by apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC), which is inversely proportional to the integrity of cell membrane and the cellularity of tissue [16–18]. Previous studies have shown the reasonable accuracy of ADC and signal intensity (SI) in the detection of GBC and differentiating GBC from benign gallbladder disease [19–21]. A published research also indicates that ADC and its standard deviation (SD) could have potential value in differentiate TAL from TDL in patients with liver invasion by T3 staged GBC, but its area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) and the sensitivity was moderate (0.673 to 0.731) [22]. In order to obtain a better performance to preoperative distinguishing between TAL and TDL using MRI in patients with liver invasion by T3 staged GBC, we aimed to develop a novel model based on ADC and SI of TAL and TDL to quantify their internal subtle difference for the future potential study on the determination of reasonable liver resection range with MRI.

Materials and methods

Patients

The institutional review board of the Affiliated Hospital of North Sichuan Medical College approved this retrospective study (IRB number: 2023ER108-1) in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, and the requirements for informed consent were waived due to a retrospective study.

Patients with confirmed T3-staged GBC who underwent preoperative MRI and DWI following surgical resection were recruited from two independent hospitals between January 2017 to March 2023. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (a) all the patients were diagnosed as T3-staged GBC and invaded the adjacent liver parenchyma confirmed by postoperative pathology, (b) the multi-sequence MRI including multi-b values DWI were performed one week before the surgery; (c) the tumor was considered resectable and the surgical cut margins of all resected specimens were negative, and (d) no contraindications to surgery were identified, and patients expressed a desire to undergo surgical resection. A total of 70 consecutive patients with T3-staged GBC from both hospitals were included in the study. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (a) the patient was treated with preoperative neoadjuvant radiation therapy and/or chemotherapy before MRI (n = 3), or (b) the MRI were of poor quality due to the motion artifact, which affected on the identification of lesion boundaries and the delineation of regions of interest (n = 2). Ultimately, 5 patients were excluded, and a total of 65 patients were included in this study.

In all enrolled patients, 54 consecutive patients from hospital 1 were randomly assigned to the training (TC, n = 43) and internal validation cohorts (IVC, n = 11) at an 8:2 ratio [16], and 11 patients from hospital 2 were enrolled into the external validation cohort (EVC, n = 11). In three cohorts, all GBC patients did not treat with preoperative neoadjuvant radiation therapy and/or chemotherapy, and the surgical cut margins of all resected specimens were negative.

Image acquisitions

MRI was performed using a 3.0-T MRI system (Discovery MR 750, GE Medical Systems, Milwaukee, WI, USA) with a 32-channel torso phase-array coil, together with electrocardiographic and respiratory monitoring. All patients were required to abstain from food for a minimum of six hours prior to scanning in order to minimise peristalsis within the intestinal tract. Additionally, breathing training was provided to all patients prior to scanning in order to reduce the potential for respiratory artifacts. To minimise the potential for artifacts to be introduced by cardiac beats and respiratory movements, electrocardiographic and respiratory gating techniques were employed on all scanning sequences. The patients were examined in the supine position. The imaging coverage extended from the diaphragm to the lower pole of the kidneys.

The following scanning sequences were performed, including DWI at multiple b values of 0, 600, 800 and 1000 s/mm2, axial liver acquisition with volume acceleration (LAVA)-Flex sequence, axial fast recovery fast spin-echo T2-weighted imaging (T2WI) with fat saturation, single-shot fast spin-echo radial series slab magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography, and dynamic contrast-enhanced three-dimensional LAVA-flex with fat saturation images. The scanning parameters of the above sequences are listed in Suplementary Table 1. Three-dimensional LAVA dynamic enhancement was performed with 20 ml of gadopentetate dimeglumine (Magnevist; Bayer AG, Leverkusen, Germany) administered intravenously at a rate of 2–3 ml/s, followed by a 20 ml saline solution flush. Dynamic enhancement of the arterial phase (AP), portal-venous phase (PP) and delayed phase (DP) were performed at 20–40 s, 45–65 s and 3–5 min after the injection of the contrast medium, respectively. The MRI data were subsequently transferred to the workstation for analysis.

Image analysis

The data derived from MRI scans were reviewed on the work station (GE Advantage Workstation Version 4.4–09, Sun Microsystems, Palo Alto, CA, USA). For image analysis, it was essential to identify GBC, which was presented as irregular thickening of gallbladder wall or intraluminal mass, significant high signal intensity on DWI due to the remarkable restricted Brownian motion of water molecules within the tumor, and hyperintensity or slightly hyperintensity on contrast–enhanced images. Thus, the location of GBC was identified on axial T1WI, T2WI, DWI, and contrast-enhanced images. Clinically, an extended cholecystectomy is generally recommended for patients with T3-staged GBC, which involves cholecystectomy and a limited hepatic resection (typically segments IVb and V), or a wedge resection of the gallbladder fossa within 2 cm non-neoplastic liver tissue to ensure negative margins and reduce the possible recurrence [8, 9]. Based on the resection range of liver tissue in GBC, the TAL and TDL were defined on axial T1WI, T2WI, DWI, and contrast-enhanced images as follows: the liver tissues about 2 cm range of segments IVb and V away from the margin of the tumor was defined as TAL, and the rest of the segments IVb and V was defined as TDL.

ADC and SD values of the TAL and TDL were measured on DWI at b-values of 0 and 600 s/mm2, 0 and 800 s/mm2, and 0 and 1000 s/mm2 by using a mono-exponential model with the logarithmic equation ADC = ln(S2/S1)/(b1−b2). In the aforementioned equation, the b is the degree of diffusion sensitization, S1 and S2 are the tissue signal intensity on DWI at corresponding b1 and b2, respectively [23]. The region of interests (ROIs) for TAL and TDL were continuously outlined slice by slice using the FuncTool software until all the corresponding liver tissues were encompassed on DWI at different b values. This software automatically copied and pasted ROIs to ADC maps and worked out the corresponding ADC and SD values (Fig. 1). The ultimate ADC and SD values were calculated by averaged ADC and SD across all slices, respectively. When delineating the ROIs, meticulous attention was paid to the avoid cystic or necrotic, haemorrhagic, adipose tissues, and visible vessel.

SIs of TAL, TDL and erector spinae (ES) were automatically measured by the software on T1WI, T2WI, AP, PP and DP enhanced images by free-hand outlining the corresponding ROIs (Fig. 1) slice by slice until the whole corresponding liver tissues were covered. The ultimate SI values of the corresponding tissues were calculated by averaged SI values across all slices. In addition, the delineation of the ROIs to obtain SIs of TAL and TDL covered the corresponding tissues as completely as possible, copying and pasting the same size ROIs for each measurement on all sequences, and the ROI of ES ranged from 40 mm2 to 60 mm2 for each measurement on all sequences. Ultimately, the SI ratios (SIRs) (including SI of TAL to ES, and SI of TDL to ES) were calculated on the above sequences. The SIRs of TAL and TDL to ES for the corresponding sequences were recorded as SIRA−E and SIRD−E, respectively.

The previous ADC and its SD, and SIs of the TAL and TDL, and SIRs were blindly obtained independently by two radiologists (radiologist 1 with 13 years of experience in abdominal radiology, and radiologist 2 with 8 years of experience in abdominal radiology) to evaluate the inter-observer reliability. Prior to the delineation process, the two radiologists were trained by the radiologic professor on how to standardize the ROI delineations using 15 cases. In addition, radiologist 1 repeated the previous measurements after a 2-week interval to evaluate the intra-observer reliability.

Statistical analyses

The statistical software packages SPSS (version 23 for Windows; IBM, Armonk, New York, USA) and MedCalc (version 20.111, MedCalc Software Ltd, Ostend, Belgium) were selected for use in the analysis. A p-value of less than 0.05 was indicative of a statistically significant difference.

The intra- and inter-observer agreements in ADCs, SDs and SIs of the TAL and TDL were evaluated using the interclass correlation coefficient (ICC) in the training cohort. ICC values between 0.61 and 0.80, and greater than 0.80 are indicative of good and excellent agreements, respectively. Otherwise, the agreement is deemed to be unsatisfactory [24]. In the event that the inter- and intra-observer ICCs were 0.80 or more, the values of the first measurements from observer 1 were considered as the final results for further analyses. If the inter- and/or intra-observer ICCs were less than 0.80, the two measurements from observer 1 and the first measurement from observer 2 were averagd to get the final results.

In the TC, the t test or Mann-Whitney U test were used to assess whether the parameters mentioned above were significantly different between TAL and TDL. Subsequently, the parameters with statistical difference were enrolled in the binary logistic regression analyses to determine independent indexes to differentiate TAL from TDL. The model for differentiating TAL from TDL was constructed based on the independent indexes from the training cohort, the diagnostic efficacy of the model was evaluated using the ROC analysis. Ultimately, the predictive performance of the model derived from the TC was validated by the DeLong’s test in the IVC and EVC, following the same procedures as in the TC.

Results

Clinical characteristics in training and validation cohorts

In the training and validation cohorts, all cases were T3-staged adenocarcinoma and invaded the adjacent liver parenchyma as shown on postoperative pathology. In the TC (n = 43), the age was 64.51 ± 11.71, including 17 males and 26 females, the histologic differentiation appeared as poorly differentiated (n = 3), moderately differentiated (n = 12), and well-differentiated (n = 28), the nodal staging included pN0 (n = 18), pN1 (n = 23) and pN2 (n = 2). In the IVC (n = 11), the age was 67.55 ± 10.56, including 4 males and 7 females, the histologic differentiation appeared as poorly differentiated (n = 1), moderately differentiated (n = 3), and well-differentiated (n = 7), the nodal staging included pN0 (n = 4), pN1 (n = 6) and pN2 (n = 1). In the EVC (n = 11), the age was 68.18 ± 8.44, including 5 males and 6 females, the histologic differentiation appeared as poorly differentiated (n = 1), moderately differentiated (n = 4), and well-differentiated (n = 6), and the nodal stages included pN0 (n = 6), pN1 (n = 4) and pN2 (n = 1).

Development of prediction model and model’s ROC analyses in training cohort

Based on the above parameters with differences between tumor-adjacent and the tumor-distant liver tissues in the TC, SIR on AP image (SIRAP) and PP image (SIRPP), SI on PP image (SIPP) and DP image (SIDP), and SD of ADC at b-values of 0 and 1000 s/mm2 (SD1000) were independent differentiating indexes as demonstrated by the binary logistic regression analyses (P < 0.05 for all), and the logistic regression model equation was shown to be “-4.809 × SIRAP − 2.024 × SIRPP + 0.002 × SIPP − 0.002 × SIDP + 7.294 × SD1000 + 2.137”. The ROC analysis showed that the model had excellent performance with AUC of 0.879 to differentiate TAL from TDL in T3-staged GBC compared with any independent index (Table 5; Fig. 2).

Evaluation of the prediction models’ performance

The diagnostic accuracies of this model to differentiate TAL from TDL in T3-staged GBC was then validated using the DeLong’s test in the IVC and EVC. The test showed that the model obtained a good consistency in the IVC (AUC: 0.934) and EVC (AUC: 0.909), indicating that the model could be reliable to differentiate TAL from TDL (Fig. 3).

Discussion

T3-staged GBC is the most commonly accompanied by adjacent structure involvement, especially involvement of liver invasion [25]. If the area of potential liver invasion can be accurately identified before extended cholecystectomy, the risk of postoperative tumor recurrence can be effectively reduced. Therefore, it is important to quantify the difference in internal subtle structure of TAL and TDL before surgery to determine the coverage of surgical resection to ensure the resection of the morphologically normal cancerous area. In this study, we measured the mean ADC and its SD of TAL and TDL on DWI with b-values of 0 and 600 s/mm2, 0 and 800 s/mm2, and 0 and 1000 s/mm2. Meanwhile, the mean SI of TAL, TDL and ES together with the SIR values (including SI of TAL to that of ES, and SI of TDL to that of ES) were calculated on axial T1WI, T2WI, AP, PP and DP images. We explored the feasibility of combination of ADC and its SD, SI, and SIR to quantify internal subtle difference between TAL and TDL in T3-staged resectable GBC.

Our study revealed that the mean ADC and its SD of TAL were significantly lower than those of the TDL. Some previous studies have shown that the ADC value is negatively correlated with the degree of diffusion restriction of water molecules in tissues. Tumor cells have stronger proliferation ability, higher cell density, narrower extracellular space distortion and more complex interstitial components, which lead to significantly limited diffusion movement of water molecules, so tumor tissues show lower ADC values [8, 9]. Furthermore, Marques et al. [18] have demonstrated that the amount and mobility of water molecules are altered during the transition from normal to cancerous cells, which may result in differences in the diffusion of water molecules in tissues at varying stages of carcinogenesis. DU et al. and Dunet et al. [26, 27] also found that ADC was negatively correlated with tumor grade and the number of tumor cells. The above studies suggest that water molecular diffusion limitation becomes more pronounced as cancer progresses. Based on the results of this study, we could presume that cancerization could be more remarkable in the TAL than in the TDL in GBC, which leads to a more obvious diffusion restriction of water molecules in TAL than in TDL.

In addition, this is the first study to quantify SI and SIR of TAL and TDL on multiple-sequence MRI in T3-staged GBC. Our study pointed out that SI and SIR in TAL were larger than in TDL on T1WI, AP, PP and DP images. The results may be explained by the fact as follows. Kato et al. [28] found that the high SI area on T1WI contained more neutrophils. GBC is often accompanied by gallbladder inflammation [29]. We can speculate that increased T1WI signal in TAL may be related to a tumor-induced immune reaction or related to the cholecystitis that often accompanies GBC, but TDL can be less likely to have the previous involvements, so signal changes in TDL on T1WI may not be obvious. When contrast-enhanced MRI is performed, TAL invaded by GBC has increased capillaries, resulting in increased density of local contrast material. In addition, the contrast material is easy to delayed retention in the fiber matrix of tumor cells and demonstrates delay emptying, resulting in SI and SIR of the TAL higher than those of the TDL on contrast-enhanced AP, PP and DP images [30].

For the first time, this study revealed that SIRAP, SIRPP, SIPP, SIDP and SD1000 could be the independent predictors for quantifying the internal difference between TAL and TDL in T3-staged GBC. The logistic regression model was constructed based on the previous independent indexes for the differentiation. Compared with the individual independent indexes, our ROC analyses showed that the novel model could obtain the best AUC (0.879) and sensitivity (97.7%). The results in the IVC and EVC also show a similar tendency. Moreover, in comparison with the published research [22], the present study improved the AUC from 0.673 to 0.879 and elevated sensitivity from 73.1% to 97.7% for quantifying the internal difference between TAL and TDL in T3-staged GBC. Therefore, we believe that the novel non-invasive model could have the potential to differentiate the internal difference in TAL from that of TDL in T3-staged GBC.

There are several limitations in the present study. Firstly, it was a retrospective analysis and the results need to be verified by prospective study. Secondly, there was actually no direct histopathologically verified correlation between MR findings and histopathology of tumor-adjacent liver tissues. In addition, we did not use hepatobiliary-specific contrast agent to differentiate TAL from TDL in T3-staged GBC. We will conduct the relevant studies in the future.

Conclusions

Our study revealed that SIRAP, SIRPP, SIPP, SIDP and SD1000 could be independent indexes for quantifying the internal difference between TAL and TDL in T3-staged GBC. The model (-4.809 × SIRAP − 2.024 × SIRPP + 0.002 × SIPP − 0.002 × SIDP + 7.294 × SD1000 + 2.137) has been developed, and can be excellent to perform the differentiation with an AUC of more than 0.85. We hope that our model can be helpful for quantitative assessment of morphologically normal cancerous area in TDL in GBC patients.

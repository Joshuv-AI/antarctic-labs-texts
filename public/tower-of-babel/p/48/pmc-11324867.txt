Spatial characteristics and dynamic differences of power industry’s low carbon transition efficiency
Authors: Chaoyong Qin, Yizheng Liang, Yun Cao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The power industry’s low carbon transition is pivotal for achieving carbon reduction and sustainable development. This study uses the super epsilon-based measurement (Super-EBM) model and the Malmquist index to evaluate the power industry’s low carbon transition efficiency using data from 30 provinces in China from 2010 to 2020, and utilizes the Tobit model to comprehensively analyze the factors affecting the low carbon transition of power industry. In addition, this paper examines the spatial differences in the power industry's low carbon transition efficiency as well as its distributional characteristics and dynamic evolutionary patterns. Conclusion is drawn as follows this paper analyzes the regional differences, spatial distribution characteristics and dynamic evolutionary trends of the power industry’s low carbon transition. The main conclusions are as follows: (1) The power industry’s low carbon transition efficiency in China shows an uptrend, with the western China region having the highest overall level of efficiency, greater fluctuations in the central China region, and more stability in the eastern China region, technological progress is a central factor in increasing total factor productivity, the efficiency of the power industry’s low carbon transition is positively influenced by the electricity prices, and negatively influenced by the energy structure, environmental regulations and economic structure; (2) the Intraregional differences and hypervariable density are the main reasons sources of the overall differences in the efficiency of the power industry’s low carbon transition; Intraregional differences in the eastern, central, and western China regions are decreasing year by year, but the efficiency of the power industry’s low carbon transition in the western China region is still distributed in a multipolar way; (3) The dynamic evolutionary trends of the efficiency distribution of the low carbon transition in power industry is influenced by the type of spatial lag in the neighboring area. Where areas with low efficiency makes it difficult to achieve short-term leapfrog development, and areas with a cluster of high-efficiency provinces are prone to “Siphon Effect”. The findings provide a theoretical basis for promoting the efficiency of the power industry's low carbon transition and coordinating the strategic adjustment of economic and environmental green development.

Introduction

The current impediments to sustainable development and imminent threats to humanity's survival primarily stem from environmental pollution and resource scarcity. Reducing carbon emissions and promoting green development has become a global consensus. In 2020, China formally established its “Carbon Peaking and Carbon Neutrality Goals”, that is, accelerating the implementation of the overall strategy to address climate change, strictly controlling the total consumption of energy from fossil fuels, curbing the blind development of large energy-consuming and high-emission industries, and improving the quality of economic development, with a view to reaching the goal of carbon peaking by 2030. All along, China is gradually adjusting the growth rate of economic development, rationally optimizing and adjusting the energy structure, and plans to reach the carbon neutral target by 2060.

Controlling carbon emissions remains a difficult long-term task, and in this context, many studies have predicted that China will complete carbon neutrality by 2050 from scenario analysis, artificial neural networks and other methods1,2. According to data published in the Statistical Bulletin of the People's Republic of China According to the Statistical Communiqué of the People’s Republic of China regarding the 2022 National Economic and Social Development data, China consumed 5.41 billion tons of standard coal in 2022, indicating a year-on-year increase of 2.9%, with power consumption increasing by 3.6% and the consumption of clean energy such as hydroelectricity, nuclear power, natural gas, wind power, and solar power accounting for only 25.9% of total energy consumption. Currently, although the proportion of renewable energy generation is constantly increasing, thermal power generation is still a significant part (the proportion of thermal power generation is still high), making the power industry still the main source of carbon emissions3. In order to carry out the green development model for the power industry, China has carried out years of reforms for the internal and external environment of the power industry. In 2011, China entered the “12th Five-Year Plan”, began the strict implementation of wind power, nuclear power and other clean energy medium and long-term plans, and increased investment in clean energy power generation science and technology, put forward the power supply and grid construction is a key part of power generation and power transmission, emphasized the importance of promoting the construction of the smart grid. During “the 13th Five-Year Plan” period, China accelerated the pace of thermal power transformation and upgrading, gradually eliminated low thermal power backward production capacity, and made significant improvements in the mechanism of the power trading market, by deepening the power system reform and participating in the improvement of the power market system to promote the transformation and development of the power industry. Improving the efficiency of low carbon transformation in power industry has become an urgent task to achieve carbon reduction at present.

China has the highest electricity generation and consumption in the world, while its environmental efficiency is still relatively low4. On the other hand, China is abundant in natural resources and offers vast amounts of space for the development of renewable resources in many regions. Due to the technological constraints of renewable power generation and the incoherence of inter-regional power transmission and resource distribution, the efficiency of the power industry's low carbon transition across the various provinces has also resulted in unbalanced development. Accurate evaluation of the efficiency of power transformation can not only help decision-makers accurately identify the current transformation status of the power industry, but also provide a basis for decision-makers to formulate differentiated policies.

In addition, dynamic evolutionary trends of the spatial and differential efficiency of the low carbon power transition are also important to study. Most studies have ignored the spatial interactions related to the power industry's low carbon transition in provinces and cities, thus perhaps producing inaccurate results when using only efficiency to assess provinces with different regions and neighborhood types. The transition efficiency of the power industry greatly depends on different resource endowments, power generation technologies, and different policies in each province and city. Existing studies have only considered the impacts of different regions and types of neighborhoods in the efficiency assessment, ignoring the spatial differences and the dynamic evolution trends of the transition efficiency, which makes the results of the study incomplete. A proper assessment of the spatial differences in the power industry's low carbon transition efficiency in provinces can provide comprehensive suggestions for decision-makers to formulate policies, which is conducive to finding a better path to reduce emissions in the energy industry, and is of great significance for optimizing the input–output structure of the power industry, improving the carbon liability system, promoting the improvement of intra- and inter-regional competition relations5–7.

To fill above gaps, this study selects provincial power industry data during the implementation of China's “the 12th Five-Year Plan” and “the 13th Five-Year Plan” from 2010 to 2020 for empirical study. The contributions are as follows. First, this study adopts a more accurate super epsilon-based measurement (Super-EBM) to measure the efficiency of the low carbon transition of power, considering radial adjustment and the ordering of effective units. And this paper incorporates clean energy generation and secondary industry GDP into the desired output, which avoids the phenomenon that industrially backward but economically developed provinces lead to inflated efficiency by accepting electricity transmission. The Tobit model is utilized to explore the influencing factors of the efficiency of low carbon transition of power industry in different regions from the dimensions of energy composition and external environment. Second, the spatial distribution and sources of spatial differences in power industry's low carbon transition efficiency in provinces and cities of China are revealed, and takes full account of intra- and inter-regional differences in power industry. Third, the power industry's low carbon transition efficiency is comprehensively considered at the level of dynamic difference evolution and long-term spatial transfer trends. Forecasting the development of China's low-carbon transition in power industry, yielding a decision-making reference to improve regional synergies and realize the sustainability of the power industry’s low-carbon transition. Based on the results of the study, this paper makes recommendations for power industry companies, power market regulators and the government.

Literature review

Measurement methods

The evaluation of system efficiency has been a hot research topic. Frontier analysis is the most common method for assessing efficiency, encompassing both stochastic frontier analysis (SFA) for parametric types and data envelopment analysis (DEA) for non-parametric types8,9.

For measuring efficiency in the energy industry, many scholars have conducted in-depth studies in both of these directions. The strength of SFA is that it incorporates the effect of stochastic factors on output, and it separates real output into a production function, a stochastic component, and technical efficiency. Therefore, many studies have used SFA as the primary method for measuring energy efficiency10–12. However, SFA requires a predefined production function and is more complex in terms of processing multiple outputs. However, the preconditions for measuring efficiency in this method are different from the actual situation in the energy production industry.

The most used of the nonparametric methods is data envelopment analysis (DEA), which compares the relative efficiency of DMUs by constructing a deterministic production frontier. DEA is suitable for analyzing multiple inputs and outputs and does not use predefined production functions. Therefore, the DEA model is more advantageous than the parametric method for measuring efficiency at the industry level. However, the traditional DEA model requires equal proportional changes in inputs and outputs, and as environmental protection has been emphasized, most of the literature adds non-expected outputs to the DEA model to improve the accuracy of the measurements, and it is widely used in measurements of the efficiency of ecology, the environment, energy, and the economy13,14.

To account for possible redundancies and shortages in inputs and outputs and to consider non-expected outputs, Tone extended the DEA to a metric model (SBM) with non-expected outputs that accounts for variable slack15. Whereas in reality there may be a certain percentage of radial adjustment in the measurement of efficiency, The SBM model lacks some of the information about the target versus actual values of inputs and outputs, which leads to the calculated efficiency values being lower than the actual efficiency values, so Tone and Tsutsui proposed the epsilon-based measurement model (EBM) with the inclusion of a hybrid distance function to improve the accuracy of the model16. Ren et al.17 employed the super-efficient EBM model to measure the ecological efficiency of Chinese cities for the first time and analyzed the spatial externality of ecological efficiency and the influencing factors. However, applications of the EBM model in the field of electric power are relatively scarce.

Research on the power industry's low carbon transition efficiency

The efficiency of the power industry's low-carbon transition refers to the relative efficiency of the power industry in each region in achieving structural optimization in terms of inputs and outputs to enable a shift from traditional fossil energy generation to clean energy generation. In recent years, energy and environmental policy literature often uses DEA modeling for research DEA has been widely used in energy and environmental policy research18, and research concerning power has focused on system efficiency evaluations19. Most of the studies focus on thermal power enterprises and aim to analyze technical efficiency, scale efficiency, and the factors affecting the productivity of thermal power enterprises from the perspective of input redundancy20. Färe21 firstly consider pollutants within a data envelopment model for environmental performance analysis. Xiao et al.22 measuring carbon emission efficiency in multiple countries globally using the Super-SBM model while most scholars in China have focused on the macro level. Yan et al.23 measured the carbon emission efficiency of the power generation industry in each province of China using SBM and DEA models. Guo et al.24 first proposed the E-DEA model to measure carbon efficiency from the perspective of potential emissions. However, a paucity of studies has measured the efficiency of regional low carbon transitions of power from the perspective of carbon emissions reductions. At present, regions with higher power generation capacity are likely to discharge more carbon. However, the power grid construction model in China makes it difficult to clarify the environmental responsibilities of different power generation industries in different regions24,25, and it is also difficult to distinguish regional differences in the efficiency of industrial carbon emissions26. To evaluate the efficiency of regional low carbon transitions of power, it is necessary to choose more accurate input–output indicators when measuring the efficiency and to account for interregional and intraregional differences and the existence of dynamic patterns of evolution.

The research on the factors influencing the efficiency of power transition is more extensive, and a part of scholars explore the external environment of the power industry. As the development of China's power market has not yet matured, some studies have found that segmentation of the power market or irrational pricing of power indirectly leads to monopolization and reduced efficiency of resource allocation27,28. Unlike other commodity markets, government intervention in the electricity market has instead contributed to the development of the power industry. Environmental regulations, openness to the outside world, and regional industrial structure also generally have an impact on the power industry’s low carbon transition29. From within the power industry, factors such as energy structure, foreign investment and human capital may also be key factors in improving energy efficiency30. Most of the research tends to consider these factors from a single perspective, either internal or external, and ignores the differences in the extent and direction of their influence in different provinces.

In addition, extensive literature discussing carbon emissions in the power industry has treated DMUs as separate entities, neglecting the spatial interactions among DMUs, the transfer of environmental and economic pressures in the transmission of electricity is also ignored. However, the characteristics of a region are likely to be linked to those of its neighboring regions, and these regional differences and relevance should be taken into account28,31. When studying carbon and various harmful gas emissions, many scholars have considered the impact of adjacent spaces32. For China, improving the power industry's low carbon transition efficiency is crucial for alleviating negative externalities related to energy consumption, technology, and capital flows. However, there are significant differences in power generation technologies, resource endowments, policies and regulations, and economic development in different regions, resulting in different power generation technologies and resource endowments in these regions. The development of power in the eastern, central, and western China regions also presents different characteristics23. The eastern region is the leader of China's industrialization and modernization, while the central and western regions, despite having resource advantages, face serious environmental problems. Regional differences require more targeted policy development33, and provinces are both homogeneous and heterogeneous socially, economically and geographically34,35.

General summary

Although many studies have addressed energy and environmental efficiency, fewer studies have focused on the power industry's low carbon transition and there is a lack of regionally differentiated exploration of internal and external impacts. Additionally, these studies ignored the existence of radial regulation. Finally, competitive cooperation and spatial dependence between neighboring regions are ignored, and there is a dearth of research concerning the spatial characteristics and evolutionary laws of the low carbon transition of power industry.

Methodology

Super-EBM model

Two basic efficiency pathways are categorized in the DEA model as radial and non-radial. However, the radial model neglects the non-radial relaxation in efficiency scoring36; for the non-radial model, it is easy to lose information on the raw radial ratios of inputs to outputs on the efficiency frontier, and the EBM model combines the advantages of both, thus improving the measurement model’s superiority. The super-efficiency model is able to rank the effective DMUs on the frontier. Considering the energy-related and economic benefits of the power industry's low carbon transition and the undesirable outputs of carbon emissions, this study employs the Super-EBM model to measure the efficiency of the low carbon transition of power in each province24.

Evaluation indicators and data sources

Input variables

Considering the input factors of the provincial power industry, combining the current research regarding the measurement of green development efficiency, we select capital, labor, and technology as input factors40. Investment in fixed assets is an important manifestation of the supply of capital to industrial enterprises, reflecting the actual demand, capital adequacy and operating conditions of the region's electric power industry. Fixed-asset investment is used to measure the capital input41. Due to the data limitations of the power industry, “the number of employees in the power, heat and water production and supply industry” in tens of thousands of people is used to measure the labor input7,17. According to China’s many power reform policy preferences, power supply and grid construction are key to the low carbon transition of the power industry. To better fit the research theme, we chose power grid completion investments as technical inputs. Power supply and grid construction represent the construction and operation of the power generation and transmission ends, and are the key technologies of the power industry, so the use of investment in power supply and grid completion as a proxy variable for technology better reflects the degree of investment in technology in the region's power industry. The completed investment in power grids and power supplies is used to measure the technical input42.

Desired output variables

The maturity of the power industry is closely linked to the economic conditions of the region. Data from the National Bureau of Statistics show that the proportion of electricity consumption in the secondary industry in the whole society is always above 60%, and in provinces where the power industry is more mature, the total output value of the secondary industry often occupies more than 50% of GDP, and the contribution of electricity to economic growth can be more accurately reflected by using secondary sector output as the desired output, so we adopt " GDP growth of secondary sector" as the desired output, which is measured in billions of yuan. Therefore, the "GDP growth value of the secondary industry" in billions of yuan is adopted as the desired output43. The core of China's power low carbon transition is to build a renewable energy-led power system. Renewable energy power generation to a certain extent reflects the ability of a region's power to realize self-output, which is an important part of the low-carbon transition process, so we use renewable energy power generation as the desired output.

Undesirable output variables

Total carbon emissions are a key indicator of the extent of clean output in the power industry, so we use total carbon emissions in tons as the undesired output.

Indicators of influencing factors

The power industry's low carbon transition efficiency is closely related not only to the energy structure, but also to the social environment and economic development. Therefore, we comprehensively examine the influencing factors from three aspects: social, economic and energy. The influencing factors are categorized as the equipment upgrading, environmental regulation, energy structure, electricity price, degree of openness to the outside world, economic structure.

Equipment upgrading represents the maturity of the region's power industry and the completeness of its power infrastructure, and its magnitude reflects the region's generating capacity, which is an important internal influence.

Environmental regulations reflect the extent to which the government of a region restricts the polluting output of industry. According to Porter's hypothesis, stringent environmental regulations can induce efficiency and encourage innovation. However, some studies have found that the relationship between mandatory environmental regulations and industrial environmental efficiency is U-shaped40,44, This suggests that environmental regulation may also have an inhibiting effect on the low-carbon transition of the power industry to some extent.

The energy structure reflects the share of coal consumption in total energy and is a visualization of a region's dependence on thermal power40, the larger the proportion, the greater the carbon emissions from power generation, and likewise the greater the resistance to the region's power transition.

Electricity price setting is one of the focuses of China's current policy attention, the fluctuation of electricity price affects the efficiency of resource allocation and energy consumption, but also has the potential to have an impact on the energy monopoly market, part of the study found that the price of electricity affects the restructuring of the power industry28.

The degree of regional openness to the outside world affects the sharing of new technologies in the power industry, and economic openness facilitates the redistribution of factors of production across regions, ultimately affecting the transformation of the power industry45.

As the economic development of most provinces relies heavily on secondary industries, which are mainly energy-intensive, changes in the economic structure will have a greater impact on electricity consumption and the efficiency of energy consumption46.

The application of ML index compensates for the disadvantage that the efficiency values calculated via the EBM model do not reflect the state of efficiency changes. As is evident based on the data in Table 4:

First, mean value of the ML index for the national region is 2.04, indicating an overall improvement in the efficiency of large-scale inputs to power generation in all provinces across the country. The eastern region of the country has the highest mean value for the ML index, followed by the central part, and it is lowest in the western part of the country. The reason for this phenomenon can be seen from EC and TC. The eastern region is ahead of the central and western regions both in terms of technological efficiency and technological progress, and due to its leading economic development, the eastern region possesses a higher scale of technological inputs and a more advanced technological management experience, and it is also the preferred demonstration area for the industrialization of new energy. The ML indexes of Henan, Guangxi, Guizhou, Yunnan, Qinghai, and Xinjiang are slightly lower than 1, indicating a declining trend in total factor productivity. In most of the remaining provinces, total factor productivity is characterized by a sustained growth trend.

Second, Eastern region's technical efficiency EC is higher than the national average, while Central and Western region's EC is slightly lower than the national average, but both show an increasing trend. The EC of Jiangxi, Hunan, and Henan is less than 1, and displays a decreasing trend, while the EC of the remaining 27 provinces and cities either grow or are unchanged. This suggests that there are still a few provinces in China that do not realize the full use of input factors under the existing technical level, and the technical management experience is not sufficiently advanced to maximize the efficiency of the input factors. At the same time, it also indicates that these provinces and cities have potential for technological improvement, which can be reasonably arranged in terms of factor input scale, thereby improving overall technological efficiency.

Third, the national TC is greater than the EC, indicating that technological advances are the core factor increasing the total factor productivity. The TC of Beijing, Guangdong, Hainan, Yunnan, Shaanxi, Qinghai, and Xinjiang is less than 1, suggests that the decline in technical progress has inhibited the increase in total factor productivity in these provinces. Although China’s technological innovation in the power system has been effective, but a problem persists regarding the excessively large gap between technological progress in the east, center, and west. Therefore, the power industry in all provinces should endeavor to improve its innovation capacity and try to move away from reliance on traditional technologies as a way to improve the clean energy output of the power industry.

The negative impact of environmental regulation on the power industry's low carbon transition efficiency in the western regions is at the 1% level of significance. This suggests that immature environmental regulations still exist in the western regions, which can pose challenges to the power industry to a certain extent, such as excessive investment in new energy sources, continued growth in the cost of coal power, and corporate losses, compared to the eastern region, where environmental regulations are more suitable for the development trend of the region's power industry.

The significance level of the positive effect of electricity price on the efficiency of low carbon transition of power industry is 10%, which is particularly significant in the central region and the eastern region. Due to the special characteristics of the electricity product itself, such as electricity supply and demand must be balanced in real time, large-scale economic energy storage is not possible, and electricity demand is inelastic. The rationalization of electricity price also affects the balance of electricity supply and demand and the allocation of energy, which in turn promotes the power industry's low carbon transition. Provinces in the central region and the eastern region do not have frequent electricity transactions, and the market mechanism has not yet been perfected to form a fair and reasonable market price system, thus requiring the government to take the necessary measures to establish an independent transmission and distribution tariff system with clear rules and scientific transparency.

At the 5% significance level, energy structure has a negative impact, especially in the western and eastern regions. For the western region, industrial development is the main driver of the economy, and in order to promote economic development with lower power generation costs, industrially developed provinces have increased the share of coal as an energy source in industry, which increases carbon emissions and reduces the power industry's low carbon transition efficiency. For the eastern region, most provinces have serious power capacity deficits and need other provinces for power transmission, while clean energy power generation is prone to seasonal power shortages, and in order to make up for power supply deficits, the eastern provinces also engage in large-scale thermal power generation.

At the 5% significance level, economic structure has a negative impact on the low carbon transition of the national power industry, especially in the eastern region. This suggests that an increase in the value added of the secondary industry as a proportion of GDP will lead to an increase in total employment, fixed asset investment and total energy consumption, while for the eastern region, the relatively immature power industry and new clean energy sources that have not yet been fully exploited will lead to redundant investment in science and technology, as well as greater environmental burdens arising from its own excessive energy consumption in the absence of resource endowments. However, with the implementation of economic restructuring policies, the economic development of the western and central regions will gradually shift from reliance on energy- and pollution-intensive industries to energy-saving and high-value-added industries, and the negative effects of the expansion of the secondary sector will be effectively controlled.

The interregional efficiency differences between the eastern and other regions show a fluctuating downward trend. The kernel density curve two-dimensional map is constructed by selecting the efficiency for the national region and the eastern, central and western regions from 2010 to 2020.

The overall dynamic evolution of the power industry's low carbon transition efficiency in China is shown in Fig. 4a, which indicates that the main peak's position shifted to the left and then shifted to the right, suggesting that the total efficiency from 2010 to 2014 and 2014 to 2020 experienced an evolutionary trend of “decreasing first and then increasing.” In the sample period, the overall curve displays a long-term bimodal pattern, but the side peaks have lower peaks, indicating that the polarization phenomenon of the national low carbon transition of power industry is not obvious. The above results suggest that between 2010 and 2020, the low carbon transition efficiency of the power industry exists with a high probability of maintaining the original level, indicating that areas with low efficiency in power industry's low carbon transition are difficult to break through the bottleneck period of efficiency improvement. Based on the diagonal and non-diagonal probability comparison, the Markov chain probability transfer matrix for the diagonal matrix elements is substantially larger than the non-diagonal matrix elements, and the probability of the type of transfer occurring between adjacent levels is much larger than the probability of cross-level transfers, indicating that substantial increases in the energy-related and technological constraints make it difficult to achieve short-term progress.

As shown in the spatial Markov chain probability transfer matrix, a clear "club convergence" phenomenon exists in the power industry's low carbon transition. As can be seen in Table 4, in type I, II, and III, the transfer probability on the main diagonal of each region is greater than that on the non-main diagonal, indicating that the probability of the efficiency being at each level is relatively stable. In addition, when a neighboring province is at an inefficient level, the probability of that province's efficiency decreasing is greater. However, when the efficiency of neighboring provinces is at a high level, the regions at the low level of the low carbon transition have a high probability of improving to the medium–low level, meaning that low-level regions can utilize the positive spillover effects of high-level regions to break through their own efficiency bottlenecks. However, when provinces with high efficiency levels are clustered together, there is a "siphon effect" in the clustered areas, whereby some of the provinces with higher efficiency in the low carbon transition attract technology and investment within the region where the efficiency levels are clustered, leading to the phenomenon of “single nucleus”. Conversely, for regions with moderately low and moderately high efficiency levels, an upward shift in probability is more likely to occur if they are near provinces with higher efficiency in electricity low carbon transition.

Conclusions and policy implications

Conclusions

This paper has measured the power low carbon transition efficiency of 30 provinces in China from 2010 to 2020 and analyzed the internal and external factors affecting efficiency using the Tobit model. The paper uses the Gini coefficient, a kernel density estimation, and a Markov chain analysis to explore the spatial patterns and evolutionary trends of the power industry's low carbon transition efficiency. The conclusions we have drawn are as follows:The overall power industry's low carbon transition efficiency in the country had improved over time. Most of the provinces and cities had smaller increases in efficiency, and a few provinces and cities had exhibited a downward trend in efficiency. Provinces in the western region have the highest efficiencies and a steady upward trend in efficiencies; central provinces fluctuated more, and eastern provinces were more stable. From a spatial distribution perspective, there was a concentration of high-efficiency provinces in the western region, and the efficiency of provinces in the northeastern region was both “extremely good” and “extremely bad”. In terms of index decomposition, the efficiency of large-scale power generation inputs has improved in all provinces across the country as a whole. The technical efficiency of the eastern region exceeds the national average, while that of the central and western regions did not reach the national average, but all of them are on an upward trend. Technological progress is the core factor for improving total factor productivity.In terms of the factors influencing efficiency, the government and the market play an essential role in the transformation of the electric power industry, and the setting of electricity prices and the strength of environmental regulation are important external factors influencing efficiency, with a particularly pronounced role in the central and western regions. The proportion of renewable energy utilization and industrial energy structure are internal factors affecting efficiency, and are also constraints on efficiency improvement in the western and eastern regions.In terms of overall trends, overall regional differences declined from 2010 to 2016, and spatial imbalances in efficiency across provinces and cities remained significant. Intra-regional differences are largest in the east and smallest in the west; inter-regional differences are largest in the east-central region and smallest in the central and western regions. Inter-regional differences and density overshoots are the main sources of efficiency differences. The efficiency differences within the three regions have narrowed slightly, but the degree of synergistic efficiency remains low in the west. It is difficult to improve efficiency in China, and it is difficult to realize leapfrog progress in the short term under the constraints of energy and technological conditions. Regions with a concentration of high-efficiency provinces are prone to a "siphon effect", whereby some provinces in the region attract technology and investment, leading to a shift in the efficiency.

Policy implications

The above results reveal the spatial differences and dynamic evolution law of the power industry's low carbon transition efficiency. To promote the overall improvement of such efficiency and to reduce regional differences, combined with the conclusions of the study, the following recommendations are proposed in favor of the development of the power's low carbon transition:The proportion of renewable energy and the energy structure of industries within the region are internal factors affecting the efficiency of the low carbon transition of the power industry. Specifically, the electric power industry in the western and eastern regions should emphasize the proportion of renewable energy inputs, and industrial enterprises in the region should rationally adjust the energy structure and reduce the amount of coal. At the same time, in the seasonal shortage of renewable energy should do a good job of preventive measures, in the seasonal peak period to prevent thermal power overcapacity, do a good job of energy reserves. Gradually reduce investment in thermal power infrastructure and increase investment in research and new energy generation technology.Rationalization of electricity tariffs and environmental regulations are external factors that influence the efficiency of the low-carbon transition in electricity. The Government should guide the diversification of the energy industry and the marketization of renewable energy on the premise of improving the electricity market system, while increasing investment in technological innovation and stimulating the development of renewable energy. Tariff-setting authorities should work with the market to adjust tariff fluctuations and effectively adjust demand and supply. The environmental management department should adjust the corresponding regulation efforts, and try to avoid the vicious increase of energy costs and the reduction of power generation efficiency in economically backward provinces due to environmental regulation.The differences between provinces and the spatial spillover effect cannot be ignored. In response to interregional barriers to technology exchanges and investment transactions, the Government has strengthened joint-venture interventions, broken market segmentation and accelerated regionally synergized power system reform. In low-efficiency provinces, it is necessary to prevent the migration of carbon emissions and other pollution through the functional division of labor between neighboring cities, improve ecological efficiency and synergy, optimize the power deployment between power-scarce regions and regions with excess power capacity, and improve the power supply-side compensation mechanism to reduce the economic and environmental pressure on the corresponding provinces. High-efficiency provinces should accelerate the construction of smart grids, strengthen technical and economic cooperation with low-efficiency regions, integrate domestic supply chains for innovative energy technologies, create an open domestic market, and strive to achieve a balance between electricity supply and demand.

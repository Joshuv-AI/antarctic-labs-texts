Estimated glomerular filtration rate discordance and cardiopulmonary morbidity in sickle cell disease
Authors: Kabir O Olaniran, Alecia C Nero, Robert D Toto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Key Points•Adults with SCD exhibit greater eGFR discordance than adults without SCD.•This eGFR discordance is strongly associated with prevalent cardiopulmonary morbidity in SCD.

Introduction

Sickle cell disease (SCD) is associated with severe cardiopulmonary morbidity and early mortality partly because of accelerated rates of kidney function decline, which results in a 2.5 times higher relative risk of incident chronic kidney disease (CKD) than the general population without SCD.1, 2, 3, 4, 5 Accurate staging of CKD currently relies on estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) using serum creatinine (eGFRcr) and/or cystatin C (eGFRcys).6 However, in SCD, both GFR estimating equations exhibit significant bias and suboptimal accuracy.7, 8, 9, 10 This is important because CKD is a cardiovascular risk–enhancing comorbidity, and inaccuracies in CKD staging can misrepresent systemic cardiovascular risk.11,12

The unique pathophysiology of SCD influences non-GFR determinants that can systematically bias eGFRcr and eGFRcys in opposite directions.7, 8, 9, 10,13 eGFRcr is prone to overestimating true GFR in SCD for 2 primary reasons. First, glomerular hyperfiltration and increased proximal tubular secretion of creatinine are well described in SCD, and both significantly lower serum creatinine.13,14 Second, creatinine is derived from muscle, and relative sarcopenia is well described in SCD as a consequence of chronic inflammation, poor exercise tolerance, and recurrent ischemia-reperfusion injury.15 By contrast, eGFRcys often underestimates GFR in SCD.8,10 Cystatin C is a nonglycosylated cysteine protease inhibitor produced at a constant rate by all nucleated cells and freely filtered by the glomerulus.16 Cystatin C production is noted to be higher in conditions that are underpinned by chronic inflammation.17, 18, 19 SCD is a chronic inflammatory disease driven by neutrophil activation and proinflammatory cytokines.20,21

Rather than representing measurement error, prospective data from multiple large non-SCD cohorts have demonstrated that the direction and magnitude of eGFR discordance are powerful and independent predictors of adverse cardiovascular outcomes, such as heart failure (HF), stroke, and all-cause mortality.17,22,23 Therefore, eGFR discordance may serve as a biomarker for cardiovascular risk evaluation.24,25 This is particularly important in SCD, where cardiopulmonary diseases, particularly HF, pulmonary hypertension (pHTN), and stroke, are leading causes of mortality in adults with SCD and occur at a much younger age compared to the general population.3,26,27 The clinical implications of the eGFR discordance observed in GFR measurement studies of patients with SCD have not been examined in a larger population with SCD, nor has this discordance been compared to that in a population without SCD.

Therefore, the objectives of this investigation were as follows: (1) quantify the magnitude and direction of eGFR discordance in SCD compared with a propensity-weighted non-SCD cohort, (2) determine the cross-sectional association between high-risk eGFR discordance and prevalent cardiopulmonary morbidities in SCD, (3) explore associations between the magnitude of eGFR discordance and clinical factors in SCD,13,21,28, 29, 30 and (4) explore trends of eGFR discordance across kidney function strata.

Methods

Study population and data sources

We conducted a cross-sectional analysis of adults aged ≥18 years evaluated at the University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center (UTSW) and Parkland Health and Hospital System (PHHS) from 1 January 2020 to 30 June 2025. Patients requiring maintenance dialysis or with a prior kidney transplantation were excluded. The analytic cohort was derived from 3 integrated institutional data sources: comprehensive outpatient electronic health records from the UTSW and PHHS clinical systems; the UTSW Sickle Cell Kidney Clinical Registry, which encompasses patients with SCD referred for specialized nephrology evaluation; and the UTSW Sickle Cell Kidney Study (NCT07064174), a prospective natural history biorepository wherein participants undergo standardized baseline renal phenotyping, including simultaneous serum creatinine and cystatin C quantification. All laboratory values and clinical encounter data for both the SCD and non-SCD cohorts were restricted exclusively to outpatient encounters.

Derivation of SCD and control cohorts

The primary exposure group was defined as adult patients with SCD previously identified by hemoglobin electrophoresis. All eligible patients with SCD self-identified as Black. To create a comparable non-SCD cohort (reference population), we first restricted the electronic health record query to individuals who self-reported as Black without an encounter diagnosis code for SCD or sickle cell trait. This rigorous restriction was implemented a priori to minimize unmeasured confounding from genetic determinants of CKD that are endemic in populations of African descent, such as Apolipoprotein L1 (APOL1) high-risk variants,31,32 which were not measured. This cohort was not matched on other demographic or clinical characteristics at the time of data extraction. Baseline was determined by index date, which was defined as the first available simultaneous creatinine and cystatin C test on or after 1 January 2020. After application of clinical and laboratory inclusion criteria (Figure 1), the final analytic cohort comprised 1099 Black adults (223 with SCD and 876 controls without SCD). After excluding patients with SCD who are on transfusion therapies and/or lacking same-day complete blood count with differentials with index creatinine/cystatin C, the SCD sensitivity analysis cohort included 110 patients.

Derivation of the analytic cohorts. The SCD and non-SCD cohorts were assembled in parallel before application of laboratory inclusion criteria.

eGFR discordance definitions

Baseline eGFR was calculated using the race-free 2021 Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration (CKD-EPI) creatinine equation and the 2012 CKD-EPI cystatin C equation.6 All serum creatinine measurements were performed using a quantitative enzymatic assay standardized to isotope dilution mass spectrometry at the UTSW/PHHS central laboratories. Cystatin C was quantified via immunoturbidimetry at the UTSW/PHHS central laboratories. eGFR discordance was defined as the percentage difference between the 2 estimates: [(eGFRcys − eGFRcr)/eGFRcr] × 100, which is a standardized metric accounting for wide and heterogeneous ranges of eGFRcr.17,22,23,33,34 We also evaluated a high-risk eGFR discordance phenotype, defined as an eGFRcys value ≥30% lower than the simultaneous eGFRcr. This specific threshold has been robustly associated with adverse cardiovascular outcomes and all-cause mortality in populations without SCD.22,23,33,35

Cardiopulmonary disease definitions and validation

To determine the clinical implications of eGFR discordance within the SCD cohort, we evaluated its association with prevalent cardiopulmonary morbidities commonly seen in SCD, specifically HF, pHTN, left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH), and stroke.29,36, 37, 38 Coronary artery disease (CAD) was included because of its associations with eGFR discordance in non-SCD.

Diagnoses of HF, pHTN, stroke, and CAD were initially identified using outpatient encounter codes for SCD and non-SCD in the International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision.

In the SCD cohort only, all cardiopulmonary diagnoses were independently validated through systematic chart review by the first author. Validation of HF required at least 1 of 3 criteria: (1) reduced or mildly reduced left ventricular ejection fraction (<50% on echocardiography), (2) preserved left ventricular ejection fraction (≥50%) with grade ≥2 diastolic dysfunction and/or left atrial volume index >34 mL/m2 on echocardiography, or (3) explicit HF diagnosis documented by a cardiologist or hematologist with at least 2 clinical markers (prescription for cardiac indication of loop diuretics and/or natriuretic peptide above threshold) in the absence of disqualifying echocardiographic data (supplemental Table 1).39,40

pHTN validation was characterized as (1) confirmed (right heart catheterization with mean pulmonary arterial pressure >20 mmHg), (2) presumed by echocardiogram (right ventricular systolic pressure ≥35 mmHg and/or tricuspid regurgitant velocity ≥2.5 m/s) with at least 1 clinical marker (defined as presumed pHTN diagnosis documented by a pulmonologist, cardiologist, or hematologist and/or prescription of pHTN-specific pharmacotherapy), or (3) presumed by radiographic imaging (pulmonary artery diameter >29 mm) with at least 1 clinical marker (supplemental Table 2).41, 42, 43

CAD was validated using documented left heart catheterization or coronary calcification on computed tomography.44 LVH was defined by sex-specific left ventricular mass index thresholds on echocardiography (>95 g/m2 in females; >115 g/m2 in males) using echocardiograms performed within a 2-year window (±1 year) of index biomarker assessment.45, 46, 47 Stroke diagnoses were confirmed as overt clinical stroke with documentation by a hematologist and/or neurologist.

Clinical and laboratory covariates

All demographic, clinical, and laboratory covariates were derived exclusively from outpatient encounters. Demographic characteristics (age, sex, and smoking status), vital signs (blood pressure and body mass index [BMI]), and active pharmacological treatments (renin-angiotensin system inhibitors [RASi], sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors [SGLT2i], and SCD-modifying therapies) were verified through manual chart review. Active medications were defined as those prescribed or refilled within 3 months of the index date. Baseline comorbidities, including diabetes mellitus (DM), were ascertained using the established encounter codes in the International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision. Laboratory covariates in SCD (lactate dehydrogenase [LDH], hemoglobin, absolute neutrophil count [ANC], serum ferritin, and absolute reticulocyte count [ARC]) were extracted from electronic health records using the measurement closest to the index date within a strict 3-month window (± 3 months). Hemoglobin and ANC were derived from complete blood count panels and were therefore measured on the same day. LDH, ferritin, and ARC were ordered together less frequently and with greater temporal variability from the index date.

Covariate balance and overlap weighting

To rigorously account for baseline covariate imbalances between the SCD and non-SCD control cohorts, we used propensity score overlap weighting, which emphasizes the subpopulation with the greatest clinical equipoise by mitigating extreme propensity scores.48,49 Propensity scores (conditional probability of a patient having SCD) were estimated using a multivariable logistic regression model incorporating clinically relevant baseline variables: age, sex, BMI, systolic and diastolic blood pressure, smoking status, DM, HF, stroke, CAD, and eGFRcr. We explicitly included eGFRcr in the propensity score model to ensure that comparative analyses of eGFR discordance were evaluated across equivalent strata of baseline renal function between the SCD and non-SCD cohorts. Covariate balance was assessed using standardized mean differences (SMD), with SMD <0.10 established a priori as the balance threshold. Propensity score estimation and weighting were performed in RStudio, version 2026.01.0+392.

Statistical analyses

Baseline clinical characteristics were summarized using means with standard deviations for continuous parametric variables, medians with interquartile ranges (IQR) for nonparametric variables, and percentages for categorical variables.

To compare the magnitude of eGFR discordance between the SCD and non-SCD cohorts, we performed unadjusted and adjusted quantile regression (estimated at the 50th percentile) to calculate adjusted median differences for continuous eGFR discordance. Unadjusted and adjusted logistic regression was used to calculate odds ratio (OR) for the binary high-risk eGFR discordance phenotype. Both models achieved double robustness by incorporating propensity score overlap weights alongside direct adjustment for baseline covariates: age, sex, smoking status, BMI, systolic and diastolic blood pressure, DM, HF, stroke, CAD, RASi, and SGLT2i. Robust sandwich variance estimators were applied to all weighted models to accurately calculate standard errors and 95% confidence interval (CI).

Within the SCD cohort, cross-sectional associations between eGFR discordance (as continuous variable and binary high-risk eGFR discordance) and prevalent cardiopulmonary morbidities were evaluated using unadjusted and adjusted logistic regression models. To prevent overfitting,50 given the low frequency of most prevalent conditions, the adjusted models were restricted a priori to 3 fundamental biological and demographic drivers of sickle cell cardiopulmonary disease: age, sex, and hemoglobin. Sensitivity analysis replacing hemoglobin with hydroxyurea was performed.

In exploratory analyses, Spearman correlation coefficient was used to assess the continuous relationship between eGFR discordance and established biomarkers of hemolysis (hemoglobin, LDH, ARC)51 and inflammation (ANC, ferritin)20,52 in SCD.

To explore the clinical factors associated with eGFR discordance in SCD, we performed a prespecified multivariable median regression analysis using the complete SCD analytic cohort (n = 223). Covariates were selected a priori based on 3 frameworks: (1) non-GFR determinants of cystatin C and creatinine (including age, sex, BMI, and systolic and diastolic blood pressure); (2) predictors of eGFR discordance established in the non-SCD cohort, including DM and smoking status23; and (3) SCD-specific clinical and biological factors hypothesized to influence hemolytic burden, systemic inflammation, or creatinine generation, including SCD genotype, pHTN, HF, stroke, hydroxyurea use, transfusion therapies, hemoglobin, log-transformed ANC, and log-transformed urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio. RASi and SGLT2i were excluded a priori given the lack of hypothetical mechanisms linking either drug to modification of non-GFR determinants of creatinine and cystatin C in SCD. Results are presented as unadjusted coefficients from separate univariable models and as fully adjusted coefficients from the multivariable model, expressed with 95% CI.

Prespecified sensitivity analyses were performed in a cohort restricted to same-day eGFRcr, eGFRcys, hemoglobin, and ANC and excluding transfused patients (n = 110; Figure 1) to replicate analyses of the association between discordance and cardiopulmonary outcomes and between clinical factors and discordance.

Conventional maximum likelihood and asymptotic standard errors were used for all unweighted logistic and quantile regression models given the finite-sample limitations of sandwich estimators in this cohort.

Finally, a nonparametric Jonckheere-Terpstra test was used to evaluate trends in eGFR discordance across descending eGFRcr strata of baseline renal function, categorized according to established categories in Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes.53

All analyses were conducted using STATA 19 (StataCorp, College Station, TX). Two-sided P < .05 was considered statistically significant.

Ethical considerations

The study protocol was approved by the institutional review boards at UTSW and PHHS. Written informed consent was obtained from all prospective Sickle Cell Kidney Study participants (STU-2022-1024). A waiver of informed consent was granted for retrospective extraction of electronic health record and registry data because of the minimal-risk nature of the research (STU-2021-0097).

Results

Baseline characteristics

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics before and after propensity score overlap weighting are shown in Table 1. In the overall cohort (N = 1099), mean age of all patients was 60 ± 17 years, mean eGFRcr was 61 ± 34 mL/min per 1.73 m2, and mean eGFRcys was 51 ± 31 mL/min per 1.73 m2. Before weighting, patients with SCD were younger (41 ± 13 vs 61 ± 16 years) and had lower blood pressure, BMI, prevalent DM, and prevalent cardiopulmonary disease but higher prevalent RASi and SGLT2i compared to patients without SCD. Eighty-three percent of all patients with SCD were on at least 1 SCD-modifying therapy, predominantly hydroxyurea. Both eGFRcr and eGFRcys were higher in the SCD cohort than in the non-SCD cohort. After overlap weighting, SMD for all covariates was <0.10, indicating excellent balance between the SCD and non-SCD cohorts on demographics, comorbidities, medications, and eGFRcr. A strong, significant correlation was observed between eGFRcr and eGFRcys (Figure 2) in both the non-SCD cohort (r = 0.79; P < .01) and SCD cohort (r = 0.85, P < .01).

eGFR discordance in SCD compared to non-SCD

Magnitude of eGFR discordance was significantly more pronounced in patients with SCD compared to the non-SCD control group (Table 2). After overlap weighting, median eGFR discordance was −25% (IQR, −38.9 to −11.0) in the SCD cohort, compared to −12.2% (IQR, −34.9 to +5.5) in the non-SCD cohort. High-risk eGFR discordance (eGFRcys ≥30% lower than simultaneous eGFRcr) was observed in 42% of the SCD cohort vs 30% of the non-SCD cohort.

Clinical factors associated with eGFR discordance in SCD

Results are shown in supplemental Table 5. In univariable analysis, the variables most strongly associated with eGFR discordance were HF, pHTN, and hemoglobin. In the fully adjusted multivariable model, only HF (adjusted median difference, −14.83; 95% CI, −25.02 to −4.64) and hemoglobin (adjusted median difference, +2.42; 95% CI, +0.72 to +4.13) remained significantly associated. After adjustment, hydroxyurea was associated with a nonsignificant positive trend, whereas pHTN was confounded by HF and became nonsignificant. Genotype (HBSS/SΒ0 vs other) was not independently associated with discordance after full adjustment. Age, sex, BMI, DM, and smoking status (principal drivers of discordance in general population cohorts) were not independently associated with discordance after full adjustment.

SCD sensitivity cohort analysis (same-day lab values, excluding transfusion therapies; supplemental Table 6) showed similar results to the main analysis after adjustment. However, there was 1 notable exception: stroke (n = 11) became significantly associated with positive discordance because of both survivorship bias (10 patients with SCD with prior stroke, remote chronic exchange transfusions, and hydroxyurea maintenance with preserved eGFR) and reversal of discordance from negative to positive in 1 patient with SCD with advanced CKD (as shown in Figure 4).

Trend of eGFR discordance across kidney function categories

In the SCD cohort, eGFR discordance exhibited a statistically significant trend across descending categories of baseline creatinine eGFR (Figure 4; P for trend = .01). Across baseline creatinine eGFR strata from >90 down to 15 to 29 mL/min per 1.73 m2, median relative eGFR discordance became progressively more negative. However, this trajectory began to reverse within the lowest strata of renal function (<30 mL/min per 1.73 m2), where median discordance became progressively less negative and ultimately crossed to a positive value in the <15 mL/min per 1.73 m2 stratum, suggesting that cystatin C–based eGFR exceeded creatinine-based eGFR in a subset of patients with the most advanced renal impairment.

In contrast, the non-SCD cohort demonstrated no significant trend in eGFR discordance as baseline creatinine eGFR diminished (Figure 4; P for trend = .78).

Discussion

We report, to our knowledge, the first analysis to characterize the prevalence and clinical significance of eGFR discordance in adults with SCD. The principal finding of this study is that eGFR discordance is significantly more pronounced in adults with SCD than in those without SCD who self-identified as Black (control). This occurred despite the SCD cohort being substantially younger and having lower BMI, which are key demographics associated with less pronounced discordance in the non-SCD cohort.22,23,33 These findings highlight a critical limitation in relying solely on eGFRcr in SCD. In SCD, the trifecta of glomerular hyperfiltration, augmented proximal tubular secretion of creatinine, and chronic inflammation-induced sarcopenia combines to exaggerate eGFRcr relative to true GFR, whereas simultaneous non-GFR determinants of cystatin C production may act in the opposing direction, yielding the observed pronounced negative discordance.14,15,54 Of note, CKD-EPI eGFRcr and eGFRcys equations were derived and validated in populations without SCD,55 and their accuracy in SCD remains an area of active investigation.8,10 Although the correlation between eGFRcr and eGFRcys was higher in the SCD cohort (Figure 2), this reflects tighter cotracking across a broader eGFRcr range in SCD (Table 1) rather than closer agreement in absolute values. The non-SCD cohort was concentrated in the moderate-to-severe CKD range, where the compressed GFR range limits the variance available for the 2 equations to covary, yielding a lower correlation coefficient. To our knowledge, this is the first and largest study of eGFR discordance and cardiopulmonary outcomes in SCD.

High-risk eGFR discordance (eGFRcys ≥30% lower than simultaneous eGFRcr) was significantly more prevalent in the SCD cohort compared to the non-SCD cohort (Table 2). This threshold is identical to that used in a landmark meta-analysis that established high-risk discordance as a robust independent predictor of all-cause mortality, cardiovascular mortality, HF, atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, and kidney failure across >860 000 participants in 25 international cohorts.23 That our significantly younger and leaner SCD cohort exhibited a substantially greater prevalence and magnitude of negative discordance despite these demographic characteristics being associated with less discordance in the general population23,33 is an important, paradoxical finding of this study. Importantly, this discordance does not represent a mathematical artifact, as evidenced by the strong and independent association of eGFR discordance with prevalent HF and pHTN (Table 3; supplemental Tables 3 and 4) in the SCD cohort, both of which are well-described drivers of mortality in adult SCD.3,28,56

Although GFR discordance is a strong predictor of cardiovascular risk in the general population,17,22,23,33,57, 58, 59 it is hypothesized that this predictive association is due in part to systemic inflammation,60,61 a critical non-GFR determinant of cystatin C production.22,62 This is particularly relevant in SCD, which has long been recognized as a chronic inflammatory condition driven by continuous hemolysis, endothelial activation, and neutrophil-mediated cytokine release.20,21 Evidence from large non-SCD cohorts demonstrates that inflammatory mediators are strongly associated with elevated cystatin C levels independent of measured GFR while simultaneously driving higher eGFRcr through sarcopenia and glomerular hyperfiltration.18,19,61,63, 64, 65, 66 Our findings indirectly support this mechanism: eGFR discordance correlated significantly, albeit weakly, with routine clinical markers of hemolytic activity (hemoglobin, ARC, and LDH) and inflammatory activity (ANC and ferritin) in the SCD cohort (Figure 3). However, anemia-mediated hyperdynamic circulation may contribute independently to glomerular hyperfiltration and upward bias of eGFRcr in SCD,13,14,38 consistent with hemoglobin’s independent association with discordance and its partial attenuation of cardiopulmonary-discordance associations in this cohort. These observations are consistent with, though do not confirm, an anemia-, hemolysis-, and inflammation-driven mechanism of discordance in SCD and warrant prospective investigation.

A prespecified analysis of clinical factors associated with discordance (supplemental Table 5) corroborated the preceding findings: HF was the strongest independent associate of more negative discordance, and higher hemoglobin was independently associated with less negative discordance. Hydroxyurea showed a nonsignificant trend toward attenuation. General population discordance drivers (age, sex, BMI, diabetes, and smoking) were not independently associated, reinforcing the disease-specific nature of discordance in SCD.23,33

In addition, we observed a significant trend wherein eGFR discordance became progressively more negative across descending categories of baseline creatinine eGFR (P for trend = .01; Figure 4). This trajectory appeared to reverse toward the lowest strata, a pattern that was absent in the non-SCD control cohort (P for trend = .78). We hypothesize that progressive loss of tubular mass and compensatory hyperfiltration in advanced SCD CKD narrow the gap and, in SCD, eventually reverse the association between estimating equations.67,68 This hypothesis is highly speculative, given the small number of participants in the <30 mL/min per 1.73 m2 strata. Notably, the non-SCD control cohort demonstrated no significant trend across any stratum of baseline renal function, further highlighting the disease-specific nature of this discordance pattern in SCD. Larger SCD cohort studies are needed to better understand this trend.

Despite the strengths of this study, several limitations merit consideration. First, the cross-sectional design precludes the establishment of temporal or causal relationships between eGFR discordance and cardiopulmonary morbidity. Second, cystatin C testing in routine clinical practice is nonrandom and may disproportionately represent those with clinically suspected CKD or greater disease severity, introducing selection bias in both cohorts. Third, the UTSW/PHHS clinics serve as a quaternary referral center for SCD; therefore, the SCD cohort may overrepresent severe disease phenotypes (irrespective of SCD genotype), potentially limiting generalizability. Fourth, mean age of the SCD cohort reflects an older adult population with SCD, given a US median SCD survival of 50 years.69 This further limits the generalizability of our findings to younger adults and children with SCD. Fifth, the lack of direct GFR and cytokine measurements limits the mechanistic assessment of non-GFR determinants in this cohort. Finally, mortality data were insufficient to model survival outcomes.

In conclusion, eGFR discordance is significantly more pronounced in adults with SCD than in controls, despite the SCD cohort being younger and leaner, a demographic paradox that highlights the limitations of creatinine-based eGFR as a sole measure of kidney function in this disease. Within the SCD cohort, high-risk discordance was independently associated with HF and pHTN, demonstrating that the observed discordance is not a mathematical artifact of the estimating equations but carries genuine clinical significance in this population. Multivariable analysis identified HF and hemoglobin as independent clinical factors associated with discordance, the latter pointing to hemolysis as a potential mechanistic contributor. Our findings suggest that routine cystatin C measurement alongside creatinine in adults with SCD warrants prospective evaluation because eGFRcys has been shown to improve cardiovascular risk classification beyond eGFRcr in general population cohorts.25,70,71 Prospective studies incorporating measured GFR and inflammatory biomarkers are needed to validate eGFR discordance as a predictive marker for incident cardiopulmonary events and mortality in SCD and to clarify its underlying hemolytic and inflammatory mechanisms.

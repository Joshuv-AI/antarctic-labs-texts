1 - A / O Three-dimension evaluation of mandibular canal location: a retrospective study

Jéssica Feliciano, Iman Bugaighis, Joana Mendes Borga, Luís Proença, Pedro Mariano Pereira

Instituto Universitário Egas Moniz, Almada, Portugal

Correspondence: Jéssica Feliciano, jessicafeliciano0@gmail.com

BMC Proceedings 2025, 19(21): 1 - A / O

Introduction: Orthodontics continuously faces the challenge of developing and implementing new techniques, materials, and approaches to enhance the effectiveness of treatments. The use of micro-implants as temporary skeletal anchorage devices has become increasingly prevalent in modern orthodontics. However, placing micro-implants in the mandibular platform presents specific challenges, mainly due to the proximity to the mandibular canal. Previous studies have not evaluated the optimal insertion angle that avoids the risk of interference with the mandibular canal. Therefore, this retrospective study aims to address this gap in the literature.

Objectives: The objective of the study was to determine the safest location on the mandibular platform for micro-implant insertion without interfering with the mandibular canal, considering the insertion angle and micro-implant length. Additionally, it aimed to assess the influence of age and sex on the location.

Materials and methods: The sample was derived from an analysis of clinical records of patients who underwent Cone Beam Computed Tomography (CBCT) at a university clinic between January 2023 and March 2024. Following sample size calculation and the application of inclusion and exclusion criteria, the final sample comprised 96 patients (58 females and 38 males) with a mean age of 25.5 years (±10.2). Bilateral measurements were taken in four regions of the mandibular platform at the level of the lower second molars: tangent to the distal surface, distobuccal cusp, buccal groove, and mesiobuccal cusp. After orienting the CBCT images, the transverse superficial midpoint of the mandibular platform was determined. The maximum insertion angle from this point to the mandibular canal relative to the vertical plane was then calculated. Using this angle, the distance from the midpoint of the mandibular platform to the mandibular canal was measured. To assess intra-examiner reliability, measurements were repeated on 10% of the sample, yielding correlation coefficients ranging from 0.926 to 0.996. Descriptive statistical analyses were performed alongside inferential comparison methods, with a significance level set at 5% for the latter.

Results: Results showed that the transverse width of the mandibular platform and the distance from its midpoint to the nearest point of the mandibular canal progressively increased in a posterior direction, while the insertion angle progressively decreased. This indicates that the farther posterior the location, the more vertical the relationship between the mandibular canal and the midpoint of the platform. At the mesial cusp level, the mandibular platform width was significantly greater in males (P=0.011), and the distance to the canal was greater at all measurement sites (p<0.05), except at the distal cusp of the left lower second molar. No significant differences were observed between age groups, except for the insertion angle at the mesial cusp of the left second molar in patients older than 22 years (p=0.035).

Conclusions: Based on the findings, the optimal location for micro-implant insertion on the mandibular platform is the region tangent to the distal surface of the second molar. However, due to the variability in the results, it is recommended to use CBCT prior to insertion to minimize the risk of injury to the inferior alveolar nerve.

2 - B / O Evaluation of cell behavior on implant surfaces with biological functionalization by laser

Andreia Bandeira Luis1, Joana Faria Marques1, Óscar Carvalho2, Gonçalo Garret2, Mariana Brito Cruz1, António Duarte Mata1

1Faculdade de Medicina Dentária da Universidade de Lisboa, Lisboa, Portugal; 2Center for Microelectromechanical Systems (CMEMS), Departamento de Engenharia Mecânica, Universidade do Minho, Guimarães, Portugal

Correspondence: Andreia Bandeira Luis, andreialuisvieira@gmail.com

BMC Proceedings 2025, 19(21): 2 - B / O

Introduction: Dental implants are widely used in everyday Dental Medicine. Strategies have been proposed to optimize the physical, chemical, and biological properties of these materials through modifications of the implant surface. In vitro studies report that these modifications lead to an increase in the proliferation, adhesion, and viability of osteoblastic cells.

Objectives: To evaluate and compare the behavior of Human Fetal Osteoblasts (hFOB 1.19), regarding their viability and morphology on implant surfaces with different laser texturization patterns and surface functionalization with hydroxyapatite (HA).

Materials and methods:Titanium discs were produced with surface texturization using a Neodymium-doped Yttrium Aluminum Garnet (Nd:YAG) laser with a wavelength (λ) of 1064 nm and a pulse of 10 ns, forming a square pattern of 0.25 mm and 0.8 mm. Subsequently, the discs were functionalized with HA by conventional sintering, while others used Carbon Dioxide Laser Sintering, amounting to a total of 6 study groups: 0.25 mm pattern (0.25), 0.25 mm pattern + conventional sintering (0.25SC), 0.25 mm pattern + laser sintering (0.25SL), 0.8 mm pattern (0.8), 0.8 mm pattern + conventional sintering (0.8SC), 0.8 mm pattern + laser sintering (0.8SL), and a negative control, each with an n=15 for all study variables. The samples were seeded for 3 days with an immortalized hFOB 1.19 cell line, following the repository’s guidelines. Cell viability (days 1, 2, and 3) was assessed using the fluorometric resorufin assay. Cell morphology was evaluated by SEM. Data were statistically analyzed using the ANOVA test, with Tukey and Dunnet post-hoc tests as appropriate (α=0.05).

Results:The group with samples textured with the 0.8 mm pattern showed lower cell viability compared to the 0.25 mm group (p<0.05). When HA was added, regardless of the sintering technique, a decrease in cell viability was observed compared to the textured samples (p<0.05). The SEM image analysis demonstrated cellular morphological changes consistent with these results.

Conclusions: Samples textured by laser with a 0.25 mm pattern showed better cell behavior with osteoblasts. Further studies will be needed with other intermediate size patterns and other forms of HA retention.

4 - B / O Evaluation of implant characteristics after implantoplasty: in vitro study

Diogo Banaco, João Carlos Ramos, Carolina Varela, Orlando Martins

Faculdade de Medicina da Universidade de Coimbra, Coimbra, Portugal

Correspondence: Diogo Banaco, diogobanaco@gmail.com

BMC Proceedings 2025, 19(21): 4 - B / O

Introduction: Peri-implantitis is a plaque-associated condition affecting tissues around dental implants, characterized by inflammation and progressive bone loss. Its etiology is multifactorial, with poor oral hygiene, history of periodontal disease, and lack of maintenance visits identified as risk factors. The roughness of the implant surface once exposed to the oral cavity may also influence disease progression and treatment efficacy. There is no consensus on a standardized treatment protocol for peri-implantitis. Implantoplasty, a resective surgery technique, involves smoothing rough implant surfaces to reduce bacterial colonization and has shown clinical improvement and bone level maintenance over three years. However, implantoplasty may lead to mechanical complications such as implant fracture, deformation, and loosening, as well as biological complications like thermal damage to bone and inflammatory reactions from titanium particles.

Objectives: The aim is to evaluate the impact of implantoplasty on the resistance of the implant to fracture through compression tests. A secondary objective is to determine the amount of structure removed from the implant and to assess correlations with resistance to fracture.

Materials and methods: Twenty-six implants (Conical Bioneck TRI RP 4.3x13mm, DÉRIG, Brasil) were divided into two groups: Test Group (n=13) and Control Group (n=13). In the Test Group, all implants were scanned before and after implantoplasty to determine volume reduction. Using a digital caliper, the diameter reduction was also measured before and after implantoplasty. Implantoplasty was performed by using a sequence of burs (cylindric super coarse size 150μm > fine size 45μm diamond grit bur > final polishment with Arksansas stone). Finally, both groups underwent compression tests to evaluate the maximum force each implant could withstand before failure, and the nature of the failure was characterized. An healing abutment (4.3mm x 5 mm TRI, EXAKTUS®, France) was screwed to each implant. Both test and control groups implants were submitted to static loading test using AG-IS, SHIMADZU, 10kN (SHIMADZU CORPORATION, Kyoto Japan) following the specifications of the international standard (ISO 14801:2016). Each specimen was held in a custom-designed steel holder which allowed the implant to be at 30° ± 2º from its long axis. The compression load was applied vertically, at a speed of 1mm/min. The test stopped when complete fracture occurred, or the specimen reached a 5mm displacement. The intergroup difference was assessed using the Student's t-test (p<0.05). For the Test Group, the correlation between volume reduction and resistance, as well as diameter reduction and resistance, was evaluated using Pearson’s correlation coefficient (p0.05) or diameter reduction and maximum force (Pearson correlation 0.523; p >0.05).

Results: In the Test Group, volume and diameter reductions were 11.480±2.436 mm³ and 0.305±0.051 mm, respectively. The average maximum force supported was 798.724N ± 213.282N in the Control Group and 815.466N ± 105.712N in the Test Group, with no significant intergroup difference (p=0.809). The Control Group implants failed through deformation, while the Test Group implants showed both deformation and fracture. No significant correlation was found between volume reduction and maximum force (Pearson correlation 0.080; p >0.05) or diameter reduction and maximum force (Pearson correlation 0.523; p >0.05).

Conclusions: Within the limits of this study, implantoplasty did not show an impact in fracture strength of 4.3mm diameter implants. Nonetheless the modes of failure of implants were more severe in test group. Diameter and volume reduction did not show a statistically significant impact in the Fmax achieved by the implants.

5 - B / O Optical behavior of ceramic restorations cemented on darkened substrates

Raul Yehudi, Ricardo Dias, Cristiano Pereira Alves, Ana Messias, Fernando Guerra

Faculdade de Medicina da Universidade de Coimbra, Coimbra, Portugal

Correspondence: Raul Yehudi, raulpeixotop@hotmail.com

BMC Proceedings 2025, 19(21): 5 - B / O

Introduction: Dentists face challenges in fixed ceramic restorations to mimic natural dentition. Dark substrates, ceramic thickness, optical properties of ceramics, and cement opacities are pointed out as essential determinants. This in vitro study compares the impact of different zirconia thicknesses and different cements on the final color of the restoration over darkened substrates.

Materials and methods: Zirconia ceramic samples with three thicknesses (0.5, 1, and 1.5 mm) were tested on four darkened resin substrates (ND6, ND7, ND8, ND9) (n=1). The ceramics were photographed over the substrates with three different resin cements of varying opacity (Transparent, Opaque White, and Universal). Photos of the cements alone were taken before and after photopolymerization to compare the color modification impact of photopolymerization. Color measurements (L*, a*, b*) were taken using Adobe Photoshop (version 25.9.1), and ΔE was calculated, with the results statistically analyzed. Friedman’s 2-way analysis of variance was applied to evaluate the variation in ΔE (dependent variable) within each group. Tukey’s post hoc tests were used to check for differences between groups. The significance level was set at p < 0.05.

Results: In general, substrate color (p 2.3 (p<0.05). The ND8 resin had ΔE below or close to 2.3. The ND9 substrates showed perceptible ΔE with the 0.5 mm ceramic and lower ΔE with 1 and 1.5 mm ceramics. The resin cement showed a highly perceptible color change after polymerization. The results suggest that dentists should manage the concepts that influence the color properties of the final restoration. The results showed that ceramic thickness and substrate color are determinant factors, but not in the expected sense. Less dark substrates demonstrated higher color perception values. The 0.5 mm ceramic thickness always resulted in perceptible changes across all substrates. For 1.0 mm and 1.5 mm, the color change perception was more noticeable in less dark substrates. The ND8 resin combined with 1.5 mm ceramic showed the least color variation. Therefore, not only is luminosity important, but chroma also seems decisive in obtaining more predictable aesthetic results. The role of different opacity cements seems neutral in color differences. However, color variation in cement after polymerization could introduce a significant modification in the final color perception. Further investigations are needed to overcome some limitations found in this preliminary study.

Conclusions: The color perception of the ceramic is influenced by thickness and the shade of the substrate. The cement appears neutral in color perception, but changes occur after cement polymerization. Evaluating both luminosity and chroma is essential for the integration of ceramic color.

6 - D / O Endodontic microsurgery of a maxillary first molar with maxillary sinus involvement after foreign body reaction: clinical case

Sofia Moura Furtado, Ana Filipa Silva Marques, Jorge Martins, Mário Rito, António Ginjeira

Faculdade de Medicina Dentária da Universidade de Lisboa, Lisboa, Portugal

Correspondence: Sofia Moura Furtado, sofia.moura96@hotmail.com

BMC Proceedings 2025, 19(21): 6 - D / O

Introduction: Foreign bodies found in the maxillary sinus include dental roots, filling materials, dental implants, among others. A foreign object can reach the apical region accidentally or iatrogenically during dental procedures. In most cases, these materials enter through an oroantral communication. The presence of a foreign body in the periapical tissues can cause endodontic failure by triggering an inflammatory response and a subsequent foreign body reaction. The presence of a foreign body inside the maxillary sinus can result in complaints of nasal discharge, occasional episodes of pus, a taste of blood in the mouth, and intermittent headaches. Small foreign materials may be spontaneously expelled, but in most cases, they need to be removed through surgical procedures.

Clinical case description: A 73-year-old female patient presented with moderate spontaneous pain and pain during chewing in the region of the second quadrant. The patient reported constant nasal discharge, primarily from the left nasal cavity, and recurrent headaches. The upper left first molar had undergone endodontic treatment more than 20 years prior. There was no previous history suggesting the presence of sinusitis or oroantral fistula immediately after the prior endodontic treatment. The clinical examination did not reveal any signs of intraoral inflammatory changes, such as an oroantral fistula, but detected pain on vertical and horizontal percussion. The periapical radiograph, which included the left perinasal sinus, revealed the presence of a radiopaque foreign body inside the sinus cavity. A CBCT scan was also performed, which, in addition to the foreign body and the associated inflammatory reaction inside the sinus, revealed an extensive radiolucent periapical lesion associated with the mesiobuccal and palatal roots. Endodontic microsurgery was performed on tooth 26, where the periapical lesions associated with the mesiobuccal and palatal roots were curetted, and the sinus was involved to remove the foreign body inside it. The mesiobuccal, distobuccal, and palatal canals were retro-prepared and retrofilled with bioceramic material. To facilitate the regeneration of Schneider's membrane, two L-PRF membranes obtained from the patient's centrifuged blood and an L-PRF block, formed by combining an L-PRF membrane with biomaterial and plasma, were used. At the six-month follow-up, a favorable outcome was observed, and the patient reported no longer experiencing symptoms compatible with sinusitis.

Conclusions: It is generally accepted that immediate surgical intervention to remove a foreign body is recommended to avoid possible sequelae, such as acute/chronic sinusitis or the formation of mucosal cysts. The approach to the sinus is performed through open surgery via a Caldwell-Luc sinusotomy, which, in addition to the removal of the foreign body, aims to clear and drain the sinus. Samples of the foreign body and periapical lesions were collected for histopathological examination, which subsequently revealed "a sample composed of fragments of hyalinized fibrous connective tissue, with a dense inflammatory infiltrate predominantly of lymphoplasmacytic cells, numerous polymorphonuclear cells, foamy macrophages, and some cholesterol crystal clefts." After noting a favorable clinical evolution, the surgical approach undertaken can be considered successful.

Informed consent and authorization for the publication of the data in an open-access journal were obtained from the patient.

7 - D / O Guided bone regeneration with non-resorbable membrane in the aesthetic zone – clinical case

Francisco Correia, Honorato Vidal, Ricardo Faria e Almeida

Faculdade de Medicina Dentária da Universidade do Porto, Porto, Portugal

Correspondence: Francisco Correia, franciscodcorreia@gmail.com

BMC Proceedings 2025, 19(21): 7 - D / O

Introduction: Placing an implant in an ideal 3D position often requires performing bone regeneration. Different surgical techniques are reported in the literature to perform bone regeneration, with different success rates and associated complications. The choice of surgical technique depends not only on systemic conditions and the patient's habits, but also on local conditions such as the quantity/quality of tissues. The clinical case describes how we managed to rehabilitate tooth 21 (with a dental implant) achieving bone and mucogingival conditions and overcoming post-surgical complications.

Clinical case description: A 25-year-old man, without systemic pathologies, non-smoker, with a history of several dental traumas and previous dental treatments on tooth 21. After a new avulsion, tooth 21 showed mobility and exposure of the root to the apex. After clinical and radiographic analysis, considering the tooth's prognosis and the patient's aesthetic expectations, it was decided to extract the tooth and rehabilitate it with an implant-supported crown. For 4 months, different grinds were consecutively carried out on the root of the tooth, with the aim of promoting the growth of gingival tissues. At the 4th month, the tooth was extracted, and a tomography was requested. At the 5th month, local anesthesia was used, an intra-sucular incision was made from 13 to 23 with a distal discharge. The flap was elevated in full thickness. Autograft bone was collected from the mandible branch using a bone scraper. The non-resorbable PTFE membrane reinforced with titanium was adapted to the defect and fixed at the buccal apical level with two pins. The xenograft mixed with autologous bone was placed, the membrane was fixed in the remaining places and sutured (polyamide 4.0). At 5.5 months, due to the exposure of the membrane in the apical area, an implant was placed. Anesthetized, from 13 to 23 an incision was made and the flap was subsequently raised in full thickness. The membrane was removed and the implant (4.3x10) was placed. It was sutured with 4.0 and 6.0 polyamide. At 4 months, the second surgical phase was carried out and a temporary crown was placed to model the tissues. The zirconia crown was placed after 6 months. After a year and a half of follow-up, the patient is very satisfied with the aesthetic results achieved.

Conclusions: The wear of the root allowed the maturation and growth of the gingival tissues, avoiding the need for mucogingival surgery prior to implant placement. Even though the membrane was exposed, both the planned clinical results and the patient's expectations were achieved.

Informed consent and authorization for the publication of the data in an open-access journal were obtained from the patient.

8 - D / O Pyostomatitis vegetans: a diagnostic challenge – case report

Mariana Costa Branco, Sofia Vieira, Orlando Martins, Francisco Marques

Faculdade de Medicina da Universidade de Coimbra, Coimbra, Portugal

Correspondence: Mariana Costa Branco, marianacostabranco11@gmail.com

BMC Proceedings 2025, 19(21): 8 - D / O

Introduction: Pyostomatitis vegetans (PV) is a rare mucocutaneous condition of unknown etiology. The cutaneous lesions may affect the genital region, axillae, and scalp. Orally, the lesions are characterized by multiple white or yellow pustules that can evolve into ulceration or superficial erosions. It is frequently associated with peripheral eosinophilia. Histologically, there is cellular hyperplasia, superficial acanthosis, and superficial ulceration with infraepithelial and/or subepithelial microabscesses containing a large number of eosinophils and neutrophils. Pyostomatitis vegetans is a highly specific marker for inflammatory bowel disease, and its correct recognition can lead to the diagnosis of ulcerative colitis or Crohn's disease following a thorough and appropriate gastrointestinal examination. The aim of this study is to present a clinical case of possible PV and the corresponding approach.

Clinical case description: A 64-year-old male patient, with no allergies, alcohol or tobacco habits, and poor oral hygiene, was referred to the Oral Medicine consultation. He was referred due to several small yellow vesiculobullous lesions on the buccal mucosa and lips, with a three-month evolution, associated with generalized pain in the oral cavity. He reported complaints in the genital tract without clarified pathology and was medicated with Solifenacin and Tamsulosin Hydrochloride. Clinical analyses (29/03/23) demonstrated leukopenia. In June 2023, he was treated with various antifungals without improvement. On 02/10/23, an incisional biopsy was performed, the result of which was partially compatible with PV. Topical and systemic corticosteroids were prescribed, showing improvement after one week. Since then, the dosage has been reduced to find a minimal therapeutic dose compatible with lesion control. However, after starting the dosage reduction, lesions reappeared, prompting another incisional biopsy for direct immunofluorescence (15/04/24) and the need to increase the therapeutic dosage. The biopsy reported buccal mucosa with slight parakeratosis and sparse inflammatory infiltrate of the lamina propria, with negative immunofluorescence for IgG, IgM, and IgA. Currently, there has been a progressive reduction in the dosage, with corresponding lesion control. The case has a 7-month follow-up.

Conclusions: Although both biopsies were not entirely characteristic, the adopted therapeutic strategy demonstrated efficacy, provided the gradual reduction of the established dosage is observed. The initial treatment of oral PV lesions involves topical and systemic corticosteroids. However, recurrence may occur during the reduction or suspension of medication, requiring consideration of second-line therapeutic options: dapsone, azathioprine, cyclosporine, methotrexate, and infliximab. In this case, the goal was to find a minimal effective corticosteroid dose for long-term management. Due to the association of this condition with inflammatory gastrointestinal disorders, it is important to refer patients for a complete gastrointestinal evaluation. 9 - D / O Lateral periodontal cyst - clinical case

Teresa Carvalho e Branco, Rita Cacodcar, Filipe Freitas, André Moreira, Helena Francisco, João Caramês

Faculdade de Medicina Dentária da Universidade de Lisboa, Lisboa, Portugal

Correspondence: Teresa Carvalho e Branco, mariabranco@campus.ul.pt

BMC Proceedings 2025, 19(21): 9 - D / O

Introduction: The lateral periodontal cyst (LPC) is a developmental odontogenic cyst of controversial etiology, which appears mainly on the side of the root of a vital tooth and represents approximately 0.8% to 2% of all jaw cysts. It is most prevalent in the fifth and sixth decades of life. Approximately 80% are detected in the mandible, mainly in the lateral incisors, canines and premolars region, followed by the anterior maxillary region.

Clinical case description: Male patient, caucasian, 44 years old, seeks an oral medicine consultation at FMDUL complaining of discomfort and swelling in the region of the lower left first premolar, with approximately 6 months of evolution. He denies relevant personal or family medical history, allergies, smoking or alcoholic habits, as well as habitual intake of any medication. Upon objective examination, a nodular, circular lesion was identified, located buccally between teeth 34 and 35, measuring approximately 7 mm in diameter, covered by healthy mucosa. The responses to pulp sensitivity tests to cold and percussion in these teeth were within normal limits. Radiographically, a radiolucent, unilocular, well-defined area with sclerotic edges, approximately 6 mm in diameter, was observed between the roots of teeth 34 and 35. Under local anesthesia, the lesion was enucleated, followed by conservative curettage. The histopathological examination described a cystic lesion with a fibrous wall, without significant inflammatory infiltrate, with a thin epithelial lining of cubic non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium. Clinical, radiographic and histopathological features supported the diagnosis of lateral periodontal cyst (PLQ). A control consultation was carried out 4 months after surgery. Clinically, there was an absence of recurrence and symptoms and, radiographically, there was an increase in bone density in the area of ​​the lesion. The patient remains under periodic clinical and radiographic control.

Conclusions: There is controversy regarding the etiopathogenesis of these lesions. They are believed to originate from odontogenic epithelial remnants. QPL is often a radiographic finding, due to the absence of symptoms and is characterized by presenting a well-circumscribed circular or drop-shaped radiolucent area, with sclerotic edges, lateral to the root of a vital tooth. Radiographically, QPL may resemble a laterally located inflammatory cyst of endodontic or periodontal origin or an odontogenic keratocyst. The initial diagnosis is generally made after clinical and radiographic examination, but the definitive diagnosis is histological. Despite their benign behavior, with late diagnosis, they can reach considerable sizes. The most recommended treatment in the literature is enucleation of the lesion, followed by curettage, which was, therefore, the approach chosen in the present case. Most clinical studies have short follow-up periods, which may lead to an underestimation of their recurrence rate. Therefore, periodic radiographic control of the lesion site is recommended. A correct and early clinical-histopathological diagnosis can avoid inappropriate treatment, reducing the risk of recurrence, progression of the lesion and consequent loss of bone tissue.

10 - D / O Medium/long-term splinting of teeth with severe periodontal compromise: case series

Bruno Matos1, Orlando Martins2, Sérgio Matos2, João Carlos Ramos2

1Instituto Português de Medicina Dentária, Aveiro, Portugal; 2Faculdade de Medicina da Universidade de Coimbra, Coimbra, Portugal

Correspondence: Bruno Matos, brunofilipe.rmatos@gmail.com

BMC Proceedings 2025, 19(21): 10 - D / O

Introduction: Periodontitis is an inflammatory disease, caused by the accumulation of bacterial plaque which, if not properly diagnosed and treated, leads to a loss of tooth-supporting tissues, accompanied by an increase in tooth mobility which, ultimately, can jeopardize their maintenance. When we are dealing with teeth with large loss of periodontal attachment, with marked mobility, splinting is a viable treatment option, increasing the patient's chewing and phonetic comfort. Likewise, it facilitates oral hygiene procedures, essential for controlling bacterial plaque and consequently periodontitis. The occlusal balance of these patients is equally important, as although occlusal trauma is not the primary cause for the initiation of periodontitis, the simultaneous presence of both pathologies causes an acceleration in tissue destruction associated with periodontal inflammation. Recent evidence indicates that teeth with mobility are more likely to be extracted, so splinting these teeth could be a procedure that reduces tooth loss. Although a definitive conclusion on the effectiveness of splinting cannot currently be established, recent Clinical Practice Guidelines for the treatment of patients with bone loss due to periodontitis state that temporary splinting and/or selective occlusal adjustments can be considered in all phases of periodontal treatment in order to increase patient comfort and/or facilitate periodontal therapy. The objectives of the present work are to retrospectively evaluate a series of clinical cases, which present severe periodontal compromise in the 5th sextant, treated with a reinforced composite resin splint. The primary outcome is the determination of the survival of splinted teeth (tooth loss), and as secondary outcomes we intend to evaluate the radiographic bone level and the clinical performance of splints in the medium/long term (>10 years), through need for repair and acceptance of treatment by the patient.

Clinical case description: 10 patients were selected, with a minimum follow-up period of 12 years and a maximum of 23 years (16.50± 2.99 years), aged between 50 and 85 years, male (N=6) and female gender (N=4), with bone loss (72.20 ± 9.41%) due to periodontitis and tooth mobility grade 2 or 3. Patients were monitored from a periodontal and occlusal point of view and the teeth of the 5th sextant, with periodontal compromise (N=50) were splinted with a reinforced composite resin splint. The survival rate was 92%, with only one case losing 4 teeth, due to root fracture, at a follow up of 23 years.

Conclusions: The splinting of teeth with residual periodontal support, supported by periodontal treatment, occlusal balance and the patient's hygienic instruction and motivation, could be a solution that allows the maintenance of teeth and residual bone level, during long periods of follow-up. Although patient acceptance levels are high, this treatment is not immune from the need for repairs.

11 - D / O Mandibular squamous cell carcinoma oral rehabilitation case report

Jorge Sousa1, Fernando Guerra1, Salomão Rocha1, Patrícia Dias1, Ivan Cabo1, Teresa Lopes2

1Faculdade de Medicina da Universidade de Coimbra, Coimbra, Portugal; 2Centro Hospitalar Universitário de Coimbra, Coimbra, Portugal

Correspondence: Jorge Sousa, dentinhos.pt@gmail.com

BMC Proceedings 2025, 19(21): 11 - D / O

Introduction: Oral cavity squamous cell carcinoma is the most common malignancy of the oral cavity. Reconstruction of large defects and complete oral rehabilitation for an optimal quality of life, mainly after ablative oncologic surgery in the maxillofacial region, assisted with radiotherapy and chemotherapy, is a desirable but challenging and difficult goal to achieve. Oral rehabilitation in these complex cases requires a multidisciplinary approach, planning and execution, in order to obtain the best possible outcome, in favor of the Patient's interest and quality of life.

Clinical case description: A 70-year-old Caucasian male patient, retired, under medication with pravastatin and acetylsalicylic acid, was diagnosed with well-differentiated squamous cell carcinoma of the mandible, presenting with multifocal neural invasion (both intratumoral and peritumoral), vascular invasion, and minimal surgical margins.

The patient underwent supraomohyoid and submental neck dissection. Metastases were detected in 2 out of 3 lymph nodes excised, with a pattern of invasion classified as WPOI-5. A segmental mandibulectomy was performed, followed by reconstruction using a titanium plate and a supraclavicular flap.

Due to plate fracture, surgical removal of the reconstruction plate was performed via left cervicotomy. A free osteomyofasciocutaneous fibula flap was harvested and used for mandibular reconstruction. Intraoral dehiscence was reconstructed using a left nasogenian flap and split-thickness skin grafts harvested from the thoracic and cervical regions.

The patient was evaluated in Oral Rehabilitation, following the standard clinical and radiological assessment protocol, presenting treatment plans and options. The the chosen treatment plan involved mandibular implant-supported metal-acrylic overdenture retained on a Dolder bar over two implants, aiming to provide labial support, containment of liquids and food, and adequate, accessible hygiene. The maxillary arch was rehabilitated with screw-retained ceramic crowns on Ti-base abutments over implants.

Functional tests on a semi-adjustable articulator and intraoral try-ins were conducted to determine vertical dimension of occlusion (VDO), intercuspal position (ICP), and optimal support, ensuring proper load distribution and avoidance of critical anatomical structures. Guided surgery was successfully performed for the placement of dental implants in previously irradiated, microvascularized free fibula grafts. Implant osseointegration was achieved, except for site 2.6, which demonstrated failure; explantation was performed with bone preservation, limiting the occlusal platform to tooth 25.

After asymptomatic and stable osseointegration of the implants, prosthetic oral rehabilitation was carried out as previously planned, with attention to phonetics and swallowing tests to confirm appropriate tissue support and patient comfort. Definitive prostheses were delivered, followed by post-insertion follow-ups.

Conclusions: Cancer of the oral cavity is a potentially fatal disease, its early diagnosis and treatment are fundamental, even so, the patient's oral functions can be severely affected, requiring a complex and multidisciplinary oral rehabilitation, seeking to restore the functions to an acceptable state, guaranteeing the patient comfort and dignified and acceptable quality of life.

1 - A / P Impact of medical comorbidities on postoperative complications in dento-alveolar surgery : a study dental school population cohort

Anthony Charles Joseph Delhoume, Elisa La Torre, Carolina Venda Nova, Paulo Macedo, Jorge Pereira, Otília Pereira Lopes

Faculdade de Ciências da Saúde Universidade Fernando Pessoa, Porto, Portugal

Correspondence: Anthony Charles Joseph Delhoume, 39530@ufp.edu.pt

BMC Proceedings 2025, 19(21): 1 - A / P

Introduction: Due to the increasing number of patients with chronic medical problems, a thorough evaluation is essential to the successful dental management of a medically compromised patient. During dentoalveolar extractions, several post-operative complications can arise. These unforeseen events may be due to the possible comorbidities that these patients suffer. Some associations may have repercussions on patients, while others will have none at all.

A conservative approach should be the starting point in the simplest cases, and although sialolithiasis is rare in the paediatric population, dentists should be aware of this possibility and be able to assess, recognize and plan the treatment of these cases.

Conclusions: Verruciform xanthoma is a rare lesion of the oral mucosa. However, in some cases, it may also mimic malignant lesions like squamous cell or verrucous carcinoma.

A case of vitreoretinal PTLD after HSCT for aplastic anemia
Authors: Yifan Zhang, Xiaoning Wang
Published: 2026

A 44-year-old man diagnosed with severe aplastic anemia underwent haploidentical hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT). Intermittent monitoring of peripheral blood Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) DNA remained positive 2 months after transplantation. EBV DNA converted to negative after 1 week of therapy with human immunoglobulin (10 g per day) and ganciclovir (5 mg/kg every 12 hours). The patient developed progressive enlargement of cervical and axillary lymph nodes. Histopathological and immunohistochemical findings are consistent with posttransplant lymphoproliferative disorder (PTLD). Following treatment with rituximab (600 mg per dose, 2 doses), the lymphadenopathy completely resolved. The patient presented with a shadow and vision loss in the right eye 7 months after transplantation. Intraocular fluid analysis showed elevated interleukin-6 (IL-6; 17 435.6 pg/mL; normal <7) and IL-10 (47.9 pg/mL; normal <12.9), along with EBV nucleic acid. No significant symptomatic improvement was observed following 4 intravitreal injections of sodium phosphonate. Fundus examination revealed a distinct mass (panel A, right eye, white arrow). Optical coherence tomography revealed elevated macular lesions (panel B, right eye, white arrow). Cranial plain and contrast-enhanced scans revealed no clear evidence of primary central nervous system lymphoma. Subsequently, vitrectomy was performed. Postoperative cytology showed scattered atypical lymphocytes (panel C, Wright-Giemsa stain, 40× objective lens). Combined with immunohistochemistry, the findings were consistent with vitreoretinal lymphoma, diagnosed 2 months after the initial detection of EBV-DNA positivity. The patient received 7 intravitreal methotrexate injections, resulting in significant lesion regression.

Lipid indices and coronary collateralization in STEMI: Expanding the metabolic perspective
Authors: Ioannis Skalidis, Lisa Simioni, Mesmine Mawa Kamdem, Giulia S. Beretta, Serban Puricel
Published: 2026

The recently published study by Zhang et al. examining the association between lipid indices and coronary collateral circulation (CCC) in patients with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) provides valuable insight into the metabolic determinants of collateral development during acute coronary occlusion [1]. In a cohort of 421 STEMI patients with acute total occlusion, higher levels of LDL-C, non-HDL-C, and the non-HDL-C/HDL-C ratio were independently associated with impaired CCC, with non-HDL-C demonstrating the strongest discriminatory ability for predicting poor collateralization.

These findings support the concept that atherogenic lipid burden may influence ischemia-driven vascular remodeling. However, several points merit further consideration. First, CCC assessment relied on the Rentrop grading system, which provides a semi-quantitative angiographic evaluation and may not fully capture the functional capacity of collateral flow. Physiological approaches, such as collateral flow index or pressure-derived measurements, could help clarify whether the observed associations translate into meaningful differences in collateral perfusion.

Second, the magnitude of the reported odds ratios for LDL-C and non-HDL-C appears substantial despite relatively modest differences in baseline lipid values between groups. This observation raises the possibility that these lipid indices may reflect broader metabolic or inflammatory phenotypes influencing vascular remodeling rather than acting as isolated determinants of collateral development. Integration of inflammatory or metabolic markers may therefore provide additional mechanistic insight.

Finally, the exclusion of patients receiving lipid-lowering therapy allowed evaluation of untreated lipid profiles but leaves open the clinically relevant question of whether aggressive lipid-lowering strategies might influence collateral maturation over time.

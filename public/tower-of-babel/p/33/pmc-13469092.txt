A strong robust multi level database image watermark embedding scheme based on the Chinese remainder theorem
Authors: Huizheng Geng, Sixu Guo, Yingqing Liu, Chunmei Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Due to the fact that most existing digital watermarking schemes can only add watermark information once, repeated additions will overwrite the original watermark information, making it impossible to achieve multi-level traceability of data. In response to the above issues, this paper proposes a multi-level database image watermark embedding scheme (MIWC) based on the Chinese remainder theorem. Taking image watermarks as carriers, MIWC preprocesses images using methods such as Haar wavelet transform and Bloom filter to form a pixel. It then performs secret segmentation on watermark information in accordance with the properties of the Chinese Remainder Theorem, enabling hierarchical and item-by-item addition of the watermark information. Furthermore, it records and traces the entire data flow chain. Functional analysis demonstrates that MIWC possesses the multi-level watermark embedding capability that existing schemes lack, thus holding high practical application value. Experimental results indicate that MIWC is reversible, with both watermark embedding and extraction exhibiting high efficiency. Even with the embedding of multi-level watermarks, MIWC remains highly efficient and lightweight. Meanwhile, MIWC also demonstrates strong robustness, being capable of resisting geometric attacks (including rotation and scaling) and common attacks (including JPEG compression, JPEG2000, Gaussian white noise, and salt-and-pepper noise) targeting the watermark.

Introduction

Background

The origins of digital watermarking technology research1,2 primarily arise from the information security challenges induced by the swift advancement of the Internet and multimedia information technology. As multimedia information becomes widely disseminated and easily modifiable, concerns such as copyright ownership, information integrity, and authenticity have become increasingly significant. These problems not only damage the legitimate rights and interests of copyright owners, but also may involve broader information security issues such as personal privacy, corporate secrets and national security3,4.

Digital watermarking technology offers a solution to address these challenges by embedding watermark information into digital carriers without causing visual distortion or interfering with its normal use. This technology not only serves copyright protection but also ensures that copyright ownership can be identified when the work is tampered with or stolen, by embedding watermark information representing the copyright owner. Additionally, it can be utilized to trace the origin of pirated digital content, providing robust support in combating copyright infringement.

In addition, digital watermarking technology also has the characteristics of imperceptibility, verifiability and no password control, which makes it an indispensable part of many digital copyright protection standards. With the deepening of research, digital watermarking technology has been mainly applied to still digital images from the beginning, and gradually expanded to other fields such as video, audio and documents, providing a more comprehensive and effective guarantee for information security. Consequently, the study of digital watermarking holds significant practical importance and presents broad prospects for application. With ongoing technological progress and refinement, digital watermarking is poised to play an increasingly crucial role in ensuring information security and fostering the robust development of multimedia information systems.

Related work

As a key branch in the field of digital watermarking, reversible watermarking technology5,6 has always attracted the attention of researchers. However, digital watermarking faces multiple challenges in practical applications, such as easy to be identified and erased by attackers, specific requirements on media quality and carrier, and adaptability differences in different media application fields. For instance, countermeasure training is employed to bolster watermarking robustness. Furthermore, techniques like multiple and separate watermarking are harnessed to augment the adaptability and resilience of watermarking. Moreover, the integration of digital watermarking with encryption technology fortifies copyright protection mechanisms7.

In order to prevent malicious attackers from copying, altering, and disseminating copyrighted information without the permission of the copyright owner, database owners embed watermarks in the database to prove copyright ownership or verify data integrity2. In the research of reversible database watermarking algorithms, Zhang8 proposed a classic algorithm based on histogram shift, which embeds watermark information by creating additional redundant space and following specific rules. In order to further improve Zhang’s algorithm, Hu9 innovatively introduced genetic algorithm to find the optimal watermark embedding position through algorithm training. They developed a novel reversible watermarking method based on genetic algorithm by combining group key and histogram shift technology. In addition, numerous novel digital watermarking schemes have been proposed to improve the robustness of watermarks. For example, Gupta10 proposed a reversible database blind watermarking scheme based on differential extension; Jawad11 utilized genetic algorithms and differential extension techniques to design reversible database watermarks; Shi attempted to solve the problem of data distortion by restoring the original data and embedding watermark information; Ge12 has conducted in-depth research and improvement in watermark capacity and data distortion, and has increased the embedding capacity of watermarks within an acceptable range of distortion; Guan13 applied the theory of large capacity and high error tolerance QR codes to transform domain image watermarking algorithms, significantly enhancing the robustness of watermarks.

Image compression based on Haar wavelet transform

Haar wavelet transform is a kind of wavelet transform based on the Haar wavelet function14,15. The original signal is decomposed into components of different frequencies through average and difference operations to obtain the wavelet coefficient, which has the advantages of simple calculation and fast speed. The core idea of image compression algorithm based on Haar wavelet transform is to decompose the image into several high-frequency images and one low-frequency image by calculating the average value and the difference value to the detail coefficient, which can initially compress the original image without affecting the main information and achieve the purpose of reducing the amount of image information16.

Bloom filter

Bloom introduced the concept of the Bloom filter (BF) in 197017. BF is a probabilistic data structure used to determine whether an element exists in a set. The false positive rate \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${P_{fp}}$$\end{document} of a Bloom filter is determined by the bit array length m, the number of hash functions k, and the number of inserted elements n:3\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} P_{f p} \approx \left( 1-e^{-\frac{k n}{m}}\right) ^k \end{aligned}$$\end{document}The optimal k that minimizes \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${P_{fp}}$$\end{document} is \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$k = \frac{m}{n}\ln 2$$\end{document}. In this work, parameters are configured according to this rule to ensure low false positive rate.

The BF operates by utilizing an array, denoted as m, which is initialized with zeros. Then, k Hash functions are randomly selected to map n elements in the data field \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathcal{Y}=\left( {{y_1},{y_2}, \ldots ,{y_n}} \right)$$\end{document} to the above array one by one. For each element, its corresponding k hash values are computed using the selected hash functions, and the respective positions within the array are set to ‘1’. Once a hash position is set to ‘1’, it remains unchanged. This process is illustrated in Fig. 1.

To determine whether element x exists in the data field, first compute the Hash values of the above k Hash functions corresponding to element x. If all Hash values are equal to ‘1’, then element x is present in the data field.

When adding a new element to a Bloom filter, k different hash functions are required to perform hash calculations on it. Map this element to the k bits of the Bloom filter and set these bits to ‘1’.

When querying whether an element exists in a Bloom filter, it is necessary to use pre-defined k hash functions to map the element to k bits of the Bloom filter. If all the values corresponding to these k bits are ‘1’, it indicates that the element is likely present in the set. However, if there is at least one position that is not ‘1’, the element is definitely not in the set2,18.

Proposed models

This work proposes a multi-level database image watermark embedding scheme based on the CRT (MIWC). This scheme uses the CRT to hierarchically insert preprocessed image watermark information into the database, allowing for the gradual addition of multiple watermark information, achieving recording and tracing of the entire data flow chain, while ensuring that the original database can be restored lossless after the watermark is extracted. Watermark preprocessing

The traditional structured data watermarking scheme directly embeds strings or binary bit strings as watermark information into the data. This method can carry a limited length of watermark information and can cause significant distortion of the original data. In order to reconcile the conflict between embedding capacity and data distortion, while striving to reduce the length of the watermark while embedding more identity information, we use image information to carry the watermark information to be embedded. Before embedding the watermark into the data, we process the watermark image in advance. The preprocessing process of watermark images includes 3 steps: Compressing the image using Haar wavelet transform;Encoding the coordinates of key pixels into Bloom filters;Secretly slicing the Bloom filter values based on the CRT.Inspired by color conversion-based steganography, the feature expression of watermark images is optimized.

Security analysis and attack resistance

For the MIWC scheme, its security analysis and attack resistance capabilities are presented through formal security analysis, including key space, tamper resistance, deletion resistance, and forgery resistance. Detailed definitions and strict mathematical proofs are given as follows. To further strengthen the security of the watermarking system, we introduce chaotic mapping and box design theories22–24 to improve the confidentiality and anti attack capability of the entire scheme.

Key space analysis

The key space of the MIWC scheme determines its ability to resist brute-force attacks. The total key system consists of four parts: the symmetric HMAC authentication key k, the set of pairwise coprime moduli used in the CRT, the parameter set of the Bloom filter, and the watermark fragment parameters. Let the total key space be K. The HMAC key uses at least 128-bit secure keys. The number of optional groups of coprime moduli is at least \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${2^{64}}$$\end{document}. The parameter space of the Bloom filter and the fragment selection space provide additional security entropy. Therefore, the total key space size satisfies:14\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} |K| \ge 2^{128} \cdot 2^{64} \cdot 2^{32} \cdot 2^{32}=2^{256} \end{aligned}$$\end{document}The key space size is larger than \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${2^{256}}\$$$\end{document}, which meets the security requirements of modern cryptography and can resist all existing brute-force attacks.

Tamper resistance

Definition

Tamper resistance means that any unauthorized modification of the database will cause the watermark to be extracted incorrectly or fail verification, so that the tampering behavior can be detected.

Proof

The embedding position of each watermark fragment is determined by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$HMA{C_k}\left( {\mathrm{{MK}}} \right) \bmod \;MN$$\end{document} where MK is the primary key of the tuple. If an attacker modifies any data record, the primary key or numerical attribute will change. As a result, the grouping index calculated by the HMAC function will not match the original structure. The extracted watermark fragments will be disordered or missing, which violates the reconstruction constraints of the CRT. Thus, the correct watermark cannot be recovered, and the tampering can be detected immediately. Therefore, the MIWC scheme is tamper-resistant. □

Deletion resistance

Definition

Deletion resistance means that deleting some records in the database cannot completely erase the watermark, and the complete watermark can still be recovered as long as enough effective fragments remain.

Proof

The MIWC scheme adopts a threshold secret sharing mechanism based on the CRT. As long as the number of remaining valid fragments is greater than or equal to t, the complete watermark information can be reconstructed. Formally, let S be the complete fragment set and S' ∈ S be the remaining fragment set after deletion. If, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left| {S'} \right| \le t$$\end{document} then \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathrm{{Recover}}\left( {S'} \right) = W$$\end{document} where W is the original watermark. Therefore, deleting part of the data cannot completely destroy the watermark, and the scheme has strong deletion resistance. □

Forgery resistance

Definition

Forgery resistance means that an attacker without a legal key cannot forge a valid watermark that can pass verification.

Proof

To generate a legal watermark, the attacker needs to satisfy three conditions at the same time: correct Bloom filter pixel coordinates, valid CRT secret fragments, and legal HMAC grouping rules. Without the symmetric key k, the attacker cannot calculate the correct grouping position. Without the CRT modulus set, the forged fragments cannot satisfy the congruence equation. Therefore, any forged watermark will be identified as invalid in the verification stage. Formally, for any forged watermark \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${W_f}$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathrm{{Verify}}\left( {{W_f} = false} \right)$$\end{document}. Therefore, the MIWC scheme is forgery-resistant under the existing computational complexity assumptions.

The impact of each core component on the MIWC scheme’s performance is analyzed in detail below: For the HWT, Group B (without HWT) showed a notable decline in all performance metrics compared to Group A. Under JPEG compression, Gaussian noise, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$30^{\circ }$$\end{document} rotation, the BER increased by 237.5%, 173.3%, and 168.6%, respectively. This confirms that HWT effectively compresses image watermarks, extracts low-frequency components, and enhances resistance to common attacks. Additionally, Group B’s embedding time was 215.6% longer than Group A’s, as uncompressed original image pixels increase the computational burden of embedding. The imperceptibility BER of Group B was 230.2% higher than that of Group A, demonstrating that HWT reduces data distortion caused by watermark embedding and improves the scheme’s imperceptibility.

Regarding the BF, Group C (without BF) exhibited higher BER values than Group A under all attack types -134.4%, 97.8%, and 88.2% increases under JPEG compression, Gaussian noise, and rotation, respectively. BF is responsible for encoding and verifying key pixel coordinates, filtering invalid pixel information to improve the accuracy of watermark fragment extraction. Without BF, extracted pixel coordinates are prone to errors, leading to reduced robustness. The imperceptibility BER of Group C was 90.0% higher than that of Group A, as BF optimizes embedding position selection to minimize distortion. While Group C’s embedding time was 20.3% shorter than Group A, this slight efficiency gain comes at the cost of significant performance loss, making it impractical for real-world applications.

For CRT-based secret sharing, Group D (without CRT) could not support multi-level watermark embedding, which is the core functional advantage of the MIWC scheme. In terms of robustness, Group D’s BER was slightly higher than Group A’s (28.1%, 17.8%, and 21.6% increases under the 3 attacks), as CRT-based secret sharing disperses watermark fragments, reducing the impact of partial data loss or attacks on the entire watermark. Group D’s embedding time was 25.8% shorter than Group A, but this is because it only supports single-level embedding and avoids the computational cost of secret segmentation and reconstruction. Overall, CRT is irreplaceable as it enables multi-level embedding and ensures data traceability.

The ablation experimental results confirm that HWT, BF, and CRT-based secret sharing are all indispensable core components of the MIWC scheme. Each module plays a key role in enhancing the scheme’s robustness, embedding efficiency, and imperceptibility. The complete MIWC scheme integrates the advantages of these 3 components, achieving optimal comprehensive performance, which further validates the rationality and effectiveness of the MIWC.

Under the same experimental settings in Section "Experimental index" with two level embedding, we conduct parameter sensitivity analysis by adjusting one variable while fixing the other two, evaluating robustness, embedding efficiency, and imperceptibility. Results show that a larger \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${T_s}$$\end{document} selects more stable low frequency pixels to improve robustness at the cost of slightly higher time overhead, with negligible influence on data distortion; hence \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${T_s} = 1900$$\end{document} is chosen for balance. Increasing α expands the modulus space of CRT secret shares and enhances anti interference capability, while moderate computation growth is acceptable, so α = 10 is adopted. The selected parameter combination achieves a favorable trade off among robustness, efficiency and imperceptibility, and stable performance within reasonable ranges verifies the practicality of our scheme.

Application status of MIWC

The structured data watermarking scheme presented in this paper MIWC was independently developed and implemented. The primary focus of the scheme is to address scenarios related to data outsourcing computing34,35 and data circulation sharing. This includes resolving ownership issues and traceability challenges that arise during cross-domain and cross-subject data circulation following any potential leaks. Figure 14 illustrates the overall architecture of the proposed scheme. In contrast to traditional local data processing models, the data outsourcing model introduces risks such as data leakage or misuse during the processes of data uploading, distribution36, and transmission37. To mitigate these risks, MIWC incorporates watermarks into data cross-domain nodes, thereby ensuring data ownership and traceability while reducing potential security vulnerabilities.

Conclusions and future prospects

With the development of data business, enterprises have an increasing demand for data, and their own data often cannot meet the growing business needs. Data circulation has become an inevitable trend. Digital watermarking technology is an effective means to solve this problem, but traditional watermarking technology has many shortcomings when applied to structured data. Our work independently designed a new watermark information encoding and watermark embedding algorithm, and proposed a multi-level database watermark embedding scheme MIWC for structured databases using techniques such as CRT, Bloom filter, and Haar wavelet transform.

Although experimental results have shown that this scheme has significant advantages in functionality, execution efficiency, and robustness, MIWC scheme has several limitations. Firstly, MIWC is designed for structured databases and not directly applicable to unstructured data; Secondly, when the number of watermark fragments is extremely large, embedding time increases linearly; Bloom filter also introduces a small false-positive rate. In the future, there is still room for further research and improvement in watermark based data authentication, and there is still room for optimization in the efficiency, robustness, and usability of watermark technology. In addition, watermark technology can be integrated with various other technologies to achieve data authentication, such as blockchain based data operation for on chain certificate traceability. Once ownership disputes arise, they can be verified through on chain records. Blockchain and data watermarking can also complement each other’s shortcomings. Data watermarking technology can also be linked with data classification and grading, providing different authentication measures for different levels of data.

Comment on “Impact of pre-operative frailty on hospital length of stay in major abdominal oncological surgeries: A prospective, observational study”
Authors: Ruchi Singh, Savita Gupta
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with great interest the article by Sharma et al.,[1] titled “Impact of pre-operative frailty on hospital length of stay in major abdominal oncological surgeries: A prospective, observational study”. We congratulate the authors for investigating the impact of frailty in perioperative oncologic surgical patients. Further, we would like to offer a few observations that may add to the clinical utility of the study findings.

Firstly, the Fried Frailty Phenotype, despite being a recognised standard for frailty assessment, relies on patient-reported parameters such as exhaustion and weight loss, making it partially subjective. In an oncological population, cancer-related cachexia often overlaps with biological frailty; hence, utilising an entirely objective, performance-based tool such as the Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB) might provide a more precise and unbiased assessment. The SPPB may better reflect a patient’s physical reserve and functional ability to withstand “surgical stress”. However, this requires further evaluation through dedicated clinical trials.

Secondly, it is noted that 47.8% of the patients were identified as frail. This high prevalence highlights an urgent need for intervention in the form of prehabilitation. Multi-disciplinary prehabilitation—incorporating physical, psychological, nutritional, and medical domains—may improve patients’ overall physiological reserves and functional capacity.[2] Robust prospective studies are required to compare “Standard Care” with “Multimodal Prehabilitation” in patients identified as frail before oncological surgeries.

Assessment of frailty is a vital step in the comprehensive pre-anaesthetic evaluation of the oncological patient.[3] However, the next frontier for the anaesthesiologist is to mitigate perioperative risks by implementing prehabilitation protocols tailored to each patient’s needs and degree of frailty.

Evaluation of diagnostic accuracy and molecular adequacy between conventional endobronchial ultrasound, contrast-enhanced endobronchial ultrasound and elastography for hilar and mediastinal lymphadenopathies: a retrospective observational study
Authors: Luca Frasca, Ilaria Suriano, Antonio Sarubbi, Filippo Longo, Pierfilippo Crucitti
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEndobronchial ultrasound-guided transbronchial needle aspiration (EBUS-TBNA) is a cornerstone for mediastinal staging in lung cancer; however, sampling of bulky or necrotic lymph nodes remains challenging, often leading to inadequate tissue for molecular analysis. This study aimed to compare the diagnostic accuracy and molecular adequacy of conventional EBUS, contrast-enhanced EBUS (CE-EBUS) and EBUS elastography in patients with hilar and mediastinal lymphadenopathies.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective, single-center study from April 2021 until January 2025, including 74 consecutive patients with mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathies suspicious for malignancy on computed tomography (CT) and/or positron emission tomography-CT using 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose (18F-FDG PET-CT) [maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) >2.5]. Patients with suspected autoimmune or lymphoproliferative disease were excluded. All lymph nodes were bulky (>30 mm) and/or showed radiologic necrosis. All patients were distributed into three groups based on the technique used for lymph node evaluation: Conventional EBUS (n=26), CE-EBUS (n=23), and elastography (n=25). CE-EBUS was performed following the intravenous administration of 4.8 mL sulphur hexafluoride. Final diagnosis was based on histopathology and clinical-radiological follow-up in negative cases. Molecular adequacy was defined as the availability of sufficient viable material for standard molecular analyses. Diagnostic accuracy and molecular adequacy were compared across techniques, including a subgroup analysis focusing on bulky or necrotic lymph nodes.ResultsBaseline demographic, clinical, radiologic, and metabolic characteristics were comparable across groups. Overall diagnostic accuracy did not differ significantly among techniques. In analyses adjusted for bulky and necrotic lymphadenopathy, CE-EBUS was associated with higher molecular adequacy compared with conventional EBUS, while no statistically significant differences were observed between conventional EBUS and elastography.ConclusionsCE-EBUS seems to improve diagnostic and molecular yield, particularly in bulky or necrotic lymph nodes, by identifying vital tissue. Elastography provides complementary stiffness information, enhancing overall lymph node characterization. These results could expand upon prior evidence supporting CE-EBUS utility in mediastinal staging.Keywords: Endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS), contrast-enhanced endobronchial ultrasound (CE-EBUS), elastography, lymphadenopathy, lung cancer

What is known and what is new?

• EBUS is the standard in the diagnosis and staging of lung cancer.

• This tool could improve the staging and diagnosis in lung cancer patients. In future, it could be included in protocols especially in patients presenting large necrosis areas and bulky lymphadenopathies.

Introduction

The evaluation of mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy is crucial in lung cancer staging and management. Standard endobronchial ultrasound-guided transbronchial needle aspiration (EBUS-TBNA) is a minimally invasive technique that has become a standard procedure and provides both diagnostic and staging information (1). However, tissue necrosis and non-homogeneous vascularity can lead to poor sampling. In such settings, conventional B-mode ultrasound guidance may result in sampling of non-viable tissue, compromising both diagnostic accuracy and, increasingly, molecular adequacy, which is essential for contemporary oncologic decision-making (2). To address these limitations, adjunct EBUS techniques have been developed to improve intranodal characterization and guide biopsy toward representative tissue. Among these, contrast-enhanced EBUS (CE-EBUS) and EBUS elastography are meant to improve the real-time assessment of lymph nodes while assisting with tissue targeting. They rely on fundamentally different biological principles. Elastography assesses tissue stiffness, exploiting the higher rigidity typically associated with malignant infiltration. While this approach can enhance malignancy prediction, its reliability may be reduced in necrotic lymph nodes, where liquefaction and tissue breakdown often result in mechanically soft regions that may obscure viable tumor foci. Conversely, CE-EBUS evaluates microvascular perfusion following intravenous administration of an ultrasound contrast agent, allowing real-time visualization of vascularized, viable tissue and indirect identification of necrotic areas through absence of enhancement. This “negative imaging” capability may be particularly advantageous in large or necrotic lymphadenopathies, in which viable tumor is frequently confined to peripheral regions (3-6). A pilot study (7) has shown CE-EBUS improves molecular yield compared with EBUS in patients with bulky and necrotic lymphadenopathy, though in that cohort the second adjunct, elastography, was not investigated. The present retrospective study is the extension of our previous work, and was therefore designed to compare conventional EBUS, CE-EBUS, and EBUS elastography in a clinical setting, with particular focus on diagnostic accuracy and molecular adequacy in patients with bulky and/or necrotic mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2025-aw-2298/rc).

Methods

We conducted a retrospective, single-center study at the Department of Thoracic Surgery of Fondazione Policlinico Campus Biomedico. Consecutive 74 patients who underwent EBUS-TBNA sampling for mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy at our center between April 2021 and January 2025 were included. Eligible patients presented mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathies suspected of malignancy based on computed tomography (CT) and/or positron emission tomography-computed tomography (PET-CT) findings. Patients with suspected autoimmune or lymphoproliferative diseases were excluded. All participants underwent a pre-operative CT-scan and 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose (18F-FDG) PET scan showing uptake in one or more mediastinal or hilar lymph nodes [maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) >2.5]. We also assessed malignant features on CT scans: absent hilum, presence of necrosis areas, and irregular margins. The lesions on CT scans were divided into central, peripheral and hilar, considering the long axis (8,9). According to the imaging technique used, three groups of patients were defined: EBUS (n=26), CE-EBUS (n=23) and elastography (n=25) (Figure 1). Allocation was non-random and reflected routine clinical practice during the study period. The choice of imaging modality was influenced by procedural workflow, operator choice, and equipment availability, rather than by predefined radiologic or sonographic selection criteria. CE-EBUS and elastography were not systematically reserved for lymph nodes considered more complex on B-mode ultrasound. Patients included in the CE-EBUS group received 4.8 mL of sulphur hexafluoride injected intravenously (IV). In the elastography group stiffness of tissue were assessed using a color mapping of the lymph nodes. Our standard EBUS-TBNA protocol was used for each patient, thus a systematic exploration, both in homolateral and contralateral hilum, was performed, considering patient’s compliance during the procedures (10). All procedures were performed under conscious sedation using remifentanil-based anesthesia. Three needle passes were obtained per lymph node using a 22-gauge aspiration-cutting needle (11,12). Morphological, echogenic and vascular characteristics of lymph nodes were assessed. The primary objective of this study was to evaluate the independent role of CE-EBUS and elastography in improving diagnostic and molecular yield. Specifically, we employed a multivariable logistic regression model to assess the effect of these advanced imaging techniques on the probability of obtaining a diagnostic sample, while adjusting for potential confounding variables such as bulky lymph node and the presence of necrosis. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. Ethical Committee approval was waived due to the retrospective analysis of the study according to National Laws of the study according to observational retrospective studies (Italian law nr. 11960, released on 13/07/2004). Written informed consent was obtained from the patients for participation and publication.

Sample size considerations

No formal sample size calculation was performed, as this study was exploratory and based on the total number of eligible patients during the study period. The relatively limited sample size, particularly in subgroup analyses of bulky and necrotic lymph nodes, restricts statistical power and increases the risk of Type II error. Consequently, the results of subgroup analyses should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating and intended to inform future prospective studies.

CE-EBUS examination

At present, there is no standardized protocol for CE-EBUS. To improve the consistency and diagnostic reliability of lymph node sampling, we applied a structured institutional protocol, described below. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients before the examination.

Anesthesia

Topical anesthesia of the upper airways was achieved by nebulizing 2% lidocaine into the nasal and oral cavities to desensitize the pharynx and larynx. Conscious sedation was initiated approximately five minutes before bronchoscopy through intravenous administration of remifentanil, starting at 1 ng/mL and titrated up to 6 ng/mL to reach adequate procedural comfort. The depth of sedation was continuously adjusted to maintain patient safety and optimize endoscopic conditions throughout the procedure.

Procedure

CE-EBUS was performed with the patient in a supine position using a convex-probe EBUS bronchoscope (EB-530US, Fujifilm Echobronchoscope®, Lexington, MA, USA) operating at a frequency of 10 MHz. For each targeted lymph node or mass, a 4.8 mL bolus of sulfur hexafluoride was administered IV as a ultrasound contrast agent (UCA) via a peripheral venous cannula. Contrast injection was repeated for each sampled site. Approximately 16–17 seconds after injection, enhancement of the vascular pattern became clearly visible, followed by a gradual wash-out phase around 32 seconds post-injection (9,13).

Under continuous real-time ultrasound guidance, tissue samples were obtained using a 22-gauge aspirating-cutting needle (SonoTip TopGain®, Medi-Globe GmbH, Rohrdorf/Achenmühle, Germany). The needle was advanced and retracted within the lymph node several times to ensure adequate sampling, with three passes per target (3). CE-EBUS imaging provided detailed visualization of nodal morphology, echogenicity, and vascular architecture, enabling differentiation between viable and necrotic tissue and allowing biopsies to be directed toward perfused, non-necrotic regions. Following the procedure, all patients were observed for a minimum of three hours to monitor for immediate complications.

Pathological and molecular analysis

All collected specimens were processed for histopathological evaluation and subjected to molecular testing to assess tissue adequacy for genetic and immunohistochemical studies. Two standardized assays were employed: Myriapod NGS Cancer Panel DNA Assay Kit (Diatech Pharmacogenetics, CE-IVD, Jesi, Italy), requiring at least 20–25 ng of extracted DNA per specimen for valid sequencing results; VENTANA PD-L1 (SP263) Assay (CE-IVD), performed according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

A sample was considered diagnostically successful only when both tests could be completed on the obtained material, emphasizing the need for sufficient and viable tissue acquisition.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are reported as median ± interquartile range (IQR), while categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages. Differences in diagnostic yield between groups were initially assessed using the Chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Subsequently, a multivariable logistic regression model was constructed to identify predictors of diagnostic success. To account for the events per variable (EPV) limitation inherent in our sample size (n=74), the model was restricted to key clinically relevant confounders bulky lymph node and the presence of necrosis. Odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated. Given the small number of failure events, the regression results are intended as exploratory, and wide CIs were interpreted as indicators of limited statistical precision. A P value <0.05 was considered significant. All statistical analyses were performed with Jamovi software version 2.3.28.0.

The primary outcome was diagnostic accuracy of the three techniques. Final diagnosis was established based on histopathological results obtained by EBUS-TBNA and, in cases of negative findings, confirmed by surgical pathology and/or clinical-radiological follow-up demonstrating disease stability or progression.

The secondary outcome was molecular adequacy, defined as the ability to successfully perform both next-generation sequencing and programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) immunohistochemical assessment on the same specimen. This endpoint was selected to reflect current clinical requirements in lung cancer management.

Results

Seventy-four patients with mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathies underwent EBUS-based sampling between April 2021 and January 2025, divided among conventional EBUS (n=26), CE-EBUS (n=23), and elastography (n=25). The male population was 61.5% in the EBUS group, 47.8% in the CE-EBUS group, and 56.0% in the elastography group, with comparable median ages of 70±9.2, 69.8±8.4, and 69±9 years, respectively. The majority were current or former smokers, while never-smokers represented a minority across all cohorts. Adenocarcinoma was the predominant histologic subtype (68% in EBUS, 38% in CE-EBUS, and 48% in elastography), followed by squamous cell carcinoma and small or large cell variants. Most patients presented with advanced disease: stages IIIC–IVB were observed in 16–34.6% of cases across the three groups. Tumors were mainly peripheral on CT (65% in both EBUS and CE-EBUS, 60% in elastography), and their size ranged between 5 and 7 cm in 38–48% of cases. PET-SUVmax values between 4 and 7.9 were most frequent (54% in EBUS, 48% in CE-EBUS, and 52% in elastography). Overall, demographic, radiologic, and metabolic profiles were largely comparable among the three diagnostic modalities (Table 1).

After the adjustment for bulky and necrotic lymph nodes, CE-EBUS appeared to provide a higher molecular assessment yield. However, the multivariable analysis yielded a wide 95% CI (1.38–58.44) for the OR. While the result was statistically significant (P<0.05), we acknowledge that this extreme imprecision reflects the small sample size and the low number of events in this subgroup. Therefore, the term “significant” should be interpreted with caution, as the true effect size could range from marginal to substantial. These findings should be considered hypothesis-generating rather than definitive.

Discussion

Accurate mediastinal staging continues to be important for surgical candidacy determination and systemic therapy decisions in patients with non-small-cell lung cancer (14-16). Recently, the utilization of EBUS for mediastinal staging in lung cancer has increased significantly due to its high diagnostic accuracy (17). In light of the advances in precision oncology, there has been greater emphasis on adequate tissue sampling during EBUS-TBNA as it will allow for greater molecular analysis profiling (18-21). CE-EBUS, which was first described by Dietrich et al. (3), continues to demonstrate that it is a feasible method for imaging microvascular perfusion and differentiating viable areas from necrotic areas. Similar to contrast-enhanced endoscopic ultrasound (CE-EUS) a procedure used in pancreatic or hepatic lesions, CE-EBUS utilizes real-time perfusion mapping for needle placement (22-25). Studies have reported that hypoenhanced areas correspond to necrosis while hyperenhanced areas represent viable tumor tissue which are principles that reinforce the diagnostic performance of CE-EBUS in our study (26-29). Taken together the current data supports the hypothesis that contrast enhancement delineating microvasculature areas could lead to potential improvement in biopsy sampling and adequate for molecular profiling, a metric that is becoming increasingly relevant in the age of targeted therapies and immunomodulation.

In contrast, elastography evaluates mechanical capacities rather than perfusion. Multiple studies (30-33) provide evidence that malignant lymph nodes often manifest as blue-preferring (stiff) zones associated with greater cellularity (Figure 2).

Huang et al. (34) showed that the use of elastography of EBUS in correlation with rapid on-site evaluation (ROSE) had a higher than 90% matching success rate (35,36). Our data is consistent with these findings. Thus, elastography alone showed acceptable accuracy; however, CE-EBUS may represent a potential more effective tool in detecting viable sampling sites in necrotic lymph nodes (37,38). Though the accuracy was not superior to traditional EBUS-TBNA, elastography yielded further mechanical property information about lymph nodes stiffness, allowing for differentiation between major fibrous and cellular content (39). The diagnostic performance (72.0%) of elastography in this series is similar to the study done by Patel in 2024 (40) using artificial intelligence techniques that reported an overall accuracy of 70.6%, a sensitivity (43.0%) and specificity (90.7%) prediction of malignancy based on stiffness to-area ratio (SAR). Preliminary evidence suggests that CE-EBUS may offer potential advantages over conventional EBUS-TBNA, particularly in lesions with central necrosis, where the risk of sampling error may be increased (41-44).

Molecular adequacy has emerged as a key performance metric, extending beyond simple histological confirmation, considering the therapeutic relevance of oncogenic alterations, including PD-L1 assessment (19,45,46). The simultaneous use of CE-EBUS and elastography benefits from their complementary nature; CE-EBUS identifies the enhanced vasculature within a lymph node and reveals viable areas while elastography provides quantitative measurements of stiffness. Prior studies have demonstrated that malignant lymph nodes are stiffer, as well as showing centripetal and heterogeneous enhancement, when studying microvessel architecture with explanations based upon density of tumor cells and neoangiogenesis within viable tumor foci (47-50). In these exploratory findings, subgroup analyses suggested that CE-EBUS might facilitate the identification of viable areas even within large necrotic lymph nodes. When all cases with available molecular matching were considered, nearly 80% demonstrated a successful molecular match. Elastography accounted for 62.5% of molecularly adequate samples, indicating a possible complementary role in supporting multi-pass sampling strategies.

The absence of statistically significant differences among the various modalities in logistic regression models may reflect broadly comparable performance of the three approaches when lymph nodes are categorized according to anatomical characteristics.

Nevertheless, in an exploratory context the relatively elevated OR observed for molecular adequacy in the necrotic/volume subgroup (OR 9.0) should be interpreted with caution, but may suggest a potential added value of CE-EBUS in more complex sampling contexts. Artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted elastography, conducted by Patel et al. in their analysis of elastographic images, demonstrated a high specificity [90.7], as well as an area under the curve of 0.82 for discriminating malignancy vs. benignity. Importantly, using automated stiffness quantification, facilitated by a deep neural network (NeuralSeg), allowed for the elimination of potential human observer bias from technician/investigator choice of region of interest (40). Our study, based on manual interpretation, is likely an underestimation of elastographic parameters, particularly in conjunction with real-time AI-based analysis. Moving forward, the combined use of CE-EBUS, in combination with automated elastographic segmentation, could provide simultaneous perfusion and stiffness data, ultimately optimizing both diagnostic accuracy and molecular accuracy. Limitations in our study included its single-center, retrospective design, and small sample size which limited multivariable modeling and overall statistical power for subgroups. Variability between operators and absence of quantitative elastography measurements are additional limitations. Future prospective multicenter studies could further validate our findings in the context of integrating CE-EBUS and elastography into a multimodal algorithm, potentially alongside real-time AI analysis to dynamically direct sampling by taking advantage of perfusion and stiffness mapping to allow even greater diagnostic accuracy. The quantitative perfusion and stiffness measurements could be incorporated into an automated single predictive model, to automatically determine sites most optimal for biopsy. In addition, taking advantage of the convenience of using a molecular suitability as a predetermined primary endpoint as opposed to a secondary endpoint could better reflect the current diagnostic emphasis.

Conclusions

Our findings suggest that CE-EBUS may be associated with an increased molecular yield in bulky or necrotic lymphadenopathies compared with standard EBUS, particularly in settings where sampling accuracy may be affected by tissue heterogeneity. From a clinical perspective, this could potentially translate into a reduced need for repeat procedures.

In addition, the combined use of CE-EBUS and elastography within the same procedure might enable a more dynamic targeting strategy, whereby CE-EBUS may help identify perfused, viable areas, while elastography could offer complementary information on tissue stiffness, potentially enhancing operator confidence in lesion representativeness.

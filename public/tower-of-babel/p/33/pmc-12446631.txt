Long-term disease-free survival of a patient diagnosed with peri-ampullary carcinoma treated with adjunct Ayurvedic treatment – A case report
Authors: Sadanand Sardeshmukh, Vineeta Deshmukh, Swapna Kulkarni, Vasanti Godse, Vinita Awalkanthe, Shreenivas Datar, Shweta Gujar, Sandeep Chavan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This is a case of a female Indian patient diagnosed as Peri-Ampullary Carcinoma, with metastasis to regional lymph nodes, and reported 5-year disease free survival rate of 20–50%, treated with integration of personalized Ayurvedic treatment along with conventional treatments viz., surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy.The patient was diagnosed with adenocarcinoma through biopsy from the ampulla of Vater during registration at our Centre in May 2010. She had yet to start her conventional treatment which was advised viz., surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy. In June 2010, she underwent Whipple's resection. Histopathology report showed moderately differentiated adenocarcinoma of the periampullary region with metastasis to regional lymph nodes — Completely resected (R0). According to TNM (Tumor Node Metastasis) Staging System as per Union for International Cancer Control (UICC- 6th edition) patient was staged as pT3N1- Stage IIB. After completion of conventional treatment (surgery and chemotherapy) in October 2010, she continued with Ayurvedic treatment and follow-ups till date. Her tumour marker CA19.9 and sonography – abdomen, pelvis after 12 years revealed no evidence of malignancy. In May 2017, she was hospitalized for hematemesis and melena, diagnosed as mild gastritis with clean base ulcers at the surgical anastomosed (Gastrojejunostomy) site. She was treated accordingly with Ayurvedic medicines and her follow-up report showed the resolution of ulcers by September 2017. In May 2014, her mildly raised blood sugar (Fasting 138 mg/dL; Post-Prandial 146 mg/dL) was managed by a healthy lifestyle but in 2019, her blood glucose levels were moderately raised (Fasting 153 mg/dL, Post-Prandial 187 mg/dL) hence she was started on oral hypoglycemics which are continued till date.Though the pancreaticobiliary subtype is associated with poor prognosis, it was possible to achieve 12 years and 6 months of disease-free survival with adjunct Oral Ayurvedic Medicines (OAM) possessing antioxidant, immune-modulatory, anti-cancer, and hepato-protective action. She could tolerate chemotherapy with minimal side effects. Her quality of life improved. Her blood sugar levels have been maintained by lifestyle changes and oral hypoglycemics.

Introduction

Ampullary cancer arising from Ampulla of Vater is rare and its prevalence is 6% of the cancers arising in the periampullary area [1]. The most frequent sites of metastasis are lung, lymph nodes, liver, peritoneum, kidney, bone, and rarely the skin. Over all 5-year survival rate is 27.7% [2]. Factors such as higher tumor stage, deep invasion of the tumor, positive lymph nodes, involvement of blood vessel and lymphatics and raised levels of CA 19-9 (higher than 37 U/L) are factors affecting long-term prognosis in carcinomas of the periampullary region [3]. Lymph node involvement is an important factor in determining the overall prognosis. The five-year survival rate varies for positive or negative status of disease. In negative nodes survival is between 70% and 80% as compared to 20%–50% for node-positive disease after completing conventional treatment [4]. Patients with ≥2 positive lymph node metastases had a poor survival [5].

A study conducted to assess long term effects of surgery viz., type of pancreatectomy and its effects on pancreatic function reported consequences which included unintentional weight loss, insulin dependence, post-surgery repeated hospitalization for fluctuating blood glucose levels, gastrointestinal problems and abdominal operations like hernia. Long-term medical complication was reported in more than half of patients after surgery while those who underwent Whipple's surgery were affected more [6].

The present case is of periampullary carcinoma, diagnosed at later age (60 years) with raised tumour marker CA19.9 (168.93 U/mL). She was advised for surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy. Her surgery went uneventful but she had metastatic lymph nodes which could lead to early recurrence or disease progression. Hence, the need of an hour was to make her conventional treatment hurdle free so that her treatment schedule will not hamper, which might help to prevent the disease progression, or recurrence. Further, it will extend Disease-Free Survival (DFS) and maintain good quality of life. This case report highlights the use of Ayurvedic treatment (Shamana, Rasayana chikitsa) to alleviate the side effects of conventional treatment, achieve uninterrupted therapeutic schedule, increase survival and also improve the quality of life.

Patient information

A case of female patient age 60 years, diagnosed with Periampullary carcinoma registered in our cancer OPD in May 2010. She had undergone a biopsy (May 3, 2010) which showed Adenocarcinoma. She has a family history of Diabetes Mellitus among her siblings (1 brother, 1 sister) and uterine cancer to her mother at the age of 54 years. She had familial and financial stress prior to diagnosis.

Clinical findings

She enrolled and started Ayurvedic treatment at our center on May 7, 2010 after being diagnosed with peri-ampullary adenocarcinoma and was advised surgery. She had complaints of weakness, reduced appetite, and constipation while her weight was 55 kg at the time of enrolment.

Timeline of health events and treatment

The patient was diagnosed with adenocarcinoma through a biopsy of the mass at the ampulla of Vater done on May 3, 2010. After enrolment in our centre (after starting Ayurvedic treatment), she underwent planned surgery Pancreatico-Duodenectomy on June 4, 2010. Post-surgery she underwent 4 cycles of chemotherapy with Inj. Gemcitabine 1560 mg in a protocol of Day 1—Day 8 -– Day 15 from June 2010 to October 2010. She continued with the Ayurvedic treatment post-conventional treatment. In May 2017, she suffered from clean base ulcers at the anastomoses (Gastrojejunostomy) site which were resolved by Ayurvedic intervention. She is free from recurrence to date. Therapeutic interventions

Oral Ayurvedic Medicine (OAM) protocols were administered according to the status of the patient at various stages (Supplementary File 1 Table S1). Along with OAM she was also given dietary and lifestyle guidelines. She was advised to take home-made light to digest food, especially green gram dal, roasted rice, rice flakes, millets, flat bread (Bhakari), cow's milk, cow's ghee, fresh fruits and vegetables, condiments like cumin seeds, coriander seeds, ginger, garlic, mint leaves, and rock salt. She was advised to avoid curds, excess of sweet food items, green chilies, fermented food, bakery products, hot, pungent and deep-fried food. She was advised to listen to Indian soft music for mental relaxation, short strolls in fresh air, avoid daytime sleep but to get up early in the morning and go to sleep early at night. Prognosis of the patient

This patient was diagnosed with periampullary carcinoma at the age of 60 years. She had a lymph node positive cancer which is a negative factor in prognosis showing 20%–50% survival [4].

Follow-ups

Initially, the patient had monthly twice follow-ups, and later on monthly or quarterly follow-ups. Patient's compliance to OAM was satisfactory as the patient and her relatives were aware of the poor prognosis of the disease despite of completing conventional treatment. She adhered to all the instructions and diet advice during the course. Patient would be reluctant to perform periodic investigations and much coaxing was needed which was the only hurdle faced.

She did not have any adverse events after long administration of Ayurvedic medicines, which is confirmed in view of normal values of Haemogram, Liver Function Test and Kidney Function Test throughout the treatment period.

Diagnostic assessment and outcome

Patient had side effects of chemotherapy namely weakness (Grade 2), constipation (Grade 1) and loss of appetite (Grade 2) (Table 2). She tolerated chemotherapy with minimal side-effects and her weight was maintained during chemotherapy and gradually increased. Only after last chemotherapy cycle she underwent a 1-pint blood transfusion due to anemia.

In 2014, she was detected with borderline raised blood sugar which was managed by diet and lifestyle modification for up to 5 years which included morning walk, regular diet timings and reduction in sweet and fried food. In 2017, she was hospitalized for hematemesis and melena, diagnosed as mild gastritis due to clean-based ulcers at the anastomosed (Gastrojejunostomy) site which was resolved by medicines. In 2019, in view of raised blood sugar, she was started with oral hypoglycemic drugs which are continued to date.

Her tumour marker (CA 19.9) and Sonography – Abdomen and Pelvis indicated cancer-free status after 12 years. Discussion

The major concern, in this case, was increasing her immunity to avoid consequences of conventional treatment (short-term and long-term) and the reported prognosis of periampullary cancer. The oral Ayurvedic medicines selected were mainly immunomodulatory (Rasayana), anti-oxidant and hepato-protective which would help improve digestion and assimilation; thus, aiding the metabolism since abdominal discomfort and diabetes are some of the consequences of the conservative treatment.

Throughout her treatment protocols, Kamdudha rasa (Mauktikyukta) was consistently administered. Her 1st set of Ayurvedic medicines was administered with an objective to reduce the side effects of chemotherapy viz., myelosuppression, Transaminase elevation, mild proteinuria, hematuria, headache, dizziness, etc. [7] in this case. Her medicines were Suvarna Makshikyukta Kamduddha (SMKD) and Mauktikyukta Praval Panchamruta with half a cup of milk adding a teaspoon of cow ghee (clarified butter) and Gorakhchinchavaleha. SMKD is a proprietary medicine which is a combination of Suvarnamakshik Bhasma (incinerated chalcopyrite) [8], 21/26–28; [9] and Kamdudha Rasa [10][8], 23/139–141] [11], Part II; [12]. This formulation has anti-inflammatory, hematinic, and immunomodulatory actions. Gorakhachincha (Adansonia digitata) has hepatoprotective and antioxidant activity [13]. Praval Panchamrut (Mauktikyukta) is beneficial in lack of appetite, indigestion, excessive belching, flatulence, colitis, diarrhea, diabetes, difficulty in micturition, and renal calculus [14] (see details in Supplementary File 2 Table S2).

These medicines helped her to complete her chemotherapy course in a stipulated time without alteration in dosage or any major events. Moreover, she did not develop drug resistance during the course.

Post-chemotherapy her treatment aimed at improving her metabolism thereby her immunity and strengthening her digestive system. She had complaints of itching over the body [15], chronic constipation and weakness [16] denoting derangement of Pittadosha. In her 2nd set of medicines Kamdudha Vati (Mauktikyukta), Praval Pishti Vati, Shatavari Kalpa, Gorakhchinchavaleha and Sukhsarak Vati were given with water. Praval Pishti Vati is beneficial in acidity, gastritis, bleeding disorders, non-productive cough, and general debility [[10], Part I]. Osteoporosis is one of the long-term consequences of conventional treatment. Praval Pishti is a natural source of minerals required for the treatment of osteoporosis [17] (see details in Supplementary File 2). Shatavari has potential antioxidant actions and is used to treat debility [18], 3/184–187; [19]. Sukhasarak Vati is traditionally used as a laxative, digestive, blood purifier, and for liver disorders. It exhibits antioxidant and α-Amylase inhibition activity under in vitro conditions [18], 3/289; [20].

In June 2014, she was detected with mildly raised blood sugar. In her 3rd set of medicines, Aloe Plus Tab was started along with the earlier medicines viz. Kamdudha Vati (Mauktikyukta), Praval Pishti Vati and Sukhsarak Vati. Aloe Plus Tab is effective in improving appetite and digestion. Aloe vera possesses hepatoprotective, anti-inflammatory, and anti-oxidant activity and also improves insulin sensitivity [18],3/229–230; [21].

In 2017, when she was hospitalized for hematemesis and melena, she complained fatigue, heaviness in her head, a burning sensation in the body and uneasiness. Her GI endoscopy showed mild gastritis, and clean-base ulcers at the anastomosed site (Forrest class III) (Table 3). After her discharge, she came for a follow-up at our center where she was started with 4th set of medicines viz., Suvarna Sutshekhar Rasa, Kamduddha Vati (Mauktikyukta), Praval Pishti Vati, Yashtimadhu Ghrut and Prasham tablet with water. Considering her episodes of hematemesis and melena, which according to Ayurveda are symptoms of aggravated Pitta dosha, she was started on Suvarna Sutshekhar rasa. This formulation is beneficial in hyperacidity (Amlapitta) [22]. It helped to rejuvenate, improve digestion [23] and reduce inflammation [24,25]. Yashtimadhu Ghruta was administered to reduce ulcerations and inflammation [18], 1/145–146; [26]. Tablet Prasham was prescribed to reduce anxiety and maintain a positive attitude[18], 3/281; [27].

In 2019, her blood sugar levels were raised and she was started on oral anti-diabetic medication.

She started with 5th set of medicines consisting of Suvarna Sutshekhar Rasa, Kamduddha (Mauktikyukta), Aloe Plus tab, Madhusudan vati, and Gandharva Haritaki. The ingredients of Madhusudan Vati possess antidiabetic, anti-inflammatory, hepatoprotective, immunomodulatory (rasayana), antioxidant, antimutagenic, antibacterial and anti-cancerous pharmacological activities [[28], [29], [30], [31]]. Considering her chronic complaint of constipation, she was treated with Gandharva Haritaki which has a laxative action [18], 1/20–21, 19/23–25; [32]. It also exhibits antidiabetic, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, insecticidal, hepatoprotective, and wound-healing activities [33].

In September 2022, she has been started on 6th set of medicines viz., Vasant Kusumakar Rasa, Kamduddha (Mauktikyukta), Aarogyavardhini Vati, Aamalaki vati and Methika-Ajmoda powder with warm water to rejuvenate the dhatus (tissues) and thus improving the tissue metabolism. Vasant Kusumakar rasa is a herbo-mineral-metallic formulation which possesses rejuvenating and immunomodulatory (rasayana) properties having action on all the tissues of the body. It is nervine tonic, cardioprotective, and affects digestive and respiratory system. It is beneficial in Diabetes [10], Part IV; [14,34]. Aarogyavardhini Vati kindles appetite, improves digestion, beneficial in heart diseases, obesity and skin disorders (Kushtha) and has hepatoprotective action [10], Part II; [35]. Amalaki is an antioxidant and possesses anti-cancer activity [18], 1/38–40; [25]; [28]. Methika and Ajwain are anti-oxidant, digestive, antispasmodic, anti-inflammatory; reduces blood sugar, and possesses anti-cancer properties [18],1/78–79, 93–95; [36,37].

In this case, the patient has good DFS. The review of literature regarding the achievement of disease-free survival suggests that pancreatic adenocarcinoma carries the worst prognosis. Studies have shown that pancreatic carcinoma has the shortest median survival of 17.1 months of all periampullary carcinomas. With the development of metastasis, the median survival tends to reduce to 3–6 months. A case report of metastatic periampullary pancreatic adenocarcinoma that received minimal and interrupted chemotherapy due to poor tolerance managed 17 months of survival [38]. Another study stated that among the patients of periampullary adenocarcinoma with resected margins, resected lymph node status and degree of the tumour differentiation significantly influence the long-term outcome. Particularly, in pancreatic adenocarcinoma, 5 years of survival is not corresponded with a cure, because many patients die of recurrent disease>5 years after resection [39]. In the reported study of 137 patients, who underwent pancreaticoduodenectomy (PD) for periampullary adenocarcinoma (PAAC), it was found that the median survival across all patients was 33 months; 26 months for patients with PDAC (5-year survival was 24.3%), and 37 months for ampullary adenocarcinoma (5-year survival was 42.1%) [40].

Long-term effects of surgery could be controlled. Though she was diagnosed with Diabetes, it could be managed initially through lifestyle changes. Later she was started on oral anti-diabetic medications and is still managed on oral glycemic as opposed to insulin.

The assessments of the symptoms and well-being from patient's perspective, showed improvement in Agni (digestive power), Bala (physical strength), Utsaha (vigour/energy), Sharira swasthya (daily activity strength) and Manasa swasthya (cognitive health). Her body weight and KPS, both improved consistently during the course. Her quality of life scores evaluation (C-30, PAN-26), presented with improvement in physical, role and social functioning and other scores like emotional and cognitive functioning are maintained. While, symptom scores exhibited improvement in fatigue, pain, appetite loss, and constipation. However, flatulence and bloating did not show marked improvement, this could be due to her surgical consequence [41]. Overall, her higher functional and global score and lower symptoms score have shown a good response to Ayurvedic treatment (Table 2). Due to her delicate body constitution (Sukumar prakruti) she could not be administered Panchakarma treatment which could have given more enhanced effect.

While treating such patients, it is important to consider the strength of the patient and disease (rugna bala and vyadhi bala), appetite (agni) and also the side-effects of conventional treatment being given to the patient.

Patient perspective

"I am a cancer patient, age 73 years. In the year 2010, I had a fever for which I did investigations. The doctor diagnosed it as jaundice and treated it accordingly, but I did not get relief. I consulted at Ruby Hospital where I was diagnosed with Pancreatic cancer. I went to Tata Hospital for an opinion where I was advised to undergo surgery within one month. After diagnosis, I met Dr SPS and started on Ayurvedic medicines. I benefited from the medicines during my chemotherapy treatment. I did not incur any side effects. After completion of my chemotherapy, I continued with the Ayurvedic medicines. It is 12 years since I started my treatment. I benefited from it."

Conclusion

The present case of pancreaticobiliary subtype is associated with poor prognosis, but the patient managed to sustain a good quality of life and disease-free survival of 12 years 6 months. Along with cancer control, she could complete her chemotherapy course as per schedule without alteration in dosage or any major events, she experienced minimal side-effects of chemotherapy, could manage with co-morbidities like diabetes and gastritis. Remarkable improvement is seen in Quality of Life (QoL) with OAM possessing hepatoprotective, anti-oxidant, immunomodulatory and anti-cancer action.

This type of integrated treatment can prove beneficial to patients diagnosed with peri-ampullary carcinoma and can be studied in a broader population.

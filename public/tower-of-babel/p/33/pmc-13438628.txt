Parameter calibration of finite element model of reinforced concrete arch bridge based on ISOA-RBF neural network
Authors: Wenping Peng, Zujun Zhang, Zhongchu Tian, Jijin Zhang, Guoliang Zeng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo achieve efficient and accurate finite element (FE) model updating for reinforced concrete (RC) arch bridges, this study proposes a novel methodology that integrates an Improved Seagull Optimization Algorithm (ISOA) with a Radial Basis Function (RBF) neural network. First, the base Seagull Optimization Algorithm (SOA) is enhanced through Logistics chaotic mapping and an adaptive attack strategy, forming the ISOA to improve search efficiency and convergence. Second, leveraging the superior nonlinear fitting capability of the RBF neural network, a surrogate model is constructed to map the relationship between key structural parameters and the corresponding static/dynamic responses of the bridge, thereby replacing the computationally expensive full FE model during iterative optimization. Finally, a multi-objective optimization model is formulated by combining static deflections (from arch T-beams) and modal frequencies, enabling a holistic FE model update based on in-situ load test data. The proposed ISOA-RBF framework is applied to a long-span RC arch bridge case study. Results demonstrate that: (1) the ISOA effectively solves the inverse optimization problem in FE model updating; (2) the RBF surrogate model achieves high approximation accuracy, with an average relative error of only 0.62% across 50 validation samples; (3) after updating, discrepancies between computed and measured T-beam deflections are significantly reduced, showing average relative errors below 10% under two static load cases; (4) errors in the first three modal frequencies are less than 5%, with good orthogonality maintained among modes and no modal mixing observed, confirming consistency in dynamic characteristics before and after model updating.Keywords: Reinforced concrete arch bridge, Finite element model updating, ISOA-RBF neural network, Surrogate model, Load testSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Natural hazards

Introduction

In the field of civil engineering structure analysis, reinforced concrete arch bridges play a pivotal role in bridge construction due to their elegant morphology and excellent spanning capacity. However, facing complex and variable operating environments and increasing traffic loads, ensuring the safety, durability, and economy of arch bridges has become a critical challenge requiring immediate attention from engineers. As an important tool for predicting structural responses, the accuracy of finite element analysis models directly determines the reliability of analysis results. Nevertheless, due to the complexity of arch bridge structures and the diversity of operating environments, finite element models established based on design data often exhibit limitations and certain deviations when simulating their actual mechanical behaviors1–3. Therefore, in-depth updating and optimization of finite element models for reinforced concrete arch bridges have become a key link in improving the level of bridge engineering design and evaluation.

Regarding the finite element model updating of reinforced concrete arch bridges, although numerous studies have achieved remarkable progress, many challenges remain in practical applications. Wang Q S et al.4 proposed a finite element model updating method for long-span suspension bridges based on the substructure-based RBF Neural Network and Sparrow Search Algorithm (SSA). By establishing the coupled response surface of suspension bridge substructures, efficient and accurate updating of the finite element model was realized. To address the problem of large structural computation in finite element model updating, He Z H et al.5 proposed a finite element model updating method combined with the Support Vector Regression (SVR) response surface. Taking a flat plate as the research object, the superiority of the surrogate model in updating problems was verified. Zhu L M et al.6 conducted finite element model updating for extradosed cable-stayed bridges by capturing the mechanical response behaviors of the bridge under different load cases through load tests, thereby achieving the parameter updating of the finite element model. Mei C et al.7 proposed a finite element model updating method for continuous rigid-frame bridges based on the Kriging surrogate model, which avoids the low efficiency of traditional updating methods. Yang W F et al.8 established the optimal RBF Neural Network surrogate model for long-span suspension bridges and performed dynamic characteristic updating of the finite element model using bridge dynamic response data obtained from vibration monitoring systems, effectively improving the computational accuracy of the finite element model. To address the structural damage of medium and small-span bridges, Qin F9 proposed a finite element model updating method based on deflection influence line residuals, realizing structural damage localization and finite element model updating of hollow slab bridges.

From the above research, it can be concluded that in recent years, machine learning methods have demonstrated significant advantages in the fields of system identification, damage diagnosis, and model updating within structural engineering10,11. Compared with traditional methods, neural networks, due to their powerful nonlinear mapping capabilities and potential for handling high-dimensional data, can effectively establish surrogate models between input parameters and output responses in complex structural systems. This effectively circumvents the high computational cost associated with direct finite element analysis, offering a viable path for efficient model updating. In the specific domain of model updating, architectures such as Feedforward Neural Networks and Radial Basis Function (RBF) networks have been explored to construct structural response surfaces and have been integrated with various optimization algorithms to solve inverse problems. However, existing applications still face common challenges. On one hand, the accuracy and generalization capability of neural network surrogate models are heavily dependent on the quality and quantity of training samples12,13. On the other hand, most studies treat the neural network merely as an independent fitting tool, lacking in-depth synergistic mechanisms with optimization algorithms.

Building upon this, and to address the aforementioned challenges for achieving efficient and high-accuracy finite element model updating of reinforced concrete arch bridges, this paper proposes a novel updating framework that deeply integrates an Improved Seagull Optimization Algorithm (ISOA) with a Radial Basis Function (RBF) neural network. This research not only leverages the RBF neural network to construct a parameter-response surrogate model for the arch bridge to enhance computational efficiency but, more importantly, focuses on improving the base SOA by introducing Logistic chaotic mapping and an adaptive attack strategy. This improvement aims to enhance the global exploration and local exploitation capabilities of the optimization process, thereby addressing issues in traditional methods such as susceptibility to local optima and slow convergence within complex, nonlinear search spaces. Ultimately, a multi-objective optimization model incorporating both the static deflection of the arch T-beams and the global modal frequencies of the structure is established. This model is solved using the proposed ISOA-RBF method, aiming to provide a novel and effective approach for the refined model updating of this specific bridge type.

Project overview and finite element model

Project overview and finite element model

The superstructure of the main bridge of Shatuo Bridge is a reinforced concrete box arch bridge with a main span of 240 m, and the substructure consists of reinforced concrete arch abutments. The design speed of the carriageway is 40 km/h, and the vehicle load class adopts Highway Class-I. The main arch ring of the bridge is a constant-height catenary reinforced concrete box section, with a clear span of 240 m, a clear rise of 40 m, a clear rise-span ratio of 1/6, an arch axis coefficient m = 1.85, and a box section width of 10 m and height of 4.5 m. The main arch ring was constructed using the cantilever casting method with hanging baskets. Seagull optimization algorithm based on adaptive behavior

The Seagull Optimization Algorithm (SOA) is a novel swarm intelligence optimization algorithm proposed by Gaurav Dhiman and Vijay Kumar in 201814–16. This algorithm mainly simulates the migration of seagulls in nature and their foraging behavior during the migration process. Through these two behaviors, it balances the global search capability and local search capability of the algorithm, and has the advantages of strong optimization ability and a large number of adjustable parameters, as shown in Fig. 4. In this paper, improvements are made on the standard Seagull Optimization Algorithm, and the improved algorithm is adopted to optimize and solve the finite element model updating parameters.

Although the standard Seagull Optimization Algorithm (SOA) exhibits high convergence speed in terms of theoretical performance, optimization models in practical engineering problems are all nonlinear multimodal problems. When applied to practical engineering problems, the standard SOA still suffers from issues such as low convergence efficiency and premature convergence of the population. Therefore, this paper improves some execution strategies in the seagull optimization algorithm to enhance its adaptability in engineering problems, thereby meeting the performance requirements for parameter optimization of finite element models17–19.

Establishment of response surface for arch bridge based on RBF neural network

During the finite element model parameter updating, it is often necessary to constantly call the finite element model for repeated iterative calculations of parameters under different combinations. This process involves a large computational load and high time cost. To improve the computational speed of the finite element model under different parameter combinations and quickly obtain the structural response characteristics under various parameter combinations, this paper adopts the RBF Neural Network to establish the structural surrogate model of reinforced concrete arch bridges. Through the neural network algorithm, the nonlinear mapping relationship between the random variables affecting the structural response of reinforced concrete arch bridges and their structural response characteristics is fitted, replacing the repeated iterative solution process of the finite element model in the parameter optimization process and improving the efficiency of finite element model parameter updating.

Finite element model correction based on ISOA-RBF neural network

To realize the finite element model parameter updating of in-service reinforced concrete arch bridges, based on the improved seagull optimization algorithm (ISOA) proposed in this paper and the response surface modeling theory of the RBF Neural Network, the problem of finite element model parameter updating for reinforced concrete arch bridges is transformed into an optimization problem with the minimum error between the finite element calculation values and on-site measured values under vehicle loads. A mathematical optimization model for the finite element model parameter updating of reinforced concrete arch bridges based on the ISOA-RBF Neural Network is established.

Revision process

Based on the RBF Neural Network structural surrogate model and the improved seagull optimization algorithm (ISOA), the parameter updating process for the finite element model of reinforced concrete arch bridges is designed. The seagull population is used to optimize and solve each design update parameter controlled by the objective function. The RBF Neural Network is adopted to predict the structural response after each iteration, which is compared with the measured structural response values. The parameters are continuously updated to achieve accurate fitting between the predicted structural response and the measured response. The finite element model parameter updating process based on the RBF Neural Network and ISOA is shown in Fig. 6, with the implementation steps as follows.

Process of finite element model correction based on ISOA-RBF neural network.

Step 1: Establish the initial finite element model. An initial finite element structural model of the reinforced concrete arch bridge is established according to the design drawings and design material parameters, serving as the basic model for finite element model parameter updating.

Step 2: Train the RBF Neural Network structural surrogate model. The Latin hypercube sampling method is used to generate RBF Neural Network training sample points. With the mean value of random variables as the center, the sample points have a value range of (µ-3δ,µ + 3δ). A total of 100 sets of sample points are selected to form the training sample set, which is trained and fitted by the RBF Neural Network. The fitting error of the surrogate model is set to 0.01. If the training accuracy meets the requirements, the RBF Neural Network parameter model is output; if not, the linear interpolation method is used to expand the sample points and the training and fitting are performed again.

Step 3: Initialize ISOA parameters. According to the finite element model parameter updating mathematical model in Eq. (18), the ISOA parameters are initialized. The maximum number of iterations of the algorithm is set to 200, the population size is 50, and the dimension is 6. The 0th generation population position is generated by random initialization, and then the 0th generation population is subjected to chaotic mapping operation according to the Logistics chaotic mapping formula to generate the initial population position.

Step 4: Algorithm iteration and optimization. Based on the objective function in Eq. (18), the RBF Neural Network surrogate model is used to calculate the fitness value of the seagull population and the optimal population position. The population position is updated through nonlinear migration and adaptive attack. It is judged whether the algorithm reaches the maximum number of iterations. If yes, the optimal position of the seagull population is output, and the optimal parameter combination for finite element model updating is decoded and output; if not, the population fitness value is recalculated and the population position update operation is performed again.

The coupling effect of the two makes the model more consistent with the actual structural response. As the main load-bearing structure, the arch ring has a small parameter updating amplitude, indicating that the arch ring has good integrity, is in a relatively healthy service state, and its mechanical properties are closer to the design assumptions. The ISOA algorithm achieves reasonable parameter calibration through iterative optimization, and the updated data are consistent with the time-dependent deterioration law of reinforced concrete materials, laying a precise numerical model foundation for the subsequent analysis of structural static and dynamic characteristics.

Conclusion

To address the problems of large computational load and high updating difficulty in the finite element model updating of reinforced concrete arch bridges, a finite element model updating method based on the RBF Neural Network and improved seagull optimization algorithm (ISOA) is proposed. The RBF Neural Network is adopted to fit the characteristics of the structural response surface, and a mathematical optimization model for finite element parameters is established by combining the deflection response of T-beams on the arch and the main modal frequencies of reinforced concrete arch bridges. The improved seagull optimization algorithm is used for solving, and the conclusions are as follows:

The parameter updating problem of reinforced concrete finite element models can be transformed into a multi-objective parameter optimization problem with the goal of minimizing response error. A solving method based on the improved seagull optimization algorithm is proposed. Compared with the standard algorithm, the improved seagull optimization algorithm has better optimization performance and faster convergence speed for the mathematical optimization problem of finite element model parameter updating.

The parameter updating problem of reinforced concrete finite element models can be transformed into a multi-objective parameter optimization problem with the goal of minimizing response error. A solving method based on the improved seagull optimization algorithm is proposed. Compared with the standard algorithm, the improved seagull optimization algorithm has better optimization performance and faster convergence speed for the mathematical optimization problem of finite element model parameter updating.

A surrogate model construction method for reinforced concrete arch bridges based on the RBF Neural Network is proposed. By fitting the mapping relationship between the random variables and structural responses of reinforced concrete arch bridges using the RBF Neural Network, a response surface model of reinforced concrete arch bridges based on the RBF Neural Network is established. The average relative error of 50 sample points is only 0.62%, indicating high fitting accuracy. The surrogate model significantly reduces the program’s computational load during the finite element model updating process and greatly improves the model updating efficiency.

After updating, the average relative errors of the two static load conditions are both less than 10%.

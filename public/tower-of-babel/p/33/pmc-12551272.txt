Prevalence and clustering trends association of modifiable cardiovascular disease risk factors and ASCVD risk change in ethnic areas of the Southwest China plateau: progressively increasing sex disparity
Authors: Yu Xia, Wenlong Zhu, Linhong Pang, Mingjing Tang, Li Yang, Kai Bai, Yue Gao, Lin Duo, Zhiling Luo, Liping He, Jiang Lu, Da Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe purpose of the study was to determine the prevalence and clustering changes of cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk factors, the atherosclerotic cardiovascular diseases (ASCVD) risk change in ethnic minority areas of the Southwest China Plateau from the perspective of sex disparity.MethodsThis study conducted two cross-sectional studies in Yunnan Province, China, in 2018 and 2024, respectively, enrolling 1,197 and 1,047 participants aged 35–75 years with no history of CVD. The China-PAR model was used to assess participants’ 10-year risk of ASCVD. Changes in the prevalence and clustering of 9 CVD risk factors were described. Multivariate-adjusted Poisson regression by robust was used to calculate the association between the number of CVD risk factors and high risk of ASCVD.ResultsCompared with 2018, the age-standardized prevalence of modifiable metabolic CVD risk factors such as hypertension (22.4% to 41.7%), central obesity (24.2% to 39.1%), and overweight and obesity (11.2% to 15.1%) has risen rapidly; the increase among males was much higher than among females, while the age-standardized prevalence of modifiable behavioral CVD risk factors has declined. There was no significant change in the clustering number of CVD risk factors, but there was a significant change in the combination of CVD risk factors, particularly a significant increase associated with hypertension. The proportion of individuals classified as being at high risk for ASCVD also increased significantly (6.7% vs 10.6%). For each CVD risk factor, the high risk of ASCVD increased by 5%, with a higher risk among males and 55–75 years.ConclusionFrom 2018 to 2024, modifiable metabolic CVD risk factors increased substantially in high-altitude ethnic minority regions of Southwest China. Comprehensive lifestyle interventions should be implemented in the future to promote the prevention and control of ASCVD risk and gradually reduce sex disparity.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-025-05241-2.

Background

“Global Burden of Cardiovascular Disease Report” in 2019 reported that about 523 million people suffered from cardiovascular disease (CVD) worldwide, a doubling increase from 1990, and 4.58 million people died from CVD, making it the first cause of death and disability in the world [1]. 80% of these deaths occurred in low-and middle-income countries, and 58% occurred in Asia [2, 3]. With the rapid progress of social and economic development and population aging, the burden of CVD in China was rising rapidly [4]. 61% of CVD events in China resulted from atherosclerotic cardiovascular diseases (ASCVD) such as stroke and myocardial infarction [5]. Previous studies have shown that hypertension [6, 7], smoking [8, 9], alcohol consumption [3, 10], overweight and obesity [11, 12], diabetes [13, 14], inadequate intake of vegetables and fruits [15, 16], insufficient physical activity [17], central obesity [18, 19] were the main and modifiable risk factors for ASCVD. These factors were strongly associated with the emergence of adverse outcomes such as acute myocardial infarction, stroke, heart failure, and CVD death [20]. About 70% of all CVD events are attributable to these modifiable risk factors, and the clustering of CVD risk factors exacerbates the risk of adverse outcomes [21]. Evidence suggests that at least 80% of CVD events can be prevented by improving unhealthy lifestyles and related risk factors [22]. Therefore, the study on risk factors as well as the clustering association of CVD and ASCVD risk change was important.

Previous studies have reported that two or more CVD risk factors clustered in the population were common simultaneously, especially in males [23, 24]. Some studies have further described the association between the clustering or combination of individual CVD risk factors and the risk of CVD [25]. This study is the first to highlight the prevalence and clustering changes of CVD risk factors in ethnic minority areas in the southwestern plateau of China from a sex perspective, and to assess the association between the clustering of CVD risk factors and high risk of ASCVD. The results of this study will provide a reference for comprehensive intervention and prevention of CVD risk factors and ASCVD in ethnic minority areas in the plateau regions in the future.

Methods

Data sources and study population

Between 2017–2018 and 2023–2024, two cross-sectional surveys were conducted in Shangri-La City, Diqing Tibetan Autonomous Prefecture, Yunnan Province, China, where ethnic minorities account for 89.4% of the city’s population and the mean altitude exceeds 2,500 m, respectively, while ethnic minorities account for only 8.9% of the total population in China. The first cross-sectional survey data were obtained from the “Yunnan Province Adult Chronic Disease and Nutrition Monitoring Survey,” using a multistage stratified random sampling method. First, Shangri-La City was divided into streets and townships, and one street and three townships within Shangri-La City were selected through simple random sampling. Second, two resident/village committees were randomly selected from each street and township. Then, two resident/village groups were randomly selected from each resident/village committee. Finally, 97 households residing in the area for more than six months and with members aged 18 years or older were randomly selected from each resident/village group as survey participants.

The second cross-sectional survey data were obtained from the “Yunnan Province High-Altitude Population Cardiovascular Disease Risk Factor Survey.” A multistage stratified random sampling method was used to divide Shangri-La City into streets and townships. First, one street and one township were randomly selected. Second, four resident/village committees were selected from each street and township. Finally, 150 residents aged 35 to 75 years who had resided in the area for more than six months were selected from each resident/village committee as survey participants. A concise English version of the two surveys can be found in the supplementary materials.

Data collection and measurement

Two cross-sectional surveys were conducted in township health centers. The survey teams were composed of trained physicians, nurses and medical students. Questionnaires, physical examination and blood sample tests were carried out for the research subjects. Both survey questionnaires collected demographic characteristics, lifestyle (Current smoking, Current drinking, physical activity, vegetable and fruit intake), family history of CVD and cerebrovascular disease. Height is measured using a metal pillar stadiometer with an accuracy of 0.1 cm; weight is measured using an electronic weighing scale with an accuracy of 0.1 kg; waist circumference is measured using a soft tape measure with a length of 0–200 cm and an accuracy of 0.1 cm, following the measurement methods specified in the WHO STEPS Manual [26]. Body mass index was calculated as weight in kg divided by height in meters squared (kg/m2). After participants rested in a seated position for 5 min, blood pressure (BP) was measured twice on the right upper arm using an Omron HBP-1300 electronic blood pressure monitor. The two measurements were taken at least 1 min apart. If the difference between the two measurements exceeded 10 mm Hg, a third measurement was taken, and the analysis was based on the average of the three measurements. Blood samples from the participants were collected after at least 8 h of fasting and analyzed in the local hospital laboratory using the same standardized methods to measure total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), and fasting blood glucose. All the above results were entered into the iPad data collection system, and the quality control team conducted spot checks. All above results were entered into the iPad data acquisition system, and the quality control team conducted spot checks.

The data from the two surveys were combined into one database for analysis. Samples with a previous history of CVD and missing ASCVD risk assessment variables were excluded. Participants under 35 years were excluded. Finally, 1197 and 1047 people were included in 2018 and 2024, respectively. The specific inclusion process is shown in Fig. 1.

Definitions

Cardiovascular-related 9 kinds of risk factors definition: hypertension was defined as mean systolic blood pressure (SBP) ≥ 140 mmHg and/or mean diastolic blood pressure (DBP) ≥ 90 mmHg, or taking an antihypertensive drug [27]. Current smoking was the people who had smoked daily for the 30 days before the survey [28]. Current drinking was at least 1 drink per week in the past year [29]. Insufficient physical activity was weekly moderate or high intensity physical activity ≤ 150 min or < 75 min [27]. Consumption of vegetables and fruits less than 3 days/month was defined as insufficient intake of vegetables and fruits [28]. Body mass index (BMI) was defined [27]: BMI from 24.0 ~ 27.9 kg/m2 as overweight, and BMI ≥ 28 kg/m2 as obesity. Diabetes mellitus was defined as fasting glucose ≥ 7.0 mmol/L or a history of diabetes mellitus [27]. Dyslipidemia is the presence of one or more of the following four conditions: LDL-C ≥ 4.1 mmol/L, HDL-C < 1.0 mmol/L, TC ≥ 6.2mmol/L, TG ≥ 2.3 mmol/L [30], the waist circumference (WC) ≥90 cm in males and ≥85 cm in females as central obesity [15].

In this study, the number of CVD risk factors clustered between 0 and 9, with clustering defined as the presence of two or more CVD risk factors. Metabolic CVD risk factors include: hypertension, diabetes mellitus, central obesity, dyslipidemia and overweight and obesity. Behavioral CVD risk factors include: Current smoking, Current drinking, insufficient physical activity and insufficient vegetable and fruit intake.

The China-PAR model was used to evaluate the 10-year ASCVD risk, as the model has shown good internal consistency and external validity [31]. The China-PAR model included the following variables: sex, age, current residence (urban/rural, southern/northern), WC, TC, HDL-C, SBP, DBP, whether to take antihypertensive drugs, diabetes mellitus, current smoking, and a family history of CVD and cerebrovascular disease. The higher the score, the higher the risk; the grouping criteria were < 5% as low risk, 5%~9% as medium risk, ≥10% as high risk.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables are expressed as frequencies and percentages and the Chi-square test was used to compare differences between groups. The mean ± standard deviation was used to describe continuous variables. Age-standardized prevalence rates of CVD risk factors were calculated by direct standardization. All participants were analyzed by stratified description with different sex. The “Upset” package in the R software was used to describe the clustering of CVD risk factors.

The association between the clustering of CVD risk factors and high risk of ASCVD was analyzed using a Poisson regression model with robust estimates, and prevalence ratios (PRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated. We designed a two-step model adjustment strategy by referencing model adjustment approaches from previous similar studies [25, 30] and following two principles: (1) variables included in the China-PAR model should not be adjusted; (2) potential mediating variables (e.g., LDL, medication) and variables prone to double control should not be adjusted (e.g., Current smoking, drinking). Model 1 showed the result without any adjustment. Model 2 was the result after adjusting for ethnicity, educational attainment and altitude. Subsequently, the participants were grouped according to characteristics such as age and sex, and the association between CVD risk factor clustering and high risk of ASCVD was reassessed. The interaction was calculated by adding the product of the grouping variable and the number of CVD risk factor clustering variables to Model 2. In the sensitivity analysis, we excluded the Han Chinese population from both surveys to test whether imbalances in ethnic composition would affect the comparative trends in the prevalence and clustering of CVD risk factors. All statistical analyses were performed using R 4.3.0 software, and all statistical analyses were two-sided with a test level α = 0.05.

Results

A total of 1197 individuals in 2018 and 1047 in 2024 were included; ethnic minorities accounted for 80.4% and 96.1% respectively. Stratified by sex, the study described socio-demographic indicators such as age and residence, as well as changes in anthropometrics as BMI, WC and blood test indicators as TG, etc. The illiteracy rate of females has decreased from 50.6% in 2018 to 41.5% in 2024, but is always higher than in males. The BMI, WC, SBP and DBP increased significantly, and the proportion of antihypertensive drugs and glucose-lowering medication used increased, and this trend was more significant in the male participants (Table 1).

The change in the age-standardized prevalence of CVD risk factors

Over the past six years, the prevalence of nine modifiable CVD risk factors in the participants has undergone significant changes. The prevalence of metabolic modifiable CVD risk factors has increased rapidly, such as overweight and obesity, central obesity and hypertension, which rose from 11.2%, 24.2%, and 22.4% in 2018 to 15.1%, 39.1% and 41.7% in 2024, respectively, with the most notable increase observed in hypertension (86.7%), with a greater increase observed in males (116.5%). In contrast, the prevalence of behavioral modifiable CVD risk factors showed a decreasing trend, with the prevalence of insufficient vegetable and fruit intake and insufficient physical activity decreasing from 69.4% to 58.5% in 2018 to 50.9% and 48.8% in 2024. However, it should be noted that insufficient vegetable and fruit intake and insufficient physical activity remain the most prevalent CVD risk factors in the participants (Table 2).

The changes in the clustering patterns of CVD risk factors

Compared with 2018, there was no significant change in the number of CVD risk factors among the whole participants, with the proportion of participants with three or more CVD disease risk factors remaining as high as 50%. Males and aged 55 to 75 are more likely to have multiple CVD risk factors. Among the Han Chinese participants, the proportion of individuals with 3 or more risk factors has significantly increased (43.4% to 73.1%), while the proportion among minority groups has remained largely unchanged (49.8% to 49.6%) (Table 3).

Although the number of CVD risk factors in the whole population has not changed significantly, the combination of risk factors has changed significantly. In 2018, the top three combinations of CVD risk factors were: insufficient physical activity + insufficient vegetable and fruit intake; insufficient physical activity + insufficient vegetable and fruit intake + current smoking; and insufficient physical activity + insufficient vegetable and fruit intake + current smoking + current drinking. The top three combinations of CVD risk factors in 2024 were: insufficient physical activity + insufficient vegetable and fruit intake; insufficient physical activity + insufficient vegetable and fruit intake + hypertension; and hypertension + central obesity. The number of risk factor combinations associated with hypertension and central obesity increased rapidly, from 9 to 9 in 2018 to 19 and 16 in 2024. Insufficient vegetable and fruit intake and insufficient physical activity remain common in the overall combination of CVD risk factors (Figs. 2 and 3). From a sex perspective, combinations of CVD risk factors associated with hypertension, central obesity, and current smoking are more common in males than in females (Supplementary Materials Figures S1-S4).

The change in ASCVD risk score over six years

Compared with 2018, the ASCVD risk score rose rapidly from 1.9 to 4.1. The proportion of participants classified as high risk for ASCVD also showed a significant increase (6.7% to 10.6%). The ASCVD risk score was higher in males than in females (Supplementary Materials Table S1). In the fully adjusted Model 2, compared with participants without CVD risk factors, participants with 2, 3 or more CVD risk factors were more likely to be classified as high risk for ASCVD, with prevalence ratios of 1.03 (95% CI = 1.01–1.05), 1.13 (95% CI = 1.10–1.16), respectively. For each additional CVD risk factor, the likelihood of being classified as high risk for ASCVD increased by 5.0% (1.05, 95% CI: 1.04–1.07). It is worth noting that the risk was significantly higher in male participants than in female participants (P-interaction < 0.05, Table 4).

Subgroup and sensitivity analysis

After stratification by age, ethnicity, residence, and educational attainment, the likelihood of individuals being classified as high risk for ASCVD increased with the number of CVD risk factors (P-trend < 0.05); this effect was more pronounced in the 55–75 age group (P-interaction < 0.05) (Supplementary Materials Table S2). In the sensitivity analysis, we continued to observe trends in the prevalence and clustering of cardiovascular risk factors consistent with the primary findings (Supplementary Materials Table S3-S4).

Discussion

This study was conducted in an autonomous prefecture for ethnic minorities in the plateau region of Yunnan Province in southwestern China. Our findings suggest that future trends in modifiable CVD risk factors among high-altitude ethnic minority populations may follow two directions: metabolic risk factors are likely to increase, especially hypertension and central obesity, while behavioral risk factors will persist. As the number of CVD factors increases, individuals are classified as being at higher risk for ASCVD. These results undoubtedly further exacerbate the health burden on high-altitude ethnic minority populations, particularly among males.

In 2024, the prevalence of insufficient vegetable and fruit intake and physical inactivity among ethnic minority residents in plateau regions reached as high as 50.9% and 48.8%, respectively, showing a certain degree of decline compared to 2018, but still significantly higher than the average in China [32], and remain an unresolved public health issue. This may be related to the Chinese government’s ongoing promotion of the Western Development Strategy, which has effectively stimulated economic development and gradually improved transportation infrastructure, thereby enhancing residents’ ability to consume vegetables and fruits. However, due to the unique environmental conditions of high-altitude regions, large-scale cultivation of vegetables and fruits is impractical, making it challenging for residents to obtain sufficient intake of these foods. Additionally, the low temperatures and oxygen-deficient environment of high-altitude regions restrict physical activity, forcing residents to reduce the intensity and frequency of their physical activities. The prevalence of hypertension and central obesity has increased by more than 50%, which is inseparable from the rapid economic development of the region [33, 34]. At the same time, the special habits of the plateau population have exacerbated this phenomenon (high-calorie and high-salt diets, such as Tibetan butter tea and Zan ba). Another hypothesis may be concerned with the COVID-19 pandemic. On the one hand, the virus infection may damage vascular endothelial function, triggering or worsening hypertension and diabetes [35, 36]. On the other hand, the pandemic may change changes in people’s lifestyles (such as increased sedentary time) [37] and negative psychological emotions (such as anxiety and fear) [38]. The increase in risk factors for males is higher than that for females, which is related to the fact that males are more likely to have unhealthy lifestyles such as smoking and drinking, these factors have been proven to be closely related to hypertension and obesity. The 2021 Global Burden of Disease Report shows that hypertension, overweight and obesity rank first and fifth in disability-adjusted life years, while the 9 risk factors that contribute to the burden of CVD and disability-adjusted life years lost in the Chinese population rank high among the 88 risk factors [39].

Yunnan Province is a region with a deeply rooted tobacco and alcohol culture, where cigarettes and alcohol are frequently consumed in social activities, particularly among male and ethnic minority groups. This unique cultural context has led to a high prevalence of smoking and alcohol consumption among Yunnan residents. In our study, smoking remains one of the top three risk factors among men, with a smoking rate that is ten times higher than that of women (55.0% vs. 1.7%). This disparity has been documented globally [40]. With 55.0% of males smoking, this proportion is far higher than the global average of 34.4% [41], consistent with reports indicating that approximately 1 to 3 million people in China die annually from smoking-related causes [42]. Smoking is one of the leading causes of mortality among males. Encouragingly, during the study period, the prevalence of current smoking and drinking among males and females decreased significantly, indicating that comprehensive tobacco control strategies and health education on the harms of tobacco and alcohol have made progress in the highland ethnic minority regions of Yunnan Province. However, these proportions remain high, and robust smoking and drinking cessation programs should continue to be implemented. At the same time, we have observed that the rate of increase and proportion of individuals with three or more CVD risk factor clusters in the Han Chinese group are significantly higher than ethnic minority groups, similar to findings from a study in Yunnan Province. However, these ethnic differences may also be related to the smaller proportion of Han individuals in the second survey. Furthermore, this disparity in ethnic composition may introduce bias into trend comparison analyses. After all, ethnicity/race represents a complex social construct rather than a purely biological one, often reflecting multiple differences in cultural background, socioeconomic status, lifestyle, and healthcare access [27], although our sensitivity analysis indicated that the imbalanced ethnic composition did not affect the results of trend comparison analyses. In the future, the Han Chinese population living in high-altitude ethnic minority regions may be a focus of attention in CVD prevention and treatment, but these findings require validation in longitudinal cohort studies.

Our study found that with time changes, among the plateau resident ethnic minority population, the combination of metabolism-related changeable clustering risk factors were mainly around hypertension, central obesity. Sex differences in cluster combination were obvious, and the combination of hypertension, smoking and central obesity became more common in males, compared to the insufficient intake of vegetables and fruits and insufficient physical activity remaining the main cluster patterns among females. Generally, the study also found clusters of 1,2 and 3 types of risk factors were 93.6%,76.7% and 50.5% respectively in 2024, It was similar to the studies of Malaysia [43] and Nepal [44], but significantly higher than the previous report of 89.9%, 63.0% and 32.2% in Yunnan Province [27]. Furthermore, the study showed the same results as the cohort of APSCO cohort study; those with clustered risk factors have higher cardiovascular risk than those without clusters. The Dutch study also showed that clusters 1,2,3 CVD risk were 1.65, 2.61, and 3.25 times higher than people without clustered risk factors [25]. Hypertension and smoking were often key to both high-risk cluster combinations (HR = 4.13 in Asian populations and HR = 3.07 in New Zealand and Australian populations) [30]. With the rapid economic development and lifestyle changes in southern countries around the world, related hypertension, central obesity, and changeable metabolic risk factors deserve further attention.

This study adapted China-PAR models to innovatively evaluate the effect of clustering changes in CVD risk factors on the risk of ASCVD. The study found that the high-risk of ASCVD increased with the number of clusters, increasing by 5.0% for each risk factor gathered, the trends remained in different sex, age and other groups and more pronounced in male and age 55–75 group, which is related to their greater tendency to cluster more CVD risk factors, and the synergistic effects that often exist between these CVD risk factors. Such as smoking seemed to affect the influence of BMI and TC on coronary heart disease risk and exacerbated the effect of high SBP on the risk of hemorrhagic stroke [45, 46]. These individuals need greater attention in future CVD prevention and management.

A comprehensive health management project in China showed that the risk of CVD recurrence in the intervention group was significantly lower than that in the control group [47], with a single risk factor control, 60% adults need treatment,157 CVD events per 100,000 people per year would be avoided, while the comprehensive intervention approach, only 5% adults need treatment, 92 cardiovascular events would be avoided per 100,000 people per year [48]. Given the increasing trend in risk factors in the plateau minority areas, the clustering changes of CVD risk factors, especially in males. In the plateau minority areas of Yunnan Province, it was important to strengthen the comprehensive intervention measures for CVD risk factors, with a focus on a certain male group. However, achieving timely and effective access to healthcare services and lifestyle or clinical interventions in high-altitude ethnic minority regions remains challenging. Most ethnic minorities reside in remote mountainous or rural areas with poor transportation access, where healthcare professionals are scarce, clinical skills are poor, and medical infrastructure is inadequate [49, 50]. Concurrently, ethnic minority populations generally exhibit lower educational attainment, lack basic health literacy, and present cultural differences [51]. These adverse factors may pose barriers to implementing future lifestyle or clinical interventions. But the Chinese government has consistently sought solutions: including promoting the New Rural Cooperative Medical Scheme (NRCMS), enhancing the quality of primary healthcare providers by aligning incentives and governance between hospitals and primary healthcare systems, educating the public on the value of prevention and health maintenance, and building an integrated system based on primary healthcare [52]. Evidence from China indicates that these measures have effectively enhanced healthcare service provision in remote ethnic minority regions, although shortcomings persist [52–54], with the Chinese government’s strong political commitment and a series of targeted policy supports, relevant issues are expected to develop in a positive direction.

Our study may have the following limitations: Firstly, the ASCVD outcome is not real, even though the outcome was derived from a validated and reliable model, which may weaken the reliability of our results. Secondly, the sample size was modest in both cross-sectional surveys, because of the survey faced challenges such as high altitude in the plateau region, language communication difficulties, and low population density, which limited the possibility of obtaining a larger sample size and made it impossible to assess the impact of different specific risk factor combinations on the high risk of ASCVD, which may affect the external validity of the conclusions, therefore, caution is needed when extrapolating the results. Thirdly, the measurement methods for certain risk factors were based on participants’ self-reports (e.g., smoking, alcohol consumption, and frequency of vegetable and fruit intake), which may be subject to reporting bias, thereby overestimating or underestimating the prevalence. In summary, these findings may be considered tentative, and longitudinal studies or intervention trials to validate our findings and provide reasonable prevention strategies.

Conclusions

Through two cross-sectional studies, we found that the prevalence of metabolic modifiable CVD risk factors is rapidly increasing in ethnic minority areas in the southwestern plateau of China, with a much higher increase in males than in females, while behavioral CVD risk factors are decreasing. The accumulation of CVD risk factors significantly increases the high risk of ASCVD in individuals. Comprehensive lifestyle interventions should be implemented in the future to promote the prevention and control of ASCVD risk and gradually reduce sex disparity.

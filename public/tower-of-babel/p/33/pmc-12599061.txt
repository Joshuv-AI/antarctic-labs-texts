Molecular pathology of microRNA-339 during tumor progression
Authors: Iman Akhlaghipour, Negin Taghehchian, Meysam Moghbeli
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCancer, as one of the main causes of human deaths, has an adverse effect on the quality of life in cancer patients. Despite many advances in diagnostic and therapeutic methods, there is still a poor prognosis in cancer patients that can be associated with the late tumor diagnosis. Therefore, investigating the molecular pathology of cancer can help to introduce early diagnostic markers. MicroRNAs (miRNAs) have key roles in regulation of cell proliferation, migration, and apoptosis. Deregulations of miRNAs have been widely reported in various cancers. Regarding the high stability of miRNAs in body fluids, they can be used as the non-invasive diagnostic markers in tumor screening programs and early detection. Considering the aberrant expression of miR-339 in a wide range of tumors, we discussed the role of miR-339 during tumor progressions. MiR-339 mainly functions as a tumor suppressor by the modulation of apoptosis, transcription factors, and signaling pathways. This review can be an effective step towards suggesting miR-339 as a therapeutic target and diagnostic marker in cancer patients.Keywords: MicroRNA-339, Cancer, Non-invasive, Diagnosis, Prognosis

Background

Cancer is one of the main reasons of human deaths in the world [1]. Chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and targeted therapies are the main treatment options in cancer patients [2]. Late diagnosis can be associated with the poor prognosis among cancer patients. Therefore, it is required to assess the molecular tumor biology to introduce novel tumor markers to diagnose cancer in early stages before the occurrence of distant metastasis. MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are involved in post-transcriptional regulation by translational inhibition or mRNA degradation [3]. They have key roles in regulation of cell differentiation, migration, apoptosis, and proliferation [4–6]. Aberrant miRNA expressions have been found in different malignancies [7, 8]. MiRNAs can modulate the tumor metastasis, angiogenesis, and drug resistance [9, 10]. Regarding the high stability of miRNAs in body fluids; they can be suggested as the efficient non-invasive markers in cancer screening and early stage tumor detection [11]. MiR-339 is implicated in the modulation of cell proliferation, inflammatory response, apoptosis, and tumor progression [12–15]. Deregulation of miR-339 has been correlated with prognosis in various types of cancer [16, 17]. Tumor-suppressive functions of miR-339 were mainly investigated in melanoma, hepatocellular carcinoma, and pancreatic cancer, whereas the oncogenic functions of miR-339 were reported in lymphoma [18–21]. Therefore, in the present review we discussed the role of miR-339 during tumor progression to introduce that as a probable diagnostic marker and therapeutic target in cancer patients (Table 1).

Signaling pathways

PI3K/AKT is a key regulator of cell proliferation and metabolism that can be activated by receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs) and G-protein coupled receptors (GPCRs) [22, 23]. MiR-339 is involved in tumor progression by regulation of PI3K/AKT pathway (fig.1). Growth factors triggers the PI3K/AKT signaling by RTKs [24]. IGF1R is a heterotetrameric trans-membrane RTK involved in tumor cell proliferation and survival [25]. IGF1R is autophosphorylated upon binding to its ligand and subsequently associates with adaptor molecules like Shc and insulin receptor substrates to activate MAPK/ERK1/2 and PI3K/AKT, which modulate the tyrosine kinase functions of IGF1R in cancer cells [25]. Circular RNAs (CircRNAs) are single-stranded, endogenous noncoding RNAs that form covalently closed continuous loop structures without polarity in the 5’ to 3’ direction, and lack a polyadenylated tail [26, 27]. They function in various cellular mechanisms via interacting with miRNAs and RNA-binding proteins [28, 29]. It has been investigated that circDOCK1 increased the osteosarcoma cell invasion and cisplatin resistance by modulating IGF1R expression via miR-339-3p sponging [30]. Long noncoding RNAs (LncRNAs) have pivotal roles in modulation of the cellular mechanisms through regulating organelle production, RNA metabolism, and chromosome structure [31–33]. NFKB1 is a nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) subunit implicated in tumorigenesis [34]. NFKB1 was involved in IGF1 up regulation. MAFG-AS1 significantly promoted metastasis and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in ovarian tumor cells through miR-339-5p sponging that resulted in NFKB1/IGF1 up regulation. MAFG-AS1 induced EMT by CDH1 down regulation, while VIM, CDH2, MMP-2, and MMP-9 up regulations in ovarian tumor cells [35].

MAPK is a pivotal signaling pathway that is involved in extracellular signal processing. It can process both mitogenic and anti-mitogenic signals through the activation of ERK, p38, and JNK. MiR-339 is involved in tumor progression by regulation of MAPK pathway (fig.1). TRPC3 belongs to the transient receptor potential (TRP) cation channel family, which is participated in tumor progression [36]. TRPC3 modulates breast cancer cell growth through RASA4/MAPK axis [37]. SNHG3 inhibition reduced ovarian cancer (OC) cell proliferation by miR-339-5p/TRPC3 targeting. The levels of SNHG3 expressions were positively associated with poor prognosis, lymph node metastasis, and histological grade in OC patients [38].

Transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) is a conserved family of secreted proteins involved in regulation of many pathophysiological cellular processes. They activate receptor kinases to mediate cytoplasmic SMAD phosphorylation and nuclear import. Subsequently, SMAD proteins regulate the target genes associated with cellular processes such as cell proliferation, apoptosis, and migration. MiR-339 is involved in tumor progression by regulation of TGF-β pathway (fig.1). TGF-β1 is a cytokine involved in the regulation of tumor microenvironment and osteosarcoma progression [39, 40]. It has been shown that NEAT1 promoted tumor progression by miR-339‐5p/TGF‐β1 axis in osteosarcoma cells [41]. SKI is a negative regulator of the TGF-β signaling that inhibits Smad-mediated transcription as a co-repressor [42]. There was a significant LINC00467 up regulation in AML patients. LINC00467 knockdown suppressed AML development through targeting the miR-339/SKI axis [14]. Cisplatin has been widely utilized in cancer therapy that can significantly increase patient survival rates [43]. Cisplatin causes DNA damage by interacting with the nitrogen atoms of DNA bases and subsequently induces tumor cell apoptosis [44, 45]. Autophagy is a crucial cell defense mechanism against damages caused by oxidative stress, starvation, and poisonous substances. It is also implicated in drug-resistant development of cancer cells [46, 47]. TGF-β-activated kinase 1 (TAK1) modulates the activation of the p38 and JNK cascades, which are essential for TGF-β activities in apoptosis and EMT [48]. It has been investigated that miR-339-5p decreased laryngeal carcinoma cell autophagy and cisplatin resistance via TAK1 targeting. Moreover, miR-339-5p down regulated AMPK and mTOR in cisplatin-resistant cells, and TAK1 was able to reverse these effects [49]. JAK/STAT signaling pathway is used for the cellular responses toward various cytokines and growth factors such as hormones, interferons, and interleukins. Therefore, JAK/STAT has a key role in hematopoiesis, inflammation, apoptosis, and tumor progression. JAKs mediate phosphorylation of cytokine receptors to recruit STAT proteins. Subsequently, dimerized STAT proteins transport into the nucleus to regulate the JAK/STAT target genes [50]. MiR-339 is involved in tumor progression by regulation of JAK/STAT pathway (fig.1). STAT5A phosphorylation and activation by JAK2 result in the formation of STAT5A/B dimers, nuclear translocation, and DNA interaction [51]. It has been observed that there was miR-339-5p down regulation in prostate cancer (PCa) specimens that was negatively correlated with STAT5A and SNHG17 expressions. SNHG17 up regulated STAT5A by miR-339-5p sponging that resulted in SNORA71B up regulation. SNHG17 promoted PCa progression via the miR-339-5p/STAT5A/SNORA71B pathway [52].

Transcription factors

Transcription factors are the main regulators of cellular processes that can be affected by the external stimuli and signaling pathways [53, 54]. It has been shown that miR-339 is involved in tumor progression by regulation of transcription factors (fig.2). SHOX2 belongs to the homeobox transcriptional regulators that are involved in embryogenesis and differentiation. There were SNHG11 up regulations in CRC tissues that were associated with lymph node metastasis, TNM stage, and differentiation. SNHG11 inhibition reduced CRC cell invasion and migration. SNHG11 promoted malignant behaviors of CRC by miR-339-3p sponging that resulted in SHOX2 up regulation [55]. B-cell lymphoma 6 (BCL6) is a transcriptional repressor that participates in cell cycle regulation, differentiation, and apoptosis inhibition [56]. MiR-339-5p suppressed lung adenocarcinoma cell invasion via BCL6 targeting [57]. Acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) is characterized by impaired apoptosis, aberrant cell growth, and the accumulation of maturation-arrested myeloid progenitor cells [58, 59]. MiRNAs are involved in the progression of AML through regulation of cell proliferation, autophagy, apoptosis, and chemotherapy resistance [60, 61]. SOX4 belongs to the SOX (SRY-related HMG-box) transcription factors that are involved in embryogenesis and cell death. It was demonstrated that miR-339-5p inhibited cell growth, while promoted apoptosis in AML through SOX4 targeting [62]. Zinc finger (ZNF)-containing transcription factors are involved in regulation of apoptosis, metabolism, autophagy, and stemness maintenance [63–66]. MiR-339-5p inhibited pancreatic tumor cell invasion through ZNF689 targeting [20]. There was miR-339 down regulation in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) tissues compared with normal liver tissues. MiR-339 reduced HCC cell invasion and proliferation, while promoted apoptosis via ZNF689 down regulation. Decreased levels of miR-339 expressions were associated with histological grade, clinical stage, and tumor metastasis in HCC patients [21].

Estrogen receptor (ER) positive (+) breast cancer account for almost 66% of all estrogen-responsive breast cancers [67]. ER-alpha mediates estrogen signaling pathway to induce breast cancer progression [68]. Tamoxifen as an anti-estrogen is a standard therapy for ER + breast cancer and has been shown to reduce approximately 33% of total deaths among advanced stage patients. Cross-talk between the cell cycle and ER pathway has been associated with endocrine resistance. CDK4/6 inhibitors are widely utilized in clinical settings. However, CDK2 is involved in resistance to CDK4/6 inhibitors [69]. There was MAFG Antisense RNA 1 (MAFG-AS1) up regulation by estrogen in ER + breast cancer that was correlated with poor prognosis. MAFG-AS1 promoted colony formation and cell proliferation in ER + breast cancer through miR-339-5p sponging that up regulated CDK2 and accelerated G1/S phase transition. CDK2-mediated phosphorylation of FOXO1 also prevented the activation of apoptotic pathways. There was a positive feedback between the expression levels of ERα and MAFG-AS1 in ER + breast cancer. Moreover, high levels of CDK2 and MAFG-AS1 were correlated with tamoxifen resistance and poor survival in cancer patients [70]. FOSL2 is a transcription factor that is aberrantly activated in various cancers to induce cell migration and proliferation [71, 72]. It has been reported that there was a correlation between the SNHG17 overexpression and poor clinical outcomes in CRC patients. SNHG17 modulated CRC progression through miR-339-5p sponging that restored the FOSL2 activity. Moreover, FOSL2 transcriptionally activated SNHG17 expression through binding at the promoter region of SNHG17. Therefore, targeting the positive feedback loop of SNHG17/miR-339-5p/FOSL2/SNHG17 might be a CRC treatment strategy [73]. The long non-coding RNA FAM66C was found to be significantly down regulated in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) tissues and cell lines, while miR-339-3p was up regulated. Functional experiments demonstrated that FAM66C inhibited LUAD cell proliferation and invasion, and promoted apoptosis, whereas these suppressive effects were reversed by simultaneous overexpression of miR-339-3p. A dual-luciferase reporter assay confirmed that FAM66C directly binds to miR-339-3p. Clinically, low FAM66C expression was significantly associated with advanced TNM stage, lymph node metastasis, and poorer overall survival, identifying it as an independent prognostic factor for LUAD patients [74].

Apoptosis and ferroptosis

Apoptosis is a key cellular process that is required for the normal cell and tissue homeostasis. Therefore, apoptosis deregulation can be frequently observed in different cancers. MiR-339 is involved in tumor progression by apoptosis regulation (fig.3). The p53 is an important transcription factor implicated in various stress-sensing signaling pathways, genotoxic stress response, and tumor suppression [75]. MDM2 as an ubiquitin ligase is one of the main negative regulators of p53. MDM2 is also a p53 target gene that forms a negative regulatory cycle to modulate p53 activity and levels [76]. It has been observed that miR-339-5p regulated p53 and its tumor suppressing functions via MDM2 targeting. There was also a positive relationship between the expression levels of miR-339-5p and cellular p53. Suppression of miR-339-5p up regulated MDM2 while down regulated p21 in response to p53 in breast tumor cells. MiR-339-5p suppression also increased cell growth in both the absence and presence of genotoxic stress [12]. BAX is associated with BCL2 to destabilize the mitochondrial membrane and inducing cytochrome C release. BCL2L11 is an important pro-apoptotic protein that initiates apoptosis by interacting with BCL2 protein family [77]. IL-3 and other growth factors improve hematopoietic progenitor survival by BCL2L11 down regulation through the activation of PI3K/mTOR and Raf/MAPK pathways [78]. MiR-339-5p promoted Stem Cell Leukemia/Lymphoma cell survival and proliferation through BAX and BCL2L11 down regulations [18]. Resistance of melanoma cells to chemotherapeutic treatments is assumed to be mainly caused by aberrant expression of anti-apoptotic factors such as MCL1 and BCL2. MCL1 up regulation is found to be contributed with melanoma chemo resistance following direct therapeutic targeting of MEK and BRAF [79, 80]. MCL1 is involved in melanoma cell growth, apoptosis, and EMT [81, 82]. Anoikis as an apoptotic process is triggered by the loss of cell-extracellular matrix or cell-cell contact that promotes tumor cell invasion and migration [83, 84]. MCL1 interferes with the induction of anoikis, which increases the dissemination of cancer cells [85, 86]. MiR-339-3p significantly inhibited melanoma tumor cell invasion by MCL1 targeting [19]. Up regulation of circPVT1 has been found in gallbladder cancer (GBC) that was associated with advanced clinical stage, poor prognosis, lymph node metastasis, and overall survival. CircPVT1 induced GBC cell invasion, while suppressed apoptosis through miR-339-3p/MCL-1 cascade [87]. BLCAP is an apoptosis stimulator that functions through a p53 and NF-kb independent pathway. There was MALAT1 up regulation in breast cancer tissues, which was a prognostic biomarker for survival in BC patients diagnosed before the age of 60 or those diagnosed with infiltrating ductal carcinoma. MALAT1 modulated BLCAP by interacting with miR-339-5p that resulted in a poor prognosis in BC patients [88].

Role of miR-339 during tumor progression by regulation of apoptosis, ferroptosis, EMT process, RNA-binding proteins, and protein phosphatases.(Created with BioRender.com)

Ferroptosis is a non-apoptotic and iron-dependent cell death that is recognized by the accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) in cells [89, 90]. It can reduce cellular antioxidant capacity, ROS accumulation, and ultimately cell oxidative death, which are crucial for tumor progression [89, 91, 92]. SLC7A11 was found to be adversely associated with ferroptosis [93]. There was uc.339 up regulation in lung adenocarcinoma tissues compared with normal samples that was inversely associated with survival. Inhibition of Uc.339 prevented the invasion of lung tumor cells. Uc.339 inhibited miR-339 synthesis by preventing the Drosha-DGCR8 complex. Deregulation of miR-339 resulted in up regulation of SLC7A11, consequently leading to a decrease in ferroptosis levels, and ultimately lung tumor metastasis [94]. There was significant miR-339-5p up regulation in liver cancer tissues that was associated with poor patient prognosis. Inhibition of miR-339-5p suppressed liver cancer cell proliferation, migration, and colony formation, while promoted cell death. Mechanistically, miR-339-5p was found to directly target ATG7 as a key autophagy-related gene, leading to the autophagic degradation of ferritin heavy chain 1 (FTH1), which in turn reduced intracellular iron levels and suppressed ferroptosis. Silencing ATG7 reversed the effects of miR-339-5p inhibition, confirming the role of the miR-339-5p/ATG7/FTH1 axis in promoting ferroptosis resistance and liver cancer progression [95].

Structural factors

Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) is an important cellular process in tumor metastasis [96, 97]. It is recognized by the up modulation of N-cadherin (a mesenchymal marker) and the down modulation of E-cadherin (epithelial marker) [98]. MiR-339 is involved in tumor progression by regulation of EMT related factors (fig.3). Matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) are implicated in the degradation of extracellular matrix in both physiological and pathological conditions, such as tissue remodeling, tumor metastasis, and embryonic development [99]. There was MAFA-AS1 up regulation in breast cancer (BC) tissues in comparison with normal margins. MAFG-AS1 increased malignant behaviors of BC via modulating the miR-339-5p/MMP15 axis. MAFG-AS1 also promoted EMT in breast tumor cells via N-cadherin up regulation, while E-cadherin down regulation [100]. There was also significant MAFG-AS1 up regulation in NSCLC tissues compared with normal samples that was directly correlated with tumor size and TNM stage. MAFG-AS1 promoted NSCLC cell EMT and invasion by targeting the miR-339-5p/MMP15 axis. It promoted the EMT process in NSCLC tumors through CDH1 down regulation and CDH2 up regulation [101]. Collagen Type XI Alpha 1 Chain (COL11A1) is one of the main components in extracellular matrix that can be involved in tumor progression and metastasis [102]. MiR-339-5p inhibited the progression of colon carcinoma via COL11A1 targeting [103]. Microtubules are composed of α-tubulins and β-tubulins, which play critical roles in cell motility and shape [104]. Tubulin beta-2 A chain (TUBB2A), a subunit of tubulin, accounts for approximately one-third of β-tubulins in the brain [105]. Cutaneous melanoma (CM) is an aggressive skin cancer with a rising incidence and is responsible for the majority of skin-cancer-related mortalities [106]. There was LINC00665 up regulation in CM cells. Inhibition of LINC00665 suppressed CM cell invasion, while promoted apoptosis through miR-339-3p/TUBB axis [107].

Phosphatases are also the key regulators of intracellular signaling pathways that have opposite function of the kinases. Therefore, they are involved in various cellular and molecular processes to maintain the cellular homeostasis. MiR-339 is involved in tumor progression by regulation of protein phosphatases (fig.3). CDC25A is a protein phosphatase that is involved in G1 to S progression. There were circ_0004771 up regulations in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) specimens that were correlated with poor survival. Inhibition of circ_0004771 suppressed ESCC cell proliferation via regulation of miR-339-5p/CDC25A axis [108]. Runx3 promoted radio sensitivity in ESCC through miR-339-5p that down regulated the CDC25A. MiR-339-5p was reported to be significantly increased in T1/T2 stage ESCC patients when compared to those in T3/T4 stage that was negatively associated with cancer recurrence [109]. Radiotherapy is one of the most effective treatment options for lung cancer [110]. Resistance to radiotherapy is the main factor that limits its effectiveness and results in treatment failure and local recurrence [111, 112]. Phosphatase of Regenerating Liver-1 (PRL-1) is a protein tyrosine phosphatase that is involved in tumor metastasis [113, 114]. It has also been identified as a particular promoter of the ERK1/2 cascade [115, 116]. It has been demonstrated that miR-339-5p increased radio sensitivity by PRL-1 targeting in lung tumor cells [117]. There was miR-339-5p down regulations in CRC tissues that were adversely correlated with metastatic ability of tumor cells. MiR-339-5p suppressed CRC invasion through PRL-1 targeting. Moreover, miR-339-5p inhibition resulted in PRL-1 up regulation and enhanced p-Erk1/2 levels [118].

RNA-binding proteins (RBPs) are the key transcriptional effectors that can recognize specific transcripts to maintain cellular homeostasis. RBPs are involved in regulation of splicing, mRNA transport, translation, and decay. MiR-339 is involved in tumor progression by regulation of RBPs (fig.3). NOVA1 is a neuronal RBP that regulates alternative splicing. There was miR-339 down regulations in GC tissues that were significantly associated with lymph node metastasis. MiR-339 inhibited GC cell proliferation through NOVA1 targeting [119]. Heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein A1 (HNRNPA1) is a pivotal RNA binding protein that is implicated in RNA metabolism and tumor development [120, 121]. RP11-81H3.2 induced GC progression by regulation of miR-339/HNRNPA1 axis [122]. High mobility group box 1 (HMGB1) is a non-histone nuclear protein that promotes tumor cell migration and invasion by initiating inflammation through advanced glycation products or Toll-like receptors [123, 124]. There was miR-339-5p down regulation in glioma tissues. MiR-339-5p decreased the levels of HMGB1 and PTP4A1 expressions to inhibit the vascularization, cell invasion and migration in glioma cells [125].

S-phase kinase-associated protein 2 (Skp2) contributes to tumor cell survival as an oncogene through its ability to promote protein degradation [126–128]. Overexpression of Skp2 promoted progression and metastasis of NSCLC cells and was correlated with a poor prognosis in these patients [129–131]. MiR-339 inhibited lung tumor cell growth through Skp2 targeting [132]. IP6K2 belongs to the inositol phosphokinase (IPK) protein family that is involved in production of diphosphoinositol pentakisphosphate (InsP7/PP-InsP5) to regulate interferon-beta function in tumor cells. LINC00467 was found to promote metastasis in glioma by up regulating IP6K2 through miR-339-3p sponging, and concurrently down regulating p53 via interaction with DNMT1 [133].

Conclusions

MiR-339 mainly functions as a tumor suppressor via modulation of apoptosis, transcription factors, and signaling pathways. Its frequent down regulation and role in inhibiting key cancer processes make it a promising candidate for future diagnostic and therapeutic strategies. Restoring miR-339-5p levels could be a viable strategy to slow tumor progression. This approach might be particularly effective in cancers with wild-type p53, as it can boost the natural p53 tumor-suppressor response. Another strategy involves using miR-339 to target DNA methyltransferase 3B (DNMT3B), potentially reactivating silenced tumor suppressor genes. The function of miR-339 depends heavily on cellular context, and its effects are mediated through different target genes in different cancers. Future work should focus on creating a comprehensive map of its context-dependent targets. The detection of miR-339 in body fluids is also a key advantage for its use as a non-invasive biomarker. This review can be an effective step towards introducing miR-339 as a diagnostic marker and therapeutic target in cancer patients. However, more clinical studies on the circulating miR-339 levels are required to use that as a non-invasive diagnostic marker in clinics. Regarding the therapeutic value of miR-338, more animal studies and clinical trials are also needed to use that as a therapeutic marker among cancer patients. Moving from laboratory research to clinical application requires standardized and cost-effective detection methods, validation in large-scale clinical trials, and exploration of therapeutic delivery systems for miR-339 mimics or inhibitors.

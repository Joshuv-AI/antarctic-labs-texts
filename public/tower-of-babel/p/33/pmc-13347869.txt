Contact aspiration versus stent retriever thrombectomy for acute basilar artery occlusion (ANGEL-COAST): rationale and design of a multicentre, prospective, randomised, open-label, blinded-endpoint trial
Authors: Sun Xuan, Ming Yang, Dapeng Sun, Ligang Song, Feng Gao, Dapeng Mo, Ning Ma, Yuesong Pan, Liping Liu, Xingquan Zhao, Yilong Wang, Yongjun Wang, Raul Nogueira, Zhongrong Miao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundContact aspiration (CA) and stent retriever (SR) thrombectomy are two equally recommended first-line endovascular techniques for treating acute ischaemic stroke due to large vessel occlusion. Whether CA is more effective in achieving complete reperfusion compared with SR in patients with acute basilar artery occlusion (ABAO) remains unclear.AimThis study aims to compare the efficacy and safety of CA versus SR as the first-line strategy during endovascular treatment in improving the rates of first-pass effect (FPE) for patients with ABAO.Methods and designThe ANGEL-COAST study is a prospective, multicentre, randomised controlled, open-label, blinded-endpoint (PROBE) clinical trial. Patients with acute ischaemic stroke due to ABAO within 24 hours from symptom onset will be recruited. Participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either the CA or SR group. If the assigned treatment fails after three attempts, investigators may opt for alternative treatment strategies based on their clinical judgement.Study outcomesThe primary endpoint is the FPE rate, defined as eTICI (Extended Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction) 2C/3 recanalisation after the first thrombectomy attempt without any rescue strategy. Key secondary endpoints include rates of modified FPE (eTICI ≥2b50), successful recanalisation (eTICI ≥2b50, eTICI ≥2b67, eTICI3) within ≤3 passes with the assigned device on conclusion of the procedure, procedural duration, use of rescue techniques and functional outcomes at 90 days, including modified Rankin Scale (mRS) 0–2 and mRS ordinal shift analysis. Safety outcomes include the rates of symptomatic intracerebral haemorrhage (sICH) at 36±12 hours, all-cause 90-day mortality and procedure-related serious adverse events.DiscussionThis is a head-to-head randomised trial to directly compare CA and SR in ABAO. The findings will help establish the optimal first-line endovascular treatment strategy for ABAO, potentially improving clinical outcomes in this high-risk group.Trial registration numberhttps://www.clinicaltrials.gov; Unique identifier: NCT05615038.

Introduction

Acute ischaemic stroke due to basilar artery occlusion (ABAO) carries a poor prognosis, often resulting in high mortality and disability, with only 21%–35% of patients achieving functional independence at 90 days.1 Several studies have verified the critical importance of achieving rapid and successful arterial recanalisation to improve outcomes in patients with ABAO.24 Current guidelines provide the highest level of recommendation for acute endovascular treatment in ABAO patients within 24 hours of symptom onset.5

Stent retrievers (SR) are one of the most commonly used thrombectomy devices in clinical practice for anterior circulation large vessel occlusion (LVO).6 7 The contact aspiration (CA) thrombectomy technique employs a large-bore catheter to directly engage the proximal aspect of the thrombus and then remove it under continuous suction. Compared to SR, CA is technically simpler, has less impact on vascular branches and carries a lower risk of vascular endothelial injury.8 In patients with anterior circulation LVO, multiple trials have demonstrated that CA is non-inferior to SR as a first-line recommended approach for mechanical thrombectomy. However, the vertebrobasilar circulation has distinct anatomical characteristics and different mechanisms of occlusion compared with the anterior circulation.6 7 9 Indeed, several prior observational studies have suggested that CA may be particularly well-suited for ABAO.1015

Despite these theoretical advantages, no randomised controlled trials have directly compared CA and SR in ABAO. The purpose of the ANGEL-COAST trial is to compare the efficacy and safety of CA versus SR as a first-line thrombectomy strategy, with a primary focus on achieving the first-pass effect (FPE).16 17

Methods

The ANGEL-COAST study is a prospective, randomised, multi-centre, controlled, open-label, blinded-endpoint (PROBE) academic trial designed to compare the outcomes of CA versus SR thrombectomy in ABAO. Patients will be recruited from 26 high-volume comprehensive stroke centres in China, which routinely perform both CA and SR procedures. A ‘high-volume’ centre is defined as performing at least 100 thrombectomy procedures annually, with all participating operators having performed a minimum of 10 thrombectomy procedures with SR and CA technique, respectively. This study was ethically approved by the Institutional Review Board of Beijing Tiantan Hospital and registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (identifier: NCT05615038). Once enrolled, patients undergo a standardised clinical and imaging assessment, similar to routine practice, including detailed medical history (including cardiovascular and metabolic co-morbidities and medication usage) baseline NIHSS score, glucose level, and a CT or MRI scan to rule out acute intracranial haemorrhage (ICH) and confirm the presence of basilar artery occlusion.

Randomisation

Eligible patients will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either the CA or SR group as the first-line intervention. To ensure a balanced allocation based on key factors associated with successful endovascular reperfusion and clinical outcomes,4 we will employ a minimisation randomisation approach accounting for baseline NIHSS scores (≤16 or >16) and time from symptom onset to randomisation (≤6 hours or >6 hours). Patient enrolment and randomisation will be conducted by vascular neurologists or interventional neuroradiologists, with randomisation numbers assigned sequentially according to the order of patient inclusion.

Intervention

The choice of sedation modality (general anaesthesia or conscious sedation) and arterial access (femoral or radial) will be left at the discretion of the treating team, considering individual patient’s characteristics. Baseline digital subtraction angiography (DSA) will be conducted to assess vascular anatomy, collateral flow and the baseline eTICI score. Once ABAO is confirmed, patients will be immediately randomised, and thrombectomy will be initiated using the assigned treatment approach.

The operator must try at least three recanalisation attempts with the designated technique before switching to an alternative endovascular procedure or rescue therapy, in accordance with guideline recommendations. The eTICI score is recorded after each attempt. The decision to proceed with rescue therapy and the choice of alternative technique are left at the operator’s discretion. Permissible rescue techniques include combined CA and SR techniques, angioplasty (with or without a stent placement) and intra-arterial thrombolysis. After endovascular thrombectomy, subjects are transferred to the stroke unit for standardised high-level post-procedural care. The use of intravenous and/or intra-arterial glycoprotein IIb/IIIa receptor antagonist (eg, tirofiban or eptifibatide) is allowed at the operator’s discretion, particularly in cases of suspected intracranial atherosclerosis or potential risk of re-occlusion.

Contact aspiration technique

A coaxial system consisting of a microcatheter (0.021–0.027 inches) or navigation catheter, a 0.014–0.016-inch micro-guidewire, an aspiration catheter (IAC 71/54, DEEPIN Technology, Beijing, China) and a guiding catheter or long sheath will be used to facilitate the navigation of the aspiration catheter to the site of the occlusive thrombus. Once the aspiration catheter engaged with the thrombus, the navigation catheter/microcatheter and micro-guidewire are withdrawn as a unit, while aspiration with consistent negative pressure is applied. Aspiration should be maintained for a minimum of 90 s while applying gentle forward-backward tension on the aspiration catheter to optimise thrombus removal.18 Clot removal occurs in one of two ways: (1) the thrombus is completely aspirated through the catheter (‘clot ingestion’) or (2) the thrombus gets lodged at the catheter tip on suction and is removed along with the catheter (‘clot corking’). This procedure can be repeated until successful recanalisation (eTICI 2b/3) is achieved or the procedure is terminated.

Standard stent retriever technique

The technique used in the SR group must follow the manufacturer’s instructions for device use. A delivery microcatheter is navigated over a micro-guidewire to the occluded basilar artery and across the thrombus. Selective angiography may be performed to confirm optimal positioning. The SR is then deployed across the occlusion site. An incubation time of at least 90 s must take place prior to the SR removal. A distal access catheter (DAC) can be used at the operator’s discretion, particularly in cases with a tortuous anatomy. However, a combined strategy (stentriever thrombectomy (ST) plus CA) should be avoided. This process can be repeated until successful recanalisation (eTICI 2b/3) is achieved or the procedure is concluded.

Protocol for switching to alternative thrombectomy or rescue therapy

The operator must conduct at least three recanalisation passes with the assigned technique before switching to an alternative procedure or rescue therapy. However, if the aspiration catheter fails to contact the thrombus, or the micro-guidewire and/or microcatheter cannot cross the occlusion, the operator may switch to an alternative thrombectomy procedure or rescue therapy earlier.

Technical considerations for atherosclerotic lesions

The literature indicates that intracranial atherosclerotic stenosis (ICAS) is relatively prevalent in the Asian population among patients with ABAO.24 Therefore, technical strategies targeting underlying atherosclerotic occlusion are protocolised.

For patients with atherosclerotic tandem occlusion (figure 1c), proximal balloon angioplasty may be performed to facilitate access for the guiding catheter. Once basilar artery recanalisation is achieved, further decisions regarding treatment (balloon angioplasty or stent implantation, figure 1b,c) of any remaining intracranial or extracranial arterial lesions are left at the discretion of the operator depending on the specific conditions.

Post-treatment angiographic assessment, procedure care and follow-up

Following the endovascular procedure, angiographic imaging will be obtained in anterior-posterior, lateral and working projections. The eTICI score will be assessed at the completion of the procedure. The flowchart of the ANGEL-COAST trial is shown in figure 2.

Flowchart of ANGEL-COAST trial.

Postoperative medical management will be standardised according to guidelines for both treatment groups. Neurological and functional assessments, including the NIHSS and modified Rankin Scale (mRS), will be performed within 24±12 hours following randomisation. Non-contrast CT or MRI scans will be conducted within 24±12 hours to evaluate for haemorrhagic transformation. Intracranial haemorrhages will be classified according to the Heidelberg Classification.20

Symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage (sICH) is defined as evidence of ICH on CT within 48 hours, accompanied by a ≥4-point deterioration in the NIHSS. Follow-up assessments will be conducted at 90 days to evaluate mRS scores. Follow-up evaluations will be conducted by trained investigators who are blinded to treatment group and through face-to-face or telephone interviews (table 1).

Primary outcome

The primary outcome is the rate of FPE.16 17 FPE is defined as near-full reperfusion (2c/3) on the first thrombectomy pass without rescue treatment.

Rescue treatment is defined as thrombectomy strategy changes (including CA, SR, combined CA and SR), intraprocedural treatment with balloon angioplasty and/or stenting for the underlying intracranial vertebrobasilar artery steno-occlusive lesion after thrombectomy attempts or intra-arterial thrombolysis (recombinant tissue plasminogen activator, urokinase or tenecteplase). Balloon angioplasty of the extracranial vertebrobasilar artery steno-occlusive lesion, performed to facilitate access in tandem lesions, is not considered rescue treatment (figure 1c).

Safety outcomes

The primary safety outcomes include sICH (Heidelberg Classification: characterised by a worsening NIHSS score ≥4 points or ≥2 points in one NIHSS category or leading to intubation/hemicraniectomy/External Ventricular Drain (EVD) placement or other major medical/surgical intervention within 48 hours post-procedure20 and all-cause mortality at 90 days).

Secondary safety outcomes include any ICH within 48 hours and perioperative complications including arterial dissection, arterial perforation, embolism in new territory, vascular spasm and re-occlusion.

Blinding

For the primary outcome, an independent imaging core laboratory will assess the eTICI score, collateral flow grade and any complications. In cases of discrepancies between two interpreters, an experienced neurointerventionalist from the central review team will review the angiograms and determine the final outcome for relevant endpoints. The imaging core laboratory will perform all neuroimaging analyses, including determining the site of occlusion, evaluating any haemorrhagic transformation and clot burden score. All analyses will remain blinded to procedural allocations. Three Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) members, who are also blinded to the treatment assignments, will adjudicate procedure-related complications and any serious adverse events. Finally, trained investigators—who are unaware of the group assignments—will assess the mRS scores at 90 days.

Independent data safety monitoring board (DSMB)

The DSMB’s is responsible for assessing unmasked accumulated data. The board consists of neurologists and interventionists, ensuring representation from both disciplines. All members are independent of the study investigators. The DSMB will review the incidence of adverse events and monitor its balance between two arms during the study period, providing recommendations to the Executive Committee.

Data collection

An eCRF has been used for data collection. Key data for monitoring endpoints are regularly identified throughout the trial by the data management department according to predefined rules. Finally, data administrators will conduct comprehensive automated monitoring after data entry is completed. Any identified discrepancies will be addressed through systematic queries and resolution workflows.

Statistical hypotheses

Based on the primary endpoint of FPE rate,16 17 this study establishes the following statistical hypotheses for superiority:

Null Hypothesis H0: PCA - PSR≤0.

Alternative Hypothesis H1: PCA - PSR>0.

The two-sided significance level α is set at 0.05.

Here, PCA and PSR represent the FPE rates for the CA population and SR population, respectively. If the lower limit of the 95% CI for the difference between the two rates is greater than 0, the null hypothesis will be rejected, and the conclusion of superiority will hold true, that is, the CA technique is superior to the SR technique in achieving FPE.

Sample size estimates

Based on a recent meta-analysis, the FPE for patients with ABAO undergoing thrombectomy with first-line CA and ST are 47.3% and 24.5%, respectively.21 22 Given that this analysis only included observational studies, the efficacy of the two thrombectomy strategies may be different in randomised controlled trials. Therefore, we hypothesise that in the target population, the FPE rates for first-line CA compared with ST are 45% and 30%, respectively. Based on this hypothesis, the expected absolute increase in efficacy for the CA group compared with the ST group is 15%, which corresponds to a 50% relative improvement. To ensure the statistical significance of the study, we set the adjusted significance level α at 0.025 (one-sided) and the power (1-β) at 0.80. Using PASS software for sample size calculation, the required sample size was determined to be 160 cases per group. Considering an anticipated 5% loss to follow-up, the final calculated sample size is 338 cases, specifically allocated as 169 cases per group. This sample size estimate is intended to ensure that the study has enough statistical power to evaluate the impact of first-line CA versus ST on the FPE rate in patients with acute ABAO.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analysis for the ANGEL-COAST study will be conducted by the statistical department of an independent national neurology centre. Statisticians will be aware of group allocations during the analysis. An SAS 9.4 software will be used for statistical analysis, with a two-sided significance level α of 0.05 for all statistical tests.

All analyses will confirm to the intention-to-treat principle, based on original random group assignments, regardless of any protocol deviations.

For the primary endpoint of FPE rate, statistical descriptions include the frequencies and percentages in each group. The 95% CIs for each group and the differences between groups will be calculated. The criterion for rejecting the null hypothesis, that is, determining that the efficacy of the CA group is superior to that of the SR group, will be based on the lower limit of the 95% CI being greater than 0. If necessary, clinical considerations can be incorporated to adjust the analysis models.

For secondary endpoints, descriptive statistical analyses will be conducted based on the type of variables, and the 95% CIs for the differences between groups will be calculated to determine whether the differences between groups are statistically significant.

Detailed information on the statistical analysis methods will be detailed in the statistical analysis plan.

Handling of missing data and imputation methods

Since primary outcomes will be assessed immediately after endovascular treatment, missing value imputation is not planned. In cases of catheter insertion failure, all patients will have their primary outcome defined as a failure, irrespective of treatment allocation. In cases where assessment results from the core laboratory are missing (for any reason), on-site assessment of eTICI grading will be used for primary outcome determination. For other secondary outcomes, in addition to the core laboratory assessment, no imputation procedures will be used, and missing values will be substituted with onsite assessments. This design ensures data processing consistency and a rigorous framework to more accurately reflect research results.

Study organisation and funding

This research was funded in October 2022 and is sponsored by DEEPIN Technology, Beijing, China. Enrolment, core laboratory review, (data site) verification and data integrity checks by the coordinating centre are ongoing. The clinical coordination for the trial is managed at the Beijing Tiantan Hospital, Capital Medical University, China.

Discussion

ABAO, as one of the most severe forms of acute ischaemic stroke, often results in rapidly progressive neurological deficits, leading to severe disability or death. Both CA and SR have been validated as the first-line thrombectomy strategies for improving recanalisation rates and clinical outcomes. Recent research has highlighted the FPE as a key predictor of favourable outcomes in LVO patients. Identifying the most effective first-line strategy in achieving complete recanalisation on the first attempt is critical for optimising the prognosis of ABAO.

The ANGEL-COAST trial is a randomised controlled trial using a PROBE design to assess the efficacy and safety of first-line endovascular strategies for ABAO. This trial directly compares the efficacy of CA versus SR thrombectomy in achieving FPE (eTICI 2c/3) as the primary outcome.

Similar to the framework established by the ASTER/COMPASS trials,6 7 the structure of the ANGEL-COAST trial is designed to assess the complete reperfusion outcomes associated with aspiration or SR devices in mechanical thrombectomy. We draw on the design of the ASTER, ASTER2 and COMPASS trial, which aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety profile of CA or SR in first-line endovascular strategies. We hypothesise that a first-line CA strategy will improve the rates of first pass complete recanalisation and reduce procedural times for ABAO. Simultaneously, the pc-ASTER trial (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT05320263) is underway, comparing the first-pass CA vs SR thrombectomy in achieving complete reperfusion. A joint study integrating the data from PC-ASTER and ANGEL-COAST trial, covering Western countries and eastern Asia respectively, will give us more intriguing results.

Achieving optimal reperfusion (eTICI 2c/3) is a critical goal during endovascular treatment, as it serves as a key early marker of treatment success. Previous studies have established a strong correlation between higher eTICI scores and improved clinical outcomes, with evidence indicating that patients achieving eTICI 2b or higher experience lower rates of disability.16 17 Consequently, the ANGEL-COAST trial prioritises eTICI 2c/3 recanalisation as an essential benchmark for successful intervention in ABAO.

Summary and conclusions

The ANGEL-COAST trial is a randomised trial to directly compare CA and SR as first-line thrombectomy strategies in ABAO patients. This study aims to generate high-level evidence to guide the prioritisation of first-line thrombectomy strategies and ultimately enhance functional outcomes for ABAO patients.

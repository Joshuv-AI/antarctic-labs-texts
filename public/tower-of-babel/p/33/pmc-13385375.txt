Parallel fusion model for complex multi-source vibration time-series prediction
Authors: Wei Huang, Jian Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAiming at the insufficient prediction accuracy caused by the non-stationary and multi-frequency coupling characteristics of complex multi-source vibration signals, this study proposes a parallel fusion prediction model that takes hyperparameter optimization as the core preprocessing step and integrates multivariate variational mode decomposition (MVMD) with parallel weighted KNN-XGBoost. Firstly, a “training-validation-test” three-set data separation framework is constructed, and a stepwise grid search strategy is adopted to optimize key hyperparameters. With the error validation set as the objective function, the number of neighbors for K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), the tree depth and learning rate for eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), and the optimal fusion weights of KNN and XGBoost are determined sequentially. This fundamentally avoids the subjective bias inherent in manual parameter tuning. Secondly, MVMD is employed to decompose complex multi-source vibration signals into 8 intrinsic mode functions (IMFs) with different frequency scales. This addresses the mode mixing issue of traditional decomposition methods, reduces the complexity of vibration signals, and retains the multi-source coupling features. Thirdly, a parallel weighted KNN-XGBoost structure is built based on the optimized parameters. Through independent prediction and weighted fusion, error propagation in serial structures is avoided, enabling the synergy of “local correction-global fitting”. Multiple comparative and ablation experiments demonstrate that, compared with other models, the MVMD-parallel-KNN-XGBoost model achieves the optimal balanced performance. This study provides a parallel fusion technical pathway of “parameter optimization-modal separation-model fusion” for complex multi-source vibration time-series prediction, which can meet the high-precision prediction requirements of complex multi-source vibration in fields such as aerospace, rail transit, and industrial engineering etc.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-53236-6.Keywords: Complex multi-source vibration, Time-series prediction, Parallel fusion model, MVMD, KNN, XGBoost, Parallel weighted fusion, Hyperparameter optimizationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Complex multi-source vibration signals are widely prevalent in key engineering fields such as aerospace, rail transit, intelligent manufacturing, and energy power systems. Accurate time-series prediction of complex multi-source vibration not only serves as a critical technical support for equipment health monitoring and fault early warning but also constitutes a core prerequisite for ensuring the safe and stable operation of engineering systems, reducing operation and maintenance costs, and improving operational efficiency. During the operation of high-speed trains, precise prediction of wheel-rail coupling vibration can proactively mitigate safety risks induced by track irregularities; in industrial robot operation scenarios, time-series prediction of joint vibration enables the optimization of control strategies and reduction of mechanical wear. Furthermore, in the fields of spacecraft and precision manufacturing, time-series prediction of micro-vibrations is an essential prerequisite for achieving active vibration control and ensuring equipment functional accuracy. For instance, by predicting the amplitude and frequency variations of spacecraft micro-vibrations, attitude compensation strategies can be adjusted in advance.

However, complex multi-source vibration prediction faces numerous inherent challenges. On one hand, vibration signals exhibit strong non-stationarity and multi-frequency component superposition characteristics. Vibration components from different sources (e.g., fixed harmonics of equipment vibration, random fluctuations of environmental vibration) are prone to frequency overlap and mode mixing, rendering signal feature extraction extremely difficult. On the other hand, the randomness of multi-source interference and the non-linearity of coupling relationships result in complex and variable evolution laws of vibration time series. Traditional prediction methods based on physical models struggle to establish accurate mathematical expressions.

Early vibration prediction primarily relied on traditional physical modeling and statistical methods, such as the ARIMA model1. These methods construct models based on linear assumptions and, while simple in principle and low in computational complexity, fail to adapt to the non-linear and non-stationary characteristics of complex multi-source vibration. Their prediction accuracy and generalization ability are limited, making them unable to meet the demand for high-precision prediction in engineering practice. With the development of artificial intelligence technology, machine learning and deep learning methods have gradually become research hotspots in vibration prediction. Traditional machine learning methods (e.g., SVM, Random Forest, KNN) excavate the mapping relationship between features and target values through data-driven approaches, demonstrating certain advantages in processing low-dimensional, stationary vibration data. However, when confronted with high-dimensional complex vibration signals with multi-source coupling, they are prone to insufficient feature extraction and attenuation of generalization performance2–4. Deep learning methods (e.g., LSTM, GRU) possess potential in non-linear signal processing by capturing temporal dependencies through deep network structures, but they suffer from drawbacks such as high model complexity, large demand for training data, and sensitivity to noise. Additionally, there remains room for improvement in the collaborative optimization of multi-source vibration mode separation and local feature capture5,6. Ma et al.7 summarized the latest applications of digital twin -inspired intelligent fault diagnosis (IFD) and remaining useful life (RUL) prediction, including virtual mathematical model construction, model parameter update, and deep learning technology to connect virtual data and real data for IFD and RUL prediction. Fan et al.8 proposed a novel lightweight denoising diffusion probability model for generating fault signals in limited sample scenarios. This method incorporated multi-dconv head transposed attention into the U-Net architecture, shifting attention calculations from the pixel dimension to the channel dimension by combining point-wise and depth-wise convolution, thereby significantly reducing computational complexity.

In existing research, single models often struggle to balance the global law fitting and local detail capture of complex multi-source vibration, while simple model fusion strategies are prone to new problems such as error propagation and feature redundancy. More critically, as core factors influencing prediction performance, model hyperparameters optimized through manual tuning are highly subjective and experience-dependent, making it difficult to adapt to the dynamic characteristics of complex multi-source vibration. This easily leads to overfitting or underfitting of the model. For example, an excessively small number of neighbors (K) in KNN enhances noise sensitivity, while an excessively large K weakens local features; an imbalance between tree depth and learning rate in XGBoost directly affects global fitting accuracy and generalization ability9.

To address the prediction difficulties of complex multi-source vibration and the pain points of hyperparameter optimization, this study proposes a hybrid prediction model integrating MVMD, parallel weighted KNN-XGBoost, and stepwise grid search for hyperparameter optimization. The model first designs a “training-validation-test” three-set data separation framework. Through stepwise grid search, it sequentially optimizes the number of neighbors for KNN, the fusion weights of KNN and XGBoost, and the tree depth and learning rate of XGBoost. The optimal parameter combination is determined with the RMSE metrics of the validation set as the objective function, effectively avoiding the limitations of manual parameter tuning. MVMD is employed to achieve mode separation of multi-source vibration signals, resolving issues of frequency overlap and mode mixing. Subsequently, a parallel weighted KNN-XGBoost structure is constructed: KNN is utilized to capture local similarity features, while XGBoost fits global non-linear relationships, thereby avoiding error propagation in serial structures.

Through multiple comparative experiments and ablation tests, this study systematically verifies the superiority of the proposed model in complex multi-source vibration prediction, providing an integrated technical pathway of “parameter optimization-modal separation-model fusion” for high-precision vibration prediction in engineering fields. Related theories

KNN

The KNN algorithm is a classic machine learning method that can be applied to both classification and regression tasks10,11. Its fundamental principle is: for a given test sample, identify the K nearest neighbor points in the training set and use these neighbors to predict the output of the test sample. The core mechanism lies in realizing regression prediction of target samples by measuring the feature space distance between samples.

In vibration signal prediction tasks, the algorithm first calculates the similarity distance between the vibration sample to be predicted and all samples in the training set based on distance metrics such as Euclidean distance. Subsequently, the samples are sorted in ascending order of distance, and the top K nearest neighbor samples are selected to form a local neighborhood. Finally, the mean value (or weighted mean value) of vibration responses of samples in this neighborhood is taken as the output result of the sample to be predicted.

XGBoost

XGBoost adopts decision trees as base learners, integrating the superior performance of tree models and gradient boosting algorithms12,13. Its core idea is to serially train multiple weak learners and combine them into a strong model. In each training iteration, XGBoost optimizes the loss function through gradient boosting, incorporates regularization terms to prevent overfitting, and employs an advanced weighted iterative strategy—each round of learning focuses on correcting the residuals of the previous round’s model.

During the learning process, XGBoost not only pays attention to the prediction error of the model but also considers the relative importance of each sample. By leveraging information derived from calculating gradients and the Hessian matrix, the model prioritizes samples that are difficult to fit. XGBoost also exhibits high flexibility in feature processing: it can handle missing values and perform automatic feature selection based on feature importance. Additionally, its parallel computing capability is another prominent feature—supporting parallel data processing in a multi-threaded environment enables XGBoost to maintain excellent performance when dealing with large-scale datasets.

XGBoost incorporates regularization strategies in model construction, including tree complexity control, sample weight adjustment, and learning rate reduction in each iteration step. These mechanisms effectively enhance the model’s generalization ability, allowing XGBoost to excel in handling large-scale, high-dimensional data.

MVMD

In 2019, Rehman and Aftab proposed the MVMD method for multi-channel signals, building upon the theoretical framework of VMD18,19. Compared with the traditional Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD)20, MVMD is constructed through rigorous variational optimization theory, featuring an explicit mathematical expression and clear physical meaning. This effectively overcomes inherent limitations of EMD, such as mode mixing and insufficient theoretical foundation.

A core advantage of MVMD lies in its ability to fully exploit and leverage the intrinsic correlations among multi-channel signals, preserving the coupling characteristics of data across channels during mode separation. Consequently, its decomposition accuracy significantly outperforms traditional methods including EMD and single-channel VMD. As an important extension of VMD in multivariate signal analysis, MVMD provides an effective solution for processing vibration signals with complex multi-source component coupling. It demonstrates unique technical advantages, particularly in handling vibration time-series data mixed with multi-source interference and complex components, enabling more precise mode separation and feature extraction21,22.

One of the key challenges in multi-source vibration data analysis is the frequency overlap among components from different sources. In such scenarios, traditional VMD tends to suffer from mode mixing when separating closely spaced frequency components. MVMD still maintains core advantages over traditional VMD in this regard, attributed to the inherent adaptability of its multivariate algorithmic framework to the implicit multi-source features of signals.

Within the univariate framework, VMD controls mode separation solely through single-channel bandwidth constraints. When the frequency difference between two components is smaller than the bandwidth threshold, they are merged into a single IMF, making it impossible to distinguish between different vibration sources in subsequent analyses. Even with single-channel input, MVMD’s variational framework retains the mathematical logic of multivariate constraints. During mode update, it achieves more refined differentiation of local feature differences among frequency-overlapping components (e.g., the aperiodicity of pedestrian vibration, the impulsive periodicity of construction vibration, and the fixed harmonics of equipment vibration) through residual linkage across virtual channels.

Proposed strategy

Model hyperparameter optimization: a critical preprocessing step

The number of neighbors (K) in the KNN algorithm determines the model’s smoothing degree: a smaller K value makes the model more prone to overfitting, while a larger K value leads to excessive model simplicity. The optimization logic adopted in this study is as follows: candidate values within the search range are iteratively evaluated, and the optimal K value is selected based on the RMSE of the validation set, thereby balancing bias and variance.

KNN excels at capturing local patterns, while XGBoost is proficient in modeling global non-linear relationships. The contributions of the two models are balanced through fusion weights ( and , with + = 1). The objective of weight optimization is to identify the weight combination that minimizes the validation set RMSE, maximizing the complementarity between the two models.

In the XGBoost model, the maximum tree depth is a core hyperparameter regulating the complexity of individual decision trees. Its primary function is to limit the maximum growth depth of decision trees, thereby balancing the model’s fitting ability and generalization performance:

If the parameter is excessively small (e.g., 1 or 2), the decision tree structure becomes overly simplified, failing to capture complex patterns and non-linear relationships implicit in the data. This results in underfitting, characterized by high training and test errors, as the model cannot fully exploit data information.

If the parameter is excessively small (e.g., 1 or 2), the decision tree structure becomes overly simplified, failing to capture complex patterns and non-linear relationships implicit in the data. This results in underfitting, characterized by high training and test errors, as the model cannot fully exploit data information.

Conversely, an excessively large maximum depth (e.g., 10 or 20) causes decision trees to grow excessively deep, overfitting noise and local details in the training data. This leads to overfitting, manifested by a significant reduction in training error but a substantial increase in test error. Additionally, the model’s training time increases noticeably, and the complexity of the tree structure impairs model interpretability.

Conversely, an excessively large maximum depth (e.g., 10 or 20) causes decision trees to grow excessively deep, overfitting noise and local details in the training data. This leads to overfitting, manifested by a significant reduction in training error but a substantial increase in test error. Additionally, the model’s training time increases noticeably, and the complexity of the tree structure impairs model interpretability.

Thus, the optimal value of the maximum tree depth must be tuned through strategies such as cross-validation to achieve an optimal balance between fitting ability and generalization performance. Furthermore, a smaller learning rate enhances model robustness but requires more iterations. In this study, the optimization logic involves traversing parameter combinations via grid search and selecting the optimal depth and learning rate that minimize the validation set RMSE, balancing model complexity and generalization ability.

In summary, the automatic hyperparameter optimization of the proposed hybrid model aims to enhance the model’s prediction generalization performance. By constructing a “training-validation-test” three-set data separation framework and adopting a stepwise grid search strategy, we sequentially optimize the number of neighbors in KNN, the fusion weights of KNN and XGBoost, and the core hyperparameters of XGBoost. The RMSE of the validation set is used as the objective function for optimization, and the generalization ability of the optimal parameter combination is ultimately verified using an independent test set. Strategy progression: ablation experiments

Step 1: KNN regression prediction with optimal parameters.

KNN regression was performed using the optimal parameter configuration: = 3, cosine distance as the similarity metric, and the ‘equal’ weight calculation method (uniform weights). Cosine distance is a widely used similarity measure in machine learning. In vibration time-series prediction tasks, it is particularly advantageous when the shape of feature vectors (e.g., similarity in vibration patterns) is more critical than their magnitude (vibration intensity). This metric facilitates the identification of vibration signals with similar variation trends but different amplitudes, which is highly relevant for capturing intrinsic temporal patterns in multi-source vibration data. The ‘equal’ weight method treats all K nearest neighbors as equally important in the prediction process. For regression tasks, the predicted value of a new sample is computed as the arithmetic mean of the output values of these K neighboring samples. Step 3: serial-KNN-XGBoost.

A serial hybrid model was constructed by integrating KNN and XGBoost with their respective optimal parameter configurations: = 3, , and .

In the serial-KNN-XGBoost strategy, KNN serves as a feature enhancement module. It first excavates local similarity features from the raw vibration data, which are then concatenated/fused with the original features to form the input for XGBoost. This design enables XGBoost to simultaneously learn both local patterns (captured by KNN) and global non-linear relationships (inherent in the raw data). Specifically, KNN functions as a feature extractor rather than a direct predictor: it extracts local similarity features from the raw data, merges these features with the original feature set, and the combined feature matrix is ultimately fed into XGBoost for final prediction. The core idea is to leverage the predictive information learned by KNN as prior knowledge or auxiliary features to facilitate XGBoost’s learning process. XGBoost can adaptively assess the reliability of KNN’s predictive results—relying on KNN’s outputs when they are trustworthy and adjusting predictions based on the original features when KNN’s results are deemed less reliable. The significantly high MAPE of 154.24% in the test set of the serial‑KNN‑XGBoost model is mainly attributed to the inherent characteristics of the MAPE and the defect of the serial fusion structure. MAPE is highly sensitive to small‑value vibration samples near zero, and tiny prediction deviations will be remarkably enlarged. Meanwhile, the serial structure leads to obvious error propagation: the local bias generated by KNN is directly transferred to XGBoost and further amplified in the final prediction. The proposed parallel weighted framework avoids error propagation and effectively alleviates this issue, demonstrating its superiority in stability and prediction reliability.

Nevertheless, the KNN-based feature enhancement strategy proves its feasibility: by supplementing XGBoost with neighborhood similarity features, it effectively improves the model’s fitting precision on the training set. This validates the rationality of integrating local pattern information into the global non-linear fitting framework, laying a foundation for the subsequent parallel fusion design.

Step 5: MVMD-XGBoost.

To further improve vibration prediction accuracy, MVMD is integrated with the XGBoost model configured with optimal hyperparameters. The core idea adopts a three-step framework: modal decomposition → modal-specific prediction → result fusion, which refines the prediction process by leveraging the frequency separation capability of MVMD.

Figure 11 presents the MVMD decomposition results of the raw vibration data. As illustrated, the raw vibration signal is decomposed into 8 IMF components, where each IMF corresponds to a vibration pattern with distinct frequency/scales (IMF1 represents high-frequency components, while IMF8 corresponds to low-frequency components). Vibration data typically consists of mixed multi-frequency signals; MVMD effectively decomposes these complex signals into simple, stationary sub-signals, thereby reducing the prediction complexity for the XGBoost model. In this study, the original signal is decomposed into 8 IMFs, which is comprehensively determined through spectrum analysis, center frequency observation and mode separation verification, aiming to avoid insufficient decomposition or excessive decomposition.

In summary, the MVMD-parallel-KNN-XGBoost model emerges as a balanced and optimal solution for multi-source vibration prediction. Among its core components, MVMD decomposition serves as the primary driver of performance leap: by decoupling complex vibration modes into stationary, frequency-separated sub-signals, it substantially reduces the inherent complexity of the prediction task and eliminates information interference between multi-source components. Meanwhile, parallel weighted fusion acts as a critical refinement mechanism. By synergistically integrating KNN’s strength in capturing local similarity patterns and XGBoost’s proficiency in modeling global non-linear relationships, it further enhances the model’s robustness against extreme samples and distribution shifts on the basis of MVMD’s modal separation capability.

This hybrid framework achieves a harmonious balance between fitting accuracy, generalization ability, and robustness, effectively addressing the core challenges of multi-source vibration signals (e.g., multi-frequency mixing, non-stationarity, and extreme value sensitivity) and providing a reliable technical solution for practical vibration prediction scenarios.

The computational efficiency of the proposed MVMD-parallel-KNN-XGBoost model is analyzed as follows: the MVMD decomposition is performed once offline during data preprocessing, and its computational cost is negligible for subsequent online prediction; the KNN and XGBoost models run in parallel during the inference stage, which effectively shortens the prediction time compared with the serial structure; the weighted fusion step involves only simple linear combination and introduces almost no additional computation. Overall, the proposed model achieves a favorable balance between high prediction accuracy and acceptable computational cost, making it suitable for real-world multi-source vibration time-series prediction.

Conclusions and future work

Research conclusions

Aiming at the core challenges of complex multi-source vibration signals—including non-stationarity, multi-frequency coupling, and insufficient prediction accuracy—this study constructs a technical framework centered on “hyperparameter optimization - signal preprocessing - model fusion”. Through comparative experiments and ablation studies, the parallel fusion model (i.e. MVMD-parallel-KNN-XGBoost model) is ultimately proposed. The key conclusions are as follows:

Stepwise grid search hyperparameter optimization ensures generalization performance

A “training-validation-test” three-set separation architecture was designed to optimize critical hyperparameters, including the number of neighbors for KNN, and the maximum tree depth and learning rate for XGBoost. With the validation set RMSE as the objective function, a stepwise optimization strategy was implemented. This approach eliminates the subjectivity of manual parameter tuning, reducing the training set MAPE of the MVMD-parallel-KNN-XGBoost model.

MVMD modal decomposition lays a critical foundation for performance improvement

MVMD accurately decomposes complex multi-source vibration signals into 8 IMF components spanning different frequency scales (ranging from high to low frequencies). The modal-specific prediction strategy not only reduces the complexity of vibration signals but also preserves the coupling characteristics between multi-source components, enabling subsequent models to more efficiently capture vibration patterns across various frequency bands.

Parallel weighted fusion outperforms serial feature enhancement, achieving complementary advantages

Performance comparisons between the serial-KNN-XGBoost and parallel-KNN-XGBoost models reveal:

In the serial structure, feature biases from KNN are directly propagated to XGBoost, resulting in a high test set MAPE and significant degradation of generalization ability.

In contrast, the parallel structure—based on independent prediction and weighted fusion—leverages KNN’s strength in capturing local similar vibration patterns while retaining XGBoost’s proficiency in modeling global non-linear relationships.

MVMD-parallel-KNN-XGBoost delivers optimal comprehensive performance for complex multi-source vibration prediction

Multi-model comparative experiments demonstrate that the MVMD-parallel-KNN-XGBoost model achieves the smallest test set MAE, MSE, and RMSE among all compared models, with the highest R². Although its test set MAPE increases slightly due to the influence of small-value samples, it remains lower than that of most baseline models (e.g., standalone KNN and the serial-KNN-XGBoost model). Radar chart comparisons further confirm that the proposed model exhibits the best performance across three key dimensions: fitting accuracy, generalization ability, and error stability. Thus, it serves as a balanced and optimal solution for time-series prediction of complex multi-source vibration signals.

Future outlook

While the model proposed in this study demonstrates excellent performance in complex multi-source vibration prediction, several avenues for further exploration remain:

Optimization of dynamic weight fusion mechanism. The current parallel framework employs fixed weights. Moving forward, adaptive weighting strategies (e.g., dynamically adjusting the contribution ratio of KNN and XGBoost based on sample neighborhood density or vibration frequency features) can be introduced to enhance the model’s robustness against extreme vibration samples.

Moving forward, adaptive weighting strategies (e.g., dynamically adjusting the contribution ratio of KNN and XGBoost based on sample neighborhood density or vibration frequency features) can be introduced to enhance the model’s robustness against extreme vibration samples.

Integration of multi-modal decomposition methods. Combining MVMD with wavelet decomposition and empirical wavelet transform (EWT) to construct a multi-scale decomposition framework could further refine the frequency components of vibration signals, enabling better adaptation to more complex multi-source coupling scenarios.

Model lightweighting and engineering deployment. The current model, which integrates modal decomposition and dual-model fusion, exhibits high computational complexity. Future efforts can focus on feature selection (e.g., removing redundant IMF components via SHAP values) and model compression (e.g., pruning XGBoost decision trees) to reduce computational costs, thereby facilitating its application in real-time vibration monitoring devices.

This would provide more comprehensive technical support for the health management of engineering systems.

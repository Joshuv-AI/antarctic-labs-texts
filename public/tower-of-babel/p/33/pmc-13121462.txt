Mode hopping via nonlinear magnon-magnon coupling in a synthetic antiferromagnet
Authors: Mujin You, Moojune Song, Jun Seok Seo, Donghyeon Lee, Seungha Yoon, Daiju Hayashi, Yoichi Shiota, Teruo Ono, Sanghoon Kim, Se Kwon Kim, Albert Min Gyu Park, Kab-Jin Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Identifying nonlinear interactions among magnons is crucial for advancing the field of magnonics and developing next-generation spintronic devices. In this work, we report the experimental observation of nonlinear magnon mode hopping and hysteretic behavior in a synthetic antiferromagnet (SAF). Under strong radio-frequency excitation, the magnon system exhibits abrupt transitions between acoustic and optic modes, accompanied by GHz-scale frequency jumps—orders of magnitude larger than previously reported in magnon-based hybrid systems. We further show that this mode hopping is hysteretic, reflecting multistable nonlinear dynamics, and support these findings through theory and micromagnetic simulations. These results establish a new regime of nonlinear magnon dynamics, going beyond previously reported strong-coupling effects, and highlight the potential of SAFs as platforms for magnon-based frequency converters and switches for information processing.

Introduction

Nonlinear phenomena play a crucial role in a wide range of physical systems, offering deep insights into complex interactions and emergent behaviors. From the turbulence of fluid dynamics1,2 to the formation of solitons in optics2,3, nonlinear physics has advanced our understanding of many-body systems4 and collective excitations5. In condensed matter physics, nonlinear interactions often give rise to unique phenomena6–8 such as chaos, pattern formation9, and bifurcations10, all of which are essential for advancing both fundamental theory11 and technological applications.

In magnonic systems, nonlinear physics has also been a focus of considerable attention due to its potential for manipulating spin-waves and enabling new functionalities in devices12,13. Nonlinear magnon interactions14 have been predominantly explored through multi-magnon scattering, which can lead to phenomena, such as magnon Bose-Einstein condensation (BEC)15–17, which is typically observed in incoherent magnon interactions where the magnons have random phases. More recently, magnon nonlinearity has been explored in hybrid systems18,19, where phase-defined coherent magnons couple with other quasiparticles, such as phonons or photons20–25, giving rise to phenomena such as frequency bi-stability26, gain-driven auto-oscillation27 and anticrossing gap closing28.

Direct nonlinear magnon-magnon interactions, however, remain far less understood. While several recent reports have demonstrated nonlinear magnon-magnon coupling phenomena12,29–32, most of these works have focused on spectral signatures such as frequency mixing or gap modulation33–37. What has not yet been explored—and what we focus on here—is the emergence of mode-hopping with hysteresis—an abrupt transition between two magnon modes under strong drive.

In this work, we experimentally demonstrate such nonlinear mode hopping in a synthetic antiferromagnet (SAF). The SAF supports two distinct magnon modes: optic (high-frequency) and acoustic (low-frequency) magnons. By tuning an external magnetic field, we coupled these modes and observed anticrossing behavior, consistent with the established strong coupling of two magnon modes. More importantly, under strong RF excitation, we uncovered a series of nonlinear effects that constitute the central findings of this work: (1) abrupt mode hopping between the optic and acoustic modes, accompanied by frequency jumps up to 5 GHz; (2) pronounced hysteresis of the mode hopping depending on the field sweep direction; and (3) a frequency redshift and closure of the anticrossing gap.

To understand these effects, we developed a theoretical model that qualitatively matches our experimental results. The model shows that the observed nonlinearities stem from interactions among propagating and Kittel magnon modes, both optic and acoustic branches. As the RF driving power increases, these magnons then participate in a three-magnon process between the optic and acoustic modes, resulting in the observed mode hopping and its hysteretic character. These findings advance the understanding of nonlinear magnon dynamics beyond previously reported strong-coupling effects, and highlight SAFs as a promising platform for magnon-based switches and frequency converters where large GHz-scale jumps can be exploited for information processing.

Results

Samples and measurement set-up

For this study, we fabricated a sample with a SAF structure consisting of Ru (3 nm)/CoFeB (15 nm)/Ru (0.6 nm)/CoFeB (15 nm)/Pt (1 nm)/Ru (3 nm)/Ta (3 nm). The two CoFeB layers with in-plane magnetization are separated by a 0.6 nm Ru spacer to induce antiferromagnetic coupling via the Ruderman-Kittel-Kasuya-Yoshida (RKKY) interaction. The sample was patterned into a strip with dimensions of 10 μm in width and 100 μm in length, and a 60 nm-thick SiO2 insulating layer was deposited above the strip. Finally, two coplanar waveguide (CPW) antennas were patterned on top of the insulating layer to generate the RF driving field. Figure 1b illustrates the schematic of our experimental setup. We drive an RF field through CPW1 to generate spin-waves, which propagate towards CPW2 within external in-plane magnetic field at an angle \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\theta }_{{{{\rm{k}}}}}$$\end{document}θk with respect to the propagating direction. The transmitted spin-waves are then detected using the S21 parameter of a vector network analyzer (VNA). Since our sample has a SAF structure, it supports two distinct magnon modes: the optic mode and the acoustic mode38, as depicted in Fig. 1c, d. In the optic mode (Fig. 1c), the magnetizations of the two CoFeB layers precess out-of-phase, resulting in a higher frequency, whereas in the acoustic mode (Fig. 1d), they precess in-phase, leading to a lower frequency. These two modes can be selectively excited by adjusting the angle of the applied magnetic field. When the field is applied at an angle of 0°, tilting the magnetization along the x-axis, RF excitation predominantly drives the optic mode. Conversely, when the magnetic field is applied at 90°, tilting the magnetization along the y-axis, the in-phase acoustic mode is excited (see Supplementary Fig. S1). The crossing of two magnon modes can be achieved when the magnetic field is applied at a 45° angle, where both modes are excited, resulting in their coupling. This coupling is manifested as an anticrossing with large cooperativity (see Supplementary Note 1)33–35,37. The observation of anticrossing indicates that the propagating spin-wave is dominant in our measurement, rather than the non-propagating Kittel mode, since the coupling between optic and acoustic modes in SAF occurs only for propagating spin-waves through dynamic dipolar interaction37. We note that, unlike previous reports on van der Waals magnets36—where an anticrossing gap was observed by applying out-of-plane (OOP) magnetic field—our SAF system shows no such OOP-induced anticrossing (Supplementary Note 2), which can be attributed to the relatively weak interlayer exchange field in our system36,39.

Nonlinear magnon-magnon coupling

We next investigate the nonlinear behavior of the magnon-magnon coupling. To do this, we fixed the magnetic field angle at 45 ° and gradually increased the RF driving power while measuring the spin-wave spectra. To avoid the influence of magnetization switching, the sample was first saturated at positive field and then returned to zero before increasing the field again [the effect of RF driving power on magnetization switching is discussed in Supplementary Note 3]. As shown in Fig. 2a–c, from −20 dBm to 0 dBm, the spectra show minimal change, with the anticrossing behavior clearly visible. Several distinct nonlinear phenomena are observed for high RF driving power. First, above the threshold, the overall resonant frequency experiences a redshift. Figure 2g quantifies this, showing the magnon frequency as a function of driving power, where a sudden frequency drop occurs once the threshold is exceeded. Second, we observe the closure of the anticrossing gap beyond this threshold. For clearer visualization, Fig. 2h presents the frequency spectrum at the anticrossing point as the driving power increases, showing the gradual closing of the gap. These observations—frequency redshift and gap closure—suggest that the propagating magnons, which are primarily responsible for the frequency anticrossing, weaken in the nonlinear regime, and the lower-frequency Kittel magnons may become dominant.

Most notably, mode hopping is observe once the power exceeds the threshold. As we increase the magnetic field, we observed that at around 20 mT—where the frequency of the optic magnon, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\omega }_{{{{\rm{op}}}}}$$\end{document}ωop, is nearly twice that of the acoustic magnon, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\omega }_{{{{\rm{ac}}}}}$$\end{document}ωac, —the optic mode abruptly transitions into the acoustic mode (as highlighted by the red guide in Fig. 2d–f. This suggests that power-induced nonlinearity in the magnons may trigger an optic-to-acoustic mode transition via a three-magnon scattering process40,41, where one optic magnon with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\omega }_{{{{\rm{op}}}}}$$\end{document}ωop splits into two acoustic magnons with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\omega }_{{{{\rm{ac}}}}}$$\end{document}ωac. Importantly, additional measurements under out-of-plane magnetic fields and at different in-plane field angles are presented in Supplementary Note 4, which further support the robustness of these nonlinear behaviors.

It should be noted that although our measurements employ a single excitation frequency, nonlinear interactions between magnons at different frequencies can still manifest. Recent studies have clearly demonstrated this point: ref. 29. showed that single-frequency FMR excitation in a SAF can nevertheless reveal acoustic–optic three-magnon scattering, while ref. 42. reported nonlinear frequency doubling under high-power drive using heterodyne MOKE. These findings establish that single-frequency measurements can capture signatures of multi-frequency magnon interactions, consistent with the observed mode hopping.

Hysteretic behavior in nonlinear magnon-magnon coupling

To further investigate the nonlinear-induced mode hopping, we explore the field sweep direction dependence, as hysteresis is commonly observed in nonlinear systems. Under the same experimental conditions as before, we measured the spin-wave spectra while sweeping the magnetic field forward (increase) and backward (decrease). As shown in Fig. 3a, b, below the threshold power (0 dBm), there is no noticeable difference in the spin-wave spectra for forward (Fig. 3a) or backward (Fig. 3b) field sweeps. However, at an RF power of 2.5 dBm, which exceeds the threshold, the spin-wave spectra exhibit clear hysteresis, with the mode hopping point depending on the direction of the field sweep, as seen in Fig. 3c (forward) and 3d (backward).Fig. 3The hysteretic behavior of the spin-wave spectra with respect to in-plane field sweep direction.a–d At 0 dBm RF excitation power, the spin-wave spectra at the hybrid magnon mode show no significant change when comparing the forward field sweep (a) with the backward field sweep (b). However, at 2.5 dBm RF power, a noticeable hysteresis appears in the spin-wave spectrum, as seen in the forward sweep (c) and backward sweep (d). e–h Traces of the maximum spin-wave peak positions during forward field sweeps (red plots) and backward field sweeps (blue plots). In (e), the forward and backward sweeps produce nearly identical peak positions at 0 dBm, indicating the absence of hysteresis. To provide a more quantitative analysis, we extract the resonant frequencies from each spectrum and plot them as a function of magnetic field for forward (red) and backward (blue) sweep, as shown in Fig. 3e–h. As the power increases, we observe that the hysteresis gap gradually narrows: at 2.5 dBm, the gap is approximately 15 mT (Fig. 3f), while at 10 dBm, the gap reduces to less than 5 mT (Fig. 3h). Discussion

The strong agreement between our theoretical calculations and experimental results suggests that G1 effectively captures the influence of RF power. In our model, G1 represents the linear magnonic gain associated with the transition from propagating magnons to Kittel magnons. This picture naturally explains the experimental results: as RF power increases, propagating magnons are progressively converted into Kittel magnons, and the growing Kittel magnon population drives the hysteresis gap narrowing. The mechanism can be understood in terms of three-magnon scattering. Mode hopping arises when the resonance condition \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${{{{\rm{\omega }}}}}_{{{{\rm{op}}}}}=2{{{{\rm{\omega }}}}}_{{{{\rm{ac}}}}}$$\end{document}ωop=2ωac is satisfied, enabling the conversion between optic and acoustic magnons. If only Kittel magnons were present, mode hopping would occur only at this sharp resonance condition, and hysteresis would be minimal. However, in our system both Kittel and propagating magnons coexist. For propagating magnons, broader scattering channels are allowed, e.g., \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${{{{\rm{\omega }}}}}_{{{{\rm{op}}}}}\left(k+q\right)\to {{{{\rm{\omega }}}}}_{{{{\rm{ac}}}}}\left(k\right)+{{{{\rm{\omega }}}}}_{{{{\rm{ac}}}}}\left(q\right)$$\end{document}ωopk+q→ωack+ωacq where two acoustic magnons with different frequencies and wave vectors participate. These relaxed conditions extend the field range over which mode hopping can occur, thereby broadening the hysteresis. More specifically, as the magnetic field increases, the three-magnon process involving Kittel magnons is triggered first. However, if the population of Kittel magnons is insufficient, the field must be further increased until the condition for the propagating three-magnon process is satisfied, at which point mode hopping can be observed. Therefore, as the relative population of Kittel magnons increases with RF power, the system favors the sharper Kittel scattering channel, and the hysteresis gap narrows.

This RF-power–driven conversion can be corroborated by micromagnetic simulations, which directly show the transition from propagating to Kittel magnons with increasing drive power [see Supplementary Note 6]. Furthermore, recent k-resolved MOKE experiments have provided direct evidence that the population near k = 0 grows with RF power33, fully consistent with our interpretation. Taken together, the experimental data, theoretical modeling, and micromagnetic simulations converge to establish that the RF-induced conversion from propagating magnons to Kittel magnons underlies both the onset of mode hopping and the narrowing of hysteresis in our SAF system. We note that while four-magnon interactions may also contribute to the observed nonlinearities, their effect is negligible compared with the dominant three-magnon process [see Supplementary Note 7].

It should be noted that this mode hopping is accompanied by a frequency jump of up to 5 GHz, which is orders of magnitude larger than previously reported in magnon-based hybrid systems26,27. Such a significant frequency shift could be utilized in two-state magnon mode converters embedded in spin-wave logic devices, with a hysteretic character. It could also be applied to nonlinear decouplers (coupling switches), even in ultrastrong magnon-magnon coupled systems, by slightly modulating the RF power.

In summary, we have demonstrated nonlinear mode hopping between optic and acoustic magnon modes in a synthetic antiferromagnet. We observed that the optic magnon mode transitions into the acoustic mode as the magnetic field increases, with the reverse process occurring as the field decreases. This mode hopping shows a hysteric behavior depending on the field sweeping direction, and is accompanied by a frequency shift of up to 5 GHz. Our theoretical model successfully captures the observed nonlinear magnon dynamics, highlighting the critical role of the interaction between propagating and non-propagating magnons in these phenomena. Our work not only elucidates the fundamental physics of nonlinear magnon-magnon interactions, but also offers promising applications for magnon-based devices, such as mode converters and nonlinear coupling switches in spin-wave logic systems.

Methods

Film preparation and device fabrication

The Ru (3 nm)/ CoFeB (15 nm)/ Ru (0.6 nm)/ CoFeB (15 nm)/ Pt (1 nm)/ Ru (3 nm)/ Ta (3 nm) synthetic antiferromagnetic film (illustrative sample stack is described at the Supplementary Note S1) were deposited on Si/SiO2 (200 nm) substrates using AJA compact UHV sputtering system under a base pressure of ~10-9 Torr. The working pressure was maintained at 3 mTorr with an Ar gas flow of 30 sccm into the chamber. All the metallic layers were deposited by DC magnetron sputtering.

The film was patterned into the strip-line structure with dimensions of 10 µm in width and 100 µm in length using photolithography (MicroWriter ML3 Baby, Durham Magneto Optics) and Ar ion milling (KVET-B2000, KOREA VACUUM TECH) under a low base pressure of 7 × 10-7 Torr. The Ar ion beam was generated by flowing 10 sccm Ar gas into the chamber, maintaining a working pressure of 0.5 mTorr. On top of the pattern, we deposited 65 µm-width 90 µm-wide and 60 nm-thick SiO2 insulating layer (blue region in Fig. 1a) to prevent flowing of leakage current from metallic sample to coplanar waveguide (CPW) antennas.

The CPW antennas for the GSG contact were fabricated using a Ti (5 nm)/Au (150 nm) through photolithography, following the same process as the sample patterning, while the central parts were designed by E-beam lithography (TESCAN essence MIRA, EBL kit). After the lift-off process, the CPW geometry is given by 1.28 µm-width signal-line and 0.68 µm-width ground-line. The centre-to-centre distance between signal lines is 4.94 µm, while the shortest distance between ground lines is just 1 µm, as illustrated in Fig. 1a, with an enlarged view provided in Supplementary Fig. S1d.

RF measurements set-up

The spin-wave spectra were measured using a vector network analyser (VNA), specifically the Keysight PNA E5080, while applying an in-plane magnetic field. The RF-signal processing was conducted over a frequency range of 100 MHz to 15 GHz, with IF-bandwidth of 500 Hz. The magnetic field was applied using a customized in-plane electromagnet from KR-TECH (Korea), with a resolution of 0.1 mT. The measurements were performed using a home-built probe station equipped with GSG probes and RF measurement toolkits.

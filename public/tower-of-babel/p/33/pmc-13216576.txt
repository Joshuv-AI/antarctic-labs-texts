Artistic collaborative design optimization of urban public architecture based on faster region convolutional neural network and artificial intelligence
Authors: Miao Wang, Kai Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study proposes a collaborative design optimization framework based on the improved Faster Region-based Convolutional Neural Network (Faster R-CNN). It aims to handle the current situation of over-reliance on subjective experience and low collaborative efficiency in the art design of urban public architecture. Through domain adaptation of the standard Faster R-CNN model, modules such as architectural image pre-training, bidirectional multi-scale feature fusion, texture enhancement, and style-aware attention are introduced; these can remarkably improve the model’s detection accuracy for complex artistic elements. On this basis, a human-machine collaborative system integrating intelligent analysis, visual interaction, and feedback learning is constructed. This approach combines the quantitative analysis capability of artificial intelligence (AI) with the professional creativity of designers. Experiments are conducted based on the public ADE20K dataset. The improved model achieves a mean average precision (mAP) of 73.5%, representing an 8.4% increase compared with the baseline model. In actual collaborative scenarios, the proposed system can effectively improve the style consistency and specification compliance rate of design schemes while shortening the average design cycle. This study provides systematic theoretical methods and practical paths for AI to empower architectural art design, promoting the transformation of the design process toward a data-driven, human-machine collaborative, and intelligent direction.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-47727-9.

Introduction

Urban public architecture serves as the core carrier of a city1. Venues such as libraries, museums, and theaters are far more than functional spaces; they also represent the cultural connotation and public spirit of the city2,3. The art design of these buildings—ranging from external form and spatial experience to material details and landscape integration—exerts a profound impact on the public’s aesthetic perception and the city’s overall style4–6. At present, the traditional art design process of public architecture is facing a series of practical challenges; this process has long been highly dependent on the personal experience and subjective inspiration of designers, leading to time-consuming and costly scheme review and optimization7,8. Meanwhile, modern architectural design projects usually involve complex collaboration among multiple disciplines and teams. During scheme iteration, minor adjustments to a certain artistic element can trigger chain reactions. However, traditional methods struggle to quickly and accurately evaluate the comprehensive impact of such changes on the overall artistic style and environmental coordination9–11.

Artificial intelligence (AI) technology, represented by deep learning (DL), is transforming the way of architectural design. As an important branch of AI, computer vision offers powerful new tools12. For example, through object detection technology, computers can automatically identify specific building components and decorative elements in design drawings or real-scene photos, and accurately mark their positions13–15. This enables design analysis to be based on concrete data rather than mere subjective textual descriptions, offering new possibilities for more scientific design decision-making16–18. It frees design analysis from the previous mode that mainly relied on empirical descriptions and subjective perceptions19. Now, this study can evaluate design schemes using specific data, such as the number of new Chinese-style window panes in a certain area or the spatial distribution density of decorative elements.

Despite remarkable progress in general object detection technology, direct application to the detection of architectural art elements still faces three major challenges. First, architectural art elements exhibit a high degree of stylistic abstraction, such as the Baroque style’s complexity and the simplicity of Minimalism. Such aesthetic characteristics are difficult to fully represent using conventional bounding boxes and category labels. Second, art elements cover an extremely wide range of scales, from large-scale facade composition to subtle relief textures. The multi-scale fusion mechanism of existing detection networks often loses critical low-level details when transmitting high-level semantic information. Third, architectural art detection requires both regulatory compliance and aesthetic consistency; this goes beyond the scope of traditional detection tasks that only focus on localization accuracy. Therefore, in response to the above-mentioned unique challenges, this study proposes an improved Faster R-CNN framework integrating texture enhancement, bidirectional multi-scale feature fusion, and style-aware attention; it also constructs a human-machine collaborative optimization mechanism. This framework aims to fill the theoretical gap in the application of general detection models in the field of architectural art. At the same time, systematic methodological support can be provided for AI-enabled architectural art design. Literature review

The research on the art design of urban public architecture has a long history. As pointed out by Amin (2008)20, public architecture was not merely a physical space, but also a spiritual container that carried urban culture and collective memory. Many scholars, such as Pan (2025)21 and Abd Elrahman & Asaad (2021)22, systematically summarized the basic principles of public architectural design. They emphasized that it should meet multiple requirements of function, form, technology, and symbolic significance simultaneously. However, traditional evaluations mostly rely on expert experience and qualitative descriptions, lacking objective and quantifiable analytical tools, which brings uncertainty to design optimization.

With the increasing complexity of projects, the collaborative design model has become mainstream in industry practice. Zhang et al. (2020)23 summarized that technological development greatly promoted multi-disciplinary collaboration, evolving from early CAD collaboration to today’s BIM platforms. Nevertheless, its core still focuses on the integration of engineering data and processes. In recent years, with the penetration of AI, many frameworks aimed at improving collaborative intelligence have emerged. For example, An et al. (2025)24 proposed an architectural generation design framework based on a multi-agent system; their research realized automatic scheme optimization under multi-disciplinary constraints. Saad et al. (2023)25 developed a BIM plug-in integrating natural language processing; this allowed designers to adjust model parameters through semantic instructions. However, the core objectives of existing tools, whether traditional BIM or emerging AI collaborative frameworks, still concentrate on functionality, compliance, and engineering efficiency; meanwhile, the ability to support collaborative analysis and optimization of soft elements (e.g., artistic style and aesthetic images) is obviously insufficient. This constitutes an important gap that this study aims to fill: the lack of a system that can deeply understand artistic semantics and drive the human-machine collaborative creative process.

In architectural image analysis, computer vision technology has made remarkable progress. Obeso et al. (2019)26 verified that a convolutional neural network (CNN) was successfully applied to architectural style classification, facade segmentation, and component recognition. For instance, Cantemir & Kandemir (2024)27 used CNN to automatically identify historical architectural styles, demonstrating the potential of this technology in style analysis. In terms of generative design, as explored by Abdollahi et al. (2020)28, the generative adversarial network (GAN) was adopted to generate architectural volume or facade schemes, initiating a new paradigm of data-driven design. Shi et al. (2024)29 used diffusion models to generate highly realistic and diverse architectural facade images, showing the great potential of generative AI in creative inspiration. However, the existing generation methods mostly focus on the generation of the whole form; there is no in-depth study on the identification, analysis, and controllable generation of fine-grained artistic elements such as specific decorative components. At the same time, most of these studies are in the one-way stage of analysis or generation; besides, the results of visual perception are not seamlessly embedded in the dynamic collaborative design workflow in a closed-loop manner30,31.

Although Faster R-CNN is very mature in general object detection, its systematic application in urban public architectural art design is still in its infancy. Current applications are mostly concentrated on the simple survey or damage detection of building elements, such as detecting cracks in external walls or window types. In the field of architectural element detection, existing studies mainly focus on the identification and localization of common architectural components (e.g., doors, windows, columns, and beams); they serve engineering tasks such as automatic reconstruction of building information models or urban remote sensing analysis. However, research dedicated to the detection of architectural art elements remains relatively scarce. The core challenge of such elements lies in the fact that they are not only geometric entities but also carriers of style and culture; their aesthetic value often transcends physical form. General detection models such as the standard Faster R-CNN perform well in general object detection tasks. However, their feature extraction networks lack sufficient sensitivity to textural details and high-level stylistic semantics; meanwhile, they struggle to distinguish subtle differences between different artistic schools (e.g., the distinction between Gothic and Renaissance decorations). Thus, the improved framework proposed in this study is uniquely necessary. It introduces a texture enhancement module to capture fine-grained decorative details and adopts a style-aware attention mechanism to align high-level stylistic semantics. In the meantime, it incorporates aesthetic dimensions into optimization objectives through a style consistency loss function. These designs collectively form a dedicated solution for architectural art element detection and compensate for the lack of artistic perception in existing methods.

Research methodology

Overall framework design

In order to effectively integrate the Faster R-CNN model into the practical workflow of urban public architectural art design, this study proposes a closed-loop human-machine collaborative design optimization framework. The core objective of this framework is to build an intelligent cycle integrating perception, comprehension, and decision-making; it deeply combines the powerful data analysis capability of AI with the professional creative judgment of designers, thereby improving design efficiency and quality.

The overall framework of the proposed AI-assisted collaborative design optimization system is illustrated in Fig. 2. The entire framework is a dynamic cyclic system consisting of four main phases. Initially, multi-source design data and prior knowledge are input into the system. Such data then enters the intelligent analysis module, where the improved Faster R-CNN model conducts automatic identification and quantitative analysis of artistic elements, and generates specific optimization suggestions. Next, in the design generation and adjustment phase, designers can intuitively review these suggestions and selectively adopt or modify the schemes through a human-machine interactive interface. Finally, the designers’ decision feedback is recorded by the system and used to optimize the model, forming a complete closed loop.

Faster R-CNN model adaptation and improvement

Design drawings and real-scene images in public architectural art design contain a lot of elements with specific artistic styles, such as decorative components, window pane patterns, and facade divisions. Although the standard Faster R-CNN model boasts strong general object detection capability, it still faces challenges when directly applied to architectural art images. To improve the model’s detection performance for architectural artistic elements, this study modifies the standard Faster R-CNN, including feature enhancement, multi-scale detection optimization, and attention mechanism integration. These improvements enable the model to better capture the geometric rules, texture details, and style features in architectural art images. Considering the strong structural characteristics and rich textures of artistic elements in architectural images, this study adopts ResNet-101-FPN as the backbone network. Meanwhile, it introduces dilated convolution into each layer of the feature pyramid to expand the receptive field. The Gram matrix describes the correlation between different feature channels; it can effectively express the overall texture patterns of artistic styles and the interaction between features, such as the co-occurrence laws of different colors and texture directions. This loss ensures that the detection results are accurate in position by constraining the similarity between the Gram matrices of the prediction and the real target; meanwhile, it is consistent in deep stylistic semantics; this is the core difference between art element detection and general industrial detection.

The feature map X used to calculate the Gram matrix in this study is derived from the style attention-weighted features in the backbone network; the style feature extraction function ϕ refers to this Gram matrix calculation process. In order to capture the subtle differences of architectural style, the backbone network is subjected to domain-adaptive pre-training on an architectural style image dataset. The pre-training dataset contains general scenes; it also covers a large number of architectural facades and indoor images from classical to modern, with style labels such as Neoclassicism, Art Deco, New Chinese Style, and so on. Through this domain-specific pre-training, the feature X extracted from the network and its derived Gram matrix G can more sensitively encode the unique statistical laws of different architectural styles in texture, color, and composition, thus supporting the effective calculation of style consistency loss.

Collaborative design optimization mechanism

The core of the collaborative optimization mechanism lies in converting design knowledge and experience of architectural art into rules understandable and applicable to computers. This study constructs a structured design knowledge base, serving as the “brain” for the system’s intelligent analysis. The process of constructing a knowledge base that embeds design rules and style features is demonstrated in Fig. 5. This study extracts core design principles and style rules from massive classic public architecture cases, including drawings, documents, and real-scene data. Through expert annotation and data mining, such knowledge is collated and structured to form a set of computable design rule systems. This system incorporates mandatory design specifications, such as fire separation distances and accessibility requirements. More importantly, it integrates a rich aesthetic experience. Examples encompass the unique visual elements, proportional relationships, and material matching principles corresponding to different architectural styles (New Chinese style, modernism, art deco, etc.).

Fig. 5Workflow for embedding design rules and style features into the knowledge base.

Workflow for embedding design rules and style features into the knowledge base.

This study adopts two technologies—knowledge graph and vector embedding—to organize such knowledge. The knowledge graph clearly expresses the logical relationships between elements and rules. For instance, “New Chinese style” includes features like a dark gray sloped roof and a wooden lattice window. In contrast, vector embedding converts the artistic conception of styles into numerical values in a high-dimensional space, enabling the system to perform quantitative calculations of style similarity.

True optimization is not accomplished unilaterally by machines, but rather results from in-depth dialogue and joint iteration between designers and intelligent systems. Experimental design and performance evaluation

Datasets collection

This study selects the public ADE20K dataset as the experimental foundation. The official website is http://groups.csail.mit.edu/vision/datasets/ADE20K/. This dataset contains more than 20,000 finely annotated indoor and outdoor scene images, covering a rich variety of architecture-related elements. However, the ADE20K is not designed for the analysis of architectural art details. Therefore, this study carries out a series of rigorous professional treatments and reconstructions on it. The specific processes and quality control measures are as follows.

Image screening and category definition criteria

Image screening and category definition criteria

Image screening and category definition criteria

Images containing typical urban public architectures, such as museums, libraries, stations, theatres, and municipal halls, are manually screened from ADE20K, and a total of 5246 images are obtained. The screening criteria are as follows: the main building is clear and identifiable, and has obvious public and artistic characteristics. In order to focus on artistic elements, this study cooperates with three architects with more than five years of practical experience to discuss and determine 12 categories of core artistic components. The definition standard is mainly based on its universality and importance in shaping architectural style recognition, aesthetic value, and symbolic significance. The final categories include decorative columns, artistic window lattice, relief sculptures, pediments, metal decorative components, mosaics/inlaid paintings, artistic railings/grilles, stained glass, monumental sculptures, canopy/door head decorations, artistic signs, and murals/wall decorations.

(2)Annotation protocol and quality control

(2)Annotation protocol and quality control

Annotation protocol and quality control

For annotation protocols, a detailed PB-Art12 Annotation Guide is formulated; it clarifies the definition, visual discriminant features, and bounding box drawing specifications for each category of elements. For example, the annotation of reliefs needs to include the attached background wall area, as well as the handling principles for difficult cases. The annotation work is independently completed by two annotators trained with this guide. For quality assurance, a two-stage quality control process is implemented. In the first stage, a senior annotator conducts an initial verification and unified correction of all annotation results. In the second stage, the cooperating designers are invited to conduct a final professional review of 10% of the annotated images selected by random sampling; this ensures that the annotation results conform to professional architectural cognition. In order to quantify the reliability of the annotations, the study calculates the intersection-over-union (IoU) consistency of the two initial annotators on 200 random samples, and the average IoU reaches 0.85; this indicates good consistency of the annotation results.

(3)Dataset composition and partitioning

(3)Dataset composition and partitioning

Dataset composition and partitioning

Through the above process, the study constructs a dedicated dataset named PB-Art12; this dataset contains a total of approximately 84,000 high-quality instance annotations of artistic elements. To ensure the impartiality of the evaluation, the study divides the dataset into training, validation, and test sets at a ratio of 7:1.5:1.5. Stratified sampling is strictly conducted according to building types and scene complexity during the division; this guarantees a balanced distribution of building types and visual conditions in each subset. All images are uniformly scaled to 1024 × 1024 pixels before being input into the network.

(4)Description of data source characteristics

(4)Description of data source characteristics

Description of data source characteristics

It should be noted that PB-Art12 is derived from ADE20K, and its original images are mostly standard photographic works from the internet. Therefore, most of the data are captured from conventional eye-level perspectives under sufficient daylight conditions. The weather conditions in the images are mostly sunny; this shows a certain degree of concentration. Samples under extreme conditions (e.g., low light and severe weather) and unconventional perspectives (e.g., aerial photography and extreme upward shooting) are relatively limited. The 12 categories currently defined are mainly targeted at geometric decorative components that can be clearly defined by bounding boxes.

Experimental environment

All experiments are conducted in the same stable computing environment. The hardware platform is powered by two NVIDIA Real Time eXchange (RTX) 4090 graphics processing units (GPUs), each with 24 gigabytes (GB) of GDDR6X video memory, see in Appendix A. This study selects the RTX 4090 mainly based on its excellent single-precision floating-point computing performance and support for large-batch processing; this is important for training object detection models on high-resolution images. The system is equipped with an Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) Ryzen 9 7950 × 16-core processor and 128GB of Double Data Rate 5 memory. Sufficient memory bandwidth ensures high efficiency in large-scale data loading and preprocessing. Data are stored on a 2 TB NVMe PCIe 4.0 solid-state drive, guaranteeing high-speed data reading and writing and reducing the impact of Input/Output (I/O) bottlenecks on training speed.

The software environment is built on the Ubuntu 22.04 LTS operating system. The DL framework adopts PyTorch 2.0.0, paired with CUDA 12.1 and cuDNN 8.9 drivers to give full play to the hardware acceleration potential of GPUs. The core development language is Python 3.10. All experimental codes are managed in versions through Git, and the Weights & Biases (W&B) platform is used for experiment tracking, hyperparameter recording, and result visualization. To simplify environment deployment and ensure consistency, the entire project is encapsulated in a Docker container. The container image has been made public for reproducibility.

Parameters setting

To ensure the scientificity, reproducibility, and optimal performance of the model training process, this study conducts systematic configuration and optimization of all hyperparameters. Table 1 details the main parameters used in this study and their setting values. These parameter settings are not initial values, but results obtained through a series of ablation experiments and Bayesian optimization. For instance, the learning rate is selected via logarithmic scale search ranging from 1e-4 to 1e-2, and the final value of 1e-3 achieves the fastest convergence speed and the lowest loss on the validation set. This study compares the Adam and AdamW optimizers, finding that AdamW stably achieves higher mean average precision (mAP) on the PB-Art12 dataset.

This study sets the per-GPU batch size to 8 to accommodate the high-resolution 1024 × 1024 input with limited GPU memory. However, the relatively small batch size may affect the accuracy of batch statistics estimation by batch normalization layers. In order to alleviate this problem and make full use of hardware, this study adopts synchronous batch normalization; this can ensure that the normalized statistics are calculated based on a global batch size of 16 when data are trained in parallel on two RTX 4090 GPUs. This effectively stabilizes the training process and maintains the benefits of normalization. In addition, this study uses a cosine annealing learning rate scheduler with a warm-up stage to offset the gradient noise and training instability that may be caused by small batches. The final model converges smoothly on the verification set, and there is no significant performance fluctuation induced by batch size; this shows that the above strategy is effective.

As can be found from the comparisons in Figs. 7 and 8, the improved model achieves the best overall performance among all comparison models, with an overall mAP@0.5 and recall reaching 73.5% (95% CI: [72.1%, 74.9%])and 75.6% (95% CI: [74.2%, 77.0%]). Compared with the original Faster R-CNN baseline (65.1% [95% CI: 63.7%, 66.5%]), the improvements of 8.4 and 8.0% points are statistically significant (paired t-test, p < 0.001). Notably, this model shows the most remarkable performance advantage for small-scale, complex-texture artistic elements such as reliefs and mural paintings, with mAP improved by more than 10%. This directly verifies the effectiveness of the introduced multi-scale feature fusion and texture enhancement modules. Although the one-stage detector YOLOv5 runs faster, its accuracy, especially the recall for small targets, is significantly lower than that of this model. The Transformer-based DEtection TRansformer (DETR) model performs poorly on this dataset, which may be related to its characteristic of requiring large amounts of data for training. Consequently, the carefully improved two-stage detection framework for specific fields still has obvious advantages in accuracy.

In addition to comparisons with the aforementioned general detection models, the proposed method differs fundamentally in design philosophy from specialized studies targeting the architectural domain in recent years. For instance, the multi-scale attention approach proposed by Xu et al. (2023)32 focused on the detection of ordinary architectural components such as doors, windows, columns, and beams; they emphasized scale variations in geometric forms rather than textural features and stylistic semantics of artistic elements. Huang et al. (2021)33 concentrated on mural disease detection. Although their method involves texture analysis, it is designed for specific defect patterns and lacks generalization ability across different artistic styles. In contrast, the innovation of this study is reflected in three aspects. First, in terms of problem positioning, it clearly targets artistic elements as a neglected detection object; it also directly addresses the triple challenges of dense textures, large-scale spans, and abstract styles. Second, considering methodology, it systematically integrates texture enhancement, multi-scale fusion, and style perception instead of improving only a single module. Third, regarding effect verification, it reports the overall accuracy; it also proves the model’s remarkable advantages on the most challenging small-scale and high-complexity elements through fine-grained analysis. These factors together constitute the differentiated contributions of this study relative to existing academic work.

According to the element category analysis in Table 2, the performance improvement of the model is not uniformly distributed but shows obvious category preferences. The most significant improvements are observed in mosaic/inlaid works (+ 11.1% points) and murals/wall decorations (+ 11.1% points). These two types of elements share a key feature: they often lack clear geometric boundaries; their artistry is precisely reflected in the continuous visual field formed by color gradients, material textures, and interwoven patterns. Traditional detection models are accustomed to locating objects through edges and corners; they often fail to deal with such blurred-boundary targets. The proposed model, however, can capture recurring pattern features from the channel dimension through the texture enhancement module; it can also identify a mosaic region based on texture consistency even without clear contour lines. In contrast, monumental sculptures and decorative columns with relatively small improvements share the common feature of having a clear main structure and outline; besides, the baseline models themselves can be well identified, thus leaving relatively limited room for improvement.

The analysis of the target scale dimension reveals an important phenomenon. The model’s improvement on small-scale targets (+ 12.1% points) is much greater than that on large-scale targets (+ 6.2% points); however, the improvement is not linear. Further observation shows that elements that are small but delicate benefit the most. For example, hollow carvings on window panes and acanthus leaf ornaments on column caps are small in area but carry key information for style recognition. Such elements often disappear with the downsampling of feature maps in the standard FPN. In contrast, the proposed bidirectional fusion mechanism opens a direct information channel for these small but important details; this enables them to cross hierarchical differences and directly participate in subsequent detection decisions. Besides, large-scale elements with limited improvement, such as full pediments or large murals, are already well preserved in high-level features; the additional detailed information provides little help for their recognition.

The most noteworthy finding comes from the texture complexity dimension. The model’s performance improvement shows a significant positive correlation with texture complexity; it has an increase of 5.7% points for low complexity and 12.3% points for high complexity. However, this relationship is not a simple linear increase. A close examination of the data reveals further differentiation within the high-complexity category. Categories with repetitive texture patterns (e.g., regularly arranged geometric mosaics) improve more significantly than categories with random textures, such as natural rough stone walls. This indicates that the style-aware attention module is better at capturing regular repetitions, namely pattern features that form stylistic identifiers, rather than purely random details. This finding also suggests the potential boundaries of the proposed method. For artistic expressions that lack regularity and rely more on the natural texture of materials, the model’s perception ability still needs to be improved.

The above analysis shows that the improvement of the proposed model does not take effect uniformly in all scenarios. Instead, it exhibits the most prominent advantages under specific conditions, namely targets with blurred boundaries but regular textures and small scales but dense information. These are exactly the most challenging scenarios in architectural art detection that best reflect artistry; this verifies the pertinence and effectiveness of the proposed modules.

Results of the ablation experiment

To rigorously verify the specific contributions of the proposed improvement measures to the final performance, a series of ablation experiments is carried out. The experiments take the standard Faster R-CNN with ResNet-101-FPN as the baseline model A0. On this basis, key improvement modules are gradually introduced. Adding architectural image domain pre-training to the baseline model yields Model A1. Then, the improved multi-scale detection strategy is introduced using A1 to construct Model A2. This study sequentially adds the texture enhancement module and the style-aware attention module to form Models A3 and A4, respectively, thereby evaluating the roles of these components designed for fine-grained features of artistic elements. Adding the style consistency loss function based on A4 constitutes the proposed complete model A5. Model A6 has all components of the complete model except for style consistency. Model A7 uses the standard FPN instead of the improved multi-scale strategy. To validate the contribution of each proposed component, a systematic ablation study was conducted. The results are summarized in Fig. 10.

Among them, architectural image domain pre-training contributes the largest single-step improvement, increasing mAP and recall by 2.7 and 2.5% points, respectively (p < 0.01); this highlights the key role of transferring general visual prior knowledge to the professional architectural field. Discussion

The above experimental results verify the proposed framework’s effectiveness from multiple dimensions. Gomaa & Abdalrazik (2024)34 pointed out that improving object detection performance often requires model adaptation according to the data distribution of specific fields. This study follows this idea and remarkably improves the model’s perception ability for artistic elements through strategies such as architectural image pre-training and multi-scale architecture optimization. It is particularly noteworthy that the model’s performance improvement for small-scale complex texture elements (e.g., murals and reliefs) exceeds 10% points. This forms a sharp contrast with the challenges of small object detection mentioned in early studies. As emphasized by Mustafa et al. (2019)35 in their work on FPN, multi-scale feature fusion was the key to solving problems of scale differences. On this basis, the bidirectional feature fusion mechanism and texture enhancement module introduced here further strengthen the model’s ability to extract detailed features; this is particularly important in the fine-grained task of artistic element detection.

Compared with the traditional design process that relies on designers’ experience and subjective judgment, this system provides a data-driven and quantifiable analysis framework. Traditional methods often require multiple team meetings and repeated revisions in the scheme evaluation stage. In contrast, the proposed system can offer real-time quantitative feedback in the early stage of design36. For example, in style consistency evaluation, traditional methods rely on experts’ intuition and experience accumulation; this system can objectively analyze the matching degree between design elements and target styles through the embedded style feature library and attention mechanism. This comparison highlights the unique value of AI technology in the design field. As pointed out in the studies by Tan & Luhrs (2024)37 and Wang et al. (2023)38, the design process is essentially a dialogue with the context.

While providing objective style metrics, the framework also prompts in-depth reflection on a core question: how to address the inherent subjectivity of style, and what is the appropriate role of AI in the creative process? First and foremost, style is not a rigid template, and great designs often stem from intentional deviation or fusion of styles. The principle adopted by the system to handle such cases is to describe rather than prescribe. Its core function is to quantitatively reveal the stylistic attributes of each element in a scheme and their statistical relationship with preset objectives. For example, it prompts that a classical pediment is detected with a similarity of X to the overall characteristics of the modern style, rather than arbitrarily judging “right” or “wrong”. This transforms subjective aesthetic choices into discussable objective data; meanwhile, it can assist designers in making more in-depth creative decisions on an informed basis.

This leads to the question of the role boundary of AI in creative work. The study holds that an ideal AI should be a combination of a rigorous compliance inspector and an inspiration stimulator. On the one hand, it can efficiently verify explicit rules and internal consistency, such as codes and specifications, structural logic, and proportional principles of the same stylistic system. On the other hand, combined with generative models, it can proactively generate multiple alternative schemes or modified components based on analysis results (e.g., element style conflict or lack of texture variation), serving as “creative sparks” to inspire inspiration. Therefore, the goal of this framework is to facilitate an enhanced creative dialogue. AI is responsible for processing data and revealing patterns and contradictions; human designers rely on their cultural understanding and aesthetic intuition to make final interpretation, evaluation, and creative synthesis.

The proposed framework is not only an experimental verification but also aims to bring changes to actual architectural design workflows. Its practical significance lies in that this framework can be embedded into mainstream BIM or CAD environments as an independent design intelligent analysis plug-in. The system can automatically associate the detected artistic elements and their style attributes with corresponding components in the BIM model, enriching their semantic information. At the same time, the generated design suggestions, such as style inconsistency warnings and element replacement recommendations, are analyzed. These suggestions can be visually superimposed on the three-dimensional model view to realize the seamless connection between analysis and design. This is expected to upgrade BIM from a tool that focuses on geometry and engineering information management to a comprehensive platform that carries both aesthetic and cultural heritage information.

Besides, moving this study from experimental environment to engineering application, there are still some challenges to be solved. First of all, it is the balance between computational efficiency and real-time performance. Although the model is excellent in accuracy, the inference speed needs to be further optimized to meet the needs of interactive design when dealing with high-resolution and full-size architectural renderings or point cloud data. Second is the continuous evolution and renewal of domain knowledge. Architectural style and aesthetic paradigm are not static. Therefore, the style feature library and the Design Rule Library in the system must have scalability and online learning ability in order to absorb new cases and designers’ feedback. Third is the design of the human-machine interaction interface. How to transform complex AI analysis results into an intuitive design language that designers can understand and operate is the key to the widespread adoption of the system.

It should be pointed out that the empirical verification of this study shows the effectiveness and potential of the human-machine collaborative framework in depth, but it is still an exploratory study in breadth and scale. Limited by research resources, this study has not conducted a large-scale longitudinal comparative study across multiple design institutions. Hence, the conclusions of “significantly improving design efficiency” and “generally improving style consistency” still need to be further verified in a wider range of practical design projects and a larger group of designers. In the future, the study will focus on conducting more strictly controlled field experiments with a larger sample size, so as to more stably quantify the average effect of the framework in different cultural backgrounds, project types, and design teams.

Conclusion

Research contribution

This study goes beyond the improvement of simple algorithm performance; it proposes and verifies a new human-machine collaborative design paradigm in the field of urban public architectural art design. Its core innovation and advantages over existing methods are mainly reflected in the fact that the constructed framework breaks through the conventional positioning of AI as a passive analysis tool; it is also promoted as an active collaboration partner in the design cycle. This study realizes a paradigm shift from computer-aided drawing to AI-augmented creativity by transforming subjective artistic perception into computable and interactive objective indicators; this provides a new idea for the expansion of computational design theory in the artistic dimension. Compared with general computer vision models applied in the architectural field, the proposed domain-adaptive model achieves a significant improvement in detection accuracy. More importantly, it enhances the ability to capture stylistic semantics-related features, making it more in line with the needs of artistic evaluation. Compared with traditional collaborative design platforms, the system deeply integrates real-time visual feedback and machine learning-based scheme generation mechanisms, thus significantly improving objective design quality indicators such as style consistency while shortening the design cycle. In short, this study provides a complete methodology from model improvement, system construction to workflow integration. It effectively bridges the gap between quantitative AI analysis and qualitative artistic creation; this enables the design team to make decisions with the support of data. At the same time, it guarantees and enhances the originality of creation. This framework lays a foundation for the future development of a new generation of human-machine collaborative design systems that can respond to complex aesthetic needs and meet functional specifications efficiently.

Future works and research limitations

Although this study has achieved preliminary results, it still has the following limitations.

First, insufficient dataset diversity. The PB-Art12 dataset used in this study is derived from ADE20K, and its images are mainly photographs taken from conventional perspectives under good lighting conditions. Fine annotations for 12 categories of artistic elements are constructed through professional labelling. Still, the data lacks sufficient coverage in terms of cultural and geographical distribution, historical style span, and extreme imaging conditions such as night scenes, rainy and foggy weather, and aerial perspectives; these may affect the generalization ability of the model in complex field scenarios.

Second, the representational limitation of the detection paradigm for non-geometric artistic elements. The current bounding-box-based detection paradigm is essentially suitable for decorative components with clear geometric forms. However, the value of architectural art is also reflected in elements difficult to quantify with bounding boxes; they include material textures such as stone roughness and glass light transmittance, dynamic light and shadow effects, color matching relationships, and spatial proportional rhythm. These elements are important components of architectural aesthetics, but the framework cannot effectively analyze and quantify them; this limits the system’s depth of understanding of the integrity of architectural art.

Third, the limitation in the quantitative depth and cultural dimension of stylistic semantics. This study introduces the statistical representation of architectural styles through style-aware attention and style consistency loss; this can effectively distinguish textures and color distributions of different schools. However, such feature-statistics-based methods still stay in the quantification of superficial aesthetic features; it is difficult to effectively model high-level semantics (e.g., deeper cultural metaphors, historical contexts, symbolic meanings, and regional aesthetic differences). This may lead to deviations between system suggestions and designers’ professional judgments when facing design tasks with complex cultural backgrounds.

Fourth, the sample limitation in the verification of the human-machine collaborative mechanism. The empirical analysis of the collaborative mechanism is based on limited samples. Although the feasibility of the system is preliminarily verified, the sample size is small; this makes it difficult to comprehensively evaluate the differences in collaboration effects between designers with various experience levels and design style preferences and the system.

In response to the above limitations, future research can construct a larger-scale and more culturally diverse architectural art dataset; it can proactively include more images under non-ideal imaging conditions (e.g., different time periods, weather, and perspectives) to improve the model’s universality and robustness. Concurrently, going beyond instance detection, it examines multi-task learning frameworks combining semantic segmentation, material classification, and style transfer to quantitatively analyze material texture, color matching, and overall stylistic context; this can achieve a more comprehensive computational understanding of architectural art. The future will also study how to transform the above-mentioned non-geometric artistic semantics, such as “creating a sense of heaviness for stone materials” and “enhancing the dramatic effect of light and shadow”, into interactive design parameters or generation constraints; meanwhile, it can develop a new generation of human-machine collaborative design systems that can understand and respond to high-level aesthetic instructions. In terms of real-time performance, the model inference and system interaction pipeline will be optimized to achieve low-latency real-time analysis and feedback; thus, a technical foundation can be provided for smooth and natural collaborative dialogue between humans and machines, enhancing the immersion and efficiency of the design experience. At the same time, it will explore the combination of this study’s analytical ability with diffusion models, GAN, and other generative AI. The goal is to build a system that can actively generate artistic element schemes and even overall composition sketches that conform to specific styles, codes, or contexts based on analysis results.

Effect of LY2109761 on TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad Pathway and Hepatic Fibrosis Cysts Formation in Rats Infected with Echinococcus granulosus: an In Vitro and In Vivo Study
Authors: Shaohua Zhai, Yueqi Yang, Meihe Hu, Yang Zhou, Yanshuang Yang, Nijun Zou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeEchinococcus granulosus infection is a zoonotic parasite disease that threatens animal husbandry development and farmer and herder health. Hepatic fibrosis cysts induced by protoscoleces (PSCs) are a common pathological alteration. The formation of these cysts is related to the stimulation of TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad signaling in hepatic stellate cells (HSCs). TGF-β1 activates the TGFβRI/Smad pathway in HSCs, which is needed in the formation of fibrosis cysts.MethodsLY2109761, a TGF-β receptor inhibitor, is hypothesized to reduce the formation of hepatic fibrosis cysts by limiting TGF-β1 binding to TGFβRI receptors on HSCs and decreasing the activation of the TGFβRI/Smad pathway. Herein, a liver infection model in rats with PSCs was constructed, and the infected rats were treated with LY2109761 in vivo. The pathological morphology, serum IL-1β, IL-2, IL-4, IL5, IL-6, IL-10, IL-13, IL-17 concentrations, TGF-β1 cytokine, and fibrosis TGF-β1/Smad pathway protein expression were detected to verify the mechanism how PSCs infection activates the HSC TGF-β1/ Smad pathway to promote liver fibrosis.ResultsThe study examined how LY2109761 affected TGF-β1 secretion in hepatocytes co-cultured with PSCs and BRL cells in vitro, and the activation of the TGF-β1/Smad in HSC-T6 cells induced by the co-culture supernatant, which ultimately led to liver fibrosis. Prolonged infection duration raised blood concentrations of IL-1β, IL-2, IL-4, IL5,IL-6, IL-10, and IL-17 as well as hepatocyte TGF-β1 production and secretion.ConclusionTGF-β1 acts on HSC surface, where it binds to TGF-βRI to stimulate the production of hepatic fibrosis cysts via TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad. Blocking TGF-β1 linking to the TGFβRI receptor can lessen PSCs damage to liver cells, prevent fibrotic cyst formation, and activate the immune system in the body to kill invaders.Keywords: Echinococcus granulosus, Cyst fibrosis, TGF-β1, TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad signaling pathway

Introduction

Echinococcosis granulosus, or cystic echinococcosis, is a zoonotic parasitic disease caused by Echinococcus granulosus that infects humans and various mammals [1, 2]. The disease is distributed worldwide, and has been listed by the World Health Organization as one neglected tropical disease and one of the most serious helminth diseases [3, 4]. Xinjiang is a central province for developing animal husbandry in China and is one of the areas seriously threatened by echinococcosis. Cattle and sheep are economically important animals that are seriously damaged by E. granulosus. The incidence rate of human echinococcosis is 4.4% (23/521) there, including 4.0% (21/521) of the cystic subtype. The infection rate is 4.52% (131/2898) in sheep and 4.84% (34/703) in cattle. It has become a basic socioeconomic and public health issue [5–7].

Livers are the most important organ invaded by cystic echinococcosis [8, 9]. Humans or animals are infected by ingesting the eggs of E. granulosus through the digestive tract, which develop into oncospheres in the duodenum. The oncospheres colonize livers through the intestinal wall capillaries and develop into larvae (Protoscoleces, PSCs) through systemic circulation. The PSCs develop into cysts in liver tissues, and further form space-occupying lesions to oppress surrounding parenchymal tissues, causing tissue inflammatory cell infiltration, fibroblast proliferation, and Collagen expression and finally forming fibrous cysts that result in weakened liver functions and life-threatening conditions [10–12]. The generation of the fibrotic cysts in PSCs establishes a histological obstacle between the injured and normal liver tissues, which prevents PSCs in the cysts from damaging the surrounding liver tissues [13, 14]. Moreover, the appearance of fibrotic cysts weakens the immune recognition and killing effects on PSCs in the body, achieves immune evasion, and creates a living environment for its long-term parasitism [15, 16]. PSCs parasitize the cysts for long time. PSCs affect peripheral liver cells via asexual reproduction and metabolite production, and thus result in an increasing range of liver cell damage, a gradual expansion of the cyst volume, and more serious harm to the body [17].

The TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad pathway is pivotal in the formation of cyst fibrosis [18]. TGF-β1 has various bioeffects on the body, and thus can control cell proliferation and differentiation and participate in various immune and pathophysiological processes. It can also activate HSCs and promote their extracellular matrix secretion [19], an effect that is significant in the mechanism of organ fibrosis [20]. In addition to the participation in liver injury fibrosis due to various causes, TGF-β1 can also cause fibrosis to multiple organs and tissues throughout the body, such as hepatic, renal, and intestinal fibrosis [21–23]. Continuous in-depth research reveals that TGF-β1 expression rises after cystic hepatic echinococcosis, showing a significant pro-fibrotic effect [24]. TGF-β1 is crucial in liver fibrosis through different signal transduction pathways. TGF-β1 binds to its membrane receptors (TGFβR1/2), activating downstream Smad transcription factors and are divided into several subtypes [25, 26]. TGF-β1 plays different roles in developing liver fibrosis after signal transduction through different subtypes of Smad proteins [27]. Smad2/3 can aggravate liver fibrosis. The TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad pathway contributes to the expression/ production of collagen COL-I and COL-III, while Smad 4 can promote the activation of Smad2/3 and further promote collagen formation [28].

To explore the pathogenic mechanism by which the TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad pathway activation in HSCs contributes to fibrotic cyst formation around PSCs, we established a rat model of PSCs infection in vivo, and used an LY2109761 inhibitor to interfere with the HSC TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad pathway. We also detected pathological morphology, inflammatory cytokines, fibrotic TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad pathway proteins and other indicators. A co-culture system of PSCs and BRL cells in vitro was established to investigate their direct interactive effect on HSCs and to further validate the role of this pathway in promoting the expressions of fibrosis- related proteins induced by PSCs. This study provides an important research basis for clarifying the molecular mechanism of hepatic fibrosis cyst formation caused by PSCs, the effect of fibrosis cyst formation on the natural immune function in the body, the screening of anti-hydatid drug targets, and new production.

Materials and Methods

Ethical Norms Statement

All animals were kept in clean cages at stable temperature (23 ± 2 °C) and humidity under (50 ± 5%) a 12-h light/dark cycle and with free access to rat food and purified water. Approval was obtained from the Animal Experimental Ethics Committee of Xinjiang Agricultural University (License No: 2022036).

Isolation and In Vitro Culture of PSCs

Five sheep livers containing E. granulosus cysts were obtained from Hualing Slaughterhouse in Urumqi, Xinjiang. The cyst fluids were extracted using sterile syringes into antiseptic 50 mL centrifuge tubes. The PSCs depositing at the tube bottom were collected via natural precipitation, and the supernatant was discarded. The PSCs were rinsed repeatedly with phosphate-buffered saline containing double antibiotics (Penicillin 100 U/mL + Streptomycin 100 μg/mL) to eliminate potential bacterial contamination during isolation.The washed PSCs were cultured in a 37 °C incubator incubator RPMI-1640 medium added with 10% FBS [29, 30].

Establishment of Rat PSCs Infection Model

SD rats, half males and half females (100 ± 10 g), were bought from the Experimental Animal Center of Xinjiang Medical University. After a week of adaptive feeding, each rat was anaesthetized (n = 6), the abdominal cavity was opened, and the livers were exposed via surgical operation. A suspension with 0.5 mL of PSCs (Trypan blue staining, viability > 90%, 2 × 104 PSCs/mL) was drawn up with a 1 mL syringe, and the right liver lobe was obliquely pierced at 45°. The suspension was injected to a depth of 1 cm, and dry cotton swabs were used to compress the injection site to stop bleeding. At the same time, 0.5 mL of oil procaine penicillin was injected into the abdominal cavity to prevent infection. The abdominal wall and skin were sutured in layers and allowed to exist in their natural states to restore activity. In this study, LY2109761 was used for in vivo intervention in rats infected with protoscoleces (PSCs) to explore its effects on hepatic PSCs infection. Starting on day 7 post-infection, rats were intragastrically administered with the TGF-β RI/RII inhibitor LY2109761 at a dose of 100 mg/kg for 21 consecutive days. The dosage was referenced from relevant literature [31], and the drug was suspended in 0.5% sodium carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC-Na) solution prior to use.

Hematoxylin and Eosin (HE) Staining

The sample blocks containing liver cysts and surrounding liver tissues (1.5 × 1 × 0.5 cm3 range) were cut from the model six rats (n = 6), fixed with a 10% neutral formalin solution, and sent to gradient dehydration with ethanol, and tissue transparency with xylene. The specimens were embedded, sliced, and stained with HE. Digital images were recorded using a scanner (Motic, China).

Masson Cytochemical Staining

The aforementioned paraffin-embedded liver sections from each group (n = 6) were dewaxed in xylene and rehydrated through a graded ethanol series. The morphology of liver cyst fibrous tissues was observed according to the operation of a Masson staining kit (Solaibao, G1340, China).

Quantitative Detection of Cytokines in Liver Tissues

Pericystic liver tissues (50 mg) collected from each rat (n = 6) were homogenized in nine volumes of sterile normal saline. Then the homogenate was centrifuged to precipitate cell debris, and the supernatant was collected as the sample. Then a rat TGF-β1 ELISA kit (Multi Sciences Ek981-48, Hangzhou, China) was operated in accordance with the procedures.

ELISA of Serum Cytokine Levels

Peripheral blood of model rats was collected (n = 6). The serum concentrations of IL-1β (EK301B/2-48, Multi Science, Shanghai, China), IL-2 (EK302/2-48, Multi Science), IL-4 (EK304/2-48, Multi Science), IL-5 (CSB-E07435r, Huamei, Wuhan, China), IL-6 (EK306/2-48, Multi Science), IL-10 (EK310/2-48, Multi Science), IL-13 (CSB-E07454r, Huamei), and IL-17 (EK317/3-48, Multi Science) (all from Liankesheng unless otherwise noted) in the model animals were determined by ELISA.

Co-culture of PSCs with BRL Cells

BRL cells (Buffalo rat liver cells, RRID: CVCL_4565) and HSC-T6 cells (immortalized rat hepatic stellate cells, RRID: CVCL_0315) were purchased from Wuhan Procell Life Science & Technology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). Pre-cultured BRL cells were digested with trypsin and re-suspended at 1 × 106 cells/mL. Cells suspensions were then seeded and grouped as follows: (1) BRL alone (blank control, n = 6), (2) BRL co-cultured with PSCs (1000 PSCs/mL, n = 6) [32], and (3) BRL co-cultured with PSCs (1000 PSCs/mL) in the presence of LY2109761 (5 μM, n = 6). All groups were cultivated in DMEM added with 10% FBS in 25 cm2 flasks. After 72 h, morphological changes were observed, and both cells and supernatant were collected for subsequent analysis.

Processing of HSC-T6 Cells in Conditioned Media

Pre-cultured HSC-T6 cells were digested with trypsin and seeded at 1 × 106 cells/mL. After attachment, the cells were treated with the conditioned media collected from the BRL co-culture groups described in Sect. 2.8 (supernatant was mixed with a fresh medium at 1:9, n = 6). Morphological changes were observed at 24, 48 and 72 h after treatment. Then the cells were trypsinized, cleaned with PBS, and collected for further analysis.

Indirect Immunofluorescence Test

The trypsinized cells were diluted to 1 × 106 cells/mL, put onto 96-well plates (100 uL/well, n = 3), fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA, 15 min), permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 (10 min), and blocked with 5% skimmed milk powder (1 h). The cells were incubated with a primary antibody working solution overnight at 4 °C, and then a secondary antibody working solution 37 °C for 1 h. Then a 90% glycerol solution was added, and the cell fluorescence was detected by laser confocal microscopy (LCM).

Cell Double-Labeled Immunofluorescence Assay

BRL and HSC HST-6 cells were fixed with 4% PFA for 15 min (n = 3), 0.1% Triton-X100 for 10 min, and 5% skimmed milk powder for 1 h. The TGF-β1 and TGFβRI antibodies were blended at a volume ratio of 1:1 and added to the culture dish at 37 °C for 1 h. The mixtures were incubated with TRITC-labeled goat anti-rabbit IgG, and with FITC-labeled goat anti-mouse IgG, each at 37 °C for 1 h. A DAPI staining solution was added dropwise and cultured for 10 min. After slight drying, an autofluorescence quencher was added for 5 min, and the fluorescence intensity was observed under LCM.

Western Blotting

Liver tissues and cell proteins were extracted as protein samples (n = 3), which were detected with a BSA protein detection kit (Biosharp, China). The samples were diluted to the same concentration using RIPA lysis buffer, and 1/4 volume of an SDS-PAGE specimen using buffer was loaded and processed at 100 °C for 10 min. Protein samples (30–60 μg) were processed via SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes according to standard procedures. The membranes were blocked with skim milk dissolved in TBST (20 mM, Tris–HCl, pH 7.5, 150 mM NaCl, 0.1% Tween 20) for 2 h. The membranes were cultured with the primary antibody overnight at 4 °C, and with the secondary antibody at room temperature for 1.5 h, The band was observed and photographed via enhanced chemiluminescence (BioRad, USA).

Antibodies

Mouse antibodies included anti-alpha smooth muscle actin antibody (Boaosen, 1:1000, Beijing, China); anti-collagen I antibody (Abcam, 1:500, Shanghai, China); recombinant anti-Smad 2/3 antibody (Abcam, 1:500, ab202445); phospho-Smad 2/3 (Boaosen, 1:400, bs-8853R); Smad 4 polyclonal antibody (Wuhan Sanying, 1:500, 10,231–1-AP, Wuhan, China); anti-TGFβRI antibody (Boster, 1:500, PB1154, Wuhan, China); β-actin (Sino Biological, 1:1000, 100,166-MM10, Beijing, China).The secondary antibodies included goat anti-mouse IgG H&L (HRP) (Abcam, ab205719, 1:5000) and goat anti-rabbit IgG H&L (HRP) (Abcam, ab6721, 1:5000), Goat anti-rabbit IgG H&L (Alexa Fluor 488, Abcam, ab150077, 1:500), Goat anti-rabbit IgG H&L (Alexa Fluor 488, Abcam, ab150077, 1:500) and goat anti-mouse IgG H&L (Alexa Fluor 594, Abcam, ab150120, 1:500).

Statistical Analysis

The significance of differences was examined one-way analysis of variance (one-way ANOVA), and p values were displayed in the GP format (**** p < 0.0001, *** p < 0.001, ** p < 0.01, * p < 0.05, ns ≥ 0.05). All graphical data were reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analysis and mapping were finished on GraphPad Prism 9.5.0.

Results

Quantitative Analysis of TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad Protein Expression in Hepatocytes Around Hepatic Cysts of Rats at Various Stages of PSCs Infection

To detect the role of TGF-β1 cytokines in promoting fibrotic cyst formation by activating TGFβRI/Smad proteins in HSCs during hepatic cyst development infected by PSCs, we established a rat model of PSCs infection and observed the pathological morphology of liver cysts at 1, 3 and 6 months post-infection (mpi). ELISA and WB were used to quantitatively analyze TGF-β1 factors and TGFβRI/Smad pathway proteins in liver tissues at different stages of infection. Results showed that normal rat liver tissues were in regular morphology with flat surface (Fig. 1A-a). The liver surface of rats at 1 mpi showed grey-white vesicles (Fig. 1A-b). In rats at 3 mpi, the volume of vesicles on the liver increased, and the vesicle wall was tense (Fig. 1A-c). In rats at 6 mpi, multiple cysts appeared in the liver and the range of liver parenchymal damage increased (Fig. 1A-d).

Histopathological observation showed that normal rats had a regular morphology of hepatocytes and a clear structure of hepatic sinus space (Fig. 1B-a, b). In rats at 1 mpi, a large number of PSCs gathered in the liver parenchyma, and the liver cells were damaged extensively and lost the structure (Fig. 1B-c, d). At 3 mpi, cyst cavity was expanded, and apparent inflammatory cell bands appeared at the cyst-normal liver interface, along with collagen fiber deposition and granulation tissue formation in the cyst wall (Fig. 1B-e, f). In rats at 6 mpi, the liver cyst volume was significantly enlarged, the cyst wall was thinned, and the inflammatory cells around the cyst wall were significantly reduced (Fig. 1B-g, h).

Masson staining showed that the vascular smooth muscles of portal vein and central vein in normal rat liver tissues were stained blue (Fig. 1C-a, b). At 1 mpi, PSCs infection caused extensive hepatocyte damage and stimulated collagen deposition (blue staining) in the surrounding liver tissues, which began to encapsulate the PSCs (Fig. 1C-c, d). At 3 mpi, the hepatocytes around the cyst became lighter in color, the nucleus disappeared, the fibrous tissues of the cyst wall proliferated significantly and extended to the peripheral damaged hepatocytes to form a more obvious lamellar structure (Fig. 1C-e, f). At 6 mpi, the liver cysts developed and matured, the deposition of collagen in the capsule wall increased, and the staining became darker. The collagen in the capsule wall extended to the peripheral liver tissues and gradually replaced the damaged hepatocytes (Fig. 1C-g,h).

The expressions of TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad proteins TGFβRI and p-Smad2/3 in liver tissues were significantly upregulatead, the Smad4 protein expression was downregulated, but the expressions of Smd2/3 protein were not significantly different. Furthermore, the expressions of COL1A1 and α-SMA increased positively with infection time and cyst development (Fig. 1D-a, b). The above results suggest the volume of the fibrotic cysts gradually enlarges during the parasitic process of PSCs in the liver. Moreover, TGF-β1 levels in peri-cystic areas were associated with activation of the TGFβRI/Smad pathway in liver tissues. The concomitant increase in the expressions of the fibrotic proteins COL1A1 and α-SMA, likely in HSCs, underscores the crucial role of this pathway in PSCs-induced cyst fibrosis.

Changes in Levels of Inflammatory Factors in Hepatocytes at Different Stages of PSCs Infection in Rats

To assess the extent of inflammatory response and the release level of inflammatory factors in hepatocytes by intrahepatic parasites of PSCs, we measured the levels of interleukins (ILs) in liver cells around the liver cysts of rats infected at different cercariae stages. Cercariae parasitic infection in the liver stimulated the body to release pro-inflammatory ILs (eg: IL-1β, IL-2, IL-4, IL5, IL-6, IL-10, IL-13 and IL-17A). As the internal liver infection by cercariae was prolonged, the levels of these ILs remarkably rose. Notably, the level of anti-inflammatory IL-10 also rose with the duration of PSCs infection. The pro-fibrotic IL-1β, IL-4, IL-5, IL-6, IL-13, and IL-17A produced abundant extracellular matrix (collagen fibers) in the liver during the stimulation and differentiation of stellate cells, facilitating the formation of liver fibrosis, but the level of IL-13 did not change significantly at each stage of PSCs infection. In the intrahepatic parasitism of PSCs, it can stimulate natural immunity, activate blood inflammatory cells, and release ILs to mediate the pathological response in the body. It was critical in mediating the chemotaxis of inflammatory cells to liver cells around the cysts, and in the progressive damage and fibrosis of liver cells (Fig. 2).

Impact of LY2109761 Intervention on Formation of Hepatic Fibrosis Cysts Caused by PSCs In Vivo

The formation of fibrotic cysts is a major consequence of liver injury induced by protoscoleces (PSCs). To investigate the therapeutic potential of targeting the TGF-β pathway, PSCs-infected rats were treated orally with the TGF-β receptor inhibitor LY2109761 for 21 days, starting at 7 days post-infection (dpi) (Fig. 3A). Gross morphological examination showed that the LY2109761 treatment significantly reduced the size of liver surface cysts compared to the untreated 1-mpi group. The cysts in the treated rats were millet-sized and grey-white spots (Fig. 3B). Histopathological observation showed that after LY2109761 intervention in vivo, lesions appeared at the liver edge, the inflammatory cells were reduced, and no PSCs were observed (Fig. 3C). Masson's trichrome staining demonstrated that collagen deposition in the lesions of treated rats was looser, with lighter staining (indicating less collagen) and a clear boundary with the surrounding liver tissues(Fig. 3D). In consistency with the reduced inflammatory, LY2109761 treatment significantly lowered TGF-β1 levels (Fig. 3E). Western blot further confirmed that LY2109761 treatment downregulated the expressions of TGFβRI and p-Smad2/3, and significantly reduced the levels of COL1A1, α-SMA in liver tissues (Fig. 3F-a,b). These results indicate that blocking the binding of TGF-β1 to TGFβRI on the surface of hepatic stellate cells (HSCs) inhibits HSC activation and the expression of fibrosis-related proteins. This mechanism is closely associated with fibrotic cyst lesions induced by protoscoleces (PSCs).

LY2109761 Inhibited Production of Inflammatory Factors in Hepatocytes After PSCs Infection In Vivo

To assess the impact of LY2109761 on the formation of hepatic fibrotic cysts, the degree of inflammatory response and the release of inflammatory factors in hepatocytes in vivo were examined. The infected rats at 7 dpi were orally administered with LY2109761 for 21 d. The peripheral blood of the rats was collected, and the serum was separated to determine the ILs of the infected animals. Compared with the model group, LY2109761 significantly lowered the production of pro-inflammatory ILs in the body stimulated by PSCs, and the serum concentrations of IL-1β, IL-2, IL-4, IL-5, IL6, IL-10, IL-13 and IL-17A significantly decreased. The serum concentration of inflammatory IL-10 also significantly dropped. The secretion factors of profibrotic IL-1β, IL-4, IL-5, IL6, IL-13 and IL-17A remarkably declined (Fig. 4A–H). The results indicate that LY2109761 could inhibit the formation of liver fibrosis cysts in PSCs-infected rats in vivo. In the process, the degree of liver injury, and the inflammatory response were significantly reduced. The natural immune mechanism is involved in antigen cercariae infection in the body. Hence, inhibiting liver fibrosis formation can reduce the progressive damage of liver cells and the inflammatory response.

Co-culture of PSCs and BRL Cells Promotes Collagen Expression in HSC-T6 Cells

To probe into the mechanism in vitro, we examined whether PSCs induced TGF-β1 secretion from hepatocytes and thereby activated the TGF-βI/TGFβRI/Smad pathway in HSCs. When BRL cells were co-cultured with PSCs, they underwent morphological changes, including cell atrophy, detachment, and a decreased cell proliferation rate over time (Fig. 5A). After the LY2109761 intervention, BRL cells were evenly distributed, exfoliated cells were significantly reduced, and the cell proliferation rate was as expected (Fig. 5B). Quantitative detection of TGF-β1 in BRL cells showed that LY2109761 could reduce the secretion of TGF-β1 in hepatocytes mediated by PSCs (Fig. 5C). HSC-T6 cells were processed with the co-culture supernatant of PSCs and BRL cells, which promoted the transformation of some HSC-T6 cells into long spindle fibroblasts. After the co-culture supernatant of LY2109761-treated PSCs and BRL cells acted on HSC-T6 cells, the cell morphology was regular, and the growth was uniform. Indirect immunofluorescence assay showed that the fluorescence signal of COL1A1 protein in HSC-T6 cells treated with the co-culture of PSCs and BRL was significantly more potent than in the supernatant of co-cultured cells treated with LY2109761 (Fig. 5D-a, b). Western blotting revealed the expressions of TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad proteins TGFβRI, p-Smad2/3 and fibrosis proteins COL1A1 and α-SMA significantly increased in HSC-T6 co-cultured with PSCs. The expressions of TGFβRI, p-Smad2/3, Smad2/3, COL1A1 and α-SMA in the LY2109761 intervention group decreased, indicating TGF-β1 is crucial in the formation of hepatic injury and fibrosis and activates HSC cell fibrosis through the TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad pathway in HSCs (Fig. 5E-a, b).

Discussion

We confirm the liver parasitism of PSCs increases the secretion of TGF-β1 in hepatocytes, blocks the specific binding of TGF-β1 to TGFβRI on the surface of HSCs, and hinders the formation of liver fibrosis cysts. It also reduces the stimulation of the TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad pathway, the expressions of liver fibrosis proteins, and the production of liver proinflammatory ILs, reducing the damage of PSCs to hepatocytes. Our findings highlight the central role of the TGF-β pathway in PSCs-induced liver pathology and identify its inhibition as a potential therapeutic strategy against E. granulosus-induced liver injury.

Cystic echinococcosis due to E. granulosus at the larval stage is a significant zoonotic parasitic disease. The main organ of its parasitism and invasion is the liver [33, 34]. Typical pathological features of this disease are hepatocyte injury and fibrocystic formation [35–37]. The growth of PSCs in the liver seriously impacts liver metabolism, differentiation cycle, and normal functions of cells in the parasitic site [38]. Hepatocyte injury releases cytokines such as TGF-β1 and VEGF to activate collagen expression in HSCs [39, 40], forming fibrous cysts based on granulation tissues and forming liver space-occupying lesions, which have profound effects on liver functions. Therefore, it is significant for anti-E. granulosus infection to clarify the cause of liver cell damage by PSCs, reduce or block the formation of hepatic fibrosis cysts, and break the parasitic environment of PSCs so as to make the body play an immune role.

We observed the pathological progression in rat livers at different stages of PSCs infection. In the early stage, hepatocytes are lost extensively in the parasitic area. The PSCs develop into cysts, which are encapsulated by fibrous tissues. With the prolongation of infection time, the structure of the germinal layer in the cyst wall is apparent, granulation tissues with abundant blood vessels in the outer layer of the cyst (stratum corneum) are formed, the nucleus of the liver around the cyst disappears, and the fibrous tissues invade and grow around the diseased liver cells [41]. The production of fibrosis is dynamically associated with liver cell injury [42]. As infection goes on, hepatocyte death is more serious, and the expression of TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad protein in liver fibrosis also significantly increases.

To verify the role of the TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad pathway in the formation of PSCs cysts, we used the LY2109761 inhibitor to act on PSCs in early infection in vivo to block the TGF-β1/TGFβR/Smad pathway in rat livers. Pathological morphology observations showed that the formation of hepatic fibrosis cysts was significantly reduced, and the inflammatory indicators were also significantly affected. Accordingly, the expression of TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad protein in liver fibrosis was downregulated. It is indicated the activated HSC TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad pathway is crucial for hepatic fibrosis caused by PSCs. However, LY2109761 may also directly affect the growth and viability of PSCs [17]. The TGFβ signaling pathway is conserved and critical in Echinococcus, regulating protoscolex formation, metacestode development, and parasite survival [43]. As a dual TGFβR1/2 inhibitor, LY2109761 may interfere with the parasite’s endogenous TGFβ-like pathway, impairing PSCs viability, proliferation, or cyst differentiation [31]. In addition, inhibition of parasite TGFβ signaling may weaken its stress responses and metabolism, promoting PSCs death [16]. The in vivo results may reflect both host pathway inhibition and direct anti-parasitic effects, and further in vitro experiments are needed to verify its direct action [19].

Hepatocyte injury caused by PSCs is the pathological basis for the formation of fibrotic cysts [44, 45]. First, hepatocyte injury can stimulate the formation of liver granulation tissues [46], and establish a histological obstacle between injured and normal liver tissues to prevent the PSCs in the cyst from damaging the surrounding liver tissues. Second, the formation of fibrotic cysts weakens the immune recognition and killing effect on PSCs, realizes the immune escape of PSCs, and creates a living environment for its long-term parasitism. We detected inflammatory IL-1β, IL-2, IL-4, IL-5, IL-6, IL-10, IL-13, IL-17 in peripheral blood of rats at different stages of PSCs infection. The concentrations of inflammatory factors in hepatocytes increased with the prolongation of PSCs infection. The concentrations of these factors in peripheral blood of model rats treated with LY2109761 in vivo decreased significantly. This result indicates the formation of parasitic cysts in the liver can break the histological barrier of PSCs parasitism, promote the immune mechanism of the body to play an immune role, kill the PSCs invading the body, and reduce the damage of PSCs to liver cells.

TGFβRI includes various cell surface receptors in the body [47, 48]. The specific binding of TGF-β1 to TGFβRI widely affects life processes, including cell proliferation, division, and production of products in the extracellular matrix [49–51]. However, whether the increased expression of TGF-β1 in hepatocytes caused by the intrahepatic parasitism of PSCs directly affects the TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad pathway in HSCs remains unclear. Here, PSCs were co-cultured with BRL cells in vitro, and BRL cells treated with TGF-β1 recombinant protein were used as a control. LY2109761 was used to interfere with the co-culture system of PSCs and BRL. The LY2109761 inhibitor could alleviate hepatocyte injury [52, 53]. PSCs, TGF-β1 protein, LY2109761 and BRL cells were co-cultured with HSC-T6 cells separately. TGF-β1 produced by PSCs acting on hepatocytes could co-localize with TGFβRI on the surface of HSC-T6 cells. LY2109761 can significantly reduce the co-localization of TGF-β1 and TGFβRI fluorescence on the surface of HSC-T6 cells, and the collagen expression in HSC-T6 cells was obviously down-regulated. The experimental results fully demonstrate that TGF-β1 may play an essential role in PSCs infection and activate HSC fibrosis protein production through the TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad pathway of HSCs.

Conclusions

With the prolongation of infection time, the serum concentrations of IL-1β, IL-2, IL-4, IL5, IL-6, IL-10 and IL-17 rose, and the expression and secretion of TGF-β1 in hepatocytes increased. TGF-β1 acts on TGFβRI on the surface of HSCs to promote the formation of liver fibrosis cysts through the TGF-β1/TGFβRI/Smad pathway. Blocking the binding of TGF-β1 to its receptor TGF-βRI can effectively alleviate PSCs-induced hepatocellular damage, inhibit the progression of hepatic fibrotic cyst formation, and suppress the activation of the HSC fibrotic signaling pathway to ameliorate liver fibrotic lesions.

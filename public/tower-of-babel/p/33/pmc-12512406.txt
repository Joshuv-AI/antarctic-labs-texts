Transcriptomic analysis reveals regulatory mechanisms of LncRNAs and mRNAs in hulless barley in response to cold stress
Authors: Mingzhai Yu, Deqing Zhuoga, Dabin Zhuang, Zhaxi Luobu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, RNA-seq analysis of 36 samples from two varieties at six temperature gradients detected a total of 101,193 expressed transcripts, including 25,809 lncRNAs and 75,384 mRNAs. Using 24 °C, 12 °C, and 5 °C as the cold group and 0 °C, -5 °C, and − 8 °C as the freezing group, 2719 CT_24°C_vs_5°C differentially expressed lncRNAs were detected in the cold group, of which 581 were up-regulated and 2138 were down-regulated, and 2,563 differentially expressed lncRNAs were detected in the freezing group, of which 601 were up-regulated and 1962 were down-regulated. In the group CT_0°C_vs_-8 °C, differentially expressed lncRNAs were 1,415, of which 250 were up-regulated and 1,165 were down-regulated, and in CS_0°C_vs_-8 °C, differentially expressed lncRNAs were 1,301, of which 228 were up-regulated and 1,073 were down-regulated.In the cold-damaged group, the total number of predicted paired lncRNA-mRNA pairs was 1904, of which 1152 pairs were paired by cis, and 752 pairs were paired by tran. In the freezing group, 922 pairs of lncRNA-mRNA pairs were predicted, of which 473 pairs were in the cis mode and 449 pairs were in the tran mode. In the cold group, 656 differential lncRNA target genes intersected with differentially expressed mRNAs in the CT_24℃_vs_5℃ comparison, and 668 in the CS_24℃_vs_5℃ comparison. In the freezing group, 187 differentially expressed mRNAs intersected with differentially expressed lncRNA target genes in the CT_0°C_vs_-8 °C comparison, and 146 in the CS_0°C_vs_-8 °C comparison. GO enrichment analysis indicated significant enrichment in the metabolic process and response to stimulus pathways in both the cold and freezing groups. KEGG enrichment analysis revealed significant enrichment in the Nucleotide excision repair (ko03420) and DNA replication (ko03030) pathways in the cold-damaged group, and in the Ascorbate and aldarate metabolism (ko00053) pathway in the freeze-damaged group. This suggests that the Ascorbate metabolic pathway plays a significant role in the barley response to frost damage.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-025-07403-y.Keywords: Hulless barley, Cold stress, LncRNA, Transcriptome sequencing

Background

Barley is one of the earliest crops domesticated by human [1], accounting for 12% of global cereal production It is the fourth largest food crop after maize, rice and wheat [2]. hulless barley (Hordeum vulgare L. var. nudum Hook. f.), locally termed “Qingke,” serves as a staple food and fodder source on the Tibetan Plateau [3]. Its hulless nature enhances nutrient density and cultivation efficiency [4], while the extreme plateau environment (high altitude, hypoxia, and intense UV radiation) promotes accumulation of β-glucans and phenolic compounds [5, 6]. Rich phenolic compounds reduce oxidative stress associated with metabolic diseases and promote health by modulating degenerative diseases such as cardiovascular disease, inflammation and cancer [7].

Low temperature is an important abiotic stress that causes crop yield reduction and even crop extinction [8, 9]. In recent years, with the drastic changes in global climate, extreme low temperatures have occurred frequently [10], and such situations have had a significant impact on Tibetan hulless Barley production in Tibet, China, due to the large altitude differences and numerous microclimates, which have resulted in a high frequency of extreme weather events and greater temperature differences. With years of breeding focused on yield as well as quality, it is inevitable that there is a loss of genetic diversity for crop stress tolerance. Cold stress includes chilling (0–15 °C) and freezing (< 0 °C) [9], and chilling firstly hardens plant cell membranes [11] and secondly, destabilizes proteins or protein Secondly, it destabilizes proteins or protein complexes and reduces the activity of enzymes, such as ROS scavenging enzymes, which in turn affects photosynthesis and causes membrane damage [12]. Freezing causes more irreversible damage than chilling, and in severe cases, it can cause death [8]. In response to such damage, transcription of a large number of RNA molecules occurs in plants, including coding RNA (mRNA) and non-coding RNA (ncRNA) [13].

lncRNAs are present in all aspects of life, and there is a higher correlation between organismal complexity and the diversity and size of non-coding RNA expression pools compared to protein-coding genes [14]. In the human genome, there are more non-coding RNAs than protein-coding genes [15]. Non-coding RNAs mainly include short-stranded non-coding RNAs, which are microRNAs, and long-stranded non-coding RNAs, which are a class of transcripts greater than 200 nt in length that do not have significant protein-coding capacity or lack open reading frames (ORFs) coding for more than 100 amino acids [16]. “long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs)” play an important role in regulating plant responses to environmental stress by acting as essential regulators of gene expression [17]. In cereals (such as barley, wheat and oat), the functional research of lncRNA is still in its infancy, but its potential in regulating agronomic traits (such as yield, stress resistance and quality) has gradually emerged. LncRNA interacts with mRNA mainly through the following mechanisms: First, lncRNA can play a role by regulating the expression of its neighboring genes [18]. For example, lncRNAs may influence transcription of neighboring mRNAs through chromatin remodeling or transcription factor recruitment. Second, lncrnas interact with distant mRNAs, usually through the formation of competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) networks or direct mRNA binding to achieve regulation [19]. In addition, lncRNA can act as a “sponge” of miRNA and indirectly regulate mRNA expression through competitive binding of miRNA [20]. lncRNAs can act as scaffolds, decoys, or signals in cis or trans, leading to down- or up-regulation of target genes, as well as variable splicing alternative splicing, translation and post-transcriptional epigenetic regulation of genes, among various other processes [21]. In addition, the functional diversity of lncRNAs is reflected in the fact that they can directly bind and act in all three dimensions: DNA, RNA, and protein [22]. Compared to protein-coding mRNAs, lncRNAs have lower sequence conservation and lower expression levels; therefore, they were initially considered as transcriptional “noise” [23]. Currently, research on lncRNAs has focused on human and mouse diseases [24]. In plants, lncRNA studies have focused on roles in developmental regulation, reproductive development, photomorphogenesis, Pi homeostasis, and abiotic or biotic stress responses [25–27]. The Qinghai-Tibet Plateau is renowned for its significant elevation variations, diverse microclimates, and frequent extreme weather events. Hordeum vulgare var. coeleste, a crucial food crop on the plateau, often faces challenges from low-temperature stress during its growth and development, severely impacting its yield and quality. This study employs a joint analysis of low-temperature-responsive physiological indicators and transcriptomic data to identify lncRNA-mRNA pairs associated with the low-temperature stress response. The research aims to pinpoint key lncRNAs and genes involved in Hordeum vulgare var. coeleste’s adaptation to low-temperature stress, providing new insights into its cold adaptation mechanisms. These findings will facilitate the breeding of cold-tolerant, high-yield, and stable varieties, thereby ensuring food security on the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau and promoting sustainable agricultural development in alpine regions.

Materials and methods

Experimental materials and treatments

The experiment was conducted with spring hulless barley varieties Zangqing 17 (cold-tolerant) and Zangqing 311 (cold-sensitive) as test materials (both varieties from the Tibet Autonomous Region Academy of Agricultural and Animal Husbandry Sciences). Seeds with full grains and no insect pests were selected, disinfected with 10% NaClO for 10 min, rinsed with aseptic water two to three times, and placed in Petri dishes Lined with two layers of filter paper to germinate the seeds. About 30 seeds per pot were sown in pots (10 cm × 12 cm) equipped with seedling substrate and sand at a ratio of 1:1. When the seeds were white, they were sown in pots (10 cm × 12 cm) with a 1:1 ratio of seedling substrate to sand, with about 30 seeds per pot. Seedlings were placed under room temperature with sufficient light, managed routinely, and cultivated until the three-leaf stage, and then put into an artificial climate chamber for low temperature treatment (Light 14 h/dark 10 h). A gradient cooling method was adopted, with 24 h of treatment on each temperature gradient, and 15 pots of each treatment. The treatment temperatures were 24℃, 12℃, 5℃, 0℃, −5℃ and − 8℃. At the end of each temperature gradient, the leaves were taken and quickly frozen with liquid nitrogen and then put into a −80℃ ultra-low temperature refrigerator for storage. The experiment was set up with 3 replications.

Physiological and biochemical indicators

Malondialdehyde (MDA) was determined by the thiobarbituric acid method. The enzyme solution was extracted from 0.5 g of hulless barley leaves between treatments, added to the reaction solution, sealed in a boiling water bath for 15 min and then quickly cooled and centrifuged to obtain the supernatant, and colorimetrically measured at 600, 532, and 450 nm.

Soluble proteins were colorimetrically developed with Caumas Brilliant Blue. The above enzyme solution was added to the reaction solution and left for 2 min, colorimetry was done at 595 nm, while a blank control was done.

Anthrone method was chosen for the determination of soluble sugar. Barley leaves take 0.5 g grinding evenly after distilled water to 110mL, test tube boiling water bath boiling 20 min, filtered and cooled for the reaction solution. Take 0.5mL of reaction solution, add 1.5mL of water and 5mL of anthrone reagent, boil for 10 min and then colorimetry at 620 nm.

Identification of LncRNAs by high-throughput sequencing

Hulless Barley leaves of resistant and sensitive varieties were taken from six temperature gradients, and three samples were taken under each treatment for a total of 36 sample materials for lncRNA high-throughput sequencing. Total RNA was extracted from 36 samples and RNA libraries were constructed. The library construction and lncRNA sequencing were performed by Shenzhen Beijing Genomics institution Co. The sequencing data are available in the BioProject database (PRJNA1195400)of the NCBI system.

Analysis of differences between groups

Analysis of variance, which compares the expression of samples, excludes genes with insignificant changes in expression and retains genes with significant differences. Between-group difference analysis, which compares the gene expression of two groups of samples, can have more than one sample in a group, usually one is the control group, and one is the treatment group. We used the difference analysis software DEGseq for between-group difference analysis. Filter conditions for significantly different genes:

DEGseq screening for differentially expressed lncRNAs and mRNAs was performed using log2Fold Change > 1.00 and Adjusted Pvalue < 0.05 as criteria.

Differently expressed gene annotation and GO enrichment analysis.

To quantitatively assess whether a functional module is enriched in candidate genes, the hypergeometric model is used to calculate the enrichment of candidate genes for each functional module. Each functional module has a pvalue, and the smaller the pvalue, the more enriched the candidate genes are on this functional module. The pvalue was then FDR-corrected, and functions with a set FDR < = 0.01 were considered significantly enriched. GO and KEGG enrichment analysis was performed using the Goseq-R software package and GlueGO with a p-value < 0.05.

LncRNA target gene prediction

lncRNA itself does not have coding ability, its complementary pairing with mRNA plays a role. lncRNA function is mainly realized through cis or trans mode of action on the target gene, target gene analysis method: Calculate two correlation coefficients between the lncRNA and the mRNA, spearman and pearson, require spearman_cor > = 0.6 and pearson_cor > = 0.6. cis and trans analysis: lncRNA within 10k upstream of mRNA or 20k downstream of mRNA is judged as cis. beyond that, it is determined to be cis. The analysis of cis and trans: if the lncRNA is within 10k upstream of the mRNA or within 20k downstream of the mRNA, it will be determined as cis. If it is beyond this range, the binding energy between the lncRNA and the mRNA will be analyzed by RNAplex, and if the binding energy is <−30, then it will be determined as tran [28, 29].

qPCR verification of LncRNA and mRNA expression

The RNA of sample materials under different temperature gradient treatments (24 °C, 12 °C, 5 °C, 0 °C, −5 °C, −8 °C) was extracted using the total RNA extraction kit, and the cDNA was reverse transcribed using the PrimeScript RT kit, and the primers were designed through the NCBI (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/primer- blast/index.cgi) online website for primer design, and a fluorescent quantitative PCR instrument, Light Cycler96 Real-Time PCR System (Roche, Switzerland), was used to detect gene expression. The reaction was performed in a two-step method with the following setup program: pre-denaturation 95 °C, 30 s; deformation 95 °C, 15 s; annealing 60 °C, 30 s, 40 cycles. The fluorescence value change curve and melting curve were analyzed at the end of the reaction, and three biological replicates were set for each sample. The results of gene relative expression level calculation were plotted by excel software, and the significance was analyzed by Stats software.

Results

Changes in physiological indexes of hulless barley under low temperature stress

The two hulless Barley varieties suffered from low temperatures in the seedling stage have different morphological changes (Fig. 1A), with the continuous reduction of temperature, the two varieties are in different degrees of wilting, of which the cold-sensitive (CS) at 5 ℃ leaves began to droop, with the continuous reduction of temperature drooping more and more obvious, at temperatures down to −5 ℃ wilting, and cold-tolerance (CT) in the face of low temperature is significantly better than CS, CT leaves remained straight when CS leaves drooped at −8 ℃ low temperature before CT wilting occurred. cold tolerance (CT) was significantly better than CS in the face of low temperatures. The leaves of CT were still straight when the leaves of CS were drooping, and wilting occurred only in CT at a low temperature of −8℃.

Treatments and physiological parameters. six temperature gradients (A), Superoxide Dismutase (B), Peroxidase (C), Relative Conductivity Rate (D), soluble proteins (E)for the two varieties CT and CS

In addition, in order to verify the effect of low temperature stress on highland barley, we analyzed the physiological indicators. With the decrease in temperature, the Superoxide Dismutase (SOD) and Peroxidase (POD) of resistant and sensitive varieties increased sharply and then decreased after 0 °C. The decrease in SOD was more drastic than that of POD, and the content of resistant varieties was higher at all gradients than that of sensitive varieties (Fig. 1B, C).The Relative Conductivity Rate (RCR) and Soluble Protein (SP) increased gradually with the decrease in temperature, and the content of resistant varieties was higher than that of sensitive varieties in each temperature gradient. The relative conductivity increased sharply after 0 °C, while soluble protein increased slowly. (Fig. 1C, E).

Identification of high throughput sequencing of lncRNAs in hulless barley seedlings in response to low temperature stress

A total of 36 Hordeum_vulgare samples were measured using the Zebra platform, with each sample yielding an average of 12.65 Gb of data, and the average ratio of the genomes was 79.65%. A total of 101,193 transcripts were detected for expression, of which 25,809 were lncRNAs and 75,384 were mRNAs. The average ratio of Q20s for Read1 was 98%, the average ratio of Q20s for Read2 was 97.55%, the average ratio of Q30s for Read1 was 94.38%, and the average ratio of Q30s for Read2 was 93.6%. The average ratio of clean reads is 98.19%, the average Total Mapping Ratio is 97.21% and all the samples are above 95%, and the average Uniquely Mapping Ratio is 56.03% (Supplementary Table 1).

After obtaining new transcripts, we will predict the coding ability of the transcripts to distinguish between mRNAs and lncRNAs.We use three prediction software and one database for prediction. The three software are CPC [30], txCdsPredict, and CNCI [31]; the database is pfam [32].All three prediction software scored the coding ability of the transcripts and then set a scoring threshold to distinguish lncRNAs from mRNAs. Pfam is a protein database, and a transcript that can be compared to Pfam is considered to be an mRNA; otherwise, it is an lncRNA. At least three of the four judgments were consistent with each other before we confirmed that the transcript was either an mRNA or an lncRNA.(Fig. 2A).

To further characterize the lncRNAs in hulless Barley under cold stress, we compared the exon numbers of lncRNAs and mRNAs. lncRNAs had fewer exons than mRNA transcripts, and most of the lncRNA transcripts had only one exon, whereas mRNAs had more than two or three exons (Fig. 2B). The length of lncRNAs was mainly concentrated in the range of 100–500 nt, and the length of mRNAs was mainly between 500 and 2500 nt (Fig. 2C). We also counted the distribution of lncRNAs and mRNAs on each chromosome, and both lncRNAs and mRNAs were more distributed on chromosomes 2 and 5 (Fig. 2D). The expression levels of lncRNA and mRNA were counted, and the FPKM value of mRNA was significantly higher than that of lncRNA (Fig. 2E). Regarding the number of transcripts, the majority of lncRNAs had only one transcript, while mRNAs had at least one transcript, but more than one transcript was also widely distributed (Fig. 2F).

Although the magnitude of variation in the expression levels of the selected lncRNAs and mRNAs varied, the expression trends were consistent with those expected from sequencing, indicating that the sequencing data were reliable.

LncRNA target gene prediction

The function of lncRNAs is mainly realized by acting on target genes by cis or trans mode. The basic principle of cis action target gene prediction considers that the function of lncRNAs is related to the protein coding genes adjacent to their coordinates, and therefore the mRNAs neighboring the lncRNAs are screened out to be their target genes. In contrast, trans regulation does not depend on the positional relationship and is predicted by calculating the binding energy. In the cold group, a total of 1904 pairs of lncRNA-mRNA pairs were predicted, of which 1152 pairs were paired by cis (664 pairs by mRNA overlap, 175 pairs by mRNA upstream, and 313 pairs by mRNA downstream), and 752 pairs were paired by trans. The total number of paired lncRNA-mRNA pairs predicted in the freezing group was 922, with 473 pairs in cis (261 pairs in mRNA overlap, 90 pairs upstream of mRNA, and 122 pairs downstream of mRNA) and 449 pairs in trans.

In order to identify the differentially expressed lncRNA-mRNA pairs, the differential lncRNA target genes were intersected with the differentially expressed mRNAs, and there were 656 intersections of the differential lncRNA target genes with the differentially expressed mRNAs in the CT_24℃_vs_5℃ comparison of the freeze-hazard group, and 668 intersections of the differential lncRNA target genes with the differentially expressed mRNAs in the CS_24℃_vs_5℃ comparison. There were 187 differential lncRNA target genes interspersed with differentially expressed mRNAs in the CT_0℃_vs_−8℃ comparison in the freezing group and 146 in the CS_0℃_vs_−8℃ comparison. The CT_24℃_vs_5℃ and CS_24℃_vs_5℃ comparisons were taken as intersections to look for differential resistance genes in the cold group, of which there were 490 genes in common, and 166 genes unique to CT_24℃_vs_5℃ (Supplementary Fig. 2). The intersection of CT_0℃_vs_−8℃ and CS_0℃_vs_−8℃ was compared to find the differential resistance genes in the cold group, among which there were 82 genes in common and 105 genes unique to CT_0℃_vs_−8℃ (Supplementary Fig. 2).

We analyzed the functions of the 490 genes in the intersection of the cold group and the 82 genes in the freeze group in the Venn plot to pick out the kinase and transcription factors; a total of 23 kinase and 7 transcription factors were picked out in the cold group, and 1 kinase was picked out in the freeze group (Fig. 9). These kinase were differential expressed in Ca + ion transport and hormonal pathways. 7 transcription factors were bHLH112, WRKY71, RF2B, TFB1-1, MAD51, TCP20, PHL11. By means of the heat-map (Fig. 9) we found that in the cold-damaged group there was a decreasing trend in HvWRKY71, which was sharply elevated in sensitive varieties at 12 °C and then leveled off. HvWRKY71 was not expressed in the freezing group, while the resistant varieties showed a flat increase in cold damage and were still expressed in freezing at 0 °C. HvbHLH112 decreased gradually with decreasing temperatures in both varieties in the freezing group. lncRNA XR_006622464.1 was present in the form of cis_mRNA_dw10k, downstream of its target gene bHLH112. lncRNA LTCONS_00033085 is present as mRNA-CompleteIn-LncExon with the gene HvWRKY71.

Heat-map of differential lncRNA-mRNA and differential mRNA intersecting kinase and transcription factors in cold- and freeze-damaged groups

These transcription factors are associated with the plant hormones ABA and ethylene. These hormones in turn are highly correlated with the antioxidant enzyme system, a physiological indicator of plant stress tolerance. This implies that these lncRNAs respond to cold stress through the expression of regulated kinase and transcription factors (mRNAs) thereby indirectly regulating redox processes.

WGCNA analysis identifies hub low-temperature-related genes

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) is to classify genes with similar expression patterns into modules, so as to study genes with similarity more effectively. WGCNA (Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis) was performed on 8497 lncRNAs identified in the cold damage group and 6656 lncRNAs identified in the frost damage group. The results showed that the co-expression gene module separated the genes in the cold damage group into 26 different modules, among which the turquoise module (1258) gene was the most gene, followed by the blue (836) module (Fig. 10A). The genes were isolated into 15 different modules in the frost damage group, among which the turquoise module (949) gene was the most gene, followed by the blue (836) module (Fig. 10C). After obtaining the expression pattern modules, we analyzed the correlation between the previously measured physiological data and the modules. The correlation between module eigengenes and physiological indices was examined to determine their relationships and significance levels, with correlation heatmaps generated. The results revealed that under chilling stress, SOD exhibited the highest positive correlation with the black module (0.69), p-value < 0.001, and the highest negative correlation with the green and turquoise modules (−0.65), p-value < 0.01. POD showed the highest positive correlation with the black module (0.75), p-value < 0.001, and the highest negative correlation with the blue module (−0.81), p-value < 0.001. RCR demonstrated the highest positive correlation with the midnightblue module (0.87), p-value < 0.001, and the highest negative correlation with the green and turquoise modules (−0.7), p-value < 0.01. SP had the highest positive correlation with the midnightblue module (0.75), p-value < 0.001, and the highest negative correlation with the turquoise module (0.84), p-value < 0.001 (Fig. 10B). Under freezing stress, SOD displayed the highest positive correlation with the yellow module (0.83), p-value < 0.001, and the highest negative correlation with the black module (−0.5), p-value < 0.05. POD exhibited the highest positive correlation with the yellow module (0.49), p-value < 0.05, and the highest negative correlation with the midnightblue module (−0.44), p-value < 0.07. RCR showed the highest correlation with the black module (0.57), p-value < 0.05, and the highest negative correlation with the blue module (−0.78), p-value < 0.001. SP had the highest correlation with the black module (0.72), p-value < 0.001, and the highest negative correlation with the blue module (−0.9), p-value < 0.001 (Fig. 10D).

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) of differentially expressed genes (DEGs). Co-expression module cluster tree in cold damage group (A), correlation between physiological indexes and module association in cold damage group (B), co-expression module cluster tree in frost damage group (C), correlation between physiological indexes and module association in frost damage group (D)

This study aimed to deeply analyze the modules significantly associated with physiological indicators (superoxide dismutase SOD, peroxidase POD, respiratory control rate RCR, and soluble protein SP). During the screening process, two key hub long-stranded non-coding RNAs (hub lncRNAs) were identified and compared with the predicted lncRNA-mRNA pairings. In the cold damage treatment group, five lncRNAs (XR006611493.1, LTCONS00012305 were positively correlated with SOD, POD, RCR, and SP, while LTCONS00034478, LTCONS00063024, and XR006630304.1 were negatively correlated with these physiological indicators) had predicted mRNA pairing. Through the mRNA annotation file, the mRNA corresponding to the lncRNA LTCONS00012305 was determined to be the myb family transcription factor HvPHL11. LTCONS00012305 and its target gene HvPHL11 exist in the form of Lnc-AntiOverlap-mRNA, while other target genes encode proteins with unknown functions. In the freeze damage treatment group, two other lncRNAs (LTCONS00004357 negatively correlated with SOD and POD, LTCONS00052975 positively correlated with SOD and POD, and negatively correlated with RCR and SP) were identified in this study) that had predicted mRNA pairing. The paired mRNA of the LTCONS00004357 is the zinc finger CCCH domain-containing protein 33-like, which exists in cismRNAdw10k form. These findings suggest that LTCONS00012305 and HvPHL11 may be involved in regulating plant stress response under cold damage. Given the importance of HvPHL11 as a myb family transcription factor in plant growth and development and stress response, LTCONS00012305 may affect plant cold tolerance by regulating the expression of HvPHL11. In addition, LTCONS_00004357 and zinc finger CCCH domain-containing protein 33-like protein, a pair of lncRNA-mRNAs, may be involved in regulating the frost resistance mechanism of plants.

Discussion

At the level of plant transcription, there exists a part of transcripts that cannot encode proteins, but they can exercise biological functions at the level of RNA, which are equally important biologically, and lncRNA belongs to this type of transcripts. In recent years, studies have reported that long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) play important roles in plant developmental processes, signal transduction, and stress processes, including cold response [33]. Recent advances in sequencing technology have made possible the genome-wide identification of lncRNAs in a variety of plant species, including Modern and Wild Wheats [34, 35]. Hulless Barley is a widely grown food crop in the plateau region, and low temperatures limit plant growth and development as well as the formation of the final yield [36]. Up to now, Little has been reported on the lncRNA mechanism of hulless barley in response to low temperatures, so it is necessary to explore the molecular mechanism of lncRNAs in response to low temperatures in barley. In this study, high-throughput sequencing was performed on 36 hulless barley samples, and the average matching rate between the sequencing data and the reference genome was as high as 93.6%, which indicates that the sequencing results in this study are relatively reliable.

In this study, 25,809 lncRNAs and 75,384 mRNA transcripts were initially identified in the leaves of 2 extreme varieties of hulless barley responding to low temperatures, and 14,072 lncRNAs with high confidence were finally identified. By comparing the lengths of lncRNAs and mRNAs, and exon numbers, it was found that hulless barley lncRNAs were shorter in length relative to the encoded proteins and possessed a smaller number of exons, as shown by previous work in rye [13]., Medicago truncatula [17] similar results were found, suggesting that lncRNAs have similar length and distribution characteristics in different plants.

To investigate whether lncRNAs have biologically relevant functions through interactions with target genes, GO and KEGG enrichment analyses of their target genes were carried out in this study. The GO enrichment results showed that the cellular processes, metabolic process, and response to stimulus were enriched in multiple groups. The different genes co-enriched in metabolic process and response to stimulus may have key roles in the response to cold stress in hulless barley. Response to stimulus co-enriched differential genes may have a key role in hulless barley response to cold stress. In addition, the same gene was found to be enriched in multiple functional terms, which suggests that the regulatory network of the plant is intertwined; each gene may play multiple roles in different pathways. In the classification of KEGG pathway of the lncRNA target genes, the two varieties in the cold-damaged group were mainly enriched in Nucleotide excision repair (ko03420) and DNA replication (ko03030). In the freezing group, they were enriched in Ascorbate and aldarate metabolism (ko00053). Differential expressed mRNAs were also significantly enriched in Ascorbate and aldarate metabolism (ko00053), suggesting that DEGs of these pathways are closely related to low temperature stress.

Currently, the mechanism of lncRNA-mRNA interactions can be functionally annotated through positional relationships with protein-coding genes and regulatory mRNAs to predict the biological functions of lncRNAs [21]. In this study, target gene prediction was performed by two modes of lncRNA regulation on target genes, and a total of 1904 pairs of lncRNA-mRNA were predicted in the cold-damaged group, of which 1152 pairs were in the cis mode and 752 pairs were in the trans mode. In the freezing group, 922 pairs of lncRNA-mRNA were predicted, of which 473 pairs were paired by cis, and 449 pairs were paired by trans. Most of the differential lncRNAs were found to regulate target genes by cis-action, which is similar to the regulation of lncRNAs in Populus trichocarpa [37]. The reason for this result may mainly be that the cis approach identifies genes within 100 kb upstream and downstream of the lncRNA based on positional information, whereas trans requires complementarity between the target genes and the lncRNA sequences, and the latter case is more demanding, and thus would be much smaller in number [38]. The intersection of differential lncRNA target genes and differential expressed mRNAs in the cold group in this study had 656 genes in the CT_24°C_vs_5°C comparison and 668 genes in the CS_24°C_vs_5°C comparison, and in the freezing group had 187 genes in the CT_0°C_vs_−8 °C comparison and 146 genes in the CS_0°C_vs_−8 °C comparison. This indicates that these differential mRNAs play a role in the function of lncRNAs. Comparing CT_24°C_vs_5°C and CS_24°C_vs_5°C in the cold group and taking the intersection, we found that there were 490 lncRNA-mRNA pairs, and analyzing the mRNA function, we found that there were 24 kinases and 7 transcription factors, namely bHLH112, WRKY71, RF2B, TFB1-1, MAD51, TCP20, PHL11, and MAD51, PHL11. CT_0°C_vs_−8 °C and CS_0°C_vs_−8 °C were compared to take the intersection to find the differential lncRNA-mRNA pairs in the cold group, of which there were 82 pairs, and the mRNA function analysis found one kinase.

Using weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), this study further elucidated the significant correlation between the long non-coding RNA (lncRNA)-messenger RNA (mRNA) module and physiological indicators such as superoxide dismutase (SOD), peroxidase (POD), relative conductivity rate (RCR), and soluble protein (SP). The association between LTCONS00012305 and HvPHL11 (a transcription factor belonging to the MYB family) in cold-damaged samples, as well as the synergistic effect LTCONS00004357 zinc finger protein in freeze-damaged samples, suggests that lncRNAs may indirectly affect plant antioxidant defense mechanisms and cell membrane stability by regulating transcription factors or RNA-binding proteins. These findings are consistent with previous findings that MYB and WRKY transcription factors exert their biological functions by regulating downstream target genes (such as antioxidant enzyme encoding genes) during plant response to abiotic stress.

WRKY22 (a downstream target gene of MPK3) and TGACG binding factor 1 (TGA1)-associated SA signaling pathway are involved in freezing stress defense mechanism [39]. It has been found that over-expression of AtWRKY71 leads to an increase in dwarf branches in Arabidopsis plants [40]. In this study, the expression of HvWRKY71 increased dramatically with decreasing temperatures in sensitive varieties under cold stress, whereas the expression of resistant varieties increased gently; it was basically unexpressed in freezing, and was still expressed in freezing at 0 °C. This may be due to the fact that resistant varieties are able to survive the cold stress, but they are also able to survive the freezing. This may be one of the important factors for the ability of resistant varieties to withstand cold stress and to mature in ultra-high freezing pastures. Over-expression of AtWRKY71 resulted in dwarf plants probably related to the expression of ABA, which is an important hormone against low temperature in cold stress, and LTCONS_00033085 (lncRNA) and the target gene HvWRKY71 were expressed as mRNA-CompleteIn-LncRNAs. The existence of CompleteIn-LncExon with the target gene HvWRKY71 in the form of mRNA-LncExon may be related to the realization of the function of the target gene transcription factor, and the lncRNA affects the ABA pathway through the regulation of HvWRKY71 to achieve the purpose of resisting low temperature, which will need to be further verified. ICE1 is a MYC-type bHLH transcription factor. bHLH1 (ICE1) is a typical transcriptional activator of CBF, an important gene for cold stress [41]., and the bHLH superfamily genes are widely involved in various aspects related to plant stress tolerance. It has been shown that in zucchini bHLH112 is induced by cold stress and is associated with ABA expression [42]. In this study, HvbHLH112 decreased with the decrease of temperature in cold stress and rebounded in freezing stress. As for XR_006622464.1 (lncRNA), 3000 bp downstream of its target genes may regulate the transcription of target genes as well as the function of the target genes, to realize the response to cold stress. How this is realized needs to be verified by further research.

Twenty-three kinases were found in the cold-damaged group and seven kinases were found in the freeze-damaged group, while only one kinase, LTCONS00033085 (lncRNA)-HvWRKY71, XR006622464.1 (lncRNA)-HvbHLH112, was also found in the cold-damaged group, which may be due to the smaller differences in RNA responses between cold-damaged and freeze-damaged groups when subjected to low temperatures. It could also be caused by the fact that cold damage (24 °C, 12 °C, 5 °C) was controlled at 24 °C, whereas freeze damage (0 °C, −5 °C, −8 °C) was controlled at 0 °C.

HvPHL11 was identified as a key node in weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) and differentially expressed long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) target genes. LTCONS00012305 showed positive correlations with superoxide dismutase (SOD), peroxidase (POD), respiratory control rate (RCR), and antioxidant capacity (SP), and among the most correlated modules in RCR and SP, LTCONS00012305 as a hub gene, which may affect the expression pattern of antioxidant enzyme genes by regulating the expression of MYB transcription factor families. In addition, LTCONS_00004357 interacted with zinc finger protein genes, suggesting that it contributed to maintaining cell membrane stability by regulating the expression of genes related to cell membranes.

Conclusions

Hulless barley is an important food crop widely grown on the Tibetan Plateau. However, with the impact of global warming, extreme low temperatures occur frequently on the Tibetan Plateau. Previous studies on low temperature lncRNA-mRNA regulation have only focused on model crops, and less research has been done in hulless barley. In this study, we performed GO enrichment of differential mRNAs and differential lncRNAs corresponding to target genes and showed significant enrichment in the metabolic process and response to stimulus pathway in both the cold-affected and freeze-affected groups. KEGG enrichment revealed that the cold-damaged group was significantly enriched in Nucleotide excision repair (ko03420) and DNA replication (ko03030), while the frost-damaged group was significantly enriched in Ascorbate and aldarate metabolism (ko00053) pathway, which indicated that the Ascorbate metabolism pathway plays an important role in responding to frost damage in hulless barley. To accurately understand the changes in mRNA expression and to identify potential target genes of lncRNAs, differential mRNAs and differential lncRNAs were de-intersected, and a total of 1,904 pairs of lncRNA-mRNA were predicted in the cold-damaged group, and 922 pairs were predicted in the frost-damaged group. There were 490 gene pairs in the cold-damaged group and 86 gene pairs in the freeze-damaged group. The lncRNA-mRNA pairs in which the mRNA was a transcription factor or a kinase were selected and finally screened LTCONS00033085 (lncRNA)-HvWRKY71, XR006622464.1 (lncRNA)- HvbHLH112 gene pairs that might respond to the cold stress by regulating the ABA pathway. WGCNA analysis identified key modules related to cold stress response, and lncRNAs in these modules may be involved in highland barley’s adaptation to cold stress by regulating antioxidant defense systems, membrane structure maintenance, and osmoregulation. LTCONS_00012305-HvPHL11 gene responds to low temperature stress through the expression of MYB family transcription factors.

Photodynamic therapy combined with a radiation-emitting metallic stent for extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma: a first-in-human case report
Authors: Jianwen Ye, Yaming Zan, Zilin Wei, Yuan Liao, Wenlong Zhai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundExtrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (eCCA) carries a dismal prognosis, and the majority of patients present with unresectable disease at diagnosis. Photodynamic therapy (PDT) and radiation-emitting metallic stents (REMS) have each demonstrated palliative benefit in malignant biliary obstruction; however, their sequential combination has never previously been reported in humans. We describe herein the first-in-human application of this combined locoregional approach.Case DescriptionA 76-year-old male presented with a 1-month history of progressive fatigue and was diagnosed with eCCA on the basis of contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) and biliary biopsy. In view of his poor performance status [Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) score of 2] and personal refusal of surgery, he underwent percutaneous transhepatic cholangiodrainage (PTCD), followed by two sequential sessions of PDT and subsequent implantation of an iodine-125 (125I) seed-loaded REMS. At 6-month follow-up, the patient’s ECOG performance status had improved to 0, liver function tests had been normalized, serum carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA19-9) had decreased from 57.90 to 13.6 U/mL (within the normal reference range), and imaging demonstrated tumor regression with maintained stent patency.ConclusionsThis case provides the first proof-of-concept for PDT combined with REMS implantation as a unified locoregional strategy for unresectable eCCA. The favorable short-term outcomes support the scientific and clinical rationale for this combined approach and underscore the need for prospective, controlled studies to validate its safety, efficacy, and optimal technical parameters.

Introduction

Extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (eCCA) is an aggressive malignancy arising from the epithelial lining of the extrahepatic bile ducts. Owing to its insidious onset and the absence of early warning symptoms, the majority of patients present at an advanced, unresectable stage, with a 5-year overall survival rate consistently below 10%. For patients with unresectable disease, the principal therapeutic objectives are biliary decompression and locoregional tumor control. Systemic therapies, including gemcitabine-cisplatin chemotherapy, targeted agents, and immune checkpoint inhibitors, have modestly expanded the treatment landscape for advanced biliary tract cancers (BTCs) (1,2); however, durable locoregional control remains a substantial unmet need, particularly for patients with poor performance status who are ineligible for systemic therapy.

Photodynamic therapy (PDT) is an established palliative modality for unresectable cholangiocarcinoma, in which a photosensitizer selectively accumulating in tumor tissue is activated by wavelength-specific laser irradiation, generating cytotoxic reactive oxygen species (ROS) that induce tumor cell apoptosis and necrosis (3,4). Despite its proven benefit in improving biliary stent patency and quality of life, PDT’s efficacy is fundamentally constrained by a tissue penetration depth of only 4–6 mm, rendering it insufficient as a standalone treatment for tumors with deep invasion or regional lymph node involvement. Conventional self-expanding metallic stents (SEMS), while effective for biliary decompression, offer no intrinsic antitumor activity. The radiation-emitting metallic stent (REMS) addresses this gap by integrating structural biliary drainage with localized brachytherapy via embedded iodine-125 (125I) seeds, enabling continuous three-dimensional low-dose-rate irradiation of the tumor bed (5). To our knowledge, no prior human case has described the sequential combination of PDT and REMS implantation as a unified locoregional strategy for eCCA. We report herein the first such case, describe the procedural rationale and technique, and discuss its clinical implications. We present this article in accordance with the CARE reporting checklist (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-2026-1-0097/rc).

Staging and diagnosis

Based on preoperative imaging and pathological findings, the tumor was staged as T2N0M0 (stage II) per the 8th edition American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) Cancer Staging Manual. Imaging demonstrated no evidence of regional lymph node or distant metastasis.

Decision for non-surgical management

The patient’s Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status was 2 at the time of presentation. Taking into consideration his advanced age, poor functional reserve, and personal preference, the patient declined surgical resection after the risks and benefits had been fully explained by the treating team. Consistent with established principles of patient autonomy in oncological decision-making, his preference was respected and the management plan was adapted accordingly. No systemic chemotherapy was administered prior to, concurrent with, or following the locoregional combination therapy, given his borderline performance status and the patient’s informed preference for a locoregional-only approach. An ECOG performance status of 2 is generally considered the lower boundary of eligibility for gemcitabine-based combination chemotherapy, and the treating multidisciplinary team judged that the risk-benefit balance did not favor systemic therapy initiation in this patient. Anesthesia risk was independently assessed as American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) Physical Status Class II–III, further supporting the multidisciplinary decision to avoid general anesthesia.

Treatment

Percutaneous transhepatic cholangiodrainage (PTCD) and biopsy

As the initial therapeutic intervention, ultrasound-guided PTCD was performed to achieve biliary decompression and to obtain tissue for pathological diagnosis. Histopathological examination of the biopsy specimen confirmed the diagnosis of cholangiocarcinoma, thereby establishing the indication for subsequent locoregional therapy.

PDT

Forty-eight hours prior to the first PDT session, hematoporphyrin derivative (3 mg/kg) was administered intravenously, and the patient was maintained on strict photoprotective precautions throughout the photosensitization period. A cylindrical optical fiber was subsequently introduced through the established PTCD tract and positioned at the tumor site under fluoroscopic guidance. The first PDT session was delivered using a 630-nm laser (irradiation length: 5 cm) at a power output of 800 mW for 21 minutes, yielding a total energy delivery of 1,000 J (Figure 1B,1C). Twenty-four hours later, a second PDT session was performed using identical laser parameters (630 nm; irradiation length 5 cm; 800 mW) for 10.5 minutes, delivering a total energy of 500 J. The reduced energy in the second session was intended to consolidate the cytotoxic effect on residual viable tumor cells while minimizing acute local inflammatory edema and tissue swelling prior to stent deployment, thereby reducing the risk of procedural complications during REMS placement. A balloon catheter was subsequently employed to dilate the biliary stricture in preparation for stent deployment (Figure 1D). The specific treatment parameters employed in this case were selected based on established protocols for cholangiocarcinoma PDT and standard brachytherapy dosimetry practices for biliary malignancies.

REMS implantation

Immediately following the second PDT session and balloon dilation, the REMS was implanted across the biliary stricture. The device comprised an outer self-expanding nitinol stent (diameter: 10 mm; length: 60 mm) loaded with 16 125I seeds and a coaxially positioned inner stent (diameter: 8 mm; length: 60 mm) (Figure 1E,1F).

The 125I seeds used were Iodine [125I] Brachytherapy Sources [Seeds Biological Pharmacy (Tianjin) Ltd., Tianjin, China; active length: 4.50±0.20 mm; outer diameter: 0.80±0.03 mm], with a per-seed activity of 0.100–6.000 mCi (3.7–222.0 MBq) and a total 16-seed activity of 1.600–96.000 mCi (59.2–3,552.0 MBq). The prescribed dose to the gross tumor volume (D90) was 130–150 Gy.

The device was developed by Nanjing Rongsheng Medical Technology Co., Ltd. and has received approval from the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) of China (No. 20233130621).

The decision to perform PDT prior to REMS implantation was guided by a pathophysiological rationale: PDT-induced tumor cell apoptosis and necrosis were expected to reduce local tumor bulk and improve the apposition of the stent against the bile duct wall, thereby optimizing brachytherapy dose delivery to residual viable tumor tissue. Furthermore, PDT-mediated microvascular disruption may enhance the radiosensitivity of surviving tumor cells, suggesting a potential additive or synergistic interaction with the continuous low-dose-rate irradiation subsequently delivered by the 125I seeds. The risk of PDT-induced local edema complicating stent deployment was mitigated by performing REMS implantation immediately after the second PDT session and balloon dilation.

The PTCD catheter was retained for 2 weeks following stent implantation. Once adequate biliary drainage through the REMS was confirmed, the catheter was clamped and subsequently removed after the patient reported no discomfort.

Outcomes and follow-up

No immediate or procedure-related complications, including cholangitis, hemorrhage, pancreatitis, or biliary perforation, were observed during either PDT session or the subsequent stent placement procedure. Intermittent, self-limiting abdominal discomfort was noted during the follow-up period; this was mild, well tolerated, and resolved without intervention. No other delayed or late complications were identified.

At the 6-month follow-up assessment, the patient demonstrated marked clinical improvement, with an ECOG performance status of 0. Liver function parameters showed significant normalization relative to baseline values across all measured indices. Serum CA19-9 had decreased from a pretreatment value of 57.90 to 13.6 U/mL, falling within the normal reference range (0.01–37 U/mL).

Follow-up contrast-enhanced abdominal CT at 6 months demonstrated effective biliary decompression with maintained stent patency at the treated segment. The maximal bile duct wall thickness at the stricture site decreased from 6.02 mm at baseline to 2.62 mm at 6-month follow-up (Figure 2A,2B), consistent with partial response per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) 1.1 criteria; post-treatment measurements were obtained in the presence of stent-related susceptibility artifact and should be interpreted accordingly.

Patient perspective

Before this treatment, I had been feeling extremely tired for about a month and I had noticed I was feeling increasingly unwell and very tired. I was frightened and worried that there was nothing the doctors could do. After the procedures, I experienced some mild discomfort at times, but it was manageable, and my energy slowly came back and the yellowing faded. Six months later, I was able to walk around the neighborhood again and join my family for meals, which I had not been able to do before. I am very grateful to the medical team for their patience and care throughout the process.

Discussion

Therapeutic landscape and rationale for the combined approach

eCCA represents one of the most therapeutically challenging gastrointestinal malignancies, with surgical resection remaining the only potentially curative option. The majority of patients are diagnosed at a stage that precludes curative-intent surgery, and the prognosis for unresectable disease remains dismal, with a median overall survival of approximately 12–15 months even with contemporary systemic treatments (1,2). For patients with poor performance status ineligible for systemic therapy, effective locoregional control options represent an especially significant unmet need.

PDT has been evaluated as a locoregional strategy for unresectable cholangiocarcinoma, with evidence from systematic reviews and meta-analyses supporting improvements in biliary stent patency and, in some analyses, overall survival compared with stenting alone (3,4). Its mechanism relies on photosensitizer-mediated generation of singlet oxygen and ROS upon laser activation, inducing direct tumor cytotoxicity, vascular damage, and immune activation. However, the intrinsic 4–6 mm tissue penetration depth represents a ceiling on its efficacy against deeply invasive or lymph node-involved tumors. The REMS directly addresses this limitation by integrating continuous brachytherapy into the stenting procedure, enabling irradiation of deeper tumor layers with a therapeutic radius substantially exceeding the PDT penetration limit. The sequential combination of these two modalities therefore represents a conceptually sound strategy for more comprehensive locoregional tumor control. A critical interpretive limitation of this case must be explicitly stated: the observed clinical improvements, including resolution of cholestatic symptoms, normalization of liver function, and reduction in CA19-9, could substantially or even primarily reflect the effect of effective biliary decompression achieved by stent placement alone, rather than specific antitumor contributions of PDT or brachytherapy. While the sustained CA19-9 normalization and radiographic tumor regression at 6 months are consistent with a potential antitumor effect beyond mere drainage, a single uncontrolled case cannot distinguish the individual contributions of PDT, brachytherapy, and biliary decompression. This fundamental confound must be rigorously addressed in future comparative studies.

Long-term management considerations

The use of uncovered metallic stents in this case carries inherent long-term management implications, as uncovered SEMS are susceptible to tumor ingrowth through the stent mesh, representing the principal mechanism of late stent failure in malignant biliary obstruction. In the present case, the continuous brachytherapy from the embedded 125I seeds is expected to suppress local tumor regrowth within the stent lumen; however, as radioactivity decays over time (physical half-life of 125I: approximately 59.4 days), this protective effect will progressively diminish. Our planned surveillance strategy includes regular clinical, biochemical, and imaging assessments at approximately 3-month intervals, with secondary endoscopic or percutaneous interventions available if symptomatic stent occlusion occurs.

However, the optimal combined dosing strategy for this dual-modality approach has not been established, and future studies should aim to define standardized dosing protocols, investigate whether alternative sequencing or inter-modality timing intervals could yield superior outcomes, and evaluate dose-response relationships through systematic dosimetric modeling. Broader clinical adoption will additionally require addressing multidisciplinary infrastructure requirements, radiation safety considerations, and cost-effectiveness relative to established alternatives. Additionally, the percutaneous transhepatic approach required for both PTCD and REMS implantation represents a procedural prerequisite that may limit the applicability of this combined strategy in centres without established interventional biliary expertise.

Limitations and future directions

This report is subject to several important limitations. First, as a single-center, single-patient case report without a comparison arm, it cannot provide evidence-level conclusions regarding the efficacy, safety, or generalizability of this combined approach. Second, the 6-month follow-up duration is insufficient to evaluate long-term oncological endpoints, including overall survival, progression-free survival, and durable stent patency beyond 12 months. Third, the long-term safety profile of a permanently implanted radiation-emitting device in the biliary tract remains incompletely characterized; late-stage complications including radiation-induced biliary fibrosis, fistula formation, and delayed perforation are known to manifest beyond 12 months in brachytherapy applications. Fourth, the cumulative local toxicity of combining PDT-mediated tissue injury with prolonged low-dose-rate irradiation on peri-biliary structures warrants dedicated prospective monitoring. Fifth, the long-term biological effects of 125I radioactive decay on surrounding healthy tissues over a multi-year horizon require systematic investigation.

Future investigations should involve prospective, multicenter studies with extended follow-up of at least 24 months. A randomized controlled trial comparing PDT combined with REMS versus REMS alone, PDT alone, or conventional SEMS would be required to determine whether the combination confers additive or synergistic benefit over either modality independently, and should incorporate standardized tumor response assessments per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) 1.1, comprehensive adverse event documentation, and validated quality-of-life measures.

Conclusions

To our knowledge, this is the first reported case globally of PDT combined with REMS implantation as a unified locoregional strategy for the treatment of eCCA. The combined procedure was technically feasible, well tolerated, and associated with favorable short-term clinical and imaging outcomes at 6-month follow-up, including marked improvement in functional status, normalization of liver function parameters, reduction in CA19-9 to within the normal reference range, and radiographic evidence of tumor response with maintained stent patency. While these preliminary findings provide proof-of-concept for this novel combined approach, they must be interpreted with caution given the inherent limitations of a single case report, the short follow-up duration, and the inability to isolate the individual contributions of each modality. The results nonetheless establish a clinical and biological rationale for this dual-modality locoregional strategy and support the conduct of future prospective, controlled studies to rigorously define its safety profile, optimal technical parameters, and long-term oncological efficacy.

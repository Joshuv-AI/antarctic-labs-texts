Optimal operation model of ecological flow of hydropower station based on genetic algorithm and neural network
Authors: Yong Luo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAmid diverse water demands, the conflict between ecological flow adjustments and power generation flow adjustments in hydropower station water resource dispatching, coupled with an increasing diversity of constraints, complicates the issue of water resource optimization and dispatching. How to scientifically develop feedback regulation mechanisms to promote a virtuous ecological cycle, fine tune existing hydropower station scheduling plans, and achieve multi-objective optimization scheduling is the core issue in researching the comprehensive utilization of water resources. This study establishes a reservoir flow regulation model with the objective functions of maximizing power generation and achieving optimal average ecological protection. The model, constrained by water balance, reservoir capacity, unit output, and unit overflow, was optimized and solved using the genetic algorithm-backpropagation neural network (GA-BPNN) approach. This study establishes a reservoir flow regulation model with the GA-BPNN algorithm was applied in multi-objective optimization to identify an optimal solution that maximizes overall system performance while adhering to ecological flow constraints. The results indicate that the optimized plan has performed effectively in hydropower station operations. It not only satisfies ecological flow requirements, but also significantly enhances power generation efficiency. Specifically, power generation increased by 32,680 kw·h, a 23.85 % growth rate. The optimized plan significantly enhances the comprehensive utilization efficiency of water resources. It offers a scientific basis for the rational planning, dispatching and utilization of water resources in hydropower stations and reservoirs.Keywords: Ecological flow, Optimal dispatching, GA-BPNN, Neural network, Hydropower station

Introduction

Ecological flow is a key indicator to ensure the ecological function of rivers and an important basis for river ecological health evaluation and restoration work [1]. Ecological flow is related to the water distribution of upstream and downstream, which can affect downstream production and daily water use and also have a significant impact on the overall downstream ecosystem [2,3]. FANG H et al. [4,5]. Employed the monthly minimum ecological flow method, RVA method, DC method, and monthly frequency method to calculate the ecological flow process at the Shuibuya and Geheyan Hydropower Station dam sites [6]. They then used the Tennant method to analyze the results. ZHANG J et al. [7]. Used the Guanting Hydropower Station as a case study. Based on the calculation of ecological flow under hydrological variability, they defined the ecological security level of the water-reduced reach and established a multi-objective optimal operation model for hydropower generation and ecological security [8]. YAN B et al. [9]. Focused on the main stream of the Hanjiang River, analyzing the current situation of ecological flow assurance and management and identifying the key issues. Integrating resources and demands both inside and outside the river, determining the ecological flow, and meeting ecological water demands have become central topics in ecological flow assurance research [10].

The ecological operation of hydropower stations is a new operational mode based on traditional hydropower station operations, whiach also takes into account the ecological service functions of both the stations and rivers. When building ecological scheduling models, ecological factors are usually regarded as constraints (based on constraints) or objective functions (based on objectives). REN P et al. [11]. Explored the conflicting relationship between hydropower generation and eco-efficiency using the NSGA-II algorithm. They established a multi-objective optimal operation model of hydropower stations with the goal of maximizing annual power generation [12] and average ecological flow under different time scales and achieved higher power generation benefits [8]. JIANG W et al. [13]. Studied the applicability of the R2CROSS method for calculating the ecological flow of small rivers in South China and proposed optimization suggestions [14]. WU C et al. [15]. Developed a multi-objective joint operation model that considers the ecological water demands of downstream rivers, using simulation and optimization methods for hydropower stations. They compared and analyzed the multi-objective operational benefits of hydropower stations under different ecological water demands [16]. The results showed that multi-objective operation has become a crucial approach to balancing the competitiveness of each objective [17].

In the context of hydropower development [18]. The water resources of a hydropower station in China take into account multiple functions such as hydropower generation, agricultural irrigation, urban water supply, flood control regulation, ecological flow and so on [[19], [20], [21]] However, the construction of hydropower stations poses a major challenge to maintain the balance between ecological flow and other objectives such as power generation and water supply [22].

Traditionally, restriction on ecological flow have been based on fixed rules and regulations that do not fully consider the dynamic nature of river ecosystems [23]. These restrictions often lead to unsatisfactory ecological flow management and may have a negative impact on the ecological health of rivers [24,25].

In order to solve this problem, this paper proposes a new method using GA-BPNN algorithm to combine multi-objective correlation analysis and multi-objective optimization [26,27]. This method aims to optimize the allocation of water resources with different objectives, including ecological flow, power generation and water supply [28,29]. The Multi objective correlation analysis is used to identify the relationship between ecological flow and other objectives, such as power generation and water supply. By analyzing the correlation between these objectives, researchers can deeply understand the trade-offs and synergies between them [30].

Based on the results of correlation analysis, GA-BPNN algorithm is used for multi-objective optimization to find the optimal solution to maximize the overall performance of the system while meeting the ecological flow constraints [31]. The GA-BPNN algorithm combines genetic algorithm (GA) and back propagation neural network (BPNN) to effectively search the optimal solution in a large search space. The proposed approach and method provide a new framework for ecological flow regulation, management and protection in the context of hydropower development [32]. By considering the dynamic nature of river ecosystem and optimizing the allocation of water resources, this method can help achieve a better balance between ecological flow and other objectives [33].

In addition, the method can be applied to other hydropower projects and river systems to provide a scalable solution for sustainable water resources management [34]. By combining the principles of multi-objective optimization and correlation analysis, decision makers can make wise decisions, consider the complex interactions between different objectives and ensure the long-term sustainability of river ecosystem. In short, this paper proposes a coupling multi-objective correlation analysis and multi-objective optimization method based on GA-BPNN algorithm for ecological flow scheduling, management and protection [35] The proposed method provides a new perspective and method for solving the challenge of balancing ecological flow and other objectives in the context of hydropower development. By optimizing the allocation of water resources, decision makers can achieve more sustainable and efficient water use while protecting the ecological health of river ecosystems.

When adjusting the flow of water, it is necessary to take into account the protection of river ecosystems and the living environment of aquatic organisms. The power generation efficiency of a hydroelectric power station is constrained by the storage capacity of the reservoir. Reservoir storage capacity refers to the amount of water that can be stored in the reservoir and it directly affects the power generation capacity and efficiency of the hydroelectric power station. The size of the reservoir's storage capacity determines the power generation capacity of the hydroelectric power station. A larger reservoir capacity means that more water can be stored, allowing for longer periods of continuous power generation [41]. Conversely, if the reservoir has a smaller storage capacity, the power generation capacity of the hydroelectric power station will be limited and may not be able to meet the electricity demand during peak periods.

Reservoir storage capacity also affects the power generation efficiency of the hydroelectric power station. A larger reservoir capacity allows the hydroelectric power station to better regulate the water flow and achieve higher power generation efficiency. When the reservoir storage capacity is sufficient, the hydroelectric power station can flexibly adjust the water flow according to the electricity demand to achieve optimal power generation efficiency. However, if the reservoir storage capacity is small, the hydroelectric power station may need to operate at a lower water flow rate, resulting in a decrease in power generation efficiency.

The output constraint of hydropower station's power generation efficiency refers to the limitation and constraint on the output of the units during the power generation process of the hydropower station. These constraints can be technical or economic [42]. Technical constraints refer to the limitations on the output of the units due to their technical performance. For example, the maximum output of the units is limited by factors such as water flow, head and rotor speed. When the water flow is low, the output of the units will also decrease accordingly; when the head is low, the output of the units will also be limited.

In addition, the rotor speed of the units also affects the output and excessively high or low speeds will cause a decrease in output. Economic constraints refer to the limitations on the output of the units due to economic factors during the power generation process of the hydropower station. This includes factors such as the operating costs of the units and electricity market prices. When the electricity market prices are low, the output of the units may be limited to avoid high operating costs [43]. In addition, the maintenance and repair costs of the units also affect the output and if the maintenance and repair costs are too high, the output of the units may be limited.

The overcurrent constraint on the power generation efficiency of a hydroelectric power plant refers to the constraint and control of the overcurrent of the units in order to ensure the safe operation and power generation efficiency of the units during the power generation process of the hydroelectric power plant. The hydroelectric power plant units are driven by the flow of water to rotate the turbine, which in turn drives the generator to generate electricity. During the operation of the hydroelectric power plant, changes in the flow rate and head of the water directly affect the operating status and power generation efficiency of the units. When the flow rate is too large or the head is too high, the units are prone to overcurrent phenomena, that is the load of the units exceeds the design load, resulting in unstable operation of the units and even possible failures and damage to the units [44].

In order to avoid the safety hazards and economic losses caused by overcurrent of the units, the hydroelectric power plant has taken a series of measures to constrain the overcurrent of the units. Firstly, the hydroelectric power plant will formulate reasonable operating procedures and operating guidelines based on changes in water flow and unit characteristics, clearly defining the operating range and limiting conditions of the units. During operation, the hydroelectric power plant will monitor the operating status and load of the units in real time through monitoring and control systems. Once an overcurrent phenomenon is detected, measures will be immediately taken to adjust and control the units to ensure their safe operation.

Genetic algorithm neural network model and optimization algorithm

The genetic algorithm is a powerful iterative algorithm that possesses the ability to perform global searches. It draws inspiration from the concept of chromosomes in biological genetics and utilizes them to encode the parameters of the problem solution space. Through a series of iterations, these encoded chromosomes undergo various operations such as selection, reproduction, crossover and mutation.

After these operations,an evaluation is conducted on the resulting chromosomes using a fitness function. This evaluation helps identify the most optimal chromosome individuals. The selected optimal chromosome is then decoded and transformed into the actual problem solution space, allowing for further solving. In this particular study, the genetic algorithm is employed to optimize the BP neural network. This approach takes advantage of the nonlinear mapping capabilities of neural networks and the global optimization capabilities of the genetic algorithm. By combining these two techniques, the learning speed of the neural network is accelerated and the overall prediction model's accuracy and fitting ability are significantly improved.

Conclusions

This paper presents a novel approach that utilizes a genetic algorithm to optimize the backpropagation neural network. The goal is to address multiple objectives, including maximizing power generation, enhancing ecological protection, and ensuring water balance, reservoir capacity, unit output, and unit overcurrent, all while considering various constraints. To demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method, a specific hydropower station is used as a case study, resulting in an optimal water dispatching scheme for the station.

The practical implementation of the optimized scheme at the hydropower station showcases promising results. Not only does it meet the requirements of ecological flow, but it also significantly enhances power generation efficiency. Specifically, the power generation has witnessed a remarkable increase of 32,680 kw·h, representing a growth rate of 23.85 %. Moreover, the comprehensive utilization efficiency of water resources has been greatly improved as a result of the optimized scheme.

Overall, this research highlights the potential of the genetic algorithm in optimizing the backpropagation neural network for hydropower stations. By addressing multiple objectives and constraints, the proposed method provides a comprehensive and effective approach to water dispatching optimization. The positive outcomes observed at a specific hydropower station demonstrate the practical applicability and benefits of this approach in real-world scenarios.

Safety and immunogenicity of a modified mRNA-lipid nanoparticle vaccine candidate against COVID-19: Results from a phase 1, dose-escalation study
Authors: Brandon J. Essink, Craig Shapiro, Marie Grace Dawn Isidro, Paul Bradley, Antoinette Pragalos, Mark Bloch, Joel Santiaguel, Melchor Victor Frias, Spiros Miyakis, Margarida Alves de Mesquita, Stefano Berrè, Charlotte Servais, Natasha Waugh, Claudia Hoffmann, Emna Baba, Oliver Schönborn-Kellenberger, Olaf-Oliver Wolz, Sven D. Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThis phase 1, open-label, dose-escalation, multi-center study (NCT05477186) assessed the safety and immunogenicity of a booster dose of an mRNA COVID-19 vaccine (CV0501) encoding the SARS-CoV-2 Omicron BA.1 spike protein. Participants aged ≥ 18 years previously vaccinated with ≥ 2 doses of an mRNA COVID-19 vaccine received CV0501 doses ranging from 12 to 200 μg. After assessment of safety and immunogenicity of the 12 μg dose in 30 adults, 30 adults ≤ 64 years were randomized to receive either a 3 or 6 μg dose. Solicited adverse events (AEs) were collected for 7 days, unsolicited AEs for 28 days, and serious AEs (SAEs), medically attended AEs (MAAEs), and AEs of special interest (AESIs) until day (D) 181 post-vaccination. Serum neutralizing titers specific to SARS-CoV-2 BA.1, wild-type, Delta, and additional Omicron subvariants were assessed at D1, D15, D29, D91, and D181. Of 180 vaccinated participants (mean age: 49.3 years; 57.8% women), 70.6% had prior SARS-CoV-2 infection. Most solicited local (98.1%) and systemic (96.7%) AEs were of mild-to-moderate severity; the most common were injection site pain (57.5%; 33.3–73.3% across groups) and myalgia (36.9%; 13.3–56.7%). Unsolicited AEs were reported by 14.4% (6.7–26.7%) of participants (mild-to-moderate severity in 88.5% of the participants). Three participants (1.7%) reported SAEs, 16.7% (6.7–30.0%) reported MAAEs, and 8.3% (0.0–13.3%) reported AESIs (15 COVID-19 cases), none related to vaccination. Geometric means of serum neutralizing titers increased from baseline to D15 and D29 (dose-dependent), and then decreased over time. The safety and immunogenicity results supported advancement to a phase 2 trial.

Introduction

To date, coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) caused by the severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) accounts for over 776 million reported cases and over 7 million reported deaths globally.1 COVID-19 poses a continuing threat to public health due to the emergence of variants characterized by enhanced transmissibility and immune evasion that contribute to the evolving epidemiology of the virus.2,3

The global health threat of COVID-19 and the lack of effective treatments required prompt introduction of interventions to combat the disease. In this context, vaccination with mRNA COVID-19 vaccines emerged as a safe and effective approach to COVID-19 prevention. Although research on mRNA vaccines started over three decades ago, their tremendous potential had not been truly uncovered until the COVID-19 pandemic,4 when mRNA vaccine candidates were among the first to obtain regulatory approval. Global vaccination campaigns with mRNA vaccines further illustrated that this technology was safe and effective in preventing COVID-19. This unprecedented success highlights its broader potential application not only to other infectious diseases, but conceivably also to other conditions, such as cancer and inherited diseases.5

To date, more than 65% of the world population has received a primary vaccination series against COVID-19, and around 32% has received a booster dose.1 Effectiveness of the currently approved mRNA vaccines against SARS-CoV-2 wanes over time, particularly in older individuals.6,7 In addition, due to SARS-CoV-2 evolution, the vaccines that encoded the original Wuhan SARS-CoV-2 variant (D614, hereafter referred to as wild type [WT]) provided partial protection against emerging variants such as Omicron and its sublineages.8,9 Similarly, bivalent vaccines against the WT variant and Omicron subvariants BA.4 and BA.5 have shown less vaccine-mediated immunity demonstrated by lower neutralizing responses against Omicron XBB subvariants.10 Although less virulent than the Delta variant,11–13 Omicron and its subvariants are characterized by increased transmissibility and immune escape potential due to critical spike (S) mutations enabling them to evade the immunity induced by the previous infection or initial COVID-19 vaccines based on WT S protein.10,13,14 The World Health Organization assessment of the evolution of SARS-CoV-2 and the performance of approved vaccines support the need to update COVID-19 vaccine formulations to more closely match circulating variants.15–17

An mRNA vaccine candidate (CVnCoV) using the unmodified SARS-CoV-2 WT S protein mRNA sequence was evaluated in clinical studies. Interim results from a phase 2b/3 trial conducted in 39,680 participants from Europe and Latin America showed a vaccine efficacy of 48.2% (96% confidence interval [CI]: 31.0; 61.4) against COVID-19 of any severity and of 70.7% (95% CI: 42.5; 86.1) against moderate to severe COVID-19 across all age groups and across 15 different virus variants (including Delta, but before emergence of Omicron), with an acceptable vaccine safety profile.18 However, activation of the innate immune system could pose challenges to the utility of unmodified mRNA as a vaccine platform.4 The incorporation of modified nucleosides diminishes the innate immune response.4 Furthermore, lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) improve vaccine delivery and act as a potent adjuvant.19

Nucleoside-modified mRNA encapsulated in LNPs has been successfully used in the development of safe and highly effective, WT- and variant-matched COVID-19 vaccines.5,20–22 In addition, this vaccine platform could be easily adapted to express different antigens or antigen combinations, thus allowing the potential development of multivalent or universal vaccines against different pathogens.23

CV0501 is a nucleoside-modified mRNA vaccine encoding Omicron BA.1 variant S protein microencapsulated on an improved backbone in LNPs that has been optimized to improve intracellular mRNA stability and translation for increased and extended protein expression, compared with the first-generation candidate, which was based on a previous backbone. The CV0501 backbone contains optimized non-coding regions (i.e., 5′ and 3’ untranslated regions) followed by a histone stem – loop motif and a poly(A) sequence, which showed improved immunogenicity and protective efficacy in non-human primates compared with the non-optimized form.24 In a preclinical study on small animals, CV0501 was shown to induce robust serum neutralizing titers against the Omicron BA.1, BA.2, and BA.5 subvariants and to reduce Omicron BA.2 subvariant viral loads in the respiratory airway.25

In this first-in-human, phase 1 study, we evaluated the safety and immunogenicity of a single booster dose of CV0501 vaccine in adults who have previously received at least two doses of a licensed mRNA COVID-19 vaccine.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This phase 1, open-label, dose-escalation, multi-center study was conducted between August 2022 and August 2023 in the United States, Australia, and the Philippines. Previously vaccinated adults ≥18 years of age received a CV0501 booster dose on day 1 and were followed for 6 months post-vaccination (Supplementary Material, Figure S1).

The study comprised two parts (Part A and Part B). In Part A, participants were enrolled to receive a single dose level of CV0501 according to five dose groups. Participants were enrolled by age strata: ≥18 to <65 years old (groups 1a–5a) and ≥65 years old (groups 1b–5b). The doses for the groups 1, 2, and 3 were 12 μg, 25 μg, and 50 μg, respectively. The doses administered to groups 4 and 5 were decided by a Safety Review Team (SRT) based on the reactogenicity and safety findings from groups 1–3, independently by age strata. The dose selected was 100 μg in group 4 and 200 μg in group 5 for each of the respective age strata. Each group was planned to include a maximum of 30 participants, distributed between the two age strata, with ≥ 10 participants from each age stratum in each group (Figure S1). Safety data were monitored on an ongoing basis to identify any event that could potentially trigger a holding rule. In the event a holding rule was triggered, the study intervention administration and enrollment were planned to be paused, while the SRT would review available safety data from all vaccinated participants. Participants who had already received the vaccine would continue to attend follow-up visits as planned per protocol.

Enrollment in Part A began with group 1a and progressed to 1b upon positive review of safety data from ≥ 10 participants, at 7 days post-vaccination. The same approach was used for enrollment in all subsequent groups and age strata (Figure S1). Groups could be dropped at any time if an unfavorable safety profile was observed (see Text S1 for details).

Part B was a planned dose de-escalation portion of the study in ≥ 30 adults ≥ 18 to <65 years of age who were randomly assigned to receive lower dose levels (i.e., 3 μg and 6 μg). The rationale for testing these additional lower dose levels was to further evaluate the lower end of the dose response range after the 12 μg dose was shown to be immunogenic. Part B was therefore started after an assessment of safety and immunogenicity data up to day 29 of the 12 μg dose.

Healthy adults were eligible for enrollment if they signed an informed consent form, had received at least two doses of BNT162b2 (Comirnaty, Pfizer/BioNTech) or mRNA-1273 (Spikevax, Moderna) COVID-19 vaccine, with the last dose administered at least 6 months prior to study screening, and were negative for SARS-CoV-2 infection by reverse-transcription polymerase chain reaction test at screening. See Text S2 for additional details on eligibility criteria.

Participants in Part A of the study were assigned sequentially to a dose-level group by age strata, while participants in Part B were randomly assigned to one of the two groups. The randomization schedule was generated with SAS v9.4 and administered using Interactive Response Technology.

In this open-label study, the investigators, study staff, and participants were unblinded. Unblinded safety summaries were provided to the SRT to inform study decisions.

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the principles of Good Clinical Practice. Informed consent was obtained prior to enrollment from all participants. The protocol, amendments, and informed consent forms were reviewed and approved by independent ethics committees/institutional review boards at each site. The study is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05477186) and study details, including a full list of inclusion/exclusion criteria, are available at gsk-studyregister.com (study 218595).

Study vaccine

CV0501 consists of sequence-optimized, capped and polyadenylated, base-substituted mRNA, encoding the full-length S protein of the SARS-CoV-2 Omicron BA.1 variant, microencapsulated in LNPs (composed of a cationic lipid, PEGylated lipid, sterol lipid, and phospholipid). Each dose level was prepared prior to vaccination by dilution of a unit with a dose strength of 270 μg mRNA/mL with a solution of sodium chloride of 0.9%. The vaccine (volumes of 0.3 mL to 0.7 mL, depending on the dose level) was administered intramuscularly.

Objectives

The primary objective of the study (for both Part A and Part B) was to evaluate the safety and tolerability of CV0501 at each dose level. Secondary objectives included evaluating serum neutralizing titers and seroresponse rates based on neutralizing titers against S protein from SARS-CoV-2 variants. The full list of study objectives and outcomes is available in Text S3.

Assessments

Solicited local and systemic adverse events (AEs) occurring within 7 days from vaccination were recorded by the participants in eDiaries. All other AEs, including unsolicited AEs monitored for 28 days after vaccination, and serious AEs (SAEs), medically attended AEs (MAAEs), and AEs of special interest (AESIs) monitored up to study end were recorded in electronic case report forms and followed to satisfactory resolution, until the investigator deemed the event to be chronic or not clinically significant, the event was considered stable, or the participant was lost to follow-up.

AESIs included virologically confirmed COVID-19, potential immune-mediated disorders, anaphylaxis, or severe hypersensitivity within 24 hours after vaccine administration, myocarditis, and pericarditis. Physical examinations, vital sign measurements, and clinical safety laboratory assessments (at screening, baseline, and at 7 days post-vaccination) were conducted. The intensity of solicited AEs was graded by the participant as detailed in Text S4. The maximum intensity that occurred over the duration of the event for all unsolicited AEs, including SAEs, recorded during the study was assessed by the investigator.

All solicited AEs were considered causally related to vaccination. Causality of all other AEs was assessed by the investigators.

All suspected or confirmed cases of symptomatic myocarditis or pericarditis occurring after vaccination were to be monitored throughout the study. In addition, at the day 8 visit, all participants were assessed by 12-lead electrocardiogram to screen for evidence of asymptomatic myocarditis or pericarditis (see details in Text S5).

Blood samples for humoral immunogenicity assessments were collected at baseline (pre-vaccination) and 14 days, 28 days, 3 months, and 6 months post-vaccination. SARS-CoV-2 serum neutralizing levels were measured using pseudotyped virus neutralization assays,26 with the pseudotyped virus displaying S protein from SARS-CoV-2 WT, Omicron (BA.1, BA.2, and BA.5), or Delta variants. Pseudotyped virus particles are made from a modified vesicular stomatitis virus (VSVΔG) backbone, contain a luciferase reporter gene, and bear the S glycoprotein of the SARS-CoV-2 variant of interest.26

Binding immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibody concentrations against SARS-CoV-2 S protein from WT and other variants were measured using a quantitative indirect binding electro-chemiluminescence assay based on the Meso Scale Discovery technology.

Cell-mediated immune (CMI) responses were evaluated by intracellular cytokine staining from peripheral blood mononuclear cells, as previously described,27,28 at baseline, and 1 and 2 weeks post-vaccination, in a subset of participants in study Part A. Briefly, peripheral blood mononuclear cells were stimulated in vitro, either with pools of 15-mer peptides overlapping by 11 amino acids and spanning the sequences of the S antigens (WT or variant(s)) or with medium as negative control. After 2 hours of incubation at 37°C, brefeldin A was added to inhibit cytokine secretion for an additional overnight incubation. Cells were subsequently harvested, stained for surface markers (cluster of differentiation [CD] 4 and CD8 together with a viability dye) and fixed. Fixed cells were then permeabilized and stained with labeled antibodies specific for the following immune markers: CD40L, interleukin (IL)-2, tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α, interferon (IFN)-γ, IL-13, IL-17, and 4-1BB and analyzed by flow-cytometry. The intracellular cytokine staining assay output is the quantification of vaccine-induced SARS-CoV-2 S-specific polypositive CD4+ and CD8+ T-cells expressing two or more markers among CD40L, IL-2, TNF-α, IFN-γ, IL-13, IL-17, or 4-1BB and including at least one cytokine (among IFN-γ, IL-2, TNF-α, IL-17). The frequency was expressed as the number of all polypositive cells per 106 T-cells and obtained by summing the frequency of associated S protein subunits or peptide pools: S1-WU and S2-WU for the WT antigen, P1-O-bA.1 and P2-O-bA.1 for Omicron BA.1, and P1-O-bA.5 and P2-O-bA.5 for Omicron BA.5. In addition, the frequency of antigen-specific CD4+ T helper (Th) cells expressing at least IFN-γ, at least IL-13 or at least IL-17 was also reported as a readout of Th1, Th2, and Th17 responses, respectively.

Statistical analyses

With 30 participants in each of the five groups ≥12 µg, there was a 78.5% probability to observe at least one AE for an incidence rate of 5% and a 95.8% probability to observe at least one AE for an incidence rate of 10%. With a 10% unevaluable rate for immunogenicity results and a standard deviation of 0.45 for log10-transformed increase from baseline, the ratio of the upper limit of a 2-sided 95% CI and the point estimate of geometric mean increase (GMI) was 1.5. The sample size in the groups in Part B of the study (3 µg and 6 µg) was based on clinical feasibility; with 15 participants in each group, there was a 79.4% probability to observe at least one AE for a true incidence rate of 10%.

Safety analyses were conducted on the safety set, including all participants who received a CV0501 dose. The percentage of participants with solicited and unsolicited AEs, SAEs, MAAEs, and AESIs were computed. All analyses were descriptive.

Immunogenicity analyses were conducted on the per-protocol immunogenicity set, including all eligible participants who received a dose of CV0501 per protocol and who had data for serum neutralizing titers against pseudovirus bearing S protein from the Omicron BA.1 variant at baseline and 14 days post-vaccination, and had no important protocol deviation impacting immunogenicity data.

Geometric means of serum neutralizing titers (GMTs) and GMIs (defined as the ratio of post-vaccination titers to pre-vaccination titers) against pseudovirus bearing the S protein from SARS-CoV-2 WT, Omicron (BA.1, BA.2 and BA.5) and Delta variants, and binding IgG antibody geometric mean concentrations (GMCs) against the S protein were calculated at each timepoint. GMT/Cs and GMIs were calculated from log-transformed values, with 95% CIs calculated using the t-distribution of log-transformed values, then back transformed. Neutralizing seroresponse rates, defined as ≥ 4-fold increase in titers compared to baseline or post-booster titer ≥ 4 times the lower limit of quantification in participants with baseline titers below the lower limit of quantification, were estimated at day 29 post-vaccination. The proportion of participants with neutralizing seroresponse rates against SARS-CoV-2 WT, Omicron (BA.1, BA.2, BA.5), and Delta variants was tabulated with 2-sided 95% Clopper Pearson CIs, at day 29.

The geometric mean frequency of SARS-CoV-2 S-specific CD4+/CD8+ T-cells was calculated by taking the anti-log of the mean of the log frequency transformations. The 95% CI for the geometric mean was obtained by taking the anti-log of the 95% CI of the mean of log frequency transformations. Box plots of CMI response for cytokines by dose group, parameter, and timepoint were produced.

All analyses were performed with SAS v9.4.

Unsolicited AEs were infrequent and were reported by a total of 14.4% of the participants across groups, ranging from 6.7% in the 6 μg, 12 μg, and 25 μg groups to 26.7% in the 3 μg and 200 μg groups (Table 2). COVID-19 was the most common unsolicited AE reported by eight (4.4%) participants across groups during the 28-day follow-up period. During the 6-month follow-up period, COVID-19 was reported by 15 (8.3%) participants across all cohorts (Table 2). Of note, COVID-19 cases were the only AESIs reported, and all were of mild or moderate intensity. Most (88.5%) unsolicited AEs were mild to moderate in nature. Grade 3 unsolicited AEs were reported by three (1.7%) participants in the safety set (Table 2); one participant in the 50 µg group reported cholecystitis (considered as an SAE), one participant in the 25 µg group had a hand fracture, and one participant in the 50 µg group had hypertension.

Across both age groups, four unsolicited AEs considered by the investigator as related to the study vaccine were reported by three (1.7%) participants (Table 2); one participant in the 200 µg group reported two events, i.e., administration site pain and injection site erythema with onset day 9 and 10, respectively, post-vaccination, one participant in the 25 µg group reported vomiting with onset day 1 post-vaccination, and one participant in the 50 µg group reported hypertension with onset day 26 post-vaccination, not requiring medical attention. This participant had risk factors for high blood pressure: overweight, ≥65 years of age, and elevated blood pressure prior to vaccination, supporting an undiagnosed preexisting history of hypertension.

All but one unsolicited AEs were recovered/resolved by study end. The unresolved unsolicited AE was an exacerbation of allergic rhinitis in a participant from the 100 μg group of moderate intensity and considered as not related to the study vaccine.

SAEs for hospitalizations were reported by three (1.7%) participants, one participant in the 50 μg group (grade 3 cholecystitis mentioned above) and two participants in the 100 μg group (diarrhea and syncope); none of these were considered related to vaccination (Table 2), and all were recovered/resolved by study end.

MAAEs were reported by 16.7% of the participants, ranging from 6.7% in the 25 µg group to 30.0% in the 12 µg group (Table 2); most of these (86.7%) were mild to moderate in severity. Severe MAAEs were reported by four (2.2%) participants, one in the 25 µg, one in the 50 µg group, and two in the 100 µg group.

No potential immune-mediated disorders, myocarditis/pericarditis, or anaphylaxis were reported in any of the cohorts. There were no fatal SAEs.

Immunogenicity

In all groups, CV0501 induced increased serum neutralizing GMTs from baseline throughout all post-vaccination timepoints and against all tested variants. GMTs were highest at day 15 and subsequently decreased, but overall remained higher than pre-boost levels at day 181. Serum neutralizing GMTs tended to be higher against the WT than against the other variants. Overall, at all timepoints, GMIs were highest for the Omicron BA.1 subvariant, ranging from 3.72 to 23.51 at day 15 and from 1.39 to 6.30 at day 181. A trend toward a dose effect was observed against all tested variants, with the neutralizing response increasing with the dose level (Figure 3). Similar observations were made for serum neutralizing GMTs adjusted for baseline values (Table S1). Discussion

In this phase 1 study, a booster dose with CV0501 was well tolerated and associated with mild to moderate reactogenicity in adults previously vaccinated with at least two doses of a COVID-19 vaccine. Doses from 3 μg to 200 μg of CV0501 induced immune responses not only against the vaccine-matched Omicron BA.1 variant but also against the SARS-CoV-2 WT, other Omicron subvariants, and the Delta variant.

The vaccine was well tolerated. After a booster dose of CV0501, the majority (98.1%) of local solicited AEs were mild to moderate. Severe local AEs (redness and swelling) were only observed in two (1.1%) adults following the administration of a single 200 μg dose. Similarly, the majority of solicited systemic AEs (96.7%) were mild to moderate. Overall, the reactogenicity profile, with symptoms including mostly fatigue, headache, myalgia, and limited fever, is likely related to the induction of the innate immune response and is consistent with that reported for other mRNA SARS-CoV-2 vaccines.29–31 Unsolicited AEs related to the study vaccine (1.7%) were limited.

After a CV0501 booster dose, all dose levels elicited an increase in neutralization responses against not only the vaccine matched Omicron BA.1 variant but also against SARS-CoV-2 WT, Delta variant, and other Omicron subvariants at all post-vaccination timepoints compared to baseline. Serum neutralizing GMTs were highest at day 15 post-vaccination. A trend toward a dose-dependent effect, more pronounced at day 15 and 29 post-vaccination was observed for all tested variants. For all dose-levels and across timepoints, the GMIs from baseline to post-vaccination tended to be higher for the Omicron BA.1 subvariant than for the other tested variants. Neutralizing seroresponse rates at day 29 post-vaccination also showed a trend toward dose-effect for dose levels from 12 μg to 200 μg and tended to be higher for Omicron subvariants compared to the other variants. These results suggest that a booster dose of CV0501 can induce a cross-neutralization response, consistent with findings in small animal models.25 Our results are also in line with clinical data for other modified mRNA vaccines, including vaccines based on the BA.1 variant.30,32,33 In addition, the induced immune responses seemed improved when compared with those elicited by the first-generation candidate vaccine, CVnCoV33,34 that showed efficacy against non-vaccine matched variants.18

A booster dose of CV0501 also induced CMI responses against WT, BA.1, and BA.5 variants, with a trend for higher frequencies of polypositive CD4+ in contrast to CD8+ T-cells, in line with previous reports indicating different magnitudes and kinetics for CD4+ and CD8+ CMI responses.35,36 Polypositive CD4+ cellular immune responses were increased compared to baseline at day 8 and 15 post-vaccination, suggesting a booster effect; however, this was not observed for CD8+ responses. Our findings of increased CD4+ T-cells expressing at least IFN-γ are consistent with the results of a recent study that showed that Th1 CD4+ cell responses increased post-booster vaccination with an mRNA vaccine and remained above baseline levels up to 6 months.37

Our study has several limitations due to the small sample size for each group and the open-label design. The trend toward lower immune responses observed for the 25 μg dose level may be due to the low sample size and not indicate a true biological effect. The trial was conducted during the COVID-19 pandemic when comparator COVID-19 vaccines could not be easily procured for use in clinical trials.38 Despite these limitations, the study provides compelling data to support the safety and immunogenicity of the vaccine.

Based on this, a phase 2 study of vaccine candidates using the same technology is currently ongoing (NCT05960097).

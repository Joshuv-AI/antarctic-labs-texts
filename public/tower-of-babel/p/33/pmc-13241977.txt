Acute Neurologic Decline in a Patient With a Headache
Authors: Jacquelyn Pearlmutter, Dillon Warr
Published: 2026

Case Presentation

A middle-aged woman with hypertension presented with 5 days of progressive occipital-to-frontal headache that acutely worsened 30 minutes before arrival. She denied trauma, fever, neck pain, visual changes, focal deficits, or anticoagulant use. Blood pressure was 173/89 mm Hg, and neurologic examination was initially nonfocal. After transient improvement with migraine-directed therapy, she developed vomiting, dizziness, and gait instability. Computed tomography angiography of the head and neck (Figs 1 and 2) demonstrated acute intraventricular hemorrhage involving the lateral, third, and fourth ventricles without aneurysm or vascular malformation.

Axial computed tomography. demonstrating hyperdense material within the lateral ventricle (arrow).

Sagittal computed tomography. demonstrating intraventricular hemorrhage involving the lateral (red arrow), third (black arrow), and fourth (white arrow) ventricles.

Diagnosis

Primary Intraventricular Hemorrhage

This uncommon form of nontraumatic intracranial hemorrhage (∼3%) originates within the ventricular system without parenchymal bleeding.1 Hypertension is the most common risk factor, although vascular malformations, coagulopathy, and tumors may contribute.2, 3, 4 Morbidity results from obstruction of cerebrospinal fluid flow, leading to acute hydrocephalus and elevated intracranial pressure, often without focal deficits. Clinical deterioration may be abrupt or preceded by transient improvement, as in this case.2, 3, 4 Emergency management includes rapid computed tomography diagnosis, blood pressure control, reversal of anticoagulation when indicated, and prompt neurosurgical consultation for consideration of external ventricular drain placement.5

This patient became progressively somnolent, required intubation, and underwent emergent external ventricular drain placement. She was discharged after a prolonged hospitalization with mild residual diplopia and gait instability.

Adjunctive rifampicin–ofloxacin therapy in histoid Hansen disease with high bacillary burden: a case report
Authors: Vangala Naga Nitya, Adarshlata Singh, Bhushan Sevakaram Madke, Ambika Kondalkar, Varun H
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHistoid Hansen disease is a distinct variant of lepromatous leprosy characterized by nodular lesions, spindle-shaped histiocytes and a very high bacillary load, often associated with relapse and drug resistance. While standard multibacillary multidrug therapy (MB-MDT) remains the mainstay of treatment, very short-course bactericidal regimens appear inadequate in bacillary-rich disease. We report a case of histoid leprosy treated with adjunctive rifampicin and ofloxacin for 3 months alongside MB-MDT, resulting in marked clinical regression. Although causality cannot be inferred from a single case, this observation suggests a potential role for short-term intensified bactericidal therapy as an adjunct in selected high-risk morphological variants.

Histoid leprosy is a distinct clinicopathological variant within the lepromatous spectrum of leprosy, first described by Wade in 1963.1 Clinically, it presents as well-defined, firm, skin-coloured to erythematous papules and nodules arising on apparently healthy skin, often distributed symmetrically and progressively increasing in number and size. Histopathologically, it is characterized by well-circumscribed dermal nodules composed of spindle-shaped histiocytes arranged in a storiform pattern, typically separated from the epidermis by a grenz zone.1,2 Unlike classical lepromatous leprosy, histoid lesions show minimal foamy change and frequently harbour abundant viable Mycobacterium leprae, resulting in very high bacterial indices.2,3

Histoid leprosy is clinically significant due to its association with high bacillary load, increased risk of relapse and potential drug resistance, particularly in patients with prior inadequate or irregular therapy.3–5 Additionally, transepidermal elimination of bacilli has been described, suggesting a possible role in ongoing disease transmission.4,5 These features support the concept of histoid leprosy as a high-risk bacillary phenotype, in which bacteriological clearance may be slower with standard therapy alone.

While World Health Organization (WHO)-recommended multibacillary multidrug therapy (MB-MDT) remains the standard of care, very short-course bactericidal regimens have proven inadequate in bacillary-rich disease.6,7 Given the potent bactericidal activity of rifampicin and the additional activity of ofloxacin against M. leprae,8,9 short-term adjunctive rifampicin–ofloxacin therapy alongside MB-MDT represents a biologically plausible strategy for therapeutic intensification in selected high-risk cases. We report a case illustrating this approach.

Case report

A man in his late fifties presented with multiple asymptomatic, skin-coloured, raised lesions over the trunk for 10 months, with progressive involvement of the face, ears, trunk and all four limbs over the preceding 4 months. He reported persistent hoarseness of voice for 6 months, suggestive of laryngeal involvement (Video S1; see Supporting Information), along with gradually progressive tingling and numbness of the upper and lower limbs. There was a history of occasional epistaxis and difficulty retaining footwear, suggestive of sensory impairment. He had been diagnosed with Hansen disease at a primary health centre and had been receiving WHO-recommended MB-MDT for 2 months, with mild subjective sensory improvement but no appreciable change in cutaneous lesions.

Cutaneous examination revealed multiple well-defined, shiny nodules over the trunk and extremities, with several showing central crusting and ulceration. Smaller nodules were noted over the helix and antihelix of both ears. Peripheral nerve examination demonstrated thickening of the bilateral ulnar and popliteal nerves, with glove-and-stocking hypoesthaesia (Figure 1).

Slit-skin smears from the ear lobule, pinna and eyebrow demonstrated numerous acid-fast bacilli arranged in clumps and globi, consistent with a high bacteriological (6+) and morphological index (Figure 2). Histopathological examination revealed a thinned epidermis with a subepidermal grenz zone and a well-circumscribed dermal infiltrate composed predominantly of spindle-shaped histiocytes arranged in intersecting bundles, without granuloma formation, consistent with histoid morphology (Figure 3).

Slit-skin smear stained by the modified Ziehl–Neelsen technique shows abundant fragmented acid-fast bacilli arranged in multiple clusters and well-defined globi, set against a pale blue background with a predominance of mononuclear (macrophage) inflammatory cells. These findings indicate a high bacillary burden (bacteriological index 6+) with a markedly elevated morphological index (95%). The image was obtained at ×100 magnification.

Based on clinical, bacteriological and histopathological findings, a diagnosis of nodular histoid leprosy within the lepromatous spectrum was established. As the patient was already on MB-MDT, adjunctive rifampicin (600 mg) and ofloxacin (400 mg) were administered once daily in the morning for a duration of 3 months, in view of the histoid morphology and high bacillary burden. Oral prednisolone (0.5 mg kg–1 daily) was added to mitigate lepra reactions and tapered gradually. No significant adverse effects were observed during treatment or in the immediate post-treatment period. During follow-up, clinical assessment at 3 months demonstrated flattening and partial regression of nodular lesions, with healing of ulcerated areas. The patient was followed up for a total duration of 6 months, during which continued improvement was observed without evidence of relapse.

Discussion

Histoid Hansen disease is a distinctive clinicopathological variant within the lepromatous spectrum, characterized by nodular lesions composed of spindle-shaped histiocytes arranged in a storiform pattern and separated from the epidermis by a grenz zone. It is recognized as a bacillary-rich form associated with a high bacterial index and increased risk of relapse.1–4 The findings in the present case are consistent with classical descriptions and reflect an altered interaction between the host and the bacillus.

Histopathologically, variations beyond the classical spindle-cell pattern have been described, including lesions with mixed cellular infiltrates and atypical bacillary arrangements. The bacilli are often elongated and arranged in parallel bundles or globi (histoid habitus), highlighting the importance of clinicopathological correlation.10–13

Clinically, histoid leprosy carries important implications due to its high bacillary burden and association with relapse and drug resistance, particularly in inadequately treated patients.2–5 These features support its classification as a high-risk bacillary phenotype, in which bacteriological clearance may be delayed with standard therapy alone.

Current treatment strategies are based on WHO-recommended MB-MDT, which remains the cornerstone of management.6 However, bacteriological decline is often slow in bacillary-rich variants, and clinical improvement may precede measurable reduction in bacterial index. Evidence from clinical studies indicates that very short-course bactericidal regimens are inadequate in multibacillary disease, with high relapse rates reported in such settings.7 In this context, adjunctive rifampicin–ofloxacin therapy alongside MB-MDT represents a biologically plausible strategy for therapeutic intensification, given the potent bactericidal activity of rifampicin and the complementary action of ofloxacin.8,9

In the present case, adjunctive therapy was administered due to extensive disease and high bacterial index, with early clinical regression observed over 3 months, despite the expected slow decline in bacteriological indices. This supports a potential role for intensified early bactericidal exposure in selected high-risk cases.

As a single case, definitive conclusions regarding efficacy ­cannot be drawn. Nevertheless, this report highlights the ­potential utility of individualized therapeutic intensification in bacillary-rich histoid leprosy, warranting further systematic evaluation.

As a single case, this report cannot establish the efficacy or optimal duration of adjunctive rifampicin–ofloxacin therapy, nor clearly distinguish its effect from standard MB-MDT. Nevertheless, it supports consideration of individualized therapeutic intensification in bacillary-rich histoid leprosy and highlights the need for systematic evaluation of adjunctive bactericidal strategies in high-risk, relapse-prone disease.

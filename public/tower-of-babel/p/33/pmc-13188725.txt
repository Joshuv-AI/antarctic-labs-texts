Integrin α2: mode of regulation and functioning in the metastatic cancer cascade
Authors: Lu Wang, Mengxuan Sun, Hanci Zhu, Hualiang Deng, Sichao Zhang, Changgang Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIntegrin α2β1, a major collagen-binding receptor, functions as a key mediator of cell-extracellular matrix (ECM) communication, mechanosensing, and adhesion-dependent signaling. As a core molecular switch in cancer biology, integrin α2 profoundly influences the metastatic cascade—from local invasion, intravasation, survival in circulation, and pre-metastatic niche formation to dormancy and colonization. Although extensive studies indicate that integrin α2β1 regulates tumor proliferation, migration, ECM remodeling, angiogenesis, and immune modulation, its mechanochemical activation mechanisms and context-dependent functions across different stages of metastasis remain incompletely defined.Main bodyThis review systematically summarizes the structural characteristics, activation mechanisms, regulatory networks, and functional roles of integrin α2β1 in the cancer metastatic cascade. We first describe the domain architecture of integrin α2, emphasizing the structural determinants underlying ligand specificity and metal ion-dependent adhesion. We then outline multi-level regulatory mechanisms that drive variable integrin α2 expression across tumor types. The dual biochemical and mechanical modes of integrin α2β1 activation are discussed, with emphasis on nanocluster formation, focal adhesion maturation, force-dependent conformational changes, and key downstream signaling pathways. We further dissect the stage-specific contributions of integrin α2β1 at each stage of metastasis, including its contributions to epithelial-mesenchymal transition, matrix metalloproteinase - mediated ECM degradation, vascular remodeling, circulating tumor cell survival, immune evasion, pre-metastatic niche formation, tumor dormancy, and organotropic colonization. Finally, we review current progress in integrin α2-targeted therapies—including small-molecule inhibitors, monoclonal antibodies, natural compounds, and nanomedicine-based delivery systems—and discuss future directions that leverage mechanobiology, integrin-targeted biomaterials, and computational drug design.ConclusionsIntegrin α2β1 is a central mechanochemical regulator of the metastatic cascade, coordinating tumor cell responses to extracellular and intracellular cues. Its functions span critical metastatic processes, making integrin α2 and its downstream pathways promising therapeutic targets. Continued advances in mechanobiology and multi-omics technologies will be essential to refine integrin-centered strategies and improve interventions against metastatic cancer.

Introduction

The integrin family, as key transmembrane signaling hubs, functions as molecular switches. The complex process of metastasis, the most lethal biological feature of malignant tumours, has always been a central proposition in the field of cancer research [1]. Classical theories [2] summarise the metastatic cascade into seven key stages: local invasion, intravasation, circulation, pre-metastatic niche formation, extravasation, dormancy, and metastatic colonization. During this process, tumor cells acquire invasive capabilities through epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), which enables them to breach the basement membrane and enter the bloodstream as circulating tumour cells (CTCs) [3, 4]. These CTCs face various selective pressures, including hemodynamic shear stress, immune surveillance, and resistance to anoikis, and only a few survive through biomechanical adaptation. The surviving cells extravasate into the target organ as disseminated tumor cells (DTCs) [5, 6]. Notably, DTCs undergo epigenetic reprogramming and metabolic adjustments to enter a state of mitotic dormancy [7], and maintain viability until they are reactivated by microenvironmental signals, such as inflammation or angiogenesis. This reactivation triggers proliferation programs, which leads to the formation of macrometastases [8]. This multi-stage, dynamic evolutionary process is governed by a complex mechano-chemical signalling network, in which the integrin family proteins, as key transmembrane signaling hubs, occupy a central position as molecular switches [9].

The integrin family consists of 18 alpha subunits and 8 beta subunits that combine to form 24 distinct heterodimers through specific binding interactions. Based on their ligand-binding specificity, integrins can be classified into four main groups: collagen-binding integrins, arginine-glycine-aspartate (RGD) receptor integrins, laminin-binding integrins, and leukocyte-specific integrins [10]. Integrins are the primary way in which cells sense and respond to their microenvironment and regulate cell behaviour involved in development, tissue homeostasis [11], immunodeficiency [12], inflammatory diseases [13] and tumour-related pathophysiology. As core mediators for cells to sense their microenvironment, integrins regulate biological behaviours such as cell adhesion, migration, proliferation and survival through bidirectional signaling mechanisms. Their aberrant expression is closely related to tumourigenesis and development.

The function of integrins is related to collagen, the most abundant ECM component in the tumor microenvironment [14, 15]. Some integrins [16] (e.g., integrins α1β1, α2β1, α10β1, α11β1) are critical for tumour cell adhesion, migration and signalling by recognising and binding to collagen, and much current research focuses on integrin α2β1 [17].

Integrin α2β1, the only heterodimeric form of the integrin α2 subunit, primarily functions on the basis of structural domain characteristics and expression levels of integrin α2. Considerable evidence suggests that integrin α2 is strongly implicated in tumour metastasis [17, 18]. However, its molecular regulatory network within the metastatic cascade remains incompletely understood. How does the α-structural domain of integrin α2 sense microenvironmental mechanosignals and translate them into biochemical signals? In addition, the specific mechanism of integrin α2’s role in metastatic cascade processes such as pre-metastatic ecological niche formation needs to be further elucidated.

This article delves into the protein structural domains, mechanisms of activation, regulation of expression, and multiple biological functions of integrin α2 in the metastatic cascade. The aim is to systematically elucidate the functional contribution of integrin α2 to tumor metastasis and provide a theoretical basis for the development of ITGA2-based molecular targeted therapies.

Structure and biological function

The integrin α2 subunit by itself has limited functionality and can only perform its full biological function if it binds to the β1 subunit via non-covalent bonding to form a complete α2β1 integrin molecule (Fig. 1).

Structurally, the integrin α2β1 receptor is characterized by typical integrin family features, consisting of two transmembrane glycoprotein subunits. Each subunit contains a prominent extracellular domain, a transmembrane domain, and a relatively short intracellular domain [19]. Integrin α2β1 is a member of the ‘I-domain’ integrin subfamily. The extracellular domain of the α2 subunit contains an autonomously folded structural domain of about 220 amino acid residues, the I domain [20], which is responsible for recognition of and binding to ligands, especially collagen. The β1 subunit also has a similar ligand recognition domain, which compensates as a ligand recognition domain in the absence of the I domain in the α subunit. Within the I domain, a highly conserved metal ion-dependent adhesion site (MIDAS) [21] mediates the binding of divalent cations such as Mg²⁺ and Mn²⁺, which are essential for accurate collagen recognition. The β1 subunit, on the other hand, has two metal ion-dependent adhesion sites, MIDAS and adjacent to MIDAS (ADMIDAS) [22]. The ADMIDAS region normally binds to Ca²⁺, which inhibits its activity; however, binding to Mn²⁺ induces a conformational rearrangement of the integrin that transforms it into an activated state.

Integrin α2β1 is a crucial cell surface receptor involved in various physiological processes. It mediates cell-matrix interactions by binding to multiple ECM ligands, such as collagen [23], laminin [24], and proteoglycans (e.g., opticin [25], perlecan [26], endorepellin [27], and decorin [28]). During embryonic development, integrin α2β1 regulates cellular behaviours, such as cell adhesion, directed migration and mitosis, that are involved in tissue morphogenesis and organogenesis. In maintaining tissue homeostasis, integrin α2β1 functions as a collagen receptor, contributing to collagen deposition and remodeling, and promotes tissue repair and regeneration through downstream signaling pathways after an injury. In hemostasis, integrin α2β1 specifically recognizes von Willebrand factor (vWF), and facilitates platelet activation, aggregation, and thrombus stabilization [29]. Integrin α2β1 interacts with certain complement system components (e.g., C1q and collectin family proteins) and participates in intrinsic and adaptive immune responses by regulating the activation and migration of immune cells (e.g., T cells and macrophages) [30]. In addition, the receptor serves as a cell surface receptor for certain viruses (e.g., human rotavirus A [31] and human echoviruses 1 and 8 [32]) and is thus implicated in the process of viral infection.

Expression and function in different cancer types

Integrin α2 (CD49b), encoded by the ITGA2 gene located at chromosome 5q11.2, is predominantly expressed in fibroblasts, endothelial cells, hematopoietic cells and epithelial cells [18]. Studies have demonstrated that ITGA2 expression is linked to the development, metastasis, and poor prognosis of various cancers. Intricate mechanisms at multiple levels govern ITGA2 expression and function, as summarized in Table 1. These mechanisms include transcriptional regulation, primarily through single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), transcription factors, and epigenetic modifications; post-transcriptional regulation, largely mediated by non-coding RNAs; and post-translational modifications. A comprehensive analysis of the data reveals that ITGA2 expression is particularly associated with cancer metastasis.

Activation modes and metastasis

Integrin α2β1 serves as a primary molecular connection between cells and the ECM and functions as a bidirectional hub for signal transmission between cells and their environment [54]. Its activity is regulated by both biochemical and mechanical properties [55], that enable it to sense and respond to mechanical and biochemical activating signals, thus facilitating tumor metastasis.

Biochemical signal activation

Biochemical activation of integrin α2β1 is a multistep regulatory process that consists of the following three main phases: (1) ligand binding and initial activation; (2) formation of integrin clusters; and (3) assembly of mature focal adhesions (Fig. 2).

First, the ligand binding affinity of integrins is precisely regulated by inside-out signalling pathways. The intracellular bridging protein Talin binds to the cytoplasmic tail of the integrin β-subunit and induces a conformational change in the α/β subunit, which in turn triggers a high-affinity state in the extracellular structural domain [54]. Integrin α2β1 specifically recognises GFOGER-like sequences, a conserved sequence that is present in a wide range of ECM molecules such as collagen, fibronectin, laminin and osteoblastin. The concentration-dependent agonistic and antagonistic behaviours of integrins reflect the dual part of ligand concentration in the regulation of their function: low concentrations of ligand may inhibit integrin activation, whereas high concentrations promote their recruitment and cell adhesion, a property that is particularly critical during tumor metastasis [56, 57]. Recently, researchers have designed a gold-iron oxide-based hierarchical nanostructure whose surface ligand arrangement can be remotely switched by an external magnetic field. In the absence of a magnetic field, the ligands show a highly anisotropic distribution that promotes integrin aggregation and tumor cell adhesion; whereas, after the application of a magnetic field, the spatial distribution of the ligands reconfigures to a low anisotropic statethat blocks integrin signalling and inhibits cell migration and invasion [58].

Second, upon ligand binding, integrin α2β1 forms nanoclusters through intermolecular interactions. These nanoclusters serve as precursor structures for adhesion sites and mark the transition of integrins from a dispersed to a functionally aggregated state. The formation of nanoclusters not only increases the local concentration of integrins but also provides a molecular scaffold for the subsequent hierarchical assembly of signalling complexes. Compared to individual receptors, nanoclusters exhibit increased signalling affinity, sensitivity, and robustness, while also facilitating rapid signal degradation, thus contributing to dynamic cellular responses [59]. Remarkably, active and inactive integrins are not randomly mixed but precisely segregated into different nanoclusters. This observation unveils a novel regulatory mechanism of integrin activity: rather than being independently regulated at the individual molecule level, the active state of integrins may be collectively modulated through local molecular coordination within nanoclusters (e.g., conformational synchronization or co-receptor interactions) [60]. This spatial segregation mechanism holds dual significance for tumor metastasis. On one hand, active integrin nanoclusters recruit downstream effector proteins that promote cell spreading and migration. On the other hand, disruption of microcluster formation significantly impedes invasive pseudopod extension and trans-matrix migration of tumor cells and thereby blocks the metastatic cascade [61].

Finally, nanoclusters progressively mature into focal adhesions (FAs) via recruitment of critical proteins, including Talin, Vinculin, and Paxillin. This maturation process is highly complex, and a variety of adhesion complexes with different structures and functions can be formed, including nascent adhesions, focal adhesions, and fibrillar adhesions [62, 63]. Notably, the various types of adhesion complexes differ significantly in size, morphology and molecular composition that reflect their specific biological functions. The turnover of FAs, especially via the process of integrin recirculation, is a key step in depolymerisation and activation of adhesion plaques, which is essential for cancer cell metastasis [64, 65].

After maturation of FAs, mechanical signals are converted into chemical signals by recruiting and activating key signalling molecules such as FAK [66], Paxillin [67], SRC [68] and ERK [69]. These signalling cascade responses significantly promote local infiltration and distant metastasis of tumors by regulating a variety of biological behaviours such as cell migration, proliferation and survival, thus becoming a key driver of tumor metastasis.

Mechanical signal activation

Current studies on the regulation of integrin activation by mechanical signals focus mainly on three core dimensions of RGD-type integrin receptors: (1) the regulation of ligand binding kinetics by mechanical forces; (2) the mechanism of integrin conformational activation; and (3) the kinetics of integrin microcluster formation and diffusion [54]. The mechanical regulation of integrins has significant subtype specificity. By comparing the functional differences of integrin isoforms such as α5β1, αvβ3 [70], α2β1 [71], and αvβ6 [72], researchers have found that this specificity is an important molecular basis for the precise response of cells to mechanical signals. However, there is still a lack of research on the mechanism for the mechanical activation of this particular isoform of integrin α2β1. The systematic elucidation of the mechanism of mechanical signal transduction and regulation of different types of integrins has become the focus of research.

Although integrin α2β1 is significantly less mechanosensitive than RGD-type integrins [73] due to the specific conformation of the αI structural domain, this receptor still possesses a unique mechanical response profile. Notably, integrin α2β1 specifically mediates the activation of cell adhesion to type I collagen under fluid shear, whereas it is mechanically inert in fibronectin or fibrinogen matrices [74], which suggests a strict ligand-dependent mechanical signalling. Mechanical coupling established by fibroblasts via integrin α2β1 is critical for extracellular matrix mechanosensing in the maintenance of homeostasis of the tissue microenvironment. Integrin α2 knockout models indicate that defective fibroblasts have approximately 40% reduced mechanical force generation capacity compared to wild type [75], and this phenotypic alteration is closely associated with abnormal spatial distribution of adhesion patches and dysregulation of the Rho-GTPase signalling pathway. Only one recent study suggests that the mechanoresponsive efficacy of integrin α2β1 is dually regulated by both ligand type and cell lineage. Particularly in the context of tumor heterogeneity, this receptor may be involved in tumor stromal remodelling processes through differential mechanical signal transduction. Therefore, systematic assessment of the constitutive relationship between mechanical parameters of the tumor microenvironment and the receptor will be key to elucidating its mechanobiological mechanisms.

As mentioned above, mechanical and biochemical signals can activate integrin α2β1 to participate in cancer metastasis. However, the factors influencing the role of this receptor in cancer metastasis still need to be explored.

Pro-metastatic regulatory mechanisms

Although integrin α2β1 has been identified as a suppressor of cancer metastasis [76], its pro-metastatic mechanisms remain the core focus of current research. As a molecular transducer, integrin can bind to ligands to promote cancer metastasis, and thus the ligand type exerts a profound impact on metastatic processes. Collagen serves as a classic ligand for integrin α2β1, with a particular affinity for type I and type IV collagen. Upon binding to and activating integrin α2β1, type I collagen can trigger RhoC GTPase activation to facilitate tumor metastasis [77–79]; it can also promote bone metastasis upon binding to integrin α2β1. Additionally, studies have demonstrated that the ligation of type I collagen to integrin α2β1 drives liver metastasis of breast cancer [80]. The fibrous arrangement of type I collagen can be sensed by integrin α2β1, which converts external physical structural signals into intracellular proliferative signals in tumor cells to enhance tumor invasion [81]. Binding to type IV collagen, by contrast, promotes liver metastasis of tumor cells [82, 83]. Beyond collagens, several anaerobic bacteria can also bind to integrin α2β1 to modulate tumor metastasis. For instance, Solobacterium moorei can bind to integrin α2β1 and trigger the intracellular PI3K-AKT-mTOR-C-myc signaling pathway, thereby promoting tumor progression [84]. Peptostreptococcus anaerobius can also interact with integrin α2β1 to activate the intracellular NF-κB signaling pathway, which induces tumor cells to secrete CXCL1 and recruit CXCR2-expressing myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) into the tumor microenvironment to accelerate tumor progression [85]. As a unique ligand, CDH17 has been found to bind to and activate integrin α2β1, thus facilitating tumor metastasis [86]. Laminin β1, another ligand of integrin α2β1, can activate the FAK/Src and ROCK2 signaling pathways to promote cytoskeletal reorganization, which directly drives tumor invasion and metastasis [87].

The pro-metastatic effects of integrin are also associated with cell lineage. As an important cell surface receptor, integrin α2β1 is expressed on immune cells, stromal cells and tumor cells. Current studies have revealed that immune cells can anchor to collagen via integrin α2β1 and cooperate with the immunosuppressive collagen receptor LAIR-1 to promote CD8 + T cell exhaustion, thereby enabling tumor cells to escape immune surveillance and facilitating tumor metastasis [88]. Cancer-associated fibroblasts secrete extracellular vesicles, which specifically recognize and bind to lung fibroblasts via integrin α2β1, and further trigger the intracellular TGF-β signaling pathway in lung fibroblasts to remodel the pulmonary microenvironment and promote tumor cell colonization [89]. Integrin α2β1 expressed on the surface of tumor cells itself exerts a direct pro-metastatic effect [85, 90–92].

Current research on integrin α2β1 has mainly focused on its pro-metastatic functions, yet the specific roles of integrin α2β1 in distinct stages of cancer metastasis remain unclear. Therefore, this study intends to systematically elucidate the functional regulatory mechanisms of integrin α2 in different tumor types across various stages of cancer metastasis from the dual perspectives of gene expression and functional complex formation.

Functional regulation in the cancer metastatic cascade response

Tumor cell metastasis is a complex multistep pathological process that involves the detachment of tumor cells from the primary site, their entry into the peripheral circulation, their passage through the vessel wall and finally the formation of metastatic foci in distant organs. In this series reactions, the function of integrin α2 significantly influences the metastatic potential of tumor cells (Figs. 3 and 4).

Local invasion

The initial step in the metastatic cascade of cancer involves the invasion of tumor cells into adjacent tissues. This process is characterized by the detachment of tumor cells from the primary site, followed by their invasion and penetration of the basement membrane and surrounding mesenchymal tissues. Integrin α2β1 facilitates local invasion of tumor cells by promoting sustained proliferation of tumor cells, helping tumor cells to detach from the primary tumor site and acquire an invasive phenotype.

The abnormal proliferation and expansion of tumor cells increase their contact area with surrounding tissues, providing a quantitative basis for invasion. This process is accompanied by abnormal deposition, remodeling, and enhanced cross-linking of the ECM, generating intrinsic stress [93, 94]and altering the biomechanical properties of the tumor microenvironment. These mechanical signals promote the maturation of adhesion plaques and induce the RhoA/ROCK pathway through the activation of integrins and FAK, ultimately enhancing the local invasive capacity of tumors [95]. One study found that the proliferation and invasion of hepatocellular carcinoma cells are controlled by a fine-tuned network between α2β1-FAK signaling, the AKT-mTOR pathway, and the CDK-Cyclin axis [96]. The expression level of ITGA2 is also closely related to the disordered proliferative capacity of tumor cells: The study found that ITGA2 can also promote cancer cell proliferation by influencing the TGF-β pathway [97], the FAK/AKT pathway [98], and the COL1A1-ITGA2 signalling pathway [99], which further demonstrates that ITGA2 drives tumorigenesis.

In cancer, the destruction of the basement membrane marks the transition from carcinoma in situ to invasive carcinoma [100]. During this process, some tumor cells become ‘restless’ and detach from the primary tumor by secreting matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), such as MMP2, MMP7, MMP9 and membrane type-1 (MT1-MMP), which lead to the destruction and remodelling of the basement membrane and ECM. Integrin α2β1 can regulate the invasive ability of tumor cells by influencing the secretion of MMPs. In PCa, the reorganisation and aggregation of integrin α2 subunit was shown to activate the FAK/src/paxillin/Rac/JNK signalling pathway [101], which led to an increase in MMP-2 and MMP-9 activity. In hepatocellular carcinoma, integrin α2β1 binds to collagen IV, inhibits MST1 and LATS1—core kinases of the Hippo pathway—and activates YAP-driven transcription, thereby upregulating MMP-7 expression [102]. MCF10A human breast epithelial cells enhanced the binding of C-reactive protein to FcγRI and integrin α2 through autocrine effects, further upregulating MMP-9 expression [34]. Conversely, inhibition of integrin α2β1 promotes its binding to MT1-MMP, which reduces MT1-MMP internalization and subsequently decreases cell invasiveness [103].

Cancer cells can hijack the EMT program to acquire mesenchymal characteristics, enhance stem cell-like properties, and enhance migration [104]. ITGA2 primarily induces EMT by activating downstream pathways: in gliomas, its overexpression activates STAT3 phosphorylation to initiate EMT [105]; in BC, ITGA2 mediates direct interaction between platelets and breast cancer cells. It promotes EMT by activating the Wnt-β-catenin pathway and the downstream autocrine TGF-β1/pSmad3 signaling [106]; in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, it induces EMT through the FAK/AKT phosphorylation cascade [107]. Snail indirectly regulates pathways involved in EMT. For instance, in colorectal cancer, collagen deposition activates the integrin-triggered PI3K/AKT pathway, upregulates Snail, and inhibits E-cadherin to induce EMT. whereas in prostate cancer, Snail downregulation upregulates ITGA2 through negative regulation of the MAPK pathway, thereby promoting EMT [108, 109].

Cancer cells can invade blood vessels or lymphatic vessels in clusters or bundles, with integrin α2β1 regulating their individual and collective cell migration. On one hand, alterations in the mechanical properties of the ECM can activate the effector cell YAP signaling pathway [110], promoting periostein secretion, thereby enhancing collagen cross-linking and increasing the mechanical stiffness of the tumor microenvironment, forming a positive feedback loop. On the other hand, integrin α2β1 and related proteins can perceive ECM mechanical signals, potentially triggering the integrin/FAK/ERK/Cdc42/Rac1 pathway to facilitate the invasion of BC cells [111].

Intravasation and extravasation

Cancer cells enter the circulatory system through intravasation and, under appropriate conditions, subsequently migrate from the vasculature into the extravascular stroma through the process of extravasation. Through multiple mechanisms, integrin α2β1 can regulate the entry and exit of locally invasive cancer cells into and out of the vascular lumen.

During the intravasation phase, tumor cells cross the endothelial layer to enter the circulation. This process typically involves the disruption of vascular integrity, including the degradation of the basement membrane and endothelial barrier, resulting in increased vascular permeability that facilitates tumor cell invasion. The sclerotic ECM activates the mechanosensor integrin α2β1 on the surface of tumor cells and induces the formation of invasive pseudopods [112, 113]. These pseudopods are specialized, actin-rich protrusions that enable tumor cells to release proteases, such as MMPs, that efficiently degrade the ECM, thereby promoting the entry of tumor cells into circulation.

In addition, integrin α2β1 promotes endothelial cell proliferation, regulates angiogenesis [13], and increases vascular permeability, which facilitates cancer cell intravasation and extravasation, thereby accelerating tumor spread. For instance, in BC, integrin α2β1 induces the expression of VEGF in endothelial cells and promotes their proliferation via the activation of the MAPK/ERK signaling pathway [114]. Furthermore, the balance between integrin α2β1 and integrin α1β1 is essential for maintaining angiogenic homeostasis [115]. During angiogenesis, the permeability of the neovasculature increases and interstitial fluid pressure rises [116]. Subsequently, this subtle mechanical signal can be captured by integrin α2β1 that through a series of signalling cascade reactions, ultimately mediates the process of vascular intravasation and extravasation of tumor cells.

Circulation

Primary tumors release CTCs into the bloodstream during exfoliation. These CTCs encounter numerous survival challenges in circulation, including shear stress, anoikis, and immune surveillance [117–119]. To overcome these obstacles, CTCs upregulate the expression of integrin α2β1, which improves their adhesion, migration, and resistance to apoptosis and enhances their survival in the bloodstream.

The shear stress in the circulatory system serves as a critical rate-limiting factor for cancer cell survival, particularly the high-intensity mechanical forces in the ventricular region, which can disrupt non-adapted cells or induce apoptosis. Integrin α2β1 enhances the structural stability and adhesion capacity of CTCs, facilitating their adaptation to shear stress. This adaptation may occur through two mechanisms: activating integrin α2β1 to promote CTCs’ adhesion to vascular walls, thereby mitigating mechanical damage; or modulating epigenetic modifications such as histone acetylation, chromatin structure, and gene expression to enhance shear resistance, as well as adjusting cytoskeletal and morphological structures to aid in resistance. Additionally, shear stress-induced histone acetylation and nuclear amplification are also key mechanisms through which CTCs adapt to shear stress.

Since CTCs originate from epithelial cells, when they leave the primary tumor site and enter the bloodstream, they lose direct contact with surrounding cells, which would normally lead to the phenomenon of anoikis. However, the non-anchorage-dependent survival of cancer cells in circulation enables them to withstand anoikis and establish metastatic colonies in distant organs. Internal inhibition of nest-leaving apoptosis through the activation of cell adhesion-independent integrin α2β1 is an essential condition for the survival of circulating tumor cells (CTCs) in the blood. Integrin α2β1 can regulate the expression of anti-apoptotic proteins BCL-2/BCL-XL [98], promote mitochondrial release of cytochrome c, and activate effector Caspases via the PI3K pathway [120], or activate death receptors such as FAS to bind with specific ligands and subsequently activate Caspase-8, thereby inducing apoptosis.

CTCs are susceptible to immune attack after detaching from the primary tumor, with abnormal expression of immune checkpoints and interaction with blood cells being key mechanisms of immune evasion. ITGA2 can upregulate PD-L1 expression on CTCs by promoting STAT3 phosphorylation [121]. PD-L1 binds to T-cell PD-1, leading to T-cell activation or apoptosis, thereby assisting CTCs in evading immune surveillance [122–124]. Studies have shown that CTCs secrete heat shock protein 47 to induce collagen deposition, and these collagens can serve as “anchors” for integrin α2β1. Moreover, research by Mazzucato et al. has confirmed that beyond its adhesive function, α2β1 is a key receptor mediating platelet calcium signaling—its binding to collagen triggers a two-stage calcium signal, which is the core mechanism underlying platelet activation, granule secretion, and aggregation [125, 126]. Thus, collagen exposure induced by CTCs activates platelets via the collagen receptors GPVI or α2β1 on the platelet surface, a process dependent on the α2β1-mediated calcium signaling pathway, ultimately promoting platelet aggregation on the surface of CTCsto form cancer cell-rich microclusters that are protected from shear stress and natural killer (NK) cell attack [127, 128].

Pre-metastatic niche

Through paracrine effects, primary tumors can generate a microenvironment in distant organs that attracts DTCs. This pre-metastatic niche is primarily composed of factors secreted by the primary tumor, tumor-mobilized bone marrow-derived cells, and local stromal components [129–131]. At the early stage of tumor metastasis, integrin α2β1 affects the recruitment and functional activation of immune cells, thus conferring immunosuppressive and inflammatory properties to the pre-metastatic ecosystems that creates a suitable soil for subsequent metastasis. The expression of integrin α2β1 is crucial for the functional maturation of regulatory T cells (Tregs), which in turn can suppress immune responses by secreting immunosuppressive molecules such as IL-10, TGF-β, and adenosine, while depleting the essential IL-2 and reducing levels of immune co-stimulatory molecules. Furthermore, integrin α2β1 binds to anaerobically digested streptococci and activates the NF-kB signaling pathway and promotes the secretion of CXCL1, which facilitates the migration and activation of myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs). Integrin α2β1 can trigger the degranulation response of mast cells by directly binding to complement C1q, which promotes the release of histamine, trypsin-like enzymes, and pro-inflammatory factors (TNF-α, IL-6), thus initiating a local inflammatory response [132]. Especially in the peritoneum, mast cells can actively ‘build’ the inflammatory microenvironment through integrin α2β1 and recruit neutrophils to strengthen the immune response [133].

Dormancy

When DTCs invade secondary sites, they produce metastatic dormancy, which comprises multiple components including quiescence, angiogenic dormancy, generation of a balance between cancer cell proliferation and death, and immune dormancy [134]. The regulation of tumor cell dormancy mostly involves mutual crosstalk between the environment and the mechanisms controlling the transcriptional programme [135]. As a mediator of intracellular-extracellular signalling, integrin α2β1 may assist in the transition of cell states in the dormancy and reawakening of DTCs. Specifically, integrin α2β1 modulates tumorigenic and dormant states [136, 137] by influencing the balance between ERK [138] and p38 MAPK [139] signaling pathways. Additionally, the tumor-derived, type III collagen-rich ECM environment contributes to maintaining the dormant state of tumor cells [140]. Disruption of this type III collagen ECM activates STAT1 signaling via discoidal structural domain receptor 1 (DDR1), thereby restoring the proliferative capacity of tumor cells [141]. Therefore, integrin α2β1 and DDR1 may competitively bind type III collagen, though integrin α2β1 exhibits a higher affinity. In addition, persistent host organ inflammation accelerates the end of the dormant process of tumor cells, while the hypoxic microenvironment induced by the disordered proliferation and disturbed angiogenesis of cancer cells is a key mechanism for inducing them to enter a dormant state [94, 142]. For example, in patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC, the downregulation of ITGA2 expression can induce CTCs to enter dormancy under hypoxic conditions [143].

Colonisation

Tumor cells exit dormant mode and can achieve tumor growth and organ colonisation through integrin α2β1-mediated tumor cell proliferation, angiogenesis and immunosuppression. Studies have shown that there are significant differences in the metastatic potential of tumor cells in different organs, a phenomenon known as organotropism [144]. Integrin α2β1 can affect the organ tropism of tumor cell colonisation by regulating multiple signalling pathways. For instance, in epithelial ovarian cancer, integrin α2β1 on tumor cells interacts with collagen in the greater omentum, which promotes peritoneal metastasis via the activation of the FAK/MAPK signaling pathway [145]. In GC, overexpression of integrin α2β1 enhances cell adhesion and facilitates cancer cell implantation in the peritoneal cavity, a process closely linked to the regulation of transcription factors AP-1 and Sp1 [38, 127, 128]. In PCa, integrin α2β1 on the tumor cell surface, in conjunction with type I collagen in bone tissue, activates the RhoC signaling pathway, thereby initiating tumor cell colonisation in the skeletal microenvironment [98]. In CRC, integrin α2β1 binds to type IV collagen in the liver [82], triggering the FAK signaling pathway and promoting tumor cell colonization in the liver parenchyma [146].

Considering the critical role of integrin α2β1 in tumor metastasis, further research and the development of clinically targeted therapeutic strategies against ITGA2 are of paramount importance. The following section explores targeted therapeutic approaches for integrin α2 and aims to offer new insights and strategies for cancer treatment.

Status of clinical targeted therapies

The overexpression of integrin α2 in numerous cancer types has made it a high-profile target in drug development [82, 97, 107, 147–150]. Research in molecular therapies targeted to integrin α2 have made significant progress (Table 2). Precisely targeting and inhibiting the key part of integrin α2 subunit and preventing its interaction with ECM proteins or other ligands can effectively block the adhesion, proliferation and migration processes of tumor cells and provides a new strategy for anti-tumor therapy. With further research, integrin α2-targeted therapies are anticipated to contribute to tumor microenvironment regulation and anti-angiogenesis therapies, and ultimately improve patient outcomes.

Integrin alpha 2 targeted therapeutic drug data

Targeting integrin α2β1 as an anti-metastatic strategy faces two main challenges. First, functional redundancy with other collagen-binding integrins (α1β1, α10β1, α11β1) and compensatory non-collagen receptors (α5β1, αv-integrins) can limit efficacy, as adaptive adhesion allows tumors to bypass integrin α2β1 inhibition [171, 172]. Yet, in highly invasive, collagen-rich tumors, integrin α2β1 often acts as a dominant mechanosensor driving pro-invasive signaling, underscoring the need for combination approaches [173]. Second, integrin α2β1 is broadly expressed in normal tissues, contributing to hemostasis, tissue integrity, and wound repair, raising toxicity concerns [174, 175]. Tumor-specific activation in stiff, collagen-dense microenvironments, however, represents a pathologically amplified state, supporting context-restricted or tumor-targeted interventions.

Effective clinical use will also require patient stratification to identify contexts where integrin α2β1’s pro-metastatic functions are most pronounced. elevated integrin α2β1 in rigid, collagen-rich tumors correlates with invasion, EMT, survival in circulation, and metastasis, whereas metastasis-suppressive roles are largely limited to early lesions or homeostatic contexts [176–179]. Overall, integrin α2β1 predominantly promotes metastasis in advanced, mechanically active tumors, and selective, biomarker-driven targeting may enable rational clinical translation.

Conclusions and future perspectives

ITGA2 is the specific functional unit that determines integrin α2β1 activity [18]. Decades of extensive research have demonstrated that integrin α2 constitutes a core component of intercellular communication, mechanosensing and cell motility mechanisms. It is capable of sensing physical and chemical signals from the environment that through a series of signaling pathways, enables it to exhibit distinct spatiotemporal programming properties within the tumor metastatic cascade.

This article provides a comprehensive overview of the dynamic regulatory mechanisms of integrin α2β1 in the metastatic cascade of cancer. In the early stage of tumorigenesis, integrin α2β1 assists tumor cells to break through the basement membrane barrier by inducing tumor cell proliferation, EMT and MMPs secretion. Subsequently, integrin α2β1 activates MMPs in response to physicochemical signals from the ECM, which promotes angiogenesis and improves vascular permeability, and assists cancer cells to invade the vascular lumen. Integrin α2β1 confers CTCs with an anti-shearing force after they enter the circulatory system, as well as anoikis resistance and immune escape ability to achieve systemic dissemination. In the process of distal organ colonisation, integrin α2β1 guides the retention of cancer cells in the microvessels of target organs such as liver and bone by recognising the organ tropism code of specific ECM ligands, and creates microenvironmental support for extravasation and survival by remodelling the immune-suppressive and inflammatory properties of the pre-metastatic ecosystem. In the new environment, integrin α2β1 senses hypoxia and nutrient limitation signals and induces cancer cells to enter a dormant state. Ultimately, through the combined effects of microenvironmental reprogramming and stem cell activation, these dormant cells re-enter the cell cycle and form micrometastatic foci, completing the metastatic cascade.

Therapeutic strategies targeting integrin α2 may offer opportunities to interfere with specific steps of the metastatic process and to complement existing treatment modalities within a precision medicine framework. Several integrin α2-targeting agents, including E7820 and vatelizumab, have entered preclinical or early clinical evaluation and have shown biological activity in selected solid tumor contexts. However, the clinical translation of integrin α2-targeted therapies faces multiple conceptual and practical challenges. On one hand, there is an urgent need to address the temporal and spatial coupling of mechanistic signaling and biochemical signaling networks and to develop variant modulators that can specifically block pathomechanical conduction without affecting physiological functions. On the other hand, the design of bifunctional antibodies based on the glycosylation modification features of ITGA2 may help overcome certain limitations of current immunotherapeutic strategies [180]. Additionally, artificial intelligence-driven molecular dynamics simulations offer the potential to predict accurately conformational changes in integrins and thus guide the rational design of novel inhibitors [181]. Future research should continue to optimise drug structures and improve targeting selectivity, and should further explore synergistic interactions with immune checkpoint inhibitors and conventional chemotherapeutic agents to develop rational combination treatment strategies [182, 183]. In recent years, the screening of integrin α2 inhibitors from natural products [184–186] has provided a new research direction for the combination of Chinese and Western medicine in anti-tumor therapy, which suggests that we can explore more active ingredients with targeting potential from the treasure trove of traditional medicine and open up new avenues for individualized treatment of malignant tumors.

In recent years, innovations in molecular mechanics probe technology, including the development of advanced tools such as ForceChrono [187], reversible shearing DNA-based tension probes (RSDTP) [188], molecular tension fluorescence microscopy (MTFM) [189], and LR probes [190], have greatly advanced the field of integrin-mediated mechanosignalling research. These high-precision probes are capable of monitoring and quantifying the mechanistic features of integrin-ligand interactions in real time, revealing the dynamic regulatory mechanisms of cellular mechanotransduction pathways. By integrating fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET) imaging with atomic force microscopy (AFM), researchers have successfully visualized the spatial-temporal characteristics of integrin force conduction. This has established a critical experimental foundation for understanding the mechanistic abnormalities within the tumor microenvironment. These breakthrough discoveries not only deepen our understanding of the ECM-integrin-cytoskeleton signalling axis but also provide new molecular targets and theoretical support for the development of precise therapeutic strategies aimed at the tumor mechanical microenvironment.

The dynamic function of integrin α2 in the metastatic cascade of cancer is well-documented; however, research has predominantly focused on RGD-binding integrins and limited attention to integrin α2 in clinical studies. This gap restricts our comprehensive understanding of integrin α2 function. Nonetheless, accumulating evidence suggests that integrin α2β1 activity may modulate tumor cell responses to conventional treatments, including surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and molecularly targeted therapies, in a context-dependent manner [191–198]. Moving forward, integrin α2 may represent a complementary therapeutic target in molecularly or microenvironmentally defined patient subsets. Further exploration of the interaction of integrin α2 with other cancer-related molecules will help reveal the regulatory pathways of cancer metastasis and provide a theoretical basis for precision medicine. Additionally, advances in high-throughput sequencing technologies and bioinformatics techniques have made the identification of integrin α2-related biomarkers more feasible. The identification and clinical validation of such biomarkers may facilitate biomarker-driven patient stratification and inform more precise therapeutic decision-making.

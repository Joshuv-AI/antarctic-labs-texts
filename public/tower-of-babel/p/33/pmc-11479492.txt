Effects of Inc parameter on quality of VMAT radiotherapy plans for right breast cancer based on dosimetric study
Authors: Chong Shen, Hailong Liao, Qi Wang, Hai Liu, Xiaonan Sun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to investigate the influence of gantry angle increment (Inc) parameters on the Monaco treatment planning system for volumetric modulated arc therapy (VMAT) in right breast cancer, and to determine the optimal Inc parameters. Twenty-three patients with right breast cancer at the Sir Run Run Shaw Hospital from August 2022 to August 2023 were retrospectively selected. Four VMAT plans were generated for each patient incorporating Inc of 10°, 20° (control group), 30°, and 40°, while the other optimization parameters and constraint functions remained constant. The D98 and conformity index were lower in the Inc30 and Inc40 groups than in the Inc20 group. As Inc increased from 10° to 30°, the irradiated dose to the ipsilateral lung, including the V20, V5, and Dmax, mean dose, gradually increased. There were no statistically significant differences in the irradiated dose to the contralateral breast, contralateral lung, heart, esophagus, and brachial plexus. The number of monitor units and control points decreased with increasing Inc. Smaller Inc values can achieve higher target coverage and lower irradiated dose to the ipsilateral lung, but will reduce delivery efficiency. Considering the plan quality and delivery efficiency, an Inc of 10° or 20° is recommended for right breast cancer VMAT plans.

1. Introduction

Female breast cancer has now surpassed lung cancer to become the leading cause of cancer incidence globally in 2020, with an estimated addition of 2.3 million cases, accounting for 11.7% of all cancer cases.[1,2] Surgery is the primary treatment for breast cancer, and postoperative radiotherapy can reduce the risk of loco-regional recurrence and improve overall survival.[3–5] The radiation target areas after modified radical mastectomy for breast cancer include the chest wall, supraclavicular and infraclavicular lymph node drainage areas, axillary lymph node regions, and internal mammary regions. The radiation range is wide and irregular in shape, with many critical organs at risk (OARs), such as the lungs and heart, posing a significant challenge to radiotherapy planning. Recent studies have shown that compared to traditional three-dimensional conformal radiotherapy and intensity-modulated radiotherapy, volumetric modulated arc therapy (VMAT) can better ensure conformity and uniformity of the radiation target area, as well as reduce the radiation dose to organs such as the lungs.[6–8] VMAT optimization is divided into 2 steps: first, the ideal fluence map is obtained based on the optimization algorithm. Second, the ideal fluence is converted into an executable deliverable sequence with the multi-leaf collimator (MLC) shapes and gantry positions. When using the Monaco planning system, certain parameter settings within the planning system, such as the minimum segment width, maximum control points (CPs), and number of arcs, all have a certain impact on the dosimetric results of the radiotherapy plan.[9,10] The gantry angle increment (Inc) is an important parameter in the Monaco planning system. It divides the planned arc into multiple equal sectors, where the MLC leaves move in only 1 direction within each sector, and then reverses direction in the next sector region, repeating this process to control beam delivery.

Previous studies have primarily focused on the impact of Inc parameter on the quality of VMAT plans for cervical cancer.[11–13] However, the influence of Inc on VMAT plans for breast cancer has not been studied properly. Since the target areas and beam field requirements of modified radical mastectomy radiotherapy for breast cancer are significantly different from those for cervical cancer. Herein, this study explores the impact of the Inc parameter on the quality of VMAT plans for modified radical mastectomy breast cancer, which could provide a reference for VMAT plan design.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Case selection

Between August 2022 and August 2023, 23 patients with right-sided breast cancer who received adjuvant radiotherapy at the Sir Run Run Shaw Hospital of Zhejiang University were retrospectively selected. These patients had a tumor stage between II and III, with a median age of 49 years. This retrospective study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Sir Run Shaw Hospital of Zhejiang University (Approval No. 2024-2261-01). The need for written informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study.

2.2. CT simulation and contouring

Patients were positioned supine with free breathing on a supine breast board, with the left arm placed on the side of the body and the right arm raised and placed on the board to fully expose the right breast area. Conventional 3DCT scans were performed using a large aperture Philips computed tomography simulator (SOMATOM Definition AS) with a scanning layer thickness of 3 mm, and the scanning range was from the mandible to the diaphragm. OARs were automatically contoured using the automatic contouring software AccuContour 3.2 and then further modified.[14,15] Following the National Comprehensive Cancer Network and Radiation Therapy Oncology Group guidelines, the clinical target volume was delineated, including the chest wall, supraclavicular and infraclavicular regions, axillary lymph nodes, and internal mammary lymph node region.[16,17] All clinical target volumes were merged into a single target area, uniformly expanded by 5 mm, and then subcutaneously retreated by 3 mm to create the planning target volume (PTV) for treatment planning.

2.3. Treatment planning

All treatment plans were performed on the Infinity LINAC (Elekta Ltd., Sweden) with 6 MV photon beams in the Monaco (version 5.1) treatment planning system. The prescribed dose of PTV was 50 Gy delivered in 25 fractions. A single dual-arc of 240° was applied for VMAT plans with gantry angles rotating from 190° to 70°, then rotating counterclockwise by the same angle, with a collimator angle of 0°. For each case, 4 different Inc angles (10, 20, 30, and 40) were selected for optimization under the names Inc10, Inc20, Inc30, and Inc40, respectively, to ensure that all 4 group plans met the requirements of the International Commission on Radiation Units and Measurements report number 83.[18] As shown in Figure 1, when the Inc angle was set to 20°, the 240° beam was divided into 12 equally spaced sectors. All the plans used the same optimization parameters and constraint functions to accurately compare the influence of the Inc on plan quality. The Monaco planning system was optimized using the Monte Carlo algorithm. The planning statistical uncertainty was set to 1% per calculation, the calculation grid spacing was 3 mm and the auto flash was 2 cm. The maximum number of control points was 180 and the minimum segment width was 5 mm.

OARs evaluation parameters included: V30, V20, V5, and Dmean of the ipsilateral lung; V5 and Dmean of the contralateral lung; V5 and Dmean of the heart; Dmean of the contralateral breast; and maximum dose (Dmax) of the spinal cord, esophagus, and brachial plexus. where V30, V20, and V5 represents the percentage of OARs volumes that received doses exceeding 30 Gy, 20 Gy, and 5 Gy, respectively. Meanwhile, monitor units (MUs) and CPs were also recorded to evaluate the delivery efficiency of the VMAT plans.

The results are presented as the mean ± standard deviation. The parameters of Inc20 were used as the reference group, and the results of Inc10, Inc30, and Inc40 were compared to those of Inc20. All data were tested for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk (S–W) test. For normally distributed data, the paired-sample t test was used. For non-normally distributed data, the Mann–Whitney–Wilcoxon test was used. SPSS version 27.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY) was used for all data analyze, and P-value < .05 were considered statistically significant.

4. Discussion

The PTV after modified radical mastectomy for breast cancer is wide and irregular in shape, with many critical OARs. In comparison to conventional tangential field irradiation and tangential field combined with fixed field static intensification, VMAT has advantages in optimizing dose distribution and protecting critical organs.[19] The VMAT plan design involves a multitude of optimization parameters, and the optimization time is longer than that of the previous 2 methods. Studying the impact of different optimization parameters on plan quality can not only reduce the time required for plan design but also improve the efficiency of obtaining clinically satisfactory plans. The Monaco planning system is a planning system developed by Elekta based on the Monte Carlo algorithm, which completes the planning design by optimizing and adjusting the parameters of the machine model. The gantry angle increment is an important parameter that divides the planned arc into multiple equal sectors, where the MLC leaves move in only 1 direction within each sector, and then reverses direction in the next sector region, repeating this process to control the beam delivery. The Monaco Planning System User’s Manual mentions that a smaller Inc theoretically results in better plan quality, but when Inc is set too small, plan quality is not significantly improved, and patient treatment time is prolonged.

In this study, 23 patients with modified radical right-sided breast cancer were selected to analyze and compare the quality and delivery efficiency of VMAT plans with different Inc, including the D98, D2, CI, and HI of the PTV, as well as the doses to OARs, MUs, and CPs. The results showed that all 4 groups of plans met all the clinical requirements, with D98 in the Inc30 and Inc40 groups being lower than Inc20, and the irradiated doses of V20, V5, and Dmean in the ipsilateral lung increased gradually as Inc increased from 10 to 30, whereas there were no statistically significant differences in irradiated doses in the contralateral breast, contralateral lung, heart, esophagus, and brachial plexus.

During radiotherapy, the lung tissue is inevitably affected by irradiation, and radiation-induced lung injury is one of the most common complications of postoperative radiotherapy in breast cancer, severely affecting the quality of life of patients and even increasing non-cancer mortality rates. Studies have shown that dosimetric factors such as V10, V20, and V30 of the ipsilateral lung are associated with radiation pneumonitis in radiotherapy-induced lung injury.[20] Among these factors, V20 and V30 may be useful dose predictive factors for evaluating the risk of asymptomatic radiation pneumonitis in the ipsilateral lung of patients with breast cancer.[21] In this study, as Inc increased from 10 to 30, V20 gradually increased with statistically significant differences. Regarding V30, the Inc10 group was slightly lower than the Inc20 group, but the difference was not statistically significant, whereas the Inc20 group was lower than the Inc30 group and had a statistically significant difference, indicating that a smaller Inc (10° and 20°) is more advantageous for lung protection.

As survival rates improve, long-term radiation toxicity has become a major concern in patients with breast cancer. The heart is the most important organ affected by radiotherapy for breast cancer, and cardiac irradiation is associated with long-term cardiac complications, particularly coronary artery disease. Darby et al showed that for every gray increase in the average cardiac dose, the incidence of major coronary events increased linearly by 7.4%.[22] Both V5 and mean dose to the heart increased with increasing Inc in this study, but the results were not statistically different, which may be due to the fact that the target area of the right breast cancer is some distance away from the heart compared to the left breast cancer, and the cardiac constraint function was not weighted heavily during the plan optimization process.

In a similar study, a larger Inc (30° and 40°) yielded better plan than a smaller Inc (10° and 20°) in postoperative cervical cancer.[12] The results obtained in this study differed significantly from those of cervical cancer, which is closely related to the dimension of PTV for different types of tumors. As shown in Figure 4, a comparison of the width of a typical cervical and breast cancer PTV at 15° increment under beam eye view is presented. It can be seen that the width of the PTV for breast cancer is smaller than that for cervical cancer in the ranges of −150° to −100° and 30° to 70°. When Inc is small, the MLC does not have a large range of motion in the x-direction due to the smaller width of the PTV, and the planning system is more fully optimized for a limited beam-out time. Although the treatment arc was given a 240° field interval from −170° to 70° in the plan design, during optimization the dose was mainly distributed in the tangential direction of the PTV (at −150° to −100° and 30° to 70°) owing to the protection of the ipsilateral lung. Due to these reasons, smaller Inc (10° and 20°) can be optimized better and get higher plan quality. However, MUs increased significantly with higher plan quality. As shown in Figure 3, compared to the Inc30 plan, the MUs increased by 25.6% and 12.1% for the Inc10 and Inc20 plans, respectively, and the treatment efficiency decreased.

From the above, it can also be seen that the selection of Inc has a close relationship with the width of the tumor PTV. In future work, more diseases (such as nasopharyngeal, lung, and rectal cancers.) will be selected, and further comprehensive analysis will be conducted to explore the connection between Inc and the width of the PTV, in order to provide more valuable references for VMAT plan design.

5. Conclusions

The selection of Inc will affect the VMAT plan quality and delivery efficiency for right-sided breast cancer after modified radical surgery, and the appropriate Inc value can be selected according to the actual situation. In this paper, an Inc of 10° or 20° is recommended for right breast cancer VMAT plans, especially in complex cases, but while actually treating patients, motion management with active breath control or any other modality would be required.

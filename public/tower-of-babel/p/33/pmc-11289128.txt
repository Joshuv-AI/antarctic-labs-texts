Simulation and experimental study of flexible adsorption and localization mechanism for vegetable grafting machines
Authors: Yanjun Li, Chao Wang, Hongxin Wu, Yaqian Zhao, Jian Song
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Because of the lack of good seedling positioning during vegetable grafting, there are issues such as high labor costs and long grafting time. This article proposes a negative pressure suction seedling positioning method for seed leaves based on the characteristic parameters of cucumber spike wood, and designs a flexible adsorption positioning mechanism for spike wood. Firstly, the ventral surface curve trajectories of cucumber cotyledons were extracted using Matlab software, and then a shape-adaptive design was applied to the attachment surface of the flexible suction positioning mechanism, and a computational fluid dynamics model of the airflow field was established. By combining Fluent simulation analysis with orthogonal experiments, the effect of suction hole diameter, vacuum negative pressure value, suction hole quantity, and suction hole depth on the adsorption effect of the suction head was analyzed, the main and secondary factors and operational indicators that affect the adsorption effect are evaluated. The optimal parameter combination: suction hole diameter of 1.5 mm, vacuum negative pressure value of 2 kPa, suction hole quantity of 42, and suction hole depth of 2 mm, has been found. A verification experiment was conducted on a test bench, and the experimental results show that the success rate of leaf absorption using the optimal parameter combination is 97.69%, which indicates that the suction head is designed reasonably and meets the requirements of grafting.

Introduction

Vegetable seedling cultivation is a key link in vegetable planting, but continuous cropping of vegetables leads to soil degradation, exacerbation of pests and diseases, and a decrease in vegetable quality1,2. Currently, relying on the breeding of disease-resistant varieties and the development of pesticides has proven to be less than ideal, and it can also lead to environmental pollution3–5. Grafting seedlings are a green and efficient production technology that can prevent soil-borne diseases, enhance plant disease resistance, improve seedling stress resistance, increase crop yield, enhance crop quality, and have significant importance for the development of the seedling industry. Today, a lot of equipment has been designed and constructed in order to improve the speed and accuracy of vegetable seedling grafting6–8.

Currently, Japan’s Inoguchi Company9–12 has designed an automatic upper seedling device for cucumber grafting machines, which significantly improves the grafting efficiency of the machine. Jia Jing13,14 has designed a machine vision-driven hole tray seedling grafting operation positioning method, with a successful recognition rate of 88% for single tomato seedlings and a seedling success rate of 83%. Peng Yuping15–17 has designed a transplanting chain and a transfer hand-assisted manual upper seedling for a grafting machine for eggplants, with a successful rate of 92%. Tian Yufeng18,19 has designed a machine vision-based automatic stock supplier for pumpkin single-seedling characteristics, with a successful recognition rate of 90%, a transplanting success rate of 80%, and a successful rate of 71.4%. The above-mentioned study lacks research on flexible scion positioning methods, leading to a significant impact of seedling standardization on scion accuracy and the presence of seedling injury issues. Current grafting machines often rely on manual scion placement, which can easily result in cotyledon splitting or stem damage, affecting the healing and viability quality of grafted seedlings. Therefore, there is a need to design a scion positioning mechanism that ensures minimal damage to the grafted seedlings while accurately positioning the scion, aiming to reduce labor costs and enhance grafting machine efficiency.

A flexible adsorption and positioning mechanism of cucumber spikewood is designed for the lack of seedling positioning problems in the vegetable grafting process, and the curve equation of the cucumber spikewood cotyledon ventral surface is obtained through MATLAB software, and the adsorption action surface of the flexible adsorption and positioning mechanism is designed by using this to simulate the shape. Fluent software is used to carry out fluid simulation analysis to obtain the results of the influence of various factors on the adsorption effect, and combined with the bench test to determine the optimal operating parameters of the flexible adsorption and positioning mechanism, to realize the accurate positioning of the vegetable grafting process, and to provide a reference for the development and structural improvement of the vegetable grafting robot.

Suction head adsorption mechanism

The location of the scion is critical to the process of grafting and splicing. Inaccurate positioning can directly impact the quality of the graft. Existing grafting machines typically rely on manual operation to hold the scion in place during the grafting process. To improve the automation degree of vegetable grafting machines while ensuring the precision of the grafting process, according to the agronomic requirements of double-broken-root grafting, a flexible scion suction positioning mechanism has been designed. This mechanism employs a form-fitting design on the cotyledon surface and utilizes negative pressure suction to achieve flexible suction positioning of the scion cotyledons of the broken-root spike wood. This innovation is of great significance in realizing the full automation of splice-type vegetable grafting machines and improving the graft quality of seedlings.

The design of the suction head

During the clamping process of the spike, there may be a situation where the spike position is skewed. The position of the spike is crucial for grafting, and positional deviation may directly lead to grafting failure20. Therefore, a flexible spike adsorption positioning mechanism is designed to accurately position the spike by adsorbing the sub-leaf of the spike, as shown in Fig. 1.Figure 1Adsorption positioning schematic diagram.

The design of the suction head structure needs to be based on the geometric parameters of the cucumber stem and the compressive mechanics of the leaflet. Thirty cucumber stems were selected for geometric parameter measurement, with a plant height of H1 of 45.24 mm, leaflet span A1 of 40.12 mm, leaflet width D1 of 13.42 mm, and leaflet length L1 of 18.36 mm. The WDW-5 electronic universal testing machine in the laboratory was used to measure the maximum pressure that the leaflet could withstand. The position on both sides of the main vein of the leaflet was selected for measurement, with a press rod diameter of 6 mm and a sample size of 300 plants. The maximum pressure P that the leaflet could withstand was obtained as 3.16 kPa.

The adsorption site of the suction head is the ventral surface of the leaflet on the cucumber stem. In order to achieve good adsorption effect, it is necessary to perform a mimic design of the suction head of the cucumber stem. By photographing the image of the cucumber stem leaflet and using MATLAB software for processing21, the ventral curve trajectory of the leaflet under natural state was fitted to provide a theoretical basis for the rational design of the suction head structure of the cucumber stem.

Trajectory extraction process

Fitting method of the ventral curve trajectory of the leaflet of cucumber stem:

The cucumber seedlings were placed on the test bench ensuring sufficient light illumination. Clear full-frontal images of the cucumber seedlings were captured using a high-precision camera.

The captured images were imported into MATLAB software, where the im2bw function from the DIP toolbox in MATLAB software was used to convert the grayscale image to a binary image using threshold transformation, and the Canny edge detection algorithm was called to extract the boundary of the image, as shown in Fig. 3a. The image of the leaves was cropped, and the aforementioned operations were repeated, as shown in Fig. 3b. After the binarization, a 0–1 matrix was obtained, and the find function was used to find the point set where 1 was located, to obtain the initial curve coordinates.Figure 3Extraction of the curve on the underside of ear stalk cotyledons.

Extraction of the curve on the underside of ear stalk cotyledons.

Subtract the mean from the obtained curve coordinates to obtain new coordinate points. Then convert the processed coordinates to polar coordinates, sort them in ascending order of the abscissa, and obtain the value of the abscissa in Cartesian coordinates.

Call the cftool curve fitting toolbox in MATLAB software, fit the coordinate points obtained in step (3), use polynomial approximation, and perform a cubic fit to obtain the ventral curve trajectory of the single leaflet, as shown in Fig. 4.Figure 4Fitting of the curve on the underside of cotyledons.

Materials and methods

Single-factor simulation test

In order to validate the rationality of the adsorption device structure obtained through biomimetic design, single-factor simulation analysis was conducted using Fluent software. The study investigated the influence of varying suction hole diameters, the number of suction holes, suction hole depths, and vacuum negative pressure values on the distribution of the internal airflow field.

Using SolidWorks 2020, a three-dimensional model of the leaf gas suction head of cotyledonary air suction was established. The mesh was divided using the FLUENT software’s pre-processing software mesh. The hexahedral mesh method was selected for grid division, and the mesh unit length was 0.5 mm, as shown in Fig. 7. Based on the working requirements of the leaf gas suction head, appropriate solution parameters were set. The control equation was discretized using the finite volume method, and a standard turbulence model23,24 was selected with the pressure-based SIMPLE separation algorithm used for solving.Figure 7Mesh division of air suction model.

Mesh division of air suction model.

In the software, the flow field simulation conditions of the cucumber ear shoots gas suction head were set as follows: the entrance pressure was set at static pressure, and the dynamic viscosity coefficient was set as 17.9 × 10–6 Pa s. The air density was set as 1.29 kg/m3, as per standard atmospheric pressure. The outlet pressure used a negative pressure value, and the pressure regulation range was 0–5 kPa. The wall condition was set to a no-slip boundary condition, and standard wall function was used for calculation and processing.

Experiments results and discussion

Results of one-factor simulation test

The effect of suction hole diameter on the negative pressure flow field

The effect of the suction hole diameter on the negative pressure flow field was studied. When the vacuum negative pressure value of the gas suction outlet was 2 kPa, and the inlet pressure was the standard atmospheric pressure, fluid simulations with suction hole diameters of 1 mm, 1.5 mm, 2 mm, and 2.5 mm were conducted. The pressure and velocity distribution of the gas flow field were obtained, as shown in Figs. 11 and 12.Figure 11Different aperture airflow field pressure distribution.Figure 12Different aperture airflow field velocity distribution.

Different aperture airflow field pressure distribution.

Different aperture airflow field velocity distribution.

The analysis showed that as the diameter of the suction hole increases, the area of the inlet also increases, and the vacuum inside the gas chamber becomes larger. When the aperture is 1 mm or 1.5 mm, the pressure distribution inside the gas chamber is uneven. When the aperture is 2 mm or 2.5 mm, the pressure distribution inside the gas chamber is relatively uniform. As the diameter of the suction hole increases, the overall flow rate inside the gas chamber also increases. The air velocity and flow rate at the outlet also increase continuously. The adsorption area of the airflow on the leaf also increases, so the aperture of the suction hole should not be too large. Based on the fluid simulation results and the characteristics of the seedlings, a diameter of 1.5–2.5 mm was chosen, which can effectively improve the adsorption stability of the leaf.

The effect of the vacuum negative pressure value on the negative pressure flow field

The inlet pressure of the gas suction head uses standard atmospheric pressure, and when the suction hole diameter is 2 mm, fluid simulations were conducted for vacuum negative pressure values of 1 kPa, 1.5 kPa, 2 kPa, and 2.5 kPa at the outlet, and the pressure and velocity distribution of the gas flow field were obtained, as shown in Figs. 13 and 14.Figure 13Different pressure distributions of airflow field under different vacuum negative pressures.Figure 14Different velocity distributions of airflow field under different vacuum negative pressures.

Different pressure distributions of airflow field under different vacuum negative pressures.

Different velocity distributions of airflow field under different vacuum negative pressures.

From Fig. 13, it can be seen that as the vacuum negative pressure value at the outlet increases, the vacuum inside the gas chamber also increases, and the pressure distribution is relatively uniform. The air velocity inside the gas chamber also continues to increase, and the air velocity and flow rate at the outlet also increase continuously.

From Fig. 14, it can be seen that when the vacuum negative pressure value is 2.5 kPa, eddies appear inside the gas chamber. Therefore, to ensure the safe adsorption of the leaf, a vacuum negative pressure value of around 2 kPa is chosen, which will be more conducive to maintaining the adsorption stability of the leaf.

Result of the Bench experiment

Three groups of suitable cucumber seedlings were selected, of which each group contained 300 seedlings. The suction head parameters were set as follows: vacuum negative pressure of 2 kPa, a suction hole diameter of 1.5 mm, and a suction hole depth of 2 mm. Discussion

Based on the above simulation analysis and calculations performed on the data obtained from the orthogonal experiment, we can derive the following patterns.

From the results of Table 4, it can be seen that under the set experimental conditions, the impeller adsorption success rate of seedlings reached 97.69%, indicating good adsorption effect. Kang Kai et al. designed a negative pressure adsorption device and conducted experiments on pumpkin seedlings, obtaining cotyledon adsorption success rate of 96.67% and comprehensive seedling success rate of 96.03%, which is 1.02% higher than the adsorption success rate. The main reason is that the suction head adsorption surface adopts a shape imitation design, which can fit the impeller well. The reason for adsorption failure is that the angle of the seedlings is too small, and they cannot effectively spread out during adsorption. In addition, the growth direction of the impeller also affects the adsorption effect. Therefore, controlling the growth status of the seedlings is the key to successful adsorption. In the experiment, there was no damage to the seedlings, indicating that the shape imitation design of the suction head’s adsorption surface had significant effect. The set experimental conditions matched the actual situations and played a role in flexible adsorption.

Through FLUENT simulation experiments, it is found that as the suction hole diameter increases, the adsorption effect of the suction head gradually stabilizes. Combined with the simulation results, the suction hole diameter within the range of 1.5–2.5 mm is selected, which can achieve better suction effect. As the vacuum negative pressure increases, the air flow velocity inside the air chamber also increases. When the vacuum negative pressure exceeds 2.5 kPa, the flow field distribution inside the air chamber becomes uneven. Therefore, a vacuum negative pressure value of about 2 kPa is selected for impeller adsorption. As the number of suction holes increases, the flow field inside the air chamber also changes. When the number of suction holes is 42, the simulation results show that the flow field distribution inside the air chamber is relatively uniform. The depth of the suction hole has little effect on the flow field inside the air chamber. To ensure that the suction head has good suction effect, and considering the manufacturability of the suction head, a suction hole depth range of 1.5–2.5 mm can be chosen during design.

Based on the range of experimental factors, an orthogonal experiment was designed to obtain the optimal combination of suction head factors (vacuum negative pressure of 2 kPa, a suction hole diameter of 1.5 mm, and a suction hole depth of 2 mm). The design of the test bench was used to verify the adsorption effect of the optimal factor combination. The results showed that under the optimal factor level conditions, the adsorption effect was good, which can meet the requirements of actual grafting operations.

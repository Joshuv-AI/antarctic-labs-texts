Combining volatilomics and untargeted metabolomics revealed the important role of sinigrin in flavor development of Zhacai
Authors: Wenfeng Li, Fangtian Bao, Yimeng Wang, Chan Liang, Wanjie Zhang, Jian Ming
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractZhacai is a traditional Chinese pickled vegetable. Research has yet to explore the potential role of inherent glucosinolates in the flavor formation of Zhacai. This study employed volatilomics and untargeted metabolomics to investigate the effect of sinigrin on Zhacai flavor. The results confirmed the impact of lactic acid bacteria (LAB) on the quality of Zhacai, while also revealing, for the first time, how sinigrin affects its flavor, color, and pH level. In addition, sinigrin influenced the microflora in Zhacai fermented with and without LAB. Sinigrin significantly impacted the 2,4,6-cycloheptatrien-1-one, 2-hydroxy-4-(1-methylethyl)-, galbetal1,3GlcNAc, and 10E,12Z-octadecadienoic acid flavor compounds in the Zhacai, which exhibited a positive correlation with its flavor quality, while 2-isothiocyanatoethyl-benzene, 1-hepten-3-one, and 10-undecenal demonstrated a negative association. These findings emphasized the importance of monitoring the levels of sinigrin and its hydrolytic products to regulate Zhacai flavor quality.Keywords: Tumorous stem mustard, Sinigrin, Flavor, Lactic acid bacteria, Omics

Introduction

Zhacai is a traditional fermented vegetable that has been consumed in China for over a century (Zhang et al., 2021). Tuber mustard (Brassica juncea var. tumida) (AABB, 2n = 36), a stem mustard that belongs to the Brassicaceae family, is an agriculturally and economically important crop in China and some Southeast Asian countries and is primarily used as a raw material for Zhacai production (Cheng et al., 2019). The “triple salting and pressing” method is traditionally used for Zhacai production, which involves immersing the raw tuber in salt solutions at concentrations of 40 g/L, 80 g/L, and 140–160 g/L (w/v) for approximately 7 d, 20–30 d, and 4–6 months, respectively (Li et al., 2023). Spontaneous fermentation is a crucial step in this process, significantly contributing to the unique organoleptic properties of Zhacai (Yang et al., 2021) and exhibiting a particularly high abundance of lactic acid bacteria (LAB) (Zhang et al., 2023). LAB can metabolize carbohydrates to produce lactic acid, ethanol, and acetic acid, which are known flavor materials (Yang et al., 2021). Aspergillus, another microorganism found in Zhacai, can secrete protease to hydrolyze protein into amino acids and peptides, offering sweet and bitter flavors (Yang et al., 2021). Additionally, bacteria such as Leuconostoc, Clostridium, and Devosia are closely associated with the flavor compounds in Zhacai (Li et al., 2023). In addition to microbial contributions, the phytochemicals in vegetables play a significant role in the fermentation process and final product quality (Niu et al., 2021).

Fresh Zhacai (tumorous stem mustard, Fig. 1A) is characterized by its distinctive spicy flavor, reminiscent of radish (Raphanus sativus L., Zheng et al., 2024), which can be attributed to the high glucosinolate level (Li et al., 2020). Glucosinolates are known for their antioxidant, anti-cancer, anti-inflammatory, and anti-obesity properties (Wu et al., 2022), as well as their fungal (Martín & Higuera, 2016) and bacterial (Saavedra et al., 2010) inhibitory activities. Sinigrin, the most dominant glucosinolate in Zhacai (50.59 %, Li et al., 2024), is correlated with both bitterness and taste preference for Brassica group of vegetables (Beck, Jensen, Bjoern, & Kidmose, 2014). Our previous research indicated that Zhacai sinigrin underwent degradation during the fermentation process (Li et al., 2020). Wu et al. (2022) suggested that LAB were crucial for glucosinolate degradation. Due to their molecular structures, glucosinolates can be hydrolyzed into isothiocyanates, epithionitriles, nitriles, and glucose (Casajus et al., 2020), which contribute bitter, sweet, and aromatic flavors, and participate in the chemical reactions that generate various flavor components (Henning, de VOS, Maliepaard, Dekker, Verkerk, & Bonnema, 2014). Consequently, this study proposes that glucosinolates may influence the natural fermentation processes of Zhacai, consequently impacting the development of its flavor profile. However, the precise role of glucosinolates in flavor development during Zhacai fermentation remains unclear.

The current study aims to investigate the role of sinigrin in flavor development during the natural and LAB-supplemented fermentation of Zhacai. The findings can be applied to enhance quality control during Zhacai production.

Materials and methods

Raw materials and reagents

Three varieties of tumorous stem mustard, namely Fuyou 928, Fuza No. 5, and Yongan xiaoye, were collected from an experimental field at Yangtze Normal University, and their swollen stems were used as the subsequent experiments (Fig. 1A). The LAB pickle baking powder was purchased from Beijing Chuanxiu International Trade Co. Ltd. (Beijing, China) while the sinigrin (purity>98 %) was obtained from Shanghai Yuanye Biotechnology Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China). All the other reagents used in this work were analytically pure.

Sample preparation

The highly lignified peels were removed from the swollen stems of the tumorous stem mustard, after which they were sliced into pieces of 4 mm thick (Fig. 1B) and subjected to natural indoor dehydration until the moisture content was below 80 %. In preparation for fermentation, the dehydrated slices were divided into two groups: one treated with a 20 mg/mL sinigrin solution, and the other with water. These treatments were applied by misting the slice surfaces. The natural fermentation groups were then treated with 8 % NaCl (Li et al., 2019), while an additional 2 mg/kg of LAB was introduced into the respective experimental groups. All treatments were subsequently fermented in hermetic conditions for 30 d to produce Zhacai.

Color analysis

The L*, a*, and b* color values of samples were measured using a colorimeter (NR60CP, Shenzhen Sanenchi Technology Co. Ltd., Shenzhen, China). Furthermore, the color and texture of the samples were illustrated via photographic documentation.

Sensory evaluation

Six well-trained panelists majoring in Food Science and Engineering at Yangtze Normal University performed the sensory evaluation of the Zhacai. They assessed five sensory attributes of the Zhacai: color, aroma, flavor, shape, and texture, each of which was assessed using a scoring scale from 1 to 9. Scores ranging from 7 to 10 were assigned when the color was predominantly a deep brown, scores of 4 to 6 indicated minimal presence of brown, and scores between 0 and 3 reflected a primary coloration of light yellow. The slice thickness was given 7 to 9 points for uniform thickness, 4 to 6 points for moderately uniform thickness, and 1 to 3 points for non-uniform thickness. Scores between 7 and 9 points indicated a robust, distinctive flavor profile of pickled mustard greens, while scores ranging between 4 and 6 points suggested a subtle aroma of the same ingredients. Conversely, scores between 1 and 3 points indicated an undesirable taste, while scores between 7 and 9 points denoted the robust, distinctively briny, and salty flavor of pickled mustard greens. A score of 4 to 6 points reflected a mild yet distinct briny and salty taste. Conversely, a score of 1 to 3 points signified the presence of an undesirable, bitter, and peculiar flavor. The texture exhibited a crisp quality at scores of 7 to 9, transitioned to a slightly soft consistency at scores of 4 to 6, and became soft and mushy at scores of 1 to 3. All the samples in this sensory evaluation were food products, and since no invasive procedures or personal data collection were involved, ethical approval was not required. All assessors were provided with detailed information about the production materials, processes, and potential safety risks associated with the food to be evaluated, and subsequently consented to participate in the assessment.

Determination of the pH values

The sample was mixed with double its volume of pure water. After a boiling water bath for 30 min, the mixture was homogenized, and the pH value was determined using a pH meter (FE28, Mettler-Toledo Instruments (Shanghai) Co. Ltd., Shanghai, China).

Analysis of the microbial diversity

The cetyltrimethylammonium bromide method was employed for deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) extraction using a commercial kit (GL2816, Beijing BioLead Technology Co. Ltd., Beijing, China). The DNA was diluted to 1 ng/μL after determining the purity and concentrations via agarose gel electrophoresis. The 16S V4 region was polymerase chain reaction (PCR)-amplified using the Phusion® High-Fidelity PCR Master Mix with GC Buffer (New England Biotechnology (Beijing) Co. Ltd., Beijing, China). The PCR products were analyzed via electrophoresis on a 2 % agarose gel, after which the qualified products were purified using magnetic beads, followed by enzyme-labeled quantification. The products were recovered using the QIAEX II Gel Extraction Kit (Qiagen, Düsseldorf, Germany), while a TruSeq DNA PCR-Free Sample Preparation Kit (Illumina, Inc., Delaware, USA) was employed for library construction. Qubit and Q-PCR were employed for quantification, while the eligible library was sequenced using the NovaSeq6000 platform (Illumina, Inc., Delaware, USA).

The fastp tool (version 0.22.0) was used to obtain high-quality reads, which were combined using FLASH (version 1.2.11) to generate the tag data. All effective tags were clustered together using a 97 % identity threshold to categorize sequences into operational taxonomic units (OTUs). The OTUs were further refined to derive amplicon sequence variants (ASV) by reducing noise with Deblur (version 1.1.1). Mothur (version 1.48) and SILVA 138.1 (http://www.arb-silva.de/) were used for the taxonomic species annotation of the ASV sequences.

Volatilomics analysis via gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC–MS)

A 500 mg Zhacai sample was cryogenically ground with liquid nitrogen using a ball grinder (MM400, Verder (Shanghai) Instrument Equipment Co. Ltd., Shanghai, China) and vibrated at 60 °C for 5 min, after which the volatile constituents were adsorbed using DVB/CWR/PDMS (120 μm, Agilent, CA, USA) for a headspace duration of 15 min. Then, the volatiles were desorbed at 250 °C for 5 min.

The compounds were separated using a capillary column (DB-5MS, 30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm, Agilent J&W Scientific, Folsom, CA, USA) and high-purity helium (>99.999 %) as the carrier gas at a flow rate of 1.2 mL/min. The temperature program started with 40 °C for 0–3.5 min, followed by an increase from 40 °C to 100 °C at a rate of 10 °C/min, from 100 °C to 180 °C at a rate of 7 °C/min, and from 180 °C to 280 °C at a rate of 25 °C/min, where it was maintained for 5 min.

The other parameters included an electron impact (EI) ion source temperature of 230 °C, quadrupole and mass spectrum interface temperatures of 150 °C and 280 °C, respectively, and an electron energy value of 70 eV. A selected ion monitoring (SIM) model was used to obtain the data. The deconvolution parameters in the MassHunter software (Agilent, CA, USA) were configured to a peak width of 20, while the resolution, sensitivity, and chromatographic peak shape settings were adjusted to a medium level. The minimum matching factor was set to 70. The compounds were identified by comparing the mass spectrometry data with the mass spectra of the reference standards obtained from the NIST library (2020 version). Eq. (1) was used to calculate the retention index (RI) of each compound using a normal alkane mixture (C7-C40) as the reference standard. The qualitative identification was further confirmed by comparing the calculated RI values with those reported in the literature (Yuan et al., 2022; Yuan et al., 2024).

where TR(x), TR(z), and TR(z + 1) represent the respective retention temperatures of the components and n-alkanes with carbon numbers of Z, Z + 1, and it holds that TR(z) < TR(x) < TR(z + 1).

A dedicated database for the target volatiles was constructed using previously described methods. Substances for which both the retention time and characteristic fragment ions met the specified parameters were defined as target compounds. The target ions in each group were detected separately within defined time windows, following the order of peak emergence. The compound was confirmed when the retention time of a detected chromatographic peak matched that of the reference standard and all the selected ions were present in the sample mass spectrum after background subtraction (Yuan et al., 2022; Yuan et al., 2024). The quantitative ions were then selected for peak integration and correction to improve the quantification accuracy.

Untargeted metabolomics analysis via ultra-high performance liquid chromatography-time of flight mass spectrometry (UHPLC-q-TOF)

The samples were vacuum freeze-dried and ground into a powder. Then, 50 mg of the powder was mixed with 1.2 mL of a 70 % methanol-water solution (−20 °C) and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 3 min, after which the supernatant was passed through a 0.22 μm filter for UHPLC-q-TOF analysis.

Next, 0.4 μL of the sample was eluted via high-performance liquid chromatography (LC-30 A, Shimadzu, Tokyo, Japan) equipped with an ACQUITY Premier HSS T3 column (1.8 μm i.d., 2.1 mm × 100 mm, Waters, Milford, USA). Mobile phase A consisted of 0.1 % CH3COOH-H2O, while mobile phase B comprised 0.1 % CH3COOH-C2H3N. The parameters included a column temperature of 40 °C and a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min. The gradient elution program included 5 %–20 % B for 0–2 min, 20 %–60 % B for 2–5 min, 60 %–99 % B for 5–6 min, 99 %–99 % B for 6–7.5 min, 99 %–5 % B for 7.5–7.6 min, and 5 %–5 % B for 7.6–10 min.

Electrospray ionization (ESI) was performed in both positive and negative modes at 5000 V and − 4000 V, respectively, and ion source temperatures of 550 °C and 450 °C. The other parameters included 50 psi, 60 psi, and 35 psi for ion source gas1, gas2, and curtain gas, respectively, and a collision energy of 15 V. The raw data were converted into mzXML format using ProteoWizard, while the XCMS software was employed for peak extraction, alignment, and retention time correction. The MS1 parent ion search library accepted a mass error of 25 ppm, while the MS2 search library allowed for a mass error of 50 ppm, with the retention time serving as a filtering criterion with a permissible deviation of 60 s. The scoring criteria for the MS2 database search were defined as follows: the fragment score contributed 10 %, the forward database search score contributed 30 %, and the reverse database search score contributed 60 %. Peaks with missing rates exceeding 50 % in any sample group were excluded, and missing values were imputed using the k-nearest neighbor method (Kokla et al., 2019). Peak areas were then corrected using the SVR method (Sánchez-Illana et al., 2018). The corrected and filtered peaks were then identified by comparing them against the in-house laboratory database, integrated public databases, and predictive databases, as well as by applying the metDNA method (Sánchez-Illana et al., 2018). Finally, the compounds in the quality control (QC) samples displaying the highest comprehensive scores and the lowest coefficients of variation were retained, while the positive and negative ionization modes were subsequently combined to generate the final data matrix.

Molecular docking

The crystal structure of the metabotropic glutamate receptor (PDB ID: 1EWK) was employed to construct a homology model using the SWISS-MODEL online service (Chang et al., 2024). The pdb files for glutamate and histidine-histidine-valine-alanine-tyrosine (His-His-Val-Ala-Tyr) were obtained from the Automated Topology Builder and Repository (version 3.0, https://atb.uq.edu.au/), while the receptor and ligand files were prepared using AutoDock 4.2.6. Semi-flexible molecular docking was performed using the AutoDock Vina program, while PyMOL (version 4.6.0) was employed to generate the images.

Statistical analysis

The results are presented as the mean ± standard deviation. The ANOVA least significant difference (LSD) test was used to assess the significant differences between the groups. A P-value below 0.05 indicated statistical significance. Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed using the muma R package. Orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) was employed to select the differential metabolites using the MetaboAnalystR package, while the mixOmics R package was used for sparse PLS (sPLS).

Results and discussion

Sinigrin influenced the quality of Zhacai fermented with and without LAB

As illustrated in Fig. 1B, natural dehydration resulted in color and morphological alterations, which were consistent with our previous findings (Li et al., 2019). Additionally, both natural and LAB-driven fermentation enhanced the color intensity of Zhacai (Fig. 1B). Sinigrin significantly influenced the L* and b* values of the Zhacai produced using Fuza No. 5. Additionally, the Zhacai fermented using the LAB from Fuza No. 5 and Fuyou 928 exhibited notably lower a* and b* values compared to naturally fermented samples (Fig. 1C). Neither the sinigrin nor the LAB treatments significantly altered the pH of the Zhacai, which remained between 4.4 and 4.6 across all samples (Fig. 1D). This pH range was notably lower than the previously reported values of 5.56 to 5.28 (Zhang et al., 2021). This discrepancy may be due to the increased production of organic acids in the samples during the first 30 d of fermentation, as suggested by Xiao et al. (2018).

Next, the sensory quality of the Zhacai was assessed (Fig. 1E and Fig. S1A, B). The Zhacai derived from Fuza No. 5 exhibited the most balanced sensory quality, yielding higher color values than Yongan xiaoye and Fuyou 928. In addition, natural Fuza No. 5 fermentation resulted in the best taste and mouthfeel, while LAB fermentation yielded the most favorable flavor profile in the Zhacai produced using Fuza No. 5. Moreover, sinigrin treatment decreased the taste and mouthfeel scores of the Fuza No. 5 Zhacai. Conversely, LAB fermentation enhanced the smell quality of Zhacai made from Fuza No. 5. As shown in Fig. 1F, the Zhacai fermented with LAB yielded higher total sensory scores than the other samples. These results validated the importance of LAB in enhancing Zhacai flavor development, as reported by previous studies (Yang et al., 2021). Moreover, Fuza No. 5 received the highest sensory evaluation scores regardless of whether natural or LAB fermentation was used (Fig. 1F). Therefore, Fuza No. 5 was used in the subsequent analysis, as it yielded high-quality Zhacai that was influenced by sinigrin and LAB.

Sinigrin changed the microbiome of the Zhacai fermented with and without LAB

A previous study has suggested that the abundance of Lactobacillus-related species, Halomonas spp., Idiomarina loihiensis, as well as the yeast Debaryomyces hansenii, were strongly correlated with the flavor compounds in Zhacai (Zhang et al., 2021). However, the LAB needed more than 30 d to become dominant during Zhacai fermentation (Zhang et al., 2021). The current study did not detect the Firmicutes phylum, including LAB, in the Zhacai fermented naturally for 30 d (Fig. 2A). The addition of LAB resulted in the emergence of the Firmicutes phylum as one of the microbial taxa. This suggests that adding LAB may promote flavor formation and reduce the fermentation time. As shown in Fig. 2B, the second principal component (PC) effectively discriminated between the Zhacai samples derived from natural fermentation and those obtained via LAB fermentation. However, PCA failed to distinguish between treatments with and without sinigrin. These findings indicated that the impact of added LAB on the microbial composition in the Zhacai was stronger than that of sinigrin treatment. A Venn diagram revealed that the inclusion of LAB caused a more pronounced variation in the microbial compositions compared to sinigrin treatment (Fig. 2C, Fig. 2S). The heatmap of the relative abundance (RA) at the genus level demonstrated the dominance of Lactiplantibacillus in the groups treated with LAB (Fig. 2D). This finding further implies that the addition of LAB may expedite the maturation process of the characteristic flavors in Zhacai. These observations were consistent with previous studies (Li et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2021), which identified Chryseobacterium and Acinetobacter as dominant bacterial genera in Zhacai. Furthermore, Psychrobacter and Acinetobacter represented the main bacterial genera in the Zhacai fermented with LAB. These genera are also important in Chinese pickles and have been related to flavor compounds (An et al., 2021). Yu et al. (2020) identified the main fermentation bacteria in chili pickles as Acinetobacter, Enterobacter, Pseudomonas, and Sphingomonas genera, all of which were also detected in the Zhacai samples in the current study (Fig. 2D). The α-diversity analysis revealed that LAB supplementation significantly enhanced the Chao1 and ACE values, indicating that the addition of LAB effectively increased microbial diversity in Zhacai (Fig. 2E). This finding further supports the previously hypothesized role of bacteria in shaping the α-diversity in pickles (Lin, Chen, Cai, Zhang, & Gao, 2022). Although sinigrin treatment also elevated bacterial α-diversity in the Zhacai, these increases lacked statistical significance (Fig. 2E). These results demonstrated that the addition of LAB had a more significant effect on the microbial community involved in the Zhacai fermentation process compared to sinigrin supplementation.

Sinigrin impacted volatile compound formation in the Zhacai fermented with and without LAB

In recent years, metabolomic analysis techniques based on GC–MS have gained popularity for evaluating flavor profiles (Fan et al., 2023). This study detected 1131 volatile compounds in the Zhacai using GC–MS, which were categorized into 15 distinct groups (Fig. 3A). This represented an 18-fold increase compared to a previous study (Zhang et al., 2021), providing a more comprehensive understanding of the volatile components in Zhacai. The main volatile compounds included esters, terpenoids, heterocyclic compounds, ketones, and alcohols. Notably, esters, alcohols, and heterocycles were also identified as the primary volatile metabolites in the Zhacai in prior research (Zhang et al., 2021). Next, the relative odor activity values (rOAV) for these compounds were calculated. As shown in Fig. 3B, the rOAV values of most compounds ranged between 0 and 2500. PCA was then conducted to evaluate the overall impact of the LAB and sinigrin on the flavor (Fig. 3C). The first PC effectively identified the presence of LAB during the Zhacai fermentation process, accounting for 67.6 % of the observed variation. However, none of the PCs effectively detected the presence of sinigrin during the Zhacai fermentation process (Fig. 3C). These findings also suggested that the LAB exerted a stronger influence on the flavor constituents of the Zhacai than sinigrin. A volcano plot was employed to identify the differential volatile compounds, focusing on molecules with fold changes >2 and P-values <0.05. Sinigrin intervention significantly altered 66 volatile components in the LAB-fermented Zhacai (Fig. 3D). Furthermore, the addition of LAB during the sinigrin treatment induced significant changes in 27 volatile compounds (Fig. 3E). However, neither sinigrin nor LAB notably affected the volatile components in the Zhacai when used individually (Figs. S3A and B). To further explore these findings, another PCA analysis was performed using the 83 differentially expressed volatile compounds. This subset proved more effective in distinguishing sinigrin- and LAB-treated Zhacai compared to analyzing all the compounds (Fig. 3F). These volatile compounds mainly affected the green, fruity, sweet, and herbal flavors in the Zhacai (Fig. 3G).

Sinigrin altered non-volatility compounds fermented with or without LAB

To address the limitations of GC–MS in analyzing the volatile components, the non-volatile components in the Zhacai were analyzed using untargeted metabolomics based on UHPLC-q-TOF (Yang et al., 2023). A total of 5519 non-volatile compounds were detected, including 3460 cationic and 2059 anionic substances. These compounds primarily included alkaloids, amino acids and derivatives, benzene and substituted derivatives, lipids, and organic acids (Fig. 4A). These results were consistent with previous findings on Zhacai metabonomics (Li et al., 2020). PCA yielded a similar score plot between the cationic and anionic compounds (Figs. 4B and C). Notably, regardless of the addition of sinigrin, the naturally fermented Zhacai was segregated from the LAB-fermented sample. Sinigrin significantly changed the non-volatile substance composition in the Zhacai fermented with LAB (Figs. 4B and C). An OPLS-DA model (Figs. S4A and B) was constructed to determine the variables responsible for these differences. Next, the volcano plots were used for univariate analysis to examine the screening results of the model. This process identified 1360 substances that differentiated between Zhacai fermented with LAB and those that were not, and confirmed the presence of sinigrin during fermentation (Fig. 4F). Of these substances, only 30 were common across all comparisons (Fig. 4G). A comparative analysis was performed to further examine the accumulated variable importance in projection (VIP) values for different metabolites in the various groups, categorized by substance. As shown in Figs. 4H-J and Fig. S4C, the different compounds primarily consisted of amino acids and their derivatives, as well as organic acids. These findings validated previous studies that elucidated the mechanism of flavor development in Zhacai by analyzing the relationships between microorganisms and organic or amino acids (Li et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2021). The results of the present study provided a multidimensional explanation for the pivotal role of amino and organic acids in characterizing the flavor profile of Zhacai. Furthermore, the accumulated VIP values from the substances altered by LAB (Fig. S4C) were lower than those affected by sinigrin (Fig. 4J). However, combined treatment with LAB and sinigrin resulted in the most pronounced changes in the accumulated VIPs of amino acids and their derivatives, as well as organic acids (Figs. 4H and I). These results confirmed the influence of LAB on the amino and organic acids in the Zhacai and highlighted the novel impact of sinigrin on these compounds.

The correlation between the flavor substances and the volatile and non-volatile compounds altered by sinigrin

Previous studies have extensively explored the effect of LAB on the flavor profile of Zhacai (Li et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2021). This work shifts the focus to investigating the influence of sinigrin on the formation of flavor compounds in Zhacai. The correlations between the sinigrin-induced compounds and sensory attributes, such as smell and taste, were evaluated. Fig. 5A and B illustrate the volatile and non-volatile compounds, respectively, that are strongly associated with taste and smell. The taste of the Zhacai was positively correlated with 2,4,6-cycloheptatrien-1-one and 2-hydroxy-4-(1-methylethyl)-, which presented a woody aroma and inhibited both fungal and bacterial activities (Morita et al., 2004). However, sinigrin treatment reduced the 2,4,6-cycloheptatrien-1-one and 2-hydroxy-4-(1-methylethyl)- content in the Zhacai (Fig. 5C). Conversely, 1-hepten-3-one negatively influenced taste and showed no strong correlation with smell. This could be attributed to the herbaceous and metallic odor characteristics of 1-hepten-3-one (Bravo et al., 1992). Additionally, 2-isothiocyanatoethyl-benzene was identified as a degradation product of gluconasturtiin, a glucosinolate found in Zhacai (Li et al., 2024). Previous research also identified 2-isothiocyanatoethyl-benzene as an important volatile compound in Zhacai, recognized for its hot and pungent aroma (Liu, Li et al., 2009). This accounted for the significant negative correlation between 2-isothiocyanatoethyl-benzene and the smell of Zhacai. Although sinigrin slightly increased the 2-isothiocyanatoethyl-benzene and 1-hepten-3-one levels in the naturally fermented Zhacai, sinigrin dramatically reduced the content of these compounds in the LAB-fermented Zhacai (Fig. 5C). Sinigrin could further decrease these undesirable flavor substances. Studies have shown that 10-undecenal is a characteristic flavor compound in Hawk tea yogurt, presenting a rose, mandarin, and citrus aroma (Zhang et al., 2013). Although 10-undecenal was negatively correlated with the smell of Zhacai, it displayed a slight positive association with taste. The combined addition of sinigrin and LAB markedly reduced the 10-undecenal content in the Zhacai (Fig. 5C), possibly because sinigrin inhibited 10-undecenal production by the LAB. Although sinigrin exhibited the ability to elevate the 8-cyclohexadecen-1-one and pyrazine methyl-aromatic compound levels (Hang et al., 2021; Weenen, 1998), these two substances demonstrated a strong negative correlation with the taste of Zhacai (Fig. 5A, Fig. S5).

This study identified the top 21 compounds most closely associated with taste and smell (Fig. 5B). Of these, Galbeta1,3GluNAc (LNB), an oligosaccharide found in milk, promotes probiotic proliferation and provides a sweet taste (Nishimoto & Kitaoka, 2007). Similarly, 10E,12Z-Octadecadienoic acid (OA) is considered a precursor for flavor synthesis, particularly lipid-derived aldehydes and alcohols (Aziz et al., 2015). Therefore, elevated LNB and OA levels enhanced the gustatory attributes of Zhacai, while sinigrin treatment notably decreased the related concentrations of these substances (Fig. 5D). Additionally, the His-His-Val-Ala-Tyr pentapeptide exhibited a positive correlation with Zhacai taste, acting as a potential umami substance. Molecular docking is a valuable tool for determining umami taste in peptides (Jia, Yang, Liao, Wu, & Yang, 2024). Therefore, molecular docking was employed to preliminarily determine whether His-His-Val-Ala-Tyr acted as a potential umami peptide. The His-His-Val-Ala-Tyr peptide could not access the active site of the umami receptor, unlike glutamate, which could achieve this (Fig. 5E). These findings suggested that sinigrin played a significant role in the development of the flavor profile of Zhacai, while also indicating that sinigrin and its metabolites reduced flavor quality.

Conclusion

This study confirmed that LAB promoted the formation of pleasant flavors in the Zhacai. Moreover, the interaction between the microorganisms and sinigrin significantly influenced Zhacai flavor modulation. However, sinigrin may not be conducive to producing a desirable Zhacai flavor. Further investigation is necessary to determine whether the glucose produced via sinigrin degradation positively affects Zhacai flavor formation. It is essential to closely monitor glucosinolates such as sinigrin and their hydrolytic products during flavor regulation. This approach may be vital for producing high-quality Zhacai.

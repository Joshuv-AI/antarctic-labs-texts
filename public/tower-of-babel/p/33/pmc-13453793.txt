Saline-immersion technique for endoscopic submucosal dissection with traction for an ileocecal adenoma involving the ileal outlet
Authors: Georgios Mavrogenis, Stamatina Vogli, Dimitrios Madianos
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and AimsEndoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) of lesions involving the ileocecal valve and terminal ileum is technically challenging. The saline-immersion technique for ESD combined with countertraction may improve visualization, safety, and dissection efficiency in these complex locations.MethodsA 71-year-old patient underwent ESD for a 4-cm nongranular flat adenoma involving the entire ileocecal valve and extending into the distal terminal ileum. The procedure was performed with the patient under general anesthesia using a pediatric colonoscope (PCF-H190TL; Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) with a tapered hood (ST Hood; Fujifilm, Tokyo, Japan). After chromoendoscopic margin delineation, the lumen was filled with saline solution, and submucosal dissection was completed under saline immersion using ENDO CUT current (Erbe VIO 3, Tübingen, Germany). Two clip-and-band complexes provided internal countertraction to improve exposure and facilitate controlled dissection.ResultsEn bloc resection, including the ileocecal valve and ileal extension, was achieved. Hemostasis was secured with hot biopsy forceps, and partial defect closure was performed with clips. The postoperative course was uneventful, and the patient was discharged after 48 hours. Oral budesonide was administered for 2 months to reduce the risk of stricture. At 3-month follow-up, the patient was asymptomatic without endoscopic recurrence.ConclusionsUnder–saline solution ESD with clip-and-band traction appears safe and effective for extensive ileocecal lesions, providing stable visualization, reliable countertraction, and easy access to the submucosal space.

Introduction

Endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) is an established technique for en bloc resection of large colorectal polyps. However, lesions involving the ileocecal valve and terminal ileum remain technically challenging because of unstable scope positioning, paradoxical movements, and abundant submucosal adipose tissue that obscures the dissection plane.1, 2, 3, 4

The saline-immersion technique for ESD5 has been introduced as a strategy to improve visualization and tissue handling by exploiting the optical and physical properties of saline immersion. In addition, internal traction methods2,4,6 can enhance exposure of the submucosal layer and facilitate controlled dissection of the ileocecal valve.2 However, the combination of these 2 techniques has not yet been broadly adapted for the resection of ileocecal lesions.

Case description and technique

A 71-year-old patient was referred for endoscopic resection of a 4-cm nongranular flat adenoma (Narrow-Band Imaging Expert Team 2A) involving a major portion of the ileocecal valve and the ileal outlet (Figs. 1 and 2, Video 1, available online at www.videogie.org). A pediatric colonoscope (PCF-H190TL; Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) fitted with a tapered hood (ST Hood; Fujifilm, Tokyo, Japan) was used to improve maneuverability. The colonic lumen was then filled with normal saline solution, and the lesion margins were delineated using narrow-band imaging (Fig. 3).Figure 3Delineation of the ileal portion of the lesion.

Delineation of the ileal portion of the lesion.

After a circumferential mucosal incision (Fig. 4) with a ball-type knife (FlushKnife BTs 1.5; Fujifilm), submucosal dissection was performed under saline solution using Endocut current (ENDO CUT Q, effect 2, duration 3, interval 3; Erbe VIO 3, Tübingen, Germany) to reduce the production of bubbles and to cut efficiently the rich adipose tissue that was encountered at the submucosa. Sustained submucosal lifting was maintained with the knife's water-jet function, allowing precise identification of the dissection plane despite the presence of adipose tissue. To improve exposure and counteract gravity and scope instability, internal countertraction was applied using 2 clip-and-band complexes (Figs. 5 and 6), which were sequentially repositioned as dissection progressed.Figure 4Mucosal incision and trimming within the terminal ileum, involving approximately two-thirds of its circumference. Results

En bloc resection of the lesion was successfully achieved (Figs. 7 and 8), including the entire ileocecal valve and the involved segment of the terminal ileum. Adequate hemostasis was secured using hot biopsy forceps. Partial closure of the mucosal defect was performed with multiple clips to reduce the risk of delayed adverse events (Fig. 9).Figure 7Exposed ileocecal valve at the end of the procedure.Figure 8Clip closure of the postresection defect, with preservation of terminal ileum patency.Figure 9Resected specimen.

The patient had an uneventful postoperative course and was discharged after 48 hours of observation.

Given the absence of established preventive strategies for ileal stricture after extensive ESD, we empirically administered budesonide (Budenofalk 9-mg granules; Dr Falk Pharma GmbH, Freiburg, Germany) once daily for 2 months.

At 9-month follow-up, the patient remained asymptomatic, and surveillance endoscopy demonstrated complete healing of the resection site with no evidence of residual or recurrent adenoma.

Discussion

ESD involving the ileocecal valve and terminal ileum is among the most challenging colorectal ESD scenarios. Saline immersion improves visualization by magnifying the submucosal space, reducing smoke and splashing of fat, and counterbalancing gravitational forces.7 When combined with clip-and-band traction, the saline-immersion technique for ESD provides stable and sustained exposure of the submucosal layer, facilitating safe and efficient dissection even in areas with significant adipose tissue.

Key technical considerations include maintaining stable saline immersion, frequent flushing to minimize bubble formation, and the use of ENDO CUT current for efficient tissue division under saline solution. Internal traction should be dynamically adjusted during the procedure to optimize visualization and facilitate access to the submucosal space.

Conclusion

The saline-immersion technique for ESD combined with clip-and-band traction is a feasible and effective approach for the en bloc resection of extensive adenomas of the ileocecal valve.

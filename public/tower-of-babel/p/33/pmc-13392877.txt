Circular RNAs in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma: What do we know so far?
Authors: Mingxin Yang, Jun Ye, Jiahao Xiong, Yuhua Chen, Yanlei Zhao, Zhitong Wang, Shun Xu, Jiajun Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryEsophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is a highly lethal malignancy, with limited early diagnostic tools and effective therapeutic targets. Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are a class of stable non-coding RNAs that have emerged as crucial regulators in the process of ESCC tumorigenesis, highlighting their great application potential in ESCC diagnosis and treatment. This review provides a comprehensive overview of current advances and prospects of circRNAs in ESCC. We provide an overview of the biogenesis mechanism of circRNAs and summarize their expression and biological functions in ESCC. Among them, we specifically emphasize the mechanism by which circRNAs regulate ESCC through interaction with proteins and the role of circRNAs in regulating the tumor microenvironment. The review also systematically summarizes the existing circRNA research databases and the use of high-throughput techniques to study dysregulated circRNA expressions. In addition, we discuss the translational potential of emerging technologies, including advanced circRNA detection tools, targeted delivery systems, and circRNA-based vaccines, which are expected to open up new avenues for ESCC diagnosis and treatment. circRNAs are central regulators of ESCC tumor progression, and they have the dual potential as biomarkers and therapeutic targets. The latest developments in emerging technologies for circRNA-based treatment have shown encouraging results, but the transformation of circRNA-based strategies into clinical practice is still limited by small sample sizes, lack of multicenter verification, and inconsistent detection methods. Future research should continue to carry out mechanism verification, multi-omics integration, and prospective clinical trials to promote its transformation into ESCC therapies.Keywords: circRNA, esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, molecular mechanism, database resources, biomarkers

Introduction

Esophageal cancer (EC) is one of the most common malignant tumors worldwide and a major cause of cancer-related deaths. EC represents a significant global health burden, with approximately 511,000 reported new cases and 445,000 deaths annually. EC is classified into two histological subtypes: esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) and esophageal adenocarcinoma. China has a high incidence of EC, with ESCC accounting for more than 90% of EC cases.1 Known risk factors for ESCC include alcohol, pickled or hot foods, and low socioeconomic status.2 Clinically, ESCC first shows symptoms such as dysphagia and weight loss when the disease has progressed to an intermediate or advanced stage with lymph node or distant metastasis. As a result, the five-year survival rate for these patients is below 20%.3 ESCC pathogenesis includes different polygenic drivers and a multistage progression.4 Consequently, there is an urgent need to investigate the molecular mechanisms of ESCC progression in order to identify novel and potentially critical therapeutic targets and prognostic biomarkers.

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are key members of the non-coding RNAs that have attracted research interest in recent years. They were first discovered in the viroids of plants in 1976.5 Their presence was later confirmed in eukaryotic cells.6 With the rapid development of high-throughput sequencing and dedicated bioinformatics analyses, more than 2.6 million unique circRNAs have been annotated across 20 species, which is far more numerous than protein-coding genes.7 However, the vast majority of mammalian circRNAs are expressed at extremely low levels and are largely non-functional products of splicing errors, with more than 97% of circRNA production being inert.8 Meanwhile, many circRNAs have been reported to have abnormal expression in multisystem diseases.9 Unlike linear RNAs, circRNAs have a covalently closed single-stranded structure that is responsible for their unique molecular characteristics. circRNAs lack a 5′ cap and a 3′ poly(A) tail, allowing them to effectively resist exonuclease degradation, which gives them higher stability than most linear RNAs, supporting their potential as biomarkers.10

The present review systematically summarizes circRNA cyclization mechanisms, explains how abnormally expressed circRNAs regulate ESCC progression, and evaluates their potential as diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers.

Literature search strategy

This work is a narrative review and does not follow a formal systematic review methodology, and the literature search and selection strategy is designed to organize and summarize the existing research findings on circRNAs related to ESCC. We searched the PubMed (MEDLINE) database for articles regarding ESCC-associated circRNAs from inception to August 31, 2025, using the following search terms: “esophageal squamous cell carcinoma,” “esophageal cancer,” “non-coding RNA,” “circular RNAs,” “biomarker,” “prognosis,” “diagnosis,” “mechanism,” “ceRNA,” “expression,” and “signaling pathway.” Records excluded were letters to the editor, editorials, research protocols, case reports, brief correspondence, animal studies, and studies involving multiple cancer types. Additional records were identified through reference screening of the included articles.

Following database searching, a total of 295 records met the initial eligibility criteria and were subjected to a stepwise selection process including title and abstract screening, followed by full-text assessment. Study selection prioritized peer-reviewed journals with established editorial standards and emphasized studies that investigated the molecular mechanisms and potential clinical applicability. Articles of borderline relevance or methodological uncertainty, prior to their final inclusion, were discussed among members of the research team. Ultimately, a total of 151 studies were included in the narrative synthesis.

Biogenesis of circRNAs

Typically, precursor messenger RNAs (pre-mRNAs) transcribed by RNA polymerase II contain exons and introns. After being transcribed, pre-mRNAs are spliced at the canonical splice sites (5′-GU and 3′-AG at introns) by spliceosomes, which ensures their maturity and translatability.11 Typically, pre-mRNAs generate mature mRNAs through constitutive or alternative splicing. In addition, a special type of pre-mRNA splicing known as back-splicing links the downstream 5′ splice site to the upstream 3' splice site, forming covalently closed circRNAs.12 Furthermore, circRNAs are classified into three main subtypes according to their sequence composition as follows: exonic circRNAs (EcRNAs), exon-intron circRNAs (EIciRNAs), and circular intronic RNAs (ciRNAs). EcRNAs originate from exons and are mostly located in the cytoplasm where some may regulate gene expression by acting as microRNA (miRNA) sponges. EIciRNAs are formed from both exonic and intronic sequences. ciRNAs form exclusively from intronic sequences. Both EIciRNAs and ciRNAs are enriched in the nucleus where they are involved in transcriptional regulation.13

Current understanding of circRNA biogenesis includes three canonical models (Figure 1). One model involves intron pairing-driven circularization, where cis-elements called intronic complementary sequences (ICSs) are located in the introns flanking circRNA-forming exons.14 These elements promote complementary base pairing, which reduces the spatial distance between the upstream and downstream exons. The alternative splicing machinery then promotes exon circularization, eventually producing circRNAs.15 Notably, Alu elements serve as the major repetitive ICSs that mediate intron pairing-driven circRNA circularization; this Alu-mediated circularization is primarily suppressed by DHX9, and ADAR acts synergistically with DHX9 to further inhibit this process in normal cells.16 The second model deals with RNA-binding protein (RBP)-associated pairing-driven circularization, which involves trans-acting RBPs that recognize specific cis-elements in the intronic or other non-exonic regions. These RBPs bring the upstream 5′ splice site and downstream 3′ splice site closer together and promote the back-splicing of exons to form circRNAs.17 The third model is lariat-driven circularization, which refers to the reverse ligation of splice acceptor and splice donor sites during back-splicing to generate exon-containing circRNAs. Core spliceosomal factors like U1 and U2 small nuclear ribonucleoproteins (snRNPs) directly recognize and bind to cis-elements in this process and then collaborate with RNA Pol II to catalyze reverse ligation and promote exon circularization.18 Notably, intronic lariats excised during mRNA splicing can avoid degradation and form ciRNAs. These ciRNAs typically contain GU-rich and C-rich sequences. Their enhanced structural stability gives them resistance to debranching reactions.13

(F) circRNAs can be translated into functional peptides.

(G) Nuclear circRNAs associate with RNA polymerase II and U1 small nuclear ribonucleoprotein to regulate associated gene transcription.

Research shows that cis-elements (ICSs) and trans-factors (RBPs) can act independently or synergistically to promote back-splicing. Deletion or mutation of cis-elements suppresses circRNA biogenesis.19 New circRNAs can be produced in cancer cells when chromosomal translocations form new ICS pairs.20 Most introns have abundant repetitive elements, which promote intronic pairing among different ICS groups and thus support complex circRNA expression patterns. For instance, base pairing between ICSs in a single intron promotes linear RNA biogenesis.21 In contrast, pairing between ICS combinations containing alternative back-splice sites can activate their selection, enabling a single gene locus to generate multiple circRNA isoforms.15 RBPs are core trans-factors in RBP-driven circularization, including serine-arginine proteins and heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoproteins, which regulate circRNA expression through binding to intronic cis-elements flanking circularized exons.22

Growing evidence shows that different cis-elements and trans-acting RBPs collaboratively regulate circRNA levels in ESCC. For instance, trans-acting factors p53 and RBM25 act synergistically as a core upstream regulatory module for circCNTNAP3. p53 transcriptionally activates RBM25 expression, while RBM25 binds a CGGGCA motif cis-splicing enhancer in CNTNAP3 pre-mRNA and promotes back-splicing of exons 5–9 to generate circCNTNAP3.23 Similarly, circ0006168 biogenesis depends on cis-N⁶-methyladenosine (m6A) motifs and the trans-factors METTL3 and IGF2BP2. METTL3 recognizes the RRACH m6A consensus motif in circ0006168 after exons 2–4 of CNOT6L undergo back-splicing and catalyzes its m6A modification. IGF2BP2 then binds to the m6A-modified circRNA and enhances circ0006168 stability to facilitate intracellular accumulation.24 circCPSF6 arises from back-splicing of CPSF6 exons 2–9, the process promoted by the RBP CPSF6.25 circ0001165 is generated by back-splicing of NCOA3 pre-mRNA exons 4–9, and EIF4A3 binds to cis-elements to stimulate circ0001165 biogenesis.26 Finally, circRUNX1 originates from RUNX1 pre-mRNA back-splicing and is stabilized by IGF2BP2.27

Following back-splicing and maturation in the nucleus, the majority of circRNAs are translocated to the cytoplasm to exert biological functions. Their nuclear export to the cytoplasm is regulated by distinct mechanisms, one of which is a length-dependent mechanism. For example, in human cells, UAP56 (DDX39B) and URH49 (DDX39A), the two homologs of Drosophila Hel25E, specifically mediate the nuclear export of long and short circRNAs, respectively.28 The other represents a dedicated nuclear export pathway for circRNAs. By directly binding to circRNAs, the adaptor protein IGF2BP1 recruits Ran-GTP and exportin-2 to mediate circRNA nuclear export, employing a mechanism analogous to protein export but distinct from canonical mRNA export.29

Regulatory circRNA mechanisms in ESCC

circRNAs play significant roles in tumor progression regulation due to their unique structural and functional characteristics. Evidence increasingly shows that circRNAs contribute to ESCC pathogenesis through diverse molecular mechanisms, such as interactions with miRNAs and proteins, peptide encoding, and gene regulation.30 The following section details the specific mechanisms by which circRNAs regulate ESCC cells (Figure 1).

miRNA sponges

Some circRNAs that localize in the cytoplasm have multiple miRNA-binding sites in their structure, giving them a strong capacity to bind to miRNAs. As typical competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs), circRNAs can regulate gene expression by sponging miRNAs.31 This function reduces the post-transcriptional target gene repression by miRNAs and modulates gene expression involved in tumor progression regulation.32 Interestingly, circRNAs act as reservoirs by sponging miRNAs in specific situations.33

circFNDC3B

The FNDC3B gene produces two circRNA isoforms that are collectively called circFNDC3B. circ0001361 arises from exons 2–3, with a mature length of 215 bp, while circ0006948 originates from exons 2–4 and measures 292 nt. Both isoforms are highly expressed and promote ESCC progression.34,35 Among them, circ0006948 has been more studied. It enhances LASP1 expression by sponging miR-3612, thus aggravating ESCC by increasing the metabolic activity.36 Moreover, circ0006948 binds to miR-214-3p to increase CDC25A expression, promoting proliferation and metastasis.37 circ0006948 binds to miR-490-3p to upregulate HMGA2, inducing epithelial-mesenchymal transition and promoting metastasis.38 circ0006948 also binds to miR-136-5p to reduce its repression of MAPK1.39 ESCC-derived exosome circ0006948 binds to miR-490-5p in ESCC cells, leading to TXNRD1 upregulation.35 The other isoform circ0001361 has also attracted research interest. circ0001361 promotes proliferation, migration, and invasion of ESCC cells and suppresses apoptosis via the miR-370-3p-MYO5A and miR-136-5p-MYO5A axes.34,40

circCNOT6L

circCNOT6L (circ0006168) is highly expressed in ESCC, with elevated levels correlating with larger tumor size, positive lymph node metastasis, and advanced TNM stage. circCNOT6L upregulates multiple targets by binding to miR-384. It increases STAT3 levels to stimulate proliferation, migration, and invasion24 and raises FN1 levels to induce intracellular iron metabolism disturbances.41 It also upregulates RBBP7, which augments glucose consumption and lactate production via S6K/S6 signaling to strengthen ESCC glycolytic capacity.42 Additionally, circCNOT6L targets miR-516b-5p to upregulate XBP143 and binds to miR-100 to elevate mTOR levels,44 thus promoting the malignant biological behaviors of ESCC. Furthermore, circCNOT6L enhances taxol resistance in ESCC cells by binding to miR-194-5p to increase JMJD1C levels.45

Several circRNAs regulate ESCC progression through ceRNA mechanisms. For example, ciRS-7 is the first identified circRNA that functions as an miRNA sponge.46 It also acts in ESCC by sequestering miR-876-5p to relieve MAGE-A family repression and enhancing ESCC cell malignancy.47 Similarly, circCD44 binds to miR-23b-5p to relieve its TAB1 inhibition, which activates the TAB1-NF-κB pathway and drives tumor malignancy.48 IGF2BP2 binds to and stabilizes circRUNX1. Then, circRUNX1 binds to miR-449b-5p to upregulate FOXP3.27 Other circRNAs also contribute to ESCC progression as ceRNAs (Table 1).

Summary of circRNAs acting as miRNA sponges

Importantly, miRNA-circRNA interactions do not always result in miRNA suppression. circRNAs also function as a reservoir or a stabilizing miRNA factor.146 ZEB1 binds to the DOCK5 promoter to downregulate eIF4A3 and inhibit circDOCK5 biogenesis. circDOCK5 maintains miR-627-3p stability by serving as a reservoir. miR-627-3p targets TGFB2 to inhibit TGF-β secretion. Elevated TGF-β further promotes ZEB1 expression, forming a positive feedback loop of ZEB1-circ-DOCK5-miR-627-3p-TGF-β-ZEB1, promoting ESCC cell metastasis.147

In addition, existing studies have pointed out that the vast majority of circRNAs do not have more miRNA-binding sites than expected by chance, suggesting their limited potential to serve as endogenous miRNA sponges.148 Additionally, a meaningful stoichiometry is required between the miRNA recognition elements on circRNAs and target mRNAs. Such a stoichiometric balance is rarely achieved in studies because most circRNAs claimed to be miRNA sponges have very few miRNA-binding sites and are expressed at low levels.149 Only a few circRNAs, such as ciRS-7 and circZNF91, satisfy this requirement.150

Interaction with proteins

Different from most common regulatory miRNA sponge mechanisms, circRNAs also exert their functions through interactions with proteins.151 In ESCC, circRNA-protein interactions are a key molecular mechanism in tumorigenesis and progression that occur via four modes: tethering or sequestering proteins, forming circRNA-protein-mRNA ternary complexes, altering protein-protein interactions, and translocating or redistributing proteins.152 These modes and their roles in ESCC are discussed next (Figures 2 and 3). Summary of circRNAs interacting with proteins or encoding peptides

Tethering or sequestering proteins

A circRNA combines with only one protein to compromise the protein’s original function. Relevant studies in ESCC can be classified into two types based on the downstream consequences: circRNA blocks proteins from interacting with RNA or other proteins. For instance, METTL3 mediates m6A modification of circCREBBP, enabling circCREBBP to bind to the m6A reader IGF2BP3. circCREBBP binding to IGF2BP3 disrupts this interaction because IGF2BP3 upregulates MYC mRNA and promotes its stability, downregulating MYC and increasing ESCC radiosensitivity.153 Similarly, the m6A reader IGF2BP1 recognizes m6A sites on ACSBG1 mRNA and promotes its stability. However, circSLC22A3 competitively binds to IGF2BP1 to disrupt this function, reducing ACSBG1 mRNA stability.154

circRNA-protein interactions in ESCC can block proteins from interacting other proteins. For instance, circGSK3β is highly expressed in ESCC and promotes ESCC cell migration and invasion. Studies have shown that β-catenin is phosphorylated by GSK3β and sequentially ubiquitinated and targeted for degradation.173 circGSK3β can decrease β-catenin phosphorylation and its sequential ubiquitination to inhibit its kinase activity through interaction with GSK3β.155 Similarly, ESCC cell-derived exosomes promote lymphangiogenesis by transferring circ0026611 into human lymphatic endothelial cells (HLECs). There, circ0026611 interacts with NAA10 to inhibit NAA10-mediated PROX1 acetylation with subsequent ubiquitination and degradation.156 The deubiquitinase USP7 removes ubiquitin molecules from substrates and prevents substrate degradation.174 circNF1 binds competitively to ANXA1, impeding the interaction of USP7 with ANXA1 and subsequently leading to ANXA1 degradation. Strikingly, the weakened binding of USP7 to ANXA1 instead enhances USP7 binding to PD-L1, decreasing PD-L1 degradation and promoting ESCC cell immune escape.157 Furthermore, UQCRC2 interacts with UQCRC1 to form a core dimer in mitochondrial complex III, which helps pump hydrion from the mitochondrial matrix to the mitochondrial intermembrane space and enhances oxidative phosphorylation.175 circPUM1 originates from the nuclear genome and is localized to the mitochondria. It is upregulated in CoCl2-induced hypoxic ESCC cells. circPUM1 regulates the oxidative phosphorylation of mitochondria by interacting with UQCRC2 and preventing mitochondrial complex III assembly.158

Translocating proteins

circRNA binds to proteins and facilitates nuclear protein import in ESCC. Nuclear translocation of CtBP1 usually depends on dimerization with its homolog CtBP2. However, circIMMP2L binds to CtBP1 in the cytoplasm to facilitate its nuclear entry independent of CtBP2.159 Similarly, circJPH1 interacts with XRCC6 to promote its nuclear translocation, thus activating the NF-κB pathway. Activated NF-κB upregulates HERC5, which supports the malignant ESCC progression.160

Forming circRNA-protein-mRNA complexes

circRNAs in ESCC typically enable the interaction between proteins and mRNAs by forming circRNA-protein-mRNA complexes, enhancing mRNA stability and promoting translation. For example, circCD44 binds to EIF4A3 and GBX2 mRNA to form a circCD44-EIF4A3-GBX2 mRNA complex, consequently stabilizing and upregulating GBX2 and promoting ESCC progression and angiogenesis.161 Similarly, circCD44 forms a circCD44-METTL3-HMBOX1 mRNA complex to enhance HMBOX1 mRNA m6A modification, elevating the expression of both HMBOX1 mRNA and protein, thus promoting ESCC progression.162 circFIRRE binds to HNRNPC and GLI2 mRNA in the cytoplasm and stabilizes GLI2 mRNA, thus activating the Hedgehog pathway.163

Another study investigated this mechanism more thoroughly, showing that circTMEM45A binds to the m5C reader ALYREF, which recognizes m5C sites on NLRP3 mRNA to form a circTMEM45A-ALYREF-NLRP3 mRNA complex and promotes NLRP3 mRNA nuclear export to the cytoplasm. Subsequently, circTMEM45A interacts with YBX1 and NLRP3 mRNA to form a complex in the cytoplasm and promotes YBX1-mediated m5C recognition on NLRP3 mRNA, thus stabilizing and upregulating NLRP3 and then activating caspase-1. Additionally, circTMEM45A binds to U2AF2 and IL1B mRNA and enhances IL1B mRNA stability and translation into pro-IL-1β. Activated caspase-1 then promotes pro-IL-1β maturation, increasing IL-1β synthesis and secretion.164

Altering interactions between proteins

circRNAs can also serve as a molecular adaptor to enhance protein-protein interactions in ESCC. circDOPEY2 is downregulated in cisplatin-resistant ESCC cells. circDOPEY2 recruits TRIM25 as well as binds to CPEB4, enhancing TRIM25-mediated CPEB4 degradation. Because CPEB4 promotes Mcl-1 mRNA translation, circDOPEY2-induced CPEB4 depletion reduces Mcl-1 expression, thus promoting cisplatin-induced apoptosis.165 circ231 is highly expressed in ESCC and promotes ESCC cell proliferation and invasion. circ231 binds to a double-stranded RBP, STAU1, and helicase eIF4A3 to form a complex. This complex recognizes and binds to the secondary structures in the TPI1 and PRDX6 mRNAs and unfolds them to promote translation.166 Additionally, circIMMP2L facilitates the interaction between CtBP1 and HDAC1 in the nucleus, subsequently inhibiting E-cadherin and p21 transcription.159

Other manners

Three additional studies have also verified circRNA-protein interactions. However, because detailed mechanistic research is lacking, they cannot be readily classified. circCYP24A1 and circPRKAR1B are highly expressed in ESCC, and both bind to PKM2 to mediate NF-κB phosphorylation. NF-κB then translocates to the nucleus and promotes the transcription of its downstream genes CCL3 and CCL5. The upregulation of CCL3 and CCL5 promotes their secretion, thereby enhancing ESCC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion.167,168 Additionally, circPRKCA interacts with YBX1 to inhibit YBX1 ubiquitination and degradation. Accumulated YBX1 acts as an m5C reader and binds to m5C sites on CSF2 mRNA, stabilizing and upregulating CSF2 and promoting ESCC metastasis.169

circRNAs encode peptides or proteins

Early studies have suggested that circRNAs lack protein-coding ability, as their closed circular structure prevents translation through the canonical eukaryotic initiation pathway.176 However, a study in 2017 first demonstrated that circRNAs can encode functional polypeptides, confirming their translational potential.177 Existing studies have demonstrated that ESCC relies on internal ribosomal entry site (IRES)-dependent translation initiation. circRNAs recruit the 40S ribosomal subunit and translation initiation factors via intrinsic circRNA IRES sequences. The open reading frame (ORF) on the circRNA serves as a template to mediate protein synthesis.178

circUBE4B is highly expressed in ESCC and encodes a 173-amino-acid polypeptide (circUBE4B-173-aa), which is initiated by IRES-mediated ORF translation. The polypeptide promotes ESCC cell proliferation by activating the MAPK-ERK pathway by binding to MAPK1, thus enhancing ESCC cell proliferation.170 circ8199 is downregulated in ESCC and affects cell proliferation and migration. It initiates circ8199-235-aa translation via the IRES sequence upstream of the ORF. Because circ8199-235-aa binds to O-GlcNAc transferase (OGT) and inhibits OGT enzyme activity, the decrease in circ8199 expression stimulates the binding activity between OGT and its downstream gene JAK2, promoting O-GlcNAc glycosylation modification of JAK2 and activating the JAK2-STAT3 pathway.171 circPDE5A is also downregulated in ESCC. It encodes PDE5A-500-aa, initiating the IRES-mediated ORF translation. PDE5A-500-aa binds to PIK3IP1, promotes USP14-mediated PIK3IP1 deubiquitination, stabilizes PIK3IP1, and further inhibits the PI3K-AKT pathway to suppresses ESCC cell proliferation and metastasis.172 All of these circRNAs have been organized in Table 2.

Although relevant studies have reported the translational potential of circRNAs, the coding capacity of circRNAs remains highly controversial, with substantial opposing evidence. For example, Ho-Xuan et al. revealed that the protein expression detected in circZNF609 overexpression systems is derived from linear splicing by-products of plasmids, rather than the circRNA itself.179 Additionally, Hansen challenged mainstream approaches for validating circRNA translation, demonstrating that circRNA-specific RiboSeq reads and mass spectrometry-identified peptides are mostly background noise and false discoveries.180 Nicolet et al. demonstrated that despite widespread predicted ORF during terminal hematopoiesis, robust ribosome profiling combined with mass spectrometry analysis barely detected endogenous translation products of circRNAs.181 Stagsted et al. comprehensively assessed the translational potential of AUG circRNAs and other circRNAs, thus solidly demonstrating that there is no credible evidence for circRNA translation or peptide production.182 These studies underscore core technical pitfalls in the circRNA translation field, which demand stringent experimental design to confirm bona fide circRNA translation.

circRNAs participate in gene expression regulation

Beyond their cytoplasmic roles, certain nuclear-localized circRNAs, such as EIciRNAs and ciRNAs, can directly regulate parental gene transcription through cis-regulatory elements in their sequences. EIciRNAs can combine with U1 snRNP and then interact with RNA Pol II to enhance parental gene expression.152 circEIF3J and circPAIP2 localize to the promoters of their respective parental genes EIF3J and PAIP2 via their intrinsic U1 snRNP-binding motifs and form a complex with U1 snRNP and RNA Pol II to promote the transcription of their parental genes.183 Likewise, ciRNAs are accumulated at their synthesis sites and increase parental gene expression by modulating RNA Pol II elongation activity. For example, ci-ankrd52 largely accumulates at its transcription sites, associates with RNA Pol II elongation machinery, and acts as a positive regulator of RNA Pol II transcription.13 However, the functional significance of these nuclear circRNA-mediated transcriptional mechanisms in ESCC remains largely unexplored.

Biological function of circRNAs in ESCC

The abnormally expressed circRNAs in ESCC can significantly promote the malignant biological behaviors of cancer cells. These circRNAs regulate key biological processes, including cell proliferation, apoptosis, cell cycle, metastasis, metabolism, and drug resistance (Figure 4). The functions of circRNAs as novel ESCC tumor microenvironment (TME) regulators are summarized next (Figure 5). Moreover, the detailed biological functions and regulatory mechanisms of the ESCC-associated circRNAs are summarized in Tables 1 and 2. Proliferation, apoptosis, and cell cycle

Cell proliferation and apoptosis are highly regulated events in cells that together maintain tissue homeostasis.184 The cell cycle is normally a tightly regulated process with multiple control points at different cell growth phases, including cyclins, cyclin-dependent kinases, and their inhibitors. These may be inactivated or mutated in cancer cells and lead to uncontrolled proliferation and apoptosis resistance, which result in abnormal cell accumulation and consequent cancer progression.185

Numerous circRNAs in ESCC have been identified to dysregulate cellular processes via diverse molecular mechanisms. For example, circ0006168 is upregulated in ESCC patient tissues and TE1 cell line. Overexpression of circ0006168 promotes cell proliferation, accelerates cell cycle progression, and inhibits apoptosis by sponging miR-384 to upregulate STAT3 and Snail, with its knockdown exerting opposite effects.24 Triptolide downregulates circNOX4 to suppress cell proliferation, block cell cycle progression, and promote apoptosis in KYSE410 and ECA109 cell lines, whereas circNOX4 sponges miR-153-3p to release its target SATB1, thereby restoring malignant phenotypes.65 Another oncogenic circRNA, circ0120816, exerts its effects by sequestering miR-1305 to upregulate TXNRD1 in KYSE410 and KYSE510 cell lines, which enhances cell proliferation and inhibits apoptosis.112 Additionally, circ0001821 is transcriptionally upregulated by RUNX3. Knockdown of circ0001821 suppresses TE-1 and TE-10 cell proliferation and induces G2-M phase arrest. Mechanistically, circ0001821 facilitates ESCC cell progression by sponging miR-423-5p to upregulate BTRC expression.94 In contrast, tumor-suppressive circRNAs counteract ESCC progression by inhibiting cell proliferation and inducing apoptosis. For instance, downregulation of circCNTNAP3 and circCPSF6 leads to G0-G1 phase arrest and increased apoptosis.23,25 Collectively, these findings underscore the dual role of circRNAs in regulating core cellular ESCC functions, with ceRNAs being the most common mechanism.

Metabolism

Cellular metabolic homeostasis is crucial for maintaining normal cellular functions.186 The uncontrolled and rapid cell growth in ESCC tumors require the reprogramming of complex cellular metabolic networks, providing cancer cells with the necessary energy and promoting anabolism to support rapid cell proliferation, metastasis, and treatment resistance.187 Studies have shown that circRNAs regulate ESCC cell metabolism to drive tumor progression. Highly expressed circSFMBT2 sponges miR-107 to relieve its SLC1A5 inhibition in KYSE150 and TE-1 cell lines. Knockdown of circSFMBT2 reduces glutamine consumption and the production of glutamate and α-ketoglutarate, thereby inhibiting excessive glutaminolysis.107 circ0023990 binds to miR-6884-5p to release PAK1 inhibition. Given that PAK1 can activate glycolysis-related signaling, knockdown of circ0023990 suppresses glucose consumption, lactate production, and ATP levels in ECA109 and KYSE30 cell lines.66 circGOT1 binds to miR-606 to upregulate GOT1, which activates glycolytic enzymes, such as HK2 and PKM. Knockdown of circGOT1 inhibits aerobic glycolysis and reverses cisplatin resistance in KYSE150 and KYSE510 cell lines.106 ESCC-upregulated circ0001093 binds to miR-579-3p to relieve its inhibition on GLS. Overexpression of circ0001093 promotes GLS expression and glutaminolysis, with its knockdown exerting the opposite effects.102

Radiation and drug resistance

Chemotherapy and radiotherapy are currently the core treatments for patients with ESCC; however, the development of chemoresistance and radioresistance, respectively, with these treatments severely limits their efficacy, posing a major challenge to improving ESCC prognosis.188 The regulation of therapeutic resistance by circRNAs has been studied in ESCC. circ0007022 is identified from radioresistant Kyse30R ESCC cells. In addition, circ0007022 overexpression reduces the radiosensitivity of Kyse150 and TE1 cell lines, while circ0007022 knockdown exhibits the opposite effect. It binds to miR-338-3p to upregulate NRP1, activating the PI3K-AKT pathway.104 circVRK1 levels are low in ESCC tissues as well as in KYSE150 and KYSE450 cell lines. Its overexpression enhances ESCC cells’ radiosensitivity, and it acts via the miR-624-3p-PTEN-PI3K-AKT pathway.91 circ0014879 binds to miR-519-3p to relieve its CDC25A inhibition, and knockdown of circ0014879 enhances the radiosensitivity of radio-resistant cells of KYSE150R and ECA109R through this regulatory axis.132 circPDE3B is overexpressed in ESCC tissues and EC9706 and KYSE30 cell lines. It binds to miR-873-5p to upregulate SOX4 and regulates the Wnt-β-catenin pathway; thus, the knockdown of circPDE3B inhibits cisplatin resistance.55

TME regulation

TME is a complex local environment for tumor cell survival and a key determinant of cancer progression, immune evasion, and therapeutic resistance. Cancer cells, immune cells, stromal cells, and other components constitute the TME in tumor tissues. TME regulates cancer progression through crosstalk between malignant cells and stromal components. TME is not a single-cell population but a dynamic, heterogeneous ecosystem.189

Abnormal angiogenesis

The vascular system of the original tissue cannot fulfill the energy and nutrient requirements for rapidly proliferating tumor tissues. Dysregulation of circRNAs prompts tumor cells to secrete vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), thereby inducing the formation of new blood vessels. The abnormal new blood vessels have a higher permeability. They enable tumor cells to invade the bloodstream and undergo distant metastasis via vascular wall leakage.190 For example, circ0008726 knockdown reduces human umbilical vein endothelial cell (HUVEC) tube formation via VEGFA downregulation.126 Similarly, conditioned medium from TE-1 and Eca-109 cells with circCHSY1 knockdown suppressed the tube formation ability of HUVECs, suggesting that circCHSY1 promotes angiogenesis via the circCHSY1-miR-1229-3p-TCTN1 axis.121 The ECA109 and KYSE410 cell lines secrete exosomes with high expression of circZNF609, which are internalized by HUVECs. circZNF609 overexpression promotes its binding to miR-150-5p to upregulate VEGFA and enhance HUVEC tube formation, and it binds to HuR to inhibit mRNA translation of tight junction proteins.88 Furthermore, circPRKCA, circ0096710, and circCD44 can all regulate HUVEC tube formation and tumor progression.120,161,169

Metastasis

Metastasis is a key characteristic of malignant tumors. Tumor cells detach from the primary lesion, cross the interstitium to invade blood vessels or lymphatic vessels, reach distant organs or tissues with the blood flow, colonize at distant sites, and proliferate to form clones, thereby achieving distant tumor metastasis.191 This is manifested in the functions of ESCC cells as their ability to migrate and invade. circ0046534 is highly expressed in ESCC tissues and ECA109 and KYSE30 cell lines, and it binds to miR-339-5p to relieve miR-339-5p-mediated inhibition of its target gene MMP2, leading to increased MMP2 expression.93 Similarly, circ0001165 is highly expressed in KYSE30 and KYSE150 cell lines and acts as a sponge for miR-381-3p, upregulating TNS3 and promoting ESCC cell migration and invasion.26 Furthermore, several circRNAs also regulate metastasis via associated signaling axes, including PI3K-Akt, Wnt-β-catenin, and TGF-β. PDE5A-500-aa encoded by circPDE5A inhibits tumor metastasis by suppressing the PI3K-AKT pathway.172 circGSK3β binds to GSK3β to activate Wnt-β-catenin signaling.155 circDOCK5 overexpression represses TGF-β secretion by enhancing miR-627-3p stability and blocking TGF-β-induced metastasis.147

Hypoxia

Although angiogenesis in the TME is increased, it can barely meet the oxygen demands of cancer cells, consequently leading to a hypoxic microenvironment. This hypoxia regulates malignant tumor behaviors by activating hypoxia-related signaling pathways.192 In addition, circRNAs were confirmed to participate in the ESCC pathological regulatory network under hypoxic conditions. For example, hypoxia induces circDUSP16 expression in ECA109 and KYSE410 cell lines and promotes malignant ESCC phenotypes via the circDUSP16-miR-497-5p-TKTL1 axis.111 Hypoxia also induces ECA109 and KYSE410 cell lines to secrete circZNF609-containing exosomes to promote ESCC cell metastasis88 and circ0048117-containing exosomes to encourage M2 macrophage polarization that favors the malignant behaviors of Eca-109 and TE-1 cell lines.137 Additionally, hypoxia promotes HIF-1α accumulation, regulates circPUM1 production, and promotes oxidative phosphorylation of mitochondria.158

Immune microenvironment

Phagocytic and cytotoxic macrophage capabilities allow them to be recognized as immunoreactive cells that can polarize into anti-tumor M1 macrophages or pro-tumor M2 macrophages in response to microenvironmental signals. Macrophages are often induced to promote them acquire M2-like tumor-associated phenotype in the TME. These cells inhibit anti-tumor immune response, driving tumor progression.193 For example, circTCFL5 overexpression promotes ECA109 and KYSE30 cell line progression by modulating M2 macrophage polarization via the miR-543-FMNL2 axis.134 Similarly, exosomal circ0048117 derived from hypoxic Eca-109 upregulates TLR4 in macrophages to induce M2 polarization. M2 macrophages enhance Eca-109 and TE-1 invasion and migration abilities by secreting Arg1, IL-10, and TGF-β.137

PD-L1 is expressed on tumor or immune cells in the TME. It inhibits T cell activity by binding to PD-1 on immune cells, allowing tumor cells to evade immune attacks via PD-L1-mediated immune evasion.194 Studies on ESCC have shown that circRNAs regulate the tumor immune microenvironment via PD-L1-related immune evasion. circNF1 modulates PD-L1 at the transcriptional and post-translational levels. Transcriptionally, circNF1 drives the IL-6-induced oncogenic activation of the JAK-STAT3 pathway, stimulating p-STAT3 binding of the PD-L1 promoter regions. Post-translationally, circNF1 physically interacts with ANXA1, blocking the ANXA1 deubiquitination induced by USP7, which results in increased interaction between USP7 and PD-L1 and augmented PD-L1 stability. Elevated PD-L1 binds to PD-1 on CD8+ T cells, inhibiting their cytotoxicity and enabling ESCC immune evasion.157

Notably, the expression and functional characteristics of the four circRNAs reported in ESCC were found to be opposite to those in other squamous cell carcinomas. circFAM120B is downregulated in ESCC tissues. Its overexpression restores the expression of PPM1L by sponging miR-661, thereby inhibiting the proliferation and metastasis of TE1 and KYSE150 cells.87 circFAM120B is also annotated as circ0001666 and upregulated in non-small cell lung cancer tissue. It exerts oncogenic functions in the lung squamous cell carcinoma cell line H226 through the miR-330-5p-HMGA2 axis.195 circFoxo3 is lowly expressed in ESCC tissues, and its overexpression inhibits the proliferation and metastasis and promotes apoptosis in ESCC cell lines.113 Conversely, circFoxo3 is highly expressed in oral squamous cell carcinoma tissues and exerts oncogenic functions.196 circUBAP2 is highly expressed in ESCC tissues. Knockdown of circUBAP2 inhibits the proliferation and metastasis of TE9 cells.140 In addition, the UBAP2 gene can generate multiple circUBAP2 isoforms through alternative back-splicing. circUBAP2(9,12) is a circRNA formed by the circularization of exons 9 to 12 of the UBAP2 gene. circUBAP2(9,12) is downregulated in nasopharyngeal carcinoma tissues. Overexpression of circUBAP2(9,12) exerts oncogenic functions, whereas the opposite effect is observed upon its downregulation.197 circCREBBP is downregulated in chemoradiotherapy-resistant ESCC tissues. circCREBBP overexpression enhances the radiosensitivity of cells.153 circCREBBP is also annotated as circ0007637, which is highly expressed in nasopharyngeal carcinoma tissues and exerts oncogenic functions.198

Clinical applications of circRNAs in ESCC

With the rapid advancement of high-throughput sequencing technologies and bioinformatics analysis tools and the establishment of specialized circRNA databases, extensive studies have uncovered the crucial roles of circRNAs in ESCC, significantly promoting clinical translational research efforts in this subject area. These specialized databases provide solid support for the systematic analysis and mechanistic exploration of circRNAs, laying a vital foundation for their clinical application transformation. circRNAs have demonstrated enormous potential in multiple clinical aspects of ESCC, including early non-invasive diagnosis, accurate prognostic evaluation, and comprehensive analysis of clinicopathological features. They hold great promise for optimizing clinical management strategies for ESCC patients.

Abnormal circRNA expression in ESCC

Early traditional circRNA identification methods like northern blotting and reverse-transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) have limitations of low throughput and poor specificity. Breakthroughs have been made in high-throughput screening technologies in recent years, represented by RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) and microarrays. RNA-seq has become the preferred technique due to its high throughput and high resolution properties.232 The key steps in distinguishing circRNAs from linear RNAs using RNA-seq include specifically enriching circRNAs and identifying their unique junction sites. This method confirms known circRNAs and allows unbiased prediction of novel circRNA transcripts.233 More and more research using high-throughput technology shows dysregulated circRNA expression in ESCC. Zhang et al. performed high-throughput sequencing on the radiotherapy-resistant ESCC cell line Kyse30R and its parental line Kyse30 and found that 668 circRNAs were upregulated and 675 were downregulated in Kyse30R cells.104 Wang et al. analyzed differential circRNA expression via RNA-seq and bioinformatics analyses. They found 537 upregulated and 117 downregulated circRNAs in ESCC tissues compared with the numbers in normal ones.234 However, this method is limited by complex analysis pipelines and false positivity outcomes.

Microarrays are a mature high-throughput technology and an important supplement for validation and in-depth analysis. circRNA-specific microarrays are designed based on circular molecule characteristics. Their main value is in eliminating RNA-seq uncertainty by allowing multi-sample, high-throughput validation of RNA-seq-predicted circRNAs. Zhang et al. identified 149,789 circRNAs by using a circRNA microarray. Among these, 15,908 showed significant differential expression (7,161 upregulated and 8,747 downregulated). circRNAs from chr1 and chr2 made up the largest proportion of the sample.235 However, this method was ineffective in determining unannotated novel circRNAs.236 Studies on dysregulated circRNA expression using high-throughput technology are summarized in Table S2.

circRNAs serve as biomarkers for ESCC diagnosis and prognosis

Early stage ESCC screening is crucial for improving treatment outcomes and reducing mortality. Although endoscopy with tissue biopsy is currently the clinical gold standard for ESCC diagnosis, effective ESCC biomarkers are indispensable for early detection. Among these, blood/serum-based epigenetic biomarkers are highly promising diagnostic tools due to their non-invasiveness and cost effectiveness. Additionally, highly stable circRNAs are highly enriched in body fluids like plasma, serum, and exosomes, thus representing potential as novel biomarkers. Most circRNAs studied in ESCC show high sensitivity and specificity, thereby serving as molecular indicators for early cancer diagnosis and prognosis prediction.237 For example, plasma circCYP24A1’s area under the curve (AUC) value reached 0.94 for ESCC patients, with sensitivity of 0.84 and specificity of 0.93. ESCC patients with high circCYP24A1 plasma levels had lower overall survival. Furthermore, plasma circCYP24A1 level was higher in lymph node metastasis (LNM)-positive ESCC patients than in LNM-negative ones, and its AUC for predicting LNM was 0.76.238 Serum exosomal circ0026611 was also upregulated in ESCC. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis revealed that the AUC for circ0026611 in ESCC patients with LNM was 0.724, with sensitivity of 0.800 and specificity of 0.529.70 The expression of tissue circMMP11 in the postoperative recurrence/metastasis group was higher than that in the group without recurrence and metastasis. The AUC for the ROC curve was 0.838, with sensitivity of 0.75 and specificity of 0.7807. High circMMP11 levels correlated with TNM stage and LNM status and was an independent risk factor for postoperative recurrence/metastasis in ESCC.118 The diagnostic efficacy and functions of different circRNAs are listed in Table 4.

Expression of circRNAs and their relationship with clinicopathological factors

Notably, a few biomarker studies have utilized well-characterized patient and appropriate validation cohorts. Meanwhile, a major challenge in this field is that the detection results of circRNA expression levels are highly sensitive to tissue composition, as test samples are not exclusively composed of cancer cells. In particular, circRNAs tend to accumulate in quiescent cells due to their high stability, while being diluted in rapidly proliferating cells due to their slow biogenesis. Therefore, most differentially expressed circRNAs identified from bulk tissue profiling do not represent genuine expression alterations in cancer cells, but rather arise as spurious discrepancies driven by variations in the cellular composition across samples. For this reason, thorough tissue characterization is crucial when using bulk tissues for detecting circRNA biomarkers.244,245,246

Conclusion and perspectives

ESCC is one of the most common malignant tumors worldwide. This group of malignancies originates from the epithelial cells of the esophagus. The early ESCC stages are often unremarkable, leading to poor prognosis and a high mortality rate during the mid-to-late disease stages. Therefore, new treatments need to be identified, and the mechanisms underlying ESCC need to be explored to improve ESCC patient prognosis.188 circRNAs are characterized by a stable, covalent closed-loop structure and exhibit tissue-specific and developmental stage-specific distribution. The biological roles of circRNAs have been extensively investigated. Research has demonstrated that dysregulated circRNAs modulate cell proliferation, drug resistance, angiogenesis, and metastasis at the molecular mechanism level by acting as miRNA sponges, interacting with proteins, and encoding functional polypeptides, which greatly promote the progression of numerous cancers including ESCC.9 This enables circRNAs to become a potentially efficient diagnostic marker that can be used to predict prognosis.

Additionally, most published studies focusing on circRNAs in ESCC rely on quantitative reverse-transcription PCR (RT-qPCR) for quantification. However, the majority of relevant studies have not complied with the MIQE guidelines, which provide the minimum reporting standards for reliable qPCR experiments.247 The MIQE guidelines recommend that authors should report the specific experimental details of RNA extraction and quantification, as well as the methods for RNA integrity assessment, inhibition testing, and contamination evaluation. For reverse transcription, the conditions, reagents, and protocols of circRNA reverse transcription must be elaborated. Because circRNAs lack a poly(A) tail, conventional oligo(dT)-primed reverse transcription will lead to underestimated quantification and poor reproducibility. Detailed qPCR target information including amplicon length, in silico specificity screening, the exon/intron locations of primers, and the types of targeted splice variants should also be specified, but most studies have overlooked this part. This is particularly crucial for circRNAs because circRNAs cannot be distinguished from linear RNAs if divergent primers spanning the back-splice junction are not used. Meanwhile, the specific reaction conditions, reaction systems, and instrument parameters should be clearly stated when performing RT-qPCR. Specificity validation, such as supplementary melting curve analysis or amplicon sequencing, is also required. Given the frequent difficulties in reproducing RT-qPCR data from published circRNA studies, we strongly recommend that researchers adhere to these guidelines in future work; otherwise, further studies on circRNAs as tumor biomarkers will lack clinical translational significance.

As mentioned earlier, circRNAs seem to possess excellent characteristics, such as specificity, sensitivity, detectability, and predictability in plasma, saliva, urine, or even exosomes. However, the low abundance of circRNAs in some bodily fluid samples may result in insufficient detection sensitivity or stability.248 Moreover, traditional detection assays for circRNAs require skilled personnel and complex, time-consuming sample processing methods. These limitations have hindered their translational potential in clinical applications. Consequently, substantial efforts have been dedicated to addressing these challenges. In particular, NanoString nCounter Technology is promising for the clinical detection of circRNA biomarkers. As an enzyme-free method, it avoids the experimental bias caused by enzymatic steps in traditional assays and is suitable for the accurate detection and quantification of circRNAs. Meanwhile, this technique requires little hands-on time and performs well with formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded clinical samples.249,250 Additionally, Oxford NanoString nCounter is a third-generation sequencing technology. It enables full-length sequencing of entire circRNA molecules in a single read, particularly for long circRNAs, and allows for accurate identification of back-splicing junctions, thus serving as a powerful tool for circRNA detection and comprehensive profiling.251,252 A novel fluorescent DNA tetrahedral nanoprobe has been developed based on the miRNA sponge amplification property of circRNAs for high-sensitivity detection of circMTO1 in clinical samples, with a low limit of detection of 79 pM. DNA tetrahedron nanoprobe could also be used to perform precise in situ biological imaging of endogenous circRNAs in living cells.253 Furthermore, the Casμchip platform integrates droplet microfluidics with CRISPR-Cas13a/b orthogonality to simultaneously quantify circCK1γ3 and circWDR37. It achieves single-droplet dual-target detection at sensitivity of 100 copies/reaction in just 70 min by leveraging distinct base-cleaving preferences of LwaCas13a (uridine) and PsmCas13b (adenosine). This chip-based system combines RNA extraction, droplet digital quantification, and CRISPR activation in one device, enabling rapid, portable early detection and effective postoperative monitoring. The technology significantly advances point-of-care cancer diagnostics.254 Southern Medical University developed bimodal extracellular vesicle (EV)-circRNA in situ analyzer, a bimodal extracellular vesicle-circRNA analyzer using DNA rectangular frame-guided hybridization chain reaction to non-destructively penetrate EV membranes for detecting intravesicular circRNAs. It utilizes a self-validating dual-signal output, achieving ultra-low detection limits of 0.55 fM fluorescence and 8.70 a.m. electrochemical signals, while minimizing RNA loss by circumventing EV lysis. Integrated with machine learning, BEISA distinguishes healthy donors from gastric cancer patients with 86.7% sensitivity and 90% specificity in clinical validation and achieves 85.0% diagnostic accuracy for early- and late-stage cancer.255 The advancements of these technologies have substantially improved the diagnostic sensitivity of circRNAs, making them clinically more valuable diagnostic biomarkers and paving the way for their widespread clinical application in the future.

The high stability and low immunogenicity of circRNAs effectively avoid the common immune activation side effects of linear nucleic acid drugs. Their characteristics of high efficacy and safety make them a better vaccine vector than mRNAs.256 For example, a new circRNA neoantigen vaccine constructed using an in vitro cyclization strategy based on an endogenous mutation in liver cancer tumors (Ptpn2_I383T) can efficiently activate dendritic cells (DCs) and promote DC-mediated T cell killing activity.257 Another type of cytokine co-expression vaccine uses lipid nanoparticle (LNP)-delivered circRNAs to co-express CXCL13 and viral antigens. It can target lymph nodes, reshape their immune microenvironment, enhance antigen-specific T cell responses, and produce cross-reactive antibodies against influenza viruses and SARS-CoV-2.258 The inherent stability of circRNAs can enhance the expression level and duration of antigen presentation within the DC vaccines, which enhance their anti-tumor efficacy. In a pancreatic cancer model, a circRNA DC vaccine encoding the tumor antigens FAPα and survivin and combined with low-dose gemcitabine achieved an 89% tumor inhibition rate.259 In conclusion, these advancements indicate that circRNA-based vaccines, with their diverse design strategies (neoantigen targeting, cytokine co-expression, or DC vaccines combined with chemotherapy) and inherent biological advantages, provide a versatile and highly promising platform for the development of targeted vaccines against ESCC and other malignant tumors. They hold enormous potential for clinical translation.

The key point for circRNA-based tumor therapy is the achievement of efficient intracellular delivery. Building on the successful experience of mRNA vaccines, LNPs currently serve as the mainstream circRNA delivery system.260,261 However, aspects such as liver targeting preference, reticuloendothelial system clearance, and T cell transfection relying on antibody modification still need to be optimized.262,263,264 Recent studies have achieved some key breakthroughs. A tumor-customized LNP named H1L1A1B3 and obtained through high-throughput technology showed a transfection efficiency four times that of the industry standard ALC-0315. A single intratumoral injection of H1L1A1B3 LNP loaded with circRNA encoding IL-12 can induce a strong immune response, leading to significant tumor regression.265 Cardiolipin-mimetic lipid (PL40) can improve T cell transfection without targeting ligands. It prolongs the expression duration in the spleen and T cells after encapsulating circRNAs and successfully delivers the mRNA encoding uPAR-chimeric antigen receptor, thereby alleviating uPAR-related liver fibrosis and rheumatoid arthritis.266 In addition, guanidinylated serinol charge-altering releasable transporters achieve 97% lung targeting and 98% spleen targeting without the need for targeting ligands. Their RNA release is triggered by charge neutralization at physiological pH, a mechanism that contributes to their controlled delivery performance in vivo.267 These breakthroughs have addressed the key limitations of traditional carriers. However, whether the differences in lipid receptor expression in ESCC tissues and individual variations can affect the delivery efficiency of circRNAs requires further optimization and verification.

With the in-depth integration of interdisciplinary fields, the biological functions of circRNAs will be further elucidated in the future, and associated diagnostic technologies and therapeutic strategies will keep advancing. circRNAs are expected to evolve into a core modality for early screening, precise treatment, and prognostic assessment of malignant tumors, including ESCC, thus significantly enhancing the quality of life of tumor patients and ushering in revolutionary advancements of the diagnosis and treatment of malignant tumors overall.

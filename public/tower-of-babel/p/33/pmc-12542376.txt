Ferroptosis and non-coding RNAs in lung cancer: exploring molecular mechanisms and diagnostic/therapeutic implications
Authors: Hedong Xue, Melika Malek, Liangyu Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractLung cancer is the second most prevalent type of cancer on a global scale, and is widely known for being the deadliest. Revealing the fundamental molecular mechanisms of this condition has the potential to unlock fresh possibilities and avenues for managing it. Ferroptosis is a newly discovered type of controlled cell death that distinguishes itself from traditional forms of programmed cell death, specifically apoptosis and necrosis, through its distinct biochemical and structural characteristics. Changes made to the mechanism that regulates ferroptosis can have a significant influence on the development of various illnesses, such as, though not limited to, lung cancer. Based on scientific studies, it has been proven that cancer cells possess a significant capability to inhibit ferroptosis, leading to their continuous growth and survival. The presence of non-coding RNAs has a profound influence on controlling a wide range of cellular functions, with a particular focus on cancer. They have been closely associated with all key features of lung cancer. Fresh findings have indicated that these particles also have the task of controlling ferroptosis. Thus, the use of non-coding RNA as a therapeutic strategy offers a potentially effective option for regulating ferroptosis in the management of cancer. The main focus of this overview is on ncRNAs and their function in controlling ferroptosis, specifically within the context of lung cancer.Keywords: Ferroptosis, Non-coding RNAs, Lung cancer, Cell death, Molecular mechanisms

Introduction

Cancer is a disease where cells grow and spread uncontrollably, often resulting in its spread to other areas of the body. It typically has a hidden onset and a prolonged course of treatment [1, 2]. Based on the latest figures from the American Cancer Society as of 2020, lung cancer remains a prevalent type of cancer and remains the leading cause of cancer-related fatalities globally [3, 4]. There are two main types of lung cancer: non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and small cell lung cancer (SCLC). Non-small cell lung cancer, also known as NSCLC, is the most common type of lung cancer, accounting for approximately 85–90% of all diagnosed cases. When looking at SCLC and NSCLC, NSCLC tends to develop at a slower rate and have a more optimistic forecast [5]. A novel form of cellular death has been revealed, regulated by the existence of iron and the consequent oxidation of lipids [6]. Understanding the procedures and mechanisms of cellular demise is crucial in the effort to prevent and treat cancer. Additional forms of cellular demise mechanisms consist of necrosis, apoptosis, and pyroptosis. The advance of lung cancer heavily relies on the evolution of ferroptosis, similar to numerous other forms of cancer. The development of lung cancer is greatly impacted by the interconnected interactions of vital signaling molecules, including epidermal growth factor receptors, mitogen-activated protein kinase, hypoxia-inducible factor, tumor suppressor P53, and mammalian target of rapamycin. The presence of these compounds is crucial for the rise in cases of lung cancer. However, the control of ferroptosis encompasses a diverse range of non-coding RNA molecules such as microRNA, long non-coding RNA, and circular RNA [7]. Ferroptosis refers to the physiological process in which cells are destroyed due to an overwhelming level of oxidative stress. This method of cell demise stands out from others like necrosis, autophagy, and apoptosis in terms of its distinctiveness. The reason for this phenomenon is the inhibition of the body’s natural defense system against damaging oxidants, which occurs when the level of reduced glutathione (GSH) in cells decreases. Hence, the formation of harmful reactive oxygen species (ROS) is greatly influenced by lipids [8]. The p53 protein plays a key role in the effectiveness of ferroptosis, which plays a crucial role in hindering tumor growth in this specific situation [9]. In addition, a multitude of research has indicated a link between the mechanism of lipid peroxidation, build-up of iron, and the onset of ferroptosis [10, 11].

NcRNAs, otherwise known as non-coding RNAs, encompass a wide range of transcripts that do not possess the capability to generate proteins. Despite the fact that they have limitations, they are essential for many cellular functions because of their unique molecular mechanisms [6]. Initially, it was thought that their purpose held little significance. The idea that proteins are the sole functional outcome of gene expression has significantly shifted with the discovery and analysis of numerous functional ncRNAs [7]. In broad terms, these particles can be categorized into two primary categories: short or long non-coding RNAs, depending on their specific lengths (typically no more than 200 nucleotides), or organized according to their diverse functions and objectives [8]. Functional non-coding RNAs come in three main types – microRNAs (short), long non-coding RNAs, and circular RNAs. They possess inherent traits that enable them to exhibit unique properties linked to bodily tissues or conditions, and can be found in various bodily fluids, making them valuable for their potential role as indicators of biological processes [9]. Moreover, non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) are commonly dysregulated in different medical conditions, such as cancer [10]. Drug resistance in cancers has been frequently linked to their participation [11–14]. Therefore, harnessing the power of ncRNAs as a treatment strategy could effectively modify drug resistance mechanisms in cancer cells, resulting in improved patient outcomes [15].

Ferroptosis and lung cancer

Overview of ferroptosis

The death of cells is an inescapable and enduring occurrence in the operation of the body [16, 17]. Currently, there are multiple pathways that control the process of cell death, such as apoptosis, necroptosis, autophagy, ferroptosis, pyroptosis, and necrosis [18]. In 2012, Scott J Dixon and his team introduced the idea of ferroptosis as a type of cell death that is unique from other known forms and relies on iron [19]. Ferroptosis results in a disruption of the typical factors that signify cell demise, such as cell shrinkage, DNA compression, production of apoptotic bodies, and breakdown of the cytoskeleton, which are all commonly observed in apoptosis [20, 21]. The most common observation when using an electron microscope is the presence of different changes in the appearance of mitochondria, including smaller size, fewer cristae, thicker membrane, and breakage of the outer membrane [19, 22]. Ferroptosis refers to a state characterized by elevated levels of iron, reactive oxygen species, and lipid peroxides, combined with a decrease in the production of glutathione peroxidase 4. This enzyme is essential for the functioning of the antioxidant glutathione system within cells [23].

Ferroptosis in lung cancer

The regulation of ferroptosis involves several critical elements, such as controlling the levels of iron and lipids and maintaining an equilibrium in the redox state, as depicted in Fig. 1. The predominant factor behind both chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and the heightened vulnerability to lung cancer is the habit of smoking cigarettes. The most recent findings have shown that cigarette smoke extract is responsible for triggering Ferroptosis. This procedure has been determined to be harmful to the cells of the bronchial airways, resulting in the onset of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) among those who have already been diagnosed with lung cancer [24, 25]. This finding signifies the first identification of a link between ferroptosis, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and lung cancer. Moreover, the act of smoking cigarettes has been associated with the occurrence of ferroptosis as a result of an increase in ferritinophagy, which leads to a buildup of iron in the body. Therefore, the lung epithelial cells secrete both DAMPs and pro-inflammatory cytokines as a result [25]. Extensive research in the field of bioinformatics has revealed that the presence of five distinct genes (ALOX5, DPP4, FANCD2, GCLC, and SLC7A11 (a component of the system Xc⁻ cystine/glutamate antiporter that maintains glutathione levels and ferroptosis resistance)) associated with ferroptosis could significantly influence the progression and prognosis of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) [26]. The latest research provides highly convincing evidence that the regulation of ferroptosis plays a vital role in the development of lung cancer and is carried out through various mechanisms, as illustrated in Fig. 2. Conducting further investigations and experiments is crucial to fully comprehend the underlying mechanisms of ferroptosis in lung cancer. This will also aid in the exploration of potential treatments, such as ferroptosis inhibitors. Lung cancer cases often show a notable incidence of KRAS mutations, specifically KRAS-G12C. These mutations are often linked with a negative outcome and treatment resistance. Earlier findings have solidly established ferroptosis as a form of cellular demise triggered by oncomodulatory RAS, thus further supporting the idea of utilizing ferroptosis as a therapeutic approach. In accordance with this idea, erastin and RSL3, which are recognized for their capacity to induce ferroptosis, have demonstrated significant toxicity towards cells carrying KRAS mutations, such as those found in various forms of lung cancer like Calu-1 [27, 28]. Furthermore, the presence of KRAS mutations in lung cancer cells increases their vulnerability to ferroptosis, due to the inhibition of SLC7A11 [29]. There is currently a discussion about how genetic mutations can impact an individual’s susceptibility to ferroptosis, as this particular type of cell death can be triggered by various factors that are either connected or disconnected to the RAS gene. Numerous theories have been suggested to explain the pro-ferroptotic impact of KRAS that leads to cell death. Initially, the activation of malignant RAS signaling leads to a rise in cellular iron levels due to the upregulation of TFRC gene expression [28]. Additionally, it has been suggested that KRAS mutation could enhance NFE2L2-mediated SLC7A11 expression, thereby preventing further potential targets for SLC7A11 inhibitors [29, 30]. Given the influence that both wild-type and mutant KRAS have on one another within the realm of mutant KRAS-led tumors, further examination of the impact of this interplay on ferroptosis presents a compelling avenue for exploration [31]. Based on suggestions, the role of TP53 in triggering ferroptosis in cancer cells following radiation therapy is substantial. The process of radiotherapy triggers the activation of TP53, leading to the increase in ferroptosis in different types of cancers such as lung cancer [32].

Modulation of ferroptosis as an effective strategy against cancer

The use of agents that trigger ferroptosis greatly improves the effectiveness of radiotherapy in suppressing tumors derived from lung cancer patients with TP53 mutations [32]. Nevertheless, the exact impact of radiotherapy-induced apoptosis in this scenario remains unclear. TP63 and TP73 are identical in structure and share a common evolutionary origin with TP53. The ∆Np63α form of the TP63 gene has a notably increased occurrence among those with lung cancer [33]. Ferroptosis is regulated by ∆Np63α through its influence on the expression of specific genes involved in the metabolism of glutathione (GSH). These include GCLC, GSS, GPX2, and IDH2 [34]. As previously stated, the role of GCLC and GSS in preventing ferroptosis is to enhance the generation of GSH within the cell. The role of GPX2 in controlling reactive oxygen species could involve the use of GSH to make up for the absence of GPX4 (glutathione peroxidase 4, an antioxidant enzyme that protects against lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis). IDH2 is an essential protein that helps produce NADPH, an important molecule that is integral in the process of replenishing GSH. Its activity serves as the determining factor in the pace at which GSH is replenished. It will be intriguing to examine the potential impacts of TP53 and TP63 on pathways that are not reliant on GSH and that counteract ferroptosis. USP11 and ATF2 not only regulate the expression of NFE2L2 in lung cancer cells, but also control its activity. In H1299 cells, the deubiquitinase USP11 prevents ferroptotic cell death by sustaining the NFE2L2 protein through mechanical means [35]. Conversely, the complete absence of USP11 renders cells more vulnerable to triggering ferroptosis, effectively halting the growth of lung cancer cells [35]. ATF2, a protein found in A549 cells, reduces the effectiveness of JQ1 in inducing ferroptosis by elevating the levels of NFE2L2 [36]. A highly effective approach to improving the use of ferroptosis as a means of managing lung cancer could be the utilization of drugs that hinder the NFE2L2 pathway. The cellular survival pathway, known as Nrf2, is responsible for inhibiting ferroptosis. By suppressing NFE2L2, the potential of ferroptosis to attack lung cancer cells may be enhanced. YAP, a significant protein responsible for controlling the expression of genes, serves as an essential factor for transcription within the Hippo signaling pathway. The promotion of solid tumor growth, invasion, and dissemination, particularly in cases of lung cancer, is crucial [37]. Erastin’s ability to boost the levels of internal glutamate makes lung cancer cells highly vulnerable to ferroptosis, potentially due to its suppression of YAP expressio [38]. By preventing the function of YAP, a protein known for promoting resistance to ferroptosis in lung cancer cells due to excessive iron levels, ADCY10 effectively suppresses this cellular process. To successfully decrease the levels of ferritin, a protein responsible for storing iron, one can hinder its production or accelerate its breakdown, known as ferritinophagy. As a result, the levels of iron are increased, making lung cancer cells more susceptible to ferroptosis [38].

In a mechanical process, an increase in glutamate prompts the Ca2+-dependent activation of ADCY10. Therefore, the release of cAMP causes activation of the protein kinase A (PKA) pathway. Consequently, YAP is completely subdued in cells affected by lung cancer [38]. On the other hand, inhibiting YAP could potentially increase the ability to withstand ferroptosis in specific types of cancer. Cell-to-cell contact may influence ferroptosis sensitivity, although this effect varies across cancer types [39]. On the other hand, the excessive production of YAP-S127A, an altered form of YAP, causes YAP to gather in the nucleus, ultimately enhancing ferroptosis in HCT116 colon cancer cells through the activation of TFRC and ACSL4 expression [39]. A comprehensive analysis of the activating and inhibitory signals of YAP is integral to developing an efficient approach for combatting tumors through targeting YAP-associated ferroptosis. Interestingly, the role of YAP in ferroptosis appears to be context-dependent and somewhat contradictory [40, 41]. In some studies, YAP promotes ferroptosis by upregulating pro-ferroptotic targets such as TFRC and ACSL4, enhancing iron uptake and lipid peroxidation [42–44]. However, other evidence suggests that YAP can suppress ferroptosis by activating antioxidant genes or cooperating with ferroptosis-inhibiting pathways like FSP1 [45, 46]. These conflicting findings may reflect differences in cancer type, upstream Hippo signaling context, or compensatory regulatory mechanisms. Therefore, further investigation is needed to clarify whether YAP functions primarily as a ferroptosis sensitizer or suppressor in lung cancer.

LSH, also known as lymphoid-specific helicase, belongs to the SNF2 protein family and has the ability to modify the composition of chromatin. LSH is widely found in tissues impacted by lung cancer and has been linked to harmful results for individuals with a diagnosis of this disease [47].

The combined efforts of LSH and WDR76 have been found to successfully counter ferroptosis in both scientific experiments and living beings. This is achieved through the collaboration’s facilitation of metabolic genes, such as SLC2A1/GLUT1, SCD, and FADS2 [48]. SCD and FADS2 are essential members of the fatty acid desaturase family, playing a crucial role in the production of monounsaturated fatty acids (MUFAs). One prominent ability they possess is the ability to inhibit ferroptosis [49]. Disabling SCD and FADS2 activities is a vital process in initiating ferroptosis in lung cancer cells [50]. Moreover, the process by which glucose is transported via SLC2A1 may play a role in promoting the advancement of ferroptosis due to its capacity to enhance the synthesis of fatty acids. The legitimacy of this theory has been confirmed in cases of pancreatic cancer [50], Additional verification is necessary in regards to lung cancer cells. Moreover, the initiation of LSH could potentially be caused by the inhibition of HIF1A, which is achieved by the connection between EGLN1 and the MYC pathway [50]. The findings suggest that in lung cancer cells, LSH pathways utilize both oxygen deprivation and the processing of fatty acids to control susceptibility to ferroptosis.

In summary, ferroptosis is an iron-dependent form of cell death driven by lipid peroxidation and ROS accumulation. It plays a significant role in lung cancer progression and therapy response, particularly through the regulation of genes like SLC7A11, GPX4, and ACSL4. The involvement of tumor suppressors such as p53 and oncogenes like KRAS further emphasizes its clinical relevance. A clearer understanding of ferroptosis in lung cancer provides a promising avenue for targeted therapy.

Non-coding RNAs and lung cancer

Non-coding RNA molecules (NcRNAs) are integral to many important biological mechanisms and pathways that are vital for the progress and growth of lung cancer (Fig. 3).

The biogenesis and functional roles of non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) in lung cancer progression

LncRNAs

A distinct kind of RNA, called long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), is characterized by its length of over 200 nucleotides and its inability to generate proteins. They can still impact gene expression by influencing various cellular mechanisms, including modifying chromatin, controlling transcription, and regulating post-transcriptional processes.

By altering the chromatin structure, lncRNAs have the ability to impact the formation of chromosomes, which is the intricate combination of DNA and proteins [51]. Controlling gene expression is a crucial function of lncRNAs as they interact with transcription factors and other regulatory proteins, ultimately leading to the transcription of specific target genes [52]. Their capacity to function as supportive components allows them to connect different elements of the transcription process, either increasing or decreasing the degree of gene expression. LncRNAs have the ability to influence various aspects of messenger RNA, including splicing, stability, and translation, among others. This can have an effect on the synthesis of proteins that are derived from these mRNAs. A thorough investigation was conducted by LC into a specific lncRNA identified as MALAT1 (Metastasis-Associated Lung Adenocarcinoma Transcript 1) [53]. The presence of the protein is greatly noticeable in tissues affected by LC, and this is strongly associated with a poor patient outcome. Extensive studies have confirmed that MALAT1 is a crucial factor in promoting cell division and spread through various pathways, resulting in increased cell proliferation and the transportation of cancer cells to other parts of the body. This component strongly affects and plays a role in both the advancement of the cell cycle and the spread of cancer throughout different areas of the body, due to its direct interactions with important regulatory molecules. The ability to regulate key genes associated with the growth and metastasis of tumors allows it to increase the aggressiveness of LC cells.

LncRNA MALAT1 is particularly noteworthy among these, as it is markedly overexpressed in lung cancer. Moreover, it has been associated with a negative mindset for individuals [54]. By interacting with essential regulatory molecules and modifying gene activity, it promotes cell growth and proliferation.

They possess the capacity to recruit chromatin-modifying groups to specific sites on the genetic code, ultimately influencing the arrangements of genetic activity in cells. Depending on the conditions and the type of long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) used, the act of recruiting can have the effect of activating or inhibiting certain genetic material, which is shown in Fig. 3 [55]. lncRNAs play a significant role in cancer advancement, as they are vital for the creation of transcriptional complexes. These combinations of molecules ultimately trigger the activation of cancer-causing genes while also suppressing the activity of genes that prevent tumor growth, ultimately contributing to the progression of the disease [56]. The function of lncRNAs as ceRNAs is one of the most extensively researched aspects of cancer. LncRNAs possess the capability to bind to miRNAs, functioning as a “sponge” to hinder their bonding with specific messenger RNAs (mRNAs) [57]. This communication has the potential to cause an upsurge in the manifestation of genes which would normally be restrained by miRNAs. The role of LCAT1, a lengthy non-coding RNA, is well-documented in its capacity to serve as a reservoir for miR-4715-5p. As a consequence of this, there is a rise in the quantity of RAC1, a type of small GTPase within the Rac category, leading to the promotion and proliferation of cancerous cells [58].

MiRNAs

MicroRNAs, referred to as miRNAs, are small, single-stranded RNA molecules responsible for regulating gene expression after the transcription process has occurred. Their approach revolves around attaching themselves to specific segments of the designated messenger RNA (mRNA), which could result in the elimination of the mRNA or hindering its translation. Experts in LC have concluded that miRNAs can act in two ways, either promoting the advancement of cancer or curbing the growth of tumor [59]. MicroRNAs, also known as miRNAs, are minuscule particles made up of approximately 22 units of nucleotides. Their impact is significant in controlling the activation of genes. Their roles encompass a diverse array of actions in living beings, including development, differentiation, procreation, and pre-planned cellular demise [60]. MiRNAs primarily exert their regulatory power by attaching to target mRNA molecules during the post-transcriptional process. This connection can result in two predominant consequences. When a miRNA and its target mRNA are highly similar, the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC) has the ability to activate and prompt the breakdown of the mRNA. As a result, the associated protein’s quantity is greatly reduced [61]. If there is no precise correspondence, the miRNA is capable of impeding the translation of the target mRNA, without causing its degradation. As a result, there is a decrease in the synthesis of proteins from the messenger RNA [62]. Within the realm of lung cancer, microRNAs (miRNAs) possess the capacity to function as either cancer-promoting genes or tumor-suppressing genes. This is contingent on the disparity in their levels of manifestation and the precise proteins that they govern in cells. The disruption of their normal regulation is frequently linked to the start, advancement, and spread of cancer. Cancer-promoting miRNAs, referred to as OncomiRs, exhibit elevated levels in cancer cells and serve an essential function in the progression of tumors by specifically attaching to genes that impede tumor growth. There has been a significant amount of scientific exploration into the role of MiR-21 in promoting cancer, particularly in lung cancer. This microRNA has consistently been found to be elevated in various types of the disease, with a particular focus on non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) [63]. The vital role of PTEN lies in its ability to enhance the growth and spread of tumors through the direct inhibition of key tumor-suppressing genes.

One crucial factor for this is its role as a key regulator of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, which controls cellular survival and proliferation. The pathway is experiencing an increased level of activation due to the upregulation of PTEN, causing a more intense increase in cellular proliferation and survival [64]. The key function of PDCD4 is to suppress translation and trigger apoptosis, serving as a critical factor in suppressing tumor growth. The inhibition of PDCD4 by MiR-21 allows cells to undergo uninterrupted multiplication and evade programmed cell death mechanisms. This leads to a boost in cancer cell growth and the speedy progression of tumors [65]. Conversely, particular miRNAs play a role in suppressing tumors by having reduced levels of expression in cancer. When they stop functioning properly, it can result in an increased expression of oncogenes, ultimately leading to the development of tumours. LC commonly exhibits a decrease in this specific miRNA variant, which hinders many cancer-causing genes involved in regulating cellular division and apoptosis [66]. If the presence of miR-34 is absent, it can lead to uncontrolled cellular growth and hinder the process of cell death, ultimately aiding the progression of cancer [67].

CircRNAs

Circular RNAs, also known as circRNAs, are a unique set of RNA molecules that can be identified by their distinctive circular shape. Due to their distinctive curved shape, these molecules possess a higher level of fortitude in contrast to straight chain RNAs, making it possible for them to survive for a longer duration in the cellular milieu. The diverse capabilities of circular RNAs (circRNAs) include their ability to serve as potent inhibitors for microRNAs (miRNAs), which has been extensively researched [68]. Circular RNAs (circRNAs) have the ability to hinder the interaction between small RNA molecules and their target mRNAs, ultimately resulting in boosted expression of these specific genes by attaching to miRNAs. CircRNAs possess the capacity to attach to and influence the activity of RNA-binding proteins, ultimately modifying the expression of connected genes. This circular RNA, which is also known as ciRS-7, has been extensively studied because it has the ability to bind to miR-7, a small RNA that has been shown to have anti-tumor properties [69]. The CDR1as molecule is a crucial factor in impeding the proper regulation of targeted genes, resulting in an increase in the activation of genes that promote the growth and spread of lung cancer cells. To achieve this objective, its interaction with miR-7 is utilized [69]. The process highlights the importance of circRNAs in driving cancer growth through their ability to control the levels of miRNAs, which typically inhibit tumor progression [70]. The operation of LC heavily depends on the part played by lncRNAs and circRNAs in regulating gene expression and other crucial cellular processes. One of the main factors that contributes to their crucial role in the intricate framework of cancer biology is their capacity to engage with a vast array of molecular elements present in the cell.

Currently, there is ongoing research on two previously identified LC biomarkers, circFARSA and circFoxo3, in order to better utilize them for the treatment of LC. It has been discovered that CircFARSA are notably higher in the blood of individuals with NSCLC [71].

An escalated manifestation is linked to the aggressive nature of tumors and a negative projection for improvement. CircFARSA has the ability to function as a reliable biomarker for NSCLC, with the potential to accurately diagnose and predict the course of the disease without the need for invasive procedures. Through convenient blood tests, it can enable early identification and continual monitoring of disease advancement [72]. The expression of CircFoxo3 varies in different kinds of cancer, like LC, as opposed to its consistent expression seen in other types of cancer. Foxo3 levels have been noted to decline greatly in both cancerous tissues and cell lines, particularly in regards to non-small cell lung cancer [73]. The main function of the tumor suppressor is to impede the growth, mobility, and infiltration of cells by utilizing various molecular pathways, with a particular focus on capturing miRNAs that promote the advancement of cancer. The diminished presence of circFoxo3 in samples from NSCLC patients is linked to a more advanced stage of the tumor and inferior survival rates, emphasizing its use as a predictive indicator [74]. The case of circFARSA and circFoxo3 serves as a strong demonstration of the considerable potential of circular RNAs in serving as accurate and directed biomarkers in liquid biopsies, offering a broad range of clinical uses. The changes in how they are expressed in LC patients can be used as a means of diagnosis and prediction, potentially enhancing the ability to detect and treat this fatal illness at an earlier stage [75]. CircRNAs are a distinct group of non-coding RNAs with a characteristic circular shape, setting them apart from other types of RNA and providing them with exceptional resilience [76]. One of the roles they can serve is that of a miRNA sponge (a molecule—often a lncRNA or circRNA—that binds and sequesters miRNAs, preventing them from acting on their target mRNAs), in which they absorb miRNAs and hinder their ability to bind with their intended mRNA targets. CDR1 circRNA effectively blocks the normal function of miR-7, leading to an increase in the expression of genes regulated by miR-7. This ultimately promotes the proliferation and metastasis of lung cancer cells [77].

NcRNAs—including miRNAs, lncRNAs, and circRNAs—play diverse roles in lung cancer by regulating gene expression involved in proliferation, apoptosis, and metastasis. Specific ncRNAs like MALAT1 and circFARSA also show strong potential as biomarkers for diagnosis and prognosis. Their expression profiles and interactions with key oncogenic pathways make them valuable candidates for therapeutic targeting.

MiRNAs

There is still a lack of comprehensive knowledge about miRNA’s contribution to the development of ferroptosis in cancer, however, there is substantial proof indicating that they have a vital regulatory role in this mechanism within cancer cells (depicted in Fig. 2). The significant presence of MiR-214-3p plays a crucial role in the progression of ferroptosis in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) by actively decreasing the levels of activating transcription factor 4 (ATF4) [78]. When the human body detects stress, it triggers ATF4 to become active, which plays a crucial role in protecting cells from ferroptosis by promoting the production of SLC7A11 [79]. Furthermore, ATF4 is a key regulator in the activation of particular genetic elements essential for the differentiation, growth, and formation of new blood vessels [80]. ATF4 is heavily involved in promoting blood vessel development in gliomas, but this can be thwarted if ferroptosis inducers (compounds that trigger ferroptosis by disrupting antioxidant defenses or increasing iron-dependent lipid peroxidation) are incorporated during studies [81].

Research has revealed that miR-137 has the ability to completely halt lipid peroxidation and the accumulation of iron in melanoma cells. This phenomenon has been noticed under controlled laboratory setups as well as in actual living beings. The reason for this is due to the fact that miR-137 is capable of specifically homing in on and functioning on solute carrier family 1 member 5 (SLC1A5) [82]. SLC1A5 operates separately from system xc- and serves as a carrier for a range of neutral amino acids, including alanine, serine, cysteine, and glutamine [83]. Due to this, decreased levels of the essential glutamine transporter result in a lower absorption of glutamine, activity of glutaminolysis, and buildup of MDA [82]. Once inside the body, glutamine is broken down through digestion, leading to the creation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and initiating the process of ferroptosis [84]. Moreover, numerous pathways have been identified as connecting miR-137 to the TNM staging, metastasis, and resistance to medication in different types of cancer [85–88].

LncRNAs

The drug Erastin induces the synthesis of a specific type of RNA called GABPB1-AS1 in liver cancer cells. The role of this RNA is to counteract the functioning of the transcription factor beta subunit 1 of the GA binding protein. When protein translation is stopped, it results in the blocking of GABPB1, which is a key element of the GA-binding protein transcription factor. This portion is crucial in initiating the activation process of nuclear respiration factor 2, which is also referred to as GA-binding protein. Significantly reducing the amount of GABPB1 protein leads to a notable decrease in the synthesis of PRDX5 peroxidase. This results in a decrease in the cells’ ability to combat free radicals, causing an escalation in reactive oxygen species (ROS) and malondialdehyde (MDA) levels, as well as a reduction in the overall survival rate of the cells [89].

The lncRNA known as MT1DP is essential for regulating the ferroptosis process induced by erastin through its control over NRF2 [90]. High amounts of MT1DP in non-small cell lung cancer cause a rise in reactive oxygen species and malondialdehyde, as well as a greater presence of ferrous iron in the cells. At the same time, it has been observed that erastin has the ability to significantly reduce the levels of glutathione in cancer cells. Through the process of up-regulating miR-365a-3p, the performance of NRF2 is diminished, resulting in the acquisition of these results. This miRNA usually regulates the production of NRF2 mrna. In a notable development, Gai et al. have developed liposome nanoparticles that are modified with folate, a type of vitamin, in order to improve the absorption and effectiveness of delivering both erastin and MT1DP to their intended targets. A recent study on live mice has produced promising results in terms of the ability of erastin to initiate ferroptosis in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) by specifically targeting it [90].

CircRNAs

The three rounded RNA molecules, designated as cIARS, circKDM4C, and circ_0000190, possess the capability to induce ferroptosis. The IARS gene is responsible for producing cIARS, a substance that increases significantly in amount when sorafenib treatment is used for HCC. In their research, Liu and colleagues determined that sorafenib treatment leads to ferroptosis through the activation of autophagy and ferritinophagy. However, ferritinophagy plays only a partial role in this process. This specific circular RNA engages in a direct physical interaction with AlkB Homolog 5 (ALKBH5), an RNA binding protein [91]. The presence of Sorafenib greatly enhances the cIARS-ALKBH5 interaction, possibly as a result of the increased levels of cIARS that are induced by sorafenib. It is worth noting that ALKBH5 protein levels are not influenced by this particular kinase inhibitor. As a result, cIARS inhibits ALKBH5’s detrimental effects on autophagy, resulting in an increase in autophagy, ferritinophagy, and ferroptosis [91]. Currently, cIARS stands alone as the sole circular RNA known to control ferroptosis by binding to a specific protein. In addition, research has demonstrated that cervical cancer is characterized by increased amounts of circEPSTI1, which promotes the growth and multiplication of cells. CircEPSTI1 serves as a storage site for three distinct miRNAs, namely miR-375, miR-409-3p, and miR-515-5p. This protein’s primary role is to absorb and hinder the action of these particular miRNAs. These particular miRNAs specifically target SLC7A11, a crucial protein responsible for preventing ferroptosis. Many other circular RNA molecules have been found to control the expression of SLC7A11. Several circular RNAs have been found to play important roles in different types of cancer. For example, circ0097009 acts as a decoy for miR-1261 and affects the development of hepatocellular carcinoma. In thyroid cancer, circ_0067934 influences the activity of miR-545-3p. In glioma, circCDK14 interacts with miR-3938. Finally, in esophageal cancer, circPVT1 works as a sponge for miR-30a-5p [92–96].

NcRNAs modulate ferroptosis in multiple cancer types by targeting genes responsible for iron metabolism, lipid peroxidation, and antioxidant defenses. These interactions highlight the broad influence of ncRNAs in regulating cell death mechanisms beyond lung cancer. Understanding these regulatory relationships lays the groundwork for developing novel ncRNA-based strategies to induce ferroptosis in resistant tumors.

Non-coding RNAs and ferroptosis in lung cancer

MiRNAs and ferroptosis in lung cancer

Ferroptosis is chiefly controlled by the concentrations of iron and lipids, as well as the equilibrium of redox reactions. Various aspects are performed through molecular mechanisms that are specifically controlled by certain genes. Ultimately, miRNAs have effectively functioned in triggering and advancing cancer through their regulation of the ferroptosis pathway [97]. Numerous pieces of evidence strongly suggest that miRNAs have a notable impact on either initiating or preventing ferroptosis. The scientists have the capability to observe and track various crucial phases of ferroptosis, such as the mechanism of GSH-GPX4, the transport of glutamate/cystine, control of iron, and manipulation of lipids. Furthermore, miRNAs involved in ferroptosis have been linked to resistance to drugs and the spread of cancer, by influencing processes such as angiogenesis and the release of cytokines [97–99].

The vital miRNA targets in lung cancer are the main proteins associated with ferroptosis, such as SLC38A1, SLC1A5, SLC3A2, and SLC7A11. According to Lu et al., in the context of NSCLC, suppressing miR-27a-3p leads to hindered ferroptosis due to increased expression of SLC7A11 [100]. A significant discovery is that SLC7A11, a protein overexpressed in different forms of cancer, is integral in facilitating the import of cysteine into cells. This is vital in the creation of GSH and safeguarding against oxidative harm, ultimately inhibiting ferroptosis. Furthermore, the replication of miR-34c-3p effectively impedes the synthesis of cysteine and GSH by directly targeting SLC7A11 [101]. Focusing on SLC7A11 has shown potential as an effective approach for treating cancer. As per the findings of Wang et al., reducing the levels of miR-520a-5p in lung cancer could potentially result in a decline in both ferroptosis and the progression of tumors. Definitely, the action of miR-520a-5p has a major inhibitory effect on the expression of SLC7A11, leading to a detrimental effect on the onset of ferroptosis [102]. Similarly, in lung adenocarcinoma, the presence of miR-1184 was diminished, leading to an elevation in GSH synthesis and a decline in the prevalence of ferroptosis. To sum up, an elevation in miR-1184 levels has the potential to reduce SLC7A11 expression and facilitate ferroptosis [103]. Moreover, Zhang and colleagues have emphasized that a decrease in miR-339 levels results in an upregulation of SCL7A11 expression, which in turn promotes proliferation and migration of lung cancer cells. In contrast, activation of miR-339 leads to a decrease in SCL7A11 expression, ultimately inducing ferroptosis [104]. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) greatly reduces the levels of MiR-299-3p, an important regulator of cell proliferation and ferroptosis. In the investigation conducted by Liang et al., a thorough elucidation is presented regarding how the upregulation of miR-299-3p results in the inhibition of cellular proliferation and accelerates the onset of ferroptosis. The fifth figure exhibits the effective suppression of SLC38A1, resulting in a rise in lipid oxidation and iron buildup in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). This demonstrates that inhibiting SLC38A1 can initiate the onset of lipid oxidation and iron buildup in NSCLC [105]. New research has revealed a link between NSCLC and decreased levels of miR-302a-3p, a substance known for its ability to fight against cancer. The artificially created version of MiR-302a-3p accelerates lipid peroxidation, leading to a rise in iron levels and initiating ferroptosis, ultimately hindering the growth of NSCLC cells. Specifically, miR-302a-3p binds to and influences the 3′ untranslated region (UTR) of FPN, resulting in a decrease in the production of FPN protein [106].

FPN is classified as a ferroptosis inhibitor, as its primary role is to regulate the levels of iron within cells as an iron-exporting protein, maintaining proper iron balance within the cell. When FPN is muted, there is a decrease in the export of iron and a rise in the levels of internal iron, resulting in heightened susceptibility to ferroptosis (as demonstrated in Fig. 5) [107]. On the other hand, CBS, or cystathionine-β-synthase, is an equally important factor and acts as a substitute indicator in ferroptosis. The presence of CBS has a significant influence in suppressing ferroptosis in a wide range of cancer types. MiR-6852 plays a crucial role in inhibiting lung cancer by regulating the expression of CBS, thus controlling cell growth [107]. A new approach to treating cancer focuses on directing efforts towards cystathionine-β-synthase (CBS) in order to trigger ferroptosis [108]. Similarly, decreased amounts of miR-324-3p, which acts as a suppressor for tumors, have been noted in NSCLC. This has been found to hinder ferroptosis and increase resistance to the chemotherapy drug cisplatin. In contrast, an excessive amount of miR-324-3p leads to the activation of ferroptosis and enhances the efficacy of cisplatin as a remedy. The gene GPX4 is specifically targeted by miR-324-3p, a component located deeper in the signaling pathway [109, 110].

The image number five displays the miRNAs related to ferroptosis and their role in lung cancer. These minuscule RNA molecules play a crucial role in regulating different proteins and genes, thus influencing ferroptosis

Contrarily, the research conducted by Shanshan and colleagues showed that GPX4, a recognized tumor suppressor in NSCLC, is additionally suppressed by miR-1287-5p (as illustrated in Fig. 5) [111]. The method by which cisplatin operates is widely acknowledged to involve apoptosis, pyroptosis, and ferroptosis. In their research, Song and his team verified that a particular form of short RNA, known as miR4443, plays a crucial role in the development of resistance to cisplatin in patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). This is achieved by increasing the production of the protein FSP1 (ferroptosis suppressor protein 1, which inhibits ferroptosis by regenerating CoQ10 to reduce lipid peroxides), which acts as a suppressor of ferroptosis. In addition, miR-4443 significantly inhibited critical components of ferroptosis and upregulated the expression of FSP1. FSP1’s ability to prevent ferroptosis is due to CoQ10, which effectively suppresses and counteracts the damaging lipid peroxyl radicals [112]. The suggestion is to utilize the suppression of miR-4443 as an effective method to enhance cancer cells’ response to chemotherapy. Additionally, they found that miR-29a levels were elevated in lung cancer patients compared to non-cancer controls, suggesting that this microRNA may serve as a potential biomarker for lung cancer diagnosis. This implies that this specific compound could play a crucial role in the advancement of the illness. This may present fresh possibilities for comprehending and potentially curing lung cancer. Furthermore, the genetic variation rs1062980, found in the non-coding region of the IREB2 gene responsible for binding to iron-responsive elements, has the ability to alter the functioning of IREB2 by potentially disrupting its interaction with miR-29a. Additionally, the variation in consumption of iron through diet, could potentially affect the probability of individuals developing lung cancer [113].

The IREB2 gene is widely used as an indicator to detect the onset of ferroptosis. There has been a significant increase in the levels of miR-155 in the cells of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) that are resistant to doxorubicin. Conversely, if miR-155 is removed, the activation of different genes related to resistance against medication is prevented. This includes GSH-S-transferase-π, which has been linked to cancer development by influencing ferroptosis. GSH-S-transferase-π is crucial for detoxification as it facilitates the binding of various hydrophobic and electrophilic substances with GSH [114]. There are three primary techniques employed in managing lung cancer: operative procedures, chemotherapy medications, and radiation therapy. The overall likelihood of survival for individuals with lung cancer is minimal due to the typically concurrent presence of malignant cell dissemination and resistance. There is a pressing need for the creation of innovative treatment methods or powerful and effective new drugs to address the issue of lung cancer. Based on the provided details, it can be concluded that miRNAs have a crucial impact on different phases of cancer progression with regards to ferroptosis.

Numerous research has solidly confirmed that the focus on ferroptosis could potentially lead to a ground-breaking treatment for lung cancer, as examined previously [115]. In the past, cisplatin was primarily used for its chemotherapy effects. New findings have demonstrated that it has the capability to trigger ferroptosis in cancerous cells found in the lungs. The task is achieved by increasing the concentration of lipid reactive oxygen species (ROS) and reducing the levels of glutathione (GSH), while also deactivating glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) [116]. Ferroptosis can be successfully triggered in non-small cell lung cancer cells that are resistant to cisplatin by simultaneously administering other treatments, such as ginkgetin or GPX4 inhibitors. This results in a significant improvement in the efficacy of cisplatin as a treatment [117]. The presence of Buthionine Sulfoximine greatly amplifies ferroptosis through its ability to impede the creation of GSH, leading to an increased level of reactive oxygen species in experiments involving non-neuroendocrine small-cell lung cancer [118]. After being identified, the drug, Dihydroartemisinin, known for treating malaria, has been classified as a potential trigger for ferroptosis. This procedure is implemented by disturbing the typical iron equilibrium and impeding the PRIM2/SLC7A11 connection within cancerous cells situated in the respiratory system. Chlorin e6, when incorporated into photodynamic-resistant lung cancer cells, significantly enhances its ability to induce ferroptosis, making it an incredibly effective treatment option. This means that the presence of chlorin e6 has a significant impact on the potential success of this treatment approach for lung cancer [119]. Orlistat, a pharmaceutical used for weight loss, exerts a powerful effect on ferroptosis in cancer cells of the lungs. This is achieved through the inhibition of fatty acid synthase (FASN), resulting in a decrease in lipid droplet production. It is essential to conduct a thorough investigation in order to fully comprehend the entire process, specifically its role in lipophagy [120].

Ferroptosis can potentially be initiated by CD8 + T cells, which serve as vital components in the body’s defensive mechanisms. In tumor cells, the intake of cystine is limited and the process of lipid peroxidation is encouraged, leading to cell death.

In lung cancer specifically, ncRNAs regulate ferroptosis by targeting core mediators like SLC7A11, GPX4, and FSP1. Several miRNAs promote ferroptosis by downregulating antioxidant defenses, while lncRNAs and circRNAs act as sponges or regulators in feedback loops. These molecules also play roles in immune modulation, drug resistance, and exosomal signaling, emphasizing their potential in lung cancer therapy.

Long non-coding RNAs and ferroptosis in lung cancer

G3BP1 plays a crucial role in promoting the exchange of information between lung cancer and Ras GTPase-activating protein-binding protein 1 (G3BP1) in the cytoplasm [121]. The P53RRA and G3BP1 molecules in the cytosol combine, resulting in the complete dissociation of p53 from the G3BP1 complex. Consequently, the p53 protein is enclosed in the nucleus, causing the cessation of cell division, triggering apoptosis, and kickstarting ferroptosis. LINC00336, a type of RNA that does not produce proteins, has been identified as a significant factor in fueling the growth of cancer cells, specifically in cases of lung cancer. The process of controlling ferroptosis within the nucleus is responsible for this event [122]. LINC00336’s main role is to engage with ELAV-like RNA-binding protein 1 (ELAVL1) in order to uphold its stability through the attachment to adenylate and uridylate (AU)-rich elements (AREs). These AREs are specific segments that control the lifespan of RNA molecules. Furthermore, LSH can also be boosted through the p53 signaling pathway, as it causes an increase in the expression of ELAVL1. When LINC00336 expression is increased, it functions as an internal binding agent for miR-6852, effectively blocking the miRNA’s ability to decrease levels of CBS. Extensive studies have confirmed that LINC00336 possesses the capability to effectively reduce the oxidative stress through different mechnaisms such as modulation of ferroptosis and mitochondrial related pathways [122].

NEAT1, a well-respected form of long non-coding RNA, has been recognized as a potent oncogene positioned adjacent to the nucleus. NEAT1 has been discovered to have significant contributions to a variety of non-cancer related conditions, in addition to its role in cancer [123, 124]. Multiple characteristics of cancers such as growth, cell division, spreading, movement, and programmed cell death are linked to this [125]. In their research, Wu and his team proved that NEAT1 effectively attaches to the messenger RNA of ACSL4, resulting in a decrease in its production. This, in turn, plays a role in the development of ferroptosis in non-small cell lung cancer [126]. Although NEAT1 has been demonstrated to play a part in promoting cellular demise, its association with ferroptosis in people with non-small cell lung cancer is not connected to this specific mechanism. In addition, ACSL4 is responsible for promoting ferroptosis, as shown by the lack of effect on other essential factors, including SLC7A11, GPX4, and TfR1 levels, when erastin is utilized [126]. In their research, Tai et al. employed the technique of RNA sequencing to examine the effects of RAS selective lethal 3 and ferrostatin 1 on A549 cells. Using this approach, they successfully pinpointed the particular messenger RNAs and long non-coding RNAs linked to the process of ferroptosis. Consequently, their understanding of the intricate processes involved in ferroptosis and its significance in relation to lung cancer was enhanced [127]. The results showed a significant rise in the levels of LUCAT1, a type of long non-coding RNA, in both lung adenocarcinoma and lung squamous cell carcinoma tissues. Through an analysis of mRNAs and lncRNAs with varying levels of expression, it was found that LUCAT1 plays a critical part in facilitating ferroptosis. After being exposed to RSL3, there was a marked increase in the expression levels of LUCAT1 in A549 cells. However, when co-incubated with Fer 1, this effect was significantly inhibited. The enhanced production of LUCAT1 resulted in a marked rise in cell growth and effectively hindered the initiation of ferroptosis caused by RSL3 and Erastin. On the other hand, when LUCAT1 levels were reduced, cellular growth slowed down and ferroptosis levels increased. The decline of LUCAT1 ultimately resulted in a concomitant decrease in GTP cyclohydrolase 1 (GCH1) and ferroptosis suppressor protein 1 (FSP1), implemented through a distinct process. In addition, the suppression of LUCAT1 effectively elevated the levels of miRNA 34a 5p and consequently decreased GCH1 expression. The results indicate that inhibiting LUCAT1 resulted in heightened levels of ferroptosis by controlling the decrease of GCH1 through the influence of miR-34a-5p. Thus, utilizing a combination of compounds that promote ferroptosis and suppress LUCAT1 levels shows potential as an effective treatment strategy for lung cancer [127].

In their study, Wang and colleagues aim to investigate the role of long non-coding RNAs in relation to ferroptosis in lung adenocarcinoma. They also seek to develop a predictive model that can estimate the prognosis of this type of cancer [128]. Data on the physiological and physical characteristics of individuals and their genetic sequencing were gathered from The Cancer Genome Atlas. Furthermore, the FerrDb database was used to collect messenger RNAs linked to the process of ferroptosis. The method of Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator Cox regression was utilized to produce a thorough predictive model that relates to long non-coding RNA and its association with ferroptosis. Furthermore, a thorough assessment was carried out to determine the possible danger posed by lncRNAs in relation to ferroptosis. Following this, patients who were identified with lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) were split into two distinct categories, that is, high-risk or low-risk, depending on their risk score which was established using the median value. A K-M plots and nomograms technique was utilized in creating a predictive model. A method called gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was employed to investigate the possible relationship between immune reactions and lncRNAs associated with ferroptosis. Through thorough investigation, it was determined that a grouping of 10 lncRNAs associated with ferroptosis could potentially act as reliable indicators for the outcome of lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD). The named lncRNAs are RP11-386M24.3, LINC00592, FENDRR, AC104699.1, AC091132.1, LANCL1-AS1, LINC-PINT, IFNG-AS1, LINC00968, and AC006129.2. Ultimately, the trajectory exhibited conclusive evidence that the established markers were extremely dependable in precisely predicting the outcome of LUAD. the nomogram technique, the efficiency of the existing signatures was assessed. Additionally, following the utilization of GSEA for analysis, it was discovered that the 10 specific lncRNAs associated with ferroptosis may have a role in regulating immune reactions in LUAD. In general, the outcomes of this study can offer new perspectives for creating innovative treatments or methods to detect early stages of LUAD [128].

Circular RNAs ferroptosis in lung cancer

The occurrence and mortality rate of lung cancer is notably high in China [129]. Research has shown that a reduction in circDTL levels leads to the onset of ferroptosis in non-small cell lung cancer cells. CircDTL sponges miR-1287-5p, which normally targets GPX4 mRNA, thereby indirectly increasing GPX4 expression and suppressing ferroptosis [111, 130, 131].

A recent study discovered that CircP4HB works as an effective inhibitor of ferritinase in lung adenocarcinoma, making it a new and unique finding. The function of CircP4HB is to control the production of glutathione (GSH) by interacting with SLC7A11. This process serves as a vessel for miR-1184 in order to effectively carry out its regulatory functions. Ultimately, this leads to the prevention of ferroptosis, resulting in the stimulation of cancerous cell growth and division at its fundamental level. In upcoming times, CircP4HB may potentially serve as a viable treatment option for lung cancer, thus enhancing the way it is controlled and treated [103]. The regulation of ferroptosis by exosome-derived circRNA_101093 (cir93) plays an essential role in driving the advancement of lung adenocarcinoma. Elevated cir93 levels significantly diminished the effects of erastin, a chemical compound known for triggering ferroptosis, resulting in a decrease in lipid oxidation. Therefore, the process of triggering ferroptosis was suppressed. The accomplishment was carried out by excessively expressing fatty acid binding protein 3 (FABP3). Furthermore, cir93 also had an impact on FABP3, which resulted in the control of NAT (a compound formed from taurine and AA). As a result, the implementation of the regulation ultimately caused a reduction in the levels of ACSL4, LPCAT3, and PLTP, impeding the progression of ferroptosis. There is potential for this to be a highly effective approach towards treating lung adenocarcinoma in the future [132].

The presence of CircSCN8A in lung cancer has been associated with increased Fe2+, ROS, and MDA concentrations, as well as decreased levels of GSH.The Western blot procedure clearly demonstrated that the gradual rise in circSCN8A was the primary factor behind the reduced levels of SLC7A11 and GPX4 proteins, ultimately triggering ferroptosis. Ferrostatin-1 (Fer-1), a well-known inhibitor of ferroptosis, efficiently mitigated this influence. In addition, circSCN8A was found to bind with miR-1290, resulting in an elevation of ACSL4 levels. In the end, this advanced the spread and spreading of lung cancer, while also preventing the occurrence of ferroptosis. In conclusion, the immense potential of circSCN8A as a crucial treatment target for lung cancer cannot be overstated [133].

Exosomal non-coding RNAs and ferroptosis in lung cancer

Exosomes, small extracellular vesicles secreted by various cell types, can deliver non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) that influence ferroptosis in lung cancer. Their role as carriers allows oncogenic or tumor-suppressive ncRNAs to modulate cell death pathways across the tumor microenvironment (Fig. 6).

Exosomes and ferroptosis play important roles in tumours. (A) These mechanisms are the main culprits behind the development of chemoresistance. (B) As a solution, there has been a focus on artificially modifying agents to specifically target cancer therapies

A notable example is the exosomal transfer of miR-4443 from cisplatin-resistant NSCLC cells to sensitive ones. This ncRNA targets METTL3, leading to upregulation of FSP1, a ferroptosis suppressor, thereby reducing ferroptosis and promoting chemoresistance [134].

Similarly, circRNA_101093 (cir93), enriched in exosomes from LUAD patients, was shown to reduce lipid peroxidation and inhibit ferroptosis by modulating fatty acid metabolism via FABP3, ultimately downregulating ACSL4 and LPCAT3 [132].

These findings underscore the dual role of exosomal ncRNAs in mediating drug resistance and suppressing ferroptosis in lung adenocarcinoma, highlighting their potential as diagnostic biomarkers or therapeutic targets.

Conclusions

Ferroptosis is a distinct form of regulated cell death characterized by iron accumulation and lipid peroxidation. In lung cancer, growing evidence demonstrates that ferroptosis plays a crucial role in tumor progression, drug resistance, and immune modulation. Non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs), including miRNAs, lncRNAs, and circRNAs, are now recognized as pivotal regulators of ferroptosis, acting through gene expression control, epigenetic modification, and competitive endogenous RNA networks.

This review highlights that specific ncRNAs such as miR-324-3p, miR-1184, LINC00336, and circP4HB modulate ferroptosis by targeting key regulators like SLC7A11, GPX4, and FSP1. Some ncRNAs promote ferroptosis and enhance chemosensitivity, while others suppress it and support tumor survival. These dual roles offer both challenges and opportunities for therapeutic development.

Combined All-trans Retinoic Acid and Rosiglitazone Alleviates Scleroderma Fibrosis by Suppressing the STAT3/Th17 Axis
Authors: HONG-MEI SHAO, JUN-JIE CHEN, SHUANG-LIN CAO
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background/AimEffective disease-modifying therapies for systemic sclerosis (scleroderma) remain a major clinical challenge. While all-trans retinoic acid (ATRA) and the PPARγ agonist rosiglitazone (RGZ) possess individual anti-fibrotic properties, their combined therapeutic potential and underlying mechanisms in scleroderma are unexplored.Materials and MethodsThis study investigated the efficacy and mechanisms of combined ATRA and RGZ therapy in a bleomycin (BLM)-induced murine scleroderma model and TGF-β1-stimulated NIH-3T3 fibroblasts. Pathological changes were assessed via histological analyses, quantitative PCR, ELISA, and western blotting.ResultsBLM-challenged mice exhibited pronounced dermal thickening, excessive collagen accumulation, and elevated Th17-related inflammatory responses. Strikingly, the ATRA+RGZ combination therapy significantly outperformed either monotherapy, robustly restoring normal skin architecture, reducing collagen density, and suppressing the expression of fibrotic mediators (TGF-β1, COL1a1) and Th17-associated factors (IL-17A, IL-6). In vitro experiments confirmed that this combination directly prevented TGF-β1-induced fibroblast-to-myofibroblast transition. Mechanistically, the dual therapy synergistically inhibited STAT3 phosphorylation and RORγt nuclear translocation. Importantly, pharmacological reactivation of STAT3 by Colivelin completely abrogated the anti-fibrotic and anti-inflammatory benefits of the combination in vivo.ConclusionThe concurrent administration of ATRA and RGZ exerts superior protection against scleroderma fibrosis by converging on the inhibition of the STAT3/Th17 signaling axis. This study provides a compelling mechanistic rationale for repurposing and combining ATRA and RGZ as a translational strategy for scleroderma treatment.

Introduction

Fibrotic disorders such as systemic sclerosis (scleroderma) present major therapeutic challenges because of their complex pathophysiology, which involves excessive extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition, dermal thickening, and progressive multi-organ dysfunction (1). Current treatments are largely palliative and have limited ability to reverse established fibrosis (2). Disease pathogenesis is driven by aberrant fibroblast activation and immune dysregulation, with cytokines such as TGF-β and IL-6 sustaining chronic inflammation and collagen overproduction (3). Signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) function as a central signaling node integrating these profibrotic cues (4). Its hyperactivation in fibrotic tissues promotes fibroblast-to-myofibroblast differentiation, ECM remodeling, and inflammatory cell infiltration, which are key features observed in both human scleroderma and experimental models, including bleomycin-induced fibrosis (5). Although therapeutic approaches targeting individual pathways (e.g., TGF-β inhibition) have been explored, their efficacy is often limited by signaling redundancy. In addition, metabolic abnormalities, particularly peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor γ (PPARγ) dysfunction, further exacerbate fibroblast activation by disrupting lipid homeostasis and increasing oxidative stress, underscoring the need for combinatorial strategies that address both immunomodulatory and metabolic pathways in fibrosis (6).

Aberrant immune responses, particularly the expansion of T helper 17 (Th17) cells, are central drivers of systemic sclerosis (7). Th17 cells secrete interleukin-17A (IL-17A), which promotes collagen deposition and sustains chronic inflammation (8). The orphan nuclear receptor RORγt is the master regulator of Th17 lineage commitment and a direct downstream target of STAT3 signaling (9). PPARγ functions as a negative regulator of this pathway; its activation has been shown to interact with or suppress the transcriptional activity of the STAT3/RORγt axis, thereby inhibiting Th17 differentiation (10). Collectively, these interactions provide a mechanistic rationale for targeting the PPARγ-STAT3-RORγt axis to control inflammation and fibrosis associated with scleroderma.

All-trans retinoic acid (ATRA) is an intermediate metabolite of vitamin A and a ligand for the retinoic acid receptor (RAR), with well-established anti-inflammatory and antiproliferative properties (11). Recent studies have further demonstrated that ATRA exerts antifibrotic effects. In systemic sclerosis, ATRA ameliorates fibrosis by suppressing the profibrotic factor Fra2/AP-1 (12). In addition, Chiba et al. reported that ATRA treatment attenuated macrophage-dependent injury and fibrosis following acute kidney injury (AKI) by regulating macrophage activation (13). The PPARγ agonist rosiglitazone (RGZ) also exhibits broad antifibrotic activity (14). RGZ prevents subconjunctival fibrosis after glaucoma filtration surgery by inhibiting profibrotic gene expression through mechanisms involving enhanced autophagy in local fibroblasts (15). Moreover, serelaxin has been shown to potentiate the antifibrotic effects of RGZ in a model of hepatic fibrosis (16). However, the combined efficacy of ATRA and RGZ in fibrosis, particularly in scleroderma-associated skin pathology, and their effects on inflammatory cell infiltration, extracellular matrix remodeling, and tissue ultrastructure have not yet been investigated.

This study demonstrates that combined ATRA and RGZ therapy markedly attenuates skin fibrosis in a bleomycin-induced scleroderma model through coordinated inhibition of STAT3 signaling. Compared with either agent alone, ATRA+RGZ more effectively reduces dermal thickening, collagen deposition, and inflammatory cell infiltration, while preserving hair follicle architecture. Mechanistically, the combination robustly suppresses STAT3 phosphorylation and RORγt nuclear translocation, leading to reduced expression of fibrotic mediators (TGF-β1, COL1a1) and Th17-associated factors (IL-17A, IL-6). Importantly, pharmacological activation of STAT3 abolishes these protective effects, identifying STAT3 as the central mediator of the enhanced antifibrotic response. Collectively, these findings support a translational strategy for scleroderma that integrates ATRA-driven antifibrotic reprogramming with RGZ-mediated metabolic modulation to disrupt the STAT3 axis.

Materials and Methods

Animals and experimental protocols. Female BALB/c mice (six weeks old) were obtained from the Laboratory Animal Center of Nantong University. Female mice were chosen due to the strong female predominance in human systemic sclerosis. Mice were housed under a 12 h light-dark cycle with ad libitum access to food and water. Following acclimation, mice were randomly allocated into designated experimental groups (n=6 mice per group). Dorsal skin tissues from all animals were harvested, and the exact sample sizes used for various downstream assays are detailed in the respective figure legends. Scleroderma was induced by daily subcutaneous injection of bleomycin (300 μg/kg; Cat. No. 970592; Nippon Kayaku, Tokyo, Japan) into the dorsal skin for four weeks (17). To evaluate antifibrotic effects, all-trans retinoic acid (ATRA; Cat. No. R2625; Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) was administered intraperitoneally at 25 mg/kg three times per week for four weeks, while rosiglitazone (RGZ; Cat. No. R2408; Sigma-Aldrich) was administered by oral gavage at 8 mg/kg once daily for four weeks. Mice in the combination group (A+R) received both treatments simultaneously via their respective routes for four weeks. To assess pathway-specific reversibility, the STAT3 agonist Colivelin (Cat. No. 6245; 1 mg/kg in PBS; Tocris Bioscience, Bristol, UK) was administered intraperitoneally once daily from day 14 to day 28. Control mice received subcutaneous injections of PBS (Cat. No. P7059; Sigma-Aldrich). On day 29, mice were euthanized by CO₂ asphyxiation followed by cervical dislocation, and dorsal skin tissues were collected for subsequent analyses. All animal procedures were conducted in accordance with institutional guidelines and were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Nantong University (Approval No. A-2024-026).

Histology and immunohistochemistry.For histological analysis, dorsal skin tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde at room temperature for 24 h and then transferred to 70% ethanol. Samples were paraffin-embedded and sectioned at a thickness of 4 μm. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining was performed using hematoxylin (Cat. No. CS700; Dako, Glostrup, Denmark) and eosin (Cat. No. CS701; Dako). Masson’s trichrome staining was conducted with a commercial kit (Cat. No. ab15068; Abcam, Cambridge, UK) to assess collagen fiber density. Dermal thickness was measured using a light microscope (Leica DM750, Leica Microsystems, Wetzlar, Germany). For the quantification of collagen deposition, Masson’s trichrome-stained sections were analyzed using ImageJ software (NIH, Bethesda, MD, USA). The collagen volume fraction (CVF) was calculated as the ratio of the blue-stained collagen area to the total tissue area. For each mouse, at least three randomly selected high-power fields were measured, and the average value was used for statistical analysis. All histological evaluations and quantitative analyses were performed by two independent investigators who were blinded to the treatment groups.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA).Skin tissues were homogenized in ice-cold PBS (pH 7.4) containing 1% protease inhibitor cocktail (HY-K0020; MedChemExpress, Monmouth Junction, NJ, USA) using a tissue homogenizer (Bertin Technologies, Montigny-le-Bretonneux, France). Homogenates were centrifuged at 12,000×g for 15 min at 4°C, and the supernatants were collected for analysis. Protein concentrations were determined using a BCA assay (Cat. No. 23225; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Levels of IL-6, TGF-β1, IL-17A, RORγt, and COL1a1 were quantified using commercially available ELISA kits according to the manufacturers’ instructions: Mouse IL-6 ELISA Kit (KE10007; Proteintech, Rosemont, IL, USA), Mouse TGF-β1 ELISA Kit (DY1679; R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA), Mouse IL-17A ELISA Kit (BMS6001; Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA), Mouse RORγt ELISA Kit (CSB-EL009339MO; Cusabio, Wuhan, Hubei, PR China), and Mouse COL1a1 ELISA Kit (MBS261957; MyBioSource, San Diego, CA, USA). Briefly, 50 μl of samples or standards were added to pre-coated 96-well plates and incubated for 2 h at 37°C. Plates were washed five times with PBS containing 0.05% Tween-20 (Cat. No. P1379; Sigma-Aldrich), followed by incubation with biotinylated detection antibodies for 1 h at 37°C. After additional washing, streptavidin-HRP (Cat. No. N100; Thermo Fisher Scientific) was added and incubated for 30 min at 37°C. Color development was achieved using TMB substrate solution (Cat. No. 555214; BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA) for 15 min in the dark, and reactions were stopped with 2 N H₂SO₄ (Cat. No. 258105; Sigma-Aldrich). Optical density was measured at 450 nm with correction at 570 nm using a microplate reader (BioTek Synergy H1; BioTek Instruments, Winooski, VT, USA).

Target protein levels were normalized to total protein content and expressed as pg/mg or ng/mg of total tissue protein. All samples were analyzed in duplicate, and experiments were repeated in three independent biological replicates.

Quantitative PCR. Total RNA was extracted from skin tissues using TRIzol reagent (Cat. No. 15596018; Invitrogen). Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized with the PrimeScript RT Kit (Cat. No. RR036; Takara, Kusatsu, Shiga, Japan). Quantitative real-time PCR was performed on an ABI 7500 Fast Real-Time PCR System using SYBR Premix Ex Taq (Cat. No. 6110A; Takara). Relative mRNA expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. Primers for IL-6, TGF-β1, IL-17A, retinoic acid-related orphan receptor γt (RORγt), collagen type I alpha 1 (COL1a1), and glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) were synthesized by Shanghai Jierui Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, PR China. The primer sequences are presented in Table I.

Cell culture and treatment.Mouse embryonic fibroblast cell line NIH-3T3 was cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin at 37°C with 5% CO2. To induce a pro-fibrotic phenotype, cells were starved in serum-free medium for 12 h and then stimulated with recombinant mouse TGF-β1 (10 ng/ml, Cat. No. 240-B; R&D Systems) for 24 h. For drug treatments, cells were co-treated with ATRA (5 μM), RGZ (10 μM), or their combination in the presence of TGF-β1. Total proteins were then extracted for western blot analysis.

Western blot.Skin tissues were homogenized in ice-cold RIPA lysis buffer (Cat. No. P0013B; Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, PR China) supplemented with 1 mM PMSF (Cat. No. ST506; Beyotime Biotechnology) and a protease/phosphatase inhibitor cocktail (Cat. No. HY-K0010; MedChemExpress). Protein concentrations were determined using a BCA Protein Assay Kit (Cat. No. 23227; Thermo Fisher Scientific). Equal amounts of protein (30 μg per lane) were separated using 10% SDS-PAGE (Cat. No. PG113; Epizyme Biotech, Shanghai, PR China) and transferred onto PVDF membranes (Cat. No. IPVH00010; Millipore, Burlington, MA, USA). Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk (Cat. No. 232100; BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA) in TBST for 1 h at room temperature and then incubated overnight at 4°C with the following primary antibodies: phospho-STAT3 (Tyr705) (Cat. No. 9145; 1:1000; Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA), total STAT3 (Cat. No. 12640; 1:1,000; Cell Signaling Technology), α-SMA (Cat. No. ab124964; 1:1,000; Abcam), COL1A1 (Cat. No. ab270993; 1:1,000; Abcam), RORγt (Cat. No. sc-28559; 1:500; Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Dallas, TX, USA), and GAPDH (Cat. No. 60004-1-Ig; 1:5,000; Proteintech, Rosemont, IL, USA). Membranes were subsequently incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies, including goat anti-rabbit IgG (Cat. No. SA00001-2; 1:5,000; Proteintech) and goat anti-mouse IgG (Cat. No. SA00001-1; 1:5,000; Proteintech). Protein bands were detected using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) kit (Cat. No. K-12045-D50; Advansta, San Jose, CA, USA) and quantified by densitometric analysis with ImageJ software (v1.53; NIH). Protein expression levels were normalized to GAPDH, except for p-STAT3, which was normalized to t-STAT3.

Statistical analysis. Data are presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). One-way or two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by Tukey’s or Bonferroni’s post hoc multiple-comparison test, was performed as appropriate. A p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism software (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA).

Results

Enhanced anti-fibrotic effects of combined ATRA and RGZ treatment. To evaluate the therapeutic efficacy of ATRA and RGZ on scleroderma-like skin pathology, a bleomycin (BLM)-induced murine model was established according to the experimental timeline (Figure 1A). Histological evaluation of dorsal skin tissues was performed using hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining (Figure 1C-G) and Masson’s trichrome staining (Figure 1H-L). Compared to the normal skin architecture with a thin dermis in the PBS control group (Figure 1C and H), mice in the BLM-challenged group exhibited pronounced fibrotic characteristics. These included marked dermal thickening (indicated by black arrows), dense and disorganized collagen deposition (stained blue in Masson’s trichrome), inflammatory cell infiltration, and a significant loss of subcutaneous fat and hair follicles (Figure 1D and I). Quantitative analysis of dermal thickness confirmed a dramatic increase in the BLM group relative to the PBS control (p <0.05, Figure 1B).

Treatment with either ATRA (Figure 1E and J) or RGZ (Figure 1F and K) alone partially attenuated these pathological changes, displaying modest reductions in dermal thickness and collagen accumulation. Strikingly, the combined ATRA+RGZ treatment (Figure 1G and L) demonstrated superior therapeutic efficacy. The skin architecture in the combination group closely resembled that of normal skin, characterized by a loosely arranged collagen network, minimal inflammatory infiltration, and preservation of adnexal structures. Furthermore, quantitative assessment revealed that the combination therapy significantly reduced dermal thickness not only compared to the BLM model group but also compared to either ATRA or RGZ monotherapy (p<0.05, Figure 1B). These findings visually and quantitatively demonstrate that ATRA and RGZ exert enhanced anti-fibrotic effects in alleviating scleroderma-like dermal fibrosis.

Superior suppression of inflammatory and fibrotic gene expression by combined ATRA/RGZ therapy.To examine the effects of pharmacological interventions on inflammatory and fibrotic gene expression in skin tissue, mRNA levels of IL-6, TGF-β1, IL-17A, RORγt, and COL1a1 were quantified using real-time PCR in the PBS, BLM, ATRA, RGZ, and ATRA+RGZ groups (Figure 2). All targets were significantly up-regulated in the BLM group compared with the PBS group (p<0.05). Treatment with ATRA, RGZ, or ATRA+RGZ significantly reduced their expression relative to the BLM group (p<0.05). Notably, expression levels were further decreased in the ATRA+RGZ group compared with either ATRA or RGZ alone (p<0.05), whereas no significant differences were observed between the ATRA and RGZ groups (p>0.05). These findings indicate that pharmacological treatment effectively attenuates inflammatory and fibrotic responses, with combined therapy providing superior efficacy.

Combined ATRA and RGZ treatment directly suppresses fibroblast activation in vitro via STAT3 inhibition.To verify whether the enhanced anti-fibrotic effects of the ATRA and RGZ combination act directly on fibroblast activation, an in vitro model was established using NIH-3T3 fibroblasts stimulated with the pro-fibrotic cytokine TGF-β1. As shown in Figure 5, exposure to TGF-β1 dramatically induced the expression of the extracellular matrix protein COL1A1 and the myofibroblast marker α-SMA, accompanied by a profound elevation in p-STAT3 compared to the vehicle control (p<0.05). Strikingly, the combination of ATRA and RGZ exerted an enhanced suppressive effect, reducing the expression levels of COL1A1 and p-STAT3 significantly more than either monotherapy (p<0.05 vs. ATRA or RGZ alone). These in vitro findings confirm that the ATRA+RGZ combination directly prevents fibroblast-to-myofibroblast transition and excessive collagen production by blocking the STAT3 signaling pathway.

STAT3 activation reverses ATRA+RGZ-mediated amelioration of skin fibrosis in mice.To further investigate the molecular mechanisms underlying ATRA+RGZ-mediated attenuation of skin fibrosis, a STAT3 agonist was administered intraperitoneally in parallel with ATRA+RGZ treatment to assess its effects on fibrotic progression in mice (Figure 6). Histological analysis showed that the PBS control group (Figure 6A and E) maintained normal skin architecture, whereas the BLM group (Figure 6B and F) developed pronounced fibrotic changes. Consistent with earlier findings, ATRA+RGZ treatment markedly reduced skin fibrosis (Figure 6C and G). In contrast, co-administration of a STAT3 agonist with ATRA+RGZ (Figure 6D and H) reversed these protective effects, resulting in increased dermal fibrosis. These results indicate that the antifibrotic efficacy of ATRA+RGZ is mediated, at least in part, through inhibition of STAT3 signaling.

Reactivation of STAT3 Signaling abolishes the anti-fibrotic and anti-inflammatory efficacy of the combination therapy. To further confirm that the therapeutic effects of the ATRA+RGZ combination are specifically mediated through inhibition of STAT3 signaling, a rescue experiment was performed using Colivelin, a selective STAT3 agonist. As shown in Figure 7, Colivelin administration markedly reversed the suppressive effects of ATRA+RGZ on key pathogenic markers. ATRA+RGZ treatment significantly reduced the mRNA expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6 and IL-17A) and the Th17-associated transcription factor RORγt compared with the BLM group (Figure 7A, C, and D), whereas co-treatment with Colivelin significantly restored the expression of these genes (p<0.05 vs. ATRA+RGZ). Similarly, the ATRA+RGZ-induced reductions in fibrosis-related markers, including TGF-β1 and COL1a1, were substantially abrogated by Colivelin treatment (Figure 7B and E).

To verify pathway activation, STAT3 phosphorylation was assessed using western blotting. Consistent with the transcriptional findings, ATRA+RGZ treatment significantly inhibited STAT3 phosphorylation relative to the BLM group, whereas this inhibition was effectively reversed by Colivelin, resulting in restored p-STAT3 levels (Figure 7F and G). Collectively, these results demonstrate that the anti-inflammatory and antifibrotic effects of ATRA+RGZ are critically dependent on suppression of the STAT3 signaling pathway.

Discussion

This study demonstrates that ATRA and RGZ combination treatment significantly alleviates skin fibrosis in a bleomycin-induced scleroderma model through coordinated targeting of STAT3 signaling. Histological and molecular analyses show that ATRA+RGZ is more effective than either monotherapy in reducing dermal thickening, collagen deposition, and inflammatory cell infiltration. Notably, the combination robustly suppresses STAT3 phosphorylation and RORγt nuclear translocation, resulting in down-regulation of fibrotic mediators (TGF-β1 and COL1a1) and Th17-associated cytokines (IL-17A and IL-6). Reversal of these therapeutic effects by STAT3 agonism identifies STAT3 as the central mediator of the observed enhanced efficacy.

The effects of ATRA are mediated through the retinoic acid receptor, a nuclear receptor that regulates gene transcription in the nucleus and modulates signal transduction in the cytoplasm (18). ATRA exhibits context-dependent antifibrotic activity, although the precise mechanisms remain incompletely understood. Notably, ATRA has been shown to suppress STAT3 signaling during skin carcinogenesis, suggesting a conserved mechanism relevant to fibrosis (19). Conversely, ATRA can enhance both priming signaling and glycolytic metabolism required for activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome in human macrophages (20). This pro-inflammatory effect may partly explain the limited efficacy observed with ATRA monotherapy. Importantly, our results suggest that the addition of RGZ may serve as a critical pharmacological counterbalance. While ATRA drives anti-fibrotic reprogramming, its potential to trigger inflammatory pathways like the NLRP3 inflammasome could inadvertently sustain a microenvironment conducive to fibroblast activation. RGZ, through its potent PPARγ-mediated anti-inflammatory actions, likely neutralizes these off-target pro-inflammatory signals. This synergy–where RGZ mitigates the inherent limitations of ATRA while both agents converge to robustly suppress the STAT3 axis–provides a compelling mechanistic rationale for the superior therapeutic outcomes observed with the combination therapy.

In this context, RGZ, a PPARγ agonist, possesses potent anti-inflammatory properties. For example, RGZ has been shown to reduce apoptosis and inflammatory responses in lipopolysaccharide-stimulated human umbilical vein endothelial cells (21). The combination of ATRA and RGZ may enhance ATRA’s antifibrotic efficacy, potentially by counteracting the pro-inflammatory effects of ATRA through RGZ’s anti-inflammatory action.

Our findings are consistent with previous studies demonstrating that the PPARγ agonist pioglitazone prevents TGF-β-induced renal fibrosis by repressing STAT3 (22). In this study, ATRA+RGZ co-treatment markedly inhibited STAT3 phosphorylation (p-STAT3), the critical activation step for this transcription factor. This inhibition was substantially greater than that observed with either agent alone and directly correlated with the combination’s superior suppression of downstream fibrotic responses. The essential role of STAT3 blockade was further confirmed by rescue experiments, in which artificial activation of STAT3 completely abrogated the antifibrotic effects of ATRA+RGZ. Notably, the administration of Colivelin was strategically initiated on day 14, a time point that typically marks the transition from the initial inflammatory phase to the active fibrotic progression phase in the bleomycin-induced model (23). By reactivating STAT3 during this critical window (days 14-28), we successfully demonstrated that the sustained inhibition of the STAT3 axis is indispensable for attenuating bleomycin-induced fibrotic cues and preventing excessive collagen deposition. This temporal intervention provides robust mechanistic evidence that the therapeutic efficacy of the combination therapy is directly dependent on its ability to halt STAT3-driven fibroblast activation during the peak stage of tissue remodeling.

The therapeutic impact of STAT3 inhibition operates through multiple interconnected pathways relevant to scleroderma pathogenesis. First, it disrupts the pathogenic Th17 axis, as evidenced by strong inhibition of RORγt nuclear translocation, which is dependent on STAT3 signaling, and a consequent reduction in IL-17A expression. This effectively attenuates the inflammatory Th17 response, a key driver of autoimmunity and fibrosis. Second, combination therapy significantly down-regulated the expression of canonical profibrotic mediators, including TGF-β1, IL-6, and COL1a1. STAT3 functions as a central hub integrating signals from TGF-β and IL-6 family cytokines, thereby amplifying their profibrotic effects; its inhibition by ATRA+RGZ simultaneously suppresses both cytokine production and downstream signaling (24). The modulation of myofibroblast differentiation by nutritional derivatives presents a promising therapeutic avenue. Similar to the antagonizing effect of Vitamin D on TGF-β/SMAD signaling in myofibroblasts as reported by Shany et al. (25), our findings illustrate that the Vitamin A derivative (ATRA) effectively intercepts the TGF-β/STAT3 profibrotic crosstalk.

The observed synergy in STAT3 pathway inhibition by ATRA+RGZ represents a novel and significant finding. While both agents individually exert anti-inflammatory and antifibrotic effects through various PPARγ- and RAR-dependent and -independent mechanisms, their combined action appears to specifically converge on and maximally suppress STAT3 activation. This provides a mechanistic explanation for the supra-additive effects of ATRA+RGZ on dermal thickness, collagen deposition, and inflammatory infiltration observed in both histological and molecular analyses. This supra-additive effect is highly consistent with previous findings in other disease models. For instance, Rosas et al. demonstrated that retinoids can significantly augment thiazolidinedione-induced PPARγ activation in oral cancer cells (26).

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the bleomycin-induced mouse model of scleroderma does not fully recapitulate the complex and heterogeneous pathophysiology of human scleroderma, which involves genetic, environmental, and epigenetic factors (27). As elegantly reviewed by Nogueira et al. regarding renal fibrosis, animal models are indispensable for elucidating complex pathophysiological mechanisms, yet single-inducer models often fail to capture the full spectrum of human chronic fibrotic progression (28). Thus, further validation in varied preclinical models is warranted. Second, the doses and routes of ATRA and RGZ administration were based on previous murine studies and may not represent the optimal regimen for maximizing therapeutic efficacy while minimizing adverse effects. Further investigations are required to determine the most effective and safe dosing strategies for these agents in the context of scleroderma. Third, our in vitro mechanistic experiments were conducted using the NIH-3T3 embryonic fibroblast cell line. While NIH-3T3 is a well-established and robust model for investigating canonical fibrotic signaling, embryonic fibroblasts may not fully recapitulate the specific phenotypic, epigenetic, and microenvironmental features of adult dermal fibroblasts (29). Therefore, future studies employing primary adult dermal fibroblasts or patient-derived scleroderma cells are warranted to further validate these findings in a more clinically relevant context.

While whole-tissue homogenates capture the cumulative anti-fibrotic effect, skin is highly heterogeneous. To address this, our in vitro experiments specifically isolated the fibroblast compartment. The robust suppression of TGF-β1-induced p-STAT3 and COL1A1 by the ATRA+RGZ combination in isolated fibroblasts unambiguously demonstrates that targeting the STAT3 axis directly halts the fibroblast-to-myofibroblast transition. Although the combination therapy likely acts on both inflammatory cells (as evidenced by reduced Th17 markers in vivo) and resident fibroblasts, our in vitro data provides direct mechanistic evidence that fibroblasts are a primary and direct target of this enhanced drug combination.

Conclusion

In summary, our study demonstrates that the combination of ATRA and RGZ provides a superior therapeutic advantage over monotherapies in ameliorating dermal fibrosis and inflammation in a murine model of scleroderma. We identified that the enhanced efficacy of this dual therapy is critically dependent on the coordinated suppression of the STAT3/RORγt/Th17 signaling axis, which directly halts both aberrant immune responses and fibroblast activation. Given that both ATRA and RGZ are clinically approved agents with well-characterized safety profiles, our findings provide a robust mechanistic foundation for integrating ATRA-driven antifibrotic reprogramming with RGZ-mediated metabolic modulation. This combination represents a highly promising and readily translatable strategy for the treatment of scleroderma.

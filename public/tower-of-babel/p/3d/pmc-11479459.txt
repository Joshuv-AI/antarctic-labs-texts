Is My Doctor Human? Acceptance of AI among Patients with Breast Cancer
Authors: Nitzan Kenig, Aina Muntaner Vives, Javier Monton Echeverria
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Summary:Artificial intelligence (AI) is becoming increasingly important in society, and medicine can benefit from its advantages. What scenario can we envision when AI becomes as powerful and accurate as human physicians? How will the traditional patient–doctor relationship be affected by AI? Will patients come to trust and accept AI-assisted healthcare as much as their human counterparts? Our research team has been working on applications of AI in plastic surgery for more than 4 years. Between 2020 and 2024, AI algorithms were developed by the authors and applied on patients for symmetry evaluation after breast cancer surgery. Patients were aware of being evaluated with images for AI model training and assessment. Feedback was reported, and a survey was carried out among patients who underwent evaluation by our team. Among patients with breast cancer who underwent surgical reconstruction, 65% of patients reported very high levels of comfort with AI, given that it was mediated by a human doctor. Patients stated that nondoctor-mediated AI in medicine would greatly reduce trust. The influence of AI on the patient–doctor relationship is an important aspect that will greatly affect medicine. In this preliminary work, patients showed high levels of trust and comfort with the use of AI in healthcare, despite stating that they knew little about AI. Patients insisted that the mediation of a human doctor is key for acceptance. Currently, little is known about the acceptance of AI in medical roles among patients.

Takeaways

Question: Artificial intelligence (AI) is bound to revolutionize society and medicine. But how will patients react to AI-assisted healthcare? What will be the acceptance and ethical viewpoint about using AI among patients?

Findings: AI models for postoperative evaluations were applied in more than 212 patients between 2020 and 2024. Twenty patients were interviewed and expressed high levels of comfort and trust with AI. However, patients expressed the need for AI to be mediated by a human physician.

Meaning: Although AI will become increasingly more relevant in medicine, with patients showing high level of acceptance, the human factor is currently still demanded by patients.

INTRODUCTION

The patient–doctor relationship is a pillar of medicine. As the profession has evolved, the human aspect of relating to patients has been a constant benchmark. The advent of modern technological medicine did not forsake the fundamental human quality of caregiving.

Artificial intelligence (AI) is becoming part of medicine, prompting changes in the patient–doctor relationship. Humans have different ways to respond to new technology. Although some readily accept novelty, others respond with hesitation. Concerns have been raised about how the use of AI could change plastic surgery,1 medicine,2 and science.3 Trust and acceptance issues with AI will soon emerge.

AI is set to revolutionize plastic surgery.4 AI models have been used to predict public interest in surgery by following their social media,5 eliciting ethical concerns. In the clinical setting, AI has been used for counseling surgical patients with accurate results6 and for postoperative evaluation.7 However, the real-world interaction between patient and AI is still largely untested. Although professionals currently present little to no experience with AI,8 questions remain about how the human–machine interaction will develop in healthcare. In this work, we study the acceptance of AI among patients with breast cancer.

Between 2020 and 2024, our research team developed and applied AI models for evaluation of surgical results in oncological breast reconstruction. In total, we evaluated 212 patients with different AI models. Our team used previously validated AI model Pyolo89 and other publicly available models such as ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude. For this specific iteration, 77 patients were evaluated with frontal images that were then channeled to AI models for a global breast symmetry score. Patients were aware that they were being assessed by AI for their breast symmetry, and the images would be used for training AI models. Through the 4 years of working with AI models in the clinical setting, our team received feedback of emotional response by patients, but the subject has not been closely monitored before this work, which had the aim of taking a deeper look into patient’s reactions to AI in healthcare. Patients were recruited as a series of cases. Inclusion criteria were patients who underwent reconstruction for breast cancer and had been evaluated by our research team with AI models. Patients who consented completed a questionnaire about their reaction to being assessed by AI. The questionnaire for patients included five categories: (1) general knowledge about technology, (2) general knowledge about AI, (3) trust in AI, (4) comfort with being evaluated by AI, and (5) ethical correctness of using AI in healthcare. Participants were offered an open section named “in your own words,” in which they were able to express any further information they wished to share. Scores were evaluated by patients on a scale from 1 to 10, and later divided into 5 categories: 1–2 very low, 3–4 low, 5–6 moderate, 7–8 high, and 9–10 very high. Scores for comfort with the use of AI were very high for 65% of patients. In the trust category, 50% of patients reported high trust levels (Fig. 1). In the “own words” section, recurring themes were the need for a human doctor as a mediator during examination with AI. General acceptance of the use of AI in healthcare was high, but patients requested that the physician could guarantee the AI model was accurate. Some patients stated that they understand AI is an inevitable future, but they do not wish to see the human doctor removed from healthcare.

DISCUSSION

Technological revolutions have recurred throughout history. Once upcoming technology offers greater advantages than older methods, a transitional time is due to appear, in which longstanding approaches are substituted with newer ones. The acceptance of new technology among humans has been subject to different studies, with the technology acceptance theory representing a cornerstone in this field.10 For widespread acceptance to occur, there must be a correlation between the belief in the usefulness of new technology and the intention to use it. Further research refers to trust and external influences as other factors.11

In this pioneer study, patients with breast cancer were largely favorable in accepting the use of AI for their care and expressed high levels of comfort, given the presence of a human doctor during their examination. It is important to mention that a relatively low number of patients are currently interacting with functional AI healthcare models under controlled environments, limiting the total number of patients that can be effectively interviewed about their reactions to AI in healthcare. AI in medicine is in its infancy and a very limited number of studies about acceptance among patients are available; in surgery, perhaps none have been previously published. Further limitations of the study are the little knowledge patients and professionals currently hold about AI, and the subjective aspects of acceptance, trust, and comfort.

Acceptance and trust of AI-assisted healthcare among patients is bound to be a key factor in the future. The human doctor is bound to act as an intermediary and will be responsible for accuracy, safety, and ethics. The emotional response of patients to AI in medicine will be important for universal approval. Further factors could be accessibility, price, and efficiency. Ethical questions and regulations are issues that will need to be addressed by multidisciplinary teams and are currently uncharted waters.

The influence of AI on the patient–doctor relationship is an important aspect that will greatly affect medicine. In this preliminary study, patients expressed high levels of trust and comfort with the use of AI in healthcare. Patients insisted that the mediation of a human doctor is key for their acceptance. Currently, little is known about the acceptance of AI in healthcare among patients, and more research is needed to identify the effects AI will have on healthcare.

Real-world effectiveness of rezvilutamide plus androgen deprivation therapy in patients with high-volume metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer: a retrospective multicenter cohort study
Authors: Baojun Wang, Dan Xia, Yu Pan, Jia Hu, Huaijun Liu, Xiangxiang Zhang, Dong Chen, Hong Huang, Guoqiang Chen, Jiquan Fan, Hongtuan Zhang, Hongyi Wu, Zixiang Li, Gang Zhao, Qi Wang, Li Li, Mingji Ye, Guanglin Yang, Yuanlin Liu, Jianqun Lin, Yanqin Gu, Feng Liu, Zhi Chen, Jin Luo, Liping Wang, Zhou Ying, Ting He, Chaoyu Lin, Xu Zhang, Shaoxi Niu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRezvilutamide has demonstrated significant clinical benefits in patients with high-volume metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) in the phase III CHART study. However, patients enrolled in clinical trials may not fully represent those encountered in routine clinical practice, and real-world evidence in this population remains limited. This study aimed to evaluate the real-world effectiveness of rezvilutamide in combination with androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) in patients with high-volume mHSPC.MethodsThis multicenter retrospective cohort study included patients with high-volume mHSPC who received rezvilutamide plus ADT between August 29, 2023, and April 1, 2026 across 21 offline centers and one online center in China. Outcomes included prostate-specific antigen (PSA) 50 (defined as a ≥50% decline from baseline), PSA90 (defined as a ≥90% decline from baseline), and undetectable PSA (PSA <0.2 ng/mL) rates at 3, 6, 9, and 12 months, as well as the 12-month PSA progression-free rate. Prespecified subgroup analyses were performed according to age, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status, and Gleason score.ResultsA total of 1,888 patients were included, with a median age of 73 years [interquartile range (IQR), 68–79 years]. The median baseline PSA was 70.9 ng/mL (IQR, 5.9–146.0 ng/mL). At 3 months, the PSA50, PSA90, and undetectable PSA rates were 83.3% [1,572/1,888, 95% exact confidence interval (CI): 81.5–84.9%], 71.7% (1,353/1,888, 95% exact CI: 69.6–73.7%), and 44.8% (845/1,888, 95% exact CI: 42.5–47.1%), respectively. Overall, the PSA response rates showed a gradual increasing trend over time. By month 12, the PSA50, PSA90, and undetectable PSA rates had reached 86.0% (282/328, 95% exact CI: 81.7–89.6%), 78.7% (258/328, 95% exact CI: 73.8–83.0%), and 72.0% (236/328, 95% exact CI: 66.8–76.8%), respectively. The PSA progression-free rate at 12 months was 93.4%.ConclusionsThese real-world findings support the clinical utility of rezvilutamide combined with ADT in achieving durable PSA suppression in patients with high-volume mHSPC. However, the absence of comprehensive safety data and the retrospective nature of the study warrant cautious interpretation, and further prospective validation is needed.Keywords: Rezvilutamide, high-volume, hormone-sensitive, castration-sensitive, metastatic prostate cancer

Highlight box

Key findings

• This study evaluated the real-world effectiveness of rezvilutamide in combination with androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) in patients with high-volume metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC).

• Rapid prostate-specific antigen (PSA) responses were observed as early as 3 months after treatment initiation, with 83.3% of patients achieving a PSA decline ≥50% (PSA50), 71.7% achieving a PSA decline ≥90% (PSA90), and 44.8% achieving undetectable PSA levels.

• These responses were sustained and deepened over time, with the PSA50, PSA90, and undetectable PSA rates reaching 86.0%, 78.7%, and 72.0%, respectively, at 12 months, along with a 12-month PSA progression-free rate of 93.4%.

What is known, and what is new?

• Second-generation androgen receptor inhibitors combined with ADT are the standard of care for mHSPC. The CHART study established the survival benefit of rezvilutamide in high-volume disease. However, real-world evidence in this population remains limited.

• This study provides the largest real-world evidence to date in patients with high-volume mHSPC, confirming that the efficacy of rezvilutamide plus ADT observed in clinical trials is reproducible in routine clinical practice, with sustained and progressively deepening PSA responses over time.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• These findings further support the use of rezvilutamide plus ADT as an effective first-line treatment for patients with high-volume mHSPC and its role in achieving durable PSA suppression in real-world practice.

• Future efforts should focus on longer-term real-world follow-up to better characterize survival outcomes and on evaluating outcomes in underrepresented populations, such as patients with poor performance status.

Introduction

Second-generation androgen receptor (AR) inhibitors in combination with androgen deprivation therapy (ADT), with or without docetaxel, have significantly improved survival outcomes in patients with metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) and are currently considered the standard of care in this population (1,2). Among the AR inhibitors approved for this indication, rezvilutamide—the first novel oral AR inhibitor developed in China—combined with ADT has been shown to be a highly effective doublet regimen for the treatment of high-volume mHSPC (3).

Rezvilutamide binds to the ligand-binding domain of the AR and competitively inhibits androgen-AR interactions, thereby suppressing AR signaling and reducing prostate-specific antigen (PSA) secretion (4,5). In the randomized phase III CHART study, rezvilutamide plus ADT significantly improved radiographic progression-free survival (rPFS) and overall survival (OS) compared with bicalutamide plus ADT in patients with high-volume mHSPC, while demonstrating a favorable safety profile (6). Based on these findings, rezvilutamide in combination with ADT was approved in China for the treatment of high-volume mHSPC in 2022 (5), and its use has subsequently expanded in routine clinical practice.

However, the generalizability of CHART findings to routine practice remains to be further evaluated. As a pivotal phase III trial, CHART was conducted under controlled clinical trial conditions and enrolled a selected patient population, including only patients with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) of 0–1 and adequate organ function (6). In contrast, patients treated in daily practice are often more heterogeneous in age, performance status, comorbidity burden, and treatment background. Therefore, real-world evidence is needed to complement clinical trial findings and better characterize the effectiveness of rezvilutamide in broader patient populations encountered in routine care.

To date, real-world evidence regarding rezvilutamide in mHSPC remains limited. A previous multicenter real-world study conducted by our group showed that rezvilutamide plus ADT achieved rapid, deep, and sustained PSA responses in patients with low-volume mHSPC (7). Another multicenter real-world study in China evaluated rezvilutamide plus ADT in patients with high-volume mHSPC, but was limited by a relatively small sample size and short follow-up duration (8). Thus, additional real-world evidence with a larger sample size and longer follow-up is needed in patients with high-volume mHSPC.

PSA response is a readily available biomarker associated with long-term clinical outcomes in patients with mHSPC (9-11). Exploratory analyses from the CHART study demonstrated that achievement of PSA90 and undetectable PSA was significantly associated with improved OS, rPFS, time to PSA progression, and time to castration resistance (11). Therefore, longitudinal evaluation of PSA response in real-world practice may provide clinically meaningful information regarding treatment effectiveness.

This study aimed to evaluate the real-world effectiveness of rezvilutamide in combination with ADT in patients with high-volume mHSPC, with a particular focus on longitudinal PSA response outcomes. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://tau.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tau-2026-0456/rc).

Methods

Study design and population

This was a multicenter retrospective real-world cohort study conducted across 21 offline centers and one online center in China (Table S1). Eligible patients were identified through routine medical records at participating centers and screened against the predefined study eligibility criteria. Patients were eligible if they were aged ≥18 years, had high-volume mHSPC, and received rezvilutamide during the study period. Patients enrolled in other clinical trials of rezvilutamide or considered unsuitable for inclusion by the investigators were excluded from the study. Unsuitability for inclusion referred to cases with insufficient essential baseline information for analysis or clinical conditions deemed inappropriate for initiation of rezvilutamide in routine practice. High-volume disease was defined as the presence of ≥4 bone metastases (with ≥1 lesion located outside the pelvis or vertebral column) or the presence of visceral metastases (excluding lymph node involvement). This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Chinese PLA General Hospital (No. S2022-623-04) and was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. All patients provided written informed consent. All participating hospitals and institutions were informed of and approved the study.

Data collection

Clinical data were collected at each participating center through medical records and/or patient interviews and entered into a centralized electronic data capture system using electronic case report forms. Predefined data fields and standardized variable definitions were used across all participating sites. Data completeness, consistency, and accuracy were routinely reviewed through centralized data monitoring, and data queries were resolved by site investigators when necessary. Baseline variables included age, ECOG PS, Gleason score, baseline PSA levels, prior therapies, time from diagnosis to the first dose of rezvilutamide, and treatment regimens involving rezvilutamide. The effectiveness outcomes assessed in this study included PSA50, PSA90, and undetectable PSA rates at 3, 6, 9, and 12 months after treatment initiation, as well as the 12-month PSA progression-free rate. PSA50 and PSA90 were defined as a ≥50% and ≥90% decline in PSA from baseline, respectively, confirmed by a repeat measurement at least 3 weeks later. Undetectable PSA was defined as the proportion of patients with a baseline PSA ≥0.2 ng/mL who achieved a PSA level <0.2 ng/mL during the study period. PSA progression was defined according to the Prostate Cancer Working Group 3 criteria. PSA measurements obtained within the first 12 weeks were not included in the assessment of progression. PSA progression was defined as a ≥25% increase (with an absolute increase of ≥2 ng/mL) from baseline (if the PSA level at week 12 was not lower than baseline) or from the nadir (if the PSA level at week 12 was lower than baseline). An initial determination of PSA progression required confirmation at least 3 weeks later. Follow-up information was obtained through routine clinical visits and review of available medical records at each participating center. PSA assessments were performed according to routine clinical practice.

Statistical analysis

Because this was a retrospective observational study, no formal sample size calculation was performed. All eligible patients meeting the predefined inclusion criteria during the study period were included. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the study variables. Continuous variables were presented as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs), and categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. The 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for the PSA response rates were calculated using the Clopper-Pearson method. Prespecified subgroup analyses were performed stratifying patients by baseline age (<75 vs. ≥75 years), ECOG PS (0–1 vs. ≥2), and Gleason score (<8 vs. ≥8) to evaluate PSA responses across different subpopulations. Analyses were performed using available data for each variable. Missing baseline variables were reported as missing. PSA response analyses were based on an available-case approach at each predefined time point, and patients were not treated as a fixed longitudinal cohort. All statistical analyses were performed using SAS software version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA).

Results

Baseline characteristics

Between August 29, 2023, and April 1, 2026, a total of 1,888 patients with high-volume mHSPC were included in the analysis. The median age of the patients was 73 years (IQR, 68–79 years), and 42.6% were aged ≥75 years. The ECOG PS was 0 in 414 patients (21.9%), 1 in 1,099 (58.2%), 2 in 250 (13.2%), and ≥3 in 74 (3.9%). A total of 42.2% of the patients had a Gleason score ≥8. The median PSA level was 70.9 ng/mL (IQR, 5.9–146.0 ng/mL). The median time from diagnosis to the first dose of rezvilutamide was 13 days (IQR, 6–67 days). Most patients (1,867; 98.9%) received concomitant ADT, while 10 patients (0.5%) received ADT plus chemotherapy, and 11 patients (0.6%) received ADT in combination with other therapies. The starting dose of rezvilutamide was 240 mg in all patients, with no dose adjustments during the study period. ADT was administered in accordance with local guidelines. PSA response

Longitudinal analysis showed a continued improvement in PSA response, driven mainly by an increase in the proportion of patients achieving undetectable levels.

At 3 months, 83.3% of the patients (1,572/1,888; 95% CI: 81.5–84.9%) achieved a PSA50 response. This increased to 85.6% (864/1,009; 95% CI: 83.3–87.7%) at 6 months, 86.1% (502/583; 95% CI: 83.0–88.8%) at 9 months, and remained high at 86.0% (282/328; 95% CI: 81.7–89.6%) at 12 months.

For PSA90, the response rate was 71.7% (1,353/1,888; 95% CI: 69.6–73.7%) at 3 months, increasing to 76.5% (772/1,009; 95% CI: 73.8–79.1%) at 6 months and 76.7% (447/583; 95% CI: 73.0–80.1%) at 9 months, and further increasing to 78.7% (258/328; 95% CI: 73.8–83.0%) at 12 months, demonstrating a consistent upward trend.

The improvement in the undetectable PSA rate was particularly notable. At 3 months, the undetectable PSA rate was 44.8% (845/1,888; 95% CI: 42.5–47.1%), increasing markedly to 58.2% (587/1,009; 95% CI: 55.1–61.2%) at 6 months and 62.3% (363/583; 95% CI: 58.2–66.2%) at 9 months, and further increasing to 72.0% (236/328; 95% CI: 66.8–76.8%) at 12 months. Subgroup analysis

Overall, except for the ECOG PS ≥2 subgroup, the PSA50, PSA90, and undetectable PSA rates increased with longer follow-up across all subgroups, with the most pronounced improvement observed in the undetectable PSA rate. Specifically, the undetectable PSA rate increased from 49.2% at 3 months to 72.5% at 12 months in patients aged <75 years, and from 38.8% to 71.2% in patients aged ≥75 years; from 47.1% to 80.9% in the ECOG PS 0–1 group, and from 33.0% to 33.3% in the ECOG PS ≥2 group. It also increased from 55.2% to 73.5% in patients with a Gleason score <8 and from 44.4% to 77.7% in those with a Gleason score ≥8 (Table 3).

In the age subgroups, differences in the PSA50 and PSA90 responses between patients aged <75 and ≥75 years were relatively small. The PSA50 rates at 3, 6, 9, and 12 months were 82.8% vs. 83.9%, 84.7% vs. 87.0%, 84.2% vs. 88.5%, and 84.7% vs. 87.8%, respectively. The corresponding PSA90 rates were 71.8% vs. 71.4%, 74.4% vs. 79.6%, 76.4% vs. 77.1%, and 77.8% vs. 79.9%, respectively. The undetectable PSA rate was consistently slightly higher in the <75 years group than in the ≥75 years group at all time points, although the difference narrowed over time: 49.2% vs. 38.8% at 3 months, 59.7% vs. 56.0% at 6 months, 65.5% vs. 58.1% at 9 months, and 72.5% vs. 71.2% at 12 months.

The patients with an ECOG PS of 0–1 consistently demonstrated higher PSA50, PSA90, and undetectable PSA rates than those with an ECOG PS of ≥2 at all time points. At 3 months, the PSA50, PSA90, and undetectable PSA rates were 85.4% vs. 74.1%, 73.4% vs. 64.2%, and 47.1% vs. 33.0%, respectively. At 6 months, the corresponding rates were 88.2% vs. 75.1%, 79.1% vs. 65.8%, and 63.2% vs. 35.8%; at 9 months, 87.2% vs. 82.4%, 78.2% vs. 71.2%, and 69.0% vs. 36.0%; and at 12 months, 89.8% vs. 69.8%, 82.4% vs. 63.5%, and 80.9% vs. 33.3%, respectively.

Between the Gleason score <8 and ≥8 subgroups, the PSA50 and PSA90 responses were generally comparable across all time points. At 3 months, the PSA50 and PSA90 rates were 83.1% vs. 84.9% and 75.1% vs. 73.4%, respectively. At 6 months, they were 86.0% vs. 86.4% and 76.7% vs. 79.2%; at 9 months, 88.2% vs. 86.1% and 76.5% vs. 79.0%; and at 12 months, 88.2% vs. 87.1% and 85.3% vs. 79.9%, respectively. For the undetectable PSA rates, the Gleason score <8 group showed higher rates than the Gleason score ≥8 group in the early phase (55.2% vs. 44.4% at 3 months; 66.3% vs. 60.1% at 6 months), but this difference gradually narrowed over time, showing a reversed trend in later follow-up (68.6% vs. 66.7% at 9 months; 73.5% vs. 77.7% at 12 months).

Discussion

To our knowledge, this study represents the largest real-world analysis to date evaluating rezvilutamide in patients with high-volume mHSPC. Our findings demonstrate that rezvilutamide in combination with ADT achieves rapid, deep, and sustained PSA declines in a real-world setting, with a 12-month PSA progression-free rate as high as 93.4%.

PSA levels are an important prognostic marker for disease progression and survival in metastatic prostate cancer. PSA typically rises earlier than radiographic progression and serves as a readily available and cost-effective prognostic biomarker. Evidence from previous clinical trials and real-world studies has consistently shown that PSA kinetics—including the speed, magnitude, and duration of decline—are closely associated with clinical outcomes such as rPFS, OS, and time to castration resistance (12-15). Exploratory analyses from the CHART study also indicated that PSA dynamics are a valuable prognostic indicator in patients with mHSPC treated with rezvilutamide plus ADT, with achievement of PSA90 and undetectable PSA being significantly associated with improved OS, rPFS, time to PSA progression, and time to castration resistance (11). In the present study, rezvilutamide combined with ADT demonstrated rapid, deep, and sustained PSA responses, suggesting a potential beneficial impact on long-term outcomes in patients with high-volume mHSPC.

In the present study, the undetectable PSA rates at 3, 6, 9, and 12 months were 44.8%, 58.2%, 62.3%, and 72.0%, respectively. These rates were numerically higher than those reported in several phase III clinical trials involving patients with high-volume mHSPC, including the CHART study (38.3%, 52.2%, not reported [NR], and 61.7%, respectively) (11), the ARASENS study (26.0%, 43.0%, 51.0%, and NR) (10), the ARANOTE study (NR, 44.0%, 48.0%, and 50.0%) (16), and the LATITUDE study (31.8%, 47.6%, NR, and 55.0%) (17). Unlike randomized clinical trials with prespecified assessment schedules and fixed analysis populations, the present study was conducted in routine clinical practice. PSA response analyses were performed according to predefined evaluation time points, and only patients with available PSA assessments were included in the corresponding analyses. Consequently, the number of evaluable patients progressively decreased over time because of incomplete follow-up assessments, staggered enrollment, and varying follow-up durations. As a result, later analyses may have included a greater proportion of patients who remained on treatment and had sufficient follow-up data, potentially contributing to higher observed response rates. Therefore, direct cross-study comparisons should be interpreted with caution. However, the 3- and 6-month undetectable PSA rates in the present study were slightly lower than those reported in the TITAN study in the overall population (51.0%, 61.0%, NR, and 64.0% at 3, 6, 9, and 12 months, respectively) (15). This discrepancy may be explained by the generally poorer prognosis of patients with high-volume disease compared with those with low-volume disease, a finding consistently reported across multiple phase III trials, including TITAN (18-20). Notably, in the present study, the PSA response rates in the high-volume population were overall lower than those previously reported by our group in patients with low-volume mHSPC, further supporting this observation (7). Taken together, these findings suggest that disease burden remains an important determinant of the depth of PSA response. Despite differences in study populations and study designs, the overall PSA response patterns observed in the present study were broadly consistent with those reported in pivotal clinical trials, supporting the generalizability of the clinical trial evidence to routine clinical practice.

Compared with real-world studies evaluating other standard treatments for mHSPC (12-14), the PSA response rates observed in the present study were numerically favorable. However, such comparisons should be interpreted with caution because of differences in study populations and analytical approaches. In addition, although a previous real-world study in China showed that rezvilutamide combined with ADT achieved rapid and deep PSA responses in patients with high-volume mHSPC, this study was limited by a relatively small sample size (n=236) and only reported 3-month PSA response data (8). Conversely, the present study reported PSA response rates from 3 to 12 months in a large real-world population. Importantly, by providing longitudinal evidence from a substantially larger cohort, the present study extends the currently available real-world evidence and demonstrates that the favorable PSA responses observed during the early treatment period can be maintained and further deepened over time in routine clinical practice.

In the present study, the 12-month PSA progression-free rate was 93.4%, which is comparable to the rates reported in the overall populations of the CHART, ARASENS, and ARCHES studies (10,11,20). This finding is consistent with the high undetectable PSA rates observed in this study, which have been shown to be significantly associated with PSA progression (10,15,16). Taken together, the consistency between the PSA response outcomes and PSA progression-free outcomes further supports the effectiveness of rezvilutamide plus ADT in maintaining durable disease control in routine clinical practice.

Across subgroups defined by baseline characteristics, the PSA50, PSA90, and undetectable PSA rates generally increased with longer treatment duration, with the most pronounced improvement observed in the undetectable PSA rate, except in the ECOG PS ≥2 subgroup. These findings suggest that rezvilutamide in combination with ADT provides substantial PSA responses across most clinically relevant patient subgroups. In this study, the impact of age and Gleason score on PSA response was limited, indicating that both elderly patients and those with high Gleason scores can benefit from this regimen. This is consistent with the OS subgroup analyses in the CHART study (11). These findings suggest that advanced age or a high Gleason score alone should not preclude consideration of rezvilutamide plus ADT, as meaningful PSA responses can still be achieved across these patient groups. Similarly, a real-world study evaluating apalutamide reported that age and Gleason score had limited influence on short-term PSA response (8). Conversely, performance status had a more pronounced impact on PSA response. The patients with an ECOG PS of 0–1 consistently demonstrated higher PSA50, PSA90, and undetectable PSA rates at all time points compared with those with an ECOG PS of ≥2. Moreover, in the ECOG PS ≥2 subgroup, the undetectable PSA rate showed minimal improvement over time, suggesting that this population may be less likely to achieve sustained and progressively deeper PSA responses. Notably, most phase III clinical trials, including CHART, have excluded such patients (10,11,16,17), and the ECOG PS ≥2 subgroup in the present study was relatively small. Therefore, these findings should be interpreted with caution and warrant validation in larger prospective cohorts. Nevertheless, the observed differences suggest that baseline performance status may influence the depth and durability of PSA response and should be considered when counseling patients regarding treatment expectations in routine clinical practice.

Overall, the present study provides important complementary evidence to the CHART trial by demonstrating that the favorable PSA responses achieved with rezvilutamide plus ADT under controlled trial conditions can also be observed in a large and heterogeneous real-world population. These findings reinforce the role of rezvilutamide plus ADT as an effective treatment option for patients with high-volume mHSPC in routine clinical practice.

This study had several limitations. First, its retrospective design introduces the potential for selection bias, which may affect the robustness of the findings. Second, due to the inherent constraints of real-world retrospective data collection, data incompleteness was inevitable across participating centers. Adverse-event information was not collected in a standardized manner, and some baseline and longitudinal data were missing, which may have influenced the analysis and interpretation of the results. Therefore, the present study primarily reflects treatment effectiveness and does not provide a complete assessment of the safety profile of rezvilutamide in routine clinical practice. In addition, information regarding treatment discontinuation and its reasons, disease progression, and mortality outcomes was not systematically captured across participating centers. Third, the number of evaluable patients decreased at later time points because of staggered enrollment, limited follow-up duration, and incomplete follow-up assessments. Therefore, later time-point analyses should be interpreted as outcomes among patients with available PSA measurements at each specific time point rather than a complete longitudinal cohort, and may be subject to potential attrition bias with overrepresentation of patients remaining on treatment. Fourth, certain subgroups, such as patients with an ECOG PS of ≥2, had relatively small sample sizes, which may limit the interpretability of subgroup findings. Finally, given the descriptive and single-arm nature of the study, multivariable analyses were not performed, and residual confounding cannot be excluded. Overall, this study focused on PSA response, a surrogate endpoint that has been consistently associated with long-term outcomes in both randomized clinical trials and real-world studies. The multicenter design and large sample size may enhance the robustness and generalizability of the findings.

Conclusions

To our knowledge, this study represents the largest real-world investigation to date focusing specifically on patients with high-volume mHSPC. Consistent with findings from the phase III CHART trials, rezvilutamide in combination with ADT was associated with rapid, deep, and sustained PSA responses—a key prognostic indicator of survival—as well as a high 12-month PSA progression-free rate in routine clinical practice. These findings provide important real-world evidence supporting the effectiveness of rezvilutamide plus ADT in patient with high-volume mHSPC. However, given the retrospective nature of the study and the absence of comprehensive safety data, the results should be interpreted with appropriate caution.

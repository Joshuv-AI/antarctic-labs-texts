Association between renal function and simple renal cyst growth: a retrospective study
Authors: Yanhao Shen, Yinan Li, Jiawen Yang, Dong Wang, Dian Gu, Biao Tao, Kai Wang, Xianlin Xu, Yan Shen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveSimple renal cysts (SRC) are associated with a high incidence of proteinuria and renal insufficiency. However, the effects of renal function, particularly estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), on SRC size remains unclear. This study aimed to investigate the impact of renal function on SRC size.MethodsThis retrospective study included 502 patients diagnosed with SRC from Sir Run Run Hospital, Nanjing Medical University, between August 2019 and February 2022. Among them, 185 patients were followed up. SRC size and growth were monitored by B-ultrasound. The relationship between eGFR levels and SRC size/growth was analyzed by linear regression and logistic regression analysis.ResultsWe found that patients with lower eGFR had significantly larger SRC sizes and higher growth rates. Low eGFR level was associated with increased the risk of larger maximum SRC volume. Furthermore, eGFR level was also negatively correlated with the growth rate of SRC. Multivariate analysis revealed that low eGFR levels were related to larger size and faster growth of SRC.ConclusionRenal function, as measured by eGFR, was inversely associated with both the size and growth rate of SRC. Monitoring renal function in patients with low eGFR could help in early detection and management of SRC growth.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40001-025-03312-w.Keywords: Simple renal cysts, Estimated glomerular filtration rate, Renal function, Cyst growth

Introduction

Simple renal cysts (SRC) are the most common type of cystic kidney disease, and their incidence increases with age [1]. Almost half of people over 50 have one or more renal cysts [1]. The formation of SRC is related to various factors, including age, male gender, obesity, hyperuricemia, diabetes, hypertension and renal dysfunction [2]. However, SRC is now regarded by more and more scholars as not so simple, which was previously considered to have a minor impact on body function and did not require follow-up [3–5]. In addition, due to the lack of preventive measures for the growth of renal cysts, once patients are diagnosed with SRC, they usually have to wait until the cyst size requires surgical treatment. Thus, it is considered necessary to identify the risk factors for SRC growth.

The influence of SRC on renal function has been widely studied throughout the world [6–8]. Some researchers have found that SRC has no association with declined renal function [8, 9], while others believe that the formation and development of SRC associated with the decline of renal function [1, 6, 7]. However, few researchers focus on the impact of renal function on the growth of SRC. Glomerular filtration rate (GFR) is a key indicator for assessing renal function, which reflects the ability of the kidneys to remove waste and excess fluid from the blood. Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) is a GFR estimate calculated based on factors such as serum creatinine level, age, gender, and race, providing a quick and simple tool for evaluating renal function in clinic. It is widely used to diagnose chronic kidney disease and assess its progression [10]. Therefore, we performed this retrospective observational study to explore the effects of renal function on the size and growth rate of SRC based on eGFR.

Materials and methods

Patients and assessments

This retrospective observational study was performed according to the criteria of the STROBE guidelines [11]. The patients hospitalized in Sir Run Run Hospital Nanjing Medical University from August 2019 to February 2022 who were diagnosed with SRC by B-ultrasound were collected. Inclusion criteria were: (1) age over 18 years; (2) renal cysts diagnosed by B-ultrasound. Exclusion criteria were: (1) missing key laboratory data; (2) lack of imaging evidence; (3) polycystic kidney disease; (4) eGFR < 15 mL/min/1.73 m2; (5) with malignant disease, infection disease, or acute heart failure; (6) pregnancy; (7) history of renal cyst treatment or renal transplantation; (8) missing data of other variables. This study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by Sir Run Run Hospital Nanjing Medical University Ethics Committee (2021-SR-036). All patients enrolled in this research signed informed consent in advance. A total of 502 patients were included in this study (Fig. 1). Among them, 185 patients had follow-up data of SRC examined by B-ultrasound.

Clinical variables

Demographic data (age, gender and BMI), and laboratory examination data [urea nitrogen (BUN), serum creatinine (SCr), serum sodium, serum potassium, serum chlorine, fasting plasma glucose (FBG), serum globulin, serum albumin, total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) and lipoprotein A] were collected from medical records. History of complications such as hypertension, diabetes and kidney stone were also recorded. Assessments of SRC

The SRC data of length, width, height, number and location were all obtained from B-ultrasound examination report. All B-ultrasound examinations were performed by professional B-ultrasound doctors in our hospital and conducted according to standardized protocols. SRC was defined as the lesion on kidney ultrasound appears echolucent, with a smooth and clear boundary and a thin but distinct wall, as described in our previous study [13]. Statistical analysis

Statistical Product and Service Solutions 25.0 software (IBM Company, USA) was used for the analysis of data in this study. Normality of the data was tested using Kolmogorov–Smirnov test and graphical methods. Continuous variables that conform to a normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation, while those that conform to a non-normal distribution were expressed as the median (interquartile range). Continuous variables were compared using Student’s t test for independent samples or Mann–Whitney U test, depending on the results of normality test. Categorical data were presented as frequency (percentage) and compared by Chi square test or Fisher’s exact test. The analysis of differences for continuous variables among more than two groups were conducted by ANOVA test. The associations between eGFR and the size and growth rate of SRC were used linear regression analysis and logistic regression analysis. Any baseline variables that reached statistical significance were adjusted in the multivariate model as covariates. In addition, the variables of hypertension and diabetes known to have influence on eGFR were also adjusted in the multivariate model as covariates. When exploring the relationship between eGFR and the growth rate of SRC, the data were weighted by follow-up time to correct for the problem of data heterogeneity caused by unbalanced follow-up time. Restricted cubic spline (RCS) regression model with 4 knots of eGFR was used to explore any nonlinear association between eGFR and SRC size. Sensitivity analysis was employed to verify the stability of the results. P < 0.05 indicates that the difference is statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics of patients with SRC

A total of 603 patients were diagnosed with renal cysts, and according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 502 patients were eventually enrolled (Fig. 1). Among them, 353 (70.3%) were male and 149 (29.7%) were female. The median age of these patients was 62.0 (53.0 ~ 71.0) years. According to the median volume of the maximum SRC, we divided these patients into two groups: large cyst group (n = 251) and small cyst group (n = 251). Patients with larger cysts had higher BMI, higher levels of BUN, SCr, TC, and TG, as well as lower levels of eGFR and serum globulin. In addition, patients with larger cysts tended to have multiple cysts and bilateral cysts (Table 1). Among 502 patients with SRC, 185 patients have follow-up data of SRC size records. The median follow-up time was 30 (2 ~ 63) months. There were no significant statistical differences in the several baseline characteristics between these 185 patients and the 317 patients without follow-up data, except for gender and cyst number (Table S2).

Relationship between eGFR level and SRC size

To explore the association between eGFR level and SRC size, we firstly divided the patients into four quartiles based on the level of eGFR. When compared with the patients in the first quartile, the patients in the fourth quartile had significantly smaller maximum SRC volumes (Fig. 2A). A negative relationship between eGFR and SRC size after logarithmic transformation was also observed in Fig. 2B. In the logistic regression models, whether or not the covariates were adjusted, low eGFR levels were still a risk factor for the occurrence of larger SRC size (Table 2). In the multivariate logistic regression model, compared with the reference of the lowest eGFR quartile, the ORs with 95% CIs for larger SRC size in the highest quartile were 0.494 (0.288–0.863). Subsequently, we divided the eGFR into a binary variable based on the cut-off value of 90 mL/min/1.73m2. We found that the risk of the patients with eGFR ≥ 90 mL/min/1.73m2 developing larger size of SRC decreased by 39.3% when comparing with the patients with eGFR < 90 mL/min/1.73m2 (Table S1). RCS curves indicated that no non-linear correlation was observed between eGFR and maximum SRC volume, regardless of whether the covariates were adjusted or not (Fig. 3).

Association between eGFR level and SRC growth rate

In order to determine the causal relationship between eGFR level and SRC size, we followed up on the cyst size change of these SRC patients. The median SRC growth rate was 0.3 cm3 per year. According to this value, 185 SRC patients with follow-up were divided into two groups: high SRC growth rate group (n = 92) and low SRC growth rate group (n = 93). When compared with the patients with low SRC growth rate, the patients with high SRC growth rate had higher rate of male, higher BMI, higher levels of SCr, as well as lower rate of kidney stone, lower levels of eGFR and HDL-C. In addition, the patients with high SRC growth rate also had higher baseline and follow-up maximum cyst volume (Table S3). Correlation analysis result indicated that baseline eGFR level was negatively related to SRC growth rate (Fig. 4). Then, the weighted linear regression analysis was employed to further explore the association between eGFR level and SRC growth rate (Table 3). The results suggested that the growth rate of cysts could increase by 0.021 cm3/year for each unit decrease in baseline eGFR after the model adjusted with covariates. Low eGFR level could be an independent risk factor for higher growth rate of SRC.

Sensitivity analysis

In sensitivity analysis, we firstly we conducted an unweighted linear regression analysis. The results showed that eGFR level still negatively correlated with SRC growth rate in the univariate and multivariate analysis (Table S4). Then, continuous variable of eGFR were divided into four quartiles and weighted logistic regression analysis was performed. Compared with the reference of the lowest eGFR quartile, the ORs with 95% CIs for the risk of higher SRC growth rate in the second, third and fourth quartile were 0.776 (0.641–0.939), 0.378 (0.317–0.450) and 0.261 (0.216–0.315), respectively (Table S5). The unweighted logistic regression analysis also indicated that the risk of higher SRC growth rate in the patients of the highest quartile was 74.2% lower than that in the lowest quartile (Table S6). All these indicated that our results were stable.

Discussion

This retrospective study investigated the association between renal function and the size and growth rate of SRC based on the patients from a single-center. Our results that eGFR level was negatively correlated to the size and growth rate of SRC. Moreover, lower eGFR level was the independent risk factor for larger SRC size and higher SRC growth rate. Decreased renal function may be associated with SRC growth.

Currently, the most commonly used renal function indicators in clinical include eGFR, SCr, cystatin C, and so on. eGFR is calculated based on parameters such as SCr, age, gender, and race. When compared with SCr, eGFR can more accurately quantify the glomerular filtration capacity and is one of the gold standards for evaluating renal excretory function. Previous researches mainly focused on the effect of SRC on renal function [1, 6–9, 14–17]. A large-scale cross-sectional study found that individuals with SRC had a significantly increased risk of proteinuria and decreased eGFR, especially in patients with multiple cysts and larger cyst diameters [7]. In addition, the continuous growth and increased quantity of SRC are also believed to be related to the further deterioration of renal function [1]. Large SRC, especially those with a diameter exceeding 2.2 cm, seems to be positively correlated with proteinuria and decreased renal function [4]. Notably, the presence of SRC may also affect young adults, leading to early signs of kidney damage such as proteinuria [16]. However, few scholars have paid attention to the influence of renal function on the volume and growth rate of SRC.

Our results indicated that patients with lower eGFR levels had significantly larger baseline maximum SRC volumes. Furthermore, our results also suggested that the growth rate of cysts could increase by 0.021 cm3/year for each unit decrease in baseline eGFR level. Based on these results, we speculate that there may be a reciprocal relationship between the decline in renal function and the growth of SRC. Although this change might appear small on a yearly basis, it could become clinically significant over a longer follow-up period, particularly in patients with impaired renal function. While increased SRC size is rarely fatal, it has the potential to cause symptoms such as flank pain, hypertension, or even cyst rupture. Therefore, for patients with SRC and a low baseline eGFR, we recommend including monitoring of SRC growth as part of their overall health management.

The decrease of renal function may promote the SRC growth through multiple mechanisms, including local hypertension and increased renal blood flow [18]. To maintain the stability of the internal environment, the kidneys may increase the load in certain areas through adaptive changes in the renal tubules and glomerulus, leading to excessive physical stress in the nephron where the cyst is located, thereby promoting the expansion of the cyst. This heightened stress could accelerate cyst expansion. At the same time, the decrease of renal function might also be accompanied by changes in endocrine function, especially abnormal water and salt metabolism in the kidneys and fluctuations in hormone levels [19]. These changes may affect the proliferation and apoptosis of local cells in the kidneys, further promoting the growth of cysts. Furthermore, the reduction in eGFR leads to impaired filtration function, which might cause urine retention in the renal tubules and increasing intracystic pressure and promoting cyst growth [17]. The decline in eGFR is also frequently accompanied by chronic inflammation and fibrosis, which can alter vascular architecture, impair blood supply, and induce local hypoxia, thus creating a microenvironment conducive to cyst expansion [17]. In the current research, compared with the reference of the lowest eGFR quartile, only the highest eGFR quartile showed a statistically significant association with lower SRC size (Table 2). However, the 2nd and 3rd quartile were not significant. This might be limited by small sample size, resulting in insufficient statistical power and the inability to detect smaller or moderate effects. The effect in the highest quartile was greater, and it was more likely to be detected as significant.

There are some limitations in the current research. To begin with, all the patients in this study were Chinese. Caution should be exercised in interpreting the association between eGFR and the size and growth rate of SRC using the results of this study. Moreover, this research was designed as a single center study. The sample size was small and the follow-up time was relative short. Long-term follow-up multicenter studies with large sample are still necessary. Furthermore, the current study did not exclude the effects of drugs on the growth of SRC, which might not represent the natural process of SRC. Lastly, the size of all SRC was evaluated by B-ultrasound. The differences among B-ultrasound operators could not be corrected.

Conclusion

Our results showed that eGFR level was negatively associated with larger maximum SRC size and higher SRC growth rate. Close follow-up of renal function for patients with low eGFR level and the adoption of appropriate intervention measures might help prevent the SRC growth.

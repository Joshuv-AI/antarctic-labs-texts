Safety and efficacy of the feed additives l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride (monohydrate and anhydrous) for all animal species (Wacker Chemie AG)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Ilen Röhe, Katerina Theodoridou, Montserrat Anguita, Matteo L Innocenti, Joana P Firmino
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the safety and efficacy of l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride (monohydrate and anhydrous) as flavouring feed additives for all animal species. The use in feed of l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride (monohydrate and anhydrous), produced by electrochemical reduction of l‐cystine, was considered safe for the target species when supplemented at concentrations up to 25 mg/kg complete feed. The FEEDAP Panel considered that the use of l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride (monohydrate and anhydrous) in animal nutrition is safe for the consumers of products from animals receiving the additives, and to the environment. Regarding user safety, l‐cysteine was not considered a skin and eye irritant nor a skin sensitiser. The additives l‐cysteine hydrochloride anhydrous and l‐cysteine hydrochloride monohydrate are corrosive to eyes and respiratory tract, but no conclusion can be reached on their skin irritation potential; therefore, any exposure is considered a risk. As l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride are used in food as flavouring compounds, it is expected that they can provide a similar function in feed, and no further demonstration of efficacy is necessary under the proposed conditions of use.Keywords: efficacy, Escherichia coli DSM 34232, flavourings, l‐cysteine, l‐cysteine hydrochloride, l‐cystine, safety, sensory additives

INTRODUCTION

Background and Terms of Reference

Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 1 establishes the rules governing the Community authorisation of additives for use in animal nutrition. In particular, Article 4(1) of that Regulation lays down that any person seeking authorisation for a feed additive or for a new use of a feed additive shall submit an application in accordance with Article 7.

The European Commission received a request from Wacker Chemie AG 2 for the authorisation of the additives l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride in its monohydrate and anhydrous forms, produced by electrochemical reduction of l‐cystine (produced with Escherichia coli DSM 34232), when used as feed additives for all animal species (category: nutritional additives, functional group: amino acids, their salts and analogues; and category: sensory additives, functional group: flavouring compounds). During the assessment, the applicant withdrew the application as nutritional additives. 3

According to Article 7(1) of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, the Commission forwarded the application to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) as an application under Article 4(1) (authorisation of a feed additive or new use of a feed additive). The dossier was received on 28 June 2023, and the general information and supporting documentation are available at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA‐Q‐2023‐00437. The particulars and documents in support of the application were considered valid by EFSA as of 10 June 2024.

According to Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, EFSA, after verifying the particulars and documents submitted by the applicant, shall undertake an assessment in order to determine whether the feed additives comply with the conditions laid down in Article 5. EFSA shall deliver an opinion on the safety for the target animals, consumer, user and the environment and on the efficacy of the feed additives l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride (monohydrate and anhydrous) when used under the proposed conditions of use (see Section 3.1.3 ).

Additional information

l‐Cysteine produced by chemical synthesis or protein hydrolysis is currently authorised as a sensory additive for use in feed for all animal species (2b17033). 4

l‐Cysteine hydrochloride monohydrate produced by hydrolysis of keratin from avian feathers is currently authorised for use in feed for cats and dogs (2b920). 5 l‐Cysteine hydrochloride monohydrate produced by chemical synthesis or protein hydrolysis is currently authorised as a sensory additive for use in feed for all animal species except cats and dogs (2b920). 6

l‐Cysteine hydrochloride monohydrate produced by fermentation using different production strains is currently authorised for its use in all animal species as a sensory additive (2b920i). 7

EFSA issued several opinions on the safety and efficacy of these products when used in feed for all animal species (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013a, 2013b; EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2020a, 2020b).

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The present assessment is based on data submitted by the applicant in the form of a technical dossier 8 in support of the authorisation request for the use of l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride (monohydrate and anhydrous) as feed additives.

In accordance with Article 38 of the Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 9 and taking into account the protection of confidential information and of personal data in accordance with Articles 39 to 39e of the same Regulation, and of the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down practical arrangements concerning transparency and confidentiality, 10 a non‐confidential version of the dossier has been published on Open.EFSA.

According to Article 32c(2) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 and to the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down the practical arrangements on pre‐submission phase and public consultations, EFSA carried out a public consultation on the non‐confidential version of the technical dossier from 17 December 2024 to 07 January 2025, for which no comments were received.

The confidential version of the technical dossier was subject to a target consultation of the interested Member States from 27 June 2024 to 27 September 2024, for which the received comments were considered for the assessment.

The FEEDAP Panel used the data provided by the applicant together with data from other sources, such as previous risk assessments by EFSA and peer‐reviewed scientific papers, to deliver the present output.

EFSA has verified the European Union Reference Laboratory (EURL) report as it relates to the methods used for the control of l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride (monohydrate and anhydrous) in animal feed. 11

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and efficacy of l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride (monohydrate and anhydrous) is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 12 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the consumer (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017a); Guidance on the identity, characterisation and conditions of use of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017b); Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the target species (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017c); Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the environment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2019); Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the users (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023); and Guidance on the assessment of the efficacy of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024).

ASSESSMENT

The products subject of this application are l‐cysteine, l‐cysteine hydrochloride monohydrate and l‐cysteine hydrochloride anhydrous (≥ 98% l‐cysteine on an as is basis), produced by electrochemical reduction of l‐cystine produced with Escherichia coli DSM 34232. They are intended to be used as sensory additives (functional group: flavouring compounds) in feed for all animal species.

Characterisation

Manufacturing process 13

The additives under assessment are chemically produced using as starting material l‐cystine produced by fermentation with a genetically modified E. coli K‐12 derivative (DSM 34232). The l‐cystine and the production strain are fully characterised in a previous EFSA opinion (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2025).

l‐Cysteine hydrochloride monohydrate is produced by electrochemical reduction of l‐cystine ■■■■■. It is produced in the form of colourless crystals, which are then isolated by filtration or centrifugation and dried. The obtained l‐cysteine hydrochloride monohydrate is available in two forms (according to the particle size), referred to as l‐cysteine hydrochloride monohydrate or l‐cysteine hydrochloride monohydrate ‘mesh20’.

The l‐cysteine hydrochloride monohydrate can be converted into its anhydrous form ■■■■■.

l‐Cysteine is obtained ■■■■■, and subsequent crystallisation, filtering and drying.

The data provided showed compliance with the specifications set by the applicant. The total amount of identified material on an as is basis was > 99%. The FEEDAP Panel considers that the microbial contamination and the levels of the impurities analysed in these additives are of no concern.

Conditions of use

l‐Cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride (monohydrate and anhydrous) are intended to be used as sensory additives in complete feed for all animal species at a maximum recommended level of 25 mg/kg complete feed.

Safety

Safety for the target species, consumers and the environment

The additives l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride (monohydrate and anhydrous) are produced by electrochemical reduction of l‐cystine produced by fermentation with a genetically modified E. coli K‐12 derivative (DSM 34232). Concerns on the use of the additives would not derive from the l‐cysteine or l‐cysteine hydrochloride, which are considered safe, but may arise from residues of the fermentation process/production strain remaining in the final product. No safety concerns were identified for the production strain, the fermentation process and its residues/metabolites (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2025). Moreover, the additives l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride are highly purified (≥ 98% l‐cysteine and < 1% unidentified material on an as is basis). Since the levels proposed for the use of l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride (monohydrate and anhydrous) as flavouring compounds (25 mg/kg complete feed) are substantially lower than the animal requirements as nutrients, the FEEDAP Panel considers l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride as safe for the target species when used as flavouring compounds.

The absorption and metabolic fate of l‐cysteine in the organism are well known. The amino acid l‐cysteine, supplemented to feed, will be incorporated into proteins of tissues and/or products of animal origin, and any of its potential excess will be metabolised and excreted. Therefore, the protein composition of tissues and products of animal origin will not be affected using l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride in animal nutrition. Therefore, the Panel considers that the use of the additives in animal nutrition is safe for the consumer.

The amino acid l‐cysteine is a physiological and natural component of animals and plants. When consumed, it will be absorbed, and the non‐absorbed fraction will be incorporated into the intestinal microbial mass and excreted as such. The use of the additives l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride in animal nutrition would not lead to any localised increase in the concentration in the environment. It is concluded that the use of l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride as feed additives does not represent a risk to the environment.

Safety for the user

Based on the highest dusting potential measured value for l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride (see Section 3.1.2 ), the FEEDAP Panel considers that the exposure of users through inhalation is likely.

The skin irritation potential of l‐cysteine was tested in a study performed according to OECD Testing Guideline (TG) 404, 17 which showed that the additive is not a skin irritant (UN GHS No Category).

An acute dermal toxicity study with l‐cysteine was performed according to the OECD Guideline 402. 18 The results showed that there were no signs of toxicity (UN GHS No Category).

The eye irritation potential of l‐cysteine was tested in a study performed according to OECD TG 405, 19 which showed that the additive is not an eye irritant (UN GHS No Category).

The skin sensitisation potential of l‐cysteine was tested in a study performed according to OECD TG 429 20 and the Method B42 Skin sensitisation of Commission Regulation (EC) No 440/2008, 21 which showed that the additive is not a skin sensitiser (UN GHS No Category).

The skin corrosion potential of l‐cysteine hydrochloride anhydrous was tested in a study performed according to the OECD TG 431, 22 which showed that the additive is not corrosive to the skin (UN GHS No Category).

The eye irritation potential of l‐cysteine hydrochloride anhydrous was tested in a study performed according to OECD TG 437, 23 which showed that l‐cysteine hydrochloride anhydrous is corrosive to the eyes (UN GHS Category 1).

The FEEDAP Panel considers that, by applying read‐across, the results of l‐cysteine hydrochloride anhydrous are extended to l‐cysteine hydrochloride monohydrate and monohydrate ‘mesh20’.

Conclusions on the safety for the user

Based on the information available, the additive l‐cysteine is not irritant to the skin and eyes nor a skin sensitiser. The additive l‐cysteine hydrochloride anhydrous is considered to be corrosive to eyes and the respiratory tract, but no conclusion can be reached on the skin irritation potential. By applying read‐across, the results of l‐cysteine hydrochloride anhydrous are extended to l‐cysteine hydrochloride monohydrate.

Efficacy

As l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride are used in food as flavouring compounds, it is expected that they can provide a similar function in feed and no further demonstration of efficacy is necessary.

CONCLUSIONS

The use of l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride (monohydrate and anhydrous) in feed is safe for the target species when supplemented at concentrations up to 25 mg/kg complete feed.

The use of l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride (monohydrate and anhydrous) in animal nutrition is considered safe for the consumer and for the environment.

Regarding user safety, l‐cysteine is not considered a skin and eye irritant nor a skin sensitiser. The additives l‐cysteine hydrochloride anhydrous and l‐cysteine hydrochloride monohydrate are considered to be corrosive to eyes and the respiratory tract, but no conclusion can be reached on their skin irritation potential; therefore, any exposure is considered a risk.

As l‐cysteine and l‐cysteine hydrochloride are used in food as flavouring compounds, it is expected that they can provide a similar function in feed and no further demonstration of efficacy is necessary.

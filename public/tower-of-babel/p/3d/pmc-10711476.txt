Effect of yoga on adipokine levels among overweight and obese people: A systematic review
Authors: Meenakshi P. Chobe, Ramesh Mavathur Nanjundaiah, Shivaji Chobe, Nagaratna Raghuram
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAdipokines have an important role in the pathophysiology of overweight and obesity and associated inflammatory diseases. Objective: The present review aims to evaluate the role of Yoga on adipokines among people with overweight and obesity.MethodsAuthors performed a systematic search for relevant research studies as per the PRISMA guidelines in Google Scholar, Medline/Pubmed, Scopus, Web of Science, PsychInfo electronic databases.Two independent authors conducted the selection of articles, data extraction, assessment of the risk of bias for individual studies. Any disagreements were resolved by discussion with the third author.ResultsEight randomized trials and four uncontrolled trials involving a total of 1054 participants were included. Yoga with varying frequencies was administered for different durations. The studied adipokines among overweight and obese were leptin, adiponectin, interleukin-6 (IL-6), Tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), chemerin, visfatin, plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1), and transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β). The methodological quality of the included studies was low to moderate on the Cochrane risk of bias tool and Newcastle-Ottawa Quality Assessment Scale. The higher the frequency and duration of Yoga practice, the more significant changes in the adipokine levels were seen.ConclusionThe present review indicates that Yoga practices positively impacts adipokines among people with overweight and obesity. However, the present study precludes the generalizability of results due to the methodological heterogeneity, the type of Yoga intervention, and settings.

Introduction

Obesity is a state of abnormally accumulated adipocytes that has the potential to impair health. Global prevalance of obesity 5% among childrens and 12% among adults. [1]. Obesity is increasing globally, while in 70 countries it become double in the last 40 years [1]. Obesity is higher in females than in males. 11 % of adult males and 15 % of adult females all over the world have obesity [2]. Obesity is characterized by inflammation, in which numerous pro-inflammatory cytokines circulate in the blood. The adipokines released from adipose tissue have a significant role in regulating the metabolic processes and maintaining the homeostasis between food intake and energy balance [3]. Excess of fats in obesity alters the adipokine profile and is associated with disturbed metabolic functions [4]. Upregulation of adipokines like IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α, leptin, resistin, and irisin exerts pro-inflammatory effects. Adiponectin, TGF-beta, and visfatin are the two crucial adipokines with anti-inflammatory effects [5]. The upregulation of pro-inflammatory adipokines is responsible for different non-communicable diseases. It leads to insulin resistance [6], high blood pressure [7], increased risk of cardiovascular complications [8,9], and cancer [10]. There are various treatment options for weight reduction, like herbal formulations [11], conventional medications [12], surgery [13], and behavioral treatment like diet and exercise [1,14]. Weight loss by pharmacological treatments takes a longer time, and bariatric surgeries have several side effects [15]. Hence cost-effective, safe interventions for weight loss in overweight and obesity are essential. In recent times, the use of mind-body therapies for weight reduction is increased. Mind-body interventions like Yoga, Qi gong, acupuncture, guided relaxation, hypnosis, and biofeedback effectively reduce weight [16].

Yoga is a spiritual discipline that originated in ancient times in India. Sage Patanjali's Yoga sutras explain the essence of Yoga in eight limbs or steps. Hatha yoga also explains various techniques of Yoga practice for health, wellbeing, and spiritual growth. Yoga is lifestyle intervention. It comprises asanas (physical postures), kriyas (cleansing techniques), pranayama (breathing practices), and dhyana (meditation). Self-discipline and moral conduct are also essential components of yoga practice. Yoga-based interventions have either one or more components of these practices. In recent times, Yoga is considered one of the mind-body interventions. It is cost-effective and safer therapy. Yoga is beneficial for improving the quality of life, prevent many illnesses, and bring harmony to the physiological processes by restoring homeostasis. The practice of Yoga is effective in non-communicable diseases (NCDs) like obesity, diabetes, heart disease, and respiratory diseases [17,18]. Yoga also improves mental and cognitive health [19]. Previous studies show that Yoga has a positive effect on reducing body weight [20]. Another study reported that Yoga regulates lipid metabolism and energy expenditure [21]. Among various pathways of obesity, adipokines play a vital role in pathogenesis. The present study is planned to conduct a systematic review to assess the effectiveness of yoga interventions on adipokines in overweight and obese people.

Methods

The reporting framework for this review is based on PRISMA guidelines (Preferred Items for Systematic Reviews and Metanalysis) [22]. We did not achieve the apriori registration to conduct this review.

Eligibility criteria

Types of studies

All the randomized controlled trials (RCTs), non-randomized controlled trials(N-RCTs), and uncontrolled studies conducted among overweight and obese subjects were included in this review. Studies published only in the English language and available only as full papers were eligible and included. Studies excluded yoga along with other physical activity as intervention, studies on childhood obesity, case reports and studies published in other than english languages were excluded.

Types of intervention

Studies including at least one of the yoga-based techniques like kriyas, asanas, pranayama, dharana, dhyana, chanting, and relaxation as an intervention were included. No limitations regarding the type, duration, and frequency of Yoga intervention were placed. The control groups with no treatment or any active control treatment were eligible.

Types of participants

All the studies involving subjects with body mass index ≥23 kg/m2 were included [23,24].

Types of outcome measures

Studies had to assess at least one adipokine as an outcome measure.

Search methods

A comprehensive search in Google Scholar, Medline/Pubmed, Scopus, Web of Science, PsychInfo electronic database was carried out for papers published till September 2020. The literature search was constructed around the search terms for 'yoga,' search terms for 'adipokines' and search terms for 'overweight and obesity.' The search strategy used for Medline/Pubmed was Yoga [Title/Abstract] AND Adipokines [MeSH Terms]. Two authors independently did the literature search and identified the relevant papers for the review. The authors read all the research articles separately to determine whether the papers met the criteria for eligibility.

Data extraction and management

A data extraction form was developed, and the two reviewers extracted the data independently. The extracted data included the details of participants (age, gender), interventions (yoga protocol), control group (number of control groups, type of interventions), outcome measures (adipokines), and findings. Any disagreements were resolved by discussion with the third author.

Assessment of risk of bias for individual studies

The risk of bias in RCTs was assessed using the Cochrane risk of bias tool for seven criteria [25]: random sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding of participants and personnel, blinding of outcome assessment, incomplete outcome data, selective reporting, and another source of bias. The risk of bias was assessed by two review authors independently. For each of the criteria, the risk of bias is considered as low, high, or unclear risk of bias.

The risk of bias for uncontrolled trials was assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Quality assessment scale for four criteria [26]: selecting participants, comparability of groups, exposure, and outcome assessment. A total of 10 stars can be given to each study using this instrument. The more the number of stars, the lower the risk of bias for the study. Any disagreements were resolved by discussion with the third review author.

Results

Literature search

Through the online literature search, a total of 104 records were extracted. Filtering of irrelevant and duplicate articles rendered 32 relevant articles with full papers for review. Of these, 12 research articles were relevant to this review. After going through the abstracts, the studies were categorized as RCTs, N-RCTs, single group pre-post/ uncontrolled studies. Eight RCTs and four uncontrolled studies were included in this systematic review to evaluate the efficacy of Yoga on various adipokine levels among overweight and obese people [Fig. 1]. Participants

The studies, in total, included 1054 participants with an age range between 17 and 76 years; sample sizes ranged from 16 to 260. The participants of all the studies included were overweight or obese. Participants with overweight and obese were decided based on the mean body mass index mentioned in each study. All the studies included both male and female participants except for three studies [27,29,37].

Intervention

Yoga interventions of different styles were used in the included studies. Yoga interventions used in these studies included one or more of the following components-cleansing techniques (kriyas), physical postures(asanas), breathing techniques(pranayama), relaxation, and meditation [Table 3]. The duration of yoga intervention varied from 1 week to 1 year in these studies. The duration of each session was ranging from 45 to 120 min, with a maximum of 60–300 min of yoga intervention in a day. Among RCTs, one study included yoga session three times a week for 16 weeks [27], one study included twice a day for two weeks supplemented with a vegetarian diet of 1650 kcal/day [28], one study for six weeks [30], one study included three weekly sessions for a year [31], two studies applied yoga sessions for 12 weeks [29,32], two studies included Yoga for 1 h and 2 h a day respectively for five days a week for eight weeks [33,34]. Among single group pretest-posttest studies, one study applied daily yoga sessions for one week [35], two studies for 2 h each day for ten days spread over two weeks [36,37]. Dropouts and attendance

Eight studies mentioned the drop out of the subjects during the course of the intervention [28,30,[32], [33], [34],37,38]. The dropout rate was varying from 0% to 41.17 %. Four studies [28,32,37,38] had a dropout rate of more than 30 %, and the remaining studies have less than 20 % (0–18.5 %) dropout rate. Only one RCT reported the attendance of the participants [29].

Adverse events

Two studies reported explicitly about adverse effects; one study mentioned that there was no adverse event during Yoga intervention [28]. Another study reported [29] as back and shoulder problems in two study participants.

Outcome measures

Six studies assessed the impact of Yoga on leptin levels; six studies assessed the adiponectin levels along with other adipokines except one [27]. Six studies [29,32,34,36,37] assessed the interleukin-6 (IL-6) levels and four studies assessed the tumor necrosis factor-alpha(TNF-α) levels [29,32,34,36]. Effect of yoga on adipokines

Four studies reported a decrease in the leptin levels [[31], [32], [33],35], one study reported an increase in the leptin levels [28], and one study reported no changes in the leptin levels [30] due to yoga intervention. Six studies [27,[30], [31], [32], [33],37] reported a significant increase in adiponectin levels after the practice of yoga intervention, whereas one study reported an insignificant decrease in adiponectin levels [Telles S et al., / 2014]. All the six studies [29,32,34,[36], [37], [38]] reported a significant decrease in IL-6 levels, three studies [29,34,36] reported a significant reduction in TNF-alpha levels, and one study [32]reported an insignificant increase in TNF-alpha after 12-week yoga intervention. One study [39] reported a nonsignificant decrease in vaspin levels, one study [31] reported a significant reduction in chemerin, PAI-1, and visfatin levels, and one study reported a significant decrease in TGF-beta levels [34] following the yoga practice. Discussion

This systematic review aims to assess the role of Yoga on adipokine levels in overweight and obese people. Of the eight RCTs included in the study, five reported a significant change in adipokines after Yoga practice, compared to the control [27,29,31,33,34]. Other four uncontrolled trials also reported positive outcomes of adipokines after Yoga practices among overweight and obese. Widely assessed adipokines were leptin, adiponectin, IL-6, and TNF-alpha. Yoga interventions have shown a positive impact on these adipokines. Yoga has also shown positive results on other adipokines like TGF-beta, vaspin, chemerin, PAI-1. These adipokines were assessed in only one study.

In the included studies in the review the frequency of yoga practice ranged from once a week to six days a week. It was observed that studies with increased frequency and overall duration of yoga practice yielded significant positive changes when compared to the control. This finding is congruent with the study conducted by Kiecolt-Glaser JK et al., in 2012. In this study, novice and expert yoga practitioners compared to assess the health benefits of Yoga. It was found that long-term and/or more intensive yoga practice improves health by altering leptin and adiponectin production [40]. Other research study by N Moliver et al., in 2011 also concludes that increased yoga practice predicts lower body mass index [41]. Overweight and obesity are common manifestation of the energy imbalance where energy intake exceeds the energy expenditure [42]. This manifests in the body as imbalance in adipokine levels, disturbed hypothalamo-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis and dysregulated autonomic nervous system function. Pro-inflammatory cytokines which are not only the markers for inflammation but also indicate a negative impact on the health, are associated with increased adiposity and its complications. It is observed in the reviewed studies that Yoga intervention downregulates pro-inflammatory adipokines (leptin, IL-6, TNF-alpha) and increased the anti-inflammatory adipokine (adiponectin). Falkenberg R et al., in 2018 reported similar findings that Yoga downregulates IL-6 and TNF-alpha levels in the body. Yoga is a potential complementary intervention to treat and prevent inflammation-associated diseases [43]. Yoga, through its impact on adipokine levels, autonomic nervous system and HPA axis modulation, improves the energy balance and thereby helps to make the physical, physiological and psychological status better in the overweight and obese [44]. No systematic review of Yoga and adipokines among overweight and obese people has been conducted to date. This review included uncontrolled and randomized controlled studies. Our review shows positive evidence of efficacy for Yoga on overweight and obesity by downregulating the leptin, adiponectin, and IL-6 levels in the body. It is also concluded that more research on a wide range of adipokines is needed before definite conclusions could be drawn and generalized.

Limitations and future directions

More RCTs are required to draw a definite conclusion about the results. Included RCTs weakly adopt the methodological standards for RCTs. Using the standard methodological design is essential in future studies. Studies included in this review incorporated different Yoga techniques within the yoga domain. Homogeneity in the yoga protocol and following standard methodology would have yielded a better outcome. Comparing Yoga intervention with available gold standard physical interventions will help to assess the superiority of Yoga. The trials were not reported explicitly (except two RCTs) about any adverse events related to the intervention. Though Yoga interventions are safer reporting them explicitly in studies is essential. Studies were lacking to report Yoga session attendance. The participant's attendance record needs to be maintained to understand adherence to Yoga and its impact on adipokines. Yoga is a mind-body intervention; mental awareness and cognitive processes stability play a significant role during practice. A well-versed Yoga therapist could deliver appropriate instructions and proper understanding of Yoga techniques to the participants. Hence, reporting about the training, the experience of Yoga therapists or teachers will also add value to Yoga intervention quality. Further, a meta-analysis could not be done due to the availability of less number of randomized controlled trials and heterogeneity of yoga intervention in the available studies. Yoga studies without a control group has high risk of bias. In RCTs Risk of bias on Cochrane risk of bias tool was low to moderate. It is necessary to plan the clinical trials appropriately to keep the risk of bias at minimum level.

Conclusion

The present review indicates that Yoga practices positively improves adipokines among people with overweight and obesity. Majority studies shows yoga practice reduces leptin, IL-6, TNF-α level and increases adiponectin level. Reduced leptin decreased adipose tissue, improvement in leptin resistance, regulation of satiety and energy regulation that helps in weight loss. Increased adiponectin and decreased IL-6 and TNF- α level reduces inflammation, healthier modulation of endocrine and immune function among obese and overweight people.

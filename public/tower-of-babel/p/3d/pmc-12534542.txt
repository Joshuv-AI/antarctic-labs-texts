A dynamic lightweight blockchain sharding protocol for autonomous collaborative combat of UAV swarms in denied environments
Authors: Yifan Xu, Xin Zhu, Yuhao Bai, Ziliang Chen, Meng Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn recent years, major military powers have been advancing the “Anti-Access/Area Denial” (A2/AD) defense strategy. unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) swarms operating in denied environments require robust information security measures, and blockchain technology offers a potential solution for enhancing their security. However, traditional UAV blockchain systems in denied environments face significant challenges, including excessive resource consumption, unbalanced workloads, inefficient consensus mechanisms, and insufficient security. To tackle these challenges, we propose the Collaborative Operations UAV Blockchain Sharding Protocol (COUAVChain). First, we design a hierarchical sharding blockchain architecture and a hybrid lightweight/global node storage scheme, significantly reducing communication and storage overhead in heterogeneous UAV swarms. Second, we propose a dynamic reputation mechanism to assess node trustworthiness and introduce the Resource-Reputation Constrained Label Propagation Algorithm (R2CLPA), which optimizes resource-aware load balancing and prevents Byzantine node clustering while minimizing cross-shard transactions. Finally, we introduce the Reputation-Bayesian HotStuff (RB-HotStuff) consensus algorithm, which leverages a dynamic reputation mechanism and Bayesian inference to establish a weighted voting model. This approach enhances consensus reliability and efficiency while improving shard scalability by reducing intra-shard communication overhead. Experimental evaluations and comparative analysis demonstrate that the proposed protocol outperforms the baseline schemes in terms of system throughput, consensus latency, workload balancing, resource consumption, scalability, and security. It effectively enhances the collaborative operations of UAV swarms in denied environments.Keywords: Denied environments, Unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) swarms, Blockchain, Sharding technology, Information securitySubject terms: Computer science, Information technology

Introduction

In recent years, the rapid advancement of military technologies, particularly in unmanned systems, has positioned UAV warfare as a key element of modern combat. Due to their agility, stealth, and efficiency, UAVs have shown great potential in reconnaissance, strike missions, and logistical support. Notably, UAV swarm warfare, with its exceptional flexibility, adaptability, and cost efficiency, has gained widespread adoption in military operations. Recognized as a vital component of future warfare, UAV swarms exhibit significant effectiveness in battlefield electronic warfare, terrain reconnaissance, precision strikes, and surveillance tracking. However, the deployment of UAV swarms in actual combat missions presents several challenges. In A2/AD environments, UAV swarms must contend with severe electromagnetic interference, concentrated firepower, and cyber threats, leading to challenges such as single-point failures, information security risks, and degraded operational coordination. These issues not only hinder the operational effectiveness of UAV swarms but also present significant risks to their survivability and mission success. Consequently, achieving secure and efficient coordination of UAV swarms in denied environments while maintaining operational effectiveness is a pressing challenge.

Blockchain technology has found extensive applications in domains such as digital currencies1–3, the Internet of Things (IoT)4–7, and healthcare8–11, serving as a secure data management framework. By integrating cryptographic methods, blockchain technology guarantees data confidentiality, integrity, availability, traceability, and resistance to tampering. Recent advancements have further extended blockchain applications into more complex, data-intensive, and security-sensitive scenarios. For instance, blockchain has been combined with transfer learning to enable adaptive and efficient authentication in Industrial IoT environments, reducing model training time while preserving user privacy6. In 5G-enabled intelligent transportation systems, hierarchical trust evaluation frameworks have been proposed based on federated learning and multi-layer blockchain architecture, supporting dynamic and fair trust management for both users and service providers12. To address the challenge of efficient data retrieval and scalability, semantic sharding techniques have been introduced, enabling structure-aware storage and trust feature indexing on-chain13. Meanwhile, in edge computing empowered IoT, blockchain-based secure data aggregation strategies have emerged, incorporating access control via security labels and energy-efficient task scheduling under privacy constraints14. These approaches provide useful perspectives for addressing the challenges of security, scalability, and trust in UAV blockchain systems. The fundamental similarity between blockchain systems and multi-UAV systems as distributed self-organizing networks offers insights into leveraging blockchain technology for autonomous cooperative UAV control. On one hand, blockchain’s inherent security features help mitigate threats like single points of failure and cyberattacks in UAV systems. On the other hand, blockchain’s smart contract-based distributed coordination offers a unified foundational platform for key aspects of multi-UAV autonomous control. However, traditional UAV blockchain systems often fail to consider the mixed computational capabilities and limited resources of heterogeneous UAV swarms in denied environments. All UAVs store a full copy of the blockchain or mission data, exponentially increasing overall system storage costs and resulting in significant data redundancy. Among the consensus mechanisms commonly used in UAV blockchain systems, the Proof of Work (PoW) consensus demands substantial computational resources; Delegated Proof of Stake (DPoS) enhances efficiency by limiting consensus participants but at the cost of decentralization; PBFT ensures correct consensus when less than one-third of nodes are malicious. However, its high communication overhead hinders scalability, making it impractical for large UAV swarms. Blockchain sharding, introduced in 201615, enhances performance and scalability by dividing nodes into multiple shards, each handling a subset of data and transactions while maintaining security. Integrating sharding into UAV blockchain systems helps mitigate storage and communication overhead. However, in UAV swarm operations under denied environments, existing blockchain sharding solutions face several critical challenges. First, UAV swarms operate in highly dynamic and demanding battlefield environments, which result in frequent cross-shard transactions. A high ratio of such transactions increases system workload and significantly raises communication overhead. Second, UAV formations face varying mission complexities and data loads. Existing sharding approaches often overlook load balancing, leading to “hot” shards, which suffer from transaction congestion and increased confirmation delays, and “cold” shards, which have low transaction density, wasting computational and bandwidth resources. This imbalance reduces system efficiency and operational effectiveness. Third, sharding reduces the number of nodes per UAV sub-swarm, amplifying the risk of Byzantine node clustering. Existing schemes largely overlook this issue, potentially concentrating malicious or faulty nodes within specific shards, thereby compromising security and diminishing combat effectiveness. Fourth, existing sharding schemes typically use PoW or PBFT for intra-shard consensus. PoW-based sharding incurs high transaction latency, while PBFT-based schemes suffer from poor scalability, as communication complexity escalates with swarm size. Neither approach meets the real-time demands of UAV swarm combat.

To tackle these challenges, we propose COUAVChain, a dynamic and lightweight blockchain sharding scheme designed for UAV swarm operations in denied environments. The key contributions are as follows:

This layered approach significantly reduces communication overhead. Furthermore, a hybrid lightweight/global node storage scheme minimizes storage costs for heterogeneous UAV swarms.

Related work

Blockchain for UAVs

Blockchain is regarded as a potential solution for enhancing UAV network security, with blockchain consensus playing a key role in maintaining synchronized UAV operations. Consequently, numerous studies have investigated the integration of blockchain into UAV networks. Xu et al.16 introduced a UAV-assisted IoT data collection approach, where UAVs collect data from IoT devices within their range and log it as transactions on a blockchain they maintain, enhancing resistance to malicious UAV attacks. Guan et al.17 developed a master-sub blockchain framework for UAV data protection, where the master blockchain is shared by all servers and UAVs, while sub-blockchains are managed by UAV formations to ensure trusted UAV positioning in case of ground station disconnection. Yu et al.18 proposed a UAV network security framework that integrates MEC and blockchain to enhance the security and privacy of computation offloading between UAVs and IoT devices. Kang et al.19 developed a blockchain-enabled permissioned B5G UAV network, leveraging a blockchain managed by ground base stations to secure UAV data storage and sharing. A major drawback of these approaches is the lack of consideration for UAVs’ constrained storage and computing resources, as they require UAVs to store full blockchain copies or task data, limiting scalability and hindering large-scale deployment. Recent research has begun addressing UAVs’ constrained storage and computational resources. Xiong et al.20 proposed AerialBC, a lightweight blockchain framework designed for resource-limited UANET environments, utilizing an optimized PBFT consensus algorithm and smart contract-driven task allocation to enhance UAV collaboration. Bera et al.21 proposed a blockchain-powered low-energy secure data transmission and collection model, ensuring the protection of IoT data gathered by UAVs and managed through a ground-station-maintained blockchain. Singh et al.22 introduced a blockchain-based cybersecurity mechanism for UAVs, ensuring secure inter-UAV communication. Additionally, they developed a Deep Boltzmann Machine consensus mechanism that optimizes UAV consensus by factoring in computational resources, battery power, and flight duration. Feng et al.23 proposed a blockchain-driven secure data-sharing model for 5G UAVs, incorporating attribute-based encryption (ABE) to safeguard command transmission and data exchanges. Wang et al.24 proposed RescueChain, a UAV-assisted disaster response system integrating blockchain and reinforcement learning, effectively tackling security and resource constraints via a lightweight blockchain framework and vehicle fog computing-based offloading strategy. A key limitation of these methods is their reliance on centralized servers or computing nodes for data collection, storage, and consensus, rendering them ineffective for decentralized UAV swarm operations in denied environments.

Reputation mechanism

Reputation mechanisms are crucial for maintaining security and reliability in distributed systems. As a decentralized system, blockchain is vulnerable to various malicious activities, including Byzantine failures, fraud, and cyberattacks. To address these threats, reputation mechanisms can be employed to reward honest nodes, identify and penalize malicious nodes, and thereby enhance system security and stability. Xu et al.25 proposed Score Grouping-PBFT (SG-PBFT), a reputation-enhanced PBFT variant, in which reputation scores are updated every 50 voting rounds, and low-scoring nodes are relegated to the end of the vehicular consensus node pool. Zhou et al.26 developed a lightweight searchable data-sharing model integrating reputation-enhanced PBFT with public key encryption, equation testing, and proxy re-encryption to enable efficient and secure data exchange. Tong et al.27 introduced a reputation-based voting system that enhances the probability of high-credit nodes being elected as primary nodes through an inter-node voting process. While these approaches enhance security, reduce consensus communication overhead, and improve system efficiency, they overlook the dynamic nature of reputation assessment, potentially leading to high-reputation nodes monopolizing consensus and fostering centralization. Zhang et al.28 proposed the Dynamic Vehicle Reputation Consensus (DVRC) algorithm, which leverages blockchain to enhance IoV security and communication efficiency by assessing vehicle behavior and consensus participation. Oliveira et al.29 proposed a reputation-driven consensus model in which only nodes surpassing a predefined trust threshold can append blocks. A randomly assigned panel of judges evaluates consensus node behavior and updates their reputation scores. While these methods enable dynamic reputation assessment and updating, their reliance on fixed evaluation intervals makes them vulnerable to sophisticated attacks, such as on-off strategies. Furthermore, the lack of reputation decay for inactive users creates a security loophole, as attackers could hijack dormant high-reputation accounts to compromise the network.

Blockchain sharding

To address the scalability bottleneck of blockchain, namely the optimization of throughput, transaction latency, and storage overhead, numerous researchers have proposed various solutions. Elastico15, introduced in 2016, was the first permissionless blockchain to implement sharding. Since then, extensive research has been conducted on blockchain sharding, leading to the development of several advanced protocols. Elastico achieves approximately linear computational scalability by having committees verify disjoint transaction sets in parallel, selects nodes using PoW, periodically reassigns nodes, and tolerates up to 1/4 of nodes being compromised. OmniLedger, introduced by Kokoris-Kogias et al.30, utilizes a verifiable random function (VRF) for leader selection and employs an unbiased random number for shard partitioning. However, like Elastico, it has a limited capacity to tolerate malicious nodes at the system level. Furthermore, Elastico implements only transaction sharding, meaning each node must store the entire state ledger, leading to significant storage and communication overhead. Its small shard sizes make it vulnerable to malicious control, whereas OmniLedger’s larger shards result in higher intra-shard consensus costs. To overcome the limitations of Elastico and OmniLedger, RapidChain31 applies sharding within a synchronous protocol to enhance throughput, It employs the cuckoo rule for node reconfiguration, ensuring shard security by relocating only a small subset of nodes at each reshuffling, and can tolerate up to 1/3 of malicious nodes. Monoxide32 introduces asynchronous consensus zones and employs node address-based network sharding. It also proposes Chu-ko-nu mining, which ensures that attacking a single shard under the PoW consensus mechanism incurs the same cost as attacking the entire network. However, malicious nodes can easily predict their shard assignments, and the PoW consensus algorithm, combined with a high percentage of cross-shard transactions, leads to higher energy consumption than most other sharding solutions. Huang et al.33 proposed RepChain, an innovative reputation-based blockchain system that significantly improves blockchain throughput and security by integrating sharding technology with a dual-chain architecture consisting of a transaction chain and a reputation chain. Hong et al.34 introduced Pyramid, a hierarchical sharding blockchain protocol designed for linear scalability and efficient cross-shard transaction handling. However, its static hierarchical sharding configuration lacks adaptability to dynamic environmental changes, which significantly compromises system security. Xi et al.35 proposed HMMDShard, a dynamic sharding scheme leveraging the Hidden Markov Model (HMM) to enable adaptive incremental updates in blockchain shards. This method significantly reduces cross-shard transaction rates, but the authors do not clarify how shard states should be adjusted in response to network changes. Zhang et al.36 introduced a reputation-based dynamic shard reconfiguration scheme that incorporates node performance and behavioral characteristics into reputation calculations while introducing substitute leaders to ensure shard system stability. This approach mitigates the risk of collusion attacks resulting from the clustering of malicious nodes within a shard and lowers blockchain system latency. However, it does not specifically address cross-shard transactions involving related transaction data. Furthermore, these approaches predominantly employ PoW or PBFT for intra-shard consensus, which results in either significant consensus delays or limited shard scalability.

COUAVChain protocol design

In this section, we present COUAVChain, a dynamic, lightweight state sharding protocol specifically designed to support UAV swarm autonomous collaborative combat in denied environments. We begin by outlining the foundational infrastructure and storage architecture of COUAVChain, followed by a detailed discussion of its dynamic reputation mechanisms, adaptive sharding algorithms, and lightweight consensus protocols.

Infrastructure and storage scheme

Considering the features of a denied environment, combat operations in such scenarios can be categorized into three stages: multi-platform transportation and deployment, ad hoc networking and approach flight, and autonomous collaborative mission execution. This process is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Blockchain-assisted UAV Swarm autonomous collaborative combat in denied environments.

During the Multi-platform transportation and deployment phase, various transport platforms, such as advanced motherships, strategic transport aircraft, or land-based launch vehicles, are utilized to deploy UAV combat groups across air, sea, and land, depending on mission needs. At this stage, the temporary command and control center on the transport platform serves as the command node, overseeing UAV swarm operations, defining flight paths, identifying combat objectives, and initializing UAV parameters and mission data, such as identity ID, model, storage, computational power, speed, altitude, battery life, and operational status (online/offline). The command node retains global mission data and UAV attribute parameters, covering mission types (e.g., reconnaissance, strike), operational maps, target details, task assignments, and predefined flight paths. Additionally, the command node categorizes UAVs into global nodes and lightweight nodes depending on storage capacity, ensuring that global nodes receive comprehensive mission data, while lightweight nodes retain only the necessary details of their assigned tasks, flight paths, subtask IDs, and attributes. This storage strategy minimizes overall storage overhead by concentrating data on a few global nodes while ensuring lightweight nodes store only essential information. Once mission data and parameters are initialized, UAVs are sharded based on subtask IDs, forming multiple execution-layer shards. Within each shard, nodes communicate and maintain an execution-layer blockchain that records UAV identities and mission data. In the ad hoc networking and approach flight stage, UAVs from different platforms navigate to designated locations and integrate into fully functional combat swarms, achieving autonomous coordination and dynamic networking in denied environments. Upon nearing the combat zone, each shard elects the node with the highest computational power and mission data storage capability as its command node. These command nodes communicate to build a command-layer blockchain, storing UAV identities and mission data. This results in a hierarchical dual-layer UAV blockchain, with command-layer nodes coordinating missions to minimize communication costs. During the autonomous cooperative mission execution phase in a denied environment, multiple UAV groups form mission clusters. UAV nodes achieve consensus on task status data, task command data, UAV status data, and task collection data. Through mutually coordinated reconnaissance search, cooperative target tracking, and collaborative ground attack, complex missions are carried out in the denied environment, thereby enhancing operational effectiveness.

To meet the stringent confidentiality requirements of military UAV deployments, COUAVChain introduces a lightweight data privacy protection scheme tailored for denied environments. This scheme is designed to safeguard sensitive information against eavesdropping and other potential threats. Specifically, it ensures that UAV status data (e.g., location, battery level, mission state), task-related information (e.g., commands, targets), reputation metrics (e.g., node trust scores), and system state data (e.g., UAV identities and shard assignments) are encrypted before being recorded on the blockchain. The encryption process leverages the Elliptic Curve Integrated Encryption Scheme (ECIES) based on the secp256k1 curve, which provides strong cryptographic security while imposing minimal computational and bandwidth overhead—making it particularly suitable for resource-constrained UAV platforms. Access control is enforced via public key cryptography, allowing only authorized nodes with valid operational clearance to decrypt the data using securely managed private keys. This ensures that even if blockchain data is intercepted, adversaries cannot extract intelligible content. By leveraging the immutability of the blockchain, the scheme guarantees data confidentiality while preserving the integrity and auditability of encrypted records. Seamlessly integrated with COUAVChain’s hierarchical sharding and lightweight storage architecture, the proposed privacy mechanism enables secure and autonomous UAV swarm operations in denied environments.

System workflow

The key operational workflow of COUAVChain consists of four major phases, as depicted in Fig. 2.

Task and reputation consensus phase.

Task and reputation consensus phase.

At the start of each epoch, execution shards aggregate multiple task transactions from the transaction pool and generate task blocks through the RB-HotStuff algorithm. The number of task blocks created per epoch by an execution shard is influenced by the leader’s tenure and network parameters like bandwidth. In every epoch, the first task block produced by an execution shard follows the state block, which documents the previous epoch’s state partitioning. Once intra-shard consensus is achieved, the command node disseminates the task blocks and updated reputation scores to the command shard for inter-shard consensus. After inter-shard consensus, the reputation scores are appended to the reputation chain, and task blocks are stored in the local ledger by both the command node and global nodes within each shard.

(2)State graph partitioning phase.

State graph partitioning phase.

The command shard continuously ingests transactions from new execution shard blocks, updating the global state graph accordingly. When all task blocks for the current epoch are generated, the state graph remains static. The command shard then partitions the state graph using the R2CLPA to reduce cross-shard transactions and maintain a balance of resource-aware load and reputation distribution among shards, as explained in section “Design of the R2CLPA”.

(3)State block consensus phase.

State block consensus phase.

Based on the partitioned state graph from the previous phase, COUAVChain reorganizes the execution shards. The RB-HotStuff consensus mechanism is applied once more to validate the partitioning results, generating state blocks that are subsequently added to the state chain.

(4)Shard reconfiguration phase.

Shard reconfiguration phase.

Once consensus is established on the partitioning results, the command shard disseminates the updated state block to all relevant execution shards. Upon receiving the state block, nodes adjust their configurations based on the new partitioning scheme, enabling transactions in the next epoch to be routed to the appropriate shards.

Threat model and security assumptions

COUAVChain is designed to operate in adversarial, resource-constrained, and partially connected UAV swarm networks, commonly found in denied environments such as electronic warfare zones or decentralized military operations. This section outlines the threats, security goals, and the defensive assumptions underlying the protocol design.

Threat categories

COUAVChain considers the following adversarial threats:

Byzantine nodes.

Byzantine nodes.

Compromised UAVs may exhibit arbitrary behavior that undermines the integrity of the system. Such behavior includes proposing invalid or conflicting blocks to disrupt the blockchain’s consistency, refusing to participate in the consensus process to hinder decision-making, or colluding with other malicious nodes to engage in double voting or sabotage the leader selection mechanism, thereby threatening the overall coordination and security of the UAV swarm.

(2)Sybil attacks.

Sybil attacks.

An attacker may inject multiple pseudonymous nodes (Sybil identities) into the network in an attempt to increase their presence in one or more shards. If not mitigated, this can lead to shard takeover or compromise of consensus security.

(3)Denial-of-Service (DoS) attacks.

Denial-of-Service (DoS) attacks.

Malicious nodes may flood the network with redundant messages, delay communication channels, or overload consensus processes to degrade liveness.

(4)Eavesdropping and traffic analysis.

Eavesdropping and traffic analysis.

Since blockchain data is broadcast or accessible, adversaries may intercept on-chain UAV status records, extracting sensitive mission information.

(5)Shard targeting attacks.

Shard targeting attacks.

Attackers may attempt to cluster malicious nodes into the same shard during reorganization, aiming to subvert intra-shard consensus.

(6)Slow-Burn (Stealth) attacks.

Slow-Burn (Stealth) attacks.

Malicious nodes may remain dormant and behave honestly for extended periods to gradually build up reputation. Once sufficient trust is accumulated, they launch coordinated malicious actions within or across shards. This type of delayed attack undermines reputation-based security models and threatens long-term consensus stability.

Tamper Resistance: Signed blocks and RB-HotStuff consensus prevent transaction manipulation or rollback.

Defensive mechanisms

COUAVChain employs a multi-layered defense strategy to enhance the security and resilience of UAV swarm operations. A multi-factor trust regulation mechanism maintains a dynamic trust environment by decaying trust scores over time for inactive or malicious participants, preventing reputation concentration or prolonged influence by compromised nodes. The R2CLPA forms communities based on network topology and node trustworthiness, mitigating the risk of Byzantine node clustering and enhancing the security of shard reorganization. To counter Sybil attacks, COUAVChain assumes that each UAV node is provisioned with a unique and authenticated identity ID issued by a trusted command center. This identity management forms a foundational defense against forged identities. In addition, R2CLPA incorporates reputation-aware sharding constraints, preventing Sybil nodes with low initial reputation from clustering within the same shard, thereby significantly raising the threshold for shard-level takeover.

To defend against long-term infiltration strategies—such as slow-burn attacks where malicious nodes accumulate reputation through seemingly honest behavior before launching coordinated actions—COUAVChain introduces a dual-layer reputation safeguard. First, the incentive function R(S) imposes a “reputation ceiling” that slows the growth rate for high-reputation nodes, curbing their potential to quickly dominate consensus. Second, the system applies a decay mechanism to reduce the reputation of inactive nodes over time, preventing attackers from maintaining influence through dormancy.

RB-HotStuff further reinforces system security through reputation-weighted voting, significantly reducing the impact of low-trust or idle nodes while improving consensus efficiency and fault tolerance. Moreover, lightweight encryption techniques such as ECIES are applied to UAV status data before blockchain storage, addressing confidentiality concerns and protecting sensitive mission information from interception or tampering. Together, these mechanisms constitute a robust defense framework that enables secure, adaptive, and resilient blockchain operations for UAV swarms in denied environments.

Dynamic reputation mechanism

Reputation level mechanism

This paper introduces a dynamic reputation ranking model, using reputation scores as key indicators to assess UAV nodes’ honesty and participation in blockchain consensus. Based on reputation scores, nodes are categorized into four tiers: high, medium, low, and suspicious reputation nodes. Suspicious nodes cannot cast effective votes in consensus but still have their reputation scores adjusted. To restore voting rights, suspicious nodes must consistently participate in honest consensus over multiple rounds until their reputation exceeds the network’s tolerance threshold. This mechanism provides a recovery pathway for well-behaved nodes that lose reputation due to exceptional circumstances, while raising the cost of malicious actions and limiting Byzantine nodes’ influence. Dynamic sharding algorithm R2CLPA

This study formulates the trade-off among cross-shard transaction volume, resource-aware load distribution, and reputation balance as a dynamic network partitioning problem. Adopting a community detection approach, we propose the R2CLPA, building upon the Constrained Label Propagation Algorithm (CLPA)37. The designed algorithm not only exploits community clustering properties to minimize cross-shard transactions but also introduces a novel resource-aware balancing mechanism. It considers heterogeneous node resources (e.g., energy and storage) alongside transaction load and reputation, fundamentally addressing the risk of resource exhaustion in high-performance nodes and resource idling in low-performance nodes.

Design of the R2CLPA

In complex networks, a community is a cluster of nodes that are highly interconnected. Nodes within a community exhibit strong internal connectivity. Conversely, inter-community connections are relatively sparse. Community detection aims to identify distinct communities in a complex network. In a blockchain, nodes and transactions together form a social network, with transactions representing interactions between nodes. Figure 3 illustrates that a naive partitioning strategy can lead to excessive cross-shard transactions.

Simple partitioning and community detection-based partitioning.

By utilizing community properties, the number of cross-shard transactions can be minimized. In a blockchain sharding system, each shard can be viewed as a community consisting of multiple nodes. Put differently, identifying communities across the system and mapping them to specific shards can help reduce cross-shard transactions. Following this principle, developing a dynamic community detection algorithm can optimize sharding system performance. Over the past few years, researchers have actively explored community detection in social networks. Notable community detection algorithms include the LPA38, the Louvain method39, and the Infomap algorithm40. Nevertheless, these advanced community detection methods are not directly applicable to the node sharding problem, as traditional community detection algorithms do not impose restrictions on community size, which can result in overly large communities and, consequently, workload imbalance. Furthermore, while the Louvain algorithm performs well on large networks, it is unsuitable for dynamic networks since it must be recomputed after each network change. The Infomap algorithm supports diverse network types but exhibits higher computational complexity than LPA and Louvain. Neither of these algorithm types is well-suited for dynamic UAV networks with heterogeneous computational capabilities. The LPA is a well-known lightweight community detection method that offers high efficiency and has been applied to classical graph partitioning tasks. In the standard LPA algorithm, nodes are initially assigned random labels and iteratively update them based on their neighbors’ labels until convergence is achieved. The CLPA, an enhancement of LPA, reduces cross-shard transactions while maintaining balanced workload distribution among shards. Building upon CLPA, this study introduces a novel dynamic community-aware node sharding method, the R2CLPA, designed to optimize cross-shard transactions, workload balancing, and shard reputation equilibrium.

It is crucial to note that R2CLPA is not executed by all UAV nodes. Instead, the computational task of shard reorganization is exclusively performed by Command Nodes within the Command Shard. These nodes act as global coordinators equipped with superior computational and storage capabilities. This architectural choice ensures that the dynamic sharding mechanism does not impose additional computational burden on resource-constrained UAVs, thereby preserving the system’s lightweight and efficient nature.

Moreover, shard reorganization via R2CLPA is not triggered after each transaction. Rather, it is scheduled periodically at the end of each epoch. An epoch spans a configurable time window that may encompass hundreds or even thousands of task blocks. This design effectively amortizes the algorithm’s computational cost over time, minimizing its impact on system responsiveness and real-time performance.

Adaptability to dynamic network topologies

The physical topology of a UAV swarm in denied environments is highly dynamic due to factors such as maneuvering, interference, and node failures. A core design principle of COUAVChain is the decoupling of logical sharding from physical connectivity via a layered protocol architecture.Importantly, the state graph that R2CLPA optimizes is not static; it is periodically reconstructed at the end of each epoch based on recent inter-node communication and transaction activities, thereby reflecting the system’s current interaction patterns.

R2CLPA operates at the application layer and focuses on optimizing the logical state graph, which reflects transaction and communication patterns among nodes. The goal is to colocate frequently interacting nodes within the same shard to reduce application-layer cross-shard transactions. In contrast, maintaining physical connectivity—whether direct or multi-hop—is the responsibility of the network layer. At the network layer, we assume the use of standard Mobile Ad-hoc Network (MANET) routing protocols, such as Ad-hoc On-demand Distance Vector (AODV) or Optimized Link State Routing (OLSR). These protocols are specifically designed to dynamically discover and maintain routes in response to rapid changes in network topology.

As a result, even if nodes within the same shard become physically distant, the MANET layer ensures connectivity through multi-hop routing. R2CLPA is therefore robust to such transient changes. Moreover, because shard reconfiguration in COUAVChain occurs periodically based on epochs, the system is designed to adapt to persistent shifts in communication patterns, rather than reacting to every short-term fluctuation.

Consensus algorithm design

Byzantine Fault Tolerance (BFT) consensus algorithms provide fault tolerance in distributed systems, ensuring operational stability despite the presence of malicious or faulty nodes. Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT) is a widely recognized BFT algorithm that maintains system consistency by tolerating up to one-third of faulty or malicious nodes. However, PBFT suffers from high communication overhead, restricting system scalability. Each node must exchange messages with all other nodes multiple times, leading to a communication complexity of O(n2). Consequently, communication costs grow quadratically with the number of nodes. HotStuff, an optimized version of PBFT, shifts from node-wide broadcast voting to Leader-collected voting, reducing communication complexity from O(n2) to O(n). Each voting round finalizes consensus on the current request while reaffirming the previous round’s decision. The Leader node changes every round to expedite leader replacement in case of failure. In traditional HotStuff consensus, combined with the proposed reputation mechanism, votes from more than two-thirds of the total nodes must still be collected at each stage. Although votes from nodes below the reputation threshold are disregarded, high- and low-reputation nodes hold equal voting power, posing the following challenges in UAV autonomous operations:

The HotStuff Leader must still communicate frequently with other nodes, leading to significant communication overhead, especially during frequent view changes.

Treating votes from low- and high-reputation nodes equally means that even poorly behaving nodes exert limited negative impact. Meanwhile, high-reputation nodes with strong historical performance do not receive additional voting weight. Malicious nodes may exploit this by colluding to manipulate consensus, where their collective influence remains substantial despite their low individual reputations.

To tackle the first challenge, extending the Leader’s tenure allows multiple consensus rounds per term, reducing the frequency of view changes and thereby alleviating communication overhead for the UAV Leader. For the second issue, since reputation scores quantify historical node behavior, they can serve as weights in the voting process. By applying Bayesian inference, voting weights can be dynamically adjusted based on node reputation, establishing an adaptive weighted voting mechanism.

In light of this analysis, we propose RB-HotStuff, a lightweight HotStuff consensus algorithm that incorporates a dynamic reputation mechanism and Bayesian inference. (2)Leader election.

Leader election.

The Leader election process is crucial for maintaining consensus efficiency and security in UAV swarm networks. To enhance fairness, unpredictability, and attack resistance, this paper proposes a VRF-based Leader election mechanism that randomly selects a Leader from high-reputation global nodes. The Leader oversees consensus coordination and execution during its term, while other global nodes function as Monitors, managing the dynamic addition and removal of nodes within each shard. By integrating a dynamic reputation update mechanism, the random Leader selection approach ensures that high-reputation nodes have a fair chance of election, preventing centralization and avoiding the dominance of a few high-reputation nodes.

VRF integrates randomness and verifiability, guaranteeing a fair, transparent, and tamper-resistant election process. Consider a shard containing a global node set M = {m1,m2,…,ma}, where only global nodes with a reputation score exceeding a predefined threshold can participate in Leader elections. The lightweight node set L = {l1,l2,…,lb} does not engage in Leader elections but contributes to the verification process. The Leader election process consists of the following steps:

Step 1: Each participating global node mi obtains the hash of the previous consensus block as the seed Seedi.

Step 2: Each node inputs its private key SKi and Seedi into the VRF to produce a random value Randi and its corresponding proof Proofi, where Randi=VRF_Hash(SKi,Seedi) and Proofi=VRF_Proof(SKi,Seedi).

Step 3: The node transmits Seedi, Randi, and Proofi to all nodes within the shard. Other nodes validate the legitimacy of Randi using the node’s public key PKi. If Resulti=VRF_Verify (PKi,Seedi,Randi,Proofi) is True, the random value is verified; otherwise, it is rejected.

Step 4: The global node that generates the smallest verified random value is designated as the Leader for this election round.

To ensure fairness, decentralization, and security in the leader election process, especially under conditions where some global nodes may be temporarily offline due to task load or communication delays, we introduce a mechanism called Minimum Active Candidates into the VRF-based election process. This mechanism introduces a threshold parameter, denoted as θactive, which specifies the minimum proportion of active global nodes required for an election round to proceed. We set this threshold to 2⁄3, drawing from the classical Byzantine Fault Tolerance model. According to BFT theory, a distributed system must have at least 2f + 1 honest nodes out of 3f + 1 to tolerate up to f faulty or malicious participants. Applying this logic to our context, ensuring that at least two-thirds of the eligible nodes are active provides a theoretical guarantee that the election process remains resilient against up to one-third of adversarial behavior.

Before each leader election round begins, a monitor node within each shard counts the number of active global nodes that are eligible to participate. The VRF election process is allowed to proceed only if this number exceeds θactive times the total number of global nodes in the shard. If the number of active candidates falls below the threshold, the system temporarily suspends the initiation of new consensus rounds and triggers an alert. This safeguard ensures that elections do not proceed within an excessively small or potentially manipulable candidate set, thereby preserving system-wide fairness, decentralization, and robustness.

(3)Consensus procedure.

Consensus procedure.

The proposed consensus process consists of intra-shard and inter-shard consensus, leveraging the RB-HotStuff algorithm that combines a reputation mechanism with Bayesian inference. During intra-shard consensus, the Leader records votes from suspicious nodes during the validation phase but does not assign them voting weight. If a node votes honestly, its reputation score is increased after validation. In inter-shard consensus, the Leader disseminates validated intra-shard blocks to other shard Leaders. Upon achieving inter-shard consensus, each shard Leader records the block in the local ledger and updates the reputation chain with revised node scores. Furthermore, each Leader must update its shard’s Monitors with validated blocks and reputation values. The Leader is responsible for maintaining the blockchain’s node state graph, and executes R2CLPA following a set number of inter-shard consensus rounds, recording the partitioning results in the state chain, and then reorganizes the shards accordingly. At the conclusion of an epoch, the R2CLPA dynamically reorganizes shards, broadcasting the updated shard structure before initiating the next epoch.

To mitigate the high communication overhead caused by frequent view changes in HotStuff, Leader nodes are permitted to conduct multiple rounds of block consensus during their term. Rather than being replaced after a single block consensus round, the Leader remains in position until predefined conditions are satisfied. These conditions include reaching a predefined maximum number of rounds or encountering a Leader failure. By reducing the frequency of view changes, this approach effectively lowers the communication overhead, especially for UAVs acting as Leaders, significantly alleviating their communication burden.

Experimental evaluation and analysis

Experimental setup: The experiments were conducted on a computer with Windows 10 (64-bit), an Intel Core i7-10700 CPU (2.90 GHz), and 16GB of RAM. The software environment includes Python 3.9.12 and Golang 1.22.4 for algorithm validation, testing, and platform development. Dataset: The dataset consists of real Ethereum transactions42, spanning block heights from 10,000,000 to 10,999,999. These historical transactions were replayed to facilitate simulation testing.

Baselines: The following three benchmark methods were considered. Monoxide32 employs static sharding using the first few bits of node addresses, with no subsequent reassignment. RapidChain31 dynamically redistributes nodes using the Cuckoo rule to balance transaction loads. Metis43 is a widely recognized heuristic graph partitioning tool that partitions the state graph into non-overlapping segments while minimizing cross-shard transactions, ensuring balanced state distribution. COUAVChain, proposed in this study, employs the R2CLPA for dynamic shard partitioning. Furthermore, the proposed protocol is compared against conventional sharding approaches in terms of functionality, intra-shard consensus efficiency, security, and scalability.

Other configurations: The dynamic reputation mechanism parameters ar, ap, ad, Cr, cp, n0, cd, Kr, Kp, and Kd are set to 0.1, 0.1, 0.1, 10, 0.5, 5, 0.5, 5, 5, and 5, respectively. For the R2CLPA, the parameters η, γ, φ, and ε are configured as 0.5, 0.5, 100, and 50, respectively. In RB-HotStuff, the parameter Θ is configured as 0.5.

Cross-shard transaction ratio

Sharding improves system scalability by enabling parallel transaction processing, but it also introduces the challenge of cross-shard transactions, which typically require more time and coordination. To evaluate how well each protocol handles this challenge in real-world conditions, we designed a hybrid load model that better reflects the communication behavior of UAV swarms.

This model includes two types of traffic. The baseline traffic is taken from real Ethereum transaction data, representing general-purpose, unpredictable activity. On top of this, we add a periodic burst load that simulates UAV-specific tasks. At the beginning of each of the 30 simulated views, we inject a burst of transactions that accounts for 15% of that view’s total traffic. In each burst, one shard is randomly selected as the “event initiator,” and its nodes send urgent coordination messages to random nodes in the other shards. This design mimics real UAV scenarios like emergency alerts, mission redistribution, or swarm regrouping. Figure 8 shows the cross-shard transaction ratio for Monoxide, RapidChain, Metis, and COUAVChain under this hybrid load.

Cross-shard transaction ratio under different numbers of shards.

As expected, all protocols experience higher cross-shard transaction ratios as the number of shards increases. The event-driven bursts raise the overall level of cross-shard communication, confirming that UAV operations put more strain on the sharding system.

The results show clear differences in adaptability. Monoxide uses fixed, address-based sharding and cannot adjust to the sudden increase in communication, resulting in the highest cross-shard transaction ratio. RapidChain includes a reconfiguration process, but it mainly addresses node churn and does not reduce cross-shard traffic effectively, only improving by 0.5–2.2% over Monoxide. Metis reduces cross-shard traffic at the start by minimizing inter-shard links in the state graph, but it does not respond to the changes caused by event bursts. As a result, its cross-shard transaction ratio remains 12.7–16.3% higher than COUAVChain. In contrast, COUAVChain adjusts dynamically. Its R2CLPA algorithm detects which nodes are frequently communicating during each event and reorganizes the shard layout at the end of each epoch to place these nodes in the same shard. This reduces cross-shard transactions and helps the system maintain higher performance even under demanding UAV coordination tasks.

Resource-aware load balancing and reputation balancing

This subsection evaluates the load balancing and reputation balancing performance of the three protocols. The test environment consists of 64 shards, each initially containing 20 nodes. At the beginning of each epoch, 300,000 transactions are pre-generated in chronological order and replayed into the sharded blockchain system at an injection rate of 2,000 transactions per second. Each transaction is assigned to a shard based on the address of the participating nodes. Each epoch lasts approximately 15 s, and the simulation covers 10 consecutive epochs. To evaluate the alignment between actual load and resource capacity in each shard, we define the relative resource load index LRrel as the ratio between a shard’s individual load-to-resource ratio LRk and the system-wide average . The closer LRrel is to 1, the better the match between workload and resource capability in that shard. A value above 1.75 indicates significant resource overload, while a value below 0.25 suggests severe resource underutilization. We also monitor the total reputation score of each shard. Shards with values between 1,750 and 2,000 are considered over-reputed, while those between 0 and 250 are classified as under-reputed. The detailed results for resource-aware load balancing and reputation balancing are presented in Figs. 9 and 10 respectively.

Reputation balancing comparison. (a) Monoxide, (b) RapidChain, (c) COUAVChain.

As shown in Fig. 9, Monoxide exhibits poor load-to-resource matching, with a significant number of shards experiencing either overload or severe underutilization. This reflects the limitations of its static sharding strategy in adapting to dynamic transaction distributions and heterogeneous node capabilities. RapidChain mitigates some of the extreme load imbalances, yet several shards still exhibit resource overload or idle capacity. This indicates that its resource-aware load balancing remains limited in effectiveness. In contrast, COUAVChain achieves a more stable and balanced resource distribution throughout the experiment. This disrupts potential Byzantine alliances and enhances the system’s long-term security and decentralization.

Consensus failure rate

In state-sharded blockchain systems, nodes engage in intra-shard consensus independently. A high concentration of Byzantine nodes within a shard can significantly degrade block production rates and system availability. This section evaluates the impact of varying Byzantine node counts on consensus failure rates. The experimental setup consisted of a blockchain sharding network with 64 nodes distributed across 8 shards.

Figure 11 illustrates that Monoxide experiences the highest consensus failure rate, peaking at 0.55 as Byzantine node numbers increase. This is attributed to its static sharding mechanism, which can cause Byzantine nodes to cluster within the same shard, leading to intra-shard consensus failures. The proposed protocol maintains a consensus failure rate between 0.04 and 0.15, stabilizing when the number of Byzantine nodes surpasses 15. This stability is attributed to COUAVChain’s improved HotStuff consensus algorithm, which incorporates a dynamic reputation mechanism. By assessing node reputation based on consensus participation and disregarding votes from suspicious low-reputation nodes, it effectively mitigates the influence of Byzantine nodes. As a result, the protocol significantly lowers consensus failure rates, ensuring secure UAV blockchain consensus and enhancing operational safety and efficiency.

Consensus failure rate under different numbers of Byzantine nodes.

In denied environments, node behavior may not be entirely malicious but may still mimic Byzantine faults due to electromagnetic interference, intermittent communication loss, or UAV disconnection. These semi-faulty behaviors can disrupt consensus if not properly managed. COUAVChain addresses this by evaluating node reputation based on consistency and participation. Votes from nodes exhibiting irregular behavior—such as delays or dropouts—are down-weighted or discarded. This enables the system to sustain low consensus failure rates even under harsh conditions, ensuring secure and reliable blockchain operations in contested airspace.

Analysis of dynamic sharding security thresholds

To evaluate the resilience of dynamic sharding against long-term infiltration attacks, we simulate a slow-burn attack scenario where malicious nodes gradually accumulate reputation by behaving honestly over time, eventually surpassing the reputation threshold (set to a score of 30) required for participating in consensus voting. Once a sufficient number of these high-reputation malicious nodes are assigned to the same shard, they can collectively disrupt the consensus process.

In the experiment, a network of 1000 nodes is simulated, with the proportion of malicious nodes ranging from 10% to 40%. The simulation runs for E epochs, where each epoch represents a complete cycle of reputation updates, shard reorganization, and consensus execution. At the end of each epoch, we check all shards to determine whether they are functionally failed. A shard is considered functionally failed if more than one-third of its members are malicious and possess reputation scores above 30, meaning that the malicious nodes within the shard have sufficient voting power to block or manipulate the local consensus process.

The shard failure rate is defined as the ratio between the total number of functionally failed shards observed over all epochs and the total number of shard instances examined. Specifically, it is calculated by dividing the total number of failed shards across all epochs by the product of the number of shards per epoch and the total number of epochs (E). In this experiment, we set E = 100.

Figure 13 presents the shard failure rates under three different shard sizes: Ns=20, 50, and 100. The results indicate that smaller shards are more susceptible to coordinated infiltration. When Ns=20, the failure rate increases significantly, reaching about 4.8% when 40% of nodes are malicious. In contrast, when Ns=100, the failure rate remains below 1% even under high attack ratios.

Shard failure rate under varying malicious node proportions across different shard sizes.

These results demonstrate a clear relationship between shard size and system security. Larger shards reduce the probability that high-reputation malicious nodes are co-located within the same shard. This benefit stems from the combination of reputation equilibrium constraints in R2CLPA, which suppress the aggregation of malicious influence during shard reorganization.

Comparison with non-blockchain secure distributed systems

Although COUAVChain is evaluated against mainstream blockchain protocols, it is also important to consider non-blockchain secure distributed systems that offer similar functionalities but follow different architectural approaches. These systems aim to provide security, consistency, or decentralized coordination, but often make assumptions that limit their use in denied or adversarial environments. For example, Raft45 and Paxos46 are crash-fault-tolerant consensus protocols widely used in systems such as etcd and ZooKeeper. While efficient under stable and well-connected conditions, they rely on trusted communication and lack resistance to malicious behavior, making them less effective in dynamic or contested networks. Trusted Execution Environments (TEEs), such as Intel SGX, provide secure computation through hardware-based isolation and remote attestation. However, their dependence on specialized hardware and fixed deployment limits their flexibility in mobile, decentralized systems like UAV swarms. Federated Learning (FL)47 and Multi-Party Computation (MPC) focus on protecting data privacy through distributed training and secure collaboration. Yet, they often require consistent connectivity, reliable coordination, and lack mechanisms for data traceability or tamper-proof recording—features essential for trust in adversarial missions. DAG-based ledgers, such as Hashgraph48 and IOTA, improve performance by removing traditional blocks and mining, but their security models are still evolving and have not been thoroughly tested against Byzantine attacks in highly dynamic networks. In contrast, COUAVChain integrates lightweight Byzantine consensus, adaptive reputation-based sharding, and probabilistic trust control, providing a more practical solution for UAV swarm coordination under limited connectivity, high mobility, and potential adversarial interference.

Discussion

The COUAVChain protocol offers a novel blockchain sharding solution tailored for UAV swarm operations in denied environments. This section discusses the theoretical innovations and comparative advantages over prior work, the justification for employing blockchain in resource-limited and denied settings, and the limitations and scope of the proposed protocol.

Theoretical innovation and comparative analysis

In contrast to existing blockchain sharding protocols, COUAVChain introduces several key innovations specifically designed for UAV swarms in denied environments, each supported by robust theoretical foundations.

Hierarchical Sharding Structure: Unlike traditional flat sharding protocols such as Elastico and OmniLedger, COUAVChain employs a hierarchical “command-execution” sharding structure. This design reduces communication overhead by organizing UAVs into execution shards based on mission roles and electing command nodes to form a command shard. This layered approach is particularly suited for the dynamic and resource-constrained nature of UAV swarms.

Dynamic Reputation Mechanism: To enhance security, COUAVChain incorporates a dynamic reputation mechanism that adjusts node trustworthiness through incentive, penalty, and decay factors. This mechanism mitigates the impact of Byzantine nodes and prevents the emergence of “reputation oligarchs,” ensuring a decentralized and secure consensus process. This is a significant improvement over static reputation systems used in other blockchain protocols.

Dynamic Sharding Algorithm R2CLPA: The R2CLPA leverages community clustering to minimize cross-shard transactions while performing resource-aware load balancing and ensuring reputation fairness across heterogeneous nodes. The optimization model for dynamic sharding (Eq. (15)) formalizes these trade-offs, providing a theoretical basis for the algorithm that explicitly incorporates node resources (energy and storage). This approach reduces the cross-shard transaction ratio compared to existing methods like Metis, as demonstrated in the experimental results. The algorithm’s convergence and time complexity ensure its feasibility for UAV networks. By dynamically adjusting shard assignments, R2CLPA maintains efficiency, longevity, and security in the face of changing network conditions.

RB-HotStuff Consensus Mechanism: Building on the HotStuff consensus protocol, RB-HotStuff integrates dynamic reputation weighting and Bayesian inference to optimize block validation. The Bayesian inference provides a probabilistic framework that enhances fault tolerance by adjusting voting weights based on node reputations. This enhancement reduces intra-shard communication complexity to O(n) and allows for multiple block finalizations per leader term, improving scalability and efficiency beyond traditional consensus mechanisms. Nevertheless, further theoretical exploration could focus on adaptive consensus thresholds based on real-time network conditions to enhance robustness in severely denied environments.

These innovations collectively address the unique challenges of UAV swarms in denied environments, setting COUAVChain apart from existing blockchain sharding schemes.

Blockchain justification in denied and resource-limited environments

Blockchain technology is essential for UAV swarms operating in denied environments, as its decentralized architecture eliminates single points of failure and enhances resilience against electromagnetic interference and cyberattacks. The COUAVChain protocol leverages a lightweight storage scheme and an efficient consensus mechanism specifically designed to meet the resource constraints of UAVs, enabling coordinated operations without overloading individual nodes. Its dynamic reputation system and RB-HotStuff consensus further enhance security by mitigating the influence of malicious nodes through fast and reliable agreement processes.

Compared to non-blockchain secure distributed systems, which often lack Byzantine fault tolerance and adaptability to highly dynamic networks, COUAVChain provides the core capabilities required for establishing trust, traceability, and autonomous coordination in adversarial conditions. The absence of these capabilities in non-blockchain architectures makes the blockchain foundation of COUAVChain not a redundant design choice but a necessary prerequisite for achieving secure and autonomous collaboration in denied environments.

Scope, limitations, and future work

The proposed COUAVChain protocol is designed for UAV swarm systems operating in denied environments, where collaborative tasks must be carried out without access to stable central infrastructure. These swarms typically consist of heterogeneous nodes with differences in computational power, storage capacity, and battery level. The protocol assumes that the swarm possesses basic Mobile Ad-hoc Network (MANET) communication capabilities to support peer-to-peer coordination under dynamic topological conditions. COUAVChain offers several key advantages in such environments. Its decentralized architecture ensures data consistency, integrity, and traceability without relying on a centralized server. It enables intelligent task-resource matching across heterogeneous nodes and suppresses the concentration of Byzantine participants, thereby maintaining operational safety and resilience within the swarm.

Despite the strong theoretical and empirical performance of COUAVChain, we acknowledge several limitations that suggest promising directions for future research.

First, in terms of scalability and efficiency, although the R2CLPA algorithm exhibits linear complexity, periodic global shard reconfiguration may still pose challenges for very large-scale UAV swarms. Future work could explore more efficient incremental or localized adjustment algorithms to reduce computational overhead.

Second, the current system model can be further refined in two key dimensions. On one hand, the state graph used for sharding is built primarily on direct transactional relationships, which is a simplified yet effective abstraction. However, complex smart contract interactions often involve higher-order dependencies (e.g., contract-to-contract calls). Future research may consider adopting hypergraphs or graph neural networks (GNNs) to capture such semantics more accurately. On the other hand, the reputation mechanism proposed in this work is general-purpose and lacks contextual awareness. It does not differentiate node behavior across diverse mission types. A promising direction is to incorporate smart contract-based Service-Level Agreements (SLAs). For instance, an SLA for a mission in a high-interference environment could specify a higher tolerance for communication latency, thereby distinguishing between delays caused by environmental factors and true malicious passivity. This would allow node behavior to be evaluated relative to the pre-agreed operational parameters of the current task. Furthermore, reputation rewards and penalties could be dynamically weighted based on task criticality, enabling fine-grained, fair, and adaptive trust evaluation tailored to mission requirements.

Third, regarding the synergy between physical topology and logical sharding, the protocol adopts a layered architecture that decouples logical shard formation from physical connectivity. While this design is robust under typical conditions, it may exhibit limitations in cases of severe network fragmentation, where nodes in the same shard become physically disconnected. To address this, future work should explore cross-layer optimization strategies whereby persistent disconnection at the network layer triggers emergency shard reconfiguration at the application layer to preserve system liveness and performance.

Finally, concerning empirical validation, while our enhanced hybrid load model provides a robust simulation-based evaluation, the critical next step is to validate the protocol’s performance using datasets collected from real-world UAV flight tests. This would provide definitive insights into its effectiveness under practical physical constraints and clear the path for real-world deployment.

Conclusion

This study addresses key challenges faced by UAV swarms conducting autonomous collaborative missions in denied environments, including excessive resource consumption, load imbalance, inefficient consensus, and insufficient security. To this end, we propose COUAVChain, a dynamic and lightweight blockchain sharding protocol tailored for such conditions.

The protocol introduces three major innovations. First, by adopting a hierarchical “command-execution” sharding architecture and a lightweight hybrid storage scheme, it significantly reduces communication and storage overhead for heterogeneous UAV nodes. Second, we propose a novel R2CLPA, which not only minimizes cross-shard transactions but also accounts for heterogeneous resource constraints to achieve load-resource balance while effectively suppressing the clustering of Byzantine nodes. Third, the RB-HotStuff consensus algorithm integrates dynamic reputation and Bayesian inference to simplify the consensus process, thereby improving throughput and reducing latency without compromising security.

Comprehensive experimental comparisons with existing schemes demonstrate that COUAVChain achieves significant advantages in system throughput, consensus efficiency, workload-resource optimization, scalability, and security. This work provides both a solid theoretical foundation and a practical technical solution for secure and efficient UAV swarm coordination in denied environments.

Future work will focus on improving algorithmic efficiency, enhancing model granularity, strengthening cross-layer coordination, and conducting empirical validation with real-world UAV data, with the goal of further enhancing the protocol’s adaptability and robustness in complex operational scenarios.

Enhancing transdermal delivery of retinyl acetate via transethosomes with emphasis on the impact of edge activators on penetration, safety, and efficacy
Authors: Nichaporn Wongsirojkul, Masahiro Takagi, Yodsathorn Wongngam, Apsorn Sattayakhom, Issara Prachongsai, Warangkana Chunglok, Pakorn Opaprakasit, Duangporn polpanich
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Transethosomes are advanced lipid-based nanocarriers engineered to improve the transdermal delivery of therapeutic agents by combining phospholipids, ethanol, and edge activators (EAs), resulting in enhanced membrane fluidity, deformability, and skin penetration. This study aims to develop and optimize a novel transethosome-based delivery system for retinyl acetate (RA) to overcome its inherent limitations—including poor stability, low bioavailability, and skin irritation—thereby enhancing its therapeutic potential for dermatological and cosmetic applications. The transethosomes were prepared using phosphatidylcholine (PC) from soybean lecithin (SoyPC)/cholesterol (Chol) and 1,2-dioleoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine/1,2-dipalmitoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine/Chol (DOPC/DPPC/Chol), with various EAs including Tween-20, Tween-80, oleic acid (OA), and palmitic acid (PA). Physicochemical characterization revealed vesicle sizes ranging from 168.2 to 246.6 nm, low polydispersity index (PDI ≈ 0.2), and zeta potentials (ζ potential) between − 28 to − 30 mV, indicating uniform distribution and colloidal stability. All formulations exhibited spherical morphology, negative surface charge, and comparable encapsulation efficiencies (close to 90%). In vitro antioxidant activity showed moderate free radical scavenging effects, with up to ~ 40% and ~ 30% in the DPPH and ABTS assay. All formulations showed biocompatibility and improved migration in HaCaT cell assays. Due to the greater membrane fluidity of the SoyPC/Chol system, RA release may be more hindered compared to the DOPC/DPPC/Chol system. Consequently, RA release from the DOPC/DPPC/Chol system was more pronounced, resulting in the greatest enhancement in wound healing and outperforming all other formulations containing different EAs. In contrast, among the SoyPC/Chol formulations, those containing OA and PA exhibited more pronounced and effective results compared to those with Tween-based EAs. This suggests that OA and PA disrupt membrane stability to a greater extent than Tween, thereby facilitating enhanced RA release in the SoyPC/Chol system. Skin permeation studies also revealed that the type of EAs significantly influenced transdermal delivery. Confocal laser scanning microscopy (CLSM) further validated the role of EAs in modulating membrane organization. Overall, RA-loaded transethosomes enhanced stability, bioavailability, and skin penetration while minimizing irritancy of RA, supporting their potential in dermatological applications. Future studies should investigate their clinical efficacy and biophysical mechanisms, including line tension analysis, to optimize formulation performance for anti-aging and therapeutic skincare.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-19050-2.

Introduction

Skin consists of three primary layers: the outer epidermis, the middle dermis, and the inner subcutaneous tissue. The epidermis, being the thickest layer, includes several sublayers, starting from the outside with the stratum corneum, followed by the transparent, granular, spinous and finally, the basal layers1. The stratum corneum is the outermost layer of the skin, consisting of 20 to 30 layers of cells. It is mainly composed of keratin and is made up of dead keratinocytes, referred to as anucleate squamous cells, which form a protective barrier2. This barrier limits the delivery of all types of drugs to the deeper tissue.

Topical drug delivery has gained significant attention in recent years as a result of its potential to overcome limitations due to the skin’s barrier function, which restricts the absorption of active ingredients, especially larger or hydrophilic molecules. Furthermore, these treatments can provide inconsistent delivery and localized effects, leading to suboptimal therapeutic outcomes. Variability in skin permeability between individuals can further complicate efficacy, while issues such as irritation or allergic reactions can limit patient compliance3. Recent advances in formulations and technologies have improved the effectiveness of topical delivery systems, including the development of transdermal patches4, nanoemulsions, and liposomal formulations5. These innovations enhance drug permeation through the skin by improving solubility and stability, thereby facilitating absorption and therapeutic efficacy. Simple vesicles are composed of a lipid bilayer surrounding an aqueous core, allowing them to carry both hydrophilic and hydrophobic drugs. They are biocompatible, biodegradable, and non-toxic, making them suitable for various formulations such as gels, ointments, and eye drops6. Although liposomes offer significant advantages for drug delivery, they face several challenges, including instability, aggregation, the use of high organic solvents during manufacturing, and vulnerability to phagocytosis7. In addition, they are often limited by poor penetration through biological membranes, which restricts their effectiveness in transdermal applications6,8,9. Johnston, M. J. and colleagues reported that a liposome system containing 1,2-distearoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine/Chol (55/45 mol%) improves the half-life and stability properties of the drug10. Their release profile can also be influenced by the content of the drug and the liposome system. Cholesterol (Chol) has been reported to play a role in improving stability, prolonging circulation time, and reducing drug permeability, increasing drug retention of release11. To address these issues and improve targeted drug delivery, advanced generations of liposomes have been developed.

Various derivatives of liposome vesicles have been developed, including ethosomes, transferosomes, and transethosomes. Among these, transethosomes have shown significant improvements, showing high drug-loading capacity, excellent entrapment efficiency, superior deformability index, and enhanced skin permeation ability12. Transethosomes, a novel type of ultradeformable vesicle, were first proposed by Song et al. in 201213. Their phospholipids act as carriers, facilitating the delivery of drugs or bioactive compounds to the skin. They are organized as a circular lipid bilayer, with their hydrophilic heads oriented outwards and their hydrophobic tails tucked inside the bilayer membrane. Transethosomes are incorporated with edge activators (EAs) and a small amount of ethanol to further enhance flexibility and deformability, allowing deeper penetrate into the skin14, avoidance of hepatic first-pass metabolism15, and a reduced risk of adverse effects16. The presence of surfactants and ethanol also provides good stability by maintaining the size of the vesicle and preventing coalescence17. The EAs are crucial components in enhancing the flexibility and permeability of transethosomes18, allowing them to penetrate deeper into the skin by deformation through narrow spaces between skin cells. Additionally, EAs stabilize vesicles reduce surface tension19,20, and disrupt the membrane of the transethosomes lipid bilayers, further improving skin permeability. This combination of properties enables transethosomes to maintain structural integrity and providing efficient delivery of drugs through the skin. Commonly used EAs include Tween-20, Tween-80, Span-20, sodium cholate, and oleic acid (OA). Tween-20 (polyoxyethylene sorbitan monolaurate) enhances steric stabilization by forming a protective barrier around the surface of particles, which prevents nanoparticles from aggregating and stabilizes the colloidal system. Tween-80 is another widely used nonionic surfactant recognized in various pharmaceutical formulations. Its versatility and biocompatibility make it an ideal choice for enhancing the solubility and stability of drugs, while facilitating the efficient delivery of hydrophilic and lipophilic compounds21. Increasing the concentration of Span-20 effectively reduces interfacial tension, likely due to enhanced surfactant alignment at the interface22. Sodium cholate engages in hydrophobic interactions, increasing membrane fluidity and flexibility, thereby promoting insulin delivery23. Additionally, OA has been shown to suppress surface tension, line tension, and ordered chain structure, contributing to its effectiveness in drug formulations8,24,25. The membrane of transethosomes is composed of unsaturated and/or saturated phospholipids, along with entrapped EAs, which typically exhibit phase separation behavior. The interfacial tension at the boundary of phase-separated domains, known as line tension, is a critical factor in maintaining the stability of particles and phase-separated structures by preserving their structural integrity. Alteration of line tension allows for the control of the shape, size, membrane permeability, and overall stability of these phase-separated domains, which in turn optimizes the performance and efficiency of drug delivery systems. This control is essential to enhance the delivery process, improving the way drugs reach their target areas while maintaining the structural properties of nanoparticles.

Retinyl acetate (RA) is a stable ester form of retinol (vitamin A), well known for its lipid-soluble antioxidant properties that contribute significantly to the health of the skin and hair. It plays a crucial role in improving skin elasticity, reducing roughness, preventing photoaging, and alleviating severe acne, while also soothing inflammation26. Its anti-aging effects include reducing the appearance of wrinkles, fine lines, and irregular pigmentation by promoting the turnover and renewal of keratinocytes27. Furthermore, RA regulates growth factors and cytokines in the extracellular matrix, inhibits matrix metalloproteinases, increases collagen synthesis, and helps mitigate oxidative stress, making it a potent agent in cosmetic dermatology28. Despite their remarkable properties, the dermal use of retinol and its derivatives is constrained by several challenges. Retinol is reorganized for its potential for irritation, leading to dryness, peeling, and redness upon topical application29. In addition, it is prone to instability when exposed to light and oxygen, resulting in rapid degradation that compromises its efficacy in pharmaceutical formulations29. Its limited water solubility poses a significant challenge in the development of stable and effective topical formulations. Additionally, excessive intake of retinol can lead to toxicity, including teratogenic effects, particularly during pregnancy, thereby restricting its use at high doses30.

To address these challenges, the present study aims to develop a transethosome-based drug delivery system for RA with enhanced stability, permeability, and reduced irritation potential. Specifically, the effects of phospholipid types and EAs on the physicochemical properties and functional behavior of transethosomal membranes were investigated. Transethosomes were formulated using phosphatidylcholine (PC) from soybean lecithin (SoyPC)/Chol and 1,2-dioleoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine/1,2-dipalmitoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine/Chol (DOPC/DPPC/Chol) blend, incorporating Tween-20, Tween-80, OA, and palmitic acid (PA) as EAs. All formulations were characterized for vesicle size, ζ potential, morphology, encapsulation efficiency (%EE) and anti-oxidation activity. Cytotoxicity and cell migration were assessed in HaCaT cells using MTT and scratch assays, respectively. The permeation-enhancing effects of EAs were assessed in SoyPC/Chol-based transethosomes. Membrane organization of DOPC/DPPC/Chol-based vesicles was visualized via fluorescence microscope and CLSM using lipid-specific fluorescent probes, Rho-DHPE and NBD-PE. The findings clarify how EAs influence the physicochemical characteristics and membrane phase behavior, which in turn affect the stability and performance of transethosomes in topical drug delivery.

Characterizations

Particle size and ζ potential

The average vesicular size and polydispersity index (PDI) of the bare transethosome and RA-loaded transethosomes were measured using dynamic light scattering (DLS) with Zetasizer Nano-S equipment (Malvern Instruments, Malvern, UK) at room temperature. The scattered light intensity was converted to the diffusion coefficient (D, m2/s) to determine the size using the Stokes–Einstein equation. The ζ potential was evaluated using laser Doppler anemometry in a thermostated cell, measured with the Zetasizer Nano-S equipment (Malvern Instruments, Malvern, UK). For both measurements, the samples (1 mL) were diluted with DI water.

Encapsulation efficiency

The prepared RA-loaded transethosomes (1 mg/mL) were centrifuged using a centrifugal filter with a molecular weight cutoff of 100 K (Amicon Ultra-15, Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA) to separate free RA. Transmission electron microscope (TEM)

Morphology of all samples was evaluated using TEM (Jeol, JEM-2100, MA, USA). A drop of very diluted suspensions was applied to the carbon-coated copper grid. After drying overnight, the sample was examined using a TEM at 15,000 magnifications with 200 kV.

Radical scavenging assay

Radical scavenging activity of the prepared RA-loaded transethosomes was performed using two assays, i.e. 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) and 2,2'-azino-bis(3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulfonic acid) (ABTS) assays31. DPPH and ABTS solution (100 µM) were freshly prepared in ethanol and DI water, respectively. Eight transethosome formulations (TE1–TE8) at concentrations ranging from 0.10 to 100.0 mg/mL (100 μL) were individually mixed with the DPPH and ABTS solution (100 μL) in a 96-well microplate. The reaction mixtures were incubated in complete darkness at room temperature for 30 min to allow for radical scavenging. Following incubation, the absorbance was measured at 517 and 743 nm for DPPH and ABTS assays using a microplate reader (Biotek, PowerWave XS, USA). Ethanol and DI water served as the blank reference for DPPH and ABTS assay. All assays were conducted in triplicate. L-ascorbic acid and ( ±)-α-tocopherol were used as positive controls. Cell culture

Human keratinocyte (HaCaT) cells were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (catalogue number 300493, CLS Cell lines Service GmbH, Eppelheim, Germany) at passage no. 30–40 were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) (cell culture grade; HyClone, Logan, Utah, USA) supplemented with fetal bovine serum (FBS, 10%) (cell culture grade; HyClone, Logan, Utah, USA) and penicillin/streptomycin (1%) (cell culture grade; HyClone, Logan, Utah, USA). They were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2. Cells were seeded in a 96-well plate and treated with transethosomes after reaching 80% confluence (5 × 103 cells/well) for 24 h.

Cell viability assay

Cytotoxicity of the RA-loaded transethosomes was evaluated using the 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) assay. HaCaT cells were treated with various concentrations of the transethosomes (15–1000 nmol/mL) for 24 h at 37 °C. Then, MTT reagent (0.5 mg/mL) was added to each well. The cells were incubated for 4 h, after which the MTT-containing medium was carefully removed and replaced with DMSO (200 µL) to dissolve the formazan crystals produced by viable cells. The absorbance of the solubilized formazan was measured in triplicate at 570 nm using a microplate spectrophotometer (EonTM, BioTek Instruments, Inc., VT, USA). Scratch assays

The migratory ability of HaCaT cells was assessed using a scratch wound healing assay. Cells were seeded in 24-well culture plates at a density of 2.0 × 104 cells/cm2 in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin–streptomycin. Upon reaching approximately 80% confluence, a uniform linear scratch (~ 0.5 mm in width) was created across the cell monolayer using a sterile pipette tip (200 µL). Transethosome formulations (TE1–TE8) were applied at final concentrations of 500 nmol/mL and 1000 nmol/mL. Digital images of the wound area were captured at 0-, 6-, 24-, and 48-h post-scratch using an inverted phase-contrast microscope (CK30, Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) at 4× magnification.

In vitro skin permeation and in vitro release profile

The permeation of RA-loaded transethosomes prepared using various EAs was determined using synthetic skins (Strat-M®, EMD Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA) mimicking the tight stratum corneum of human skin. The membrane was directly place between the donor and receptor chambers of Franz diffusion cells, which have an effective permeation area of 3.14 cm2. Phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) (1X, pH 7.4) was added to the receptor chamber to a volume of 7.5 mL and incubated at 37 °C with a magnetic stirrer while gently stirring at 500 rpm. To compare the permeability ability, the prepared RA-loaded transethosomes prepared using different EA (TE1–TE4, EA concentration of 10% v/v) were studied. They were applied to the donor chamber. A sample (0.5 mL) was withdrawn from the receptor chamber at specific time intervals and replaced with an equal volume of PBS. The withdrawn samples were then analyzed for RA content using UV–Vis spectrophotometry at a wavelength of 327 nm. where Q is the cumulative content (µg/24-h), A is the diffusion area (cm2), and t is the lag time (h). In this experiment, a 24-h duration was used to determine both the lag time of permeation and the steady-state flux.

Fluorescent microscope (FM)

To explore the effect of RA on phase organization, the components such as EA and Chol were excluded in preparation for this study. The vesicles were prepared with DOPC/DPPC (5 mM) at a 50:50 molar ratio, and with RA incorporated at a DOPC/DPPC/RA (5 mM) ratio of 40:40:20. In these formulations, Rho-DHPE was used to selectively label the DOPC-rich domains, excited at 530–550 nm with emission at 575 nm using the BZX-800 automated fluorescence microscope (Keyence corp, Osaka, Japan). This approach enabled direct visualization of phase separation and domain organization influenced by RA.

CLSM

To further investigate the influence of EAs on membrane phase behavior, vesicles composed of DOPC and DPPC (TE5–TE8) were prepared without RA with fluorescent tracers (0.1 µM) and visualized using fluorescence microscopy (FV1000, Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) at room temperature. Rho-DHPE, which preferentially partitions into the DOPC-rich liquid-disordered (Ld) phase, was excited at 530–550 nm with emission at 575 nm. NBD-PE, a marker for the DPPC-rich liquid-ordered (Lo) phase, was excited at 470–495 nm with emission at 510–550 nm. Observations were limited to under 90 s to minimize photobleaching and photooxidation.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed with GraphPad Prism version 9.0.0 (GraphPad Software, CA, USA). Data were presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Comparisons between groups were analyzed using analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey–Kramer multiple comparison tests. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Effects of phospholopids and type of EAs on vesicular size, ζ potential, and %EE of RA-loaded transethosomes

To elucidate the influence of phospholipid composition and the type of EAs on the physicochemical properties of transethosomal vesicles, formulations were prepared using two distinct lipid systems: SoyPC/Chol and DOPC/DPPC/Chol. The concentrations of RA and EAs were fixed at 10% across all formulations: SoyPC/Chol/EAs/RA (70:10:10:10, TE1–TE4) and DOPC/DPPC/Chol/EAs/RA (35:35:10:10:10, TE5–TE8), as summarized in Table 1A.

DLS analysis revealed vesicle sizes ranging from 108.4 to 336.8 nm (Table S1). The incorporation of 10% Chol led to a reduction in vesicle size compared to Chol-free systems (TE0%, TE10%, TE30%, TE50%; Table 1B), consistent with the membrane-condensing and stabilizing effect of Chol, which promotes tighter packing and size minimization.

Regarding the type of EA used, formulations containing Tween-20, Tween-80, or OA exhibited significantly smaller vesicle sizes than those with PA in both lipid systems (p < 0.05; Fig. 1). This trend likely reflects the membrane-fluidizing properties of these EAs, which enhance bilayer flexibility and facilitate vesicle deformation. In contrast, PA, a saturated fatty acid with a high melting point, increased membrane rigidity, resulting in larger vesicles and broader size distributions. Morphology of transethosomes containing Tween-20, Tween-80, OA, and PA

The morphology of the prepared vesicles was evaluated using TEM (Fig. 2). DPPH and ABTS radical scavenging activity of RA-loaded transethosomes

The DPPH assay is widely employed to assess the free radical scavenging ability of antioxidants through the reduction of the stable DPPH radical, which results in a measurable decline in absorbance at 517 nm. Owing to its solubility in organic solvents such as methanol and ethanol, this assay is particularly sensitive to lipophilic compounds. In the present study, all RA-loaded transethosomal formulations (TE1–TE8) exhibited a concentration-dependent increase in DPPH scavenging activity, consistent with the lipophilic character of RA. Among the tested formulations, TE1 (SoyPC with Tween-20) demonstrated the highest DPPH inhibition, which may be attributed to improved dispersion, solubilization, and bioavailability of RA within this system (Fig. 3A). Nevertheless, none of the formulations achieved 50% inhibition (IC₅₀) at the highest tested concentration (100 mg/mL), indicating moderate radical scavenging efficiency under these conditions. The ABTS assay evaluates antioxidant activity through the generation of the ABTS⁺• radical cation via the oxidation of ABTS with potassium persulfate. The resulting green–blue chromophore, which absorbs maximally at 734 nm, is soluble in both aqueous and organic solvents—making this method suitable for assessing both hydrophilic and lipophilic antioxidants. In the present study, using ABTS assay, all RA-loaded transethosomal formulations exhibited measurable antioxidant activity, with TE2, TE1, TE3, and TE4—based on SoyPC/Chol—demonstrating the highest scavenging rates of 27%, 25%, 24%, and 23%, respectively (Fig. 3B and Figure S1). These results suggest enhanced RA accessibility and release from SoyPC-based vesicles, potentially facilitated by the amphiphilic nature of the system and the emulsifying action of Tween-20 in TE1. Antioxidant efficiency decreased at lower concentrations, indicating a concentration-dependent relationship between RA availability and radical scavenging capacity. In contrast, formulations TE5–TE8, which employed DOPC/DPPC/Chol membranes and various EAs (Tween-80, Span-80, OA, and PA), showed significantly lower ABTS activity (p < 0.05), particularly at higher concentrations. TE7, containing OA, demonstrated the highest %inhibition at 25 mg/mL, possibly due to enhanced membrane fluidity or partial RA release under specific conditions. However, none of the formulations achieved IC₅₀, indicating limited antioxidant activity compared to standard reference compounds. For comparison, common antioxidants such as L-ascorbic acid and ( ±)-α-tocopherol exhibited much lower IC₅₀ values (0.006 ± 0.000 and 0.026 ± 0.002 mg/mL, respectively), highlighting the moderate antioxidant capacity of the RA-loaded transethosomes.

Franz diffusion and in vitro drug release studies

Since the vesicle sizes, ζ potential, and migration patterns exhibited similar trends between the SoyPC/Chol and DOPC/DPPC/Chol membrane systems, the influence of EAs on transdermal delivery potential was specifically evaluated using SoyPC/Chol membrane systems (TE1-TE4). This approach excluded the potential effects of phase separation associated with DOPC and DPPC membranes. Franz diffusion studies were conducted using a synthetic Strat-M®membrane, a synthetic multilayer membrane designed to mimic human skin. The membrane consists of a dense top layer made of polyethersulfone, which simulates the stratum corneum, underlying layers composed of polyolefin representing the viable epidermis, and a bottom polyethersulfone layer mimicking the dermis. Its total thickness is approximately 300 µm, closely aligning with the barrier layer thickness of human skin. The cumulative percentage of RA permeation from diffusion cells was recorded at various time intervals over a 24-h period using UV–Vis spectrophotometry. The results are presented in Fig. 6A. The permeation of free RA is only 11.22% in 24 h, while SoyPC/Chol/Tween-80/RA showed the highest diffusion rate, followed by SoyPC/Chol/OA/RA, SoyPC/Chol/PA/RA, and SoyPC/Chol/Tween-20/RA, achieving permeation rates of 97.7 ± 2.2, 91.1 ± 1.5, 85.9 ± 12.3, and 56.3 ± 7.4%, respectively, at 24 h. RA permeation from transethosomal vesicles containing Tween-20, Tween-80, OA, and PA was significantly improved compared to free RA (p < 0.05). In particular, each transethosome formulation demonstrated a remarkable ability to improve RA delivery, underscoring their superior efficiency in promoting skin permeation. These results highlight the potential of the prepared transethosomal vesicles as a powerful platform to improve RA permeation, offering an exciting alternative to conventional delivery systems. The TE2 and TE3 containing Tween-80 and OA exhibited significantly higher permeation compared to those containing PA and Tween-20. A comparison of the permeation profiles for TE2 and TE3 showed that their profiles were closely aligned up to 8 h. However, at 12 and 24 h, vesicles with TE2 demonstrated higher permeation than that of TE3. Flux quantifies the rate of TE1–TE4 permeation through the membrane per unit area over time. The prolonged lag time of 24 h observed in the Franz diffusion study is primarily due to TE1–TE4’s high molecular weight and hydrophilic character, which limit passive diffusion. Furthermore, the barrier properties of the synthetic skin membrane (Strat-M®) contribute to the reduced permeation rate. The flux values of TE1–TE4 were 0.75, 1.30, 1.21, and 0.98 µg/cm2/h, respectively, whereas that of free RA was 0.15 µg/cm2/h (Fig. 6B). Discussion

RA’s structure—featuring a polyene hydrophobic tail with five double bonds—promotes its incorporation into the lipid bilayer. The observed vesicle sizes are in the nanoscale and consistent with previous reports, indicating that increasing RA concentration tends to enlarge vesicle size due to the incorporation of RA into the vesicle membrane34. A progressive increase in vesicle size was observed in the TE0%, TE10%, and TE30% formulations. However, a size reduction in TE50% was noted, with a marked increase in PDI observed, which may result from saturated incorporation levels. Excessive RA may aggregate and be unable to effectively integrate into the transethosomal membrane, thereby presenting the polydispersity of vesicle sizes. In terms of RA incorporation into the membrane, the increased fluidity of SoyPC vesicles arises from the greater integration of RA’s polyunsaturated hydrophobic tail into the lipid bilayer. These molecular interactions disrupt the tight packing of SoyPC acyl chains, enhancing membrane fluidity by increasing conformational freedom within the bilayer—a process driven by a favorable increase in entropy. It was reported that RA functions as a linactant—a molecule that reduces line tension at the boundaries between coexisting membrane phases35. Moreover, the abundant polyunsaturated hydrophobic tails of RA can further contribute to size heterogeneity and elevated PDI value36,37. The disruption of the alignment of hydrophobic lipid tails results in reduced line tension and increased membrane fluidity. Although extrusion initially reduces vesicle size, the diminished line tension resulting from high RA incorporation increases membrane susceptibility to fusion, flattening, or aggregation. This promotes the formation of larger vesicles with lower curvature38.

For the SoyPC/Chol system (TE1-TE4), since SoyPC is composed of two unsaturated fatty acid tails—18:2 (cis 9,12) and 18:1 (cis 9)—the incorporation of OA (unsaturated EAs) results in a more fluid membrane compared to PA (saturated EAs). Tween-20, although it contains a saturated alkyl chain, functions as a surfactant; its structure, which includes sorbitan and PEG moieties, facilitates better membrane incorporation than PA. Similarly, Tween-80 contains an unsaturated alkyl chain along with sorbitan and PEG moieties, further enhancing membrane integration. The results showed an increasing vesicle size in the order: PA > OA > Tween-20 > Tween-80. Since RA incorporation enhances membrane fluidity, its effect is more pronounced in systems with higher rigidity, such as those containing saturated EAs. For the DOPC/DPPC/Chol system (TE5-TE8), phase separation naturally occurs, forming a DOPC-rich (Lo) phase and a DPPC/Chol-rich (Ld) phase. RA destabilizes the tightly packed structure and reduces the curvature energy at the interface between Lo and Ld domains, resulting in lower line tension and increase membrane fluidity. Results showed that RA preferentially incorporates into systems containing OA and PA more than those containing the surfactants Tween-20 and Tween-80. Due to the ability of the sorbitan and PEG moieties in Tween-based surfactants to reduce curvature energy more effectively than OA and PA, the membrane becomes more stable, which may hinder RA incorporation.39.

The ζ potential of approximately ± 30 mV are generally considered indicative of good long-term colloidal stability for nanoparticles40. As presented in Table 2, the ζ potential values of transethosomal vesicles (TE0–TE50%) ranged from − 28.0 to − 31.4 mV, confirming stable dispersion. The ζ potential value reflects the degree of repulsion between vesicles, which can indicate the stability of the vesicles. The absolute ζ potential value decreased slightly with increasing RA content, and a significant reduction was observed at 50% RA (Fig. 1). This may be due to the effect of RA that disrupts the packaging of SoyPC, alter the dynamics of the negative phosphate heads, and decrease the repulsive forces by increasing membrane fluidity41,42. Table S1 showed that TE1-TE4 exhibited ζ potential values close to − 30 mV, whereas TE5-TE8 exhibited less negative ζ potential values than TE1-TE4. The SoyPC/Chol system demonstrated slightly greater stability compared to the DOPC/DPPC/Chol system. This increased stability correlates with the larger vesicle size and higher RA incorporation. The presence of negatively charged phosphate groups contributes to the more negative ζ potential values, which may result from membrane reorganization and destabilization from interactions with unsaturated lipids33,43,44. Comparing to SoyPC system without Chol, the system containing Chol exhibited a reduced size and more negative ζ potential values, consistent with its role in modulating membrane fluidity and packing, thereby enhancing vesicle stability45–47.

The %EE of TE1–TE8 formulations was close to 90%. Interestingly, DOPC/DPPC/Chol system showed slightly higher %EE than the SoyPC/Chol system. This may be attributed to the looser packing of SoyPC molecules, which contain two unsaturated tails, whereas the DOPC/DPPC/Chol system forms a more tightly packed Ld phase8,48. These findings are consistent with the results for vesicle size and charge.

According to the TEM and %EE results, these smaller molecules may represent residual Tween-20 and PA molecules that might not have been fully incorporated into the membrane49. Interestingly, the SoyPC/Chol containing Tween-80 and OA showed smaller vesicle sizes than those with Tween-20 and PA. A similar trend was observed between the Tween-20 and PA, as well as Tween-80 and OA in DOPC/DPPC systems, which could be attributed to the similar structure of the hydrophobic tails of the EAs (Tween-20 and PA contain saturated hydrophobic tails, while Tween-80 and OA contain unsaturated hydrophobic tails). The trend in vesicle sizes suggests that the choice of EA significantly affects vesicular size morphology and stability.

Antioxidant activity of all formulations was evaluated using DPPH and ABTS assays. TE1 exhibited the highest DPPH radical scavenging activity, achieving up to 35% inhibition. In comparison, previous studies have reported that pure retinol can scavenge up to 70% of DPPH radicals50, indicative of its superior antioxidant capacity. Additionally, pectin enriched with retinyl palmitate demonstrated approximately fivefold greater DPPH scavenging activity (~ 30%) compared to other polysaccharides such as xanthan gum, chitin, and sodium alginate51 highlighting the critical role of antioxidant structure, degree of unsaturation of retinol derivatives, and formulation environment in modulating free radical neutralization. This finding is consistent with the effect of RA observed in our study. In contrast, antioxidant activity measured by the ABTS assay (25%) was slightly lower than that observed with DPPH, likely due to differences in radical hydrophobicity and assay sensitivity. Variations in ABTS scavenging among formulations can be attributed to differences in lipid composition, type of EA, membrane saturation, and physicochemical properties of RA, including polarity, solubility, and hydrogen-bonding capacity52. Interestingly, the observed antioxidant potency aligns with previously reported activity levels of vitamin A derivatives, typically ranging between 20–30%51.

The cell viability results demonstrated a range from 98.7 to 111.2%, confirming that HaCaT cells maintained high viability across all tested concentrations. Statistical analysis revealed no significant differences in cell viability among the various EAs types or formulation compositions (p > 0.05), indicating that none of the tested components exerted cytotoxic effects under the experimental conditions. These findings suggest that the EAs, including Tween-20, Tween-80, OA, and PA, are biocompatible with keratinocyte cell lines. This biocompatibility supports their potential for safe use in topical delivery systems. The absence of cytotoxicity also highlights the suitability of these formulations for further development in skin-related applications, aligning with previous studies that report the non-toxic nature of non-ionic surfactants and fatty acids at comparable concentrations. The wound-healing efficacy of transethosomal formulations (TE1–TE8) were also investigated in comparison to free RA using an in vitro scratch assay on HaCaT keratinocyte cells. The results demonstrated that several transethosomal formulations significantly enhanced cell migration and reduced the width of wound over 24 h, indicating their potential in promoting skin repair. Among the tested formulations, TE3 to TE7 showed the most pronounced wound closure effects, suggesting that the composition of the transethosomal membrane plays a crucial role in modulating biological activity. Notably, transethosomes formulated with SoyPC/Chol demonstrated effective wound healing, particularly when OA and PA were used as EAs. These fatty acids more effectively disrupt the lipid bilayer and enhance membrane fluidity thereby facilitating the release of the encapsulated vitamin A and promoting keratinocyte migration and proliferation. Results are in agreement with the size and %EE. In contrast, DOPC/DPPC/Chol system exhibited superior performance. The wound-healing effects among each EAs within this system were not significantly different. This outcome is likely due to both higher RA encapsulation and RA release. Increased membrane fluidity may reduce RA release, whereas more solid-like systems may promote release due to their energetically favorable configuration. Importantly, the transethosomal delivery system demonstrated greater efficacy in promoting wound healing compared to free RA. This finding supports the hypothesis that encapsulating RA in lipid-based nanocarriers can improve its stability, solubility, and targeted cellular uptake, which are critical for clinical translation in topical wound therapies.

We compared the transdermal delivery properties of each EA using the SoyPC/Chol system and investigated their effects on membrane incorporation using the DOPC/DPPC/Chol system, based on Rho tracing of the DOPC-rich phase and NBD-PE tracing of the DPPC/Chol-rich phase. Our results confirmed that the transferosome systems composed of PC, Chol, Tween-80, and ethanol were transported transcellularly and enhanced the transdermal delivery of RA. The observed improvement across all transethosomal formulations highlights the critical role of ethanol and EAs in facilitating skin permeation53. Ethanol contributes to this effect through its ‒OH groups, which exhibit stronger electronegativity than the ‒NH groups in stratum corneum ceramides. By competing with ceramides for hydrogen bond formation, ethanol disrupts the compact lipid structure of the stratum corneum, thereby increasing permeability and facilitating RA delivery54. The highest permeation was observed with TE2 and TE3, likely due to their unsaturated hydrophobic tails, which enhance membrane fluidity and vesicle deformability. These features allow the vesicles to pass more efficiently through the synthetic Strat-M® membrane. Notably, the permeation profiles of Tween-80 and OA were comparable up to 8 h, but Tween-80 outperformed OA at 12 and 24 h, possibly due to the added steric and fluidizing effects of the polyoxyethylene head group in Tween-80. Our results align with prior work13,55,56. PA-containing vesicles (TE4) showed better permeation than those with Tween-20 (TE1) but lower than Tween-80 (TE2) and OA (TE3). This is attributed to the saturated nature of PA (C16:0), which increases membrane rigidity but also enhances its interaction with lipid bilayers through stronger van der Waals forces and tighter packing. These properties may facilitate deeper penetration despite reduced deformability compared to OA and Tween-80. Tween-20, which has a shorter saturated tail, showed the lowest permeation, indicating limited ability to disrupt the membrane or enhance vesicle flexibility. Although Tween-80, OA, and PA formulations demonstrated higher flux values, the differences were not statistically significant (p > 0.05), suggesting a comparable enhancement in delivery potential among these EAs. Overall, these findings highlight the significant of EA selection in modulating vesicle properties and optimizing transdermal drug delivery systems. The incorporation of RA into DOPC/DPPC system exhibited the transform of So (DPPC-rich phase)/Ld phase to Lo/Ld phase (Fig. 8). Also, the CLSM images highlight the significant role of EA molecular structure in influencing membrane organization and Chol distribution. Saturated fatty acid chains in PA (C16:0) and Tween-20 (C12:0) contribute to tighter lipid packing, promoting exclusion of Chol from ordered domains, as reflected by the So/Ld phase pattern8. PA, with its longer saturated tail, exhibited a stronger tendency for Chol exclusion than Tween-20. Conversely, OA and Tween-80, which possess unsaturated hydrophobic tails, facilitated the formation of Lo/Ld phases characterized by more fluidic membrane environments33. This suggests that unsaturated EAs enhance lipid bilayer flexibility and promote Chol incorporation into ordered domains. These findings highlight the importance of selecting suitable EAs to tailor membrane properties, which may affect the structural integrity, flexibility, and drug delivery performance of transethosomal vesicles.

Conclusions

RA-loaded transethosomal vesicles incorporating various EAs—Tween-20, Tween-80, OA, and PA—were successfully formulated with nanoscale dimensions and spherical morphology. These vesicles consistently exhibited negative surface charges, with absolute ζ potential values exceeding 30 mV, indicating excellent colloidal stability. High %EE (> 87%) was achieved across all formulations, with vesicle size increasing in response to higher RA concentrations, particularly notable in PA-containing systems. Moderate antioxidant activity was observed using DPPH and ABTS assays. Importantly, all formulations demonstrated negligible cytotoxicity toward HaCaT cells, confirming their biocompatibility. Compared to free RA, the transethosomal formulations enhanced wound healing in HaCaT cells, with the most notable improvements observed in the DOPC/DPPC/Chol systems with all EAs, and in the SoyPC-based systems containing OA and PA. The choice of EA had a significant influenced on physicochemical characteristics and transdermal permeation profiles. Tween-80–based vesicles achieved the highest RA permeation, followed by OA, PA, and Tween-20 formulations. The superior permeation of Tween-80 and OA formulations is likely attributed to their capacity to enhance membrane fluidity, as also demonstrated by CLSM images, whereas Tween-20 and PA conferred increased membrane rigidity. These results highlight the critical influence of EA selection on modulating vesicle physicochemical properties to enhance transdermal delivery efficiency, with Tween-80 demonstrating the greatest potential to facilitate RA permeation through the skin. Future investigations should focus on elucidating the mechanistic role of membrane line tension and conducting comprehensive in vivo evaluations to validate the therapeutic efficacy and biocompatibility of these formulations.

Functional Outcomes of Total Hip Replacement in Inflammatory Hip Arthritis: Influence of Disease-Modifying Anti-Rheumatic Drugs and Postoperative Analgesic Management
Authors: S Ganesh Kumar, K. Balaji Subramaniam, S. Pajani, K. Arul Vignesh, Panneerselvam Periasamy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Inflammatory hip arthritis caused by conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis and ankylosing spondylitis leads to progressive joint destruction, severe pain, and disability. Total hip replacement (THR) is an effective surgical intervention; however, pharmacological factors such as disease-modifying anti-rheumatic drugs (DMARDs), and postoperative analgesic management may influence outcomes.Aim:To evaluate functional outcomes of THR in inflammatory hip arthritis and assess the influence of DMARD therapy and postoperative analgesic requirements.Methodology:A prospective observational study was conducted on 30 patients with inflammatory hip arthritis undergoing primary THR. Functional outcomes were assessed using Harris hip score (HHS), visual analogue scale (VAS), and range of motion. Data on perioperative DMARD use and postoperative analgesic requirements were recorded. Patients were followed for 12 months.Results:Mean HHS improved from 38.6 ± 7.2 preoperatively to 87.4 ± 6.8 at 12 months (P < 0.001). Mean VAS decreased from 8.1 ± 1.0 to 1.3 ± 0.6. Patients on optimized DMARD therapy demonstrated better functional recovery and lower postoperative pain scores. Increased analgesic requirement was observed during early postoperative periods, but reduced significantly over time.Conclusion:THR provides excellent functional improvement in inflammatory hip arthritis. DMARD optimization and effective analgesic management significantly influence postoperative recovery and patient outcomes.

INTRODUCTION

Inflammatory arthritis affecting the hip joint is a disabling condition commonly seen in diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, and psoriatic arthritis. Chronic inflammation leads to cartilage destruction, joint deformity, and functional impairment.[1] Despite advances in pharmacotherapy, including disease-modifying anti-rheumatic drugs (DMARDs), many patients progress to end-stage joint destruction requiring surgical intervention.[2]

Total hip replacement (THR) is a well-established procedure that provides pain relief and restores mobility in patients with advanced hip arthritis.[3] However, outcomes following THR in inflammatory arthritis depend not only on surgical technique but also on pharmacological factors such as ongoing DMARD therapy and postoperative pain management strategies.

DMARDs play a crucial role in controlling systemic inflammation and may influence postoperative recovery, implant survival, and complication rates.[4] Additionally, effective postoperative analgesic management is essential for early mobilization and rehabilitation.[5]

Although functional outcomes of THR have been widely studied, limited literature exists on the combined influence of pharmacotherapy and surgical outcomes in inflammatory arthritis patients. This study aims to evaluate functional outcomes and examine the role of DMARD therapy and analgesic management in influencing recovery after THR.

AIM

To evaluate functional outcomes of total hip replacement in inflammatory hip arthritis and assess the influence of DMARD therapy and postoperative analgesic management.

METHODOLOGY

This prospective observational study was conducted in the Department of Orthopedics at a tertiary care center over a period of 24 months. Thirty patients aged between 18 and 70 years diagnosed with inflammatory hip arthritis and indicated for primary total hip replacement were included. Patients with active infection, neuromuscular disorders affecting gait, revision arthroplasty, or severe comorbidities were excluded. All patients underwent cementless THR using a posterolateral approach under spinal or combined anesthesia.

Preoperative assessment included clinical evaluation, radiographic analysis, and documentation of ongoing DMARD therapy. Patients were categorized based on whether they were on regular DMARD treatment. Postoperative analgesic management was standardized using non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), with additional opioids administered as needed based on pain severity.

Functional outcomes were assessed using the Harris hip score (HHS), visual analogue scale (VAS), and range of motion. Follow-up evaluations were conducted at 6 weeks, 3 months, 6 months, and 12 months. Data on analgesic consumption (duration and need for additional analgesics) were recorded. Statistical analysis was performed using paired t-test, with P < 0.05 considered significant.

RESULTS

A total of 30 patients were included in the study, with a mean age of 46.2 years. Significant improvement in functional outcomes was observed following total hip replacement. The mean HHS improved markedly from 38.6 ± 7.2 preoperatively to 87.4 ± 6.8 at final follow-up (P < 0.001). Similarly, the mean VAS score decreased from 8.1 ± 1.0 to 1.3 ± 0.6, indicating substantial pain relief. Hip flexion also improved significantly from 62° preoperatively to 108° postoperatively [Table 1].

KEY FINDINGS

Significant improvement in functional outcomes was observed following THR, with mean Harris Hip Score increasing from 38.6 preoperatively to 87.4 at final follow-up [Table 1]. Pain scores decreased markedly, indicating effective pain relief. Patients receiving DMARD therapy demonstrated better functional outcomes and lower early postoperative pain scores compared to those not on DMARDs [Table 2]. Analgesic requirements were higher during the early postoperative period, but decreased progressively over time. Functional recovery showed a steady upward trend across follow-up intervals [Figure 1], reflecting sustained improvement in hip function.

DISCUSSION

The present study demonstrates that total hip replacement is highly effective in improving function and reducing pain in patients with inflammatory hip arthritis. The significant improvement in HHS observed in this study is consistent with previous reports highlighting the success of THR in inflammatory arthropathies.[367]

DMARD therapy appears to play a beneficial role in postoperative outcomes. Patients receiving DMARDs showed better functional recovery and lower pain scores, likely due to better control of systemic inflammation. Similar findings have been reported in studies evaluating the impact of pharmacotherapy on surgical outcomes in inflammatory arthritis.[4]

Postoperative analgesic management is a critical determinant of early recovery. Higher analgesic requirements observed in the early postoperative period gradually decreased, indicating effective pain control and adaptation. Adequate pain management facilitates early mobilization, which is essential for optimal functional recovery.[5]

The study also highlights the importance of integrating pharmacological and surgical approaches in managing inflammatory arthritis. The interplay between DMARD therapy and surgical outcomes underscores the need for multidisciplinary care involving orthopedic surgeons and pharmacologists.

The low complication rate observed in this study supports the safety and efficacy of modern THR techniques. However, careful patient selection and perioperative optimization remain essential.

CONCLUSION

Total hip replacement provides excellent pain relief and significant functional improvement in patients with inflammatory hip arthritis.

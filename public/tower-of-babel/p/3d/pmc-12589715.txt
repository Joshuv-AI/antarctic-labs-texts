Efficacy of a standardized extract (HemoHIM G) from Angelica sinensis, Ligusticum chuanxiong, and Paeonia lactiflora on cyclophosphamide-induced immunosuppression in BALB/c mice
Authors: Su-Bin Bak, Hansol Choi, Hak Sung Lee, Ju Gyeong Kim, Ji Eun Shin, Jung-Jae Lee, Jae-Yeong Ju
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAngelica sinensis, Ligusticum chuanxiong, and Paeonia lactiflora have traditionally been used as medicinal herbs in East Asia; HemoHIM G is a standardized extract of the three herbs. However, its immune improvement abilities are unexplored. Hence, this study aimed to investigate the immune-enhancing effects of HemoHIM G on cyclophosphamide (CTX)-induced immunosuppression in a murine model. As a result, HemoHIM G improved weight recovery lowered by CTX and helped improve white blood cell counts and other hematologic indicators. In addition, CTX-reduced Natural Killer (NK) cell activity was significantly improved. Cell density was significantly reduced by improving recovery of the spleen white pulp. Furthermore, HemoHIM G significantly increased interleukin-2 and interferon gamma expressions in splenocytes and induced spleen lymphocyte proliferation. Thus, HemoHIM G increased NK cell activity and played an important role in enhancing immunity through cytokine expression. The study findings suggest HemoHIM G as a novel functional food with immunomodulatory efficacy.Keywords: Immunosuppression, Cyclophosphamide, Immunoregulation, NK cell activity, HemoHIM G

Introduction

The awareness regarding immune-related diseases since the coronavirus disease pandemic has rapidly increased the demand for immunity-boosting medicines and functional foods. The National Business Journal reported an increase in the sales of immune supplements from approximately $3.4 billion in 2019 to approximately $6 billion by the end of 2020 (Crawford et al., 2022). The innate defense system is an immediate nonspecific response mediated by various types of immune cells, including natural killer (NK) cells, dendritic cells (DCs), and cytotoxic T lymphocytes (Iwasaki and Pillai, 2014). A decline in immune function makes the body vulnerable to various infectious diseases, which require the restoration of immunity through health management. NK cells play a pivotal role in the innate immune response to infection and express activation receptors which recognize invaders (Yokoyama, 2005). Additionally, activated macrophages and CD4+ T helper (Th) cells secrete cytokines such as interleukin (IL)-2 and tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α, which act as meditators for recruiting NK cells, neutrophils, and eosinophils.

Cyclophosphamide (CTX) is a widely used anticancer drug with known immunosuppressive effects. CTX is an alkylating agent; it belongs to the oxazaphosphorine group, compounds from which have been used to treat immune-mediated diseases for more than 40 years and exhibit immunosuppressive and immunomodulatory properties (Ahlmann and Hempel, 2016). Immunosuppressive effects are primarily attributed to the metabolites formed when CTX is metabolically activated in the body. These metabolites inhibit the proliferation and function of immune cells such as T and B cells. Specifically, CTX interferes with immune cell DNA synthesis and induces cell death, thereby suppressing immune responses (Cupps et al., 1982). Recently, a CTX-induced immunosuppression model has been designed to confirm the immunity-enhancing ability of substances (Jie et al., 2020; Noh et al., 2019).

Angelica sinensis, Ligusticum chuanxiong, and Paeonia lactiflora are traditional plants used in China, Korea, and Japan that exhibit various pharmacological effects, including immune improvement. A. sinensis is commonly used in herbal medicine to treat gynecological diseases and hematopoiesis (Wei et al., 2016). Typical components include ferulic acid, coniferyl ferulate, and Z-ligustilide. The main physiologically active immunomodulatory components are polysaccharides and ferulic acid (Chao and Linn, 2011). L. chuanxiong is a traditional medicine with various physiological and pharmacological effects and is known to have anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and antibacterial effects (Shi et al., 2020). P. lactiflora is a medicinal plant with known efficacy in immunomodulatory and autoimmune diseases (He and Dai, 2011). Its active ingredients include benzoic acid, paeoniflorin, and albiflorin (Jiang et al., 2020). Paeoniflorin and total glucose exert immunomodulatory effects (Kim et al., 2021a, b).

Recently, various studies have demonstrated the efficacy of immunity-enhancing functional foods using traditional medicines such as Bu-Zhong-Yi-Qi-Tang and Qingfei Paidu Tang (Liu et al., 2019; Tu et al., 2019). However, to our knowledge, the immune improvement abilities of A. sinensis, L. chuanxiong, and P. lactiflora have not been explored to date. Therefore, this study suggests novel compounds from these three medicinal herbs which can effectively improve immune function. The standardized extract of HemoHIM G is a functional food containing A. sinensis, L. chuanxiong, and P. lactiflora. It is obtained by adding the immunity-enhancing polysaccharide fraction to a hot water extract of an herb mixture.

This study aimed to evaluate the immunomodulatory efficacy of HemoHIM G in a CTX-induced immunosuppression model and propose a novel functional food, HemoHIM G, as a healthy alternative with immunomodulatory efficacy.

Materials and methods

Preparation of HemoHIM G

1-year-old A.sinensis roots cultivated in the Gansu province of China, 1-year-old L.chuanxiong roots cultivated in the Sichuan province of China, and 4 ~ 5-year-old P. lactiflora roots cultivated in the Anhui province of China were used. All the cultivation area was managed according to good agricultural practices (GAP). The dried and cut A. sinensis, L. chuanxiong, and P. lactiflora were mixed in a specific ratio and extracted for two times (4–6 h and 2–4 h) in hot water. Standardized HemoHIM G (Lot No. 2100001) was manufactured by Kolmar BNH Co., Ltd. (Sejong-si, Republic of Korea). The rhizomes of L. chuanxiong and the roots of A. sinensis and P. lactiflora were extracted for 4–6 h in hot water to obtain the total extract. A fraction of the water extract was concentrated and precipitated with 95% ethanol to obtain the ethanol-insoluble polysaccharide fraction. The water extract and polysaccharide fraction were concentrated using a rotary evaporator-concentrator (N-1110, Eyela, Tokyo Rikakikai) to a solid content of 30 ± 3%. Finally, each concentrate was mixed in a specific ratio, freeze-dried (FDU-1110, Eyela), and stored at 4 °C until use. HemoHIM G extract is standardized for its consistency with chlorogenic acid (more than 80% and less than 140% of 0.216 mg/g) derived from Angelica sinensis and Ligusticum chuanxiong, and paeoniflorin (more than 80% and less than 140% of 2.869 mg/g) derived from Paeonia lactiflora, as marker substances. Additionally, total polyphenol (more than 80% and less than 140% of 5.985 mg/g) and polysaccharides (more than 80% and less than 140% of 8.95%) are set for the control marker.

HPLC analysis

HPLC analysis of the HemoHIM G herbal extract was performed on a Shimadzu Nexera Lite (Shimadzu Corporation, Kyoto, Japan) system using a photodiode array detector. A standard mixture (gallic acid, chlorogenic acid, paeoniflorin, and ferulic acid) and HemoHIM G were separated on YMC-Pack Pro C18 column (4.6 × 250 mm, 5 µm, 80 Å, YMC, Kyoto, Japan). The mobile phase consisted of a binary solvent system comprising of 0.1% phosphoric acid in water (solvent A) and acetonitrile (Solvent B).

Animals and study design

BALB/c male mice (10-week-old; DooYeol Biotech, Seoul, Republic of Korea) were used in this study for approximately 3 weeks. A total of 60 mice were acclimatized for a week, and a maximum of five mice were co-housed in cages and maintained in a light–dark cycle of 12 h (the light cycle was between 8 a.m. and 8 p.m.) in a breeding environment (23 ± 2 °C, 60% ± 5 humidity). After a week of acclimation, each mouse was assigned to one of the following five treatment groups based on body weight: (1) wild-type(CTL) as the negative control group (n = 12), (2) CTX as the positive control group (n = 12), (3) CTX + HemoHIM G 125 mg/kg/day (n = 12), (4) CTX + HemoHIM G 250 mg/kg/day, and (5) CTX + HemoHIM G 500 mg/kg/day (n = 12). All groups were orally administered HemoHIM G 18 days after the acclimation period, and the treatment groups were administered 100 mg/kg/day CTX (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) intraperitoneally for three consecutive days after administering HemoHIM G for 7 days. Body weights were measured daily.

Analysis of blood biochemistry

Before euthanization, all mice were anesthetized using 1–2% isoflurane, and whole blood was drawn from the abdominal aortic vein into EDTA lavender tubes (Becton Dickinson and Company, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA). The collected blood was subjected to a complete blood count using an ADVIA 2120i hematology system (Siemens, Munich, Germany).

Analysis of NK cell activity

Each 0.8 mL of blood collected was transferred to a tube containing heparin (JW Pharmaceutical, Seocho, Republic of Korea). The activator was added to the collection tube and gently mixed for 1 h at room temperature. Following this, the supernatant was transferred in a 96-well plate and incubated for 24 h at 37 °C. After incubation, the samples were transferred into the plate which stained IFN-γ and the assay was performed using a Murine NK activity kit (NK MAX Bio, Seongnam, Republic of Korea).

Hematoxylin and eosin staining of the spleen

Isolated spleen tissues from mice were fixed with 10% neutral buffered formalin (Biosesang, Yongin, Republic of Korea), embedded in paraffin, and sectioned into 3 μm thick-sections using Finesse ME microtome (Thermo Fisher scientific, Waltham, USA). The sections were dried, deparaffinized, washed with distilled water, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin.

Analysis of cytokine levels in the spleen

The following cytokines were assayed: IL-2, IL-4, TNF-α, and IFN-γ. All cytokines were assayed in splenocytes isolated from experimental mouse spleen tissues. The spleens were aseptically excised and meshed in RPMI 1640 medium (Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA) with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco) and 1% antibiotics and antimycotics (Gibco). Thereafter, 1 × 106 cells/mL were dispensed to a 48-well plate and the total volume was adjusted to 150 μL. The cells were then treated with 50 ng/mL Phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate (PMA; MERCK, Darmstadt, Germany) and 2 μg/mL Ionomycin (MERCK) diluted in RPMI 1640 medium and incubated for 48 h in a 37 °C, 5% CO2 incubator. After 48 h, the supernatant was subjected to ELISA (R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA), and the absorbance at 450 nm was measured.

Analysis of splenic lymphocyte proliferation

Splenocytes were isolated from spleen tissues of normal mice. The spleens were aseptically removed and meshed in RPMI 1640 medium (Gibco) supplemented with 10% FBS (Gibco) and 1% antibiotics and antimycotics (Gibco). Following this, 1 × 106 cells/mL were dispensed to a 96-well plate such that the total volume was 100 μL. The cells were then treated with 50 ng/mL Phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate (MERCK) and 2 μg/mL Ionomycin (MERCK) diluted in the RPMI 1640 medium and incubated for 35 h in a 37 °C, 5% CO2 incubator. After 35 h, the cells were treated with WST-8 for 1 h and the absorbance value was obtained at 450 nm using a microplate reader (TECAN, Mannedorf, Switzerland).

Statistical analyses

Data were analyzed using PRISM 7 (GraphPad Software, CA, USA). Continuous data were expressed as mean ± standard error of mean. Consistent variables were compared using one-way analysis of variance with Dunnet’s multiple comparison test for all the groups. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results and discussion

Identification of HemoHIM G

We identified the four main compounds in HemoHIM G, namely chlorogenic acid and ferulic acid derived from A. sinensis and L. chuanxiong, and gallic acid and paeoniflorin derived from P. lactiflora (Fig. 1B). This finding resonates with the previous studies that identified polysaccharides and ferulic acid as major bioactive immunomodulatory components of A. sinensis (Lao et al., 2004; Shi et al., 2020). Numerous studies have investigated the mechanisms by which these polysaccharides improve immune response, demonstrating their direct effects on macrophages, antigen-presenting cells, and immune cells such as lymphocytes (T, B, and NK cells) and neutrophils (Ying and Hao, 2023). Ferulic acid exhibits antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties; in particular, it is known to have excellent antioxidant effects by being involved in the NRF-2/HO-1 pathway (Li et al., 2021). Polysaccharides and ferulic acid have also been reported as the primary components derived from L. chuanxiong (Wang et al., 2022; Yi et al., 2016). The representative compounds of P. lactiflora are benzoic acid, paeoniflorin, and albiflorin. Paeoniflorin and total glucosides exert immunomodulatory effects against inflammatory and immune diseases (Kim et al., 2021a, b; Lao et al., 2004). These findings suggested that HemoHIM G would likely be effective in improving immunity due to the presence of the abovementioned active substances of the three herbs.

Effects of HemoHIM G on body weight and hematological indices in immunosuppressed mice

CTX is widely used to design immunosuppressive models and inhibit the proliferation and function of immune cells, such as T and B cells. Therefore, CTX may induce weight loss, peripheral blood imbalance, and organ atrophy in the immune tissues of mice (Zhang et al., 2021). Here, we designed a CTX-induced immunosuppression model by following a standard method known for inducing adequate immunosuppression (Chen et al., 2019; Hwang et al. 2022). Following CTX administration, all experimental groups exhibited significant changes compared to CTL group (Fig. 2B). Subsequently, noticeable weight loss patterns were observed in CTX-treated groups compared to CTL group, thereby confirming that CTX successfully induced immunosuppression. At the end of the experiments, the HemoHIM G group (HG125, HG250, and HG 500) exhibited a more pronounced recovery pattern compared to CTX group. At day 18 of administration, the CTX group showed significant weight loss compared to CTL, and the hemoHIMG administration group showed significant weight gain compared to the CTX group (p < 0.001; CTX, p < 0.001; CTX + HG125, p < 0.05; CTX + HG500). Further, the CTX group showed a significant decrease in WBC compared to CTL (p < 0.001; CTL vs. CTX groups) (Fig. 2C). However, the WBC was found to increase proportionally with increasing concentrations of HemoHIM G. Notably, a significant increase in WBC was clearly observed in the CTX + HG250 and CTX + HG500 groups in dose-dependent manner of HemoHIM G (p < 0.05; CTX + HG250, p < 0.001; CTX + HG500). These findings highlight the effects of HemoHIM G on hematological indices in immunosuppressed mice, thereby confirming its ability to restore the immune status disrupted by CTX.

Effects of HemoHIM G on NK cell activity in immunosuppressed mice

In this study, a significant decrease in the NK cell activity was observed in the CTX group compared to that in the CTL group, which confirmed that immunosuppression was induced successfully (Fig. 3) (p < 0.001; CTX group). NK cells are specialized WBCs that play an important role in the immune system by helping in the detection and elimination of infected or abnormal cells (Lanier, 2005). Therefore, the observed decrease in NK cell activity in the CTX group in this study indicates the initiation of immunocompromise. NK cells interact with other cells through cell surface proteins such as killer cell Immunoglobulin-like Receptors or Natural Killer Group 2D (NKG2D) that help distinguish between normal and abnormal cells and transmit signals that recognize and target abnormal cells (Deng et al., 2015). Also, they are activated on detecting infected or abnormal cells and secrete IFN-γ, which plays an important role in various aspects of the immune response and exhibits several physiological effects (Paolini et al., 2015). Consequently, IFN-γ secretion by NK cells enhances immune functions, aiding the immune system in coping with infections, tumors, and ultimately promoting overall immune recovery. In our study, NK cell activity tended to increase in a HemoHIM G dose-dependent manner the HemoHIM G groups compared to that in the CTX group; notably, the HemoHIM G 500 mg/kg/day group demonstrated a significant increase in NK cell activity (p < 0.01; CTX + HG500 group). Thus, it can be inferred that HemoHIM G alleviates compromised immunity by enhancing NK cell activity.

Effects of HemoHIM G on histopathological analysis of the spleen in immunosuppressed mice

CTX is known to induce atrophy in immune cells; resulting in a decreased cell density in the white pulp of the spleen, which induces an immunocompromise state. The cell density in the CTX group was significantly lower than that in the CTL group (Fig. 4A), which confirmed that immunosuppression was properly induced by CTX. The spleen, as the largest part of the immune system, filters out foreign substances and blood from old and damaged blood cells using the white and red pulps; hence, the degree of spleen function can be determined by examining cell morphology (Cesta, 2006). In all HemoHIM G groups, cell density exhibited a pattern of recovery; notably, the HemoHIM G 500 mg/kg/day group showed significant histological improvement (Fig. 4A). Additionally, all HemoHIM G-treated groups exhibited a significant decrease in cell density than CTX group (p < 0.001; CTX + HG125, CTX + HG250, and CTX + HG500 groups) (Fig. 4B). These results suggest that HemoHIM G effectively improves immune function.

Effects of HemoHIM G on cytokine expression in immunosuppressed mice

IL-2 levels tended to decrease in the CTX group, but the expression of IL-2 significantly increased in the HemoHIM G group (p < 0.001; HemoHIM G groups) (Fig. 5A). Moreover, IFN-γ levels significantly decreased in the CTX group (p < 0.001; CTX group) but increased in the CTX + HG500 group (p < 0.01; CTX + 500 group) (Fig. 5B). IL-4 levels significantly increased in all the HemoHIM G groups (p < 0.01; CTX + HG125, p < 0.05; CTX + HG250, p < 0.05; CTX + HG500 groups) (Fig. 5C). In addition, TNF-α levels tended to increase in the CTX + HG125 and CTX + HG 250 groups (Fig. 5D). Cytokines such as IL-2 are polypeptide mediators that play various regulatory roles in the host defense and immune homeostasis mechanisms. They play an important role in the immune system by exerting a cell-destroying effect on infectious agents or tumor cells by directly destroying infected cells or activating cells that have the potential to remove them (Cohen and Cohen, 1996). CTX induces an immunocompromised state by reducing cytokine expression (Noh et al., 2019; Zhao et al., 2023). The decrease in cytokine expression in the CTX group in this study can be explained by the induction of immunocompromised state by CTX. Also, HemoHIM G increases the expression of these cytokines to enhance the immune capacity lowered by CTX, thereby improving immune function.

Effects of HemoHIM G on splenic lymphocyte proliferation

To evaluate the efficacy of HemoHIM G in normal mice, we treated the spleens of normal mice with HemoHIM G. HemoHIM G treatment significantly induced splenocyte proliferation relative to the CTL group (Fig. 6). Significant splenocyte proliferation was confirmed in all groups treated with HemoHIM G (p < 0.01; HG125; p < 0.001, HG250; p < 0.01, HG500). The ability of the spleen to induce lymphocyte proliferation is an important indicator of immune response in the body. Lymphocytes are important in adaptive immune responses, and lymphocyte proliferation experiments are frequently used to assess these responses (Han et al., 2018). Our findings suggest that HemoHIM G has the ability to improve immunity by activating the proliferation of splenic lymphocyte.

In conclusion, this study confirmed that HemoHIM G exhibited immune-enhancing effects by increasing the WBC count, NK cell activity, and cytokine levels in an immune-suppressed mouse model. In addition, HemoHIM G restores the white pulp density of splenocytes and increases the proliferative capacity of lymphocytes. The mechanism underlying this immune-improving effect is thought to be related to the innate immune system through the regulation of NK cell activity. Therefore, our findings suggest the potential of HemoHIM G as a promising food supplement to improve immunity. However, further studies are needed to identify the specific mechanism underlying the efficacy of HemoHIM G.

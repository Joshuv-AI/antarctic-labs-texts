Revealing the selectivity source of di-2-ethylhexyl-phosphoric acid for trivalent Gd, Tb, and Dy separation
Authors: Shengjie Yu, Youshi Lan, Wanle Liu, Guoshu Mao, Kun Huang, Yuanzhen Yue, Zhijun Song, Xuejie Sun, Xian Zhang, Shengdong Zhang, Youqian Ding
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The outstanding performance of the radioactive nuclide 161Tb in tumor treatment has made it another emerging candidate following 177Lu. Currently, it is mainly obtained through the irradiation of 160Gd, with a small amount of 161Dy as a side product. Therefore, the efficient separation of Gd/Tb/Dy is a key technology for achieving large-scale production. The acidic phosphorus extractant P204 is widely used for trivalent lanthanide ion separation and has demonstrated robust performance. However, we do not fully understand its coordination mechanism and selectivity source towards lanthanides until now, which severely restricts improvements to this technique. Therefore, we performed ab initio calculations to explore the coordination properties of P204 with Gd/Tb/Dy and conducted static resin adsorption and liquid-phase extraction separation experiments to contrast with calculation results. It is found that although both the 4f and 5d orbitals participate in the coordination, selectivity is mainly determined by bond interaction between 4f and phosphate oxygens. In addition, we discovered that the O–P–O group has a precise size recognition ability towards lanthanide ions, which allows it to adopt two different coordination modes when extracting light and heavy lanthanide ions. Our results provide a clear understanding of the lanthanide separation mechanism of P204 and have guiding significance to the optimization of trivalent lanthanide separation extractants.

Introduction

Lanthanides are essential in optical applications owing to their unique electronic structures;1–4 at the same time, they also play an important role in cancer therapy due to their excellent nuclear properties. In 2022, there were nearly 20 million new cancer cases globally and 9.7 million cancer deaths, making cancer one of the main causes of human mortality.5,6 The core advantage of radiopharmaceuticals lies in their targeted delivery of radiation, which not only specifically kills tumor cells expressing the target but also destroys distant lesions through multiple radiation effects. Combined with the theranostic model, it enables precise patient screening and efficient treatment with low toxicity.7161Tb is a highly promising novel medical isotope. While its nuclear properties are similar to the current star nuclide 177Lu, it additionally emits a large number of Auger and conversion electrons,8,9 which have a shorter range and higher linear energy transfer, possessing stronger tumor-killing potential.10–12 Meanwhile, the low-energy γ-rays released during its decay can be applied for tumor imaging. These excellent behaviours make 161Tb a wonderful therapeutic and diagnostic isotope.13–16

Medical 161Tb is mainly produced via a nuclear reaction 160Gd (n,γ) 161Gd → 161Tb → 161Dy,13 but it is concealed in the bulk Gd matrix with a small amount of Dy; thus, after the radiation reaction, further separation and purification are required. Extraction chromatography is an effective way to deal with this case when a proper resin,17–22 such as di-2-ethylhexyl-phosphoric acid (P204), is selected. Early in 1957, D. F. Peppard et al.23 carried out an experimental study on the trivalent lanthanide ion separation performance of P204 by radioactive tracer technology and determined the extraction species as Ln(P204)3. Later, in 1989, T. SATO24 conducted a similar work in hydrochloric acid solution and pointed out that the extraction species should be Ln(P204–H–P204)3; this study obtained separation factors of 1.6 and 1.4 for Tb/Gd and Dy/Tb of P204, respectively. In 2014, R. D. Abreu et al.25 redetermined that the separation factor of P204 for Tb/Dy is 2.5, which is higher than SATO's result. In 2015, F. Monroy-Guzman et al.26 tested the performance of P204 for Tb/Gd on resin, obtaining a 5–6 separation factor. Since it is challenging to study the structural properties of resin on the atomic level, very few publications have focused on the coordination mechanism of P204. X. Cao et al.27 used the hypersensitive transition spectroscopy identification technology to illustrate the coordination behavior of P204 to Er3+ and pointed out that P204 does not directly coordinate bidentately with Er3+ but form a dimer (P204D) by intramolecular hydrogen bonds for each pair of P204, resulting in the assembly of three P204D around the central metal ion with a six-coordinate octahedral geometry, which reflect SATO's view point. As can be seen, although acidic phosphorus extractants, like P204, possess reliable separation capabilities for trivalent lanthanide ions and have been extensively studied through experiments and applications, the specific coordination configuration between P204 and lanthanide ions and the mechanism behind their separation performance remain unclear.

In this work, we employ ab initio methods to investigate the coordination structure and separation performance of P204 towards Gd3+, Tb3+, and Dy3+, elucidating the source of its separation selectivity for lanthanide ions at the quantum level. Furthermore, we conducted static resin adsorption and liquid-phase extraction separation experiments to comprehensively examine the distribution ratio and separation coefficient of P204 for Gd3+, Tb3+, and Dy3+, corroborating the theoretical calculation results. Our research findings not only provide essential basic parameters for the separation and purification of medical isotopes using P204 but also elucidate the source of P204's selectivity for trivalent lanthanide-ion separation, offering guidance for the optimal design of extractants.

Methods

Ab initio calculations

Geometrical optimization and frequency analysis were performed using the B3LYP28,29 functional, as implemented in Gaussian16 (ref. 30) with D3BJ31 dispersion correction developed by Grimme to consider the long-range interaction. The triple-zeta basis set def2-TZVP32 developed by the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology for Turbomole was used for atoms in the first three periods, while the Stuttgart pseudopotential with the standard basis set SDD was used for atoms in the later four periods. For considering the hydration energy, the exact molecular structure of Ln(III) hydrate in required. The number of water molecules in the first coordination shell of trivalent lanthanide ions remain uncertain, but they are mainly located in a range of 8–10, with several theoretical and experimental evidences supported.33,34 After carefully checking, we find that the 9-hydrated structure is more stable for Gd3+, Tb3+ and Dy3+ under our calculation level, which is consistent with the hydration crystal structure reported in the Cambridge Crystallographic Data Centre (CCDC).35 Thus, the nonahydrate Ln(III) was adopted as the reference, and the binding energy (ΔE) and separation selectivity (K) between the extractant and metal ion were calculated using the following formulas:Here, Ecomplex·nH2O represents the energy of the complex, nH2O indicates the number of water molecules remaining in the complex, ELn(III)·9H2O represents the energy of the nona-hydrated lanthanide ion, Eextractant represents the energy of the extractant, E19H2O represents the energy of the 19-water cluster, and E18H2O represents the energy of the 18-water cluster.36–38 ΔE1 and ΔE2 represent the binding binding energy differences between the extractant and different ions. Static resin adsorption experiments

The static resin absorption experiment was also carried out in a 15 mL centrifuge tube, with 0.1 g (±0.3%) of P204 resin (BP 211D semi-micro analytical balance, Sartorius Co., Ltd) added to 20 mg L−1 Ln(NO3)3 solution and shaken for 20 h (25 °C, 250 rpm). After that, the mixture was filtered through a 0.45 µm pore size polytetrafluoroethylene filter. The concentrations of residual Gd3+, Tb3+, and Dy3+ in the filtrate were measured by ICP-OES (iCAP Pro, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Liquid–liquid extraction experiments

In the extraction experiments, kerosene was used as the diluent to prepare a 0.12 mol L−1 P204 solution. In this experiment, extraction was conducted by mixing 2.5 mL of 0.12 mol L−1 extractant solution with 2.5 mL of 20 mg L−1 Ln(NO3)3 solution in a 15 mL centrifuge tube with different acidities. The tube was further placed in a 25 °C 250 rpm oscillator for 6 h shaking to achieve extraction equilibrium. Results

Coordination configuration and theoretical performance

Guided by X. Cao's structural insights,27 we constructed analogous coordination models for Gd3+, Tb3+, and Dy3+ with P204 and further performed geometry optimizations and frequency analyses using density functional theory, revealing that these proposed configurations are energetically stable (Fig. 1). Binding energies are further calculated with the nona-hydrated Ln3+ ions [Ln(H2O)9]3+ used as reference states to account for the hydration effects.39,40 As summarized in Table 1, the binding affinity of P204 increases progressively from Gd to Dy, while the differences between adjacent lanthanides diminish, which is consistent with the lanthanide contraction regularity. An analysis of the electronic density of states (DOS) reveals that the total DOS profiles of the three complexes are remarkably similar, suggesting their analogous coordination interactions (Fig. 2). In partial DOS, s and p are mainly contributed from the P204 ligands, while d and f originate from the 4f and 5d orbitals of the Ln3+ ions. Notably, in the −0.6 to −0.2 a.u. region, there is an obvious overlap between s/p and d/f, indicating significant covalent interactions between the Ln3+ ions and P204, which can be strongly validated by bond orbitals displayed in the DOS diagram. By comparing the DOS of these three metal ions, we can find that the distributions of the d component for Gd3+, Tb3+ and Dy3+ are nearly identical, with the highest occupied states centered at −0.34 a.u., while the occupied f orbitals exhibit the peaks at −0.40, −0.43, and −0.44 a.u., respectively, correlating well with the trends in binding energy and selectivity variation. For confirming that the interaction between the 4f of Ln(III) and 2p of P204 is the key factor to the separation selectivity, we performed natural bonding orbital (NBO) analysis on the three complexes. As can be seen, although the electron populations in the 4f orbitals are very close to those of the pure ionic state (4f7, 4f8 and 4f9 for Gd3+, Tb3+ and Dy3+, respectively) due to the coordination effect, the actual total valence shell occupancies are 8.17, 9.15, and 10.14, corresponding to a similar extra gain of 1.17, 1.15, and 1.14 electrons, respectively. Nevertheless, the changes in the 4f orbital electron populations reveal the essential differences. The Gd 4f orbital, being in a relatively stable half-filled state, accepts only 0.03 electrons. In contrast, the Tb 4f orbital not only rejects electrons but also donates 0.54 electrons outward, and similarly, the Dy 4f orbital refuses to accept electrons and releases 0.47 electrons. We thus infer that the large separation selectivity between Tb3+ and Gd3+ originates from the fundamental difference in positive vs. negative electron feedback, while the weak separation selectivity between Dy3+ and Tb3+ arises from the gradual weakening of the negative feedback. The above analysis suggests that the selectivity of P204 for trivalent lanthanides originates from subtle differences in the coordination interaction contributed by f orbitals, which arise from the progressive electron filling of the 4f subshell. However, some unreasonable aspects do exist. The calculated selectivity of Tb/Gd can reach 1053.8, which deviates markedly from literature reports.

Static resin adsorption and liquid–liquid extraction

To check the aforementioned computational results, a static adsorption experiment to these three ions was conducted on P204. As shown in Fig. 3, the distribution ratios obtained were generally consistent with the binding energy trends predicted by theoretical calculations. However, a significant discrepancy emerged when comparing the separation factors: in the experiment, separation factor for Dy/Tb was about 2.8, which is close to our prediction, while the separation factor for Tb/Gd was around 5.6, markedly lower than the calculated value of 1053. Considering that the rigidity of the resin matrix, heterogeneity of the pore channels, and limited possibility of synergistic coordination may lead to deviations from the idealized coordination geometry assumed in the calculations, we further performed liquid–liquid extraction experiments. However, results from liquid–liquid extraction closely mirrored those from the resin adsorption experiments: although the separation factors in liquid–liquid extraction varied with acidity, the Dy/Tb separation factor remained around 3.0 and the Tb/Gd separation factor around 5.0 (Table 2). The absolute distribution ratios in liquid–liquid extraction were approximately two orders of magnitude lower than those in static resin adsorption; this should be primarily attributed to differences in the mass transfer efficiency across the phase interfaces.

Overview of the coordination structure and separation mechanism

The discrepancy between theoretical calculations and experiments prompted us to revisit the coordination configurations of Gd3+, Tb3+ and Dy3+ with P204. Therefore, we returned to the starting point of coordination configurations and considered the possible coordination configurations between the P204 molecule and [Ln(H2O)9]3+ (Fig. 5), which are the (a) monodentate coordination of undissociated P204 with Ln3+, (b) monodentate coordination of dissociated P204 with Ln3+, (c) bidentate coordination of dissociated P204 with Ln3+, and (d) bidentate coordination of two P204 molecules forming an internal hydrogen bond with Ln3+. The calculation results (Table 3) show that the coordination ability of undissociated P204 with Ln3+ is very weak, corresponding to extraction under high acidity conditions, while its coordination ability significantly increases upon dissociation, which explains the exponential change in distribution ratio with acidity, as observed in experiments. Surprisingly, the optimal coordination configurations differ for different ions. For Gd3+, the energy favourable structure is direct bidentate coordination with one P204 molecule (Table 3). For Tb3+ and Dy3+, the situation is completely different: direct bidentate coordination is even less effective than monodentate coordination, thus they preferentially coordinate bidentately with a P204 dimer formed by two P204 with an internal hydrogen bond. The reason for this distinct difference lies in the fact that the two coordinating oxygen atoms on P204 have large openings due to the long P–O bond, which directly leads to the precise size recognition ability of the O–P–O functional group. This ability enables P204 to adopt bidentate coordination for light lanthanides before Gd and internal hydrogen bonding dimer bidentate coordination for heavy lanthanides after Gd. This valuable capability makes P204 stand out and widely used among such resins. From Table 4, we can see that the calculated separation selectivity considering the two different coordination configurations well match the experimental results, in which Tb/Gd selectivity is 8.5 and Dy/Tb selectivity is 1.7, corresponding to the separation factors of 5–6 and 2–3 from experiments, respectively, indicating that our inference is correct. Moreover, we find that the selectivity for Tb/Gd increases from 2.6 to 8.5 when the coordination number increases, while selectivity for Dy/Tb drops from 3.0 to 1.7; thus an experimental separation factor is usually observed in these ranges. This phenomenon should also attribute to the different coordination configuration of P204 to Gd, Tb and Dy (Fig. 6).

Conclusions

In this work, we explored the coordination properties of acidic phosphonium extractant P204 with Gd3+, Tb3+, and Dy3+, through quantum mechanics calculations and separation experiments. It is found that both the 4f and 5d orbitals of lanthanides contribute to the coordination interaction, but the separation selectivity mainly comes from the different electron transfer behaviours of 4f orbitals. To our surprise, there were significant differences in the optimal coordination structures of P204 with Gd3+, Tb3+, and Dy3+. For Gd3+, it directly forms an Ln(P204)3 structure through a bidentate coordination, while for Tb3+ and Dy3+, it connects with another P204 by internal hydrogen bonds, becoming a dimer and finally forming an Ln(P204D)3 complex, which indicates that P204 is sensitive to ion size, making it capable to accurately distinguish light and heavy lanthanides and adopt two sets of coordination configurations in extraction. These findings provide us with a deep understanding to the mechanism of selective trivalent lanthanide separation by acidic phosphonium extractants and have guiding significance for the optimal design of extractant molecules.

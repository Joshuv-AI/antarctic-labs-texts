Statins for the prevention of exacerbations in COPD: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials
Authors: Guizuo Wang, Wenli Shang, Dong Han
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe role of statins in reduction of acute exacerbations in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) remains unclear. This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to determine the preventive effect of statins on exacerbations in COPD patients.MethodsA systematic search was conducted in PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and clinicaltrials.gov, without language restrictions. Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) on treatment of COPD with statins, compared with placebo, were reviewed. Estimated effects of included studies were pooled as risk ratios (RRs) and weighted mean differences (WMDs), with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). The certainty of evidence was assessed using the GRADE (Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development, and Evaluations) framework.ResultsThree RCTs (enrolling 1190 patients) met the inclusion criteria. Compared with placebo, the exacerbation related hospitalization rate (RR 0.87, 95% CI 0.77 to 0.97; GRADE = low) and severe exacerbation rate (RR 0.88, 95% CI 0.78 to 0.99; low) were significantly lower in statins groups. There was no statistically significant difference in moderate exacerbation rate (RR 0.71, 95% CI 0.48 to 1.04; very low), annualized exacerbation rate (WMD -0.21 per person-year, 95% CI -0.61 to 0.20; very low), and annualized moderate-to-severe exacerbation rate (WMD -0.21 per person-year, 95% CI -0.57 to 0.15; very low) between the two groups.ConclusionsIn the absence of single-inhaler triple therapy (SITT), statins were associated with a reduction in severe exacerbations and hospitalization rates of COPD. Given the limitations of the evidence we found, further large-scale, high-quality studies are needed to clarify the impact of statins on prognosis and the beneficiary population.Systematic review registrationPROSPERO CRD42025644971.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13643-026-03203-z.Keywords: Statins, COPD, Exacerbation, Hospitalization, Meta-analysis

Introduction

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a common chronic respiratory disease that causes a gradual worsening of symptoms [1]. Previous systematic reviews on the epidemiology of COPD have reported a global prevalence ranging from 7.6% in 2004 to 11.4% in 2014 [2, 3]. In 2019, COPD was the third leading cause of death worldwide based on the age-standardized mortality rate [1]. In 2019, there were 212.3 million prevalent cases of COPD reported globally, with COPD responsible for 3.3 million deaths and 74.4 million disability adjusted life years (DALYs) [1]. The global age-standardized point prevalence, mortality, and DALY rates for COPD were 2638.2, 42.5, and 926.1 per 100,000 population [1]. In 2010, COPD in the United States was estimated to incur direct costs of $29.5 billion and indirect costs of $20.4 billion [4] Moreover, COPD can affect the prognosis of other diseases, such as COVID-19, cancer, mental health disorders, cardiovascular diseases, gastrointestinal diseases, and musculoskeletal disorders [1]. COPD can lead to acute exacerbations, which are characterized by worsening respiratory symptoms and hypercapnic acute-on-chronic respiratory failure [5]. Acute exacerbations are associated with impaired quality of life, reduced lung function, increased hospitalization rates, higher healthcare costs, and elevated mortality [6–8]. The estimated cost per exacerbation is up to $7,757 [4]. Patients hospitalized for COPD exacerbation have a 6% risk of in-hospital mortality [5]. However, effective therapies for preventing exacerbations of COPD are limited [9].

Statins, inhibitor of 3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl-coenzyme A (HMG-CoA) reductase, are formerly used to reduce cholesterol levels in clinic [10]. Some observational studies and meta-analyses have shown that treatment with statins in COPD patients was safe and associated with improvement in lung function, reduction in exacerbations, and even decrease in mortality [11–13]. However, the conclusions of several subsequent randomized controlled trials (RCTs) regarding whether statins can reduce exacerbations of COPD are not consistent [14, 15].

Therefore, the aim of this study was to conduct a systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs to determine whether statins can prevent exacerbations in patients with COPD.

Methods

Data sources and search strategy

This systematic review and meta−analysis was based on the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta−Analyses (PRISMA) statement [16]. The protocol was previously registered in January 2025 in the PROSPERO database (Review register: CRD42025644971). The PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and clinicaltrials.gov were searched for studies up to March 20, 2025.

Study selection

To be eligible for inclusion in the meta-analysis studies had to meet the following criteria: (a) inclusion of COPD patients according to the guidelines of the Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) [9]; (b) use of a randomized controlled design to make a comparison of statins with placebo; and (c) follow-up for 12 weeks or longer to observe exacerbations. Studies were excluded if they enrolled patients with any of the following conditions: (a) current statins use; (b) statins therapy within the previous 4 weeks; (c) concomitant use of drugs contraindicated with statins; (d) prior established indication for statins use; and (e) statins allergy or hypersensitivity to statins. The search strings used for PubMed were (“statins” OR “atorvastatin” OR “rosuvastatin” OR “pravastatin” OR “simvastatin” OR “fluvastatin” OR “pitavastatin” OR “lovastatin”) AND (“COPD” OR “chronic obstructive pulmonary disease”). Bibliography of any relevant review articles were also screened to identify studies that might have been missed in this search. No language restrictions were applied to our study selection process. The full search strategies for all databases are provided in Table S1.

Data extraction and quality assessment

Two reviewers independently screened articles according to the inclusion criteria. The reviewers compared selected studies and differences were resolved by consensus. Data tables were used to collect all relevant data from texts, tables and figures of each included trial, including author, year of publication or last update posted, patient number and age, duration of follow-up, treatment category, baseline forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV1), smoking history, GOLD stage, and outcomes such as hospitalization, exacerbation, and annualized exacerbation rate.

Risk of bias of included trials

Two reviewers independently assessed the risk of bias using the Cochrane collaboration risk of bias tool for RCTs [18].

Data synthesis and statistical analysis

Meta-analyses were conducted where applicable; otherwise, outcomes were presented in narrative form. Data were analyzed using the RevMan Version 5.1 (The Cochrane Collaboration). Next, risk ratios (RRs) for discontinuous outcomes, and weighted mean differences (WMDs) for continuous outcomes, with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were computed for individual trials. Chi-squared and Higgins I2 tests were used to assess heterogeneity among included trials. Since fixed-effect models may lead to overly narrow CIs and severely inflated Type I error, we used a random-effects model [19, 20]. Statistical significance was defined as a P value < 0.05.

Certainty of evidence

The Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) method was used to grade the quality or certainty of the outcomes and the strength of recommendations [21]. The evidence certainty was classified as high, moderate, low, or very low.

Results

Study selection and characteristics

Of 4536 studies recognized by the initial search, 41 were retrieved for more detailed assessment, and 3 trials were included in this meta-analysis (Fig. 1) [14, 15, 22]. Baseline characteristics of trials included in this meta-analysis are shown in Table 2. Of the 3 included RCTs, 2 were multicenter [14, 22], and one was single-center [15]. All included RCTs were published between 2014 and 2021. The included trials had some differences, such as in sample size, follow-up duration, GOLD stage, and statins type. The number of participants ranged from 96 to 885, and the follow-up duration ranged from 3 to 36 months [14, 15, 22]. Two studies used simvastatin [14, 15], and the other used rosuvastatin [22]. One study included patients with COPD without predefined lung function criteria [22], and the other two studies enrolled patients with GOLD stage 2–4 [14, 15]. A total of 1190 patients were included: 585 assigned to the statins therapy groups and 605 to the placebo groups. The risk of bias results are summarized in Fig. 2.

Assessment on risk of bias for included RCTs

Hospitalization

Three RCTs reported data on hospitalizations related to exacerbations (1190 patients). The hospitalization rate was significantly lower in statins groups compared to placebo groups (RR 0.87, 95% CI 0.77 to 0.97; P = 0.02 (Fig. 3A)). There was no significant heterogeneity (I2 = 0%; P = 0.46). The exacerbation related hospitalization rate in statins groups was 43.76% compared with 50.58% in placebo groups.

Forest plot assessing the efficacy of statins versus placebo on A hospitalizations related to exacerbations, B severe exacerbations, C moderate exacerbations, D annualized exacerbation rate, and E annualized moderate-to-severe exacerbation rate

Severe exacerbations

Data on severe exacerbations were extracted from three RCTs (1190 patients). The severe exacerbation rate was significantly lower in statins groups (RR 0.88, 95% CI 0.78 to 0.99; P = 0.04 (Fig. 3B)), with a rate of 41.71% versus 47.44%. There was no significant heterogeneity (I2 = 0%; P = 0.44).

Moderate exacerbations

Data on moderate exacerbations were available from three trials (1190 patients). The moderate exacerbation rate did not differ significantly between the two groups (RR 0.71, 95% CI 0.48 to 1.04; P = 0.08 (Fig. 3C)). There was significant heterogeneity (I2 = 83%; P = 0.003). The moderate exacerbation rate in statins groups was 33.16% compared with 41.65% in placebo groups.

Annualized exacerbation rate

Two RCTs reported data on annualized COPD exacerbation rate (1094 patients). The annualized exacerbation rate did not differ significantly between the two groups (WMD −0.21 per person-year, 95% CI −0.61 to 0.20; P = 0.32 (Fig. 3D)). There was significant heterogeneity (I2 = 70%; P = 0.07).

Annualized moderate-to-severe exacerbation rate

Data on annualized moderate-to-severe exacerbation rate were extracted from two RCTs (1094 patients). The annualized moderate-to-severe exacerbation rate did not differ significantly between the two groups (WMD −0.21 per person-year, 95% CI −0.57 to 0.15; P = 0.25 (Fig. 3E)). There was significant heterogeneity (I2 = 71%; P = 0.06).

Certainty of evidence

The GRADE assessment for the certainty of evidence for outcomes is summarized in Table S2. GRADE assessment indicated low certainty for hospitalization, severe exacerbations, and very low certainty for moderate exacerbations, annualized exacerbation rate, and annualized moderate-to-severe exacerbation rate. The most common reasons for downgrading are significant inconsistency and imprecision.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this meta-analysis was the first designed specifically to evaluate RCTs that have explored the preventive effect of statins on exacerbations in patients with COPD. Based on the current results, we observed that the incidence of severe exacerbations and hospitalization rates were significantly lower in statins groups. This study has some differences from previous ones. Previous meta-analyses either focused on the changes in inflammatory markers [23], or lung function [23, 24], or included observational studies [13]. This study focused on clinically meaningful outcomes closely related to the management of COPD, including exacerbation-related hospitalization rate, severe exacerbation rate, moderate exacerbation rate, and annualized exacerbation rate. These outcome indicators are not only core metrics that comprehensively reflect the severity of COPD and the effectiveness of intervention measures but also closely linked to patients’ clinical prognosis, quality of life, and medical care burden. Focusing on these clinically relevant outcomes ensures that the findings directly address practical clinical needs and provide clear, actionable guidance for clinicians in developing individualized prevention and treatment strategies for patients with COPD. Of note, this meta-analysis included a relatively small number of studies, and there were substantial differences among the included trials in terms of sample size and follow-up duration. In addition, the study with the largest weight was terminated early for futility. These factors may influence the overall conclusions of the meta-analysis.

The GRADE assessment for the outcomes in this meta-analysis was rated as low and very low certainty. This downgrading was attributed to two key factors. First, imprecision was evident in the effect estimates. The confidence intervals for these outcomes either overlapped or were very close to the line of no effect, indicating that we cannot be confident regarding the direction or magnitude of the true treatment effect. Second, inconsistency was observed across the included trials. Substantial heterogeneity among individual studies reduced the reliability of the pooled results, as the statins type, sample size, and follow-up duration varied widely across trials. These findings highlight important limitations of the current evidence. While our meta-analysis provides a comprehensive overview of available data, the low and very low certainty of evidence for the outcomes suggests that clinical decisions should be made with caution. Future large-scale, well-designed trials with homogeneous populations, standardized outcome measures, and adequate sample sizes are warranted to improve the precision and reliability of the evidence.

COPD with its comorbidities is one of the five major causes of death worldwide [9]. COPD exacerbations have a significant impact on both individual patients and healthcare systems, and represent the largest contributor to the socioeconomic burden of COPD [4]. Exacerbations account for the largest proportion of COPD burden, estimated to cause 50–75% of direct COPD costs [25]. And the costs are related to the severity of exacerbations [4]. The largest component of the total costs of exacerbations was usually hospitalization [4, 26]. The average costs per episode of mild, moderate, and severe exacerbations were €86 ($150), €579 ($1,011), and €4,007 ($6,996) respectively [27]. The costs of severe exacerbations were 7 times and 47 times higher than those of mild and moderate exacerbations respectively [27]. In this study, statins significantly reduced severe exacerbations and hospitalizations by 12–13%, thus significantly alleviating the social and economic burden.

There has been a special attention in pleiotropic effects of statins in recent years [28]. Statins have considerable effects on oxidative stress and inflammation, and modulate immune responses at multiple levels [29]. Statins are thought to exert their pleiotropic effects through multiple pathways: adhesion molecules, cell migration, proliferation, endothelial function, matrix degradation, apoptosis, thrombosis, and a series of inflammatory mediators [30].

This study included trials that enrolled patients up to 2017, in which at least 54.79% of patients received multiple-inhaler triple therapy (MITT) [14, 15, 22]. In recent years, single-inhaler triple therapy (SITT) has gradually become the mainstream in COPD management, as SITT may be more convenient and effective [31–35]. The effect of statins when combined with SITT may differ from that with MITT, and further research is warranted.

Currently, only one ongoing clinical trial of statins for COPD is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (Identifier: NCT04789057) [36]. This is a randomized, multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial conducted across 12 pulmonary departments in Poland. It aims to evaluate atorvastatin’s efficacy in reducing inflammation and identifying potential biomarkers for personalized COPD treatment. This study was initiated on February 11, 2022, with an estimated completion date of May 31, 2027. A total of 460 participants will be enrolled to receive either atorvastatin 40 mg once daily or placebo. Primary outcomes included the rate of COPD exacerbations and time to first COPD exacerbation over 56 weeks. Secondary outcomes comprised changes in FEV1, health-related quality of life, inflammatory pathway gene expression, peripheral blood leukocyte count, as well as circulating levels of fibrinogen, interleukin-6, and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein.

This study met most of the methodological criteria recommended for systematic reviews and meta-analyses [37]. The statistical analysis in this study is rigorous and robust. It followed PRISMA and was registered in PROSPERO. A comprehensive database search with strict PICOS criteria was applied. Dual review, Cochrane bias assessment, and GRADE grading guaranteed rigor. A random-effects model was used to address heterogeneity, improving result robustness. However, some limitations need to be considered when interpreting the results of this study. Firstly, the number of included studies was small, which may have reduced the power of the results. Secondly, the type (simvastatin or rosuvastatin) and dose of statins varied between studies. Thirdly, the sample size and follow-up duration varied between studies. Fourthly, significant heterogeneity was observed in some outcomes. All these factors may affect the interpretation and robustness of the pooled estimates.

Conclusions

In the absence of SITT, statins were associated with a reduction in severe exacerbations and hospitalization rates in patients with COPD. Given the limitations of the evidence we found, further large-scale, high-quality studies are needed to clarify the impact of statins on prognosis and the beneficiary population.

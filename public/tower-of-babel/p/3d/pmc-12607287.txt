Results from the SCIENCE and Danish ASC trials using allogeneic mesenchymal stromal cells to treat ischemic heart failure patients
Authors: Nadia Chaaban, Jens Kastrup, Abbas Ali Qayyum
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and aimsAllogeneic mesenchymal stromal cell (ASC) therapy is a potential treatment option in patients with ischemic heart failure (HF). We aimed to investigate the effect of allogeneic Cardiology Stem Cell Center Adipose tissue derived mesenchymal Stromal Cell product (CSCC_ASC) by joining data from the international SCIENCE and the Danish ASC trial.MethodsData from two double-blinded placebo-controlled phase II studies including patients with same inclusion and exclusion criteria and identical endpoints were combined. Patients had left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) < 45% and New York Heart Association (NYHA) II-III without further treatment options.ResultsTwo hundred and fourteen patients were randomly assigned to receive CSCC_ASCs or placebo injections. There was no difference in baseline characteristics between groups.No significant differences from baseline to 6-month follow-up were detected between the intervention and placebo-group in left ventricular end-systolic volume, end-diastolic volume or LVEF (p = 0.973, p = 0.601, p = 0.152). The 6-min walking test, NYHA-classification and quality of life score were unchanged in both groups. The difference between groups in N-terminal pro B-type natriuretic peptide and C-reactive protein at 6 months was statistically significant (p = 0.045, p = 0.021).ConclusionsPost-hoc analysis demonstrated that a single intramyocardial CSCC_ASC injection in patients with chronic ischemic no-option HF did not improve cardiac function.KEYWORDS: Adipose tissue derived mesenchymal stromal cells, allogeneic cell therapy, heart failure, ischemic heart disease, mesenchymal stromal cell, randomized clinical trial, stem cell

Introduction

Ischemic heart disease (IHD) is still one of the leading causes of morbidity and mortality worldwide and is a consequence of a mismatch between oxygen supply and demand to the myocardium [1,2]. The underlying pathophysiology includes endothelial dysfunction and inflammation, which can potentially lead to progressive myocardial dysfunction and injury resulting in impaired cardiac function and heart failure (HF).

Despite advancements in pharmacological and interventional therapies, HF remains a major clinical and health care challenge. HF due to IHD accounts for approximately 65% in men and 48% in women and is a chronic progressive disease with a 5-years mortality rate of 50% [3]. Moreover, 1–2% of all hospitalizations in the western health care systems are due to HF causing a burden to the health care systems. It is estimated that healthcare systems annually spend around 25 000 euro per patient [3].

In HF patients with reduced ejection fraction, myocardial injury is a key result due to the inflammatory response, involving both the innate and adaptive immune systems, resulting in elevated biochemical markers, detectable in blood samples [4–6].

As a result of an aging population with a growing burden of comorbidities in addition to IHD, including renal failure, arterial hypertension, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, diabetes mellitus, metabolic syndromes etc. all characterized by chronic inflammation, these factors may contribute to disease progression [3,7]. Increasing evidence suggests that systemic inflammation may not only cause but also exacerbate cardiac dysfunction as well as promote pathological remodeling, thereby causing long-term structural and functional cardiac impairment [5,6,8,9].

Recently, mesenchymal stromal cells (MSCs) have emerged as a promising therapeutic therapy in cardiovascular medicine due to their regenerative capacity and potential for immunomodulatory effects [10–12]. Given the dual role of inflammation in both the cause and progression of HF, further investigation into the therapeutic mechanisms of MSCs is warranted. A subset of MSC is adipose-derived stromal cells (ASCs) that represents an advantageous source due to its less invasive collection process compared to bone marrow, reduced cost and availability in larger quantities [10,12,13].

Several studies have investigated the use of ASCs in cardiovascular therapy for HF-patients [14–17]. Previously, we have investigated the effect of intramyocardial injection of ASC in patients with ischemic HF in the SCIENCE and the Danish ASC trial [12,15,16]. In this paper, we aim to explore the effect of ASCs in a larger population by combining data from the SCIENCE and the Danish ASC trial with identical study design.

Patients & methods

Study design

This article presents the combined analysis of two separate studies, which have been merged to provide a more comprehensive evaluation of the effect of ASC therapy in patients with ischemic HF [15,16].

Both studies were investigator-initiated, randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blinded, phase II clinical trials and conducted almost simultaneously. The SCIENCE trial was conducted across six European countries; Austria, Denmark, Germany, Netherlands, Poland and Slovenia (EudraCT No: 2015-002929-19 and ClinicalTrial.gov: NCT02673164). In opposition, the Danish ASC trial was conducted with patients included in four different hospitals in the greater region of Copenhagen; Rigshospitalet, Bispebjerg hospital, Herlev hospital and Hvidovre hospital (EudraCT NO: 2015-001560-19 and ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT03092284). In this trial, patients were screened and followed at local cardiology departments, while the randomization and treatment were performed at Rigshospitalet, Denmark.

The Danish ASC trial was approved by the Danish National Committee on Health Research Ethics (No. 1,717,872) and the Danish Medicines Agency (No. 1,015,050,799). The SCIENCE trial was approved by the National Competent Authorities and Ethics Committees in the participating countries. Both trials were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Study population

The study enrolled patients aged between 30 and 80 years, diagnosed with chronic symptomatic ischemic HF. This condition was defined by an impaired left ventricle ejection fraction (LVEF) < 45%, measured by echocardiography and New York Heart Association (NYHA) class II-III. Furthermore, included patients were required to have elevated plasma N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) levels above 300 pg/mL or 35 pmol/L.

Patients were required to receive maximal tolerated doses of guideline-recommended medical therapy for HF, with no indication for additional medical treatment or invasive interventions, such as revascularization or mechanical circulatory support.

Overview of the study flow

Both studies screened in total 306 patients and randomized 214 patients (Figure 1). The patients were randomized in a blinded 2:1 ratio to receive injections of either 110 × 106 CSCC_ASCs in CryoStor or placebo (5 mL saline) injections. Follow-up assessments were conducted at 1-, 3-, 6-, and 12-months post-intervention [15,16]. The randomization process was performed at cell-manufacturing unit at Cardiology Stem Cell Centre (CSCC). The treatment blinded vials were delivered by CSCC but locally stored prior to treatment of patients.

Overview of the study.

In the SCIENCE trial, the Dutch Medical Research Ethics Committee (METC) requested an HLA antibody screening to be performed on all patients prior to randomization to allocate treatment to donors with no specific alloantibodies. Thus, in the Dutch population, leukocyte antigen (HLA) tissue matching was done between donor and participant if randomized to CSCC_ASC treatment, but no tissue type matching was performed in any other country [15].

Clinical teams performing the screening, treatment and follow-up of patients were blinded from participating in the treatment procedure to ensure unbiased outcome assessment [15–17].

Cell production

The CSCC_ASC vials were produced at the CSCC, Rigshospitalet University Hospital, Copenhagen, Denmark in a Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) facility (manufacturing no. 23,909 and tissue establishment no. 32,298, issued by Danish authorities). Danish Medicines Agency and Danish Patient Safety Authority inspect the facility every second year to ensure compliance with EU guidelines for GMP of Medicinal Products for Human Use (certificate of GMP compliance no. DK IMP 92,217) [16].

At Printzlau Private Hospital, Denmark, five healthy donors with no history of medications were included to donate ASCs by abdominal liposuction for the Danish study population [16]. For the European populations, there were 6 donors in total [17].

Consent forms were signed in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Donors were tested for HIV 1/2, hepatitis B and C, syphilis and Human T-cell lymphotropic virus I/II screening at 30 and 1 day prior to the liposuction procedure.

For culture expansion of ASCs, Quantum Cell Expansion System was used [18]. Cells from all bioreactor expansions were harvested and confirming absence of bacteria, fungi and endotoxins in the final cell products. Flow cytometry confirmed stable positive markers CD90, CD105 and CD73 as well as negative markers CD45 and HLA-DR. The viability of ASCs was > 80%.

The final batch of CSCC_ASC consisted of 110 million ASCs/5 mL in CryoStor CS10 (BioLife Solutions) [14,19,20]. Each vial consisted of ASCs from only one donor. Placebo vials were visually identical, and all vials were stored in nitrogen dry storage until use.

NOGA-guided injection

NOGA XP© mapping system was used in both studies to generate a 3D map of the left ventricle before injections into the myocardium (Biological Delivery System, Cordis, Johnson & Johnson, USA) [21,22]. Viable myocardial cells had a unipolar voltage > 12 mV, bipolar voltage > 2.5 mV and local linear shortening-LLS > 6%. Non-viable myocardium was defined as unipolar voltage < 6 mV, bipolar voltage < 1.5 mL, LLS < 4%. Border zones were defined by unipolar voltage 6–12 mV, bipolar voltage 1.5–2.5 mL, LLS 4%–6%. Injections were targeted to the border zone of the infarcted tissue (unipolar voltage > 6 mV).

Injections were performed using MYOSTAR© injection catheter (Biological Delivery System, Cordis, Johnson & Johnson, USA) [15,16]. Both study arms delivered 0.4 mL initially, followed by 0.3 mL doses. A total of 12–15 injections were performed into the myocardium. The injection catheter was positioned perpendicular to the ventricular wall, and an extrasystole was induced by the needle deployment before injection to ensure correct positioning.

Cardiac echocardiographic scans and analysis

Echocardiographic (ECHO) scans were performed at baseline and 6-months follow-up, according to a standardized instruction manual [15–17]. ECHO parameters, including left ventricle end systolic volume (LVESV), left ventricle end diastolic volume (LVEDV) and left ventricle ejection fraction (LVEF), were analyzed using Philips Intellispace software. Baseline and 6-months ECHO images in the SCIENCE trial were independently analyzed by two analyzers at the Stem Cell Imaging Core Lab, University Medical Centre Utrecht, Utrecht, The Netherlands, and at the Advanced Heart Failure and Transplantation Center, University Medical Center, Ljubljana, Slovenia with no access to each other’s analysis. If inter-observer variability exceeded ±2 standard deviations (SD) in LVESV and LVEDV, a third independent reviewer was contacted.

In the Danish ASC study, all images were stored on a central server and analyzed by two independent observers with no access to each other’s analyzed images. The data were transferred to an independent third person.

In both studies, mean values of the analyses were used for further statistical analyses.

Quality of life

Health-related quality of life was assessed using Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ), containing 23 items evaluating various aspects of HF health [23].

Physical limitation as well as symptom stability, frequency and burden combine to generate the Clinical Summary Score. The absolute values were compared over time in the groups and compared between groups.

Biochemical parameters

Blood samples from the patients were collected at baseline, and at 3-, 6- and 12-months follow-up, to monitor changes over time. Kidney function, inflammatory parameters and pro-BNP were analyzed.

Endpoints

The primary endpoint in SCIENCE and the Danish ASC trial was change in LVESV at 6 months follow-up after treatment, measured by echocardiography. Secondary endpoints included LVEDV, LVEF, NYHA class, KCCQ score, 6-min walk test and NT-proBNP levels. Additionally, CRP levels were assessed to evaluate inflammatory response to treatment.

The safety of the CSCC_ASC treatment was assessed by documenting the occurrence of serious adverse events (SAEs), as well as any suspected unrelated SAEs, at the 12-months follow-up.

A composite endpoint of death, HF hospitalization, implantation of cardiac resynchronization therapy device, episodes of ventricular tachycardia, or aborted sudden cardiac death 1, 2, and 3 years after treatment were also measured.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses are performed using R version 4.4.2 (“Pile of Leaves,” released 2024–10-31).

Given that only a small proportion of missing data was observed, only complete case analysis was applied, whereby individuals with missing values in any of relevant variables were excluded from respective analysis.

Paired t-tests are used for within-group comparisons and unpaired t-tests for between-group differences. The Mann–Whitney U-test is used for analyzing non-normally distributed data.

Fischer’s exact test and Chi-square tests were used to compare categorical variables as appropriate. Kaplan–Meier curves using log-rank test were used to analyze the incidence of combined cardiac endpoint. A two-sided p-value of < 0.05 is considered statistically significant [15,16].

The study exhibited well-balanced baseline characteristics with no significant differences observed in key demographic variables such as age and gender, nor in biochemical variables including NT-ProBNP, total cholesterol, creatinine, and CRP levels. No significant differences were observed between the two groups at baseline in echocardiographic data or functional capacity assessed via the 6-min walking test.

A significant difference was observed in blood pressure (systolic p = 0.005 and diastolic p = 0.016) and CCS class (p = 0.001), both were higher in the ASC group compared to the placebo group. A significantly higher percentage of patients in the placebo group had a history of myocardial infarction compared to the ASC group (94.3% vs 79.9%, p = 0.011).

In the SCIENCE trial, one patient had a small pericardial effusion, detected by ECHO, which was treated with diuretics. One patient developed ventricular tachycardia during movement of the catheter in the left ventricle, which was treated with direct current cardioversion, and the patient converted to sinus rhythm. During the follow-up period, three patients from the ASC-group and two from the placebo group died due to progression of HF. Furthermore, one patient in the ASC group was diagnosed with B-cell lymphoma 10 months after treatment, which was not considered to be related to the cell treatment.

In the Danish ASC study, 3 patients in total died during the follow-up period in the ASC group. One patient died 1 month post-treatment due to ventricular fibrillation. Another died 4 months post-treatment due to stroke, and the third patient died 8 months post-treatment due to pulmonary embolism.

For both study populations, there were no significant differences in the occurrence of each SAE between the groups.

The overall mean time to occurrence of combined cardiac adverse event during the 3-year follow-up period was 30 ± 2 months for the ASC group and 29 ± 2 months in the placebo group (p = 0.722) (Figure 2).

In the ASC or placebo-group, CRP did not significantly change from baseline to the 6-months follow-up (p = 0.197 and p = 0.053, respectively), or 12 months after treatment (p = 0.388 and p = 0.134, respectively). A significant difference in CRP change between the ASC and placebo-group was observed from baseline to 6-months follow-up (−2.58 ± 1.11, p = 0.021), whereas no significant difference was detected at the 12-months follow-up between groups (0.39 ± 2.38, p = 0.871).

Sub-analysis of patients divided into groups based on CRP did not result in any differences between groups in LVEDV, LVESV or LVEF.

NYHA classification, 6-MWT and Quality-of-life

A significant reduction in NYHA class was observed in both the ASC group and the placebo-group both from baseline to the 6-months follow-up (p = 0.002 and p < 0.001, respectively) and from baseline to the 12-months follow-up (p = 0.003 and p = 0.005, respectively). However, when comparing the two groups, no significant difference was found between baseline and the 6-months (p = 0.224) or at 12-months follow-up (p = 0.396) (Table 5).

The 6-min walk test did not change in the ASC group, but a significant change was observed in the placebo-group from baseline to 6-months follow-up (p = 0.495 and p = 0.012, respectively). There were no statistically significant changes at 12-months follow-up in the ASC or placebo-group (p = 0.120 and p = 0.136, respectively). No significant differences between the ASC and placebo-group were observed at 6-months or 12-months follow-up (p = 0.110 and p = 0.641, respectively) (Table 5).

Discussion

Autologous and allogenic ASC therapies have demonstrated safety and tolerability, with promising efficacy results [14,22,24]. In our analysis of combined data from two clinical trials with a total of 214 patients, the treatment with cryopreserved allogeneic CSCC_ASC cells, obtained from healthy donors and cultured in an automated closed bioreactor system, also demonstrated safety during the follow-up.

Present analysis showed that, the primary endpoint, LVESV, did not change significantly between cell and placebo treated groups. Furthermore, although LVEF increased from baseline to 6-months follow-up in both the intervention and placebo groups, the difference was not statistically significant between groups. This suggests that ASC therapy may not lead to adequate improvements in cardiac function in these chronic no-option patients.

Inflammation and biomarker response

Exploratory analyses were performed at different CRP thresholds (≥2 mg/L, ≥3 mg/L … up to ≥10 mg/L) to explore whether higher levels of inflammation were associated with changes in echocardiographic parameters. At none of these thresholds did CRP level modify the treatment effect, and no significant differences in echocardiographic parameters such as LVEF, LVESV or LVEDV were observed compared to the placebo group in these chronic no-option ischemic HF patients.

CSCC_ASC injections as treatment for HF has been tried in inflammatory environments, as HF often is associated with comorbidities such as diabetes, obesity, and metabolic syndrome, which contribute to chronic systemic inflammation [4]. Persistent inflammation, driven by elevated levels of proinflammatory factors and cytokines such as interleukin-1 and 6 and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), has shown to induce systolic and diastolic dysfunction and promote adverse cardiac remodeling and may hinder effectiveness of regenerative therapies [4]. In the present study, it was observed that NT-proBNP in the ASC group at 6-months follow-up increased transiently which could be a result of factors not controlled for in the study.

This is consistent with preclinical findings where intramyocardial injection of cryopreserved allogeneic ASCs induced an early immune response involving monocytes/macrophages and T-cells in rats with chronic ischemic cardiomyopathy [25]. Such immunological activation may contribute to myocardial stress responses, including elevated NT-proBNP.

These findings are in contrast with those from the DREAM-HF trial, which also investigated mesenchymal precursor cells (MPCs) in a population of 565 patients with ischemic and non-ischemic HF [11]. DREAM-HF trial demonstrated that a one-time intramyocardial injection of MPCs produced long-term clinical benefits, particularly in reducing mortality and major adverse cardiovascular events in those with elevated CRP ≥ 2 mg/L. However, the benefits in DREAM-HF became more apparent over an extended follow-up period, which raised the consideration that our 12-months follow-up period may have been insufficient in detecting the full therapeutic potential of ASC treatment. Since treatment with allogeneic ASCs are still in the beginning stages of clinical testing, extended and prolonged monitoring may be necessary to decent modulating immune responses after administered therapy.

The divergent findings between the present study and the DREAM-HF trial could reflect differences in cell source and their biology beside the included study population. The DREAM-HF trial used bone marrow-derived mesenchymal precursor cells (MPCs), whereas present study investigated ASCs. Although both cell-types belong to the mesenchymal stromal cell family, these cell populations may differ in their mode of action. However, bone-marrow derived stromal cells occur at a frequency of about 1 per 105 cells and require extensive culture expansion, but MPCs represent a more immature, highly proliferative subpopulation of bone marrow stromal cells, which could have a stronger immunomodulatory and angiogenic potential compared with ASCs [13,26]. By contrast, ASCs are more abundant and easily accessible, contributing to more clinically feasible treatment option, but may differ in immunomodulatory signaling pathways, and integration and survival after administration [13,27]. In DREAM-HF trial, 58% of patients had ischemic etiology in contrast to the current study. Interaction between ASCs and the resident cells to restore functional myocardium may not be possible in these chronic no-option ischemic HF patients. Furthermore, there were a difference in the number of cells delivered (150 million. MPCs and 110 million ASCs).

Limitations and future perspectives

A limitation of the present study is that assessment of diastolic function was not made, as the study protocol focused on the systolic indices. While LVEF, LVESV, and LVEDV are clinically relevant measures, evaluation of diastolic parameters could have provided a more comprehensive picture of cardiac function.

While CRP levels trended downward in the ASC group and increased in the placebo group from baseline to follow-up at 6 months, the lack of significant changes in CRP in the ASC group at 6-months follow-up suggested that the anti-inflammatory effects of a single ASC dose might not be enough. This might be secondary to the chronic inflammation that characterizes HF-patients which might lead to need for repetitive treatment or higher dose to induce a significant reduction in CRP-levels.

This leads to speculations regarding the possible effects in relation to the number of treatments with cells. Clinical data on repeated dosing are still emerging; however preclinical studies suggest that multiple doses of ASC may contribute to improved cardiac function compared to a single administration [28]. Moreover, early treatment in the disease development may also play a role.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the combined data from the SCIENCE and the Danish ASC trial did not find any effect of single intramyocardial injections of 100 × 106 CSCC_ASCs in chronic no-option ischemic HF patients. Our observations propose future studies to explore the effect of multiple ASC administrations and selection of patients with high inflammatory burden.

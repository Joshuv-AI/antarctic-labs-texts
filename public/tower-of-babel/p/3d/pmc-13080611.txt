Reply
Authors: Oleksandr Danylenko, Sonya Babu-Narayan, Wei Li
Published: 2026

We thank Dr Bouma and colleagues for their interest in our paper.1 We did indeed make the point that patients with congenital heart disease, as per guidelines, should be followed up by a specialist team at reasonable intervals based on their congenital heart disease lesions and the results of repair.

We do not disagree with guidelines suggesting that routine follow-up may not be needed for a small group of adult congenital heart disease (ACHD) patients. We support the European Society of Cardiology ACHD guidelines, which specify that patients with “completely normal findings” after sufficiently early atrial septal defect repair do not require routine follow-up. However, these normal findings should be demonstrated by those with sufficient expertise and accreditation in postrepair ACHD imaging evaluation, which was not the case here, and the patient's history reflects that earlier imaging and surgical repair were performed at another hospital.

Nevertheless, patients with so-called “simple” anatomical lesions may, from time to time, present with unexpected complications or complex physiology. Our practice would always default to believing the patient's history, and symptoms should trigger appropriate re-evaluation, even if patients have previously been told that they had “completely normal findings,” which in this case was misleading. Those working in specialist centers focused on high-volume ACHD care also have a duty to support colleagues in their specialist ACHD network or in local centers when escalation is required. In this case, healthcare costs might have been reduced had the patient been signposted to the specialist center earlier, as this would have avoided repetition of the same imaging tests.

Perhaps the most important message is that symptoms such as fatigue, desaturation, exercise intolerance, or palpitations are an important reason for patients—whether intentionally or unintentionally not under specialist (network or surgical center) ACHD care—to be referred promptly.

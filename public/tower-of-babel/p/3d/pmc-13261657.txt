Landscape of protein S-palmitoylation and its role in suppressing cytosolic translation under cold stress in rice
Authors: Jiada Huang, Zhengting Chen, Hao Hu, Xuelu Wei, Guiyu Xiao, Songming Li, Mingjie You, Xiangzhu Meng, Haiyan Fang, Xuan Ma, Xiaoyang Chen, Meng Yang, Jisen Zhang, Qiutao Xu
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

Protein S-palmitoylation is an evolutionarily conserved, reversible post-translational modification (PTM) that is essential for cellular signaling, metabolism, and immunity in animal cells (S. Mesquita et al., 2024). Although extensively studied in animals, where it modifies approximately 25% of the proteome, its roles in plants have only recently emerged as critical for growth and stress adaptation (Zhou et al., 2018; Liu et al., 2024a, 2024b; Li et al., 2024; Ye et al., 2024). However, global studies in crops remain scarce, leaving a significant gap in our understanding of its agricultural importance. To address this gap, we performed a proteome-wide analysis in Oryza sativa (rice) and identified 5310 S-palmitoylated proteins. We found that cold stress significantly enhances the S-palmitoylation of ribosomal proteins. Furthermore, we uncover a novel role for ribosomal protein S-palmitoylation in regulating cytosolic protein translation, providing mechanistic insight into its function during stress adaptation.

To identify S-palmitoylated proteins in rice, we first employed the acyl-biotin exchange (ABE) assay, a well-established method for detecting protein S-palmitoylation. Strong signals observed in immunoblots confirmed the presence of S-palmitoylated proteins in rice (Figure 1A). We next conducted a systematic analysis of S-palmitoylated proteins isolated from wild-type rice seedlings using mass spectrometry (Figure 1B and Supplemental Figure 1). In total, we identified 5466 proteins containing 9787 S-palmitoylation sites, a number that substantially exceeds previous reports of S-palmitoylation in plants (Hemsley et al., 2013; Srivastava et al., 2016; Kumar et al., 2022). Although immunoblots also detected faint signals in samples without hydroxylamine (HA) treatment (Figure 1A), suggesting potential false positives, we further analyzed HA-depleted samples using mass spectrometry and identified 298 S-palmitoylated proteins (335 sites). After removing these potential false positives, we refined the dataset to 5407 proteins with 9549 modification sites. Previous studies have also noted that proteins containing thioester linkages may represent additional false positives (Hemsley et al., 2013). To further improve data reliability, we removed these proteins and obtained a final dataset of 5310 proteins with 9330 S-palmitoylation sites (Supplemental Dataset 1) for subsequent analysis. To experimentally validate the rice S-palmitoylome, we employed the ABE assay to examine 11 candidate proteins, all of which were confirmed to undergo S-palmitoylation (Figure 1C and Supplemental Figure 2). Furthermore, we selected four target proteins—HSP70, RPS3A, RPS6, and ACTIN—and generated mutant forms in which all identified S-palmitoylated cysteine residues were substituted with serine, following a previous report (Ye et al., 2024). Compared with their wild-type counterparts, S-palmitoylation of the mutant proteins was reduced to below the detection limit (Supplemental Figure 3). Collectively, these findings validate the accuracy and reliability of our rice S-palmitoylome dataset. Analysis of the number of S-palmitoylation sites per protein revealed that a large fraction of the identified proteins contained two or more modification sites (Figure 1D and Supplemental Figure 4), highlighting the functional significance of S-palmitoylation. To investigate potential sequence motifs associated with S-palmitoylation, we analyzed the local sequence context surrounding the modification sites. Leucine, isoleucine, and asparagine were significantly enriched at the residue immediately upstream of the modification site (position 10), whereas proline, threonine, and aspartic acid were enriched at the residue immediately downstream (position 12) (Figure 1E). To characterize the potential functions of S-palmitoylation, we first examined the subcellular localization of S-palmitoylated proteins. The results showed that S-palmitoylated proteins were distributed across multiple cellular compartments, including the chloroplast, cytoplasm, nucleus, plasma membrane, and mitochondria (Figure 1F), supporting the diverse roles of S-palmitoylation in cellular functions. Further Gene Ontology (GO) analysis revealed significant enrichment in processes such as gene expression, amino acid metabolism, response to stimulus, RNA metabolic processes, translation, and stress responses (Figure 1G), suggesting that S-palmitoylation may play critical roles in various aspects of plant biology.

This analysis also showed that S-palmitoylated proteins were enriched in pathways associated with cold stress responses (Figure 1G). To investigate whether cold stress affects S-palmitoylation in rice, we performed a comparative palmitoylome analysis of wild-type plants under control conditions (25°C) and cold stress (4°C for 3 h) using mass spectrometry. To account for potential variations in protein abundance, the S-palmitoylome data were normalized against the corresponding proteome data. Two biological replicates were analyzed and showed strong reproducibility, with high Pearson correlations and consistent protein abundance patterns (Supplemental Figure 6), supporting the reliability of the dataset. Comparative analysis revealed that overall protein S-palmitoylation levels in rice seedlings under cold stress were significantly higher than those in plants under control（CK） conditions (Figure 1H). Differential analysis identified 417 hyper-S-palmitoylation sites (increased S-palmitoylation; fold change > 1.2, p < 0.05) and 229 hypo-S-palmitoylation sites (decreased S-palmitoylation; fold change > 1.2, p < 0.05) in rice plants under cold stress (Figure 1I; Supplemental Dataset 2). Analysis of S-palmitoylation levels in five selected target proteins under CK and cold stress conditions verified the dynamic changes observed in the proteomic data (Supplemental Figure 7). GO analysis further showed that hyper-S-palmitoylated proteins were most strongly enriched in gene expression and translation pathways (Figure 1J), suggesting a role in protein synthesis during cold stress. Based on these results, we focused on ribosomal proteins. In total, 93 ribosomal proteins (129 sites; Supplemental Dataset 3) were identified as S-palmitoylated under normal conditions, and this modification was experimentally validated (Figure 1K). Cross-species comparisons (Blanc et al., 2019; Kumar et al., 2022; Duan et al., 2024) indicated that S-palmitoylation of ribosomal proteins is conserved in eukaryotes (Supplemental Figure 8). Importantly, cold stress significantly increased S-palmitoylation of ribosomal proteins, with 46 sites showing elevated modification, including 11 with statistically significant increases (Figures 1L and 1M). Immunoblot analysis of total ribosomes and RPL3 further confirmed enhanced S-palmitoylation under cold stress (Figure 1N).

Previous studies indicate that PTMs of ribosomal proteins, such as acetylation, can affect translation (Xu et al., 2021). We therefore hypothesizedthat S-palmitoylation may also play a role in translational regulation. Polysome profiling revealed that cold stress reduced ribosome fractions, suggesting that hyper-S-palmitoylation may attenuate translation (Supplemental Figure 9A). To further assess the regulatory role of ribosomal protein S-palmitoylation in translation, we inhibited palmitoylation using 2-bromopalmitate (2-BP). Treatment of rice seedlings with 2-BP markedly reduced global protein S-palmitoylation (Supplemental Figure 9B), including that of ribosomal proteins (Supplemental Figure 10A). Polysome profiling further revealed that reduced palmitoylation promoted the accumulation of ribosome fractions (Supplemental Figure 10B), indicating enhanced translational activity. In parallel, we examined a PROTEIN S-ACYLTRANSFERASE 13 (OsDHHC13) mutant, pal13, whose gene expression was strongly induced under cold stress (Supplemental Figure 9C) and which exhibited decreased S-palmitoylation of both total and ribosomal proteins (Supplemental Figures 9B and 10A). Consistent with the 2-BP results, polysome profiling revealed increased accumulation of ribosome fractions in pal13 (Supplemental Figure 10B). Collectively, these results suggest that hyper-S-palmitoylation of ribosomal proteins suppresses translation, whereas reduced palmitoylation promotes ribosome assembly and enhances translational activity.

To obtain a genome-wide view of the impact of ribosomal protein S-palmitoylation on translational control under cold stress, we performed ribosome profiling sequencing (Ribo-seq) and RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) (Supplemental Figures 11 and 12A). Data quality analysis revealed that Ribo-seq reads peaked at 26–28 nt (Supplemental Figure 12B), consistent with previous reports (Xu et al., 2021). Furthermore, more than 88% of the reads mapped to coding DNA sequences (Supplemental Figures 12C and 12D), and strong triplet periodicity was observed (Supplemental Figure 12E). Collectively, these results indicate that the Ribo-seq datasets are of high quality. Statistical analysis revealed that cold stress resulted in 2203 mRNAs (fold change > 2, p < 0.05; Supplemental Figure 13A) with significantly decreased translation, more than double the number of mRNAs (n = 1098; fold change > 2, p < 0.05; Supplemental Figure 13A) that exhibited increased translation. This finding further supports the notion that S-palmitoylation of ribosomal proteins inhibits cytosolic translation. Because translation dynamics may also be influenced by transcription, we examined the relationship between translation and transcription and observed only a weak positive correlation (Supplemental Figure 13B). To eliminate this potential bias, we normalized ribosome-binding data (Ribo-seq) to mRNA abundance (RNA-seq) and calculated translational efficiency (TE). A total of 2439 transcripts showed significantly decreased TE (fold change > 2, p < 0.05), whereas only 1490 transcripts exhibited significantly increased TE (fold change > 2, p < 0.05) under cold stress (Figure 1O; Supplemental Dataset 4). Transcripts with reduced TE were associated with the most highly expressed genes in the genome (Supplemental Figure 13C). Their 5′ UTR lengths were comparable to the genomic average (Supplemental Figure 13D) and were enriched in CCT-rich sequences (Supplemental Figure 13E). GO enrichment analysis revealed that these genes were primarily involved in key processes such as metabolic pathways (including carbohydrate metabolism, nucleic acid metabolism, and amino sugar metabolism), cell division, and developmental processes (including post-embryonic development and regulation of organ growth) (Supplemental Figure 13F). Collectively, these results suggest that cold stress-induced S-palmitoylation of ribosomal proteins may reduce the translation of highly expressed genes with CCT-rich 5′ UTRs, thereby minimizing energy consumption and enhancing plant survival under cold stress.

To further validate the Ribo-seq results, we compared proteomic data from CK and cold-stressed samples. The analysis revealed an overall decrease in protein abundance under cold stress (Figure 1P). Specifically, 452 proteins (fold change > 1.2, p < 0.05) exhibited decreased abundance, whereas only 98 proteins (fold change > 1.2, p < 0.05) showed increased levels (Figure 1Q; Supplemental Dataset 5). Moreover, the abundance of proteins encoded by 2439 mRNAs with decreased TE was significantly reduced in cold-stressed plants compared with CK plants (Figure 1R), further supporting the hypothesis that increased S-palmitoylation of ribosomal proteins impairs translation. Immunoblot analysis of tubulin and chlorophyll a/b-binding proteins, both of which showed markedly reduced levels under cold stress, further corroborated this conclusion (Figure 1S).

In this study, we present the first comprehensive S-palmitoylome of rice, identifying 5310 S-palmitoylated proteins. Using the latest Orbitrap Astral mass spectrometer, we detected a palmitoylation rate of 9.4% (S-palmitoylated proteins/total proteins)—markedly higher than those reported for Arabidopsis (2.5%–8.1%) and Populus (1.3%). Our data indicate that cold stress induces hyper-S-palmitoylation of ribosomal proteins, which is associated with impaired ribosome assembly and reduced cytosolic translation. We propose that increased S-palmitoylation promotes the association of ribosomal proteins with the endoplasmic reticulum or other intracellular membranes, thereby limiting their availability in the cytosol for ribosome assembly. Such translational attenuation may represent an adaptive mechanism that conserves energy and reallocates resources toward stress-responsive pathways under cold conditions. Collectively, this dataset substantially expands the known landscape of plant PTMs and provides a valuable resource for further exploration of the roles of S-palmitoylation in plant biology.

Oral hygiene: “The cornerstone of peri-implant health”
Authors: Baljeet Singh
Published: 2026

As India celebrates National Oral Hygiene Day, this August 1st, it is timely to reflect upon a paradigm shift in contemporary dentistry: The success of dental implants in the oral cavity depends solely not only on the phenomenon of osseointegration but equally on the long-term maintenance and preservation of peri-implant tissue health.

In the present era, when implant therapy has become a routine procedure, maintaining the peri-implant tissue health has probably emerged as one of the foremost challenges for dentists in general and periodontists in particular.

Dental implants have revolutionised the process of oral rehabilitation, giving smiles and restoring the masticatory functions of millions, worldwide. However, dental implants are not immune to disease. Studies have shown that more than 50% of the patients receiving dental implants were affected by Peri-Implant diseases (PID’s), when followed up for a period of 10 years. Further, it is estimated that one in five individuals treated with implants shall present with signs of Peri-implantitis (PI) over a 20 year follow up period.[1] The increasing prevalence of peri-implant mucositis (PM) and peri-implantitis has highlighted a truth which cannot be denied: an implant can only be as healthy as its oral environment, particularly the biofilm surrounding it.

There is substantial evidence indicating towards the fact that microbial biofilms play the core role in the progression and development of PID’s.[2] It is considered that similar to the periodontal diseases around the natural teeth, inflammation in the peri-implant tissues is also primarily initiated by the microbial plaque. Contemporary evidence points towards the fact that inadequate plaque control remains among the strongest modifiable risk factors for peri-implant disease development and progression. Prevention, therefore, is not merely preferable-it is indispensable.

The recent international consensus reports have reinforced the fact that Supportive Peri-implant Care (SPIC) and meticulous oral hygiene are the lifeline for the long-term success of dental implants. The 2024 joint consensus report of the American Academy of Periodontology and the Academy of Osseointegration have emphasized that management of PID’s must prioritize risk factor control, plaque reduction, and the maintenance therapy as the cornerstone of implant care.[3]

The European Federation of Periodontology’s evidence-based S3 guideline also recommend that the prevention of peri-implant diseases should follow the principles of primordial prevention, i.e., the thought process of prevention of PID’s should begin even before the implant is placed and should continue throughout the life of the implant by SPIC. These recommendations highlight the significance of oral health education, behavioural modification, and regular professional maintenance to preserve the health of peri-implant tissues.[4]

Contemporary literature underscores the need of a structured SPIC program for each implant patient. We must understand that the significance of oral hygiene extends beyond the mechanical plaque removal. The SPIC programs should include patient motivation, tailor-made and individualized home care regimens, regular professional debridement and elimination of plaque-retentive factors.[5]

As periodontists and oral healthcare professionals, we must remember that our implant therapy does not end with the delivery of prosthesis - “It begins there.” In my personal opinion, the very future of implant dentistry lies not only in surgical innovations and advanced biomaterials but also in fostering and promoting the culture of preventive implant healthcare.

The forthcoming “National Oral Hygiene Day” serves as a reminder for all of us that every toothbrush stroke contributes not merely to preserving the teeth but also to improve the longevity of implants in a healthy state. In the quest for enduring implant success, oral hygiene remains the simplest, most economical, and most effective intervention available to professionals and the patients alike.

We as periodontists must believe in and propagate the message to dentists in general that “Healthy Implants begin with healthy habits. Oral Hygiene is not an adjunct to implant care - it is the FOUNDATION.”

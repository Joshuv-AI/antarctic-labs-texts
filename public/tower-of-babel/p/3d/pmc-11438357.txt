Nano-selenium supplementation: improving growth, digestibility and mineral absorption in freshwater fish, Catla catla
Authors: Nisar Ahmad, Syed Makhdoom Hussain, Shafaqat Ali, Muhammad Farrukh Tahir, Pallab K. Sarker, Mudassar Shahid
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis research investigated the impact of selenium-nanoparticles (Se-NPs) supplemented in sunflower meal (SM)-based diets on digestibility, mineral absorption, and growth performance of Catla catla. Se-NPs were added to seven test meals in varying amounts (0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5 and 3 mgkg− 1). A total of 315 fish were evenly allocated three replicate tanks, with 15 fish per tank, and given diets equivalent to (5%) of their wet biomass daily for a period of 90 days.ResultsThe test diet (T-D) supplemented with 1.5 mgkg− 1 Se-NPs yielded remarkable results, including a significant increase in mean weight gain (14 g), an impressive average weight gain percentage (208%), a 100% survival rate, with no mortality observed and the highest specific growth rate (1.25). Moreover, 1.5 mgkg− 1 Se-NPs (T-D-IV) demonstrated superior nutritional digestibility (CP, 76%; CF, 79% and GE, 74%). Additionally, the diet 1.5 mgkg− 1 Se-NPs showed enhanced mineral absorption (K, P, Ca, Na, Zn, Cu and Fe) than other diets.ConclusionIt was practically proven that the growth indices, nutrient absorption, and mineral status of C. catla were significantly improved by Se-NPs supplemented SM-based diets, with the optimum level of supplementation being 1.5 mgkg− 1.

Introduction

Globally, the aquaculture industry, particularly in fish production, can help ensure a sustainable supply of high-quality animal-based protein in meeting the needs of a rapidly expanding human population in the future [1]. In order to obtain high-quality protein, fish must receive high-quality nutrition. Aquaculture seeks to develop new solutions to increase production by modifying fish feed formulations and assessing valuable additives. Recently, nanotechnology has been introduced in aquaculture, since it may have the power to solve many issues for aquatic species. Nano-sized materials are more easily absorbed than bigger ones; they promote immune development and make organisms more resistant to ailments [2, 3]. Another very important feature in NPs is high surface activity which ultimately leads to absorption efficiency in a very novel way [4]. Selenium (Se), a trace element, not only improves various animal health parameters and productivity, but it has also been demonstrated that Se-NPs enhanced the immune response, antioxidant status, and tissue health of aquatic organisms, resulting in improved disease resistance and reducing the likelihood of getting pathogenic infection and genotoxicity [5]. This micronutrient is necessary for healthy growth and development of organisms; it strengthens the immune system against pathogenic attacks and oxidative stress [6]. Supplements containing Se are available in various forms, including inorganic and organic compounds [7]. The utilization of organic and inorganic Se supplements in fish feed has been extensively investigated, with research revealing distinct differences in their absorption, metabolic processing, and distribution patterns within the body [8]. Furthermore, a novel form of Se, known as nano-Se, has recently gained attention for its distinct properties, including its highest bioavailability which is is due to its diminutive size and exceptionally high surface-to-volume ratio [9].

The world is projected to encounter a significant obstacle in the near future: the overpopulation of human beings. Unfortunately, many underdeveloped countries, despite struggling with poverty and lacking a plan to provide quality food for their growing populations, are not adequately addressing this issue. This scenario has the potential to become alarming. Therefore, it is imperative that we address this issue urgently. Aquaculture can be adopted as a valuable tool to mitigate this crisis. Although significant advancements have been made in this industry, several challenges remain to be addressed [10]. Feed costs account for more than half of a fish farm’s overall running cost [11] poses a significant burden for farmers. When comparing different protein sources, fish meal is a viable option, but its high cost is a major drawback [12]. Researchers emphasize the need for low-cost fish feed sources [13]. This is one of the primary reasons why alternative protein sources, such as sunflower meal (SM), have been recommended for use in fish feeds. SM contains 45–48% crude protein contet and is an environment friendly plant byproduct [14]. Previous research has demonstrated its excellent qualities, making it a suitable ingredient for use in fish feed formulations [15].

SM is commonly used as feed supplementation for Indian major carps. Market demand of C. catla is increasing locally due to its flesh quality and taste development and it contributes as a member of Indian major carps [16]. C. catla is one of the best aquaculture species to cultivate commercially [17]. This study aimed to supplement Se-NPs in SM-based diets to assess their impacts on C. catla. The present research also examined the growth, digestibility, and mineral absorption parameters in C. catla under dietary exposure to Se-NPs.

Materials and methods

C. catla were collected from the local Fish Seed Hatchery, Faisalabad, Pakistan, and transferred with the help of specialized transport tanks to the Fish Nutrition Laboratory at GC University and then the basal diet was administered once a day and acclimatized in cement tanks for 15 days [18, 19]. The capillary aeration system ensured adequate oxygenation, while water quality parameters were strictly maintained within the following ranges: pH (7.6–8.5), temperature (26–28 °C), and dissolved oxygen (4–6 mgL-1). As a prophylactic measure, a 5% NaCl solution was applied to the fish to reduce the risk of infection [20].

Characterization of NPs

Se-NPs were taken from the marketplace and their size and pure crystalline structure were confirmed employing transmission electron microscopy and X-ray diffraction (XRD), respectively. Additionally, feed components were examined using required research methodologies [21].

Preparation of stock solution and feed formulation

Ingredients for feed were pulverised to a size of 0.5 mm. After combining all the ingredients of the feed for around five minutes, fish oil was added gradually. To produce dough, a little water is gradually added. Pellets were created utilizing a pelleting machine [22]. Instructions for preparing NPs stock solutions were followed by Federici et al. [21]. A 6-8 hour sonication process was employed to produce a stock solution of Se-NPs with 100% purity, which was subsequently diluted (0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, and 3 mgkg− 1). Following sonication of 12.5 mg Se-NPs in 50 ml purified water, the resulting solution contained 0.25 mg Se-NPs per ml. Before being sprayed on baseline diets, Se-NPs solutions were sonicated for fifteen minutes. One kilogram of feed was placed in a mixing device, and produced dilutions were progressively sprayed over it. Seven test meals were made by combining Se-NPs levels of 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5 and 3 mgkg− 1. The pellets were oven-dried for a period of 24 h at 105 °C.

Feeding protocol and sample collection

For a period of 90 days, the recommended seven diets were provided to the fish equivalent to 5% of their total wet biomass per day, with one serving as the control and the others administered with 0.5-3 mgkg− 1 NPs (Tables 1 and 2). The diets were provided to each replicate (triplicated) consisted of 15 fingerlings (N = 315), which were kept in specially designed V-shaped steel tanks to facilitate efficient feces collection. Feces were carefully collected into fecal collection tubes to minimize nutrient leaching [23]. The 90-day growth and digestibility trials were simultaneously conducted.

Chemical examination of feed, feces and determination of minerals

Chemical examination of the diets and feces was conducted by implying techniques of [18]. Feces and feed samples were dissolved using established methods in a 1:2 solution of boiling nitric acid perchloric acid. Following the proper dilution, mineral concentrations were assessed using an atomic absorption spectrometer. For the purpose of developing calibrated standards for mineral estimation, commercially applicable conditions were employed. Using a flame photometer, the concentrations of potassium and sodium had been established. Ammonium molybdate was implied as a reagent and Phosphorus (P) content was determined colorimetrically with absorbance measured at 720 nm [18]. Using a spectrophotometer, the chromic oxide in feed and faeces was quantified at an absorbance of 370 nm after oxidation with molybdate reagent [24].

Growth study

Growth assessment were determined by using standard formulae as determined by [25].

Calculation of digestibility

The standard approach was implied to compute the apparent nutritional and mineral digestibility of the experimental diets [24, 25].

Statistical analysis

Data of different indices, growth, nutritional digestibility, and mineral absorption were analysed using ANOVA [26]. The notable differences between various experimental levels using Tukey’s Honesty Significant Difference Test were investigated (p < 0.05) [27]. The CoStat software, version 6.303, was utilized to perform statistical analyses.

Results

Se-NPs characterization

The characterization (Fig. 1) and precise size and form of Se-NPs (Fig. 1a and b) was observed by TEM. The spheric form of Se-NPs with an approximate homogeneous structural format and a diameter of 8–10 nm is explained by the image acquired from TEM.

(a) Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) view of selenium nanospheres at 10 nm (b) 50 nm (magnified form)

The XRD approach also displays the crystalline structure of Se-NPs. With many peaks and a multi-crystalline structure, the powdered material’s XRD revealed Se at 2θ = 23.50°, 26.93°, 29.50°, 53.03°, and 54.25°. The crystalline size of Se-NPs was in between 35 and 50 nm, based on the Debye–Scherer formula (Fig. 2).

Growth performance

Overall, growth parameters were optimal at a supplementation level of 1.5 mgkg− 1 Se-NPs (Fig. 3). Although these values started to decrease towards control diet but the lowest weight gain (10 g), weight gain (%) (151%) and SGR (1.02) were noted at 3 mgkg− 1 Se-NPs level. Nutrient digestibility

The data of nutrients digestibility is given in (Fig. 5) which shows that best digestibility quantities of CP (Crude protein 76%), CF (Crude fiber 79%) and GE (Gross energy 74%) were found at 1.5 mgkg− 1 of Se-NPs level. The fish given 3 mgkg− 1 Se-NPs (T-D-VII) had the lower most results (CP: 52%, EE: 54%, and GE: 46%).

Mineral absorption

During trial, highest mineral’s discharge was observed when fingerlings were exposed to control diet. With an increase in Se-NPs supplementation up to the level of 1.5 mgkg− 1 mineral discharge in water was decreased (Table 3). With more increase in Se-NPs supplementation up to the level of 3 mgkg− 1, increased minerals discharge was observed also. Minerals discharge (K, Ca, P, Na, Zn, Fe and Cu) was detected lowest at 1.5 mgkg− 1 diets. Lowest Mn and Cr discharge at 2 mgkg− 1 of Se-NPs level whereas Mg excretion through feces was measured most minimum in the fingerlings exposed to diet with 0.5 mgkg− 1 supplementation of Se-NPs. These figures were lesser (p < 0.05) than the control group.

Discussion

The nano-product sector has transformed the aquaculture industry by significantly improving the quality and quantity of products [1]. Notably, nano-products (NPs) have emerged as exceptional fish feed additives, capable of augmenting immune function and overall well-being [3]. One of the NPs, Se-NPs, holds great significance due to their proven effectiveness in promoting fish health. Their efficiency can be attributed to their nano size, which offers a large surface-to-volume ratio [5]. This study investigates the impact of varying concentrations of Se-NPs on growth indices, mineral absorption, and nutrient digestibility in fingerlings (C. catla).

Many researchers have employed NPs in their study to promote fish growth. NPs promote fish growth by increasing the absorption of nutrients into the bloodstream, leading to improved circulation [4]. Our study has demonstrated that best growth values were detected in the fingerlings fed at 1.5 mgkg− 1 of Se-NPs supplementation level [28]. found that feeding Macrobrachium rosenbergii post-larvae with iron oxide NPs increased growth parameters. Same pattern was detected in Labeo rohita fed with Fe-oxide-NPs added diets and in M. rosenbergii exposed to Zn and Cu-NPs [29, 30]. Se’s ability to enhance feed utilization in fish can be attributed to its role in maintaining intestinal integrity, which is achieved by increasing the intestinal surface area and promoting the secretion of brush border enzymes, ultimately facilitating efficient nutrient absorption [11, 31].

The effective breakdown and uptake of nutrients in food is crucial for maintaining optimal health in organisms [2]. Our investigation has demonstrated that the inclusion of Se-NPs to diets at a level of 1.5 mgkg− 1 significantly enhanced nutrient digestibility and uptake in C. catla fingerlings in contrast to the control group. The formation of a halo (corona) by soluble proteins is the primary mechanism underlying the effectiveness of NPs in enhancing nutrient digestion in carps [32]. Se-NPs supplementation led to superior feed efficiency and elongated intestinal villi length, possibly resulting from improved intestinal integrity [31]. NPs inclusion in diets improve the functionality of gut in rats [2]. Contrasting results have been observed across various animal species, which may be attributed to differences in experimental design, such as the species used (e.g., goats), the type of NPs (e.g., Zn), or varying levels of NP exposure. In the aforementioned cases, nanoparticles either failed to exhibit beneficial effects or elicited adverse responses, ultimately leading to toxicity [33–35] [14]. observed the toxic effects in juvenile fish, Epinephelus coioides, exposed to Cu-NPs. Almost all the enzymes supporting digestion were negatively affected, ultimately leading to stunted growth. In contrast, parallel studies in rats showed improved growth characteristics [36].

The present study have proved that addition of Se-NPs to diets increases the uptake of minerals by C. catla fingerlings leading to improved growth [37]. find that gastrointestinal tract favours higher absorption of NPs due to reduced dimensions and size which in turn increses mineral avalibility. The minute-sized pores of blood capillaries allow for easier penetration of NPs compared to larger particles. Nano-technology is being used for all kinds of minerals and vitamins, with main goal of their increasing bioavailability [38]. Furthermore, it was also observed that stomach wall absorbs NPs more quickly than other minerals and NPs support endothelial cells in cell culture [2].

There are a number of reasons and ways by which NPs show their efficiency regarding minerals absorption, nutrient digestibility and growth but in carps formation of halo (crona) by soluble proteins is very important. Thus, protein folding can be interfered and protein cross-linking can be improved by nano-protein cronas [32].

Conclusion

The aquaculture sector has recently been significantly impacted by nanotechnology-based feed additives. Accordingly, the current findings showed that the addition of dietary Se-NPs significantly increased the growth indices, nutritional digestibility, and mineral absorption of C. catla. In summary, the optimal levels of Se-NPs for C. catla growth, digestibility, and mineral utilization were found at 1.5 mgkg− 1. However, fish health was negatively impacted at higher concentrations. Therefore, Se-NPs should be utilized in the future to address Se-related issues in aquaculture, improving its affordability, environmental sustainability, and health benefits for human consumption.

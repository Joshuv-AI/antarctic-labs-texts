Minimally Invasive, Maximally Effective: Outpatient Strategies for Paediatric Pilonidal Disease
Authors: Collette Teresa Donnelly, Noora Al Shahwani, Noriza Que, Gerald James Lopez Roxas, Melanie Aquino, Biji Oommen, Azka Ahmad, Maraeh Mancha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPilonidal sinus disease (PNS) in children and adolescents lacks standardised management pathways. Minimally invasive and outpatient‐based strategies are increasingly adopted, but paediatric‐specific data remain limited. This study evaluated outcomes following implementation of a structured, tiered outpatient pathway. A retrospective single‐centre cohort study was conducted including patients aged ≤ 18 years treated for PNS between February 2023 and August 2024. Management followed a stepwise protocol: structured conservative care, in‐clinic debridement and operative intervention (trephination or limited excision) for refractory or severe disease. Primary outcome was recurrence after documented healing. Secondary outcomes included time to healing, clinic utilisation and associations with clinical variables. 69 patients were included (median age 15 years [IQR 14–16]; 64% male). Twenty‐three patients (33.3%) required operative management. Recurrence occurred in 6/23 (26.1%) in the operative group and 1/46 (2.2%) in the non‐operative group (Fisher's exact p = 0.0045). Median follow‐up duration did not differ significantly between groups. Prior infection at presentation showed a numerical but not statistically significant association with recurrence. Time to healing was prolonged in both groups and did not differ significantly. Within a structured outpatient pathway, paediatric patients demonstrated low overall recurrence rates. Conservative management was associated with lower recurrence; however, patients undergoing operative intervention likely represented a more severe subgroup. Prospective severity‐adjusted studies are required to define optimal paediatric wound management strategies for PNS.Keywords: minimally invasive surgery, outpatient care, paediatrics, pilonidal sinus disease, recurrence, wound healing

Introduction

Pilonidal sinus disease (PNS) is a chronic inflammatory condition of the natal cleft characterized by sinus tract formation, abscess development and recurrent drainage. Although frequently described in young adults, PNS commonly affects adolescents, where the condition may significantly impact mobility, school attendance and psychosocial well‐being [1].

Management strategies range from hygiene‐based conservative care to minimally invasive procedures and wide excisional surgery [2, 3]. Reported recurrence rates vary substantially, reflecting differences in definitions, follow‐up duration and patient selection. Adult‐based guidelines exist, including those from the American Society of Colon and Rectal Surgeons; however, paediatric‐specific pathways remain poorly defined [4].

Children and adolescents represent a distinct subgroup. Compliance, hygiene behaviours, body habitus and psychosocial factors differ from adult populations and may influence both treatment selection and outcomes [3, 5]. Standardised outpatient models may reduce surgical burden while maintaining acceptable recurrence rates.

This study evaluates clinical outcomes following implementation of a structured, tiered outpatient pathway for paediatric PNS.

Definitions

Healing was defined as complete epithelialization without drainage, swelling, or visible sinus openings.

Recurrence was defined as new sinus formation, swelling, or purulent drainage following documented healing for ≥ 2 weeks.

Prolonged wound healing was defined exploratively as epithelialization exceeding 30 days.

Statistical Analysis

Descriptive statistics were calculated. Continuous variables were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were analysed using Fisher's exact test. Due to limited sample size, multivariable modelling was not performed. Findings should be interpreted as exploratory.

Patients escalated to surgery likely represented a more severe or refractory subgroup, and this association should not be interpreted as causal superiority of conservative management.

Prior infection at presentation was numerically associated with recurrence (15.8% vs. 3.2%), but this did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.119).

Prolonged wound healing (> 30 days) showed statistical association with recurrence (p = 0.038), though this threshold was exploratory.

Discussion

This study describes outcomes following implementation of a structured outpatient pathway for paediatric PNS. Patients managed non‐operatively demonstrated lower recurrence rates compared with those requiring surgery. However, this association must be interpreted cautiously, as escalation likely reflects greater baseline disease severity.

Time to healing was prolonged in both cohorts, consistent with the chronic inflammatory nature of PNS. These durations align with published paediatric series and underscore the importance of longitudinal follow‐up in wound management [6].

Prior infection was numerically associated with recurrence but not statistically significant. Antibiotic exposure should be interpreted as a marker of disease severity rather than a causal determinant of recurrence.

Hair control remains an important adjunctive strategy. Contemporary evidence, including recent prospective studies, suggests that structured laser depilation may reduce recurrence in selected populations [7]. While older studies questioned laser depilation efficacy, more recent prospective data—including Minneci et al.—demonstrate reduced recurrence with structured laser hair removal protocols. Therefore, laser should not be dismissed and may serve as an adjunct in selected adolescents.

Importantly, paediatric patients differ from adults in compliance, hygiene habits and psychosocial impact. Structured follow‐up, education and early intervention may contribute to improved outcomes independent of procedural choice.

From a wound‐care perspective, structured outpatient monitoring, hygiene optimization and early intervention may reduce the need for extensive surgical excision. However, randomised or prospective severity‐adjusted studies are required to define optimal paediatric‐specific protocols.

Limitations

This study is limited by its retrospective design, introducing recall bias and missing information. This is a single‐centre study, limiting its scope. Applying the same algorithm to other hospitals would help validate and verify the findings. Expanding beyond our centre will also help address the small sample size. Lack of formal disease severity grading is preventing classification of baseline risk of recurrence, and this introduces potential confounding to who receives surgical treatment and who doesn't. We also currently lack a definition of prolonged healing of pilonidal wound. Our current protocol doesn't dictate a specific follow‐up schedule, which has led to heterogeneous follow‐up durations. Currently, causal inference cannot be established.

Conclusion

A structured outpatient pathway for paediatric PNS was associated with low overall recurrence rates. Conservative management was associated with fewer recurrences; however, patients requiring operative intervention likely represented a more severe subgroup.

Value of ZSCAN9 in the treatment and survival prediction of hepatocellular carcinoma: a bioinformatics study
Authors: Suwei Jing, Wenlei Dong, Chao Zhan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundZinc finger and SCAN domain containing 9 (ZSCAN9), also known as zinc finger factor 193 (ZNF193), has been associated with the enhanced expression of X-chromosomal genes in certain organs. However, its role in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains unclear. This study examines the mechanism of ZSCAN9 in HCC and analyze its expression, prognostic value, clinical relevance, immune correlation, signaling pathways, and drug sensitivity, thus providing new insights into its potential as a therapeutic target for HCC.MethodsThe Tumor Immune Estimation Resource (TIMER) 2.0 database was used to analyze the pancancer expression of ZSCAN9, its differential expression between tumor and normal tissues, and the associations with overall survival (OS) and recurrence-free survival (RFS). Additionally, The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis (GEPIA) databases, along with Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), were used to analyze the pathways, immune levels, and drug sensitivity related to ZSCAN9’s involvement in HCC.ResultsZSCAN9 was differentially expressed in HCC, with its high expression being associated with poor prognosis (P<0.05). ZSCAN9 was also correlated with several biological functions, such as DNA replication, G2M checkpoint, tumor proliferation, DNA repair (R>0.3; P<0.05), and fatty acid degradation (R<−0.3; P<0.05). The results of immune correlation analysis showed that ZSCAN9 was positively correlated with the abundance of T helper cells (R>0.2; P<0.05) and negatively correlated with that of dendritic cells (DCs), cytotoxic cells, neutrophils, plasmacytoid DCs, B cells, and interdigitating DCs (R<−0.2; P<0.05). ZSCAN9 was positively correlated with CD274 level (R=0.29), and the results of drug sensitivity analysis indicated that patients with high ZSCAN9 were more responsive to several drugs, including sorafenib, 5-fluorouracil, etoposide, and AKT inhibitor VIII, as compared to those with low expression.ConclusionsZSCAN9 may be a biological target capable of predicting recurrence and survival time in patients with HCC and may be involved in the regulation of angiogenesis; moreover, its expression may influence the drug sensitivity of sorafenib, 5-fluorouracil, etoposide, and AKT inhibitor VIII.Keywords: Zinc finger and SCAN domain containing 9 (ZSCAN9), hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), prognostic marker, bioinformatics analysis

Highlight box

Key findings

• Zinc finger and SCAN domain containing 9 (ZSCAN9) is upregulated in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) tissues, and its high expression is associated with poor overall survival and disease-free survival.

• ZSCAN9 is involved in key biological processes including DNA replication, tumor proliferation, and immune cell modulation.

• High ZSCAN9 expression correlates with increased sensitivity to HCC treatment drugs, including sorafenib, 5-fluorouracil, etoposide, and AKT inhibitor VIII.

What is known and what is new?

• Zinc finger proteins are a large family of transcription factors that regulate various biological processes such as cell differentiation, proliferation, migration, and apoptosis.

• ZSCAN9 has been previously linked to the elevated expression of X-chromosomal genes in certain organs, but its role in cancer, especially in HCC, has not been extensively characterized.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The findings suggest that ZSCAN9 can be used as a reliable biomarker for predicting recurrence and survival time in patients with HCC, potentially guiding clinical decision-making.

• Future studies should validate the clinical utility of ZSCAN9 as a prognostic marker in larger patient cohorts to confirm its role in predicting recurrence and treatment outcomes.

• Future research should focus on therapeutic strategies targeting ZSCAN9 to clarify its ability to enhance treatment efficacy and overcome resistance in HCC therapies.

Introduction

Primary liver cancer is one of the most common malignant tumors worldwide, with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) accounting for 80% of its incidence (1). In 2020 data, there were approximately 1 million new cases of liver cancer and more than 800,000 liver cancer-related deaths worldwide (2,3). The main causative factors include viral hepatitis, alcoholism, smoking, obesity, and genetics. Although radical therapies (i.e., surgery, hepatic radiofrequency ablation, and liver transplantation) remain the mainstay treatment for HCC and can provide long-term survival (4), postoperative HCC is prone to short-term recurrence due to the presence of high-risk factors for recurrence, such as cirrhosis and microvascular invasion (5). There remains a clinical need for effective biological targets that can predict the prognosis and recurrence of patients with HCC postoperatively to provide a reference for treatment and review.

A strong association exists between liver cancer recurrence and gene expression. The abnormal expression of certain genes is associated with an increased risk of liver cancer recurrence. Zinc fingers (ZNFs) are small, functional, autonomously folded domains that require the coordination of one or more zinc ions to stabilize their structure (6). ZNFs are DNA-binding domains that contain the largest family of transcription factors. They also bind RNA, facilitate protein interactions, have structural roles, etc. (7). Thus, zinc finger proteins (ZFPs) can act as the transcriptional repressors and activators of a variety of genes, thereby regulating a multitude of biological processes, including cell differentiation, cell proliferation, migration, invasion, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), apoptosis, and stemness maintenance (Figure 1) (8). It has also been shown that the ZFP family plays important role in the process of tumor recurrence. For example, ZNF281 is a component of the EMT regulatory transcriptional network and controls the migration, invasion, stemness, and metastasis of colorectal cancer (CRC); furthermore, the upregulation of ZNF281 in the primary tumors of patients with CRC increases the risk of cancer recurrence (9). In addition, ZNF384 promotes HCC cell proliferation by upregulating cyclin D1 expression. This in turn promotes the occurrence and development of HCC (10). ZFP521 can arrest apoptosis and enhance the proliferation, migration, and invasion of gastric cancer cells (11).

The expression of ZSCAN9, also known as zinc finger factor 193 (ZNF193), has been reported previously to correlate with organ-specific enhanced expression of X-chromosomal genes that escape inactivation (12). However, our study is the first to analyze the mechanism of action and clinical value of ZSCAN9 in HCC. We performed expression, prognostic, clinical relevance, immune correlation, signaling pathway correlation, and drug sensitivity analyses of ZSCAN9 using bioinformatics analysis. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-1597/rc).

Methods

Differential analysis of ZSCAN9 in HCC, survival analysis, and protein expression in the Human Protein Atlas

The differential expression of ZSCAN9 (ZNF193) in more than 20 malignant tumors was analyzed with Tumor Immune Estimation Resource (TIMER) 2.0 database (https://compbio.cn/timer2/) (acquisition date: July 2024), and the expression of ZSCAN9 (ZNF193) in different cancer types was plotted according to the results. The results were categorized into three classes according to the magnitude of the P value: P<0.001, P<0.01, and P<0.05. The differential expression of ZSCAN9 was analyzed via the “limma”, “ggplot2”, and “ggpubr” R software packages (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing), while survival analysis was performed with the “survival” and “survminer” packages; P<0.05 was considered statistically significant, the data were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) (acquisition date: August 2022). This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Analysis of ZSCAN9-related prognostic factors

The prognostic information of the selected genes was obtained from TCGA, and the correlation between the expression of target genes and prognostic factors was analyzed and visualized via the “limma” and “ggpubr” R packages. Additionally, the correlation between target gene expression and prognostic factors was further analyzed and plotted via the “survival”, “survminer”, “timeROC”, “regplot”, and “rms” R packages to generate receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, calibration curves, and nomograms for prognostic analysis. One-way and multivariable Cox analyses were performed with the R “survival” package, and forest plots were generated. A significance level of P<0.05 was applied in these analyses.

Prediction of gene-related pathways and functional gene sets

RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq) data and relevant clinical information for HCC were extracted from TCGA database. We then identified the genes in the involved pathways and analyzed them via the “GSVA” package in R software (parameter method = “ssgsea”). Finally, correlations between genes and pathway scores were analyzed via Spearman correlation. In all analyses, the significance level was set at P<0.05, and pathways with correlation coefficients (R) >0.6 were used as the screening criteria. Finally, BioRender software (https://biorender.com) (Toronto, Ontario, Canada) was used for mechanism mapping.

Gene coexpression networks, differential genes, and prognostic survival analysis

Coexpression information was extracted from TCGA database, and the selected genes were visualized with the “limma”, “circlize”, and “corrplot” R packages. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was applied to statistically analyze the target genes based on the characteristics of the data format through use of the “stats” and “car” R packages. Additionally, the “ggplot2” package was used for data visualization, the “survival” package was employed for proportional hazard hypothesis testing and survival regression modeling, and the results were visualized with the “survminer” and “ggplot2” packages.

ZSCAN9-related immunoassays

The correlation of ZSCAN9 with various types of immune cells and immune checkpoints and the plotting of immunotherapy analysis were carried via the including “limma”, “reshape2”, “ggplot2”, “ggpubr”, “vioplot”, “ggExtra”, and “corrplot” R packages. Data for immunotherapy analysis were obtained from The Cancer Imaging Archive (TCIA) (http://tcia.at/) (acquisition date: July 2024), while the Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis (GEPIA) database (http://gepia.cancer-pku.cn) (acquisition date: July 2024) was used to determine the correlation between ZSCAN9 and PDCD1, CD274, and CTLA4. The P value was calculated via the Spearman statistics; a positive correlation was defined as P<0.05 and R>0.2, while a negative correlation was defined as P<0.05 and R<−0.2.

Drug sensitivity analysis

RNA-seq data and corresponding clinical information for HCC were obtained from TCGA dataset (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). Based on the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) (https://www.cancerrxgene.org) (acquisition date: July 2024), the therapeutic efficacy of each sample was predicted. The prediction process was implemented with the R package “pRRophetic” [pRRophetic predicts the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) values of new compounds by extracting their chemical features and using machine learning models to establish the relationship between chemical features and IC50 values]. The IC50 of the samples was estimated using ridge regression, with all parameters set to their default values. The combatting batch effect (ComBat) was applied for batch effect adjustment, all tissue types were included, and the replicate gene expression was averaged.

Statistical analysis

The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to compare the differences between two groups. We used the “DESeq2” and “survival” R packages to analyze the differential expression data. Survival rates were determined via ROC curve analysis, along with one-way and multifactorial Cox regression analysis. The correlation between the central gene and immune function was determined using the Spearman rank correlation coefficient. The significance threshold was set at P<0.05.

Results

Expression level of ZSCAN9 and the prognosis of HCC

The expression of ZSCAN9 (ZNF193) was analyzed in over 20 malignant tumor types with the TIMER 2.0 database, revealing that ZSCAN9 expression was significantly upregulated in more than 10 tumor types, including HCC (Figure 1A-1C). Additionally, in terms of progression-free interval (PFI) and overall survival (OS), the high-ZSCAN9 expression group had a worse prognosis compared to the low-expression group (P<0.01) (Figure 1D,1E).

Clinical correlates and predictive level analysis of ZSCAN9

Clinical factors related to ZSCAN9 were analyzed with TCGA database, revealing significant differences (P<0.05) in vascular invasion, alpha fetoprotein (AFP) level, tumor status, histologic grade (between G1 and G3 and between G2 and G3), and T stage (between T1 and T2) (Figure 2A). The predictive value of ZSCAN9 for prognosis in patients with HCC was also assessed, and calibration curves were plotted. The results suggest that ZSCAN9 can serve as a potential biological target for predicting recurrence and survival time in these patients (Figure 2B,2C).

Furthermore, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses based on TCGA database were performed to examine the association of clinical factors and ZSCAN9 expression with OS and PFI in patients with HCC. For OS, T stage and ZSCAN9 expression were identified as independent risk factors (Table 1). For PFI, T stage and ZSCAN9 expression were independent risk factors for recurrence (Table 2). Based on these findings, T stage and ZSCAN9 expression were incorporated into a prediction model, and column line graphs were plotted to predict survival and recurrence (Figure 2D,2E).

Correlation and enrichment analysis of ZSCAN9-related hub genes and angiogenesis-related gene sets

First, a heatmap of ZSCAN9-related differentially expressed genes was drawn [P<0.05, false dicovery rate (FDR) <0.05] (Figure 3A), followed by an analysis of the relationship between ZSCAN9 and angiogenesis-related gene sets. A strong correlation was observed between ZSCAN9 and VEGFA (R>0.4; P<0.05) (Figure 3B). In the Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis, it was found that ZSCAN9 may be involved in biological processes such as nuclear division and meiosis, cellular components such as microtubules and condensed chromosomes, and molecular functions such as microtubule binding (Figure 3C). These findings suggest that ZSCAN9 may play a role in DNA replication, cell cycle regulation, and other biological processes. In the subsequent Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis (P<0.05, q value <0.05), the results indicated that ZSCAN9 was significantly enriched in cell cycle regulatory processes (P<0.05, q value <0.05) (Figure 3D). Furthermore, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) suggested that ZSCAN9 may be involved in processes such as complement and coagulation cascades; fatty-acid metabolism; glycine, serine, and threonine metabolism; and peroxisome and isoleucine metabolism. In contrast, processes such as valine, leucine, and isoleucine degradation were found to be negatively correlated with ZSCAN9 expression (P<0.05, q value <0.05) (Figure 3E). Based on the scores of the ZSCAN9-related functional gene set in TCGA database (R>0.3; P<0.05), five functional gene sets or signaling pathways with potential relevance to ZSCAN9 were selected. The analysis revealed that ZSCAN9 was positively correlated with DNA replication, the G2M checkpoint, the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway, and DNA repair (R>0.3; P<0.05), but negatively correlated with fatty-acid degradation (R<−0.3; P<0.05) (Figure 3F).

Differential expression levels and prognosis of ZSCAN9 coexpressed genes

Through gene coexpression analysis, we identified genes (Figure 5A) that were strongly positively correlated with ZSCAN9, including ZSCAN26, ZKSCAN8, ATAT1, PRPF4B, ZSCAN16, and CDKAL1 (R>0.4; P<0.05). Additionally, ZSCAN9 exhibited a negative correlation with HP, LDHD, C8A, HMGCL, and DECR1 (R<−0.4; P<0.05). Finally, we analyzed the differential expression of these coexpressed genes (Figure 5B). The results show that high expression of ATAT1, PRPF4B, LDHD, and ZSCAN26 is associated with poor OS and PFI in hepatocellular carcinoma patients, while high expression of ZSCAN16 is specifically linked to a poorer PFI (Figure 5C).

ZSCAN9-related immunoassays

The correlation between ZSCAN9 and immune cell infiltration levels, as well as immune checkpoints, was analyzed using R packages and data from the GEPIA and TCGA databases. ZSCAN9 was found to be positively correlated with the abundance of T helper cells (R>0.2; P<0.05) and negatively correlated with that of dendritic cells (DCs), cytotoxic cells, neutrophils, plasmacytoid DCs, B cells, and interdigitating DCs (R<−0.2; P<0.05) (Figure 6A). To further inform the individualized treatment related to ZSCAN9 in patients with HCC, we examined the relationship between risk scores and the IC50 of a variety of drugs applied to the clinical treatment of HCC, including sorafenib, 5-fluorouracil, and etoposide. Discussion

ZNFs, the largest family of sequence-specific DNA-binding proteins, play important roles as transcription factors in the regulation of gene expression and participate in a variety of biological processes such as differentiation, development, metabolism, apoptosis, autophagy, and stemness maintenance (13-17). Thus far, eight different classes of ZNF motifs have been reported, including Cys2His2 (C2H2) like, Gag knuckle, Treble clef, Zinc ribbon, Zn2/Cys6, TAZ2 domain like, Zinc binding loops, and metallothionein (6). The different members of the ZNF family exert varying effects depending on the tumor type. For instance, in breast cancer, ZNF24 inhibits VEGF expression by binding to the proximal VEGF promoter and negatively regulates tumor growth by inhibiting angiogenesis (18). In gastric cancer, microRNAs inhibiting ZNF24 can promote the migration and invasive ability of tumor (19). Meanwhile, zinc finger E-box-binding homeobox (ZEB1) is involved in the EMT process in several cancer types including HCC (20-22) and may promote TGF-β and platelet-driven growth factor receptor-α signaling (23,24), thereby promoting the occurrence and progression of tumors. Moreover, in non-small cell lung cancer, ZEB1 overexpression further promotes EMT-related epidermal growth factor receptor-tyrosine kinase inhibitor (EGFR-TKI) acquired resistance through transcriptional upregulation of E-cadherin, ST14, and vimentin (25). The overexpression of ZNF384 in HCC tissues is significantly correlated with tumor recurrence, and ZNF384 promotes the proliferation of HCC cells by directly upregulating cyclin D1 expression (8). MIZ1 sequesters the oncoprotein metadherin (MTDH), preventing MTDH from activating transcription factor nuclear factor kB (NF-kB), which in turn limits hepatocyte-driven inflammation to suppress HCC (26). In addition to this, certain ZNFs exhibit dual roles in different malignant tumors. In skin cancer, hypoxia-induced ZNF395 is able to transcriptionally upregulate cancer-related and interferon-stimulated genes, such as IFIT1/ISG56, IFI44, and IFI16 (27), in an IKK signaling-dependent manner, which in turn promotes the progression of malignancy. In one study of HCC, miR-525-3p was able to promote tumor cell migration and invasion by directly targeting ZNF395 (28).

The correlation of ZNF395 with organ-specific enhanced expression of X-chromosomal genes that escape inactivation has been reported (12), but apart from this, few reports on ZSCAN9 have been published. Our analysis of publicly available databases revealed that ZSCAN9 was significantly highly expressed in HCC (P<0.01), and patients in the high-risk group (ZSCAN9 expression higher than the median value) had a poorer prognosis in terms of OS and PFI compared with those in the low-risk group (ZSCAN9 expression lower than the median value), which suggests that ZSCAN9 may have the potential to become a biological target in HCC. Moreover, there were significant differences in ZSCAN9 expression, tumor grade, stage, AFP level, vascular invasion, and tumor status between the high- and low-risk groups (P<0.05). In terms of diagnostic efficacy, ZSCAN9 showed good sensitivity and specificity for OS and PFI prediction, and on the basis of this, we further improved the efficacy of the prediction model by incorporating other independent risk factors after univariate and multivariate Cox analyses. We then analyzed the potential signaling pathways associated with ZSCAN9 and found that ZSCAN9 may be involved in process related to the development and progression of HCC. Furthermore, there was a positive correlation between ZSCAN9 expression and DNA repair, DNA replication, G2M checkpoint, and tumor proliferation, which suggests that ZSCAN9 may fulfill some of the functions of ZNF family members and participate in the regulation of these biological processes in HCC. In our subsequent analysis, we found a moderate correlation (R>0.4) between ZSCAN9 expression and angiogenesis-related factors, especially vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGFA). VEGFA is recognized as a biological target associated with poor prognosis and tumor recurrence in HCC and is involved in tumor growth, proliferation, invasion, metastasis, and other processes (29). In addition, we analyzed the drug sensitivity of four drugs for HCC treatment and found that there was a significant correlation between the expression of ZSCAN9 and the IC50 of these drugs. Although this study reveals the potential role of ZSCAN9 in HCC through bioinformatics analysis, there are areas that require further improvement. First, functional validation through cell and animal models is needed to enhance the reliability of the results. Second, the specific mechanisms of ZSCAN9 in angiogenesis and drug sensitivity need to be further explored. While the study analyzes the correlation between ZSCAN9 and immune checkpoints, further validation of immune cell infiltration in clinical samples is needed. Finally, although drug sensitivity predictions show promise, clinical trials are necessary to confirm the effectiveness of ZSCAN9 as a therapeutic target. Future research should focus on these directions to promote the clinical application of ZSCAN9 in HCC. Based on the potential therapeutic mechanisms of ZSCAN9 in tumor treatment, it may help future research in exploring new targets for tumor-targeted therapies. Furthermore, immune checkpoint inhibitors (such as PD-L1) or promising immune modulators (such as prodigosin, hinokitiol, etc.) may synergize with the immune modulation and tumor response effects of ZSCAN9 (30-32). These natural compounds could help reduce inflammation or immune resistance, complementing ZSCAN9’s role in immune cell regulation, thereby enhancing cancer prevention and treatment outcomes.

In conclusion, inhibiting ZSCAN9 could potentially enhance drug sensitivity and improve therapeutic outcomes. In this study, we identified ZSCAN9 as a clinical prognostic marker in HCC and examined its involvement in angiogenesis and drug sensitivity. Our findings align with recent advancements in personalized medicine, suggesting that targeted therapies could improve chemotherapeutic outcomes for HCC patients (33). These results also contribute to the ongoing research into synthetic drugs and natural products that target cancer-related pathways, similar to the strategies explored (34). Ultimately, our findings may provide a basis for developing more effective, individualized treatment approaches for HCC.

Conclusions

ZSCAN9 may serve as a biomarker for predicting recurrence and survival in HCC, regulate angiogenesis, and affect sensitivity to drugs such as sorafenib, 5-fluorouracil, etoposide, and AKT inhibitor VIII.

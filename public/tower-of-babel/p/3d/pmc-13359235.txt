Robert Zeller, MD (1938–2025)
Authors: Gary D. Clark, Phillip L. Pearl
Published: 2026

Figure: Robert Stuart Zeller, MD. Used with permission from the Child Neurology Society.

A beloved child neurologist known for his clinical acumen, gentle nature, and duck sounds that put children at ease and reduced families' stress levels, Robert “Bob” Zeller was a master clinician who trained scores of pediatric neurology residents at Texas Children's Hospital.

A native of Buffalo, Bob attended Princeton University and received his MD degree in 1963 from the University of Buffalo. He completed his pediatric internship and residency at Baylor College of Medicine (1963−1966) and published his first paper, which was on translocation heterozygosis, that is, structural chromosomal derangements, during his residency (1966). He then served as a captain in the US Air Force, during which he actively practiced and wrote a publication on the complications of ventriculo‐arterial shunting for hydrocephalus. This was followed by child neurology training at the renowned program at Columbia University in New York City with the child neurology leaders and luminaries of that era, including Drs. Sidney Carter, Abe Chutorian, and Arnold Gold.

After his training, Bob settled in Houston. He became a charter member of the Child Neurology Society in 1972. He was appointed chief of pediatric neurology at Texas Children's Hospital and served in this position for 5 years, with publications on eastern equine encephalitis and cerebrovascular thrombosis in ketoacidotic coma and clinical trials in Duchenne muscular dystrophy. He collaborated with Baylor neuropathologists Jan Goddard‐Finegold and Dawna Armstrong in experimental investigations of intraventricular hemorrhage using a neonatal beagle model.

Dr. Zeller had an innate capacity to support others and bring out their best. He was a walking model of compassion and had extraordinary abilities in diagnosis and communication. He was known for duck‐like quacks, Sesame Street puppets, and cowboy boots, all of which raised humor and reduced stress levels for patients and families.

Dr. Zeller was appointed Clinical Professor of Pediatrics at Baylor in 1993 and named chief of the Blue Bird Circle Clinic of Texas Children's Hospital in 2005. He served with extraordinary commitment, expressing the sense that his patients gave more to him than he gave to them. When he learned that a patient with epilepsy was not allowed to enroll in a summer camp, he created, with his own investment and fundraising, “Camp For AII” to serve the needs of children, adolescents, and adults with disabilities. More than 100,000 campers have benefited from this model facility by now.

A devoted family man, Bob was married to the love of his life, Susan Cooper, for 56 years, and they have a close‐knit family of three children and eight grandchildren. He enjoyed building and fixing things, from restoring his own 1929 Mercedes SSK to tennis backboards and playhouses. Dr. Zeller was recognized with the Arnold P. Gold Humanism in Medicine Award of the Child Neurology Society in 2015. He personified compassion and commitment at the highest levels. He leaves a legacy of colleagues and trainees who have gone on to mentor and advise scores more, in the true meaning of altruism, humanism, and clinical mastery.

Safety and efficacy of itacitinib, a selective JAK1 inhibitor, in advanced hepatocellular cancer: Phase 1b trial (JAKAL)
Authors: Alessandro Troiani, Maria Martinez, Caroline Ward, Charlotte Wilhelm Benartzi, David J Pinato, Rohini Sharma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTOveractivation of the JAK/STAT pathway is one of the drivers for the pathophysiology of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). We propose a Phase Ib study to evaluate the safety and efficacy of itacitinib, a selective JAK1 inhibitor, as a second-line treatment for patients with advanced or metastatic HCC.Twenty-five patients will receive 400 mg itacitinib orally daily, 28-day cycle. Safety will be reviewed prior to each cycle. Tumor response assessed every 2 months until disease progression, death or withdrawal. Tumor biopsies and blood samples will be taken for presence of JAK1 mutations.Activation of JAK/STAT pathway drives HCC development and is associated with immunotherapy resistance. Itacitinib is hypothesized to be safe and effective in HCC patients that have progressed after first-line therapies.Clinical Trial Registration: EudraCT: 2017-004437-81 NCT04358185 (ClinicalTrials.gov)Keywords: : efficacy, hepatocellular cancer, INCB039110, itacitinib, JAK/STAT, liver cirrhosis, Phase I, safety

Introduction

Background & rationale

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the third most common cause of cancer death , as well as being the main cause of death in patients with history of chronic liver disease (CLD) in the Western world [1,2]. Up to 90% of HCCs develop in patients with pre-existing chronic liver inflammation: this can be secondary to chronic infections from pathogens such as hepatitis virus B and C strains (HBV and HCV respectively), alcohol-induced liver disease and other less common triggers, including non-alcoholic steatohepatitis and haemochromatosis [3].

The persistent harm to the liver parenchyma and necrosis of its cells caused by these conditions drives immune cell infiltration, activation and cytokine secretion. Together with the increased rate of hepatocyte turnover, the prolonged exposure to pro-inflammatory molecules (such as IL-6, TNF-alpha and reactive oxygen species) leads to the formation of oncogenic mutations and thus the development of malignant cells [4,5]. In addition, systemic inflammation has been associated with the development of paraneoplastic syndromes such as cancer cachexia, and levels of inflammatory markers have been reported as possible predictors of patient survival post-resection [6,7].

The Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer staging system is favored to determine liver cancer stage, which is then used to guide therapeutic options. If HCC is diagnosed early, the only curative treatments are resection or transplant [8]. Unfortunately, primary liver neoplasms are more likely to present at an intermediate or advanced stage for which there are limited treatment options [9].

Currently, first-line therapeutic options for advanced HCC include combination of atezolizumab and bevacizumab and the multi-kinase inhibitors sorafenib and lenvatinib. However, options are limited in the second-line setting. Regorafenib, cabozantinib, and ramucirumab all have been shown to impart survival benefit following sorafenib failure or discontinuation but there is paucity of data regarding their activity following immunotherapy [10–13]. Furthermore, there is increasing interest in combining of multikinase inhibitors with immunotherapeutic agents leaving a further void as to what agent to use in the second line setting [14–17].

Moreover, the above studies were restricted to those with preserved liver function (Child–Pugh A). As indicated, HCC develops on a background of CLD, thus patients have two life-limiting illnesses: HCC and progressive liver impairment, the latter of which dictates the receipt of second-line therapy [18]. There is however, increasing evidence that patients with Child–Pugh Class B cirrhosis do benefit from systemic therapy, and there is a need to further study systemic treatments in this setting [19–21].

JAK is a family of tyrosine kinase proteins that respond to extracellular immunogenic stimuli, for example in presence of IL-6 and IFNγ, and activate STAT proteins. The latter enter the nuclei to activate genes involved in cell proliferation, survival and other essential functions [22].

In inflammatory states and carcinomas, STAT proteins, particularly STAT3, are activated constantly, which is thought to contribute to cancer development and evasion from immune-mediated rejection [23,24]. The persistent activation can affect the expression of regulatory proteins, such as suppressors of cytokine signalling, and loss of tyrosine phosphatase proteins responsible for the deactivation of the JAK and STAT [23]. Neoplastic progression driven by JAK/STAT pathway activation, which has been associated with HCC pathophysiology, involves a variety of mechanisms, including proliferation and avoidance of apoptosis; control of invasiveness, metastasis and angiogenesis; and chemotherapeutic resistance [4,25,26].

In addition, although immune checkpoint blockade therapy has shown efficacy in many solid cancers, a substantial proportion of patients develop resistance to immunotherapeutic agents [27]. Prolonged IFN exposure and subsequent activation or mutation of the JAK proteins have been identified as possible key factors in mechanisms of resistance [28,29].

Based on the above evidence, it is hypothesized that the inhibition of the JAK/STAT pathway may contribute to reducing cancer proliferation and progression. Itacitinib is a small molecule inhibitor, selective for JAK1, used in the management of Graft-versus-host disease [30]. Also known as INCB039110, its role in the management of solid tumors and B-Cell malignancies remains to be elucidated [31,32].

The effect of itacitinib on the JAK/STAT pathway and the subsequence reduction in inflammation has been demonstrated preclinically and, as of the data cut-off date (30 APR 2021), 34 clinical studies with itacitinib (18 Phase I, 7 Phase I/II, 7 Phase II and 2 Phase III), including studies of the drug as monotherapy and in combination with other therapeutic agents, have either been completed or are ongoing [33]. In total, itacitinib has been administered to 1699 participants, of which 543 were healthy and 278 received itacitinib monotherapy, contributing to the data on safety, pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics for this drug [34–36].

To this date, there is a lack of Phase I studies that assess the effects of the JAK1 inhibitor itacitinib as a second-line treatment on patients with advanced HCC. Due to the cytokines produced by inflammatory cells in the cirrhotic liver, the JAK/STAT pathway is activated and leads to the expression of genes that are involved in cancer cell formation, including cell activation, survival, and proliferation [4]. In addition, mechanisms of resistance to immune checkpoint blockade have been shown to be linked to JAK-regulated pathways [29]. It is therefore hypothesized that the ability of itacitinib to inhibit the JAK pathway may cause a positive response in patients’ tumors. The study aims to assess the effects and safety of the JAK1 inhibitor itacitinib as a second/third-line treatment in patients with advanced HCC.

Trial objectives

The primary objective of this study is to assess the efficacy of itacitinib in patients with advanced HCC by overall response rate (ORR). ORR will be defined as a complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD) or progressive disease (PD) at 8 weeks post-treatment initiation according to the modified RECIST criteria (mRECIST) version 1.1. Furthermore, the co-primary objective of this study is to investigate the safety and tolerability of itacitinib, using Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Effects (CTCAE) version 4.03.

Secondary objectives include progression free survival, defined as the time elapsed since entering the study to the first evidence of disease progression as described by mRECIST 1.1 or death due to any cause; overall survival (OS) defined as the time elapsed since entering the study to death due to any cause.

Translational end points include the assessment of the presence of predefined JAK1 mutations via next-generation sequencing in the tumor and correlate findings with tumor outcomes as indicated by the mRECIST v1.1; the assessment of the presence of predefined JAK1 mutations in circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) via next-generation sequencing; and the correlation of changes in proinflammatory cytokines via multiplex cytokine bead array with the treatment response, according to mRECIST v.1.1.

Materials & methods

Study setting & eligibility criteria

Participants are recruited from Hammersmith Hospital, Imperial College Healthcare NHS Trust, starting from January 2019. Participants are given a full spoken and written explanation of all essential aspects of the study; an information leaflet shall also be offered, and a minimum of 24 h are given prior to consent. All participants are free to withdraw at any time from the protocol treatment without giving reasons and without prejudicing further treatment. The appendix contains a model participant information and consent form.

Eligible patients must have a diagnosis of primary HCC based on histology, or other accepted radiological or combined criteria from the American Association of Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) guidelines, as well as documented progression or intolerance to first-line therapy [37]. Patients must be Child–Pugh class A or B to be eligible (maximum of 7 points).

Recruitment was completed in December 2022.

Intervention

In this single-arm Phase Ib study to evaluate the effect of itacitinib (a JAK1 inhibitor), up to 25 eligible patients with advanced HCC will be recruited. The patients will receive a 400 mg dose of oral sustained release (SR) itacitinib once daily (QD), based on the safety and pharmacokinetics profile from previous trials [34–36].

Patient timeline

All patients will undergo baseline screening on their first visit, including blood samples, RECIST tumor assessment via CT scan, biopsy and ctDNA analysis. The first ten patients will undergo attenuated pharmacokinetic profiling in the first study cycle (400 mg QD). Treatment will begin within 28 days from informed consent. Patients will then be seen in the clinic on day 1 and day 7 of cycle 1; then every 4 weeks (from date of day 1) while on treatment for all following cycles.

During each visit, patients will undergo baseline blood tests and adverse events assessment. Concomitant medications will also be recorded at each visit. Research bloods, ctDNA and CT scan will be repeated every 8 weeks from the first dose of itacitinib until the end of the treatment (see Table 2 for full assessment details).

Safety

Throughout the study, concomitant medication will be monitored. CYP3A4 inhibitors and inducers shall be monitored and avoided if possible. Administration of any other anticancer therapy, another immunosuppressant, JAK1 inhibitor or live-attenuated vaccines during the study is prohibited.

Toxicity shall be monitored throughout the study. Clinically indicated dose interruptions may be possible if less than 14 days in duration. Any dose interruption longer than 14 days will lead to a withdrawal from the study. Any toxicity will be graded based on NCI-CTCAE version 4.03. In the case of significant toxicity, patients may reduce the dose from 400 mg to 300 mg QD (Table 3 for full details).

Data analysis

Data collection

The frequency of adverse events (AEs) will be assessed for severity (NCI CTCAE v4.03), expectedness, seriousness, and causal relationship to study drugs(s). In addition, AEs will be summarized by toxicity type, impact on study drug(s) and timing.

All efficacy data in the study will be analysed on an intention-to-treat basis, regardless of the treatment received. To be considered ‘evaluable’ for the primary efficacy analysis, a patient must have a week 8 tumor assessment or evidence of disease progression or death prior to week 8.

For the translational/exploratory end points, appropriate descriptive measures, as well as univariate models, will be shown as dependent on whether the relevant measure is categorical or continuous, whether the test is paired or not and whether is it normally or non-normally distributed as indicated below. Translational end points may not be included in the Clinical Study Report.

Statistical methods

This is a single-arm Phase Ib pilot study to evaluate the effect of itacitinib in 25 patients with advanced HCC. The two primary outcomes of this study are safety and tolerability as well as ORR. The study will be powered on the ORR as per the below. A trial steering committee will convene every 6 months to review safety and clinical data.

The clinical data will be entered and stored in InForm™ database. Statistical analysis will be conducted using R version 3.0.1 and Stata version 14.

The minimum acceptable activity of the overall response for this treatment is 25%, while the maximum unacceptable activity of the overall response is 5%.

Simon’s minimax two-stage design will be used to analyse the results of this study. This includes an interim comparison of the activity of the primary efficacy end point for the first 15 patients that complete the study. If at least one patient shows an overall response above the threshold within the 15, a further ten patients shall be recruited, and further analysis will be done at the end of their treatment. Otherwise, the study will be stopped. This design yields a one-sided type I error rate of 0.05 and a statistical power of 0.90 when the true response rate is 0.25.

One interim analysis is planned as described above for the efficacy primary end point. A final analysis will be conducted at the end of the study. Analyses will include an intent-to-treat analysis including all patients enrolled and a per-protocol analysis using all patients who complete the study without major protocol violations. A data monitoring committee will be established to review data from the interim analysis. In addition, any SUSARs will be evaluated by the committee and go/no-go decision made.

Due to sample size constraints, for the primary outcomes of assessing the safety and tolerability of itacitinib and ORR, we will use descriptive measures such as frequencies and percentages. All patients who receive at least one dose of study treatment will be included in the safety analysis set.

Univariate models will be performed for all demographic and other study variables, as statistically appropriate, as well as to test for confounding due to sample size constraints, with models such as Fisher’s exact test or the Chi-square test utilized for categorical variables and t-tests or the Wilcoxon test (if the continuous variable does not follow a normal distribution) for continuous ones. For all estimates, 95% CI will be calculated.

Histograms and boxplots will be used to check the distribution and possible outliers for continuous variables. Continuous variables that follow a normal distribution will be summarized using means and standard deviations. Skewed continuous variables will be summarized using medians and interquartile ranges. Categorical variables will be summarized using frequencies and percentages.

A separate statistical analysis plan will be prepared and finalized prior to database lock. Any deviation(s) from the final statistical plan will be described in the final analysis and justification will be given in the final report.

Conclusion

Although second-line treatments for patients with HCC have been approved, these are limited to those with preserved liver function and those with advanced cirrhosis do not have access to therapy [38]. HCC most commonly develops on a chronic inflammatory background and the JAK/STAT pathway has been shown to be an important signalling pathway potentiating the release of inflammatory cytokines [25,26,39]. Furthermore, immunotherapy resistance has been linked to JAK/STAT pathway activation and mutations [28,29]. Inhibition of the above pathway is therefore hypothesized to improve survival in HCC patients with CLD that have progressed after first-line therapies. This study aims to determine the efficacy and safety of itacitinib, a selective JAK1 inhibitor, as second-line treatment for patients with advanced HCC and cirrhosis with Child–Pugh class A or B.

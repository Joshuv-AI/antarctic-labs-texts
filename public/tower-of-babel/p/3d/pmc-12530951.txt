Artificial Intelligence in Neuroendovascular Therapy: Current Applications and Future Directions
Authors: Shinya Sonobe, Kuniyasu Niizuma, Hidenori Endo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
In recent years, artificial intelligence (AI) has made remarkable progress. In the near future, AI will become an indispensable technology in daily clinical practice in the field of neuroendovascular therapy. Clinicians who understand the information processing and limitations of AI will create new and comfortable working styles. This article introduces current applications of AI in the field of neuroendovascular therapy and presents a vision for its future directions.

Introduction

In recent years, artificial intelligence (AI) has made remarkable progress in assisting with judgment, planning, and execution. In particular, in the field of neuroendovascular therapy—where rapid clinical judgment, meticulous treatment planning, and efficient procedural execution are essential—AI holds the potential to significantly transform daily clinical practice. At the same time, such transformative changes may also bring about temporary disruptions. These changes and disruptions are likely to occur in the near future. To prepare for that future, we must understand what AI can do and why it can do it, as well as understand its limitations.

AI has already become widespread in fields outside medicine, and many clinicians are likely to have a general understanding of its strengths and weaknesses. However, they may not yet be familiar with its implementation in clinical practice. This article introduces current applications of AI in the field of neuroendovascular therapy and presents a vision for its future directions.

Current Applications of AI in Neuroendovascular Therapy

AI is developed to perform information processing on behalf of clinical staff and to reduce their workload. Various AI technologies are under development, and some have already reached practical implementation. Below are the conceptual goals of 10 projects currently underway (Table 1).

Representative applications of AI

AI, artificial intelligence; LVO, large vessel occlusion

- Large vessel occlusion (LVO) identification: AI identifies patients with LVO based on easily observable signs (e.g., those that can be collected in an ambulance). This will enable earlier thrombectomy for patients who have developed LVO.1)

- Ischemia evaluation: AI evaluates the extent and severity of cerebral ischemia from CT or MRI in patients with acute ischemic stroke (AIS). This will provide more confident clinical insights for staff who are less experienced with AIS.2)

- Vessel extraction: AI extracts only the vessels of interest from CTA or MRA. This will provide a simpler interface operation for staff who are not skilled in image processing.3)

- Aneurysm detection: AI detects cerebral aneurysms from CTA or MRA. This will provide more sensitive primary screening to patients at high risk.4) Similarly, AI detection of hematomas and thrombi will provide opportunities for more timely intervention.

- Anatomical recognition: AI recognizes anatomical structures from vessel images. This will provide a more accurate pathophysiological understanding for staff involved in the procedure.5)

- Procedure proposal: AI proposes which devices to use in neuroendovascular treatment. This will provide a more thoughtful preparation framework for staff who are unfamiliar with the devices.6)

- Risk estimation: AI estimates the risks of procedural complications in neuroendovascular treatment. This will provide safer treatment strategies for staff with limited procedural training.7)

- Displacement tracking: AI detects catheter movements and vascular deformation. This will provide more improved hazard awareness for staff concentrating on the procedure.8)

- Outcome assessment: AI assesses treatment outcomes of neuroendovascular procedures. This will provide more precise recurrence predictions for patients requiring continuous follow-up.9)

- Document generation: AI generates documents based on the patient’s clinical course. This will provide more supportive record management for staff exhausted from clinical duties.10)

All of these are AI tools designed to support clinicians in their daily judgment, planning, and execution. Such AI can free clinicians from too many confirmations, allowing them to focus on what they consider the most essential aspects of patient care, and may further stimulate clinical research.

Information Processing of AI

AI is not merely a machine that returns numerical values—it produces different types of outputs depending on the situation. But how does AI produce such different types of outputs? Is it performing some kind of special information processing? In fact, when we look closely at how AI processes information in detail, it is nothing more than a series of simple Yes/No decisions. Combinations of such simple Yes/No decisions enable complex information processing.

We can illustrate this process using dodgeball as an example. When a ball is coming toward the right side of your body, you dodge to the left—and vice versa. AI works in a similar way. It decides whether to dodge left or right using a simple Yes/No decision. But what happens when multiple balls are thrown at you simultaneously? For the 1st ball, you have 1 set of choices; for the 2nd, another; and so on for the 3rd and all others. What is the best way to avoid all of them? Each individual decision is simple, but when many of them are combined, it becomes difficult for humans to manage. On the other hand, AI can immediately determine the optimal response, even in situations involving many overlapping options.

Any situation that requires an output can be transformed in some way into a combination of simple Yes/No decisions. AI learns whether each of these decisions should be Yes or No based on an enormous amount of data. In other words, AI adjusts the Yes/No balance at each decision point to achieve better outcomes, based on all the given information. These combinations of decisions enable AI to produce different types of outputs. Rather than performing some kind of special information processing, AI processes a lot of simple Yes/No decisions.

Limitations of AI

AI has the potential to offer us a more comfortable working style through its information processing. However, its outputs are not always the best answers in real clinical practice. We need to pay attention to the fact that there are limitations in AI information processing. Below are 5 representative limitations of AI (Table 2).

Table 2Representative limitations of AI- Bias from the data used in learning- Fragility to different data formats- Inadaptability to evolving clinical standards- Lack of awareness of clinical circumstances- Insufficient explainabilityAI, artificial intelligence

Representative limitations of AI

AI, artificial intelligence

- Bias from data used in learning: AI cannot return useful outputs if the data used in learning is biased. For example, if an AI learns about the length of stay at rehabilitation hospitals, it will fail to predict the length of stay at tertiary hospitals.

- Fragility to different data formats: AI cannot handle data formats that differ from the data formats used in learning. For example, if an AI learns to detect arterial stenosis based on CTA, it will fail to detect arterial stenosis in MRA.

- Inadaptability to evolving clinical standards: AI cannot update its knowledge after the time of learning. For example, if an AI learns treatment plans based on older data, it will fail to suggest treatment plans after diagnostic criteria have changed.

- Lack of awareness of clinical circumstances: AI cannot deal with information that cannot be measured. For example, it will fail to determine treatment goals when factors like clinical course or patient preferences need to be considered.

- Insufficient explainability: AI cannot explain the underlying context behind its own outputs. For example, even if an AI can predict a poor outcome for a patient with severely impaired consciousness, it cannot explain the course leading to the poor outcome.

We need to understand that AI has such limitations in its information processing. Imagine a situation where someone is asking you over the phone how to move a catheter. Compared to a situation where you actually manipulate the catheter yourself, the accuracy of decision-making would be low, especially if the person on the phone is not an expert. AI faces similar kinds of limitations. This understanding will help us work effectively with AI.

Future Direction of AI in Neuroendovascular Therapy

How should clinicians engage with AI? Clinicians who understand both the advantages and limitations of AI can make 3 constructive choices.

The 1st is to become a smart AI user. For example, in the case of catheters, there are proper handling techniques and commonly encountered pitfalls. Generally, skilled clinicians are well acquainted with these techniques and pitfalls, even if they are not familiar with the materials or structures of catheters. This is also true for AI. A smart AI user may not fully understand how AI is developed, but they know the techniques and pitfalls associated with using AI in clinical practice. By acting as a bridge between AI and humans in clinical practice, clinicians will be able to deliver healthcare more effectively.

The 2nd is to become a smart AI adopter. No matter how excellent an AI may be, it is meaningless if it cannot reach its full potential. AI must be properly included in the clinical workflow, with considerations for reliability and accountability, so that it can reach its full potential. Clinicians need to organize the entire workflow as leaders of the clinical team. The way to organize the entire workflow is different at each institution, and leaders must seek the best way for their own situation. By optimizing the clinical environment, clinicians will be able to establish a system where AI can work effectively.

The 3rd is to become a smart AI collaborator. Aside from mathematical techniques, AI development requires retrospective data for learning and prospective data for feedback. These kinds of data need to be large in volume and high in quality, and they are essential for development. Clinicians systematically collect such data by understanding what AI is. In recent years, in the field of neuroendovascular therapy, large and detailed datasets such as Japanese Registry of Neuroendovascular Therapy (JR-NET) and Japan Stroke Data Bank (JSDB) have been accumulated.11,12) These datasets strongly promote AI development. By enhancing the value of data from daily clinical practice, clinicians will be able to contribute to a more comfortable future working style.

Clinicians can engage with AI through these 3 approaches. In parallel, it is essential to establish ethical guidelines for AI, ensuring that its integration into clinical practice proceeds smoothly and responsibly.

In the near future, everyone will feel that clinical work cannot run seamlessly without AI. Clinicians who engage wisely with AI will create new, more comfortable working styles in daily clinical practice.

Conclusion

In the near future, AI will become an indispensable technology in daily clinical practice in the field of neuroendovascular therapy. Clinicians who understand the information processing and limitations of AI will create new and comfortable working styles.

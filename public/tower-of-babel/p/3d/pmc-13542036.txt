Defining Heart Failure Endpoint in ATTR Amyloidosis Clinical Trials: Time for an Update?
Authors: Diona Gjermeni, Kevin M Alexander, James L Januzzi, Kenneth W Mahaffey
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis viewpoint highlights the critical need for a standardized definition of heart failure (HF) endpoints in clinical trials specific to transthyretin cardiac amyloidosis (ATTR-CA). Current endpoint definitions vary significantly across standard HF and amyloidosis trials. The objective of this viewpoint is to support clinical trialists by providing practical considerations on how to better define HF events when designing amyloidosis clinical trials, as well as during the adjudication process. We suggest defining HF events based on three major components: acute hospitalizations or inpatient visits for HF, urgent outpatient HF visits, and recurrent outpatient visits. By refining these definitions, we aim to create a framework that accurately reflects the complexities of HF in amyloidosis and promotes consistency in endpoint definitions or HF event adjudication.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s40119-026-00462-8.Keywords: Cardiac amyloidosis, Heart failure, Clinical trials

Key Summary Points

Heart failure (HF) event adjudication and endpoint definition in transthyretin cardiac amyloidosis (ATTR-CA) clinical trials remain challenging due to heterogeneous definitions and variability in the application of diagnostic criteria.These inconsistencies may lead to underestimation of HF burden and reduced accuracy in HF event adjudication, potentially impacting trial endpoints.In this viewpoint, we propose a framework that reflects the complexities of HF in amyloidosis by integrating three principal elements: acute hospitalizations or inpatient visits for HF, urgent outpatient HF visits, as well as recurrent outpatient visits.

Introduction to ATTR Amyloidosis and Rationale for the Viewpoint

Cardiac amyloidosis (CA) is caused by the extracellular deposit of misfolded proteins in the myocardium, and transthyretin amyloidosis (ATTR) is the most common type [1]. ATTR amyloidosis exists in two forms: hereditary or variant ATTR (ATTRv) and wild-type ATTR (ATTRwt) [2]. ATTR can manifest as cardiomyopathy, neuropathy or both, highlighting systemic implications as well as the variable clinical manifestations of the disease. ATTR cardiac amyloidosis (ATTR-CA) is typically associated with heart failure (HF) and atrial arrhythmias.

ATTR-CA is Often Underdiagnosed

ATTR-CA prevalence ranges from 6.1/million in the U.S. to 232/million in endemic regions for inherited amyloidosis such as Portugal [3] and it remains significantly underdiagnosed, particularly among older adults and Black populations [1]. Patients with cardiac amyloidosis are generally enrolled in dedicated disease-specific trials conducted in tertiary referral centers with expertise in amyloidosis [4, 5]. The true representation of patients with amyloidosis in major HF clinical trials, particularly in trials of heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), remains largely unknown [6, 7]. The diagnostic accuracy of ATTR-CA is rapidly increasing [1, 8] through better clinical understanding and development of screening tools. Diagnosis of ATTR-CA relies on a multimodal assessment that incorporates clinicals signs and symptoms, imaging parameters, biomarkers as well as histological characteristics. Typically, the clinical presentation of ATTR-CA begins with HF signs and symptoms, such as dyspnea, fatigue, or dizziness, whereas the echocardiographic profile, similarly to age-related HFpEF, is mainly characterized by wall hypertrophy and markers of increased diastolic pressures in the left ventricle [1]. Because of the systemic nature of ATTR, initial presentation may include extracardiac manifestations. Misconceptions about ATTR manifestation, combined with its non-specific, often late presentation and frequent multiorgan involvement, contribute to its under- or delayed diagnosis.

Importance of Staging ATTR-CA

The progression of ATTR amyloidosis is nonlinear and some of the most common score systems, including the National Amyloidosis Center (NAC, Gillmore et al.), Mayo (Grogan et al.) and Columbia (Cheng et al.) define the progression of ATTR amyloidosis by three stages [1, 9]. Parameters reflecting the multiorgan involvement, such as increased creatinine levels, are incorporated for correct disease staging (Table 1). One of the pillars for amyloidosis staging is the assessment of cardiac biomarkers (NT-proBNP or troponin I/T); these biomarkers are both useful for understanding the presence and severity of the diagnosis, as they are frequently elevated in proportion to disease severity. New York Heart Association (NYHA) class and the daily dose of furosemide or equivalent medication are also important to properly stage ATTR-CA [1, 10].

Although medical treatments exist for ATTR-CA, these are particularly effective in improving health status and reducing events in earlier-stage disease. Currently, there are no reliable medical treatments effective in the management of end-stage HF in patients with ATTR-CA [8–11]. Thus, there is a clear need for correct staging of HF in patients with ATTR-CA in clinical practice. Because these scores are essential for accurate risk stratification and reflect meaningful clinical status in patients with amyloidosis, omitting clinically validated staging parameters (Table 1) from the definition of HF endpoints in amyloidosis clinical trials could be a limitation in how HF events are adjudicated compared to HF events from other etiologies such as ischemic cardiomyopathy.

Current HF Endpoint Definition in Clinical Trials

According to the American Heart Association (AHA), HF progression is categorized in four stages and is defined as follows: asymptomatic individuals at risk for HF (stage A), individuals without symptoms/signs but with structural heart disease (stage B), impaired cardiac function in a symptomatic individual (stage C) and advanced HF with the need for recurrent hospitalizations despite optimization of guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) (stage D) [13].

A consensus report for defining endpoints in clinical trials states that a “HF event” consists of hospitalization for at least 24 h in patients with primary diagnosis of HF and documentation of worsening HF through clinical symptoms, physical examination, laboratory criteria, imaging (invasive and non-invasive), as well as change in medication such as increase of diuretic therapy or use of vasoactive agents or mechanical/surgical intervention [14]. Another routinely used endpoint in clinical trials for patients with HF, consists of “urgent outpatient visit”, in which changes of the oral diuretic intake (ODI) or the need for intravenous diuretic are included in the definition [14, 15]. More recently, a summary article discusses the HF endpoints in pivotal Food and Drug Administration (FDA) trials [16]. A framework that requires one HF sign, one symptom, one objective measure, and rescue therapy with unscheduled events is preferred in established patients with HF in order to capture a more meaningful HF worsening, whereas outpatient worsening, that associate with increased mortality, may be more relevant in prevention trials [16].

While clinical trials that included patients with HFpEF or HF with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) are consistent in defining clinical HF endpoints [6–8, 15], trials including patients with ATTR-CA use a slightly different spectrum of clinical endpoints (Table 2). Major randomized controlled trials (RCTs) in ATTR-CM have evaluated transthyretin stabilizers against placebo using composite endpoints that include mortality and cardiovascular (CV) hospitalization [4, 5]. However, definitions of HF-related hospitalizations or events vary across trials and may extend beyond traditional inpatient admissions to include urgent outpatient encounters for treatment of acute HF decompensation [17, 18]. To our knowledge, there is no dedicated consensus specifically standardizing endpoint definitions for amyloidosis clinical trials. One expert statement has previously proposed general imaging frameworks [2].

Experience from Adjudication of HF Events in Amyloidosis Clinical Trials

Adjudicating HF clinical events in ATTR-CA trials is challenging. Insights were collected from a small group of adjudicators involved in previous amyloidosis trials through a purpose-designed, non-validated survey consisting of open-ended general questions regarding major challenges encountered during adjudication (available in the supplement). The questionnaire assessed the difficulty of applying the HF definition from the study charter, identified commonly missing clinical information (symptoms, physical exam findings, laboratory and imaging parameters, and treatment changes), and evaluated the usability of adjudication forms, including suggestions for improvement. While the definition of a HF events provided in the study charter was described as clear, its application was often limited by insufficient clinical data from sites. In particular, incomplete physical exam documentation such as missing jugular venous pressure assessments, lung findings, and abnormal heart sounds emerged as a recurring deficiency. Even if information from physical exam was not entirely absent, a common challenge was determining whether reported findings represented new or worsening HF, especially in patients with amyloidosis and chronic conditions, where biomarkers are often persistently elevated and clinical status fluctuates subtly. Lastly, there was consensus that the adjudication forms could be streamlined. Particularly it was seen as difficult to accurately adjudicate cases assessing HF presence in non-primary HF hospital admissions.

Aims of the Viewpoint

The evaluation of clinical status in cardiac amyloidosis remains particularly challenging due to the complex and often subtle interplay between restrictive cardiomyopathy, volume status, and systemic involvement. Unlike other forms of HF, cardiac amyloidosis is characterized by progressive extracellular deposition of amyloid fibrils, leading to increased ventricular stiffness, impaired diastolic filling, and a narrow hemodynamic reserve [19]. As a result, small fluctuations in preload or afterload can translate into disproportionate clinical changes, often without overt or easily detectable signs on routine examination. Moreover, standardized documentation of physical examination findings can be incomplete or inconsistent, limiting the ability to accurately assess disease progression or therapeutic response.

This gap underscores the need for a more pathophysiologically grounded framework that integrates specific clinical parameters with meaningful endpoints tailored to the unique features of this population. The aim of this viewpoint is to provide practical considerations for clinical trialists and endpoint adjudication committees on how HF events in ATTR-CA could be more consistently defined in future studies. By addressing variability of clinical presentation of HF in ATTR-CA, complexity of the disease and inconsistencies across current HF event definition in amyloidosis clinical trials, we propose a framework that may better capture the clinical spectrum of HF in amyloidosis and supports greater standardization across amyloidosis trials.

Discussion

HF Hospitalization in Patients with ATTR-CA

Duration of Hospital Admission

The definition of hospitalization for HF in amyloidosis clinical trials (Table 2) differs when compared with the HF hospitalization definition in the consensus report [14] on the duration of stay at an urgent care facility. Two major RCTs for ATTR amyloidosis compared two transthyretin stabilizers, tafamidis and acoramidis, with placebo. The primary endpoints in both studies were all-cause mortality or CV-related hospitalizations, with hospitalizations for worsening HF making up for half of CV hospitalizations [4, 5]. The endpoint hospitalizations for worsening HF, included unplanned visits to an emergency department, urgent care clinic, or a day clinic of < 24 h for the management of HF requiring treatment with an intravenous diuretic. Given recurrent need for medical care in patients with amyloidosis, healthcare visits can have an impact in the quality of life, reduce the time away from family and work, and impact healthcare resources and costs. Since the need for intravenous diuretic therapy in patients with ATTR-CA indicates therapy intensification and likely has prognostic implications, a presentation of < 24 h in a healthcare setting for intravenous diuretics should be considered a valid criterion for defining hospitalizations due to HF worsening (Tables 2 and 3).

Neuropathy and Functional Endpoints

Due to the systemic nature of ATTR and multiorgan involvement the primary hospitalization reason in patients with ATTR may not always directly reflect HF progression, but it may instead be driven by extracardiac symptoms. ATTR often presents with progressive neuropathy (sensory-motor and autonomic) and cardiomyopathy, but the onset time and severity of manifestations vary depending on the specific mutation and between patients. Autonomic neuropathy often appears early, sometimes preceding overt cardiac symptoms by years, and dominates the early clinical picture, overlapping cardiac involvement. It can mask or mimic symptoms of worsening HF such as orthostatic dysregulation and resting tachycardia [20] delaying diagnosis and complicating management of CA. Neuropathy can also obscure symptoms of myocardial ischemia and reduce exercise tolerance, worsening quality of life. Because neuropathy progression correlates with exacerbation of HF and contributes to poorer prognosis in hereditary ATTR [20] (Table 4), it also represents an important determinant of functional capacity. Assessment of functionality such as with the 6-Min Walk test (6MWT) is a reasonable measure to quantify fatigue and exercise capacity and has been used as a therapy efficacy endpoint in various RCTs [4, 5, 21, 22]. Even though potentially challenging to collect in an outpatient study because of the concomitant neuropathy in some patients with ATTR, we suggest adding the 6MWT as a form of functional assessment for outpatient worsening HF in clinical trials.

Initial Hospitalization Reason and the “Typical” HF Symptoms and Signs

The multi-systemic and diverse range of ATTR symptomatic manifestations can lead to an underestimation of disease burden. For instance, 20–70% of patients with CA present with atrial fibrillation. Episodes of brady- or tachyarrhythmia might play an important role in the setting of acute HF decompensation. Besides typical HF symptoms such as increased congestion, leading clinical symptoms in these individuals can be palpitations or dizziness.

Other arrythmias, such as ventricular tachycardia (VT), are also very common in patients with ATTR-CA [9, 23]. New atrio-ventricular (AV) block with the main clinical symptom a syncope or dizziness, which are not considered typical HF symptoms, can sometimes be primary hospitalization reason for worsening HF in patients with ATTR-CA. These patients can also develop angina pectoris with signs of myocardial ischemia (amyloid accumulation in the coronary arteries) that associate with worsening of HF and increased hospitalizations. Further important comorbidities or complications in patients with CA are aortic stenosis and thrombo-embolisms [1]. One study determined that patients with decompensated diastolic HF because of amyloidosis hospitalize mostly because of arrhythmias (50%), acute kidney failure (40%) or acute respiratory failure (25%) [24]. Thus, to optimize identification of patients with ATTR-CA hospitalized for worsening HF, we suggest considering the initial reason for hospitalization as a broader classification variable and to consider hospitalizations for all CV-related issues if patients also have clinical findings consistent with HF worsening (Table 3), rather than limiting to events with primary hospitalization only for HF. Additionally, broadening the spectrum of symptoms and signs used to define worsening of HF may help capture HF events that may not otherwise be recognized. A consensus report for defining cardiac endpoints recommends that a “HF event” requires hospitalization for at least 24 h in patients with primary diagnosis of HF and documentation of HF worsening through clinical symptoms, physical examination, laboratory criteria, imaging, and change or intensification in diuretic medication [14].

Pulmonary edema in cardiac amyloidosis reflects the pathophysiological consequence of markedly elevated left-sided filling pressures due to restrictive ventricular physiology and impaired diastolic relaxation. Amyloid infiltration leads to increased myocardial stiffness, resulting in a steep pressure–volume relationship in which even minor increases in intravascular volume precipitate a rapid rise in left atrial and pulmonary capillary pressures. This, in turn, promotes transudation of fluid into the alveolar space. As such, pulmonary edema represents not only an acute decompensatory event, but also a marker of disease progression and diminishing cardiac reserve [19]. In parallel, pleural effusions are frequently observed in amyloidosis and may arise from a combination of elevated systemic venous pressures, impaired lymphatic drainage, and direct serosal involvement by amyloid deposits [13, 19]. Their presence further reflects advanced hemodynamic compromise and should be considered a relevant clinical feature when assessing disease severity and progression [13]. The signs and specific treatments of cardiogenic pleural effusion were not included in the previous HF endpoint definition and should be considered when defining worsening of HF in ATTR-CA [14]. Moreover, non-invasive respiratory support modalities or mechanical respiration are not part of invasive treatments to define worsening HF. Since acute pulmonary edema can be an important clinical manifestation of worsening HF, especially in amyloidosis, we suggest adding these treatments in the definition. Our suggestion is to consider adding the need for continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP), high-flow nasal canula (HFNC) or mechanical respiration for cardiogenic pulmonary edema as a one of the treatments necessary for defining HF endpoint. From the perspective of clinical event adjudication, broadening the spectrum of symptoms and signs to define worsening HF might help capture HF events that could otherwise go unnoticed.

Implantation of ventricular assist device or heart transplantation indicate a progression of CA towards “end-stage disease” with only limited therapeutic resources available [1]. One amyloidosis RCT categorized participants undergoing these procedures as part of the mortality outcome [5] so we suggest that these procedures should not be considered as components of the HF endpoints in amyloidosis trials.

Incorporating Other Biomarkers

Previous amyloidosis trials considered change in baseline levels of NT-proBNP, rather than NT-proBNP increase above a certain threshold as part of their primary endpoints [5, 12]. Currently, the consensus on definition of CV events suggest the use of increased NT-proBNP in the first 24 h of hospitalization with clinical manifestation of HF symptoms, to determine a HF event in clinical trials [14] without specifying a cut-off. While HF clinical trials including patients with HFpEF [6, 7, 15] used relatively low NT-proBNP cut-offs to define hospitalizations for HF (300–900 pg/ml), the European consensus on management of amyloidosis suggests a NT-proBNP cut-off > 3000 pg/ml for determining progression of ATTR amyloidosis [1]. Nevertheless, increased NT-proBNP is primarily regarded as a marker of disease burden and progression in ATTR-CA [25], and has therefore been predominantly used as an exclusion criterion in major amyloidosis clinical trials rather than as a predefined threshold for endpoint adjudication [18, 22]. In this context, enrolled patients with amyloidosis typically already presented with markedly elevated NT-proBNP levels, often higher than those observed in HFpEF trials [4, 18, 21, 22]. From the perspective of disease pathophysiology NT-proBNP in light chain (AL) amyloidosis reflects not only myocardial wall stress, but also direct cardiomyocyte injury from circulating light chains, which supports its strong biological plausibility as a biomarker and, in some settings, a surrogate of survival response to therapy [26]. In non-AL amyloidosis, NT-proBNP is driven more indirectly by hemodynamic stress, and given that mean values in major RCT populations vary around ~ 2000 pg/ml, this level may serve as a pragmatic empirical threshold to help define clinically meaningful HF decompensation events in clinical trial endpoints (Table 3). However, this approach should be interpreted cautiously and ideally validated prospectively.

Notably, significant changes in NT-proBNP levels are also valuable for tracking the progression of ATTR-CA and correlate with patient’s survival [9, 27]. NT-proBNP fluctuations are used in amyloidosis trials as a parameter for measuring the efficacy of medical treatments without specifying the necessary delta [4, 4, 5].

Evidence suggest that > 20% increase in NT-proBNP increases the risk for CV mortality or HF hospitalization in patients with HF [28]. While the appropriate delta to be considered in BNP or NT-proBNP changes in patients with amyloidosis should be explored further, we suggest that for the purpose of adjudicating HF events in patients with amyloidosis the definition of “significant change” should be left to the adjudicator's arbitrary judgment.

Due to the singular multisystem pathophysiology of amyloidosis, other laboratory parameters might be relevant on defining progression of ATTR-CA. For example, because of the restrictive nature of ATTR-CA and the higher filling pressures required to maintain the cardiac output can result in organ hypoperfusion, leading to primary clinical manifestation of cardiac decompensation through acute kidney injury. Including the reduction of glomerular filtration rate (GFR) might be favorable in optimizing the denotation for worsening HF in patients with ATTR-CA, especially when also one staging score (NAC, Gillmore et. al) considers GFR as an important parameter for staging of ATTR-CA [1] (Table 1).

Ultimately, the role of transthyretin-specific biomarkers in the advancement of ATTR-CA disease remains limited, and as a result of data lacking its implication in disease progression is still unknown [9]. More data are needed about the value of tracking transthyretin as a biomarker of disease severity. Transthyretin is heavily affected by therapies for ATTR-CA with stabilizers increasing its concentration and silencers reducing it; serial measurement of the biomarker may be thus better reserved for monitoring therapy effect than using it as a biomarker for staging disease severity.

Urgent or Recurrent Outpatient Visits for HF

In cardiac amyloidosis even small shifts in congestion may indicate early hemodynamic deterioration before hospitalization. This is particularly relevant given the restrictive physiology, and preload dependence, making patients sensitive to minor volume changes [19]. An increase of ODI can be a sign of amyloidosis progression for example from HFpEF to HFrEF. Accordingly, increased diuretic requirements may reflect disease progression rather than simple symptomatic adjustment. Prior studies, further support this, showing that outpatient diuretic intensification is common and independently associated with adverse outcomes [29] as well as increased mortality [27]. Additionally, combining increased levels of NT-proBNP with intensification of ODI predicted mortality risk in patients with ATTR-CA and it is a universal parameter for determining the progression of ATTR-CA [27].

Clinical trials do include changes in ODI to evaluate the risk for CV hospitalization and outpatient HF worsening endpoints [21, 22]. One study determined that outpatient worsening HF itself, was associated with greater deterioration of functional capacity as assessed by the 6-Min Walk test (6MWT), worse quality of life as assessed by KCCQ-OS score, increases in NT-proBNP levels, and worsening of NAC stage as well as NYHA functional class during the 12-month double-blind follow-up period [22]. Moreover, when one amyloidosis trial included intensification of ODI as part of HF endpoint, estimations of event rates increased from 43% to 58% [21].

Typically, intensification of ODI is defined as initiation or a sustained increase (any increase for ≥ 7 days) in the daily dose of oral loop diuretics [21, 22].

Progression of ATTR-CA to HFrEF indicates progression toward late-stage disease with limited treatment options and has prognostic implications. Thus, collecting information about changes in the ODI is relevant when trying to optimize estimation of HF events, particularly since healthcare contact is often minimized in these patients. Given the increased use of outpatient care for individuals with HF, along with the lower risk profile of individuals with ATTR-CA in current clinical trials and practice, broadening the criteria for determining clinically relevant HF events is necessary. In this regard, we suggest the outpatient change of ODI to be considered as a criteria for defining urgent outpatient visits for HF in ATTR-CA patients (Table 3).

Lastly, in genetic ATTR-CA, the 2021 ESC guidelines underscore the importance of family screening and structured follow-up with bone-tracer scintigraphy, such as SPECT-CT, with cohort data showing a 9.4% disease detection rate at 3-year follow-up among at-risk relatives [30]. Thus, ongoing studies like ACT-EARLY [31], aim to evaluate acoramidis in very early-stage carriers, and to illustrate how therapeutic strategies are moving earlier in the disease course, increasing the relevance of sensitive clinical endpoints, including outpatient diuretic changes, which are already key outcome measures in amyloidosis trials and may become particularly informative in future prevention-focused studies.

Imaging endpoints for clinical trials in ATTR-CA have been previously summarized in a recent workforce [2], so we will not address these here.

Implications and Limitations of the Viewpoint

This report provides a rationale to update the definition of HF in clinical trials of patients with ATTR-CA. Refinement of the current HF events definition is important to address specific features of HF exacerbation in patient populations with amyloidosis. These refinements could help enhance the consistency of event adjudication and provide accurate assessment of HF endpoint in ATTR-CA clinical trials.

A potential implication of the proposed definition is an increase in the number of HF events by broadening the reliance on physical examination findings, which are often incompletely documented and therefore not consistently suitable as a strict adjudication criterion. In addition, incorporating outpatient treatment intensification may better capture “near-hospitalization” episodes, thereby identifying clinically relevant decompensations that would otherwise be missed. Although the proposed categorization of HF events is conceptually straightforward, its added value lies in providing an amyloidosis-specific framework for endpoint definition and adjudication of events. This approach, which is intended to complement rather than replace, existing HF definitions, could ultimately increase the number of positively adjudicated events, aligning with prior observations that only hospitalization-based endpoints alone might not be sufficient to reflect the real ATTR-CA burden.

However, we acknowledge important limitations: this work represents a conceptual synthesis rather than a granular, systematic analysis of prior adjudication experiences across trials. Moreover, while the proposed refinements aim to improve consistency and sensitivity of event capture, their ultimate impact on clinical trial endpoints and outcomes remains uncertain and will require prospective validation.

Wind-power curve anomaly type recognition via image-topology-semantic multi-feature fusion and contrastive learning
Authors: Xiaojuan Zhu, Boyang Pan, Yi Zhong, Hongwen Wei, Haidong Tao, Bo Pang, Kai Liao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWind-power curves form a critical basis for wind farm condition monitoring and anomaly type recognition. Current supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA)-based approaches primarily rely on single-modal numerical or visual features, which constrains their ability to capture both global morphology and local structural deviations in power curves. Furthermore, the shortage of labeled abnormal samples restricts recognition performance. To tackle these challenges, this article proposes an image-topology-semantic multi-feature fusion method under a contrastive learning framework. SCADA scatter data are transformed into grayscale images, from which global image features are extracted using a Vision Transformer. Local topological structures are modeled via Holistically-Nested Edge Detection and graph convolutional networks, while textual anomaly descriptions are encoded by a CLIP text encoder and aligned via a parameterized dual-path fusion module. By jointly integrating global morphology, local topology, and textual physical semantics, the proposed framework resolves the difficulty of distinguishing visually similar wind-power curve anomaly types under limited labeled abnormal samples. The method utilizes a two-stage training strategy: pre-training on external curve datasets and fine-tuning on a simulated wind-power curve dataset, followed by evaluation on real wind farm SCADA data under both known-category anomaly-type recognition and leave-one-anomaly-type-out zero-shot recognition scenarios. Experimental results indicate that the proposed method achieves 89.5% accuracy and 89.4% F1-score with limited-data fine-tuning, 94.2% accuracy and 94.1% F1-score with sufficient-data fine-tuning, and a 78.1% F1-score in leave-one-anomaly-type-out zero-shot recognition, demonstrating its effectiveness for predefined wind-power curve anomaly type recognition in data-scarce settings.Keywords: Wind power curve, Anomaly type recognition, SCADA, Image-topology-semantic multi-feature fusion, Contrastive learningSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction and motivation

In the context of large-scale wind farm development, enhancing the operational efficiency of wind turbines and optimizing operation and maintenance costs have emerged as critical challenges in the wind power industry1. As wind farm capacity and the number of turbines continue to increase, conventional operations and maintenance methods are increasingly unable to meet the real-time condition monitoring and abnormal operation screening of large-scale systems. The supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA) system, as the primary monitoring platform for wind turbines, utilizes continuous, high-frequency data collection to provide a crucial foundation for condition assessment and performance analysis2–4. Among the various performance indicators within the SCADA system, the wind speed-power characteristic curve, which depicts the relationship between wind speed and turbine output power, is a core metric widely used for quantitative evaluation of turbine health status and anomaly recognition. The data quality directly affects the reliability of subsequent analyses5.

However, during actual operation, SCADA data are prone to interference from various factors, such as complex operating conditions, sensor noise, manual power curtailment, and errors in data transmission or storage. These distortions often result in data quality issues, including outliers, missing values, and time-series inconsistencies. Consequently, power data may significantly deviate from their actual characteristics, thereby limiting the effectiveness of traditional numerical analysis-based anomaly recognition methods6–8.

Literature review

Currently, SCADA-based methods for wind-power curve anomaly analysis fall into four primary technical approaches: power curve fitting, data cleaning, anomaly type recognition, and multimodal or contrastive learning.

The first category relies on wind power curve modeling9. This method establishes a theoretical power curve as a benchmark, and observation points that deviate significantly from it are classified as anomalies. Techniques such as Gaussian processes10,11, nonparametric regression12, and models combining smoothing splines with first-order moments3 have demonstrated excellent fitting capabilities. With advances in deep learning, researchers have utilized data-driven models, including deep belief networks5,13, density long short-term memory networks3,14, and optimizers combined with asymmetric error loss functions15,16, which have substantially improved the accuracy of power curve modeling. Furthermore, the application of machine vision technologies such as generative adversarial networks has enabled image-based methods for representing power curve modeling14,17.

The second route directly leverages SCADA data features, often involving data preprocessing. After data preprocessing, this method employs statistical or spatiotemporal features of data for anomaly detection. For instance, anomaly cleaning frameworks integrating physical rules with regression learning or multi-stage composite frameworks are proposed by18–20, respectively, both of which effectively enhance data quality. Recent research has further investigated deep learning, graph-based models, and transfer learning in wind power anomaly detection. A graph convolutional autoencoder for multivariate time-series anomaly detection was proposed21, where a sensor graph is constructed to capture nonlinear relationships between wind speed and power. Transfer learning based on autoencoders across wind turbines is examined in22, demonstrating effective anomaly detection with only a small amount of target-domain data. To further tackle the scarcity of fault samples, data augmentation techniques based on generative models have been explored. For instance, a denoising diffusion network that combines multimodal non-Gaussian modeling and conditional generative adversarial mechanisms is proposed to generate high-fidelity synthetic samples for fault diagnosis23,24.

Based on cleaned data, anomaly type recognition methods aim to distinguish specific abnormal patterns. A spatio-temporal prediction method based on improved DBSCAN and adaptive graph convolutional recurrent networks is proposed25,42, achieving efficient prediction for large-scale wind farms while retaining partial interpretability. Recently, graph neural networks (GNNs) are effectively applied to fault diagnosis; for instance, PIGMind integrates physical energy indicators into graph structures26, GCI-ODG27 employs causal intervention for out-of-distribution generalization, and DCMLDF28 infers dynamic causal graphs. These methods indicate that incorporating physical or causal semantics into GNNs can improve robustness and interpretability. Moreover, meta-learning techniques, including MAML-based 1DCNN29, and CNTE30 have demonstrated potential for few-shot fault detection in wind turbines. However, they are still confined to single-modal numerical data.

Notably, condition monitoring algorithms based on data-image conversion convert SCADA data into images and extract principal components via mathematical morphological operations, effectively converting the abnormal wind-power curve analysis into an image-based classification task31. However, most existing methods still rely exclusively on numerical SCADA parameters and fail to integrate images or semantic descriptions effectively. Hence, it is difficult to comprehensively capture multi-dimensional anomaly characteristics. Furthermore, many deep learning models remain black-box systems, and validation is typically limited on single wind farms with limited cross-environment transferability32. To inspire multimodal feature learning, several advanced intelligent methodologies have emerged, including automatic prompt design via particle swarm optimization33, multi-hop reasoning with relation-based node quality evaluation34, and neural architecture search based on brain storm optimization35. These techniques further motivate the proposed image-topology-semantic fusion framework.

In summary, although existing studies have achieved significant progress, they still face two common bottlenecks. First, traditional single-feature analysis methods cannot fully characterize abnormal features of wind power curves5,14. These curves exhibit complex nonlinear characteristics, yet traditional methods predominantly rely on unimodal analysis focusing solely on power-wind speed relationships. Such approaches demonstrate limited ability to simultaneously capture global structural patterns and local deviations. Consequently, model adaptability under complex operational conditions remains insufficient, significantly compromising recognition accuracy and robustness. Secondly, recognition performance is constrained by data scarcity and environmental variations. Most existing methodologies are validated using limited real-world datasets or simplistically simulated data17,36,37, failing to represent real-world complexities. Notably, there are substantial environmental disparities across geographically distributed wind farms, including variations in wind speed distributions and temperature fluctuations, which ultimately lead to insufficient model generalization capability.

In this article, the problem is formulated as a multi-class anomaly type recognition task, where the goal is to assign each wind power curve image to one of eight predefined abnormal patterns or normal state. The proposed method addresses the above gaps by introducing a multi-feature fusion framework for image-topology-semantic features under contrastive learning. Nevertheless, several critical research gaps remain.

Proposed model

The overall architecture of the proposed method is shown in Fig. 1, which includes five core stages: multi-feature fusion data construction, feature extraction, cross-modal feature fusion, contrastive learning, and test verification. The key innovation of this framework is the introduction of a parameterized dual-path fusion mechanism that jointly fuses visual-structural and semantic-structural features. First, features are extracted from images, topologies, and texts through dedicated encoders. Then, multi-feature fusion representations are aligned and optimized within a contrastive learning framework to enhance the discriminability of abnormal features. To improve model performance, a two-stage training strategy is employed. The model is first pre-trained on external curve datasets and then fine-tuned on a simulated wind-power dataset to provide lightweight domain adaptation to typical wind-speed-power distributions defined by turbine operating parameters.

SCADA data preprocessing and filtering

The SCADA data acquisition process consists of four stages: sensor measurement, data conversion, data transmission, and data storage. Interference at any stage, such as sensor faults, storage errors, or communication disruptions, may introduce anomalies. Moreover, they may lead to substantial deviations in power values from true measurements and fail to reflect the actual operational characteristics of wind turbines. Consequently, this section employs a systematic approach to identify and eliminate anomalous values in the raw SCADA data. Hence, a reliable data foundation for subsequent analysis is established. Through two stages: initial data filtering and binning-based secondary data filtering (Fig. 2), abnormal data are preliminarily filtered and processed to eliminate invalid data points and ensure data accuracy and reliability.

General flow chart of preprocessing.

To ensure the rigor, authenticity, and traceability of the experimental dataset, the construction process and labeling mechanism of the real wind farm SCADA dataset are described in detail. The raw data comes from 12 wind turbines in an actual wind farm cluster, with a 10-min sampling interval and a total time span of 12 consecutive months. A total of 630719 valid labeled samples are obtained. The anomaly labels are established through a strict multi-source verification mechanism: firstly, the official operation and maintenance records, fault logs, shutdown reports, power curtailment schedules and sensor calibration records provided by the wind farm are taken as the basis; secondly, senior wind turbine operation and maintenance experts with rich field experience conduct manual review and confirmation one by one according to the shape of the wind speed-power curve, operating and fault characteristics; finally, physical logic verification is carried out combined with cut-in wind speed, cut-out wind speed, rated power, rotor speed and other constraints to eliminate false anomalies and uncertain labels. All abnormal samples have been confirmed by maintenance records or on-site expert diagnosis, and each sample is uniquely associated with a turbine number, timestamp, and operating state, making it fully traceable. The dataset includes 1 normal state and 8 typical abnormal types of wind power curves, covering common abnormal scenarios such as environmental constraints, equipment faults, control anomalies, and performance degradation. The specific sample distribution of each category is as follows: normal condition (498593), insufficient wind speed (32147), excessive wind speed (18359), power surge (11243), premature rated power (9827), blade imbalance (12671), derated operation (24189), fault shutdown (14273), curve deviation (9417).

The SCADA data are sampled at 10-min intervals. For grayscale image generation, the samples within a 24-h period are aggregated to form one wind speed-power distribution image for multi-feature extraction. The timestamp information is not used as a direct training input, but is retained as auxiliary information during testing to distinguish transient fluctuations from continuous anomalies.

Stage 1: Initial Data Filtering

This stage aims to eliminate gross outliers from the raw SCADA data by establishing filtering rules based on the wind turbine operational principles. The rules are based on two core state parameters: wind speed and output power, supplemented by rotational speed. Invalid data points are identified and removed according to different operating stages of wind turbines.

The first is the shutdown phase. It occurs when the wind speed is below the cut-in speed (v < vin) or exceeds the cut-out speed (v > vout), resulting in zero power output and rotational speed. The second is the start-up or braking phase. It is characterized by a wind speed above the cut-in threshold (v ≥ vin) but with non-positive power output and rotational speed (P ≤ 0, ω ≤ 0). This indicates that the turbine is not in a normal power-generating state.

Stage 2: Binning-based Secondary Data Filtering

Although the initial data filtering step removes gross outliers, residual abnormal points may still exist. There is a persistent issue that anemometer measurements exhibit inherent randomness, leading to significant fluctuations in instantaneous wind speed. This contrasts with the relatively stable power output expected over a given period, rendering individual scatter points unreliable for characterizing the true wind speed-power relationship. Hence, probabilistic statistical methods are needed to extract representative power performance from aggregated data and filter remaining outliers. For reproducibility, all key hyperparameters for SCADA data-to-grayscale image conversion are explicitly specified as follows. The wind speed-power plane is discretized into an N × N grid, where N = 224, consistent with the input size of the Vision Transformer. The logarithmic mapping parameter is set to k = 0.8 to enhance the contrast of low-frequency regions while compressing high-frequency amplitudes. The frequency matrix is normalized to [0, 1] using min–max normalization and then scaled to the grayscale range [0, 255]. Since SCADA data are sampled every 10 min, all samples within a 24-h period are aggregated to construct a single wind speed-power grayscale image that represents the turbine’s daily wind speed-power distribution.

To clarify performance attribution, ablation experiments show that replacing the proposed binning-based filtering and logarithmic mapping with a simple frequency-to-gray conversion results in an approximately 4.2% drop in accuracy and a 4.5% drop in F1-score, confirming that the image construction strategy contributes to the final performance, while the main performance gain still comes from the proposed image-topology-semantic fusion framework.

In the image-topology-semantic framework, textual prompts for different anomalies may share common vocabulary, leading to spurious semantic overlap. Negative Constraint Loss explicitly penalizes the positive similarities between mismatched visual-structural and semantic-structural features, acting as a vital cross-modal regularizer to prevent semantic confusion.

The highest weight is assigned to the NT-Xent loss (λ2), as it serves as the primary driver of alignment across multi-feature representations. The Triplet and Negative Constraint losses function as auxiliary terms, designed to refine inter-class boundaries and ensure cross-modal decoupling without destabilizing the global convergence.

Based on the above algorithm, a wind turbine wind speed-power curve anomaly recognition model using multi-feature fusion information contrastive learning is proposed, as shown in Fig. 7. This model uses the generated wind-power curve images as visual inputs, extracts global image features through the ViT branch, extracts local topological features through the HED-GCN branch, and uses the CLIP text encoder to extract anomaly semantic features. Then, it integrates global image features and text semantic features through a parameterized fusion mechanism. Subsequently, it jointly optimizes the triplet loss to optimize feature alignment. Finally, a pre-training dataset for curve anomaly recognition is constructed to pre-train the model, and data fine-tuning is performed using simulated wind speed-power curves, enabling the evaluation of the operating characteristics of wind speed-power curves and the intelligent recognition of abnormal operating conditions.

The layout of the proposed model based on multi-feature fusion information contrastive learning.

To improve model interpretability, Score-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Score-CAM) is used to visualize the key decision regions on wind speed-power grayscale images, and the contribution of image, topology, and semantic features is quantified. Results show that the model focuses on physically meaningful abnormal regions, making the decision process traceable and interpretable.

ViT captures global wind speed-power patterns, while topological features of HED and GCN depict local edge contours. However, both are purely data-driven and lack connections to physical fault semantics. To address this, a CLIP-based textual semantic branch is introduced. It processes natural language prompts and generates semantic embeddings that are adaptively aligned with visual and topological features via a parameterized dual-path attention fusion module. This enables the model to capture morphological differences and semantic distinctions among anomaly types.

To enable the model to adapt to the subtle characteristics of specific wind farms, a small amount of simulated anomaly data or labeled data is introduced to construct a fine-tuning dataset. Due to the sparsity of wind power curve data, relying solely on single-source wind power data is insufficient to meet the model training demand for high-dimensional features. Therefore, it is necessary to integrate multi-source data as the foundation for training. The model adopts a vision transformer to extract global image features, the holistically nested edge detection, and the graph convolutional network to capture local topological characteristics, and the CLIP text encoder to generate semantic features. These features are fused through the IGT module, with the total loss function as the optimization objective, thus conducting model training in two phases: pre-training and fine-tuning. The steps are as follows:

Step1: Pre-training stage. A unified curve anomaly recognition dataset is constructed, and multi-feature fusion and contrastive learning are employed to integrate five types of multi-source data: Case Western Reserve University Bearing Fault Diagnosis Dataset38, Southeast University Gearbox Fault Dataset39, NASA Bearing Life Prediction Dataset40, Mathematical Curve Dataset, and Synthetic Curve Dataset25. The pre-training dataset contains about 5000 image-text pairs, enabling the model to acquire basic capabilities in curve shape analysis and anomaly recognition.

Step2: Fine-tuning stage. A simulated wind power curve dataset is constructed to meet the specific needs of wind power anomaly recognition tasks. The dataset focuses on the nonlinear characteristics and fault signatures specific to the wind power domain, comprising 1000 image-text pairs. By optimizing the projection matrix and fusion weight parameters of the IGT module, the recognition accuracy of the model in wind power scenarios is enhanced. The dataset simulates four typical wind farm environments, including low-wind-speed inland wind farms represented by plains, medium-wind-speed inland wind farms represented by hilly regions, high-wind-speed coastal wind farms represented by coastal areas, and ultra-high-wind-speed offshore wind farms represented by the open waters of the South China Sea. Specific parameters are detailed in Table 2. For each wind farm type, 250 image-text pairs are generated, resulting in a total of 1000 pairs. The images have a resolution of 224 × 224 pixels, with the horizontal axis representing wind speed from 0 to 30 m/s and the vertical axis representing power.

The simulated dataset covers eight typical abnormal patterns of wind power curves. The insufficient wind speed is characterized by wind speeds below the cut-in speed, resulting in zero power output. The excessive wind speed manifests as wind speeds exceeding the cut-out speed, triggering shutdown protection and zero power output. The premature rated power occurs when the turbine outputs rated power before reaching the rated wind speed. Core evaluation of anomaly type recognition

Based on the experimental data and preprocessing procedure described above, this section further evaluates the proposed method under two settings for anomaly type recognition. The first setting focuses on known-category anomaly type recognition, where all anomaly types in the evaluation set are included in the training process and different amounts of real SCADA data are used for fine-tuning. The second setting focuses on leave-one-anomaly-type-out zero-shot recognition, where one anomaly type is evaluated without using any visual samples or image-text pairs of that type during training, while its textual semantic prototype remains available for inference.

(1) Baseline Models and Evaluation Metrics

To evaluate the recognition performance of the proposed model, five representative baseline models are selected for comparison and denoted as B1-B5. Specifically, B1 is Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN), representing the temporal sequence modeling baseline; B2 is Residual Network-50 (ResNet-50), representing the convolutional image feature extraction baseline; B3 is ViT-Base, representing the Transformer-based visual representation baseline; B4 is GCN-only, representing the graph topology modeling baseline; and B5 is CLIP, representing the image-text semantic alignment baseline. The proposed complete model is denoted as M0, namely IGT-Fusion.

The selected baselines cover the main feature representation paradigms related to wind-power curve anomaly type recognition. TCN models temporal dependencies in SCADA data and provides a comparison with sequence-based representation. ResNet-50 extracts hierarchical convolutional features from wind-power curve images and serves as a conventional image-based baseline. ViT-Base models global spatial relationships through self-attention and is used to evaluate the effect of Transformer-based visual representation. GCN-only models structural relationships among key points of wind-power curve and is used to evaluate the contribution of topology information alone. CLIP aligns wind-power curve images with textual anomaly descriptions in a shared semantic space and serves as the multimodal semantic baseline. Compared with these baselines, IGT-Fusion integrates image, topology, and semantic features through the proposed multi-feature fusion framework.

For fair comparison, all trainable models are optimized under the same basic training settings unless otherwise specified. The Adam optimizer is used with a learning rate of 1 × 10−4, a batch size of 32, and 100 training epochs. Input wind-power curve images are resized to 224 × 224 pixels and normalized using mean [0.485, 0.456, 0.406] and standard deviation [0.229, 0.224, 0.225].

(3) Experiment II: Unseen-Category Anomaly Type Recognition under Zero-Shot Evaluation

Experiment II evaluates zero-shot anomaly type recognition under a leave-one-anomaly-type-out protocol. Unlike Experiment I, where all anomaly types are included during model training, this experiment removes all image samples and image-text pairs of one selected anomaly type from task-specific pre-training and fine-tuning in each evaluation round. The textual description of the selected anomaly type is retained as a semantic prototype. Therefore, zero-shot recognition in this work refers to recognizing an anomaly type without visual training samples, while its textual semantic description is available for semantic matching.

In each round, one anomaly type is selected as the evaluated unseen anomaly type, including insufficient wind speed, excessive wind speed, power surge, premature rated power, blade imbalance, derated operation, fault shutdown, and curve deviation. All image samples and image-text pairs associated with the selected anomaly type are removed from the task-specific pre-training and fine-tuning datasets. The remaining anomaly types and the normal class are used for model training and fine-tuning. During inference, the textual semantic prototype of the evaluated unseen anomaly type participates in similarity-based classification together with the other candidate category prototypes.

In this experiment, B1-B4 are not directly applicable to the zero-shot setting because they rely on fixed supervised classification heads and cannot classify categories that are absent from training without modifying or retraining the classifier. Therefore, the comparison focuses on models that support semantic-prototype-based classification. B5 is used as the image-text semantic baseline. In addition, an IGT-Fusion variant without the topology branch is included to examine the effect of local topological information. The compared models include B5, IGT-Fusion w/o topology, and the complete model M0.

For each test sample, the fused visual-topological representation is compared with the semantic prototypes of all candidate categories. The similarity score reflects the degree of match between the input wind-power curve and each candidate anomaly description, but it is not interpreted as a probability value. The category with the highest similarity score is selected only when the score exceeds the decision threshold θ, which is determined on the validation set and fixed during testing. If all anomaly similarity scores are below θ, the sample is classified as the normal state.

These results indicate that the proposed method provides low-latency inference and linear scalability across turbines, making it suitable for practical SCADA-based wind farm monitoring.

Conclusions

This article systematically investigates a novel anomaly-type recognition framework for wind power curves that fuses image, topological and semantic information via multi-feature fusion. The main findings are summarized as follows:

The proposed multi-feature fusion algorithm significantly outperforms conventional benchmark models, accurately recognizing predefined anomaly types in wind-power curves. The evolution from dual-feature to triple-feature fusion demonstrates significant enhancements in class discriminability and model robustness for high-dimensional wind power anomaly type recognition.

In addition, the simulated wind-power curve dataset cannot fully cover all real operating conditions, and future work will focus on larger-scale cross-wind-farm validation and online incremental updating.

Data on the analysis of draft genome sequence of Raoultella ornithinolytica isolate carrying antimicrobial resistance genes, plasmid and CRISPR-Cas system
Authors: Anna Karpenko, Yulia Mikhaylova, Andrey Shelenkov, Aleksey Tutelyan, Vasiliy Akimkin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractEnvironmental bacterial species Raoultella ornithinolytica is an emerging pathogen becoming increasingly important in causing human infections. Thus far, the clinical isolates of this species have not exhibited multidrug resistance very often, but some reports underline the necessity for continuous monitoring of this potentially dangerous pathogen. Currently, epidemiological surveillance and antimicrobial resistance investigations of any bacterial pathogen usually rely on whole genome sequencing, which is becoming more affordable while providing increasingly important data in the recent years. However, R. ornithinolytica genomic information is scantily presented in public databases. Here, we report, to the best of our knowledge, the first whole genome sequence and corresponding raw data for a clinical R. ornithinolytica isolate from Russian Federation, which carried antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes, virulence factors, one plasmid, and CRISPR-Cas system of type I-F. The data provided will facilitate epidemiological surveillance and antimicrobial resistance monitoring of this emerging pathogen.Keywords: Whole genome sequencing, Antibiotic resistance, Virulence, Core genome analysis

Background

Raoultella ornithinolytica is a Gram-negative bacteria belonging to the Enterobacteriaceae [1], that was initially classified as Klebsiella ornithinolytica, but was transferred and renamed to the new genus when advanced phylogenetic testing became available. The number of reports regarding human infections, including the cases of bacteremia, caused by R. ornithinolytica has increased, while some of them led to mortality in immunocompromised patients [[2], [3], [4]]. Most infections in young patients were related to trauma or associated with healthcare [5]. Correct identification of R. ornithinolytica is rather difficult since some instruments may misidentify it as Klebsiella oxytoca [4].

Despite the fact that this pathogen is involved in multicenter epidemiological monitoring of antibiotic-resistant microorganisms in Russia [6] and several clinical isolates were reported and characterized [7], no whole genome sequences of clinical Russian isolates have been uploaded to public databases yet, to the best of our knowledge. Thus, the availability of such data would be beneficial for epidemiological studies of this pathogen worldwide.

In this report, we present the data for the first genome of clinical R. ornithinolytica isolate from Russia and provide phenotypic and genotypic antimicrobial resistance information, as well as plasmid and CRISPR-Cas system characterization.

Data Description

The isolate under investigation CriePt486 was obtained in Moscow tertiary care hospital in 2021 from the sputum of the patient who was admitted there due to acute Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) and discharged from the hospital upon ten days of successful amikacin inhalation treatment.

Initially, whole genome sequencing on Illumina NextSeq instrument yielded 2,679,478 pairs of short reads of length 150. The reads were prepared for assembly by Q15 filtering, quality trimming at the end of the reads, removal of library adapter sequences, removal of A/T stretches and short reads (less than 60 bp). Upon this procedure, 2,679,425 pairs were left and used in assembly process. The minimal contig length for assembly was set to 500.

The size of the assembled genome was approximately 5.6 Mb. Main characteristics of the draft genome assembly and its annotation features are presented in Table 1.

The clinical R. ornithinolytica isolate possessed a phenotypic resistance to ampicillin (AMP) and amoxicillin (AMO), which complied well with the WGS data obtained (Figure 1). In silico searching for plasmids revealed IncFIB (pKPHS1) plasmid replicon of about 111 kb length. According to the prediction by MOB-suite [9], a plasmid from the CriePt486 was non-mobilizable and did not carry any AMR or virulence factors. The closest database match for this plasmid was revealed for Klebsiella oxytoca plasmid pKO_JKo3_2 (acc. number AP014953) of the same type, with which it had a 98% similarity. The reference plasmid also did not carry any resistance or virulence determinants.

A type I-F CRISPR-Cas system was identified in CriePt486 chromosome that included intact and apparently functional cas1, cas3-cas2, csy1, csy2, csy3 and cas6 genes. The scheme of the system is shown in Fig. 2.

The scheme of the CRISPR-Cas system of R. ornithinolytica isolate CriePt486.

We have performed the homology search for the Cas1 protein possessing endonuclease activity, which revealed Cas1f protein (acc. number WP_128321335.1) from R. ornithinolytica having exactly the same sequence as translated nucleotide sequence of Cas1 from CriePt486.

In order to perform reliable phylogenetic analysis, we compared our isolate to 130 reference genomes available at NCBI RefSeq database, for which the assembly level was reported as either ‘Chromosome’, ‘Scaffold’ or ‘Complete Genome’ (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/datasets/taxonomy/54291/, accessed on October 14, 2023).

The maximum likelihood tree was based on 3743 core genes and is presented in Figure S1. We selected the clade including CriePt486 and three closest reference isolates from this tree. This set of isolates shared 4478 core genes, and the resulting tree is shown in Fig. 3.

Maximum likelihood tree for the reference genomes most similar to CriePt486.

At the same time, the number of genes in accessory genome exceeded 15,000 that shows high genomic variability and potential to acquire pathogenic factors in future for global R. ornithinolytica population.

The closest reference genome was GCF_030717445 (strain RSM7096 from Uganda), while two other included GCF_010594705 (strain AS012358, USA) and GCF_001598295 (strain NBRC 105727). RSM7096 and NBRC 105727 shared the set of antimicrobial resistance genes with CriePt486, while AS012358 additionally possessed beta-lactamase genes blaLAP-1 and blaTEM-1B, which were likely to be acquired via plasmid transfer.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Sample characterization

Initially, analyzed bacterial culture was identified incorrectly by time-of-flight mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS) with the VITEK MS (bioMerieux, Marcy-l’Étoile, France) as Klebsiella pneumoniae, but later a correct identification was obtained, which was additionally confirmed by whole genome sequencing. This isolate was tested for susceptibility/resistance to the following drugs by the Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC) method using a VITEK 2 Compact 30 analyzer (bioMerieux, Marcy-l’Étoile, France): ampicillin (AMP), amoxicillin (AMO), piperacillin (PIC), cefepime (FEP), ceftazidime (CZD), cefotaxime (CTX), ceftriaxone (CRO), imipenem (IPM), meropenem (MEM), ertapenem (ETP), gentamicin (GMN), tobramycin (TOB), levofloxacin (LVX), and ciprofloxacin (CIP). EUCAST clinical breakpoints, version 11.0 (https://www.eucast.org/clinical_breakpoints/, accessed on 20 December 2021), were applied to interpret the susceptibility/resistance results obtained.

DNA Isolation, Genome Sequencing, and Annotation

Genomic DNA was isolated with the DNeasy Blood and Tissue kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany); then, the Nextera™ DNA Sample Prep Kit (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA) was applied to prepare paired-end library for whole-genome sequencing of the isolate on an Illumina NextSeq 2000 platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). The quality and quantity of libraries were confirmed by capillary gel electrophoresis (Agilent Bioanalyzer 2100, Agilent, Santa Clara, CA, USA) and Qubit 4.0 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, AS, USA).

SPAdes version 3.15.2 [10] was used to obtain the genome assembly. Initial genome annotation was implemented using the NCBI Prokaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline (PGAP) [11]. The pipeline described earlier [12,13] was used for the raw data filtration and assembled genome processing and annotation. Resfinder 4.3.0 software was used for antimicrobial gene detection (https://cge.cbs.dtu.dk/services/ResFinder/, accessed on October 14, 2023, using default parameters). Virulence factors were revealed using the VFDB (http://www.mgc.ac.cn/VFs/main.htm, accessed on October 14, 2023, using default parameters).

CRISPRCasFinder [14] was used to identify the presence of CRISPR/Cas systems in the genomes studied. Plasmid replicon type was revealed by MOB-suite [9] using default parameters (mob_typer version 3.1.2).

Since no MLST or cgMLST typing scheme is currently available for R. ornithinolytica, phylogenetic analysis was performed by building maximum likelihood tree based on ad hoc core genome using roary 3.13.0 [15] and RAxML 8.2.11 [16].

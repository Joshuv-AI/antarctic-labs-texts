Exosome-related gene identification and diagnostic model construction in hepatic ischemia-reperfusion injury
Authors: Yujuan You, Shoulin Chen, Binquan Tang, Xianliang Xing, Huanling Deng, Yiguo Wu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractHepatic ischemia-reperfusion injury (HIRI) may cause severe hepatic impairment, acute hepatic insufficiency, and multiorgan system collapse. Exosomes can alleviate HIRI. Therefore, this study explored the role of exosomal-related genes (ERGs) in HIRI using bioinformatics to determine the underlying molecular mechanisms and novel diagnostic markers for HIRI. We merged the GSE12720, GSE14951, and GSE15480 datasets obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database into a combined gene dataset (CGD). CGD was used to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) based on a comparison of the HIRI and healthy control cohorts. The impact of these DEGs on HIRI was assessed through gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and gene set variation analysis (GSVA). ERGs were retrieved from the GeneCards database and prior studies, and overlapped with the identified DEGs to yield the set of exosome-related differentially expressed genes (ERDEGs). Functional annotations and enrichment pathways of these genes were determined using Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analyses. Diagnostic models for HIRI were developed using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression and support vector machine (SVM) algorithms. Key genes with diagnostic value were identified from the overlap, and single-sample gene-set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was conducted to evaluate the immune infiltration characteristics. A molecular regulatory interaction network was established using Cytoscape software to elucidate the intricate regulatory mechanisms of key genes in HIRI. Finally, exosome score (Es) was obtained using ssGSEA and the HIRI group was divided into the Es_High and Es_Low groups based on the median Es. Gene expression was analyzed to understand the impact of all genes in the CGD on HIRI. Finally, the relative expression levels of the five key genes in the hypoxia-reoxygenation (H/R) model were determined using quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR). A total of 3810 DEGs were identified through differential expression analysis of the CGD, and 61 of these ERDEGs were screened. Based on GO and KEGG enrichment analyses, the ERDEGs were mainly enriched in wound healing, MAPK, protein kinase B signaling, and other pathways. GSEA and GSVA revealed that these genes were mainly enriched in the TP53, MAPK, TGF, JAK-STAT, MAPK, and NFKB pathways. Five key genes (ANXA1, HNRNPA2B1, ICAM1, PTEN, and THBS1) with diagnostic value were screened using the LASSO regression and SVM algorithms and their molecular interaction network was established using Cytoscape software. Based on ssGSEA, substantial variations were found in the expression of 18 immune cell types among the groups (p < 0.05). Finally, the Es of each HIRI patient was calculated. ERDEGs in the Es_High and Es_Low groups were enriched in the IL18, TP53, MAPK, TGF, and JAK-STAT pathways. The differential expression of these five key genes in the H/R model was verified using qRT-PCR. Herein, five key genes were identified as potential diagnostic markers. Moreover, the potential impact of these genes on pathways and the regulatory mechanisms of their interaction network in HIRI were revealed. Altogether, our findings may serve as a theoretical foundation for enhancing clinical diagnosis and elucidating underlying pathogeneses.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-73441-5.Keywords: Hepatic ischemia-reperfusion injury, Exosome, Immune microenvironment, GEO, Diagnostic markerSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Biomarkers

Introduction

Hepatic ischemia-reperfusion injury (HIRI) occurs in various medical conditions, including hepatectomy, liver transplantation, and hemorrhagic shock1. HIRI can result in acute liver failure and multiple organ dysfunction, thereby markedly impacting the prognosis of patients with liver diseases2. The main mechanisms of HIRI include macrophage polarization, apoptosis, cell membrane sodium pump inactivation, mitochondrial autophagy, calcium overload, DNA damage, immune activation, oxidative stress, and inflammatory cascade3. However, the exact mechanisms underlying HIRI remain unclear. Moreover, no successful treatments are available for this condition, and the pathological mechanism of HIRI must be further explored. Therefore, key molecular targets should be identified to reduce liver injury and provide therapeutic targets for the clinical treatment of HIRI.

Exosomes are nanoscale (approximately 30–150 nm in size), membrane-bound extracellular vesicles produced by stem cells and released into the extracellular environment. These vesicles contain various molecular components from donor cells, including RNA, DNA, lipids, and metabolites, which determine their structure, characteristics, and functions4. As exosomes engage target cells via surface ligand attachment and disperse their encapsulated components, recipient cells regulate numerous biological functions. Notably, exosomes are involved in cell survival, inflammation, neurogenesis, angiogenesis, apoptosis, and fibrosis, and regulate the pathological processes of recipient cells5. Overall, exosomes have remarkable therapeutic potential for chronic wound healing, nerve injury, and cardiovascular dysfunction6.

Exosomes derived from mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) are used in regenerative medicine for signal transduction between cells and tissues7. MSC-derived exosomes (MSC-exos) exhibit protective effects against organ injuries, including those of the brain, lungs, kidneys, and liver. These exosomes possess anti-inflammatory, antioxidative, and anti-apoptotic properties, and promote regeneration8. Exosomes released by MSCs play vital molecular regulatory roles in ischemia-reperfusion injury9. In a mouse model of ischemic cerebral damage, dental pulp stem cell-sourced exosomes ameliorated inflammation in the brain tissue. This exosome type may possess anti-inflammatory properties, which might be due to the inhibition of the HMGB1/TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB pathway10. Exosomes derived from MSCs also alleviate ischemia-reperfusion injury by suppressing the expression of autophagy and apoptosis molecules, such as caspase-3, mTOR, and LC3II, via miR-20a in the liver11. Adipose-derived MSCs reduce HIRI by mitigating mitochondrial fission, and enhancing mitochondrial fusion and mitochondrial regeneration after hepatectomy12. Nong et al. administered exosomes from pluripotent stem cell-derived mesenchymal stromal cells (hiPSC-MSC-Exos) to rats with HIRI; they found significantly reduced hepatocyte necrosis and sinusoidal congestion, number of macrophages and neutrophils, and level of inflammatory factors. Furthermore, the expression of the apoptotic protein caspase-3 was reduced, whereas that of the anti-apoptotic protein, Bcl-2, was increased. These data suggest that hiPSC-MSC-exos exhibit anti-inflammatory and anti-apoptotic effects on hepatocytes13. Exosomes reprogrammed with CD47 and HuR enable targeted delivery of CsA and miR inhibitors to hepatocytes. These strategies protect the mitochondria by inhibiting permeability transition pore (mPTP) opening, enhancing mitochondrial transcription factor A (TFAM) expression, and stabilizing mitochondrial function, thereby preventing HIRI progression14. Exosome-based therapy may be a novel approach for HIRI. However, the molecular mechanisms underlying exosomal-related genes (ERGs) in HIRI remain unclear.

This study aimed to assess ERG expression in patients with HIRI using the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) and ERG datasets, and identify ERDEGs and their biological functions. The LASSO regression and support vector machine (SVM) algorithms were used to establish an HIRI diagnosis model for selecting key genes with diagnostic value, analyzing immune infiltration characteristics of key genes via single-sample gene-set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA), and establishing a molecular interaction regulatory network using the Cytoscape software. Finally, the expression of the key genes in vivo were verified via quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) to understand the complex mechanism of key gene expression in the regulation of HIRI and their diagnostic value in HIRI.

Diagnostic model for HIRI

A univariate logistic regression model was generated based on the 61 ERDEGs to determine their diagnostic value in HIRI; 56 ERDEGs were statistically significant (p < 0.05; Table S3).

Based on these, a LASSO shrinkage and selection operator regression model was constructed for the diagnosis of HIRI. LASSO regression model (Fig. 7A) and LASSO variable trajectory (Fig. 7B) were used for visualization. The LASSO regression model included 16 ERDEGs (model genes), ADRA1B, ANXA1, ARPC5L, DPP4, GTF2I, HNRNPA2B1, HSP90AA1, ICAM1, PTEN, RAB27A, RAB27B, RAB5A, TGFBR1, THBS1, TLR4, and TUG1. The SVM model was established using 56 ERDEGs and the SVM algorithm. The number of genes that yielded the lowest error rate (Fig. 7C) and highest accuracy (Fig. 7D) was 7. Finally, we identified seven ERDEGs, ICAM1, PTEN, THBS1, ARF6, HNRNPA2B1, ANXA1, and STAT3.

A calibration curve was generated, and the performance of the HIRI model in predicting the actual outcomes was evaluated using the fit between the predicted and actual probabilities under different conditions (Fig. 8B). The calibration line deviated slightly from the ideal diagonal model, but was a relatively close fit. A DCA plot was generated to assess the clinical practicability of the HIRI diagnostic models based on the model genes from the CGD (Fig. 8C). The line of the model remained stable within a range and was higher than all positives and negatives. The model offered a higher net benefit, indicating its good performance. ROC curves of linear predictors for the logistic regression models in different groups from the CGD also revealed good diagnostic effects (Fig. 8D).

To explore the difference in gene sets (h.all.v7.4.symbols.gmt) between the linear predictors in the high and low groups in the logistic regression model, GSVA was performed on all genes from the CGD. Differential expression between the linear predictors in the high and low groups in the logistic regression model was analyzed and visualized using a heat map (Fig. 8E).

The TFs binding to the five key genes were obtained from the ChIPBase database. In addition, the mRNA-TF regulatory network (Fig. 9B), comprising 4 key genes (ANXA1, HNRNPA2B1, ICAM1, THBS1) and 16 TFs (Table S5), was constructed and visualized using Cytoscape.

Following analysis of the CTD, we identified potential drugs or compounds associated with the key genes. An mRNA-drug regulatory network was generated using the Cytoscape software (Fig. 9C). Four key genes (ANXA1, ICAM1, PTEN, and THBS1) and 17 drugs or molecular compounds were identified (Table S6). Finally, the GeneMANIA website was used to predict genes similar to our key genes, and an interaction network was generated to visualize their physical interactions, shared protein domains, and gene interactions (Fig. 9D).

Discussion

Liver ischemia causes ATP depletion, increased ROS, calcium overload, and other damages, leading to hepatic mitochondrial dysfunction, hepatocyte apoptosis, oxidative stress, and inflammatory reactions after reperfusion3,18. However, no effective prevention or treatment measures exist. Exosomes exhibited significant therapeutic potential for ischemia-reperfusion injury19. Through influencing functional alterations in calcium channels20, initiating the NLRP3 inflammasome pathway21, and suppressing autophagy11, exosomes contribute to the onset and progression of HIRI.In addition, exosomes may serve as molecular markers for cancer diagnosis; supported by the different concentrations of exosomes found in the urine of patients with renal cell carcinoma22. However, the molecular targets and interactions of exosomes in HIRI and their diagnostic value remain unclear. Therefore, we explored the potential molecular mechanisms of exosomes in HIRI and their diagnostic value through bioinformatics methods.

A total of 61 ERDEGs, which were mainly linked to cell endocytosis and inflammatory response pathways, were identified in this study. GSEA and GSVA revealed that these genes were mainly enriched in the TP53, MAPK, TGF, JAK−STAT, MAPK, and NFKB pathways. Using LASSO regression and the SVM algorithm, we established an HIRI diagnosis model and identified five key genes (ANXA1, HNRNPA2B1, ICAM1, PTEN, and THBS1). All five genes exhibited significant diagnostic values using strategies such as Calibration and DCA analysis. The expression level differences of key genes in CGD between HIRI and Normal groups are consistent with the in vitro cell experiment results, and the expression matrix analysis suggests a certain correlation and functional similarity among key genes. This provides a strong theoretical basis for our subsequent research on molecular mechanisms. Categorizing the HIRI group into high and low groups based on the median Es of key genes showed significant enrichment in the IL18, TP53, MAPK, TGF, and JAK-STAT pathways, among other pathways related to inflammation. These results suggested that the exosome-related key genes primarily influence the inflammatory response pathway in the process of HIRI, providing new theoretical basis and potential molecular targets for the treatment of HIRI via exosomes.

Annexin A1 (ANXA1), known as annexin due to its bonding with the phospholipid membrane of cells, is the first member to be discovered in the annexin superfamily23. As a calcium-dependent phospholipid binding protein, ANXA1 mediates the anti-inflammatory effect of glucocorticoids by inhibiting phospholipase A2 activity and limiting arachidonic acid supply for prostaglandin synthesis, thereby inhibiting inflammation24. Based on in vitro experiments of renal tubular epithelial cells, the ANXA1 protein interacted with NF-κB p65 to inhibit NF-κB signaling and downregulate pro-inflammatory factors, reducing endothelial cell inflammation25. It is noteworthy that in HIRI, NF-κB activation may drive the expression of various inflammatory factors (TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β), resulting in an increase in inflammation and cell damage26. This indicates that inhibiting the NF-κB signaling pathway might alleviate the inflammatory response, thereby mitigating liver damage caused by HIRI. This was consistent with our functional enrichment study. In our study, immune infiltration analysis (ssGSEA) of HIRI and Normal groups suggested that ANXA1 correlated with most immune cells and positively correlated with neutrophils. This implies that ANXA1 may participate in HIRI through mediating the activity of Neutrophils. In fact, In a mouse model of cerebral ischemia, ANXA1 promoted the phagocytosis of neutrophils to platelets and regression of post-cerebral ischemia inflammatory responses, thereby playing a protective role in cerebral ischemia reperfusion injury. Thus, AnxA1 may have clinical potential in stroke prediction and treatment27. ANXA1 exhibited significantly diagnostic effect in HIRI according to ROC curve analysis(AUC = 0.89). This suggests that ANXA1 may exert a protective effect against HIRI by mediating immune cell infiltration and anti-inflammatory regulation, making it a potential therapeutic target.

Heteroplasmic ribonucleoprotein A2B1 (hnRNPA2B1) is an RNA-binding protein composed of two subunits, HNRNPA2 and HNRNPB1 hnRNPA2B1 plays a vital role in RNA metabolism, including mRNA/miRNA processing and m6A RNA binding28. hnRNPA2B1 is essential for exosome formation and function, regulates selective RNA packaging and transport, and influences exosome composition and function29. For example, hnRNPA2B1, through binding to the GGAG sequence of miR-934, mediates the packaging of miR-934 into exosomes in rectal cancer cells and its transfer to macrophages30; The p53 G245S mutant interacts with hnRNPA2B1, enhancing the translation of AGAP1 through mRNA stabilization and modulating its expression. These interactions facilitate exosome formation, driving the proliferation and metastasis of esophageal cancer cells31. Interestingly, hnRNPA2B1 regulates PTEN and the PI3K/AKT pathway, affecting M2 macrophage polarization and ultimately promoting liver metastasis of colorectal cancer cells32. In our study, enrichment analysis(KEGG) revealed ERDEGs involved in Endocytosis and PI3K-Akt pathway. In the rat liver ischemia reperfusion model, targeting the PTEN pathway to regulate the PI3K/Akt pathway can mitigate liver ischemia reperfusion injury33. These suggests that regulating the PI3K/Akt pathway might reduce inflammation, thereby alleviating liver ischemia reperfusion injury (HIRI). The above reveals the key role of hnRNPA2B1 and PTEN in mediating intercellular communication during the formation of exosomes and the progression of disease. This aligns with our results. Moreover, the nomograms based on model gene expression reveal that HNRNPA2B1 exhibits the highest diagnostic utility for HIRI. Besides, q-PCR results indicated that HNRNPA2B1 was significantly upregulated in the HIRI group. These findings suggested that hnRNPA2B1 is a potential molecular marker for predicting HIRI. It may affect the modulation of the pathogenesis of HIRI through the regulation of the formation process of exosomes, which worth further research into the mechanism by which hnRNPA2B1 mediates the formation of exosomes and participates in HIRI.

Intercellular adhesion molecule-1 (ICAM-1) is an immunoglobulin expressed in most cell types. By functioning as a glycoprotein and adhesion receptor on the cell surface, ICAM-1 participates in cell signal transduction, activation, immune responses, and inflammation34. In inflammatory reactions, ICAM-1 expression is notably increased on the surface of epithelial and immune cells35. HIRI is often accompanied by an increase in cell adhesion factors. During the initial phase of HIRI, damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) are released from damaged cells, and increased IL-1β is stimulated by resident Kupffer cells that act as sentinels. Consequently, this upregulation activated ICAM1 on liver macrophages, promoting an increase in pro-inflammatory mediators36. This is consistent with our GSVA research, IL-18 is an important pro-inflammatory cytokine, and its signaling pathway plays a key role in inflammatory diseases37. Targeting IL-18 signaling pathways might help alleviate the inflammatory response and tissue damage in HIRI. In addition, an atherosclerosis model suggested that the release of miR23a-3p from extracellular vesicles increases ICAM-1, VCAM-1, and E-selectin expression in endothelial cells. This is achieved by targeting Dusp5 and promoting ERK1/2 phosphorylation, which stimulates intimal inflammation, arterial wall thickening, and luminal narrowing, thereby promoting monocyte adhesion38. These researches imply that ICAM-1 participates in the onset and development of HIRI, and promotes the activation and release of inflammatory signals during HIRI. This is consistent with the functional enrichment and immunoinfiltration analysis results in our study. Interference with ICAM-1 expression significantly inhibits this binding and weakens the suppressive effect of tumor exosomes on CD8 T cells39. The MCP Counter algorithm used in our study suggested a positive correlation between ICAM1 and immune cells such as neutrophils, indicating that ICAM-1 might mediate immune cell interactions through the exosomes to promote HI/R-induced liver damage. Therefore, ICAM-1 is a potential factor worth further exploration. In our study, ROC analysis suggested that the difference in ICAM1 expression in HIRI had high accuracy between the between the HIRI and the Normal groups(AUC > 0.9). These results suggest that ICAM-1 holds potential in the regulation of HIRI through exosomes.

Phosphatase and tensin homolog protein (PTEN) is the first discovered tumor suppressor gene with phosphatase activity. The abnormal expression of PTEN is related to cancer cell growth, apoptosis, adhesion, migration, invasion, and other processes40. PTEN dysfunction causes PI3K/AKT overactivation, leading to accelerated tumorigenesis and resistance. Consequently, PTEN is a tumor suppressor41. PTEN also plays an important regulatory role in liver diseases. Alterations in PTEN expression and activity in hepatocytes may be important molecular events during HIRI. Reduced ALT level, liver injury histological score, macrophage activation, neutrophil accumulation and pro-inflammatory factor expression showed that PTEN knockout activates β-catenin, then inhibits Foxo1 and reduces TLR4-driven inflammatory response to exert hepatocyte protective42. During HIRI, PTEN activation inhibits the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway, resulting in reduced activity of cytoplasmic malic enzyme 1 (ME1), which reduces NADPH levels and increases ferroptosis, thereby exacerbating hepatic damage43. Our study indicated the PTEN had a negative correlation with THBS1. It is worth noting that THBS1 and PTEN are involved in the regulation of immune modulation and inflammatory response-mediated neuroprotection in ischemic stroke44. Emerging evidence also highlights the role of PTEN in protecting the liver from ischemia-reperfusion injury, which is consistent with our findings that PTEN might be a potential therapeutic target for HIRI. Based on transcriptome analysis, miR-25 released from BMSC exosomes may alleviate I/R injury by targeting the PTEN-p53 signaling pathway and reducing hypoxia-induced hepatocyte apoptosis in the HIRI mouse model45. This is consistent with our enrichment analysis involving the p53 signaling pathway in the Es_High and the Es_Low group. Research confirms that TP53 plays a critical role in cellular stress response, apoptosis, and DNA repair46,47. This suggests that regulating TP53 signaling pathways might help balance cell survival and apoptosis, thereby mitigating cell damage caused by HIRI. Therefore, PTEN might involved in exosome regulation in HIRI and has remarkable research potential.

Thrombosponin1 (THBS1) serves multiple roles as a glycoprotein secreted from platelets, macrophages, and adipocytes, and participates in numerous biological activities, including tissue remodeling, wound healing, angiogenesis, and inflammation regulation. Furthermore, THBS1 can modulate the activation of transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1)48. An animal experiment suggested that MSCs inhibit THBS1 secretion by platelets, reduce THBS1-mediated TGF-β activation and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), and alleviate hepatocyte stress and EMT induced by hepatectomy, thereby protecting liver injury from HIRI49. Consistent with our enrichment analysis, the TGF pathway may be involved in HIRI according to our Es analysis. Interestingly, TGF-β may exacerbate liver injury by promoting fibrosis and immune response50,51. This suggests that inhibiting the TGF-β signaling pathway may help reduce tissue fibrosis, alleviate organ damage and dysfunction caused by HIRI. Moreover, THBS1 is the predominant protein in exosomes from oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) cells. THBS1 is involved in polarizing macrophages and inducing the M1 phenotype, which depends on OSCC-derived exosomes; this facilitates a novel paracrine loop between cancer and tumor cells, activating p38, Akt, and SAPK/JNK signaling in macrophages. Hence, exosomes from OSCC cells upregulate the M1 phenotype via THBS1 in macrophages52. This suggested that THBS1 might act as an exosomes content and participate in the signaling between immune cells. Interestingly, THBS1 and ICAM1 exhibited significant differences in NSCLC cell-derived exosomes, suggesting that their interactions in the tumor microenvironment and the immune system might influence tumor growth and progression53. Additionally, THBS1 and PTEN play a role in regulating immune modulation and inflammatory response-mediated neuroprotection in ischemic stroke44. THBS1 and ANAX1 can participate in immune regulation mediated by exosomes in heart transplantation through regulating T cell activation and migration, which subsequently impacts graft rejection54.Immunoinfiltration analysis in our study indicated a positive correlation between THBS1 and neutrophils. Thus, THBS1 might mediate the role of exosomes in intercellular signal transduction in neutrophils. THBS1 possesses the potential to regulate the signal axis involved in exosome-mediated HIRI immunomodulation for therapeutic purposes.

During the ischemic phase, hepatocytes release DAMPs and recruit immune cells from circulating tissues55. After reperfusion, the immune cells in the liver release several inflammatory factors, resulting in an extensive inflammatory response and severe HIRI. Therefore, controlling this immune-mediated inflammatory process in the liver is crucial for the effective management of HIRI36.This is consistent with our KEGG results, which indicate that the enriched pathways related to inflammation include various cytokines and their receptors, playing a crucial role in immunity and inflammation. In HIRI, multiple cytokines may act jointly, driving inflammation and cell damage3,55. Targeting key factors in the inflammatory pathways may help alleviate inflammatory responses, thereby mitigating HIRI. Meawhile, Exosomes from pluripotent stem cells derived from mesenchymal stromal cells (hiPSC-MSC-Exos) significantly reduced hepatocyte necrosis and sinusoidal congestion, thereby lowering the levels of macrophages, neutrophils, and inflammatory factors. Therefore, hiPSC-MSC-Exos may exert anti-inflammatory effects mediated by immune cells to mitigate liver I/R injury13. Consistent with our immune infiltration analysis, MCPCounter indicated a certain positive correlation between ANXA1 and THBS1 and Neutrophils. This indicates that the key genes of exosome might target the activation of immune cells and induce an inflammatory response, thereby participating in the regulation of liver ischemia reperfusion injury mechanisms. Thus exosomes appeared to play an important role in HIRI protection by targeting immune cells and regulating inflammatory responses. Bioinformatic data indicate that many cell types in the liver microenvironment, including parenchymal and non-parenchymal cells, are important for HIRI. Various immune cells, such as macrophages, dendritic cells, neutrophils, and T lymphocytes, play a significant role in mediating inflammatory reactions in HIRI56,57. In our study, immune infiltration analysis indicated that 18 types of immune cells were involved in the key genes mediating HIRI, with neutrophils, B lineage cells, and T cells being closely related to the pathology of HIRI. In conclusion, the key genes of exosome potentially participate in the inflammatory response mediated by immune cell infiltration or migration, acting as potential molecular targets for future HIRI immunotherapy.

This study revealed the molecular targets and effect pathways affected by exosome-related genes in HIRI, starting with the identification of ERDEGs which were then screened through LASSO regression and SVM algorithms to establish a diagnostic model and identify key genes. The obtained key genes were demonstrated to have significant diagnostic and potential therapeutic value through Calibration and DCA analysis. Combined The high accuracy and feature selection of the LASSO regression with the advantage of the optimal decision boundary of SVM, the diagnostic models performed well in the research samples, allowing us to screen out the most diagnostically valuable genes from a large pool of potential features, providing new insights for the diagnosis of HIRI. Subsequently, the differences in expression of key genes were explored in the HIRI group and validated at the mRNA level. The correlation between key genes was investigated and a regulatory network was constructed for various molecular types. Moreover, multiple algorithms was conducted to identify potential downstream pathways related to HIRI and characteristics of immune infiltration. These results aligned with previous research on the role of exosomes in HIRI and demonstrated the unique role of exosomes in regulating the function of inflammatory signal pathways. This study provided novel insights into the potential mechanism and molecular targets of exosomes in HIRI for future research on the diagnosis and treatment strategies of hepatic I/R injury. Although preliminary results are relatively reliable, our study had several limitations. Firstly, the limited important clinical features of the public databases may have affected the efficiency of the current prediction models. These models have not been externally validated, therefore, we cannot guarantee their applicability and reliability in different populations. More datasets should be collected and an independent external database should be established to further enhance the credibility of the prediction model. Secondly, although ssGSEA performs well in revealing the enrichment status of genesets in HIRI, it fails to sufficiently consider the complex interactions and functional associations among genes, which may not fully capture the complexity of these interactions and regulatory mechanisms. Future research should integrate multi-omics data (such as transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics) to provide a more comprehensive and systematic understanding of HIRI. Thirdly, more experiments are needed to obtain direct evidence for the endocytic process of exosomes in the HIRI model and confirm the effects of the five genes on the molecular mechanism of HIRI. Whether hepatocyte H/R injury causes immune cell infiltration, or immune cell aggregation leads to liver cell injury also remain unclear. Further exploration on the potential mechanisms for the interaction between the key genes of exosomes and immune cells in the HIRI is necessary.

In summary, the five genes((ANXA1, HNRNPA2B1, ICAM1, PTEN, and THBS1) ) can be used as molecular targets and diagnostic markers for HIRI. Altogether, these findings provide a theoretical foundation for the clinical diagnosis and understanding of the pathogenesis of ERGs in HIRI.

Methods

Data download

Using the GEO query58 R package, datasets were downloaded from the GEO database, including GSE1272059, GSE1495160, and GSE1548061 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). The microarray platform for GSE12720 (HIRI:21, Normal:21) and GSE1495 (HIRI:5, Normal:5) was the GPL570[HG-U133_Plus_2] Affymetrix Human Genome U133 Plus 2.0 Array, whereas that for GSE15480 (HIRI:6, Normal:6) was the GPL6244[HuGene-1_0-st] Affymetrix Human Gene 1.0 ST Array [transcript (gene) version]. The included samples were from Homo sapiens, and relevant information is presented in Table 4.

HIRI data set information list.

The R package sva62 was used to debatch the GSE12720, GSE14951, and GSE15480 datasets to obtain the combined GEO datasets (CGDs), which contained 32 HIRI and 32 normal samples. Finally, the CGDs were standardized using the R package limma63, with annotation probes undergoing standardization and normalization. In order to address the potential deviations introduced by different datasets from the GEO database, we adopted standardized methods for data integration and processing, and used batch differences normalization to minimize the impact of these deviations. Before and after removing batch effects, the expression matrices were subjected to principal component analysis (PCA)64 to verify the effectiveness of batch effect elimination. Moreover, in order to identify the DEGREs associated with HIRI, ERGs were obtained from the GeneCards database65 (https://www.genecards.org/). Using “exosome” as the search word, ERGs annotated as “protein coding” with a relevance score exceeding 3 were included, resulting in 158 ERGs. “Exosome” was also used as the keyword for the PubMed search, resulting in 121 ERGs being retrieved from the published literature66. Finally, 277 ERGs were obtained by merging the ERGs from the GeneCards database and PubMed literature (Table S1). And performed intersection analysis between these genes and the list of DEGs.

Exosome-related differentially expressed genes associated with HIRI

The CGD samples were divided into HIRI and normal groups. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified between the two groups using the R package limma. The criteria for identifying DEGs with significant changes in expression were |logFC| > 0 and p < 0.05. Genes with logFC > 0 and p < 0.05 were considered to be upregulated and those with logFC < 0 and p < 0.05 were considered to be downregulated.

To identify the ERDEGs associated with HIRI, the CGD was obtained via a difference analysis of DEGs with the following criteria: |logFC| > 0 and p < 0.05. The intersection of these DEGs with ERGs was visualized using a Venn diagram. The relevant results were visualized as a volcano map and difference ranking map using the R package ggplot2.

Functional (Gene Ontology [GO]) and pathway (Kyoto Encyclopedia of genes and genomes [KEGG]) enrichment analyses

To understand the functional roles of ERDEGs, GO67 was performed to obtain biological information, including the enriched biological processes (BP), cell components (CCs), and molecular functions (MFs). KEGG enrichment analysis was performed to obtain relevant information on genomes and biological pathways using the R package clusterProfiler68. p < 0.05 and FDR (q value) < 0.05 indicated significant difference.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and gene set variation analysis (GSVA)

Genes from the CGD were ranked according to their logFC values and subjected to GSEA69 to determine their contribution to the phenotype using the R package clusterProfiler. The parameters for GSEA included a seed value of 2020, calculation number of 1000, minimum gene set number of 10, and maximum gene set number of 500. The gene sets of c2.cp.all.v2022.1.Hs.symbols.gmt [All Canonical Pathways](3050) were obtained from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB)70 (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/) for GSEA. The criteria for the GSEA algorithm were p < 0.05 and FDR (q value) < 0.25.

The reference gene set, “H.A.v7.4.Symbols.gmt,” was retrieved from the MSigDB. Functional differences in genes between different groups of CGD were analyzed using GSVA71 to assess the enrichment pathways in distinct samples. Significant enrichment was determined using a screening criterion of p < 0.05.

Construction of a diagnostic model for HIRI

To establish the diagnostic models of HIRI in the CGD, ERDEGs were identified based on p < 0.05. A logistic regression model was generated using the ERDEGs in the HIRI/normal group. Based on the ERDEGs in the logistic regression model through the R package glmnet72. set.seed (500) and family= “binomial” were set as parameters to generate the LASSO regression model.

LASSO regression analysis was performed using a diagnostic model and variable trajectory diagram. The LASSO regression results constituted the HIRI diagnostic model, and the ERDEGs included in the model were considered model genes. The LASSO risk score (RiskScore) was computed using the risk coefficients from the LASSO regression analysis. The score was calculated using the following formula:

By employing the ERDEGs incorporated in the model, an SVM model was constructed using SVM algorithm73. Thereafter, the ERDEGs were identified based on the number of genes that yielded the highest accuracy and the lowest error rate. We selected key genes (mRNA) identified in the LASSO regression model and SVM analysis for subsequent analysis.

Validation of the diagnostic model for HIRI

We established a nomogram for ERDEGs using the R package rms and generated a decision curve analysis (DCA) plot using the R package ggDCA. This plot enabled us to verify the accuracy and discriminatory capacity of the logistic regression model. We also generated receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves for the CGD and calculated the area under the curve (AUC) using the R package pROC.

mRNA-RBP, mRNA-TF, mRNA-Drug interaction networks

Using the StarBase v3.0 database74, we predicted the target RNA-binding proteins (RBPs)75 of the key genes and obtained the mRNA-RBP regulatory network. The regulatory impacts on key genes (mRNAs) were analyzed using the transcription factors (TFs) retrieved from the ChIPBase database76. The mRNA-TF regulatory network was established using Cytoscape software.

We predicted the drug targets for key genes from the comparative toxicogenomics database (CTD)77 to explore their interactions with drugs. The regulatory and interaction networks were generated using Cytoscape software, thereby yielding a comprehensive molecule network. Finally, genes functionally similar to the key genes (mRNAs) were retrieved from the GeneMANIA website78.

Differential expression analysis of key genes

To explore the potential biological mechanisms, characteristics, and pathways of key genes, the expression differences of key genes were analyzed between the HIRI and normal groups using the Mann–Whitney U test (Wilcoxon rank-sum test), and the results were visualized through group comparison maps using the R package ggplot2.

Key genes were identified according to the results of expression difference analysis. The results were further analyzed using the ROC curve79 and presented. A ROC curve was plotted for the key genes and their AUCs were calculated to assess the diagnostic impact of ERDEG expression on patients with HIRI using the R package proc.

Immune infiltration analysis (ssGSEA)

Samples with p < 0.05 were selected to generate the immune cell infiltration matrix via ssGSEA80. Differences in immune cell infiltration abundance between the HIRI and normal groups were visualized using group comparison charts. Correlation heat maps were created to display the results of the correlation analysis of immune cells and ERDEGs using the R package pheatmap.

Immune infiltration analysis (MCPCounter)

The infiltration of immune and other stromal cells into the tissue was analyzed to determine the expression profile using the MCPCounter81. Spearman correlation analysis was performed according to MCPCounter abundance estimation and antigen gene expression, and p < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Calculation of the exosome score (Es)

We used ssGSEA in the R package GSVA, to calculate the Es of each sample in the HIRI group from the CGD. Using the expression matrix of the HIRI group in the CGD, HIRI samples were divided into Es_High and Es_Low based on the median Es.

Functional similarity analysis

The GO semantic similarity of key genes and the geometric mean values of the key genes at the BP, CC, and MF levels were calculated to determine the final scores using the GOSemSim package. The results of the functional similarity analysis were visually presented.

Prediction of the protein domains

We used the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database (AlphaFoldDB)82 to predict and visualize the protein structures of model genes related to prognostic risk. AlphaFoldDB produced a predicted local distance difference test (pLDDT) score between 0 and 100.

In vitro validation of key genes at the mRNA expression level

To detect the mRNA expression of five key genes in in vitro models, AML12 cells (ATCC, USA; RRID: CVCL_0140) were then cultured in DMEM/F12 (Sigma-Aldrich) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco, USA). After 7 days of culture, logarithmic-phase cells were used for subsequent experiments. To simulate the I/R model in vitro, according to a previous study83, cells are subjected to H/R treatment: AML12 cells were exposed to anoxic conditions (5% CO2, 94% N2, and 1% O2) with none-glucose DMEM at 37 °C for 4 h. After the hypoxia treatment, the cells were transferred to a fresh culture medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum and cultured in a normal oxygen incubators (5% CO2, 74% N2, 21% O2) for 6 h to establish an hypoxia-reoxygenation (H/R) cell model in vitro.

According to the manufacturer’s instructions, total RNA was extracted from the AML12 cell model of H/R using TRIzol® reagent (Thermo Fisher). Thereafter, 1 µg of total RNA was reverse transcribed to generate cDNA using the ReverTra Ace®qPCR RT Kit (Toyobo, Japan). The relative expression levels of key genes were determined using qRT-PCR with SYBR®Green Realtime PCR Master Mix with β-actin as the internal reference gene. The final reaction volume was 20 µl with 1xSuperMix (Platinum SYBR Green qPCR Kit, Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA), cDNA and 0.5 mM forward and reverse primers. Amplification conditions were as follows :50 °C (2 min), 95 °C (5 min), 95 °C (15 s), 60 °C (30 s) 50 cycles. Data were obtained from three biological replicates, and the experiment was repeated three times. The expression level of the key gene was calculated and normalized to the relative expression of β-actin using the 2−ΔΔCt method. The relevant primer sequences are listed in the Table 5.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using the R software (version 4.2.2). Continuous variables in the two groups were compared using independent Student’s t-tests. If not normally distributed, the Mann–Whitney U Test (Wilcoxon rank-sum test) was employed. The Kruskal–Wallis test was performed to compare three or more groups. Spearman correlation analysis was conducted to determine the correlation coefficient among various molecules. All statistical p-values were two-sided, and p < 0.05 indicated statistical significance.

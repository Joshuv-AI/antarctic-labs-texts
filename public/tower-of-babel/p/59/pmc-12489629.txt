Understanding the Phase Transition of FAPbI3 Films through In Situ Raman Studies for Enhanced Solar Cell Performance
Authors: Sanjeev Sajan, Shana Sudhakaran, Vinod E. Madhavan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Perovskite solar cells have attracted considerable attention because of their potential to achieve high efficiency at low costs. In this study, the temperature-dependent phase transitions and decomposition pathways of formamidinium lead iodide (FAPbI3) thin films were studied using in situ Raman spectroscopy. FAPbI3 is a promising material for perovskite solar cells due to its narrow bandgap and high thermal stability. This study explores the evolution of the photoactive α-phase and metastable intermediate phases during thermal annealing over a temperature range of −150 to 250 °C by in situ Raman spectroscopy at ambient conditions. Also, annealing at 125, 150, and 175 °C under an inert atmosphere resulted in systematic tuning of the optical bandgap, as confirmed by photoluminescence measurements and Tauc plot analysis. XRD and Raman spectroscopy revealed the presence of mixed phases at lower temperatures and stabilization of the cubic α-phase at higher temperatures. SEM imaging correlated the annealing temperature to morphological changes, showing improved crystallinity and film quality at higher temperatures. These findings provide valuable insights into the phase formation behavior and thermal evolution of FAPbI3 films, contributing to the development of more stable and efficient perovskite solar cells with improved performance. The comprehensive exploration of crystallization mechanisms through Raman studies and phase transition dynamics helps to address one of the most pressing challenges: structural stability in the FAPbI3 perovskite.

Introduction

Perovskite solar cells have garnered significant interest due to their ability to offer high efficiency while remaining cost-effective. The efficiency of PSCs has significantly improved from an initial 3% in 2009 to above 27% in 2025, positioning them as strong contenders for commercial solar-energy applications. Formamidinium lead iodide (FAPbI3) has emerged as a promising material for next-generation photovoltaic devices, offering an optimal bandgap, long carrier diffusion lengths, and favorable light absorption across the solar spectrum. Nonetheless, despite its exceptional photovoltaic characteristics, the actual implementation of FAPbI3-based solar cells is impeded by their intrinsic phase instability. − The material experiences an unfavorable phase transition from the photoactive black α-phase to the nonphotoactive yellow δ-phase under ambient circumstances, primarily influenced by environmental factors such as temperature variations, humidity, and extended light exposure. This instability significantly restricts the long-term operational efficacy of PSCs, requiring measures to stabilize the α-phase and inhibit the creation of the δ-phase. ,

The tendency of FAPbI3 to convert from the photoactive black α-phase to the nonperovskite δ-phase under ambient conditions directly impacts device performance and longevity, − necessitating a detailed understanding of its crystallographic and chemical evolution under operational stresses. , Although traditional characterization techniques, such as X-ray diffraction (XRD) and UV–vis spectroscopy, provide valuable insights into phase transformations, they often lack the spatial, temporal, or vibrational sensitivity required to unravel subtle dynamics at the local scale. Raman spectroscopy offers a nondestructive and highly sensitive probe of lattice vibrations, molecular cation interactions, and halide framework distortions, making it particularly suitable for monitoring phase transitions and chemical degradation via in situ methods.

Recently, multiple techniques have been investigated to address the phase transition of FAPbI3. Research has shown that solvent engineering is essential for regulating perovskite crystallization and enhancing phase stability. The use of cosolvents, such as dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and dimethylformamide (DMF), in certain ratios has demonstrated the ability to control nucleation kinetics and improve film uniformity, resulting in enhanced phase purity and diminished defect density. Moreover, the incorporation of molecular additives, such as ionic liquids, and passivating agents, such as formamidinium thiocyanate (FA-SCN), has been documented to mitigate defect-mediated phase transitions and enhance the temperature and moisture stability of FAPbI3 films. ,

Ligand-assisted passivation strategies have also emerged as effective approaches for stabilizing perovskite films. , The use of organic and inorganic capping ligands such as phenylethylammonium (PEA) and butylammonium (BA) has been shown to form 2D/3D heterostructures that enhance the stability of the α-phase while simultaneously reducing surface defects. − Additionally, halide mixing strategies involving the partial substitution of iodide (I–) with bromide (Br–) or chloride (Cl–) have demonstrated promising results in modulating lattice strain and improving the overall stability of perovskite films. The incorporation of cesium (Cs+) as a secondary cation has also been reported to enhance the tolerance factor of FAPbI3, thereby increasing its resistance to phase degradation. −

The annealing process is another crucial aspect that affects the stability of the perovskite phases. Thermal annealing is crucial to the crystallization dynamics of perovskite thin films, directly influencing the grain size, defect density, and phase composition. Prior research has shown that meticulous regulation of the annealing temperature can markedly influence the development of the stable α-phase while inhibiting the nonphotoactive δ-phase. In addition, in situ characterization methods, such as grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) and temperature-dependent Raman spectroscopy, have yielded significant insights into the phase transition mechanisms of perovskite materials, aiding the optimization of processing conditions.

Building on these findings, this study aimed to systematically investigate the structural evolution of FAPbI3 at different temperatures by using Raman spectroscopy. Initially, structural and optical properties of FAPbI3 thin films annealed at different temperatures (125, 150, and 175 °C) were studied to determine the optimal conditions for promoting the formation of a stable α-phase. In situ Raman spectroscopy was employed to analyze the phase transition behavior of FAPbI3 under various thermal conditions, right from −150 to 250 °C through various phase formations, and the vibrational properties of the material were monitored through the evolution of the characteristic Raman modes, providing deeper insights into the kinetics of the α-to-δ phase transformation. Furthermore, the optical properties of the annealed FAPbI3 films were characterized using UV–visible spectroscopy and photoluminescence (PL) emission measurements to determine the bandgap. By correlating the annealing temperature with the phase composition, this study aims to contribute to a fundamental understanding of perovskite phase formation and stability.

Experimental Section

Materials and Solvents

The following chemicals were used: PbI2 (99.99%) and FAI (99.99%) were from TCI Chemicals, and premium-grade ethanol (100%) was from Hayman. Dimethyl sulfoxide (≥99.9%), and N,N-dimethylformamide (≥99.9%) were from Qualigens, and chlorobenzene (>98.0%) was from TCI Chemicals.

Perovskite Film Fabrication

The glass substrates were thoroughly cleaned to remove any surface contaminants, dried, and then treated with UV ozone before being transferred to the glovebox for deposition. The perovskite solution was prepared inside a Jacomax GP glovebox filled with N2. To prepare the FAPbI3 thin films, nonstoichiometric amounts of PbI2 and FAI were weighed in a 1.1:1.05 molar ratio (see the Supporting Information for details), which were then transferred to a glass vial containing a mixed solvent of DMF and DMSO in a ratio of 4:1. The solution was stirred for 45 min at room temperature to ensure the complete dissolution of the precursors, resulting in a clear precursor solution. Subsequently, 50 μL of this solution was spin-coated onto the cleaned glass substrates through a two-step process (1000 rpm for 20 s followed by 5000 rpm for 20 s). In the last 10 s of the 5000 rpm step, an antisolvent of 110 μL of chlorobenzene was dynamically spin-coated onto FAPbI3 to control the rate of surface morphology. The films were immediately annealed on a hot plate at different temperatures (125 °C, 150 °C, and 175 °C) for 1 h (Figure S4). Various characterizations such as UV-vis and PL spectroscopy, SEM, Raman spectroscopy, and XRD were performed on thin films. −

Characterization

Field emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM, Carl Zeiss Sigma 300) was used to examine the surface morphology of the samples. Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) analysis was performed by using a Zeiss SmartEDX detector to determine the elemental composition. The transmission spectra were measured using an ultraviolet–visible spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific UV–vis spectrophotometer) to investigate the changes in the absorption and bandgap properties of the perovskite films. Raman spectroscopy was employed (HORIBA LabRam HR Evolution spectrometer) with a 532 nm laser at 0.09 mW power at 10× magnification for an accumulation time of 5 s to investigate the vibrational modes and phase purity of the FAPbI3 thin films annealed at different temperatures. For PL emission spectra, the same experimental setup was used with 532 nm excitation. The crystallinity and stability of the perovskite films were observed by X-ray diffraction with a PANalytical X’Pert3 powder X-ray diffractometer with Cu–Kα as the source in a 2θ range of 10°to 60°. In-situ temperature-dependent Raman studies were performed using a Linkam THMS600 heating stage along with the above Raman spectrometer to investigate the phase transition behavior of FAPbI3 in the temperature range of −150 to 250 °C.

Results and Discussion

XRD Analysis of FAPbI3

X-ray diffraction (XRD) was employed to investigate the crystal structure and phase purity of the FAPbI3 thin films annealed at different temperatures. The XRD pattern of the film annealed at 125 °C shown in Figure a exhibited peaks corresponding to both the hexagonal δ-phase (P63mc) and the cubic α-phase (Pm-3m) of FAPbI3, indicating a mixed-phase composition. The dominance of the hexagonal δ-phase suggests that the annealing temperature was insufficient to fully convert the material to the desired cubic α-phase. When the annealing temperature was increased to 150 °C, the XRD pattern in Figure b revealed a significant increase in the intensity of the peaks corresponding to the cubic α-phase. This indicates that higher temperatures such as 150 °C promote the formation of the desired photoactive phase. However, the presence of weak peaks attributed to the rhombohedral phase of PbI2 (R3̅m) at 150 °C suggests the onset of partial decomposition of the perovskite material. At an annealing temperature of 175 °C, the XRD pattern in Figure c exhibits a significant increase in the intensity of the cubic α-phase (Pm-3m) peaks, which is the desired phase for photovoltaic applications, and the absence of any detectable peaks corresponding to the hexagonal δ-phase of FAPbI3 indicates complete conversion to the cubic phase. Nevertheless, the increased presence of peaks corresponding to PbI2 is attributed to the hexagonal phase of PbI2 (P3̅m1). This suggests that higher annealing temperatures can lead to PbI2’s conversion to the hexagonal phase (see the Supporting Information for details). This highlights the delicate balance between achieving the desired phase and minimizing decomposition during the annealing process. A better understanding can be obtained using Raman spectroscopy studies through phase formation temperatures.

Raman Spectroscopy of FAPbI3

Figure a shows the Raman spectrum of the FAPbI3 sample annealed at 125 °C. Raman modes at 65 cm–1, 87 cm–1, and 107 cm–1 are characteristic of the hexagonal δ-phase of FAPbI3. Here, mode R1 represents the molecular in-plane rotation around a corner H, mode R2 represents the molecular out-of-plane rotation around the N–N axis, and mode R3 represents the molecular translation. , Upon increasing the annealing temperature to 150 °C as shown in Figure b, a shift in these modes to 63 cm–1, 77 cm–1, and 103 cm–1, respectively was observed, indicating a phase transition to the cubic α-phase. ,, The broadening of the Raman peaks suggests the presence of defects or disorder within the crystal structure, possibly due to the phase separation of PbI2. In Figure c, the absence of distinct Raman modes for the sample annealed at 175 °C suggests a complete phase transition to the Raman-inactive cubic α-phase, which is also evident from the XRD data plotted in Figure c. −

UV–Vis and PL Emission Spectra of FAPbI3 Layer

The optical properties of the annealed films are strongly influenced by the annealing temperature, as demonstrated by UV–vis transmission and photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy (Figure ). The UV–vis spectra show a distinct absorption edge at approximately 800 nm for all samples. Of the three films, the one annealed at 175 °C showed lower transmittance than those treated at 125 and 150 °C, suggesting more pronounced sub-bandgap absorption. , Similarly, PL emission spectra also revealed distinct emission peaks around the 800 nm region for all the samples, indicative of band-edge radiative recombination. The sample annealed at 175 °C exhibited the highest PL intensity, indicating reduced nonradiative recombination and enhanced exciton confinement. The films annealed at 125 °C exhibited a blue-shifted PL peak with the least intensity, indicating incomplete phase transformation, internal compressive strain, and a high concentration of defects serving as charge traps. Annealing at 150 °C partially relaxes the lattice structure while maintaining structural disorders, which can lead to significant band tail states. This results in increased sub-bandgap absorption and enhanced nonradiative recombination, thus reducing efficiency and open circuit voltage in perovskite devices. These results indicate that systematic variation in the annealing temperature modulates both the band-edge position and recombination dynamics in the film. ,

(a) UV–vis transmission spectra and (b) PL emission spectra for the FAPbI3 thin films annealed at 125, 150, and 175 °C. (c) Corresponding Tauc plots for the FAPbI3 thin films annealed at 150 °C. (d) Variation of bandgap vs temperature.

The simultaneous red shift in PL emission, coupled with alterations in transmittance profiles, suggests a subtle interaction between optical absorption and radiative recombination efficiency. A red shift in the PL peak position typically indicates lattice relaxation or defect-state coupling, while an increase in PL intensity points to improved crystallinity and reduced structural defects. Together, these observations strongly support the idea that precise control of film processing conditions not only adjusts the bandgap position but also manages the balance between radiative and nonradiative recombination dynamics.

The optical bandgaps of FAPbI3 films, annealed at various temperatures, were determined through Tauc analysis, as illustrated in Figure c. This analysis complements the transmission and photoluminescence data by offering quantitative bandgap values. The Tauc plots were constructed by using the following equation: 1 (αhν)n=A(hν−Eg)

Here, α is the absorption coefficient; hν is the photon energy, 2 α=1t⁡ln(1T) where t is the thickness of the film, found from the SEM cross-section as 1260 nm; T is the normalized transmission; A is a constant; n is a constant with a value of 2 for a direct bandgap and 1/2 for an indirect bandgap semiconductor; and E g is the optical bandgap.

From Tauc plots, we obtained bandgap values of 1.51 eV, 1.50 eV, and 1.49 eV for FAPbI3 films annealed at 125 °C, 150 °C, and 175 °C, respectively (Figure d). These values exhibit a slight yet notable trend of decreasing bandgap with increasing annealing temperature, as shown in Figure (d). The bandgap value obtained (1.50 eV) is indicative of the cubic, photoactive α-phase of FAPbI3, confirming the successful formation of the desired perovskite phase. The slight narrowing of the bandgap at higher annealing temperatures implies enhanced electronic coupling within the perovskite framework, possibly due to improved crystallinity and reduced structural disorder, which is evident from the high PL intensity we observed in the sample annealed at 175 °C. These findings underscore the importance of precise control over annealing temperature to optimize the optoelectronic properties of FAPbI3 films, with potential implications for the efficiency and stability of perovskite-based photovoltaic and optoelectronic devices ,

Characterization of FAPbI3 Using SEM

The surface morphology of FAPbI3 films annealed at varying temperatures was examined by using scanning electron microscopy (SEM), revealing a distinct correlation between thermal processing conditions, film microstructure, and optoelectronic properties. Figure displays the top-view SEM images of FAPbI3 films annealed at 125 °C (a), 150 °C (b), and 175 °C (c), illustrating significant morphological changes with increasing annealing temperature.

SEM images of FAPbI3 samples annealed at (a) 125 °C, (b) 150 °C, and (c) 175 °C.

The film annealed at 125 °C (Figure a) shows surface morphology with limited crystalline features, appearing mainly as a planar film with subtle textural variations but minimal grain definition. This underdeveloped crystallinity suggests an incomplete phase transformation at lower annealing temperatures, which directly corresponds to the structural and optical properties previously observed. The less intense photoluminescence signal measured for this sample can be attributed to this less compact microstructure, which may allow increased nonradiative recombination sites at grain boundaries.

In contrast, the film annealed at 150 °C (Figure b) exhibits a marked improvement in crystallinity, characterized by the emergence of a well-defined surface with elongated structures and improved film coverage. This morphological enhancement represents an intermediate stage of crystallization that correlates with the observed increase in PL intensity. The formation of these defined grains likely contributes to more efficient PL emission.

At the highest annealing temperature of 175 °C (Figure c), the perovskite film demonstrates further evolution toward a fully crystallized morphology with well-developed grains and a compact structure. This highly crystalline nature explains the significant increase in the PL intensity. At higher annealing temperatures, while promoting crystallinity, it may also reduce crystal defects or phase impurities that act as nonradiative recombination centers. The morphological evolution observed in the SEM images correlates strongly with the optical properties derived from transmission and PL measurements.

Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) analysis confirmed the consistent chemical composition across all samples, with the presence of Pb, I, C, and N, validating the formation of the FAPbI3 perovskite structure, regardless of the annealing temperature (Figure S2).

A comprehensive analysis of the morphological and optical properties demonstrated that the annealing temperature acts as a critical processing parameter that orchestrates a delicate balance between the crystallinity, defect formation, and optoelectronic performance of the FAPbI3 perovskite films. Therefore, the optimal annealing temperature must be carefully selected to maximize beneficial crystallization while minimizing detrimental defect formation in order to achieve superior performance in photovoltaic and optoelectronic applications.

In Situ Temperature-Dependent Raman Study

In situ temperature-dependent Raman spectroscopy was performed to study the phase transition and vibrational properties of the FAPbI3 thin films over a wide temperature range (−150 to 250 °C). A schematic diagram of the experiment is shown in Figure . The experiments were performed using a Linkam THMS600 heating stage equipped with a T96-S temperature controller. Initially, the sample was cooled to −150 °C at a rate of 10 °C/min using liquid nitrogen and then stabilized for 30 s at this temperature. Subsequently, the temperature was gradually increased to 250 °C at a heating rate of 10 °C/min, with a stabilization period of 30 s at each targeted temperature to ensure thermal equilibrium.

In-situ Raman spectra of FAPbI3 at annealing temperatures ranging from −150 to 250 °C are shown in Figure a–g; three prominent Raman-active vibrational modes were observed at R1 (64–68 cm–1), R2 (89–94 cm–1), and R3 (106–107 cm–1) as discussed in Figure . ,

In-situ Raman spectra of FAPbI3 at different annealing temperatures: (a) −150 °C, (b) −100 °C, (c) −50 °C, (d) 0 °C, (e) 50 °C, (f) 100 °C, (g) 150 °C, (h) 200 °C, (i) 250 °C, and (j) stacked plot of in situ Raman spectra from −150 to 250 °C.

A significant phase transition is detected above 150 °C, marked by the broadening and eventual disappearance of these Raman peaks, as shown in Figure h. This behavior is attributed to the transformation from the nonphotoactive yellow δ-phase to the photoactive black cubic α-phase. The suppression of the Raman modes in the α-phase corresponds to the emergence of a centrosymmetric lattice structure at higher temperatures. The phase transition mechanism involves dynamic changes in the interaction between the formamidinium (FA+) cations and iodide (I–) anions within the perovskite lattice. At elevated temperatures, the isotropic spherical rotation of FA+ and the accelerated displacement of I– ions restore the lattice centrosymmetry, rendering the α-phase Raman-inactive. , The tables in each figure provide the areas under each peak, which are correlated with the intensity of the Raman scattering for that specific vibrational mode. The alterations in peak areas strongly indicate that a phase transition is occurring within the FAPbI3 thin film as a function of temperature. Figure depicts the variation of the area of vibrational modes (R1, R2, R3) as a function of temperature.

Variation of characteristic Raman modes of FAPbI3 vs temperature.

The R1 mode shows relatively low intensity and area compared to those of R2 and R3. It increases slightly at higher temperatures. Both R2 and R3 follow similar patterns in their variations. Both show low intensity at −150, 50, and 150 °C and high intensity at −50 and 100 °C. The R3 mode dominates in terms of area and intensity.

This comprehensive temperature-dependent analysis highlights the critical role of molecular and lattice dynamics in the phase stability of FAPbI3. These findings contribute to a deeper understanding of the structural transitions in perovskites, offering valuable insights for improving the thermal stability and performance of FAPbI3-based perovskite solar cells. ,

Conclusion

This comprehensive investigation of FAPbI3 thin films has significantly advanced our understanding of the phase transition characteristics and structural properties of this promising perovskite material for solar cell applications. The study employed multiple analytical techniques, including XRD, Raman spectroscopy, UV–vis spectroscopy, PL spectroscopy, and SEM, to assess the effects of the annealing temperature on the crystal structure, phase purity, and optical properties of FAPbI3. The findings elucidate a detailed correlation between annealing temperature and phase composition, identifying >150 °C as the adequate temperature for achieving the desired cubic α-phase. This temperature-focused research underscores the critical balance between phase transitions and material stability. The gradual alteration in the film’s structural composition with increasing temperature, transitioning from a mixed-phase condition to a predominantly cubic α-phase, underscores the need for precise temperature control during production. The in situ Raman spectroscopy data provide unique real-time insights into the kinetics of phase transitions, confirming the transition from the δ-phase to the α-phase above 150 °C. This observation, coupled with XRD analysis indicating complete phase transition at 175 °C, offers a comprehensive understanding of the thermal behavior of FAPbI3 thin films. The optical characterization results, particularly the 1.50 eV bandgap of the cubic α-phase, affirm the suitability of the material for photovoltaic applications. The enhanced crystallization and film quality observed at higher annealing temperatures suggest potential strategies for improving the device performance. While these findings represent significant progress, they also highlight critical challenges that warrant further investigation. The regeneration of PbI2 at high annealing temperatures and the need to stabilize the α-phase under ambient conditions require further exploration. Thus, this study enhances our fundamental understanding of FAPbI3 and provides valuable guidance for the fabrication of high-efficiency and stable perovskite solar cells.

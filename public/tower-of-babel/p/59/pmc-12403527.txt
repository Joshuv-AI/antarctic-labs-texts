Sliding volume-based streak artifact reduction network (S-STAR Net) for ultra-sparse-view computed tomography
Authors: Shiang Zhang, Yibo Hu, Ziheng Deng, Yujie Wang, Jun Zhao, Jianqi Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) is a widely used imaging technique. In practical applications, reducing projection views can decrease radiation exposure and accelerate scanning speed, with potential benefits for stationary CT systems. However, ultra-sparse-view acquisition (e.g., ≤ 30 views) introduces severe streak artifacts that degrade image quality. This poses a critical challenge because it is difficult to differentiate artifacts from real structures.MethodsWe propose a sliding volume-based streak artifact reduction network (S-STAR Net) to remove artifacts while preserving structural details. Our method introduces three key technical innovations: (1) A sliding sampling sub-volume approach to process 3D sub-volumes, fully leveraging spatial context. (2) A difference enhancement (DE) loss to help separate artifacts from real structures. (3) Novel network includes volume-attention aided residual (VAR) blocks and Fourier transform convolution (FTC) blocks for multi-domain feature learning.ResultsEvaluated under 30 projection views, the method was tested on two datasets: a walnut dataset and the CQ500 head CT dataset. Quantitative metrics (PSNR/SSIM) and qualitative assessments (multi-planar visualization, residual error maps) demonstrate that S-STAR Net achieves superior performance in both artifacts suppression and detail preservation compared to existing approaches.ConclusionsThe proposed method effectively addresses streak artifacts and recovers subtle structures in ultra-sparse-view CBCT reconstruction. Its robustness suggests broad applicability for medical image denoising, artifact reduction, and 3D image enhancement tasks.

Background

CT is a widely adopted imaging modality with diverse applications in clinical diagnosis, industrial inspection, and scientific research [1–4]. Cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) employs cone-shaped X-ray beams to obtain 2D projections from multiple views, enabling efficient volumetric imaging.

In CT systems, minimizing radiation exposure is critical to comply with the as low as reasonably achievable (ALARA) principle [5]. Reducing projection views is an effective strategy for lowering radiation dose. It can also bring benefits such as faster imaging speed, reduced hardware costs and space occupation in stationary CT systems. However, this strategy will sacrifice image quality due to insufficient information according to the Fourier Central Slice Theorem. Particularly in ultra-sparse-view CBCT imaging, severe streak artifacts will obscure important image details [6]. This situation poses a great challenge for CBCT reconstruction with ultra-sparse-view data.

With the development of deep learning techniques, some studies leveraging deep image prior (DIP) are proposed to address this ill-posed problem [7–11]. Meanwhile, streak artifact reduction in the image domain offers a promising approach for ultra-sparse-view CBCT reconstruction [12–16]. The Pix2Pix algorithm [17], developing from conditional generative adversarial nets (CGAN) [18], has been a representative method for image-to-image translation. Additionally, the U-former [19], inspired by the U-Net architecture and Transformer [20], demonstrate excellence in capturing long-range dependencies. This capability makes it well-suited for restoring high-fidelity details in degraded images. However, existing researches on CT image post-processing [21–25] typically process the entire volume as individual slices, overlooking the spatial contextual information in the CBCT image. Specifically, ultra-sparse-view CT introduces prominent streak artifacts and damages structural details compared to conventional CT, rendering single-slice details difficult to discern. This makes slice-by-slice processing infeasible. Moreover, networks are rarely specifically designed for ultra-sparse-view conditions. With limited features available, guiding networks to extract discriminative information poses challenges—existing methods struggle to distinguish streak artifacts from real structures, leading to inferior results.

To address this problem, in this paper, we present a sliding volume-based streak artifact reduction network (S-STAR.

There are three key points to improve the performance: Firstly, to fully exploit the information from the 3D property of CT images, we process the volume as sub-volumes instead of individual slices, and propose a sliding sampling sub-volume approach. This approach not only reduces the memory pressure of training a 3D model, but also enhances the diversity of training samples. Secondly, the proposed method utilizes difference enhancement (DE) loss to guide the network to distinguish artifacts from real structures. Thirdly, the architecture of the S-STAR Net is specially designed. The volume-attention aided residual (VAR) blocks are utilized to strengthen the dimensional interaction within the volume. We conduct comprehensive experiments on a walnut dataset [26] and the CQ500 head CT dataset [27]. Methods

Overview

Considering the 3D characteristic of CBCT images, the continuity and similarity between adjacent slices can be regarded as crucial prior knowledge. These can assist the network to differentiate artifacts from image details. Hence, we propose a novel streak artifact reduction approach. Instead of processing images slice by slice as other studies, we recommend handling them as sub-volumes to fully leverage the inherent spatial relationships within the volume. The inputs of the networks are 3D sub-volumes obtained through the sliding sampling sub-volume approach. Additionally, to guide the S-STAR Net to distinguish streak artifacts from real structures, we incorporate the DE loss. The overall architecture of the S-STAR Net is shown as Fig. 1. It views the restoration of ultra-sparse-view CT images to full-view CT images as a domain transformation task [28]. It employs two pairs of generators and discriminators with identical architectures but separate weights. One pair is dedicated to converting images from the ultra-sparse-view domain to the full-view domain, which is the process of reducing the artifacts and adding the structures. The other pair performs the reverse process, reducing the structures and adding the artifacts to the images. In this cyclic process, the network will gradually learn the critical features of artifacts and structures, and enhance the ability to find their difference. During the adversarial training of generators and discriminators, generators aim to mimic the data distribution of the target domain and deceive discriminators. At the same time, discriminators enhance their ability to distinguish between different domains.

The structure of generator plays a crucial role in the reconstruction quality. As shown in Fig. 2(a), it consists of an encoder, a bottleneck, and a decoder. Initially, the encoder, which is equipped with a series of 3D convolutional layers, focuses on extracting essential features from the input while simultaneously reducing computational cost. Then the encoded information is conveyed to the bottleneck, which consists of six successive VAR blocks. Subsequently, it will be restored to the original scale through three transpose convolution operations. Moreover, the FTC blocks are added in the skip connection between feature maps of the same size. The discriminator, inspired by PatchGAN [29], consists of 3D convolution layers, instance normalization and LeakyReLU, generating a discriminative feature map to determine whether the generated image is real or not.

Sliding sampling sub-volume approach

Training 3D networks typically requires GPUs with large video memory and a large amount of training data. In this paper, we propose a sliding sampling sub-volume approach to reduce video memory occupation and data requirement, as illustrated in Fig. 2(c). During training, this method samples at random locations to enhance the diversity of samples and sends them to the network. In the testing process, the method samples in sequential order of position. After being processed by the S-STAR Net, the sub-volumes are compiled together in the order of the sampling sequence. The sampling process is like sliding a window within a CT image. In this manner, it is possible to achieve large-size 3D image processing with limited memory.

VAR block

The attention mechanism in neural networks is designed to prioritize and focus more on the segments of data that are crucial for improving the performance. In the universal computational procedure, each objective in the source is regarded as a series of data consisting of a key and its.

corresponding value. At the same time, each objective also has a corresponding query in the target. Subsequently, the similarity between the query and each key is computed to obtain a weight coefficient. The value is then multiplied by the weight coefficient and summed to get the final attention value. The process can be expressed by the mathematical formula:

FTC block

Fourier transform is a renowned technique in digital image processing. The analytic CBCT reconstruction algorithm, Feldkamp-Davis-Kress (FDK) algorithm [31] which is widely used in clinical devices, is highly related to Fourier transform. Its processing involves filtering in the frequency domain. Additionally, through the transformation, the network can acquire frequency information that possesses a global receptive field and contains long-range information [32–34]. Hence, we incorporate FTC blocks in the S-STAR Net. The FTC block comprises the following steps: Firstly, the input undergoes a fast Fourier transform. The result will be complex numbers consisting of a real part and an imaginary part. Since neural network layers cannot operate directly on complex numbers, we stack the two parts and process them separately. The operations include a 3D convolution layer, batch normalization, and a ReLU activation. After that, the two parts are combined back into complex numbers and undergo inverse Fourier transform. The architecture of FTC block is shown in Fig. 2(d) and is placed at the skip connection between feature maps of the same size.

Loss function

In the reduction of streak artifacts for ultra-sparse-view CBCT images, the process can be conceived as domain transformation from ultra-sparse-view domain (U) to full-view domain (F). The S-STAR Net is composed of two pairs of generators and discriminators that perform bidirectional domain conversion.

As illustrated in Fig. 1, the loss function of the S-STAR Net consists of three parts: GAN loss, DE loss and reconstruction loss. Inspired by CycleGAN [35], a CT image should revert to its original state after two opposite transformations. The DE loss measures the discrepancy between the cyclically transformed image and the original one. Additionally, the intermediate result is also compared with its corresponding full-view image label to calculate the reconstruction loss. Dataset and experiment settings

We train and evaluate our reconstruction algorithm on a walnut dataset and the CQ500 head CT dataset. The walnut dataset offers original projections. The full-view projection data comprises 1200 views, among which we uniformly sample 30 views as ultra-sparse-view. This dataset includes a total of 42 walnut scans. We use the first 35 walnuts for training and the following 7 for evaluation. The volume size is 360 × 360 × 150. Regarding the CQ500 dataset, it does not provide real projection data. Therefore, we leverage the ASTRA Toolbox to simulate the forward projection process from CT images, yielding 30 projection views. The trajectory parameters are as follows: source-to-object distance of 1405 mm, source-to-detector distance of 2579 mm, a 960 × 960 detector matrix, and a detector resolution of 0.45 × 0.45 mm2. We then reconstruct these projections into CT images using the FDK algorithm. A total of 30 data samples are randomly selected for training, with 10 designated for evaluation. The volume size is 256 × 256 × 256. The S-STAR Net is trained on a NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 GPU for 300 epochs using Adam optimizer. The learning rate is 2 × 10− 4 in the first 150 epochs and decays to zero over the subsequent 150 epochs. In each epoch, a region of 128 × 128 × 24 sub-volume is sampled using the sliding sampling sub-volume approach. Among the comparison algorithms, U-former is an outstanding representative of 2D image restoration methods. Pix2Pix3D enhances the original 2D Pix2Pix framework by extending it to a 3D network, enabling direct 3D image-to-image translation. Additionally, PTNet3D [38] is one of the state-of-the-art methods in the field of 3D image translation.

Results

The artifact reduction results

Figure 3 shows the artifact reduction result of the walnut dataset. While the methods all effectively eliminate most of the streak artifacts in the ultra-sparse-view image and recover primary structures of the walnut, some issues still remain within the reconstruction results. In PTNet3D, some secondary artifacts appear, which illustrates the limited ability of this method to distinguish the original structures and streak artifacts. The result of Pix2Pix3D suffers from over-smooth and loss of image details. We consider that it is caused by the utilization of L1 norm measurement in the loss function and relatively simple network architecture. U-former and S-STAR achieve relatively better performance in removing the artifacts and remaining satisfying visual effect. However, when we take a closer observation of the orange box region in the first row of Fig. 3, the internal structural details of the walnut are well preserved by the S-STAR network. However, U-former fails to distinguish the narrow crack from streak artifacts and thus remove it at the same time. From the second row, which shows the coronal plane of the walnut, this phenomenon can be observed more clearly. In the red box region, there is a tumor-like structure surrounded by a narrow gap. The S-STAR network achieves to restore it, while U-former fails. That is because the S-STAR network processes the CBCT image as sub-volumes instead of slices, and thus can incorporate structural continuity information from adjacent slices, which makes the structural details easier to be distinguished from artifacts. Figure 4 shows the color difference maps between reconstruction results and ground truth. In the color difference maps, colder color represents smaller difference. The proposed method produces the least difference, especially in the white box regions, demonstrating superior restoration capability of structural details.

Conclusions

This study presents a S-STAR Net that can effectively remove streak artifacts and restore more structural details for ultra-sparse-view CBCT reconstruction. Our approach substantially reduces radiation exposure by minimizing projection views, aligning with the ALARA principle. Additionally, it offers distinct advantages in scenarios demanding high scanning speed, such as emergency diagnostics. For next-generation.

stationary CT systems—where projection limits arise from hardware constraints and cost considerations—our method addresses the critical challenge of ultra-sparse-view reconstruction. The VAR block and FTC block are designed following the plug-and-play concept, which can be applied conveniently to other networks. The idea of employing a 3D network and sliding sampling sub-volume approach holds substantial potential not only in this context but also in other 3D image processing tasks. Ultra-sparse-view CT reconstruction remains a challenging yet significant task. We are dedicated to boosting performance by refining algorithms and conducting broader clinical validation.

Mechanistic insights into the degradation and cis-trans transformation of the new tasty analogs of hydroxy-γ-sanshool pungent dietary components: A DFT study
Authors: Jiacheng Hong, Yutong Li, Lisha Cai, Haiying Hu, Xinyu Wang, Kexian Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Sanshool tasty dietary components in Sichuan peppers are structurally unstable and their conformers structurally in series have different oral pungency. To explore the structural change rules behind their possible pungency, the degradation and cis-trans transformation mechanism of hydroxy-coco-sanshool conformers as the new tasty analogs of pungent hydroxy-γ-sanshool have been investigated in this paper. Besides, these conformers could be structurally activated by the light radiation especially in polar environment, which may largely accelerate their mutual isomerization or degradation. Furthermore, their structural activation to form excited states is found to be easier than that of hydroxy-γ-sanshool or hydroxy-α-sanshool conformers. Additionally, eight conformers are found to be dominantly distributed and may be feasibly detected experimentally.

Introduction

The Sichuan peppers and their relevant pungent foods are consumed widely in many countries mainly due to their appealing health benefits (Liang et al., 2024; Luo, Ke, et al., 2022; Wijaya et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2021), special color and appearance, and unique pungent (má) tastes (Chen et al., 2021; Chruma et al., 2018), where the má tastes are the soul of Sichuan peppers and their relevant pungent foods, and the sanshool dietary components are the key compounds responsible for such pungency (Bader et al., 2014; Chen et al., 2018; Huang et al., 2012).

Though great efforts have been done towards the pungent identification & purification (Cheng et al., 2023; Hao et al., 2023), mechanism(Bautista et al., 2008; Luo, Ke, et al., 2022; Riera et al., 2009; Wang, Wu, et al., 2023), evaluation(Feng et al., 2023; Zhang, Xu, et al., 2018), prediction(Chen et al., 2021) and control &oral bioavailability (Chen, Zhang, et al., 2024; Meng et al., 2021; Meng et al., 2022), the structural changes behind the pungency are largely overlooked. Many pungent studies have always attributed the pungent change observations of foods to the types of Sichuan peppers or the pungency, composition and content of known sanshools (Bader et al., 2014; Chen et al., 2021), but some sanshools with low content and/or strong pungency may be ignored owing to their unstable structural characters of conjugated unsaturated CC bonds, and their possible degradation during the pretreatment or the detection processes for the content analysis. Actually, some sanshools have shown excellent antioxidant activities (Chen, Tan, et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2023). Besides, the surrounding environment (e.g. aerobic conditions, light radiation, thermal process and acidic solvent) that sanshools involved can largely change their structures, where relevant examples have been experimentally verified even for both the well-known hydroxy-γ-sanshool and hydroxy-α-sanshool (Chen, Zhang, et al., 2024; Duan et al., 2024; Luo, Hou, et al., 2022; Sugai et al., 2014; Wang, Rao, et al., 2023; Yan et al., 2017; Yang et al., 2018; Zhang, Jin, et al., 2018). Specially, some conformers structurally in series have different oral pungency (Bader et al., 2014; Chen et al., 2021). Therefore, the dynamic structural changes of pungent components under different conditions should be focused on during the pungent evaluations of relevant foods, not just aware of the content of the commonly known sanshools, or the apparent pungent observations. To get a mature understanding of or predict the accurate pungency of sanshools, it is very important to investigate the rules of their structural changes, which will be also very important for the evaluations on their biological activities (Chen et al., 2018).

The aim in this paper to comprehensively study the structural changes of hydroxy-coco-sanshool conformers (Fig. 1), the new tasty analogs of pungent hydroxy-γ-sanshool (Hu et al., 2023) with one more conjugated CC bond, and establish a relationship between their structures and degradation/transformation properties through the exploration of the thermal, photochemical and solvent effects on their geometrical, electronic and energetic properties. These components could be existent but have not been identified yet, as they may have very low content or could be easily degraded. Actually, the present knowledge of má taste is mainly dependent on the relationships between the pungency and the structures of the limited number of well-known sanshools with observed pungency (Bautista et al., 2008; Chen et al., 2021; Riera et al., 2009), where the suitable conjugated CC bonds in the relatively long length of straight carbon chain, amide moiety and N-isobutyl moiety are required for this pungency (Chen et al., 2021). However, there is no definite evidence that other unknown or undetected sanshools are not pungent. Recently, the rules of the structural changes of hydroxy-γ-sanshool (Zeng et al., 2025) and hydroxy-α-sanshool (Wu et al., 2024) conformers, as well as the oxidation degradation of hydroxy-α-sanshool (unpublished data) have been addressed recently in our group. Though there are many studies on them, their purity actually needs to be addressed owing to their structural instability. Therefore, our work will also show the existent probability of hydroxy-coco-sanshool conformers in Sichuan peppers through the comparison of their key structural characters with that of the commonly well-known sanshools. Materials and methods

All the structural optimizations, vibrational calculations and energetic computations of hydroxy-coco-sanshool (C111111) (Fig. 1) were carried out within the Gaussian09 software (Revision D.01) (Frisch et al., 2013), where without other statements, the B3LYP/6-31G** were employed for the first two steps, and the B3LYP/6–311++G** were used for the last step. Totally, sixty two conformers (Cnnnnnn, n = 1,2) were constructed and confirmed to be all stable with real frequencies only. Personalized names were given for each structure in order to make them be easily remembered (Table S1). Besides, the integral equation formalism polarizable continuum model (Scalmani & Frisch, 2010) had been employed to mimic the real environment that they may be involved in the food processes or preservation, where the dielectric constants of 1.9113 (heptane), 24.852 (ethanol) and 78.3553 (water) were used to represent the different polar environment. Such polar environment can simulate some properties of the real food matrices or other interacting components, as they can form different polar environment that sanshools could be located. Furthermore, the time-dependent density functional theory (Gross & Kohn, 1990) had been utilized to study the excited state (ES) structures of the selected conformers, the results of which subsequently were compared with that of the corresponding ground state (GS) cases. Consequently, all the above results have been compared with that of hydroxy-α-sanshool (Wu et al., 2024) and hydroxy-γ-sanshool (Zeng et al., 2025).

Structural differences between hydroxy-coco-sanshool conformers

The optimized sixty four hydroxy-coco-sanshool conformers [Cnnnnnn (n = 1,2)] with different cis/trans CC bond orientations (Fig. 2) have different spatially linear or distorted geometries.

The cis-trans effects on the structural parameters of the conformers have been firstly addressed (Tables S6-S7). The changes of all geometrical parameters at the corresponding region are quite small, except for the CC bond length of sites 1–6 (Fig. 1) and their related dihedral angles. The cis-C=C bond length is somewhat (max. ∼ 0.006 Å) longer than that of the trans-cases in the same sites. The average CC bond length of each site follows the order: site 3 (1.365 Å) > site 2 and site 4 (1.363 Å) > site 5 (1.360 Å) > site 1 (1.353 Å) > site 6 (1.349 Å), where the longer bond length corresponds to the weaker bond strength. In this respect, the strength of the terminal CC bonds (site 6) seems the strongest, followed by that of the CC bonds (site 1) conjugated with both CC bonds and amide groups. Besides, the solvent polar effects on the geometrical parameters are found to be almost small except for several groups, e.g. O6C2 bonds are elongated by ca. 0.008 Å and the N3-C2 bonds are shortened ca. 0.007 Å. The dipole moments of the conformers are largely adjusted by these polar effects, which are 3.30–5.26, 3.86–6.02, 5.19–7.52 and 5.33–7.66 Debye in the gas phase, heptane, ethanol and water environment, respectively (Tables S3 and S5). The maximum and minimum dipole moment values are found for C111111 and C211111, respectively.

The electron excitation of Cnnnnnn (n = 1,2) in gas phase and liquid phase leads to the large changes in their structural parameters (Tables S6-S7) and large increasement of dipole moments (Table S5), while the changes of the corresponding dihedral angles seem relatively small. The most significant structural changes exist in the following bonds: the length of the N3-C2 and O6C2 bonds become ca. 0.012 Å and ca. 0.008 Å longer, respectively; the CC bonds are elongated by 0.022–0.048 Å, and its conjugated C—C bonds are shortened by 0.005–0.042 Å, where the larger changes are observed in CC bonds of sites 3–4 and their adjacent C—C bonds, and the minimum changes lie in the CC bonds of site 6 and its related terminal C—C bonds. Therefore, electron excitation may make the molecular electron delocalization easier, which means that these structures would be more ready for the intermolecular mutual isomerization. The similar trends were also observed in the analogous cases of hydroxy-α-sanshool (Wu et al., 2024) and hydroxy-γ-sanshool (Zeng et al., 2025).

Though hydroxy-coco-sanshool has one more conjugated CC bond than that of hydroxy-γ-sanshool, the CC bond length and the adjacent C—C bond length in the former conformers seem longer (max. ∼ 0.020 Å in gas phase and max. ∼ 0.030 Å in liquid phase) and shorter (max. ∼ 0.07 Å in gas phase and max. ∼ 0.10 Å in liquid phase) than the corresponding bonds in the latter cases, respectively. These structural differences are in line with their electron delocalization characters, which means that the hydroxy-coco-sanshool conformers are more likely to form their excited states. Comparatively, the maximum dipole moments of Cnnnnnn (n = 1,2) in different polar environment are ca. 1.0 Debye larger than that of hydroxy-γ-sanshool conformers.

The thermal effects on the cis-trans transformation

The energetic profile of Cnnnnn (n = 1,2) in the different polar environment has been explored (Table S3 and Fig. 3). Increasing the cis-C=C bond numbers in the C111111 structure with all trans-C=C bonds increases the molecular relative energies, which can be classified into six types: 6.13–8.53 kJ/mol, 14.48–18.57 kJ/mol, 22.21–28.18 kJ/mol, 30.97–37.15 kJ/mol, 41.40–45.85 kJ/mol and 52.66 kJ/mol with one to six cis-C=C bonds in gas phase; 5.79–13.63 kJ/mol, 14.29–23.31 kJ/mol, 22.69–33.47 kJ/mol, 32.22–42.00 kJ/mol, 42.14–50.48 kJ/mol and 57.11 kJ/mol with one to six cis-C=C bonds in ethanol environment. The relative energetic profile in polar environment (water and ethanol) is similar, which is different from that in inpolar cases (gas phase and heptane). Therefore, the solvent polar effects on the conformational distributions cannot be overlooked. Specially, for their most conformers, C1nnnnn are more likely existent than C2nnnnn owing to their lower relative energies (e.g. 0–42.54 kJ/mol vs 7.43–52.66 kJ/mol in gas phase, respectively).

To intuitively understand the distribution probability of each conformer, their Boltzmann distribution ratio has been calculated (Table S3). The following conformers would have occupied total > 98 % ratio in gas phase: C111111 (77.23 %), C111112 (6.51 %), C211111 (3.85 %), C111121 (2.77 %), C111211 (2.60 %), C112111 (2.47 %), C121111 (2.48 %) and C211112 (0.34 %). Such order and total ratio are almost the same to that in liquid phase, where the ratio of the former four cases was increased to some extent and others decreased. These results indicate that these conformers may be easily detected in the experiments, and the solvent polar effects cannot be overlooked.

The isomerization between the conformers with single cis/trans-C=C bond difference and close relative energy would easily occur under thermal conditions, as the energetic barriers for such reactions are always very close when the effects of electron-withdrawing or -donating group connected with these CC bonds are not considered. The transformation probablity and direction would be predicted when two conformers only with single cis/trans-C=C bond difference at the same sites are presented under the same conditions, e.g. the isomerization of C111111 to C111112 would be much easier than that to C111121, C211111 or C111211. Actually, the corresponding enthalpy changes and Gibbs free energy changes are very close to their relative energies. For instance, the isomerization enthalpy changes from C111111 to C111112 or C111121 in gas phase and liquid phase are 5.75–6.09 and 7.87–8.10 kJ/mol, respectively, while the corresponding Gibbs free energy changes are 5.13–5.47 and 7.51–7.74 kJ/mol, respectively, where the energetic values would be smaller when the solvent polarity increases. This means that such isomerizations are endothermic, and could not be spontaneous. Of course, the corresponding opposite isomerizations would spontaneously occur and be thermodynamically favorable (exothermic).

Additionally, the solvation of Cnnnnn (n = 1,2) in the addressed polar solvents is found to be exothermic (Table S2), where the solvation energies range from −29.73 to −26.95 kJ/mol in heptane environment, from −85.28 to −78.38 kJ/mol in ethanol environment and from −90.14 to 82.96 kJ/mol in water environment. These exothermic characters are just opposite to that of hydroxy-α-sanshool (Wu et al., 2024) and hydroxy-γ-sanshool (Zeng et al., 2025), which may be caused by their larger dipole moments, especially in polar solvents.

The photochemical effects on the structural instability

Many recent studies have revealed that the ultraviolet irradiation would cause the transformation and degradation of hydroxy-α-sanshool (Duan et al., 2024; Wang, Rao, et al., 2023; Yan et al., 2017) and hydroxy-γ-sanshool (Duan et al., 2024), whose structures have fewer conjugated CC bonds than that of hydroxy-coco-sanshool. As the electron delocalization degree of the latter is larger than that of the formers, its conformers would be more light-sensitive. In this section, their photochemical instability is studied and compared. The main Frontier molecular orbital energies in different polar environment and plots in water environment are shown in Tables 1 and S1.

In Table S1, the HOMO plots mainly locate in the amide groups, CC regions and terminal C atoms conjugated to CC bonds, while the LUMO plots mainly distribute in the amide groups and the C—C bonds conjugated to CC bonds. HOMO and LUMO plots show the π and π* characters, respectively, which are important for the identification of different conformers or understanding of their spectral behaviors.

Frontier molecular orbital energies can describe the molecular photochemical instability. The conformers with higher HOMO energies, lower LUMO energies and smaller HOMO-LUMO gaps would be favorable for the electron donation, acceptation and excitation to form the excited states, respectively. They range from −5.481 to −5.276 ev (max. 0.25 ev difference), from −2.605 to −2.460 ev (max. 0.145 ev difference) and from 2.744 to 2.973 ev (max. 0.229 ev difference), respectively. C122221 and C211112 have the best and worst photochemical stability among these conformers. Solvent polar effects can increase both HOMO energies and LUMO energies by ca. 0.06–0.17 ev, and decrease the HOMO-LUMO gaps by 0.01–0.03 ev. These changing trends are consistent to that of the optimized structures in the polar environment (Table S4). Specially, the above changes are more significant for C1nnnnn than that of C2nnnnn. Therefore, the photochemical instability of hydroxy-coco-sanshool is largely affected by both the effects of the intermolecular combination of different cis/trans-C=C bonds and also the surrounding solvent polarity, and the polar solvent environment would make these conformers be more unstable under the light conditions.

The above energetic parameters of hydroxy-coco-sanshool conformers can be used to compare with that of hydroxy-α-sanshool (Wu et al., 2024) and hydroxy-γ-sanshool (Zeng et al., 2025) conformers. Interestingly, all LUMO energies and the corresponding gaps of the conformers follow the orders of the corresponding conformers: hydroxy-coco-sanshool< hydroxy-γ-sanshool < hydroxy-α-sanshool, while the HOMO energies of hydroxy-coco-sanshool conformers are the largest. It means that hydroxy-coco-sanshool conformers have the largest electron acceptation capacity, best electron donation ability and easiest potential to be excited to form the excited states. Therefore, the light radiation should be avoided during their experimental operations, as the degradation and transformation of the hydroxy-α-sanshool and hydroxy-γ-sanshool with smaller CC bond numbers under such conditions easily occur (Duan et al., 2024).

To further understand their photochemical instability, the excited state structures of 14 selected conformers with the lower relative energies in gas phase have been studied. Compared to the energetic parameters between the excited states and the corresponding ground states, HOMO energies increase by ca. 0.17 and 0.24 ev, LUMO energies decrease by ca. 0.28 and 0.35 ev, and the HOMO-LUMO gaps decrease by ca. 0.45 and 0.60 ev in gas phase and liquid phase, respectively. As the length of the CC bonds and their connected C—C bonds (Tables S6-S7) become averagingly changed, the dipole moments are increased (Tables S2 and S5) from the ground states to the corresponding excited states, the electron excitation can make these conformers more structurally ready for structural activation, which can be accelerated by the surrounding solvent polarity.

The conformational distributions between excited states and ground states of these selected conformers have been compared in gas phase and liquid phase (Table 3), respectively. Totally, the electron excitation or solvent polar effects do not change the order of the relative energies of these conformers in gas phase. In gas phase, the relative energies of excited states are somewhat lower than that of the corresponding ground states except for C222222. The ground-state relative energies of C111112, C111121, C111122 and C111212 in liquid phase are smaller than that in the corresponding gas phase, while for other conformers, their relative energies increased to some extent. It indicates that the solvent polarity can increase the distribution probability of some conformers (e.g. C1111111, C111112 and C111121) under the normal conditions. Interestingly, relative energies of the excited states of most conformers are increased in liquid phase, as compared to that of the corresponding ground states in gas phase or liquid phase, or even their excited states in gas phase except for the C111112. Therefore, electron excitation in polar environment would reduce the distribution probability of most of the conformers.

Additionally, the energetic differences between the ground states and excited states in liquid phase are larger than that in gas phase for most conformers. In most cases, the solvent polar effects would be large in enlarging these energetic differences.

Conclusions

In this paper, the geometrical, electronic and energetic properties of sixty four hydroxy-coco-sanshool conformers as the new tasty hydroxy-γ-sanshool analogs have been studied, which contributes to a mature mechanistic understanding of their structural changes if they are existent in the pungent foods like Sichuan peppers, or they are operated in bioactivity or sensory evaluations under different operating conditions, and their existent probablity as compared to that of hydroxy-γ-sanshool and hydroxy-α-sanshool conformers. Our results have shown that the bond length of the six conjugated trans/cis-C=C bonds are quite differently (max. 0.016 Å of the average values), and such conjugated structures make these conformers large difference in their relative energies (max. 57.31 kJ/mol), photochemical stability (max. 0.229 ev) and dipole moments. The structurally dependent profile of the conformational distributions and the relative energies would assist food chemists to judge the possible direction of the intermolecular transformations between certain conformers and also their detected probability experimentally. Besides, as compared with that of hydroxy-γ-sanshool or hydroxy-α-sanshool conformers, these hydroxy-coco-sanshool conformers can be more easily to form excited states under the electron radiation conditions, accept/donate electron or transform mutually, which could be accelerated in polar solvent environment like ethanol. Furthermore, the solvent effects on the photochemical properties and conformational distributions are large to some extent. Specially, the solvation of hydroxy-coco-sanshool conformers in different polar solvents is found to be exothermic, just opposite to that of hydroxy-α-sanshool and hydroxy-γ-sanshool. At present, the experimental identifications from Sichuan peppers, biosynthesis, optical properties (like florescence or ultraviolet), pungent evaluations and biological activities of some hydroxy-coco-sanshool conformers are being addressed in our group.

Authors: Laxmi Prasad Sapkota, Pankaj Kumar, Vijay Kumar, Yusra A. Tashkandy, M. E. Bakr, Oluwafemi Samson Balogun, Getachew Tekle Mekiso, Ahmed M. Gemeay
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This paper investigates a novel category of probability distributions and a specific member within this category. We have formulated a new family of trigonometric distributions by utilizing the odds ratio derived from the distribution function of a base distribution. This newly devised distribution family termed the “Sine pie-power odd-G family” of distributions, is constructed through a transformation involving the sine function. The paper presents an overview of the fundamental characteristics inherent to this proposed distribution family. Using the Weibull distribution as a base reference, we have introduced a member belonging to the proposed distribution family. This member demonstrates various hazard functions such as j, reverse-j, increasing, decreasing, or bathtub shapes. The paper examines essential statistical attributes of this newly introduced distribution. The estimation of the distribution’s parameters is carried out via the maximum likelihood estimation method. The accuracy of the parameter estimation procedure is validated through Monte Carlo simulations. The outcomes of these simulations reveal a reduction in biases and mean square errors as sample sizes increase, even for small samples. Two sets of real-engineering data are considered to demonstrate the proposed distribution’s applicability. The performance of the suggested distribution is evaluated using some model selection criteria and goodness-of-fit test statistics. Empirical evidence from these evaluations substantiates that the proposed model outperforms six existing models.

Introduction

Probability distributions are found to be widespread in analyzing real-world phenomena. Studying these distributions encompasses established theories and ongoing advancements in their practical applications. Several distribution families have been formulated to depict diverse real-world occurrences. The evolution of probability theory in this regard is a continuous process. Many probability distributions proposed in the literature involve many parameters to enhance versatility. However, some authors highlight the challenge of numerically deriving these estimates1. It is preferable, nonetheless, to design models with a limited number of parameters that can effectively adapt to actual data patterns. With this objective, researchers explored novel distributions utilizing trigonometric functions2. In recent years, the allure of trigonometric models has captivated researchers due to their adaptability and mathematical manageability. Amid the diverse range of trigonometric G-families, Kumar et al.3 introduced a new category of distribution that employs the sine trigonometric function, giving rise to the sin-exponential model as one of its members. Souza4 presented a trigonometric model focused on the sine function. Similarly, Gómez-Déniz and Caldern-Ojeda5 formulated the arc-tan-G family of distributions using the arctangent function. Their study showcased the application of this novel distribution family in characterizing data from Norwegian fire insurance. This family of distributions was initially proposed for a foundational Pareto distribution, giving rise to a fresh distribution termed the Pareto arctan distribution. Importantly, comparative analysis with established distributions revealed this distribution’s exceptional goodness of fit. After that, the hyperbolic cosine-F distribution families were introduced in 2016, leveraging the hyperbolic trigonometric function by Kharazmi and Saadatinik6. Subsequently, Sakthivel and Rajkumar7 introduced the hyperbolic cosine Rayleigh distribution. Using a similar technique to the sin-G distribution, Souza et al.8 introduced the Cos-G family of distributions, which includes the Cos-Weibull distribution as a member. Additionally, Souza et al.9 introduced another sin-G class featuring a bathtub-shaped or increasing failure rate function and examined the sine inverse Weibull distribution as a particular member. Mahmood et al.10 introduced the new sin-G family and conducted an in-depth study of the sin-inverse Weibull model. Jamal and Chesneau11 subsequently introduced the sine Kumaraswamy-G family of distributions, which features two additional parameters compared to the sin-G family. Muhammad et al.12 developed the exponentiated sine-G family and specifically examined the exponentiated sine-Weibull distribution. In the same year, Chaudhary et al.13 proposed the arctan generalized exponential distribution, another probability model related to trigonometric functions. Muhammad et al.14 defined a new extended cosine family of distributions. Isa et al.15 further advanced this area by developing the sine Burr XII distribution, a two-parameter model derived from the sine-G family of distributions. Previous studies have highlighted that trigonometric distributions, associated with simple functions, offer mathematical tractability3,8. Furthermore, the sine transformation has been shown to enhance the flexibility of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G(x)$$\end{document}G(x) without the need for extra parameters11.

In this study, we have developed a single probability distribution and introduced a new family of distributions using two transformations, i.e., Sine function and pi-constant for odd ratio. Motivated by these attractive features, we have proposed a new class of trigonometric models using the sine function, and the odd ratio of the base distribution’s CDF raised to the power of the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\pi$$\end{document}π. We call this class the sine pie power odd-G (SPPO-G) family of distributions.

This study is structured as follows: Section 2 describes the model development methodology and some essential functions of the family of distributions. Section 3 introduces a specific member of the SPPO-G family and provides a detailed study of its properties. Parameter estimation and simulation study are presented in Sections 4 and 5. Sections 6 and 7 contain the study’s illustration and some concluding remarks.

We generated samples from the quantile function defined in Equation (8) for various parameter combinations of the SPPO-W distribution using the ‘maxLik‘ R package introduced by Henningsen & Toomet (2011)16. The maximum likelihood estimates (MLEs) for each sample were calculated using the ‘maxLik()‘ function with the BFGS algorithm. This method effectively addresses parameter estimation challenges such as the sharpness or flatness of the likelihood function and provides estimates of the size and direction (underestimate or overestimate) of the MLEs bias.

The simulation involved sample sizes of 30, 50, 75, 100, 150, 200, 250, 300, 325, 350, and 400. Each procedure was repeated 500 times, and we calculated the average estimate value, confidence intervals (CI) for estimates, bias, and mean square error (MSE). The results are summarized in Tables 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 7, which display the CIs for estimates, bias, and MSEs for each parameter. As shown, the MLE method is consistent, with MLEs gradually approaching the true parameter values as the sample size increases. Model analysis

We analyze several well-established goodness-of-fit statistics to examine the proposed model using data sets 1 and 2. We then compare the fitted models using various metrics, including the log-likelihood value (-2logL), Akaike information criterion (AIC), Hannan-Quinn information criterion (HQIC), Anderson-Darling (AD), Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) test, and Cramer-von Mises (CVM) test with associated p-values. Please refer to Chen and Balakrishnan19 for a more detailed explanation. All necessary computations are conducted using the R software. To assess the fitting capabilities, we have chosen specific models such as the Inverse Weibull (InvW), alpha power Weibull quantile (APWQ) as presented in Nassar et al.20, arcsine exponential (ASE) as proposed by Rahman21, new cosine-Weibull (NCW) from Ahmad et al.22, Cosine Weibull (CW) described by Souza8, and Sin inverse Weibull (SinIW) from Souza et al.9.

The contour plot of the log-likelihood function using alpha and beta parameters on the x and y axes can provide important information about the behavior of the likelihood function concerning changes in these parameters. Conclusion

In conclusion, we introduced the odd sine-G family of distributions by transforming the sine function based on the odd ratio of the CDF of baseline distribution. We explored the properties of this new trigonometric family and introduced a member named the SPPO-W distribution. This member incorporates various hazard functions, such as j-shaped, reverse-j-shaped, increasing, and bathtub-shaped. We estimated the parameters using the MLE method and verified the estimation procedure through Monte Carlo simulations, which indicated decreasing biases and mean square errors with increasing sample size. We applied the SPPO-W distribution to two real datasets and compared its performance using model selection criteria and goodness-of-fit tests against six other existing models. Our empirical results validate the superiority of the suggested model. We anticipate that this distribution family and its members can find utility in diverse fields like medical science, reliability engineering, and survival analysis and may inspire the future development of new models.

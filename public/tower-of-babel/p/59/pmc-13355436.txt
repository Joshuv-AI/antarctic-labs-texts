Protocol for integrated ubiquitination analysis of in vitro E3 ligase-DUB regulation and in vivo ubiquitin chain linkage characterization
Authors: Daniela Mennerich, Carlos Vela-Rodriguez, Lari Lehtiö, Thomas Kietzmann
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryHere, we present a protocol for the recombinant production of DTX3L and USP28, along with in vitro and in vivo procedures for examining their regulatory functions. We describe steps for reconstituting DTX3L-dependent ubiquitination in vitro with purified UBA1, UbE2D1, ubiquitin, and DTX3L and assessing USP28-mediated deubiquitination. We then detail procedures for the in vivo detection of ubiquitin chain linkage-type through co-immunoprecipitation and Western blotting. This adaptable approach enables analysis of regulatory crosstalk between E3 ligases and deubiquitinases within the ubiquitin system.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Mennerich et al.1

Before you begin

The identification of protein ubiquitination in vitro requires the recombinant expression of the components of the ubiquitination cascade. Here we describe a protocol using UBA1 (ubiquitin-activating enzyme), UbE2D1 (ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme, alternatively known as UbcH5A), DTX3L (a RING-type ubiquitin E3 ligase), ubiquitin (UniProt code: P0CG48), and USP28 (a counteracting deubiquitinating enzyme). Using DTX3L and USP28 as examples of an E3 ligase and its counteracting deubiquitinase, we outline methods for their recombinant expression and functional analysis in vitro.

While most proteins in this protocol are expressed efficiently in E. coli, full-length DTX3L (residues 1–740 of isoform 1; UniProt code: Q8TDB6-1) requires expression in the SF21 insect cell line using a baculoviral system due to its size and complexity. To facilitate bacterial expression and functional studies, a truncated but ubiquitin-ligase-competent variant of DTX3L comprising the RING-DTC domains (residues 555–740) can be cloned into a bacterial expression vector. This approach enables reconstitution of ubiquitination activity while simplifying protein production.

UBA1 and UbE2D1 are expressed in Rosetta2(DE3), USP28 in the MDS42 strain of E. coli, and ubiquitin in BL21(DE3). The reconstitution of the ubiquitination cascade described here can be adapted to other pairs of cognate E2 and E3 proteins with catalytic activity.

For in vivo experiments, HEK-293 cells, plasmid DNA, and respective detection reagents are required. The methods described here are specific for the detection of DTX3L (ubiquitin E3 ligase) and USP28 (deubiquitinating enzyme) ubiquitination in vivo. Ubiquitination of HA-tagged DTX3L and FlagM2-tagged-USP28 is detected by co-immunoprecipitation (CO-IP) using specific a-HA-Tag and a-FlagM2-Tag antibodies together with Protein G Sepharose™ 4 fast flow beads, followed by Western blot analysis. These protocols are also adaptable for detecting other ubiquitinated proteins of interest.

Innovation

This protocol presents key technical innovations in ubiquitination detection, including optimized reaction conditions for in vitro ubiquitination assays using linkage-specific ubiquitin chain inhibitors and real-time monitoring of ubiquitination dynamics. It further introduces a robust and highly reproducible workflow for in vivo ubiquitination analysis that enables clear discrimination among different polyubiquitin chain types. Together, these advances improve the reproducibility and operability of ubiquitination detection by offering a streamlined and dependable framework for connecting enzymatic ubiquitination activity to cellular ubiquitin signaling. The protocol also utilizes calcium-phosphate transfection in HEK-293 cells, ensuring strong expression of ubiquitination components while maintaining experimental consistency and cost-efficiency. Overall, this integrated approach provides enhanced mechanistic insights into ubiquitin-dependent regulatory processes.

Although the expression vectors used may differ, the protocol is applicable to all bacterial expression vectors mentioned here.1.Transform chemicompetent E. coli strains, either BL21(DE3) or Rosetta2(DE3) with the expression plasmids encoding the proteins of interest, respectively.a.Prepare LB-agar plates containing the appropriate antibiotic.b.Thaw the chemicompetent E. coli cells on ice, 25 μL cells/per plasmid in separate reaction tubes.c.Add 1 – 10 ng of each plasmid to each tube containing cells and gently mix.d.Incubate the cells on ice for 30 min.e.Place the tubes for 45 sec in a heat block or water bath warmed up to 42°C.f.Move the cells back to ice and incubate for 5 min.g.Add 1 mL of antibiotic-free SOC or LB media to the cells.h.Incubate in a shaking incubator for 1 h at 37°C and 180 – 200 rpm.i.Plate 100 – 200 μL of the cell suspension on the prepared LB-agar plates.Note: The LB-agar plates may be pre-warmed prior to plating of the cells to 37°C to increase transformation efficiency.j.Incubate the plates for 14 – 18 h at 37°C.2.Express the recombinant proteins.a.For each expression plasmid use three to four colonies from the agar plate to inoculate 10 mL of LB-media supplemented with the corresponding antibiotics.b.Incubate for 14 – 18 h at 37°C and 180 – 200 rpm in a shaking incubator.c.For each expression construct, prepare 500 mL of sterile TB-autoinduction media (TBAIM) supplemented with adequate antibiotics.d.Use 5 mL of the overnight culture to inoculate the TBAIM and grow each culture separately in a 5 L Erlenmeyer flask.e.Incubate the cultures in a shaking incubator at 37°C and 140 – 200 rpm until the O.D. reaches 0.6–1.0. For wide-angle incubators, 140 – 160 rpm is enough, while narrow-angle incubators require higher rpm.f.Decrease the temperature to 18°C and incubate for 20 h.g.Collect the cell pellet by centrifuging the cultures at 4200×g for 30 min and discard the supernatant. Determine the weight of the cell pellets; for this, the weight of the empty tubes must have been determined beforehand.h.Re-suspend the pellets in lysis buffer [50 mM HEPES (pH 7.5), 500 mM NaCl, 0.5 mM TCEP, 10% (v/v) glycerol, 10 mM imidazole] using 25 mL of buffer per 10 g of pellet.Pause point: The resuspended pellets can be stored at −20°C or below until the proteins are purified.

Expected outcomes

In vitro ubiquitination assay

The poly-ubiquitination of a given protein is recognized by a smear that appears above the molecular weight of the unmodified protein. This process can be visualized in SDS-PAGE with appropriate staining. To enhance the reliability of the results, the ubiquitination can be detected with an anti-ubiquitin antibody (Figure 1). The detection can be further explored with antibodies for the different proteins in the reaction if desired. Furthermore, the reaction can be modified to accommodate different ligases or deubiquitinases or even scaled up to use the ubiquitinated protein in downstream studies.

In vivo ubiquitination assay

To assess which ubiquitin linkages DTX3L uses to modify USP28, we performed ubiquitination assays with wildtype ubiquitin (Ub) and a lysine-null mutant (UbKR). As expected, USP28 ubiquitination was detectable with wildtype (Ub) but not with UbKR. In addition, we could also specify which ubiquitin linkage type contributes to chain formation. We found that DTX3L can form both K48- and K63-linked chains on USP28 using single lysine ubiquitin mutants (UbK48 and UbK63) (Figure 3A).

To determine which linkage USP28 removes from DTX3L, we repeated the assays with wild-type ubiquitin and the same mutants. As expected, DTX3L ubiquitination was detectable with the wild-type construct, while the UB-KR mutant abolished DTX3L ubiquitination. Furthermore, we observed that both K48- and K63-linked chains can be added to DTX3L, but USP28 selectively removes K63-linked ubiquitin, as shown by reduced K63-linked smearing (Figure 3B).

Limitations

The in vitro ubiquitination protocol is a straightforward method for testing the autoubiquitination of a ubiquitin E3 ligase or a substrate. As with many in vitro assays, the production of recombinant proteins is a limiting step. In the context of reconstituting the ubiquitination cascade, this is crucial to emphasize the need to purify at least four proteins. The majority of these proteins require specialized instrumentation to ensure the effective purification of the target proteins. This protocol involves the expression of DTX3L using a baculoviral expression system in SF21 insect cells. This system is considered time-consuming for laboratories that do not use it routinely. However, a viable alternative is to express only the domain with E3 ligase activity in E. coli, a method that can be employed to circumvent the aforementioned challenges.

The in vivo ubiquitination protocol has been optimized specifically for HEK-293 cells, and while the described cost-efficient calcium-phosphate transfection technique works efficiently in this cell line, it may not be directly transferable to other cell types without additional optimization. The workflow is designed for the study of overexpressed proteins in HEK-293 cells, with a focus on HA-DTX3L and FlagM2-USP28, though it can also be applied to other tagged proteins of interest. When adapting the protocol to examine endogenous protein levels, the primary limitation is antibody performance. Therefore, each antibody must be carefully validated to ensure specificity and suitability under experimental conditions.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Lack or weak poly-ubiquitin smear is detected in the in vitro assay.

Potential solution

It is important to keep store all proteins appropriately (−80°C in multiple aliquots of small volumes: 10 to 50 μL) and avoid freeze-thaw cycles that could negatively affect the activity of the enzymes.

If the assay is done with ligases or fragments therefrom for which no studies in vitro have been previously done, the compatibility between E2 and E3 should be evaluated from literature review a priori. Alternatively, a selection of E2 ubiquitin conjugating enzymes can be tried in the assay as the E2/E3 combination can affect the outcome of the reaction.

While the ubiquitin activating buffer with ATP does not necessarily need to be prepared fresh and can be stored at −80°C for at least six months after flash freezing in liquid nitrogen, we have noticed that old buffer (stored for over a year) results in overall lower ubiquitination activity. For new assays, we recommend the buffer to be prepared fresh.

Problem 2

Detection with anti-ubiquitin antibody results in a smear, but the visualization after probing with antibodies specific to the protein of interest does not show a smear or shows a single band.

Potential solution

Some E2s like Ube2N have been shown to produce free poly-ubiquitin (Soss et al.), the same applies to certain E3 ligases like UBE3A/E6AP (Ronchi et al.). If the smear detected with the anti-ubiquitin antibody does not reflect with other antibodies, it might be possible that while the E3 ligase does not undergo auto-modification, it can form free ubiquitin chains in the absence of a substrate. This would require further verification, like the addition of a known or putative substrate that could act as a ubiquitin acceptor.

Problem 3

Lack or weak ubiquitin smear detectable in vivo after Western blot.

Potential solution

Transfection efficiency may be too low, resulting in insufficient expression of HA-DTX3L or FlagM2-USP28. Transfect HEK-293 cells with HA-DTX3L or FlagM2-USP28 individually, perform a standard Western blot and detect overexpressed proteins using a-HA Tag or a-FlagM2 antibodies. Strong bands at the expected molecular weights indicate successful transfection. In the absence of bands, optimize DNA quality and verify that the pH of the 2x HEPES buffer is exactly adjusted to 7.05, as this is essential for efficient calcium-phosphate transfection technique.

The DNA concentration of the different overexpressed ubiquitin plasmids may also be insufficient. The ubiquitin plasmid-DNA should be increased to 3-4 μg per 10 cm cell culture dish.

The proteasome inhibitor MG132 may not be freshly prepared. MG132 stock solutions remain stable for 1-3 months at −20°C and once dissolved, they should be kept in small aliquot and protected from light.

Problem Statement or Scientific Question

Despite the availability of clear evidence-based guidelines, adherence to high-intensity statin therapy in patients at high risk of developing type 2 diabetes remains suboptimal.1, 2, 3 This project investigated how well statin prescriptions and low-density-lipoprotein (LDL-C) goals aligned with the 2024 American Diabetes Association (ADA) guidelines in a real-world outpatient clinic.

Take-Home Message

•Guideline adherence alone is not sufficient—ongoing assessment and therapy optimization, including combination treatment and structured follow-up, are critical to achieving low-density-lipoprotein targets in populations at high risk of developing diabetes.

Background/Project Intent

Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of morbidity and mortality in individuals with type 2 diabetes. The 2024 ADA guidelines recommend high-intensity statin therapy for patients aged 40 to 75 years with diabetes and elevated cardiovascular risk. However, many patients fail to meet LDL-C targets, putting them at continued atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) risk. This quality improvement initiative aimed to assess adherence to the updated ADA guidelines and identify gaps in therapy escalation and LDL-C goal achievement at the Internal Medicine Faculty Practice at Cooperman Barnabas Medical Center.

Methodology

We conducted a retrospective cohort review of 100 adult patients aged 40 to 75 years with type 2 diabetes seen at the Cooperman Barnabas Medical Center outpatient clinic between June 2024 and March 2025. Patients with known ASCVD were excluded. Data from Epic were used to assess statin prescriptions, lipid panel results, ASCVD risk factors, and use of adjunctive therapies such as ezetimibe or PCSK9 inhibitors. Patient's LDL-C levels and adherence to lipid-lowering therapies were monitored, and data were collected after these changes to assess whether these patients met the ADA guidelines for LDL-C goals. The multidisciplinary team included internal medicine residents, attending physicians, and clinical pharmacists.

Results

Among the cohort, 57% (57 of 100) of patients did not achieve the target LDL-C of <70 mg/dL. Of these, 82.5% were on high-intensity statins, 15.8% on moderate-intensity statins, and 1.8% were on low- or no-statin therapy. Therapy escalation opportunities were identified in 17.5% (10 of 57) of patients, representing those not currently on high-intensity statins. In addition, 86.0% of patients not at goal were not prescribed ezetimibe or PCSK9 inhibitors despite potential eligibility. The population had multiple ASCVD risk factors such as hypertension (87.7%), obesity (89.5%), and a history of smoking (22.8%), reinforcing the need for aggressive lipid management.

Value Proposition

This project supported value-based care by improving adherence to preventive cardiovascular guidelines, thereby reducing ASCVD risk in a high-risk population. It enhanced provider efficiency through Epic-based clinical decision tools, gave our clinic the ability to improve patient outcomes by optimizing LDL-C control, and promoted awareness around this topic to allow us to implement cost-effective care by potentially reducing future cardiovascular events and associated health care use.

Conclusions

Adherence to statin therapy was generally strong, but gaps in LDL-C goal achievement and underuse of adjunctive therapies were identified. Standardizing care through clinical decision support tools and reinforcing follow-up visits can enhance implementation of evidence-based guidelines. Future steps include expanding this initiative to a larger cohort and integrating automated prompts in the electronic health record.

Evolution of surface tension in strained molten aluminum: a liquid–vapor interface study
Authors: Zhiyong Yu, Wenjun Li, Yang Yang, Yangyang Zhao, Fangnao Xiao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
As a key physical property determining the wettability, adsorption, and structural stability of liquid materials, surface tension is of great significance in material preparation and micro-nano processing. However, traditional methods often rely on chemical composition or temperature adjustments, and how to achieve dynamic control of surface tension under pure mechanical loads remains a frontier issue in surface physics and materials science. Especially under high-frequency extreme loads, the microscopic mechanism of the surface dynamics of molten metal is still unclear, and it is necessary to establish effective theoretical models and numerical methods to reveal it. In this case, we simulated the mechanical response characteristics of the molten aluminum metal surface system to the lateral mechanical cyclic load, and analyzed the steady oscillatory behavior of the cyclic load using the dynamic surface tension of the system. This paper demonstrates that under the 50 GHz high frequency and 5% high amplitude cyclic loading conditions, the average growth rate of the dynamic surface tension of the aluminum liquid can reach approximately 5%. The peak and valley values of the instantaneous dynamic surface tension can respectively reach 30% and 15% of the equilibrium surface tension, showing a controllable trend of significant increase in surface tension with the increase of the load. We applied the previously proposed method of quantitatively adjusting the surface tension under load action to the surface system of the aluminum liquid, and obtained the conclusion that the surface tension of the aluminum liquid can also be dynamically adjusted. This verifies the reliability and universality of this regulation strategy in metal liquids, and provides strong support for the generalized intrinsic frequency and damping constant correlation theory. The analysis of liquid layering clarifies the cross-scale correlation mechanism between macroscopic mechanical response and atomic-scale dynamics, providing new insights into the microscopic mechanism of surface behavior. The research results clarify the quantitative relationship between frequency, amplitude and the rate of surface tension change. This provides direct basis for the process optimization and parameter design of liquid aluminum in precision casting, additive manufacturing and microfluidic systems. By reasonably regulating the load conditions, active control of the surface tension can be achieved. This will enhance the scientificity and process controllability of system design in applications such as wetting adjustment, interface stability improvement and flow behavior optimization.

Introduction

Instantaneous dynamic changes of molten metal surface tension play an important role in metallurgical industry and advanced manufacturing industry1–3. The dynamic spatiotemporal evolution of surface tension has a significant impact on the capillary fluctuation during the change of material properties4–7, and also plays an important role in the production and processing of many materials8–11. Using classical molecular dynamics to simulate the dynamic ultrafast evolution behavior of metal melt can effectively demonstrate the ultrafast evolution behavior of DST in molten liquid which is difficult to be directly measured experimentally. This behavior directly affects the dynamic microscopic mechanism of surface tension12. In a previous study13, we reported that ultrafast dynamic regulation of the lead liquid interface proved to be effective, and the DST varied up to 40% of its equilibrium value. Mechanical modulation results in a significant and biased adjustment of the atomic packing density and ultimately a change in the surface stress distribution along the dynamic molten metal surface. This change is also reported in our other work in the process of ultrafine modulation of the dynamic surface tension of molten metal under single pulse irradiation of femtosecond laser12, and the range of dynamic surface tension can reach more than 20% of its equilibrium value. At present, some experimenter has reported that ultrafast atom femtosecond laser-induced density fluctuations are effectively detected by ultrafast electron diffraction technique14. These understandings are insufficient, and a deeper understanding of ultrafast dynamic properties of liquid surfaces under applied loads or extreme conditions15–18 is still needed.

When studying the surface tension related to capillary wave fluctuations and curvature19,20, the liquid–vapor interface at equilibrium is widely considered to be similar to an elastic membrane system21. However, there are few studies on the mechanical response mechanism of surface tension of liquid–vapor system under extreme non-equilibrium conditions. Due to the complexity of the non-equilibrium liquid-gas phase transition and the influence of nano-liquid nuclei, the control mechanism of the cavitation pressure range remains unclear. Li et al. conducted a study that combined molecular dynamics with experiments22, revealing the phase transition path dominated by the hole fraction and elucidating the regulatory mechanism of gas core parameters on cavitation pressure. Wang et al. conducted molecular dynamics simulations on the evaporation of a thin liquid film within a sodium heat pipe23. They discovered that non-condensable gases could significantly inhibit or enhance heat transfer by adjusting the mass regulation coefficient within a specific temperature range, revealing the dual-temperature zone influence mechanism of this process on the evaporation process. Direct contact condensation is of crucial importance in science and engineering. Ying et al. conducted research by using non-equilibrium molecular dynamics simulations24, proposing a new method for constructing non-equilibrium liquid-gas interfaces, and exploring their transient mass transfer processes. The study found that in the initial stage of condensation, there are anisotropic regions at the interface, the gas phase velocity is dependent on time and space, and the condensation and evaporation coefficients are not equal. The study of the microscopic mechanism of the liquid-gas interface remains an important scientific challenge. Skarbalius et al. defined and identified transitional molecules precisely through a set of dynamic criteria based on the instantaneous positions, velocities, and energies of molecules in molecular dynamics simulations25, thereby directly observing and analyzing the dynamic process and internal mechanism of the liquid-gas interface phase transition at the microscopic scale. On the ultra-short time scale, when the liquid surface system is driven by loads, whether the surface tension follows the classical elastic theory still needs to be further investigated.

In order to understand the above problem deeply, the dynamic properties of pure liquid surface under lateral mechanical cyclic loading are studied by using molecular dynamics simulation method. In the previous research work13, we learned that the dynamic response of lead liquid dynamic surface tension also accords well with this theory. The dynamic surface tension or excess stress of aluminum liquid studied in this paper still follows the theory of driving damped oscillator in classical mechanics, but the damped oscillation line of the surface tension is obviously different at high frequency. We hypothesize that these differences are caused by the complex spatiotemporal arrangement and adjustment of atoms near the liquid surface. This study aims to reveal the non-equilibrium response mechanism of the molten aluminum liquid-gas interface under unidirectional cyclic loading. The main innovations are as follows. Through molecular dynamics simulation, it was first confirmed that the dynamic surface tension exhibits forced damping oscillation characteristics, and its average value can be increased by approximately 5%. This breaks through the limitation of the constant mean value in the classical oscillator theory. By extracting the generalized natural frequency and damping constant of the system, the underdamping characteristics of the interface were clarified and the potential resonance at 50 GHz was predicted. By revealing the non-synchronization of the surface and sub-surface stress responses, new insights into the microscopic mechanism based on the density relaxation time scale were proposed. This study provides a new theoretical basis for the active control of the behavior of liquid metal interfaces under extreme non-equilibrium conditions.

MD simulation models and methods

We simulate the molten aluminum surface (or liquid–vapor interface, LVI) at the melting point temperature Tm. The current molecular dynamics (MD) simulation technique used is the embedding atom method (EAM) for aluminum-lead alloys proposed by Mishin26. In the crystal-melt coexistence simulation, the predicted melting point Tm of aluminum is 870 K, which is consistent with the experimental results of 900 K27,28. This method has been used to explore the microstructure and thermodynamic properties of liquid aluminum phase interface systems, such as predicting the typical atomic configuration of the solid-liquid interface and the average density distribution along the temperature gradient axis, the applicability of describing the transport properties of liquid aluminum29, and the influence of different cooling rates on the formation and crystallization of liquid Ti\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$_3$$\end{document}Al glass30. The obtained transformation results of the structural characteristics of the local subsystems in the cooling process are consistent with the experimental results31.

In this paper, the simulation of molecular dynamics (MD) is performed using the large-scale Atomic/Molecular parallel simulator (LAMMPS). The time step is set to 1 femtosecond. All simulations were carried out at constant volume and constant temperature, simulating the liquid–vapor surface system of aluminum in liquid equilibrium. Periodic boundary conditions (PBCs) were applied to all three spatial dimensions. To avoid the influence of the limited size effect, this paper adopts the rectangular-shaped liquid system for simulation, as shown in the schematic diagram of the simulation system structure in Fig. 1. Researchers apply periodic strain or stress to metal systems through microscopic particle simulation techniques to explore the interfacial dynamic properties33 and the nature of crystal structure relaxation in metal systems34. We have learned that the atoms of a liquid system subject to periodic loads show a distinct collective flow velocity. In this paper, the layered temperature control technology35 is adopted to keep the liquid–vapor non-equilibrium system at a constant temperature, which has been used to solve the problem of high thermal conductivity in metal materials36. Results and discussion

After cyclic loading is applied, the DST on the surface of molten aluminum liquid–vapor system increases rapidly in a transient stage of tens of nanoseconds. However, the relaxation time length \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$t_{trans}$$\end{document} in the transient stage varies greatly in the simulation of different load frequencies and load amplitudes, as shown in Table 1. The transient phase is followed by a phase of steady oscillation. During the steady-state oscillation phase, the average dynamic surface tension converges to a constant value and the time of each coarse-granulation is about two nanoseconds. This value is higher than the equilibrium surface tension \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\gamma _{eq}}$$\end{document}= 594.125mN/m at the melting point (T = Tm).

To investigate the dynamic response of surface tension of molten aluminum liquid–vapor system under cyclic loading, the forced damped oscillator model in classical mechanics is used to discuss the DST of LVI. We find that when the transient phase approaches the steady-state phase, the mean position of the driving damping oscillator may still be at the initial equilibrium position relative to the dynamic LVI system position. However, in dynamically changing LVI systems, the average DST has risen to a very high value. This apparent difference stems from the scale effect of the LVI system as a multi-atomic system. Its immense inertia causes the entire system to undergo a slight translational movement within the observation time window44,45. However, the external driving energy strongly acts on the oscillator through local coupling, causing it to exhibit significant asymmetric oscillation cycles relative to the slowly translating LVI background. Due to the existence of a vast number of degrees of freedom within the LVI for dissipating energy46, the high dynamic response of the local oscillator (manifested as a high average DST) coexists with the apparent quasi-static nature of the system as a whole. This is a typical phenomenon that cannot be observed in small-atom systems, resulting from multi-scale energy transfer and statistical decoupling.47 Next, we will discuss the dynamic micro-adjustment in these non-equilibrium states.

To gain a deeper understanding of the microscopic mechanism of dynamic surface tension during the driven oscillation process, we systematically studied the evolution laws of the dynamic fine-grained stress field and density field on the surface of molten Al in the spatial and temporal dimensions under cyclic loading. In this study, dynamic fine-grained micro-stress is defined as the difference between the lateral pressure component and the vertical pressure component. The spatial distribution characteristics of this key physical quantity are clearly presented in Fig. 7. The analysis results show that in the hydrostatic pressure equilibrium state, the stress in the bulk area of the molten Al liquid is zero. Under dynamic loading conditions, the distribution of the stress field shows significant spatial differences. The area with zero stress corresponds to the hydrostatic pressure state. The positive stress area indicates that the local liquid is in a laterally stretched state, while the negative stress area reflects the local compression state. Through the dynamic stress distribution shown in Fig. 7, we find the following important features. Firstly, each dynamic fine-grained stress distribution curve, after reaching the peak of the maximum normal stress, exhibits a relaxation behavior where the amplitude of stress oscillation gradually decays over time. Secondly, in sharp contrast to the equilibrium state, under dynamic loading conditions, the bulk region of the molten Al liquid exhibits a continuous non-zero stress distribution. This phenomenon is particularly evident in the figure and the alternating distribution of positive and negative stress values directly reflects the dynamic tension-compression process that the local liquid area is undergoing. It is particularly worth noting that the non-zero characteristic of the stress in the block area contrasts sharply with the situation where the stress is zero in the equilibrium state. This contrast strongly confirms the existence of the non-equilibrium effect induced by cyclic loading.

Bernard summarized the theories and methods53 for understanding time correlation functions (including density-density correlation functions) through calculations. The method of extracting relaxation times and dynamic substructures from the density correlation function serves as a bridge connecting microscopic simulation observations (such as analyzing density fluctuations from MD trajectories) with macroscopic relaxation theories. According to the classical density functional theory, the free energy field of the system is related to the liquid density, and the minimum value of global free energy of the liquid system determines the steady-state arrangement and density distribution of the atomic structure of the system. According to classical density functional theory, the free energy field of the system is closely related to the liquid density54. This means that the atomic arrangement and density distribution of the liquid system are driven by the free energy minimization principle. It is inferred that the density distribution of the local liquid is deviated from the equilibrium state when cyclic loading is applied to the molten aluminum liquid. However, the system automatically adjusts the density field distribution to reduce the free energy. At a lower load frequency such as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\omega$$\end{document} = 1.25 GHz, even if the load amplitude reaches 5%, the local liquid density adjustment time is too long due to the cyclic load, and it cannot quickly adapt to the re-adjustment process of atomic arrangement inside the block. The corresponding time scale (C/4 = 200 ps) is about four orders of magnitude longer than the fluid density relaxation time of 0.57(6) picoseconds, so the dynamic fine-grained density and stress distribution of the surface in these cases are almost identical to that of the surface at equilibrium. This indicates that the relaxation path of the rearranged liquid atoms is anisotropic, resulting in fine-grained dynamic density and stress distribution on the surface being adjusted and hydrostatic pressure being generated in the liquid. Due to the extremely short time of cyclic load, the fine-grained density field may not have enough time for dynamic adjustment, and is in a relatively low free energy state. These arguments may help to explain to some extent why the mean DST of molten aluminum liquid rises after entering a steady state.

Conclusion

Based on the molecular dynamics simulation of the surface system of molten aluminum (Al) under unidirectional (x-direction) mechanical cyclic loading, the main conclusions of this paper can be summarized as the following three aspects.

Under a stable oscillation state, the response pattern of dynamic surface tension to periodic cyclic loads is, in terms of vibration form, basically consistent with the classical theory of forced damping oscillators. However, this study has discovered a key difference. The average value of the dynamic surface tension in the Al liquid system during the oscillation process systematically deviates (and is higher than) the equilibrium value without load, and can be effectively regulated. This indicates that cyclic loads not only induce periodic oscillations but also have the ability to actively improve the average mechanical state of the interface. Under extreme conditions (maximum frequency of 50 GHz, amplitude of 5%), this regulatory effect is particularly significant. The average value of dynamic surface tension can reach approximately 5% of the equilibrium surface tension, while the peak and valley values of dynamic surface tension can be 30% higher and 15% lower than the equilibrium surface tension, respectively. These findings suggest that through high-frequency mechanical loads, a significant and reversible dynamic regulation of surface tension can be achieved within a certain range. This provides a new approach for the active control of interface properties.

By analyzing the response data of the dynamic surface tension, we successfully extracted two generalized natural frequencies and two generalized damping constants that characterize the dynamic properties of this system. The parameter analysis indicates that the interface system is in an underdamped state, which explains the intrinsic mechanism for its significant oscillatory response to the load. Based on this model, we predict that resonance may occur near the highest driving frequency (50 GHz). At the microscopic mechanism level, it was found that the structural adjustment of the outermost and sub-surface atoms has a phase difference in response to the load, resulting in different contributions of the two to the macroscopic dynamic surface tension. However, a key finding is that although the responses are not synchronous, these two surfaces exhibit the same generalized natural frequency. This indicates that although the speed of local structural adjustment varies, the outermost and sub-surface layers may be controlled by the same restoring force mechanism, or their dynamic characteristic time scales are strongly coupled under the load drive. The current research cannot clearly explain the microscopic physical origin of the damping constant, which points the way for future in-depth exploration.

Surface tension is a key factor influencing droplet nucleation56, wetting57, spreading58, and pre-melting of metals59,60 - all phenomena related to fluid dynamics and capillary phenomena. Understanding the dynamic variation laws of surface tension driven by loads is crucial for comprehending how to change the dynamic assembly mode of microscopic particles by adjusting surface tension. This study breaks away from the traditional perception of liquid surfaces as static equilibrium systems, establishing a quantitatively describable dynamic forced oscillatory mechanical model for the molten metal interface system. The average value of the discovered dynamic surface tension can enhance phenomena and underdamping characteristics. This not only deepens the understanding of the dynamic physical essence of the interface but also provides important experimental cases and theoretical foundations for the emerging interdisciplinary field of dynamic interface mechanics. The establishment of this theoretical framework makes it possible to precisely and actively control the behavior of fluid interfaces through external mechanical loads. This discovery offers new theoretical guidance for optimizing industrial technologies involving dynamic processes of molten metals (such as metal casting, additive manufacturing, laser processing, etc.), and also provides scientific basis for the development of cutting-edge fields such as microfluidic device design and space material processing. Future research should focus on verifying the universality of this model in other material systems, deeply exploring the quantitative relationship between dynamic parameters and microstructure, and developing cross-scale theoretical models to fully clarify the physical origins of each parameter, thereby promoting the practical application of dynamic interface control technology.

Case report: Woven-like coronary arteries in a patient with polycythemia vera

Background

A woven coronary artery (WCA) is a rare and underdiagnosed congenital anomaly that is characterized by multiple interlaced thin channels bifurcating and weaving along an epicardial coronary artery [1]. It’s worth noting that recanalized thrombus appears similar to WCA on angiographic imaging [2]. And thromboembolism is the most common complication of polycythemia vera (PV) [3]. Here, we presented a 58-year-old female patient with PV for 8 years and the left anterior descending artery was confirmed as recanalized thrombus by optical coherence tomography, which was presented like WCA on coronary angiography. Finally, we speculated that the woven-like coronary artery caused by recanalized thrombus was a complication of PV.

Case introduction

A 58-year-old female patient was admitted to the Cardiology Department of our hospital due to intermittent chest pain for 5 years and aggravation for a week. The patient had hypertension for 10 years, with a highest blood pressure of 150/100 mmHg(1 mmHg = 0.133 kPa), which was controlled by oral amlodipine 5 mg per day and history of cerebral infarction for 23 years. She had been diagnosed with polycythaemia vera 8 years previously and had been taking hydroxyurea 0.5 g once daily. Physical examination: the body temperature was 36.5℃, pulse 76 beats/min, blood pressure 138/70mmHg, and she was not in acute distress. Routine blood tests revealed white blood cell count of 3.65 × 109/L (reference interval 3.50–9.50*109/L), platelet count of 265 × 109/L (reference interval 125–350*109/L), elevated red blood cell count of 8.15 × 1012/L (reference interval 3.80–5.10*1012/L) and elevated haemoglobin level of 237 g/L (reference interval 115–150 g/L).The troponin level was less than 0.05ng/ml(reference interval 0-0.05ng/ml). Routine coagulation, liver function, renal function were normal. Bone marrow cytology showed proliferation of granulocyte, red and megakaryocyte lineages, and JAK2-V617 mutation was detected by gene screening. Electrocardiogram showed sinus rhythm and poor R-wave progression in leads V1-V3 (Fig. 1). Echocardiography showed left ventricular ejection fraction 46%, left atrial diameter 44 mm, left ventricular end-diastolic diameter 62 mm, interventricular septal thickness 12 mm, and left ventricular wall segmental motion abnormality. Coronary angiography showed haze lesion in the middle to proximal segment of the left anterior descending artery (LAD) with forward flow TIMI grade 3(Fig. 2A). Suspected thrombus in the middle to proximal segment of the left circumflex artery (LCX) and the forward flow of TIMI was grade 3(Fig. 2B). The right coronary artery (RCA) showed aneurysmal dilatation in the proximal segment and suspected thrombus in the middle segment with forward flow TIMI grade 3 (Fig. 2C). Optical coherence tomography (OCT) performed in LAD showed organized thrombus separate the lumen into multiple micro channels(Fig. 3A-E). Clinical diagnosis: coronary artery heart disease, unstable angina, polycythemia vera, hypertension. Considering the high risk of thrombosis with the condition of the polycythemia vera and the high possibility of the side branch loss with stenting, the patient was discharged home without coronary intervention. The discharge medicine including aspirin 100 mg once daily, atorvastatin 20 mg once daily, metoprolol 47.5 mg once daily, amlodipine 5 mg once daily, hydroxyurea 0.5 g once daily. The patient was in stable condition in 9 months follow-up.

Discussion and conclusions

WCA was first described by Sane and Vidaillet in 1988 [4]. WCA is a rare and underdiagnosed congenital anomaly that involves multiple thin and tortuous epicardial arterial conduits that reassemble distally into a single lumen [5]. WCA is common in the RCA (70.2%), and most patients are male (90.9%) [6, 7]. And recanalized thrombus in coronary arteries is also common in the RCA (66.7%) and some in the LAD(33.3%), few in the LCX [8]. Recanalized thrombus occurs after thrombotic occlusion but can be easily misdiagnosed as WCA because it manifests similarly on angiographic images [9].

Although WCA is generally considered a benign incidental finding, cases of associated ischaemia, thrombus formation, atherosclerotic plaque formation, and sudden cardiac death have been reported [6, 10, 11]. Furthermore, WCA and recanalized thrombus all have complex intracoronary channels, which may affect blood flow and cause a pressure drop in distal blood perfusion, particularly for patients with more complex microchannels and longer lesions [12]. Computed tomography angiography (CTA) may present diffuse wall thickening and moderate or severe stenosis in the lesions of woven-like coronary artery. For further intravascular imaging examinations, intravascular ultrasound(IVUS) can display cross-sectional images of blood vessels and evaluate the blood vessel diameter and lumen cross-sectional area. OCT can more clearly help to clarify intracoronary artery structures and detect intracoronary thrombi [5]. In WCA, OCT findings show that each narrow vessel has a three-layered vascular structure, and the vessels are not interconnected [5, 13, 14]. In contrast, on OCT, a recanalized thrombus divides the lumen into multiple small cavities that communicate with each other without a three-layered vascular structure [9]. Additionally, fractional flow reserve may be the most appropriate method for assessing ischaemia severity [12, 15].

PV is the most common myeloproliferative neoplasm, and thromboembolism is the most common complication [2]. The incidence of thromboembolism is 46%, and that of arterial thrombosis is 2–3 times higher than that of venous thrombosis [16]. This patient had been hospitalised for cerebral infarction more than 20 years ago, and routine blood examination showed an abnormal increase in red blood cell count. Therefore, PV is believed to increase blood viscosity, which likely led to cerebral infarction in this patient. In this case, OCT confirmed changes in the LAD that were consistent with an organised thrombus based on angiographic manifestations. Therefore, we assume that the lesions in the LCX are likely to be another organised thrombus, which is a complication of PV. Recanalized thrombus usually occurs in local or short lesions within a vessel [9]. However, in this case, recanalized thrombus in both the LAD and LCX was rare.

Currently, there is no consensus regarding the treatment of WCA or woven-like coronary arteries caused by recanalized thrombus. Channel distortion may lead to difficulties in wiring and device delivery, and endothelialisation of the tissue separating multiple channels may cause occlusion of the side branch. Therefore, treatment strategies should be prudent and individualised. Some previous studies showed that OCT-guided stent implantation was reasonable for treating WCA [7, 13], and IVUS guidance was useful for ensuring that the wire traverses through the largest lumen and to manage stent position and expansion in the WCA [17]. Similarly, recanalized thrombus can also be treated by stent implantation using OCT or IVUS [8, 9]. A previous report showed that recanalized thrombus in the LAD of a patient with PV was treated by stent [18]. However, the most cases of recanalized thrombus showed it happened in a local or short lesions within a vessel [8, 9, 18], if it happens in two or three vessels simultaneously in a patient with PV, medical therapy may be the optimal treatment option.Therefore, we chose a conservative therapy for this patient. At 9 months follow-up, the patient’s chest pain was relieved without any cardiac events.

Finally, patients with PV are susceptible to arterial thrombosis and recanalized thrombus in the coronary arteries is very similar to WCA on coronary angiography. Therefore, high-resolution cross-sectional images such as OCT or IVUS would be needed for the diagnosis and differential diagnosis of it. And physicians should also take an appropriate treatment in a personalized way in patients with PV.

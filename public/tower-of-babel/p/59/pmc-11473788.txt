Grain storage temperature prediction based on chaos and enhanced RBF neural network
Authors: Fuyan Sun, Chunyan Gong, Zongwang Lyu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Grain storage has very strict temperature requirements. Aiming at the problems of nonlinear characteristics and poor prediction accuracy of temperature parameters in grain storage, a combination of chaos theory and enhanced radial basis neural network is proposed as a temperature prediction model for grain storage (C-ERBF). The model first determines the embedding dimension and time delay of the grain storage temperature sequence using chaos theory. It then calculates the Lyapunov exponent to confirm its chaotic properties and reconstructs the sequence in the phase space to extract the hidden dynamic information and structure behind the sequence. Furthermore, the q-Normalized Least Mean Square Fourth (qXE-NLMF) algorithm is designed to enhance the radial basis function (RBF) neural network model for weight updating, to improve its prediction accuracy, and to accelerate the training speed of the model. As verified by the simulation experiments of Mackey–Glass chaotic time series prediction, the enhanced RBF (ERBF) network has faster convergence speed and lower steady-state error compared to the traditional RBF network. Finally, the optimized dataset from chaos theory is input into the model to achieve accurate predictions of grain storage temperature series. The experimental results show that the proposed C-ERBF model has higher prediction accuracy compared to other time series prediction methods. It can realize the grain pile temperature in advance, and take control measures in advance. This proactive approach significantly reduces the consumption of stored grain and prevents issues before they arise.

Introduction

In the process of grain storage, temperature is one of the most critical thermodynamic variables that determine the quality of stored grain. When the temperature of grain exceeds the critical point for safe storage, it will cause problems such as mold, heat, and insect pests1. Therefore, a model that accurately predicts the trend of grain temperature is of great significance for the safe storage of grain. Machine learning and deep learning techniques can effectively address the issues of model complexity, high computation, and low prediction accuracy in traditional temperature prediction methods. This is because they can handle complex nonlinear problems and automatically learn the model parameters, thereby enhancing the overall prediction accuracy.

In recent years, scholars have applied these techniques to the study of predicting storage temperatures for wheat and corn storage temperature prediction. The literature2 proposed a support vector regression (SVR) method to predict the average temperature of the surface layer of the grain pile using meteorological data in the storage environment. Experimental results verified that the SVR model using a Gaussian radial basis function (RBF) kernel performs best in predicting the surface temperature of grain piles. In the literature3 proposes a model based on a deep spatio-temporal attention mechanism. This model combines local features extracted by the Sobel operator, global features extracted by the attention mechanism, and long-term state information of spatial factors obtained by the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network. It predicts the future temperature of the grain by using meteorological factors as external inputs for the decoder. In the literature4, the CLSTM (Combined LSTM) structure proposed introduces peephole connections into the newly created threshold cell, which combines forgetting and input gates. In this way, the CLSTM can regulate the flow of information more efficiently, thus improving the model’s performance in processing grain storage temperature sequence data. The Literature5 proposes an algorithm based on a gated recurrent unit (GRU) deep fusion model for predicting temperature variations of grains in grain silos. It improves the prediction accuracy and robustness of the model through multiple linear regression and wave filtering techniques. The literature6 proposes a neural network model based on a residual network and self-attention mechanism, which has faster model convergence and smaller loss and provides more accurate and efficient decision support for grain depot managers. However, deep learning typically requires very large amounts of data to extract valuable information from training samples and necessitates significant computational resources, including high-performance graphics processors (GPUs) and large amounts of memory. In contrast, classical neural networks can still deliver excellent performance with low computational complexity and a small amount of training data. For example, the RBF neural network used in this paper has excellent performance in terms of its nonlinear fitting capability and rapid convergence speed.

The methods mentioned above for predicting the temperature inside the grain silo only stay on the surface of the time series, neglecting the dynamics information it contains. Studies have shown that the temperature change of grain inside the silo is affected by a variety of factors, such as air temperature, silo temperature, air humidity, silo humidity, CO2 concentration, ventilation time, etc.7. These factors do not exist in isolation, instead, they interact with each other, influence each other, and jointly determine the change in the temperature series of stored grain. Due to the nonlinear characteristics and complexity of the grain temperature time series, the application of chaos theory in predicting grain storage temperature can help uncover the rich dynamics information within the series, leading to more accurate predictions. Chaotic time series prediction is an important area of application of chaos theory, involving various methods and techniques. Among them are traditional multilayer perceptron8,9, support vector machine10, radial basis function network11–13, fuzzy neural network14, convolutional neural network15,16 and deep belief neural network17. The RBF neural network offers a simple model structure, fewer tuning parameters, fast learning speed, and high prediction accuracy. It is suitable for solving nonlinear problems and has a unique advantage in time series simulation and prediction. However, RBF network is sensitive to noise in the input data, which may affect the prediction accuracy of the network if the input data contains noise. Additionally, it requires pre-determining the network structure, including the number and distribution of nodes in the input layer and the hidden layer. This process typically involves numerous experiments and adjustments, leading to a high degree of subjectivity and limitations in the RBF network model.

Phase space reconstruction

In the process of phase space reconstruction, the time delay and embedding dimension are two core parameters, and their selection has a decisive influence on the effectiveness of the reconstruction. Due to the nonlinear characteristics and complexity of the time series of grain storage temperature, the one-dimensional time series can be expanded into a multidimensional series through phase space reconstruction, and the multidimensional series contains the information of each state, thus making the prediction more realistic.

It describes the sensitivity of the chaotic system to the initial value. Since the small data volume method proposed by Rosenstein et al.19 has the advantages of a smaller computational volume and is relatively easy to operate, this paper has chosen to adopt this method to calculate the maximum Lyapunov exponent. RBF neural network

RBF neural network is favored for its strong nonlinear fitting ability and fast convergence speed, and thus has been chosen to construct a prediction model for grain bin temperature. Its structure is a three-layer feedforward network20. The transformation of the RBF network from the input space to the hidden layer space is nonlinear, while the transformation from the hidden layer space to the output layer space is linear. Enhancement of RBF neural network

Weight updating is a core step in neural network learning. It allows the network to progressively approximate the objective function to achieve accurate prediction or classification of the input data. The traditional Least Mean Fourth (LMF) algorithm has the disadvantages of slow convergence, poor adaptability to nonlinear or time-varying systems, susceptibility to noise interference, and the third power of the error signal in its updating rule, which inherently makes it prone to instability. In the Quantum Calculus-based Least Mean Fourth (q-LMF) algorithm, the performance of the conventional LMF can be improved while maintaining the stability of the algorithm through the use of an additional control term, q.

The q-calculus (Quantum-Calculus)22 is a mathematical theory that extends classical calculus and involves several concepts related to q-difference equations and q-difference operators. Its main areas of application include physics, probability theory, statistics, quantum mechanics, and numerical analysis. C-ERBF model

The strength of RBF neural networks lies in their ability to learn and map arbitrarily complex nonlinear relationships. This is important because most real-life input and output relationships are inherently nonlinear. Traditional RBF neural networks are typically trained using the gradient descent method, which is simple to implement and easy to understand. However, it has a slow convergence speed, is susceptible to noise interference, and is not suitable for solving practical application problems. This paper utilizes the qXE-NLMF algorithm to enhance the prediction performance of RBF network (ERBF) and boost the model training speed. C-ERBF modeling for predicting grain storage temperature

ERBF network model demonstrates outstanding predictive performance in Mackey–Glass theoretical chaotic systems. To validate its performance in real dynamic systems, the predictive accuracy is evaluated using time series data of grain storage temperatures. Due to the nonlinear characteristics and complexity of the grain temperature time series, incorporating chaos theory into the prediction process of stored grain temperature can capture the rich dynamic information hidden in the series more effectively and enhance the accuracy of the prediction.

Data sources and pre-processing

In this paper, the bungalow barn is the subject of study. The temperature of the grain is monitored in layers using temperature measuring cables arranged in the silo. Although this method can provide the temperature value of each monitoring point, considering that there may be hundreds of temperature detection points distributed in a grain silo, the data collection is carried out every once in a while, resulting in a significant volume of generated data. This large amount of data is not intuitive or efficient enough to analyze the temperature distribution of the grain pile effectively. Studies have shown that the main parts of grain piles susceptible to infestation are concentrated in areas of active grain temperature change, such as the surface layer of the pile. This is because the surface location of the silo experiences the greatest temperature difference, the least stable change, and the highest frequency of high temperatures. During the hot summer months, temperatures in the region about 30 to 50 cm below the grain surface may exceed 20 °C or even reach as high as 30 °C. The average temperature of the first layer is selected as an input to the prediction model.

The data originated from a grain silo in Jilin City, where the stored grain variety is corn. The temperature data of various locations within the silo were obtained through real-time monitoring of temperature sensors placed at different points inside the silo in this paper. The top view of the distribution of temperature monitoring points inside the bungalow silo is shown in Fig. 5, which is divided into seven layers, with each layer containing 60 (6 × 10) temperature monitoring points.Fig. 5Plane distribution of temperature measurement cables (top view).

Plane distribution of temperature measurement cables (top view).

To more comprehensively reflect the effectiveness and generalization of the C-ERBF model, this paper additionally sets up a set of comparison experiments, whose data come from a grain depot in Xinglong, Zhengzhou, and the stored grain variety is wheat, and a total of 2,220 sets of samples (the second group) were collected from 2022-5-15 to 2023-5-20.

Parameter setting of C-ERBF model

The structure of the ERBF neural network constructed in this paper is “16-30-1”: since the embedding dimension of the grain storage temperature sequence is 16, 16 nodes are set in the input layer. The performance of the model with different numbers of hidden layer nodes is assessed through cross-validation, and the optimal number of hidden layer nodes is chosen to be 30 nodes, the output layer is configured with 1 node. Similarly, the structure of the second ERBF network is set as 7-30-1. The learning rate of the model is set to 1e-3, the Dropout probability is set to 0. 2, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$q$$\end{document}q is set to 3. In the previous section, it has been calculated that a total of 2100 phase points were generated after phase space reconstruction. The first 1700 phase point data are used as the training set, and the last 400 phase point data are used as the test set. The normalized data is then used to train the C-ERBF network, and the network is iterated 100 times.

Conclusion

Aiming at the complex nonlinear system of temperature inside the grain silo, this paper develops a temperature prediction model for grain silos using chaos and an enhanced RBF neural network. The superiority of this model lies in the fact that the development law of the original system is first obtained from the time series of any one component using the chaotic phase space reconstruction technique. Subsequently, the hyperparameter weight updating process in the RBF neural network is optimized using the qXE-NLMF algorithm to enhance the model performance. In the simulation experiments of Makcey-Glass chaotic time series prediction, it is concluded that the ERBF network has faster convergence speed and lower steady-state error compared to the traditional RBF network. After being verified by grain storage temperature prediction experiments, the RMSE of the C-ERBF model proposed in this paper is reduced to 0.0116, and the R2 reaches 0.9950. This performance is the best and more significant compared to other models. It provides a certain reference value for monitoring the grain situation and offers a new perspective for modeling and predicting other nonlinear sequence data.

In the course of the study, it was found that the prediction method still has a lot to be explored in depth. For example, in this paper, only the average temperature of the first layer of the grain pile is used for univariate prediction, and further multivariate prediction methods can be combined to add factors such as air temperature, air humidity, bin humidity, ventilation time, and so on. Multivariate data contain more information about the prime mover system.Therefore, phase space reconstruction using multivariate data has the advantage of improving prediction accuracy, especially when the observation sequence contains noise. The phase space simply reconstructed from univariate time series may not truly reflect the dynamical properties of the original system. In contrast, multivariate time series can provide richer and more complete information about the system.

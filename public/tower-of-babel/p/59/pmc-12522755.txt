Grain shape in rice: a Genome-wide association study of the effector genes
Authors: Nansheng Wang, Muhammad Ahmad Hassan, Kang Li, Kunneng Zhou, Quan Gan, Jiafa Xia, Cuixiang Lin, Zefu Li, Dahu Ni, Fengshun Song
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRice is an important grain crop widely cultivated across the world. Its yield and quality have always been the focus of breeders’ attention, significantly impacting world food security. Rice grain shape, primarily characterized by grain length and width, is an important quantitative trait controlled by multiple genes, which not only directly affects rice yield but is also closely related to rice quality. Therefore, mining and utilizing the main QTLs and genes responsible for grain shape regulation is significant for breeding high-yielding rice varieties.ResultsIn this study, 265 natural population materials were used to characterize the related phenotypes of grain length (GL), grain width (GW), and the grain length-width ratio (GLWR) over 2 years, revealing abundant phenotypic variations. Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) combined with 4,451,085 single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) markers distributed on 12 chromosomes, identified 61 significantly related QTLs, 14 of which are similar to previously reported rice grain type-related loci. Among these 61, three GL related QTLs (qGL3.1, qGL3.2 and qGL10.2), 1 GW related QTL (qGW5.1) and 11 GLWR related QTLs (qGLWR2.1, qGLWR2.3, qGLWR3.3, qGLWR3.4, qGLWR3.5, qGLWR4.2, qGLWR5.2, qGLWR5.3, qGLWR6.1, qGLWR9.2 and qGLWR10.2) were detected in two consecutive years. Three candidate genes, LOC_Os03g39710, LOC_Os05g09480, and LOC_Os09g36350, were identified through functional annotation and haplotype analysis of these co-existing QTLs.ConclusionIn brief, we detected 14, 12, and 35 QTLs associated with GL, GW, and GLWR, respectively. We further screened the grain shape-related candidate genes by functional annotation and haplotype analysis of all genes in the QTL regions. This study lays the foundation for further cloning and genetic regulation of rice grain-shape genes, providing innovative insights for rice molecular breeding designs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-025-07420-x.Keywords: Rice, Grain shape, GWAS, QTLs, Candidate genes

Background

Rice is one of the most important food crops in the world, and more than half of the global population consumes rice as their staple food [1]. Over half of China’s population consumes rice as their staple [2, 3]. Although dwarf breeding and hybrid advantage have achieved two revolutionary leaps in rice yield [4, 5], the global food crisis has been intensifying with the continuous growth of the population base and the decreasing area of effective arable land. Global rice production is hypothesized to increase by 40% by 2030 to meet the growing population’s food needs [6]. Under this severe form, high-yield and high-quality breeding remains the main response [7]. In addition, as the standard of living improves, an increasing number of people desire to consume high-quality rice [8]. The appearance quality of rice is the most intuitive quality trait, directly affecting the consumer’s purchase and its commodity value. The grain shape traits of rice mainly include the kernel’s length, width, and thickness, which are directly related to rice quality and yield. In recent years, researchers worldwide have conducted numerous studies on rice grain shape, aiming to elucidate the genetic and molecular mechanisms underlying this trait.

According to relevant studies, the shape of rice grains is considered a quantitative trait controlled by many main-effectors and micro-effector genes [9–11]. More than 400 QTLs related to grain shape have been localized so far [12–14]. Some of the genes are involved in various signaling pathways that regulate grain shape, including the G-protein signaling pathway, mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway, ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, multiple phytohormone signaling pathways, transcriptional regulators, microRNAs (miRNAs), and epigenetic regulatory pathways. And the main primary genes that have been cloned for grain shape-related traits are grain width-related genes GW2 [15] GW5 [16]; grain length-related genes GS3 [17], qGL3 [18], OsLG3 [19], GL4 [20] and OsLG3b [21]; and the primary genes that simultaneously control both grain length and grain width are GL2/GS2 [22], GSA1 [23], GL6 [24], GL7/GW7 [25], GW6a [26], and GS9 [27]. HGW (Heading and Grain Weight) regulates grain width and tasseling and encodes a cytoplasmic and nucleus-localized plant-specific ubiquitin-associated structural domain (UBA) protein. HGW mutation reduces grain width by decreasing the number of cells in the outer layer of the glume. The expression of HGW is closely related to the expression of many key genes of the ubiquitin pathway and grain shape regulation. It is a more upstream ubiquitin pathway gene regulating grain shape [28]. GLW7 is a gene that governs grain length and grain weight and encodes an SPL family transcription factor, OsSPL13. Overexpression of GLW7 increases grain length by controlling the size of glume cells, and loss-of-function of this gene results in shorter kernels. GLW7 binds to the promoter of the small-grain gene, SRS5, and regulates SRS5 expression. GLW7 is important in selecting rice seeds for domestication in different regions [29]. GLW7 plays a crucial role in the domestication and selection of rice varieties in the various areas and is of high breeding value. Gn1a (OsCKX2), a QTL controlling grain size and yield, encodes a cytokinin oxidase/dehydrogenase, which degrades intracellular cytokinins. Reducing the expression of this gene results in the accumulation of cytokinin in the floral meristem, which promotes cell division in the meristem and ultimately results in changes in grain size and seed number [30, 31]. GIF1 encodes a cell wall translocating enzyme homologous to Mn1 in maize and LIN5 in tomato, which affects sugar metabolism by controlling the sucrase activity, mainly during the early filling stage. It thus regulates the size of the seed grain [32]. Overexpression of the gene with its promoter increased yield, whereas when GIF1 was overexpressed with the 35 S or wax promoter, plants showed a small-grain phenotype [33, 34].

The genome-wide association study (GWAS) is a powerful tool for investigating the genetic basis of complex traits in plants. It reveals many previously unknown relationships between genes and traits by analysing the association of genetic variation and phenotypes across the genome [35]. The GWAS can process large amounts of SNP data, providing unprecedented resolution and coverage for plant genome research [36].

In this study, 265 natural populations were used as experimental materials for statistical analysis of grain length, grain width, and grain aspect ratio traits, Genome-wide Association Study combined with 4,451,085 polymorphic SNPs obtained from high-quality sequencing of natural populations and two years of phenotypic data, and screening of candidate genes for QTL regions co-localized in the two years of data. It aims better to understand the genomic basis of rice grain size and provide a theoretical basis for improving it through molecular breeding.

Results

Phenotypic variation and correlation of grain size in rice

The phenotypic variation of 265 rice materials in 2022 and 2023 for grain size-related traits was rich (Table 1), with coefficients of variation ranging from 13.19% to 26.66%, among which the coefficients of variation for grain length and width ratios were the largest at 26.66% in 2023 and the smallest at 13.19% for grain length in 2023. The mean values of grain length in 2022 and 2023 were 8.52 ± 1.13 and 8.64 ± 1.14, their phenotypic values ranging from 6.08 to 11.76 mm and 6.47 to 12.03 mm, respectively; the mean values of grain width were 2.87 ± 0.44 and 2.86 ± 0.44, their phenotypic values ranging from 2.04 to 3.81 mm and 1.9 to 3.68 mm, respectively; the mean values of the grain length-to-width ratio were 3.08 ± 0.82 and 3.14 ± 0.83, their phenotypic values ranging from 1.72 to 5.62 and 1.78 to 5.38, respectively (Fig. 1). It is evident that grain-related traits were normally distributed, and multiple genes controlled these traits. Moreover, Fig. 1 exhibited the phenotypic variations of the grain size traits such as the grain length and length-to-width ratios of Xian rice were significantly higher than that of Geng rice in both 2022 and 2023, respectively; while the grain width of Geng rice was significantly higher than that of Xian rice in both 2022 and 2023, respectively.

The correlation coefficients between the 265 natural population grain size-related traits are shown in Fig. 2. The correlation coefficients between grain length and aspect ratio were significantly positive in 2022 and 2023, and between grain width and aspect ratio were significantly negative. The correlation coefficients between grain length and width were also significantly negative in both years, with a value of −0.71 (Fig. 2). These results indicated that there were significant genetic variations in these traits of the 265 rice populations. Therefore, it is rational to conduct genome-wide association studies for grain shape traits.

Genome-wide association study of grain size in rice

We identified more than 4 million high-quality SNPs in the genome sequences of 265 rice materials and measured data for phenotypes related to grain size characteristics (GL, GW, and GLWR). Genome-wide association analyses were performed using mixed linear models combining SNP and phenotypic data. We screened 300 significant SNP loci (Table S2). Considering the decay distance of rice linkage disequilibrium (LD), neighbouring SNPs with intervals less than 200 kb were defined as single QTLs. The SNP with the lowest p-value was used as the lead SNP with an association threshold of 6.76E-7 and identified 61 QTLs (Fig. 3). Among them, 14 QTLs were detected in 2022 and 2023 (Table 2); which includes 3 QTLs of grain length (GL) (qGL3.1, qGL3.2 and qGL10.2), one QTL of grain width (GW) (qGW5.1), and 10 QTLs of grain length width aspect ratio (GLWR) (qGLWR2.1, qGLWR2.3, qGLWR3.3, qGLWR3.4, qGLWR3.5, qGLWR4.2, qGLWR5.2, qGLWR5.3, qGLWR6.1, qGLWR9.2 and qGLWR10.2), respectively.

61 QTL positions associated with GL, GW, and GLWR

A total of 14 significant QTLs were detected for grain length, with phenotypic coefficients of variation ranging from 9.91% to 16.69%, in 2022 and 2023, on chromosomes 1, 3, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, and 11. Among them, qGL1.1, qGL1.2 and qGL1.3 on chromosome 1, qGL6.1 on chromosome 6, qGL7.1 on chromosome 7, qGL9.1 on chromosome 9, qGL10.1 on chromosome 10, qGL11.1 and qGL11.2 on chromosome 11 were all significant loci and all localized in 2022; qGL3.3 on chromosome 3 and qGL5.1 on chromosome 5 were also significant loci and all localized in 2023. Among them, three QTLs, qGL3.1, qGL3.2, and qGL10.2, were co-localized in both years (2022–2023). qGL1.2 was located near OsNF-YC10, a gene that has been reported to regulate grain size in rice. OsNF-YC10 and GW5, genes reported to be the main effector of grain size regulation in rice, were located near qGL1.2 and qGL5.1, respectively.

A total of 12 significant QTLs were detected for grain width in 2022 and 2023, with phenotypic coefficients of variation ranging from 10.33% to 12.83%, located on chromosomes 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 10, and 11. Among them, the QTLs on chromosomes 3 (qGW3.1), 6 (qGW6.1), and 7 (qGW7.1) were localized in the year 2022, and their phenotypic coefficients of variation ranged from 11.68% to 12.68%. While the QTLs on chromosomes 1 (qGW1.1, qGW1.2, qGW1.3), 2 (qGW2.1), 10 (qGW10.1, qGW10.2), and 11 (qGW11.1, qGW11.2) were localized in the year 2023; among them, maximum phenotypic coefficient of variation range was 12.83% for qGW11.2 on chromosome 11, and the minimum phenotypic coefficient of variation range was 10.33% for qGW10.1 on chromosome 10. In addition, qGW5.1 on chromosome 5 was co-localized in both years, and this locus identified the main effector gene GW5, which has been reported to regulate grain size in rice in previous studies. Besides this main effector locus, the genes reported to regulate grain width in rice were also found in the vicinity of the loci of qGW1.1 (OsNAC20, OsNAC020), qGW3.1 (HDR3), and qGW10.1 (OsGBP3) in previous studies.

A total of 35 significant QTLs were detected for GLWR in 2022 and 2023, with phenotypic coefficients of variation ranging from 10.03% to 16.65%. Among them, qGLWR1.3 and qGLWR1.4 on chromosome 1, qGLWR2.2 on chromosome 2, qGLWR3.2 on chromosome 3, qGLWR4.1 on chromosome 4, qGLWR5.1 on chromosome 5, qGLWR7.2 on chromosome 7, qGLWR10.3 on chromosome 10, qGLWR12.2 on chromosome 12 were all significant loci and all localized in 2022; qGLWR1.1, qGLWR1.2, qGLWR1.5 and qGLWR1.6 on chromosome 1, qGLWR3.1 on chromosome 3, qGLWR4.3 on chromosome 4, qGLWR7.1 on chromosome 7, qGLWR8.1 on chromosome 8, qGLWR9.1 on chromosome 9, qGLWR10.1 on chromosome 10, qGLWR11.1, qGLWR11.2, and qGLWR11.3 on chromosome 11, qGLWR12.1 and qGLWR12.3 on chromosome 12 were all significant loci and all localized in 2023. And among them, 11 QTLs, qGLWR2.1, qGLWR2.3, qGLWR3.3, qGLWR3.4, qGLWR3.5, qGLWR4.2, qGLWR5.2, qGLWR5.3, qGLWR6.1, qGLWR9.2, and qGLWR10.2 were co-localized in both years. Some genes reported to regulate rice grain size in previous studies, such as OsAPX9, GFR1, and OsYUC11, were also identified.

Identification of grain length candidate genes and haplotype analysis

After removing genes encoding hypothetical proteins, reverse transcriptional transposons, and transposon proteins, 16 stable QTLs were jointly identified in 2022 and 2023. These 16 QTLs were then subjected to high-density association and gene haplotype analysis to identify candidate genes. In the qGL3.2 region (22.0 ~ 22.4 Mb), a total of 21 genes were identified, among which LOC_Os03g39710 (Fig. 4) was annotated as a monosaccharide transporter gene. One SNP site is located on the promoter, three SNP sites are located on exons, and five SNP sites are located on introns. All materials can be categorized into three haplotypes (HapA, HapB, and HapC) based on the SNPs present in their complementary DNA (cDNA). These include ACAACCGAG for HapA, ACGACCGAG for HapB, and GTGGCGAGG for HapC. For LOC_Os03g39710, in HapA, 73 Xian and 1 Geng accessions were associated, in HapB, 18 Xian and 0 Geng accessions were associated, and in HapC, 3 Xian and 81 Geng accessions were associated, respectively (Table S3). Statistical analyses of grain lengths in 2022 and 2023 revealed significant differences between the haplotypes. In 2022, the mean values of HapA, HapB, and HapC were 8.96, 9.62, and 7.75 mm, respectively. In 2023, the mean values of grain length of HapA, HapB, and HapC were 9.11, 9.69, and 7.85 mm, respectively. In both years, HapB was significantly higher than HapA and HapC, while HapC was significantly lower than HapA and HapB, suggesting that LOC_Os03g39710 may be a candidate gene for regulating grain length.

Identification of grain width candidate genes and haplotype analysis

A total of 23 genes were identified in qGW5.1 at the 5.0 ~ 5.4 Mb position of chromosome 5, co-located in 2022 and 2023, of which LOC_Os05g09480 (Fig. 5) was annotated as OsIAA16-Auxin-responsive Aux and IAA gene family member. There are three promoter SNP sites. All materials can be categorized into three haplotypes (HapA, HapB, and HapC) based on the SNPs present in their cDNAs, specifically GCC for HapA, TCT for HapB, and TGT for HapC. For LOC_Os05g09480, in HapA, 6 Xian and 16 Geng accessions were associated, in HapB, 1 Xian and 73 Geng accessions were associated, and in HapC, 114 Xian and 2 Geng accessions were associated, respectively (Table S3). Statistical analysis of grain widths in 2022 and 2023 revealed significant differences between haplotypes, with the mean values of grain widths for HapA, HapB, and HapC in 2022 being 2.77, 3.30, and 2.59 mm, respectively. These values were also observed in 2023, with the mean values remaining unchanged at 2.77, 3.30, and 2.59 mm, respectively. HapC were 2.78, 3.30, and 2.57 mm, respectively. In both years, HapB was significantly higher than HapA and HapC, and HapC was significantly lower than HapA and HapB. These findings proposed that LOC_Os05g09480 might be a candidate gene for regulating grain width.

Discussion

China is the birthplace of rice, with a long history of rice culture and rich varietal resources [37]. The annual rice output in China accounts for about 37% of the yearly global production, ranking first in the world [38]. Therefore, identifying QTL associated with rice grain size traits is a key objective in rice genetic breeding. In this research investigation, 265 natural populations provided by the Rice Research Institute of Anhui Academy of Agricultural Sciences were used for phenotyping three rice grain size-related traits: grain length, width, and aspect ratio. It was found that the grain length was inversely proportional to grain width, and the aspect ratio was directly proportional to grain length and inversely proportional to grain width. The results showed that grain shape is a quantitative trait controlled by multiple genes, and it was also validated by previous findings [39, 40]. The phenotypes of this natural population have a wide range of genetic variations that can be exploited, making it an ideal population for mining high-quality rice grain size-related genes using GWAS.

In this study, we used Tassel software in GWAS to analyse the detected QTLs related to rice grain size and compared them with the grain size QTLs localized by the previous authors. We identified 17 QTLs that were similar to those reported by earlier authors. Among them, OsNF-YC10 [41], a nuclear factor Y transcription factor family member encoding a predicted histone transcription factor, was experimentally found to regulate grain size and shape by affecting glume cell proliferation. Compared with the wild type, GW5L or GW5 [42] overexpression lines had elongated grains, which affected grain length. OsNAC20 and OsNAC26 [43] mutations alone did not affect grain shape, but the OsNAC20/26 double mutants significantly reduced starch and storage protein contents, resulting in a decrease in grain width, grain thickness, and 1,000-grain weight [43]. HDR3 [26] is a ubiquitin receptor with ubiquitin-binding activity that interacts with the ubiquitin ligase GW2. HDR3 regulates rice kernel size by altering cell proliferation in the glumes of spikelets, similar to GW6a [26]. Transgenic rice overexpressing HDR3 produced larger grains than the control, while HDR3 knockout lines produced smaller grains. OsGBPs [44] are annotated as GAGA-binding transcription factors, and OsGBP3 induces grain length and plant height. Grain length, width, and plant height were reduced in OsGBP3 RNA-disturbed plants and elevated in OsGBP3 overexpressing plants. OsCKX2 (Gn1a) [45] gene, which encodes an enzyme that degrades cytokinins, significantly increased grain length and thousand-grain weight but did not change substantially grain width and grain thickness in ckx1 ckx2-19 compared to wild-type Nippon Haru; and OsCKX2 acted in the growth and development of basal internodes, tillers, leaves, spikes, and grains of rice, negatively regulating stem thickness, leaf length and width, spike and seed size [46]. Os1BGlu4 [47]was annotated as a cytoplasmic β-glucosidase. Compared with the wild type (WT), the mutant showed a decrease in grain length and width of 13.17% and 12.45%, respectively, relative to WT. OsCCS52A [48] is annotated as a cell cycle transition factor; OsCCS52A is involved in mediating the exit of cells from mitosis into the nuclear re-replication cycle in the rice endosperm, thereby maintaining normal rice seed size. It’s a pure plant mutant that shows dwarfing and a smaller grain size. OsXyn1 [49] is annotated as xylanase, and its mutant’s grain length, width, and weight were significantly reduced, as was the number of spikes in the more phenotypically severe ss1 monocot. OsCYP20-2 [50] interacted with OsSYF2 to regulate rice grain length through mRNA precursor splicing. The drt1 [51], annotated as a syntaxin, showed significant changes in the overall morphology of the mutant drt1 compared with the wild-type (WT), including reduced lengths of the aboveground part, roots, spikes, and kernel. OsAPX9 [52] is annotated as ascorbate peroxidase; the OsAPX9 overexpression line exhibited higher ascorbate peroxidase activity and was more drought-resistant. The overexpression lines had larger grains, characterized by increased grain length, width, and weight. Compared to the wild type, knockdown of GFR1 [53] resulted in decreased irrigation rate and thousand-grain weight, and overexpression of GFR1 resulted in increased grain length and width. The transcription factor OsNF-YB1 could directly interact with the promoter of OsYUC11 [54] and activate its expression to promote growth hormone biosynthesis, and the increased growth hormone level stimulated the expression of genes related to sucrose metabolism and starch synthesis in the endosperm cells, which led to an increase in starch accumulation as well as grain size, indicating the reliability of the genes or QTLs identified by this study’s phenotypic data and association analysis.

Three candidate genes, LOC_Os03g39710, LOC_Os05g09480, and LOC_Os09g36350, associated with grain size were screened by selecting commonly detected QTLs in two years, combined with haplotype analysis. We utilized the public database [RAP-DB | HOME (affrc.go.jp)] to obtain the expression levels of three genes in various tissues for analysis (Figure S1). The expression of LOC _ Os03g39710 was the lowest in Anther 0.7–1.0 mm and the highest in Palea 7.0 mm. The expression of LOC _ Os05g09480 was the lowest in Endosperm 10 DAF and the highest in Inflorescence 0.6–1.0 mm. The expression of LOC _ Os09g36350 was the lowest in Leaf blade ripening and highest in stem ripening. Among them, the LOC_Os03g39710 gene was interpreted as a monosaccharide transporter gene. It has been shown that monosaccharides are the primary source of carbon and energy. Under photosynthesis, carbon dioxide is converted into monosaccharides, such as glucose, which serve as the central unit of carbon metabolism, storage, and transport. The intermediate storage and transport form of glucose is sucrose and its derivatives [55]. Sucrose and hexoses produced from the breakdown of sucrose are essential for early grain seed development and are used to promote cell division and elongation. Seed grain development requires sugar from the phloem to support the growth and development of the substrate [56]. Meanwhile, LOC_Os03g39710 is expressed in thick-walled tissues and xylem cells [57]. Therefore, we can speculate that the LOC_Os03g39710 gene is the most likely candidate gene for grain length in rice. The LOC_Os05g09480 gene encodes an AUX/IAA family protein, OsIAA16, which is involved in the growth hormone signaling pathway. Growth hormone acts through Auxin Response Factors (ARFs), whereas AUX/IAA proteins are repressors of the growth hormone signaling pathway. Growth hormone regulates rice developmental processes, including seed growth, embryo development, and gametophyte formation, by controlling the expression of growth hormone-responsive genes [58, 59]. Therefore, we can speculate that the LOC_Os03g39710 gene is the most likely candidate for rice grain width. LOC_Os09g36350 is a glycoside hydrolase, which is thought to be related to cellulose biosynthesis [60]. The rice cell wall comprises various substances, including cellulose, hemicellulose, lignin, and proteins, with cellulose being a key component [61]. Rice grain shell and endosperm are the parts of rice that contain the most cellulose [61], and LOC_Os09g36350 may affect rice grain size by influencing the grain shell cell wall. We can hypothesize that the LOC_Os09g36350 gene is most likely to be a candidate gene for rice grain aspect ratio.

In[62] summary, we detected 14, 12, and 35 QTLs associated with GL, GW, and GLWR, respectively. We further screened the grain shape-related candidate genes by functional annotation and haplotype analysis of all genes in the QTL regions. This finding contributes to understanding the genetic basis of rice grain shape, and our study lays a good foundation for the subsequent functional validation of genes and molecular breeding designs. Currently, our research team is conducting further experimental trials to identify other related genes, their overexpression, cellular and biochemical functions, and screening of mutant lines. In the future, we plan to validate these genes through knockout and overexpression experiments to confirm that they are the primary effector genes responsible for defining rice grain shape.

Materials and methods

Experimental materials and field planting

The experimental materials were obtained from 265 natural populations, including 119 Geng rice and 146 Xian rice (Table S1). 11 of the 265 materials were from the Philippines, 3 were from Pakistan, 1 was from France, 3 were from Cambodia, 11 were from the United States, 4 were from Vietnam, and 232 were from China. All materials were planted in 2022 and 2023, in two consecutive years, at the Daxu Experimental Base of Anhui Academy of Agricultural Sciences, Hefei City, Anhui Province, China. Five rows of each variety were planted at 10 cm spacing within rows and 30 cm spacing between rows, with three replications. Conventional seedling culture and field management measures were adopted. Upon reaching the maturity stage (approx. 40 d after the flowering stage), five plants were selected from each material for harvesting, and the seeds were air-dried and stored.

Phenotype characterization and data analysis

Phenotypic characteristics, such as grain length (GL), grain width (GW), and grain length-width ratio (GLWR), of air-dried rice were determined using automated seed analysis and thousand-grain weight software (Model SC-G) in combination with an imaging device (Figure S2). The results of LD, PCA, and Kinship for the genotypes were mentioned in our previous research study [63].

The phenotypic data related to grain size were first organized using Excel software (version 2021). The statistical software, SPSS (version 29.0), was used to analyze the basic descriptive statistics of each trait, including the minimum, maximum, mean, standard deviation, and coefficient of variation for each trait. Secondly, R-studio software (version 2024.04.2 + 764) was used to analyze the correlation of rice grain size-related phenotypes, and the analysis results were plotted in heat maps using Origin software (version 2023).

DNA extraction and SNP genotyping

Sequencing was performed on 265 materials, and leaves from each rice material were collected at the tillering phase (about 30 days after transplanting seedlings). The standard CTAB method was used to extract genomic DNA. UW Genomics performed library construction, sequencing, and sequence cleanup. The sequencing data were aligned to the rice reference genome (IRGSP-1.0) using the BWA software [64]. Then, using the SAMtools (version 1.1.18), the mapped results were converted to VCF format [65]. Subsequently, SNPs with a minimum allele frequency (MAF) of less than 5% were eliminated, and those with a deletion rate of less than 20% were retained using Plink (version 1.9) [66], resulting in 4,451,085 polymorphic SNPs.

Genome-wide association study

This study analyzed 4,451,085 polymorphic SNPs and three grain-associated traits from 265 natural populations using a Genome-wide Association Study (GWAS). The phenotypic data and SNPs were subjected to GWAS using TASSEL software (version 5.2.40) [67]. The model used was the Mixed Line Model (MLM). MLM). The threshold line of the MLM model was calculated using GEC (version 1.0) [68] software. Its suggestive P-value was set to 6.74E-7. Additionally, a 200 kb section was selected as the range of overlapping identifying marker-trait-related signals. SNPs with intervals of less than 200 kb were defined as single QTLs [69, 70]. If there are one or more significant SNPs in the LD region, these SNPS are regarded as a QTL, and the smallest SNP is taken as the lead SNP [69, 70].

Candidate gene identification and haplotype analysis

Based on the results of GWAS and the attenuation distance of LD extending 200 kb to the left and right as candidate intervals, candidate regions of genes on chromosomes were identified, and the functions of genes within the candidate regions were looked up in the National Rice Database Centre (https://www.ricedata.cn/). Heterozygotes and deletion alleles were excluded by haplotype analysis, and haplotype populations with less than 10 copies were deleted. T-tests were performed for the genes identified in the QTL to determine whether the locus would change grain size-related traits.

Primer designing and quantitative real-time qRT-PCR (qRT-PCR) validation

Primers for three candidate genes qRT-PCR validation were designed using the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) website (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/). The RNA was extracted from 5 cm young spikes of K195 (Xian), K37 (Xian), K211(Geng) and K251(Geng), respectively. Then, complementary DNA (cDNA) was obtained through reverse transcription. The cDNA was diluted 5 times with ddH2O as the template. The reverse transcription mixed system was added to the 96-well plate for fluorescence quantification, covered with a sealing film, and centrifuged at 1000 rpm for 1 min using a centrifuge. Then, the qRT-PCR reaction was carried out in the fluorescence quantitative PCR instrument. For each qRT-PCR reaction, three biological replicates were performed. Data were analysed by the 2−△△Ct method and their relative expression levels were calculated. The amount of gene expressions for GL, GW and GLWR were exhibited in the Figure S3.

Conclusions

In this research study, 265 natural populations of rice were utilized as materials for performance identification of grain length, grain width, and aspect ratio. It showed abundant phenotypic variation, which conformed to normal distribution. It is a suitable population for a Genome-wide Association Study. A genome-wide association study was performed to identify QTL loci related to grain size traits by combining phenotypes and SNPs. Haplotype analysis was performed, which, combined with gene annotation, identified three candidate genes related to grain size in rice. Among these, LOC_Os03g39710 is a candidate gene for regulating grain length, LOC_Os05g09480 is a candidate gene for regulating grain width, and LOC_Os09g36350 is a candidate gene for regulating grain width. LOC_Os09g36350 is a candidate gene for regulating GLWR, laying the foundation for prospective molecular breeding designs.

Identification of the IL-1β/miRNA-326 axis as a key regulator of ferroptosis in spinal cord injury
Authors: Senming Xu, Yangyang Jiang, Yuchang Gui, Jianmin Chen, Qinhe Pan, Minli Jiang, Jianwen Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionSpinal cord injury (SCI) is a devastating condition with high rates of disability and mortality, and secondary injury mechanisms such as ferroptosis have emerged as crucial contributors to its pathophysiology. However, the regulatory mechanisms underlying ferroptosis in SCI remain largely unclear.MethodsWe analyzed peripheral blood transcriptome data (GSE151371) from 10 healthy controls, 10 non-CNS trauma patients, and 38 SCI patients to identify differentially expressed mRNAs (DE-mRNAs) and miRNAs (DE-miRNAs). Ferroptosis-related genes (FRGs) were screened by intersecting DE-mRNAs with a curated list of FRGs. Functional enrichment analyses, regulatory network construction (lncRNA–miRNA–mRNA, TF-miRNA-mRNA), and drug target predictions were conducted. Introduction

Spinal cord injury (SCI) is a severe central nervous system injury with high disability and mortality [1]. Each year, the global incidence of SCI ranges from 250,000 to 500,000 new cases [2], highlighting the extensive impact of this condition worldwide. Spinal cord injury is classified into primary and secondary types. Primary spinal cord injury refers to the immediate damage to the spinal cord that occurs at the moment of the traumatic event. Secondary injury encompasses the progressive damage and functional impairment that follow the primary injury. Inflammation, cellular swelling, and lipid peroxidation triggered by the primary injury may exacerbate the initial spinal cord damage, leading to further neuronal death and functional loss [3]. Since the primary injury cannot be reversed, effective intervention for secondary injury after SCI is considered to be the key to treatment [4–6]. However, the mechanisms of secondary injury are complex and poorly understood, hence further exploration of these mechanisms after SCI is essential [7–9].

Ferroptosis is a form of regulated, non-apoptotic cell death that is dependent on iron and characterized by lipid peroxidation [10]. Emerging evidence suggests that ferroptosis, a form of regulated cell death driven by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation, plays a key role in the pathophysiology of secondary injury after SCI [11, 12]. Neurons suffer from ferroptosis after spinal cord injury, and exhibit disordered mitochondrial quality control during ferroptosis [13]. The application of ferroptosis inhibitors has been shown to partially improve mild spinal cord injury and its prognosis [14]. Targeting ferroptosis has thus been proposed as a promising therapetic approach to reduce secondary damage and improve outcomes [15–17]. With the advancement of transcriptome sequencing technologies, comprehensive bioinformatics analyses enable systematic identification of ferroptosis-related genes (FRGs) involved in SCI. This approach offers new opportunities to unravel the intricate molecular mechanisms underlying secondary injury.

This study aimed to identify differentially expressed ferroptosis-related genes (DE-FRGs) and DE-miRNAs following SCI. The ferroptosis-related regulatory networks were then constructed based on key FRGs screened by bioinformatics analysis. Subsequently, these key DE-FRGs were validated in the GSE166009 SCI Dataset. Furthermore, we explored the interaction between miR-326 and IL-1β within this network and confirmed their expression patterns in a rat SCI model. These findings may provide novel insights into the ferroptosis-mediated molecular mechanisms of SCI and suggest potential therapeutic targets for intervention.

Materials and methods

Gene expression omnibus GEO dataset analysis

The normalized expression matrix of GSE151371 and GSE166009 dataset were downloaded from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gds/?term=GSE151371 and https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gds/?term=GSE166009). GSE151371 dataset encompassed 10 samples of peripheral blood extracted from healthy control (HC) individuals, 10 samples from trauma control (TC) patients with non-central nervous system injuries, and 38 samples from traumatic spinal cord injury (TSCI) patients. The GSE166009 dataset was used as a validation cohort, comprising 12 samples divided into four groups (2-hour group, 2-day group, 7-day group, and control group), with three samples in each group. The workflow of this study is shown in Fig. 1.

Workflow of this study

Ferroptosis database

Ferroptosis-related genes were obtained from the FerrDb database (http://www.zhounan.org/ferrdb/) [18]. This process resulted in the identification of 264 driver genes, 238 suppressor genes, and 11 marker genes. After removing any duplicate entries, we compiled a refined list of 484 unique FRGs for further analysis.

Differentially expressed miRNAs, mRNAs and FRGs

The genes from the GSE151371 dataset were annotated using the resources available on the Xiantao Academic website (http://www.xiantaozi.com/). DE-miRNAs and differentially expressed mRNAs (DE-mRNAs) were screened out by pairwise comparisons among the three groups (HC, TC, TSCI) using The Sangerbox 3.0 website (http://sangerbox.com/) [19]. |log2(Fold Change)| ≥ 1.0 and adjusted P-value ≤ 0.05 were identified as DEGs. DEGs overlapping with 484 FRGs were designated as DE-FRGs.

Enrichment analysis

Gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses, was used to explore the function of all DE-mRNAs, with the thresholds set as P ≤ 0.05 and false discovery rate (FDR) ≤ 0.1, performed via the Sangerbox 3.0 website. The analysis results were visualized as Dotplots.

Protein-protein interactions network analysis and screening of hub FRGs

To effectively analyze the functions and associations of differentially expressed ferroptosis-related genes (DE-FRGs), a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed using the STRING database (version 11.5) (http://string-db.org/). The minimum required interaction score was set as 0.400. The result was visualized using Cytoscape 3.10.0 software. CytoHubba (version 0.1) plug-in of Cytoscape was used to determine the top five genes with the highest connectivity, which were selected as the hub FRGs.

Prediction of target relationship between DE-miRNAs and hub FRGs

The target miRNAs for the 5 hub FRGs genes were predicted through the mirDIP online database (http://ophid.utoronto.ca/mirDIP/) [20, 21]. All DEmiRNAs of GSE151371 were identified using the miRDB database (https://mirdb.org/) [22, 23] to obtain mature miRNAs. All the predicted miRNAs’ intersections with the mature miRNAs were included. Then the miRNA–mRNA target relationship was confirmed based on the inverse expression patterns of the respective molecules. The interactive network was visualized using Cytoscape 3.10.0 software.

Construction of the lncRNA network

Utilizing the competitive endogenous RNA hypothesis, lncRNAs were predicted that could potentially interact with miRNAs. This prediction was done through the ENCORI dataset (http://rnasysu.com/encori/index.php) [24], focusing on the miRNAs that were part of the established miRNA-mRNA relationship pairs. The lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA network consisting of hub DE-FRGs was constructed by Cytoscape 3.10.0 software.

Establishment of the TF–miRNA–mRNA network

Based on miRNAs included in miRNA-mRNA relationship pairs, potentially interacting Transcription factors (TFs) were predicted through the TransmiR v2.0 database (http://www.cuilab.cn/transmir) [25, 26]. The TF-miRNA-mRNA network composed of hub FRGs was established using Cytoscape 3.10.0 software.

Prediction of hub FRG-targeted drugs

Prediction of hub DE-FRG-targeted drugs was carried out using DGIdb (https://dgidb.org/). These drugs are expected to target the five key DE-FRGs: PPARG, PTGS2, IL-1β, MAPK3, and IFNG. The prediction was based on the default interaction parameters available in the database. Cytoscape 3.10.0 was used to visualize the resulting data.

Validation of DE‑FRGs and DE-miRNA expression in the GSE166009 SCI dataset

The De-FRGs were validated using the GSE166009 SCI dataset, which consists of sequencing data obtained from a rat model of SCI. This dataset encompasses four groups: a 2-hour post-SCI group, a 2-day post-SCI group, a 7-day post-SCI group, and a normal control group, with three samples per group. Each sample comprises spinal cords from three rats. The dataset was analyzed using GEO2R. The threshold for DEGs was set at a p-value ≤ 0.05, with an absolute value of |Log2FoldChange| ≥ 0.4 for mRNAs and ≥ 0.26 for miRNAs.

Rat SCI model

Adult female Sprague–Dawley rats (200 ~ 250 g) were supplied by the Animal Center of Guangxi Medical University (Nanning, Guangxi, China). A total of 16 adult female Sprague–Dawley rats were used in this study. Animals were randomly assigned to either the sham group (n = 8) or the SCI group (n = 8). All animal procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee and were performed in accordance with relevant guidelines. All rats were bred under controlled environmental conditions. The care and use of all animals were in accordance with the guidelines established by the National Institutes of Health of China. The experiment was approved by the Ethics Committee of Guangxi Medical University. After being weighed and marked, all rats were anesthetized by intraperitoneal injection with 1% (w/v) pentobarbital sodium (65 mg/kg) [27], and then underwent a laminectomy to expose the T10 spinal cord. The model of TSCI was established via the modified version of Allen’s method [28]. At the end of the surgery, the incision was sutured layer by layer. Rats in the SCI group were manually urinated twice a day until the bladder function recovered.

Euthanasia

At the end of the experiments, all rats were euthanized in accordance with the Guidelines for Euthanasia of Experimental Animals (GB/T 39760 − 2021) issued by the Chinese Association for Laboratory Animal Sciences. An overdose of sodium pentobarbital (200 mg/kg) was administered intravenously to ensure a humane and painless death. All procedures were performed by trained personnel to minimize suffering.

Behavioral assessment

The motor function of rat hind limbs was evaluated by the Basso, Beattie, and Bresnahan (BBB) locomotor scale score [29]. BBB scores range from 0 to 21, a score of 0 indicates complete paralysis of the hind limbs, while a score of 21 represents normal motor function. Behavioral assessments were conducted at different time points (0, 1, 3, and 7 days) after the operation. BBB locomotor assessment was performed on all animals (n = 8 per group) at 0, 1, 3, and 7 days after surgery. Rats should be allowed to move freely in an open field for 5 min before each assessment. To reduce bias, the behavioral assessments of the animals were conducted. Three researchers carried out these assessments. The researchers were blinded to both the animal groups and the specific time points of the experiment.

Real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR)

After completion of behavioral assessments, rats were euthanized at day 7 post-injury, and spinal cord tissue (~ 5 mm) centered at the lesion epicenter (T10) was rapidly collected for RNA extraction. The same cohort of animals used for BBB analysis was used for qPCR experiments (n = 8 per group). Total RNA was extracted from the rat spinal cord using NucleoZOL (Machery-Nagel, Germany), and RNA purity was detected by a spectrophotometer at 260/280 and 260/230 nm. cDNA was prepared using a reverse transcription kit (TransGen, Beijing, China) with the total RNA as the template, and then a PerfectStart® Green qPCR SuperMix Kit (TransGen, Beijing, China) and ABI 7500 System were used for PCR amplification experiments. The thermal cycles were set as follows: 94 °C for 30 s, 40 cycles of 94 °C for 5 s, and 60 °C for 30s. Each quantitative PCR reaction was performed in triplicate wells. The results for miRNA and mRNA were normalized based on the expression of U6 and Gapdh, respectively. The primers used in this study are listed in Table 1. The relative expression levels were calculated via the 2 − ΔΔCT method.

Dual-luciferase reporter gene assay

Luciferase vectors containing the wild-type (WT) and mutant (MUT) IL-1β sequences were constructed, targeting the binding sites in the promoter regions of miRNA-326 and IL-1β. 293T cells were co-transfected with hsa-miRNA-326 mimics or negative controls (NC) along with either the WT or MUT IL-1β vectors. All luciferase reporter assays were performed in three independent biological replicates using HEK293T cells. Following a 24-hour transfection period, the luciferase activity was measured using the dual-luciferase reporter assay system on a Spectra Max L instrument (Molecular Devices).

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using Statistical Product and Service Solutions (SPSS) software version 20.0. The BBB scores were analyzed using repeated measures and two-way ANOVA, and comparisons between groups on the same day were made using Tukey’s post-treatment test. To compare the results of RT-PCR between the two groups, data that followed a normal distribution were presented as the mean ± standard deviation ( ± SD) and analyzed using a t-test. For data that did not conform to a normal distribution, non-parametric tests were employed, with the median serving as the measure of central tendency. p ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Identification of ferroptosis-related genes (FRGs) and enrichment analysis in SCI

The gene expression profile GSE151371 was utilized to identify differentially expressed mRNAs (DE-mRNAs) and miRNAs (DE-miRNAs) among the healthy control (HC), non-CNS trauma control (TC), and SCI groups, with a predetermined threshold set at |Log2FoldChange| ≥ 1.0 and adjusted P ≤ 0.05. We performed differential expression analysis for SCI vs. HC, TC vs. HC, and SCI vs. TC comparisons. The results were presented in volcano plots (Fig. 2a–c). Subsequently, Venn diagram analysis was conducted on the identified DE-mRNAs and DE-miRNAs. We selected the subset of genes differentially expressed in the SCI group but not in the TC group for further study. A total of 1645 mRNAs and 47 miRNAs were identified for further analysis (Fig. 2d–e). To explore the connection between these DEGs and ferroptosis, we intersected the DE-mRNAs with 484 known ferroptosis-related genes (FRGs) and identified 38 DE-FRGs in SCI (Fig. 2f). The list of these 38 DE-FRGs is provided in Supplementary Table 1.

GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses were performed on the 38 DE-FRGs to investigate their biological functions. The top biological process (BP) terms included “response to lipid,” “unsaturated fatty acid metabolic process,” “inflammatory response,” and “cell death” (Fig. 3a). Cellular component (CC) analysis revealed enrichment in “secretory granule lumen,” “cytoplasmic vesicle lumen,” and “vesicle lumen” (Fig. 3b). Molecular function (MF) terms such as “oxidoreductase activity,” “dioxygenase activity,” and “iron ion binding” were significantly enriched (Fig. 3c). KEGG analysis demonstrated that the DE-FRGs were enriched in IL-17, HIF-1, TNF, and NOD-like receptor signaling pathways (Fig. 3d). Validation of ferroptosis-related genes expression in a SCI rat model

To further verify the changes of IL-1β/miRNA-326 in SCI, we constructed a SCI model. A TSCI rat model was established using a modified Allen’s method. BBB scores confirmed significant motor deficits in the SCI group compared to sham controls (Fig. 10a). We further detected changes in ferroptosis-related genes. RT-qPCR analysis revealed decreased GPX4 and increased ACSL4 expression in the SCI group, consistent with ferroptosis activation (Fig. 10b–c). Notably, IL-1β expression was elevated, while miR-326-3p levels were reduced in SCI rats (Fig. 10d–e). Therefore, IL-1β/miRNA-326 is significantly changed in SCI disease and is closely related to SCI.

Discussion

In this study, we analyzed a dataset comprising peripheral blood samples from 10 healthy controls, 10 non-CNS trauma patients, and 38 SCI patients. By identifying DEGs and DE-miRNAs, and intersecting these with FRGs, we found that SCI-associated FRGs were primarily enriched in lipid metabolism and inflammation-related pathways. Through the construction of lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA regulatory networks, transcription factor analysis, and prediction of potential drug targets in SCI, we identified the IL-1β/miRNA-326 axis as a core molecular regulator of ferroptosis-related genes in SCI. We further validated these findings in an independent dataset (GSE166009) containing spinal cord tissues from a rat SCI model at different time points. Consistently, IL-1β and miRNA-326 were progressively alliterated post-SCI and closely associated with ferroptosis. Finally, in our rat SCI model, we confirmed significant changes in the expression of IL-1β and miRNA-326. Moreover, miR-326 was shown to directly target the 3’UTR of IL-1β, inhibiting its expression. Taken together, this study provides new insights into the dynamic changes and roles of ferroptosis in SCI pathogenesis. Importantly, the identification of the IL-1β/miRNA-326 axis as a key molecular pair offers potential therapeutic targets and prognostic markers for clinical SCI management.

The association between ferroptosis and SCI has garnered widespread attention [12, 17, 30]. The current perspective on the mechanism of ferroptosis in SCI suggests that the accumulation of iron is the first step [11]. Excessive iron in cells will induce mitochondrial damage and reactive oxygen species (ROS) production via the Fenton reaction [31, 32]. The primary role of ROS is to cause oxidative damage to biofilms by targeting polyunsaturated fatty acids [11]. Large amounts of ROS will trigger lipid peroxidation that damages the plasma membrane and leads to ferroptosis [33]. Moreover, the critical role of the inflammatory response in a secondary injury caused by SCI has been widely considered and demonstrated [34, 35]. It was reported that inhibiting ferroptosis helps reduce the toxicity of inflammation and enhances neuronal survival after SCI [36]. Therefore, inhibition of ferroptosis may be a crucial strategy to effectively promote the recovery of motor function after SCI.

In this study, we identified 47 DE-miRNAs and 38 FRGs in SCI. These FRGs were associated with processes such as lipid metabolism, inflammation, and various signaling pathways. Further, predicted target relationships between the DE-miRNAs and the top five hub FRGs were established. Then an LncRNA-miRNA-mRNA network and a TF-miRNA-mRNA network were constructed. The network suggests that some miRNAs, including miR-326, miR-223, miR-372, and others, might play a significant role in regulating ferroptosis following SCI. Studies have revealed that miR-326 is decreased in human hippocampal neuron injury, and this downregulation contributes to neuronal inflammation and apoptosis [37]. Remarkably, upregulating miR-326 can significantly reduce neuronal damage by suppressing NLRP3-mediated initiation of pyroptosis [38]. Also, it has been reported that IL-1β inhibits the recovery of SCI by inducing NF-κB and up-regulating miRNA-372 expression [39]. On the contrary, down-regulating IL-1β will reduce secondary injury after SCI, protect neurons, and improve the prognosis [40, 41]. Previous research indicated that the expression of long non-coding RNA SNHG1 was upregulated in neuropathic pain following SCI [42]. And SNHG1 can down-regulated level of miR-326 to some extent [43]. Therefore, SNHG1 may elevate IL-1β by inhibiting miR-326, thereby promoting ferroptosis following SCI. Our results also reveal that miR-326 is downregulated in SCI, while its target gene, IL-1β, which is also related to ferroptosis, is upregulated. Furthermore, the experiment results confirmed that the expression levels of both miR-326 and IL-1β were consistent with the results of bioinformatics analysis. It may suggest that miRNA-326 targets IL-1β to play a significant regulatory role in the mechanism of ferroptosis following SCI. Similarly, other miRNAs, LncRNAs or TFs in our regulatory network may be potential targets for the treatment of SCI.

This study further identified several drugs that target hub DE-FRGs. Rilonacept is an IL-1α and IL-1β cytokine antagonist. It was found to maintain long-term inflammatory remission in patients with a deficiency of IL-1 receptor antagonists [44]. Several studies have shown that rilonacept is used in the treatment of recurrent pericarditis [45, 46]. Furthermore, the phase 3 clinical trial revealed significant enhancements in patients’ quality of life, sleep patterns, pain management, and the severity of systemic symptoms while they were being treated with rilonacept [47]. Canakinumab is a human anti-IL-1β monoclonal antibody that interferes with the bioactivity of IL-1β [48]. Ravoxertinib, a novel small-molecule inhibitor of ERK1/2, has been shown to significantly ameliorate long-term neurological dysfunction following subarachnoid hemorrhage [49]. Some other drugs, such as balsalazide, sulfasalazine, and bromfenac, have shown anti-inflammatory effects. Therefore, our research may indicate that these drugs, when used for early treatment of SCI, would help inhibit ferroptosis and thus promote neural repair. However, these inferences need to be verified by more experiments.

We employed the GSE166009 dataset to validate the DE-FRGs and DEmiRNAs. The results showed that a total of nine DE‑FRGs were identified after interfacing with the GSE151371 dataset. Among these, four DE-FRGs showed upregulated expression (CDKN1A, TIMP1, LCN2, IL-1β), while five DE-FRGs displayed downregulated expression (TRIB2, NR1D1, NR1D2, DPP4, GLS2). Furthermore, miR326 was found to be downregulated post-SCI. And IL-1β/miRNA-326 were the core moleculars for Ferroptosis-Related Genes in SCI. We validated the expression levels of miRNA-326 and its target gene IL-1β in vivo using RT-PCR. The results were consistent with our prediction and most previous findings. To verify the interaction between miR-326 and IL1β, we performed a luciferase reporter assay and found that miR-326 directly binds to the 3’-UTR of IL-1β. Our study revealed that the target regulatory relationship between miR-326 and IL-1β might be a molecular mechanism underlying ferroptosis induced by SCI. This finding may contribute to further investigating the mechanisms of ferroptosis following SCI, as well as providing therapeutic targets for SCI. However, additional experiments are required to explore further regulatory mechanisms.

Clinical implications and potential prognostic value

Secondary injury following spinal cord injury is a dynamic and multifactorial process, with inflammation and ferroptosis playing central roles in determining neurological outcome. An important strength of the present study is that the IL-1β/miR-326 axis was initially identified using peripheral blood transcriptomic data and subsequently validated in an in vivo SCI model, highlighting its potential translational relevance. Because circulating biomarkers can be obtained rapidly and repeatedly in the acute phase, IL-1β and miR-326 may represent accessible candidates for monitoring secondary injury activity in patients with acute SCI.

From a clinical perspective, we speculate that dysregulation of the IL-1β/miR-326 axis may reflect the intensity of post-injury inflammatory and ferroptotic responses, which are closely linked to tissue loss and functional recovery. Patients exhibiting persistently elevated IL-1β levels and reduced miR-326 expression during the acute or subacute phase may experience more severe secondary damage and poorer neurological outcomes. In future prospective cohorts, integrating IL-1β/miR-326 measurements with established prognostic factors, such as baseline AIS grade, injury level, imaging findings, and patient age, may facilitate risk stratification and improve outcome prediction models for SCI.

Importantly, therapeutic interventions may influence the expression and prognostic value of inflammatory biomarkers. Pharmacological treatments, including anti-inflammatory agents and potential IL-1–targeted therapies, as well as the timing and intensity of rehabilitation, could modulate IL-1β and miR-326 levels. Therefore, future clinical studies should account for treatment exposure and rehabilitation variables when evaluating the predictive performance of the IL-1β/miR-326 axis. Collectively, these findings suggest that this regulatory pathway may serve not only as a mechanistic link between inflammation and ferroptosis, but also as a promising candidate for prognostic assessment and therapeutic monitoring in spinal cord injury.

Limitations

Several limitations of the present study should be acknowledged. First, the human transcriptomic analysis was based on a public dataset with a relatively limited sample size, which may reduce statistical power and restrict generalizability. In addition, detailed clinical information, such as injury level, baseline neurological severity, medication exposure, surgical timing, and rehabilitation intensity, was not uniformly available, preventing adjustment for potential confounding factors and limiting direct correlations with clinical outcomes. Second, gene expression profiles were derived from peripheral blood rather than spinal cord tissue, and although circulating biomarkers are attractive for clinical translation, tissue-specific molecular alterations at the lesion site may not be fully captured.

Third, the animal experiments were conducted using a single rat model and focused on one post-injury time point (day 7), corresponding to the completion of behavioral assessments. While this design enabled integrated functional and molecular analyses, it does not reflect the full temporal dynamics of ferroptosis and inflammatory responses following SCI. Future studies incorporating multiple time points, different injury severities, and both sexes may provide a more comprehensive understanding. Finally, although the direct interaction between miR-326 and IL-1β was validated by dual-luciferase reporter assays, further in vivo gain- and loss-of-function studies are required to establish a definitive causal role of the IL-1β/miR-326 axis in regulating ferroptosis and neurological recovery after spinal cord injury.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study systematically identified ferroptosis-related genes associated with spinal cord injury and demonstrated that the IL-1β/miR-326 axis plays a critical role in regulating ferroptosis during secondary injury. By integrating public transcriptomic data with in vivo experimental validation, our findings provide mechanistic insight into the interaction between inflammation, microRNA regulation, and ferroptotic cell death after SCI. These results highlight the IL-1β/miR-326 axis as a potential molecular link underlying secondary injury progression and suggest that this pathway may serve as a promising target for future prognostic assessment and therapeutic exploration in spinal cord injury.

Multi-dimensional attention transformer for vehicle and pedestrian detection in adverse weather
Authors: Soumen Biswas, Jayendra Kumar, Abhishek Mitra, Ananth Ganesh, Priyanka Singh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractReal-time object detection in adverse weather and low-light conditions is crucial for applications such as autonomous driving and intelligent surveillance. This paper presents MDAT-YOLO, a novel object detection framework designed to balance accuracy and efficiency in challenging environments. The model integrates multi-dimensional attention mechanisms and transformer-based enhancements to strengthen feature extraction and adaptability. It introduces two core modules: DWConv_O, an optimized depthwise separable convolution layer, and ODConv++, an omni-dimensional dynamic convolution module that enhances spatial, channel, and kernel-level interactions for improved feature selectivity and dynamic response. A lightweight C3 Transformer (C3TR) block further reduces computational overhead while maintaining strong representational capacity. MDAT-YOLO is evaluated on four benchmark datasets, including RTTS, VOC-Foggy, ExDark, and a custom foggy VOC-PASCAL subset, achieving accuracy improvements of 70.50%, 65.14%, 77.40%, and 49.00%, respectively. The model sustains real-time speeds up to 145 FPS, demonstrating robustness and practicality for real-world deployment under diverse environmental conditions.Keywords: Real-time object detection, Adverse weather conditions, Low-light imaging, Attention-enhanced YOLO, Dynamic convolution, Transformer-based feature extractionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Background and problem definition

Over the past decade, researchers have been focused on finding optimal solutions for detecting road objects, which can be widely applied in areas such as road surveillance and autonomous driving to enhance security and safety. Developing such models requires extensive training data, particularly in adverse weather conditions. Traditionally, various approaches have been proposed to detect road objects under specific conditions, such as foggy, daytime, dark, or rainy scenes. However, gathering diverse datasets and training a model effectively is a challenging task, especially when high-quality data is scarce. Some approaches in the literature combine data from two different weather scenarios to train models, but in real-world applications, it is essential to consider data from all possible weather conditions. This is particularly crucial since many of the most difficult detection tasks involve identifying objects in low-light or rainy conditions.

Related study

In literature, zhang et al.1 proposes a multiclass object detection method, namely, multiscale feature fusion attention-you only look once (MSFFA-YOLO) network, that can be trained and jointly achieve three tasks: visibility enhancement, object classification, and object localization. The paper shows that the presented MSFFA-YOLO can be achieved promising results on both synthetic and real foggy datasets. Training complexity and Trade-offs between accuracies of all three tasks are something that could make this method little less adopted in practical sense. Wang et al.2 describes a multi-objective visual detection algorithm for fog driving scenes based on improved YOLOv5. This paper proposes a new algorithm that improves both accuracy and speed as the existing fog-oriented detection algorithms are unable to simultaneously address the detection accuracy and detection speed. Potential drawbacks of the approach are The method might be specifically designed for a certain type of fog or light conditions. It may not perform as well in different adverse weather scenarios. The authors in Ref. 3 proposed a new method for object detection in adverse weather conditions, particularly haze. The proposed method, D-YOLO, is a novel framework that combines image dehazing and object detection tasks into a single network. D-YOLO leverages a dual-pathway structure with an attention feature fusion module. This allows the network to consider both hazy and dehazed features from the image, potentially improving the model’s ability to represent objects in hazy conditions. However, D-YOLO still have difficulties at discerning objects in rather challenging scenarios. Lyu et al.4 demonstrate a HDR-YOLO method of adaptive object detection in haze, dark, and rain scenes based on YOLO, however, it didn’t consider fog as an environmental condition to check the effectiveness of the proposed method. The survey report in Ref. 5 by Ahmed et al. examines deep learning object detection in challenging environments including low light, occlusions, and complex backgrounds. Authors analysed both two-stage (like R-CNN, Fast R-CNN, Faster R-CNN) and one-stage (like YOLOv5, SSD) deep learning architectures for object detection. The paper concludes that there is no single best method that achieves both the highest accuracy and the fastest speed. In Ref. 6, authors described a new object detection method for autonomous vehicles in bad weather conditions, specifically fog. It combines YOLOv7 with ESRGAN. ESRGAN is used to pre-process the image by removing fog and other bad weather effects, making it easier for YOLOv7 to detect objects. However, combining two separate deep learning models (YOLOv7 and ESRGAN) increases the overall complexity of the system compared to a single model. Boppana et al.7 describes a comparative analysis of single-stage YOLO algorithms for vehicle detection under extreme weather conditions, specifically focusing on rain and snow. The paper suggests that YOLOv3 can be a good choice for real-time vehicle detection systems that need to function under adverse weather conditions out of the YOLO variations tested, which included YOLOv3, YOLOv4, and YOLOv5. However, paper didn’t consider fog as a challenging condition. The authors in Ref. 8 proposed YOLOv5 model to recognize objects from street-level recordings for rainy and regular weather scenarios on 11 distinct classes of vehicles (car, truck, bike), pedestrians, and traffic signals (red, green, yellow), however, it didn’t consider fog as a challenging condition. In Ref. 9, authors proposed an ensemble framework that combines multiple deep learning models to improve object detection accuracy in challenging weather conditions and as claimed in paper it was able to improve the accuracy of object detection in foggy and rainy weather conditions. However, proposed method used RetinaResnet50 architecture instead of popular Yolo based architecture, which could impact speed of detection in real time scenarios. In Ref. 10, Ghosh et al. proposed to use Faster R-CNN using several varying sized RPNs to detect on-road vehicles in varying weather conditions. However, as this is a two-stage detection algorithm so it could impact speed of detection and may not be suitable particularly in real time detection requirement. Sharma et al. in Ref. 11 proposes a novel dataset named the Canadian Vehicle Datasets (CVD) for object detection in autonomous vehicles in different weather conditions such as hazy, snowy, rainy, gloomy, and sunny days during daytime and night time. The paper explores the performance of a YOLOv8 model trained on the existing RoboFlow dataset and compares it with the model trained on the expanded version of RoboFlow using the proposed weather-specific dataset, CVD. Their results showed that the model trained on the CVD dataset exhibited higher accuracy. This paper focuses more on location specific dataset to train the DL model and less on improving the YOLO model architecture. Boukerche et al.12 described a survey report to focus on deep learning-based object detection in traffic scenarios. The authors looked at more than 100 research papers and included different aspects such as key generic object detection frameworks, categorized object detection applications in traffic scenario, evaluation metrics, and classified datasets. In Ref. 13, authors introduced a domain adaptation model to discover a weather condition invariant feature representation, this means that the model can learn to recognize objects regardless of the weather conditions, something that existing detectors struggle with. However, unsupervised domain adaptation (when no labelled target data is used) can be susceptible to adversarial attacks where malicious actors manipulate the source data to mislead the model. Huang et al.14 proposed a new network (DSNet) for object detection in bad weather. DSNet tackles this by combining visibility enhancement with object detection using two subnets working together. It improves visibility and find objects at the same time, leading to better detection in foggy conditions. The network is mainly based on RetinaNet, which could impact the speed of network in real time processing scenarios like ADAS. Also the complexity of RetinaNet can make it harder to implement and train. Li et al.15 demonstrated a novel domain adaptive object detection framework for autonomous driving under foggy weather. This framework leverages both image-level and object-level adaptation to diminish the domain discrepancy in image style and object appearance. The proposed model adopts pipeline in Faster R-CNN for object detection, which is a two stage object detector and not suitable for scenarios like ADAS and surveillance environments where real time processing is required. Ding et al.16 introduced a new dataset (RSOD) for snowy object detection and a new CF (Cross Fusion) block for YOLOv5s to improve the detection accuracy in bad weather, such as snow. Snow can obscure the important features of objects. The Cross Fusion (CF) block helps to address the problem by fusing features from different stages of the backbone network, which helps to recover the lost information. However, this paper didn’t address the other environmental bad conditions such as fog, rain etc. In Ref. 17, authors proposed a novel framework that include two main networks: image quasi-translation network (QTNet) that generates synthetic images to connect normal and adverse weather images and feature calibration network (FCNet) that aligns feature representations of normal and adverse weather images. Using dual networks help for adapting the normal weather domain to the adverse weather domain gradually. The advantage of this method is, it avoids the need of large amounts of labelled data for adverse weather conditions. This achieves better results than existing unsupervised domain adaptation methods for detectors. R-YOLO does not change the original YOLO structure, and thus it is applicable to all the YOLO-series detectors for example, YOLOv3, YOLOv5 and YOLOX. However, the method was tested on only hazy and rainy datasets, and it is unclear as how the method will perform in different adverse weather. Dey et al.18 used video data captured by surveillance cameras in rainy situation and have experimented with basic YOLOv3 model with transfer learning to detect object in rainy conditions from videos, both daytime and night time. This work demonstrate the effectiveness of model on detecting objects in rainy conditions. However, as reported, there are such complex conditions where the system failed to detect objects. Authors in Ref. 19 proposed a method for detecting small-scale vehicles in various weather conditions including haze, dust and sandstorms, snowy and rainy weather both in day and night time. This method uses a Spatial Pyramid Pooling Network (SPP-NET) layer and a modified CSPDarknet53 architecture (YOLOv4) to improve detection accuracy. The authors also augmented the DAWN dataset with different techniques to make the detection more challenging. The paper focuses on vehicle detection. It’s unclear if the proposed method (SPP-NET with modified CSPDarknet53) generalizes well to other object detection tasks beyond small vehicles. Liu et al. in Ref. 20 proposed a novel Image-Adaptive YOLO (IA-YOLO) framework for object detection in adverse weather conditions. This framework utilizes a differentiable image processing (DIP) module to account for the adverse weather conditions. DIP module acts like a filter that adaptively enhance the input images based on the weather conditions. A separate small neural network (CNN-PP) predicts the type of weather (e.g. rain, fog, etc.) based on the image. Author tested the method in both foggy and low-light scenarios. However, the system requires joint training of the DIP module, CNN-PP for weather prediction, and the YOLOv3 detector. This can be more challenging than training a standard YOLO model. Also, if trained model encounters a new or very different weather scenario, the DIP module’s adjustments might not be suitable, impacting detection accuracy. Wahab et al.21 demonstrated a methodology by integrating the Federated Learning (FL) with YOLO CNN, that enhances object detection accuracy for Autonomous Vehicles (AVs) in snowy weather by enabling collaborative model training without data sharing. However, use of Federated Learning (FL) with YOLO CNN requires significant computational power. Also, performance of the approach here may not be robust in non-snowy adverse conditions. Kumar et al.22 introduced a novel approach to improving object detection in adverse weather conditions using YOLOv8 and data merging techniques. This approach leverages data merging and transfer learning to enhance the robustness of object detection systems in challenging weather conditions. The authors improve the performance of YOLOv8 by merging data from various open-source datasets, specifically focusing on adverse weather conditions. They use the ACDC and DAWN datasets, combining them to create a more robust training set that captures a wide range of harsh weather scenarios. However, this approach heavily depends on dataset and less on YOLO architecture enhancement. The authors in Ref. 23 explained a research methodology on object detection using YOLOv5 in bad weather by training with a unique combination of methods: (a) Real-world all-weather data, instead of limited datasets or simulated effects (b) Synthetic weather noise for a more comprehensive training experience and (c) combining object detection with image denoising. Their findings suggest real-world data training leads to superior performance. In Ref. 24, the authors propose the Geometric Spatial Constraints Network (GSCNet) for precise detection of aircraft impeller surface defects, incorporating Rich Semantic Information Representation and Spatial Correlation Awareness to capture slender defect structures and improve high-similarity defect classification. In another work25, authors propose a hierarchical dense interlaced-point detection (HDID) framework for accurately assessing the uniformity of woven aeroengine fan blade preforms, combining angular rectification and dense feature refinement to precisely detect densely distributed interlaced points and outperform state-of-the-art methods. In Ref. 26, authors propose a fully automated gold wire bonding quality inspection framework, combining a high-resolution image acquisition system, a Slenderness Attention Module (SAM), and a Wire Quality Evaluation (WQE) model to accurately detect missing and misconnected slender gold wires, achieving high precision and recall even in complex backgrounds. Recent advances in attention mechanisms have significantly improved the performance of visual detection tasks in complex scenarios. For instance, Ul Amin et al.27 proposed an efficient attention-based strategy for anomaly detection in surveillance videos, where spatial and temporal attention modules allowed the model to focus on regions of interest and handle dynamic backgrounds effectively. Similarly, Amin et al.27 demonstrated that integrating synthetic data with external attention-based transformers can enhance the detection of subtle visual patterns, such as pine wilt disease, improving generalization under varied conditions. These studies highlight the effectiveness of attention mechanisms and synthetic data in detecting small, sparse, or low-visibility features, motivating the development of robust detection models for challenging road scenes under adverse weather conditions. However, the real-world training approach might be limited by needing a lot of high-quality data, which can be expensive, time-consuming, and potentially biased.

Motivation

Previous studies and detection frameworks have shown good performance for large and clearly visible objects under normal conditions. However, they often struggle in more challenging scenarios, such as low-light, nighttime, foggy, or rainy environments, where the visibility of road objects is significantly reduced. Small or partially occluded objects are particularly difficult to detect in such conditions, and existing models frequently fail to maintain high accuracy while performing in real time.

These limitations highlight a clear need for a detection model that can robustly handle adverse weather conditions and challenging scenes without compromising speed. To address this, we develop MDAT-YOLO, a fast and efficient framework that leverages adaptive convolutional layers and transformer-based feature extraction to improve the detection of small, occluded, and low-visibility objects. Extensive experiments on multiple datasets covering various weather scenarios demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves substantial improvements in detection accuracy while maintaining real-time performance, making it suitable for practical applications in intelligent transportation systems and autonomous driving.

Although the proposed MDAT-YOLO model leverages concepts from existing ideas like lightweight convolution, dynamic convolution, and feature modeling using the Transformer model, its novelty exists in a task-driven architectural redesign for object detection in complex environments. Unlike many works that focus on adding novel components to a model, this work aims at a systematic combination of multi-dimensional attention mechanisms and a model for the global context within the YOLO model. The utility of this model exists in its ability to make improvements in detection accuracy, efficiency, and robustness for a variety of complex datasets.

Contributions

The contributions of the authors in this work are as follows:

1. A novel detection framework for adverse conditions: We propose MDAT-YOLO, a real-time object detection model specifically optimized for adverse weather and low-visibility road environments. Unlike prior YOLO variants that primarily target generic conditions, MDAT-YOLO emphasizes robustness under fog, rain, and low-light scenarios while maintaining a strong trade-off between detection accuracy and inference speed.

2. Integration of adaptive convolutional modules: The architecture introduces two new components, ODConv++ and DWConv_O, to replace conventional static convolution layers used in previous YOLO architectures. These modules enable dynamic, content-aware feature learning and enhance multi-dimensional representation efficiency.

3. ODConv++ for omni-dimensional dynamic adaptation: In contrast to earlier dynamic or attention-based convolutions that operate on limited dimensions, ODConv++ adaptively adjusts convolution kernels across spatial, channel, and kernel dimensions simultaneously. Its cross-dimensional attention fusion mechanism allows the model to retain discriminative features at multiple scales, improving robustness to visual degradation.

4. DWConv_O for efficient feature refinement: The proposed DWConv_O extends standard depthwise separable convolution by incorporating lightweight attention to strengthen inter-channel communication. Unlike existing lightweight convolution variants, DWConv_O achieves a better balance between parameter efficiency and feature selectivity, making it suitable for deployment in both the backbone and neck without compromising speed.

5. C3 Transformer (C3TR) for enhanced contextual perception: A C3TR layer is introduced to replace traditional C3 blocks. Unlike prior transformer integrations that substantially increase complexity, C3TR adopts a lightweight attention structure that captures long-range dependencies while preserving computational efficiency, thereby improving contextual awareness in feature representation.

6. Comprehensive evaluation under diverse weather conditions: Extensive experiments across foggy, rainy, dark, and normal conditions-including tests on the newly generated foggy VOC-PASCAL subset-demonstrate consistent improvements in both accuracy (mAP) and real-time performance (FPS). These results validate the effectiveness and deployment potential of MDAT-YOLO in real-world intelligent vision systems.

Methodology

Preliminary study

The present research aims to design and implement a road objects detection model to improve automatic detection efficiency in adverse weather conditions. In this research, the yolov5s model is considered a baseline model on which the modification is done. Yolov5s model is an object detection model with three different architectural parts, i.e. backbone, neck, and head2,5,17,23,28. It is challenging to construct a single model for all different scenarios. This motivates us to devise a solution for designing an object detection model for adverse weather conditions. The detection of vehicles in daylight conditions is comparatively easy compared to other weather conditions such as heavy rain, fog, and night. The architecture of yolov5s mainly consists of the following parts:

Backbone: The backbone of the architecture is used to extract input images. It consists of multiple convolution layers, C3 layers, and a spatial pyramid pooling fast (SPPF) layer. A C3 layer consists of three convolution layers, a bottleneck, and a concat layer. Finally, the output of the backbone scale to three feature maps, i.e. P3, P4, and P523.

Neck: The working principle of this part of architecture is to fuse the features of different scales from the backbone. The neck consists of multiple convolution layers, concat, and C3 layers. Like the backbone, the neck has three feature map scale outputs corresponding to P3, P4, and P523,28.

Head: The head part of the architecture is used to predict the objects and their location. The anchor-based head is responsible for detecting objects of different sizes. The head output is a vector containing probability confidence of the object category and position of the bounding box23,28.

Proposed model

This research introduces an enhanced Yolov5-based object detection model, MDAT-Yolo, to address the challenges of detecting road objects in adverse weather conditions. Adverse weather, particularly in nighttime scenes, can make object detection more difficult due to poor visibility. Additionally, objects may be tiny or obscured, and in dense foggy scenes, they can be barely visible. Object detection architectures typically involve multiple down-sampling operations on input images, which reduces the resolution of feature maps and makes object detection more difficult. To overcome these challenges, MDAT-Yolo incorporates structural improvements to the baseline Yolov5s model. This subsection outlines the architecture of the proposed MDAT-Yolo model.

Replacing convolution layer

There are some limitations of standard convolution layers and they are as follows:

Traditional convolution is limited in its capacity to learn static convolutional kernels, thus lacking the capability to dynamically adjust kernel weights. This limitation diminishes the convolution layer’s ability to generalize and adapt to varying input conditions.

To eradicate the above existing problems, the MDAT-Yolo adopted Optimized Depthwise separable convolution (DWConv_O) and omni-dimensional dynamic convolution++ (ODConv++). DWConv_O divides the computation into two stages: first, a DWConv_O applies a distinct convolutional filter for each input channel and position; second, a pointwise convolution combines the resulting outputs linearly to generate the final output. Also, the DWConv_O has fewer parameters, which is computationally cheaper and reduces the overfitting problems.

Omni-dimensional dynamic convolution++ (ODConv++)

The proposed ODConv++ is an advanced version of Omni-Dimensional Dynamic Convolution (ODConv), designed to overcome the independent gating limitation of the original dynamic convolution. While ODConv dynamically adjusts kernel weights across four dimensions-spatial, input channel, output channel, and kernel-it treats each attention gate separately. In contrast, ODConv++ introduces a cross-dimensional attention fusion mechanism and a multi-head shared controller that captures interdependencies across all dimensions.

Motivation: Traditional dynamic convolution methods such as CondConv and DyConv only adapt across the number of kernels. ODConv improved upon this by applying dimension-specific gating; however, it lacks coordination between these gates. ODConv++ addresses this gap by generating all dimension-specific attention vectors from a shared global descriptor, encouraging joint learning and better generalization.

ODConv++ with full-dimensional interaction

ODConv++ is an extension to traditional dynamic convolution, allowing full-dimensional interaction on the channel, spatial, and kernel dimensions. In particular, for a given input feature map , ODConv++ learns adaptive convolution filters using three different attention branches:”

“Three different attention branches” refers to the fact that there:

1. Channel attention: This models inter-channel dependencies to focus on informative channels.

2. Spatial attention, which represents the weight of importance in the spatial domain on the feature map;

3. Kernel attention, a mechanism for dynamically selecting and weighting a set of convolution kernels according to different visual patterns.

These weights of attention are learned jointly to be used in regulating the convolution function. Thus, the three aspects of visual features can be integrated in one attention model to provide improved flexibility. This makes ODConv++ more robust because it does not have high computations.

Optimized depthwise separable convolution (DWConv_O)

To further enhance the computational efficiency and feature representation capability of our model, we introduce DWConv_O, an optimized version of the standard depthwise separable convolution (DWConv). While DWConv reduces the computational cost by factorizing standard convolution into separate spatial and channel operations, it suffers from a lack of inter-channel communication, leading to weak feature expressiveness.

DWConv_O addresses this limitation by integrating two enhancements:

Channel Interaction Attention: Lightweight channel-wise attention is added after the depthwise operation to capture inter-channel dependencies. We adopt either a squeeze-and-excitation (SE) or efficient channel attention (ECA) module to adaptively recalibrate channel responses.

C3TR

The C3TR serves as an enhanced iteration of the C3 layer within the MDAT-Yolo backbone, enhancing feature extraction. Its key benefit lies in optimizing network efficiency by lowering computational overhead through the amalgamation of CSPNet and C3 layer advantages. Thus, its incorporation aims to streamline the transformation of low-level features into comprehensive high-level representations of feature data. The structure of C3TR layer is shown in Fig. 2.

The ODConv++ layer is well capable of dealing with small target object without neglecting spatial features, input, and output channel fluctuation. In addition, C3TR improves feature extraction by optimizing network efficiency.

Lightweight design of C3TR

For the purpose of achieving a balance in the modeling of the global context and the requirements for real-time processing, the proposed C3TR module uses a light-weight version of the Transformer network. In this case, rather than directly applying the Transformer network in place of the convolutional modules, the proposed method uses a reduced embedding dimension and a limited number of attention heads in the attention mechanism. Moreover, the proposed method selectively applies the Transformer network at various network stages.

This enables the C3TR model to add more information concerning the context of the features while still being efficient enough for real-time object detection tasks.

Experimental results and discussions

This section provides an overview of the details of the dataset, the experimental setup, the evaluation metrics, and the comparative analysis of the results. It also includes ablation studies that benchmark the proposed model against various state-of-the-art models under adverse weather conditions.

Dataset details

The experiments were conducted using three primary datasets: RTTS, ExDark, and the proposed VOC-Foggy dataset derived from PASCAL VOC 2012.

The RTTS (Real-world Task-driven Test Set)29 was used exclusively for testing and cross-domain evaluation to ensure an unbiased performance comparison. RTTS contains five object categories-Car, Motorcycle, Bus, Bicycle, and Person-with a total of 4,322 images comprising 25,371 car, 11,366 person, 698 bicycle, 1,232 motorbike, and 2,590 bus instances. The dataset includes various weather and lighting conditions such as fog, haze, and rain, making it suitable for evaluating robustness in real-world scenes.

The ExDark dataset30, which consists of 736 low-light images, was used primarily for training and validation under nighttime conditions. Five common classes (Car, Motorbike, Bus, Bicycle, and Person) were selected for consistency across datasets. The data were divided into 70% for training, 15% for validation, and 15% for testing, ensuring class balance.

To extend the training data for adverse weather conditions, a custom foggy dataset (VOC-Foggy) was generated from the PASCAL VOC 2012 dataset31, which originally contains 11,530 annotated images. The fog simulation was implemented using the algorithms described in Algorithm 1–3, producing multiple fog density levels (light, medium, heavy) for a diverse evaluation environment. The resulting dataset was split into 70% training, 20% validation, and 10% testing subsets.

All random operations, including data shuffling and augmentation, were controlled using a fixed random seed (seed = 42) to ensure reproducibility across experiments. In addition, cross-domain evaluations were performed by training on VOC-Foggy and ExDark and testing on RTTS to assess generalization under unseen weather and lighting conditions. This setup also allowed evaluation on more challenging subsets, such as heavy fog and nighttime rain, demonstrating the model’s adaptability to diverse real-world environments.

System settings and hyper parameters

All experiments were conducted on a 13th Gen Intel®Core™i9-13900K CPU running at 3000 MHz, paired with an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU with 24 GB of memory. The proposed MDAT-YOLO model was implemented using PyTorch and CUDA 12.2.

For a fair comparison, all baseline models (YOLOv5, YOLOv7, YOLOv8, and others) were trained and evaluated under identical settings, including input image size (), optimizer type, learning rate schedule, data augmentation pipeline, and early stopping criteria. Each model was trained for up to 300 epochs with a batch size of 16, and training was terminated early if no improvement in validation loss was observed for 10 consecutive epochs. The initial learning rate was set to 0.001, and data augmentations such as random horizontal flipping, mosaic, and color jitter were applied consistently across all models.

While each model was trained once under these standardized conditions to ensure direct comparability, future work will include multiple independent runs to analyze statistical variability and further confirm the consistency of the observed performance gains.

Conclusion and future scope

Detecting road objects in real time under adverse weather conditions remains a fundamental challenge for intelligent transportation systems, autonomous driving, and traffic surveillance. In this work, we introduced MDAT-YOLO, an enhanced object detection framework designed to maintain high accuracy and efficiency in complex environments such as fog, rain, and low light. By integrating DWConv_O and ODConv++ layers, the model achieves more adaptive feature extraction with reduced computational cost, while the C3 Transformer (C3TR) improves detection of small and partially occluded objects. Evaluations on the newly generated foggy VOC-PASCAL dataset, alongside RTTS, VOC-Foggy, and ExDark, demonstrate that MDAT-YOLO consistently outperforms existing models, achieving notable gains in mAP, precision, recall, and inference speed.

The strengths of this work include robust performance under diverse adverse weather conditions, efficient feature extraction, and superior detection of small and occluded objects. Limitations include reliance on synthetic weather datasets and the need for further validation across larger and more diverse real-world scenarios, which may affect generalization.

The findings highlight the potential of MDAT-YOLO for real-world deployment in autonomous driving, roadside monitoring, and intelligent surveillance, where both speed and robustness are critical.

Future research directions will focus on expanding object categories relevant to complex road scenes, incorporating adaptive domain generalization techniques for unseen weather types, and optimizing the model for low-power hardware and edge AI platforms. These directions aim to further strengthen the applicability of MDAT-YOLO in scalable, real-time intelligent vision systems49,50.

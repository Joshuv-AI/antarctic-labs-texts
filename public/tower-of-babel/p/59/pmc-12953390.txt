Clinical characteristics and therapies of multi-organ immune related adverse events
Authors: Dawei Wang, Yuxin Zhou, Hongxu Li, Yu Xin, Mengyao Yuan, Wenjing Mu, Xutong Qu, Yuhan Liu, Lifeng Shen, Yanqi Liu, Xinran Wang, Yu Xiao, Yuchen Song, Kaijiang Yu, Changsong Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractImmune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) activate the immune system by blocking PD-1, CTLA-4, and PD-L1, thereby inducing autoimmune-mediated adverse reactions known as immune-related adverse events (irAEs). When two or more organs are involved, this condition is defined as multi-organ immune-related adverse events (multi-organ irAEs). Patients with multi-organ irAEs account for approximately 20%–30% of all irAE cases; however, clinical research focusing on this subset remains limited. The purpose of this study is to analyze the clinical characteristics, optimal therapeutic approaches, and mortality-related risk factors of multi-organ irAEs. We searched all case reports of irAEs associated with ICIs in the PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, and Embase databases from their inception to January 2022. Search terms included “Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors”, “Checkpoint Inhibitors, Immune”, “Immune Checkpoint Blockers”, “PD-L1”, “CTLA-4 Inhibitor”, “PD-1”, and “Case report”. After removing duplicate literature and applying strict inclusion/exclusion criteria, a total of 2,740 articles were included, encompassing 2,964 patients (782 with multi-organ irAEs and 2,182 with single-organ irAEs). Patients were stratified by the number of affected organs to compare clinical characteristics between multi-organ and single-organ irAE groups. For patients with multi-organ irAEs, subgroup analyzes were performed based on glucocorticoid dosage to identify optimal treatment strategies, and further stratified by survival status to explore potential mortality risk factors. No statistically significant differences in age or sex were observed between patients with multi-organ irAEs and those with single-organ irAEs (P > 0.05). However, the multi-organ irAE group exhibited significantly higher proportions of cardiovascular toxicity, thyroid toxicity, skin toxicity, and severe adverse reactions (P < 0.05), as well as a significantly elevated mortality rate (P < 0.05). Among patients with severe multi-organ irAEs, there were no significant differences in sex or age between high-dose and low-dose glucocorticoid subgroups (P > 0.05); notably, the high-dose glucocorticoid group had a significantly higher mortality rate (P < 0.05). For patients with non-severe multi-organ irAEs, no statistically significant differences in sex, age, mortality, or prognosis were detected between high-dose and low-dose glucocorticoid groups (P > 0.05). Multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that cardiovascular toxicity, pulmonary toxicity, hepatotoxicity, and myositis were positively correlated with mortality in patients with multi-organ irAEs (OR > 1, P < 0.05). The most common organ combinations in multi-organ irAEs are “cardiac + neurological, cardiac + pulmonary, thyroid + pituitary, cardiac + hepatic, and gastrointestinal + skin”. Multi-organ irAEs are generally more severe and associated with poorer outcomes compared to single-organ irAEs. High-dose glucocorticoids did not demonstrate superior prognostic outcomes and may be associated with an increased mortality risk in severe irAEs.clinical decisions regarding glucocorticoid dosing should be individualized based on the severity of irAEs and the specific organs involved.Cardiovascular toxicity, pulmonary toxicity, hepatotoxicity, and myositis are potential mortality risk factors for multi-organ irAEs induced by ICIs.Keywords: Immune checkpoint inhibitors, Multi-organ immune related adverse events, Glucocorticoids, Mortality risk factors

Background

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) block PD-1, CTLA-4, or PD-L1 on immune cells, non-specifically activating the immune system, relieving T-cell inhibition, and triggering treatment-related adverse reactions termed immune-related adverse events (irAEs) [1]. Notably, irAEs can involve multiple organ systems throughout the body, with an overall incidence of 54%–96% across all grades [2]. Once myocarditis occurs, the mortality rate reaches up to 50% [2, 3]. IrAEs can be graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) [4]: Grade 1 refers to mild, asymptomatic or mild symptoms; Grade 2 refers to moderate, and the patient is limited in age-matched daily activities; Grade 3 refers to irAEs that require medical intervention, are not life-threatening, and limit personal self-care activities; Grade 4 refers to serious life-threatening irAEs; Grade 5 refers to death. When the level of irAEs is ≥ grade 3, it is called severe irAEs [5]. The onset time of irAEs is common in the first 3 months of treatment, but irAEs may also occur long after drug withdrawal [6, 7]; those persisting for ≥ 12 weeks are defined as chronic irAEs [8].

At present, the mechanism of irAEs is not clear, and researchers believe that the occurrence of irAEs may be affected by multiple factors. Potential mechanisms include T-cell immune cross-reactivity (enhanced T-cell targeting of healthy tissues), increased diversity of activated T-cell subsets, elevated autoantibody levels, upregulated inflammatory cytokine production (e.g., IL-17), enhanced complement-mediated inflammatory responses, and immune dysregulation induced by changes in the intestinal flora and their derived metabolites (short-chain fatty acids, etc.) [9–13].

Multi-organ irAEs are defined as irAEs involving two or more organs [14, 15]. Multi-organ irAEs are not uncommon: a retrospective analysis of 1,548 patients from 4 clinical trials found that multi-organ irAEs accounted for 19.81% (84/424) of all irAEs [14]. while another retrospective cohort study of 623 patients from 5 global institutions reported a prevalence of 28.16% (28/206) [15].

Despite their considerable incidence, clinical research on multi-organ irAEs—particularly regarding optimal treatments—remains scarce. This study aimed to collect clinical and therapeutic data from patients with multi-organ irAEs to analyze their clinical characteristics, evaluate treatment strategies, and identify potential mortality risk factors.

Methods

Research design

We searched all case reports of irAEs associated with ICIs in the PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, and Cochrane Library databases from their inception to January 2022. The search terms were “Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors”, “Checkpoint Inhibitors, Immune “, “Immune Checkpoint Blockers”, “PDL1”, “CTLA4 Inhibitor”, “PD-1” and “Case report”. After removing duplicates and applying inclusion/exclusion criteria, 2,740 articles were included, covering 2,964 patients (782 with multi-organ irAEs and 2,182 with single-organ irAEs).

We compared the differences between patients with single-organ and multi-organ irAEs to find out the risk factors for the occurrence of multi-organ irAEs. Patients with multi-organ irAEs were divided into different groups according to differences in treatment methods of glucocorticoid and whether they died, to compare the prognosis of different treatments and find potential Times mortality risk factors.

Data collection

The following dates were extracted from the 2740 included case reports: basic information (sex, age, underlying disease, tumor stage, prior chemotherapy, targeted therapy, radiotherapy); ICI treatment details (dosage and type of ICIs); the information of irAEs (involved organ, onset time, severity grade); the information of irAEs treatment (drug type, dose); outcome information (7-day mortality, 28-day mortality, length of hospital stay, disease outcome, etc.).The IrAE severity is evaluated according to the standards established by CTCAE-5 and NCCN guidelines [4, 5, 16–18].

Inclusion criteria

①Aged ≥ 18 years and ≤ 75 years old; ②Patients with malignant tumors (pathological diagnosis); ③Patients who received tumor ICIs (PD-1/CTLA-4/PD-L1 inhibitors) and developed irAEs.

Exclusion criteria

①Reviews or clinical trials; ②Patients with immune system diseases previously; ③Organ transplant patients.

Data analysis

When the quantitative data conforms to the normal distribution, the statistical description uses the mean ± standard deviation (7), the comparison between two groups uses the two independent sample t-test, and the comparison between multiple groups uses the analysis of variance; when it conforms to the skewed distribution, the statistical description uses the median and interquartile range [M (P25, P75)], the Wilcoxon test was used for comparison between two groups, and the Kruskal-Wallis test was used for comparison between multiple groups. When the data are qualitative data, the statistical description is presented using frequencies (percentages), and the comparison method between groups is the Chi-square test/Fisher test. Logistic regression was used to analyze influencing factors. There was a statistical difference when P < 0.05. Statistical analysis was performed by SAS9.4 software.

Clinical characteristics of patients with multi-organ IrAEs

The median time from the onset of irAEs to the last use of ICIs was 14 days; the proportion of patients taking combined ICIs accounted for 18.03%; the most commonly used ICI drug type was PD-1. Patients with severe irAEs (≥ Grade 3) accounted for 50.00%; Overall,176 patients (22.52%) died, including 96 patients (12.28%) who died related to irAEs, and a total of 30 patients (3.84%) who died with sepsis. 118 patients (15.09%) had recurrent irAEs after treatment; and 153 patients resumed ICI therapy following irAE resolution.

No statistically significant differences in sex or age were observed between the two groups (P > 0.05); there were statistical differences in radiotherapy history, type and number of ICIs (P < 0.05). The multi-organ irAE group had a higher proportion of patients receiving anti-CTLA-4 inhibitors (14.53% vs. 10.80%) and combination ICI therapy, but a lower proportion of patients receiving anti-PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors (79.84% vs. 80.73%; 5.63% vs. 8.48%) compared to the single-organ irAE group.

The incidence of cardiotoxicity, gastrointestinal toxicity, pancreatitis, hepatotoxicity, pulmonary toxicity, thyroid toxicity, skin toxicity, neurotoxicity, and severe irAEs was significantly higher in the multi-organ irAE group (P < 0.0001). Additionally, the multi-organ irAE group had higher rates of mechanical ventilation use, irAE-related mortality, irAE recurrence, and sepsis-associated death (P < 0.05), as well as a significantly poorer clinical prognosis (P = 0.0004) (Table 2).

Furthermore, single/multiple organs were treated as dependent variables and other factors were treated as independent variables, and a logistic regression model was used to analyze the influencing factors that make patients more susceptible to multi-organ irAEs. Single-factor regression analysis was performed, and statistically significant indicators were included in multi-factor regression analysis. The results showed that skin irAEs, brain irAEs, neurological irAEs, eye irAEs, cardiovascular irAEs are risk factors for the development of multi-organ irAEs (Table 3; Fig. 3).

Comparison of glucocorticoid therapy in patients with multi-organ IrAEs

Patients were divided into high-dose group and low-dose group according to the critical value of 1 mg/kg/d prednisone therapeutic equivalent [19]. Since prednisone does not damage the function of T cells in secreting cytokines (IL-2, TNF-α), it has different effects on T cells than other glucocorticoids [20]. Therefore, the patients were divided into high-dose prednisone group, low-dose prednisone group, high-dose non-prednisone group and low-dose non-prednisone group.

Since clinical guidelines recommend distinct hormonal treatment regimens for patients with severe versus non-severe irAEs, hormonal treatment regimens were analyzed separately for the two patient cohorts.

Comparison of glucocorticoid treatment groups among patients with severe IrAEs

Patients with severe multi-organ irAEs were divided into four subgroups: high-dose prednisone (N = 22), low-dose prednisone (N = 31), high-dose non-prednisone (N = 146) and low-dose non-prednisone (N = 53)(Table 4). There was no statistical difference in baseline indicators such as sex and age among the four groups (P > 0.05).

Comparison between patients with death and non-death related to multi-organ IrAEs

The death group had significantly higher incidences of cardiovascular toxicity, hepatotoxicity, pulmonary toxicity, neurotoxicity, and severe irAEs, but lower incidences of thyroid toxicity, skin toxicity, and hypophysitis (P < 0.05). Significant differences were also detected in glucocorticoid treatment efficacy, mechanical ventilation use, ICI resumption rates, and clinical prognosis between groups.

Multivariate logistic regression analysis (treating mortality as the dependent variable) revealed that myositis, cardiotoxicity, pulmonary toxicity, and hepatotoxicity were independent mortality risk factors (OR > 1, P < 0.05). Conversely, effective glucocorticoid treatment and ICI resumption were associated with a lower mortality risk (OR < 1, P < 0.05) (Table 7; Fig. 4).

Discussion

This study analyzed the clinical characteristics, glucocorticoid treatment, and mortality risk factors of 782 patients with multi-organ irAEs. It was found that the most commonly involved organ combinations are “cardiac + neurological, cardiac + pulmonary, thyroid + pituitary, cardiac + hepatic, and gastrointestinal + skin”. Compared with single-organ irAEs, multi-organ irAEs have more severe adverse reactions and worse prognosis. Among patients with severe irAEs in multiple organs, patients in the high-dose glucocorticoid treatment group had higher mortality and worse clinical prognosis. In patients with multi-organ non-severe irAEs, there were no statistical differences in baseline indicators and prognosis between high-dose glucocorticoid therapy and low-dose glucocorticoid therapy. Additionally, we identified cardiotoxicity, pulmonary toxicity, hepatotoxicity, and myositis as key mortality risk factors.

The triad of myositis, myocarditis, and myasthenia gravis induced by ICIs is called overlap syndrome, which affects both the cardiovascular and nervous system [21].This syndrome has a poor prognosis, with a mortality rate of up to 60%. Potential mechanisms include the presence of tumor-mimetic antigens in cardiac/skeletal muscle and neuromuscular junctions and complement activation [22]. Chen et al. found that toripalimab (PD-1) can induce simultaneous myocarditis and pneumonia in patients [23]. ICI-associated myocarditis is often accompanied by hepatitis (21%) [24], but the specific mechanism remains unclear. Some researchers combined and analyzed two cohorts of patients diagnosed with skin irAEs and colitis irAEs, and found that patients suffering from “skin toxicity + gastrointestinal toxicity” accounted for 15% of all patients [25]. The occurrence of skin-gut multi-organ irAEs may be related to the existence of the skin-gut axis. Intestinal flora metabolites can migrate to the skin and disrupt skin homeostasis; skin inflammation can lead to the catabolism of hyaluronic acid, releasing debris that can trigger the differentiation of intestinal fibroblasts into preadipocytes, inducing inflammatory bowel disease [25].

In our investigation, patients experiencing multi-organ irAEs exhibited more severe symptoms and a poorer prognosis. A single-center retrospective study conducted in Japan compared single-organ and multi-organ irAEs, revealing that skin toxicity, thyroid toxicity, and hepatitis were positively correlated with the occurrence of multi-organ irAEs [26].Although this study did not find a statistically significant difference in the incidence and severity of irAEs affecting other organs between the two groups, it did observe that both the incidence and the proportion of high-grade adverse events were elevated in patients with multi-organ irAEs compared to those with single-organ irAEs. These findings are consistent with our study. Our research indicates that patients with multi-organ irAEs have a higher mortality rate, with adverse reactions in specific organs serving as potential predictive factors. It is recommended that patients who develop irAEs undergo thorough screening for the presence of multi-organ irAEs and receive prompt diagnosis and treatment.

Glucocorticoid therapy constitutes the first-line management for irAEs, as it mitigates irAE pathogenesis by reducing effector T-cell numbers, attenuating T cell-mediated inflammatory factor release, upregulating PD-1 expression on T cells, and alleviating T cell-mediated cytotoxicity [27]. Our findings demonstrated that in patients with multi-organ severe irAEs, those receiving high-dose glucocorticoid therapy had a significantly higher mortality rate and a markedly lower cure/improvement rate compared with patients treated with low-dose glucocorticoids. For patients with multi-organ non-severe irAEs, no statistically significant difference in mortality was observed between the high- and low-dose glucocorticoid cohorts, yet mortality still trended higher in the high-dose group. Prior studies have also shown that patients with ipilimumab (anti-CTLA-4)-induced hypophysitis who received higher glucocorticoid doses exhibited inferior survival outcomes [28]. Glucocorticoid use is also associated with potential adverse effects, including immunosuppression (increasing susceptibility to secondary infections), peripheral edema, and osteoporosis [27]. Thus, in the cohort of patients with multi-organ irAEs in this study, high-dose glucocorticoids did not demonstrate superior prognostic outcomes and may be associated with an increased mortality risk in severe irAEs. Therefore, clinical decisions regarding glucocorticoid dosing should be individualized based on the severity of irAEs and the specific organs involved.

Multivariate regression analysis of mortality in patients with multi-organ irAEs revealed that cardiotoxicity, pulmonary toxicity, hepatotoxicity, and myositis were positively associated with mortality. Although patients in the mortality group were slightly older than those in the non-mortality group, multivariate regression analysis indicated that age had no statistical significance as a predictor of mortality (P > 0.05).A meta-analysis mentioned that the most common fatal irAEs include myocarditis, pneumonia, encephalitis and fulminant hepatitis, with a mortality rate of 0.3% to 1.3% [29], which is consistent with the results of our study. Myositis and cardiovascular toxicity are both potential risk factors for mortality and may be related to the higher mortality in patients with overlap syndrome. Wang et al. found that among PD-L1-related fatal irAEs, the causes of patient death include interstitial lung disease, sepsis, pneumonia, hemoptysis, respiratory failure and autoimmune hepatitis. Among these causes, interstitial lung disease was the most common, indicating that respiratory system-related fatal irAEs predominate in patients treated with PD-L1 inhibitors [30]. In another study, researchers analyzed fatal irAEs in patients with non-small cell lung cancer and found that infectious diseases (49/167) and respiratory diseases (47/167) were the most common causes of death in patients with irAEs [31]. In summary, cumulative evidence from multiple studies confirms that respiratory adverse events are a common cause of death in patients with irAEs. Clinicians should be alert to the occurrence of multi-organ irAEs in such patients. Once cardiovascular toxicity, pulmonary toxicity, hepatotoxicity, myositis, or irAE overlap syndrome occurs, the patient’s risk of death is significantly increased, and clinicians must pay sufficient attention to this clinical scenario.

This study has several notable limitations. First, the number of patients receiving second-line therapies (e.g., intravenous immunoglobulin) was too small to allow statistical analysis, and evidence for second-line irAE management remains limited to case reports. Second, all data were from a retrospective case series, with insufficient biomarker data available to analyze specific changes in multi-organ irAE patients.

Conclusions

The most common combinations of multi-organ irAEs are “cardiac + neurological, cardiac + pulmonary, thyroid + pituitary, cardiac + hepatic, and gastrointestinal + skin”. Multi-organ irAEs are associated with more severe adverse reactions and poorer prognosis compared to single-organ irAEs.High-dose glucocorticoids did not demonstrate superior prognostic outcomes and may be associated with an increased mortality risk in severe irAEs.clinical decisions regarding glucocorticoid dosing should be individualized based on the severity of irAEs and the specific organs involved.Cardiovascular toxicity, pulmonary toxicity, hepatotoxicity, and myositis are potential mortality risk factors for multi-organ irAEs.

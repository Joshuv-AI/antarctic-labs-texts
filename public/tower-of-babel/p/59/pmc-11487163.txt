Velocity-free event-triggered control for a class of uncertain axis-motion systems with prescribed performance
Authors: Haichuan Zhang, Wenwei Zhang, Zhijian Lv, Jinpeng Tian
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This paper investigates the fixed-time prescribed performance control problem for a class of axis-motion servo systems subject to the external disturbances and parameter uncertainties. To eliminate the requirement for velocity measurements in the control process, an auxiliary observer is first designed to reconstruct the velocity signal of the controlled plant. Subsequently, an error transformation procedure incorporating a prescribed performance index is integrated into the control system architecture. To overcome the limitations of periodic control design, a robust event-triggered controller is developed, ensuring that the dynamic error of the closed-loop system exhibits fixed-time convergence. Through rigorous theoretical analysis and proofs, it is demonstrated that the proposed event-triggered strategy guarantees Zeno-behavior-free operation. Finally, simulation results are presented to validate the effectiveness of the proposed control algorithm.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-74330-7.

Introduction

High-precision axis-motion servo systems (AMSS) are extensively employed in cutting-edge technological domains such as precision machining, integrated circuit fabrication, and advanced optical engineering. However, during practical operation, AMSS are highly vulnerable to adverse factors like external disturbances and parameter uncertainties. These factors can significantly degrade the control system’s accuracy and even lead to equipment damage1,2. To meet the stringent demands of various industries for high-reliability and high-precision control of AMSS, the design and implementation of effective and refined control strategies are crucial. Currently, several nonlinear control approaches have been developed to address the challenges posed by the coexistence of lumped uncertainties and friction disturbances in the tracking control of AMSS. These approaches include adaptive disturbance observer techniques3,4, sliding mode control methods5,6, and intelligent control strategies7,8. It’s worth noting that these aforementioned approaches can ensure that the system deviation converges to a small region around zero by adjusting the control parameters. Nevertheless, due to the existence of unknown composite function containing disturbances and unknown parameters in the stability analysis, it remains challenging to precisely characterize the steady-state error and analyze the convergence speed of the tracking error.

To achieve desired transient behavior and control performance characteristics (e.g., convergence speed, overshoot, and steady-state error) while ensuring closed-loop system stability, the concept of prescribed performance control (PPC) was introduced9,10. This approach relies on the introduction of performance functions and adaptation laws that ensure the strong robustness of control system. Initial investigations on PPC focused on single-input single-output (SISO) nonlinear systems. Then, the approach was extended to various controlled plants to achieve specified performance objectives11–13. Especially based on the idea of this method, Song et al.14,15 and Sun et al.16 designed the robust control strategies and explored the design of prescribed performance controller in the context of nonlinear system trajectory control.

It is important to highlight that the control strategies mentioned above primarily rely on time-triggered mechanisms employing periodic sampling. However, from an efficiency standpoint, periodic sampling is not optimal in terms of improving control performance and reducing communication burden. To address this limitation, the literature17,18 proposed the event-triggered control methods and further investigated by the literature19–21, in which these control strategies utilize a non-periodic sampling mode and relies on a trigger condition to update the control action. Compared to time-triggered control, event-triggered control can significantly reduce the data load on communication channels and enhance actuator efficiency. This is primarily because the triggering condition remains inactive when the control system performance satisfactorily, there is no need to transmit data and drive the actuator under this case. Event-triggered control approaches have been successfully applied to various nonlinear systems. For instance, Mustafa et al.22 developed a robust trajectory tracking control scheme for nonlinear affine models based on an event-triggered strategy, guaranteeing semi-global uniform ultimate boundedness. Zhang et al.23 designed a practical prescribed-time control method with event-triggered and self-triggered strategy for a class of nonlinear systems, and the proposed method can achieve the practical prescribed-time stabilization with fewer events. For improving the control efficiency of complex nonlinear systems (CNS), the event-triggered approaches24–26 have also been employed to guarantee the robust stability of CNS with the lumped perturbation.

Despite the appealing features of the aforementioned event-triggered control approaches, several challenges remain, particularly concerning the high-precision control of AMSS. Firstly, while event-triggered scheduling effectively enhances actuator utilization and reduces communication load, it may compromise system performance due to the inherent aperiodic sampling nature20,21. In demanding applications such as integrated circuit manufacturing and precision device processing, event-triggered approaches that cannot guarantee specified performances are unacceptable for high-tech products. Although some methods related with prescribed performance event-triggered control have been obtained in27–29, the precise time that system error reaches the preset precision cannot be specified. This also severely limits the dynamic performance of the control system. Secondly, the system state information is measured by sensors in practice application, whereas considering that the sensor signal channel is vulnerable to electromagnetic interference in some special circumstances or the complexity of the hardware layout and design, many AMSSs are not equipped with some sensors. This means that many of the existing results on full-state feedback control approaches cannot be applied directly to AMSS based on output measurements. Employing observers to estimate system states and incorporating them into the control system design to construct an output-feedback control architecture can effectively address the challenge of control laws requiring extensive sensor-based state information. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, there are currently no reports to the specific performance event-triggered controller for AMSS relied on state observer. To address these aforementioned challenges, a new output-feedback control framework based on specific performance requirement as well as event-triggered strategy is designed for AMSS with external disturbance and parameter uncertainty. Remark 2

Due to the length constraint of the bearing rail, the moving position of the slider block is bounded. On the other hand, the maximum power of the motor is directly related to the maximum linear velocity of the slide block. Since the power of the motor is finite, the linear velocity of the slide block is bounded. Thereby, Assumption 2 is reasonable.

Control objectives

This work presents a novel event-triggered control framework that obviates the requirement for velocity measurements while simultaneously achieving prescribed performance specifications. The designed control scheme guarantees the boundedness of all closed-loop signals and ensures that the tracking error converges to a predefined region within a prespecified time.

Conclusion

This paper investigates the prescribed performance tracking control problem for an axis-motion servo system subject to external disturbances and parameter uncertainties. To simplify hardware design and mitigate the impact of perturbations on the velocity signal channel, an auxiliary observer system is incorporated into the closed-loop control architecture. By leveraging the position error coordinates transformation and the velocity estimate provided by the auxiliary observer, an event-triggered control law with prescribed performance is further designed, which the designed event-triggered mechanism not only reduces the communication and data computing requirements, but also endows the closed-loop system with fine capability related to the predetermined control accuracy after fixed time. Finally, simulations have been carried out to demonstrate the effectiveness of proposed control strategy. Future work will experimentally validate event-triggered control strategies for nonlinear mechanical servo systems with predefined performance constraints. This will involve addressing challenges posed by signal delays and uncertain dynamics to ensure desired performance.

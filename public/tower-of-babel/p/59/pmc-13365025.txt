Orbital and skull metastases as the initial presentation of lung adenocarcinoma harbouring a MET exon 14 skipping mutation
Authors: Eiji Ando, Yuri Hayashi, Yasuhiro Matsuo, Hideo Saka
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryA man in his 70s presented with right ocular pain, visual disturbance, forehead swelling and left iliac pain. Diagnostic imaging revealed a large right orbital–frontal mass with skull involvement, a cavitary lesion in the right upper lobe and a left iliac lesion. Histological and molecular analyses confirmed lung adenocarcinoma with a MET exon 14 skipping mutation. Initial management focused on symptom control via palliative radiotherapy to the orbital and iliac lesions which achieved rapid relief. Following molecular confirmation, tepotinib—a selective MET tyrosine kinase inhibitor—was initiated. This resulted in clinical stability and a slight radiographic reduction of the primary lung lesion. This case highlights an atypical clinical manifestation of orbital metastasis in MET-mutated non-small-cell lung cancer. It underscores the clinical necessity of prompt tissue diagnosis and molecular profiling in patients presenting with atypical metastatic sites to facilitate the timely initiation of targeted systemic therapies.

Background

MET exon 14 skipping (METex14) mutations occur in approximately 3% of patients with non–small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and represent a distinct actionable molecular subset particularly in the elderly. While brain, bone and pleural metastases are relatively common, orbital metastases are seldom reported. We present a case of METex14-positive lung adenocarcinoma initially manifesting with orbital and skull metastases accompanied by radiologic features suggestive of carcinomatous meningitis.

Case presentation

A man in his 70s presented to our institution with a 1-month history of progressive right ocular pain, visual disturbance, forehead swelling and left iliac pain. He had a current smoking history of five cigarettes per day for approximately 50 years. His past medical history was unremarkable. Physical examination revealed proptosis of the right eye and a palpable, tender mass over the right frontal bone. Neurological examination was otherwise intact, with no focal motor or sensory deficits.

Investigations

Whole-body CT revealed a cavitary lesion in the right upper lobe of the lung measuring approximately 53×33 mm and a 60 mm mass involving the right frontal bone and orbit (figure 1A). Additionally, a destructive lesion was noted in the left ilium. Contrast-enhanced MRI demonstrated a large orbital-frontal mass infiltrating the superior rectus, lateral rectus and superior oblique muscles, as well as the lacrimal gland, ethmoid sinus and temporalis muscle. The lesion also extended through the frontal bone, with associated dural thickening and linear enhancement, findings that raised radiological suspicion for carcinomatous meningitis (figure 1C). A cerebrospinal fluid examination was not performed because the patient declined the procedure. A transbronchial lung biopsy and a forehead skin-incision biopsy were performed. Transbronchial lung biopsy confirmed adenocarcinoma (figure 2A). The forehead biopsy confirmed metastatic adenocarcinoma from the lung (figure 2B). Molecular testing (AmoyDx PCR panel) identified a METex14 mutation. No other driver alterations (EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, KRAS or RET) were detected. The PD-L1 tumour proportion score (22C3 antibody) was 1%. The final diagnosis was stage IVB (cT3N0M1c) lung adenocarcinoma.

Differential diagnosis

Based on the patient’s ocular symptoms, primary orbital tumours such as malignant lymphoma or meningioma, as well as metastatic disease from other organs, were initially considered. The identification of a cavitary lung lesion in the right upper lobe and a left iliac bone lesion on CT shifted the working diagnosis towards metastatic lung cancer, which was confirmed by biopsies of the lung and frontal lesions.

Treatment

The patient received palliative radiotherapy (20 Gy in five fractions) to the orbital and iliac lesions after frontal mass biopsy. After confirmation of a METex14 mutation, systemic therapy with tepotinib was initiated at 500 mg daily.

Outcome and follow-up

Right ocular pain, visual disturbance, forehead swelling and left iliac pain improved rapidly after palliative radiotherapy. After 2 months of tepotinib therapy, follow-up CT demonstrated a reduction in the size of the primary lung lesion from approximately 53×33 mm to 36×24 mm, with stable disease in the metastatic sites (figure 1B). The patient remains clinically stable with an improved quality of life and continues on tepotinib therapy without significant adverse events as of the last follow-up.

Discussion

To our knowledge, few reports have described METex14-positive lung adenocarcinoma presenting with orbital and skull metastases accompanied by radiologic features suggestive of carcinomatous meningitis. Orbital metastases represent an infrequently described manifestation of NSCLC, as noted in a systematic review.1

METex14 mutations occur in approximately 3% of NSCLC, more frequently in elderly patients.2 In a systematic review of METex14-positive NSCLC, brain metastases occurred in 15.0%, bone in 6.3% and pleural in 28.6%, while orbital metastases were not reported.3 This suggests the metastatic spectrum of METex14-positive NSCLC may be broader than previously recognised.

MET TKIs such as tepotinib have shown substantial efficacy. In the VISION trial, tepotinib achieved an objective response rate (ORR) of 46% with a median duration of response (DoR) of more than 11 months; among patients with brain metastases, the ORR was 55% with a median DoR of 9.5 months.4 Capmatinib has shown comparable efficacy.5

In our patient, prompt local radiotherapy relieved symptoms and enabled systemic therapy. Rapid molecular profiling identified METex14, allowing the timely initiation of tepotinib. This case highlights the importance of considering metastatic lung cancer in patients presenting with orbital tumours and underscores that timely tissue diagnosis, molecular profiling and symptom-directed local therapy are essential to guide appropriate systemic treatment in advanced NSCLC.

Patient’s perspective

The following comment was provided by the patient 2 months after initiation of treatment (originally in Japanese, translated into English by the authors): ‘I was very anxious about the pain and swelling around my eye, but radiotherapy quickly relieved the pain. I am grateful for the prompt diagnosis and initiation of treatment. Thanks to the therapy, I have been able to continue my daily life and feel reassured’.

Learning points

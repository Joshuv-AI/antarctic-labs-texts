Hierarchical random forest model, inflammation and oxidative stress as predictors of the atherogenic index of plasma and diabetes progression
Authors: Herbert F. Jelinek, Issam Muteir, Hayder Al-Aubaidy
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a chronic metabolic disease that increases the risk of cardiovascular complications. The atherogenic index of plasma (AIP) is a risk marker for T2DM and cardiovascular disease on the basis of lipid profiles. T2DM and CVD risk are also associated with nonlipid biomarkers, including oxidative stress, inflammation, and mitochondrial dysfunction, and are linked to diabetes progression. This study applies hierarchical random forest (HRF) machine learning to identify stage-specific predictors of AIP in normoglycemic, prediabetic, and diabetic individuals. Participants were divided into normal (< 5.7%), prediabetic (5.7–6.4%), and diabetic (≥ 6.5%) groups based on their HbA1c values. Clinical, oxidative, inflammatory, and mitochondrial biomarkers were included in the study. Lipid measures directly contributing to the AIP calculation were excluded to minimize collinearity. Predictive models were developed via random forest (RF) and hierarchical random forest (HRF) approaches. HRF incorporates repeated threefold cross-validation to improve stability and feature importance across subgroups. Model performance was evaluated via the coefficient of determination (R²) and mean squared error (MSE). HRF models revealed distinct biomarker profiles associated with AIP and diabetes progression associated with inflammation, oxidative stress, and mitochondrial function variables. Waist-to-height ratio was the main contributing variable in the stratified dataset. For the stratified data, mitochondrial redox markers (p66Shc, humanin) were among the top predictors in the normoglycemia group. In individuals with prediabetes, the importance of these cytokines decreased, whereas oxidative stress-associated biomarkers (GSH, 8-OHdG) provided more accurate classifications. In the diabetes group, 8-OHdG remained moderately predictive, whereas the mitochondrial peptide MOTSc and inflammatory markers (IL-1β) were key features. These results indicate that the progression from mitochondrial-associated changes in the early stages of diabetes to immunometabolic dysfunction in individuals with established diabetes is correlated with AIP. Hierarchical random forest machine learning combined with glycemic stratification reveals evolving biomarker associations with the atherogenic index of plasma linked with diabetes progression. Mitochondrial and immune markers contribute differently across disease stages, supporting their potential use in stage-specific risk stratification and targeted intervention in T2DM management.

Introduction

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a chronic and progressive metabolic disorder characterized by insulin resistance, impaired pancreatic β-cell function, systemic inflammation, and oxidative stress associated with atherogenic risk1. The global rise in T2DM incidence parallels an increase in associated comorbidities such as cardiovascular disease (CVD), hypertension, and kidney disease2,3. Consequently, the early and accurate identification of atherogenic risk provides an important clinical risk marker for timely treatment and prevention of adverse outcomes4,5.

The atherogenic index of plasma (AIP) is calculated as the logarithmic ratio of triglycerides to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) and is increasingly recognized as a marker of endothelial dysfunction, insulin resistance, and risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs)6. The AIP has also been reported as a predictive marker for early metabolic disturbances in both prediabetic and normoglycemic populations7. However, conventional glucose-based indicators of diabetes progression, such as fasting plasma glucose and glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), have demonstrated limited sensitivity in detecting early, asymptomatic disease or predictive accuracy for AIP8.

Inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-6 (IL-6), C-reactive protein (CRP), monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 (MCP-1), oxidative stress biomarkers (e.g., 8-isoprostane, 8-Iso; 8-hydroxydeoxyguanosine–8-OHdG), and endothelial-derived peptides (homocysteine, Hcy) contribute to disease progression from an asymptomatic state to overt T2DM, including diabetes complications9–11. Oxidative stress, including elevated homocysteine levels, has also been linked to diabetes disease progression12,13. In addition, several studies have reported the involvement of mitochondrial markers linked to metabolic stress, insulin resistance, and CVD14–16. The mitochondria-derived peptides include humanin (Hn) and mitochondrial open reading frame of the 12 S rRNA-c (MOTSc), which are encoded in the mitochondrial genome and regulate apoptosis, cellular stress resistance, and metabolic homeostasis. Humanin has been shown to protect against oxidative injury and β-cell apoptosis, while MOTSc enhances insulin sensitivity and glucose metabolism2,17–19. The SHC-transforming protein 1 (SHCA) subfamily of adaptor proteins includes the Src collagen homologue (Shc) p66Shc is a nuclear redox-sensitive protein that interacts with the mitochondrial electron transport chain and has also been implicated in longevity and endothelial dysfunction in hyperglycemic states20. Currently, no studies have reported the role of these biomarkers in predicting AIP across the glycemic continuum from normoglycemia to T2DM. This data mining method has shown promise in classification and risk prediction.

Multiple-marker predictive models and machine learning have been investigated for cardiovascular and metabolic disease risk prediction16,21. Artificial intelligence-based methods have been increasingly employed to identify robust biomarkers for predicting the progression of T2DM16,22 and include support vector machine (SVM), random forest (RF), and extreme gradient boosting (XGB) for the identification and classification of nonlinear and complex interactions of clinical, biochemical, and lifestyle-related features, which facilitates early risk stratification and personalized treatment planning23,24. Random forest (RF) has emerged as a highly accurate method for predicting diabetes progression on the basis of diverse biomarkers11,25. Several studies have validated the application of these approaches in identifying key predictors of T2DM complications, such as cardiovascular disease and neuropathy26. AI-based models have also identified novel associations between oxidative stress, inflammatory markers, and autonomic dysfunction in diabetic populations, which may have translational potential in advancing endocrinology and metabolic research as well as personalized treatment options2,27,28.

However, RF does not perform well with data heterogeneity, such as physiological transitions from normoglycemia to T2DM. Hierarchical random forest (HRF) has the advantage that it integrates stratified learning into ensemble models that identify localized interactions29. Hierarchical data mining, such as the hierarchical combination of convolutional neural networks or the ensemble learning approach30–32, has further shown better results for prediction. The current study investigated the effectiveness of HRF in predicting AIP levels associated with diabetes progression in terms of inflammation, oxidative stress, and clinical markers.

Methods

Study population and recruitment

The participants were stratified into three glycemic categories based on hemoglobin A1c values: normal (< 5.7%), prediabetic (HbA1c between 5.7% and 6.4%), and diabetic (HbA1c ≥ 6.5%). Recruitment and assessment were conducted as part of the Diabetes Screening Complications Research Initiative (DiabHealth) at Charles Sturt University, Australia, between 2001 and 2013. The institutional ethics committee approved the research, and all participants provided informed consent before enrollment. All experiments were carried out in accordance with the relevant guidelines (IRB 2006/042; 11/09/2012). Machine learning models were applied to the stratified cohort based on HbA1c to identify group-specific predictors of the atherogenic index of plasma (AIP).

Biomarkers

The dataset includes gender, age, oxidative stress markers (8-isoprostane, 8-Iso; 8-OHdG; reduced glutathione, (GSH); glutathione disulfide, GSSG; malondialdehyde, MDA; methemoglobin, met-Hb), inflammatory cytokines (C-reactive protein, CRP; interleukin-1beta, IL-1β;, IL-6, IL-10, monocyte chemoattractant protein-1, MCP-1; insulin growth factor − 1, IGF-1), endothelial and vascular damage markers (D-dimer;, complement 5a, C5a,; homocysteine, Hcy), mitochondrial and metabolic markers (pP66Shc, humanin, MOTSc), antioxidant status (reduced glutathione, GSH, glutathione disulfide, GSSG, GSH: GSSG ratio), body composition metrics (body mass index, BMI; waist–to-height ratio, WCHR) and clinical markers of systolic and diastolic blood pressure (SBP, DBP). Direct lipid measurements (high-density lipoprotein, HDL; low-density lipoprotein, LDL; total cholesterol, and triglycerides, Trigs) were excluded from the RF and HRF models to reduce collinearity with the AIP outcome [33Bader, 2024 #20442Jelinek, 2015 #8207].

Datamining and predictive modeling

A random forest (RF) regression model was first implemented to provide baseline predictive accuracy for AIP, using 80% of the diabetes subgroup (defined as HbA1c ≥ 6.5%) for training and 20% for testing, with 100 decision trees. To address limitations in interindividual variability and subgroup structure inherent in conventional RF models, a hierarchical random forest (HRF) approach was introduced, as the traditional RF does not account for subgroup structure or interindividual variability33,34. HRF supports a more stable estimation of predictive feature importance across various fields, including clinical data analysis, while accounting for stage-specific variation in pathophysiology by integrating repeated cross-validation within each subgroup. A threefold K-fold cross-validation loop, as a repeated cross-validation strategy, was applied to the RF models for training, and predictions were averaged across folds to improve model stability and enable stage-specific biomarker profiling. To minimize overfitting in each fold, 50 estimators per model were included.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first study to apply a hierarchical random forest (HRF) approach to model AIP across stratified glycemic groups combined with variable importance to identify the biomarkers most associated with stages of T2DM disease progression. HRF allows subgroup-specific learning by approximating hierarchical data structures such as diabetes progression, which is defined by HbA1c-based clinical stages. The results indicate the importance of including inflammatory and oxidative stress biomarkers to enhance understanding and provide clinically relevant information as part of disease progression. The main advantage of applying an HRF framework is interpretability and stability in feature selection, as is the case in the current heterogeneous dataset, where physiological transitions may obscure pooled analysis signals. Previous work has demonstrated the superiority of HRF in subgroup modeling29,38,39, but its application to atherogenic risk prediction in diabetes requires further research. By incorporating repeated cross-validation to emulate nested learning, this study demonstrates the added value of HRF in identifying stage-specific physiological drivers of cardiometabolic risk.

Assessing the performance of AIP predictors has relied primarily on conventional lipid measures or isolated biomarkers such as C-reactive protein (CRP), interleukins, or HbA1c6,9. However, few studies have explored the integration of nonlipid biomarkers, including oxidative stress and mitochondrial markers, for AIP prediction via machine learning techniques combined with stratification of the dataset by the HbA1c-defined glycemic state to assess how biomarker relevance shifts with diabetes progression2,40. The current study incorporated a broad panel of biochemical and demographic features to predict AIP using hierarchical random forest models. To determine the associations between inflammatory and oxidative stress biomarkers, lipid-related predictors were intentionally excluded. The novelty of the current approach lies in the use of an HRF model, approximated via repeated cross-validation, to enhance stability and subgroup-specific learning, including stratification of the population into three clinically relevant HbA1c categories. This stratified approach revealed clear and biologically meaningful differences in the most important predictors of AIP across the glycemic spectrum.

This dynamic remodeling of biomarker importance associated with the progression from normoglycemia to hyperglycemia, shown in Fig. 1, reflects the nonlinear trajectory of diabetes pathophysiology, where atherogenic processes may initially be driven by redox imbalance and early mitochondrial stress but are followed by a transition to immune activation in prediabetes. Finally, T2DM is associated with metabolic and mitochondrial dysfunction. In the diabetes group, the hierarchical random forest model showed the least explanatory power, which reflects the substantial variability in AIP values among individuals with established diabetes in this cohort, as well as the associations between AIP and the non-lipid biomarkers used in the model. This limited the model’s ability to account for variance in AIP and its capacity to generalize within this subgroup. The findings are consistent with prior research showing that biomarker-based models reported low explanatory strength of non-lipid biomarkers and increased heterogeneity in diabetes-related models associated with increased pathophysiological heterogeneity9,16,41.

These results also agree with the evolving systems biology models of diabetes progression that integrate mitochondrial dysfunction, chronic inflammation, and metabolic inflexibility as central components12,42–44. The two mitochondrial peptides Humanin and MOTSc were previously associated with metabolic regulation and insulin sensitivity27. This finding suggests that mitochondrial biomarkers exhibit phase-specific contributions to AIP, with p66shc, a nuclear-derived peptide important in oxidative stress, and humanin being more relevant in early stages, with MOTSc becoming more prominent as hyperglycemia and metabolic disease progress.

Clinically, recognizing this stratified modeling approach may enhance individualized risk assessment and monitoring44. For example, in normoglycemic individuals, elevated AIP in conjunction with early redox imbalance (e.g., elevated Humanin or P66) could be addressed by antioxidant or lifestyle-based interventions. In individuals with prediabetes, immune markers may be intermediate therapeutic targets, whereas in individuals with diabetes, mitochondrial markers such as MOTSc suggest more advanced cardiometabolic interventions. The ability of HRF models to uncover these subgroup-specific drivers and improve prediction stability underscores their utility in translating complex biomarker data into clinically meaningful insights.

Conclusion

The current study demonstrates that integrating machine learning with HbA1c-stratified modeling reveals dynamic shifts in biomarker profiles associated with the atherogenic index of plasma (AIP) across the glycemic spectrum. These findings highlight the evolving roles of mitochondrial and immune-related biomarkers in cardiometabolic risk and support their potential incorporation into predictive models for T2DM. Stratified approaches such as the one employed here may enable more precise, stage-specific risk assessment and inform targeted interventions in the management of diabetes-related cardiovascular complications. These advances suggest that AI-based diagnostic frameworks may soon become essential tools in managing the growing global burden of T2DM.

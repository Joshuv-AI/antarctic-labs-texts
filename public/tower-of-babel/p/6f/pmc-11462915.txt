Green synthesized iron oxide nanoparticles as a potential regulator of callus growth, plant physiology, antioxidative and microbial contamination in Oryza sativa L.
Authors: Jawad Ullah, Afia Gul, Ilham Khan, Junaid Shehzad, Rehana Kausar, Muhammad Shahzad Ahmed, Sana Batool, Murtaza Hasan, Mansour Ghorbanpour, Ghazala Mustafa
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In tissue culture, efficient nutrient availability and effective control of callus contamination are crucial for successful plantlet regeneration. This study was aimed to enhance callogenesis, callus regeneration, control callus contamination, and substitute iron (Fe) source with FeO-NPs in Murashige and Skoog (MS) media. Nanogreen iron oxide (FeO-NPs) were synthesized and well characterized with sizes ranging from 2 to 7.5 nm. FeO-NPs as a supplement in MS media at 15 ppm, significantly controlled callus contamination by (80%). Results indicated that FeCl3-based FeO-NPs induced fast callus induction (72%) and regeneration (43%), in contrast FeSO4-based FeO-NPs resulted in increased callus weight (516%), diameter (300%), number of shoots (200%), and roots (114%). Modified media with FeO-NPs as the Fe source induced fast callogenesis and regeneration compared to normal MS media. FeO-NPs, when applied foliar spray, increased Plant fresh biomass by 133% and spike weight by 350%. Plant height increased by 54% and 33%, the number of spikes by 50% and 265%, and Chlorophyll content by 51% and 34% in IRRI-6 and Kissan Basmati, respectively. Additionally, APX (Ascorbate peroxidase), SOD (Superoxide dismutase), POD (peroxidase), and CAT (catalase) increased in IRRI-6 by 27%, 29%, 283%, 62%, while in Kissan Basmati, APX increased by 70%, SOD decreased by 28%, and POD and CAT increased by 89% and 98%, respectively. Finally, FeO-NPs effectively substituted Fe source in MS media, shorten the plant life cycle, and increase chlorophyll content as well as APX, SOD, POD, and CAT activities. This protocol is applicable for tissue culture in other cereal crops as well.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-024-05627-y.

Introduction

Oryza sativa commonly known as rice, is a staple food for more than half of the world’s population, providing more than 20% of the calories consumed worldwide. It is cultivated in more than 100 countries, with 90% of the total global production coming from Asia [1]. According to the 2015/16 report, Japan achieved the highest rice yield in the world, with 4.91 t/ha, followed by China with 4.79 t/ha. (Table S1). Over the past few decades, rice consumption has increased by 40% in the caribbean and latin America [2]. In Pakistan, rice is the second largest cash crop after wheat and is one of the most valuable exportable products [3]. However, rice production in Pakistan is expected to decline by 15.2% to 17. % by themid-21st century [4]. Considering its widespread cultivation and consumption, rice is a crucial staple food crop globally. Biotechnological approaches with modern techniques were employed to develop new rice varieties to meet the food demand for growing population [5]. These approaches include the development of transgenic plants [6], virus elimination from infected materials, rescue of endangered and threatened plants [7], germplasm conservation, micropropagation of commercial plants, and functional gene studies [8–10]. Tissue culture is a crucial tool for ensuring the quality and purity of rice products. Moreover, it can also be employed to improve crop tolerance and yield. Therefore, developing an efficient protocol for the genetic manipulation of rice through tissue culture is of utmost importance [11]. Compared to traditional breeding, tissue culture offers a concise approach to quality, purity, and variety development [12, 13]. Many protocols have been established for the in vitro regeneration of rice. While various explants explants can be used in rice tissue culture, callus formation from dehusked mature seeds has proven to be the most effective [14]. Explant selection in tissue culture depends on plant variety and can vary from plant to plant. Production of immature embryos in large quantities can be laborious and time consuming. However, using seeds scutellum from mature seeds has demosnstrated the best callus formation with high totipotency [15–17]. This approach offers several advantages, including high totipotency, availability throughout the season, and ease of long-term storage compared to other rice explant.

Micronutrient such as Fe play a vital role in various physiological functions of plants, including chlorophyll synthesis and DNA formation. However, Fe is often inaccessible to plants due to high soil pH and aerobic conditions. Ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (Fe-EDTA) is a chelated form of Fe, which becomes unavailable after its release in MS media beacuse it converts to Fe-phosphate [18]. Fe deficiency can cause chlorosis, decrease yield, and negatively impact plant growth and quality [19]. Fe NPs have been found to promote plant growth in wheat by managing photosynthesis-related proteins [20–23]. To replace Fe-EDTA, FeO-NPs were used in chrysanthemum tissue culture. The results revealed that FeO-NPs enhanced plant growth and chlorophyll content compared to Fe-EDTA in MS media [24–26]. In plant tissue culture, NPs act as nutrients and disinfectants, positively affecting organogenesis, callus formation, stress tolerance, and nutrient provision. They have high affinity for absorbing contamination and combating diseases [27–29]. With the use of nano-technology, the uptake efficiency of micro- as well as macronutrients of plants can be increased. Many nanomaterials have been used successfully to improve crop nutrition as compared to conventional fertilizers. Nanomaterials provide essential elements to plants directly as well as nano additives, which increase nutrients efficiency by reducing leaching, volatilization and losses due to various fixation [30, 31]. Biological methods of NPs synthesis are preferred over physical and chemical methods due to their non-toxicity, simplicity, and cost-effectiveness [32]. Capping and reducing agents play a crucial role in plant-mediated synthesis of NPs [33–35]. Additionally, Fe is supplied in different forms to be easily available for plant absorption. Green synthesis of NPs is more effective, environmentally friendly, and serves as both nutrient source and means for removing contamination in tissue culture.

Fe is often supplied as an inorganic complex salt (FeSO4.7H2O) in MS media, which can lead to limited Fe absorption. However, recent advancements in nanotechnology have enabled the replacement of Fe salt with small-sized FeO-NPs, due to their strong catalytic activity, high surface absorption, and ultra-fine size [36, 37]. In the presence of excess Na2EDTA for chelating Fe2, the availability of other micronutrients depends on their stability with EDTA, and there is no specific formula for determining the optimal type of EDTA to be used [28]. Plant-mediated NPs synthesis is an eco-friendly, economical, and stable method [32, 38]. FeO-NPs show results than conventional Fe salt usage, providing superior physiological and biochemical effects. Additionally, FeO-NPs helps in controlling callus contamination and promote rapid callus regeneration.

Iron deficiency affects approximately one billion people globally, leading to anemia [39]. In plants, iron deficiency usually inhibits chloroplast development and chlorophyl biosynthesis, causing chlorosis, due to its role in various redox reactions [40]. Nanomaterials are more sustainable and can be utilized to enhance agricultural production [41]. Foliar applications of nanoparticles such as Fe, Mn, Zn, and Cu have shown improvements in agronomic traits [42–45].

Many studies have been carried out regarding nanomaterials but none was addressing the substitutuion of FeEDTA with FeO-NPs. No such study is recorded previously in rice tissue culture. The current study is mainly designed to introduce rapid callus formation and regeneration protocol, control callus contamination and improve Fe availability in MS media by substituting FeEDTA with FeO-NPs.

Materials and methods

FeO-NPs synthesis

Chenopodium album (C. album) and Fumaria indica (F. indica) were selected for green synthesis of FeO-NPs, due to their novel properties in NPs synthesis and their phytochemical constituents. Both plants were collected from District Upper Dir, Province KhyberPakhtunkhwa, Pakistan. For extract preparation, 20 g of plant sample (leaves) were boiled in 100 mL of distilled water at 100 °C and then filtered. The resulting plant extract was added dropwise to a 0.1 M FeCl3/FeSO4 salt solution in 1:1 ratio, which was kept on a hotplate (Hotplate Stirrer (LED), SCILOGEX MS7- H550-S, Sterlitech, Auburn, USA), at 75 °C. The pH was adjusted at 11, after 15 min of stirring, by adding 1 M NaOH. The solution was continuously stirred at 75 °C for an additional 30 min, until it turned a reddish dark-brown color. The mixture was centrifuged (HERMLE Labortechnik GmbH, Wehingen, Germany) at 8000 revolutions per minute (rpm) for 20 min, and the supernatant was discarded. The resulting pellet was washed once with 100% ethanol and twice with distilled water. The pellet was collected in a china dish and heated at 300 °C for 2 to 3 h (Fig. 1).

FeO-NPs characterization

The UV-Visible absorption spectrum of the plant extract, and NPs solution was analyzed at various time intervals within the wavelength range of 200 to 600 nm. The UV-Vis spectrum was measured using a Thermo Scientific Multiskan GO, Vantaa, Finland. The spectra were obtained after every 10-min difference in all aliquots as a function of time retortion for the optimization process using a Thermo Scientific Multiskan GO spectrophotometer (Japan).

Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR)

FTIR analysis was performed using a JASCO FT/IR-6600 typeA spectrometer (USA), to obtain an infrared spectrum with a resolution of 4 cm − 1 in the 500–4000 cm − 1 range. FTIR spectrum was used to identify the existing functional groups and deeper understanding of chemical composition and bonding interactions in the compound. FTIR was also helpful to identify various functional groups, helped in the reduction of FeO-NPs.

X-ray diffraction (XRD)

XRD analysis was performed using D8 ADVANCE, BRUKER instrument (USA) BRUKER, Billerica, USA. To examine the particle size, purity, and crystallinity FeO-NPs in powdered form were analysed by X-ray diffraction analysis. The measurement of wavelength were in the range of 20–80° at 2θ angles which provides important structural insight.

Scanning electron microscope (SEM)

Scanning electron microscope (SEM) was used to examine the surface morphology of FeO-NPs, by ZEISS microscope, Oberkochen, Germany. The nanoparticles’ external structure and shape was investigated.

Experimental design

IRRI-6 and Kissan Basmati rice varities were selected for a tissue culture study involving the use of FeO-NPs at concentration of 5, 10, and 15 ppm, with a control group receiving no FeO-NPs treatment. The experiment was conducted in three independent biological replicates. IRRI-6 and Kissan Basmati were selected for the experiment, and were obtained from the nursery of Rice Program, Crop Science Institute, National Agriculture Research Center, Islamabad (Fig S1a). Trial consists of 45 days, during which three sprays of different concentration were applied, with a 15-day interval between each spray. To prevent the sprayed NPs from being washed away by rain, the pots were covered with plastic sheets FigS1.c).

Growth media

Murashige and Skoog (MS) basal media (M519 Phyto Technology Laboratories, USA), was used for rice callus induction and callus regeneration. 2,4. D at 3 mg L− l, Sucrose at 30 g L− l, Casein Hydrolysate at 0.3 g L− l, L. Proline at 0.6 g L− l and Phytagel at concentration of 3 g L− l were used as supplementary materials (Table S2). Media pH was adjusted at 5.8. Besides this, a modified media was also prepared with substitution of Fe source with FeO-NPs. NPs at 5, 10, and 15 ppm concentration were used in MS media.

Explant and sterilization

IRRI-6 and Kissan Basmati healthy seeds were selected as an explant and dehusked manually. The method of [46] was followed with some modifications. Explant washed gently with 70% ethanol for 1 min followed by treatment with 6% sodium hypochlorite for 20 min. Explant was rinsed with distilled water 4 to 5 times and placed on autoclaved filter paper to get dry prior to inoculation.

Callus induction and regeneration

All procedures were conducted in a sterile environment. Inoculated explant was incubated at temperature of 25 ± 2 °C in dark (Hotpack, Programmed Refrigerated Incubator, Bucks County, Pennsylvania) for callus induction. For regeneration kinetin was used at 1.5 mg L− l for shooting, and Indole-3-acetic acid for rooting. The embryogenic calli were incubated at temperature of 25 ± 2 °C in growth chamber, under the influence of light range of 1500–3000 lx for 16 h photoperiod [47–49]. Callus Weight and Diameter.

Callus weight was measured using precision balance, while callus diameter was measured using simple scale.

Acclimatization

The plantlets were placed in peat moss before being transferred into the soil for few days, to develop some more roots and to acclimatize slowly into external environment. Following this, they were transferred into soil under low light for a few days to minimize stress during the transition period.

FeO-NPs solution preparation for foliar spray

The FeO-NPs were sonicated for 1 h around at 40 °C to avoid agglomeration and dissolve effectively. Three treatments (25 ppm, 50 ppm and 75 ppm) were applied as foliar spray Fig S1b).

Morphological and physiochemical parameters measurement

Spad-meter (SPAD-502Plus, Konica Minolta, Chiyoda City, Tokyo, Japan) was used, to measure chlorophyll content in average of three Fig S1.d). Shoot length was measured manually by scale. The spike number was counted before spray and after every spray with the gap of 15 days. Fresh plant and spike weight, flag leaf length, and number of tillers were also noted before and after foliar spray.

Antioxidant enzyme activity

To evaluate stress tolerance in rice by antioxidant enzymatic activity, 10 mg leaf sample was homogenized at 4 °C in 4 mL of extraction buffer using a pre-chilled pestle mortar. Extraction buffer was containing 1.4 mL of 0.07 of potassium phosphate buffer, 16 uL of 100 mM EDTA, 20 uL of 200 mM ascorbic acid, 0.08 g of 2% Polyvinyl pyridine, and water 2.5 mL. The homogenate is centrifuged at 15,000 g for 20 min at 4 °C. Supernatants were stored at 4 °C until enzyme activity estimations. This supernatant is used as enzymatic extract. The antioxidant enzyme activities of leaves were measured spectrophotometrically using supernatant of an aliquot. APX activity was measured through the procedure of Nakano and Asada [50]. Ascorbate absorbance was measured at 25 ◦C and 290 nm wavelength by a spectrophotometer. SOD activity was assessed by the method of [51]. Optical density was measured at 560 nm wavelength. POD activity was assayed through the method of Velikova [52], method, based on reduction in absorbance at 240 nm. for all UV absorbance, Thermo Scientific Multiskan GO Vantaa, Finland was used.

Statistical analysis

All data was statistically analyzed using Duncan’s multiple range (DMR) test, by SPSS Statistics software (version 26. for Mac, IBM Corp. USA). A p value of < 0.05 was considered as statistically significant.

This observation is consistent with previous studies that reported the observation of SPR at 299 nm, confirming the synthesis of FeO-NPs [53].

Number of tillers and roots in regenerated plantlet

Number of tillers in control plant were only two, and roots were seven. Number of tillers in regenerated plant, treated with FeO-NPs were 6, and roots were 15 in number (Fig. 7a). With the use of FeO-NPs, number of tillers increased by 200%, while number of roots increased by 114%.

Modified media effect on rice callus induction and regeneration

Fe was found to be not necessary for callus induction, as without it, callus induction was observed. Callus regeneration was observed only in media having Fe salt or FeO-NPs as an Fe source. Conversely, no callus regeneration was detected in the culture media lacking an Fe source (Fig. 7b).

Effect of FeO-NPs on morphological parameters

Foliar application of FeO-NPs increased plant height in IRRI-6 by 54% and in Kissan Basmati by 33%. With increase of FeO-NPs concentration plant height was positively regulated, and at concentation of 75 ppm maximum plant height was recorded. Compared to Kissan Basmati, IRRI-6 is more responsive to the use of FeO-NPs in terms of plant height (Fig. 8.a-d). Rice plant fresh weight was found to be increased by 133%, with the use of FeO-NPs foliar application. Compared to the control, at high concentration of FeO-NPs, plant fresh weight was recorded maximum. After plant harvesting, the fresh spike weight was found to be increased 350% with the NPs use. Effect of FeO-NPs on IRRI-6 and kissan basmati as a supplement in MS media

The primary objective of the current research was to develop a protocol for inducing callogenesis and accelerating callus regeneration in plants. Tissue culture is a complex process that depends on different environmental and genetic factors, optimization of various factors is required, which can be time-consuming and expensive [14]. The primary challenges with tissue culture is time and risk of contamination. Tissue culture provide energy and all other nutrients to explant with the help of basal media [66]. As plant tissue culture depends on various factors, like growth regulators, type of culture media, the strength of salt used, and concentration, among which nutrient availability is the critical one. Supplements and media ingredients are required to achieve optimal callus induction and explant regeneration [67]. To address these issues, we supplemented the media with FeO-NPs. Tissue culture most probably facing the problem of contamination. NPs has emerged to combat microbial contamination, and also positively impact callogenesis, organogenesis and plant sterilization. NPs enhances the tolerance of plants under drought stress improving biochemical activity and miRNA gene expression. Advancement in nanotechnology promoted Agribiotechnology. NPs are used to enhance plant growth, development, nutrients uptake and also make plant tolerant to various stresses and diseases.

NPs have a major role in plant tissue culture application, serving as nutrient sources and disinfectants [68, 69]. The use of FeO-NPs as a supplement in MS media for rice tissue culture has shown substantial control over contamination. Contamination frequency decreased by 67% and 81% in IRRI-6, and by 100% and 66% in Kissan Basmati, using FeCl3 and FeSO4 salt respectively (Fig. S5 and S6ab). The best results were obtained at a concentration of 15 ppm of FeO-NPs. FeO-NPs possess antibacterial activity, effectively regulating the growth and membrane-associated activity of both human and phytopathogens [70]. This antibacterial activity is attributed to the small size of the NPs, which allows them to interact with the bacterial cell wall, inactivate cellular enzymes, and hinder plasma membrane permeability, resulting in high levels of ROS that damage bacterial DNA and proteins, eventually leading to cell death [71, 72]. Thus, incorporating FeO-NPs into tissue culture media can effectively control contamination and improve the quality of the culture system. Media optimization in tissue culture is crucial, and various supplementary materials are used for this purpose. Previous research has also demonstrated that using FeO-NPs at optimal doses increased callus regeneration, callogenesis, root induction, and shoot elongation in chickpea plantlets. Conclusions

Nanogreen FeO-NPs were successfully prepared and well characterized by UV-Vis spectroscopy, FTIR, XRD, and SEM analysis. FeO-NPs significantly reduced callus contamination frequency in both rice cultivars, and positively impacted other parameters. Callus induction frequency increased in IRRI-6 by 33% and 59%, and in Kissan Basmati by 72% and 66%, using FeO-NPs based on C.FeCl3 and F.FeSO4, respectively. Callus regeneration frequency increased in IRRI-6 by 43% and 29%, and in Kissan Basmati by 33% and 33%, using FeO-NPs based on C.FeCl3 and F.FeSO4, respectively. Callus diameter increased in IRRI-6 by 190% and 179%, and in Kissan Basmati by 50% and 300%, using FeO-NPs based on C.FeCl3 and F.FeSO4, respectively. Callus weight increased in IRRI-6 by 41% and 167%, and in Kissan Basmati by 71% and 516%, using FeO-NPs based on C.FeCl3 and F.FeSO4, respectively. The Number of tillers increased by 200%, and number of roots increased by 114%. These findings suggest that FeO-NPs can substitute traditional Fe source in tissue culture, promoting healthy callus while reducing the time required for callus regeneration. Foliar application of FeO-NPs indicates that plant height increased in IRRI-6 by 54%, and in Kissan Basmati by 33%. Fresh plant weight increased by 133%, and fresh spike weight by 350%. The number of spikes increased in IRRI-6 by 50% and in Kissan Basmati by 265%, chlorophyll contents inreased in IRRI-6 by 51% and in Kissan Basmati by 34%. APX increased in IRRI-6 by 27% and in Kissan Basmati by 70%, SOD activity inreased in IRRI-6 by 29% but decreased by 28% in Kissan Basmati. POD activity increased in IRRI-6 by 283% and in Kissan Basmati by 89%, while CAT activity increased in IRRI-6 by 62% and in Kissan Basmati by 98%. This protocol can be used for plant biomass, and pharmacologically potent metabolites production.

Clinical and Histological Differences Between Choanal Polyps in Children and Adults: A 15‐Year Retrospective Study
Authors: Aleksandar Perić, Nikola Lazović, Jelena Sotirović, Gabriela Kopacheva Barsova, Biserka Vukomanović Đurđević
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractChoanal polyps (CPs) are benign, inflammatory, solitary lesions extending toward the junction between the nasal cavity/paranasal sinus and the nasopharynx through the choana. This study aimed to evaluate the clinical and histological characteristics of CPs in children compared to adult patients. Characteristics of patients (demography, main complaints, side, localization, surgical approach, histological characteristics, accompanying chronic rhinosinusitis [CRS], association with allergic rhinitis [AR] and aspirin sensitivity, postoperative follow‐up period, recurrence rates) were retrospectively reviewed. The patients were classified into children (<18 years) and adults (≥18 years). Our results suggest some clinical and histological specificities of CPs in children compared to adults. Epistaxis, oropharyngeal extension, association with AR and ipsilateral CRS, and angiomatous histological subtype of CPs are more frequent in the pediatric population. Also, the level of eosinophil infiltration of polyp tissue and recurrence rates are higher in pediatrics than in adult population.Keywords: allergic, blood vessels, child, eosinophils, epistaxis, nasal polyps, rhinitis

Choanal polyps (CPs) are benign, inflammatory, solitary lesions extending toward the junction between the nasal cavity/paranasal sinus and the nasopharynx through the choana. 1 , 2 Unlike bilateral nasal polyps, in which the edematous loose connective stromal tissue is mostly infiltrated with eosinophils, CPs are characterized by a dense fibrous stroma that is infiltrated by lymphocytes, plasma cells, neutrophils, and a much smaller number of eosinophils. 1 , 2 Etiology and pathogenesis are not known, but chronic nasal/paranasal sinus inflammation, allergy, anatomical variations within the nasal cavity/paranasal sinuses, and chronic lymphatic obstruction within the sinus mucosa are among the factors considered. 1 , 2 Unlike the adult population where they are relatively rare, CPs in children comprise about 33% of inflammatory nasal polyps. 1 , 2 , 3 This study aimed to evaluate the clinical and histological characteristics of CPs in children compared to adult patients.

Methods

This retrospective descriptive study was conducted in our hospital, according to the Declaration of Helsinki, and with Institution Review Board Approval (21/2022). Patients with a diagnosis of CPs, treated and followed up between January 2008 and December 2022 were included. Characteristics of patients (demography, main complaints, side, localization, surgical approach, histological characteristics, accompanying chronic rhinosinusitis [CRS], association with allergic rhinitis [AR] and aspirin sensitivity, postoperative follow‐up period, recurrence rates) were retrospectively reviewed. They were classified into children (<18 years) and adults (≥18 years). Computed tomography scan of the paranasal sinuses was performed in all patients. In some patients, suspicion of hypervascular lesions, and benign and malignant tumors filling the nasal cavity, sinuses, and nasopharynx led us to perform magnetic resonance imaging for differential diagnosis. Evaluation of AR was conducted according to symptoms, local findings, results of skin prick test, and serological tests. The follow‐up period was a minimum of 17 months after the surgical treatment.

Histological analysis of appropriately labeled specimens excised during surgery and embedded in paraffin molds was performed by a pathologist using a digital optical microscope (Nicon Coolscope) after the hematoxylin and eosin staining. Eosinophils were counted at ×400 magnification. The visual field was oriented along the whole length of the epithelium basement membrane at 10 randomly chosen high‐power fields (HPFs) of a single section. The eosinophils in each section were counted, and the average number per HPF was calculated.

For statistical analysis, we used the Fisher exact test to determine differences between categorical data (frequencies). For between‐group comparison, we used the nonparametric Mann‐Whitney U test. P < .05 was considered significant.

Results

One hundred and ten (n = 110) patients with CPs were included, 28 pediatric and 82 adult. Epistaxis and oropharyngeal extension of CPs were more frequent in children than in adults (P = .027; P = .002, respectively). The association with AR and ipsilateral CRS was more frequent in children than adults (P = .023; P = .031, respectively). On the other hand, we found a more frequent association with aspirin sensitivity in adults than in children (P = .032) (Table 1). In 28% of children and 7% of adults, we found the histological characteristics of an angiomatous CP (P = .002) (Figure 1, Table 1). Also, eosinophil count was higher in CP tissue in children than in adults (P = .036). After the surgical treatment, we found higher recurrence rates in pediatric than in adult patients (P = .025) (Table 1).

Discussion

This study is a continuation of our previous study from 2019. 2 After 5 years, 32 more patients were included, and 2 more parameters were added: density of eosinophilic infiltration of CP tissue and association with aspirin hypersensitivity. In contrast to our previous results, here we found a higher frequency of epistaxis and more frequent recurrences of CP in children. CPs are almost always unilateral lesions, mostly originating from the mucosa of the maxillary sinus (antrochoanal polyps—ACPs). However, less often they grow from the ethmoid and sphenoid sinus, and various parts of the nasal mucosa. 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 Our results suggest some clinical and histological specificities of CPs in children. The stronger eosinophil infiltration of CP in children can be connected by the more frequent association with AR. The more frequent oropharyngeal extension of CPs in the pediatric population can be explained by the greater potency of growth, and the more frequent nosebleeds by the greater prevalence of angiomatous CPs. 2 , 5 , 6 Although the notion of “angiomatous inflammatory polyp” has not yet been accepted as a separate clinical entity, the fact is that during histological analysis, CPs with dilated capillary spaces are found far more often in children than in adults. 2 , 5 , 6 The recurrence rate of CPs after surgery in our current study is higher in children than in adults, which fits the results of other authors. 7 , 8 , 9 According to the results published by Yang et al. 10 the counts of blood vessels and the percentage of tissue neutrophils appeared to be a potential excellent predictor of polyp recurrence in children. Apart from greater growth potential and better blood supply, the reason for the higher frequency of recurrence in children should also be sought in the surgical approach. 7 , 8 , 9 During the removal of ACPs in children, we avoided any procedure that could significantly damage the integrity of bone structures (Table 1). It is known that the disruption of the growth centers during the development of the child's maxilla can cause a disturbance in the further development of the upper jaw and teeth. 2 , 7 , 8 , 9 Therefore, in some cases, polyps in children may not be completely removed from the maxillary sinus. Extra caution is also necessary when removing CP in children, as we can expect more intense bleeding. Given that in children, a more extensive surgical approach cannot be justified, with a higher association with AR and the number of eosinophils in the tissue, a greater use of intranasal corticosteroids and antihistamines could slow the growth of CP after surgery. New information compared to previous ones is the presence of patients with aspirin sensitivity among those with CPs. This sensitivity is usually associated with Samter's triad, but some studies suggest its importance in the pathogenesis of CPs. 11 , 12 Cook et al. 12 found that 24% of the ACP patients had the Samter's triad. Among our patients, it was observed among those older than 16 years, which explains the higher frequency among adults and should certainly be the subject of further research.

Conclusion

Our results suggest that there are certain distinct characteristics and features between CPs in children and adults, such as a higher rate of epistaxis, oropharyngeal extension, association with AR, ipsilateral CRS, angiomatous histological subtype, and eosinophil infiltration of the polyp tissue in the pediatric population. Due to the greater growth potential and sparing surgical approach, the recurrence rate is higher in children. Although in children we have to be restrictive in the surgical approach, the application of corticosteroids and antihistamines could be useful in the period after polyp excision.

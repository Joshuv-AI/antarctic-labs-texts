Deer antler velvet as a multifunctional natural resource for biomedical and nutraceutical applications
Authors: Seung-Jae Lee, Seon Gyeong Bak, Ye Ji Cheon, Nisansala Chandimali, Jaehoon Bae
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Deer antler velvet (DAV), harvested from the growing antlers of deer, is a biologically dynamic animal-derived material that has attracted scientific interest due to its regenerative characteristics and complex biochemical composition. DAV contains proteins, peptides, growth-associated factors, polysaccharides, amino acids, lipids, and minerals, which have been investigated across diverse experimental systems. Although numerous studies report immunomodulatory, antioxidant, regenerative, neuroprotective, and musculoskeletal-related effects, the strength of evidence varies considerably, with the majority of data derived from in vitro and animal models and comparatively limited well-controlled human trials. This review provides a critical and mechanism-oriented synthesis of DAV research, three analytical perspectives: (1) compositional variability and its implications for standardization and reproducibility; (2) differentiation between the hierarchy of evidence distinguishing experimental plausibility from clinically validated outcomes; and (3) translational considerations, including bioavailability, pharmacokinetics of growth-associated components, and regulatory constraints. Reported molecular mechanisms converge on established pathways governing growth regulation, redox balance, immune modulation, and tissue remodeling; however, the systemic relevance of orally administered bioactive constituents, particularly growth factors such as IGF-1, remains incompletely defined. While DAV demonstrates biological activity across multiple preclinical models and limited human studies, critical challenges persist in dose standardization, long-term safety evaluation, clinical validation, and international regulatory harmonization. Future progress will depend on the establishment of defined compositional markers, rigorous pharmacokinetic assessment, and well-powered randomized controlled trials. By integrating compositional analysis, mechanistic evaluation, and translational feasibility, this review clarifies both the potential and the current limitations of DAV in biomedical and nutraceutical applications.

Introduction

Deer antler velvet (DAV) is a biologically unique mammalian tissue characterized by its rapid and fully repeatable annual regeneration. For centuries, DAV has been incorporated into traditional Chinese, Korean, and other Asian medical systems, where it has been reputed to enhance vitality, support musculoskeletal health, and promote general well-being (Cheng et al., 2022). Unlike most mammalian organs, deer antlers undergo complete cyclical regeneration, providing a rare physiological model for studying tissue growth, differentiation, angiogenesis, and mineralization. This regenerative capacity has been associated with a complex biochemical composition, including peptides, collagen, growth factors, amino acids, and glycosaminoglycans (Liu et al. 2023b; Xia et al. 2022).

In recent decades, increasing scientific interest has led to the investigation of DAV-derived extracts and fractions in various experimental systems. Preclinical studies have reported anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, neuroprotective, osteogenic, and immunomodulatory effects, suggesting potential relevance in wound healing, cartilage and bone regeneration, fatigue management, and age-associated conditions (Chen et al. 2023a, b; Liu et al. 2024). Concurrently, the nutraceutical sector has explored DAV as a functional ingredient in dietary supplements aimed at improving physical performance, metabolic resilience, and general vitality (Chen et al., 2024).

However, despite the growing body of literature, several critical issues remain insufficiently addressed. The reported biological activities are largely derived from in vitro experiments and animal models, with comparatively limited well-controlled human studies. Furthermore, compositional variability arising from species differences, harvesting stage, and processing methods complicates standardization and reproducibility. Mechanistic interpretations often extrapolate from the presence of bioactive molecules, yet questions remain regarding oral bioavailability, systemic exposure, and the relative contribution of whole extracts versus isolated components. In addition, regulatory positioning and translational feasibility for functional food or biomedical applications have not been comprehensively evaluated. These challenges highlight several structural limitations within the current DAV literature, including fragmented reporting of compositional data, inconsistent experimental designs, and limited integration between biochemical composition and functional outcomes.

Existing reviews have primarily summarized compositional profiles and reported bioactivities; however, an integrative assessment that critically examines evidence hierarchy, mechanistic plausibility, standardization challenges, and realistic application pathways remains limited. Therefore, this review aims to provide a mechanism-oriented and translationally focused synthesis of DAV by: (1) systematically evaluating its bioactive constituents in the context of compositional variability; (2) distinguishing in vitro, preclinical, and clinical levels of evidence for reported biological activities; (3) critically examining proposed molecular mechanisms in light of bioavailability considerations; and (4) discussing practical implications for nutraceutical and biomedical development. Through this structured framework, we seek to clarify both the scientific potential and the current limitations of DAV as a multifunctional animal-derived resource and to identify key research directions that may facilitate its future translational and industrial development.

Composition and bioactive constituents of deer antler velvet

DAV is a unique biological material harvested from the immature antlers of deer during their rapid growth phase. Its complex composition of proteins, growth factors, minerals, lipids, and other bioactive molecules is responsible for its broad therapeutic and nutraceutical properties (Fig. 1). The bioactive profile of DAV supports its traditional and modern applications in tissue regeneration, immune modulation, and overall health promotion (Rossetti et al., 2024; Sui et al., 2020). It should be noted that many reported biological activities are derived from studies using processed extracts, hydrolysates, or purified fractions rather than native whole antler tissue. Unless otherwise specified, references to biological effects in this section reflect findings from specific extract preparations or isolated components under defined experimental conditions. Therefore, compositional presence does not necessarily imply equivalent systemic activity following oral administration of whole DAV products. Major bioactive constituents of deer antler velvet and their reported biological activities

Proteins and peptides

Proteins form a major structural and functional component of DAV, accounting for a significant portion of its dry weight (Cao et al., 2023). Collagen, particularly type I and type II, is the most abundant protein and contributes to tissue strength, elasticity, and regenerative capacity (Shen et al., 2023). In addition to structural proteins, DAV contains numerous bioactive peptides. These peptides have been reported to exhibit immunomodulatory activity, enhancing the body’s defense mechanisms, as well as antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, which mitigate oxidative stress and tissue damage. The presence of small peptide fractions also supports wound healing and promotes cellular proliferation in various tissues (Orassay et al., 2024; Xia et al., 2022).

Growth factors

One of the defining characteristics of DAV is its richness in growth factors, which are crucial for cell proliferation, differentiation, and tissue regeneration. Insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1) is particularly notable for promoting skeletal growth, muscle repair, and cartilage regeneration. Epidermal growth factor (EGF) and transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) stimulate cellular proliferation and extracellular matrix production, enhancing tissue repair (Liu et al. 2023b; Orassay et al. 2024). Fibroblast growth factors (FGFs) in DAV contribute to angiogenesis and wound healing (Guo et al., 2025). The presence of these growth factors provides mechanistic plausibility for reported regenerative and osteogenic effects observed in experimental systems.

Collagen and glycosaminoglycans

Collagen in DAV not only provides structural integrity but also serves as a scaffold for tissue regeneration. Glycosaminoglycans (GAGs), including chondroitin sulfate and hyaluronic acid, are polysaccharides that maintain cartilage elasticity, joint lubrication, and tissue hydration (Chu et al., 2021). Collagen and GAGs are biologically complementary components that may contribute to reported cartilage and bone-related effects in experimental studies. These constituents are particularly important for nutraceutical applications aimed at joint health and anti-aging (Honvo et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2024).

Amino acids and minerals

DAV contains a wide spectrum of amino acids, both essential and non-essential, which play critical roles in protein synthesis, enzymatic functions, and overall metabolism. Essential amino acids such as lysine, methionine, and tryptophan contribute to tissue repair and neurotransmitter synthesis (Ny et al., 2022). Additionally, DAV is enriched with minerals including calcium, phosphorus, magnesium, zinc, and iron (Farms®, 2023). These minerals are fundamental for bone development, enzymatic activity, redox balance, and general physiological homeostasis, reinforcing DAV’s functional properties in both biomedical and nutraceutical contexts (Su et al., 2023).

Lipids, phospholipids, and gangliosides

Though present in smaller quantities, lipids and phospholipids in DAV are biologically significant. They contribute to cell membrane integrity, energy metabolism, and neuroprotection. Gangliosides, which are sialic acid-containing glycosphingolipids, have been reported to support neural development, cognitive function, and anti-inflammatory responses. This combination of lipids and gangliosides enhances the multifunctional bioactivity of DAV, particularly in brain health and anti-aging interventions (Ho et al., 2025; Paing & Lee, 2025).

Polysaccharides and other small bioactive molecules

Polysaccharides in DAV, including both neutral and acidic types, exhibit strong immunomodulatory and antioxidant activities, stimulating immune cell activity and reducing oxidative stress (Orassay et al., 2024; Xia et al., 2022). DAV also contains nucleotides, sialic acid, vitamins, and other small molecules that further contribute to its pharmacological properties. These compounds collectively enhance the therapeutic potential of DAV, making it suitable for dietary supplementation and functional food development (Ho et al., 2025; Sui et al., 2014).

In conclusion, the diverse bioactive composition of DAV, comprising structural proteins, growth factors, amino acids, minerals, lipids, and polysaccharides, provides the foundation for its wide-ranging biomedical and nutraceutical applications. Detailed characterization of these constituents is essential to understanding DAV’s mechanisms of action and optimizing its use as a functional material.

Optimal extraction techniques are critical to preserving the integrity of proteins, peptides, growth factors, and other bioactive molecules while minimizing degradation (Alegria-Aravena et al., 2025). Additionally, consistent processing and standardization are essential for ensuring reproducible biological activity, safety, and suitability for biomedical and nutraceutical applications (Ho et al., 2025). Extraction and processing strategies for deer antler velvet and their characteristics

Traditional and modern extraction methods

Traditionally, DAV has been processed using methods such as hot water decoction, freeze-drying, and ethanol extraction. Hot water extraction, the most common traditional method, effectively solubilizes proteins, peptides, and polysaccharides but may lead to partial denaturation of heat-sensitive growth factors (Alegria-Aravena et al., 2025; Hariyadi et al., 2019). Ethanol extraction can enrich lipid-soluble compounds and certain bioactive peptides but may reduce overall protein content (Alegria-Aravena et al., 2025).

Modern extraction approaches, including enzymatic hydrolysis, ultrasonic-assisted extraction, and supercritical fluid extraction, have been increasingly employed to improve yield, bioactivity, and reproducibility (Jang et al., 2020; Jin et al., 2015). Enzymatic hydrolysis using proteases enhances the release of bioactive peptides and growth factors while preserving their functional properties (Fadimu et al., 2022). Ultrasonic-assisted extraction accelerates the extraction process, improves efficiency, and reduces the risk of thermal degradation. Supercritical CO₂ extraction allows selective isolation of lipophilic bioactive without using harsh organic solvents, maintaining safety and bioactivity (Shen et al., 2023).

Impact of extraction method on bioactivity

The choice of extraction technique significantly affects the chemical composition and biological activity of DAV extracts. For example, growth factor levels, antioxidant activity, and anti-inflammatory properties have been shown to vary with extraction parameters such as temperature, solvent, pH, and extraction time (Alegria-Aravena et al., 2025). Enzymatically hydrolyzed DAV tends to exhibit higher immunomodulatory and osteogenic activity compared to conventional water or ethanol extracts due to improved bioavailability of peptides and growth factors. Similarly, ultrasonic-assisted extraction enhances the release of polysaccharides and amino acids, improving antioxidant potential (Orassay et al., 2024; Xia et al., 2022). Therefore, extraction methods must be carefully optimized to balance yield and preservation of bioactive functionality.

Processing techniques

Post-extraction processing, including drying, freeze-drying, and powder formulation, is essential for improving storage stability and shelf life of DAV products (Jang et al., 2020). Freeze-drying is particularly effective in maintaining the structural integrity of thermolabile proteins and growth factors (Alegria-Aravena et al., 2025). Powdered forms allow standardized dosing and ease of incorporation into functional foods, supplements, or pharmaceutical formulations. Storage conditions, including temperature and humidity, must also be controlled to prevent degradation of bioactive compounds and maintain biological efficacy (Wang et al., 2023).

Quality control and standardization challenges

Standardization of DAV remains a critical challenge due to the complexity of its composition, variability in antler source, age of the animal, and seasonal factors. Quantitative measurement of key bioactive ingredients, including collagen, peptides, IGF-1, and polysaccharides, is necessary to ensure consistency across batches. Techniques such as high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA), mass spectrometry, and amino acid analysis are commonly employed for chemical characterization. Despite advances, establishing universally accepted quality standards for DAV remains an ongoing challenge in both nutraceutical and biomedical research (Alegria-Aravena et al., 2025; Khong et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2024).

Safety and dosage considerations

Proper extraction and processing also influence the safety profile of DAV. The presence of residual solvents, microbial contamination, or improperly processed growth factors could pose health risks (Xia et al., 2022). Therefore, adherence to good manufacturing practices (GMP) and thorough safety evaluation are essential. Clinical studies and traditional usage have informed safe dosage ranges, but standardized dosing based on bioactive content is required for reproducible therapeutic outcomes (Xia et al., 2022).

Biomedical activities of deer antler velvet

Deer antler velvet (DAV) has been investigated across diverse experimental systems due to its complex composition, including proteins, growth factors, polysaccharides, peptides, and minerals (Orassay et al., 2024; Xia et al., 2022) (Fig. 3). Reported biological effects span immunomodulatory, antioxidant, neuroprotective, osteogenic, and performance-related domains. However, the strength of evidence varies considerably across indications, with most data derived from in vitro studies and animal models, and comparatively limited well-controlled human trials. The following subsections critically summarize the current evidence according to biological domain. Furthermore, reported findings across studies are often heterogeneous in terms of study design, dosage, outcome measures, and population characteristics, which complicates direct comparison and synthesis of results.

Anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects

Inflammation plays a central role in chronic diseases (Chavda et al., 2024). Several in vitro and animal studies have reported that DAV-derived peptides and polysaccharides reduce pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β, while increasing anti-inflammatory mediators including IL-10 (Liu et al., 2025; Xia et al., 2022). These effects have been observed primarily in macrophage cell lines and rodent models of induced inflammation (Liu et al. 2023a). Additionally, modulation of immune cell proliferation and activity, including T lymphocytes and macrophages, has been described (Rossetti et al., 2024). However, the majority of findings remain preclinical, and controlled human studies evaluating clinically meaningful endpoints are limited. Therefore, while DAV demonstrates immunomodulatory potential in experimental systems, its therapeutic relevance in inflammatory or autoimmune disorders requires further clinical validation.

Antioxidant and anti-aging properties

Oxidative stress contributes to aging and degenerative conditions (Chandimali et al., 2025). In vitro assays indicate that DAV extracts possess radical-scavenging activity and can reduce lipid peroxidation while increasing antioxidant enzymes such as superoxide dismutase (SOD) and glutathione peroxidase (GPx) (Orassay et al., 2024), (Jeon et al., 2024).

Animal studies further suggest attenuation of oxidative biomarkers following DAV administration. However, most antioxidant assessments rely on biochemical surrogate markers rather than long-term functional outcomes. Moreover, the relevance of in vitro radical-scavenging assays to in vivo physiological antioxidant capacity remains uncertain. Consequently, although antioxidant activity is consistently reported, its contribution to clinically meaningful anti-aging effects has not been conclusively established.

Neuroprotective and cognitive enhancing effects

DAV has been explored for potential neuroprotective properties, particularly in animal models. Bioactive components such as gangliosides, phospholipids, and growth factors including IGF-1 and EGF have been implicated in neuronal survival and synaptic plasticity (Liu et al., 2024). Experimental studies report improvements in learning and memory performance in rodents, as well as protection against oxidative or chemically induced neuronal damage (Du et al., 2022).

However, these findings are predominantly derived from model-specific experimental conditions. Direct evidence demonstrating cognitive enhancement in healthy human populations or therapeutic benefit in neurodegenerative disorders remains limited. Furthermore, the extent to which orally administered growth factors retain bioactivity following gastrointestinal digestion warrants further investigation.

Osteogenic and cartilage regenerative potential

DAV has traditionally been associated with bone and cartilage health, attributed to its collagen, glycosaminoglycan, and growth factor content, including IGF-1 and TGF-β (Wang et al., 2025). In vitro studies demonstrate stimulation of osteoblast proliferation and matrix synthesis, while animal models suggest enhanced bone formation and chondrogenesis (Yao et al., 2021).

These findings support biological plausibility; however, most data remain preclinical. Evidence from randomized human trials assessing bone mineral density, fracture risk reduction, or cartilage regeneration is scarce. Additionally, the bioavailability and systemic activity of intact growth factors after oral ingestion remain areas of ongoing debate. Therefore, while DAV shows osteogenic potential under experimental conditions, clinical translation requires more robust evaluation.

Wound healing and tissue repair

DAV-derived extracts have been reported to promote fibroblast proliferation, collagen synthesis, and angiogenesis in cell culture and animal wound models (Zhang et al., 2021a). Animal studies describe accelerated wound closure and improved tissue remodeling following DAV administration (Zhang et al. 2021b).

Although these findings align with the regenerative biology of antler tissue, most evidence is based on controlled experimental injury models. Clinical trials assessing efficacy in human wound management are limited, and standardized formulations have not been consistently applied. Consequently, while DAV exhibits regenerative potential in preclinical systems, further investigation is necessary to determine clinical applicability.

Anti-fatigue and physical performance enhancement

DAV has traditionally been used to enhance endurance and vitality. Experimental studies report improved exercise capacity, reduced fatigue markers, and enhanced recovery in animal models (Orassay et al., 2024). One clinical study has evaluated performance-related outcomes (Sleivert et al., 2003), involving a relatively small cohort (n = 38), which limits statistical power and generalizability. Moreover, reported outcomes across available human studies are not entirely consistent, further underscoring the need for cautious interpretation.

Proposed mechanisms include modulation of oxidative stress and mitochondrial function, as well as provision of amino acids and growth-associated factors. Nevertheless, heterogeneity in study design, dosage, and participant characteristics further constrains interpretation. Larger, well-controlled randomized trials are required before definitive performance-enhancing claims can be substantiated.

Endocrine and reproductive health effects

Preclinical studies suggest that DAV may influence reproductive parameters and hormone-related endpoints in animal models (Xia et al., 2022; Zang et al., 2016). Observed effects include modulation of gonadal function and fertility-related indices. These findings are consistent with the presence of bioactive peptides and growth-associated factors.

Nevertheless, human evidence remains limited, and long-term endocrine safety has not been comprehensively evaluated. Given the presence of growth factors such as IGF-1, careful consideration of hormonal balance, systemic exposure, and regulatory implications is warranted before broader therapeutic or nutraceutical positioning.

Nutraceutical and functional food applications

Deer antler velvet (DAV) has attracted attention within the nutraceutical and functional food sectors due to its complex biochemical composition and long-standing traditional use (Xia et al., 2022) (Fig. 4). Its proteinaceous components, peptides, amino acids, polysaccharides, minerals, and growth-associated factors provide a biochemical basis for potential health-related applications. However, translation from experimental findings to functional food products requires careful consideration of evidence strength, bioavailability, formulation stability, and regulatory constraints.

The proposed nutraceutical applications of DAV are largely based on its reported immunomodulatory, antioxidant, and musculoskeletal-supporting activities (Xia et al., 2022; Yoo et al., 2022). Most supporting evidence originates from in vitro assays and animal studies, where DAV-derived fractions have been shown to influence cytokine production, oxidative stress markers, and tissue repair pathways. While these findings provide biological plausibility, well-powered human intervention trials demonstrating clinically meaningful endpoints remain limited. Therefore, current functional claims should be interpreted within the context of predominantly preclinical evidence.

In functional food formulations, DAV is typically incorporated as powders, capsules, liquid extracts, or hydrolysates to enhance stability and consumer convenience (Sleivert et al., 2003). Processing techniques such as enzymatic hydrolysis, ultrasonic-assisted extraction, and freeze-drying are employed to improve solubility and potentially enrich specific bioactive fractions (Paing & Lee, 2025; Ponphaiboon et al., 2023). However, the impact of these processes on structural integrity, growth factor stability, and batch-to-batch reproducibility is not uniformly characterized. In particular, the presence of growth factors such as IGF-1, EGF, and TGF-β has been proposed as a functional advantage (Macečková et al., 2025); nevertheless, the extent to which intact growth factors survive gastrointestinal digestion and achieve systemic bioactivity following oral administration remains uncertain.

Preclinical models suggest potential benefits of DAV supplementation on bone density, joint function, and fatigue-related parameters (Jo et al., 2021). A limited number of human studies report improvements in physical endurance or quality-of-life indices (Gilbey & Perezgonzalez, 2012), although these studies often involve small sample sizes and heterogeneous outcome measures. Consequently, while preliminary data are encouraging, robust randomized controlled trials with standardized formulations are necessary to substantiate specific functional claims.

Bioavailability represents a critical determinant of translational feasibility. Proteins and growth factors are susceptible to enzymatic degradation in the gastrointestinal tract (Xia et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2021a). Strategies such as encapsulation, protective co-formulation, and controlled-release systems are being explored to enhance stability and absorption (Klojdova et al., 2023). Nonetheless, comprehensive pharmacokinetic data on intact DAV-derived growth factors or peptides in humans remain scarce, and further investigation is required to clarify systemic exposure and dose–response relationships.

Standardization is another key challenge. Variability in species, harvesting stage, processing conditions, and extraction methods may significantly influence compositional profiles (Wang et al., 2023). Establishing reproducible marker compounds—such as defined peptide fractions, collagen content, or quantified growth factor levels—would improve quality control and regulatory compliance. Moreover, regulatory frameworks differ across jurisdictions, and health claims for DAV-based products must align with evidence thresholds established by relevant authorities.

From a practical perspective, additional considerations include production cost, raw material sustainability, consumer perception of animal-derived bioactives, and ethical sourcing practices. While combination formulations with collagen, herbal extracts, or micronutrients have been explored (Park et al., 2025), evidence supporting synergistic efficacy remains limited. Therefore, positioning DAV within the functional food market requires balanced communication that reflects both its biological plausibility and the current limitations of clinical validation.

In summary, DAV demonstrates potential as a functional ingredient based on preclinical and preliminary clinical findings. However, its successful integration into nutraceutical and functional food applications depends on rigorous standardization, clarification of bioavailability and pharmacokinetics, well-designed human trials, and alignment with regulatory standards. A cautious and evidence-based approach will be essential to ensure sustainable and scientifically grounded development.

Molecular mechanisms underlying biological activities

The reported biomedical and nutraceutical effects of deer antler velvet (DAV) have been attributed to a complex interplay of growth-associated factors, peptides, polysaccharides, lipids, amino acids, and minerals (Orassay et al., 2024; Xia et al., 2022) (Fig. 5). However, mechanistic evidence varies substantially in depth and experimental validation. Most mechanistic insights derive from in vitro systems or animal models, and direct confirmation in human physiological contexts remains limited. Therefore, the molecular pathways discussed below should be interpreted within the framework of experimental plausibility rather than definitive clinical mechanisms.

One proposed mechanism involves the activation of receptor-mediated growth signaling pathways. Growth factors detected in DAV, including IGF-1, EGF, TGF-β, and FGFs, have been shown in experimental systems to engage canonical signaling cascades such as PI3K/Akt, MAPK/ERK, and JAK/STAT (Lai et al., 2007; Zhang et al., 2021a). In cell culture and animal models, activation of these pathways is associated with osteoblast proliferation, extracellular matrix synthesis, and cartilage formation. Similar signaling routes have been implicated in neuronal survival and plasticity (Ho et al., 2025). Nevertheless, it remains unclear to what extent intact growth factors within orally administered DAV reach systemic circulation in bioactive form. Consequently, some observed biological effects may reflect smaller peptide fragments or indirect signaling modulation rather than direct systemic growth factor activity.

DAV has also been reported to influence redox-sensitive molecular pathways (Park et al., 2025). In vitro and animal studies indicate upregulation of endogenous antioxidant enzymes, including SOD, catalase, and GPx, alongside reductions in ROS generation (Xia et al., 2022). These effects have been linked to modulation of transcription factors such as Nrf2 and NF-κB (Kostenko et al., 2023). While these pathways are central regulators of oxidative stress and inflammation, they are also broadly responsive to diverse bioactive compounds. Thus, although DAV appears capable of influencing redox homeostasis in experimental systems, the specificity and magnitude of these effects relative to other protein-rich animal-derived materials require further comparative evaluation.

Immunomodulatory mechanisms attributed to DAV involve interactions between polysaccharides, peptides, and immune cell signaling pathways (Baek et al., 2023; Thum et al., 2024). Experimental findings suggest suppression of pro-inflammatory mediators (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6) and enhancement of anti-inflammatory cytokines (IL-10), potentially through NF-κB inhibition and MAPK pathway modulation (Chang et al., 2018; He et al., 2022). However, these observations are predominantly derived from cell-based assays or induced inflammation models. Whether similar regulatory effects occur under physiological conditions in humans remains insufficiently characterized.

Lipids and gangliosides present in DAV have been associated with neurobiological effects (Liu et al., 2024). Gangliosides are known modulators of membrane structure and neurotrophic signaling (Sipione et al., 2020), and experimental systems suggest potential enhancement of neurite outgrowth and synaptic plasticity. Yet, the extent to which these components are absorbed intact following oral administration, and whether they achieve biologically relevant concentrations in neural tissue, remains to be clarified.

At the tissue level, DAV-associated regenerative effects have been linked to extracellular matrix remodeling, angiogenesis, and fibroblast activation (Liu et al. 2023b), (Zhang et al., 2021a). These processes are consistent with the intrinsic regenerative biology of antler tissue. Nonetheless, extrapolation from intrinsic antler growth physiology to therapeutic efficacy in humans requires caution, as systemic delivery conditions differ substantially from localized antler development.

Overall, the molecular mechanisms attributed to DAV converge on established signaling pathways governing growth, redox balance, and immune regulation. While experimental data support mechanistic plausibility, definitive causal pathways in human applications remain incompletely defined. Future studies incorporating pharmacokinetic profiling, dose–response analysis, and controlled clinical investigation will be essential to delineate which molecular interactions are biologically relevant in translational contexts. Safety, toxicology, and regulatory aspects

The safety and regulatory profile of deer antler velvet (DAV) is a critical consideration for its application in nutraceutical, functional food, and biomedical contexts. Although DAV has been consumed historically in traditional medicinal systems, historical usage alone does not substitute for systematic toxicological and regulatory evaluation (Chen et al., 2015). Variability in species, harvesting stage, processing conditions, and extraction methods may substantially influence the concentration of bioactive constituents, thereby affecting safety and reproducibility (Xia et al., 2022).

Toxicological assessments in animal models generally report low acute and subchronic toxicity following oral administration of DAV extracts. Rodent studies have shown minimal adverse effects on liver and kidney function, hematological parameters, and general behavior, even at relatively high doses (Chilakala et al., 2023; Lee et al., 2024). However, these data are primarily derived from controlled experimental conditions with specific preparations and limited duration. Comprehensive long-term studies, including reproductive toxicity, carcinogenicity, and large-scale human safety trials, remain limited. Therefore, while available preclinical data are reassuring, extrapolation to chronic human exposure requires caution.

Particular attention has been directed toward growth-associated factors such as insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1), given their established roles in cellular proliferation and endocrine regulation. Although conventional DAV supplementation has not been conclusively associated with oncogenic or endocrine adverse outcomes in humans, theoretical concerns arise from the presence of growth-promoting molecules. The systemic bioavailability of intact IGF-1 following oral ingestion remains uncertain, and the extent to which physiologically relevant concentrations are achieved in circulation has not been fully characterized. This uncertainty complicates risk–benefit interpretation and underscores the importance of standardized dosing and compositional monitoring (Wang et al., 2023). In addition, the presence of growth-associated factors has raised considerations in athletic contexts regarding compliance with anti-doping regulations, although current evidence does not clearly demonstrate supraphysiological systemic effects from oral consumption.

Allergenicity represents another potential safety consideration. As DAV contains proteinaceous components, hypersensitivity reactions are biologically plausible, particularly in individuals sensitive to animal-derived proteins. Documented severe allergic responses appear to be uncommon (Gunal-Koroglu et al., 2025); however, the absence of large-scale epidemiological surveillance limits precise risk estimation. Appropriate labeling, consumer guidance, and post-market monitoring are therefore advisable. Processing strategies such as enzymatic hydrolysis and thermal treatment may reduce allergenic potential while maintaining bioactivity (Wojcik et al., 2025), although standardized validation of allergen reduction is not consistently reported.

From a regulatory standpoint, the classification and oversight of DAV vary internationally. In East Asian countries, particularly China and Korea, DAV is incorporated into traditional medicinal systems and functional products (Wu et al., 2013). In contrast, in the United States and the European Union, DAV-based products are typically regulated as dietary supplements or natural health products, requiring adherence to good manufacturing practices (GMP), quality control standards, and substantiation of safety (Krzisnik et al., 2024). Health claims are generally restricted to structure–function statements unless supported by rigorous clinical evidence. Regulatory acceptance is further complicated by compositional variability, which challenges dose standardization and reproducibility (Du et al., 2022). Standardization therefore represents a central regulatory bottleneck. Quantification of defined marker compounds, such as IGF-1, collagen, or characterized peptide fractions, is essential to ensure consistent bioactive content and safety across batches. Analytical approaches including high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA), and mass spectrometry are commonly employed for compositional profiling and quality assurance (Krzisnik et al., 2024; Shahidi & Samarasinghe, 2025). However, internationally harmonized reference standards remain underdeveloped, which may limit global regulatory alignment. Ethical considerations, including sustainable harvesting practices and animal welfare, are also increasingly relevant for regulatory review and consumer acceptance.

Overall, available experimental data suggest that appropriately processed DAV preparations exhibit an acceptable short- to medium-term safety profile under controlled conditions. Nevertheless, limitations in long-term human safety data, pharmacokinetic characterization of growth-associated components, endocrine risk assessment, and global standardization highlight areas requiring further investigation. A precautionary, evidence-based framework—emphasizing standardized composition, transparent labeling, and regulatory compliance—will be essential for the responsible integration of DAV into functional food and nutraceutical applications. Although growth-associated factors such as IGF-1 have been detected in DAV, their physiological relevance following oral consumption remains uncertain, as many peptide-based growth factors are likely to undergo degradation during digestion. Nevertheless, the potential endocrine implications associated with long-term intake of concentrated DAV preparations warrant further investigation. Future studies addressing pharmacokinetics and systemic exposure will be important for clarifying the safety profile of DAV-derived products.

Challenges and future perspectives

Despite increasing scientific interest in deer antler velvet (DAV), several structural challenges continue to limit its standardized clinical translation and broader functional food integration. Addressing these limitations will require coordinated efforts across analytical, mechanistic, clinical, and regulatory domains.

First, compositional heterogeneity remains a fundamental obstacle. Variability associated with species, age, antler growth stage, diet, seasonal factors, and processing conditions contributes to inconsistent bioactive profiles across preparations. This variability complicates reproducibility, dose standardization, and cross-study comparison. The complex matrix of proteins, growth-associated factors, polysaccharides, and small molecules further challenges precise quantification. Future research should prioritize the establishment of defined marker compounds or compositional fingerprints that correlate with biological activity, supported by validated analytical methodologies and bioassay-guided standardization strategies.

Second, clinical validation remains insufficient relative to the breadth of reported preclinical effects. While experimental models suggest immunomodulatory, regenerative, antioxidant, and performance-related activities, large-scale randomized controlled trials evaluating clinically meaningful endpoints are limited. Clear differentiation between exploratory biomarkers and validated therapeutic outcomes is necessary. Development of standardized dosing regimens based on quantified bioactive content, alongside pharmacokinetic and dose–response characterization, will be essential to translate mechanistic plausibility into evidence-based human application.

Third, bioavailability and systemic exposure of key bioactive components require clarification. Growth-associated molecules such as IGF-1 have attracted considerable attention; however, the extent to which intact factors survive gastrointestinal digestion and exert systemic biological effects remain uncertain. Detailed pharmacokinetic profiling, metabolite identification, and mechanistic validation studies are needed to distinguish direct molecular activity from indirect or peptide-mediated effects. Without such data, mechanistic interpretation and safety evaluation remain incomplete.

Fourth, regulatory harmonization and ethical sourcing represent critical translational considerations. Differences in regulatory classification across jurisdictions, combined with limited internationally harmonized quality standards, create barriers to global market integration. Establishing reproducible compositional benchmarks, transparent labeling frameworks, and evidence-based claim substantiation will support regulatory alignment. Additionally, sustainable harvesting practices and animal welfare considerations are increasingly important for long-term industry viability and public acceptance.

Technological innovations—including optimized extraction protocols, stabilization approaches, and controlled-release delivery systems—may enhance compositional consistency and functional performance. However, such advancements should be accompanied by rigorous comparative evaluation rather than assumption of improved efficacy. Similarly, combination formulations with other bioactive materials require empirical validation before claims of synergistic benefit can be substantiated.

In summary, the future development of DAV as a biomedical and nutraceutical resource will depend not on expanding the breadth of reported effects, but on strengthening depth of validation. Establishing standardized quality markers, clarifying pharmacokinetics and bioavailability, conducting well-powered clinical trials, and aligning regulatory frameworks represent priority research directions. A structured, evidence-based approach will be essential to define the realistic scope of DAV applications and to bridge experimental findings with responsible translational practice.

Conclusion

Deer antler velvet (DAV) is a biologically complex animal-derived material that has been investigated for diverse biomedical and nutraceutical applications. Experimental studies support mechanistic plausibility for immunomodulatory, antioxidant, regenerative, neuroprotective, and musculoskeletal-related effects. However, the majority of available evidence remains preclinical, and human data are comparatively limited in scale and consistency. Thus, while DAV demonstrates biological activity across multiple experimental models, its clinical significance requires further substantiation.

Mechanistically, reported effects converge on established signaling pathways governing growth regulation, redox balance, immune modulation, and tissue remodeling. Nevertheless, the extent to which orally administered DAV components—particularly growth-associated factors—achieve systemic bioactivity in humans remains incompletely defined. Clarifying pharmacokinetic profiles and dose–response relationships will be essential for translating experimental findings into evidence-based applications.

Future advancement of DAV research should prioritize four key areas: (1) establishment of standardized compositional markers linked to biological activity; (2) rigorous pharmacokinetic and bioavailability assessment of key bioactive components; (3) well-powered, controlled clinical trials evaluating clinically meaningful endpoints; and (4) regulatory harmonization supported by reproducible quality control frameworks and ethical sourcing practices.

Rather than expanding the range of proposed indications, strengthening the depth of mechanistic validation and clinical evidence will determine the realistic scope of DAV integration into functional foods, dietary supplements, and regenerative biomedical strategies. A systematic, evidence-driven approach will be critical to define its role within modern health science.

Remote preventive and digital health promotion services in primary and community healthcare in Sub-Saharan Africa: a scoping review protocol

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPrimary healthcare (PHC) systems in Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA) continue to prioritise reactive, curative models of care, despite the region’s pressing need for health-promotive and preventative strategies. Instead of disrupting the existing curative model, the expansion of digital health often automates old, treatment-oriented systems with a limited focus on creating new tools for preventive and health-promoting functions. While there is no shortage of reviews on digital health and telemedicine in SSA, these predominantly focus on curative care and service delivery within the walls of healthcare facilities. An understanding of how remotely provided preventive and health-promoting services can be implemented by PHC facilities to strengthen the PHC overall is currently lacking. Addressing this gap is critical for guiding research, policy and practice towards designing more preventive and proactive PHC service models.Methods and analysisThis protocol details a scoping review that will identify and establish what is currently known about the implementation and feasibility of remote preventive and digital health-promoting services as potential levers to strengthen PHC in SSA. The review will consolidate evidence on digital health initiatives for prevention and identify key opportunities, barriers and gaps in the current knowledge. Results will be analysed using quantitative summaries and qualitative thematic analysis.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval is not required as this study uses publicly available literature. The findings could guide policymakers, programme implementers and researchers in reorienting PHCs towards more preventive approaches. The findings of the proposed scoping review will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed journal.RegistrationThe protocol is registered with the Open Science Framework (OSF: https://osf.io/8whkc/) to comply with methodological transparency and accountability.

Introduction

Primary healthcare (PHC) is widely seen as the foundation of a resilient, equitable and accessible public healthcare system. Since the Alma-Ata Declaration, the goal has been to have an effective PHC that can prevent illness, promote health and provide essential services in proximity to communities.1 However, in Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA), PHC systems primarily emphasise reactive and curative approaches. Resources are predominantly allocated toward the treatment of acute illnesses rather than focusing on disease prevention and promoting long-term health.2 3 This approach hampers the system’s ability to tackle the growing challenge of non-communicable diseases while still dealing with ongoing infectious diseases, leading to high rates of preventable illness and death.4 5

Evidence shows that prevention and health promotion can significantly reduce the burden of disease, lower costs and improve health equity.611 Regrettably, initiatives aimed at promoting health and preventing diseases in SSA are frequently delayed. This is primarily due to insufficient infrastructure, limited financial resources, a shortage of trained personnel and a lack of organisation within the health system.41214 This results in populations pursuing healthcare only after reaching advanced stages of illness, which perpetuates a cycle of costly reactive interventions.

Meanwhile, digital health solutions—including telemedicine, mobile health (mHealth), SMS-based programmes, AI-powered decision tools and remote monitoring systems—are rapidly proliferating in SSA.15 16 Digital technologies are increasingly recognised as valuable assets for enhancing health systems. They provide opportunities to expand services beyond conventional facility limits, effectively bringing healthcare closer to households and communities.1719 For example, mobile phone-based reminders have improved treatment adherence,20 while teleconsultations and remote monitoring platforms have enabled greater reach in rural areas.21 22 In the last 20 years, digital health initiatives in SSA have predominantly focused on curative service models. These efforts primarily emphasise the provision of treatments, medication adherence and acute consultations, while placing less emphasis on proactive prevention and public health promotion.3 23 Although there is no shortage of reviews on digital health and telemedicine in SSA,3 15 16 these primarily emphasise curative interventions, clinical management or broad service delivery within the perimeter of healthcare facilities. Few have comprehensively examined how digital tools can enable preventive and health-promoting services delivered remotely or away from healthcare facilities—such as health education via SMS and online portals, AI-driven disease-risk assessments or lifestyle modification support through mobile applications. Even fewer studies have specifically examined the feasibility and potential implementation of such interventions within SSA’s PHC systems.

We contend that a scoping review is the most appropriate research design for a project seeking to identify and establish what is known about the use of remote preventive and digital health-promoting services within SSA’s PHC. First, the existing literature on this topic is limited and fragmented, such that a full systematic review is unsuitable. Second, the available evidence spans diverse methodologies (qualitative, descriptive, pilot, mixed methods), which do not readily lend themselves to a meta-analysis. Third, the aim of this study is exploratory rather than confirmatory, seeking to map evidence and identify gaps rather than answer a tightly focused question.24 Fourth, scoping reviews are particularly effective for identifying key concepts and informing future research agendas in areas that have received limited attention. This aligns with the foundational framework for scoping reviews established by Arksey and O'Malley.

Reviews of digital health initiatives in low- and middle-income countries have typically identified barriers including inadequate infrastructure, poor interoperability and limited digital literacy.3 25 However, none to date have comprehensively mapped the evidence specific to remote preventive and health promotional services. Addressing this gap is critical for policymakers, implementers and researchers to understand whether, how and under what conditions such interventions can be deployed and scaled in SSA. Therefore, the purpose of this protocol is to provide a detailed road map, explaining how the proposed scoping review will identify and establish what is currently known about the implementation and feasibility of remote preventive and digital health-promoting services as strategies to strengthen PHC in SSA. Through this protocol, we explain how the review will map the extent, nature and gaps in the literature and:

Methodology

The proposed review will use the Arksey & O’Malley framework,26 with methodological improvements from the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI)27 and will present findings in accordance with Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) standards.28 The protocol is registered with the Open Science Framework (OSF: https://osf.io/8whkc/) to comply with methodological transparency and accountability.29

The remainder of this methodology section will detail the research question, the processes for identifying relevant studies, the study selection mechanism (including inclusion and exclusion criteria), quality assessment, methods for data charting and the approach for collating, summarising and reporting the evidence. A copy of the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) checklist can be found in (online supplemental material table S1). The protocol will conclude with a short discussion on the implications of the proposed study.

Research question

As outlined in the background, PHC in SSA faces an urgent need to shift from reactive, curative models of care toward increasingly preventive and health-promoting approaches. Despite the significant increase in mobile device access and advancements in digital health, most current initiatives—both digital and traditional—still focus on episodic treatment rather than emphasising proactive, promotive and preventive care. This creates a critical gap in evidence on whether digital health interventions (DHIs) could enable integrated remote delivery of preventive and health-promotion services to strengthen PHC in SSA. Publication quality assessment

In scoping reviews, the purpose of quality assessment is not to exclude studies but to evaluate the overall credibility, methodological robustness and transparency of the existing evidence base.24 Unlike systematic reviews that focus on effect estimation, scoping reviews aim to map the evidence, describe its characteristics, and identify conceptual or methodological gaps. Conducting a structured quality assessment, therefore, enhances the interpretive value of the synthesis by clarifying the strengths, limitations, and reliability of the literature included.33

To ensure consistency and methodological rigour, the proposed review will employ the JBI Critical Appraisal Tools. These tools are specifically designed for evaluating diverse study designs commonly encountered in health systems research, including quantitative, qualitative and mixed-methods studies. The tools are widely recognised for their clarity, adaptability and applicability to scoping reviews.34

Following full-text review and agreement on the final list of included studies, all publications on the list will be categorised by study design (eg, qualitative, economic evaluations, cross-sectional studies, Randomized Controlled Trials, systematic reviews, policy reports or grey literature) first. Then, for each category, the corresponding JBI checklist will be downloaded and used to appraise individual studies. Each checklist comprises standardised criteria that evaluate aspects such as research clarity, data collection methods, analytical rigour and reporting transparency.

Before initiating the quality assessment, all three reviewers (BR, CM and AB) will convene to discuss and agree on the JBI criteria and scoring methodology. Following this, each reviewer will independently evaluate all included studies in their entirety. Once these independent assessments are concluded, the ratings will be compared. Articles that receive identical scores from all three reviewers will maintain the same consensus rating, while those with varying assessments will be discussed collectively until a final consensus is achieved. If necessary, additional clarifications will be sought by re-reading the full text to ensure objectivity and accuracy.

Quality appraisal outcomes will be summarised alongside the primary data to offer context for interpreting the evidence base. Each study included in the review will receive a consensus quality rating (high, moderate or low) based on the final agreement among reviewers employing JBI tools. These ratings will be presented in summary tables and, where applicable, linked to thematic findings to illustrate how study quality may have influenced reported outcomes or identified evidence gaps. This structured and collaborative approach will enhance the reliability and transparency of the review by minimising subjective bias and ensuring that the overall quality profile of the included literature is clearly articulated and systematically reported.

Collating, summarising and reporting the results

The collating, summarising and reporting stage will synthesise the evidence extracted during data charting to comprehensively address the main research question: What evidence exists on the use of DHIs to provide remote preventive and health promotion services within SSA’s PHC systems?

Data from the approved charting form (table 3) will first be collated in Microsoft Excel and iteratively updated and cross-verified for completeness and accuracy. Quantitative synthesis will be conducted to describe the extent and nature of the evidence base, identifying publication trends over time, geographic distribution across SSA, research designs used and the types of DHIs described or implemented. The findings will be displayed in tables and graphical summaries to visualise how evidence is distributed across SSA and where it is concentrated or lacking.

To respond to the question on the implementation and consideration of remote preventive and digital health promotion services, a qualitative thematic synthesis will be conducted. This synthesis will organise the available literature according to primary intervention themes, modes of delivery, factors that hinder or support adoption and documented outcomes and impacts. The analysis will also seek to identify contextual elements—such as system-level constraints, workforce competencies, and prevailing sociotechnical conditions—that shaped the success or failure of these interventions.35 In addition, where the data permit, the synthesis will be extended to broader analytical dimensions relevant to the SSA context, including implementation considerations (eg, scalability, sustainability and integration into existing clinical workflows), contextual influences (such as rural–urban disparities, policy environments and infrastructure constraints including severely limited power supply capacity and poor internet connectivity) and equity- or justice-related aspects (including access, digital inclusion and differential impacts across population groups). While the review does not seek to conduct a normative or evaluative analysis of these dimensions, their identification will help contextualise the evidence and highlight areas requiring deeper investigation for future research.

Adopting this systematic analytical framework will yield a holistic perspective on the current implementation landscape of DHIs aimed at delivering remote preventive and health promotion services across SSA. By merging quantitative overviews with qualitative thematic insights, the review will identify evidence deficiencies, shed light on effective models where they exist and offer strategic insights to bolster PHC in SSA via facility-driven, digitally enabled preventive and health promotion services.

Discussion and implications

This protocol has presented a focused, methodologically rigorous plan to map existing evidence on remote, digitally enabled preventive and health-promotive services within SSA’s PHC systems. It responds to an urgent regional need to reorient PHC systems from predominantly curative models toward proactive, preventive, and health promotive care.36 The increasing burden of chronic diseases, along with limited human and financial resources, highlights the necessity for cost-effective and scalable preventive strategies.37 Simultaneously, the increasing prevalence of mobile devices throughout SSA presents a unique opportunity to broaden the reach of health promotion and preventive services beyond conventional healthcare environments, allowing them to be delivered digitally directly in homes, community spaces, and private settings.38

The proposed scoping review is both timely and strategically significant. By synthesising existing knowledge, identifying evidence gaps and analysing contextual barriers and facilitators, the review aims to fill a critical knowledge gap and provide actionable insights to guide innovation, policy development and future research priorities. It will also document current initiatives and clarify how DHIs can transform PHC systems in SSA into more preventive and proactive models of care. A scoping review design is the most suitable methodological approach for this purpose. Given the broad conceptual framework, the variety of study designs and the anticipated fragmentation in both peer-reviewed and grey literature, a comprehensive systematic review or meta-analysis would not be appropriate for this topic.24 Using the Arksey and O'Malley framework,26 supplemented by JBI refinements27 and adhering to PRISMA-ScR standards,28 this study is designed to ensure transparency and replicability. Each methodological decision—from fixed keyword strategy and country selection to the use of standardised inclusion criteria39—was designed to balance scientific rigour with practical feasibility.

The strengths of the methodology are noteworthy. Collaborating with specialist librarians will enhance the accuracy and thoroughness of the search process while minimising selection bias. The implementation of fixed search terms and a predetermined list of countries will ensure that the process can be replicated across different databases.

Additionally, the dual independent screening of full texts, complemented by a well-defined arbitration procedure, will enhance objectivity and transparency. Moreover, the application of the JBI critical appraisal tools will allow for structural assessment of methodological quality across diverse study designs—adding interpretive depth without excluding studies unnecessarily.35 Collectively, these features will underpin the review’s credibility, reliability and policy relevance.

Nonetheless, the study anticipates several limitations. The body of literature addressing remote preventive and digital health promotion in SSA is likely to be limited, unevenly distributed and methodologically diverse, which may restrict generalisability. Language restrictions to English and French may omit relevant evidence published in Portuguese or local African languages. In addition, variability in outcome reporting may complicate synthesis efforts. These constraints are mitigated through strategies such as the inclusion of grey literature, extensive database and network searches, as well as transparent documentation of all screening decisions.

Expected outputs from the review include a detailed evidence map illustrating where, how and to what extent DHIs are being applied for preventive and health promotional purposes within PHC. Quantitative summaries will outline the geographic and methodological scope of existing studies, while thematic synthesis will illuminate recurring barriers, enablers and implementation mechanisms. Together, these findings will equip policymakers and implementers with concrete evidence regarding which remote preventive functions have been tested, with which technologies, and what outcomes and challenges were observed.

Therefore, the potential impact of the review will extend beyond academic synthesis. By consolidating fragmented evidence, it will provide strategic direction for pilot interventions, funding proposals and future programme design.

The results will inform the next phases of the overarching research programme—particularly the co-design and participatory action research studies planned under Phase two, to ensure that the framework for remote preventive and health-promotion functions is grounded in empirical realities rather than theoretical abstraction. In this sense, the review will establish the foundational evidence required to develop a feasible, context-sensitive model for strengthening PHC systems in SSA.

From an ethical and practical standpoint, the study supports health systems equity by exploring how digital innovations can serve underserved populations and mitigate access barriers. It will also help identify systemic conditions—such as governance structures, digital infrastructure and workforce training needs—that must be addressed to integrate remote preventive functions into PHC sustainably.

In summary, this protocol presents a rigorous and transparent methodology for investigating an understudied yet vital paradigm shift within PHC. It balances methodological discipline with pragmatic adaptability to ensure that the resulting insights are both trustworthy and applicable.

If implemented as intended, the review will produce a robust and replicable evidence synthesis capable of guiding future research, shaping national digital health policies and supporting the transition of SSA’s PHC systems toward prevention-focused, proactive care. Through its well-thought-out design, librarian collaboration, structured screening, quality appraisal and commitment to open-science principles, the study is well-positioned to yield high-impact insights that can propel evidence-informed innovation throughout the region.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical approval is not required for this study as it involves an analysis of published and publicly available literature without human participants. The findings of this scoping review will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed journal, appropriate conference presentations and relevant stakeholder engagement meetings.

Preoperative pulmonary valve annulus diameter z score as a predictor of pulmonary regurgitation after tetralogy of Fallot repair: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Pribadi Wiranda Busro, Ahmad Adityawarman, Suprayitno Wardoyo, Retno Wibawanti, Damba Dwisepto Aulia Sakti, Olfi Lelya, Yovi Kurniawati, Oktavia Lilyasari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTetralogy of Fallot (ToF) is the most common cause of cyanotic congenital heart disease and pulmonary regurgitation (PR) remains its most frequent postoperative complication. The preoperative z score of the pulmonary valve annulus (PVA) has been associated with an increased risk of PR after ToF repair; however, the optimal cut-off value varies among studies and has not been investigated in the Indonesian population. This study aimed to determine the predictive value of the preoperative MSCT-derived PVA diameter z score for early PR after ToF repair.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study was conducted using secondary data from pediatric patients who underwent ToF repair at the National Cardiac Center Harapan Kita between January 2023 and December 2024. The preoperative PVA diameter was measured using cardiac multislice computed tomography (MSCT). Early PR was assessed via echocardiography within 45 days post-operatively. Multivariable logistic regression was performed and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analyses were performed.ResultsA total of 101 subjects were analysed. Both maximal and minimal diameters of the PVA z scores were associated with moderate or greater PR in the univariate analysis (p = 0.001 and p < 0.001, respectively). However, in multivariate analysis, only transannular patch (TAP) remained an independent predictor (p < 0.001), while PVA z scores were no longer statistically significant. ROC analysis revealed that the minimal diameter of the PVA z score (area under the curve [AUC] 0.701; cut-off − 2.5) demonstrated moderate discriminatory ability, with high specificity (90.6%) but limited sensitivity (42%).ConclusionPreoperative PVA z score is associated with early PR but does not independently predict the outcome after adjustment, as its effect is largely mediated by surgical strategy, particularly the use of TAP. While the proposed cut-off of − 2.5 demonstrates high specificity, its low sensitivity limits its utility as a screening tool. PVA z score should therefore be interpreted alongside intraoperative factors rather than used as a standalone predictor of postoperative PR, as it reflects a preoperative anatomical parameter that influences surgical decision-making.

Background

Tetralogy of Fallot (ToF) is the most common form of cyanotic congenital heart disease, accounting for approximately 3–5% of all congenital heart diseases with an incidence of 0.28–0.34 per 1000 live births [1]. The survival rate for patients who undergo surgical repair of ToF is as high as 95% at the age of 40 years [2]. However, pulmonary regurgitation (PR) remains the most frequent postoperative complication, particularly in the mid- to long-term. Chronic PR can lead to right ventricular volume overload, ventricular dysfunction, arrhythmias, and even sudden cardiac death, thereby affecting long-term prognosis [3, 4]. Moderate-to-severe PR in the early postoperative period has been shown to be a risk factor for the development of midterm PR; therefore, early detection is important [5].

In some ToF patients, right ventricular outflow tract (RVOT) stenosis can be released optimally with transannular patch (TAP) insertion. While this approach effectively alleviates RVOT obstruction, it is widely recognized as the primary determinant of postoperative PR following ToF repair [6]. TAP disrupts pulmonary valve competence and often results in significant PR.7 The size of the pulmonary valve annulus (PVA) is a key factor in determining the need for TAP, with smaller annular dimensions – reflected by lower PVA z scores – being associated with an increased likelihood of TAP use and subsequent PR [5, 8]. Therefore, the decision to perform TAP represents a critical surgical factor that may confound or mediate the relationship between preoperative PVA size and postoperative outcomes.

The PVA z score, which is a calculation used to normalize the PVA size to the patient’s body surface area, is a more informative calculation than the absolute measurement alone. Most previous studies relied on echocardiographic measurements assuming a circular annulus, potentially underestimating annular asymmetry [9]. Previous studies evaluating PVA size in ToF have predominantly relied on echocardiographic measurements or intraoperative assessment, with limited data on MSCT-derived annular quantification in predicting early or long-term postoperative PR [5, 8, 10, 11]. This study aimed to evaluate the predictive value of preoperative MSCT-derived PVA diameter z-score for early PR after ToF repair in our population. Identifying an optimal cut-off may assist in preoperative risk stratification, guide surgical planning, and improve postoperative surveillance strategies.

Patients and methods

Study design and definitions

This retrospective cohort study enrolled patients who underwent ToF repair surgery from January 2023-December 2024 at the National Cardiac Center Harapan Kita. Ethical approval was obtained from the Committee on Institutional Review Board/Health Research Ethics of National Cardiac Center Harapan Kita Hospital on October 6, 2025 under approval number DP.04.03/KEP164/EC102/2025. As this study involved retrospective analysis of existing medical records, no data extraction or analysis was performed prior to obtaining ethical approval. The study was conducted in accordance with institutional and national ethical guidelines governing retrospective research. The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the use of anonymized data and the absence of direct patient contact.

A total of 101 patients were included in this study. Subjects were patients < 18 years of age with a diagnosis of ToF. Patients with more complex lesions such as atrioventricular septal defects, anomalous pulmonary vein drainage, absence of the pulmonary valve, pulmonary atresia, and coronary artery anomalies were excluded. We also excluded patients who had undergone systemic to pulmonary artery shunt surgery or palliative procedures such as modified BTT shunt, central shunt, PDA stenting, and RVOT stenting before ToF repair surgery. Research data, including preoperative MSCT and postoperative echocardiography data, were obtained from patient medical records. The McGoon ratio, defined as the sum of the diameters of the right and left pulmonary arteries divided by the diameter of the descending aorta, was included as an indicator of pulmonary artery development and its potential influence on surgical strategy and postoperative outcomes [12].

The PVA diameter was measured retrospectively from preoperative cardiac MSCT recordings. Cardiac MSCT was performed using a standardized contrast-enhanced protocol with intravenous contrast administered at a dose 1 ml/kg body weight. Image acquisition included both arterial and venous phases. Images were reconstructed with a slice thickness of 0.6 mm. To ensure accurate PVA measurement, multiplanar reformation (MPR) was performed at the annular level to obtain the most ideal circular image, with the measurement plane carefully adjusted to avoid obliquity and to capture the true annular geometry, and then the minimum and maximum diameters were measured (Fig. 1) [9]. The obtained minimum and maximum diameters were then entered into the z score formula. Z scores for both minimal and maximal annular diameters were calculated using separate normative datasets as described by Soszyn et al. As these measurements are derived from distinct reference distributions, the resulting z scores may not be directly comparable, and the maximal diameter z score may appear more negative than the minimal diameter z score despite a larger absolute value [9]. Body surface area (BSA), which is incorporated into the z score calculation, was determined using the Haycock formula.

The PR assessment was based on transthoracic echocardiography performed during the outpatient visit within 45 days after the operation via a parasternal short-axis view. Echocardiography recordings were considered adequate if they provided a sufficient field of view and good image quality. Echocardiographic image adequacy and PR grading were primarily assessed by a single pediatric cardiologist. A second pediatric cardiologist independently reviewed a subset of studies to assess interobserver agreement. These reviewers were different from those involved in MSCT measurements. Only patients with available postoperative echocardiographic assessment within 45 days were included in the analysis; therefore, patients who did not survive to undergo echocardiographic evaluation were not included.

The decision to perform TAP repair was made intraoperatively by the operating surgeon based on the anatomical characteristics of the RVOT and the PVA. Valve-sparing repair was attempted whenever feasible. TAP was performed when significant RVOT stenosis persisted despite attempts to preserve the pulmonary valve, particularly when the RV systolic pressure exceeded 75% of the systemic systolic pressure.

For analysis, PR was dichotomized as mild or less versus moderate or greater, based on standard echocardiographic grading criteria. The criteria for mild PR are the presence of diastolic reversal flow in the main PA, a small and narrow jet (< 10 mm), and a PR/RVOT jet width ratio of < 0.2. The criteria for moderate PR are diastolic reversal flow at the pulmonary artery (PA) bifurcation, an intermediate jet size, and a PR/RVOT jet width ratio of 0.2–0.5. The criteria for severe PR are diastolic reversal flow in the PA branches, a wide jet size on color Doppler with varying penetration depths, a PR/RVOT jet width ratio of > 0.5, dense jet density and contour with early diastolic flow termination, and a pressure half time (PHT) of < 100 ms [13, 14, 15].

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed via SPSS for Windows (version 27, 2020). Patients were divided into two groups on the basis of postoperative PR: those with mild or less PR and those with moderate or greater PR. Quantitative variables are displayed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range). Categorical data are displayed as proportions (%). Bivariate analysis of the relationships between quantitative variables and categorical variables was performed via the unpaired t test or Mann-Whitney test. The chi-square or Fisher’s exact test was used to analyse the relationships between categorical variables of groups. Multivariate analysis was performed using binary logistic regression. Results with a p-value < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. The cut-off value of the PVA z score for predicting PR was obtained via a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve.

Results

A total of 101 patients who met the criteria were included in the study. The median age at surgery was 38 months (IQR 18–74.5), with a a median of body weight of 11.16 (IQR 7.85–16.15), height of 88 (IQR 75–106), and BSA of 0.54 m2 (IQR 0.41–0.68). Other patient characteristics are shown in Table 1. The average z scores of the preoperative PVA maximum and minimum diameters were − 2.22 ± 1.23 and − 1.88 ± 1.24, respectively. TAP insertion was performed in the majority of patients (64.4%). An echocardiography study after ToF repair surgery revealed that moderate or greater PR occurred in 68.3% of patients.

The median time to postoperative echocardiography assessment was 20 days (IQR 15–26 days). There was no significant difference in the timing of echocardiography between patients with moderate or greater PR and mild or less PR (median 19 [15–28] vs. 20 [16.5–25] days, p = 0.867).

PVA diameters were independently measured by two observers via MSCT images and interobserver reliability was assessed via the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). Excellent agreement was observed for the maximum PVA diameter (ICC = 0.939; 95% CI: 0.881–0.969; p < 0.001)) and good agreement was observed for the minimum PVA diameter (ICC = 0.898; 95% CI: 0.808–0.947; p < 0.001). Interobserver agreement for postoperative PR assessment via echocardiography was good (Kappa = 0.727; p = 0.001), whereas intraobserver agreement was perfect (Kappa = 1.0; p < 0.001).

According to univariate analysis, the PVA z score was significantly associated with the incidence of moderate or greater PR (Table 2), with p < 0.001 for the minimum diameter and p = 0.001 for the maximum diameter. Patients with moderate or greater PR had lower mean z scores of the maximum PVA diameter (−2.5 ± 1.17) and lower mean z scores of the minimum PVA diameter (−2.17 ± 1.18). In addition, TAP repair was strongly associated with postoperative moderate or greater PR (p < 0.001), with 61 of 65 patients (93.8%) in the TAP group developing moderate or greater PR compared to only 8 of 36 patients (22.2%) in the non-TAP group.

ROC analysis was performed for the PVA z score parameters to predict the occurrence of moderate or greater PR. Both the maximum and minimum PVA diameter z scores demonstrated moderate discriminative ability for predicting moderate or greater PR, with area under the curve (AUC) values of 0.695 and 0.701, respectively. A cut-off value of −2.1 for the z score of the PVA maximum diameter (95% CI: 0.59–0.81) yielded a sensitivity of 66.7% and a specificity of 68.7% (Fig. 2). A cut-off value of −2.5 for the z score of the PVA minimum diameter (95% CI: 0.60–0.81) yielded a sensitivity of 42% and a specificity of 90.6% (Fig. 3), demonstrating high specificity but limited sensitivity, indicating its utility in ruling in, rather than ruling out, early PR.

Multivariate analysis was performed using binary logistic regression with the enter method. Variables were selected based on clinical relevance and univariate analysis including age, sex, PVA z scores, and TAP. To avoid multicollinearity, anthropometric variables were not included as they are incorporated into z score calculation. The results revealed that TAP was the only independent predictor of moderate or greater postoperative PR (p < 0.001) with an odds ratio (OR) of 43.7 (95% CI: 11.3–168.7) (Table 3). Discussion

Patients with a low PVA z score generally have a greater risk of developing early moderate or greater PR after ToF repair [5, 8]. In patients with a small PVA, the use of a TAP is often required to enlarge the annulus to approximate the ideal annular size according to age and body weight, thereby adequately relieving pulmonary stenosis. However, annular incisions during the TAP procedure render the pulmonary valve incompetent, resulting in significant PR. Consequently, patients who undergo TAP are at high risk of developing PR [16]. The relationship between smaller PVA z-scores and postoperative PR is likely mediated through the use of TAP, as annular size is a key determinant of surgical strategy. Although the present study did not specifically analyze predictors of TAP use, this pathway is well supported in the literature and should be considered when interpreting the association between PVA and PR [17].

Moreover, even in patients without TAP, a small PVA may lead to PR through other mechanisms. To preserve the pulmonary valve in the setting of a markedly small annulus, extensive commissurotomy is often performed, followed by infundibulectomy through a relatively small PVA without right ventriculotomy. Excessive commissurotomy may extend beyond the medial layer, splitting the annulus and causing partial detachment of the pulmonary valve leaflets at the commissural regions. When combined with subclinical aneurysmal dilatation of the RVOT, this condition may result in immediate postoperative significant PR, particularly after the resolution of the restrictive right ventricular physiology phase [18].

Our findings align with those reported by Hoashi et al., in which smaller PVA z-scores were associated with PR in univariate analysis but not after multivariable adjustment. This was attributed, at least in part, to surgical decision-making, including the increased tendency to perform TAP in patients with smaller PVA [18]. In our cohort, both maximal and minimal PVA z-scores demonstrated moderate discriminatory ability on ROC analysis; however, neither remained independently associated with postoperative PR after adjustment. Instead, TAP emerged as the strongest independent predictor. These findings suggest that the apparent predictive value of PVA size is largely mediated through surgical decision-making, particularly the choice to perform TAP, rather than reflecting a direct independent effect. The strong correlation between maximal and minimal annular diameters may further limit their independent contribution in multivariable models. Nevertheless, PVA z score remains a clinically relevant preoperative parameter, as it reflects the underlying anatomical substrate that influences surgical decision-making and anticipation of TAP use. Therefore, it should be interpreted as an upstream determinant within the causal pathway of postoperative PR, rather than as a direct independent predictor.

From a surgical perspective, the primary objectives of ToF repair are complete ventricular septal defect (VSD) closure and adequate relief of RVOT obstruction. While TAP effectively relieves obstruction, it compromises pulmonary valve competence and predisposes to PR. However, excessive emphasis on pulmonary valve preservation using very low PVA z score thresholds may result in residual RVOT obstruction, which has been linked to right ventricular hypertrophy and may contribute more significantly to arrhythmogenic risk than PR itself [19]. Therefore, PVA z scores should be used judiciously to guide surgical strategies, balancing the avoidance of TAP placement against the risk of residual RVOT obstruction from overly aggressive valve-sparing repair.

The clinical utility of MSCT-derived PVA assessment lies in its ability to provide detailed three-dimensional characterization of the RVOT and annular geometry, which is often elliptical rather than circular. Compared with echocardiography, MSCT offers higher spatial resolution and more accurate delineation of the annular plane [9]. However, data from Southeast Asia on MSCT-derived PVA measurements as predictors of early postoperative PR remain limited, as most prior studies have relied on echocardiographic or intraoperative assessment [5, 8, 10, 11]. This study therefore contributes population-specific data that may complement existing literature.

Early identification of patients at high risk for PR may guide intraoperative decision making, including maximal leaflet preservation, the use of a monocusp valve during TAP, or the selection of a valved right ventricle-to-pulmonary artery conduit. In the early postoperative period, closer hemodynamic monitoring and cautious optimization of ventilation and right ventricular preload may be warranted because the presence of a PR as a residual lesion increases the risk of prolonged postoperative mechanical ventilation [20]. During mid- to long-term follow-up, early prediction of PR may facilitate closer surveillance intervals, earlier use of cardiac magnetic resonance imaging, and optimal timing of pulmonary valve intervention. Importantly, pulmonary valve replacement (PVR) has been shown to reduce right ventricular volume and lower the risk of ventricular arrhythmias and death in patients after ToF repair with PR [21, 22].

Despite its potential role in early risk stratification, the predictive performance of PVA z-score was modest (AUC 0.701). The proposed cut-off (≤ − 2.5) demonstrated high specificity but low sensitivity (42%), indicating that a substantial proportion of patients who develop moderate or greater PR would not be identified using this threshold alone. Accordingly, this parameter is not suitable as a screening tool but may serve as a rule-in marker when interpreted alongside clinical and intraoperative factors, particularly TAP use. Surgical decision-making should therefore integrate multiple parameters, including RVOT morphology, valve characteristics, and intraoperative hemodynamics, rather than relying on annular size alone.

In addition, pulmonary valve morphology—such as cusp number and leaflet dysplasia—plays a critical role in determining the feasibility of valve preservation. As highlighted by Hoashi et al., bicuspid valve morphology is associated with progression of PR following valve-sparing repair [18]. However, detailed morphological data were not consistently available in our retrospective cohort, which may have influenced surgical decision-making and contributed to the observed loss of significance of PVA z-score after adjustment. Furthermore, annular assessment in this study was based on maximal and minimal diameters derived from MSCT; direct three-dimensional cross-sectional area measurement may provide a more accurate representation but was not routinely available.

The PVA z scores in this study were derived from reference values reported by Soszyn et al., based on a non-Indonesian pediatric population. Although anthropometric parameters in our cohort were within expected clinical ranges, potential population differences may introduce systematic bias in z score estimation and shift the optimal cut-off value. Therefore, the proposed threshold of − 2.5 should be interpreted with caution and requires validation in population-specific datasets. Future studies incorporating locally derived reference standards would be essential to improve accuracy and generalizability.

Postoperative echocardiographic assessment in this study was performed within 45 days, although the exact timing varied among patients. No significant difference in assessment timing was observed between groups, suggesting that this variability is unlikely to have substantially influenced PR grading. However, this study specifically evaluates early postoperative PR, and PR severity may evolve over time due to right ventricular remodeling, changes in loading conditions, and progressive annular dilation. Therefore, the findings should be interpreted as reflecting early hemodynamic outcomes rather than long-term PR burden or late clinical events. Further longitudinal studies are required to determine whether these early findings translate into persistent or clinically significant PR over time.

In addition, detailed data on early postoperative complications and serial echocardiographic assessments during the ICU period were not systematically available due to the retrospective design. Therefore, early hemodynamic changes and immediate postoperative PR evolution, particularly in valve-sparing patients, could not be evaluated.

The median age at repair in our cohort was higher than that typically reported in Western populations, which may reflect differences in referral patterns and healthcare access. This may limit the generalizability of our findings to centers where earlier repair is routinely performed. Finally, this study was conducted at a single center with a retrospective design. Prospective studies with longer monitoring periods are needed to assess the long-term impact of PVA z scores on significant PR more accurately.

Conclusion

Preoperative PVA z score demonstrated moderate discriminatory ability in predicting early postoperative PR. However, after adjustment, it was not an independent predictor, as TAP emerged as the strongest determinant of postoperative PR. These findings suggest that the association between PVA size and PR is largely mediated through surgical decision-making, particularly the use of TAP. The proposed cut-off (≤ −2.5), while highly specific, has limited sensitivity and therefore should not be used as a standalone screening tool. Instead, PVA z scores may assist in early risk stratification as a rule-in indicator for higher-risk patients when interpreted in conjunction with intraoperative factors, particularly TAP use. It should be considered an upstream preoperative determinant within the causal pathway of postoperative PR.

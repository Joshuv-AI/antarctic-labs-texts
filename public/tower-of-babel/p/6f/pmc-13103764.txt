A New Inorganic–Organic Hybrid Crystal, (NH3(CH2)6)NH3SnCl6: Synthesis, Crystal Structure, Vibrational, Thermal, Morphological Properties, and DFT Analysis
Authors: Omar Berradi, Mohamed Berradi, Youssef Elrhayam, Abdesselam Eddaoukhi, Mouhcine Bouachri, Jalila El Azzaoui, Mustapha Ait El Had, El Mahdi Hbaiz, Mounir El Hezzat, Ahmed El Yacoubi, Meryem Hrimla, Brahim Sallek, Salah Eddine El Youbi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this study, we investigated the structural, vibrational, and elemental properties of a new crystal, [NH3(CH2)6NH3]­SnCl6, using a combination of experimental techniques (X-ray diffraction (XRD), scanning electron microscopy/energy dispersive spectroscopy (SEM/EDS), inductively coupled plasma-optical emission spectrometry (ICP-OES), infrared (IR), and Raman Spectroscopy) and theoretical methods (periodic density functional theory (DFT) with the B3LYP functional and the LanL2DZ basis set). X-ray diffraction analysis revealed that the compound crystallizes in the monoclinic system space group (P21/m, Z = 2), with lattice parameters: a = 14.1652 Å, b = 7.1967 Å, c = 9.2832 Å, and β = 122.793°. Scanning electron microscope (SEM) imaging and energy-dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) confirmed good crystallinity and a uniform elemental distribution. Inductively coupled plasma-optical emission spectrometry (ICP-OES) was used to verify the stoichiometry and elemental composition of the crystal. Thermal behavior was assessed through differential scanning calorimetry (DSC), which provided insights into the compound’s phase transition and thermal stability within the studied temperature range. The properties were analyzed using infrared (IR) and Raman Spectroscopy, and the vibrational to V/Z ratio was analyzed as a function of CH2 chain length and compared to related compounds of formula H3N­(CH2) n NH3SnCl6 (2 ≤ n ≤ 5). Theoretical studies, using periodic density functional theory (DFT) with the B3LYP functional and the LanL2DZ basis set, were performed to investigate the structural and vibrational characteristics of crystalline H3N­(CH2) n NH3SnCl6. A three-dimensional N–H···Cl hydrogen-bonding (HB) network was identified, and vibrational spectra were analyzed using factor group analysis. Symmetry reduction in the crystal lattice was found to cause mode splitting in the vibrational spectra. Hydrogen bonding was shown to influence both geometric and vibrational features of the material. Characteristic N–H, Sn–Cl, and C–H stretching as well as NH3, −CH2, and Cl–Sn–Cl bonding modes provided spectroscopic markers for hydrogen bond strength and structural symmetry across this compound series.

Introduction

Extensive research has been conducted on alkyldiammonium halometalates with the general formula NH3(CH2) n NH3MX6 (M: Sn, Te, Si, and Pt; X: Cl, Br, I, and F; 2 ≤ n ≤ 5), in order to clarify their structure, hydrogen bonds, and relationship with this type of compound. − Generally, these compounds have a layered structure composed of deformed MX6 2– octahedra, in which the NH3 groups of the organic are inserted between the inorganic layers. − Despite the availability of structural and vibrational data for the compounds NH3(CH2) n NH3SnCl6(3 ≤ n ≤ 5), there is a notable lack of detailed crystallographic and vibrational information for the n = 6 analogue. − This gap in knowledge hinders our ability to understand the effect of chain lengthening on the structure, hydrogen bonding, and possible phase behavior in these hybrid materials.

Furthermore, previous research has indicated that similar halogenated metal compounds possess fascinating optoelectronic characteristics, positioning them as potential candidates for applications in the fields of photovoltaics, sensors, and light-emitting devices. − We aim to extend the NH3(CH2) n NH3SnCl6 series toward longer alkyl chains through the synthesis and characterization of the n = 6 derivative. This could impact the dimensionality, molecular arrangement, and thermal properties of the hybrid structure. The extension of the alkylene spacer could lead to new structural patterns or phase transitions that have not been observed in the shorter-chain analogues.

It is well-known that the length and nature of the organic space in metal complexes have a direct impact on their optical and electronic characteristics. Several recent studies have demonstrated that modifying the length of the organic chain transforms not only the crystalline structure such as the tilt of the octahedra and the interlayer spacing but also the dynamics of the interactions between organic cations and metal networks (notably via hydrogen bonds or π–π interactions). These structural modifications then influence essential properties such as carrier mobility, exciton recombination, and photoluminescence emission. ,

Recent research on two-dimensional (2D) perovskites has shown that longer or more flexible organic spacers can stimulate more efficient exciton confinement, affect phase transitions, and optimize optoelectronic properties, thereby opening up unprecedented opportunities for particular applications. It is therefore crucial to conduct a comparative study of complexes with spacers of different lengths in order to understand the precise influence of the organic chain length on optical and electronic characteristics, particularly regarding their use in high-performance optoelectronic devices. −

Therefore, a comparative analysis of metal complexes containing spacers of various lengths is essential to deepen our understanding and evaluation of the influence of organic chain length on their optoelectronic characteristics. This method will help demystify the processes that control the performance of materials in advanced optoelectronic applications. −

During this research, we present the synthesis, structural analysis, vibrational spectroscopy study, and morphological investigation of compound NH3(CH2)6NH3SnCl6 (n = 6). This study contributes to a broader systematic investigation of the structure–property relationships within this class of organic–inorganic hybrid halometallates, complemented by density functional theory (DFT) calculations. By comparing the optical and electronic properties of the complexes based on the length of the spacers, we aim to clarify the influence of the chain length on the performance of the materials, thereby highlighting the scientific value of this category of compounds for advanced applications.

Experimental Study

Synthesis of the Title Compound

Single crystals of H3N­(CH2)6NH3SnCl6 were obtained by slow evaporation at room temperature from a hydrochloric acid solution containing stoichiometric amounts of NH2(CH2)6NH2 acidified with 36% HCl and SnCl4. The resulting crystals are transparent, nonhygroscopic, and large enough to be suitable for X-ray structural analysis.

The synthesis process can be described by the following reactions and 1 NH2(CH2)6NH2+2HCl→NH3(CH2)6NH3,2Cl− 2 NH3(CH2)6NH3,2Cl−+SnCl4→[NH3(CH2)6NH3]SnCl6

Instrumental Procedures

X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis was conducted using an XPERT-PRO diffractometer, fitted with a PW3050/60 goniometer and a copper (Cu) anticathode source, utilizing Cu Kα radiation with a wavelength of λ = 1.54443 Å.

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was performed with a VEGA3TESCAN microscope operating at 10 kV, equipped with an EDX detector for elemental analysis.

In this study, the device used is a differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) model Standard Cell FC from the company TA Instruments.

Inductively coupled plasma-optical emission spectrometry (ICP-OES) analysis was conducted using high-frequency argon plasma, a nebulizer for sample presentation, a quartz torch, a CCD optical spectrometer, and a computer system dedicated to data collection and processing.

Computational Details

The initial molecular geometry of (NH3-(CH2)6-NH3)2[SnCl6] was derived directly from the unit cell of its crystal structure. All quantum chemical calculations were performed using the Gaussian03 software package. Geometry optimization and vibrational frequency calculations were conducted employing the Becke three-parameter hybrid functional combined with the Lee–Yang–Parr correlation functional (B3LYP), in conjunction with the LanL2DZ basis set. − An empirical scaling coefficient of 0.75 was applied to correct for systematic errors arising from database limitations, the neglect of electronic correlation, and vibrational anharmonicity. Visualization, spectral analysis, and normal mode assignments confirmation were carried out using GaussView05 software.

Therefore, all 72 normal modes are expected to be both Raman and IR active.

For the anions, the reduction in symmetry from the ideal octahedral point group (Oh) to the lower-symmetry site group (C 2h ) gives rise to a side effect that theoretically results in the splitting of the degenerate vibrational modes. Specifically, the doubly degenerate modes (Eg) and the triply degenerate modes (T 2g , 2T 1u , and T 2u ) are expected to split into components corresponding to the irreducible representations of the C 2h group, as summarized in Table .

Experimentally, this symmetry-lowering leads to the appearance of the following vibrational modes for the anions: 4 Ag (Raman-active), 2 Bg (Raman-active), 3 Au (infrared-active), and 6 Bu (infrared-active), each occurring twice due to the presence of two formula units per unit cell. This results in a total of 12 Raman-active modes and 18 infrared-active modes.

The total number of vibrational modes is 174. Furthermore, as the structure possesses a center of inversion, characteristic of centrosymmetric crystals, the mutual exclusion principle applies. According to this rule, vibrational modes that are active in Raman spectroscopy are inactive in infrared spectroscopy and vice versa; infrared-active modes are not Raman-active. Additionally, the vibrational modes associated with both the cation and anion are doubled as a result of the presence of two cationic and two anionic units within the primitive unit cell (Z = 2).

The results obtained from the vibrational analysis, particularly the predominance of active modes consistent with C 2h symmetry and the observed splitting attributed to site symmetry effects, are in excellent agreement with the structural data derived from powder X-ray diffraction. Specifically, the XRD analysis indicates that the studied crystal crystallizes in a monoclinic system with low symmetry, thereby confirming the group factor method used in the vibrational study. The convergence of spectroscopic and crystallographic findings strongly supports the conclusion that the compound’s structure exhibits reduced symmetry, consistent with the C 2h point group.

Quantum Computational Methods

According to previous theoretical studies on alkylene diammonium halometallates, standard molecular calculations can lead to geometries that differ significantly from experimentally observed structures. ,− The NH3(CH2) n NH3MX6(n = 2–6) complexes are characterized by a three-dimensional network of N–H···Cl hydrogen bonds, as revealed by crystallographic data. This highlights the necessity of accurate quantum mechanical methods to describe their structural and vibrational behavior.

In the present work, all structural and energetic calculations were carried out using DFT at the B3LYP/LanL2DZ level, as implemented in the Gaussian03 software package, with GaussView05, serving in the graphical interface. A spin-unpolarized (nonmagnetic) configuration was adopted throughout the study, given the absence of unpaired electrons or magnetic ordering in the investigated system. Indeed, the tin center [SnCl6]2–anion exists in the Sn4+ oxidation state and is fully paired, while the organic diammonium cations are also diamagnetic.

Furthermore, no symmetry constraints were imposed during the geometry optimization in order to allow for potential of the alkyl chain symmetry-breaking distortions, particularly those induced by the flexibility and the subtle interactions between the ammonium groups and the [SnCl6]2– anion; this approach ensures that local minima are correctly captured and that the influence of hydrogen bonding and chain length on molecular geometry is accurately reflected.

To complement these calculations, isolated molecular ions, namely, [H3N­(CH2)3NH3]2+, [H3N­(CH2)4NH3]2+, [H3N­(CH2)5NH3]2+, [H3N­(CH2)6NH3]2+, and [SnCl6]2––were also studied under vacuum conditions with a minimum 15 Å separation, in order to eliminate spurious interactions. This allowed us to isolate and analyze the influence of hydrogen bonding and chain length variations on the geometric and vibrational properties of the complexes.

Although dispersion corrections (DFT-D3) were not explicitly included in the current B3LYP/lanL2ZD framework, which has proven to be effective for modeling vibrational properties of isolated molecules, we recognize that incorporating such corrections could further refine the description of intermolecular interactions. This enhancement is planned for future investigations.

The IR and Raman spectra of the [NH3(CH2)6NH3]­SnCl6 complex exhibit a variety of distinct vibrational bands corresponding to different functional groups, particularly the NH3and (CH2)6 moieties. Strong vibrational activity is observed in the 2800–3500 cm–1 region, attributed to the N–H and C–H stretching modes of these groups.

Specifically, the asymmetric stretching modes of NH3 and CH2 are assigned to the medium-intensity IR bands at approximately 3500 cm–1and 3250 cm–1, respectively. In contrast, the symmetric stretching modes of group NH3 are associated with weak IR bands near 3000 cm–1, while those of the group and CH2 are shown in Table and Figure . In addition, the Raman spectrum of the investigated crystal confirms the presence of NH3 stretching vibrations.

The asymmetric and symmetric stretching modes of the NH2 and CH2 groups are responsible for bands at 3195 cm–1 and 3130 cm–1, respectively. Raman bands at 3010 cm–1, 2980 cm–1, 2950 cm–1, 2860 cm–1, and 2750 cm–1 are attributed to the symmetric stretching modes of CH2, as indicated in Table and Figure .

Raman spectrum of NH3(CH2)6NH3SnCl6 at room temperature in the range 2000–4000 cm–1.

The 1600–1500 cm–1 frequency range corresponds to the bending modes of NH3. According to the calculations, the δ (NH3) vibrations give rise to two IR bands at 1650 cm–1 and at 1600 cm–1, and medium-intensity Raman bands at 1580 cm–1. An intense IR band at 1500 cm–1 and a weak Raman band at 1525 cm–1 are also attributed to these bonding vibrations. Notably, all NH3 deformation frequencies in the studied complexes are blue-shifted compared to the isolated cation, indicating the presence of hydrogen-bonding interactions, as shown in Table below.

In the 1400 to 1200 cm–1 spectral range, bands attributed to CH2 deformation modes are observed. These bands are broadly distributed, consistent with previous findings. ,, CH2 scissoring vibrations produce weak IR bands at 1480 cm–1, 1400 cm–1, and 1375 cm–1, and medium-intensity Raman bands at 1475 cm–1 and 1433 cm–1, as represented in Figure and Table . Bending and twisting vibrations are observed in the 1350–1325 cm–1 region. Weak CH2 rocking vibrations are detected at lower frequencies between the range 1200 and 740 cm–1 (Figure and Table ). Vibrations in the range 1050–950 cm–1 are associated with C–C stretching, slightly coupled with CH2 bending modes.

An IR band of medium intensity at 833 cm–1 (Figure ) and the Raman bands, a weak one at 980 cm–1 and a very strong one at 890 cm–1 (Figure ), are assigned to the asymmetric and symmetric stretching modes of the C–N bands, respectively. These modes appear higher than those calculated for the isolated cations IR at 800 cm–1 and Raman at 810 cm–1, suggesting shortening of the C–N bond in the hydrogenated compound. Consequently, C–N vibrational modes are sensitive to the net (C–N)-H···Cl hydrogen bonding and may serve as reliable indicators of hydrogen bond strength in this class of inorganic complexes.

The internal vibrational modes of the SnCl6 2– anion are identified in the Raman spectrum below at 500 cm–1 for NH3(CH2)6NH3SnCl6 (Figure and Table ). Analysis of the Sn–Cl stretching and Cl–Sn–Cl bending modes indicates a shift to higher frequencies when moving from isolated SnCl6 2– ions to hydrogen-bonded systems. This behavior is consistent with a shorter Sn–Cl bond length in complexes of the type NH3(CH2)nNH3SnCl6 (n between 2 and 6), facilitated by hydrogen bonding via the Cl atoms (Figure and Table ).

The data presented in this table reveal that the crystals can be categorized according to the symmetry of their respective systems. Specifically, for strange values of n = 3 and n = 5, the crystals belong to the orthorhombic system. In these cases, the space group for C3 is Pnma (Z = 4), while for C5, it is Pbca (Z = 8). In contrast, for even values of n (n = 2, 4, 6), the crystals correspond to the triclinic (n = 2) or monoclinic systems (n = 4, 6). The space group for C2is P1 (Z = 1), for C4, it is C2/m (Z = 2), and for C6, it is P21/m (Z = 2).

Upon analysis of the structural data, it becomes evident that there is no group-subgroup symmetry relationship between the underlying structures, particularly between the Pnma and P21/m, C2/m structures, or between Pbca and these same symmetries. However, such relationships are clearly observed in the case of compounds C3 (n = 3) and C5 (n = 5). Figure illustrates the variation of the V/Z ratio, where V is the unit-cell volume and Z is the formula units per cell as a function of the number of CH2 groups (n = 2 to 6) in the H3N­(CH2)nNH3SnCl6 compounds.

Variation of cell volume (V)/number of formula units per cell (Z) as a function of the number “n” of (−CH2) groups in 1, 6 hexane diammonium hexachlorostannates (n = 2 to 6).

The observed variation is not linear, and distinct deviations arise when transitioning between different values of n = 2 to 3, from n = 3 to 4, from n = 4 to 5, and from n = 5 to 6. These deviations can be attributed to the structural changes accompanying the variations in symmetry: from triclinic symmetry (n = 2) to orthorhombic (n = 3), to monoclinic (n = 4), back to orthorhombic symmetry (n = 5), and finally to monoclinic (n = 6). Alternatively, these deviations may arise from changes in the cation conformations and the number of CH2 groups (n).

Notably, the transition from triclinic symmetry (n = 2) and monoclinic symmetry (n = 4, 6) to orthorhombic symmetry (n = 3, 5) markedly deviates from the significant deviation. In the crystal of hexane-1,6-diammonium hexachlorostannateNH3(CH2)6NH3SnCl6, the SnCl6 2–octahedra exhibit distortion. The +NH3(CH2)6NH3 + cations are predominately located between the layers of inorganic anions and are linked to the anions via N–H···Cl hydrogen bonds, as observed in similar compounds. ,

In the case of the SnCl6 2– units, significant changes in the Sn­(IV)-Cl bond lengths and the Cl–Sn–Cl band angles are noted as (n) increases from 3 to 6. These variations are strongly correlated with alterations in the donor/acceptor distances of the N–H···Cl hydrogen bonds. The average deviations in the lengths of Sn­(IV)-Cl bond lengths and N···H bond distances are as follows: 0.031 Å and 1.00 Å (for n = 3), 0.0348 Å and 1.03 Å (for n = 4), 0.0385 Å and 1.058 Å (for n = 5), and 0.0495 Å and 1.095 Å (for n = 6). These findings indicate that as (n) increases, both the structural distortion and the bandwidth variations become more pronounced, underscoring the influence of the CH2 group number of the molecular architecture.

The largest Cl–Sn–Cl deviations (2.77°) are observed for the anionic octahedra in the complex with n = 3, while intermediate deviations are seen for the anions in the compounds with n = 4, 5, and 6, where the angles are considered in the cis position. The smallest deviations are found for the Cl–Sn–Cl angles in the trans position in the complex with n = 6. These variations in bond lengths and angles clearly indicate that the SnCl6 2– anions are distorted from ideal octahedral symmetry (Oh). The estimation of these results is consistent with those obtained for other substances containing the same SnCl6 2– anions. −

The calculations revealed that the structures of hexachlorostannate alkyldiammonium compounds with n = 3 to 6, as presented in Table above, exhibit a progressive increase in chain length. This trend can be attributed to the strengthening of hydrogen bonds in the solid-state structures. In addition, the computational analysis of the C–N bond lengths isolated cations, which increase in chain length with the addition of CH2 groups, as illustrated in Figure , predicted a distinct trend for the R­(C–N) bond. The shortest R­(C–N) bond length was observed for the +H3N­(CH2)6NH3 +cation. This finding suggests that the variations in the C–N bond length across the studied complexes are primarily influenced by the strength of the hydrogen bonds rather than by the length of the alkyl chain (n) in the cationic (−CH2)n group.

The calculated geometric parameters of isolated ions and complexes comprising the corresponding cations and anions connected through hydrogen bonds provide insight into the structural changes occurring during the formation of the noncovalent bond network and the effect of crystal packing. The bonds lengths measured in the complexes [H3N­(CH2) n NH3]­SnCl6, where n = 3 to 6, were found to be weaker than those of the isolated cations and anions, with observed reductions in bond lengths for: Sn–Cl (∼0.02 Å), C–N (up to 0.024 Å), and C–C (up to ∼0.134 Å) (Table ). Notably, the complex with n = 3 exhibited the most significant decrease in both C–N and C–C bond lengths, whereas the complex with n = 6 showed the least reduction. These findings suggest that such a measurement can be used to assess the strength of molecular interactions and the stacking effects within the crystal structure. Furthermore, a decrease in Sn–Cl bond lengths is observed in the hydrogen-bonded complexes compared with those of isolated ions.

A possible explanation for this could be the negative charge on the SnCl6 2– anion, which, in conjunction with the six Cl atoms, participates in noncovalent interactions that facilitate charge transfer. The hydrogen bond network in the studied complexes is characterized by two distinct types of bonds: Cl···H­(NH2) and Cl···H­(CH). The presence and cooperative effects of these bonds are notable.

In the SnCl6 2–group, each chlorine (Cl) atom participates in two hydrogen bonds, Cl···H­(NH2) and Cl···H­(CH), depending on the relative positions of the donor and acceptor groups. A hydrogen atom is bonded to a chlorine atom to form the ammonium group and also forms hydrogen bonds with the chlorine atom by positioning the CH2 group appropriately. The Cl···H­(NH2) interactions range from 1.8 to 3.4 Å, while the Cl···H­(CH) interactions are between 2.17 and 2.54 Å. It is clear that the noncovalent NH···Cl interactions in the solid-state structure significantly affect the geometry of both the cations and anions, which must be considered in geometry calculations.

The presence of distinct crystal fragments indicates good crystallinity of the compound. , The elemental composition of NH3(CH2)6NH3SnCl6 was determined by EDS microanalysis, which confirmed the presence of all constituent elements except hydrogen, which cannot be detected by this technique. The experimentally measured elemental contents were C: 56.97%, N: 35.45%, Sn: 0.07%, and Cl: 7.49%, while the corresponding theoretical values were C: 60.93%, N: 24%, Sn: 3.03%, and Cl: 6.42%, as shown in Figure (c) and Table S2, which is attached with the Supporting Information files. The uncertainty of approximately 73.6% regarding tin, as mentioned in Table S2, highlights the limitations of EDS when it comes to quantifying heavy elements present at low concentrations in an organic matrix as well as the limitation of EDS in identifying hydrogen (H). However, it did serve to confirm the presence of Sn in the crystalline structure.

The experimental data collected through EDS spectroscopy highlight significant differences compared to the theoretical values, particularly regarding the Sn content (0.07% versus 3.03%). This discrepancy can be attributed to various factors, including sample variability, measurement accuracy, and surface contamination. According to previous studies, the diversity of samples can lead to fluctuations in the distribution of elements. Furthermore, the EDS method can be modulated by elements such as the sample thickness and the surrounding matrix, which can thus affect the accuracy of the results. Finally, surface contamination can also affect the results, especially for light elements such as carbon and chlorine.

To rectify this discrepancy and conduct a more precise analysis of the elemental composition, we resorted to the ICP-OES method, which offers increased accuracy (Table S3 in the Supporting Information). The ICP-OES analysis showed a Sn concentration of 1613 ppm (0.1613%), a figure that, while still below the theoretical value (3.03%), offers a more precise assessment compared to the semiquantitative nature of EDS. It should be noted, however, that ICP-OES could also underestimate the concentration of Sn due to partial dissociation, hydrolysis of Sn4+, and the influence of the organic matrix. This conclusion supports the hypothesis that the Sn concentration is detectable, even at the lower value, which could reflect the inherent properties of the sample or other factors such as the partial distribution of elements within the crystals. Conclusion

In this study, we successfully indexed the X-ray diffraction spectrum of synthetic crystalline powder NH3(CH2)6NH3SnCl6 and proposed a crystal structure that seems to be the most probable for this compound. Structural characterization was further enhanced by the use of SEM and EDS, revealing a smooth surface and a homogeneous distribution, suggesting a layered structure.

Despite the EDS analysis indicating an Sn concentration of 0.07%, significantly lower than the theoretical value of 3.03%, we attribute this difference to the inherent limitations of the EDS technique, which is semiquantitative and susceptible to various parameters such as exploration depth, sample thickness, matrix effects, or surface contamination. Moreover, the uncertainty of approximately 73.6% regarding tin, as mentioned in Table S2, highlights the limitations of EDS when it comes to quantifying heavy elements present at low concentrations in an organic matrix as well as the limitations of EDS in identifying hydrogen (H). However, it did serve to confirm the presence of Sn in the crystalline structure.

To obtain a more accurate assessment of the elemental composition, the ICP-OES technique has been exploited, which revealed a Sn content of 1613 ppm (0.1613%). Although still below the theoretical value, this result provides a more reliable estimate than the semiquantitative nature of EDS, validating the presence of Sn in the compound. However, the lower values may reflect the intrinsic characteristics of the material or the partial distribution of elements within the crystals.

The vibrational characteristics of the compound were thoroughly analyzed by using IR and Raman spectroscopy in the solid phase. This study confirmed the formation of a three-dimensional network of N–H···Cl hydrogen bonds, a finding supported by DFT calculations on model complexes comprising SnCl6 2– ions and alkylene diammonium cations. The use of group theory techniques to refine the normal modes, including IR and Raman-active modes, provided deeper insights into the crystal’s vibrational spectra.

Finally, thermogravimetric analysis revealed three significant endothermic peaks that correspond to thermal transitions and thermal degradation processes. The first peaks around 120 °C could correspond to a phase change or melting mechanism, while the last two, at approximately 220 and 255 °C, are likely associated with more advanced chemical deterioration, possibly related to the decomposition of the ammonium complex or the removal of chloride. These thermal analyses also provide information about polymorphic characteristics and the structure–property correlation, influenced by the number of carbon atoms in the spacer.

The spectroscopic data obtained are crucial for determining the relative intensity of hydrogen-bonding interactions across the series of alkylene diammonium hexachlorostannates. This research contributes to a better understanding of the structural and vibrational characteristics of these materials, laying a solid foundation for further studies of their potential applications.

Myocardial Expression of PD-L1 as a Marker of Poor Prognosis in Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor–Associated Myocarditis
Authors: Takuro Imaoka, Shingo Sakashita, Satoshi Matsuoka, Kimi Sato, Yoichi Naito, Genki Okumura, Shohei Koyama, Koji Hozawa, Yuka Nakamura, Yuma Shibutani, Tomoko Ishizu, Ikuo Sekine, Kazuko Tajiri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundImmune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have improved cancer outcomes but are associated with severe immune-related adverse events, including myocarditis. PD-L1 plays an important role in immune regulation and homeostasis. Myocardial PD-L1 up-regulation has been reported in ICI-associated myocarditis; however, its clinical significance and prognostic value remain unclear.ObjectivesThis study sought to investigate the association between myocardial PD-L1 expression and ICI-associated myocarditis severity and prognosis.MethodsTwenty consecutive cases of biopsy-proven ICI-associated myocarditis were analyzed. Endomyocardial biopsy specimens underwent immunohistochemical staining for PD-L1, and patients were categorized into high (≥8%) or low (<8%) PD-L1 expression groups. Clinical presentation and the incidence of major adverse cardiotoxic events, defined as a composite of severe heart failure, severe arrhythmia, or cardiac death, were compared between groups.ResultsThe median follow-up time for the cohort was 189 days (Q1-Q3: 88-308 days). The absolute risk of 30-day major adverse cardiotoxic events was significantly higher in the high PD-L1 group (n = 5 of 7 [71.4%], 95% CI: 29.0%-96.3%) than in the low PD-L1 group (n = 1 of 13 [7.7%], 95% CI: 0.2%-36.0%; P = 0.007). The median peak normalized troponin level (× upper limit of normal) was significantly higher in the high PD-L1 group than in the low PD-L1 group (662 [Q1–Q3: 204–855] vs 64 [Q1–Q3: 26–108]; P = 0.010). Patients in the high PD-L1 group also had longer QRS duration on electrocardiography (124 ms [Q1-Q3: 108-131 ms] vs 96 ms [Q1-Q3: 90-102 ms]; P = 0.021). Myocardial inflammatory cell infiltration was greater in the high PD-L1 group, with higher CD3+ T cell infiltration (35.9% [Q1-Q3: 25.8%-42.8%] vs 2.6% [Q1-Q3: 0.6%-5.8%]; P = 0.007) and higher CD68+ macrophage infiltration (15.2% [Q1-Q3: 10.2%-21.1%] vs 1.23% [Q1-Q3: 0.4%-2.3%]; P = 0.007).ConclusionsHigh myocardial PD-L1 expression is associated with greater disease severity and a higher risk of major adverse cardiotoxic events in ICI-associated myocarditis. Assessment of PD-L1 expression in endomyocardial biopsy specimens may help identify patients at high risk who may benefit from early intervention and closer monitoring.Key Words: cardio-oncology, endomyocardial biopsy, heart failure, immune checkpoint inhibitor, immunotherapy, myocarditis, onco-cardiology, PD-L1

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have improved cancer outcomes and transformed cancer therapy and are now widely used for the treatment of multiple malignancies. ICIs, including monoclonal antibodies targeting CTLA-4, LAG-3, PD-1, and PD-L1, restore T cell–mediated antitumor immunity.1 However, they may also impair immune tolerance to self-antigens, leading to immune-related adverse events (irAEs). ICI-associated myocarditis is a rare irAE, affecting approximately 1% of treated patients; however, it carries the highest mortality rate among irAEs, reaching up to 50%.2, 3, 4 Current treatment strategies rely primarily on empiric corticosteroids,5 which are not universally effective and underscore the challenges associated with managing ICI-associated myocarditis.

PD-L1, a ligand for the inhibitory receptor PD-1, plays an important role in immune regulation and maintenance of immunologic homeostasis.6 Certain tumor cells overexpress PD-L1 to suppress the antitumor immune response and evade T cell–mediated cytotoxicity.7 In the heart, the PD-1/PD-L1 signaling axis contributes to the prevention of autoimmunity, as demonstrated in genetically modified mouse models and pharmacologic studies.8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13 PD-L1 is typically absent in myocardial tissue from individuals without cardiovascular disease but is expressed in diseased myocardium.14 In patients with ICI-associated myocarditis, histopathologic analyses of endomyocardial biopsy (EMB) or autopsy specimens have demonstrated PD-L1+ macrophages and cardiomyocytes within inflammatory foci.15, 16, 17, 18 Palaskas et al17 reported that myocardial PD-L1 expression was more pronounced in cases of histologically severe ICI-associated myocarditis. However, the relationship between the extent of PD-L1 expression and the clinical course of ICI-associated myocarditis remains unclear. The present study examined the association between myocardial PD-L1 expression and the clinical presentation and prognosis of patients with ICI-associated myocarditis.

Methods

An extended Methods section is provided in the Supplemental Appendix.

Study population

This retrospective cohort study included 20 consecutive cases of biopsy-confirmed ICI-associated myocarditis diagnosed between June 2016 and March 2024 at National Cancer Center Hospital East, New Tokyo Hospital, and the University of Tsukuba Hospital in Japan. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the National Cancer Center (approval number: 2022-154), and the requirement for informed consent was waived because of the retrospective study design.

Diagnosis of ICI-associated myocarditis

ICI-associated myocarditis was defined according to the 2022 European Society of Cardiology guidelines as either a pathological diagnosis of multifocal inflammatory cell infiltrates with overt cardiomyocyte loss on light microscopy or a clinical diagnosis.5

Histopathological staining

All myocardial specimens obtained by EMB were paraffin-embedded, and serial sections of 3 to 4 μm thickness were prepared for staining. For histologic evaluation, step sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin and subjected to immunohistochemistry (IHC) for CD3, CD20, CD68, and PD-L1.

To identify the specific cell types expressing PD-L1, multiplex IHC was performed. Slides were stained with antibodies against PD-L1, CD68 (a macrophage marker), α-actinin (a cardiomyocyte marker), and CD31 (an endothelial cell marker). The panel used to identify T cell subtypes included CD3, CD4, and CD8.

To validate the specificity of PD-L1 staining, PD-L1–expressing lung squamous cell carcinoma tissue was used as a positive control, whereas histologically normal myocardial tissue obtained from autopsy cases without heart disease served as the negative control (Supplemental Figures 1 and 2).

Clinical outcome measurement

The primary outcome was time to occurrence of 30-day major adverse cardiac events (MACE), defined as any of the following: severe heart failure requiring inotropes, noninvasive or invasive ventilation, or mechanical circulatory support; ventricular tachyarrhythmias; high-degree atrioventricular block or sinus node dysfunction requiring pacemaker implantation; or cardiac death within 30 days.

Day 0 was defined as the date of clinical diagnosis of ICI-associated myocarditis, corresponding to the onset of cardiac symptoms or the first clinical suspicion of myocarditis documented in the medical record rather than the biopsy date. Patients were followed for up to 30 days to assess the occurrence of MACE. For time-to-event analyses, patients who did not experience MACE were censored at 30 days or at the last available clinical follow-up within the 30-day period.

Secondary outcomes included the individual components of the primary outcome.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using EZR version 1.68 (Saitama Medical Center, Jichi Medical University),19 a graphical user interface for R (R Foundation for Statistical Computing). EZR is a modified version of R Commander that includes additional biostatistical functions.

Continuous variables were compared using the Mann-Whitney U test, and correlations between continuous variables were assessed using Spearman's rank correlation coefficient. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher exact test.

Given the limited sample size and number of events, the primary comparison for MACE was performed using the Fisher exact test to estimate absolute event rates with exact binomial CIs.

Receiver-operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis and Firth’s bias-reduced penalized logistic regression models with PD-L1 as a continuous variable were used to assess the robustness of the prognostic value of PD-L1. Time-to-event analyses using the Kaplan-Meier method and Firth’s penalized Cox proportional hazards models were presented as exploratory estimates to account for the small number of events.

Survival curves were generated using the Kaplan-Meier method, with day 0 defined as described in Clinical Outcome Measurement, and differences between groups were compared using the log-rank test. HRs with 95% CIs were estimated using Firth’s penalized Cox proportional hazards regression.

The optimal PD-L1 cutoff was determined using the Youden index from ROC curve analysis. As a sensitivity analysis, the cutoff was also evaluated by maximizing the log-rank statistic. To account for potential overfitting, the area under the curve (AUC) was corrected for optimism using bootstrap resampling (2,000 iterations).

A P value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. No correction for multiple comparisons was applied because of the exploratory nature of the analyses.

Correlations between PD-L1 expression and inflammatory cell density were evaluated using Spearman's rank correlation coefficient. Scatter plots present ln(x+1)–transformed values with an overlaid linear regression line and 95% confidence bands for visualization.

The median age of the cohort was 73 years (Q1-Q3: 69-75 years), and 15 of 20 (75%) patients were male. The median interval from the first ICI administration to the onset of myocarditis was 29 days (Q1-Q3: 21-44 days). Lung cancer (n = 6) was the most common malignancy, followed by urogenital cancer (n = 5), gynecological cancer (n = 2), head and neck cancer (n = 2), and liver cancer (n = 2).

Regarding ICI therapy, 9 (45%) of 20 patients received anti-PD-1 monotherapy, 7 (35%) of 20 received anti-PD-L1 monotherapy, and 4 (20%) of 20 received combination therapy with anti-PD-1 and anti-CTLA-4. Extracardiac irAEs occurred in 17 (85%) of 20 patients. Myositis was the most common (n = 15), followed by myasthenia gravis–like syndrome (n = 8) and rash (n = 4).

Corticosteroids were administered to 19 (95%) of 20 patients, and pulse-dose methylprednisolone was used in all treated cases. Nonsteroidal immunomodulatory therapies included intravenous immunoglobulin and abatacept in 6 (30%) of 20 each. Mechanical circulatory support was required in 3 (15%) of 20 patients.

The median follow-up time for the cohort was 189 days (Q1-Q3: 88-308 days). The primary outcome of 30-day MACE occurred in 6 (30%) of 20 patients (cases 6, 11, 12, 16, 17, and 20). Among these, 5 (25%) of 20 developed severe heart failure, 3 of 20 (15%) had high-grade atrioventricular block, 2 (10%) of 20 experienced ventricular tachyarrhythmias, and 2 (10%) of 20 died from cardiac causes.

Both the percentage of PD-L1+ cells and the PD-L1 expression score were significantly higher in the MACE group than in the non-MACE group (PD-L1+ cells: 19.6% [Q1–Q3: 8.5%–44.1%] vs 0.56% [Q1–Q3: 0.20%–2.01%]; P = 0.033; PD-L1 expression score: 34.6 [Q1–Q3: 19.0–83.5] vs 0.80 [Q1–Q3: 0.24–2.66]; P = 0.026, respectively) (Table 2). The PD-L1+ area also tended to be greater in the MACE group than in the non-MACE group, although the difference did not reach statistical significance (6.44% [Q1-Q3: 2.71%-12.04%] vs 0.10% [Q1-Q3: 0.09%-0.25%], P = 0.051).

Sensitivity analysis restricted to patients meeting pathological diagnostic criteria (Dallas criteria) yielded similar results, with higher percentages of PD-L1+ cells and higher PD-L1 expression scores observed in the MACE group than in the non-MACE group (Supplemental Table 3).

Multiplex IHC demonstrated PD-L1 expression in cardiomyocytes (α-actinin+ cells), macrophages (CD68+ cells), and endothelial cells (CD31+ cells) (Supplemental Figure 20). Quantitative analysis showed that PD-L1+ macrophages and cardiomyocytes were significantly more abundant in the MACE group than in the non-MACE group (Supplemental Table 4). In contrast, PD-L1+ endothelial cells did not differ significantly between groups.

Regarding biopsy timing, PD-L1 expression was inversely correlated with the interval from myocarditis onset to biopsy (Supplemental Figure 21) and tended to be lower in patients who had received corticosteroids before biopsy (Supplemental Table 5).

Myocardial infiltration of CD3+, CD20+, and CD68+ cells tended to be higher in the MACE group, although these differences did not reach statistical significance. CD4+ and CD8+ T cell counts were significantly higher in patients who developed MACE than in those who did not (Supplemental Table 6). The CD4/CD8 ratio did not differ between the MACE and non-MACE groups (1.09 [Q1-Q3: 0.78-1.44] vs 1.22 [Q1-Q3: 0.61-1.93], P = 0.92).

Demographic and clinical characteristics of patients with high and low PD-L1 expression

As an exploratory analysis, ROC analysis of PD-L1 expression for predicting MACE yielded an apparent AUC of 0.81 (95% CI: 0.55-1.00), with a sensitivity of 83.3% and specificity of 85.7%. After optimism correction using bootstrap resampling, the AUC was 0.79 (95% CI: 0.53-1.00).

Using the Youden index, an optimal cutoff of approximately 8% was identified (Supplemental Figure 22). Maximization of the log-rank statistic identified 5.4% as the optimal cutoff (χ2 = 9.18; P = 0.002). However, this threshold produced identical patient stratification to the Youden-derived cutoff of 8%, because no patients had PD-L1 values between 5.4% and 8.1%.

Patients were therefore stratified by PD-L1 expression into a high PD-L1 group (PD-L1+ cells ≥8%, n = 7 of 20 [35%]) and a low PD-L1 group (PD-L1+ cells <8%, n = 13 of 20 [65%]).

There were no significant differences between the high and low PD-L1 groups in age, sex, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, dyslipidemia, history of coronary artery disease, or history of heart failure (Table 1). Electrocardiography findings at admission showed significantly less sinus rhythm and longer QRS duration in the high PD-L1 group than in the low PD-L1 group.

The median peak normalized troponin level, expressed as multiples of the upper limit of normal (ULN) based on assay-specific reference values at each institution, was significantly higher in the high PD-L1 group than in the low PD-L1 group (662 [Q1-Q3: 204-855] vs 64 [Q1-Q3: 26-108]; P = 0.010) (Table 1).

There were no significant differences between groups in the use of inotropes or corticosteroids. Similarly, no differences were observed in the use of noninvasive or invasive ventilation or mechanical circulatory support.

Association between PD-L1 expression and MACE

The absolute risk of 30-day MACE was significantly higher in the high PD-L1 group (n = 5 of 7 [71.4%]; 95% CI: 29.0%-96.3%) than in the low PD-L1 group (n = 1 of 13 [7.7%]; 95% CI: 0.2%-36.0%; P = 0.007) (Supplemental Table 7).

Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated significantly lower MACE-free survival in the high PD-L1 group than in the low PD-L1 group (HR: 10.6; 95% CI: 1.48-75.5; log-rank P = 0.007) (Supplemental Figure 23).

When PD-L1 expression was analyzed as a continuous variable, Firth’s penalized logistic regression demonstrated that higher PD-L1 expression was associated with an increased risk of MACE (OR: 1.06; 95% CI: 1.01-1.15; P = 0.025) (Supplemental Table 8). Similarly, Firth’s penalized Cox proportional hazards regression showed that higher PD-L1 expression was significantly associated with a shorter time to MACE (HR: 1.06; 95% CI: 1.02-1.11; P = 0.002) (Supplemental Table 9).

Association between PD-L1 expression and myocardial inflammatory cell infiltration

There was a moderate correlation between the number of PD-L1+ cells and CD3+ cells in the myocardium (rs = 0.62; 95% CI: 0.12-0.87; P = 0.020) (Figure 3A). PD-L1 expression also strongly correlated with CD4+ (rs = 0.74; 95% CI: 0.40-0.90; P < 0.001) and CD8+ (rs = 0.79; 95% CI: 0.50-0.92; P < 0.001) T cells (Supplemental Figure 24).

Myocardial CD3+ cell infiltration was significantly greater in the high PD-L1 group than in the low PD-L1 group (35.9% [Q1-Q3: 25.8%-42.8%] vs 2.60% [Q1-Q3: 0.56%-5.83%]; P = 0.007) (Table 3). CD4+ and CD8+ T cell infiltration were also significantly higher in the high PD-L1 group (Supplemental Table 10). Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first report describing the association between myocardial PD-L1 expression and clinical outcomes in ICI-associated myocarditis. The main findings of this study are as follows: 1) PD-L1 positivity was associated with greater disease severity, as evidenced by higher peak troponin levels, QRS prolongation on electrocardiography, and a higher incidence of MACE; 2) MACE occurred more frequently in the PD-L1 ≥8% group than in the PD-L1 <8% group; and 3) PD-L1 positivity correlated with increased myocardial inflammatory cell infiltration (Central Illustration). Together, these findings add to the growing evidence linking PD-L1 expression with immune-related cardiovascular toxicity and suggest potential prognostic implications for patients receiving ICIs.

Myocardial PD-L1 Expression and Inflammatory Burden in Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor–Associated Myocarditis

High myocardial PD-L1 expression is associated with major adverse cardiotoxic events (MACE) and increased myocardial inflammatory burden in immune checkpoint inhibitor–associated myocarditis. The absolute risk of 30-day MACE was significantly higher in the high PD-L1 (PD-L1 ≥8%) group than in the low PD-L1 (PD-L1 <8%) group (n = 5 of 7 [71.4%] vs n = 1 of 13 [7.7%]; P = 0.007). PD-L1 positivity correlated with CD3+ T cell and CD68+ macrophage infiltration. These findings suggest that myocardial PD-L1 staining may help identify patients at higher risk and support earlier intervention and closer monitoring. H&E = hematoxylin and eosin.

PD-L1 regulates immune responses through interactions with the PD-1 receptor, an inhibitory immune checkpoint that attenuates T cell activity and limits excessive inflammation and myocardial injury.8,11,20 In the present study, the number of PD-L1–expressing cells correlated positively with the extent of inflammatory infiltrates, including CD3+ T cells, CD20+ B cells, and CD68+ macrophages. These findings are consistent with those reported by Palaskas et al,17 who reported greater PD-L1 expression in cases with more pronounced myocardial inflammatory infiltrates and higher histopathologic severity. The increased PD-L1 positivity observed in ICI-associated myocarditis may reflect a compensatory response to excessive immune activation driven by autoreactive T cells, a mechanism supported by prior studies demonstrating PD-L1 up-regulation in models of T cell–mediated cardiac injury.21

A key finding of this study is that MACE occurred significantly more frequently in patients with higher myocardial PD-L1 expression than in those with lower expression. Patients in the high PD-L1 group also had higher troponin levels and longer QRS durations than those in the low PD-L1 group. Moreover, the H-score, which incorporates staining intensity to quantify PD-L1 expression, was significantly higher in the MACE group. These findings suggest that greater PD-L1 staining intensity within myocardial tissue, regardless of whether staining is membranous or cytoplasmic, is associated with poorer clinical outcomes.

Few studies have investigated the association between myocardial PD-L1 expression and clinical outcomes in cardiovascular diseases. Hiraiwa et al22 evaluated myocardial PD-L1 expression in 16 patients with lymphocytic fulminant myocarditis and its association with cardiac events, defined as a composite of cardiac death and left ventricular assist device implantation. Patients with high PD-L1 expression had a significantly higher incidence of cardiac events than those with lower expression, consistent with the findings of the present study. Kushnareva et al14 examined the relationship between PD-L1 expression and echocardiographic parameters in patients with ischemic heart disease and dilated cardiomyopathy and reported a significant negative correlation between PD-L1 expression and the left ventricular ejection fraction.

These prior reports, together with the findings of the present study, suggest that myocardial PD-L1 expression correlates with the severity of heart failure and myocarditis and may serve as a complementary marker, alongside established prognostic factors, for adverse cardiovascular outcomes.

Recent studies evaluating PD-L1 immunostaining in myocardial tissue from patients with ICI-associated myocarditis have demonstrated that PD-L1 is predominantly expressed in histiocytes (primarily macrophages) and cardiomyocytes.15, 16, 17, 18 In experimental models of T cell–mediated myocarditis in mice, PD-L1 expression has also been observed in endothelial cells and appears to be induced by interferon γ.21 In the present study, PD-L1 expression was detected in cardiomyocytes and inflammatory cells, such as macrophages, consistent with these prior observations.

Champion and Stone16 reported that the number of PD-L1–expressing macrophages did not differ between low- and high-grade ICI-associated myocarditis; however, the proportion of macrophages expressing PD-L1 was higher in low-grade cases than in high-grade cases. They also identified an inverse correlation between the number of CD3+ T cells and the proportion of PD-L1+ macrophages.

In contrast, PD-L1+ cardiomyocytes were observed at a high frequency (10 of 10 high-power fields [HPFs]) in 2 of 3 cases of high-grade ICI-associated myocarditis, whereas none of the low-grade cases demonstrated such a pattern. In the present study, multiplex IHC confirmed PD-L1 expression in macrophages, cardiomyocytes, and endothelial cells. Importantly, higher PD-L1 expression on macrophages and cardiomyocytes—but not on endothelial cells—was associated with MACE, suggesting that PD-L1 up-regulation in infiltrating immune cells and injured cardiomyocytes reflects the severity of local inflammation and tissue injury.

In this cohort, myocardial PD-L1 expression was positively correlated with troponin levels. Prior studies have shown that elevated troponin levels predict MACE,23,24 suggesting that this association may reflect greater myocardial injury. Furthermore, the correlation between PD-L1 positivity and QRS duration emphasizes the potential electrophysiological consequences of immune-mediated myocardial damage. Previous studies have reported that QRS duration prolongs from baseline at the onset of ICI-associated myocarditis,25 and that a QRS duration longer than 110 ms is associated with a 3.28-fold higher risk of MACE.26

These QRS changes may result from cardiac inflammation that disrupts intracardiac conduction through several mechanisms,27,28 including interactions between immune cells and cardiac fibroblasts and cardiomyocytes,29 direct involvement of macrophages in cardiac conduction,30 and the effects of autoantibodies and cytokines on cardiomyocyte ion channels.31 PD-L1 expression may therefore reflect the degree of myocardial inflammation and associated conduction abnormalities.

Recent studies have also identified PD-L1 expression in cardiovascular conditions beyond myocarditis, including heart failure and cardiac transplantation,14,32 suggesting a broader role for PD-L1 in cardiac immune homeostasis. However, its exact function in ICI-associated myocarditis remains unclear. PD-L1 expression may represent a protective response, whereas its association with greater disease severity may reflect overwhelming immune activation that exceeds compensatory mechanisms.

Consistent with previous reports,15,16 a predominance of CD8+ T cells was observed in the myocardium. CD20+ cells were also detected, although at substantially lower densities than T cells. In this cohort, only 1 case (case 14) had a CD20+ cell score of 2 (21-40 cells per HPF), whereas all other cases had a score of 0, with counts ranging from 0 to 6 cells per HPF (Supplemental Figure 19).

Although the high PD-L1 group showed significantly greater CD20+ cell infiltration than the low PD-L1 group (Table 3), the overall CD20+ cell burden remained very low in this cohort, consistent with the findings of Palaskas et al.17 Under physiological conditions, antibody-producing plasma cells typically reside in the bone marrow, rather than migrating to sites of target organ injury. However, several case reports of ICI-associated myocarditis have documented myocardial infiltration of CD20+ cells,33,34 although the pathological significance of these cells remains unclear. Further studies are needed to clarify their potential role in the progression of ICI-associated myocarditis.

Study limitations

This study has several limitations worth mentioning. First, it was a retrospective study with a relatively small sample size, which limits the generalizability of the findings. Accordingly, the results should be considered hypothesis-generating. In addition, no adjustment for multiple comparisons was performed, and the findings require confirmation in larger, independent cohorts. Prospective studies are warranted to validate these observations.

Second, the timing of myocardial biopsy varied across patients, including the interval between myocarditis onset and biopsy and whether corticosteroid treatment had been initiated before biopsy. These factors may have influenced the observed results.

Third, EMB is inherently subject to sampling error despite obtaining 3 to 5 specimens per patient, particularly given the patchy inflammatory infiltration. Heterogeneity in the distribution or intensity of PD-L1 expression, as described in tumor tissues,35,36 may also have influenced the results.

Fourth, although total cellular PD-L1 expression was quantified, membranous and cytoplasmic localization were not distinguished because automated scoring cannot reliably differentiate these compartments across heterogeneous cell types. PD-L1 IHC in this study was performed using the clinically validated 28-8 antibody clone, which recognizes the extracellular domain of PD-L1.37 As previously reported, antibodies targeting the extracellular domain may detect not only membranous PD-L1, but also perimembranous or cytoplasmic signal,38 reflecting intracellular PD-L1 pools related to posttranslational modification, trafficking, recycling, and degradation,39 rather than nonspecific staining. Although emerging data in oncology suggest potential cell-intrinsic roles of cytoplasmic or nuclear PD-L1,40 the biological and clinical significance of subcellular PD-L1 localization has not been established in ICI-associated myocarditis.

Finally, because of the limited sample size, multivariable adjustment was not feasible. The observed association between PD-L1 expression and MACE may therefore be confounded by factors such as the type of ICI regimen, the timing of biopsy relative to myocarditis onset, and corticosteroid administration before biopsy. In this cohort, PD-L1 expression was inversely correlated with the time from myocarditis onset to biopsy (Supplemental Figure 21) and tended to be lower in patients who received corticosteroids before biopsy (Supplemental Table 5). Larger studies enabling multivariable modeling are needed to better address these potential confounding factors.

Conclusions

High myocardial PD-L1 expression is associated with more severe clinical manifestations and worse outcomes in ICI-associated myocarditis. Assessment of PD-L1 expression in myocardial tissue may help identify patients at higher risk of adverse outcomes and support early intervention and closer clinical monitoring.

COMPETENCY IN MEDICAL KNOWLEDGE: ICI-associated myocarditis is a life-threatening immune-related adverse event with heterogeneous clinical presentations. In this study, higher myocardial PD-L1 expression in EMB specimens was associated with a higher incidence of MACE, higher peak troponin levels, and QRS prolongation. Assessment of myocardial PD-L1 expression may therefore help identify patients at higher risk who require closer monitoring and early therapeutic intervention.

TRANSLATIONAL OUTLOOK: Prospective studies in larger cohorts are needed to validate the optimal cutoff value and standardize pathological scoring methods. In addition, elucidating the biological mechanisms underlying PD-L1 up-regulation may help guide the development of targeted immunomodulatory strategies for ICI-associated myocarditis.

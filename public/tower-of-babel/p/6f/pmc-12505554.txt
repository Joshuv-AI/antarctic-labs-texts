Global, regional, and national burdens of early onset gastric cancer aged 15–49 years from 1990 to 2021 with projections to 2035: a population-based study
Authors: Yi Lu, Jialin Wu, Tao Yang, Lei Lian, Honho Yu, Jiachen Sun, Jiancong Hu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEarly-onset gastric cancer (EOGC) is a distinct clinical entity with unique epidemiological and pathological features. Despite an overall decline in GC incidence in many regions, recent studies have shown an increasing trend in EOGC incidence in some areas. This study aims to systematically evaluate the global, regional, and national burden of EOGC from 1990 to 2021 using data from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 study.MethodsData on the incidence, mortality, and disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs) of EOGC were obtained from the GBD 2021 database. The age-standardized incidence rates (ASIRs), age-standardized mortality rates (ASMRs), and age-standardized DALYs rates (ASDRs) were calculated. Estimated annual percentage change (EAPC) was used to assess temporal trends. The Bayesian age-period-cohort model was employed to project trends up to 2035.ResultsGlobally, the ASIR of EOGC decreased from 5.40 per 100,000 in 1990 to 3.17 per 100,000 in 2021 (EAPC -1.8, 95% CI: -1.9 to -1.7). The ASMR decreased from 2.00 per 100,000 in 1990 to 1.31 per 100,000 in 2021 (EAPC -2.4, 95% CI: -2.5 to -2.3), and the ASDR decreased from 97.73 per 100,000 in 1990 to 63.98 per 100,000 in 2021 (EAPC -2.5, 95% CI:-2.6 to -2.4). East Asia had the highest ASIR, ASMR, and ASDR. Regions with higher Socio-demographic Index (SDI) values experienced more significant declines in EOGC incidence and mortality. High-sodium diets and smoking were identified as significant risk factors. Forecasts toward 2035 indicate a continued decline in ASIR, ASMR, and ASDR globally.ConclusionsDespite a decrease in ASIR, ASMR, and ASDR globally, the absolute burden of EOGC remains high, with significant regional disparities. Targeted prevention and treatment strategies are needed, particularly in low- and middle-income countries. Future research should focus on identifying additional risk factors and exploring the effectiveness of different preventive strategies in various regions.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-15056-1.

Introduction

Gastric cancer (GC) is one of the most common malignant tumors worldwide, with a high incidence and mortality rate, particularly in East Asia [1]. Early-onset gastric cancer (EOGC), defined as GC occurring in individuals aged 50 years or younger, has been recognized as a distinct clinical entity with unique epidemiological and pathological features [2, 3]. EOGC is often more aggressive, diagnosed at a more advanced stage, and associated with a poorer prognosis compared to GC in older populations [2]. This distinct behavior of EOGC has significant implications for public health, as it affects a younger population with potentially longer survival and greater impact on quality of life.

Despite the overall decline in gastric cancer incidence in many regions, recent studies have shown an increasing trend in the incidence of EOGC in some areas [2]. This rise in EOGC incidence is concerning, as it suggests a shift in the epidemiological pattern of gastric cancer, which may require new preventive and therapeutic strategies. The global burden of EOGC is not well characterized, with limited and outdated data hindering the development of effective public health policies and clinical interventions [2].

The Global Burden of Disease (GBD) studies provide a comprehensive and systematic approach to assessing the burden of various diseases, including cancer [4]. These studies offer valuable insights into global health trends and have been instrumental in informing health policies and resource allocation. Previous study has evaluated the incidence and mortality of EOGC (age < 50 years) from 2003 to 2017 [2], however, the disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs) was not assessed, and their analysis was not comprehensive enough. In this research, our aim was to systematically evaluate the trends in the global, regional, and national burden of EOGC from 1990 to 2021, based on the GBD 2021 data. We will examine the incidence, mortality, and DALYs of EOGC, and explore the trends and potential driving factors. Furthermore, we will project the future burden of EOGC up to 2035, providing essential information for policymakers, healthcare providers, and researchers to develop targeted interventions and improve patient outcomes.

Methods

Data sources

This study utilized data from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) Study 2021, which employs sophisticated statistical methods to address missing data and adjust for confounding factors, ensuring robust and reliable estimates. Data on the incidence, mortality, and DALYs their 95% uncertainty intervals (UIs) of EOGC were obtained from the GBD 2021 database (https://vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-results/) [4]. This research adhered to the Guidelines for Accurate and Transparent Health Estimates Reporting (GATHER) [5].

Data acquisition and preliminary analysis

We focused specifically on cases of gastric cancer diagnosed in individuals aged 15–49 years old. The age-standardized incidence rates (ASIRs), age-standardized mortality rates (ASMRs), and age-standardized DALYs rates (ASDRs) were calculated to account for differences in population age structures across countries and regions. The ASR was calculated per 100,000 individuals using the following formula:

(a.i.: the age-specific rate in ith the age group; w: the number of people in the corresponding ith age group among the standard population; A: the number of age groups).The estimated annual percentage change (EAPC) was calculated using a least squares linear regression model to assess temporal changes in ASRs [6, 7]. The regression model was formulated as ln(y)=α + βx+ϵ, where y represents the age-standardized rate, α is the intercept, x denotes the year, β is the slope, and ϵ is the normally distributed error term. The EAPC formula is (expβ − 1) ×100%.

Sociodemographic index

The Sociodemographic Index (SDI) is a composite measure that quantifies the level of development of a country or region by integrating data on fertility rates, educational attainment, and per capita income. This classification allows us to examine the relationship between the burden of EOGC and socioeconomic development, providing insights into how different levels of development may influence disease trends [8, 9].

Joinpoint regression analysis

Joinpoint regression analysis was employed to identify significant changes in the trends of EOGC incidence, mortality, and DALYs over time. The key outcome measures the annual percentage change (APC), along with its 95% confidence intervals (CIs) [10].

Decomposition analysis

Decomposition analysis was conducted to identify the primary factors contributing to changes in the EOGC disease burden. This method quantifies the independent contributions of population growth, aging, and epidemiological shifts while holding other factors constant. Each factor’s contribution to overall change is represented by black dots in the results, with positive values indicating an increase due to that factor and negative values indicating a corresponding decrease [6].

Cross-country inequality analysis

To assess health inequalities across countries, we used the slope index of inequality (SII) and the concentration index. The SII measures the absolute difference in DALYs between the highest and lowest SDI countries, while the concentration index quantifies the relative inequality in DALYs across the SDI distribution. Both indices were calculated for each year from 1990 to 2021, with trends analyzed over time [11].

Frontier analysis

Frontier analysis compares the actual burden of EOGC in each country or region to a theoretical optimal level, which represents the best possible performance given the available resources and technology. The frontier was defined using the lowest observed ASDR across all countries and regions for each year. We calculated the distance of each country or region from this frontier, this distance indicates the potential for improvement in managing EOGC burden [12].

Age-period-cohort (APC) model

The Age-Period-Cohort (APC) model was used to analyze and estimate the effects of age, period, and birth cohort on the burden of EOGC up to 2035 [13]. The model was formulated as: log(Yijk​)=µ + αi​+βj​+γk​+ϵijk​ where Yijk​ represents the observed EOGC incidence in the i-th age group, j-th period, and k-th birth cohort; µ is the overall mean; αi​, βj​, γk​ represent the age, period, and cohort effects, respectively; and ϵijk​ is the error term.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were generated for all key variables, with results presented as means and 95% UIs or 95% CIs. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered significant. Data cleaning, computation, and visualization were conducted using JD_GBDR (version 2.36.1, Jingding Medical Technology Co., Ltd.) and R software (version 4.4.2) in this study.

Results

Global level

Based on the GBD 2021 estimates, a total of 125,121 new cases (95% UI: 107,274 − 144,783) of EOGC aged 15–49 years old were reported globally, representing a 14.5% decrease from 1990. The ASIR demonstrated a slight decrease from 5.40 per 100,000 persons (95%UI: 4.68–5.89) in 1990 to 3.17 per 100,000 persons (95% UI: 2.72–3.67) in 2021 (Fig. 1). The EAPC for ASIR was − 1.8 (95% CI: −1.9 to −1.7). Mortality due to EOGC was estimated at 78,871 deaths (95% UI: 68,704 − 90,836), with an ASMR of 2.00 per 100,000 persons (95% UI: 1.74–2.30) and an EAPC of − 2.4 (95% CI: −2.5 to − 2.3). The global DALYs for EOGC in 2021 were 3,859,036 (95% UI: 3,356,411-4,428,942), with an ASDR of 97.73 per 100,000 persons (95% UI: 85.00-112.17) and an EAPC of − 2.5 (95%CI: −2.6 to − 2.4) (Supplementary Figure S1). The ASIR demonstrated notable disparities, with High-middle SDI regions experiencing the highest rate at 5.67 per 100,000 people (95% UI: 4.67–6.81), while Low SDI regions reported the lowest at 1.56 per 100,000 (95% UI: 1.22-0.81). Temporal trends in ASIR reveal divergent patterns across SDI levels, potentially indicative of varying stages of epidemiological transition. Positive relations were found between the ASIRs and the SDI (Supplementary Figure S2). High SDI regions exhibited the most substantial decline, with an EAPC of − 2.7 (95% CI: −2.8 to − 2.6), suggesting effective prevention and management strategies. The ASMR in High-middle SDI regions was 3.15 per 100,000 people (95% UI: 2.61–3.73), whereas High SDI regions reported a considerably lower rate of 1.19 per 100,000 people (95% UI: 1.14–1.31). The ASDR in High-middle SDI regions was 152.51 per 100,000 people (95% UI: 126.00, 79.62), whereas High SDI regions reported a considerably lower rate of 58.12 per 100,000 (95% UI: 55.44–63.70).

East Asia reported an ASIR of 8.67 per 100,000 people (95% UI: 6.86–11.08), while Western Sub-Saharan Africa had the lowest ASIR at 0.93 per 100,000 people (95% UI: 0.69–1.12) in 2021 (Fig. 2). These contrasting trends underscore the complex and varying dynamics of EOGC incidence across different regions, highlighting the need for targeted interventions and further research to address these disparities. Age and sex patterns

In 2021, the ASIR, ASMR, ASDR of EOGC aged 15–49 years demonstrated a progressive increase with advancing age, peaking at 45–49 years (Fig. 3). For all patients, the rates were higher in males. For subgroup analysis, at ages 15 to 34, the ASIR, ASMR, ASDR ratios for males and females were similar; however, with increasing age, the ASIR, ASMR, ASDR was consistently higher in males compared to females within the same age group. In Low SDI, Low-middle SDI regions, the ASIR, ASMR, ASDR ratios for males and females were similar; while in Middle SDI, High-middle SDI, and High SDI regions, the ASIR, ASMR, ASDR was higher in males compared to females (Table S4). Specifically, in East Asia, Eastern Europe, Central Asia, Tropical Latin America, Southern Latin America, Central Europe, Western Europe, High-income North America, and Australasia, the ASIR, ASMR, ASDR of EOGC were higher in males, while in other regions, there were no differences among genders.

Risk factors for EOGC

The study collected data on EOGC-related mortality and DALYs attributable to two risk factors: high-sodium diets and smoking, as categorized in GBD 2021. High-sodium diets and smoking affected EOGC at the.

global level, accounting for 7.8%, and 7.4% of EOGC deaths in 2021. High-sodium diets and smoking affected EOGC at the global level, accounting for 7.7% and 7.1% of EOGC DALYs in 2021, respectively.

Among the geographical regions, East Asia and Central Europe had the highest proportion of high-sodium diets-attributable deaths at 8.2%, while North Africa and Middle East had the lowest at 5.5%. Smoking also played a crucial role, with the highest proportion in Eastern Europe at 11.1%, and the lowest in Western Sub-Saharan Africa at 1.7%. As for DALYs, East Asia and Central Europe had the highest proportion of High-sodium diets-attributable DALYs at 8.2%, while North Africa and Middle East had the lowest at 5.5%. Smoking also had a substantial impact, with the highest proportion in Eastern Europe at 10.9%, and the lowest in Western Sub-Saharan Africa at 1.6%. The contribution of these risk factors to mortality and DALYs varied across SDI regions.

Decomposition analysis for EOGC

Globally, population growth and aging emerged as the primary factors driving the increasing. EOGC disease burden, while epidemiological changes helped mitigate this burden. Specifically, on a global scale, population growth and aging led to increases in DALYs due to EOGC by 122.6% and 58.1%, respectively. In contrast, epidemiological changes resulted in a 280.8% reduction in DALYs. The decomposition analysis of incidence and mortality followed similar trends.

Cross-country health inequality analysis

The results of the study showed absolute and relative income inequality in DALYs burden due to EOGC. The concentration index of DALYs ranged from − 0.01 (95%CI: −0.07 to 0.04) in 1990 to −0.16 (95%CI: −0.21 to −0.10) in 2021 (Supplementary Figure S3A). The slope index of inequality of DALYs decreased from 1.09 (95%CI: −36.27 to 38.45) in 1990 to −52.02 (95%CI: −71.18, −32.85) in 2021 (Supplementary Figure S3B). All these results demonstrated an increase in the unequal burden of EOGC. This increased inequality may be related to inadequate health care resources, and weaker implementation of protection policies in low SDI areas.

Frontier analysis

We used the 2021 DALYs and SDI to calculate the effective difference from the frontier for each country and territory. In countries exhibiting the SDI between 0.2 and 0.5, the effective difference value was relatively large. However, as the SDI increased beyond 0.5, these disparities tended to diminish. The top 15 countries with the highest effective difference from the frontier included Democratic People’s Republic of Korea, Kiribati, Marshall Islands, Micronesia, Solomon Islands, Tonga, Vanuatu, Mongolia, Bolivia, Guatemala, Afghanistan, American Samoa, Nauru, Palau, and Tuvalu. These countries have much higher EOGC DALYs rates than other countries with comparable sociodemographic resources.

Future forecasts of global burden of EOGC

The global burden of EOGC is projected to decline from 2022 to 2035 (Fig. 4). The ASIR of EOGC is expected to decrease globally from approximately 3.03 per 100,000 population in 2021 to about 2.32 per 100,000 by 2035, representing a roughly 23.4% decrease. The ASMR projected to decrease from about 1.91 per 100,000 population in 2021 to approximately 1.31 per 100,000 by 2040 (decrease by 31.4%). The ASDR for EOGC is projected to decrease from about 93.76 per 100,000 population in 2021 to approximately 63.98 per 100,000 by 2040 (decrease by 31.8%), suggesting the effect of preventive strategy. The BAPC model predicted that, the ASIR, ASMR, ASDR for the 45–49 age cohorts is expected to stabilize or plateau, contrasting with the significant reductions seen from 1990 to 2021; for other age groups, declination was predicted (Supplementary Figure S4).

Discussion

Previous studies have shown a significant rise in the proportion of GC patients aging under the age of 50 years [14, 15]. While there is disparity in incidence trends between the west and eastern countries [16]. Given the significant role of young people in society, thorough research on the unique clinical and pathological features of EOGC can help propose targeted preventive measures. The age range defining EOGC varies across the literature [17–20]. In this study, we define EOGC patients as aged 50 years and below, and evaluated the situations in individuals aged 15 to 49 years. Our comprehensive analysis provides critical insights into the epidemiological trends and potential driving factors of this disease. The findings highlight the need for targeted public health interventions and clinical strategies to address the unique challenges posed by EOGC.

Globally, the ASIR, ASMR, and ASGR of EOGC have shown decline over the past three decades, indicating overall progress in managing this disease. This trend was similar to the previous published study [1, 3, 21]. However, regional disparities remain pronounced, with East Asia consistently reporting the highest ASIR, ASMR, and ASDR. This pattern underscores the complex interplay of genetic, environmental, and lifestyle factors that contribute to the burden of EOGC in different regions [22]. As we all know that, H. pylori is widely recognized as a major risk factor for GC [23, 24]. It is estimated that nearly 90% of non-cardia GC cases are attributable to H. pylori infection [25]. In East Asia, the overall seroprevalence rate of H. pylori infection is reported to be 37.6%−51.0% [26–28]. In South Asia and Africa, despite high H. pylori seroprevalence, GC incidence remains relatively low [29]. The underlying reasons for this discrepancy remain unclear and may involve complex interactions among host, bacterial, and environmental factors. Host cytokine gene polymorphisms and the virulence of H. pylori factors have been documented as critical determinants in gastric cancer progression and patient prognosis [30]. An elevated risk of GC among individuals infected with East Asian cytotoxin associated gene A (CagA) strains was reported when compared to those infected with Western-type CagA strains [31, 32]. On the other hand, high-sodium diets and smoking are significant risk factors for EOGC. In our study, we found that high-sodium diets accounted for 7.8% of EOGC deaths and 7.7% of DALYs globally in 2021, with the highest proportions observed in East Asia and Central Europe. Smoking also played a crucial role, with the highest impact in Eastern Europe. These findings underscore the importance of public health campaigns aimed at reducing sodium intake and smoking prevalence, particularly in regions where these risk factors are most prevalent. Previous studies have identified several other modifiable risk factors for GC, including low intake of vegetables, consumption of red and processed meats, coffee, alcohol, obesity, exposure to certain chemicals and Epstein-Barr virus infection [3, 22, 33]. Future preventive strategies should focus on implementing culturally and socially appropriate interventions targeting these modifiable risk factors to reduce the incidence of GC.

The analysis of EOGC burden across different levels of the SDI revealed that regions with higher SDI values generally experienced more significant declines in EOGC incidence, mortality, and DALYs, which is inconsistent with the results of cross-country health inequality analysis. Conversely, regions with lower SDI values, particularly those in Central Latin America and Western Sub-Saharan Africa, showed minimal declines or even increases in EOGC incidence. This trend may be attributed to inadequate healthcare resources and weaker implementation of protective policies in these areas. Future work should focus on identifying additional risk factors, exploring the effectiveness of different preventive strategies in various regions, and emphasizing early screening and intervention [22, 34].

Among all countries, Palau, Mongolia, and the Republic of Korea reported the highest ASIRs in 2021, with rates of 9.95, 9.19, and 9.07 per 100,000, respectively. These high incidence rates in East Asian countries align with previous studies indicating that this region is a hotspot for GC [22, 34]. The Republic of Korea, despite having one of the highest incidence rates, has also shown significant improvements in mortality rates due to advanced screening programs and early detection initiatives. Luxembourg, and Rwanda showed the most significant decreases in ASIR, indicating successful public health interventions and improvements in healthcare infrastructure. The analysis also revealed notable disparities in ASMR and ASDR across countries. Mongolia and Palau had the highest ASMR and ASDR, indicating a significant burden of EOGC in these countries. In Mongolia, the prevalence of H. pylori infection, is notably high, about 70% among individuals presenting with gastric symptoms or diagnosed with early-stage GC [35]. Despite the high incidence of gastric cancer in Mongolia, a screening program has only recently been implemented, and thus far, satisfactory outcomes have not been achieved [36]. More attention and researches are needed for these areas. Ethiopia has achieved notable progress in the management of EOGC in the past 3 decades, with a significant decline in ASIR, ASDR and ASMR. This progress is primarily attributed to several key advancements, including human resource capacity building, the adoption of diagnostic imaging and pathological laboratory services, early detection and treatment initiatives, and surgical advancements [37]. Conversely, countries like Zimbabwe, Lesotho, and Libya experienced substantial increases in ASIR, ASMR, and ASDR, suggesting a need for urgent public health action to address the rising burden of EOGC.

The age and sex patterns of EOGC incidence and mortality further underscore the complexity of this disease. The ASIR, ASMR, and ASDR of EOGC generally increased with age, peaking in the 45–49 age group. The interesting thing is that, for ages 15 to 34, the ASIR, ASMR, ASDR ratios for males and females were similar; however, with increasing age, males has higher rates than female. The finding was similar to the previous study [2]. This sex disparity may be attributed to differences in lifestyle factors, such as higher smoking and alcohol consumption rates among males, as well as potential biological differences in susceptibility to gastric cancer. Nevertheless, one study from China has demonstrated that, in contrast to late-onset GC, there was a higher proportion of females among EOGC cases [38]. They thought that this phenomenon may be attributed to the higher levels of estrogen in young females, which are known to influence GC development through various pathways [38].

Decomposition analysis revealed that population growth and aging were the primary drivers of the increasing EOGC burden globally, while epidemiological changes helped mitigate this burden. Specifically, population growth and aging led to increases in DALYs due to EOGC by 122.6% and 58.1%, respectively. In contrast, epidemiological changes resulted in a 280.8% reduction in DALYs. This suggests that while demographic factors contribute to the overall burden, public health interventions and improvements in healthcare infrastructure can significantly reduce the impact of EOGC.

Our projections indicate that the global burden of EOGC is expected to continue declining from 2022 to 2035, with significant reductions in ASIR, ASMR, and ASDR. However, the burden is projected to stabilize or plateau in the 45–49 age cohort, highlighting the need for sustained preventive efforts. These findings provide essential information for policymakers, healthcare providers, and researchers to develop targeted interventions and improve patient outcomes.

There are some limitations in this study. First, the data on EOGC from the GBD 2021 study were derived from selected registries, often located in major cities, which may not accurately reflect the overall burden in some countries. Second, substantial random variation may occur due to the small number of patients included in the data analysis for some countries, potentially leading to insufficient statistical power. Third, only two risk factors were identified for EOGC in this analysis; other critical factors, such as H. pylori infection, were not included. Fourth, detailed information on cancer staging and different subtypes was not available for the analysis of the gastric cancer burden.

In conclusion, despite a decrease in ASIR, ASMR and ASDR in the global level, the absolute burden of EOGC remains high, with significant variations across regions, countries, and SDI regions. East Asia has the highest ASIR, ASMR, and ASDR for EOGC. Regions with higher SDI values generally experienced more significant declines in EOGC incidence and mortality, as well as in DALYs. Furthermore, sex disparities are evident in the age group of 35 to 49 years, with higher ASIR, ASMR, and ASDR observed in males. These findings underscore the need for targeted prevention and treatment strategies that address the specific needs of different populations, particularly in low- and middle-income countries. It also emphasizes the need for continued efforts to reduce risk factors, raise awareness among the general public, improve healthcare infrastructure, and address health inequalities to mitigate the global burden of EOGC.

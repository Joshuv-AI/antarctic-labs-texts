The Context of Kidney Care in Pacific Island Countries
Authors: Boris Bikbov
Published: 2025

To the Editor:

The joint editorial by Chandra et al.1 provides important highlights on kidney care in the Pacific region. Placing these findings within a broader public health and social development context requires considering diverse metrics and absolute numbers. Combining general and focused perspectives allows stakeholders to view the 16 countries of the region as “a sea of islands” rather than “islands in a far sea.”2

The countries are home to 13.4 million people (10.2 million live in Papua New Guinea), and only 2.5 million (18.6%) of them are covered by at least 1 social protection benefit. Coverage varies widely, from 4.5% in Tuvalu and 9.1% in Papua New Guinea to 100% in French Polynesia and Cook Islands. Despite progress in some nations, others (Kiribati, Vanuatu, and Fiji) experienced marked declines over the past decade.3

The multicomponent essential health services coverage index ranged from 30 in Papua New Guinea to 65 in Palau in 2021. Although most countries improved by over 10% in the past decade, coverage stagnated in Papua New Guinea, and sharply declined recently in Cook Islands and Niue.3 The index, among different components, includes metrics of hypertension and diabetes control that indirectly could indicate the management of major chronic kidney disease (CKD) risk factors. However, no data are available on access to essential medicines or their insurance coverage in the Pacific.

Absolute estimates of CKD burden are critical for planning specialized care. According to the Global Burden of Disease Study, in all 16 countries of the region, 897,200 people lived with CKD in 2019 (compared with 671,400 in 2010 and 491,000 in 2000), with high and rising age-standardized CKD rates despite relatively young population.4 CKD caused 1598 deaths in 2019,5 reflecting the gap between availability and provision of kidney replacement therapy.

These metrics, together with the insights from Chandra et al.,1 underscore the urgent need for targeted policy action. Regional and international partners must prioritize investments in primary and kidney care to build resilient, tailored to local contexts, health systems across the Pacific.

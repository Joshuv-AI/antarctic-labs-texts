Protocol for screening potential targets for the treatment of hepatocellular carcinoma based on H22 tumor-bearing mouse model
Authors: Xiaoxiang Gao, Yinghui Qiu, Wei Liao, Chao Zhao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryRegulating cancer-related microRNAs (miRNAs) may become a new generation of therapeutic modalities for cancer treatment. Here, we describe a protocol based on hepatoma-22 (H22) tumor-bearing mice to screen potential targets for treating hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). We detail the construction of H22 tumor-bearing mice and treatment with two natural compounds, Ulva lactuca L. polysaccharide (ULP) and 5-fluorouracil (5FU). We further describe the isolation of the tumor tissues for miRNA sequencing and the discovery and validation of potential miRNA gene targets against HCC.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Qiu et al.1Subject areas: Cell Biology, Cancer, Genomics

Before you begin

The following steps describe the generation of a hepatoma-22 (H22) tumor-bearing mouse model for therapeutic research. Ulva lactuca L. polysaccharide (ULP) and 5-fluorouracil (5FU) were administered to the model mice by oral gavage or intraperitoneal injection. The anti-tumor mechanisms of ULP and novel potential anti-HCC targets were identified with miRNA sequencing, RT-qPCR, and Western blotting. This approach can be used to determine the potential of other natural compounds as anticancer medicines.

Animals and H22 model generation

Male 3- to 5-week-old specific pathogen-free (SPF) Institute of Cancer Research (ICR) and Kunming (KM) mice were used in the study. Mice were acclimated for 1 week in SPF environment with the appropriate temperature and humidity. Light was controlled every 12 h to imitate diurnal fluctuations. Mice had unrestricted access to water and food during regular monitoring of health. H22 cells were injected into the peritoneal cavity of KM mice and transplanted into ICR mice to establish the H22-carrying mouse model.

CRITICAL: Growing H22 cells in mice should not exceed 8 days to prevent bloody ascites from forming. If this step lasts longer than 14 days, mice must be re-inoculated or discarded. The goal of subculture is to obtain 2 × 107 cells/mouse. Only two passes are necessary if the number of cells is adequate. A third passage is necessary if the number of cells acquired by two passes is inadequate. Tumor cell proliferation and success rate substantially accelerate when H22 cells are diluted with growth media.

Construction of H22 tumor-bearing mouse model and drug administration

Timing: days 14–21

Mice are injected with H22 cells at 2 × 107 cells/mouse and monitored daily to establish the H22 tumor-carrying mouse model. ULP and 5FU are administered to the model mice using injections and oral gavages. Other drugs or natural compounds can be administered, depending on the choice.

Inoculate the mice in the control group with 0.2 mL of normal saline.d.Weigh the mice after inoculating them.

Use both methods to administer ULP and 5FU to the Combination group. The volume of injection and gavage should equal 0.2 mL.

SampleLabel = factor(c(rep("NT", nN), rep("HCC", nT)), levels = c("NT", "HCC"))

# Target gene

target = "ENSG00000153187"

symbol = "HNRNPU"

# Build a DGEList object

dge = DGEList(counts = exp_data_final, group = SampleLabel)

# Filter

keep = rowSums(cpm(dge) > 0.5) > nN

dge_filter = dge[keep, keep.lib.sizes = FALSE]

dim(dge_filter$counts) # [1] 18672 419

# TMM normalization：The observed counts should be scaled to the library sizes before comparing the read counts.

# Get logCPM for visual display

Expected outcomes

In our published article, we chose MiR-542-3p for validation since this miRNA is upregulated to inhibit HCC progression.6 ULP increases the expression of MiR-542-3p (Figures 3A and 3B). We used miRDB and TargetScan, publicly available electronic databases, to predict new target genes of MiR-542-3p. We selected 3 genes as potential MiR-542-3p targets: heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein U (Hnrnpu), pleckstrin homology domain containing, family M, member 3 (Plekhm3), and f-box protein 9 (Fbxo9). These 3 target genes were significantly downregulated after ULP intervention (Figure 3C). We mined the TCGA database to further analyze the target genes, revealing that Hnrnpu and Fbxo9 are significantly upregulated in HCC (Figures 3D and 3F). This finding is consistent with the indication that ULP may exert its anti-tumor effect by mediating Hnrnpu and Fbxo9 downregulation. Thus, we constructed a vector overexpressing MiR-542-3p to explore the relationship between MiR-542-3p and Hnrnpu and Fbxo9. Overexpressing MiR-542-3p in HepG2 cells by transfecting them with the vector revealed that MiR-542-3p overexpression significantly inhibited Hnrnpu expression but had no effect on Fbxo9 (Figure 4A). Therefore, we verified Hnrnpu mRNA and protein levels with RT-qPCR and Western blotting, supporting that the Hnrnpu gene is a MiR-542-3p target (Figures 4B–4F). These data agree with evidence that Hnrnpu upregulation promotes HCC progression.7

If the target gene of a differential miRNA was previously unknown as tumor-associated but related to tumor suppressor genes or proto-oncogenes, design a vector overexpressing or silencing the target gene. Transfect H22 and HepG2 cells with these vectors and estimate cell viability with various assays (e.g., CCK-8, MTT, XTT, EdU, and BrdU). If a certain miRNA can reduce the viability of H22 and HepG2, this miRNA may exert its anti-tumor effect by regulating key cancer-related genes, such as tumor suppressor genes or proto-oncogenes.

Limitations

We used two natural substances, ULP and 5FU, to investigate their anti-tumor effect in H22 tumor-bearing mice by employing miRNA sequencing. Although miRNA sequences of mice and humans show high levels of similarity, they do not match completely. Hence, we cannot conclude with certainty that this natural compounds in our study also interfere with miRNA and their corresponding target genes in humans. Furthermore, our investigation lacks dual-luciferase reporters for MiR-542-3p and the Hnunpu gene to prove that MiR-542-3p controls Hnunpu expression HNUNPU.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

The culture efficiency of ascitic fluid is low, and the mouse abdomen is not enlarged (step-by-step protocol details-step 5).

Potential solution

Ensure that the number of H22 cells reaches >1.25 × 107 cells/mL and inject 0.4 mL of the cells per mouse. Verify proper intraperitoneal injection procedure. Before administering, position the mouse in dorsal recumbency with its head bent slightly downward to ensure a clear view of the abdomen. This position allows the internal organs to reposition under gravity and protects them from being punctured by the needle. Find the midline of the mouse’s abdomen and choose a location proximal to the right hind leg and thigh. Wipe the skin, wipe with ethanol and start inserting the needle at 45° to the abdomen, in line with the natural extension of the hip. Verify proper placement: inserting the needle should cause negative pressure and no reflux in the needle hub or skin trauma (e.g., bulging suggests the needle went under the skin). After injection, you can rotate the needle while withdrawing it to avoid fluid leakage.

Problem 2

Tumors in H22 tumor-bearing mice do not grow or grow slowly (step-by-step protocol details-step 5).

Potential solution

During subcutaneous injection, appropriately increase the number of H22 cells. Additionally, check the cell quantification method and pipet evenly when quantifying cells. Alternatively, use mice from a more susceptible mouse strain, such as BALB/c nude or C57BL/6 mice.

Problem 3

The mortality rate of mice during the experiment is >was 20% (step-by-step protocol details-step 9).

Potential solution

Use 5-week-old mice or slightly older that weigh >20 g for experiments. Appropriately reduce the number of tumor cells injected per mouse.

Problem 4

The experimental data show significant differences across the mouse groups after modeling (step-by-step protocol details-step 13).

Potential solution

Use mice from the same strain, supplier, animal facility, room, and cage with a similar microbiome (e.g., co-housed littermates). Ensure uniform SPF conditions. All mice should have the same age, weight (within <1 g error), and gender. Familiarize and master familiar subcutaneous injection techniques to minimize experimental errors.

Problem 5

R cannot find installed packages (step-by-step protocol details-step 16).

Potential solution

The author can successfully install the R package designed above using the command devtools::install('package'), along with command options (download.file.method = "wininet").

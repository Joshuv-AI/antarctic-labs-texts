The efficacy of blood-derived growth factor source compared to bone-derived growth factor source on bone regeneration in critical-sized rabbit calvarial bone defects
Authors: Çiğdem Karaca, Onur Koç, M. Alper Çetinkaya, Sefa B. ÇAM, Nuray Bayrakçı, Petek Korkusuz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe growth factor sources used in alveolar bone regeneration should be practical and at a level that provides maximum benefit and cost effectiveness. Autologous growth factors are easy and practical to obtain in clinical practice, but their effectiveness in bone regeneration should be assessed in vivo studies. The hypothesis of this study was that the contribution of bone derived growth factors source to bone formation is greater than that of blood-derived growth factor source.MethodsThirty male New Zealand rabbits were utilized in the experiment. Four defects of 8 mm were organized in the calvarium of each rabbit, and four treatment procedures were implemented: (1) empty defect, (2) autograft and xenograft (3) autograft+xenograft functionalized with growth factors released from autograft (4) autograft+ xenograft with injectable platelet rich fibrin (i-PRF). Rabbits were sacrificed at 4, 8 and 12 weeks of healing. Micro-computed tomography and histomorphometrically analyses were performed. The statistical difference was evaluated using two-way repeated measures analysis of variance. The significance level was set as p < 0.05.ResultsThe autograft+xenograft, autograft+xenograft functionalized with growth factors released from autograft and autograft+ xenograft with i-PRF provided comparable bone formation at 4 and 8 weeks. The autograft+xenograft with i-PRF revealed significantly higher new bone formation at 12 weeks compared to that of autograft+xenograft group. (p = 0.016) There was no difference in the new bone formation between autograft+xenograft with i-PRF and autograft+xenograft functionalized with growth factors groups. (p = 0.75) The residual graft remained comparable among treatment groups at all time points. (p = 0.05)ConclusionsThe composite graft contains growth factors obtained from i-PRF showed better new bone formation. Both blood and bone-derived growth factor sources can be utilized to functionalize the osteoconductive graft materials.

Introduction

Horizontal and vertical alveolar bone defects occurred by trauma and tumor surgery, or severe bone resorption associated with dental infection and following tooth extraction need to be repaired surgically for functional, psychological and esthetic reasons. The repair of these defects using autogenous bone is still accepted as gold standard [1]. On the other hand, a recent retrospective study has reported 20% of complication rate followed by performing alveolar augmentation with autogenous bone blocks including wound dehiscence, partial and total loss of block graft and temporary nerve damage [2]. Because of the increased morbidity rate, especially when harvesting from extraoral areas, the alternative techniques has been evaluated for the alveolar reconstruction. In comparative clinical studies, it has been reported that there is no difference either bone gain or implant survival rates in atrophic jaws reconstructed with autogenous bone blocks or other bone substitutes [3, 4]. In a review on bone grafting, it was stated that guided bone regeneration is an alternative to autogenous block grafting and recommended using the mixture of autogenous bone particles to improve the osteogenic power of graft material [5].

The mixture of autogenous bone particles with bone substitutes is a proven and reliable procedure in bone regeneration strategies [6] because the autogenous bone contains vital bone cells and releases the growth factors [7, 8]. A proteomic analysis showed that 43 different bone tissue-specific growth factors were expressed from the autogenous bone [9]. These osteoinductive properties of autogenous bone that not only affects undifferentiated mesenchymal cells but also improves the properties of an osteoconductive bone substitutes provides more benefits in bone regeneration. In this way, the osteoinductive properties of the bone substitutes are increased in bone regeneration process, and postoperative morbidity and resorption rate are decreased due to autogenous bone harvesting. In this scenario, the osteoconductive properties of bone substitutes, and how and how much osteoinduction is achieved from particulated autogenous bone gain importance [10].

Autologous platelet concentrates (APCs) contain blood derived growth factors and have been used for the soft and hard tissue regeneration over 25 years [11]. Various generations of APCs have been developed both to enhance the amount and quality of growth factors it releases [12, 13], to eliminate the undesired effects and to provide an easy application in clinical practice. Injectable platelet rich fibrin (i-PRF), which is in a liquid form, is accepted as one of the recent modifications of second generation of APCs [14]. A recent systematic review has investigated the effectiveness of i-PRF and platelet rich plasma (PRP), which is the first generation of autologous platelet concentrates and is also in a liquid form, and suggested that i-PRF offered a more sustained and prolonged release of growth factors compared to PRP [15]. In addition, concentrated platelet rich fibrin (C-PRF) and i-PRF were found to promote greater differentiation and mineralization activity than PRP in human osteoblasts in a recent study [16]. In animal and clinical studies, bone substitutes with i-PRF have histomorphometrically observed better bone regeneration compared to those without i-PRF [17, 18].

In clinical practice, the use of bone substitute with growth factor sources may diminish the need of autogenous bone graft. A recent study showed that a condition medium prepared from bone marrow mesenchymal stromal cells (BMSC) presented a more favorable proteomic profile compared to that of leukocyte and platelet rich fibrin (L-PRF) in terms of bone regeneration [19]. However, there is a need to identify growth factor sources that are cost-effective, easy to implement and practical in preclinical studies. Therefore, the aim of this study was to investigate the contribution in the bone regeneration of bone substitute functionalized with blood-derived and bone-derived growth factor sources in rabbit calvaria defects by histomorphometry and micro-CT evaluation. The hypothesis of this study was that the contribution of bone derived growth factor source to bone formation is greater than that of blood-derived growth factor source.

Materials and methods

Study design and variables

This prospective in vivo study with dependent variables (treatment methods and healing periods) was approved by Hacettepe University Animal Experimentations Local Ethics Board (approval number 2021/08). The study protocol followed the ARRIVE guideline for preclinical animal model [20]. In total, 30 male New Zealand white rabbits (3.0–4.0 kg in weights) were utilized in this experiment. These experimental animals were obtained from Abdeham Experimental Animal Production and Supply Center (Bursa/TURKEY). The sample size was estimated by G*Power software. Seven rabbits were needed in the present study for a statistical power of 95% and an alpha level of 0.05. Assuming a dropout rate of 14%, each group included ten rabbits in this study. The calculation of the sample size per healing period (4th, 8th, and 12th weeks) was based on the 12th week, considering the referenced article [21].

The animals were individually kept and numbered in cages for 15 days before surgical procedures under standardized room temperature, humidity, and ventilation with ad libitum access to food and drinking water at Hacettepe University Laboratory Animals Research and Application Centre. The general health and physical conditions of animals, such as the reduction in food and water intake, postoperative pain or discomport and postoperative infection were followed both preoperatively and postoperatively. Animals were excluded from the study when the veterinarian (A.Ç) confirmed an inappropriate situation.

Preparation of growth factor sources and surgical procedure

The blood was collected before the anesthetic agents were administered to prepare i-PRF, therefore the rabbits were placed in a restainer. Then, 10 mL of autologous blood was drawn from the ear vein of each rabbit using a 24-gauge yellow vein catheter, needle system, and red vacuum blood collection tube. The blood was centrifuged at 700 rpm for 3 min to separate the plasma from the red cells [22] (Fig. 1A-B).

Following the acquisition of i-PRF, rabbits were anesthetized with 2–3 mg/kg xylazine hydrochloride (Xylazinbio 2%, Bioveta, Czech Republic) and 100 mg/kg ketamine hydrochloride (Keta-Control 25 ml, Arion Pharma, İstanbul) by intramuscular injection. The cranium was shaved and scrubbed using povidone-iodine solution (Batticon, Adeka, Turkey) and then covered with a sterile drape. The full-thickness incision with approximately 4 cm in length was performed from the nasal bone along the mid-sagital crest under asepsis and antisepsis. After the flap elevation and exposing the cranium, the particulated autograft was harvested with a bone scraper. The autogenous bone was collected in a glass petri dish.(Fig. 1C) A mixture of 0.2 cc blood taken from the ear vein and 0.2 cc saline solution was added to the particulated autogenous bone, and left it for 20 min to form bone-derived growth factor source [23, 24] (Fig. 1D) At the end of the 20 min, the liquid mixture was collected using an insulin injector and mixed with xenograft particles in a separate glass petri dish and left for another 20 min to allow the xenograft adsorbing growth factors released from autogenous bone [24]. During the same period, the prepared i-PRF was mixed with xenograft particles, and the xenograft was brought into contact with the growth factors released from the blood. (Fig. 1E)

Then four round defects with 8 mm in inner and 9 mm in outer diameter were created using a trephine bur in each rabbit calvarium. One defect was left empty as a control group (n = 10) (Group-A), and the autograft+xenograft (positive control) (n = 10) (Group-B), the autograft+xenograft with i-PRF (n = 10) (Group-C) and the autograft+xenograft functionalized with growth factors released from autograft (n = 10) (Group-D) were placed one of four defects. In the experimental groups, the particulated autograft was harvested from calvarium using a bone scraper, and Geistlich Bio-Oss® (0.25–1 mm, Wolhusen, Switzerland) was used as particulated xenograft. The volume of the bone graft material put into the defects was measured using the water displacement method [25], and all of them were applied to the defects in the same volumes (0.2 cc autogenous bone and 0.2 cc xenograft).

The wound was closed with a resorbable suture material (3.0 Vicryl suture, Ethicon, Johson&Jonhson). They were administered 0.1 mg/kg butorphanol (Butomidor 10 mg/ml, Richter Pharma, Austria) and 2.5 mg/kg enrofloxacin (Baytril-K 5%, Bayer, USA) preoperatively, and the antibiotic administration were kept for 3–4 days postoperatively. Every rabbit was placed in a restrainer at 4, 8 and 12 weeks of healing after the surgical procedure. Then they were sacrificed with 20 ml propofol (Propofol 200 mg/20 ml) administered intravenously for microtomography and histomorphometry. The primary outcome was to define the new bone formation and residual graft volume in the defect areas to evaluate the effect of autogenous growth factor sources.

Micro-Computed Tomography (Micro-CT) analysis

The rabbits were sacrificed after 4, 8, and 12 weeks of post-surgery. Tissue blocks along with surrounding 1 mm bone were harvested and fixed in 10% buffered neutral formalin solution at room temperature. The specimens were scanned using micro-CT (Brucker Skyscan 1272, Bruker, Kontich, Belgium) at a voltage of 70 kV, current of 110 mA and a pixel resolution of 21 μm. The raw images were processed in DICOM format, and the region of interest (ROI) was reconstructed using software (Nrecon, Skyscan). The images were imported into CTan software for the analysis, and the new bone volume (NBV, mm3), total volume (TV, mm3), the percentage of NBV to TV (NBV/TV, %), residual graft volume (RGV, mm3), the percentage of RGV to TV (RGV/TV, %) were calculated within the volume of interest (VOI).

Histology and histomorphometric analysis

Following micro-CT, the samples were decalcified in a controlled manner using De Castro’s solution (chloral hydrate, nitric acid and distilled water), washed in distilled water, and embedded in paraffin blocks following routine histological tissue processing in an automated device with vacuum. The serial sections with 3–5 micrometers (µm) in thick were taken using a slide microtome, stained with Hematoxylin-Eosin (HE) and visualized under a light microscope (DM6B, Leica, Germany). High resolution micrographs were obtained on the attached camera (DFC7000T, Leica, Germany). New bone formation in the calvarial defect in micro-CT was evaluated quantitatively [26, 27]. Briefly, the amount of new bone in the defect area, the total defect area and the amount of residual graft were measured and the percentage of new bone and residual graft in the defect area were quantitatively calculated using image analysis software (ImageJ, v1.52i, USA). Measurements were centered on the defect area in the samples in all groups. The histologic and histomorphometric analysis were blindly performed by P.K and S.B.Ç.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 23.0 (Released 2015. Armonk, NY: IBM Corp.). The main effects and interaction of treatment methods and healing periods on variables of interest were evaluated using two-way repeated measures analysis of variance. In addition, mean±standard deviation as descriptive statistics and partial eta-square (η2) as effect size were presented. Assumptions of the two-way repeated measures ANOVA were evaluated for each variable including the sphericity and normality of the residuals. In case of the significance was detected, post-hoc test with Bonferroni adjustment was performed for pairwise comparison. The significance level was set as p < 0.05.

The intra-class correlation coefficient (ICC) was calculated to determine the reliability of the micro-CT measurements that were performed by the same researcher (Ç.K) at the two different time points. The reliability of the measurements was defined as “poor” when the ICC was < 0.40, as “acceptable” when the ICC was between 0.40 and 0.70, and “good” when values were > 0.70.

Results

One hundred and twenty defects in 30 rabbits were treated surgically as planned. However, 2 rabbits were lost after surgery. Twenty-eight animals remained healthy without any major complication. The defect sites were free from infection or necrosis. The samples were retrieved after 4, 8 and 12 weeks. Statistical analysis was performed using data from 27 rabbits, arranged in 3 time periods with 9 samples in each group, and data from 1 rabbit was randomly excluded from the study.

Micro-CT analysis

The treatment method was an effective factor both in the NBV/TV (p = 0.027) and in the RBV/TV (p = 0.044).

The Group-B showed the highest NBV/TV on average (26.84 ± 10.37) followed Group-C (20 ± 5.94) and Group-D (19.3 ± 4.73), with a significantly different from the Group-A (control group) (11.73 ± 5.94) (p = 0.001, p = 0.002, p = 0.000; respectively), indicating a strong effect size (partial ƞ2=0.77). In addition, Group-B was significantly different from Group-D (19.30 ± 4.73). (p = 0.03) (Fig. 3).

Histology and histomorphometric analysis

The cranial defect area was filled with varying degrees of vascularization, fibrous connective tissue and occasionally new bone spicules in all treatment groups. However, the percentage of new bone formation within the defect did not reach 50% in any of the groups at 12 weeks of healing.

The treatment methods, healing periods and interaction between the treatment methods and healing periods were histomorphometrically found as effective independent factors in NBV/TV (p = 0.000, partial η2 = 0.87; p = 0.007, partial η2 = 0.46; p = 0.005, partial η2 = 0.31, respectively).

All the experimental groups presented significantly higher ossification rates when compared to control group in all healing periods. (p = 0.001, p = 0.002, p = 0.003 at 4 weeks; p = 0.000, p = 0.001, p = 0.000 at 8 weeks; p = 0.004, p = 0.000, p = 0.000 at 12 weeks).

NBV/TV also showed similar in the Group-B (26.20±7.4), Group-C (26.43±8.7) and Group-D (22.35±8.17) groups at 8 weeks of healing (p = 1.00 and p = 1.00, respectively). However, at 12 weeks, Group-C achieved a higher percentage of new bone formation (23.41±6.88) when compared to Group-B (12.47± 5.15). (p = 0.016) It was not found statistically significant difference in NBV/TV between the Group-C (19.14±5.78) and Group-D (23.41±6.88) groups at 12 weeks. (p = 0.75) (Table 1) (Fig. 4).

Discussion

The results of this study show that bone-derived and blood-derived growth factor sources seem to functionalize xenograft in terms of early and late bone regeneration processes. The autograft+xenograft group was the positive control to estimate the bone formation. In the other experimental groups, xenograft particles were exposed with blood-derived and bone-derived growth factor sources (i-PRF and autogenous bone) for an equal period of times and then were mixed with the autograft to place into the defects. The histomorphometric results reveal that the i-PRF group showed significant new bone formation compared to the empty defect (Group-A, negative control) and autograft+xenograft (Group-B, positive control) groups at 12 weeks of healing.

In an in vitro study by orthopedic surgeons, the amount of growth factors released from reaming debris, iliac crest, reaming irrigation fluid and PRP were evaluated using ELISA method. Although the growth factors were calculated elevated levels in the reaming debris compared to iliac crest graft, it was not found any statistically difference in the level of IGF, TGF-β1 and BMP-2 between the reaming irrigation fluid and PRP [28]. In this study, iliac crest and reaming debris contained growth factors obtained from bone while PRP contained growth factors from blood, such as i-PRF, which used in the current study. However, in this study, both liquid growth factor sources (the reaming irrigation fluid and PRP) and bone-derived growth factor sources (reaming debris and iliac crest graft) were evaluated separately. In the current study, when used blood and bone-derived growth factor sources, no significant difference in bone regeneration was observed histomorphometrically in early and late bone healing periods. Shanbhag et al. functionalized collagen membranes using conditioned medium prepared from L-PRF and BMSC, implanted them into rat calvaria, and investigated the new bone formation using micro-CT and histomorphometric methods. While significantly greater hybrid bone formation was observed in the MSC-conditioned medium group, total new bone formation was similar in both groups [19].

Growth factors released from autogenous bone have not only a crucial effect in cellular differentiation and proliferation by affecting regional osteoblasts, osteoclasts, and undifferentiated mesenchymal cells in an autocrine manner [29] but also the growth factors released from autogenous bone were determined to have a paracrine effect on xenograft material and collagen barrier membrane [30, 31]. This paracrine characteristic is one of the important factors affecting the residual volume of a bone substitute in the composite graft at the end of the bone healing process. In this study, in all healing periods, RGV/TV was found highest in the Group-B (positive control group) by histomorphometry and micro-CT, and the Group-D showed the lowest RGV/TV with a significantly different from the positive control group in micro-CT. Consequently, this study may suggest that both blood and bone-derived growth factor sources functionalized the osteoconductive xenograft and contributed to bone regeneration. In a different in vivo study, growth factor source obtained from mesenchymal stem cells was mixed with β-tricalcium phosphate and it was placed into the rabbit maxillary sinus. Likewise, the β-tricalcium phosphate with growth factor group showed more bone formation at 2 and 4 weeks of healing compared to β-tricalcium phosphate group, while it was not found any difference at 8 weeks of healing [32]. Similar results have been also demonstrated in the studies performed with i-PRF. In a case series, the clinical and radiographical parameters were indicated the curing of periodontal defects using allograft with i-PRF and i-PRF coated collagen membrane [33]. In addition, Tony et al. reported better bone width gain in guided bone regeneration when they used xenograft combination with i-PRF without a collagen membrane in a randomized clinical trial [34].

It was determined an inconsistency in terms of new bone regeneration between the results of micro-CT and histomorphometric analysis in the present study. In all healing periods, NBV/TV was observed the highest in the positive control group according to micro-CT results, while higher bone formation was detected in the other experimental groups by means of histomorphometric results. It is thought that there may be two reasons for this inconsistency: firstly, the density of the particulated autograft used in the groups was similar the density of the newly formed bone so it was very difficult to distinguish newly formed bone in the treatment groups in micro-CT, even if the threshold was adjusted during the measurements. Therefore, the calculated bone in the VOI area included the particulated autograft, xenograft, and newly formed bone in micro-CT. However, newly formed bone could be easily calculated with the presence of osteocytes in their lacunae in the histomorphometrically. Secondly, both bone and blood-derived growth factor sources improved the osteoconductive properties of xenograft and increased the bone formation-resorption balance in the defects of the treatment groups. Thus, in the group using only autograft+xenograft (Group-B), the graft materials remained unchanged in both early and late healing periods, which led to higher volume in the micro-CT analysis. The lack of correlation between volumetric and histomorphometric results has been also reported in clinical studies in literature. In a clinical study, bone derived growth factors obtained from bone marrow stem cells were mixed with β-tricalcium phosphate that was used for maxillary sinus lifting in partially edentulous patients, and compared the sinus lifting performed with only β-tricalcium phosphate. After 6 months postoperatively, Hounsfield unit (HU) values were investigated by computerized tomography, and bone biopsy was performed for the histomorphometric examination. The bone formation occurred significantly higher in the β-tricalcium phosphate+growth factors in histomorphometric evaluation, while HU values were comparable between β-tricalcium phosphate with and without growth factors groups [35].

One of the limitations of present study was that either bone-derived and blood-derived growth factors or whether xenograft was functionalized by these growth factors were not determined by analysis such as ELISA, PCR or immunohistochemistry. However, both blood-derived and bone-derived growth factor sources were prepared according to references in the literature, in which confirmed the expression of growth factors [9, 36] and the xenograft functionalized with bone-derived growth factors [30]. The other limitation was the constant exposure time of xenograft with blood and bone-derived growth factor sources. In this study, xenograft was mixed with growth factor sources separately and waited for 20 min. Additional in vitro studies comparing the quantity of growth factors released from autogenous bone and blood at different time points may be useful to enhance the osteoconductive properties of the xenografts used in bone regenerative processes. In addition, the xenograft used in this study was a slowly absorbing graft material and might diminish the wound healing [37, 38]. This could be the other reason why RGV/TV did not significantly decrease over time. Therefore, different bone graft substitutes may be also tested to investigate the efficacy of bone and blood derived growth factor sources in different studies.

Conclusion

Two different autogenous growth factor sources used in this study showed that both did not differ from each other on the volumes of new bone regeneration and residual graft material. However, the addition of i-PRF to the composite graft has been found to improve the new bone formation compared to the composite graft without i-PRF at late healing period. Moreover, blood and bone-derived growth factor sources can be utilized to functionalize the osteoconductive graft materials so that residual graft volume decreases over time. Further, in vitro and in vivo studies are needed to confirm the results of this study and to explain the role of bone and blood derived growth factors in bone regeneration.

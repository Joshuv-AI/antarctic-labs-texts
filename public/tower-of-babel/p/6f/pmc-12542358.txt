Intracardiopulmonary migration of an inferior vena cava filter and concurrent coronary artery disease: a rare surgical challenge
Authors: Mohammad Esmail Gheydari, Sepide Javankiani, Ali Nasrollahizadeh, Amir Nasrollahizadeh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionInferior vena cava (IVC) filters are widely used to prevent pulmonary embolism (PE), particularly in patients with contraindications to anticoagulation or those experiencing recurrent thromboembolism. Although rare, filter migration to the heart or pulmonary arteries, although rare, can result in life-threatening complications such as arrhythmias, valvular dysfunction, or perforation. Coronary artery disease (CAD) remains a major cause of morbidity and mortality, and its coexistence with a migrated IVC filter presents unique diagnostic and therapeutic challenges. Imaging tests were ordered after an ECG and echocardiography revealed right heart strain. The mid-segment of the LAD has 80% stenosis, according to coronary angiography. The surgery was successful, and the patient recovered well with no residual complications.Conclusion (clinical key message)IVC filter migration is a rare but serious complication that requires prompt recognition and intervention. Introduction

Pulmonary embolism (PE) contributes significantly to the global cardiovascular disease burden, with an estimated annual incidence of 1 case per 1000 people [1]. Inferior vena cava (IVC) filters are widely used to prevent PE, particularly in patients with contraindications to anticoagulation or those experiencing recurrent thromboembolism despite optimal anticoagulation therapy [2]. However, these devices are not without complications, including thrombosis, fracture, tilt, or migration. Among these complications, intracardiopulmonary migration is exceedingly rare, occurring in less than 1% of cases, which can result in life-threatening complications such as arrhythmias, cardiac tamponade, valvular dysfunction, or perforation of cardiac structures [3]. Despite the rarity of this complication, the potential morbidity and mortality necessitate prompt recognition and management.

In contrast, coronary artery disease (CAD) is a frequent and well-established cardiovascular condition that remains the leading cause of mortality globally [4]. Although both conditions—IVC filter migration and significant CAD—are independently critical, their coincident occurrence in a single patient represents an exceptionally rare and challenging scenario.

In this case report, we report a rare coincidental occurrence of migrated IVC filters and significant CAD of a 65-year-old woman with a history of PE. To the best of our knowledge, this is among the first documented instances of concurrent administration of these two entities.

Case presentation

A 65-year-old woman with a history of diabetes mellitus managed with insulin, hypertension treated with Amlodipine/Valsartan (80 mg/5 mg daily), and dyslipidemia controlled with Atorvastatin (20 mg daily) presented to our center with atypical chest pain and dyspnea on exertion (functional class II).

Additionally, she had a significant history of tongue squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), which was discovered five months earlier and for which she had a cervical lymphadenectomy and surgical excision. She experienced acute PE and cardiac arrest four months ago, one month after surgical management of SCC. After a successful cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR), she was sent to the intensive care unit (ICU) and given heparin for therapeutic anticoagulation.

However, anticoagulation therapy was complicated by a significant neck hematoma, necessitating the placement of an IVC filter via the right femoral vein to prevent recurrent thromboembolism. She was subsequently transitioned to warfarin (5 mg, half a tablet daily).

Shortly after IVC filter placement, the patient experienced another cardiac arrest, this time due to ventricular tachycardia (VT), requiring brief CPR and ICU readmission. After stabilization and an uneventful recovery, she was eventually discharged.

Current presentation

The patient presented to our center with persistent atypical chest pain and dyspnea on exertion F.C II which was worse lately. She denied fever, syncope, or palpitations. Vital signs on admission were stable: blood pressure 130/80 mmHg, heart rate 86 bpm, respiratory rate 14 breaths per minute, and oxygen saturation of 92% on room air. Management

The patient experienced occasional premature ventricular contractions (PVCs) and a VT event on telemetry, but she remained hemodynamically stable throughout her hospital stay. The multidisciplinary team had an urgent discussion due to the risky location of the migrating IVC filter and the potential for deadly consequences like embolization or RV blockage. A decision was made to perform surgical retrieval of the migrated IVC filter and simultaneously address the significant LAD stenosis.

The patient was taken to the operating room for surgical intervention under general anesthesia. A median sternotomy was chosen as the optimal surgical approach. This incision provides the necessary exposure to both the heart and great vessels, allowing simultaneous access for coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) and retrieval of the migrated IVC filter. The left internal mammary artery (LIMA) was carefully dissected and prepared for grafting. A right atriotomy was made parallel to the atrioventricular groove. Under direct vision and ×4 surgical loupe magnification, the OptEase filter was identified entangled in the chordae and papillary muscles of the tricuspid valve. With meticulous dissection, the filter was successfully extracted without damaging the valvular or myocardial structures (Fig. 3). All visible struts were extracted, and irrigating the RVOT flushed any microscopic fragments into the venous return filter. Given the presence of significant mid-segment stenosis of the LAD artery, the CABG procedure was simultaneously performed. The LIMA was anastomosed to the mid-LAD to restore coronary perfusion. The anastomosis was tested by releasing the cross-clamp and observing immediate graft pulsatility.

The retrieved IVC filter

Hemostasis was meticulously established before the chest closure, and the treatment was carried out without any problems. For postoperative treatment, the patient was then transferred to the intensive care unit (ICU). Her blood pressure was 130/80 mmHg and her heart rate was 95 bpm when she arrived at the intensive care unit.

Follow-up

Postoperatively, the patient recovered well with no significant complications. Follow-up echocardiography showed that RV dysfunction had resolved, and fluoroscopic scans showed no remaining filter pieces. She was released in a stable condition. She was discharged in stable condition. This case serves as an example of the uncommon and possibly lethal migration of the IVC filter to the cardiopulmonary system, with concomitant LAD lesion. It underscores how crucial it is to closely monitor patients who have IVC filters and to seek immediate multidisciplinary care if problems develop. She showed clinical improvements after surgery and using medication. At her 6-month follow-up, TTE indices were favorable. She did not have dyspnea, diaphoresis, or chest discomfort during the one-year follow-up.

Discussion

IVC filters are commonly employed to prevent PE in patients with contraindications to anticoagulation or those experiencing recurrent thromboembolic events despite adequate medical therapy [5]. One of the most severe yet rare complications is the migration of the IVC filter to the heart or pulmonary arteries, known as intracardiopulmonary migration. This event can lead to life-threatening conditions such as cardiac tamponade, ventricular arrhythmias, or perforation of cardiac structures [6].

A recent systematic review by Bai et al. provides an updated review of intracardiopulmonary migration of IVC filters. The review emphasizes that while such migrations are uncommon, they carry significant morbidity and mortality risks. In the included studies, 24% of patients were asymptomatic at the time of diagnosis, while nearly 60% of cases presented with chest pain. Additionally, Myocardial and valvular damage were the most common complications, occurring in 30.1% of patients. They advocate for prompt recognition and management of this complication to mitigate adverse outcomes [7].

The treatment approach addressing migrated or fractured IVC filters is based on the location of the IVC filter, current symptoms, and surgical risk assessment. In most cases, the heart multidisciplinary team will decide to manage the condition via an endovascular approach, due to minimal invasiveness, reduced hospital length of stay, and lower morbidity [8].

Additionally, Sako et al. documented a successful endovascular removal of an IVC filter fragment from the right ventricle, highlighting the effectiveness of endovascular treatment even in RV ventricle [9]. However, this approach carries procedural risks such as arrhythmia, perforation, or valve injury and may be unsuitable in complex cases and surgical retrieval, although invasive, allows for definite management of complications and allows for direct vision, specifically in patients with concomitant coronary disease [7].

In our patient, regarding the coincidence of critical LAD stenosis amenable to LIMA grafting and a filter entangled in the tricuspid apparatus, a one-stage CABG plus surgical filter retrieval provided the most reliable and safe outcome.

To the best of our knowledge, our case report is the first article demonstrating the coincidence of CAD and a migrated IVC filter. The novelty of our manuscript lies in the detailed account of the clinical decision-making process, the surgical strategy employed, and the successful outcome achieved. While previous reports have documented cases of IVC filter migration, the concomitant management of significant coronary artery disease in this context is less frequently described. Our report adds valuable insights into the safe and effective management of such complex cases.

In conclusion, this case emphasizes how crucial it is to closely monitor patients with migrated IVC filters. It also emphasizes the need for a thorough, interdisciplinary approach to addressing IVC filter migration issues, particularly when they are linked to other serious cardiovascular diseases. Our experience is consistent with recent research that emphasizes the need for early detection and treatment to enhance patient outcomes in intracardiopulmonary IVC filter migration.

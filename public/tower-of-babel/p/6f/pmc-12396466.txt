Human papillomavirus vaccination willingness and influencing factors among women in China: A systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Jinghui Sun, Suyun Dong, Jie Gong, Juan Xie, Haiou Yan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aims to comprehensively review the human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccination willingness among Chinese women and explore the factors influencing their vaccination intentions.MethodsA comprehensive systematic search was conducted across nine electronic databases—China National Knowledge Infrastructure, Wanfang Data Knowledge Service Platform, VIP Journal Integration Service Platform, SinoMed, PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science—from database inception to February six, 2025, to identify studies examining HPV vaccine acceptance among Chinese women.ResultsThe pooled willingness to receive the HPV vaccine among Chinese women was estimated at 65.7 % (95 % CI: 55.2 %–76.2 %). Subgroup analyses indicated higher intent among women with a college education or above (71.1 % versus 60.1 %), urban residents (68.3 % versus 56.0 % in rural areas), southern China residents (69.0 % versus 59.7 % in northern regions), individuals with medical-related backgrounds (84.2 % versus 35.7 %), and those with prior HPV or vaccine knowledge (66.1 %/76.4 % versus 50.2 %/57.8 %), Willingness was also higher among women with a family cancer history (74.5 % versus 55.3 %), and those impacted by COVID-19 (67.5 % versus 57.5 %). Anonymous questionnaires yielded higher willingness (71.8 % versus. 58.8 %). Other influencing factors included age, attitudes toward premarital sex, and awareness of HPV risks and vaccine benefits.ConclusionsChinese women's overall willingness to receive the HPV vaccine remains below the World Health Organization (WHO)’s 90 % target, with significant disparities across subpopulations. Targeted public health efforts are urgently needed to enhance vaccine awareness and acceptance, especially among women in rural or underdeveloped areas, with lower education, non-medical backgrounds, or no family history of cancer.Keywords: Women, Human papillomavirus vaccine, China, Systematic review, meta-analysis

Introduction

Cervical cancer ranks fourth among women's cancers globally, with an estimated 660,000 new cases and 350,000 deaths in 2022 (Castle et al., 2021). Incidence and mortality are markedly higher in developing countries. In China, 150,700 new cases and 55,700 deaths were reported in 2022, ranking fifth and sixth among female malignancies. Notably, rates have risen among women aged 15–49, ranking third and second in this group (Bray et al., 2024; Han et al., 2024). The most significant increase occurred in women aged 40–44, with incidence up 73.1 % from 1990 to 2019 (Li et al., 2017).

Nearly all cervical cancer cases result from chronic infection with high-risk human papillomavirus (HPV), mainly types 16 and 18 (Rahangdale et al., 2022). While most infections are cleared by the immune system, about 10 % persist, leading to viral gene integration and malignant transformation of cervical epithelial cells (Zhao et al., 2024). HPV, primarily transmitted through sexual contact, is also associated with oropharyngeal, anal, vaginal, vulvar, and penile cancers, as well as genital warts (Swift et al., 2022a). Up to 80 % of sexually active women may contract HPV during their lifetime, especially at younger ages (Chesson et al., 2014; Voidazan et al., 2016). Preventing HPV infection is essential to reducing cervical cancer and other HPV-related diseases.

In 2018, the World Health Organization (WHO) launched the Global Strategy to Eliminate Cervical Cancer, setting “90–70-90” targets for 2030: 90 % of girls fully vaccinated against HPV by age 15, 70 % of women screened at least twice by age 45, and 90 % of those diagnosed receiving appropriate treatment (Singh et al., 2023). HPV vaccination, a cornerstone of this strategy, is safe, immunogenic, and effective in preventing cervical cancer (Gao et al., 2025). It is primarily recommended for girls aged nine-14, with extended coverage for women aged nine-45 and men aged nine-26 to enhance joint prevention. Vaccination is also advised for individuals with existing HPV infections, prior HPV-related diseases, high-risk behaviors, or immunodeficiency (Galvin et al., 2023).

Since 2006, Australia has approved and implemented HPV vaccines through its National HPV Vaccination Program. Between 2007 and 2017, vaccination coverage among 15-year-olds steadily increased, reaching 80.2 % for girls and 75.9 % for boys by 2017. This widespread uptake has led to a 92 % reduction in HPV six/11/16/18 infections among young women, fewer cases of genital warts, a 30 % decrease in cervical cytology abnormalities, and a 17 % decline in precancerous cervical lesions in women aged 25–29 (Swift et al., 2022b). These outcomes highlight the importance of high vaccine coverage for herd immunity. In contrast, HPV vaccination rates in China remain low. National coverage is under 10 %, significantly trailing the 60–80 % rates seen in many Western countries (Lin et al., 2024; Lou et al., 2024).

As of December 2023, 141 of the 194 WHO member countries (73 %) have included HPV vaccination in their National Immunization Plans (Gao et al., 2025). Since 2016, both imported (bivalent, quadrivalent, and nine-valent) and domestically developed bivalent HPV vaccines have been approved in mainland China (Gong et al., 2024). However, HPV vaccination coverage among Chinese women remains low. As of 2020, only 2.24 % of women aged nine-45 were fully vaccinated, compared to a global average of 15 %, highlighting a significant gap (Branda et al., 2024).

China's HPV vaccination policy has gradually evolved, but the vaccine is still not part of the national immunization plan (Yim et al., 2024). Only a few regions offer free vaccination to select groups, while most women must pay out-of-pocket. A demonstration project in Shenzhen aimed to support local vaccine inclusion and address implementation barriers (Wu et al., 2023; Chang et al., 2024). Limited public funding, age restrictions, and low awareness and willingness among women remain major challenges to improving vaccination rates and advancing cervical cancer prevention (Branda et al., 2024).

While many studies have examined Chinese women's willingness to receive the HPV vaccine, most are limited to specific regions or populations, with inconsistent findings and a lack of systematic analysis. This article aims to systematically review and meta-analyze existing research to assess vaccination intentions and identify key influencing factors, providing a foundation for evidence-based public health policies and targeted vaccination strategies.

Methods

Study registration

This meta-analysis and systematic review adhere to the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) guidelines. The research protocol has been registered with PROSPERO (registration code CRD42025647961). According to the protocol, we initially planned to use Stata 18 software for data synthesis and meta-analysis. However, due to limitations in the included studies, some data could not be quantitatively analyzed. As a result, we performed a qualitative analysis of these influencing factors and synthesized the findings. To ensure transparency, this deviation from the protocol is noted here.

Search strategy

Computerized searches were performed across nine databases, including China National Knowledge Infrastructure, Wanfang Data Knowledge Service Platform, VIP Journal Integration Service Platform, SinoMed, PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science, with search dates spanning from the inception of each database through February six, 2025. A combination of MeSH (Medical Subject Headings) terms and keywords was used and adapted to suit the specific characteristics of each database. The search strategy included the following MeSH terms and keywords: (China, our country, female, female college students), (HPV vaccine, cervical cancer vaccine, human papillomavirus vaccine), and (willingness, intention, attitude, acceptance, refusal, hesitation). The search was adjusted according to the specific search mechanisms of each database. Detailed search formulas for each database are provided in Table 1 of the Supplementary material.

Selection criteria

Eligibility criteria

Studies were included if they met the following criteria: (1) Cross-sectional studies conducted with the Chinese female population; (2) Studies focusing on the willingness rate and factors influencing HPV vaccination among Chinese women; (3) Studies using questionnaire surveys for data collection; (4) Studies with results published in Chinese or English.

Exclusion criteria

Studies were excluded if they met the following criteria: (1) Animal studies, reviews, commentaries, conference abstracts, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and case reports; (2) Studies with a quality score below six points; (3) Studies with subjects from Hong Kong, Macau, or Taiwan; (4) Duplicate publications or those lacking data; (5) Studies with a sample size under 300 participants.

Outcomes

Primary outcome

The primary outcome is the willingness rate of Chinese women to receive the HPV vaccine. The study will report the proportion of Chinese women willing to receive the HPV vaccine, as well as the number of women willing or unwilling to receive it. The willingness to receive vaccination will be assessed using a validated survey questionnaire. (In this study, vaccination willingness was defined as either (a) self-reported intention to vaccinate, or (b) having received ≥ one dose of the vaccine).

Secondary outcomes

The factors influencing Chinese women's willingness to receive the HPV vaccine primarily include: age, place of residence, education level, family income, whether relatives or friends have been diagnosed with cancer, whether they have attended cervical cancer prevention seminars, awareness of HPV and HPV-related knowledge, familiarity with the HPV vaccine and its associated information, sexual activity, and attitudes toward premarital sex.

Study selection

The literature was imported into EndNote 21 to remove duplicates, followed by independent screening of the literature by two researchers, who cross-checked the title, abstract, and full text. Any discrepancies were resolved by a third researcher.

Data extraction and management

Data extraction was performed by two researchers, with any disagreements resolved through discussion or reference to the original text. The following data were extracted: (1) Basic information, including first author, publication year, data collection year, research type, language, sampling method, measurement tools, and literature quality; (2) Baseline characteristics of the population, such as sample size, sample type, region, and age; (3) Outcome measures, vaccination willingness rate, and influencing factors, which are recorded in the table titled “Characteristics of Inclusion in Articles”. The studies were classified based on the year of data collection, dividing them into those conducted between 2015 and 2018, and those conducted between 2019 and 2023, based on the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic. The studies were further divided based on regions into southern and northern areas. Studies were categorized based on the source of the research sample as either single-center or multi-center. Studies were divided according to sample type into medical and non-medical student populations. Based on the measurement tools used, the studies were classified into anonymous and non-anonymous questionnaires. The studies were categorized based on literature quality into high and medium quality. Microsoft Excel 2022 was used for data extraction, and the extracted data were input into Stata 18 software.

Quality assessment

The quality of articles meeting the criteria for assessment was independently evaluated by two reviewers using 11 questionnaires recommended by healthcare research and quality agencies for assessing cross-sectional studies (Rostom et al., 2004). If the quality was uncertain, a third reviewer was consulted. For each question, a “yes” response earns one point, while “no” or “unclear” earns zero points, with a maximum total score of 11 points. A higher total score indicates better quality literature. A score of ≤ three indicates low quality, four-seven indicates moderate quality, and ≥ eight indicates high quality (Hu et al., 2015).

Statistical analysis

The overall willingness of Chinese women to receive the HPV vaccine was determined through meta-analysis. Heterogeneity of effect sizes was assessed using the I2 statistic, which quantifies the degree of heterogeneity as a percentage (Higgins et al., 2003). P ≥ 0.1 and I2 ≤ 50 % indicate no significant heterogeneity, and a fixed-effects model is applied; whereas P < 0.1 and I2 > 50 % suggest heterogeneity, requiring a random-effects model (Borenstein, 2020). A meta-analysis using a random-effects model, along with subgroup analyses, was performed on factors including cultural level, HPV knowledge, HPV vaccine knowledge, data collection year, language, sample source, sample type, measurement tool, literature quality, and geographic region. The willingness rate and 95 % confidence interval (CI) for each subgroup were calculated, and differences were compared using a chi-square test. Furthermore, to assess the robustness and reliability of the meta-analysis results and determine whether any single study contributed significantly to heterogeneity, a sensitivity analysis was conducted by sequentially excluding studies. As the single-group rate is a descriptive measure rather than a comparative one, and since no “positive” or statistically significant results are expected, a meta-analysis of single-group rates does not require publication bias tests (Borenstein, 2019). All statistical analyses were conducted using Stata 18 software.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

As this study is a systematic review, it did not involve the collection of data from human participants. Consequently, ethical approval was not required. In light of the study's design, the requirement for written informed consent was waived.

Results

Search results

A preliminary screening identified 3476 relevant studies. After removing duplicates and reviewing titles and abstracts, 3393 studies were excluded, leaving 17 studies that met the inclusion criteria. All 17 studies reported vaccination intention rates and the factors influencing vaccination willingness, as depicted in the flowchart (Fig. 1).

Descriptions of the included studies

All 17 studies employed cross-sectional designs, with 12 published in Chinese [26–37] and five in English [38–42]. These studies included 39,969 participants, with sample sizes ranging from 300 to 9855. Participants' ages ranged from nine to 60 years, and the majority were adult women. Of the 17 studies, four focused on female college students [28,32,37,39], one on female sex workers [38], one on rural women [41], and one on migrant women [33]. The studies were conducted across various regions of China between 2016 and 2022. Of the 17 studies included, 13 were conducted after the onset of COVID-19. One study was conducted across seven regions in mainland China, but the specific regions were not detailed [40]. Another study was conducted in several cities, including Jinan (Shandong), Guangzhou (Guangdong), Zhengzhou (Henan), Taiyuan (Shanxi), Urumqi (Xinjiang), Chengdu (Sichuan), and Dalian (Liaoning) [39]. The remaining studies were single-center studies conducted in Dongguan (Guangdong) [27], Ganzhou (Jiangxi) [28], Shenzhen (Guangdong) [42], Hunan [41], Guangzhou (Guangdong) [29], Haikou (Hainan) [30], Kunming (Yunnan) [36], Kaifeng (Henan) [35], Zhengzhou (Henan) [37], Changchun (Jilin) [33], Zhangshu (Jiangxi) [34], Zouping (Shandong) [26], Hefei (Anhui) [31], Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region [38], and Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region [32]. Geographically, 10 single-center studies were conducted in southern China [27–31,34,36,38,41,42], and five in northern China [26,32,33,35,37]. All studies used self-designed questionnaires to assess vaccination intentions, with more than half (n = 9) employing anonymous questionnaires [28,29,32,34,36,38–41]. The characteristics of these 17 studies are presented in Table 1. The quality scores of the included studies ranged from six to eight points, with an average of 6.71 ± 0.65 points. Two studies were rated as high quality, while the others were of moderate quality. Details of the quality assessment are provided in Table 2 of the Supplementary material.

Results of the meta-analysis

Prevalence of HPV vaccination intention

The meta-analysis of 17 studies evaluated the prevalence of HPV vaccination intentions among Chinese women. A heterogeneity test revealed an I2 statistic of 99.9 % (P < 0.01), justifying the use of the random effects model. The forest plot in Fig. 2 presents the willingness rates along with their corresponding confidence intervals. The willingness rates for HPV vaccination among Chinese women varied from 23.4 % to 96.9 %. The meta-analysis revealed that the combined willingness rate was 65.7 % (95 % confidence interval [CI]: 55.2 % - 76.2 %) [26–42].

Subgroup analysis

In this review, subgroup analyses were conducted based on literacy levels, study areas, places of residence, professions, prior awareness of HPV or the HPV vaccine, history of family members or relatives suffering from tumors, time of data collection, questionnaire type, and other factors. The results indicated that the willingness to receive the HPV vaccination was higher among Chinese women with a college education or above (71.1 %) compared to those with less than a college education (60.1 %). The willingness to receive the HPV vaccination was higher among women in the southern region (69.0 %) than in the northern region (59.7 %). Additionally, the willingness to receive the HPV vaccination was higher among those living in urban areas (68.3 %) compared to those in rural areas (56.0 %). Among female college students, the willingness to receive the vaccine was significantly higher among those majoring in medical fields (84.2 %) compared to non-medical majors (35.7 %). Furthermore, the desire to be vaccinated was higher among those who had heard of HPV (66.1 %) or the HPV vaccine (76.4 %) compared to those who had never heard of HPV (50.2 %) or the HPV vaccine (57.8 %). The willingness to be vaccinated was higher among those with family members or friends who had a history of tumors (74.5 %) compared to those with no such history (55.3 %). Furthermore, subgroup analyses by the year of data collection were conducted to address heterogeneity across studies. The willingness to receive the HPV vaccine was lower in studies that collected data before the COVID-19 pandemic (57.5 %) compared to those conducted after the pandemic (67.5 %). The willingness was also higher in studies that used anonymous questionnaires (71.8 %) compared to those using non-anonymous questionnaires (58.8 %). These differences were statistically significant (P < 0.01, Table 2).

Sensitivity analysis

Sensitivity analyses were performed using a case-by-case exclusion approach. The results indicate that the obtained data are robust. Excluding any of the data did not significantly alter the combined effect size, as shown in Fig. 3.

Meta regression analysis

To address the issue of significant heterogeneity (I2 > 99 %) observed in meta-analysis, we conducted extensive meta regression analysis to explore potential moderating factors, including: Major, Ever heard of HPV, Ever heard of HPV Vaccine, Education level, Region, Family or friends with cancer, Residence, Data collection year, and Tool. The results indicate that only one covariate significantly explained heterogeneity: Major (for example, medical versus non-medical background) reduced heterogeneity by 77.4 % (Adj R2 = 77.42 %, p = 0.03). Other covariates (for example, education, region, tool) did not significantly reduce heterogeneity: Adj R2 ranged from −1.3 % to 14.2 %, indicating minimal explanatory power. Residual I2 remained >99 % in all models. Intercept estimates were consistently significant (all p < 0.01), confirming the pooled effect's robustness despite heterogeneity, as shown in Table 3 of the Supplementary material.

Results of the systematic review

Influencing factors

Based on the 17 studies included, 12 factors were identified as influencing the willingness to receive the HPV vaccine: ① Awareness of HPV; ② Awareness of the HPV vaccine; ③ Education level; ④ Ever heard of the HPV vaccine; ⑤ Age; ⑥ Residence; ⑦ Ever heard of HPV; ⑧ Attitudes toward premarital sex; ⑨ Family or friends with cancer; ⑩ Family income; ⑪ History of sexual intercourse; ⑫ Attendance at prevention seminars. Nine of these factors were reported in six or more studies: ① Awareness of HPV; ② Awareness of the HPV vaccine; ③ Education level; ④ Ever heard of the HPV vaccine; ⑤ Age; ⑥ Residence; ⑦ Ever heard of HPV; ⑧ Attitudes toward premarital sex; ⑨ Family or friends with cancer. Among these, education level, residence, awareness of HPV, awareness of the HPV vaccine, and family or friends with cancer were suitable for subgroup analyses, however, due to substantial heterogeneity in measurement methods and reporting formats across the included studies, a meta-analysis could not be performed for certain variables, including age, attitudes toward premarital sex, and knowledge of HPV and HPV vaccination. Given that meta-analytic techniques are not universally applicable to all influencing factors, we opted instead to present a descriptive synthesis of these variables to ensure a more accurate and meaningful interpretation of the data.

Other results of interest

Most of the studies included in this review report that Chinese women show a strong willingness to receive the HPV vaccine, yet vaccination rates remain low [27,28,30,32,35–42]. Some studies explicitly mention that the HPV vaccination rate in China is significantly lower than in developed countries such as those in Europe and North America [40–42].

Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis provides the first comprehensive overview of Chinese women's willingness to receive HPV vaccines, synthesizing data from 17 studies. The pooled estimate indicates that 65.7 % (95 % CI: 55.2 % - 76.2 %) of Chinese women express willingness to be vaccinated against HPV. However, significant heterogeneity existed among studies, and sensitivity analysis confirmed the stability of this combined estimate. This substantial expressed willingness underscores both the potential for, and the challenge of, improving HPV vaccine uptake in China.

Subgroup analyses revealed key factors significantly associated with vaccination willingness: Women with higher education levels, those in medical majors, and those aware of HPV or the HPV vaccine consistently showed significantly higher willingness. This likely reflects better information access, comprehension, and understanding of HPV-related risks and vaccine benefits (Howe, 2014; Stephens et al., 2023; Du et al., 2022; Song et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2023b; Shelal et al., 2020). Conversely, lower awareness levels among less educated and non-medical populations highlight a critical need for tailored health education.

Willingness was significantly higher among women in southern regions and urban areas compared to those in central/western regions and rural areas. This disparity aligns with evidence linking lower awareness and access in less developed and rural settings to economic constraints and limited healthcare infrastructure (Wang et al., 2020; Zhou et al., 2025; You et al., 2020; Li et al., 2015; Deng et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2023a). It underscores the importance of addressing regional economic disparities and strengthening rural healthcare capacity (including facilities, personnel, and service accessibility) to improve equity.

Women reporting a family history of tumors exhibited higher willingness, suggesting heightened risk awareness may motivate vaccination (Wilson et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2022).

Studies conducted post-COVID-19 pandemic reported higher willingness than pre-pandemic studies (Jung et al., 2022), potentially reflecting increased general health awareness (Klassen et al., 2024). Anonymous questionnaires yielded higher willingness rates than non-anonymous ones, indicating potential response bias on sensitive topics.

Beyond subgroup factors, the review suggested associations between willingness and age (with complex patterns observed across studies [26, 27, 30, 40–42]), as well as attitudes supportive of or neutral toward premarital sex [28, 36, 38]. However, the relationship with premarital sex attitudes warrants further investigation, particularly given mixed findings in multivariable models [39].

A critical finding is the significant gap between expressed willingness (65.7 %) and actual HPV vaccination rates in China. Barriers such as vaccine access limitations, logistical challenges, persistent low awareness, and parental opposition are likely major contributors to this “high willingness, low uptake” dilemma (Yuan et al., 2023). The ongoing development and anticipated availability of domestic nine-valent HPV vaccines offer promise for mitigating supply and cost constraints. In the interim, a mixed vaccination strategy (initiating with available vaccines like the bivalent, followed by the nine-valent later) presents a pragmatic approach to enhance both health and economic outcomes (Gao et al., 2025).

This review has several limitations. First, all included studies are cross-sectional, preventing causal inference and limiting the rigor of factor analysis. Second, methodological quality was generally moderate: only two of 17 studies (11.8 %) were high quality (AHRQ ≥ eight), while the rest (88.2 %) scored six-seven, possibly reducing the robustness of pooled estimates due to biases in sampling, measurement, and confounding control. Future studies should apply stricter inclusion criteria. The meta-analysis also showed substantial heterogeneity, likely reflecting clinical and methodological diversity, though sensitivity analyses confirmed result stability. Moreover, most studies failed to distinguish clearly between vaccinated and vaccine-willing individuals, complicating analysis. Resource constraints limited inclusion of unpublished data. Lastly, the exclusive focus on women restricts understanding of HPV vaccination across genders, despite recent approval for male vaccination in China (Dai et al., 2022).

Conclusion

This systematic review and meta-analysis examined Chinese women's overall willingness to receive the HPV vaccine and key factors influencing it. Findings show that about one-third remain hesitant or unwilling, reflecting relatively low acceptance compared to global levels. Notable disparities exist among population subgroups. Factors contributing to hesitancy include education, urban-rural status, regional economic development, HPV awareness, cancer history, and the societal impact of COVID-19. Higher willingness is seen in women with higher education, urban residency (especially in southern China), medical backgrounds, greater HPV knowledge, liberal views on premarital sex, and a family or social cancer history. A 10 % increase in willingness followed the COVID-19 outbreak, suggesting improved public health awareness may boost vaccine acceptance. These results highlight the need for targeted, population-specific education strategies, particularly for rural residents, those with lower education, from less-developed regions, or without medical backgrounds. Addressing knowledge gaps and sociocultural barriers is essential to advancing HPV prevention and reducing disease burden in China (Wang et al., 2024).

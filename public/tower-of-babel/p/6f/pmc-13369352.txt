Imaging-based predictors of progression in brain abscess: the role of loculation and ADC gradient
Authors: Jong-Young Park, Dong-Won Shin, Kawngwoo Park, Eun Young Kim, Chan Jong You, Gi-Taek Yee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeBrain abscess remains a life-threatening condition despite advances in neurosurgical and antimicrobial therapy, and reliable imaging-based markers to guide postoperative risk stratification and surveillance remain limited. This study aimed to identify clinical and radiographic factors associated with abscess progression in neurosurgically treated patients, with particular emphasis on the apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) gradient.MethodsWe retrospectively reviewed 53 patients with brain abscess who underwent neurosurgical treatment at Gachon University Gil Medical Center between January 2005 and April 2025. Clinical characteristics, microbiology, surgical approach, and imaging findings were analyzed. Univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed to identify factors associated with progression. ADC gradients were evaluated as continuous variables and dichotomized using a receiver operating characteristic (ROC)–derived cutoff.ResultsThe cohort included 38 men and 15 women, with a median age of 56.4 years. During a mean follow-up of 28.0 months, progression occurred in 17 patients (32%). Abscess loculation was significantly associated with progression in univariable analysis. Absolute ADC values at baseline or during follow-up were not predictive. ROC analysis demonstrated good discriminatory performance of the ADC gradient (AUC 0.857), with higher gradients associated with a significantly reduced risk of progression. The ADC gradient remained independently associated with progression in multivariable analysis.ConclusionAbscess loculation and the ADC gradient are radiographic features associated with progression in surgically managed brain abscess and may serve as useful imaging markers for clinical monitoring. Incorporation of ADC gradient assessment into postoperative imaging surveillance may help identify cases with a higher likelihood of treatment resistance and support clinical decision-making. However, these findings should be interpreted with caution given the retrospective and single-center design of the study, and further validation in prospective multicenter cohorts is needed.

Introduction

Brain abscess is an uncommon but life-threatening intracranial infection. The annual incidence is estimated between 0.4 and 1.3 per 100,000 population, though higher in developing regions [2, 16]. Historically, mortality rates approached 40%, but over the past several decades, improved neuroimaging, minimally invasive neurosurgical interventions, and standardized antibiotic regimens have reduced this to approximately 10% or lower [5]. Nevertheless, some recent data report case-fatality rates between 11 and 14%, and in certain settings—especially in low- and middle-income countries—in-hospital mortality remains high, sometimes exceeding 24% [3].

Despite advances in hygiene and antimicrobial therapies, the incidence of brain abscesses appears to have climbed, notably among children following the COVID-19 pandemic [12]. A retrospective study found that hospital admission rates more than doubled post-pandemic, with similar trends observed in the U.S. and Europe [9, 17].

Although brain abscesses are still rare, their potential for catastrophic consequences—including seizures, neurologic deficits, and death—makes them clinically significant. Even survivors may endure long-term neurological sequelae, such as epilepsy or cognitive impairments, further emphasizing the morbidity burden [17].

Currently, treatment for brain abscess centers on surgical drainage and prolonged antibiotic therapy, typically spanning 6 to 8 weeks, though the course may be extended to 12 weeks or more in cases of recurrence or poor radiologic resolution [4, 7]. These extended treatments often demand hospitalization, disrupting patients’ lives and imposing substantial social and economic costs.

Complicating matters, there is no consensus guideline that tailors treatment duration or follow-up strategies based on initial imaging and clinical parameters—a significant limitation in optimizing patient outcomes.

In this study, we present our single-institutional experience managing idiopathic brain abscesses and propose a structured follow-up strategy intended to guide clinicians through treatment duration decisions, imaging surveillance, and the timing of repeat interventions.

Methods

All research involving human participants was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of GIL Medical Center and the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki, as revised and updated. The study protocol received approval from the GIL Medical Center IRB (approval number: GDIRB2025-149). Owing to the retrospective design of the study, the requirement for informed consent was waived.

Patient population

We conducted a retrospective review of the patients diagnosed with brain abscess who underwent surgical treatment at the Gachon University Gil Medical Center between January 2005 and April 2025.

Brain abscesses that occurred postoperatively were not included. Clinical and neuroimaging records were used to collect information including sex, age, abscess location, presence of loculation, surgical methods, microbiological culture results, and underlying comorbidities. Despite being a retrospective analysis, the treatment rationale and approach remained unchanged.

Clinical diagnosis and assessment

Diagnosis and assessment were conducted using brain CT and/or MRI. The diameter of the abscess was determined by calculating the mean of the maximum length and width on axial CT or MRI scans. According to previous reports, loculation was defined as an abscess containing fibrous septations that divide it into several communicating or separate chambers [10].

To evaluate the potential for a hematogenous origin of infection, laboratory tests such as complete blood count, erythrocyte sedimentation rate, C-reactive protein, blood cultures, and serum chemistry were performed. In certain cases, consultations with otolaryngology or cardiology specialists were requested to help identify the primary source of infection.

Treatment procedures

The purpose of surgical treatment was to reduce the size of the abscess, alleviate symptoms, and obtain specimens for analysis. Surgical treatment involved either stereotactic aspiration or removal of the abscess through craniotomy. Antibiotic treatment was initiated after obtaining culture samples during surgery. The initial antibiotic regimen was adjusted based on the results of susceptibility testing when necessary. In most cases, antibiotics were administered for six weeks or longer, depending on the patient’s clinical response and findings from neuroimaging studies. We regularly conducted consultations with an infectious disease specialist regarding antibiotic treatment. Abscess progression is defined as the enlargement of the abscess cavity with clinical deterioration despite the administration of appropriate antibiotic therapy.

Outcome and complication assessment

Outcome assessment was based on both radiological findings and clinical evaluation recorded during routine clinical care. Although no formal prospective observation protocol was implemented, clinical status — including assessment of consciousness, focal neurological deficits, and new-onset symptoms — was evaluated daily during inpatient stay as part of standard postoperative neurosurgical care and documented in the medical records.

Postoperative complications were defined as any deviation from the normal postoperative course occurring within 30 days of surgery. Complications were retrospectively reviewed and classified according to the Landriel Ibáñez grading system for neurosurgical complications [15], which stratifies complications based on the treatment required: Grade I, non-life-threatening complications not requiring invasive treatment; Grade II, complications requiring pharmacological or invasive (including surgical or interventional) management; Grade III, life-threatening complications requiring intensive care; and Grade IV, death directly attributable to the complication. When a patient experienced multiple complications during the 30-day postoperative period, the most severe grade was used for classification; accordingly, patients who underwent reoperation and subsequently died from a complication were classified as Grade IV rather than Grade II. The classification was performed by a single investigator based on review of medical records, operative notes, and follow-up imaging.

Radiographic follow-up

In most patients, CT scans were performed immediately after surgery and again at 24 h postoperatively to monitor changes in abscess size and to detect hemorrhage. To assess progression, CT scans were conducted every 1 to 2 weeks and MRI scans every 4 weeks. Additional imaging studies were performed whenever neurological deterioration was observed.

In the present study, we further evaluated changes in signal intensity on MRI T2-weighted images and measured the apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) values on diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI). ADC values were obtained by placing regions of interest within the abscess cavity, avoiding peripheral rim enhancement and areas of hemorrhage. All measurements were performed by a single investigator to minimize inter-observer variability.

Statistical method

Data were analyzed using SPSS software, version 21 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables were summarized as medians with interquartile ranges or means with ranges, depending on their distribution, while categorical variables were expressed as counts and percentages. Univariable logistic regression analyses were performed for each independent variable, regardless of whether it was continuous or categorical, to identify potential predictors of progression. ADC gradient was evaluated both as a continuous variable and as a categorical variable. The cutoff value for categorical analysis was determined using ROC curve analysis, selecting the point with the maximum Youden index. Variables with a p < 0.10 in the univariable analysis were subsequently included in the multivariable logistic regression model to determine independent predictors. A p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patients’ demographics

In the present study, there were 38 male and 15 female patients, with a median age of 56.4 years (interquartile range, 47.8–62.1 years). The mean duration of follow-up was 27.97 months (range 0.3–118.8 months). At the time of admission, 14 patients complained of limb weakness, 11 patients complained of mental change, and 10 patients complained of headache. Additionally, seizure, dysphagia, dizziness, and dysarthria were each complained of by 3 patients, whereas shoulder pain, visual disturbance, syncope, hemiparesis and incidental findings were each reported by 1 patient. The median diameter of brain abscesses was 3.0 cm. Lesions were located on the right side in 35 patients (66%), on the left side in 16 patients (30%), and combined in 2 patients (4%). Deep brain structures such as basal ganglia, thalamus, and caudate nucleus were involved in 17 patients. Brain abscesses were most commonly located in the frontal lobe (18 cases, 34%), followed by the parietal lobe (11 cases, 21%) and the temporal lobe (8 cases, 15%). Loculation was observed in 11 patients (21%).

Treatment

For antibiotic therapy, vancomycin, ceftriaxone, and metronidazole were administered in 25 patients (47%). The median duration of total antibiotic use was 6 weeks. Pathogens were identified in 29 patients (55%). Among these culture-positive cases, a single microorganism was isolated in 28 patients (97%), whereas polymicrobial infections were observed in 1 patient (3%). In our cohort, the most frequently isolated organisms were Streptococcus species, accounting for the majority (48%) of positive cultures. Actinomyces species (9%) and Klebsiella pneumoniae (9%) were the next most common, followed by anaerobic or facultative anaerobic organisms, including Fusobacterium, Prevotella, Porphyromonas, and Parvimonas. Less frequently, Staphylococcus, Proteus, Lactobacillus, Nocardia, Aspergillus, and a parasitic infection were identified.

Among the patients, craniotomy was performed in 15 patients (28%), and stereotactic aspiration was performed in 33 patients (62%). Reoperation was required in 9 patients (17%), and reinfection occurred in 1 patient (2%).

Post operative complications

Postoperative complications occurred in 21 of 53 patients (40%). According to the Landriel Ibáñez classification, the majority of complications were classified as Grade II (n = 15, 28%), reflecting the need for pharmacological or invasive management, including reoperation or additional drainage. Regarding radiographic findings, loculation of the brain abscess was significantly associated with progression in the univariable analysis (OR 19.125, p = 0.001). However, this effect was attenuated after adjustment in the multivariable model (OR 3.127, p = 0.365).

In the analysis of ADC values, neither the measurements obtained at admission nor those during follow-up demonstrated a significant association with progression (p = 0.180 and p = 0.178, respectively). ROC curve analysis of the ADC gradient yielded an AUC of 0.857, with an optimal cutoff of 233 × 10−3mm2/s (sensitivity 80%, specificity 85.7%). When analyzed as a continuous variable, the ADC gradient was not significantly associated with progression in either univariable or multivariable analyses (OR 0.995, p = 0.109 and 0.110, respectively). By contrast, when dichotomized using the ROC-derived cutoff, a higher ADC gradient was significantly associated with a lower risk of progression in the univariable analysis (OR 0.32, p = 0.030), and this association also remained statistically significant in the multivariable model (OR 0.042, p = 0.021).

Illustrative case

A 56-year-old woman presented to the emergency department with a headache. Brain CT demonstrated a hypodense lesion with surrounding edema, and MRI revealed a multifocal, peripherally rim-enhancing lesion with multiple loculations in the right temporal lobe (Fig. 2). She underwent emergency stereotactic aspiration, and empirical antibiotic therapy with ceftriaxone and metronidazole was initiated. Histopathology confirmed an abscess, although microbiological cultures were negative.

Thirty days after surgery, she developed a worsening headache. Follow-up MRI demonstrated persistent enlargement of the abscess cavity despite four weeks of antibiotics. A second stereotactic aspiration was attempted, but puncture was difficult due to a thickened fibrotic wall. Antibiotics were switched to vancomycin and ceftriaxone and continued for an additional four weeks. Subsequent MRI, however, revealed further enlargement of the lesion.

Given the poor response, an exploratory craniotomy with abscess drainage was performed under microscopic guidance. The abscess was successfully evacuated, and a portion of the abscess wall was excised for histological confirmation. Unexpectedly, the biopsy revealed metastatic adenocarcinoma (Fig. 3). The patient subsequently underwent gross total resection of the mass followed by adjuvant radiotherapy. Discussion

Prognostic factors and clinical outcomes

Although brain abscesses are relatively rare, they remain clinically significant because of their considerable mortality and morbidity, despite advances in neuroimaging and antimicrobial therapy. Mortality rates continue to be substantial, with several series published after 2000 reporting rates ranging from 17 to 32% [21]. In our cohort, the overall in-hospital mortality was 9% (5 of 53 patients), of which 4 deaths (8%) were directly attributable to postoperative complications (Landriel Ibáñez Grade IV), while the remaining death occurred in a patient whose initial diagnosis was later revised to metastatic adenocarcinoma and was therefore attributed to progression of the underlying malignancy rather than to a surgical complication. Given the relatively small sample size, we focused our analysis on cases in which the abscess cavity increased during treatment. Among 53 patients, 36 showed clinical improvement, whereas 17 demonstrated radiological enlargement of the abscess cavity despite therapy. We specifically investigated the factors associated with radiological progression of brain abscess. Although this study was conducted at a single center, our general patient characteristics and outcome patterns appear broadly concordant with previously published surgical brain abscess cohorts [13, 21], providing some indirect support for the external relevance of our findings.

With regard to comorbidities, numerous studies have suggested that diabetes mellitus (DM) is associated with poor prognosis in brain abscess, although a substantial number of reports have contradicted this finding [11, 13, 20]. One study concluded that glycemic control, rather than the mere presence of DM, correlated with the duration of antibiotic treatment [14]. In our series, DM was not significantly associated with abscess progression. Similarly, hypertension (HTN) was not found to be a predictor of progression and previous literature also provides little evidence linking HTN with poor outcomes in brain abscess [8].

For patients with underlying cancer, some studies have identified malignancy as an independent risk factor for short- and mid-term mortality [22]. However, in our study, only one patient had a history of cancer, which made it difficult to evaluate any statistical association.

Microbiology and antimicrobial therapy

In the present study, the most commonly isolated microorganisms were gram-positive cocci, consistent with previous reports. In recent years, anaerobic bacteria have been increasingly identified in brain abscesses [22]. In our series, however, anaerobic organisms were detected in only five cases (Actinomyces, Fusobacterium, Prevotella, Porphyromonas, and Parvimonas). In 24 out of 53 patients, no pathogens were isolated, which may be attributed to either insufficient sample volume or prior antibiotic administration before surgery.

The most commonly used antibiotic combination in our series was ceftriaxone, metronidazole, and vancomycin. Metronidazole was selected for its bactericidal activity against strict anaerobes and its ability to achieve high concentrations within pus [18]. In recent years, broader-spectrum agents such as ticarcillin, a β-lactam effective against gram-negative organisms including Pseudomonas aeruginosa, have also become increasingly important in the management of cerebral abscess [21]. In our series, parenteral antibiotic therapy was maintained for 4–12 weeks, which is consistent with current guidance recommending approximately 6–8 weeks of intravenous therapy for aspirated or conservatively managed brain abscesses, with shorter courses (typically 4–6 weeks) considered after complete surgical excision when clinical and radiographic responses are satisfactory [1].

Radiologic predictors: loculation and ADC values

Regarding loculation, a previous study reported that although loculated abscesses do not necessarily result in higher mortality, they are associated with a greater need for repeated surgical interventions [23]. Consistent with these findings, 7 of 9 patients in our cohort who required reoperation had loculated abscesses. While loculation was significantly associated with progression in univariable analysis, this association did not persist in the multivariable model, suggesting that loculation may primarily reflect treatment complexity rather than independently drive progression.

Neurosurgeons do not routinely utilize ADC values when evaluating the treatment course of brain abscess. In this study, we also investigated whether ADC values could reflect disease progression. Previous neuroradiology studies have reported that early brain abscesses typically show low ADC values. In surgical cohorts, patients who improved demonstrated an increase in ADC values postoperatively, whereas a subsequent decline in ADC was interpreted as a sign of disease reactivation [24]. In our cohort, absolute ADC values at baseline or during follow-up were not predictive of abscess progression. When the ADC gradient was analyzed as a continuous variable, lower values showed a tendency to be associated with an increased risk of progression, implying that declining ADC over time may reflect worsening disease activity; however, this trend did not reach statistical significance. By contrast, ROC curve analysis identified an optimal cutoff value of 233 × 10–3 mm2/s (AUC 0.857, sensitivity 80%, specificity 85.7%), and dichotomization using this threshold revealed a significant association in the univariable model, which also remained statistically significant in the multivariable analysis (OR = 0.042, p = 0.021). This finding suggests that the ADC gradient may not exert a uniform linear effect across its entire range but rather demonstrates a threshold effect, whereby values above the cutoff are associated with a substantially lower risk of progression. The persistence of this association in the multivariable analysis suggests that the prognostic value of the ADC gradient is independent of other clinical factors, such as loculation, supporting its potential role as an imaging biomarker that warrants validation in larger, prospective cohorts.

Among the management recommendations proposed by Mamelak and co-workers is that postoperative CT or MRI should be performed biweekly to monitor for abscess re-expansion or failure of resolution despite antibiotic therapy [19]. Based on our findings, abscesses with loculation or a lower ADC gradient demonstrated a higher likelihood of progression. These observations raise the possibility that closer imaging surveillance, including diffusion-weighted imaging for ADC gradient assessment, may be beneficial in such cases.

ADC value change during therapeutic period

Brain MRI with diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) and MR spectroscopy are useful modalities for differentiating intracranial tumors from brain abscesses. ADC is not only utilized to diagnose the disease, but also useful to follow-up therapeutic response. At the initial stage of brain abscess before treatment, ADC value is low due to restriction of water molecule by inflammatory markers, debris, or viable cells. When antibiotics is administered or surgical drainage is done, ADC value becomes higher which indicates viable cells decreased [24]. Cartes-Zumelzu et al. also described that the ADC value is a potential surveillance tool for brain abscess during antibiotics treatment [6]. Proposed follow-up framework for brain abscess

Taken together, our findings suggest that both loculation and the temporal evolution of ADC values are associated with brain abscess progression. These imaging parameters may complement clinical assessment and help inform decision-making during therapy. Based on these observations, we propose a structured follow-up framework that integrates serial diffusion MRI for ADC gradient monitoring and morphological evaluation of loculation (Fig. 5). This framework may help identify cases with a higher likelihood of treatment failure or diagnostic uncertainty, potentially supporting timely clinical decision-making such as re-aspiration or surgical exploration.Fig. 5Proposed follow-up algorithm for brain abscess management

To date, no standardized imaging-based follow-up approach has been established for brain abscess, and our proposal aims to provide a practical framework for individualized, evidence-oriented management. However, this proposed framework has not been formally validated and should be interpreted as an institutional approach. Further validation through prospective studies is needed.

Limitation

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective design inherently introduces potential bias in outcome assessment. Second, as a single-center study, the generalizability of our findings is limited. Third, postoperative complications were not originally assessed using a standardized classification system such as the Landriel-Ibanez scale. Fourth, ADC measurements were obtained manually from regions of interest, which may have introduced measurement variability. Although all measurements were performed by a single investigator using a consistent methodology, which eliminated inter-observer variability, intra-observer variability cannot be fully excluded. Automated or semi-automated ADC quantification in future prospective studies would offer greater reproducibility. Finally, the proposed treatment and follow-up framework has not been formally validated and should be interpreted as an institutional approach rather than a definitive clinical guideline. Future prospective and multicenter studies are needed to validate our findings.

Conclusion

Despite advances in neuroimaging, antibiotics, and neurosurgical techniques, brain abscess continues to pose significant challenges in diagnosis and management. Our results suggest that loculation and temporal changes in ADC values may be associated with treatment failure and abscess progression. These findings support the potential role of ADC dynamics in risk stratification and follow-up planning. However, these findings should be interpreted with caution given the retrospective and single-center design of this study. Future large-scale, prospective, and multicenter studies are needed to validate these findings and to further define their clinical utility.

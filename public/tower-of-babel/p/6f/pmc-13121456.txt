Lipodystrophy and associated factors among patients with diabetes receiving insulin therapy: a multicenter study in Ethiopia
Authors: Zegaye Agmassie Bazezew, Tirsit Ketsela Zeleke, Chernet Tafere Negesse, Gashaw Binega Mekonnen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLipodystrophy alters insulin absorption, potentially leading to unpredictable hypoglycemia and inadequate glycemic control. Although the problem is long known, it continues to persist and remains neglected, particularly among diabetic patients in developing countries. This study evaluated its magnitude and different contributing factors. This study aimed to assess the prevalence of lipodystrophy, and their contributing factors among patients receiving insulin therapy in three selected hospitals of Northwest Ethiopia. A cross-sectional study was conducted from April 5 to September 30, 2023, at the University of Gondar, Tibebe ghion and Debre Markos Comprehensive Specialized Hospitals. Standardized physical examinations were used to assess the presence of lipodystrophy and data on demographic characteristics, clinical characteristics, and drug related characteristics were used for analysis. Logistic regression was used to identify factors associated with lipodystrophy. Among participants, 54.3% had type 1 diabetes mellitus. The larger part of study participants (71.5%) reported having diabetes mellitus for over five years. Insulin-induced lipodystrophy was observed in 225 cases (53.1%). Grade 2 lipohypertrophy was the most common type (52.7%), marked by significant fat thickening, while Grade 3 lipoatrophy was rare (1.8%). The abdomen was the most affected injection site (36.5%), followed by multiple sites (32%). Failure to rotate (AOR = 1.8, p-value 0.004), needle reuse, glycemic control (AOR = 2.15, p-value 0.019), and insulin dosage per kg (AOR = 2.1, p-value 0.001) were found to have an association with lipodystrophy. The study revealed a high prevalence of lipodystrophy among diabetic patients using insulin injections, highlighting the need for greater attention to ensure comprehensive diabetes care.Keywords: Lipodystrophy, Glycemic control, Insulin, Northwestern EthiopiaSubject terms: Diseases, Endocrinology, Health care, Medical research

Introduction

Diabetes mellitus, the most prevalent non-communicable disease and a global health catastrophe of the twenty-first century, is marked by hyperglycemia, with potentially disastrous, even deadly, implications1,2. Presently, there exists a variety of treatments, such as oral medications, for managing diabetes mellitus, but there’s been a noted rise in the utilization of insulin therapy3. Insulin remains the cornerstone treatment for individuals with type 1 diabetes mellitus, and as type 2 diabetes advances, insulin therapy becomes necessary for many patients, especially those with long-standing conditions4. Weight gain, hypo and hyperglycemia, needle stick injuries, and problems at the injection site, including erythema, infection, abscess, and most significantly lipodystrophy (LD), are all risks associated with using insulin5.

Lipodystrophy is the most common local complication caused by frequent insulin injection6. Subcutaneous adipose tissue is affected by lipodystrophy, it is broadly divided into lipohypertrophy and lipoatrophy, which differ in their incidence and pathogenetic processes. Lipohypertrophy (LH) is more common than lipoatrophy (LA)7 and is characterized by a localized buildup of fat tissue at the site of a subcutaneous insulin injection8,9. Although the exact pathogenic mechanisms are still unknown, repeated mechanical trauma brought on by the insertion of needles and local trophic effects of insulin may result in abnormal development of fat tissue10. However, LA or local fat loss, is clinically recognized by noticeable subcutaneous fat tissue shrinkage and atrophic cutaneous depression at the injection site11. The pathogenic mechanism is unclear, but immune-mediated damage has been hypothesized12. Since the 1980s, when recombinant insulin was first introduced, it has been relatively unusual to find13.

While it is true that technology has advanced significantly over the past few decades, particularly in the optimization and purification of insulin to produce new varieties that have numerous advantages over the old ones, some barriers, such as skin complications, still exist and the occurrence of LH and LA continues to be reported. LD affects insulin absorption, which can result in inexplicable hypoglycemia and poor glycemic control in addition to cosmetic issues. It also increases within-subject uptake variability by three to five times14,15.

The practice of injecting insulin into lipodystrophic areas still persists, often due to its perceived lower pain level when it is injected to this area, despite the risk of erratic insulin absorption rates which makes the problem even worse16,17. Re-education on insulin injection has demonstrated greater efficacy in boosting adherence, reducing lipodystrophy, refining injection techniques, and enhancing patients’ perception of insulin therapy18.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to quantify the prevalence of lipodystrophy in adult patients undergoing insulin therapy. Our study aims to assess the prevalence of lipodystrophy in adult diabetes patients, identify contributing factors, and evaluate its impact on glycemic control.

Method and materials

Study design and population

A cross-sectional study was carried out among diabetic patients undergoing insulin therapy at three tertiary hospitals in Northwest Ethiopia between April 5 and September 30, 2023. All diabetes patients that were on follow-up at these three hospitals and on insulin were the source population, while those who fulfilled the inclusion criteria during the study period were the study population.

Study setting

The study was conducted at three tertiary hospitals of northwest Ethiopia (University of Gondar comprehensive specialized hospital (UOGCSH), Debre Markos comprehensive specialized hospital (DMCSH), and Tibebe Ghion comprehensive specialized hospital (TGCSH)) outpatient diabetes centers. These locations were chosen using a lottery method from five tertiary hospitals in northwest Ethiopia.

The University of Gondar comprehensive specialized hospital diabetes clinic is located in the central Gondar administrative zone, Amhara National Regional State, which is about 750 km northwest of Addis Ababa. Currently, UOGCSH is serving more than 13 million people in the catchment area. There are 15 distinct outpatient service areas spread across various departments, and 400,000 patients attend the outpatient centers annually.

Tibebe Gihon comprehensive specialized hospital, is located about 10 km south of the city center in Bahir Dar, Amhara National Regional State, Ethiopia. Bahir Dar is the capital city of Amhara National Regional State and it is located 562 km from Addis Ababa. Currently, the hospital serves over 7 million people living in the Amhara region and the surrounding Benishangul Gumuz region through inpatient and outpatient programs.

Debre Markos comprehensive specialized hospital, the only reference hospital in the East Gojjam administrative zone is located in Debre Markos, which is 299 km to the north-west of Addis Ababa. The hospital serves for more than 3.5 million peoples in the catchment area.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

All adult diabetic patients who are aged ≥ 18 years and are receiving insulin therapy and those who had a minimum of followed up period of 1 year were included in the study.

Where as Patients whose insulin treatment was only transient, Pregnant women, hospitalized patients, HIV patients, patients with contact dermatitis on the injection area, and those with incomplete data charts were excluded from the study.

Sample size determination

The sample size was estimated using a single population proportion formula. We used a proportion of 50% (p) in order to find the maximum sample size, at a 95% confidence interval, and 1.96 for Z and 5% for d, the sample size was:

n = 1.96*1.96*0.5(1- 0.5)/0.05*0.05 = 384.

Finally, considering a 10% contingency for possible non-response rate, and missing data = 384 + 38.4 = 423 samples were taken totally.

Sampling technique

Using six months of registry data (September 2022 to February 2023) from three comprehensive specialized hospitals, a total of 4,876 adult diabetic patients on insulin attended the diabetic centers, and proportional allocation was conducted based on this data meaning And their proportions were 1872 (1872 * 423/ 4876 = 162.3 = 162), 1728 (1728 * 423 / 4876 = 149.9 = 150), and 1276 (1276 * 423 / 4876 = 110.6 = 111) at UoGCSH, TGCSH, and DMCSHs respectively. A systematic random sampling technique was used to select samples from the study population.

Operational definitions

LD was assessed as “present” or “not present”.

When the injection site has a noticeable or palpable lump following examination, palpitation, and pinching operations, LD is present; if the injection site does not have a noticeable or palpable lump after applying all the techniques twice, LD is not present.

LD grading:

Grade 1 = clearly enlarged fat tissue yet with palpably normal consistency.

Grade 2 = fat tissue has massively thickened and is firm and,

Grade 3 (LA) = a perceptible cutaneous depression and subcutaneous fat tissue atrophy at the injection location.

LH = the first two grades.

LA = the last grade6,19.

Level of glycemic control was defined as “optimal glycemic control” and “non-optimal glycemic control”. The three most current values (average of last 3) for both HgbA1c and plasma glucose was collected and the average value was used as a measure of glycemic control.

Optimal glycemic control is when the recent (average of last 3) HbA1c levels is less than or equal to 7% for adult non-pregnant person,

Non-optimal glycemic control is > 7% in non-pregnant adults of less than or equal to 7% was considered to be optimal3.

Frequent unexplained hypoglycemia‟ is defined as suffering a hypoglycemia episode once or more times a week without a clear trigger, such as a change in medication, diet, or activity5.

Data collection tools and procedure

In this study, questionnaires, physical examination results, and interviews were utilized to gather primary data. After carefully reviewing different literatures, the questionnaire and data collection tool were developed. To ascertain whether lipodystrophy was present and if so to what extent, the evaluator used palpation and inspection techniques on the patient’s skin in the areas used for subcutaneous insulin administration.

The process involved examining each area of concern under direct and angled lighting against a dark background, taking into account the patient’s positioning during injection. It also included a thorough palpation method using slow circular and vertical fingertip motions, followed by multiple horizontal attempts at the same location. For abdominal examinations, patients were positioned lying down and then stood up afterward. For thigh examination, they sat down with bent legs and feet flat on the floor. Clinical pharmacists gently touched the skin and then gradually increased finger pressure. When they encountered firmer skin, they used a pinching technique to assess the thickness of suspected lipodystrophy compared to the surrounding areas. They repeated these methods for smaller, flatter lesions as needed. This method has a consistency rate of 96–99% with an ultrasonographic diagnosis which is the gold standard for the detection of lipodystrophy8.

Information regarding medication and clinical characteristics was obtained from patients’ medical charts. Patients were asked about their injection, storage, and handling of insulin techniques and the possibility of checking of injection sites by any healthcare professionals.

Data quality assurance

A pre-test was conducted on 5% (22 samples) of diabetic patients on insulin therapy from outside the study area, Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital diabetic center, and these individuals were subsequently excluded from the final analysis. And an appropriate modification was then made based on the pre-test result.

Data processing and analysis

The collected data was processed and analyzed by the principal investigators using IBM SPSS version 26 software. After conducting a bivariate analysis, factors with a p-value less than 0.20 enter multivariate logistic regression. Cross-tabulation was used to identify variables with cell counts less than 5, and multicollinearity, outliers, and assurance of independence observations (it has to be mutually exclusive and exhaustive) were assessed before the analysis. To determine the statistical significance of associations, all tests were conducted using two-sided analyses. Odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were employed. A p-value of less than 0.05 was chosen as the threshold to assess the relationship between dependent and independent variables. Model fitting and goodness fit tests were conducted after binary logistic regression was completed and all relevant variables had been identified. Hosmer and Lemeshow’s test leaves us with a p-value of 0.723, which is greater than 0.05. The model fitness test using Nagelkerke R square was 0.559 again we are confident that our model fits the data set in that 55.9% of our dependent variable variance is explained by this model under this data set.

Ethical approval and informed consent to participate

This study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of the School of Pharmacy, College of Medicine and Health Sciences, University of Gondar, and the study protocol was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee under reference number Sops/296/2015 E.C., on 15 July 2023.

Informed consent, obtained verbally, was secured from all participants prior to their inclusion in the study. Each individual was provided with a clear explanation of the purpose of the research, the reasons for their selection, and their right to decline participation without any impact on their routine medical care. Participants were also assured of the confidentiality and anonymity of the information collected throughout the study.

Anthropometric, clinical and medication-related characteristics of the study participants

Over half (54.3%) of the individuals in the study received treatment for type 1 diabetes mellitus. The larger part of study participants (71.5%) reported having diabetes mellitus for over five years, with an average duration of 9.66 ± 5.83 years. Of the total participants, 56.5% of them have used insulin for less than five years. A higher frequency (67.1%) of the patients had a healthy weight whereas only a few 0.7% were obese based on percentile-adjusted BMI. Regarding medication use, more than half of the participants (55.3%) of them were on NPH insulin therapy. Another observation in this study was that only 8.5% of the study participants were dependent on family members for injections but most of them (86.1%) injected the insulin by themselves. 28.3% of them have comorbid conditions and among this hypertension alone covers 31.3% whereas dyslipidemia and heart failure constitute 13% and 7.8% respectively. Lipodystrophy

Lipodystrophy was observed in 225 cases, constituting 53.1% of the prevalence, among these, grade 2 lipohypertrophy being the most common type, accounting for 117 cases, or 52.7% of the total, characterized by significant thickening of fat tissue with a firm consistency. Grade 3 lipodystrophy, or lipoatrophy, was detected in only 4 cases, representing just 1.8% of the total. The largest proportion of injection sites (81, 36.5%) where lipohypertrophy developed was in the abdomen, followed by lipohypertrophy at multiple sites (71, 32%). Predictor of lipodystrophy

When conducting bivariate logistic regression analysis, the following variables including age, presence of diabetic complications, injection site rotation, duration of insulin treatment, glycemic control, types of diabetes mellitus, insulin needle reuse, education level, presence of comorbid conditions, space measurement when injecting into the same area, and insulin dose per weight in kilograms demonstrated p-values below 0.25 and were therefore considered as candidates for multivariate logistic regression analysis. Some factors like heart failure although considered as a comorbidity they were not listed and used in analysis as complication of DM due to lack of documented association with it. Among these variables, four of them were found to have a significant association with lipodystrophy. In terms of insulin syringe usage, patients who used them 6 to 10 times had over three times the odds of developing lipodystrophy compared to those who used them fewer than three times with AOR = 3.06, 95% CI: (2.00–4.25), P < 0.001. Those who used syringes more than ten times had nearly four times the odds of developing lipodystrophy with AOR = 4.09, 95% CI: (3.42–5.19), P < 0.001 but we did not find an association between needle reuse from 3 to 6 times with lipodystrophy. Furthermore, patients who did not rotate the insulin injection site frequently were 1.8 times with AOR 1.8, 95% CI (1.02–3.19), and P-value of 0.04 more likely to develop lipodystrophy than those who don’t. Patients with higher insulin dose per weight likely had 2.1 times odds of having lipodystrophy with AOR 2.1 95% CI (1.19–3.69), P-value 0.001 than patients with lower insulin daily dose and patients with poor glycemic control had 2.15 times 2.15 (95% CI 1.13–4.1, P-value 0.019) odds of having lipodystrophy than good glycemic control (Table 4).

Discussion

To the extent of our search, this study represents the first attempt to assess the prevalence of lipodystrophy among adult diabetic patients on insulin therapy in Ethiopia. Additionally, it is one of the handful of studies to explore the health-related quality of life in this patient group and examine potential associations between them.

This study encompassed both Type 1 and Type 2 diabetic participants, with 53.1% of them exhibiting lipohypertrophy. This result is consistent with findings from different studies for instance prevalences, in Norway (53%), China (53.1%), Malaysia (51.6%), and a meta-analysis of 20 studies (51.73%)7,20–22. In some research, this figure appears to be somewhat higher compared to our findings. For instance, in India prevalence of 68%23. In England, a study specifically focusing on adult patients with type 1 diabetes reported a prevalence of 70%23. This variance could potentially be clarified by the utilization of ultrasonographic diagnostic methods, which possess a greater capacity to identify subclinical lipohypertrophy24. In contrast, in certain studies such as those conducted in Pakistan and Malaysia, the prevalence was notably lower, with figures of 22.9% and 39% respectively25,26. This could be attributed to differences in inclusion criteria, such as the incorporation of patients on insulin for shorter durations, as well as variations in examination methods and the expertise of the healthcare provider conducting the examination. And lipoatrophy was observed in 0.99% of the participants, a finding consistent with various studies conducted across different countries. For example, a study in Thailand reported a lipoatrophy prevalence of 1%, while in Italy, it was found to be 0.9%15,27. The study also found a significant association between patients who reused needles more than six times and the presence of lipodystrophy, in contrast to those who used a needle less than three times. This finding aligns with similar results from prior studies conducted in Southern Italy, China, and India28–30.

To bolster our argument further, a study conducted in Jordan revealed that the risk of lipodystrophy associated with insulin needle reuse is significantly higher compared to those who do not reuse needles. Even more compelling than ours, this study demonstrated that 70% of individuals who reuse needles develop lipodystrophy, and the risk doubles when the needle has been reused 3–5 times31. This might be because reused needles can undergo deformation, becoming barbed and burred, heightening the resistance experienced during needle insertion and removal, consequently, this may result in needle blockage and fracture, elevating the risk of lipodystrophy32. When questioned about their reasons for reusing the injection needle multiple times, the majority of participants, including those with health insurance coverage who faced no financial constraints in purchasing needles, stated that it was what healthcare providers recommended. This might suggest a potential lack of awareness within the healthcare provider community in our setting regarding the link between lipodystrophy and needle reuse. This observation could be reinforced by a study in China, indicating that only 38.7% of physicians are aware of all potential hazards associated with lipodystrophy33. Studies have also investigated the frequency of needle use to maintain effective glycemic control. They propose that using a needle fewer than three times can aid in reaching the target HgA1c levels while reducing injection-related complications such as lipodystrophy34. The Food and Drug Authority (FDA) in the United States advises that injection needles should be used only once35, yet this may not always be viable in nations like Ethiopia, where socio-economic factors necessitate the consideration of more pragmatic solutions.

According to this study patients who don’t rotate their injection site at least once weekly are more likely to have lipodystrophy when compared with those who do, this result is in line with a study done in India which showed that out of patients who refrained from rotating injection sites, 83.9% developed lipohypertrophy, while only 26.8% of patients who adhered to site rotation developed this condition36. A similar finding was mentioned in different studies conducted in various countries like China, Saudi Arabia, and Thailand27,28,37.Furthermore, a study carried out in Turkey indicated that not only was the incidence of lipohypertrophy higher in patients who neglected to alternate injection sites (systematic rotation), but it was also common among those who failed to rotate within the same site38. So it’s crucial to educate patients about the importance of rotating injection sites and offer a simple, memorable technique where each injection is administered approximately 1 cm away from previous ones, roughly the width of a finger39.The main rationale frequently cited for neglecting to rotate injection sites is the diminished pain sensation associated with injecting into the same area consistently. In our context, this accounts for 34.2%, while other factors such as practicality (27.3%), ease of administration (21.6%), and challenges in accessing specific areas (19.4%) are also noted.

Yet another finding from this study is that the frequency of lipodystrophy is significantly elevated among individuals with HgA1c levels greater than 7 by a factor of 2 times. Our findings were similarly in line with those of previous studies in this regard. Nearly all preceding studies have documented a notable decrease in insulin absorption (including instances of radiolabeled insulin clearance at the injection site), followed by an increase in patients’ glycated hemoglobin levels23,31,40. It’s been long known that insulin-induced lipodystrophy can disrupt regular insulin absorption and consequently impact glycemic control41.However, some studies fail to demonstrate an association between lipodystrophy and glycemic control17. This discrepancy could possibly stem from the concurrent increase in insulin dosage, which ultimately leads to improved glycemic control, as demonstrated by a study conducted in Turkey42.

Furthermore, it was observed that among participants who received a high daily insulin dosage of over 0.7 IU/kg in body weight, the occurrence of lipodystrophy was twice as prevalent. Studies conducted in different countries like Turkey, Italy, and Jordan have all demonstrated the association between lipodystrophy and daily insulin dosage31,43,44. The reason for this is mainly because, in diabetic patients receiving insulin therapy, the absorption of insulin plays a crucial role in controlling blood sugar levels. However, lipodystrophy has been proven to interfere with the usual uptake of insulin from subcutaneous tissue, causing an inappropriate increase in insulin dosage16.

So far, the follow-up procedure for diabetic patients in our study settings can be considered effective, with regular checks on their glycated hemoglobin levels and adjustments made to their insulin and other anti-diabetic medication doses as necessary. However, there seems to have been neglect in assessing lipodystrophy, with only 6.88% of the study participants undergoing evaluation at the insulin injection site in the past year. This oversight is worrying, considering the significant influence of lipodystrophy on both glycemic control and insulin dosage. Therefore, it’s essential to prioritize the assessment of lipodystrophy in treatment protocols and to educate about the condition and preventive measures, as education programs for both patients and staff have been demonstrated to yield positive effects on both the identification and prevention of the condition45.

Conclusion

This study suggests that lipodystrophy is a common complication among diabetic patients in the diabetic clinics of North West Ethiopia, with Grade II lipodystrophy being the most commonly observed form. These findings underscores the importance of continuous professional education by the healthcare providers, particularly regarding insulin injection techniques such as, injection site rotation and the avoidance of repeated syringe use. Furthermore, routine inspection of injection sites, especially during insulin dose adjustments and in patients experiencing frequent hypoglycemic episodes, could be considered as part of routine clinical practice. The results of this study add to the evidence base regarding the prevalence and clinical relevance of lipodystrophy, and support the need for preventive strategies within diabetic care protocols.

Study strength and limitations

While this study was conducted across three major hospitals in Northwest Ethiopia, enhancing the external validity and generalizability of the findings; the cross-sectional design limits the ability to infer causal or temporal relationships between the variables examined. Although a standardized physical examination technique was used to assess lipodystrophy, this method has limited sensitivity. The gold standard, ultrasonographic evaluation, was not employed; therefore, subclinical or non-palpable cases may have gone undetected. These factors should be considered when interpreting the prevalence estimates reported in this study.

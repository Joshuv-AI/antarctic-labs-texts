Case Summary Prompting the Project Launch

Epic is the EMR used in this project. A 2023 all-staff survey identified 50% of cardiology NPPAs reporting burnout, with work within the Epic system's inbox (InBasket) as the primary contributor. Observational data confirmed that the current process was unsustainable, with providers spending excessive time outside of clinic hours managing InBasket messages. Additionally, internal organizational changes highlighted the need for improved workflow efficiency. A quality improvement project was launched to assess the impact of dedicating 1 hour per day to InBasket management.

Project Rationale

The identified gap in quality was the time to respond to patient questions, known as “turnaround time.” There is not a standard way to manage InBasket messages that require medical decision-making, resulting in variability in the time for NPPAs to respond to these messages. The project aimed to improve turnaround time for medical decision-making messages by cardiology NPPAs by 50% by December 2024. We also aimed to improve provider satisfaction and explore whether dedicated time could enhance efficiency without negatively affecting clinical productivity.

Project Description

Stakeholders identified potential causes contributing to the gap in quality by creating a Pareto diagram and identifying the top 3 causes. These were: 1) lack of dedicated time; 2) a large volume of total messages; and 3) messages that required medical decision-making, including a change in the plan of care and coordination of patient care. We came to the consensus that the primary cause was lack of dedicated time for NPPAs to manage InBasket work.

We then outlined the workflow of InBasket messages with a process map, discovering that NPPA turnaround time was only 1 component of a larger process. The larger process included administrative and nursing workflows. Project Deliverables

Data from the Epic system were collected at baseline and at 3 and 6 months after the intervention (1 hour at 4 pm to manage InBasket messages). The messages that were considered to require medical decision-making included patient medical advice requests, patient phone calls/communications, and staff messages from providers within and outside the department of cardiovascular medicine. Depending on the experience of the NPPA and the specific question raised, some medical decision-making messages required further discussion with the attending physician.

Project Outcome, Impact, and Future Directions

The project demonstrated that dedicated time for InBasket management improved workflow efficiency by reducing the turnaround time and increasing provider satisfaction. The project also showed that InBasket volume and workload varied by subspecialty within the cardiology department and by provider. It is worth noting that during our project, the total volume of messages per day and those that required medical decision-making remained constant in both the pilot and standard-of-care groups. Interestingly, the turnaround time initially decreased, but toward the end of our intervention, it increased again. Next Steps

The following points outline future directions:•Continue dedicated InBasket time,•Expand pilot group to include additional providers,•Consider establishing a threshold of InBasket work/volume that would qualify for this intervention,•Evaluate other workflow factors affecting turnaround time (ie, paperwork, face-to-face messages, results),•Assess the role of nursing support in message triage,•Measure the impact on “pajama time” (ie, extended work hours outside of clinic).

Discussion

Our study highlights the balance between provider workload, patient care efficiency, and quality cardiovascular care. Dedicated time for InBasket management improved immediate turnaround and satisfaction, but long-term sustainability needs workflow optimizations. Incorporating nursing support and automating tasks could enhance efficiency and quality care. There is an opportunity to evaluate the use of artificial intelligence to assist with prioritizing tasks, answering basic questions, and improving workflows so that providers can focus on critical, patient-centered care.

Conclusions

Providing 1 hour of dedicated time for InBasket management significantly reduced response time and improved provider satisfaction. Our project highlighted the complex interplay of factors when managing InBasket messages and tasks, including those outside of provider control.

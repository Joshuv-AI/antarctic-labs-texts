A deep learning optimized model for classification and detection of rice leaf diseases
Authors: Shashank Chaudhary, Upendra Kumar, Biswa Mohan Sahoo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Food productivity, quantity and quality are at stake when plant diseases such as rice diseases undermine the food security. Rice leaf disease treatment necessitates accurate and timely diagnosing. This study describes a deep learning model for categorizing and forecasting rice plant diseases. Using the remora optimization algorithm (ROA) on a rice leaf dataset demonstrates its potential for plant disease classification. The ROA-DM method detects rice leaf diseases using the ROA algorithm, a deep maxout network (DMN), and a deep autoencoder (DAE). ROA is applied to the learning parameters of deep model in order to achieve better convergence and avoiding local minima, which usually happens with conventional gradient-based optimizers. Experiments show that the suggested framework is accurate and precise across illness categories. The confusion matrices display the training and validation accuracy, losses of this model. The performance of our optimal learning method with respect to other methods indicated its potential for identifying leaf diseases. The accuracy of the ROA-DM method is 98.5%.

Introduction

Agriculture contributes to feeding the world by producing food for billions of people as their principal source of nourishment. Rice is an important crop worldwide and many people derive most of their calories from rice. However, the global rice industry continues to deal with a persistent issue, plant diseases that threaten crop production and food security. Among these, rice leaf diseases are a serious menace as they can spread fast and cause huge loss in the fields. The skill to recognize plant diseases is mostly visual assessment done by experienced farmers or plant experts1,2. However, this method is considered to be time-and effort-intensive in predicting performance. There is a growing demand to develop new strategies for disease control. Efforts to promote computer vision and machine learning have resulted in the development of automatic approaches for detecting and classifying rice leaf diseases3,4.

The gradual exploration in this area can increase rice production and control outbreaks of diseases substantially and thereby decrease losses5. Nevertheless, the use of these procedures can greatly increase efficiency and accuracy in identifying diseases from rice plants. Deep learning classification models are powerful data processors6,7. While these models are capable, they can have weaknesses such as hyperparameter sensitivity, slow convergence and a tendency to be trapped in local minima due to their use of gradient-based optimization algorithms. These issues provide motivation to develop hybrid models combining deep learning and metaheuristic optimization algorithms which can be advantageous in terms of efficiency on training data as well as generalization ability8,9,10. Literature review

The issue of plant and crop diseases has been a serious problem in worldwide food production. Among agricultural products, rice is an essential food staple for a large fraction of the world’s population. It is imperative to control rice diseases to maintain the yield and food security. The development of automated tools for identifying and classifying disease symptoms on rice leaves has become increasingly attractive. This literature review summarizes the existing literature in this domain, investigating key methods such as machine learning, deep learning, and metaheuristics.

Albattah et al.13 proposed an overall system for plant disease classification. This approach is achieved by training a Custom CenterNet framework using DenseNet-77 as the base network. The approach reported in this paper can be divided into three consecutive stages. The first phase is to create annotations to select and outline the region of interest. Furthermore, a simpler CenterNet setup in which DenseNet-77 should be used for learning detailed key points. The Internet of things based on plant disease detection and classification is a one stage detector.

Arguesoa et al.14 proposed an approach based on deep learning, FSL, to detect and classify plant diseases. The critical points were calculated in the Inception V3 network. The features were then employed to train a multiclass SVM. Early rice disease detection using machine learning involved the use of random forest, SVM, and decision tree classifiers for classification of diseased rice leaves from images using handcrafted features. Although these approaches were found to be useful, the data representation failed to accommodate a variety of subtle disease manifestations. The method shows the capability for robustness in classification of plant diseases. But the paper’s findings must be kept in proportion as they stem from a small dataset. Therefore, further evaluation is necessary to assess its effectiveness on a more extensive and diversified corpus. Curing tomato crop disease was proposed by Agarwal et al.15 by using a CNN-based structure. The method consisted of three convolutional layers with max pooling to learn prominent features of the input data, followed by classification.

Shah et al.16 introduced ReSTS, a CNN-based approach for predicting the next slide in STASs. An additional decoder is included in this architecture, together with two separate classifiers (ResTeacher and ResStudent). To train both classifiers, the proposed architecture utilizes a reciprocal training technique. The representation propagated between the ResTeacher and the ResStudent serves as a proxy for determining where parts of the image are more valuable for categorization. An autoencoder is employed to preserve the main features in the image. The autoencoder decomposes and re-paints the image, stripping it of noise and retaining only the important information.

Jiang et al.17 presented an apple leaf disease localization approach, which has impact on the yield of apple fruits. However, such attempts have to cope with the challenge of finding effective and efficient approaches. In this paper, the author presents a Deep Learning approach with upgraded CNN configuration for apple leaf diseases real-time detection. The experiments were conducted on the Apple Leaf Disease Dataset. The picture annotation and also the data Augmentation were used to build-up this dataset. The experimental results seem to indicate that the proposed INAR-SSD has good detection accuracy (about 78.8%) and high detection speed (23.13 FPS). Thus, the designed method can obtain higher accuracy and a faster detection rate compared with previous methods for early diagnosis of apple plant diseases.

To enable agricultural robotics for automatic disease detection and classification of diseases that affect rice leaves, Simhadri et al.18 suggested transfer learning based using 15 pre-trained CNN models. Efficient, robust methods to diagnose and manage diseases in rice plants are widely searched by plant pathologists. Farmers often use disease-detection methods based on visual observations, which is a time-consuming process and may be accompanied by improper agricultural practices. Agricultural technology has enhanced the detection of pathogenic organisms in rice plant. Deep learning and machine learning have attracted great attention in crop disease studies.

Hossain et al.19 proposed an original CNN model for rice leaf disease detection. In all, 4199 images were employed for training and testing of the model, corresponding to five typical diseases of rice leaves. The model had decent accuracy for both training and validation rounds. The model is tested by applying it to photographs taken by the model itself, and we have seen that the obtained recognition rates are acceptable under some conditions. Thenmozhi et al.20 presented a deep CNN model with excellent performance in classifying pests. They contrasted their model with other pre-trained transfer learning models. Upadhyay et al.21 put forward disease classification of rice. This information is used to validate leaf lesion shape, colour and size from the photographs. The corresponding CNN-based fully connected method achieves 99.7% accuracy on our dataset.

Chen et al.22 introduced a novel approach, BLSNet for detecting and classifying Rice Bacterial Leaf Streak (BLS) disease. This has wide implications for rice cultivation in that it affects yield and the quality of rice. The experimental results demonstrated that BLSNet was superior in identifying damage and locating damaged locations compared to the other leading models. It may be useful as a bioassay to measure the amount of BLS disease. Stephen et al.23 introduced four CNN models to class all healthy and diseased leaves. To deal with those gradient problems, researchers used ResNet34 and ResNet50 as the architectures. In the multiclass competition, for the task that defined the proposed ResNet34 model to one’s point, obtained accuracy of 0.98.54% surpassing several approaches.

Li et al.24 developed a deep learning system for real-time detection of diseases and pests on a plant leaf in videos. To find low-quality photos, the method employed Faster R-CNN in conjunction with image-training models. Experiments showed that our custom architecture is very strong in identifying the movies on the system it was not trained at, even when compared to latest state of art approaches25.

Kawasaki et al.26 thus introduced a CNN for the detection of cucumber leaf disease. The accuracy of the system is as high as 94.9%, and it is able to identify and classify cucumber leaf disease well. Despite the positive results found in the literature, previous studies based mainly on image datasets with limited variation. Photographic evidence typically comprises photographs taken only in the laboratory or similar controlled environment rather than actual uncontrolled field situations. Field-cultivated images have commonly variable backgrounds and symptom patterns27.

Bedi et al.28 proposed a new U-Net-based model, namely AResUNet, with the help of residual links to facilitate feature propagation and network learning. This has also been used in medical imaging tasks, e.g., PCOS detection. AResUNet excellently learns the details and delicate structures in ultrasound images, thus it can distinguish between the ovaries and PCOS-affected ones. However, the complexity of this model can raise its costs and finally increase its prediction time, which may challenge the suitability of being used in real-time clinical setting.

Alamoudi et al.29 presented a fusion model that combines ultrasound images with information to decide whether or not the patient has PCOS. MobileNet has been specifically tailored for network computation and is well suited for low computational resource applications. We it uses depthwise convolutions to decrease the number of parameters and computation amount. MobileNet, as a result of its architecture and a reasonably good accuracy is eligible to become promising for PCOS detection. The combined attributes based and image feature extraction using MobileNet architecture model obtained an accuracy of 82.46%.

Preti et al.30 provided an extensive review of the development of insect pest monitoring, particularly in relations of techniques and tools adopted. Fiber optic sensors have been used in these traps as the first-generation device for images. The incorporation of IoT and AI, contemporary information technologies, has facilitated the creation and customization of diverse devices for pest surveillance. Saranya et al.31 created a method to identify insects by their thermal radiation emissions using a passive infrared sensor and picture processing. To confirm the pest’s existence in the field, image processing is used to create visual representations of it.

Edelaar et al.32 conducted a study on Azure Sand Grasshoppers to examine their color-changing behavior and their ability to blend into their surroundings as a defensive response to predators. In the context of deep convolutional neural networks, Chen et al.33 proposed using transfer learning to identify plant leaf diseases. The decision has been made to use the VGGNet model trained on the ImageNet dataset, along with the Inception module, rather than starting training from random weights. This choice is based on using pre-trained networks trained on the well-annotated, massive ImageNet dataset for weight initialization.

Although deep learning has shown promising results in plant disease identification, specialized frameworks that address the challenges of rice leaf diseases are necessary. Our work bridges this gap, by offering a comprehensive deep-learning approach for the identification and categorization of plant disease in rice leaves34,35. In this work, we apply state-of-the-art deep learning methods on a well-curated dataset with the purpose of providing efficient and accurate resolution to this agricultural issue. Data collection and preprocessing

The dataset collected from https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/emmarex/plantdisease (PlantVillage dataset) for algorithm testing in plant disease diagnosis35. The selected rice leaf disease samples from PlantVillage dataset consisting of 3050 colour leaf images across four classes. These categories consist of 500 images of brown spot, 1100 images of common rust, 1000 images of healthy leaf, and 450 images of leaf blight. All images were performed under controlled conditions, with a blank background and no significant changes in light intensity. Several images of the same leaf from random directions are taken. Quality and diversity of the dataset are crucial for the success of any deep learning model for image classification. High-quality datasets are essential for training and evaluating the efficacy of deep learning models for plant disease detection. Dark-field-featured plant diseases, including the PlantVillage datasets, have played a significant role in advancing research in this domain. These datasets include several images of various diseases with different severity levels. The images are high quality rice leaf images having different diseases with various levels of severity.

ROA-based deep model intrusion detection

The deep model is used for intrusion detection, incorporating a DMN classifier and a DAE architecture. The Hybrid deep model has demonstrated high efficacy in attaining superior detection results. The deep learning model’s training technique is executed using the suggested ROA. Nature, and more specifically the parasitic habits of remoras, serves as an inspiration for the ROA algorithm, a meta-heuristic method. When two separate host properties interact, the result is the best possible outcome: exploitation and exploration. The process of updating the switch between different modes involves identification and selection.

Moreover, the remora aspect has been designed to support efforts to enhance area regeneration. In contrast, ROA is an optimization methodology that mimics remoras’ hunting methods. The ROA improved its detection results by efficiently mitigating optimization challenges. The deep model is trained to utilize the developed optimization algorithm, ROA-DM.

The suggested ROA is used to conduct the training procedure for the deep learning method. The ROA [9] method is based on the parasitic behaviour of the remora, a model organism. Maximal efficacy is attained by using two separate classes: whales and swordfish. The switching between different approaches is efficient by choosing the most easily recognizable movement style. Moreover, the factors of remora are developed to assist in accurately modifying regional revitalization. In contrast, it aims to optimize solutions by leveraging the strategies employed by these marine creatures. The combination of ROA and WOA improved detection outcomes by effectively mitigating optimization difficulties.

During the exploration, remora factors are appended to ensure accuracy and convergence. Thanks to these good measures, ROA’s research is fruitful. The system was generated and assessed on an Intel i7 system equipped with a W-2125 CPU and 128 GB of RAM, operating at 4.00 GHz with 4 cores, a high-performance system. The construction and testing procedures were performed using MATLAB R2019a. The experiments used deep models, specifically the DMN Classifier and DAE, trained on the PlantVillage dataset comprising 3050 classes. The dataset is usually split into three groups, i.e., training, testing, and validation. The ratio for a combined set is 80% for training, 10% for testing, and 10% for validation. Adjusting the initial and final layers customizes these models for our use case. According to the model’s architecture, convolutional, softmax, fully connected, and output layers are modified. Most pre-trained models capture generic traits at lower levels, while the top layers are better at capturing task-specific information. The pre-existing model is customized for new jobs by being adjusted to higher levels, thereby capitalizing on information acquired from the first job, and all models are trained with identical hyperparameters. 40 epochs were used for training, selected based on how well the models performed during training and validation.

Using a variety of data partitioning strategies, it was determined whether the models could generalize to a larger, out-of-sample testing set. Conclusion

This paper proposed a ROA-DM method, which consists of the Deep Autoencoder, the Deep Max-out Network and Remora Optimization Algorithm for effective identification of rice leaf disease. Through matching superior feature extraction with metaheuristic-guided parameter tuning, the proposed model effectively alleviates the shortcomings of gradient-based light deep learning methods and obtains better convergence properties along with enhanced classification rates. The empirical findings confirm that the ROA-DM method outperforms existing deep-learning models and its accuracy is 98.5%. The new method performed remarkably well compared to a set of existing methods. The collected data of rice diseases results into an overall accuracy of 86.61% under complex environmental background in class prediction. Extensive experiments and comparisons have demonstrated the effectiveness of our framework, which presents an applicable solution to rice disease management.

Although the ROA-DM model has achieved high classification ability it has relatively higher computational complexity than metaheuristic and deep learning-based techniques due to the incorporation of metaheuristic with deep learning that makes its training time longer and more resource consuming. Moreover, its effectiveness will be impaired by the size of dataset, image quality and differences in real field. Future work will involve development of a light model which should be faster, acceleration the ROA convergence speed and validation of our framework on larger real time filed collected data sets for robustness poo to apply in smart agriculture system.

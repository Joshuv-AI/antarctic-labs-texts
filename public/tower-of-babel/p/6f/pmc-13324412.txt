Cellular Senescence: Emerging Therapeutic Target for Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis Pathogenic Mechanisms and Therapeutic Strategies
Authors: Nattha Suwanprakorn, Youngrok Choi, Yeong Seok Choi, Hye Jin Yoo, Su-Geun Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis is a progressive and fatal lung disease characterized by irreversible tissue scarring, structural distortion, and declining respiratory function. Although currently approved antifibrotic therapies can slow disease progression, they do not stop or reverse fibrosis, highlighting the need to better understand upstream disease mechanisms. Increasing evidence identifies cellular senescence as a central biological process linking aging, epithelial cell vulnerability, mitochondrial dysfunction, and abnormal tissue repair in pulmonary fibrosis. Senescent epithelial and mesenchymal cells remain metabolically active and release a broad array of secreted factors that promote fibroblast activation, persistence of contractile cells, and excessive matrix deposition. Recent advances using single-cell and three-dimensional culture approaches further reveal the accumulation of transitional epithelial cell states that fail to complete normal differentiation and instead perpetuate fibrotic remodeling. These findings suggest that senescence represents not merely a consequence of tissue injury, but a self-sustaining driver of disease progression. This review integrates current mechanistic insights into how cellular senescence coordinates epithelial injury, immune dysregulation, and biomechanical feedback to drive lung fibrosis. We also discuss emerging therapeutic strategies that target senescent cells or their downstream signaling pathways, highlighting their potential to complement existing antifibrotic treatments and enable more effective disease modification.

INTRODUCTION

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a relatively rare disease but represents the most common subtype of idiopathic interstitial pneumonia (IIP), a group of diffuse parenchymal lung disorders with no identifiable cause. It is also the most prevalent and progressive form of pulmonary fibrosis (PF), associated with a high symptom burden and poor prognosis. IPF is characterized by chronic and progressive fibrosis of the lung interstitium. The disease leads to a permanent decline in lung function caused by progressive fibrosis, presenting clinically with increasing cough, shortness of breath, and significant deterioration in overall quality of life. The pathological hallmark of IPF is excessive extracellular matrix (ECM) accumulation and irreversible decline in lung function. Because IPF predominantly affects older adults, the global aging population is expected to substantially increase both the prevalence and the economic burden of the disease in the coming decades.

Globally, IPF affects an estimated three million individuals, with reported incidence rates ranging from 2.9-8.65 cases per 100,000 in North America and Europe, and 2-4.16 cases per 100,000 in Asia. In the United States alone, approximately 100,000 people are currently living with IPF, and 30,000-40,000 new cases are diagnosed annually. The prognosis remains poor, with a median survival of 3-5 years following diagnosis. Moreover, the economic impact is substantial, with annual healthcare costs exceeding $40,000 per patient, not including indirect costs such as productivity loss, caregiver burden, and reduced quality of life (Huang et al., 2025; Lederer and Martinez, 2018; Maher et al., 2021; Zhou et al., 2024).

Risk factors for IPF include advanced age (typically >60 years), male sex (male-to-female ratio ≈ 2:1), cigarette smoking, and exposure to occupational hazards such as silica, metal, wood, and stone dust (Park et al., 2024). Emerging evidence also implicates air pollution, with PM2.5 exposure being associated with an increased risk of IPF (Cui et al., 2023). The pathogenesis of IPF is driven by repetitive injury to alveolar epithelial cells particularly type II alveolar epithelial cells responsible for surfactant production and tissue repair. Chronic injury disrupts epithelial-mesenchymal crosstalk, resulting in aberrant activation of fibroblasts and myofibroblasts that excessively deposit ECM proteins such as collagen and fibronectin, leading to irreversible lung stiffening (Kobayashi et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2023). Key molecular drivers include dysregulated transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) signaling, oxidative stress associated with mitochondrial dysfunction, and cellular senescence a state of irreversible cell-cycle arrest that fosters a profibrotic microenvironment through the secretion of senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) factors (Han et al., 2023; Justice et al., 2019).

Current therapeutic practice demonstrates that antifibrotic agents such as nintedanib and pirfenidone can slow the decline in forced vital capacity (FVC) and delay disease progression; however, they do not halt or reverse fibrosis. This limitation underscores the need for combination approaches pairing antifibrotics with pathway-specific or senescence-modulating agents.

GENERAL PATHOGENIC MECHANISMS OF LUNG FIBROSIS

Pulmonary fibrosis represents a maladaptive form of wound healing in which persistent epithelial damage triggers chronic inflammation and unregulated ECM deposition. In IPF, this process arises from the convergence of aging-related vulnerability, environmental exposures, oxidative injury, mitochondrial dysfunction, and genetic susceptibility.

As illustrated in Fig. 1, IPF progression can be conceptualized as a multiscale process spanning macroscopic architectural distortion, alveolar structural remodeling, and cellular-level dysfunction. Initially, normal lung architecture with compliant alveoli and intact epithelial renewal transitions into a fibrotic state characterized by reduced airspace, ECM accumulation, and impaired gas exchange. At the cellular level, this progression is driven by loss of AT2 regenerative capacity, accumulation of senescent and Keratin 8 (KRT8⁺) transitional epithelial cells, and activation of fibroblasts and myofibroblasts, ultimately establishing a self-perpetuating fibrotic loop. Multiple upstream stressors including epithelial injury, genetic susceptibility, and ER stress converge on mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress as a central pathogenic hub that drives epithelial maladaptation, immune dysregulation, and progressive fibrotic remodeling (Fig. 2).

Genetic evidence supporting epithelial vulnerability

Genetic and experimental evidence strongly supports epithelial injury as the initiating event in pulmonary fibrosis. Mutations in epithelial-restricted genes such as surfactant protein C (SFTPC), surfactant protein A2 (SFTPA2), and ATP-binding cassette sub-family A member 3 (ABCA3), particularly those affecting SFTPC, which is exclusively expressed in AT2 cells are sufficient to induce spontaneous pulmonary fibrosis in murine models, faithfully recapitulating hallmark features of human IPF (Lu et al., 2025). These findings provide direct proof that intrinsic epithelial vulnerability precedes and triggers the downstream fibrotic cascade, which then becomes self-sustaining through persistent ECM remodeling and continued myofibroblast activation (Fig. 2A).

A promoter polymorphism in MUC5B, present in more than two-thirds of patients with pulmonary fibrosis, confers a 4-6-fold increased risk of disease (Hewlett et al., 2018). Additional GWAS have identified mutations in telomerase-associated genes in up to 25% of sporadic IPF cases. Telomere shortening found in roughly 10% of patients is highly correlated with cellular senescence markers in lung tissue. Although telomerase defects are not unique to IPF and occur in other ILDs and emphysema, they provide compelling evidence for telomere driven epithelial senescence in disease pathogenesis. TOLLIP has also been associated with IPF susceptibility and mortality. Because TOLLIP negatively regulates TLR3, these variants implicate enhanced innate immune activation as another contributor to epithelial vulnerability. However, these markers have not yet translated into effective risk stratification for non-familial IPF (Kellogg et al., 2021).

ER stress as an amplifier of epithelial vulnerability

Endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress has emerged as a key modifier that magnifies epithelial susceptibility in IPF (Fig. 2B). Activation of the unfolded protein response (UPR) through PERK, IRE1, and ATF6 signaling pathways contributes to translational attenuation, stress-response gene expression, and proteostasis regulation, collectively promoting epithelial apoptosis and senescence under persistent stress conditions. Experimental evidence including studies on L188Q SFTPC transgenic mice and tunicamycin pretreatment, confirm that while ER stress alone is insufficient to induce spontaneous fibrosis, it significantly lowers the threshold for fibrotic remodeling following a secondary insult. In L188Q expressing mice, even low-dose bleomycin induces markedly greater epithelial apoptosis, enhanced caspase activation, and significantly more fibrosis compared to wild type controls. Pharmacologic induction of ER stress with tunicamycin yields a similar pattern: ER stress alone is insufficient to cause fibrosis, but tunicamycin pre-treatment dramatically potentiates bleomycin-induced fibrotic remodeling (Tanjore et al., 2012). Collectively, these findings establish ER stress as a permissive and amplifying factor that lowers the threshold for epithelial injury and accelerates fibrotic progression, reinforcing epithelial vulnerability as a central driver of IPF pathogenesis.

Epithelial injury and fibroblast activation

Recurrent microinjury to the aging alveolar epithelium, particularly alveolar type II (AT2) cells, initiates IPF pathogenesis by triggering a maladaptive epithelial response characterized by apoptosis, cellular senescence, and impaired regenerative capacity. Instead of restoring alveolar integrity, injured AT2 cells fail to complete normal differentiation into alveolar type I cells and accumulate in dysfunctional transitional states. Concurrent activation of stress-response pathways, including p53-p21 and p16^INK4a, promotes stable senescence and acquisition of a senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP), resulting in sustained release of profibrotic mediators such as TGF-β, PDGF, and CTGF (Bi et al., 2025; Enomoto et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2025). These signals recruit and activate fibroblast populations from diverse origins: resident mesenchymal cells, pericytes in the lung interstitium, circulating fibrocytes, and cells undergoing epithelial to mesenchymal or endothelial to mesenchymal transition (Gaikwad et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2024; Wynn, 2011). Activated fibroblasts then differentiate into α smooth muscle actin positive myofibroblasts, driving excessive and compositionally altered ECM deposition (Fig. 2C). (Mei et al., 2021; Rajesh et al., 2023). As ECM stiffness increases, mechanotransductive signaling pathways such as yes-associated protein/ transcriptional co-activator with PDZ-binding motif (YAP/TAZ) and Rho-associated coiled-coil kinase (Rho/ROCK) become activated, reinforcing myofibroblast survival and establishing a self-perpetuating fibrotic loop (DiGiovanni et al., 2023; Warren et al., 2023). In healthy lungs, epithelial repair mechanisms restore homeostasis; however, in IPF, persistent epithelial injury, chronic redox imbalance, and heightened inflammatory signaling impair this reparative capacity, allowing myofibroblasts and ECM expansion to persist in an unregulated manner (Lu et al., 2025; Wang and Chao, 2025). Parallel to these processes, defective epithelial regeneration leads to bronchiolization of distal airspaces, progressive architectural distortion, and aberrant reactivation of developmental pathways including Wnt/β-catenin and Hedgehog signaling that further promote fibrosis and loss of functional gas-exchange units (Effendi and Nagano, 2021; Kadota et al., 2021; Shi et al., 2017). This transition from epithelial injury to fibroblast activation and ECM deposition reflects the early cellular events underlying the structural remodeling depicted in Fig. 1.

Epithelial cells as autonomous drivers of fibrosis

New mechanistic studies demonstrate that epithelial cells are not merely injury targets but can autonomously initiate and sustain fibrosis. A lung organoid-based fibrosis model showed that bleomycin-induced DNA damage activates p53 signaling in AT2-lineage cells, driving a stress-associated AT2 to alveolar type I (AT1) transition-like state characterized by SASP production (Enomoto et al., 2023). Among SASP mediators, TGF-β emerged as the dominant epithelial-derived signal capable of inducing fibroblast-to-myofibroblast differentiation even in the complete absence of immune cells (Enomoto et al., 2023). This reflects not only a paracrine signaling axis, through which epithelial-derived TGF-β directly activates neighboring fibroblasts, but also an autocrine positive feedback loop within AT2 cells that reinforces epithelial dysfunction. Together, these autocrine and paracrine mechanisms reveal that epithelial injury can function as a primary, self-sufficient engine of fibrogenesis (Fig. 2D). Although AT2 cells may transiently enter a senescent-like transitional state during attempted regeneration, the detailed role of stable epithelial senescence in driving fibrosis is addressed in Section 4. Taken together, epithelial vulnerability and maladaptive AT2-fibroblast crosstalk emerge as central drivers of IPF, initiating fibroblast activation, ECM remodeling, mechanotransductive reinforcement, and irreversible structural deterioration of the lung. These findings reinforce the concept that epithelial dysfunction alone can initiate the fibrotic cascade illustrated at the cellular level in Fig. 1. Collectively, genetic susceptibility, ER stress, and epithelial injury-driven signaling do not operate as isolated events but converge on a shared downstream vulnerability characterized by mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress. This convergence establishes a central integrative hub that amplifies epithelial maladaptation, stabilizes senescence-associated phenotypes, and coordinates the transition toward persistent profibrotic signaling, as depicted in Fig. 2E.

Oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction

Oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction represent a central converging hub integrating multiple upstream stress signals in IPF. Reactive oxygen and nitrogen species (ROS/RNS) arise from mitochondrial electron transport, ER and peroxisomal metabolism, nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) oxidases, and inflammatory cells (Bezerra et al., 2023; Estornut et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2025), while exogenous sources such as cigarette smoke, asbestos, radiation, and hyperoxia further elevate oxidant burden (Chen et al., 2022; Pociask et al., 2004). In IPF, this imbalance overwhelms antioxidant defenses, causing cumulative epithelial injury.

AT2 cells already susceptible due to aging and genetic predisposition accumulate dysfunctional mitochondria characterized by impaired mitophagy (↓ PTEN-induced putative kinase 1; PINK1, ↓Parkin), mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) damage and leakage, reduced oxidative phosphorylation, cristae disruption, ATP depletion, and excessive mitochondrial ROS (mtROS) (Bueno et al., 2020; Cala-Garcia et al., 2023). These abnormalities increase mtROS, amplify DNA damage signaling and activate canonical senescence pathways including p53-p21 and p16^INK4a (Kumar and Thirumurugan, 2023; Schafer et al., 2017; Yao et al., 2021), positioning mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress as a central integrative hub linking upstream epithelial vulnerability to downstream fibrotic remodeling (Fig. 2E). A central mediator of this process is suppression of the sentrin-specific protease 1 (SENP1)-Sirt3 mitochondrial redox axis, demonstrated in bleomycin-induced injury (Zhang et al., 2025). Loss of SENP1-dependent deSUMOylation of Sirt3 increases Sirt3 SUMOylation, decreases SOD2 deacetylation/activity, and accelerates mtROS accumulation (Wang et al., 2019). This redox collapse promotes epithelial apoptosis and senescence and is associated with the accumulation of KRT8⁺ transitional cells, a stress-induced intermediate state arising from impaired AT2→AT1 differentiation (Sawhney et al., 2025; Strunz et al., 2020). These cells are linked to impaired epithelial regeneration and contribute to fibrotic remodeling. The detailed role of KRT8+ transitional cells as a senescence-associated profibrotic population is discussed in Section 3.3. Restoring mitochondrial redox balance reverses these maladaptive transitions. Activation of the SENP1-Sirt3 axis (e.g., via Sirt3 K223R mutation) reduces mtROS, restores SOD2 activity, decreases the KRT8⁺ transitional cell burden, and enhances AT2 proliferative and differentiation capacity (Zhang et al., 2025). NAC supplementation yields similar benefits by decreasing mtROS, reducing senescence signaling, and improving epithelial repair (Zhang et al., 2025). These mitochondrial and redox abnormalities integrate signals from genetic susceptibility, ER stress, and environmental injury, positioning oxidative stress as a central node linking upstream epithelial vulnerability to downstream fibrotic outcomes (Fig. 2E). Together, these observations establish oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and epithelial senescence as a central pathogenic hub linking epithelial injury to senescence, impaired regeneration, downstream fibrotic remodeling, and reinforcing a self-sustaining loop (Fig. 2). The amplification of senescence-associated signaling and SASP output from this mitochondrial hub provides a critical mechanistic link to downstream immune dysregulation and fibroblast activation, thereby propagating the fibrotic cascade described in Fig. 2F.

Immune dysregulation and chronic inflammation

Immune dysregulation plays a pivotal role in sustaining the fibrotic cascade in IPF (Fig. 2F). Following epithelial injury, circulating monocytes are recruited into the lung and differentiate into long-lived monocyte-derived alveolar macrophages (Mo-AMs), which gradually replace many tissue-resident macrophages (Misharin et al., 2017). These Mo-AMs acquire a mixed but predominantly profibrotic phenotype, producing TGF-β, C-C motif chemokine ligand 18 (CCL18), chitinase-3-like protein 1 (CHI3L1), osteopontin, and matrix metalloproteinases (MMP-3, MMP-7, MMP-8), all of which promote fibroblast activation, myofibroblast differentiation, and extracellular-matrix remodeling (Chen et al., 2023; Kamiya et al., 2024). Distinct macrophage subsets enriched in IPF such as CD71-negative alveolar macrophages, which correlate with mortality highlight the pathological relevance of macrophage heterogeneity (Allden et al., 2019; Puxeddu et al., 2019). Damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) released from injured AT2 cells further amplify innate immune activation via Toll-like receptors (TLRs). Genetic variants in Toll-interacting protein (TOLLIP), a negative regulator of Toll-like receptor signaling, increase susceptibility to dysregulated innate responses and worsen clinical outcomes (Shenderov et al., 2021; Zaman and Lee, 2018). Adaptive immunity also contributes to the fibrotic niche. Th2 (IL-4, IL-13) and Th17 (IL-17) cells promote fibroblast proliferation, fibrocyte differentiation, and ECM expansion (Huang et al., 2020; Lu et al., 2025) whereas protective Th1 and tissue-resident memory (TRM) CD4⁺ T-cell responses appear diminished in IPF. CD8⁺ T-cell subsets, particularly CD28-negative or PD-1 positive cells, display profibrotic transcriptional signatures and secrete IL-13 and TGF-β, reinforcing fibroblast activation (Shenderov et al., 2021). Importantly, the failure of broad immunosuppression (e.g., corticosteroids) in clinical trials highlights that IPF is driven not by classical inflammation but by dysregulated repair programs and maladaptive immune epithelial crosstalk. Collectively, abnormalities in innate and adaptive immunity interact with epithelial vulnerability, mitochondrial dysfunction, and oxidative stress to sustain chronic inflammation, aberrant wound repair, and the persistent activation of fibroblasts that underlies progressive IPF (Malaviya et al., 2020; Ren et al., 2025). These immune-epithelial interactions further amplify the feed-forward fibrotic loop highlighted in Fig. 1.

Collectively, epithelial injury, genetic susceptibility, oxidative and ER stress, and immune dysregulation converge to establish a self-perpetuating epithelial-fibroblast crosstalk that sustains progressive fibrotic remodeling in IPF, as summarized across structural and cellular levels in Fig. 1 and mechanistically expanded in Fig. 2.

CURRENT THERAPEUTIC LANDSCAPE FOR IPF

The therapeutic management of IPF has advanced substantially over the past decade, yet the disease remains incurable and progressive. Lung transplantation is available only to a small subset of eligible patients, leaving most individuals dependent on antifibrotic agents and supportive or palliative interventions. Despite clinical progress, current treatments primarily slow disease progression rather than reverse fibrosis and the median survival remains approximately two to three years if left untreated (Nakamura and Suda, 2015). An overview of approved and emerging pharmacologic therapies is summarized in Table 1, while the chemical structures and primary molecular targets of small molecule agents are shown in Fig. 3.

Approved antifibrotic therapies

Pirfenidone (Esbriet®; Pirfenex®; Pirespa®): Pirfenidone is a well-established antifibrotic drug that not only slows IPF progression but also reduces acute exacerbations, hospitalization rates, and mortality (Aimo et al., 2022; Shah et al., 2021). Its mechanism involves the inhibition of TGF-β driven fibroblast activation and collagen synthesis, alongside anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects. Beyond pulmonary fibrosis, pirfenidone has demonstrated potential in other fibrosing conditions, such as chronic hypersensitivity pneumonitis and systemic sclerosis-associated interstitial lung disease (ILD). Preclinical data also suggest beneficial effects in non-pulmonary disorders, including non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), cardiac fibrosis, and ocular or peritoneal injuries, highlighting its broad antifibrotic capacity and repurposing potential.

Nintedanib (Ofev®): Nintedanib is a multi-tyrosine kinase inhibitor targeting vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGFR1-3), fibroblast growth factor receptor (FGFR1-3), and platelet-derived growth factor receptor (PDGFRα/β) (Hajari Case and Johnson, 2017). It exerts potent antifibrotic effects by inhibiting fibroblast proliferation, migration, and differentiation into myofibroblasts. Clinical trials have demonstrated that nintedanib significantly reduces the rate of FVC decline across a broad IPF population, including patients with preserved lung function (FVC >90%). Its benefits are consistent regardless of age, baseline function, or comorbidities such as emphysema. The drug is generally well tolerated; mild gastrointestinal disturbances, particularly diarrhea, are the most frequent side effects and can typically be managed without discontinuation.

Nerandomilast (BI-1015550; Jascyd®): Nerandomilast is a recently approved selective phosphodiesterase 4B (PDE4B) inhibitor that represents a new class of antifibrotic therapy in IPF. By inhibiting PDE4B, nerandomilast increases intracellular cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) levels, leading to suppression of pro-inflammatory and profibrotic signaling pathways, including inhibition of PI3K/AKT, NF-κB, and STAT3 signaling, alongside activation of CREB-dependent pathways. These effects collectively reduce immune cell activation, cytokine production, and fibroblast-mediated fibrotic remodeling (Liu et al., 2026). Clinical studies have demonstrated that Nerandomilast significantly reduces the rate of lung function decline, as measured by forced vital capacity (FVC), with efficacy observed both as monotherapy and in combination with existing antifibrotic agents (Richeldi et al., 2022). Importantly, its mechanism is distinct from that of pirfenidone and nintedanib, offering a complementary therapeutic approach. In terms of safety, Nerandomilast has shown an improved tolerability profile compared to earlier non-selective PDE4 inhibitors, with gastrointestinal adverse events (e.g., diarrhea, nausea) being the most commonly reported but generally manageable (Richeldi et al., 2022; Wuyts et al., 2025). Given its novel mechanism and favorable clinical profile, Nerandomilast expands the current therapeutic landscape of IPF and may offer potential advantages in combination strategies targeting both fibrotic signaling and inflammatory components of disease progression.

Emerging and investigational therapies

Dasatinib (Sprycel®)+Quercetin (D+Q): Dasatinib is a multi-target tyrosine kinase inhibitor for chronic myeloid leukemia. It inhibits BCR-ABL, Src family kinases,c-Kit, and PDGFRβ, thereby disrupting pro-survival signaling and sensitizing senescent cells to apoptosis when used in combination with quercetin. By occupying the ATP-binding pocket of this enzyme, dasatinib prevents phosphorylation of downstream signaling proteins. Quercetin is a naturally occurring flavonoid that exerts senolytic activity through modulation of PI3K/AKT and B- cell lymphoma 2 (BCL-2) family signaling, promoting selective apoptosis of senescent cells while also reducing oxidative stress and SASP production. The senolytic combination of dasatinib and quercetin represents a pioneering therapeutic approach targeting cellular senescence in IPF (Justice et al., 2019). In the first-in-human, open-label pilot study involving 14 stable IPF patients, intermittent oral D+Q over three weeks was well tolerated, achieving full study retention and manageable adverse effects. Although pulmonary function remained stable, participants exhibited improved six-minute walk distance and gait speed. Correlative analyses between performance gains and SASP-associated proteins suggested biological activity, underscoring the potential of senolytic therapy in senescence-driven diseases and the need for larger controlled trials.

Admilparant (BMS-986278; LPA1 inhibitor): Admilparant is an oral antagonist of lysophosphatidic acid receptor 1 (LPA₁), a critical mediator of fibroblast activation and epithelial injury (Corte et al., 2025). In phase 2 clinical trials, admilparant significantly slowed FVC decline compared to placebo in patients with IPF and progressive pulmonary fibrosis, with favorable safety and tolerability. The drug remained effective even in combination with existing antifibrotics, supporting its compatibility with standard regimens. By inhibiting LPA₁ signaling, admilparant mitigates epithelial–mesenchymal injury and fibrotic remodeling, making it a promising candidate now under phase 3 evaluation.

Bexotegrast (integrin inhibitor): Bexotegrast is a dual αvβ₆/αvβ₁ integrin inhibitor that prevents TGF-β activation, a central event in fibrotic signaling (Lancaster et al., 2024). These integrins, expressed on epithelial cells and fibroblasts, are upregulated in IPF and promote collagen production and tissue stiffening. By blocking integrin-mediated TGF-β activation, bexotegrast represents a targeted antifibrotic strategy with potentially reduced systemic toxicity compared to conventional TGF-β inhibitors. Early clinical studies demonstrate favorable tolerability and reductions in circulating fibrosis-associated biomarkers; however, its development for IPF was subsequently discontinued.

Pamrevlumab (anti-CTGF): Pamrevlumab is a fully human monoclonal antibody that targets CTGF, a key downstream mediator of TGF-β signaling involved in fibroblast proliferation, ECM deposition, and tissue remodeling. CTGF expression is markedly upregulated in IPF lungs and correlates with disease severity. By inhibiting CTGF, pamrevlumab disrupts profibrotic signaling cascades and attenuates myofibroblast differentiation and collagen accumulation (Richeldi et al., 2020). Early-phase clinical studies suggested that pamrevlumab may slow disease progression in patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), as reflected by reduced decline in forced vital capacity (FVC) and preservation of lung structure (Richeldi et al., 2020). However, more recent Phase III trials have not consistently demonstrated significant clinical benefit compared to placebo, highlighting the complexity of targeting CTGF as a standalone therapeutic strategy (Raghu et al., 2024). Pamrevlumab has generally demonstrated a favorable safety profile, with no major increase in serious adverse events reported in clinical studies. While its targeted mechanism remains biologically compelling, current evidence suggests that CTGF inhibition alone may be insufficient, and its future role may depend on combination strategies or patient stratification.

Therapeutic overview

Despite the progress achieved with pirfenidone and nintedanib, both drugs primarily decelerate functional decline without reversing established fibrosis. Novel agents such as admilparant and bexotegrast, along with senescence-targeting combinations like dasatinib plus quercetin, represent the next frontier in IPF therapy. These approaches focus on upstream cellular mechanisms including fibroblast activation, senescence clearance, and TGF-β signaling blockade laying the foundation for integrative, multimodal treatment strategies. Next-generation biologics such as pamrevlumab primarily mitigate fibrogenic activity rather than reverse established fibrosis. Their limited capacity to restore normal alveolar architecture underscores the need for therapies that target upstream, causative mechanisms of disease persistence. With the recent approval of Nerandomilast, parts of this therapeutic class have begun transitioning from investigational to clinically validated strategies. Collectively, these agents expand the current therapeutic landscape beyond traditional antifibrotics, as summarized in Table 1, and provide the rationale for mechanism-guided combination strategies discussed in later sections.

Currently, cellular senescence has emerged as a central pathogenic axis linking aging, mitochondrial dysfunction, and aberrant wound repair. Understanding how senescent cells sustain chronic inflammation and fibrosis provides the conceptual foundation for next-generation combination strategies, integrating senolytics, senomorphics, and antifibrotic agents to achieve durable remission and tissue rejuvenation in IPF.

SENESCENCE: EMERGING THERAPEUTIC TARGET FOR IPF

Cellular senescence: New pathogenic axis for IPF

Cellular senescence is a stable, irreversible growth arrest program triggered by diverse cellular stresses including telomere erosion, DNA damage, oxidative and ER stress. Senescent cells remain metabolically active but undergo chromatin remodeling, mitochondrial dysfunction, and activation of the p53-p21 and cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor 2A (p16^INK4a; CDKN2A) pathways. While transient senescence promotes wound healing and limits proliferation of damaged cells, persistent senescent cells secrete SASP rich in IL-6, IL-8, TGF-β, chemokines, and proteases that disrupt tissue homeostasis. In IPF, this chronic SASP environment becomes a central driver of aberrant repair and progressive fibrosis. Senescence in IPF emerges from the convergence of multiple pathways that activate the p53-p21 and p16^INK4a-retinoblastoma (RB) axes. Genomic instability, telomere attrition, mitochondrial dysfunction, impaired proteostasis/ER stress, and dysregulated intercellular signaling all activate the canonical p53-p21 and p16^INK4a-retinoblastoma (RB) senescence pathways. Although several lung cell types can undergo senescence, AT2 epithelial cells are particularly vulnerable to these stresses. Because AT2 cells serve as the primary progenitors responsible for alveolar regeneration, their transition into irreversible senescence diminishes regenerative capacity and shifts them toward secretion of a SASP rich in IL-6, IL-8, TGF-β, and chemokines. This SASP milieu potently activates fibroblasts and establishes a chronic profibrotic environment that drives progressive tissue remodeling in IPF. These converging pathways are schematically organized in Fig. 4, highlighting the integration of stress signaling, senescence establishment, and downstream fibrotic remodeling. Building on the epithelial-injury cascade described in Section 1, accumulating evidence indicates that cellular senescence is not merely a downstream consequence of tissue damage but a core upstream program that sustains fibrogenesis in IPF. Although transient senescence can serve protective roles, persistent senescent cells exert long-lasting effects through SASP, establishing a self-perpetuating profibrotic niche (Han et al., 2023).

Aging, the dominant clinical risk factor for IPF, substantially increases senescent-cell burden across both epithelial and mesenchymal compartments (Fig. 4). In human IPF lungs, AT2 cells and fibroblastic foci show elevated p16^INK4a, cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor 1A (p21^Cip1/Waf1; CDKN1A), senescence-associated β-galactosidase (SA-β-Gal) activity, and phosphorylated histone H2AX (γ-H2AX), consistent with stable senescence programs that impair alveolar regeneration (Han et al., 2023). Multiple converging stressors reinforce this phenotype, including telomere shortening, ER-stress primed injury responses, and mitochondrial dysfunction with impaired mitophagy, each feeding into the p53-p21 and p16^INK4a pathways (Han et al., 2023; Zia et al., 2022). Mitochondrial dysfunction generates mtROS, which primarily reinforce p53-p21 signaling through oxidative DNA damage, while indirectly contributing to p16^INK4a activation under persistent stress conditions (Fig. 4).

Single-cell and transcriptomic datasets strengthen this framework. Weng et al. identified broad enrichment of senescence-related gene programs in IPF epithelium and validated dysregulation of CDKN2A, SRY-box transcription factor 2 (SOX2), forkhead box O3 (FOXO3), and vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGFA) (Weng et al., 2025). He and Li (2022) defined aging-associated signatures linked to senescence and mitochondrial apoptosis, nominating insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF1), RET proto-oncogene (RET), and insulin-like growth factor binding protein 2 (IGFBP2) as potential biomarkers of senescence-driven disease activity (He and Li, 2022). Collectively, these studies position senescence as a unifying biological axis that connects aging to defective epithelial repair and persistent fibroblast activation in IPF.

Experimental evidence supporting senescence-driven fibrosis

Cellular senescence promotes pulmonary fibrosis through coupled molecular and biomechanical feedback loops that lock the lung into maladaptive repair. Mitochondrial dysfunction and excessive ROS generate oxidative DNA damage in AT2 and endothelial cells, activating p53-p21 and p16^INK4a growth-arrest pathways and stabilizing senescent phenotypes (Lei et al., 2025). Senescent AT2 cells release SASP mediators, which directly stimulate fibroblast proliferation, myofibroblast differentiation, and ECM synthesis. Senescent fibroblasts further perpetuate fibrosis by resisting apoptosis and shifting matrix turnover toward accumulation reflected by increased tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases (TIMPs) and reduced matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs). The resulting matrix stiffening reinforces mechanotransductive RhoA-ROCK and YAP/TAZ signaling, which sustains both epithelial and mesenchymal senescence. Defective autophagy amplifies this process. Reduced clearance of damaged mitochondria and misfolded proteins stabilizes ROS production and SASP output, and human IPF lungs show reduced microtubule-associated protein 1 light chain 3 beta (LC3B) and Beclin-1 expression, consistent with impaired proteostasis and persistent senescence. Mechanistic studies provide depth: extracellular heat shock protein 90-alpha (HSP90α) drives fibroblast senescence and fibrosis via mitochondrial stress and p53-p21 activation, reversible by HSP90α blockade (Zhong et al., 2022); phosphatase and tensin homolog (PTEN) loss and NF-κB activation promote AT2 senescence and SASP release (Tian et al., 2019); YAP1 silencing alleviates epithelial senescence and fibrotic remodeling (Xu et al., 2021); Wnt/β-catenin-Nanog signaling links epithelial senescence to fibroblast activation (Chen et al., 2019); and fucosyltransferase 8 (FUT8)-mediated core fucosylation enhances IGF-1/ phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)-AKT-driven epithelial senescence (Sun et al., 2021b). Together, these findings show that senescence is a self-reinforcing driver not an epiphenomenon of progressive fibrosis. Proof-of-concept genetic and pharmacologic studies demonstrate that senescence actively drives fibrogenesis rather than simply marking injury. Key models show that senescent AT2 cells and fibroblasts promote SASP dependent fibroblast activation and ECM accumulation, and that blocking senescence programs or clearing senescent cells suppresses fibrosis. For example, extracellular HSP90α promotes fibroblast senescence and profibrotic signaling, reversible by antibody blockade (Zhong et al., 2022); PTEN/NF-κB dependent AT2 senescence accelerates fibrosis and is mitigated by NF-κB inhibition (Tian et al., 2019); suppression of Hippo-Yes-associated protein 1 (YAP1) signaling reduces epithelial senescence and collagen deposition (Xu et al., 2021) and Wnt/β-catenin-Nanog or IGF1/PI3K/AKT core fucosylation pathways mechanistically couple senescence to fibroblast activation (Chen et al., 2019; Sun et al., 2021b) Collectively, these studies establish senescence as a causal, druggable axis in IPF.

Senescence and Alveolar Regeneration

A major conceptual advance in IPF pathogenesis is the identification of KRT8⁺ transitional epithelial cells, a stress-induced, senescence-associated transitional state within the AT2→AT1 lineage arising downstream of mitochondrial dysfunction, DNA damage, and canonical senescence pathway activation (Fig. 4) (Jiang et al., 2020; Strunz et al., 2020). These cells are characterized by cell-cycle arrest, DNA damage signatures, mitochondrial stress, SASP expression, and impaired regenerative capacity, features aligning them with a “senescent-like” state (Bocchi et al., 2024; Jiang et al., 2020; Strunz et al., 2020). This phenotype is driven by activation of the p53-p21 and p16^INK4a-RB pathways, establishing the senescent state (Fig. 4). Under normal conditions, these transitional cells are transient and resolved as epithelial repair completes (Bocchi et al., 2024; Chan and Liu, 2022). However, in IPF they accumulate abnormally, forming a regenerative bottleneck (Kobayashi et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2023).

KRT8⁺ transitional cells exhibit a phenotype of SASP, releasing key fibrotic mediators including TGF-β, IL-6, and matrix-remodeling enzymes which directly activate fibroblast and ECM deposition (Kobayashi et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2023). Recent studies reveal that KRT8 itself regulates chemokine expression: KRT8 deficiency reduces macrophage recruitment, myofibroblast accumulation, and transitional cell persistence, demonstrating a direct mechanistic link between epithelial senescence and fibrotic progression (Bi et al., 2025; Strunz et al., 2020). These transitional cells also recruit profibrotic macrophages, forming a positive feedback loop that sustains epithelial dysfunction and accelerates fibrosis (Strunz et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2023). In parallel, progressive ECM accumulation increases tissue stiffness, activating mechanotransductive signaling pathways such as YAP/TAZ and Rho/ROCK, which further reinforce epithelial senescence and stabilize the KRT8⁺ transitional state, establishing a self-perpetuating fibrotic feedback loop. Importantly, mice and human lungs diverge in this process. In murine bleomycin models, KRT8⁺ transitional cells gradually resolve, allowing re-epithelialization and partial architectural recovery (Shen et al., 2022; Shin et al., 2025; Strunz et al., 2020). In human IPF, however, transitional cells evolve into persistent basaloid epithelial cells a pathologically senescent, dysfunctional population incapable of completing AT1 differentiation (Parimon et al., 2023). Their persistence represents a maladaptive epithelial state that drives chronic fibrosis and explains why human IPF is progressive whereas mouse fibrosis is reversible (Auyeung and Sheppard, 2021; Wang et al., 2023).

Collectively, this transitional senescence paradigm integrates upstream stress signaling, canonical senescence pathway activation, SASP mediated fibroblast activation, and mechanotransductive feedback into a unified mechanism of IPF progression (Liu et al., 2025; Merkt et al., 2020).

SENESCENCE-TARGETED THERAPEUTICS

Given the central role of senescence in AT2 dysfunction, fibroblast activation, and ECM remodeling, therapies targeting epithelial and mesenchymal senescence have strong mechanistic rationale. Senolytic agents such as dasatinib plus quercetin selectively eliminate senescent cells and have shown efficacy in reducing SASP factors, decreasing myofibroblast accumulation, and improving lung compliance in preclinical models. Early human data from an open-label phase 1 pilot trial demonstrated feasibility, improved physical performance metrics, and modulation of circulating SASP proteins (Justice et al., 2019). Other senolytic strategies under investigation include navitoclax (BCL-2/BCL-xL inhibitor), the FOXO4 D-retro-inverso senolytic peptide (FOXO4-DRI), and naturally derived compounds such as fisetin, although most remain in preclinical or early translational stages (Baar et al., 2017; Zhu et al., 2016). In addition, senolytic candidates from the Unity Biotechnology UBX-series (e.g., UBX0101, UBX1325) have been explored; however, clinical outcomes to date have been mixed, and translation into effective therapies remains ongoing (Hickson et al., 2019). Complementary “senomorphic” approaches aim to suppress the deleterious effects of the SASP without inducing cell death. These include metabolic and signaling modulators such as metformin, rapamycin, Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors, bromodomain and extraterminal (BET) inhibitors (Raffaele and Vinciguerra, 2022). Mitochondrial-targeted strategies, including mitoquinone mesylate (MitoQ), elamipretide (SS-31 peptides), and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD⁺)-boosting approaches, as well as regulators of mitochondrial homeostasis such as sirtuin-3 (SIRT3) activation, have also been investigated to mitigate oxidative stress and restore cellular function (Fang et al., 2017; Verdin, 2015). Senescence-targeted therapy can synergize with existing antifibrotics (e.g., nintedanib, pirfenidone) by addressing upstream epithelial dysfunction rather than downstream ECM deposition. Combination therapy integrating senolytics, SASP inhibitors, and mitochondrial redox modulators is a particularly promising approach, supported by strong mechanistic evidence linking SIRT3, mitophagy dysfunction, mtROS, and KRT8⁺ transitional cell accumulation (Antoniou et al., 2013; Hong et al., 2022; Spina et al., 2024; Wan et al., 2023). Together, these therapeutic strategies highlight the potential of senescence-modulating approaches to transform IPF treatment toward true disease modification, targeting the upstream epithelial drivers rather than solely inhibiting fibroblast activity.

Senescence-targeted combination therapies for IPF with advance drug delivery systems

Approved antifibrotic agents such as nintedanib and pirfenidone effectively slow fibroblast proliferation and fibrogenic signaling but do not address the persistent burden of senescent cells and the SASP that perpetuates inflammation and tissue remodeling. To overcome these limitations, current research emphasizes combining conventional antifibrotics with senescence-modulating therapeutics, including senolytics (dasatinib-quercetin, navitoclax, fisetin) and senomorphics (metformin, rapamycin, heat-shock protein 90 (HSP90) inhibitors). These adjunct agents target upstream pathogenic mechanisms, thereby disrupting the epithelial mesenchymal crosstalk that sustains fibrosis (Kirkland and Tchkonia, 2020; Raffaele and Vinciguerra, 2022).

Integrating senescence modulation into standard antifibrotic therapy is expected to yield multiple synergistic therapeutic benefits. Dual targeting may reduce SASP-derived cytokines such as IL-6, IL-8, and TGF-β, thereby attenuating paracrine fibroblast activation; restore extracellular-matrix homeostasis through balanced regulation of MMPs and TIMPs; and improve epithelial regeneration by alleviating mitochondrial dysfunction and promoting autophagy (Alqahtani et al., 2025; Goodwin and Jenkins, 2009; Wan et al., 2023). Collectively, these effects have the potential to shift IPF management from disease attenuation toward a remission-oriented paradigm.

Preclinical studies have already demonstrated the feasibility of such combination approaches. For instance, nintedanib combined with senolytics such as dasatinib-quercetin or navitoclax enables periodic clearance of senescent fibroblasts, while pirfenidone paired with senomorphics such as metformin or rapamycin suppresses SASP expression and supports mitochondrial quality control. Natural antioxidant senolytic compounds, including fisetin and quercetin, further modulate oxidative and inflammatory signaling, while emerging agents like the FOXO4-DRI peptide or HSP90 inhibitors may eliminate resistant senescent subpopulations (Huang et al., 2021).

Recent advances in nanomedicine have translated these dual-targeting concepts into practical co-delivery platforms capable of simultaneously modulating senescence, oxidative stress, and fibrogenic signaling in IPF (Fig. 5). These systems integrate small molecule antifibrotics with genetic, redox modulating, or senolytic cargos to enhance efficacy while minimizing systemic toxicity. Representative examples of these combination nanomedicine platforms are summarized in Table 2.

Inhalable co-delivery of approved antifibrotics: Lee et al. developed an inhalable microgel formulation co-delivering nintedanib and pirfenidone, in which nintedanib encapsulated in poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) nanoparticles and pirfenidone in liposomes embedded within an aerosolizable microgel scaffold (Lee et al., 2024). The particles exhibited an optimal aerodynamic diameter (~12 μm) for distal lung deposition, evaded macrophage clearance, and achieved prolonged pulmonary retention. In bleomycin-induced fibrosis model, this platform reduced TGF-β and alpha smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) expression and restored lung function more effectively than free-drug combinations, demonstrating the translational feasibility of microgel-based inhalation therapy.

Gene/small-interfering RNAs (siRNA)-antifibrotic synergy: Yu et al. designed an ionizable liposomal system co-loaded with pirfenidone and siRNA targeting TGF-β1, enabling coordinated suppression of TGF-β signaling and oxidative injury (Yu et al., 2023). This dual modality approach protected epithelial cells, attenuated ECM accumulation, and preserved alveolar architecture in vivo. Similarly, Chang et al. engineered lipid nanoparticles that co-encapsulate pirfenidone with a plasmid encoding the antioxidant transcription factor NRF2; Poly(ethylene glycol) (PEG)-optimized lipid nanoparticles enhanced intracellular delivery, reduced oxidative stress, and inhibited myofibroblast activation (Chang et al., 2023). A more targeted approach was demonstrated by Hou et al., who developed polymeric micelles conjugated to anti-platelet-derived growth factor receptor-α (PDGFRα) fragments for selective myofibroblast engagement (Hou et al., 2021). Co-delivery of siRNA targeting NADPH oxidase-4 (siNOX4; reducing fibroblast activation) and protein tyrosine phosphatase non-receptor type 13 (siPTPN13; enhancing Fas (CD95)-mediated apoptosis) disrupted fibroblastic foci and promoted selective clearance of pathogenic fibroblasts with minimal systemic toxicity.

Senolytic–antifibrotic platforms: To address the physical barriers characteristic of IPF lungs, Yang et al. created dual-barrier-penetrating inhalable liposomes co-delivering nintedanib and the senolytic agent navitoclax (Yang et al., 2024). Surface modification with tris(2-carboxyethyl)phosphine and L-arginine enabled efficient penetration through mucus and stiff fibrotic ECM, allowing uniform distribution across lung lobes. Navitoclax selectively eliminated senescent epithelial cells, thereby enhancing the antifibrotic effect of nintedanib and establishing a potent senolytic-antifibrotic combination strategy.

Targeted delivery+antifibrotic modulation: Sun et al. developed an exosome-liposome hybrid carrier combining clodronate-loaded liposomes with fibroblast-derived exosomes to achieve precise fibroblast targeting (Sun et al., 2021a). This hybrid accumulated specifically within fibrotic regions while reducing hepatic exposure and inflammation. When loaded with nintedanib, the carrier increased local drug retention and amplified antifibrotic efficacy. Complementing this, Garbuzenko et al. engineered an inhalable nanostructured lipid carrier delivering prostaglandin E₂ alongside a siRNA cocktail targeting MMP-3, C-C motif chemokine ligand 12 (CCL12), and hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α) (Garbuzenko et al., 2017). Local pulmonary administration significantly attenuated collagen deposition, improved lung tissue architecture, and enhanced survival in bleomycin-treated mice, demonstrating the therapeutic potential of multi-target, locally delivered gene-small-molecule combinations.

Collectively, these platforms exemplify a shift from monotherapy toward multi-target, mechanism- driven interventions capable of modulating both fibrogenic and senescence-driven pathways. Their success underscores the promise of nanocarrier-based strategies in achieving synergistic efficacy, reduced systemic toxicity, and precise pulmonary delivery-key advances for next generation combination therapies in IPF., Future approaches are expected to integrate nanocarrier co-delivery with gene-editing or RNA-interference technologies to directly modulate senescence pathways, including p16, p21, NF-κB, and PINK1/Parkin signaling. Parallel approaches leveraging redox autophagy co-therapy such as pairing mitophagy activators like urolithin A with antifibrotics may further disrupt the mitochondrial-ROS-senescence feedback loop that sustains epithelial dysfunction. Together, these advances point toward a new generation of multi-modal IPF therapy, uniting cellular rejuvenation with fibrosis inhibition to achieve durable lung restoration.

Translational challenges and future directions

Despite significant progress, the clinical translation of senescence-targeted and combination antifibrotic therapies remains constrained by several key barriers that require targeted solutions.

First, the lack of standardized and clinically validated biomarkers limits patient stratification and real-time monitoring of therapeutic response. Current candidates-including circulating SASP factors (e.g., IL-6, MCP-1), cell-free mitochondrial DNA, and epigenetic aging signatures—show promise but lack specificity and reproducibility across cohorts (Justice et al., 2019; Schafer et al., 2017). To address this, integrated multi-omics approaches combining transcriptomic, proteomic, and epigenetic profiling should be leveraged to define robust senescence signatures (Basisty et al., 2020; Kirkland and Tchkonia, 2020). Coupling these biomarkers with spatial and single-cell transcriptomics may further enable precise mapping of senescent cell burden within fibrotic niches (Adams et al., 2020; Habermann et al., 2020). Second, safety and target specificity of senolytic therapies remain major concerns, particularly in elderly IPF populations. Agents such as BCL-xL inhibitors can induce dose-limiting toxicities, including thrombocytopenia and off-target apoptosis in non-senescent cells (Justice et al., 2019; Zhu et al., 2016).

Potential solutions include the development of next generation senolytics with improved selectivity, intermittent dosing strategies, and targeted delivery systems to minimize systemic exposure (Kirkland and Tchkonia, 2020). Third, inefficient drug delivery to fibrotic lung tissue, characterized by dense extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition and altered tissue architecture, significantly reduces therapeutic efficacy (Nishioka et al., 2024; Wynn and Ramalingam, 2012).

To overcome this, inhalable nanocarrier systems and ligand-targeted nanoparticles should be optimized to enhance drug penetration and selective accumulation in senescent AT2 cells and activated myofibroblasts while minimizing off-target distribution (Diwan et al., 2024; Dong et al., 2026). Fourth, the heterogeneity of IPF pathogenesis complicates therapeutic response, as patients differ in senescence burden, mitochondrial dysfunction, and inflammatory signaling profiles (Wang et al., 2024; Zank et al., 2018). Addressing this requires biomarker-guided patient stratification and adaptive clinical trial designs to identify responder subpopulations and improve therapeutic precision.

Finally, the complexity of fibrotic signaling networks limits the effectiveness of single-agent therapies. Future strategies should focus on rational combination approaches integrating antifibrotics, senolytics, and senomorphics to simultaneously target fibrosis, inflammation, and cellular senescence, thereby maximizing therapeutic efficacy while minimizing toxicity.

Collectively, overcoming these barriers will require coordinated advances in biomarker development, precision drug delivery, and systems-level understanding of fibrotic disease. Such integration is expected to shift IPF management from disease attenuation toward mechanism-driven, personalized, and potentially regenerative therapeutic strategies.

CONCLUSION

Cellular senescence represents a pivotal biological bridge linking aging, oxidative stress, and aberrant wound-healing to the pathogenesis of IPF. Accumulation of senescent epithelial and mesenchymal cells drives a persistent SASP that sustains inflammation, fibroblast activation, and extracellular-matrix deposition. Therapeutically, targeting this axis through senolytics, senomorphics, or combination strategies with approved antifibrotics (nintedanib, pirfenidone) offers a promising avenue to halt or even reverse disease progression. Nanoparticle-based co-delivery systems further expand this potential by enabling precise, lung-targeted modulation of senescence and fibrosis simultaneously. Moving forward, integration of multi-omics profiling, spatial and single-cell transcriptomics, and senescence-specific biomarkers will be essential to stratify patients by senescence load and identify responders to emerging senescence-targeted interventions. These advances mark a transition toward precision and remission-oriented therapy in IPF.

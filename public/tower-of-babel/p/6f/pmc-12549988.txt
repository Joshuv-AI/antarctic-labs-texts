UAV-asisted IoT network framework with hybrid deep reinforcement and federated learning
Authors: Andreas Andreou, Constandinos X Mavromoustakis, Evangelos Markakis, Athina Bourdena, George Mastorakis
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper addresses a critical gap in Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV)-assisted Internet of Things (IoT) networks, where existing works inadequately integrate UAV deployment optimization with privacy-preserving Federated Learning (FL) and adaptive resource allocation under dynamic network conditions. The research explores the deployment of multi-UAV networks in IoT environments, emphasizing their dual roles in expanding cellular network coverage and facilitating efficient data collection. Unlike prior studies that treat UAV placement and FL-driven resource optimization separately, we present a unified hybrid framework leveraging Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) and FL. The proposed framework incorporates the Multi-UAV Network Formation (MUNF) algorithm, which employs Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) to improve the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) for effective data collection. Additionally, the Dynamic Adaptive Strategy (DAS) utilizes a Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) approach to optimize resource allocation, reduce latency, and enhance throughput. Extensive simulations demonstrate a 26% increase in data throughput, an 18% reduction in latency, and more stable SNR distribution compared to state-of-the-art baselines. These results indicate a consistent improvement in network efficiency and scalability, validating the proposed framework’s capability to address real-world UAV-assisted IoT challenges more effectively than prior work.Keywords: UAV, Federated larning, PSO, Energy efficiency, IoTSubject terms: Aerospace engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Swarms of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) are becoming important in enhancing Internet of Things (IoT) networks, particularly in next-generation network technologies. Also, they can play crucial roles in environmental monitoring and emergency communications, offering unique data collection capabilities from aerial perspectives. Their ability to establish Line-of-Sight (LoS) with IoT nodes significantly improves Quality of Service (QoS) and efficiency1, while the evolution of 6G technology extends UAV capabilities.

The research work2 has focused on integrating Federated Learning (FL) within UAV-assisted IoT networks to address critical data privacy and network performance challenges3. In these networks, UAVs collect data from IoT devices and actively participate in FL processes. The dual role enhances data collection methodologies by leveraging UAVs to establish robust communication links, essential for acquiring high-quality data from IoT nodes and training robust machine learning models.

FL is a solution that maintains data confidentiality by processing data locally at the network edge before any aggregation or central transfer occurs. This approach significantly reduces the need for large-scale data transmission, conserving bandwidth and minimizing latency4. Also, FL addresses privacy concerns by ensuring that sensitive data does not leave the user’s premises, which is particularly crucial in sectors such as healthcare. Moreover, by reducing dependency on centralized data centres, FL mitigates potential bottlenecks and vulnerabilities associated with centralized computing resources.

Based on the aforementioned, this study introduces a novel hybrid Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) and FL approach to further enhance the performance of UAV-assisted IoT networks. It also presents the Multi-UAV Network Formation (MUNF) algorithm, which utilizes Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) to optimize the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR)5,6, which improves the efficiency of UAV-assisted data collection. In addition, the Dynamic Adaptive Strategy (DAS), leveraging a Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) technique7, enhances the operational efficiency of FL by optimizing resource allocation, reducing latency, and increasing data throughput.

Motivation

The rapid proliferation of IoT devices and increasing demand for low-latency, privacy-preserving connectivity pose new challenges for next-generation networks. While UAVs have emerged as flexible aerial relays, existing methods often fail to jointly optimize UAV deployment, communication reliability, and FL-driven resource allocation under dynamic environments. Ensuring robust connectivity and efficient data collection in remote or disaster-stricken areas is challenging with traditional terrestrial communication infrastructures. UAVs offer a flexible solution by providing LoS communications, extending network coverage, and improving signal quality. However, despite their potential, a well-defined problem remains: how to simultaneously optimize UAV positioning and adaptive resource allocation while supporting privacy-preserving FL in highly dynamic, resource-constrained IoT environments. This study explicitly addresses this gap by proposing a problem formulation that integrates UAV trajectory planning, energy efficiency, and FL aggregation under realistic channel and mobility constraints. By analytically framing this challenge, our work shifts the focus from descriptive deployment benefits toward a quantifiable optimization problem grounded in UAV-assisted IoT network design.

Related works

The survey by8 explores the integration of blockchain-based FL in UAVs beyond 5G networks to address communication, privacy, and security challenges. It presents a reference architecture, potential benefits, open issues, and a logistics case study to guide future UAV solutions. The research conducted in9 developed advanced AI-embedded microcontrollers and a novel trajectory optimization algorithm for fully automated UAVs in smart cities10, achieving centimetre-level accuracy in flight paths, dynamic autonomy, and enhanced safety for urban integration.

The article in11 proposed a multi-agent reinforcement FL framework for resource allocation in a multi-UAV-enabled healthcare system to enhance Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) device connectivity, minimize latency and energy consumption, and ensure service quality, mainly when traditional 5G/Mobile Edge Computing (MEC) networks are unavailable or overloaded12. Meanwhile, the study by13 presented a generalized federated reinforcement learning approach using meta-learning for computation offloading and resource allocation in complex UAV-assisted MEC systems, achieving significantly improved and more reliable rewards by incorporating multiple smart devices into a unified model.

The research described in14 developed an optimized network coverage strategy for intelligent transportation systems using UAVs. It was achieved by deploying a novel algorithm that constructs Voronoi diagrams using circles and utilizes a PPP to determine optimal transceiver locations15, leading to full network coverage and demonstrating low time complexity. The work in16 proposed an optimized multi-UAV-assisted FL framework for IoT applications to minimize training latency and energy consumption, leveraging an online resource allocation algorithm, DRL, and a greedy scheme for optimal client selection and aggregation server placement.

The article from17 introduced a personalized federated DRL scheme for joint UAV deployment and resource allocation in 6G networks. This scheme enables UAVs to make real-time decisions to maximize long-term network throughput and adapt to dynamic conditions while preserving user privacy, demonstrating superior performance in network throughput and learning convergence. The paper18 proposed a secure and efficient FL framework for UAV-enabled networks, utilizing a secrecy-based user equipment selection scheme and an LSTM-based DDPG algorithm to optimize UAV placement, resource allocation, and training efficiency while enhancing privacy and security against eavesdroppers.

The tutorial by19 provides deep learning practitioners with an overview and a comprehensive toolkit for designing, implementing, training, and evaluating Bayesian neural networks, which use Bayesian statistics to quantify the uncertainty in deep neural network predictions. Additionally, the study20 presented a sustainable strategy for enhancing 6G+ network technologies in search and rescue operations by employing network slicing with a Multi-Agent DDPG (MADDPG) algorithm to manage workload fluctuations and prioritize signals, achieving adaptable and efficient communication in complex emergency response scenarios.

Comparison to prior work

This work builds upon our prior contributions in the areas of UAV trajectory optimization, UAV-assisted RSU deployment in V2X systems, and network slicing in 6G environments. However, it substantially extends beyond them in scope, integration, and technical novelty.

The paper9 study focused on enhancing trajectory accuracy using a modified A* algorithm combined with Haversine and Vincenty formulas to navigate complex urban topologies. While effective in optimizing flight paths, it did not address communication efficiency, FL, or dynamic resource allocation. While the current manuscript introduces a multi-objective optimization problem involving both trajectory planning and federated model aggregation under realistic network constraints. It incorporates FL and DRL for collaborative UAV-based data collection, thus significantly expanding the scope.

The research14 concentrated on coverage maximization using UAVs and Voronoi-based spatial partitioning for RSU placement. It emphasized static geometric optimization without addressing energy constraints, learning frameworks, or dynamic task adaptation. The proposed framework introduces dynamic and decentralized learning-based orchestration using DDPG, enabling UAVs to self-organize based on communication and energy feedback rather than static diagrams. Furthermore, it integrates FL to enhance privacy-aware data handling, which was not considered previously.

The study20 presented a MADDPG-based approach for network slicing in multi-agent system-of-systems (SoS) scenarios, focused on emergency communication and sustainability. Although it employed DRL for resource management, it did not involve UAV deployment, FL, or IoT-specific constraints. However, in the current research, the MADDPG logic is extended to a UAV-assisted IoT architecture where mobile agents not only allocate resources but also reposition themselves based on learned policies. Our system merges mobility control with FL-based model accuracy improvement and latency reduction—providing a unified solution that supports scalability, data privacy, and low-latency communications.

Therefore, while our previous works addressed isolated components (trajectory optimization, geometric deployment, or network slicing), this paper presents an integrated framework that jointly optimizes UAV placement, communication efficiency, and FL-based learning under dynamic and resource-constrained environments. This holistic perspective and architectural synergy mark the core novelty of our current contribution.

Dynamic resource allocation

Leveraging the MDP model and DDPG architecture, the Dynamic Resource Allocation (DRA) procedure adaptively sets CPU frequencies, bandwidth allocations, and other operational parameters.

Each UAV periodically reports its state (battery, CPU usage, local channel estimates) to a central server, which runs the DDPG update and sends the refined action (bandwidth, power, or CPU frequency scaling) back to the UAV. This centralized learning but distributed execution pattern lets the network optimize the system cost. Over multiple episodes, the algorithm converges to near-optimal settings that reduce latency, conserve energy, and maintain FL performance.

Figure 3 presents how coverage optimization and DRA work together. The process begins by specifying key UAV fleet parameters—like how many UAVs to deploy and the size of the coverage area—and setting operational constraints. With these in place, an optimization algorithm estimates the initial SNR based on UAV placements and refines their positions. Next, the Optimization Algorithm (OA) continuously adjusts resource allocations such as bandwidth and transmit power via a DDPG-based loop, responding in real-time to latency, throughput, and energy metrics. Finally, when coverage and resource allocation achieve stable performance, the OA hands off the optimized UAV configurations to a deployment coordinator, who supervises their physical placement and ongoing monitoring. The fleet adapts to maintain efficient coverage and connectivity if network requirements change.

Systematic workflow of DAS.

By integrating an MDP with DDPG, the DAS solution efficiently balances latency-sensitive data transmissions, FL performance requirements, and UAV energy budgets. In highly variable environments, this approach flexibly reallocates resources to maintain stable network performance, thereby enhancing the overall effectiveness of UAV-assisted IoT systems.

Algorithm 3 describes the combined operation of the proposed MUNF-DAS algorithm. Initially, the MUNF phase uses PSO to determine optimal UAV positions for maximizing the SNR across IoT devices. The process starts by deploying UAVs based on the PPP, followed by iterative adjustments of UAV coordinates guided by the PSO approach. Thus, it ensures robust communication links with IoT nodes, enhancing data collection efficiency and overall network coverage.

The DAS phase leverages DDPG, a model-free DRL technique, to manage UAV resource allocation dynamically in real time. It includes adaptively setting transmit power, CPU frequencies, and UAV trajectories. UAVs interact with the environment and learn optimal actions through iterative episodes, balancing trade-offs between latency, throughput, and energy usage. Finally, the FL round aggregates model updates from IoT devices into a global model, completing one iteration cycle.

Performance evaluation

The proposed MUNF-DAS frameworks’ performance was evaluated through UAV-assisted IoT network simulations. The simulation parameters were carefully selected to ensure the evaluation accurately reflects real-world conditions and challenges in UAV-assisted IoT networks. The chosen altitude range reflects the typical operational heights for UAVs, which are optimal for maintaining LoS communication with IoT devices while minimizing energy consumption and maximizing coverage area. This range allows for effective signal propagation and reliable data collection from dispersed IoT nodes.

Selecting 50 UAVs balances scalability and manageability, providing a robust test scenario for evaluating network performance. This number is sufficient to handle many IoT devices and ensures the results apply to various deployment scales in urban areas or more significant geographical regions. Deploying 500 IoT devices simulates a dense network environment, typical in urban settings or disaster response scenarios. This density tests the network’s ability to manage high data volumes, ensure reliable communication links, and efficiently collect data across a wide area.

The values range allows for a study of the trade-offs between latency and energy consumption. By varying , the simulations can evaluate how prioritizations affect network performance, providing insights into optimal settings for different operational contexts.

A duration of 1000 episodes ensures that the learning algorithms have sufficient iterations to converge and stabilize. This extended period allows for a thorough assessment of the network’s adaptive capabilities and the robustness of the proposed frameworks under varying conditions. In the context of the proposed framework, an episode refers to a single complete sequence of interactions between the UAVs and the IoT environment. It begins with an initial deployment state and continues until a predefined termination condition is met, such as achieving optimal network coverage, completing data collection tasks, or reaching the end of the allocated time steps. Each episode evaluates and enhances the UAVs’ performance through learning algorithms.

The values of initial FL rounds and maximum FL rounds reflect realistic participation levels of IoT devices. This parameter helps in evaluating the efficiency of the proposed resource management strategies.

A reward scale of 10 ensures that the rewards are appropriately scaled for practical training of the DDPG algorithm. This scaling helps balance the trade-offs between performance metrics, such as latency and throughput, ensuring that the learning process is neither too slow nor too volatile. Setting an initial latency of 50 ms provides a baseline for measuring improvements. This value reflects typical communication delays in IoT networks, allowing for assessing latency reduction techniques employed by the proposed frameworks. Limiting the number of iterations to 75 ensures the computational feasibility of the simulations while allowing enough time for the algorithms to optimize network parameters. This constraint balances the need for thorough optimization with practical computation limits.

Computational complexity and communication overhead

To assess the scalability of the proposed MUNF-DAS framework, additional simulations were conducted focusing on computational complexity and communication overhead, particularly when varying the number of UAVs and IoT devices. Computational complexity was analyzed in terms of execution time per episode and algorithm convergence speed, while communication overhead was measured based on the total data exchanged during FL training rounds and UAV control signaling.

The computational complexity of the MUNF algorithm primarily depends on the PSO procedure, characterized by complexity per iteration, where I is the number of iterations, P is the particle population size, and U is the UAV count. Similarly, the DAS-DDPG component adds complexity of per episode, with E denoting the number of training episodes, B the batch size, and H the neural network’s hidden layer sizes.

Simulation results indicate that as UAV count scales from 10 to 100, computational time increases nearly linearly, confirming practical scalability. However, scaling beyond 100 UAVs results in exponential complexity growth, highlighting potential limitations for very dense UAV deployments. Regarding IoT devices, increasing from 500 to 5000 nodes results in manageable linear growth of complexity, demonstrating strong scalability for device-intensive scenarios common in urban IoT deployments.

Communication overhead analysis reveals that overhead grows proportionally to both the UAV and IoT device count, primarily due to increased FL updates and UAV coordination signaling. In scenarios with up to 100 UAVs and 5000 IoT devices, the communication overhead remained within acceptable thresholds (under 20% bandwidth usage increase), ensuring network stability and efficiency. Beyond these thresholds, overhead may affect latency and throughput, requiring additional communication optimization strategies.

The observed scalability trends indicate that while the proposed MUNF-DAS framework effectively supports large-scale deployments, extremely dense UAV configurations could introduce computational and communicational constraints. Future research should explore distributed computation and hierarchical FL techniques to alleviate these limitations, ensuring sustained performance and scalability.

Although the DDPG-only baseline from24 offers slightly reduced computational load, it does so without achieving comparable levels of deployment optimization and dynamic adaptability provided by MUNF.

Therefore, while the MUNF-DAS framework incurs moderately higher computational demands, this cost is justified by its superior network performance, increased adaptability to IoT device mobility, and enhanced data collection efficiency. Conclusion and future work

This paper investigated advanced resource allocation strategies in UAV-assisted IoT networks, emphasizing the integration of FL and DRL. We comprehensively evaluated the proposed MUNF and DAS frameworks, leveraging the DDPG algorithm to dynamically optimize resource allocation and UAV positioning. Simulation results demonstrated that the frameworks significantly enhance network performance, achieving notable improvements in latency reduction, data throughput, and communication reliability. Specifically, increasing the resource allocation optimization intensity effectively extended UAV participation in FL tasks, thereby enhancing learning efficiency and network robustness. Furthermore, comparative analysis showed the DDPG-based DAS model markedly outperformed traditional DQN approaches, significantly reducing latency crucial for real-time IoT applications. Spatial analysis also confirmed that optimized UAV positioning strategies successfully mitigated path loss, improving overall communication quality.

Despite these advancements, several areas remain open for further exploration. Future research should focus on enhancing the scalability and applicability of the MUNF-DAS framework in more extensive and complex scenarios, including densely populated urban IoT environments and sparsely distributed rural areas. Integrating emerging 6G technologies, such as terahertz communications and intelligent reflecting surfaces, presents promising avenues for further performance gains. Additionally, energy efficiency optimization remains essential; thus, developing energy-aware algorithms, advanced battery management solutions, and renewable energy integration will be prioritized. Security and privacy enhancements, including robust encryption and secure FL protocols, also constitute critical future directions.

Real-world deployments and extensive field testing in diverse operational settings, such as smart cities and disaster recovery scenarios, will be essential to validate practical feasibility and address implementation challenges. Lastly, incorporating advanced artificial intelligence methodologies, including adaptive learning and meta-learning, could further improve network adaptability and resilience, ensuring continuous optimization in dynamic and unpredictable environments. Addressing these challenges will refine and expand the capabilities of UAV-assisted IoT networks, paving the way for their effective integration into next-generation wireless communication systems.

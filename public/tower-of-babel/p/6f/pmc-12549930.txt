Assessment of bis(monoacylglycerol)phosphate isomers by thin layer chromatography using a modified solvent system
Authors: Akira Abe, Vania Hinkovska-Galcheva, James A Shayman
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBis(monoacylglycerol)phosphates (BMPs) are biologically functional acidic lipids present in acidic cell compartments. BMPs are deacylated by LPLA2, with distinct substrate specificity for positional BMP isomers. The conversion of S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP to other BMP isomers may be crucial for LPLA2-mediated BMP degradation. Recently, we have shown that the isomerization of S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP to S,S-(2,3′-diacyl)- and S,S-(3,3′-diacyl)-BMPs is enhanced by various factors, including intracellular alkalinization, albumin, and specific metal ions. Here we present a convenient method to distinguish S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP from other BMP isomers using thin layer chromatography (TLC). The conventional acidic solvent system consisting of chloroform/methanol/acetone/acetic acid/water used to distinguish BMP isomers was replaced with chloroform/methanol/acetic acid/water (65:15:10:2, v/v), providing a better separation and shortening the development time for BMP isomers in one-dimensional TLC. Using this new TLC system, isomerization-promoting activity was found and characterized in the 150,000g supernatant of RAW 264.7 cell homogenate. Employing the new system, it was observed that the BMP band corresponding to S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP in the same cell homogenates almost completely disappeared after incubating the homogenates for 15 h at 37 °C under neutral conditions, but not at acidic pH. Two-dimensional TLC performed using the same new acidic solvent system in the first dimension revealed that the disappearance of the band observed above was mainly due to isomerization rather than decomposition of BMP. This suggests that the cells possess an endogenous promoter for the isomerization of S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMPs into other isomers. This TLC assay using the new acidic solvent system may be useful in assessing the isomerization of BMPs.Keywords: bis(monoacylglycerol)phosphate, Isomerization, Lysosomal phospholipase A2, Phospholipase A2 group 15, Thin layer chromatography

Introduction

In most mammalian cells, bis(monoacylglycerol)phosphates (BMPs) comprise a minor component of the lipidome, typically less than 1% of total cellular glycerophospholipids. Intracellular BMP is mainly localized in acidic vesicles such as late endosomes and lysosomes, accounting for 15% of total phospholipids in these compartments. S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP has a unique sn-1-glycerophospho-sn-1′-glycerol stereo-configuration in which two monoacylglycerols are linked via a phosphate group. This glycerophospholipid is a lipase- and phospholipase-resistant molecule and is the prevalent naturally occurring functional isomer [1]. BMPs display diverse biological functions, including regulation of internal vesicle formation in endosomes, cholesterol trafficking and efflux associated with NPC1 and NPC2 in lysosomes, activation of lysosomal hydrolases such as acid sphingomyelinase, LPLA2, and glucocerebrosidase, and as a stabilizing factor for endosomal/lysosomal membranes via binding to Hsp70 [[2], [3], [4], [5]]. In addition, BMPs are useful biomarkers of lysosomal dysfunction, such as CAD-induced phospholipidosis and Niemann-Pick type C mutations [6,7].

Unlike most cells, macrophages contain a large fraction of BMP, which accounts for more than 10% of total phospholipids [8,9]. Recently, we reported that the BMP isomers in the macrophage-like cell line RAW264.7 cells are predominantly S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMPs with a small amount of sn-1,3-specific lipase-sensitive BMP isomers [10]. The latter are S,S-(2,3′-diacyl)- or S,S-(3,3′-diacyl)-BMPs in which the acyl group is bound to the sn-3 or sn-3′ achiral carbon. We reported that S,S-(2,3′-diacyl)- or S,S-(3,3′-diacyl)-BMPs serve as better substrates for lysosomal phospholipase A2 (LPLA2, PLA2G15) than S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP [11], a finding which was later confirmed [12]. In addition, isomerization of S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP was promoted not only in the presence of albumin or metal cations under neutral conditions in vitro, but also by intracellular alkalization by treatment with chloroquine or ammonium chloride [10]. These results suggested that the biological functions of BMPs are regulated by the LPLA2-mediated BMP degradation pathway via S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP isomerization.

To better understand BMP isomerization and the biological functions of BMP isomers, it is important to establish methods to detect and distinguish individual BMP isomers. One method is liquid chromatography/mass spectrometry [13]. However, this is time-consuming, expensive, and is not readily available in most laboratories. An alternative method is thin-layer chromatography (TLC). In our previous study, we used one-dimensional TLC to establish that the isomerization of S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP to S,S-(3,3′-diacyl)-BMP occurs both in vitro and in cells [10]. In this study, we report an improved TLC method with better resolution of BMP isomers and reduced development time.

Materials and Methods

Reagents

1,2-Dioleoylphosphatidylcholine (DOPC), 1,2-dioleoylphosphatidylglycerol (DOPG), 1-palmitoyl-2-hydroxyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphatidyl-rac-glycerol (1-Pal-2-lysoPG), and sn-(3-oleoyl-2-hydroxy)-glycero-1-phospho-sn-1’-(3′-oleoyl-2′-hydroxy)-glycerol (S,S-(3,3′-diC18:1)-BMP) were obtained from Avanti Polar Lipids Corp. (Alabaster, AL). sn-(2-Oleoyl-3-hydroxy)-glycerol-1-phospho-sn-1'-(2′-oleoyl-3′-hydroxy)-glycerol (S,S-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP) was purchased from Echelon (Salt Lake City, UT); palmitic acid, oleic acid, and lipase from Rhizomucor miehe were from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO); recombinant human LPLA2 was from Proteos (Kalamazoo, MI). High-performance thin-layer chromatography (HPTLC) silica gel plates, 10 × 20 cm, were obtained from Merck (Darmstadt, Germany).

Preparation of S,S-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP liposomes

To detect the isomerization of S,S-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP with high sensitivity, S,S-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP liposomes were prepared [10]. S,S-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP dissolved in chloroform/methanol (C/M) (2:1, v/v) was dried with nitrogen gas (N2) in a glass tube and dispersed in 0.15 M sodium chloride solution using a probe sonicator in an ice-water bath for 8 min.

Preparation of the soluble fraction of RAW264.7 cells

RAW-264.7 macrophage-like cells were cultured in 15 cm diameter dishes containing DMEM supplemented with 10 % FCS, 2 mM l-glutamine, 100 U/ml penicillin, and 100 mg/ml streptomycin at 37 °C in a 5 % CO2 incubator. At the point of 80 % cell confluency, the adherent cells were washed three times with 10 ml of cold PBS, scraped with 4 ml of 0.25 M sucrose, 10 mM Hepes (pH 7.4), 1 mM EDTA (sucrose buffer), and collected in a single plastic conical tube. An additional 2 ml of sucrose buffer was added to the harvested dish, and the remaining cells were combined with the initially harvested cells. The cells sedimented at 350g were suspended in sucrose buffer and disrupted using a probe-type ultrasonicator (five times for 10 s) in an ice-water bath. The resultant cell homogenate was centrifuged at 150,000g for 1 h at 4 °C to obtain the soluble fraction.

Isomerization of S,S-(2,2′-dioleoyl)-BMP to BMP isomers by the soluble fraction

The conversion of S,S-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP to other BMP isomers was detected using TLC. The reaction mixture consisted of 5.2 μM S,S-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP, 135 mM sodium chloride, 20 mM sodium acetate (pH 4.5) or 20 mM Hepes (pH 7.4) in the presence or absence of the soluble fraction. In some cases, 1 mg/ml BSA or 100 μM FeCl3 was used under neutral conditions as a positive control of the isomerization reaction. The reaction was initiated by the addition of the soluble fraction, BSA, or FeCl3, and maintained at 37 °C for a certain time, and was stopped by adding chloroform (C) and methanol (M). The ratio of C/M/aqueous solution was adjusted to 2:1:0.8, v/v, and vortexed for 20 s. Lipids obtained from the resultant organic phase were developed on an HPTLC plate.

In one-dimensional TLC, the plate was developed in a new acidic solvent system consisting of C/M/acetic acid/water (65:15:10:2, v/v) for 20 min. BMPs produced by the reaction were identified using S,S-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP and S,S-(3,3′-diC18:1)-BMP prepared from each purchased BMP reagent as standards. The plate was dried in the hood without heat for 10 min and soaked in 8 % (w/v) CuSO4•5H2O, 6.8 % (v/v) H3PO4, 32 % (v/v) methanol. The completely wet plate was dried with cold air from a hair dryer and baked in an oven at 150 °C for 15 min. The charred products were scanned with a CanoScan 9000F Mark II and analyzed using ImageJ.

Whole cellular lipid extracts were analyzed using two-dimensional TLC. An individual sample dissolved in C/M (2:1, v/v) was spotted onto an HPTLC plate (10 × 10 cm) and developed for 20 min in a first-dimensional solvent system consisting of C/M/acetic acid/water (65:15:10:2, v/v). The plate was dried in a hood for 10 min, immersed in acetonitrile for 1 min, and then dried in the hood for 10 min. All drying steps were carried out without heat. The dried plate after acetonitrile treatment was developed in a second-dimensional solvent system consisting of C/M/28 % ammonium hydroxide (65:35:5, v/v) for 11 min and dried in a hood for 10 min without heating. Lipids isolated on the plate were stained and analyzed as described above.

BMPs spotted on a TLC plate tend to be lost from the plate when exposed to air. To prevent this loss, special care was taken to immediately cover the dried area with a clean glass plate after applying the sample.

Results and discussion

Improvement of separation and development time

When detecting BMPs by TLC, a basic solvent consisting of chloroform/methanol/ammonium hydroxide is typically used [9,14]. However, the use of such a solvent makes it difficult to distinguish S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP from other BMP isomers by one-dimensional TLC. In the previous study, we used one-dimensional TLC using C/M/acetone/acetic acid/water (45:15:10:10:2, v/v) to distinguish S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP from S,S-(2,3′- and 3,3′-diacyl)-BMPs although the separation of S,S-(2,3′-diacyl)-BMP, which appears as an indistinct band between S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP and S,S-(3,3′-diacyl)-BMP, was not always sufficiently resolved [10]. In this study, we attempted to improve the separation of BMP isomers by increasing the polarity of the solvent.

It typically takes 40 min to clearly separate S,S'-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP and S,S-(3,3′-diC18:1)-BMP using the previous solvent system C/M/acetone/acetic acid/water (45:15:10:10:2, v/v) (Fig. 1A). The Rf values of S,S'-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP and S,S-(3,3′- diC18:1)-BMP are 0.764 and 0.792, respectively. On the other hand, when the plate is developed to the top of the plate in the new solvent system of C/M/acetic acid/water (65:15:10:2, v/v), the Rf values of S,S'-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP and S,S-(3,3′-diC18:1)-BMP were 0.534 and 0.604, respectively (Fig. 1B, left panel). In this case, the plate can be developed for 34 min (Fig. 1B, left panel), consistent with improved mobility and separation of S,S'-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP and S,S-(3,3′-diC18:1)-BMP, and reduced the development time. Furthermore, when the development time was shortened to 18 min using the new solvent system, the individual bands of BMP became clearer, and S,S-(2,3′-diC18:1)-BMP, which was likely formed from the original BMP during storage, was also observed as a faint band between S,S'-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP and S,S-(3,3′-diC18:1)-BMP (Fig. 1B, right panel). Unlike the previous solvent of C/M/acetone/acetic acid/water, the new acidic solvent demonstrates that the Rf values of BMPs are distinct from other similar acidic phospholipids such as phosphatidylglycerol, cardiolipin, and phosphatidic acid, although the Rf value of S,S-(3,3′-diC18:1)-BMP was close to those lipids (Supplementary Fig. 1A).

Improved separation of BMP isomers.

BMP isomers, S,S-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP and S,S-(3,3′-diC18:1)-BMP, were developed in a solvent of C/M/AC/AcOH/W (45:15:10:10:2, v/v) for 40 min (A) and in a solvent of C/M/AcOH/W (65:15:10:2, v/v) for 34 min (B, left panel) or 18 min (B, right panel). 2,2′-BMP, 2,3′-BMP and 3,3′-BMP indicate S,S-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP, S,S-(2,3′-diC18:1)-BMP and S,S-(3,3′-diC18:1)-BMP, respectively. Lane 3 in the right panel of (B) is a BMP standard mixture containing 2,2′-BMP and 3,3′-BMP, which better visualizes the small amount of 2,3′-BMP generated during storage.

Given these results, we explored whether the new solvent system could facilitate the analysis of BMPs resulting from BMP-associated reactions in vitro and in cells.

Enhancement of isomerization of S,S'-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP by the soluble fraction of RAW264.7 cells

Our previous study suggested the possibility that the conversion of S,S'-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMPs, which are native and the major isomer of BMP, to other isomers is promoted by endogenous cellular factors [10]. To detect the endogenous promotion activity of S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP isomerization, RAW 264.7 cells were used and analyzed by TLC using the new acidic solvent system described above.

First, liposomes consisting of 100 percent S,S-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP were incubated under neutral conditions for 5 h with a 150,000g supernatant (soluble fraction) prepared from RAW 264.7 cell homogenates. The reaction was initiated by the addition of three different amounts of the supernatant. Five hours after the start of the reaction without the soluble fraction, a small portion of S,S-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP was converted to S,S-(2,3′-diC18:1)-BMP (Fig. 2A, left panel, #0). This spontaneous isomerization had been previously observed using liposomes consisting of 100 % S,S-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP [10]. In the presence of the soluble fraction, S,S-(2,3′-diC18:1)-BMP appeared as a major component concomitant with an increase in S,S-(3,3′-diC18:1)-BMP and a decrease in S,S-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP (Fig. 2A, left panel, #1–3). The increase in S,S-(3,3′-diC18:1)-BMP was dependent on the supernatant protein concentration (Fig. 2A, left panel, #1–3). In addition, the supernatant obtained from heat-treated soluble fraction (heated soluble fraction sup) had markedly reduced activity in promoting S,S-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP isomerization (Fig. 2A, left panel, #4). These results suggest that the soluble fraction contains an endogenous promoter of the isomerization of S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP, with loss of function upon heating or precipitation with the insoluble substances produced by heating.

FeCl3 and BSA were used as positive controls for the isomerization of S,S-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP (Fig. 2A, right panel, #5 and #6). Furthermore, when lipid extracts prepared from similar isomerization reaction mixtures to as in Fig. 2A were separated by TLC using a previous solvent system consisting of C/M/acetone (AC)/acetic acid (AcOH)/water (W) (45:15:10:10:2, v/v) (Fig. 2B). In comparison, the new acidic solvent consisting of C/M/AcOH/W (65:15:10:2, v/v) (Fig. 2A) improved in the separation of the individual isomers. The new acidic solvent in both one- and two-dimensional TLCs was used in subsequent studies.

Heat- and acid-stable S,S-2,2′-diacyl-BMP isomerization promoter

We considered the possibility that the isomerization-promoting activity was recovered in the pellet obtained by centrifugation at 150,000g of the heated soluble fraction. When the pellet was resuspended in the same volume of 0.9 % NaCl as the original soluble fraction by brief sonication using a probe-type sonicator, the loss of the isomerization-promoting activity was found in the supernatant of the heat-treated soluble fraction (Fig. 3A, #2). In contrast, the suspension prepared from the pellet obtained from heat-treated soluble fraction retained the isomerization-promoting activity (Fig. 3A, #3), indicating that the isomerization promoter is heat stable.

An S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP isomerization promoter that is thermostable and acid-resistant and enhances LPLA2-mediated BMP degradation.

The suspension obtained from heat-treated soluble fraction was centrifuged to separate the supernatant (Heated soluble fraction sup) from the pellet. The resultant pellet was resuspended in the same volume of 0.9 % NaCl (Heated ppt suspension). TCA pellet suspensions were incubated for 7 h at 37 °C (B, #5 and #6). No TCA-treated soluble fraction sup shows isomerization activity (data not shown). The stability of the isomerization factor was further studied. The soluble fraction was treated with 10% trichloroacetic acid (TCA) and centrifuged at 15,000g to separate the supernatant from the pellet. The pellet was washed with cold acetone, collected by centrifugation, and dried in a hood. The dried pellet was dispersed in the same volume of 0.1 M Hepes (pH 7.4) as the original soluble fraction by brief sonication using a probe-type sonicator. Like the pellet dispersions obtained from heat-treated soluble fraction (Fig. 3A, #3 and #3B, #3 and #4), the pellet dispersions obtained from TCA-treated soluble fraction also retained the isomerization-promoting activity (Fig. 3B, # 5 and #6). These results indicate that the endogenous isomerization-promoting activity resists severe denaturing conditions such as heat and acidic treatment.

Lysosomal phospholipase A2 degradation of BMP is enhanced by the soluble fraction of RAW264.7 cells

Consistent with the conversion of S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP to S,S-(2,3′-diacyl)- and S,S-(3,3′-diacyl)-BMPs, it was found that the soluble fraction obtained from RAW264.7 cell homogenates enhanced the degradation of BMP in the presence of LPLA2 under neutral conditions in vitro (Fig. 3B, lanes 1 and 7, and Fig. 3C). This is consistent with the observation that under neutral conditions, LPLA2 acts more specifically on S,S-(2,3′-diacyl)- and S,S-(3,3′-diacyl)-BMPs converted from S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP during incubation than on S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP or other phospholipids derived from the soluble fraction. We previously reported that, unlike S,S-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP and 1,2-dioleoyl-PC, the degradation activity of LPLA2 for S,S-(3,3′- diC18:1)-BMP is detectable even under neutral conditions [10].

Endogenous S,S-2,2′-diacyl-BMP isomerization

We previously observed that alkalization in acidic compartments in cells by chloroquine or ammonium chloride enhances the isomerization of S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP to other BMP [10]. This led us to consider whether endogenous S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMPs in the RAW264.7 cells may be converted to other BMP isomers under neutral conditions in the presence of the isomerization factor.

Cell homogenates prepared from RAW264.7 cells using an ultrasonicator were incubated at 37 °C for under neutral conditions (Fig. 4A). One-dimensional TLC using C/M/AcOH/W (65:15:10:2, v/v) solvent showed no significant difference in S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP content between samples incubated for 0 h and 1 h, regardless of the presence or absence of sn-1,3-specific lipase (Fig. 4A, upper panel, #1, #2 and #3). Notably, the S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP band almost completely disappeared in the sample incubated for 15 h (Fig. 4A, upper panel #4). In contrast and as predicted, the sample incubated for 15 h at pH 4.5 retained significant levels of S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP (Fig. 4B) [10]. One-dimensional TLC using C/M/28 % amomum hydroxide (65:35:5, v/v) solvent showed that both samples incubated under acidic and neutral conditions for 15 h contained a similar amount of BMP (Fig. 4C and D). These results indicate that the decrease in the amount of S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP in the sample incubated for 15 h under acidic conditions compared to the sample incubated for 0 h was due to degradation. On the other hand, the decrease in the amount of S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP in the sample incubated for 15 h under neutral conditions was only partly due to degradation and mainly due to the conversion of S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP to other isomers. The BMP in the latter sample overlapped with other lipids in one-dimensional TLC using the new acidic solvent system (Fig. 4A, upper panel, Fig. 4B). The mobility of BMP in the sample incubated at pH 7.4 for 15 h appeared to be only slightly faster than that at pH 4.5 (Fig. 4C and D) suggesting that BMPs in the sample incubated at pH 7.4 for 15 h mainly composed of S,S-(2,3′-diacyl)- and S,S-(3,3′-diacyl)-BMPs.

To confirm the isomerization of S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP, two-dimensional TLC was performed on individual samples in Fig. 4A (upper panel). According to Kobayashi et al. [1], S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP was easily converted to S,S-(2,3′- and 3,3′-diacyl)-BMPs during chromatography using silica gel under basic conditions. Therefore, in this study, C/M/AcOH/W (65:15:10:2, v/v) and C/M/28% amomum hydroxide (65:35:5, v/v) solvents were used as the first and second dimensional solvents, respectively. After development for 20 min in the first dimension, the plate was dried in a hood, briefly immersed in acetonitrile to facilitate the removal of acetic acid, and dried again in the hood. The dried plate was developed for 11 min in the second solvent system.

The location and separation of BMP from other known acidic lipids with similar Rf to BMP in an acidic solvent system were confirmed using the present two-dimensional TLC (Supplementary Fig. 1A). Based on these results, the following studies were carried out. The lower panel of Fig. 4A (#1, #2 and #3) showed that the BMP in individual samples incubated for 0 h and 1 h, including the sample co-incubated with lipase, appeared as a nearly single spot corresponding to S,S-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP (Supplementary Fig. 1B). In contrast, BMPs in samples incubated for 15 h were spread as multiple spots (Fig. 4A, lower panel #4; Supplementary Fig. 1C) and overlapped with other lipids (cardiolipin, enclosed in a dashed blue oval, Fig. 4A) in the first dimension.

The samples incubated for 0 h and 15 h under neutral conditions were treated with sn-1,3-specific lipase (Fig. 5). The BMP spot in the sample incubated for 0 h did not change significantly before and after lipase treatment (Fig. 5, upper panels) indicating that most of the BMPs in this sample were resistant to the lipase and consist of S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMPs. On the other hand, of the three spots detected in the sample incubated for 15 h, the two with higher Rf values were completely degraded by lipase (Fig. 5, lower right panel), indicating that these two spots corresponded to S,S-(2,3′-diacyl)- and S,S-(3,3′-diacyl)-BMPs (Fig. 5, lower left panel and Supplementary Fig. 1C).

Treatment of cell homogenates with sn-1,3 specific lipase.

Two hundred μg of protein of RAW264.7 cell homogenate was incubated at 37 °C for 0 or 15 h under neutral conditions and then treated with or without sn-1,3 specific lipase from Rhizoconcur miehei (0.84 U/ml) at 37 °C for 1 h. The reaction products were processed according to Materials and Methods. Two-dimensional TLC was performed as shown in Fig. 4. Each figure is representative of the results obtained from two separate experiments.

Taken together, the current findings obtained by one- and two-dimensional TLCs using the new acidic solvent system provide strong support for the existence of an endogenous promoter for the isomerization of cellular S,S-(2,2′-diacyl)-BMP to other BMP isomers.

In conclusion, compared with conventional TLC for BMP analysis, the present TLC using the new acidic solvent showed improved separation of BMP isomers and reduced the analysis time. This new system should be a useful, convenient, and cost-effective method for BMP analysis in most laboratories. As applied to the characterization of BMP isomers in RAW264.7 cells, this method supports the existence of an endogenous factor that promotes the non-degradative isomerization of S,S-(2,2′-diC18:1)-BMP.

The resulting lipid extracts, 2,2′- and 3,3′-BMP, were spotted at different positions on the original line of the HPLTC plate before development in the first dimension. GlcCer and CL indicate glucosylceramide and cardiolipin, respectively. Two-dimensional TLC was performed as described in Materials and Methods. Each figure is representative of the results obtained from two separate experiments.

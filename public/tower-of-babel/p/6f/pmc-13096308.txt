A study on infrared-visible fusion multimodal object detection algorithm based on cross-modal information bottleneck and minimum redundancy transformation
Authors: Weiyan Tan, Bing Geng, XiuGuang Bai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractInfrared-visible multimodal object detection plays a vital role in complex environmental conditions. However, existing approaches still suffer from significant limitations in modality redundancy suppression and feature alignment. To address these issues, this paper proposes a novel multimodal fusion detection framework that integrates a Cross-modal Information Bottleneck (CIB) with a Minimum Redundancy Transformation (MRT). The CIB module employs a compress-decompose-reconstruct pathway to selectively preserve shared semantics across modalities, thereby enhancing cross-modal consistency. The MRT module introduces sparse structural transformations along both channel and spatial dimensions, effectively suppressing modality redundancy and strengthening boundary-awareness for target regions. Additionally, we design a dual-phase training strategy based on modality isolation and fusion to stabilize the cooperative representation process. Extensive experiments conducted on two authoritative datasets, KAIST and LLVIP, validate the effectiveness of the proposed approach. Specifically, our method improves the mAP on the KAIST nighttime scenario from 42.8% (Baseline) to 44.1%, and achieves an AP@75 of 80.0% under low-light conditions in LLVIP, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art by 2.4%. Moreover, our method demonstrates consistent performance in robustness evaluations under occlusion and illumination disturbances, highlighting its advantages and application potential in multimodal perception.Keywords: Multimodal object detection, Infrared-visible fusion, Information bottleneck, Redundancy suppressionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

With the rapid development of multimodal perception technologies in fields such as autonomous driving, intelligent surveillance, and edge security, infrared-visible image fusion for object detection has become a crucial research topic in computer vision 1,2. Infrared images offer strong thermal imaging capabilities, enabling stable object acquisition in nighttime or low-light conditions, while visible images provide rich structural textures and color information. Effectively fusing the complementary characteristics of these two modalities can significantly improve model robustness and object recognition accuracy in complex environments, presenting both substantial theoretical significance and practical potential 3.

Although prior studies have explored various multimodal fusion mechanisms, existing approaches still face several critical challenges. First, infrared and visible images exhibit significant modality discrepancies and information redundancy, resulting in fused features that often contain irrelevant or conflicting information 4. Second, current feature alignment strategies mainly rely on stacking operations or attention mechanisms, making it difficult to achieve a balance between semantic consistency and structural interpretability5–7. Third, under complex scenarios such as occlusion and illumination variation, existing models struggle to adequately express key features, thereby limiting the upper bound of detection accuracy 8.

To address these issues, we propose a novel multimodal object detection framework that integrates a Cross-modal Information Bottleneck (CIB) with a Minimal Redundancy Transformation (MRT). The proposed method introduces an information compression and sparse reconstruction mechanism during feature fusion to suppress redundant channels at the source, enhance semantic selection and alignment across modalities, and boost the discriminative power of object regions through structure-aware sparse mapping. At present, many infrared–visible image fusion methods have been specifically designed to improve object detection performance 9–11. Compared with these task-oriented fusion approaches, our method emphasizes redundancy suppression and semantic reconstruction, providing a more interpretable and robust representation for complex multimodal environments.

The main contributions of this paper are threefold: (1) We introduce a cross-modal information bottleneck mechanism for the first time, enabling explicit compression of modality discrepancies and reconstruction of shared semantics. (2) We design a minimal redundancy transformation module to build orthogonal and decoupled pathways in the feature space, effectively suppressing redundant interference and enhancing structural representation. (3) We propose a dual-phase training strategy with modality isolation and fusion to further optimize the cooperative learning process. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets such as KAIST and LLVIP demonstrate that our method achieves superior robustness and detection accuracy under challenging conditions including complex illumination and occlusion.

Related work

Multimodal object detection: a taxonomy of methods

Multimodal object detection has attracted increasing attention because fusing information from heterogeneous sources (e.g., visible and infrared imagery) can effectively mitigate performance degradation caused by illumination variation, occlusion, or sensor-specific failure. Existing studies can be broadly categorized into three methodological paradigms: (1) early fusion based on direct feature stacking, (2) attention-driven intermediate fusion, and (3) task-oriented or structure-aware deep fusion. This subsection systematically reviews representative work within each class and analyzes their limitations from the perspective of semantic consistency, structure preservation, and cross-modal robustness, providing a foundation for the motivation of our proposed framework.

Early fusion via direct feature stacking

Early fusion strategies combine visible and infrared representations at shallow stages by directly concatenating image channels or stacking low-level feature maps. This paradigm assumes that heterogeneous modalities share sufficiently aligned distributions in the early encoding space, thereby enabling the network to extract unified low-level cues. Classical multispectral detection studies, such as French et al. 12 and Guan et al. 13, explicitly demonstrated that simple input-level or shallow-layer fusion can enhance contour sharpness and improve pedestrian visibility across illumination changes. Similarly, Wang et al. 14 proposed an early-fusion salient object detection framework that injects thermal priors into initial RGB processing stages, showing that early concatenation can stabilize feature perception under cross-illumination. More recently, Dupas et al. 15 introduced MEFA, an early-fusion module that aggregates multispectral channels before feeding a unified representation into modern detectors such as YOLOv8 and RT-DETRv1. Although Jiang et al. 16 focus on intermediate fusion, their experiments reveal that early fusion through direct channel stacking can capture certain low-level cues more effectively than isolated single-modality branches.

Despite these benefits, stacking-based early fusion remains fundamentally limited due to its inability to explicitly model modality discrepancies or regulate noisy modality-specific activations. Since concatenation treats heterogeneous inputs as homogeneous channels, it often generates redundant responses and unstable gradients when thermal and visible images exhibit inconsistent contrast, occlusion patterns, or noise characteristics. Prior studies 12–14 reported that early-fusion networks tend to overfit modality-specific artifacts when illumination sharply varies. Moreover, MEFA 15 and the comparative analysis in Jiang et al. 16 indicate that early stacking lacks semantic adaptability and structural interpretability, especially in complex urban environments or low-light scenes where modality imbalance is severe. These limitations motivate the development of more principled fusion mechanisms capable of regulating cross-modal redundancy, preserving structural cues, and ensuring stable alignment beyond the constraints of simple feature concatenation.

Attention-driven intermediate fusion

To address the limitations of naive stacking, attention-driven intermediate fusion has emerged as a dominant paradigm for modeling cross-modal complementarity. These methods incorporate channel, spatial, or cross-attention to dynamically reweight visible and infrared responses, enabling the network to highlight modality-relevant cues during feature interaction. Bao et al. 17 introduced an attention-guided multi-modal and multi-scale fusion mechanism that selectively enhances thermal–visible cues at different resolutions, demonstrating clear improvements in multispectral pedestrian detection. Similarly, Gao et al. 18 proposed a unified information fusion network that integrates RGB-D and RGB-T features through hierarchical attention, showing the general applicability of attention modules in heterogeneous fusion tasks. Cao et al. 19 designed a channel-switch and spatial-attention strategy to suppress redundant modality regions, while Zeng et al. 20 incorporated a gated fusion attention mechanism within MMI-Det to strengthen semantic alignment. Wang et al. 21 further explored interactive attention modeling to jointly enhance fusion and salient object detection, reinforcing the idea that attention can effectively guide cross-modal information flow.

Despite their advantages, attention-based intermediate fusion methods still face significant limitations when applied to scenes with severe modality imbalance or inconsistent cross-modal distributions. Because attention modules rely heavily on multi-stage recalibration, they may inadvertently emphasize unstable or noisy regions, resulting in ambiguous structural representations under extreme illumination changes. Lv et al. 22 demonstrated that Transformer-style cross-modal attention improves long-range interaction but is sensitive to inconsistent modality priors, leading to degraded robustness in complex RGB-T environments. Overall, although attention mechanisms improve complementarity, the lack of explicit redundancy suppression or structural decomposition often hampers their ability to maintain stable semantic alignment. These challenges motivate the development of more principled fusion frameworks capable of explicitly disentangling shared and modality-specific components while preserving structural interpretability.

Task-driven and structure-aware deep fusion

More recent advances emphasize detection-oriented fusion, domain-aware learning, and flexible semantic modulation, shifting multimodal research toward architectures that explicitly incorporate task priors or adapt to heterogeneous modality characteristics. Liu et al. 9 introduced a dual-adversarial framework that jointly regularizes modality complementarity and cross-domain alignment, demonstrating that domain-aware constraints can substantially improve fusion stability. Zhao et al. 10 proposed MetaFusion, which leverages meta-feature priors derived from detection tasks to guide the integration of low-level modalities, thereby achieving more precise semantic correspondence. In addition, Meng et al. 23 designed FQDNet with a four-branch semantic aggregation module that enhances small-object perception through multi-scale contextual reasoning, while Zhao et al. 24 incorporated edge-aware enhancement within a Transformer architecture to strengthen structural awareness in challenging infrared–visible environments.

Beyond these task-driven designs, researchers have explored more flexible and instruction-adaptive multimodal paradigms. Yang et al. 11 proposed an instruction-driven infrared–visible fusion model that modulates fusion behavior based on semantic prompts, enabling versatile adaptation across diverse downstream tasks. Despite these improvements in flexibility, semantic richness, and high-level guidance, deep fusion approaches still struggle to achieve fine-grained structural alignment and to effectively suppress modality-specific redundancy. These limitations are particularly evident in scenarios involving severe illumination variation, cross-sensor degradation, or inconsistent thermal–visible distributions, where implicit fusion alone is insufficient to maintain stable semantic consistency and robust cross-modal correspondence.

Limitations of existing methods and motivation of this work

Although multimodal object detection has progressed from early stacking to attention-based and task-driven fusion, two issues remain unresolved: (1) redundant or noisy responses arising from inconsistent modality distributions, (2) insufficient structural interpretability due to heavy reliance on attention recalibration. Existing fusion mechanisms often treat modality interactions as implicit transformations, lacking explicit constraints to decouple redundant components and preserve task-relevant semantics.

To overcome these limitations, our work introduces a cross-modal information bottleneck and a minimum redundancy transformation module, which explicitly disentangle shared and modality-specific features, suppress redundant representations, and enhance structural alignment. By integrating information-theoretic compression with orthogonal decomposition, the proposed method establishes a principled fusion framework that simultaneously improves semantic consistency, structural interpretability, and robustness across diverse infrared–visible conditions.

Cross-modal information bottleneck

To improve the collaborative efficiency of redundancy suppression and key information extraction in multimodal object detection, this paper proposes a Cross-modal Information Bottleneck (CIB) mechanism (Fig. 2). The CIB is designed to compress shared cues, deconstruct modality-specific factors, and then reconstruct–align them through a bottlenecked representation that explicitly maximizes cross-modal agreement while minimizing redundancy. Concretely, CIB instantiates the information-bottleneck principle to preserve cross-modal shared semantics by (i) limiting the mutual information between the bottleneck and the raw fusion feature, (ii) encouraging cycle-consistent reconstruction for each modality, and (iii) aligning the reconstructed features via correlation-based agreement while enforcing orthogonality between shared and specific components. Dataset and experimental setup

Dataset

KAIST multispectral pedestrian benchmark

The KAIST Multispectral Pedestrian Benchmark is one of the most representative infrared-visible fusion datasets for pedestrian detection. It is widely used in multimodal object detection research. This dataset was collected by the Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (KAIST) and covers real street scenes under various illumination conditions during both day and night. It contains more than 95,000 paired images with corresponding pedestrian annotations. Each frame includes synchronized infrared and visible images that are spatially aligned through precise calibration. This provides an ideal foundation for cross-modal feature alignment and fusion modeling. Given that this study aims to improve the robustness and semantic representation of infrared-visible object detection models in complex environments, the KAIST dataset serves as an essential benchmark for training and evaluation. It supports a comprehensive validation of the proposed Cross-modal Information Bottleneck (CIB) and Minimum Redundancy Transformation (MRT) modules in terms of fusion accuracy, redundancy suppression, and cross-modal consistency modeling.

LLVIP (low-light visible-infrared person) dataset

The LLVIP (Low-Light Visible-Infrared Person) dataset is a high-quality multimodal dataset designed to evaluate pedestrian detection performance under low-light conditions using infrared and visible image fusion. It covers a variety of real low-light scenarios and provides synchronized visible-infrared image pairs. The dataset contains nearly 15,000 annotated instances of pedestrians in diverse poses, occlusion levels, and spatial scales, making it highly challenging. Compared to traditional datasets, LLVIP places greater emphasis on cross-modal information asymmetry and redundancy in low-illumination environments. It offers a suitable validation platform for the proposed Cross-modal Information Bottleneck (CIB) mechanism. By introducing the Minimum Redundancy Transformation (MRT) strategy on this dataset, the model can effectively mitigate redundant overlap and feature interference between infrared and visible modalities. This further enhances the model’s discriminative performance and structural generalization in complex perception scenarios.

Experimental setup

The detection algorithm proposed in this study is built on the RT-DETR architecture and implemented using the Pytorch framework. All experiments are conducted on a system equipped with an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU. The shared backbone network of the model adopts the HGNetv2X architecture, which is pretrained on ImageNet to enhance convergence stability. The Multimodal Semantic Fusion Interface (MSFI) module contains one Transformer encoder layer, which encodes joint semantic features from RGB and infrared images. In the Channel-Modulated Semantic Fusion (CMSF) module, three RepBlocks are introduced to enhance nonlinear representation capability. The Transformer decoder adopts a six-layer stacked structure to ensure strong perception of object boundaries from high-level semantics.

The training process follows a two-stage strategy. In the first stage, isolation training is conducted on original synchronized RGB-IR image pairs for 10 epochs to stabilize multimodal feature representations. In the second stage, fusion training is performed to enhance cross-modal complementarity for 50 epochs. The final model is trained for a total of 60 epochs. The batch size is uniformly set to 4, and the input image size is . Backbone

This study employs HGNetv2X as its shared backbone, positioned to provide cross-modal consistent multi-scale backbone representations for RT-DETR. Specifically, before entering the fusion module, the RGB and infrared inputs undergo stepwise extraction from low-level texture to high-level semantics using the same set of HGNetv2X parameters, ensuring a strictly aligned pyramid structure for both modalities in spatial resolution, feature stride, and semantic hierarchy. This backbone is initialized with ImageNet pre-trained weights, primarily to improve early training convergence stability, reduce gradient oscillations and feature collapse risks under small batch conditions, and ensure robust foundational representations of cross-modal information before entering the Transformer decoder.

Structurally, HGNetv2X can be viewed as a typical staged convolutional backbone: the Stem performs rapid downsampling and shallow edge/texture encoding, followed by four stages to progressively expand the receptive field and enhance channel expressiveness, forming a multi-scale feature set for detection. For an input size of 640 640, Backbone outputs typically correspond to hierarchical features with strides of 4/8/16/32. These features are used in the RT-DETR framework to construct subsequent high-level semantic aggregations and interactions with the target query.

Ablation experiment

Ablation experiment results

To further verify the effectiveness of each key module in the model architecture and its contribution to overall performance, we design a series of ablation experiments. By gradually removing or replacing specific modules and performing comparative evaluations under consistent conditions, we reveal the functional role of each module at different stages. The results show a consistent improvement in detection accuracy under different lighting conditions. This is not a simple numerical accumulation. By injecting a controlled bottleneck into the feature space, the network selectively retains discriminative semantics shared across modalities and suppresses modality-specific spectral noise. Meanwhile, MRT performs sparse reorganization in both channel and spatial dimensions. It reduces coupling among redundant paths through orthogonal constraints. Through bottleneck reconstruction and redundancy minimization from an information-theoretic perspective, we jointly enhance the robustness and fine-grained localization performance of the Transformer detector in visible-infrared scenarios.

Conclusion

This paper addresses the critical challenges of information redundancy, modality inconsistency, and feature misalignment in infrared-visible multimodal object detection. We propose a novel detection framework that integrates Cross-modal Information Bottleneck and Minimal Redundancy Transformation. By leveraging structured compression and sparse reconstruction mechanisms, our approach effectively enhances semantic complementarity and local structural modeling across modalities. Extensive experiments conducted on two authoritative multimodal datasets, KAIST and LLVIP, demonstrate that our method achieves significant improvements in detection accuracy, occlusion robustness, and low-light performance, validating its practical value and generalization potential in real-world complex scenarios.

Future research will focus on extending this framework to more challenging scenarios, including dynamic video analysis, multi-object tracking, and cross-domain detection. We also plan to incorporate knowledge distillation and self-supervised learning strategies to improve the model’s generalization under few-shot and lightweight deployment conditions. Furthermore, the cross-modal information bottleneck mechanism may be integrated with vision-language models to broaden its application boundaries in intelligent surveillance and human-machine collaboration.

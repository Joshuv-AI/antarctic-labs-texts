Generation of two induced pluripotent stem cell lines from patients with Down syndrome
Authors: Wenjuan Zhu, Wenqiang Liu, Rebecca Yu, Melanie Manning, Anitra Waran Romfh, Joseph C. Wu
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Down syndrome (DS) is caused by trisomy of Homo sapiens chromosome 21 (HSA21) and is by far the most common chromosomal disorder accompanied by neurodevelopmental disorders and congenital heart disease. Here, we generated two induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) lines from two patients with DS. These two lines exhibited normal morphology, trisomy 21 karyotype, pluripotency and differentiation capability into derivatives of three germ layers. The patient-specific iPSC lines are an invaluable resource in research to model DS-related cellular and molecular pathologies and test possible therapeutic strategies for DS.

Resource utility

Two iPSC lines were generated from two Down syndrome patients caused by trisomy of HSA21. These iPSC lines can be used to elucidate molecular basis and developmental pathogenesis underlying DS and screen candidate drugs for treatment of DS-related comorbidity.

Resource details

DS, caused by trisomy of HSA21, is the most frequent survivable autosomal aneuploidy. A complete trisomy 21 is present in 95% of individuals with DS while translocation, mosaicism, and partial trisomy 21 account for ~5% of affected individuals (Antonarakis et al., 2020). Genomic dosage imbalance and variability on HSA21 contribute to phenotypic variability in DS. Its abnormalities manifest in a variety of tissues in particular the musculoskeletal, neurological and cardiovascular systems (Antonarakis et al., 2020). People with DS are more likely to develop early onset Alzheimer’s disease (AD) primarily attributed to overexpression of amyloid precursor protein, encoded by amyloid precursor protein (APP) located on HSA21, and nearly all adults with DS exhibit the neuropathological alterations of AD by the age of 40 years (Lott and Head, 2019). Up to 50% of individuals with DS suffer from congenital heart disease (CHD) accompanied by poor outcomes, and cardiovascular complication is the leading cause of death among people with DS (Dimopoulos et al., 2023). Human DS iPSC models have contributed significant knowledge of the pathophysiological mechanisms of DS. The majority of current DS iPSC research has generated cells of the nervous system to decipher mechanisms of intellectual disability underlying DS (Wu et al., 2022).

In this study, two patients diagnosed with DS, carrying a trisomy 21 (line SCVIi085-A from a 5-year-old male and line SCVIi086-A from a 27-year-old male) were recruited. iPSCs were derived from peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) using the Sendai virus vector containing Oct3/4, Sox2, Klf4, and c-Myc (Yamanaka factors). All iPSCs exhibited typical iPSC morphology (Fig. 1A, Scale bar: 890 μm). Karyotype analysis directly confirmed trisomy 21 was present in both iPSC lines and showed no chromosomal aberrations on non-chromosome 21 (Fig. 1B). Transcriptional expression of pluripotency markers SOX2 and NANOG was detected using reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) in both lines compared to positive control (Fig. 1C). Moreover, protein expression of pluripotency markers (OCT3/4, NANOG and SOX2) was verified by immunofluorescence staining (Fig. 1D, Scale bar: 130 μm). Furthermore, Sendai virus genome was negligible from line SCVIi085-A at passage 28 and from line SCVIi086-A at passage 27 (Fig. 1E). Both iPSC lines displayed full potential to differentiate into three germ layers (Fig. 1F and Supplementary Fig. 1, Scale bar: 130 μm) and were negative for mycoplasma (Supplementary Fig. 2). The sample identity between both iPSC lines and their corresponding PBMCs was validated using genotypes of 1 million SNP makers generated by genotyping array and whole genome sequencing.

Materials and methods

Reprogramming

Whole blood samples were collected from two DS patients. PBMCs were isolated from blood with Percoll density gradient medium (GE Healthcare #17089109) and purified with DPBS (ThermoFisher Scientific #14190144). PBMCs were maintained for 7 days in StemPro®-34 SFM medium (ThermoFisher Scientific #10639011) supplemented with 100 ng/mL SCF (Peprotech #300–07), 100 ng/mL FLT3 (ThermoFisher Scientific #PHC9414), 20 ng/mL IL-3 (Peprotech #200–3), 20 ng/mL IL-6 (ThermoFisher Scientific #PHC0063), and 20 ng/mL EPO (ThermoFisher Scientific #PHC9631), prior to iPSC reprograming with CytoTune™-iPSC 2.0 Sendai Reprogramming Kit (ThermoFisher Scientific #A16517). Transduced PBMCs were resuspended and plated on Matrigel-coated plates, and cultured in StemPro™-34 medium. On Day 7 post-transduction, the media was redirected to StemMACS™ iPS-Brew XF medium (Miltenyi Biotec #130–104-368), and cells were cultured until days 10–15 post-transduction. iPSC colonies were selected and further expanded for downstream experiments as previously described (Liu et al., 2021).

Cell culture

iPSC lines were maintained in Essential 8™ Medium (ThermoFisher Scientific, #A1517001) with supplement (ThermoFisher Scientific, #A15171–01), and 10 μM of ROCK inhibitor (Y27632, Selleck Chemicals, #S1049) was added for the first 24 h after passaging. Then iPSC lines were replenished with fresh medium every day until reaching confluency and ready for passaging. Cells were cultured in a humidified incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO2. The iPSC lines from patients with DS at passage 27–28 were collected for downstream experiments.

Trilineage differentiation

iPSC lines at passage 27–28 were differentiated into definitive endoderm with the StemDiff™ Definitive Endoderm Differentiation Kit (STEMCELL™ Technologies #05110), ectoderm with the Human Pluripotent Stem Cell Functional Identification Kit (R&D Systems #SC027B), and mesoderm with the addition of 6 μM CHIR (Selleck Chemicals, #S2924) dissolved in RPMI media supplemented with B27 supplement Minus Insulin (Gibco, #11875–085 and #A18956–01) for 48 h, respectively (Table 1).

Immunofluorescence (IF) staining

We used iPSCs at passage 28 for the immunostaining. At room temperature (RT), iPSCs were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde solution (Fisher Scientific #50980487) for 15 min, permeabilized with 0.1% PBST solution (Sigma-Aldrich #9036–19-5) for 10 min, and blocked for 30 min at RT in blocking solution (DPBS with 1% Bovine Serum Albumin and 5% serum (Donkey Serum (Sigma-Aldrich #D9663) or Goat serum (Sigma #G9023)) depending on the animal origin of the secondary antibody). iPSCs were then incubated with primary antibodies (Table 2) overnight at 4 °C diluted in 0.1% PBST. On the following day, the cells were washed three times, 15 min each time using 0.1% PBST. The cells were then incubated for 30 min at RT with secondary antibodies diluted in 0.1% PBST (Table 2). Subsequently, the nuclei were stained with the Molecular Probes NucBlue Fixed Cell ReadyProbes Reagent (ThermoFisher Scientific #R37606) and images were captured with the ECHO Revolve microscope.

RT-qPCR

The iPSC line from a healthy individual at passage 9 was used as a positive control, and iPSC-differentiated cardiomyocytes from the same lineage were used as a negative control for RT-qPCR analysis. Total RNA was isolated from iPSC lines using the Direct-zol™ RNA Miniprep Kit (ZYMO Research #3R2061) and converted to cDNA using iScript™ cDNA Synthesis Kit (BioRad #1708891) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Expression of SOX2, NANOG, and SEV was detected by TaqManTM Gene expression Assay (Applied Biosystems™ #4444556). Primers used for RT-qPCR analysis are listed in Table 2.

Karyotyping

To detect chromosomal anomalies, karyotyping was performed using the KaryoStat™ assay (ThermoFisher Scientific #905403) on iPSC lines at passage 27.

Analysis of sample identity

Genotyping of both iPSC lines at passage 27 was carried out using the above KaryoStat™ assay comprising ~ 1.16 million SNP markers. The genotype call rate is 98.39% and 98.75%, respectively (Supplementary Table 1). The whole genome sequencing data of their corresponding PBMCs was generated by the Trans-Omics for Precision Medicine (TOPMed) program and deposited in NCBI dbGAP database under accession number phs002338. Joint-calling variant files were downloaded from dbGAP database after approval. SNP genotypes of both PBMC samples were extracted for downstream analysis. SNP genotypes of both PBMCs and iPSC lines were merged and analyzed to identify cryptic relatedness using Plink (RRID: SCR_001757) and KING (RRID: SCR_009251) software. Finally, the genome from iPSCs and their PBMCs were analyzed for 1 million SNP markers, rather than 16 different loci from traditional STR analysis. Relationship inference from the two software indicated that iPSC line and the corresponding PBMC were duplicated samples (Supplementary Fig. 3).

Mycoplasma detection

Mycoplasma detection was performed using the MycoAlert™ Detection Kit (Lonza #LT07–318) on two iPSC lines at passage 28.

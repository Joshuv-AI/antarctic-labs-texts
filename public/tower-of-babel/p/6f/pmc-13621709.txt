Role of microRNAs in cisplatin response of cancer stem cells
Authors: Meysam Moghbeli, Bahareh Payami
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCisplatin (CDDP)-based chemotherapy remains a cornerstone of cancer treatment, but its efficacy is often limited by the development of chemo resistance that is mainly associated with cancer stem cells (CSCs). MicroRNAs (miRNAs) have emerged as pivotal post-transcriptional regulators of gene expression, critically influencing key cellular processes, including drug response. This review aims to provide a comprehensive analysis of the current evidence elucidating the role of specific miRNAs in governing the CDDP response of CSCs. We conducted a systematic assessment of all available scientific literature databases up to (Nov 2025) to identify relevant studies. Our analysis reveals that miRNAs function as a dynamic network, either promoting or reducing CDDP resistance in CSCs through regulation of signaling pathways, apoptosis, DNA repair pathways, and ABC drug transporters. We identified several consistently reported oncomiRs (e.g., miR-21, miR-765, and miR-132) that were up regulated in CSCs and confer CDDP resistance. Conversely, tumor-suppressor miRNAs were frequently down regulated and their re-expression re-sensitized CSCs to CDDP. This comprehensive review consolidates the compelling evidence that miRNAs are central regulators of CDDP response in CSCs. Understanding this intricate regulatory network provides a robust foundation for developing novel therapeutic strategies, such as miRNA-based mimics or inhibitors (anti-miRs) to overcome CDDP resistance in CSCs improve patient outcomes.Keywords: Cancer stem cells, Cisplatin, MicroRNA, Chemo resistance

Background

Cancer stem cells (CSCs) are identified as a subset of cells that possess the abilities for self-renewal and differentiation [1]. Although, CSCs constitute a minor part of the total cancer cell population, they display significant characteristics such as tumorigenicity, resistance to chemotherapy, and considerable metastatic capability, which have been linked to tumor aggressiveness [2, 3]. CSCs exhibit traits similar to those of embryonic or tissue-specific stem cells and frequently show persistent activation of several highly conserved signaling pathways that are crucial for development and tissue maintenance [4, 5]. CSCs are seen as a crucial target for the development of new cancer therapies or the enhancement of existing treatment modalities [6–8]. Nevertheless, CSCs exhibit multidrug resistance, making it essential to discover novel CSC-associated biomarkers to identify new therapeutic targets for cancer treatment. Non-coding RNAs have been reported as the essential regulators of gene expression that have also potential as the therapeutic targets in cancer patients [9]. MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are endogenous noncoding RNAs that play a critical role in the post-transcriptional regulation [10, 11]. MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are involved in various biological and pathological processes, including differentiation, metabolism, proliferation, and cancer initiation [12–15]. Aberrant expression of miRNAs has been identified in human cancers, and they are increasingly recognized as critical biomarkers for cancer diagnosis and treatment [16, 17]. They may influence the fundamental biological characteristics of CSCs, such as their self-renewal capacities and the processes of tumor formation [18].

Chemotherapy remains the foremost approach for the treatment of cancer. Nonetheless, chemo resistance poses a significant challenge to the efficacy of chemotherapy and accounts for a substantial portion of therapeutic failures. Chemo resistance may arise through three distinct mechanisms: the impairment of water-soluble drug uptake due to their degradation, reduction in apoptosis, enhancement of DNA repair, and the augmented efflux of hydrophobic drugs facilitated by ATP-binding cassette transporters [19]. Cisplatin (CDDP) is a potent anticancer agent that is commonly utilized in the treatment of various malignant tumors [20–23]. This drug induces apoptosis in tumor cells through forming crosslinks with DNA, consequently halting DNA replication and transcription [24]. Nevertheless, the emergence of drug resistance poses a significant challenge to the effective clinical utilization of CDDP. The mechanisms underlying CDDP resistance are multifaceted, encompassing reduced drug uptake, enhanced drug efflux, and increased DNA repair [25]. CSCs have a notable resistance to cisplatin which complicates their elimination [26]. Their augmented ability to repair DNA damage shields them from the cytotoxic effects of chemotherapy, resulting in the proliferation of CSCs post-treatment and contributing to the reemergence of tumors [27]. A thorough exploration of the molecular mechanisms underlying chemotherapy resistance in CSCs could unveil new therapeutic targets to augment anticancer strategies. MiRNAs play a significant role in the effectiveness of chemotherapy in various cancers [28–30]. Therefore, we discussed the role of miRNAs in CDDP response of CSCs to highlight them as the reliable targets for the elimination of CSCs (Table 1).

Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway

MicroRNAs play a critical and multifaceted role in mediating CDDP resistance through their precise regulation of the WNT signaling pathway. This intricate crosstalk establishes a network where deregulated miRNAs directly influence the transcriptional programs governed by WNT/β-catenin signaling, ultimately affecting DNA damage response, apoptosis evasion, and the acquisition of stem-like properties, all of which are hallmarks of CDDP resistance in various cancers [31, 32]. MiRNAs have key roles in CDDP response of CSCs (Fig. 1). It has been demonstrated that miR-612 effectively reduced the stem-like properties in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) by decreasing both the quantity and size of tumorspheres, as well as diminishing clonogenicity, in addition to reversing resistance to cisplatin. Furthermore, miR-612 obstructed tumorigenesis in NOD/SCID mouse models and altered the invasive characteristics of cells. Notably, the overexpression of miR-612 resulted in the sequestration of cytoplasmic β-catenin. Additionally, miR-612 was found to negatively regulate the transcriptional activity of TCF/LEF. MiR-612 down regulated CCND1 and c-Myc in HCC cells [33]. It has been shown that cisplatin-treated SW480 cells had increased expression levels of stem cell markers and higher CDDP resistance. Furthermore, miR-148a reduced the expression of cancer stem cell markers and enhanced CDDP sensitivity in colorectal cancer (CRC) cells. Additionally, miR-148a inhibited the expression of WNT10b and downstream β-catenin pathways [34].

E2F1 is a prominent transcription factor that is frequently hyperactivated in human cancers [35, 36]. CircFAM73A enhanced CSC properties thereby increased malignancy in gastric cancer (GC). Notably, higher levels of circFAM73A were indicative of a poor prognosis for patients with GC. CircFAM73A modulated HMGA2 expression by sequestering miR-490-3p, while HMGA2 promoted the transcriptional activation of FAM73A via E2F1 and increased the circularization efficacy of circFAM73A through HNRNPL, thereby creating a positive feedback mechanism that amplified circFAM73A expression. Furthermore, circFAM73A engaged HNRNPK to enhance its interaction as a stabilizer with β-catenin that reinforced stem cell-like traits in GC, consequently promoted cell proliferation, migration, and resistance to cisplatin [37].

FSTL1 is a secreted glycoprotein that interacts with Wnt ligands and Frizzled receptors to promote Wnt/β-catenin pathway. It was shown that miR-137 negatively regulated the activity of FSTL1, leading to the suppression of Wnt/β-catenin pathway, breast cancer stemness, and chemo resistance [38]. CD133 has been identified as a significant marker for lung CSCs, which have the capability to proliferate indefinitely into tumor spheres when cultured in serum-free medium supplemented with EGF and bFGF [39]. Furthermore, elevated levels of SOX2, OCT4, NANOG, and ABCG2 were correlated with enhanced stem-like characteristics in cancer cells [40, 41]. MiR-140-3p reduced CD133 + cell population, tumor sphere formation, and expression levels of pluripotency factors in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) cells. MiR-140-3p enhanced sensitivity to cisplatin while diminished stem-like properties by inhibiting Wnt/β-catenin signaling in LUAD cells [42].

Numerous variants of ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporters have key role in drug resistance by expelling chemotherapy agents from cancerous cells [43]. Increased levels of ABCG2 expression have been associated with resistance to a spectrum of anticancer therapeutics [44]. ALDH1A1 as an intracellular detoxifying enzyme has been characterized as a marker for CSCs [45]. It was shown that ALDHA1 + cells derived from primary CRC tissues had CSCs features and CDDP resistance. An increased expression of miR-199a/b has been observed in ALDHA1 + cervical cancer stem cells (CCSCs), alongside a decrease in Gsk3β levels and an increase in β-catenin and ABCG2 expression. Up regulation of miR-199a/b in CRC tissues was correlated with responses to cisplatin and had a negative impact on patient survival rates. MiR-199a/b regulated the Wnt/β-catenin pathway through Gsk3β targeting in ALDHA1 + CCSCs. ABCG2 has been shown to play a role in CDDP resistance in ALDHA1 + CCSCs and was regulated by miR-199a/b. Consequently, up regulation of miR-199a/b in ALDHA1 + CCSCs was contributed with CDDP resistance through the Wnt/β-catenin-ABCG2 pathway [46]. It was revealed that there was miR-23b down regulation in CCSCs. MiR-23b reduced the stem cell properties and CDDP resistance in CCSCs by ALDH1A1 targeting [47].

NOTCH signaling pathway

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) play a pivotal role in modulating CDDP resistance of CSCs through the regulation of NOTCH signaling pathway that is a key regulator of cell fate, proliferation, and survival (Fig. 1). Therefore, the deregulation of miRNA-NOTCH axis creates a pro-survival signaling environment that protects cancer cells from CDDP-induced apoptosis, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target for re-sensitizing tumors to treatment. The mammalian Notch family (Notch1-4) becomes activated through interactions with specific ligands. Following activation of Notch signaling, the intracellular domain of Notch (NICD) is released, and the resultant cleavage products migrate to the nucleus, where they activate Notch target genes such as Hes-1 and Hey-1 [48, 49]. MiR-181b diminished the properties associated with CSCs and improved sensitivity to cisplatin treatment by targeting Notch2. Furthermore, a significant correlation was found between decreased levels of miR-181b and elevated Notch2 expression with overall survival and CSC-like characteristics in patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) [50]. DLK1 as a member of the EGF-like protein family plays a critical role in cell differentiation. It acts as an inhibitor of Notch pathway and has been implicated in promoting tumor cell invasion and cellular stemness [51–54]. MiR-129-5p has been identified as a suppressor of stemness and chemo resistance in NSCLC through DLK1 targeting. Levels of miR-129-5p expression were found to be significantly reduced in CD133-positive stem cells compared to those in NSCLC cell lines prior to the isolation of stem cells. MiR-129-5p not only suppressed stemness but also diminished CDDP resistance in NSCLC [55].

The tumor microenvironment (TME) is a multifaceted network composed of stromal cells, signaling molecules, and extracellular matrix (ECM) components, which promotes tumor progression [56–58]. In this intricate ecosystem, cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) assume a pivotal role in determining the aggressiveness of tumors. These activated CAFs, identifiable by specific markers such as ACTA2 (α-smooth muscle actin), do not merely act as passive components but are actively engaged in tumorigenesis [59, 60]. They remodel the ECM, release growth factors, and form paracrine signaling circuits that promote cancer cell metastasis and resistance to treatment [61, 62]. Hypoxic conditions induce both transcriptional and secretory alterations in CAFs, resulting in an aggressive tumor phenotype [63]. Hypoxia was a characteristic feature of advanced tumors that drives CAFs to secrete exosomes with high levels of circSTAT3. These exosomes were taken up by triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) cells, where they stimulate NOTCH1 signaling through the sponging effect of miR-671-5p. The stability of circSTAT3 promoted prolonged NOTCH1 activation in recipient TNBC cells, thereby enhancing their long-term chemo resistance and stem-like characteristics [64].

TGF-β, hedgehog, and NF-κB signaling pathways

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) play a pivotal role in CDDP response of CSCs by regulating TGF-β, Hedgehog, and NF-κB pathways (Fig. 2). Through their post-transcriptional control of target genes, specific miRNA deregulation can lead to the aberrant activation of these pathways, which collectively promote tumor cell survival, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), and a stem-like phenotype [65, 66]. This intricate crosstalk, orchestrated by miRNA networks, establishes a protective tumor microenvironment that diminishes DNA damage-induced apoptosis and enhances DNA repair, ultimately conferring resistance to CDDP chemotherapy.

A variety of studies have established that the transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) pathway is critical for facilitating metastasis in GC [67, 68]. TGF-β isoforms can initiate canonical TGF-β signaling, leading to the phosphorylation of Smad2/3, which serve as the primary intracellular mediators of this pathway [67]. There was miR-21-5p up regulation in gastric cancer patients exhibiting elevated CD44 levels. Gastric cancer stem cells (GCSCs) had significant higher resistance to cisplatin compared to CD44-negative cells. MiR-21-5p down regulation suppressed the TGF-β2/SMAD pathway in GCSCs [69]. Ribosomal proteins are integral elements of the ribosome, playing diverse secondary roles in DNA repair, apoptosis, drug resistance, and cellular proliferation. Among these proteins, ribosomal protein L15 (RPL15) is notably up regulated in GC [70]. It was indicated that low levels of miR-567 promote the expression of stemness-associated factors such as SOX2, NANOG, and ALDH1A1, thereby enhancing the stem-like characteristics of AGS cells. MiR-567 induced the sensitivity of AGS/CDDP cells to cisplatin through the targeting of PRL15, resulting in the inactivation of the TGF-β/Smad pathway [71].

Tripartite motif containing 37 (TRIM37) has been identified as an oncogene in several cancers [72–74]. Notably, TRIM37 enhances the drug resistance of osteosarcoma cells [75]. Additionally, TRIM37 has been shown to activate the NF-κB pathway, thereby promoting the aggressiveness of non-small-cell lung cancer [73]. The ubiquitination of IκBα plays a crucial role in facilitating the nuclear translocation of p65, which subsequently leads to the activation of NF-κB pathway and induces EMT in colorectal cancer cells [76]. ASB16-AS1 up regulated TRIM37 by sequestering miR-3918 and miR-4676-3p while also promoting TRIM37 phosphorylation through the recruitment of ATM. Ultimately, ASB16-AS1 relies on TRIM37 to activate the NF-κB pathway to enhance stem cell-like properties and cisplatin resistance in gastric cancer [77]. TRG16 is a nuclear receptor binding protein with kinase activity. A decrease in TRG16 expression has been reported in breast cancer (BC) tissues, with patients exhibiting elevated TRG16 levels demonstrating improved prognostic outcomes. TRG16 reduced CD44 + cell populations, the number of mammospheres, and the expression of markers associated with stemness in BC. Furthermore, TRG16 enhanced the sensitivity of BC cells to CDDP. TRG16 inhibited the NF-κB pathway in BC cells by ANXA2 regulation. Additionally, TRG16, which is regulated by miR-765, suppressed the stem cell-like traits of BC cells by hindering the NF-κB pathway, thereby reducing cell viability and invasiveness while increasing sensitivity to CDDP [78].

Patched 1 (PTCH1) is recognized as a tumor suppressor within the Hedgehog pathway. PTCH1 inhibits the function of the smoothened receptor (SMO), which is a G-protein coupled receptor. Additionally, the transcription factors known as glioma-associated oncogenes (GLI) are rendered inactive through their interaction with suppressor of fused (SUFU). The binding of Hedgehog (Hh) ligands to PTCH1 relieves this suppression of SMO, leading to the activation of GLI [79]. GLI is crucial for the transcriptional modulation of various genes, including Sox-2, Nanog, and CD133 [80]. LINC-PINT regulated stemness and chemo resistance in laryngeal carcinoma cells through the miR-425-5p/PTCH1/SHH pathway [81].

DNA repair and apoptosis

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) play a pivotal role in CDDP resistance of CSCs by regulation of key genes involved in DNA repair and apoptotic pathways (Fig. 3). Translesion Synthesis (TLS) represents a DNA damage tolerance mechanism that utilizes specialized DNA polymerases, which are known for their reduced accuracy [82]. These TLS polymerases enable replication across DNA damage, thus preventing the collapse of the replication fork and are contributing to cisplatin resistance in CSCs [83]. TLS is vital for safeguarding CSCs against the deleterious effects of DNA-damaging agents [84, 85]. Exonuclease 1 (EXO1) is a multifunctional enzyme that is integral to several DNA damage response (DDR) pathways, particularly translesion DNA synthesis [86]. Notably, elevated levels of EXO1 have been linked to drug resistance in various cancer types [87, 88]. A crucial role of EXO1 is to create single-stranded gaps in the DNA, which are then repaired via an error-prone process that involves TLS pathways [89]. TLS polymerases Polη and Polι, which lack proofreading capabilities and exhibit low fidelity, are part of this DNA damage tolerance machinery [90]. Previous studies have indicated an increase in TLS polymerase expression in CSCs [82]. MiR-3163 was associated with the suppression of NSCLC progression. In the absence of EXO1, there was a reduction in monoubiquitinated-PCNA levels, leading to diminished recruitment of TLS polymerases to DNA lesions and a subsequent decrease in the expression of Polη and Polι. Moreover, miR-3163 was shown to down regulate EXO1 and other TLS enzymes, thereby disrupting the EXO1/Polη/Polι axis, causing an increased CDDP sensitivity in lung CSCs [91]. TLS is facilitated through the monoubiquitination of PCNA by the ubiquitin E3 ligase RAD18 and its associated E2 ubiquitin ligase RAD6. Following monoubiquitination, PCNA recruits the Polη, enabling DNA damage bypass in response to chemotherapeutic agents [92]. MiR-379-5p diminished the presence of CSCs and reduced the proliferation of cisplatin-induced CSCs by negatively regulating RAD18 and the TLS pathway. There was RAD18 up regulation in spheroids derived from ovarian cancer (OC) cells and tissues. The overexpression of miR-379-5p exerted an inhibitory effect on ovarian CSCs’ DNA repair capabilities. This diminished capacity for DNA repair in CSCs could be attributed to the suppression of RAD18 activity by miR-379-5p, which hindered the recruitment of Polη to the site of DNA lesions, thereby obstructed lesion bypass by TLS polymerases [93].

Bcl-2 family of proteins, which mediate the permeabilization of the outer mitochondrial membrane, regulates the intrinsic apoptosis pathway [94]. Furthermore, reactive oxygen species (ROS) have the capacity to form pores within the mitochondrial membrane, thereby initiating apoptosis [95]. Conversely, the extrinsic apoptosis pathway involves death receptors that relay apoptotic signals from the cell membrane to the intracellular components of the pathway [96]. CD44 serves as a pivotal marker for gastric cancer stem cells, playing an essential role in the management of self-renewal, tumorigenesis, and chemo resistance. Up regulation of miR-193a-3p was correlated with CDDP resistance in CD44 (+) gastric cancer cells by SRSF2 targeting. There were Bax, cytochrome C, and cleaved caspase 3/9 down regulations, whereas Bcl-2 up regulation in CD44(+) cells [97]. Myeloid cell leukemia 1 (MCL-1) belongs to Bcl-2 family which inhibits pro-apoptotic proteins [98, 99]. MCL-1 is frequently overexpressed and contributes to chemotherapy resistance in various cancers. Thus, MCL-1 is recognized as a significant target for cancer therapy, and its knockdown has been found to enhance the anti-tumor efficacy of chemotherapeutic agents, including cisplatin [100, 101]. MiR-519d has been shown to reduce CDDP resistance in breast cancer stem cells by MCL-1 targeting [102]. HCLS1-associated protein X-1 (HAX-1) is a protein located in the mitochondria that exhibits anti-apoptotic properties by inhibiting the accumulation of Bax, consequently blocking the mitochondrial apoptosis pathway [103, 104]. Evidence suggests that HAX-1 is overexpressed in multiple cancer types that prevent mitochondrial dysfunction and the subsequent release of apoptogenic factors, including cytochrome c, thereby shielding cancer cells from drug-induced apoptosis [105–107]. A reduced expression of miR-125a in laryngeal cancer stem cells was linked to acquired chemo resistance. MiR-125a facilitated cisplatin-induced apoptosis by HAX-1 targeting. The inhibition of HAX-1 via miR-125a re-sensitized laryngeal CSCs to cisplatin by triggering the mitochondrial apoptosis pathway [108].

Transcription factors

MicroRNAs play a pivotal role in mediating CDDP resistance in CSCs through the precise regulation of key transcription factors (figure). This intricate layer of post-transcriptional control ultimately modulates core cellular processes including proliferation, EMT, and drug efflux [109, 110], solidifying the resilient nature of CSCs and identifying miRNA-transcription factor axes as promising therapeutic targets for overcoming CDDP resistance. Sox2 plays a crucial role in the initial development and sustained maintenance of undifferentiated embryonic stem cells [111]. Deregulation of SOX2 is implicated in both the proliferation and metastasis of various cancers [112–115]. Additionally, SOX2 has been associated with the regulation of CSCs [116–118]. There was miR-2117 down regulation in liver CSCs that was correlated with a poor prognosis in patients with HCC. Inhibition of miR-2117 enhanced self-renewal and tumorigenic potentials in liver CSCs by SOX2 targeting. Notably, HCC cells with miR-2117 overexpression exhibited increased sensitivity to CDDP treatment [119]. SOX9 plays a significant role in cellular regulation and has been identified as an oncogene in various malignancies [120–122]. Emerging research indicates that SOX9 also participates in the regulation of liver CSCs [123]. SOX9 facilitates self-renewal and tumorigenic properties by promoting symmetrical cell division in cancer stem cells [122]. Overexpression of miR-613 in hepatoma cells reduced self-renewal capabilities of liver CSCs and tumorigenic potential. Additionally, HCC cells with knocked-down miR-613 exhibited reduced sensitivity to cisplatin therapies. MiR-613 suppressed the expansion of liver CSCs through the SOX9 targeting [124]. Yin Yang 1 (YY1) is a transcription factor with zinc finger domains, widely expressed across various cell types, and it is involved in a range of cellular processes including the cell growth, chromatin structure alteration, and programmed cell death [125–129]. YY1 influence several critical targets, such as ERBB2, p53, caspases, and histone deacetylases (HDACs), which are linked to the tumor progression [128]. MiR-186 reduced cisplatin resistance and development of the glioma initiating cell (GIC) phenotype by YY1 targeting. Elevated expression of miR-186 leads to a decrease in MDM2 protein levels [130]. HOXA9 is a homeodomain-containing transcription factor that has key role in stem cell expansion. Elevated levels of CDR1as significantly enhanced cellular proliferation, viability, and colony formation capabilities in NSCLC cells that are sensitive to CDDP when treated with high doses of CDDP. This effect was counteracted by the overexpression of miR-641 and down regulation of HOXA9, suggesting that the up regulation of CDR1as was contributed to the development of CDDP resistance in NSCLC cells through the modulation of miR-641/HOXA9 pathway [131]. Pou5f1 is a key transcription factor in stem cells that is involved in self-renewal. There was a significant down regulation of miR-335 in osteosarcoma stem cells. In addition, miR-335-expressing cells exhibited reduced stem cell-like properties, which can be partially attributed to the regulation of POU5F1 by miR-335. Furthermore, miR-335 enhanced CDDP sensitivity and inhibited tumor formation by specifically targeting osteosarcoma stem cells [132].

Extracellular matrix and EMT process

MicroRNAs play a pivotal role in mediating CDDP resistance in CSCs by orchestrating a multifaceted resistance program centered on the regulation of ECM and EMT process (Fig. 3). Specific miRNAs can directly target and suppress the expression of key ECM components and remodeling enzymes, thereby fostering a dense, fibrotic tumor microenvironment that acts as a physical barrier to drug penetration [133]. EMT is characterized by the alteration in the polarity of epithelial cell layers, leading to the disruption of intercellular connections and the degradation of epithelial adhesion mechanisms along with cytoskeletal elements [134, 135]. A notable feature of EMT is reduced E-cadherin expression levels paired with the increased expression of vimentin, snail, and other mesenchymal markers [136, 137]. EMT plays a crucial role in the invasive and metastatic behaviors of cancer cells. Tumor cells that have undergone EMT often display characteristics akin to CSCs, including self-renewal abilities and the potential to form tumor spheroids [138]. C-Myc is a pivotal transcription factor influencing numerous biological processes such as cell division, differentiation, and apoptosis. The up regulation of c-Myc has been implicated in the modulation of both EMT and CSC traits in breast cancer [139, 140]. It was demonstrated that c-Myc can repress miR-200c in nasopharyngeal cancer (NPC) cells. Conversely, miR-200c was found to inhibit c-Myc expression by binding to its 3′-untranslated region (3′-UTR), indicating the existence of a negative feedback loop. Notably, c-Myc compromised the activation of the EMT program and the sensitivity to chemotherapeutic agents. Moreover, miR-200 regulated the expression of ZEB1 and ZEB2. Inhibition of c-Myc or elevation of miR-200c led to the down regulation of BMI1, Suz12, and Sox2, which were recognized as key regulators of the CSC population in NPC cell lines [141]. ZEB2 serves as an indicator of the mesenchymal phenotype, which is significantly associated with stem cell characteristics. HOTTIP-miR-205-ZEB2 axis was contributed with CDDP resistance in ovarian cancer cells [142].

Stem cells are believed to reside within specialized microenvironments referred to as niches. The elements found within these niches can modulate stem cell behavior either by forming direct interactions with cell surface receptors or by activating paracrine signaling pathways indirectly. The ECM contains various components, including hyaluronic acid (HA), which has been identified in stem cell niche [143, 144]. HA is a glycosaminoglycan that is synthesized by designated HA synthases and is broken down into smaller molecules by various hyaluronidases [145]. HA is notably abundant in numerous tumor types and is enriched within stem cell niches, suggesting its involvement in the self-renewal and differentiation of normal human stem cells [143, 144]. All isoforms of CD44 possess an HA-binding site within their extracellular domains, making them primary receptors for HA [146]. The expression of CD44v3 is linked to the progression of HNSCC and is considered a significant cell surface marker for CSCs [147, 148]. Additionally, epigenetic modifications such as histone methylation have emerged as critical regulatory mechanisms that alter chromatin structure and influence gene expression during cancer progression [149]. DOT1L acts as a methyltransferase for H3K79, facilitating both the methylation of histone H3K79 and the activation of transcription [150]. Notably, DOT1L-mediated methylation at H3K79 plays a vital role in regulating transcriptional activities essential for cell cycle progression, meiotic checkpoints, and the DNA damage response [151]. HA enhanced DOT1L-mediated H3K79 methylation, which subsequently promoted the production of miR-10. This production was associated with tumor cell invasion and resistance to cisplatin in CSCs derived from HNSCC. HA/CD44-stimulated miR-10 production was crucial for the up regulation of the cytoskeletal regulator RhoC in CSC-like cells. Furthermore, miR-10b was involved in the expression of survival proteins, including c-IAP-1, c-IAP-2, and XIAP, contributing to the anti-apoptotic and survival capabilities of HNSCC cells, thereby enhancing resistance to cisplatin [152]. The mechanism by which IAPs suppress apoptosis seems to be rooted in the direct inhibition of caspases and pro-caspases [153]. Furthermore, IAPs contribute to chemo resistance by directly obstructing cell death in tumors triggered by anticancer therapies [154]. CD44 is recognized as a pivotal cell surface marker for stem cells [155, 156]. The overexpression of both CD44 and HA at tumor attachment sites indicates that the binding of HA to CD44 enhances the functions and progression of various tumor cells [157, 158]. Additionally, DNMT1 is crucial for establishing and modulating tissue-specific patterns of methylated cytosine residues and plays a role in global DNA demethylation [159]. It has been shown that the suppression of AOF1 and AOF2 leads to the degradation of DNMT1, causing global demethylation during the reprogramming of human hair follicle cells into induced pluripotent stem cells [160]. A study identified a distinct subgroup of highly tumorigenic HNSCC cells characterized by elevated levels of CD44v3 and ALDH1. HA-CD44v3-mediated pathway influenced the production of stem cell markers (e.g., Oct4, Sox2, and Nanog) associated with miR-302. The inhibition of miR-302 not only leads to an up regulation of AOF1/AOF2/DNMT1 but also reduced DNA demethylation, resulting in decreased expression of cIAP-1/cIAP-2 and XIAP that impaired self-renewal and diminished resistance to cisplatin [148]. Syndecan (SDC) proteoglycans are associated with cell proliferation, migration, and differentiation [161]. WDFY3-AS2 enhanced cell stemness and invasion, while inhibited apoptosis in OC CSCs, as indicated by the formation of tumorspheres, increased CD133-positive cell populations, and heightened expression of CSC markers. Additionally, WDFY3-AS2 acted as a sponge for miR-139-5p, thereby up regulating SDC4. WDFY3-AS2 modulated CDDP resistance and OC-CSCs via the regulation of the miR-139-5p/SDC4 axis [162].

Cellular metabolism, structural proteins, and autophagy

MicroRNAs play a pivotal role in mediating CDDP resistance in CSCs by orchestrating profound changes in both cellular metabolism and the expression of structural proteins (Fig. 3). By post-transcriptionally regulating key target genes, miRNAs can drive a metabolic rewiring towards a glycolytic phenotype or enhanced oxidative phosphorylation, thereby increasing energy production and antioxidant capacity to counteract CDDP-induced oxidative stress and DNA damage [163, 164].

Hexokinase 2 (HK2) functions as an enzyme that initiates glucose metabolism by converting glucose into glucose-6-phosphate [165]. Inhibition of HK2 decreased phosphorylation of AKT and mTOR, accompanied by an increase in p53 expression in cervical cancer (CC) cells [166]. Additionally, HK2 enhances the proliferation of CC cells and promotes tumorigenesis via modulation of the Raf/MEK/ERK pathway [167]. The silencing of HK2 leads to a reduction in the expression of OCT4 and SOX2, indicating that targeting HK2 may represent a promising therapeutic strategy to inhibit the cancer stem cell population within CC cells. Furthermore, miR-148a targeted HK2, which reduced sphere formation ability while induced the sensitivity to cisplatin in CC cells [168]. Modifications in lipid metabolism frequently occur within cancer cells, thereby facilitating tumor progression [169]. Lipids are fundamental components of the plasma membrane and the membranes surrounding most cellular organelles. Moreover, lipids function as energy reservoirs and as critical signaling entities, governing an array of cellular processes, such as cell migration, proliferation, and differentiation [170, 171]. Sterol regulatory element-binding proteins (SREBPs) are transcription factors associated with the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) that regulate the expression of genes essential for the biosynthesis and uptake of cholesterol, phospholipids, and fatty acids [172]. Prolonged exposure to cisplatin induced emergence of cisplatin-resistant NSCLC cells exhibiting cancer stem cell-like characteristics, which are marked by increased SREBP-1-mediated lipogenic activity. Furthermore, inhibition of SREBP-1 through fatostatin or via shRNA- or miRNA (specifically miR-497-5p)-mediated mechanisms enhanced the sensitivity of CDDP-resistant cells to cisplatin and diminished the stemness of lung cancer cells [173].

RAB6 is a transport protein that is crucial for the endocytosis and the transport of biosynthetic proteins [174]. Notably, Rab6 exhibits low GTPase activity and functions as a regulator of membrane trafficking, facilitating movement from the Golgi apparatus to the ER [175]. There was miR-5100 up regulation in lung CSCs that was positively correlated with the expression levels of Sox4 and Oct4, as well as with the quantity of spheres formed. MiR-5100 promoted a stem-like phenotype in lung cancer cells and was contributed to CDDP resistance by Rab6 targeting [176]. TMED is involved in Golgi kinetics and intracellular protein transport [177]. There was miR-876-3p down regulation in GC that was inversely correlated with cisplatin resistance and clinical outcomes. MiR-876-3p influenced cisplatin sensitivity and the stem cell-like properties of GC by targeting TMED3 [178].

F-box/WD repeat-containing protein 7 (FBXW7) is a key element of the ubiquitin ligase complex that is involved in the degradation of numerous oncoproteins through the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway. MiR-500a-3p was identified within exosomes as a contributor to CDDP resistance and the properties of CSCs in GC cells by down regulating FBXW7. There was miR-500a-3p up regulation in exosomes from CDDP-resistant GC cells, and the increased presence of miR-500a-3p in exosomes extracted from the plasma of stage III GC patients was correlated with CDDP-based resistance and progression of GC, suggesting its potential utility as a non-invasive biomarker for predicting chemotherapy outcomes in GC patients [179]. TRIM17 is an E3 ubiquitin ligase that has important roles in the regulation of apoptosis, autophagy, and cell proliferation [180, 181]. It has been observed that exosomes secreted by cisplatin-resistant cells and spheres carry miR-1246, which enhances the stem-like characteristics of parental lung cancer cells through the TRIM17 targeting [182].

Sirtuin 1 (SIRT1) utilizes NAD + as a cofactor to facilitate the deacetylation of both histone and non-histone proteins [183, 184]. Patients with lower levels of miR-132 had significantly improved survival rates. MiR-132 enhanced CDDP resistance in Lgr5 + GCSCs through targeting SIRT1. ABCG2 was also identified as one of the downstream targets of SIRT1, wherein silencing SIRT1 resulted in increased ABCG2 expression, likely due to enhanced acetylation of CREB [185]. There was significant miR-4461 down regulation in liver CSCs that was correlated with unfavorable prognosis in HCC patients. MiR-4461 enhanced sensitivity to cisplatin treatment in HCC cells by SIRT1 targeting [186].

MicroRNAs critically orchestrate the interplay between autophagy and CSC chemo resistance by modulating cisplatin sensitivity. Cisplatin treatment often suppresses tumor‑suppressive miRNAs that normally restrain autophagy, thereby enriching CSCs and driving drug resistance. For instance, cisplatin‑based chemotherapy significantly reduces miR-30a levels in esophageal and gastric cancer cells, and this down‑regulation is accompanied by elevated autophagic activity specifically in cells displaying CSC‑like properties [187, 188]. Restoration of miR‑30a expression attenuates Beclin-1 mediated autophagy, thereby re‑sensitizing the cells to cisplatin-induced apoptosis. Collectively, these findings illustrate a dual regulatory paradigm in which cisplatin-induced miRNA depletion unleashes an autophagy-dependent CSC phenotype, while therapeutic miRNA reconstitution suppresses autophagy and restores cisplatin efficacy.

Conclusions

Based on current research, the role of miRNAs in regulating the CDDP response of CSCs is pivotal, and their clinical application, while promising, faces significant challenges that clarify future research directions. MiRNAs are established as crucial post-transcriptional regulators of chemo sensitivity in CSCs, influencing CDDP response by regulation of signaling pathways (Wnt/β-catenin, NOTCH, TGF-β, and NF-kb), apoptosis, DNA damage repair, and EMT. Clinically, this knowledge opens avenues for using miRNAs for developing novel therapeutic strategies that involve administering tumor-suppressor miRNA mimics (e.g., miR-137, miR-129, and miR-567) or inhibiting oncomiRs (e.g., miR-21, miR-765, and miR-132) to re-sensitize CSCs to CDDP. Preclinical studies further demonstrate that restoring tumor-suppressive miRNAs with synthetic mimics or neutralizing oncogenic miRNAs with anti-miRs/antagomiRs can resensitize cisplatin-resistant CSCs to chemotherapy, underscoring their immense potential as novel therapeutic adjuvants. However, despite this robust biological rationale, the clinical translation of miRNA therapeutics for overcoming cisplatin resistance remains profoundly constrained by critical delivery barriers. Major limitations halt clinical translation, including the lack of universal CSC biomarkers across cancer types, the dynamic plasticity of CSCs, and significant delivery challenges for miRNA-based drugs such as poor cellular uptake, instability in circulation, and potential off-target effects. Addressing these delivery challenges is therefore an absolute prerequisite for advancing miRNA-based therapies toward clinical application in the context of cisplatin-resistant malignancies. In the specific context of CSCs, the delivery challenge is compounded by the need to penetrate the protective tumor niche, overcome the intrinsic drug-resistant phenotype of these cells, and achieve sustained modulation of miRNA targets without triggering systemic adverse events. Future work must therefore prioritize the development of targeted and efficient nano-delivery systems (e.g., using exosomes or chitosan nanoparticles) and combination therapies that co-target CSCs and bulk tumor cells to fundamentally overcome CDDP resistance and improve patient outcomes. Liposomes and ionizable lipid nanoparticles have been extensively engineered for nucleic acid delivery, capitalizing on their biocompatibility, scalability, and capacity for surface functionalization with targeting ligands. PEGylation and ligand-directed modifications enhance tumor specificity and reduce off-target distribution [189]. Exosomes and extracellular vesicles offer a biomimetic delivery paradigm with inherent advantages, including low immunogenicity, natural tumor-homing properties, and the ability to cross biological barriers. Engineered exosomes have shown considerable promise for overcoming cisplatin resistance in CSCs. Polymer-based nanoparticles (including PLGA, PEI, and chitosan derivatives) provide tunable physicochemical properties, stimuli-responsive release kinetics, and the capacity for co-delivery of multiple therapeutic payloads. Other emerging platforms include dendrimers, responsive hydrogels/nanogels, and inorganic nanoparticles (gold, silica, and iron oxide), each offering distinct advantages in terms of loading capacity, controlled release, and imaging compatibility. A readiness ranking based on translational maturity places functionalized LNPs at the forefront, followed by exosome/hybrid systems, with polymeric nanoparticles and dendrimers requiring further optimization [189].

The clinical translation of miRNA therapeutics has yielded valuable lessons that inform the development of combination approaches with cisplatin. The anti-miR-155 strategy in NSCLC provides a compelling paradigm for rational cisplatin combination. Preclinical translational modeling demonstrated that anti-miR-155 monotherapy achieved a median progression-free survival (PFS) of 6.7 months, comparable to standard-of-care agents. Notably, the two-drug combination of anti-miR-155 plus cisplatin yielded a median PFS of 11.3 months, while the three-drug regimen adding pembrolizumab extended PFS to 13.1 months, strongly support the clinical evaluation of miRNA-targeted agents as cisplatin sensitizers [190].

The convergence of miRNA biology, nanotechnology, and clinical oncology has created a fertile landscape for developing miRNA-directed strategies to overcome CSC-mediated cisplatin resistance. Several priorities emerge for advancing this field toward clinical reality. First, the identification and validation of CSC-specific miRNA signatures that robustly predict cisplatin response will enable patient stratification and personalized combination regimens. Second, continued refinement of nanocarrier platforms is essential to achieve tumor- and CSC-selective delivery while minimizing off-target effects and immune activation. Third, systematic evaluation of miRNA-cisplatin combinations in preclinical models that faithfully recapitulate CSC biology and the tumor microenvironment will be critical for optimizing dose ratios, sequencing, and toxicity profiles. Fourth, lessons from early clinical trials underscore the importance of rigorous safety assessment, standardized pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic monitoring, and scalable manufacturing processes to support regulatory approval. Looking forward, the integration of artificial intelligence and machine learning-guided formulation design, coupled with the emergence of exosome-mimetic and hybrid vesicle platforms promise to accelerate the translation of miRNA-based therapies. As the first wave of miRNA therapeutics matures through clinical development and the mechanistic understanding of miRNA-mediated cisplatin resistance in CSCs deepens, the prospect of combining miRNA modulators with cisplatin to eradicate chemo resistant CSC populations moves ever closer to clinical reality. The roadmap ahead demands interdisciplinary collaboration among molecular biologists, nanotechnologists, pharmacologists, and clinical oncologists to transform the promise of miRNA-based therapies into tangible benefits for patients facing cisplatin-resistant malignancies.

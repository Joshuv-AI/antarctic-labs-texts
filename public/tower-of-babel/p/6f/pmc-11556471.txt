Rare Presentation of Acinic Cell Carcinoma in the Parotid Gland
Authors: Rita Yadav, Preeti Agarwal, Mala Sagar, Malti Kumari Maurya
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Acinic cell carcinoma (ACC) arising in the salivary glands is a rare tumor. It is a low-grade malignant salivary gland tumor. It is predominantly seen in females and occurs in the fifth and sixth decades of life. It is mostly located in the parotid gland. ACC has a significant potential for recurrence and metastasis. Therefore, long-term follow-up is necessary after treatment. Here, a 28-year-old male presented with right preauricular swelling for 9 months. Ultrasound of the head-and-neck region and fine-needle aspiration cytology of preauricular swelling suggest the diagnosis of neoplasm in the parotid gland, most probably a benign tumor. After that, a total parotidectomy with facial nerve preservation was performed. On histopathological and immunohistochemical study was consistent with the diagnosis of ACC in the parotid gland.

INTRODUCTION

Acinic cell carcinoma (ACC) is a rare malignant salivary gland tumor representing about 2%–4% of the primary neoplasm of the parotid gland.[1] It is more commonly found in the females than the males.[2] ACCs usually occur in the fifth and sixth decades of life.[3] It is characterized by cytoplasmic zymogen secretory granules, which are depicted in the acinar cell serous differentiation.[4] There are no characteristic features of the ACC that occur in the ultrasound, computed tomography scan, and magnetic resonance imaging.[5] Usually, fine-needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) and biopsy are used to confirm the diagnosis.[6] Special stains such as periodic acid–Schiff after diastase (PAS-D) and immunohistochemistry (IHC) support the diagnosis of ACC.[7] Surgical excision is the main treatment strategy.[8] After the patient’s treatment, follow-up is necessary because ACC tends to recur and metastasis.[9]

CASE REPORT

Clinical finding

A 28-year-old male was presented with right preauricular, painless swelling for 9 months. The swelling was gradually increasing in size. On examination, superficial swelling was found, which measured 4.8 cm × 4.0 cm in size, firm in consistency, mobile, smooth surface, regular border, and nontender. There was no sign of facial nerve deficit. He did not have any family history of cancer or history of radiation exposure.

Management

The patient was advised for head-and-neck region ultrasound and FNAC of the preauricular swelling. USG showed a heterogeneous, hypoechoic mass with well-defined border on the right side of the superficial lobe of the parotid gland. These radiological findings suggest the diagnosis of a benign tumor in the parotid gland.

A FNAC was performed in the preauricular or parotid swelling. According to the Milan system for reporting salivary gland cytology, the FNAC of the parotid gland was categorized as IV – neoplasm. After that, a superficial parotidectomy with facial nerve preservation was performed under general anesthesia. Then, the parotidectomy specimen was sent to the pathology department for histopathological examination.

Histopathology

Grossly, a parotidectomy specimen was received as a multiple nodular mass, measuring 6.2 cm × 4.3 cm × 2.0 cm. It cuts firm. The cut surface showed multiple small nodular solid yellowish-to-whitish areas [Figure 1a]. Microscopic examination of hematoxylin and eosin stained section showed malignant tumor islands that composed of an admixture of different types of malignant tumor cells, including malignant acinar cells, clear/vacuolated cells, intercalated duct-like cells, and nonspecific glandular cells [Figure 1b]. These tumor cells are arranged in solid and microcystic patterns that infiltrate into the sclerotic stroma. Malignant acinar cells are large polygonal cells with abundant basophilic granular cytoplasm and eccentrically placed small hyperchromatic nuclei [Figure 2a-d]. Intercalated duct-like cells are cuboidal cells with amphophilic cytoplasm with centrally placed dark nuclei. Nonspecific glandular cells have vesicular nuclei with eosinophilic cytoplasm. Sheets of malignant tumor cells are also traversed by very delicate blood vessels. There is no evidence of necrosis. The morphological diagnosis was hence, as the differential diagnosis of ACC and secretory carcinoma (SC). After that, we performed a special stain PAS-D, which showed abundant PAS-positive diastase-resistant cytoplasmic granules in the malignant acinar cells [Figure 3a and b]. On further IHC, DOG-1 was positive with cytoplasmic expression in tumor cells, while mammaglobin was not expressed in tumor cells [Figure 3c and d]. This special stain and IHC almost confirmed the diagnosis of ACC in the parotid gland. Currently, the patient has remained disease-free with no signs of recurrence.

DISCUSSION

ACC is an uncommon malignant salivary gland tumor that arises from the acinar epithelium, which shows serous differentiation resembling normal acini of salivary glands. It usually presents as a low-grade malignant tumor with a favorable prognosis.[4] It was initially classified as an “acinic cell adenoma;” but later, when its malignant potential was discovered, it was classified as ACC with low-grade behavior.[8] It is the third-most common malignant salivary gland tumor, following mucoepidermoid carcinoma and adenoid cystic carcinomas.[2] ACC most commonly occurs in the parotid gland, about 80%; however, it also occurs in minor intraoral salivary glands, approximately 13%–17%.[5] In most of the literature, the female-to-male ratio is about 1.5:1.[12] Most of the studies have shown that the females (58.8%) are more commonly involved than the males (41.2%).[13] On extensive search of literature, we found only one study of Ellis and Corio, which suggested that 155 of 294 patients were male (53%).[9] Most of the studies have shown that this tumor usually occurs in the fifth and sixth decades of life.[1235] According to Ellis and Corio’s study, the peak incidence of ACC occurs in the third decade of life.[9] In our case, there is a rare presentation of ACC in the parotid gland, which means that the patient was a 28-year-old male who was diagnosed with ACC in the parotid gland. Hence, in our case, the demographic profile almost corresponds to Ellis and Corio’s study. Risk factors for ACC are previous radiation exposure, familial predisposition, and endogenous female hormones.[123] Patients with ACC typically present with a painless swelling of the salivary glands.[68] Sign of facial nerve paralysis is only seen in 3%–8% of patients.[3] In this case, the patient presented with painless swelling in the parotid gland along with no signs of facial paralysis. Histopathologically, ACC is well circumscribed with a distinct capsule, and the cut surface may appear as solid and cystic. Microscopically, they were presented in various morphological patterns, and various types of cells present. Morphological pattern includes solid pattern, microcystic, papillary cystic, and follicular pattern. Mostly, there is only one pattern seen in a single lesion, but the combination of two or three patterns may be found.[58] According to Rosero et al., the solid pattern is the most common pattern, followed by a microcystic pattern.[1] Various types of cells include acinar cells, intercalated ductal, vacuolated, clear, and nonspecific glandular cells. The acinar cell is the main histological differentiation of ACC.[6] The present case showed a classical solid pattern with cells arranged in large sheets in an organoid pattern separated by thin fibrovascular connective tissue septa, and acinar cells are the predominant cell types present. ACCs are difficult to diagnose due to their great radiological and cytological similarities with benign tumors and normal acinar components of the salivary gland.[8] Histologically, ACC may mimic other salivary gland tumors. Intense DOG1 expression, accompanied by the periodic acid–Schiff (PAS) reaction after diastase treatment, helps differentiate ACCs from morphologic mimics.[6] New entity SC is the most important morphologic mimic of ACC. The characteristic zymogen granules of ACC are positive for PAS and diastase resistant; however, the extracellular secretory material of SC is also PAS positive and diastase resistant. DOG-1 staining demonstrated weak-to-moderate apical/complete membranous and cytoplasmic staining with variable distribution.[8] DOG1 may be the most useful one, however, it is not specific as well, owing to its expression in other salivary gland tumors including adenoid cystic carcinoma, epithelial–myoepithelial carcinoma, salivary duct carcinoma, mucoepidermoid carcinoma, and even some SC.[10] In our case, ACC is positive for PAS and diastase resistant and positive cytoplasmic expression of DOG-1, while negative staining for mammaglobin differentiates it from SC. Nuclear receptor subfamily 4 group A member 3 and LIM Domain Only 2 immunostaining has been used as a novel diagnostic marker for ACC.[710] Superficial parotidectomy with facial nerve preservation is the standard diagnostic procedure. If the carcinoma is advanced or high grade, total parotidectomy with facial nerve dissection was done.[69] Long-term follow-up is necessary to predict recurrence and metastasis.[5]

CONCLUSION

We report a rare presentation of ACC arising in the parotid gland in a 28-year-old male. Special stains (PAS-D) and IHC DOG-1 may be helpful in the diagnosis of ACC. It is a low-grade malignant tumor with a good prognosis.

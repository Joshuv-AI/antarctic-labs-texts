Phase II study of trastuzumab-pkrb plus gedatolisib in patients with HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer who progressed after two or more HER2-directed therapies
Authors: JW Kim, YH Park, S Lee, HK Ahn, J Bae, S Park, H Chae, JH Kim, K-H Lee, MH Kim, KE Lee, MJ Kang, IH Park, Ys Chae, GW Lee, KU Park, EM Lee, JH Park, JE Kim, HJ Kim, TH Kim, SY Oh, S Ko, JH Byun, JY Ha, JH Kwon, S Lee, JY Choi, JY Jung, KH Park
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo evaluate the activity and safety of trastuzumab-pkrb plus gedatolisib in patients with treatment-refractory human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive metastatic breast cancer (MBC) harboring phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) pathway alterations.Patients and methodsIn this multicenter, single-arm phase II study, eligible patients had HER2-positive MBC with PI3K pathway aberrations identified by tumor or circulating cell-free DNA and had progressed after at least two prior HER2-directed therapies. Patients received trastuzumab-pkrb (8 mg/kg loading dose, then 6 mg/kg IV every 3 weeks) plus gedatolisib (180 mg IV on days 1, 8 and 15 of each 21-day cycle). The primary endpoint was objective response rate (ORR), 90% power with one-sided 5% type I error, assuming a 25% target for the combination therapy. The trial closed early after 44 patients due to changes in regulatory sponsorship.ResultsPI3K pathway alterations included PIK3CA kinase domain mutations (59%), helical domain mutations (25%), and PTEN deletions (5%). The ORR was 43.2% [95% confidence interval (CI) 28.3%-59.0%], with two complete and 17 partial responses. Disease control rate was 86.4%. Median progression-free survival (PFS) was 6.01 months (95% CI 5.03-7.69), and median overall survival (OS) was 24.74 months (95% CI 17.28-NA) after 32.05 months of follow-up. Oral mucositis (90.9%; 15.9% grade ≥3) and hyperglycemia (25.0%; 2.3% grade 3) were most frequent, and no treatment-related deaths occurred. Early circulating tumor DNA dynamics predicted PFS and OS.ConclusionTrastuzumab-pkrb plus gedatolisib demonstrated a 43.2% response rate and manageable toxicity, supporting dual HER2/PI3K pathway targeting in heavily pretreated HER2-positive MBC.Key words: breast cancer, clinical trial results, drug resistance, precision medicine

Introduction

Historically associated with poor prognosis, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive breast cancer is characterized by its aggressive nature and propensity for early metastasis.1 The prognosis of patients with HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer (MBC) has significantly improved with HER2-targeted therapies.2,3 The landscape continues to evolve with the development of new agents, including new generation HER2-ADCs and small molecule inhibitors.4,5 Despite these advances, the development of treatment resistance remains a major challenge.6 Patients who progress after multiple lines of HER2-directed therapy represent a population with limited therapeutic options and poor survival outcomes, highlighting a significant urgent unmet clinical need.

Among the known mechanisms of resistance to HER2-directed therapies, overactivation of the phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) pathway is a key driver.7 This pathway plays a critical role in cell proliferation, survival, and metabolism, and its aberrant activation can bypass the inhibitory effects of HER2 blockade.7 PI3K pathway alterations, including PIK3CA mutations and PTEN loss, are prevalent in 30.4%-51.0% of HER2-positive breast cancers and have been associated with inferior clinical outcomes.8, 9, 10 Although PI3K pathway inhibitors such as alpelisib and capivasertib have demonstrated clinical efficacy in estrogen receptor-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer,11,12 clinical evidence supporting their use in HER2-positive disease remains limited, underscoring the need for therapeutic approaches capable of overcoming PI3K-mediated resistance to HER2-targeted therapy.

Gedatolisib—a pan-PI3K/protein kinase B (AKT)/mTOR inhibitor13—showed favorable activity in HR+/HER2− breast cancer in the phase III VIKTORIA-1 study,14 providing additional support for targeting the PI3K pathway in solid tumors with pathway activation. By inhibiting both PI3K and mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR), we hypothesized that gedatolisib may enhance suppression of downstream signaling relevant to HER2-driven tumor growth, offering a mechanistic rationale for dual HER2 and PI3K/mTOR blockade.

Based on these observations, we hypothesized that dual blockade of HER2 and PI3K/mTORC1/2 signaling with trastuzumab-pkrb plus gedatolisib would overcome acquired resistance and improve clinical outcomes in patients with heavily pretreated, PI3K pathway-dependent HER2-positive MBC.

Methods

Study design and patients

This was a multicenter, single-arm phase II study (KM-10A/KCSG-BR18-13) conducted by the Korean Cancer Study Group. Eligible patients were aged 19 years or older with histologically confirmed HER2-positive metastatic or unresectable breast cancer. HER2 positivity was defined as immunohistochemistry (IHC) 3+ or IHC 2+ with a positive in situ hybridization result (either silver or chromogenic), according to the American Society of Clinical Oncology/College of American Pathologists guidelines.15 Additional eligibility criteria included an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0-2, a left ventricular ejection fraction ≥50%, and adequate organ function. Patients were required to have received at least two prior HER2-directed chemotherapies for the treatment of metastatic disease. Endocrine therapy was not administered, as these heavily pretreated patients were considered endocrine-refractory, and because combining endocrine therapy with HER2-directed treatment for HER2-positive MBC is not an approved indication in Korea.

Patients were eligible if their tumors were suspected to be PI3K pathway-dependent [defined by the presence of a PIK3CA hotspot mutation, PIK3CA copy number (CN) gain, mTOR or AKT hotspot mutation, AKT2 amplification, or PTEN loss in tumor tissue or blood by next-generation sequencing (NGS)]. All candidate alterations were centrally reviewed and confirmed before enrollment. For patients with diabetes, adequate glucose control was required [hemoglobin A1c <7% and fasting blood glucose ≤126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L)]. Additional criteria included treatment-related toxicities of ≤grade 2 [per National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE v5.0)] and measurable disease (per RECIST v1.1). We excluded patients with symptomatic brain metastases or serious medical conditions (e.g. heart failure, uncontrolled diabetes, or active infection).

The primary endpoint was objective response rate (ORR). The secondary endpoints included progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), safety, and quality of life (QoL). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines and was approved by the institutional review boards at each site. Written informed consent was obtained from all the participants.

Treatment and assessment

Patients received trastuzumab-pkrb (Herzuma®, Celltrion, Inc, Incheon, Republic of Korea) as a loading dose of 8 mg/kg on cycle 1, day 1, followed by a maintenance dose of 6 mg/kg every 3 weeks. Gedatolisib was administered at a dose of 180 mg on days 1, 8, and 15 of each cycle. Treatment was continued until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or patient withdrawal.

Tumors were assessed per RECIST v1.1 at screening, every 6 weeks for the first 3 months (from cycle 1, day 1), and every 9 weeks thereafter until disease progression. Adverse events (AEs) were recorded throughout treatment using the NCI-CTCAE v5.0. QoL was assessed using the European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) QLQ-C30 and QLQ-BR23 (Korean versions) before treatment initiation (baseline), before the third cycle (after two cycles of therapy), and at the end of treatment (EOT), defined as the time point when treatment discontinuation was determined. Scoring followed the EORTC manuals, and changes in domain scores were analyzed across time points to evaluate longitudinal trends.

Cycle and day designations [e.g. cycle 1, day 1 (C1D1); cycle 2, day 1 (C2D1); cycle 3, day 1 (C3D1)] are abbreviated as CxDy throughout the manuscript.

Biomarker analysis

Circulating cell-free DNA (cfDNA) dynamics were monitored using whole blood samples (20 mL; 10 mL per Cell-Free DNA BCT® tube, Streck, La Vista, NE) at time points of predose on cycle 1, day 1; cycle 2, day 15 (postdose, within 72 h); cycle 3, day 1; day 1 of every other cycle (e.g. cycles 5, 7); and at the EOT. Plasma samples were analyzed using the AlphaLiquid®100 assay (IMBdx, Seoul, Republic of Korea), a targeted cfDNA sequencing panel covering 118 cancer-associated genes. Libraries were barcoded and sequenced at a median depth of approximately 50 000×. Variant calling was conducted using the IMBdx proprietary pipeline, which employs a unique molecular identifier-based error correction and background modeling. We excluded patients with clonal hematopoiesis of intermediate potential-associated mutations, variants of uncertain significance, and low-variant allele frequency (VAF) events in hematopoietic genes. Circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) positivity was defined as the presence of one or more somatic variants in the plasma, previously confirmed in matched tumor tissues, with a VAF threshold of 0.02%. CN alterations were identified from the normalized molecular depth and were confirmed by single-nucleotide polymorphism allelic imbalances. CN >2.2 with P < 0.001 was considered significant. Gains were classified as CN 2.2-4 and CN ≥4 as amplifications. All procedures were carried out according to a validated clinical protocol.16

Statistical analysis

In this study, we tested the null hypothesis—the true response rate is 10%—against an alternative hypothesis of 25%. The null hypothesis would be rejected if at least 10 responses were observed among 55 patients, with a one-sided type I error of 5%. Assuming a 10% dropout rate, 62 patients were scheduled for enrollment. However, the study was prematurely terminated because of factors related to changes in the investigational medicinal product dossier (IMPD) regulatory sponsorship, resulting in 44 patients being enrolled. Under the same statistical assumptions, a binomial power analysis estimated the achieved power to be approximately 75%-82% (about 80% on average).

ORR was defined as the proportion of patients with complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) per RECIST v1.1 criteria. PFS and OS were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. Safety analysis included all patients who received at least one dose of the study treatment. QoL was assessed using the EORTC QLQ-C30 and QLQ-BR23 according to their respective manuals. Summary statistics were generated for the absolute scores and changes from baseline. Only patients with a baseline assessment and at least one postbaseline assessment were included in the QoL analysis. Statistical analyses were carried out using RStudio (version 4.3.3; Posit Software, PBC, Boston, MA) . The study was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov no. NCT03698383 (KM-10A/KCSG BR18-13).

Results

Patient characteristics

Between December 2019 and March 2024, 48 patients were screened (Figure 1). Of the 48 patients screened, 44 were included in the analysis, and their clinical characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The median patient age was 59 years (range 28-72). Hormone receptor-positive disease was observed in 66% of patients. Half of the cohort (50%) had received four or more prior anti-HER2 therapies in the metastatic setting, and 86% had received three or more prior lines. Prior palliative treatments included trastuzumab emtansine in 95%, docetaxel plus trastuzumab and pertuzumab in 66%, lapatinib plus capecitabine in 61%, trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) in 9%, in 27 adriamycin + cyclophosphamide %, and other regimens in 18% of the patients. Regarding PI3K pathway alterations, 26 patients (59%) harbored PIK3CA kinase domain mutations, 11 (25%) harbored helical domain mutations, and 2 (5%) harbored PTEN deletions.

Efficacy analysis

Among the 44 patients in the intention-to-treat population, two achieved CR and 17 achieved PR, resulting in an ORR of 43.2% [95% confidence interval (CI) 28.3%-59.0%; Figure 2A]; in the evaluable population, the ORR was 44.2% (95% CI 29.1%-60.1%). Including 19 patients with stable disease, the disease control rate (DCR) was 86.4% (95% CI 72.6%-94.8%). According to RECIST v1.1, five patients were classified as having progressive disease (PD) due to the appearance of new lesions (three in the liver and two in the brain) despite shrinkage of target lesions, and four patients were classified as having PD due to unequivocal progression of nontarget lesions, consistent with RECIST v1.1 criteria (Figure 2B). The median duration of response was 4.7 months (95% CI 3.98-7.42), as illustrated in the swimmer plot (Figure 2C).

At the time of data cutoff, the median follow-up duration was 32.05 months (IQR 23.0-53.5 months). The median PFS was 6.01 months (95% CI 5.03-7.69), with a 6- and 12-month PFS rate of 50.3% and 7.6%, respectively (Figure 2D). The median OS was 24.74 months (95% CI 17.28-NA), and the 6- and 12-month OS rates were 90.7% and 76.7%, respectively (Figure 2E).

Biomarker analysis

We carried out paired tissue and pretreatment liquid biopsy NGS analyses on 41 patients. In the concordance analysis, ERBB2 copy number variations (CNVs, gain, or amplification) were detected in the tissue and plasma of 16 patients (40%) (Figure 3A). Additionally, 18 patients had amplification detected in the tissue only, whereas six patients had no detectable alteration in both compartments. In 29 patients, PIK3CA mutations were detected concordantly in tissue and plasma (71%), with seven detected in the tissue only and three in the plasma only. Notably, an increased baseline plasma tumor fraction [maximum somatic allele frequency (MSAF) ≥10%] was associated with increased concordance between plasma and tissue ERBB2 CNV status. Among patients with MSAF at least 10%, the concordance rate was 60% compared with 48% in those with MSAF less than 10% (Figure 3B). Furthermore, in patients with high MSAF, plasma ERBB2 CN alterations showed a stronger correlation with HER2 IHC and FISH/SISH results, suggesting that the tumor fraction significantly influenced the detectability of CN alterations in liquid biopsy.

Among patients who achieved objective responses (PR and CR), variant allele frequencies of all mutations, including PIK3CA and TP53, were lowest at C3D1, but subsequently increased by the EOT (Figure 3C). In this analysis, ctDNA positivity was defined as the presence of one or more somatic variants in plasma confirmed in matched tumor tissue, with a VAF ≥0.02%. Notably, patients with undetectable ctDNA at C3D1 showed superior survival outcomes compared with those with detectable ctDNA (Figure 3D and E). In the detectable versus undetectable ctDNA groups, the median PFS was 5.4 versus 9.2 months [hazard ratio (HR) 2.75, 95% CI 1.33-5.7, P = 0.0063], and median OS was 16.3 versus 27 months (HR 4.67, 95% CI 1.62-13.44, P = 0.0042), respectively.

Safety and QoL

The safety outcomes are summarized in Table 2. AEs were observed in 95.5% of patients, with 22.7% experiencing grade ≥3 events. Dose adjustments of gedatolisib were required in nine patients (20.5%) owing to AEs. The most frequently reported AE was oral mucositis, occurring in 90.9% of patients, including 15.9% with grade ≥3 severity. Other common AEs included nausea (40.9%) and skin reactions (38.6%). Hyperglycemia was reported in 11 patients (25.0%), and one case (2.3%) was classified as grade 3.

Regarding QoL, 38 patients (86.4%) completed the QoL questionnaires at screening, C2D1, and EOT. In the EORTC QLQ-C30, the global health status (GHS) score increased slightly from 52.0 at screening to 53.8 at C2D1, indicating a modest improvement in overall QoL. However, it decreased to 48.0 at EOT (median 6.17, 95% CI 5.02-8.21) (Supplementary Figure S1A, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107706). On the EORTC QLQ-BR30, the functional scale score increased from 73.9 at screening to 77.5 at C2D1, suggesting improved functioning, whereas the symptom scale score decreased from 22.9 to 15.8, indicating a reduction in symptom burden (Supplementary Figure S1B, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107706).

Real-world outcomes with subsequent T-DXd therapy

Our study was initiated before the widespread clinical availability and regulatory approval of T-DXd. To provide context regarding subsequent treatment, we retrospectively collected data on patients who received T-DXd after progression. Among the 44 patients in the primary analysis, 4 (9.1%) had received T-DXd before study entry, and 25 (56.8%) received it after discontinuation of the study regimen. At the April 2025 cutoff, nine patients were still on T-DXd.

In an exploratory post hoc analysis of the 25 patients treated with T-DXd after progression, the median PFS was 7.03 months, comparable to 7.23 months with trastuzumab-pkrb plus gedatolisib. Among 11 patients who had completed T-DXd by the cutoff due to disease progression, the median PFS was 6.54 months versus 9.13 months with the study regimen. In the four patients who received T-DXd before study treatment, the median PFS was 4.27 months with T-DXd and 3.89 months with the study regimen. Further details on treatment sequences and PFS outcomes are presented in Supplementary Figure S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107706.

Discussion

In this study, we investigated the efficacy and safety of combining trastuzumab-pkrb with gedatolisib, a pan-PI3K/mTORC1/2 inhibitor, in patients heavily pretreated with HER2-positive metastatic or unresectable breast cancer. The combination showed a promising ORR of 43.2% and a DCR of 86.4%, with a mean PFS and OS of 6.01 and 24.74 months, respectively. These results are particularly encouraging given that most patients had received at least two prior lines of anti-HER2 therapy (median = 4), and responses were achieved without additional cytotoxic chemotherapy.

The clinical relevance of targeting the PI3K pathway in refractory MBC is being increasingly recognized. PI3K inhibitors such as alpelisib (a PI3Kα inhibitor), capivasertib (an AKT inhibitor), and inavolisib (a potent and selective PI3Kα inhibitor) in combination with endocrine therapy have demonstrated clinical efficacy in patients with PIK3CA-mutated hormone receptor-positive/HER2-negative disease,11,12,17 further underscoring the importance of PI3K pathway targeting. In contrast, despite strong preclinical18, 19, 20 and clinical evidence8,21, 22, 23, 24 supporting the role of PI3K pathway activation in resistance to HER2-targeted therapies, only a few targeted agents have demonstrated clinical benefit. The BOLERO-3 trial is one such example, where the mTOR inhibitor everolimus plus trastuzumab and vinorelbine improved PFS in trastuzumab-resistant HER2-positive advanced breast cancer.25

Recently, encouraged by the development of next-generation PI3K–AKT pathway inhibitors, two randomized phase III trials evaluating alpelisib (NCT04208178) or inavolisib (NCT05894239) in combination with trastuzumab and pertuzumab as maintenance therapy for HER2-positive MBC with PIK3CA mutations were initiated. Although the EPIK-B2 trial,26 which tested alpelisib with dual HER2 blockade, was recently halted due to administrative reasons rather than efficacy concerns, the inavolisib study remains ongoing and is expected to further clarify the therapeutic potential of PI3K pathway inhibition in this setting. In this evolving context, our findings extend the concept that simultaneous inhibition of HER2 and the PI3K/mTOR pathway can achieve clinically meaningful responses without chemotherapy, suggesting a potential shift toward targeted, chemotherapy-free regimens for selected patients with PI3K pathway activation.

The patients enrolled in this study were heavily pretreated individuals, reflecting real-world clinical challenges. The current global standard of care involves continuation of trastuzumab combined with sequential cytotoxic chemotherapy until no further options remain. Remarkably, we achieved encouraging ORR and DCR without cytotoxic agents, underscoring the potential of our regimen. From a toxicity perspective, AEs are generally low-grade and manageable in most patients. The most common AE was stomatitis, which was observed in 90.9% of the patients but was managed by a steroid gargle thereafter. Hyperglycemia, a common class effect of PI3K pathway inhibitors, was observed in 25% of patients; however, G3 hyperglycemia was found in only one patient. Overall, AEs were manageable with dose reductions of gedatolisib in 20.5% of patients. Consistent with previous studies on palliative treatments for HER2-positive MBC,27 our study also showed the preservation of GHS and QoL. We observed a modest early improvement in the GHS, followed by a decline at the EOT, suggesting that overall well-being was maintained during active therapy.

Biomarker analysis, particularly of cfDNA, adds a significant translational aspect to our findings. In detecting aberrant genes in the PI3K pathway, we observed 71% concordance between NGS genomic profiling using tumor and liquid biopsy samples. There were four patients in whom mutant genes were detected only by liquid biopsy, reinforcing the utility of cfDNA for comprehensive molecular profiling. Additionally, patients with undetectable ctDNAs at C3D1 demonstrated significantly superior survival outcomes, underscoring the prognostic power of early molecular responses, suggesting that early ctDNA assessment could be a valuable tool for monitoring treatment response and predicting prognosis, potentially guiding future adaptive treatment strategies. This observation supports the potential of early ctDNA dynamics as a biomarker to guide future adaptive treatment strategies.

T-DXd introduction has reshaped later-line therapeutic options for HER2-positive MBC, demonstrating superior efficacy over previous standards.4 As this study was conducted before T-DXd became available in Korea, only four patients received T-DXd before the study treatment. The median PFS of these patients was 4.27 months with T-DXd and 3.89 months with the study regimen. As poststudy treatment, T-DXd was administered to 25 patients, yielding a median PFS of 7.03 months, comparable to the 7.23 months achieved with the KM-10A study regimen. The observed PFS outcomes for T-DXd, both before and after the study regimen, indicate that effective subsequent therapy with T-DXd may have contributed to improved OS outcomes by providing additional disease control after progression. Although this observation was based on a relatively small sample size, the comparable median PFS between trastuzumab-pkrb plus gedatolisib and T-DXd supports the potential utility of our regimen as a chemotherapy-free option in treatment sequencing, particularly in patients with PI3K pathway alterations. Future studies should explore the optimal integration of PI3K/mTOR inhibitors with emerging agents, such as T-DXd.

Despite these promising findings, this study has certain limitations. Although the study was designed as a single-arm phase II trial, we acknowledge that this design inherently limits direct comparison and may introduce selection bias. These factors should be considered when interpreting the efficacy results. The early termination of the study owing to IMPD issues prevented us from reaching the planned sample size, resulting in a reduced statistical power (approximately 75%-82%) and limited generalizability of our findings. The termination also posed challenges to patient recruitment, which was particularly exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic during the study period. In addition, the evolving treatment landscape, including the introduction of T-DXd during the study period, complicates the interpretation of survival outcomes for subsequent therapies. Larger randomized studies are warranted to further validate these findings and to position this combination therapy within the evolving treatment paradigm for HER2-positive breast cancer.

In summary, in this phase II study, we demonstrated that combining trastuzumab-pkrb with gedatolisib achieved clinically meaningful antitumor activity and a favorable safety profile in patients with heavily pretreated HER2-positive MBC with confirmed PI3K pathway dependence. Translational findings, including the prognostic utility of early ctDNA dynamics, further highlight the potential of PI3K pathway inhibition as a strategy to overcome resistance in this setting. Collectively, these results contribute to the growing evidence that dual HER2 and PI3K/mTOR inhibition represents a rational, chemotherapy-free therapeutic approach, warranting further evaluation in future randomized trials.

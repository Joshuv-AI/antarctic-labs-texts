Multiresolution cascaded attention U-Net for localization and segmentation of optic disc and fovea in fundus images
Authors: R Shalini, Varun P Gopi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIdentification of retinal diseases in automated screening methods, such as those used in clinical settings or computer-aided diagnosis, usually depends on the localization and segmentation of the Optic Disc (OD) and fovea. However, this task is difficult since these anatomical features have irregular spatial, texture, and shape characteristics, limited sample sizes, and domain shifts due to different data distributions across datasets. This study proposes a novel Multiresolution Cascaded Attention U-Net (MCAU-Net) model that addresses these problems by optimally balancing receptive field size and computational efficiency. The MCAU-Net utilizes two skip connections to accurately localize and segment the OD and fovea in fundus images. We incorporated a Multiresolution Wavelet Pooling Module (MWPM) into the CNN at each stage of U-Net input to compensate for spatial information loss. Additionally, we integrated a cascaded connection of the spatial and channel attentions as a skip connection in MCAU-Net to concentrate precisely on the target object and improve model convergence for segmenting and localizing OD and fovea centers. The proposed model has a low parameter count of 0.8 million, improving computational efficiency and reducing the risk of overfitting. For OD segmentation, the MCAU-Net achieves high IoU values of 0.9771, 0.945, and 0.946 for the DRISHTI-GS, DRIONS-DB, and IDRiD datasets, respectively, outperforming previous results for all three datasets. For the IDRiD dataset, the MCAU-Net locates the OD center with an Euclidean Distance (ED) of 16.90 pixels and the fovea center with an ED of 33.45 pixels, demonstrating its effectiveness in overcoming the common limitations of state-of-the-art methods.Keywords: Deep learning, Optic disc, Fovea, Multiresolution, Attention module, Wavelet transform, Heatmap regression, Ablation studySubject terms: Diseases, Medical research, Engineering

Introduction

In ophthalmology, the fundus image of the retina serves as a critical diagnostic tool for various eye diseases, including glaucoma, age-related macular degeneration, and diabetic retinopathy (DR). DR, a severe complication of diabetes that affects the vasculature of the eye, impacts approximately one-third of individuals with diabetes. A statistical study conducted in 2017 projected a significant increase in the global population of individuals with diabetes, reaching 693.0 million by 2045, up from 451.0 million1. The timely detection and effective management of DR have become increasingly crucial for preserving vision and preventing further complications2. Within the retina, the optic disc (OD) and fovea are vital landmarks providing essential information about eye health3. The OD is a reference point for identifying other retinal structures and features a distinctive bright, yellowish, ellipse-shaped structure with blood vessels. In contrast, the macula, a yellow oval region of the retina, contains the fovea located at the center of the macula, temporal to the OD. The fovea plays a pivotal role in ensuring accurate central vision4. Many researchers have focused on OD and OC segmentation for glaucoma diagnosis, given the importance of the cup-to-disc ratio in assessing glaucoma risk. However, the segmentation of the OD and localization of the fovea are also critical for diagnosing and managing DR. Accurate identification of these landmarks is essential for various automated screening methods and computer-aided diagnosis systems. For instance, OD segmentation helps detect exudates, which are common in DR and can obscure the OD, thus affecting the diagnosis and progression monitoring of the disease5. Similarly, fovea localization is crucial as it helps assess macular edema, a severe complication of DR that affects central vision6.

Traditionally, ophthalmologists performed manual segmentation and localization of the OD and fovea, which proved time-consuming and prone to inter- and intra-observer variability7. However, the advent of deep learning has enabled the development of automated algorithms to achieve precise and reliable segmentation and localization of these structures. Despite the potential benefits of automation, there are several challenges to address. The OD and fovea may not always be distinguishable in images due to their low contrast relative to the background. These structures can exhibit visual similarities to neighboring structures, causing potential confusion during segmentation8. Furthermore, dust particles, flash artifacts, diffused bright areas, lubrication on the camera lens, and reflections in retinal fundus images can interfere with accurate segmentation and localization9. The limited availability of manually annotated images restricts the robustness of supervised learning systems. The presence of domain shift, caused by variations in image appearance due to different acquisition devices and modes, poses a significant challenge for deep-learning-based OD segmentation methods, leading to limited generalization ability10,11. Another challenge is the spatial loss generated by the pooling layer utilized by CNN, which can negatively impact the subsequent model processing. The researchers have explored different techniques to increase the receptive field of the neural network. These techniques include increasing the depth of the network, enlarging the filter size, or using pooling operations. However, these methods may result in higher computational costs or data loss, highlighting the need to balance the receptive field, computational efficiency, and accuracy.

This research presents a novel Multiresolution Cascaded Attention U-Net (MCAU-Net) for the accurate localization and segmentation of the OD and fovea in fundus images. MCAU-Net incorporates two crucial components of a multiresolution attention convolutional neural network:

A Multiresolution Wavelet Pooling Module (MWPM) on the input side to combine input image data from various resolutions and recover lost spatial information during pooling in the encoder, thereby improving the network input characteristics;

Related work

This section overviews previous approaches for segmenting and localizing the OD and fovea centers in retinal fundus images. Yu et al.12 proposed a faster method for OD and fovea localization using template matching and directional matched filters in the CIE lab color space. To avoid false positives, we consider vessel characteristics within the OD and define a search area based on the OD’s location and diameter to locate the fovea. Kamble et al.13 proposed a method utilizing 1-D projections of image features for precise landmark localization. The intensity variation of the optic nerve and retinal vessels is used to detect the OD, and peak-valley analysis is performed on intensity lines for OD center coordinates. We then reduce the search space for fovea detection and conduct signal-valley analysis to identify the fovea center accurately. Khaing et al.14 proposed a vessel-based method for OD localization that extracts main blood vessels using thresholding and determines OD candidates using the Exclusion Method (EM) or the line detection method (LEM). With an average accuracy rate of 94.673%, the technique exhibits slowness and struggles to localize the OD in blurred images. Although effective in specific scenarios, these methods need more robustness due to parameter settings and assumptions about OD appearance. Their limited adaptability to diverse datasets and computational complexity hinders real-time applications in clinical settings.

Deep Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have significantly transformed medical image analysis, particularly in the segmentation and localization of OD and fovea centers15–18. Babu et al.19 propose a two-stage method for joint center detection to leverage the consistent relative geometry between the OD and fovea in fundus images. In the first stage, a relation network based on Faster-RCNN with ResNet-101 draws bounding boxes around the OD and fovea. The second stage employs a simple two-layer CNN regressor to jointly regress the centers of the OD and fovea within the bounding boxes. Bhatkalkar et al.20 present a deep learning-based heatmap regression model for joint OD and fovea center detection in color fundus images. Their model surpasses state-of-the-art methods in accuracy and effectiveness, outperforming them on multiple datasets, including IDRiD. Zhang et al.21 combined a faster R-CNN with a shape-constrained level algorithm for OD localization and segmentation. However, despite these advances, accurate and robust algorithm development remains a challenge. Combining multiple techniques through hybrid approaches has shown promise in providing more reliable solutions than deep learning-based models alone.

UNet, a widely acclaimed deep-learning architecture, has gained popularity in medical image segmentation tasks due to its remarkable ability to capture complex features and spatial relationships22. Researchers have further enhanced the performance of UNet by incorporating attention mechanisms, such as channel attention, hybrid attention, and transformers23–26. These attention mechanisms allow the model to focus on relevant information, improving segmentation accuracy and interpretability in medical image analysis. Sevastopolsky,27 proposed a modified U-Net for OD and OC segmentation in fundus images. It achieved excellent results for OD segmentation, with a Dice of 95% and an IoU of 89% on the RIM-ONE V3 dataset. However, its optic cup (OC) segmentation performance was less favorable. Woo et al.28 introduced the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) for CNNs. CBAM generates attention maps sequentially along channel and spatial dimensions to adaptively refine features. It integrates easily into any CNN architecture with minimal overhead and can be trained alongside the base CNNs. The experimental results demonstrate that the sequential generation of attention maps yields more precise attention maps than parallel generation. Tabassum et al.29 designed a Cup Disc Encoder-Decoder Network (CDED-Net) for pixel-wise classification of retinal images by incorporating dense connectivity between layers. The model performs state-of-the-art OC and OD segmentation on the DRISHTI-GS and RIM-ONE datasets. The evaluation of the model using dice and Jaccard scores demonstrates exemplary performance in OD and OC segmentation on both datasets. However, the model exhibits a comparatively lower dice value on the DRISHTI-GS dataset compared to existing methods such as Zilly et al.30, Chakravarty et al.31, and Xu et al.32. Shyamalee and Meedeniya,33 developed a computational model for retinal fundus image segmentation and glaucoma detection. They employed an Attention U-Net with Inception-v3, VGG19, and ResNet50 backbones for segmentation, achieving 99.58%

The attention mechanism in UNet has limitations, including increased computational cost, limited ability to capture global contextual information, and sensitivity to irrelevant information impacting performance. Furthermore, the integration of residual skip connections in the U-shaped encoder-decoder architecture helps address the issue of spatial information loss during pooling operations. Despite including skip connections to mitigate data loss and compensate for spatial information loss during pooling, there is still scope for further enhancement in image reconstruction and overall performance. Further advancements and optimizations are necessary to address these challenges and improve the architecture’s performance and efficiency. Williams and Li34 proposed an innovative approach to enhance the accuracy of CNN by incorporating a wavelet pooling layer. This technique was evaluated on the MNIST and CIFAR-10 datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to other pooling layer methods. Similarly, a study conducted by Rossetto and Zhou35 further confirmed the effectiveness of the wavelet pooling layer and introduced Adam’s modified optimizer as an additional enhancement. The combination of the wavelet pooling layer and the modified optimizer yielded higher accuracy on various datasets, including MNIST and CIFAR-10. Inspired by previous approaches, the proposed method incorporates a wavelet-based pooling layer, enabling multiresolution analysis for accurate segmentation and analysis of structures within the OD and fovea regions. This enhances feature extraction, improves accuracy, and ensures reliable results for OD and fovea analysis tasks.

Methodology

UNet, known for its effectiveness on small datasets23, faces spatial loss due to encoder pooling. To overcome this limitation, incorporating attention modules becomes crucial, as they mitigate vanishing gradient and improve focus on essential regions. However, it is vital to acknowledge that attention modules may introduce additional computational overhead. Within the UNet framework, we modify the input side by incorporating skip connections, which combine pooling layers and wavelet transform. This modification compensates for the spatial loss encountered and enables us to leverage the advantages provided by both techniques. The integration of wavelet transform allows for multiresolution analysis, capturing features at different scales and compensating for the loss of spatial information caused by pooling layers. Additionally, the localization capability of wavelet transform enhances object and boundary localization, while its sparse representation reduces the computational burden. This combined approach offers benefits such as enhanced localization, multi-scale analysis, and efficient representation, leading to improved performance in computer vision tasks. Additionally, substituting traditional skip connections in UNet with a cascaded connection of channel and spatial attention modules enhances the model’s capacity to incorporate encoder neighborhood information. The channel attention module captures interdependencies among channels, while the spatial attention module considers spatial relationships, improving the utilization of pertinent features and contextual information. The cascaded channel and spatial attention framework offer lightweight and efficient modules that deliver improved results when integrated into CNNs with minimal computational overhead28. In our proposed MCAU-Net, we introduce two skip connections that differ from the traditional U-Net architecture to accurately locate and segment the OD and fovea in fundus images. Preprocessing

Data augmentation is a typical pre-processing step used to generalize the model by increasing the dataset size and improving its performance. It involves geometry transformations such as rotation (60%), height-width shift (20%), and zooming (30%) around the center of the image. To reduce the computational load, we resized the images to 128×128 pixels using nearest-neighbor interpolation while maintaining the original fundus image aspect ratio of approximately 1.5. We normalized the images to have unit variance and a zero mean. Training a CNN using the entire image for OD analysis can be computationally challenging since the space occupied by the OD in the fundus image is limited. We extract the Region Of Interest (ROI) surrounding the OD centroid from the fundus image to address this issue. The extracted ROI is then resized to 128×128 before OD segmentation with the MCAU-Net. Proposed MCAU-Net

The proposed MCAU-Net is based on the U-Net architecture with an encoder-decoder structure, as shown in Fig. 3. It comprises two essential modules: a Multiresolution Wavelet Pooling Module (MWPM) and a cascaded attention module (CAM) to extract multiresolution features. The CAM is integrated into the last three layers of the proposed MCAU-Net to enhance the model’s performance and reduce its complexity.

Architecture of MCAU-Net with the integration of Multiresolution Wavelet Pooling Module (MWPM) and Cascaded Attention Module (CAM) at each stage of the U-Net. Together, these components ensure precise localization and segmentation of the OD and fovea, improving the overall accuracy and performance of the model, as demonstrated in Fig. 1.

To improve the local features of the image, we concatenate the feature maps generated by MWPM with the output feature maps of each pooling layer in the modified U-Net, as shown in Fig. 3. Meanwhile, the decoder path recovers the feature map resolution using deconvolution, producing an output size comparable to the input image. The CAM acts as a skip connection between the deconvolution layer and the convolution layer, which helps to extract global and local information simultaneously and improve segmentation accuracy. The proposed MCAU-Net has a lightweight architecture with 828,754 total parameters, of which 826,910 are trainable. The convolution block consists of two sub-blocks, each with a 3×3 kernel, followed by a dropout layer. Each sub-block starts with a Conv2D layer, followed by batch normalization and ReLU activation. These convolution blocks are utilized in each encoder and decoder block. The encoder output is concatenated with the wavelet pooling module output and input into the subsequent encoder block.

Multiresolution wavelet pooling module (MWPM)

A wavelet transform is a well-known method for super-resolving images using CNN. A larger receptive field in CNN better fits the training data and improves the model performance in analyzing more spatial context36. In general, we can get a more extensive receptive field by making the network deeper, making the filter bigger, or doing a pooling operation. However, doing so will always result in higher computing costs. Pooling can extend the receptive field and boost efficacy by lowering the spatial resolution of the feature map. However, data loss might occur as a result37. The wavelet transform helps in preserving both spatial and frequency information. In practice, the transform could be applied independently to each row or column of an image, decomposing it into sub-bands. As shown in Figure. 4, the approximation coefficients (the initial sub-band of the wavelet transform) appear identical to the original image. The original image was reconstructed using an approximation sub-band image and removing the other sub-bands. It provides a more thorough description of local features, improving feature identification. As a result, the segmentation is more precise38.

Heatmap regression model

“Heatmap regression” refers to the regression of a heatmap derived from landmark coordinates. In this method, the ground truth heatmap centered at the landmark position at each channel is obtained by plotting a Gaussian distribution39. The algorithm uses the predicted heatmaps to infer landmark positions after performing pixel-level regression on the ground truth heatmap40. Predicting such heatmaps of OD and fovea is challenging due to similar pixel intensity characteristics in the fundus image. The ground truth coordinates are centered on the mean of the 2D Gaussian kernel, while the remaining kernel gives spatial context. By altering the value of σ, the quantity of spatial context may be changed, which helps the model learn the key features20. We generated 2D heatmaps using kernels centered on ground-truth coordinates to train the proposed MCAU-Net to locate the OD and fovea centers. Figure 7 illustrates using a heatmap regression technique to detect the fovea and OD centers. The projected coordinates are produced by calculating the argmax of predicted heatmap pixel values during MCAU-Net training, similar to test image output heatmaps. However, the datasets only provide those coordinates rather than 2D center heat maps. The generated heat map has high pixel values around the center of the landmark and reduces as we move away from the center. The constant σ determines the size of the Gaussian blobs in heatmap labels, and it has a distinct value for OD and foveal localization. Since the OD area is smaller than the macular region, we ensure the Gaussian blob completely encircles the landmark and its surroundings to prevent erroneous localization. If σ is very small, the Gaussian blob is relatively small, with low global and spatial context, making it prone to localization errors. Following extensive testing with various network parameter values, we chose σ values of 2.0 and 2.5 for the OD and fovea localization. Datasets

In our study, we have carefully selected three public datasets, namely DRISHTI-GS41, DRIONS-DB42, and IDRiD43, each of which contains 101, 110, and 597 (81 images for OD mask segmentation and 516 images for OD/fovea localization) retinal fundus images and has varied dimensions, data formats, and resolutions, for segmenting OD and localizing OD and fovea centers. These databases used to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed method are shown in Table 1 and were chosen based on their specific characteristics, particularly the contrast between the OD and background regions. DRISHTI-GS and DRIONS-DB datasets exhibit clear and high contrast between the OD and background, making them suitable for assessing the segmentation performance under favorable conditions. However, the IDRiD dataset poses a more significant challenge due to the low contrast between OD and the surrounding background.

Training protocol

The proposed MCAU-Net combines the benefits of the MWPM with the Attention module to provide a gradient-based learning technique for multi-scale feature extraction over layers with various inputs utilizing an improved backpropagation algorithm. The model was optimized using the Adam optimizer of learning rate (0.01), β1, and β2 of 0.92 and 0.8, respectively, utilizing the image blocks generated by the generator as training and validation datasets. The model was trained using 500 epochs for OD segmentation and 800 epochs for OD and fovea localization. Further, we used the Jaccard coefficient loss as a training parameter for OD segmentation and mean squared error for OD and fovea localization. For OD segmentation, we employed the Jaccard coefficient loss, which effectively addresses imbalances between foreground and background pixels in medical image segmentation tasks. Results and discussion

The study is implemented on a machine with two Nvidia Geforce RTX 2080 Ti 11GB GPUs and 64 GB of RAM using the TensorFlow backend in the Keras framework of the Python Deep Learning Library. Statistical differences between the methods are determined using a paired t-test with a α = 0.05 significance level. This approach ensures a diverse and representative dataset for evaluating the performance of our proposed method across different datasets.

On the DRISHTI-GS dataset, our proposed method achieves significantly better performance in IoU and Dice scores, better than other methods with statistical significance, except for the IoU index compared with Full Deformable UNet (p=0.030), and the Dice index compared with UNet (p=0.059) and Full Deformable UNet (p=0.247). On the DRIONS-DB dataset, although the proposed MCAU-Net performs better than Full Deformable UNet without statistical significance (the p-values for Dice and IoU are 0.440 and 0.068), the overall results still show that our MCAU-Net outperforms most other methods with statistical significance. On the IDRiD dataset, our proposed method demonstrates significantly superior performance in both IoU and Dice scores compared to other methods, except for the IoU score against GDC-SegNet (p = 0.028) and the Dice scores against U-Net (p = 0.056) and GDC-SegNet (p = 0.507).

Our proposed MCAU-Net demonstrated superior performance with high IoU and Dice scores compared to models evaluated on the DRISHTI-GS and DRIONS-DB datasets. This indicates that our model has effectively learned to identify and delineate target objects accurately. In medical image segmentation tasks, achieving high Dice and IoU values is crucial for reliable localization and informed decision-making by medical professionals. However, it should be noted that the IDRiD dataset presented unique challenges due to domain shift, which impacted the model performance compared to other datasets. Despite these challenges, the model achieved the second-highest Dice score and maintained a high IoU value, showcasing its ability to segment the IDRiD dataset accurately. It is essential to address domain shifts further resulting from differences in acquisition devices, imaging modes, and data sources to enhance the model performance on the IDRiD dataset.

MWPM has been introduced to solve the domain shift problem between various datasets. The spatial loss in CNN is minimized by feeding the MWPM output into U-Net. As a result, it generalizes effectively and produces good Dice scores for all datasets. The proposed MWPM module aggregates multi-scale OD and fovea feature information with a CAM placed between the encoder and decoder. Adding CAM before the last three convolutional layers of the decoder improves MCAU-Net performance by focusing on crucial regions in the image. Furthermore, considerably lowering computing costs decreases parameter counts by around 0.8 M. The proposed model significantly reduces parameters and computations, improving training and testing costs. For instance, when applied to the DRISHTI-GS dataset, the model requires 1 hour 28 min for training and 40.2 ms for testing. The DRIONS-DB dataset takes 1 hour 35 min for training and 40.5 ms for testing. In the case of the IDRiD dataset, it requires 2.0 hours for training and 46.2 ms for testing. The segmented OD results show that the OD boundaries are clear due to the ability of the proposed skip connections to mitigate lost spatial information in the encoder. MCAU-Net can reliably extract the OD borders with negligible false positive and false negative areas for all datasets.

Ablation study

We conducted ablation experiments on DRISHTI-GS, DRIONS-DB, and IDRiD datasets to quantitatively assess the effectiveness of the proposed MWPM, CAM, and Jaccard loss functions. The results in Tables 4 and 5 demonstrate the significant improvements achieved by integrating each function module into U-Net, with the baseline model representing U-Net with the Jaccard function. Initially, U-Net was trained on the three datasets using the cross-entropy loss function, as shown in the first row of Table 4. However, given that U-Net improved performance with the Jaccard loss function in segmenting data, we selected it as the baseline model. The baseline performed better on the datasets. For instance, on the IDRiD dataset, the Dice value improved by 0.86% compared to U-Net, reaching 0.9487, while the IoU increased by 0.87% to reach 0.9449. The MWPM in the baseline model further improved the IoU and Dice metrics. Notably, for the DRISHTI-GS dataset, we achieved a higher mIoU value of 0.9499 (improvement of 0.92% from the baseline model) and a Dice of 0.9512 (improvement of 0.93% from the baseline model). Additionally, adding CAM to the baseline model emphasized crucial aspects of fundus images while eliminating background noise, demonstrating its usefulness. Furthermore, by simultaneously applying MWPM and CAM to the baseline model, we obtained the MCAU-Net network topology, further improving model accuracy. The segmentation results of DRISHTI-GS showed higher IoU and Dice values of 0.9771 and 0.9821, respectively. Similarly, for DRIONS-DB, we achieved an IoU of 0.9450 and a Dice of 0.9720. For the IDRiD dataset, we obtained an IoU of 0.9460 and a Dice of 0.9590, significantly surpassing the baseline model results. The results indicate a noticeable increase in the ED of OD and fovea localization when the MWPM and CAM modules are added to the baseline model, as summarized in Table 5. These outcomes demonstrate the advantageous impact of the proposed MWPM and CAM modules on OD segmentation.

Computational efficiency and model complexity

Our proposed method, MCAU-Net, inspired by the traditional U-Net architecture, is compared against our previous models, including the modified Attention U-Net and DCAU-Net. MCAU-Net features 26 convolutional layers and 12 dense layers, with a maximum of 160 channels and a final feature map size 8×8. While the extensive use of convolutional and dense layers increases the model size due to a higher parameter count, the FLOPs are potentially lower than in other models. This reduction is achieved by optimally selecting hyperparameters such as reduced feature map size and filter depth, effectively minimizing computational complexity without compromising model performance. Given that FLOPs directly impact inference time, our method’s lower FLOPs contribute to faster inference, making it particularly suitable for real-time optic disc segmentation on hardware with limited computational resources. The proposed method for OD segmentation is highly efficient, using 0.8M parameters with a FLOPs count of 1.67 billion, and achieving the fastest inference time of 0.04 to 0.046 seconds. With a compact model size of 3.1 MB, it outperforms other models in speed and resource efficiency, as shown in Table 7.

Conclusion and future scope

The proposed MCAU-Net is a deep CNN-based method specifically designed to locate and segment the OD and fovea structures accurately. By incorporating cascaded channel and spatial attention module connections as skip connections, the model effectively focuses on the target objects and achieves improved convergence during training. The integration of wavelet transform enhances the identification of sharp changes in the image, facilitating precise local feature extraction and reducing the overall image size. The multitasking capability of MCAU-Net allows for simultaneous boundary identification and position estimation, effectively addressing challenges related to image variations and enabling accurate diagnosis and treatment. Comparative evaluations demonstrate the superior performance of the MCAU-Net compared to existing models, showcasing its scalability in handling datasets of varying sizes. We can extend the proposed approach to a practical, real-world solution by incorporating several technical advancements, such as OC segmentation for evaluating the cup-disc ratio in glaucoma, disease classification for automated retinal disease identification, and lesion detection and analysis for specific retinal abnormalities. Integration with clinical decision support systems, validation of diverse datasets, and conducting clinical trials are essential steps to enhance the practicality and reliability of the proposed approach. These technical advancements provide healthcare professionals with accurate diagnostics, effective disease monitoring, and improved patient care, ultimately leading to better clinical outcomes. Additionally, they can be extended to utilize OCT scan images to recognize multiple retinal diseases.

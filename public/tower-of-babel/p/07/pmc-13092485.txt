Clinical and Genomic Characteristics of Patients With Advanced NSCLC Who Have Long-Term Response to First-Line Immunotherapy: A Real-World Study
Authors: Sameh Daher, Walid Shalata, Natali Shirron, Talia Shentzer, Tal Goshen-Lago, Lior Biras, Mor Moskovitz, Sivan Shamai, Damien Urban, Hadas Gantz-Sorotsky, Anastasiya Lobachov, Julia Dudnik, Aharon Yehonatan Cohen, Alexander Yakobson, Alona Zer, Jair Bar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThere is a paucity of data about patients with advanced NSCLC (aNSCLC) with disease control for at least 2 years since the start of first-line (1L) immunotherapy.MethodsDatabases of five Israeli cancer centers were searched for cases of patients with aNSCLC who commenced 1L treatment with immunotherapy between 2018 and 2021 and had no progression of disease for at least 2 years since treatment initiation. Clinical, pathologic, and genomic sequencing data were retrospectively collected. The end of 2 years from the initiation of treatment was the index date. Mutations in STK11, KEAP1 and both were found in 3.9%, 0.5% and 0%. Introduction

Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide.1 The 5-year survival rate of lung cancer in the United States in 2019 was 19.4%, with the prognosis heavily influenced by the stage at which diagnosis is made.1 Traditionally, systemic treatment with platinum-based chemotherapy was the only option for first-line (1L) treatment of advanced NSCLC (aNSCLC). The outcome of this treatment was poor, with a median survival of 8 to 12 months.2,3

In recent years, the treatment for newly diagnosed aNSCLC has been determined on the basis of molecular testing or genomic sequencing, mainly performed on a tumor sample (tissue biopsy) or blood sample (liquid biopsy), conducted at the time of diagnosis. In patients harboring an actionable genomic alteration (AGA) in their tumor cells' genome, the preferred 1L treatment usually is targeted therapy. In patients who are AGA wild-type (WT), treatment is based on the use of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICI), alone or combined with chemotherapy.4,5

ICI-based therapies have been found to be vastly more efficacious than platinum-based chemotherapies in the 1L treatment of advanced AGA WT NSCLC.6, 7, 8, 9 In these pivotal trials, patients were mandated to receive ICI for a maximum of 2 years. Long-responding patients with no progression of disease (PD) within 2 years since commencing ICI continued clinical and imaging follow-up. Part of these patients continued a maintenance chemotherapy treatment (relevant only for patients with adenocarcinoma [ADC]). On the basis of these trials, most used ICI based protocols for 1L treatment of aNSCLC are the following: (1) pembrolizumab (Keytruda, Merck, Sharp & Dohme Corp., Rahway, NJ) (anti–PD-1) as a single agent; (2) pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy; and (3) nivolumab (Opdivo, Bristol Myers Squibb Company, Princeton, NJ) (an anti–PD-1) plus ipilimumab (Yervoy, Bristol Myers Squibb Company, Princeton, NJ) (an anticytotoxic T-lymphocyte–associated antigen 4) plus chemotherapy.

Several trials reported real-world data (RWD) on aNSCLC 1L treatment with ICI,10, 11, 12, 13 but only a few publications focused on patients who completed 2 years of treatment without PD.14,15 In one study, an Australian cohort including 71 patients completing 2 years of pembrolizumab for NSCLC had high rates of KRAS mutation and PD-L1 expression; progression after pembrolizumab treatment completion was higher in patients with PD-L1 less than 1% and in those without a complete response (CR). Another study included a single Israeli medical center cohort of 36 patients treated with pembrolizumab with or without chemotherapy at 1L. In this study, 28 patients remained progression-free at the study cutoff time (median follow-up time since starting treatment was 36 months (28 mo–65 mo).

Efforts are being undertaken to identify prognostic and predictive biomarkers in ICI-treated NSCLC patients.11,16, 17, 18, 19 PD-L1 expression level on tumor cells and tumor mutational burden are predictive for benefit from ICI, but are not always available, and their predictive accuracy is limited.16,17,20, 21, 22, 23 Additional molecular biomarkers are being investigated, such as STK11-LKB1 and KRAS.24, 25, 26, 27, 28 STK11-LKB1 genomic alterations were associated with shorter progression-free survival (PFS) and shorter overall survival (OS) in 1L ICI-treated patients with NSCLC.29 On the other hand, several studies reported similar efficacy of ICI in KRAS mutant compared with patients with KRAS WT NSCLC.26, 27, 28 Numerous studies are ongoing, aiming to identify immune-related gene signatures correlating with clinical benefit from ICI.30, 31, 32, 33 One of these assessed the utility of the 18-gene expression tumor inflammation signature in predicting ICI treatment outcome, and found it to predict clinical benefit of ICI in several tumors, including NSCLC.32 Another study reported the predictability of selected gene signatures and genes for discriminating patients with durable clinical benefit from ICI from those with nondurable benefit. These signatures and genes included the M1 signature, peripheral T-cell signature, CD137, and PSMB9 mRNA expression.33

Here, we report RWD on patients with aNSCLC who completed 2 years of ICI treatment with no PD. We comprehensively assessed clinical and pathologic data and genomic sequencing results as potential prognostic or predictive biomarkers for sustained benefit from ICI, beyond the 2 years of treatment.

Materials and Methods

Study Conduct

This is a retrospective pharmacoepidemiologic study. A chart review was performed of patients with aNSCLC that fit the study criteria.

Patients

Inclusion criteria are the following: age of 18 years and above; patients with aNSCLC who commenced 1L treatment with ICIs (with or without chemotherapy) between 2018 and 2021; received one of the following five treatment regimens: chemotherapy-pembrolizumab, chemotherapy-ipilimumab-nivolumab, chemotherapy-atezolizumab (Tecentriq, Genentech, Inc., South San Francisco, CA), pembrolizumab, ipilimumab-nivolumab; no progression events requiring a change of treatment line for at least 2 years since the start of 1L ICI treatment (oligo-PD treated with local radiotherapy without switching to another line of systemic therapy was allowed); treatment at one of five participating Israeli cancer centers (Rambam Health Care Campus, Sheba Medical center, Rabin Medical Center, Sourasky Medical Center and Soroka Medical Center) (see consort diagram in Supplementary Fig. 1, Supplementary Data); administration of at least one cycle of ICI. Exclusion criteria included the following: (1) previous systemic therapy for advanced or metastatic disease; (2) PD leading to a change of treatment line within 2 years from ICI initiation; or (3) switching to other systemic treatment within 2 years since the first cycle.

Data Collection

The time point of the end of 2 years from initiation of 1L ICI was the index date. Calculations of PFS and OS were performed from the index date; therefore, to avoid confusion with the standard definitions of PFS and OS, we referred to the metric we calculated as “PFS-post 2yr” and “OS-post 2yr,” respectively.

Data collected of identified patients included age, sex, smoking status, histologic diagnosis, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS), weight loss, sites of metastases, antibiotic treatment during ICI treatment or within 6 months after index date, steroid inhalers treatments during ICI treatment, brain or nonbrain radiation treatment before, during or after ICI treatment, ICI toxicity, PD-L1 levels (categorical), genomic sequencing results (performed as part of standard of care), response to 1L ICI and to rechallenge treatments, “PFS-post 2yr” according to treating physicians’ assessments and “OS-post 2yr” by death dates.

The molecular testing panel typically used in Israeli cancer centers until 2020 was the “Oncomine Solid Tumor Panel,” a focused panel of 20 to 22 genes relevant to lung cancer and other solid tumors. In 2020, the “Oncomine Comprehensive Assay Plus,” a broader next-generation sequencing panel, was approved for clinical use in Israel. This panel covers approximately 500 cancer-related genes (depending on the assay version), detecting single-nucleotide variants, insertions, deletions, copy-number alterations, and gene fusions. Most patients in the study underwent genomic testing using one of these two approaches. Tumor assessments were performed by the treating physicians on the basis of computerized tomography scans performed as part of the standard of care.

Statistics

Descriptive statistical methods were utilized to characterize patient demographics and treatment attributes. Categorical data were represented using frequency and percentage distributions, and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for proportions were derived using the normal approximation method. For continuous variables, measures of central tendency (mean, median) and dispersion (SD, interquartile range) were reported.

Missing data were addressed using variable-specific imputation strategies. Categorical variables withless than 5% missingness were imputed using the mode. Variables with higher missingness were imputed using a Random Forest classifier trained on complete numeric predictors. Continuous variables were imputed using the median of observed values. PFS-post 2yr and OS-post 2yr were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, with differences between groups assessed through the log-rank test. PFS-post 2yr was defined as the time from 24 months after initiation of therapy to either radiologically confirmed PD or death from any cause, whichever occurred first. OS-post 2yr was defined as the time from 24 months posttreatment initiation to death from any cause. Patients who had not experienced an event by the data cutoff were censored at the date of their last documented follow-up. The data cutoff date for survival analysis was February 1, 2024. The variables included in the regression models were selected on the basis of statistical thresholds, clinical relevance, and significance in preliminary analyses. All covariates known at or before the 24-month landmark were first assessed in univariable Cox models, and those with p less than 0.10 were included in the multivariable model. In addition, three clinically essential variables—ECOG PS, smoking status, and 1L treatment type—were forced into all multivariable models regardless of their univariable significance. To avoid sparse categories and unstable estimates, several variables were consolidated on the basis of clinical rationale and frequency distributions before model fitting. Cox multivariable survival regression model was applied for PFS-post 2yr and OS-post 2yr analysis with adjustment to each of the possible covariates. To evaluate whether prognostic associations differed by treatment type, sensitivity analyses were performed by stratifying the cohort into ICI-only and chemoimmunotherapy groups. The data were analyzed using Python version 3.12 (pandas, lifelines, scipy, matplotlib, and seaborn libraries). All tests were two-tailed, and a p value of 5% or less was considered statistically significant.

Ethics

Each of the participating centers received approval for collecting data from patients’ records, with the patients’ consent or a waiver for such consent where applicable. The privacy rights of human subjects and national laws have always been observed. This observational study has been approved by the ethics committee of the data-collecting center, Rambam Health Care Campus, under the protocol number RMC-0513-17. Approval was also granted by the ethics committees of the other institutions involved.

Results

A total of 206 patients fit the inclusion criteria. Their demographic, clinical, and genomic data are presented in Table 1. There were 107 patients (51.9%) who had ADC, 48 (23.3%) had squamous cell carcinoma, and 51 (24.8%) had other histologies (large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma and NSCLC not otherwise specified). The median age was 65 years (range 33–90), 64.1% of them were men, 6.3% were never-smokers, 88.8% Jews, 5.3% Arabs, 86.9% of patients had ECOG PS of 0 or 1, 17.5% lost more than 5% of body weight within 6 months before diagnosis. Furthermore, baseline brain or liver metastasis was reported in 22.3% and 4.9% of patients, respectively. There were 80.6% of patients who had oligometastatic disease (less than six metastases). A total of 62.1% of patients had PD-L1 greater than or equal to 50%, whereas 17.5% and 17.0% had PD-L1 less than 1% and 1% to 49%, respectively. Tumor next-generation sequencing detected KRAS mutation in 24.8% and TP53 mutation in 22.8% of patients. A total of 3.9% and 0.5% of patients were found to be STK11 and KEAP1-mutated, respectively. No patients were found to harbor combined STK11+KEAP1 mutations. A total of 52.4% of patients were treated with a combination of chemotherapy and immunotherapy, whereas 47.6% received ICI without chemotherapy.

Overall response rate (ORR) to 1L ICI was 95.7% (95% CI: 92.9%–98.4%), including 24.8% rate of CR. Nine patients (4.3%) had stable disease with no cases of PD as the best response. At a median follow-up of 25.0 months (95% CI: 23.0–29.0 mo) from the index date, the median PFS-post 2yr was not reached (NR) (95% CI: NR–NR); the median OS-post 2yr was also NR (95% CI: NR–NR). The rate of 2-year PFS-post 2yr from the index date was 78.5% (95% CI: 64.1%–93.0%), the rate of 2-year OS-post 2yr from the index date was 81.2% (95% CI: 71.2%–91.2%) (Fig. 1A and B). A total of 58 patients (28.2%; 95% CI: 22.0%–34.0%) experienced PD during follow-up. Of these patients, 24 patients (41.4%) underwent ICI rechallenge, with an ORR of 50.0% (Table 2). Furthermore, out of the 58 patients with PD, 15 (25.9%) and nine (15.5%) patients received nonbrain or brain radiation, respectively, out of which eight (13.8%) nonbrain-radiated and two (3.4%) brain-radiated patients belonged to the rechallenge group. Six patients (10.3%) with no PD received radiation to sites of residual disease. The rechallenge subgroups are depicted in Figure 2.

Among the 206 patients, 31 deaths occurred during follow-up. In the univariable OS-post 2yr analysis, the following baseline factors revealed association with risk of death (Supplementary Table 2, Supplementary Data): liver metastasis (yes versus no, HR 3.02, 95% CI: 0.91–10.01, p value 0.071), histologic diagnosis (squamous versus ADC, HR 2.43, 95% CI: 1.18–5.02, p value 0.016), age (>68 versus ≤68 y, HR 2.47, 95% CI: 1.15–5.30, p value 0.020), PS (≥1 versus 0, HR 2.57, 95% CI: 0.98–6.75, p value 0.055) and brain metastasis (yes versus no, HR 0.41, 95% CI: 0.14–1.16, p value 0.094). In the multivariable OS-post 2yr model (Fig. 3B), liver metastasis, squamous histologic diagnosis and older age remained significantly associated with worse survival (p value 0.004, 0.013, and 0.028, respectively).

Smoking status (current smoker versus never + past) was not significantly associated with PFS-post 2yror OS-post 2yr in either univariable (p value 0.618 and 0.939, respectively) or multivariable (p value 0.750 and 0.660, respectively) analysis, indicating no long-term prognostic effect beyond the 24-month landmark.

Treatment regimen (ICI only versus chemoimmunotherapy) was forced into multivariable models as a covariate but revealed no independent prognostic effect (PFS-post 2yr: HR 1.4, p value 0.220, OS-post 2yr: HR 1.49, p value 0.310). In stratified sensitivity analyses, multivariable models were repeated separately within each treatment group (ICI only: n = 98; chemo-ICI: n = 108) using identical covariates. Key prognostic factors suggest consistent associations across both strata, indicating that the treatment regimen did not confound the primary findings.

Discussion

We report RWD on patients with aNSCLC who completed 2 years of ICI treatment in five Israeli tertiary medical centers. As far as we know, this is one of the biggest data sets published about this unique population of patients until now.

We report on a highly selected group of patients with a very favorable prognosis. The median PFS-post 2yr and OS-post 2yr were not reached after a median follow-up of 25.0 months after the end of 2 years from ICI initiation, and the 2-year PFS-post 2yr and OS-post 2yr rates were 78.5% and 81.2%, respectively. We have observed a close to 100% ORR (95.7%) to 1L treatment in this cohort. At data cutoff, 72% of patients in our cohort were alive with no PD. The overall PD rate after ICI-completion of 28.2% is lower than what is expected. The KEYNOTE-189 and KEYNOTE-407 trials reported approximately 40% of patients who have completed 2 years of pembrolizumab to be alive and without PD at 3 years posttreatment completion, suggesting that around 60% of patients have died or had a recurrence at 5 years. However, a better comparison will be possible once our data is more mature, with extended follow-up.

It is worth noting that 6.3% of patients in our cohort had a never-smoking history, nevertheless having a long-term ICI efficacy, although, as expected, this is lower than the portion of never-smokers in general aNSCLC population (14.9% in a large U.S. cohort).34 Interestingly, more than 80% of patients in our cohort had an oligometastatic disease, defined as five or fewer metastasis. This figure is considerably higher than the incidence of oligometastatic disease in the general population of patients with aNSCLC as reported in previous publications (7%–50%, depending on the inclusion criteria).35, 36, 37 Furthermore, less than 5% of patients in our cohort had baseline liver metastasis, which is also lower than the incidence of liver metastasis in newly diagnosed patients with general aNSCLC (13%–15%).38,39 On the other hand, a considerable proportion (22.3%) of patients in our cohort had baseline brain metastasis, similar to published data regarding the prevalence of brain metastasis in newly diagnosed aNSCLC.40

In our study, 24 patients were rechallenged with ICI, and the rechallenge ORR (50.0%) was higher than reported by most studies included in a recently published review.41 It is worthwhile noting that the evaluation of response in our study was on the basis of investigators’ assessments, which were not required to be done per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors.

In multivariate analysis, nonoligometastatic disease (metastasis number > 5) was found to be associated with increased risk of disease progression after completion of 2 years of treatment. In addition, CR as best response versus partial response or stable disease was associated with a lower risk of disease progression after 2 years of treatment. At multivariate analysis for OS, older age, squamous histologic diagnosis, and liver metastasis at diagnosis were found to be associated with increased risk of death after the index date. Interestingly, our results suggest that for patients completing 2 years of ICI treatment with no PD, long-term PFS and OS are not affected by a number of recognized important prognostic factors including sex, smoking history, weight loss at diagnosis, PD-L1 expression (<1% versus 1%–49% versus ≥50%), KRAS or TP53 status (mutated versus WT), 1L treatment given (chemoimmunotherapy versus immunotherapy), treatment toxicity, antibiotic therapy during or post treatment and pre or during treatment radiation. A 2-year PFS on ICI defines a highly selected group of patients, whereby many recognized important clinical and pathologic factors lose their prognostic value.

Aiming for a pragmatic study, we considered patients who have received radiotherapy to sites of oligo-progression before the index date, similar to patients with no PD events before the index date. The fact that patients who received radiation therapy during treatment had a comparable prognosis to patients who did not receive such a radiation may support the practice of during-therapy radiation for oligoprogressive disease without changing systemic treatment. However, this comparison is retrospective and is limited to this selected cohort of patients. More importantly, the inclusion of patients who were locally treated for a local PD during 1L treatment in this cohort contradicts the usual definitions of PFS; we, therefore, use the terms PFS/OS-post 2yr. The very good outcome we report for these patients suggests that this real-world common practice is a valid approach. Calculations of PFS-post 2yr and OS-post 2yr were performed from the index date; thus, disease progression events that occurred before this time point were not taken into account in the PFS-post 2yr calculations.

With regard to pathologic and genomic characteristics, most of the patients (79.1%) in our cohort had a positive PD-L1 expression, with most (62.1%) having strongly positive (≥50%) PD-L1 expression, higher than the frequency of strongly positive PD-L1 in unselected patients with aNSCLC (∼30%).42 It should be noted that 17.5% of patients had a negative PD-L1 expression in our cohort, stressing the limited predictive value of this parameter. An additional interesting finding is the low incidence of STK11 and KEAP1 mutated patients in this cohort (3.9% and 0.5%, respectively, compared with 25%–30% and 11%–27% according to one published review),43 and the absence of patients harboring combined STK11+KEAP1 mutations (1.5% in the same publication). This finding is consistent with previously reported data about the poor outcome of STK11 orLKB1-mutated aNSCLC.29 On the other hand, the incidence of KRAS mutations in our cohort was similar to that in the general population.44 This is not surprising given previous data showing KRAS mutations to be associated with favorable responses to ICIs.27,45

A limitation of the study is its retrospective nature, with recognized drawbacks in terms of data accuracy and nonregular follow-up intervals. Furthermore, radiologic response to treatment was on the basis of the investigators’ assessment and not on Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors. Another limitation is the lack of a comparative cohort of unselected patients with aNSCLC with a variety of responses to 1L ICI. However, this study provides us with an in-depth view and characterization of a large cohort of this unique group of patients who have a sustained benefit from 1L ICI.

In conclusion, in real-life settings, aNSCLC long-term responders to 1L ICI have high rates of oligometastatic disease, high PD-L1 expression and response to treatment, and low rates of never-smoking history, baseline liver metastasis, and STK11 or KEAP1 mutations. A considerable proportion of these patients have brain metastasis at diagnosis. Except for age, histologic diagnosis, and liver metastasis, long-term survival of patients who completed 2 years of treatment with no PD is not correlated with baseline clinical, pathologic, and genomic variables.

A DLBCL Prognostic model superior to the IPI score: Mechanistic study and clinical validation based on RFC2- and RFC4-mediated DNA damage repair
Authors: Shuainan Wang, Yang Liu, Haoyu Zang, Laibing Guo, Wenjing Du, Jingyan Song, Fei Wang, Luo Lu, Qing Li, Yongqiang Shi, Tongbing Chen, Weiying Gu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
​Approximately 30% to 40% of patients with Diffuse Large B-cell Lymphoma (DLBCL) experience treatment failure, whereas the current International Prognostic Index (IPI) fails to adequately reflect the intrinsic biological features. This study aimed to investigate the prognostic value of Replication Factor C genes (RFCs) in DLBCL and construct a prognostic risk model. Three DLBCL datasets were downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (GSE181063, GSE10846, GSE31312). Multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis identified high expression of RFC2 and RFC4 as independent adverse prognostic factors (RFC2: HR = 1.46, P = 0.037; RFC4: HR = 1.36, P = 0.034), which were significantly associated with age ≥ 60 years, elevated lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) levels, and poor Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status. Mechanistically, the RFC2 high-expression group exhibited a significant increase in tumor mutational burden (TMB). Protein-protein interaction (PPI) analysis revealed that RFC2 and RFC4 interact with CHTF18, PCNA, and other proteins to maintain genomic stability. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) indicated that RFC2 and RFC4 high-expression groups were enriched in the DNA replication and DNA damage repair (DDR) pathways. Competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network analysis predicted miR-34a-5p as an upstream regulator; PARD6G-AS1, TERC, and LINC00662 upregulating RFC2 and RFC4 by sponging miR-34a-5p. The RFC-based prognostic model significantly stratified high- and low-risk patients, with superior predictive performance compared to the IPI score. Validation in our clinical cohort confirmed that the high-risk group had significantly shorter overall survival (OS) (P = 0.003). This study provides a novel tool for individualized prognostic assessment in DLBCL and suggests RFC2 and RFC4 as potential therapeutic targets.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10238-026-02135-1.

Introduction

DLBCL is the most common subtype of adult Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma (NHL) [1, 2]. DLBCL exhibits diverse clinical manifestations. Patients often present with rapidly enlarging lymph nodes as the primary symptom, while some may develop extranodal involvement in organs such as the gastrointestinal tract, thyroid, and testes [3, 4]. Based on gene expression profiling (GEP) or immunohistochemical (IHC) surrogate algorithms (such as the Hans classifier), DLBCL can be classified into at least two major subtypes: the germinal center B-cell-like (GCB) subtype and the non-germinal center B-cell-like (non-GCB) subtype [5, 6]. The R-CHOP immunochemotherapy regimen (comprising rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone) remains the gold-standard first-line therapy for DLBCL, yielding durable responses in 60% to 70% of treated patients [7, 8]. However, outcomes remain poor for high-risk populations and relapsed/refractory (R/R) cases [9]. The advent of novel targeted agents and innovative combination approaches has significantly broadened therapeutic options for R/R DLBCL patients [10]. Therefore, the accurate identification of high-risk DLBCL patients is essential for improving prognostic prediction and informing therapeutic decision-making.

Introduced in 1993, the IPI score remains the most widely used prognostic tool for DLBCL in clinical practice [11]. With the progressive adoption of techniques such as fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH), IHC, and next-generation sequencing (NGS), an expanding array of molecular markers closely associated with prognosis has been identified [12, 13]. Consequently, the IPI score fails to adequately capture the biological heterogeneity of DLBCL, limiting its utility in guiding personalized treatment [14].

The canonical RFC complex, composed of five subunits (RFC1-5), is a key regulator in DNA metabolism. Its primary function is to load the proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA) onto DNA templates, thereby safeguarding replication fidelity [15–17]. Beyond replication, this complex plays a crucial role in DDR and cell cycle checkpoint control, contributing to the maintenance of genomic stability [18–20]. As a critical DDR-related gene [21–23], dysregulated expression of RFCs has been linked to the progression and prognosis of multiple cancers. For example, high RFC3 expression is associated with invasion, metastasis, and poor outcomes in breast cancer [24]. In hepatocellular carcinoma, elevated RFC2 predicts a worse prognosis and promotes tumor cell proliferation and migration via cell cycle modulation [25]. Moreover, RFC4 has been demonstrated to serve as an independent prognostic marker and a potential therapeutic target in both colorectal and cervical cancers [26, 27].

While the functions of RFCs have been investigated in multiple solid tumors, their roles in hematological malignancies, especially in DLBCL, are remain poorly understood. Focusing on RFC2 and RFC4, this study aims to address the following core questions: (1) to define the expression patterns and independent prognostic significance of RFC2 and RFC4 in DLBCL; (2) to develop a prognostic risk model incorporating RFC2 and RFC4; (3) to elucidate the molecular mechanisms by which RFC2 and RFC4 contribute to DLBCL progression. This work seeks to establish a theoretical foundation for precise risk stratification and targeted therapeutic strategies in DLBCL.

Methods

Retrieval of public datasets and cohort establishment

The gene expression profiles and clinical characteristics of DLBCL in datasets GSE181063 (n = 1310), GSE10846 (n = 420) and GSE31312 (n = 498) were downloaded from the GEO. Eligibility criteria for DLBCL patients were: (1) histologically confirmed DLBCL; (2) availability of detailed corresponding clinical characteristics; (3) follow-up duration > 0 days.

Differential expression analysis

The GSE32018 dataset contains gene expression profiles of 114 patients with B-cell NHL, including DLBCL, follicular lymphoma (FL), mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma (MCL), chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), and nodal marginal zone lymphoma (NMZL). Seven fresh frozen lymph node specimens and six fresh frozen reactive tonsil specimens were adopted as controls. In the present study, 22 DLBCL patients and 7 control subjects (fresh frozen lymph nodes) from this dataset were selected for differential expression analysis, aiming to clarify the expression differences of RFCs between DLBCL tumor tissues and normal lymphoid tissues.

Prognostic value evaluation of RFCs

Using the “survminer::surv_cutpoint” package, we determined​ the optimal cutoff value for the expression levels of RFCs and divided the patients into high- and low-expression groups accordingly (FigS1). Survival curves were plotted using the Kaplan-Meier method, and the log-rank test was performed to preliminarily evaluate the association between RFCs high- and low-expression and OS. Variables showing a significant association with prognosis (P < 0.05) were screened through univariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis. Variables analyzed included RFCs and clinical characteristics such as age, gender, Ann Arbor stage, and cell-of-origin (COO) subtype. Significant variables identified in the univariate analysis were subsequently incorporated into multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis to validate the independent prognostic value of RFCs after adjusting for potential confounding factors.

Correlation analysis of clinical characteristics

We systematically evaluated the strength of association between RFC2 and RFC4 expression groups and the following clinical characteristics: gender, age, Ann Arbor stage, extranodal involvement, LDH level, ECOG score, COO subtype and B symptoms, using the chi-square test, and calculated the statistical significance of differences among groups. The “ComplexHeatmap” package was then employed to visualize these associations as a heatmap. Significant associations were highlighted with asterisks (* P < 0.05, ** P < 0.01, *** P < 0.001), while distinct colors were used to differentiate expression groups and clinical characteristics, clearly portraying the relationship patterns between RFC2 and RFC4 expression and clinical characteristics.

Mutational profile and Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) Analysis

Tumor mutation related analysis was conducted using data from the TCGA database. The specific steps are as follows: The “maftools” package was used to systematically parse the mutation files downloaded from TCGA in “maf” format, extracting core data such as gene expression, sample information, and mutation types (e.g., missense mutations, nonsense mutations, frameshift deletions). Subsequently, all samples were divided into high- and low-expression groups based on the median expression levels of RFC2 and RFC4. Mutation landscape plots generated via the “oncoplot” function were used to visualize the top 40 most frequently mutated genes in each group, and the distribution frequency of each mutation type was calculated to intuitively illustrate differences in the gene mutation profiles between the two groups. TMB was calculated for each sample, defined as the number of non-synonymous mutations per megabase of the genome. Box plots were used to visualize differences in TMB between the high- and low-expression groups of RFC2 and RFC4.

Protein-Protein Interaction (PPI) network analysis

The STRING database integrates experimentally validated results, computationally predicted data, and literature-mined information, providing highly reliable protein-protein interactions. In this study, a stringent confidence score threshold (≥ 0.9) was set to screen for core interacting proteins of RFC2 and RFC4, thereby constructing a PPI network to further elucidate the biological functions of the RFC family complexes.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) analysis

This analysis was carried out with the package “clusterProfiler”. Using the TCGA-DLBC dataset, we first calculated the association strength between each gene and RFC2, RFC4 high- and low-expression groups. All genes were then ranked in descending order of this association to generate an ordered gene list correlated with RFC2 and RFC4 expression levels. Subsequently, GSEA was performed using the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway “c2.cp.kegg_legacy.v2024.1.Hs.symbols.gmt” as predefined gene sets.

Competing Endogenous RNA (ceRNA) regulatory axis construction

miRNet is a comprehensive analytical tool that integrates experimentally validated and predicted interaction information from authoritative databases. In this study, we utilized this platform to systematically screen and explore miRNAs targeting RFC2 and RFC4, as well as their upstream regulatory lncRNAs, ultimately constructing an lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA regulatory network.

miRPath database (v3.0) is an online tool specifically designed for functional enrichment and pathway analysis of miRNA target genes. In this study, we input KEGG pathways related to DDR, including DNA replication, Base Excision Repair (BER), Nucleotide Excision Repair (NER), and Mismatch Repair (MMR), to perform reverse prediction and identify miRNAs that potentially regulate these DDR pathways.

starBase is a comprehensive atlas of RNA interactions equipped with an integrated analytic platform. Leveraging its Pan-Cancer module, we systematically conducted the expression correlations analysis among mRNAs, miRNAs, and lncRNAs in DLBCL.

Risk-prediction model construction and validation

A prognostic risk model was constructed based on independent prognostic variables identified through multivariate Cox proportional hazard regression analysis. The model was developed and validated using multiple datasets, as detailed in Table S1. Specifically, the GSE181063 dataset served as the training set, while the GSE10846 and GSE31312 datasets were used as two independent validation sets. First, risk scores were calculated for each patient across all three datasets. Patients were stratified into high-risk and low-risk groups using the median risk score from the training set as the cutoff. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were then constructed to compare prognosis between the two groups, and model performance was further validated across different treatment regimens and COO subtypes. Additionally, a nomogram was constructed to visualize the model. The model’s performance was comprehensively evaluated in terms of discrimination, calibration, and clinical net benefit using the concordance index (C‑index), time‑dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA) to verify its reliability and practical utility.

Our center’s validation cohort

In this study, 65 cases of DLBCL diagnosed at Changzhou First People’s Hospital from January 2018 to January 2023, with available residual tissue samples, were collected retrospectively. Additionally, 20 patients with reactive lymph node hyperplasia during the same period were selected as the control group.

All cases were reviewed by two pathologists; any uncertain diagnosis was discussed and confirmed by a third pathologist following a group discussion. The diagnosis and classification of DLBCL were determined in accordance with the World Health Organization (WHO) classification system. We collected the follow-up data of enrolled patients through the medical record system or telephone follow-up. OS was defined as the time from the date of diagnosis to death from any cause. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Changzhou First People’s Hospital (Approval No: 2025 (education) CL034-01).

Immunohistochemical (IHC) Staining and Scoring

Four-micrometer-thick sections were cut from formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissues. Bake the slides at 70 °C for 30 min. The sections were dewaxed in xylene (3 times, 10 min each) and then rehydrated in a graded series of ethanol (100%, 95%, 85%, and 75%, 5 min each). Antigen retrieval was performed by high-pressure heating in EDTA buffer (pH 8.0) for 3 min, followed by natural cooling to room temperature. Peroxidase blocker (Dako/Agilent) was added dropwise, and the sections were incubated at room temperature for 10 min. After rinsing with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), the sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with diluted primary antibodies: rabbit anti-human RFC2 (GTX105087, dilution 1:300) and rabbit anti-human RFC4 (GTX104052, dilution 1:500). On the next day, the sections were washed with PBS (3 times, 2 min each). After another PBS rinse, the sections were incubated with the secondary antibody (Dako/Agilent) at room temperature for 30 min. Following a further PBS wash (3 times, 2 min each), DAB chromogenic reagent (Dako/Agilent) was used for color development, with the reaction monitored under a microscope. The sections were then counterstained with hematoxylin, dehydrated in a graded ethanol series, cleared in xylene, and mounted with neutral balsam.

The staining of RFC2 and RFC4 was localized in the nucleus. A semi-quantitative scoring method was used to evaluate the samples, and the score was calculated as the product of staining intensity (0–3 points) and the percentage of positive tumor cells. The H-score ranged from 0 to 300. Using the median H‑score of DLBCL samples as the cutoff value, the samples were classified into high‑ and low‑expression groups for the RFC2 and RFC4 proteins.

Statistical methods

All analyses were performed using R software (4.4.2). Continuous data are expressed as mean ± SD and compared between groups with Student’s t-test. Categorical variables were expressed as percentages (%), and comparisons between groups were performed using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test. Survival curves were plotted using the Kaplan-Meier method. Prognostic factors were examined by univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression. A two-sided P < 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant.

Results

Differential expression of RFCs

Using 22 DLBCL tumor tissues and 7 normal lymph node tissues (fresh frozen) selected from the GSE32018 dataset. The mRNA expression levels of RFCs were significantly upregulated in DLBCL tissues compared with normal lymph nodes (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1a-e).

Prognostic Value Analysis of RFCs

In the GSE181063 dataset, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated that patients in the high-expression groups of RFC2, RFC3, RFC4, and RFC5 had a significantly worse OS than those in the low-expression groups (P < 0.05) (FigS2). Subsequent univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses confirmed that high expression of both RFC2 and RFC4 is an independent adverse prognostic factor in DLBCL (RFC2: HR = 1.46, 95% CI: 1.02–2.10, P = 0.037; RFC4: HR = 1.36, 95% CI: 1.02–1.81, P = 0.034) (Table S2).

Analysis of Correlation with Clinical Characteristics and Prognostic Value

Heatmap analysis based on the GSE181063 dataset revealed that RFC4 high-expression group had significantly elevated LDH levels (P = 0.004) and were more likely to be aged ≥ 60 years (P = 0.042). RFC2 high-expression group was associated with a poorer ECOG score (P = 0.042). Regarding COO subtypes, both the RFC2-high and RFC4-high groups were enriched in the GCB DLBCL subtype (RFC2, P < 0.001; RFC4, P = 0.018) (Fig. 1f-g). Nevertheless, within the GCB DLBCL subgroup, neither RFC2 nor RFC4 expression levels significantly affected prognosis (P > 0.05) (Fig. 1h-i).

Protein-Protein Interaction (PPI) network analysis

Based on the STRING database, we constructed a PPI network for RFC2 and RFC4. The results revealed that as core constituent subunits of the classical RFC complex, RFC2 and RFC4 not only maintained strong interactions with other members of the complex (RFC1, RFC3, RFC5), but also exhibited robust interactions with proteins such as CHTF18, ATAD5, and PCNA (Fig. 3a).

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) analysis

GSEA revealed significant enrichment of DDR pathways in the RFC2-high and RFC4-high groups, including DNA replication, BER, MMR and NER (Fig. 3b-c).

Identification and Construction of the Competing Endogenous RNA (ceRNA) Regulatory Network

Through integrated multi-database analyses, we delineated the ceRNA network governing RFC2 and RFC4 expression. First, miRNAs predicted to target RFC2 and RFC4 were retrieved from miRNet, among which 27 miRNAs were identified as common candidate regulators capable of simultaneously modulating both genes (Fig. 4a). Next, miPath was used to back-screen these common candidate miRNAs for involvement in DNA replication, BER, NER, and MMR pathways, yielding 22 common miRNAs (Fig. 4b). These candidates were further filtered using starBase to retain only those miRNAs whose expression was significantly and negatively correlated with both RFC2 and RFC4, pinpointing miR-34a-5p as the core regulatory miRNA. Finally, lncRNAs predicted to be targeted by miR-34a-5p were extracted from miRNet database (Fig. 4c), and only those exhibiting a negative correlation with miR-34a-5p and a positive correlation with both RFC2 and RFC4 were retained (Fig. 4d). This pipeline identified three key lncRNAs: PARD6G-AS1, TERC, and LINC00662, thereby constructing a hypothesized ceRNA regulatory axis for RFC2 and RFC4 (Fig. 4e).

Construction and independent validation of the prognostic risk model

Based on the multivariate Cox regression results, we constructed a prognostic risk model incorporating seven independent variables: age, Ann Arbor stage, LDH level, ECOG score, COO subtype, RFC2 expression, and RFC4 expression.

We calculated the risk score for each patient in the training set (GSE181063) and, using the median value of 2.12 as the cut-off, classified them into a high-risk group and a low-risk group (Fig. 5a). The same scoring formula and identical cut-off were then applied to the two independent validation sets: GSE10846 and GSE31312. In all three datasets, OS was significantly shorter in the high-risk group compared with the low-risk group (P < 0.0001) (Fig. 5b-c).

In the external validation cohort GSE31312, the model also exhibited robust predictive performance for progression-free survival (PFS) (P < 0.0001) (Fig. 5d). Given that rituximab significantly improves survival outcomes in patients with DLBCL, and considering that differences in treatment regimens and intensity may affect the applicability of prognostic models, we performed further subgroup analyses in the GSE10846 dataset. First, based on the treatment regimen, patients were stratified into a frontline R‑CHOP treatment group and a conventional CHOP treatment group. Subgroup analysis revealed that in both groups, high‑risk patients had significantly shorter OS than low‑risk patients (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5e-f). Considering that clinical practice often involves variations in treatment intensity across different COO subtypes (ABC and GCB subtypes), we further conducted stratified subgroup analyses by COO subtype. The results demonstrated that high‑risk patients consistently exhibited significantly worse prognosis both in the GCB and ABC DLBCL subtypes (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5g-h).

Construction of the prognostic nomogram and performance validation of the risk model

To facilitate its clinical application, we converted this model into an easy-to-use nomogram and systematically evaluated its discrimination and stability (Fig. 6a). In the training set (GSE181063), the C-index reached 0.754 (95% CI: 0.728–0.780), with time-dependent C-indices consistently exceeding 0.70 (Fig. 6b). In the validation set GSE10846, the C-index was 0.691 (95% CI: 0.642–0.740); in the validation set GSE31312, the concordance index was 0.667 (95% CI: 0.622–0.712). Notably, the time-dependent C-indices at all time points in both validation sets remained above 0.65 (Fig. 6c-d). Time-dependent ROC curve analyses demonstrated that, in the training set (GSE181063), the model achieved AUCs of 0.795, 0.782, and 0.805 for predicting 1-, 3-, and 5-year overall survival, respectively. In the validation set GSE10846, the corresponding AUCs were 0.740, 0.719, and 0.729, while in the validation set GSE31312, the AUCs reached 0.720, 0.680, and 0.736 at the same time points (Fig. 6e-g).

To further confirm the superiority of this model, we benchmarked it against the conventional IPI score using the same datasets. In the training set (GSE181063), the IPI score yielded AUCs of 0.725, 0.692, and 0.689 for 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival; in the validation set GSE10846, the AUCs were 0.712, 0.679, and 0.659, respectively; and in the validation set GSE31312, the AUCs were 0.703, 0.678, and 0.665 (Fig. 6e-g). These results collectively indicate that the model developed in this study outperforms the IPI score in predictive accuracy.

Calibration curves demonstrated nearly perfect agreement between predicted and observed 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival probabilities in the training set, and this concordance was preserved in both validation sets (Fig. 6h-j). Decision curve analysis further revealed that, across the clinically relevant threshold probabilities of 0.30–0.70, the net-benefit curves of the model consistently surpassed the “treat-all” and “treat-none” extremes in both validation sets, indicating that this model can deliver substantial clinical utility (Fig. 6k-m).

External validation at our center​

To clarify the expression characteristics of RFC2 and RFC4 proteins and validate the clinical applicability of this model, this study retrospectively enrolled 65 patients with pathologically confirmed DLBCL, along with 20 patients with reactive lymphoid hyperplasia during the same period as benign controls (Fig. 7a). Immunohistochemistry revealed that RFC2 and RFC4 were primarily localized in the nuclei of DLBCL cells. Among the 65 DLBCL patients, 32 (49.2%) exhibited high RFC2 protein expression, and 30 (46.2%) exhibited high RFC4 protein expression. In contrast, among the 20 cases of reactive lymphoid hyperplasia, only 3 (15.0%) showed high expression of RFC2 protein, and similarly, 3 (15.0%) showed high expression of RFC4 protein. Fisher test results demonstrated that the expression levels of both RFC2 and RFC4 proteins were significantly upregulated in DLBCL tissues compared to reactive lymphoid hyperplasia tissues (P < 0.05) (Fig. 7b). Survival analysis indicated a borderline trend in OS differences between the high- and low-expression groups for both RFC2 and RFC4 (RFC2: P = 0.078; RFC4: P = 0.081) (Fig. 7c-d), implying that high expression might be associated with poorer prognosis, though further validation with a larger sample size is required. Applying the previously established model to our center’s cohort, patients were stratified into high- and low-risk groups based on the median risk score. The high-risk group showed significantly worse OS than the low-risk group (P = 0.003) (Fig. 7e), confirming that the model has robust risk stratification capability and clinical utility in this center’s cohort.

Discussion

With the continuous advancement of targeted therapies (such as BTK inhibitors, PI3K inhibitors) and immunotherapies (including bispecific antibodies, CAR-T cell therapy, and immune checkpoint inhibitors), the overall treatment strategy for DLBCL has made significant progress [28–32]. However, the IPI score primarily incorporates clinical parameters such as age, LDH levels, and Ann Arbor stage, fails to adequately capture the highly heterogeneous biological characteristics of DLBCL, resulting in limited ability to identify high-risk patients. This shortcoming further hampers the precise formulation of individualized treatment strategies. RFCs play critical roles in DNA replication, damage repair, and cell cycle checkpoint regulation. They have been demonstrated to be closely associated with the progression and prognosis of various solid tumors, including cervical cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, and colorectal cancer [33], with potential value as both prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets. Through integrated bioinformatics analysis and experimental validation, this study systematically investigated the expression profiles and clinical significance of RFCs in DLBCL. These findings may facilitate the establishment of more precise risk stratification and the development of novel targeted therapeutic strategies for DLBCL.

Multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis in this study confirmed that high expression of both RFC2 and RFC4 constitutes an independent adverse prognostic factor for OS in DLBCL patients. Notably, although the high-expression groups of RFC2 and RFC4 were associated with high-risk clinical features such as age ≥ 60 years, elevated LDH levels, and poorer ECOG performance status, these covariates were adjusted for in the multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis, thereby further validating the independent prognostic value of these two markers. Furthermore, clinical feature correlation analysis indicated that high expression of RFC2 and RFC4 was significantly more prevalent in the GCB subtype of DLBCL (P < 0.05), suggesting their potential utility as auxiliary diagnostic markers for identifying GCB DLBCL.

Mutational analysis revealed that patients with high expression of RFC2 and RFC4 exhibited a higher mutation frequency in key DLBCL driver genes, including KMT2D, B2M, and CARD11 [34–36]. Specifically, KMT2D mutations correlate with advanced Ann Arbor stage and elevated MYC expression and are associated with significantly shorter PFS and OS [37]. B2M mutations reduce human leukocyte antigen class I/II (HLA-I/II) expression, diminishing T-cell infiltration and impairing immune recognition of EBV-positive tumor cells, thereby promoting immune evasion [38]. CARD11 mutations constitutively activate the NF-κB pathway, driving the expression of BCL2, BCL6 and PD-L1, endowing DLBCL tumor cells with enhanced anti-apoptotic capacity and immune checkpoint evasion ability [39–41].

To further elucidate the molecular regulatory network of RFC2 and RFC4 in DLBCL, PPI network analysis conducted in this study revealed four functionally distinct RFC family complexes: the canonical RFC complex (RFC1-5) and three RFC-like complexes (RLCs), namely the Ctf18-RLC (CHTF18-CHTF8-DSCC1-RFC2-5), ATAD5‑RLC (ATAD5-RFC2-5), and RAD17-RLC (RAD17-RFC2-5). These complexes function cooperatively, collectively constituting a multi-layered regulatory network essential for maintaining genomic stability.

Specifically, the canonical RFC complex performs the core function of loading PCNA onto the 3′-primer terminus at replication origins. This process provides DNA polymerase with processive synthesis capability, thereby ensuring efficient progression during the initiation phase of DNA replication [42, 43]. Ctf18-RLC functions during the DNA replication elongation phase. It not only mediates the complementary loading of PCNA onto the leading strand but also interacts with cohesin to establish sister chromatid cohesion, thereby maintaining replication fork stability [44]. ATAD5-RLC is responsible for the specific unloading of PCNA upon replication termination. Together with the loading function of the canonical RFC complex and the auxiliary loading function of the Ctf18-RLC, it forms a complete functional cycle of “loading‑auxiliary loading-unloading,” thereby maintaining the stability and integrity of the post-replicative genome [45, 46]. RAD17-RLC, activated upon DNA damage, loads the 9-1-1 (RAD9-HUS1-RAD1) complex onto 5′-recessed ends to trigger the ATR-CHK1 checkpoint pathway [47, 48]. As core subunits shared by the aforementioned RFC family complexes, RFC2 and RFC4 play crucial roles in the proper execution of their physiological functions. This functional positioning is further supported by GSEA. High expression of RFC2 and RFC4 was significantly enriched in pathways including DNA replication, BER, MMR, and NER, all of which are core components of the DDR [49].

Thus, RFC2 and RFC4 may play strikingly opposite biological roles in physiological contexts versus during tumorigenesis and cancer progression. In normal cells, high RFC2 and RFC4 expression can constitute a compensatory response to replication stress or minor DNA lesions, bolstering DDR activity to preserve genomic integrity [50]. In malignant cells, however, the same upregulation may convert DDR hyperactivation into a proliferative accelerator. Overactive repair pathways can enable tumor cells to withstand DNA damage induced by chemotherapy or radiotherapy, augment therapeutic resistance, and expedite mutational accumulation and disease progression [51]. Based on the core pathological mechanism involving dysregulated DDR pathways, RFC2 and RFC4, as core subunits of the canonical RFC complex, may represent promising therapeutic targets for sensitizing tumors to treatment [19, 52, 53]; Specifically, targeted inhibition of RFC2 can effectively overcome the resistance of castration-resistant prostate cancer to PARP inhibitors by disrupting replication fork stability and blocking the DDR pathway [54]. In contrast, downregulation of RFC4 expression can not only significantly enhance the radiosensitivity of colorectal cancer but also induce aberrant activation of the non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) pathway, further amplifying DNA damage effects [26]. Notably, ATAD5-RLC, another member of the RFC family complexes, has also been identified as a candidate biomarker for predicting sensitivity to PARP inhibitors [55], suggesting that this family has broader target value in the precision therapy of cancer.

To elucidate the regulatory mechanism of RFC2 and RFC4 expression, this study predicted a ceRNA regulatory network and found that miR-34a-5p could target and inhibit both genes, acting as their upstream inhibitory miRNA. Previous studies have confirmed that this miRNA is downregulated in DLBCL and exerts a tumor-suppressive effect [56, 57]. Furthermore, it is well-established that miR-34a-5p is a key player in the DDR pathway and the regulation of cell cycle checkpoints [58–60]. Further analyses revealed that the lncRNAs PARD6G-AS1, TERC and LINC00662 may act as molecular sponges for miR-34a-5p. Accordingly, we propose the following mechanistic hypothesis: the three lncRNAs above sequester miR-34a-5p to relieve its inhibitory effect on the 3′ UTR regions of RFC2 and RFC4, thereby upregulating the expression of both genes. High expression of RFC2 and RFC4 can enhance the DDR capacity of tumor cells, ultimately promoting tumor cell survival, proliferation and progression. If this mechanism is validated, restoring the biological activity of miR-34a-5p or disrupting the above lncRNA-miRNA interactions may serve as a novel combination therapeutic strategy for DLBCL.

The RFC-based model demonstrated that patients in the high-risk group had significantly shorter OS than those in the low-risk group in both the training and validation sets and the model outperformed the IPI score. Subgroup analyses revealed that its prognostic accuracy remained consistent across different treatment regimens and COO subtypes. Meanwhile, the model also showed significant predictive value for PFS. Immunohistochemical analysis of pathological tissues from 65 DLBCL patients in our center revealed that RFC2 and RFC4 proteins were primarily localized in the nucleus of DLBCL cells. Moreover, the high-expression rates of both proteins were significantly higher in DLBCL tissues compared to the control group (P < 0.05). Although the association of both RFC2 and RFC4 protein expression​ with OS showed borderline significance, application of the prognostic risk model to our center’s cohort demonstrated that the high-risk group had a significantly shorter OS than the low-risk group (P = 0.003). In summary, this model exhibits excellent predictive efficacy and clinical utility, providing a more precise tool for prognostic assessment in DLBCL.

This study has several limitations: (1) As a bioinformatics investigation based on public databases, variations in inclusion criteria and detection platforms across different cohorts may introduce heterogeneity and potentially affect the stability of the model; (2) The sample size of our center’s cohort is relatively limited, which may result in insufficient statistical power. Future large-scale, multi-center, prospective clinical studies are warranted to further validate the clinical applicability of the model; (3) Regarding mechanistic exploration, the ceRNA regulatory network with RFC2 and RFC4 as core targets remains at the stage of bioinformatic prediction, lacking in-depth cellular and animal experimental validation. Subsequent in vitro and in vivo functional experiments are needed to systematically elucidate the underlying mechanisms; (4) In the absence of detailed data on treatment intensity, the possibility that differences in treatment regimens may have contributed to the observed survival disparities cannot be completely excluded. Future validation of the model’s performance will be conducted in prospective cohorts with complete treatment intensity information.

Conclusion

In summary, this study for the first time establishes the value of RFC2 and RFC4 as potential prognostic biomarkers for DLBCL and, based on them, constructs a novel prognostic risk model. This model not only demonstrates significantly superior predictive performance compared to the IPI score, but also exhibits greater accuracy and stability due to its close association with the intrinsic biological properties of the disease. The model was successfully validated in our center’s cohort of 65 DLBCL patients, providing preliminary evidence for its clinical applicability.

Future studies should focus on elucidating the specific molecular mechanisms of RFC2 and RFC4 in DLBCL pathogenesis through functional experiments, and on validating the model’s generalizability in larger, multi-center clinical cohorts. These steps are essential to advance its translation into clinical practice, ultimately aiming to provide new strategies for improving prognostic management in DLBCL patients.

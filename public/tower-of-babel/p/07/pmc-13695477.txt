Unique EEG signature of atypical absence seizures in SYNGAP1‐related developmental and epileptic encephalopathy
Authors: Rainier Mark Loidor L Rapal, Joshua Chang, Douglas Nordli
Published: 2026

We report a case of a 13‐year‐old girl with SYNGAP1‐associated developmental and epileptic encephalopathy. Seizures began at age 2, with atonic and atypical absence semiology. Brain MRI was normal. She has drug‐resistant epilepsy despite lamotrigine and topiramate.

Her interictal EEG showed diffuse slowing and disorganization, with poor anterior–posterior gradient (Figure 1). There were frequent bursts of diffusely distributed and pleomorphic slow spike and wave interictal discharges. Ictal EEG during atypical absence seizures showed slow eyelid closure followed by occipitally predominant spike–wave discharges (Figure 2). This crescendoed into a generalized 2 Hz–2.5 Hz spike and polyspike and wave pattern, ending with diffuse slowing.

Interictal EEG background demonstrating diffuse slowing and disorganization with poor AP gradient.

Atypical absence ictal EEG demonstrating findings of slow eyelid closure followed by occipital‐predominant discharges that crescendo into a generalized rhythmic spike‐and‐wave pattern.

SYNGAP1‐DEE is a monogenic epilepsy with known EEG features of background slowing and generalized or multifocal discharges. 1 , 2 A unique feature is atypical absence seizures resembling Jeavons syndrome with slow eyelid closure followed by occipitally‐predominant discharges that crescendo into a generalized pattern, emphasizing the need for neurologists to consider both conditions diagnostically.

Effectiveness of dietary intervention in geriatric patients receiving new complete dentures: A randomized controlled trial
Authors: Sheetal Vijaya, Anisha Rodrigues, Meenakshi Garg, Shilpa Vijaya, Meghan J. Shetty, Shradha Dhanania
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Aim:This study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of a tailored progressive dietary intervention against the standard care instructions on the nutritional status and nutrient intake in geriatric patients receiving new complete dentures.Settings and Design:Randomized controlled trial.Materials and Methods:A randomized controlled trial was conducted on completely edentulous patients seeking complete dentures. The participants were randomly allocated into two groups, and each group received a separate set of advice. The cognitive function was assessed using the mini–mental state examination (MMSE) form. The outcome measures were assessed at the baseline and 3 months after denture insertion. The Mini Nutritional Assessment (MNA) form was used to evaluate the nutritional status and the nutrient intake was calculated by a 3-day 24-h dietary recall and fed into the DietCal software.Statistical Analysis Used:The analysis was done using the independent sample t-test, paired sample t-test, and analysis of co-variance. The significance level was set at 0.05 for all tests.Results:In total, 68 participants completed the trial. There were no significant differences in food intake between the two groups at baseline. However, at 3-month follow-up, the intervention group displayed a statistically significant improvement in body mass index (BMI) compared to the control group. Although the nutrient intake such as protein, dietary fiber, total fat, calcium, carbohydrate, energy, iron, and Vitamin B12 was higher in the intervention group than in the control group, it was not statistically significant.Conclusion:Simple, tailored dietary intervention with prosthetic restoration of the edentulous patients significantly improved BMI and MNA scores.

INTRODUCTION

Aging is a complex phenomenon influenced by physical, cellular, and psychosocial factors.[12] The physiologic changes, social factors, and chronic illness related to aging can impact the nutritional status of an individual.[1] Several studies have reported a higher risk of malnutrition among the elderly.[345] Malnutrition can negatively impact cognitive, physical and sensory functions, and increase susceptibility to disease. Malnutrition can often go unnoticed for extended periods.[6] Studies showed that physicians frequently overlook this problem, thus failing to treat malnutrition in elderly patients.[3678]

Aging is also associated with tooth loss.[8] Impaired dentition can impact an individual’s dietary choices, leading them to select foods that are easier to chew, thus increasing their susceptibility to malnutrition.[9] The reduced masticatory function and a reduction in saliva prolong the mastication and affect the bolus formation.[10]

Prosthetic rehabilitation with a complete denture in an edentulous arch can enhance a person’s ability to chew.[8] However, chewing with complete dentures is a skill to be learned, therefore, the patient has to consume a different diet in the initial days of denture wear until a few months after which the patient will be accustomed to chewing efficiently. Patient education regarding diet is an essential part of the postinsertion instructions along with denture maintenance. However, during the denture delivery appointment, the instructions do not include tailored progressive detailed dietary advice, mainly because of constraints with respect to time, knowledge, and possibly language. Tailored dietary advice is a means to deliver personalized nutritional guidance to patients, to meet their nutritional needs and improve health.[9] It would, therefore, be beneficial if dentists could evaluate the nutritional status of the edentulous patients and collaborate with nutritionists to develop dietary regimens to improve nutritional outcomes.

Evidence suggests that providing new complete dentures along with tailored dietary counseling improves the nutrient intake of edentulous individuals.[281011] However, these studies were performed on the Japanese, the United Kingdom, and French population. There are no studies conducted in India, which has a high prevalence of both edentulism and malnutrition.[71213] Hence, this clinical trial aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of new complete denture fabrication and tailored progressive dietary advice on the nutritional status and nutrient intake of completely edentulous geriatric patients in Mangalore, South India. The primary objective of this trial was to evaluate the change in the nutritional status among the completely edentulous geriatric patients receiving new complete dentures and tailored progressive dietary advice, using the Mini Nutritional Assessment (MNA) form. In addition, the effect of the intervention on nutrient intake of the geriatric new complete denture wearers using 3-day-24-h dietary recall was also investigated.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study setting

This study was a single-blind, parallel-group randomized controlled trial conducted among completely edentulous geriatric patients requiring complete dentures reporting to the department of prosthodontics at a private dental institute. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee (AJEC/REV/265/2019) and registered at the Clinical Trial Registry – India (CTRI/2021/03/032393). The trial followed the 2010 Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials statement.

Completely edentulous patients seeking new dentures, aged 60 years and above, with healthy intraoral conditions, who were at the risk of malnutrition or malnourished (MNA <24), without cognitive impairment (mini–mental state examination [MMSE] <24) and who could understand and respond to instructions and questionnaires were included in the study. The patients were enrolled regardless of their arch form and arch relationship. Patients with uncontrolled diabetes (glycated hemoglobin ≥6.5%), infectious disease or debilitating conditions, obese (body mass index [BMI] >30) or with adequate nutritional status (MNA = or >24), who received professional nutritional counseling and followed a prescribed diet plan or on oral nutritional supplements, living in an institution, and those not willing to participate in the study were excluded from the study. The informed consent was administered to the eligible participants in the language they could understand. The details of the trial were explained to the participants and any concerns regarding the same were clarified. The participants signed an informed consent form and were randomized to the control group and an intervention group.

Intervention

All participants received new complete dentures fabricated by a single clinician using conventional techniques. The participants in the control group received the standard care instructions verbally regarding denture hygiene and cleaning methods, along with care and maintenance of dentures on the day of denture trial and denture insertion. Participants in the intervention group received complete dentures with standard care instructions and the intervention in the form of tailored progressive dietary advice on the day of the denture trial and denture insertion. The diet plan was structured based on the manual “Dietary Guidelines for Indians” (National Institute of Nutrition, Hyderabad, India) and prepared with the assistance of a medical professional and a nutritionist. The diet plan was customized considering the patient’s existing diet and medical conditions while maintaining the nutrient values. Both groups received their respective advice in verbal and in pamphlet form. The pamphlet given to patients in the intervention group contained information about the food requirements, type and quantity of food, emphasizing on the consumption of proteins, dairy products, fruits, and vegetables, and the importance of nutrients in the diet was verbally explained along with the contents of the pamphlet. The diet plan was prepared in consultation with a nutritionist and was customized to individual patient requirements, considering their existing food habits, systemic conditions, and cultural background. Patient compliance was monitored at weekly intervals with reminders to adhere to the dietary intervention. All the participants were followed up for 3 months postdenture delivery.

Measurements

The demographic details of all participants such as age, gender, level of education, qualification, occupation, income, and period of edentulousness were recorded using a questionnaire. Accordingly, Kuppuswamy’s socioeconomic status scale 2019 was used to determine the socioeconomic status.

A screening procedure was carried out using the MMSE form to assess the cognitive function of each participant. In addition, anthropometric measurements such as height, weight, BMI, midarm, and calf circumference were also recorded.

The nutritional status of the participants enrolled was assessed using the MNA form [Figure 1], whereas the nutrient intake was assessed by a 3 day-24-h dietary recall (weekend and a consecutive weekday) and fed into the DietCal software to analyze the nutrient values.

Outcome variables

The primary outcome was a change in the nutritional status among the completely edentulous geriatric patients using the MNA form. The secondary outcome involved the effect of the intervention on nutrient intake of the geriatric new complete denture wearers using 3-day-24-h dietary recall. Outcome measures were measured at baseline and at 3 months posttreatment.

Sample size

Seventy patients were considered for the study. The sample size was measured with an alpha of 5% with 80% power and 95% confidence level using the OpenEpi software. The sample size was determined based on previous research that assessed the nutritional status by calculating energy as one of the dietary parameters in patients before and after the complete denture treatment. A sample size of 58 was obtained and was increased by 15% to account for the loss to follow-up to a total of 67 which was rounded up to 70.

Allocation

A total of 70 patients were randomly allocated into intervention (n = 35) and control (n = 35) groups in permuted unequal blocks of 6 and 8 subsequently in an allocation ratio of 1:1. The randomization was concealed using sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes which were opened serially when the patient was enrolled in the trial.

Statistical analysis

The baseline characteristics of the participants such as age, height, weight, MMSE, and BMI of the two groups were evaluated using the independent sample t-test. The primary outcome for nutritional status was analyzed as the change in the values of MNA scores. For comparison within groups, a paired sample t-test was performed and analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) was performed to evaluate the effect of the intervention on the nutritional status and nutrient intake among the participants. Levene’s test for homogeneity of variance was performed and the normality of data was tested using the Shapiro–Wilk test. A significance level of 0.05 was set for all tests. All statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 28 (SPSS Inc., IBM, Chicago, IL, USA).

RESULTS

Among the 70 completely edentulous participants, a total of 68 completed the trial. One participant from the intervention group was lost to follow-up due to death and one participant from the control group was lost to follow-up due to lack of interest to participate. The mean age of the participants enrolled in the study was 65.57 (standard deviation [SD] =6.05) and 65.77 (SD = 4.90) years in both intervention and control groups, respectively. DISCUSSION

Malnutrition is a prevailing concern among the geriatric population globally. Geriatric nutrition focuses on the dietary needs of the elderly and the different nutritional issues that arise from changes associated with aging, any illness, or systemic condition.[67] Dental status mainly affects diet, nutritional status, and overall health of an individual. However, the impact of edentulism and prosthetic restoration on the nutritional status of geriatrics varies significantly.[14] Ellis et al. found that edentulous subjects with complete dentures experience reduced masticatory efficiency and adapt through various methods of chewing and grinding the food to ensure adequate dietary intake.[141516] Complete denture wearers frequently alter their diet in one of two ways: (i) by the gradual elimination of difficult-to-chew foods; or (ii) through modifications in cooking practices to adjust food texture, which can be considered a contributing factor to malnutrition.

This trial aimed to evaluate if a combination of complete denture treatment with tailored dietary intervention can affect nutrient intake in geriatric patients. According to Harris et al., the dental setup could provide a suitable environment for conducting dietary assessment and counseling.[17] Moreover, numerous studies demonstrated that one-on-one dietary counseling along with dental care could influence the dietary habits of patients.[26811]

In this trial, patients in the intervention group reported a statistically significant difference in BMI (P = 0.03) at the 3-month assessment, which could be the result of the nutritional counseling and dietary advice provided. However, no significant differences were found in the protein, fat, dietary fiber, calcium, iron carbohydrate, and Vitamin B12 content. Previous studies have demonstrated that protein intake plays a major role in maintaining muscle quantity due to insufficient nutrient intake among the elderly population.[891011] Several assessment tools for measuring nutritional status have been employed in studies, including the MNA form. In a study by Shivraj et al., the nutritional status of the elderly was analyzed using the MNA scores in the Indian population, and it was found that approximately 11.6% were malnourished, whereas 46% were at risk of malnutrition and 42.4% were well-nourished.[7] In addition, studies were diverse and they did not utilize reliable and validated methods for assessing nutrition, they were underpowered and did not consider the possible confounding factors.[1819]

In the present study, the MNA scores of the edentulous participants were lower before receiving complete denture treatment, suggesting that oral health could be linked to nutritional status. Therefore, prosthetic treatment of patients with complete dentures was able to counteract the effects of tooth loss and had fewer eating difficulties. The results were consistent with a study conducted by Lamy et al.,[15] among the institutionalized elderly.

Factors such as poor socioeconomic status, constraints of time, and cultural psychology affect compliance with dietary recommendations.[19] A systematic review by Vanzella et al. reported that financial situation and personal reasons hinder the adoption of healthy eating habits.[19] People may not adopt new diets due to uncertainty about the effects on their health and physical appearance, lack of motivation, trouble in controlling their appetites, and environmental factors such as lack of support or limited access to recommended foods. In addition to this, lack of knowledge, language barriers, and social factors were also considered barriers.

This trial had some limitations. The present study found that the majority of the patients belonged to either lower or middle-class socioeconomic status. This might limit the generalizability of the study. Larger studies involving participants from different socioeconomic and cultural backgrounds are warranted. Second, our assessment period was restricted to 3 months. Changing dietary habits are a multifactorial time-dependent adaptation process and therefore can influence the effectiveness of dietary intervention. Therefore, better outcomes may be found with repeated assessment over a longer period.

Nutritional studies usually pose challenges with respect to implementation and sustainability. Therefore, future work is required that focuses mainly on the implementation strategies and sustainability to strengthen nutritional interventions. In addition, interventional studies that focus on behavior modifications are also necessary for an improved nutritional status. Moreover, for a dietary intervention to be effective, it is very important to have a collaboration between the dentists and nutritionists to formulate a diet plan.[20] Robust strategies and a standardized approach are required to accurately assess the influence of dental function on diet and nutritional outcomes and to facilitate cross-study comparisons.

As apparent from the results of the study, there was improvement seen in the MNA scores and BMI of patients receiving dietary intervention. There was no significant improvement in other nutrient intakes. This could be attributed to the short assessment period. However, patient education and nutritional counseling can potentially aid in creating awareness of nutritional issues among the patients, eventually leading to an improvement in their nutritional status.

CONCLUSION

Within the limitations of the study, the intervention group that received complete denture treatment and tailored progressive dietary advice exhibited a significant improvement in the MNA scores and BMI at 3 months after the intervention when compared to the control group.

Although the nutrient intake such as protein, dietary fiber, total fat, calcium, carbohydrate, energy, iron, and Vitamin B12 was higher in the intervention group when compared with the control group, it was not statistically significant. This could be due to a shorter assessment time. Customized dietary counseling might potentially enhance the overall nutritional status of edentulous patients.

This study involved teamwork between prosthodontists and nutritionists that provided a holistic approach toward prosthetic and nutritional requirements of completely edentulous patients. The patient-centered approach could be adopted by dentists in clinical practice for positive nutritional outcomes, especially with patients who are at risk of malnutrition or malnourished.

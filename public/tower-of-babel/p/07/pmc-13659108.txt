Prevalence of Sarcopenia in Korean Adults Based on the Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia 2025 Criteria: Analysis of the 2024 Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey
Authors: Jun-Il Yoo, Yonghan Cha, Deog-Yoon Kim, Young-Kyun Lee, Yong-Chan Ha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia (AWGS) updated its diagnostic guideline in 2025 to incorporate body mass index (BMI)-adjusted cut-off values for muscle mass and extended diagnostic criteria to middle-aged adults (50–64 years). Using nationally representative data from the 2024 Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (KNHANES), we examined how the new AWGS 2025 criteria influence the prevalence of sarcopenia among Korean adults and characterized age- and sex-specific patterns.MethodsThe study population comprised 2,366 adults aged ≥ 50 years (876 men and 1,490 women) who participated in the 2024 KNHANES. Participants were stratified by sex and age group (50–64 years and ≥ 65 years). Low muscle mass was assessed using dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) and bioimpedance analysis (BIA) according to AWGS 2025 criteria, which incorporate both height-adjusted (kg/m2) and BMI-adjusted (kg/m2 per BMI) thresholds. Prevalence estimates were weighted to account for the complex sampling design.ResultsAmong adults ≥ 80 years, sarcopenia prevalence increased sharply (35.4% in women and 28.8% in men by DEXA). Age-specific prevalence was low in the 50–54 year group (men 2.7%, women 3.2%), rose gradually through the seventies, and increased steeply thereafter. Women exhibited higher prevalence than men in the oldest age group. Comparison between BIA and DEXA measurements showed divergence with age: at 50–54 years, BIA yielded higher appendicular skeletal muscle mass than DEXA (23.77 kg vs. 23.10 kg); at ≥ 80 years, BIA yielded lower values (18.65 kg vs. 20.35 kg).ConclusionApplication of the AWGS 2025 BMI-adjusted criteria identifies a substantial proportion of Korean older adults at risk of sarcopenia, with women aged ≥ 80 years particularly vulnerable. The divergence between BIA and DEXA measurements in very old adults suggests that measurement modality should be carefully considered in clinical practice. These findings provide timely evidence for public health planning as Korea enters a super-aged society.

INTRODUCTION

Sarcopenia refers to the progressive loss of skeletal muscle mass and function with aging and is linked to frailty, falls, fractures, metabolic disorders, and increased mortality.123 Recognizing its clinical significance, the World Health Organization included sarcopenia in the International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision as M62.84 in 2016.4 The Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia (AWGS) established consensus diagnostic criteria in 2014 and updated them in 2019 and most recently in 2025.567 The AWGS 2019 guideline defined low muscle mass as a height-adjusted appendicular skeletal muscle index (SMI) < 7.0 kg/m2 in men and < 5.4 kg/m2 in women by dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) and < 7.0 kg/m2 in men and < 5.7 kg/m2 in women by bioimpedance analysis (BIA).6

However, given the rising prevalence of obesity in Asia and growing recognition of sarcopenic obesity, the AWGS 2025 consensus introduced several key refinements.7 First, body mass index (BMI)-adjusted cut-off values were added to better identify individuals with high fat mass but inadequate muscle relative to body size. Second, the diagnostic scope was expanded to include middle-aged adults (50–64 years) with validated early screening thresholds. Third, the diagnostic algorithm was simplified to require only concurrent low muscle mass and low muscle strength for sarcopenia diagnosis, with physical performance repositioned as an outcome measure rather than a diagnostic criterion.78

Understanding the prevalence of sarcopenia under the new criteria is essential for targeting interventions. Previous estimates using AWGS 2019 cut-offs reported that sarcopenia prevalence increases with age and that women in the oldest age groups are particularly vulnerable.91011 Korea is rapidly aging and became a super-aged society in 2025, with more than 20% of the population aged 65 years or older.12 However, the impact of BMI adjustment and the consistency between measurement modalities (BIA vs. DEXA) under the new AWGS 2025 criteria remain unclear in Korean populations.

Using data from the 2024 Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (KNHANES), this study evaluates the prevalence of sarcopenia in Korean adults based on the AWGS 2025 criteria and examines age- and sex-specific patterns as well as discrepancies between BIA and DEXA measurements.

METHODS

Study population

The KNHANES is an ongoing, nationally representative survey that employs a multistage, stratified sampling design to evaluate the health and nutritional status of the non-institutionalized Korean population.1314 The survey is conducted by the Korea Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (KCDC) and has been a primary source of health statistics for policy development and research. For this cross-sectional analysis, we included participants aged 50 years and older who completed both the health examination and body composition assessments in 2024. Individuals with missing data on skeletal muscle mass or key covariates were excluded.

Participants were stratified by sex (male or female) and by age group (50–54, 55–59, 60–64, 65–69, 70–74, 75–79, and ≥ 80 years). A total of 2,366 participants were included in the final analysis, comprising 876 men and 1,490 women. Sampling weights were applied to account for the complex survey design and to produce nationally representative estimates.

Diagnostic criteria for sarcopenia

Appendicular skeletal muscle mass (ASM) was measured using both DEXA (Lunar Prodigy, GE Healthcare, Madison, WI, USA) and multifrequency BIA (InBody 970, Biospace Co., Ltd, Seoul, Korea). ASM was calculated as the sum of lean soft tissue mass in both arms and legs. The AWGS 2025 guideline specifies different cut-offs by age group and measurement modality.7

For adults aged ≥ 65 years, the traditional AWGS 2019 SMI thresholds are retained (DEXA: men < 7.0 kg/m2 and women < 5.4 kg/m2; BIA: men < 7.0 kg/m2 and women < 5.7 kg/m2), but additional BMI-adjusted cut-offs are introduced (DEXA: men < 0.73 and women < 0.52; BIA: men < 0.83 and women < 0.57).67 For adults aged 50–64 years, early screening cut-offs are used (DEXA: men < 7.2 kg/m2 and women < 5.5 kg/m2; BMI-adjusted DEXA cut-offs < 0.80 and < 0.55 for men and women, respectively; BIA: men < 7.6 kg/m2 and women < 5.7 kg/m2; BMI-adjusted BIA cut-offs < 0.90 and < 0.63).7

Participants meeting either the height-adjusted SMI threshold or the BMI-adjusted threshold were classified as having low muscle mass. Because objective measures of muscle strength (e.g., handgrip strength) and physical performance (e.g., gait speed) were not uniformly available in this dataset, we defined low muscle mass as the primary outcome for prevalence estimation, which aligns with the screening approach recommended by AWGS 2025 for community-based epidemiological studies.715

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were calculated for demographic and clinical characteristics, and differences between men and women were assessed using χ2 tests for categorical variables and t-tests for continuous variables. Age-specific prevalence of low muscle mass was estimated separately for men and women using sampling weights. To evaluate agreement between BIA and DEXA, mean ASM values were compared across age groups. All analyses were conducted using R (version 4.3; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) with the survey package to properly account for the complex sampling design. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Ethics statement

The 2024 Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (KNHANES IX-3) was conducted with approval from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA IRB No. 2022-11-16-R-03), and written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

In the 70–74 year group, prevalence rose to 7.0% in women and 9.2% in men. In the 75–79 year group, 7.9% of women and 10.5% of men met the low muscle mass criteria. The most striking finding was in the ≥ 80 year group, where 35.4% of women and 28.8% of men were classified as having low muscle mass. These patterns underscore the pronounced vulnerability of very old adults, particularly women in the oldest age group.

Comparison of muscle mass measurements (BIA vs. DEXA)

The agreement between BIA and DEXA measurements of ASM varied by age group (Fig. 2). At younger ages (50–54 years), BIA yielded slightly higher mean ASM than DEXA (23.77 kg vs. 23.10 kg). This pattern persisted through the 55–59 year group (BIA: 23.41 kg vs. DEXA: 22.64 kg) and began to converge in the 60–64 and 65–69 year groups.

Starting from the 70–74 year group, DEXA values began to exceed BIA values (DEXA: 20.21 kg vs. BIA: 20.57 kg). The difference between the two methods continued to narrow with advancing age, reaching near-complete agreement in the 75–79 year group (BIA: 19.99 kg vs. DEXA: 19.97 kg). Among participants aged ≥ 80 years, however, DEXA produced substantially higher ASM values than BIA (20.35 kg vs. 18.65 kg), representing a difference of approximately 1.7 kg or 8.4%.

This crossing pattern suggests that BIA may overestimate muscle mass in younger adults and underestimate muscle mass in very old adults, or conversely, that DEXA may underestimate in younger and overestimate in older populations. Similar patterns were observed for regional muscle masses of arms and legs.

DISCUSSION

This study provides the first comprehensive estimate of low muscle mass prevalence in Korean adults based on the AWGS 2025 criteria using nationally representative data. The overall patterns observed are consistent with previous reports: low muscle mass prevalence increases with age and is higher in women at very old ages.9101116 Our findings show that approximately one in three women aged ≥ 80 years and more than one in four men meet the low muscle mass criteria under the new AWGS 2025 guidelines.

The introduction of BMI-adjusted cut-offs in the AWGS 2025 consensus represents an important advancement in sarcopenia diagnosis.717 The Foundation for the National Institutes of Health Sarcopenia Project first proposed appendicular lean mass/BMI ratio cut-offs (< 0.789 for men and < 0.512 for women) based on data from over 25,000 participants, demonstrating that BMI-adjusted muscle mass may better predict adverse functional outcomes than height-adjusted measures alone.1819 The AWGS 2025 consensus adapted this approach with Asia-specific cut-offs, addressing the growing burden of sarcopenic obesity in Asian populations where traditional height-adjusted indices may fail to identify individuals with inadequate muscle mass relative to their body size.720

The marked rise in prevalence at age 80 years and older underscores the need for targeted screening and interventions in this rapidly growing age segment. The high prevalence in women likely reflects sex differences in body composition, hormonal factors including the accelerated muscle loss following menopause, and differences in physical activity patterns.2122 These results support incorporating routine muscle mass screening into geriatric assessments and highlight the importance of maintaining muscle mass through resistance exercise and adequate protein intake.72324

The comparison of BIA and DEXA measurements revealed good agreement in younger and middle-aged adults but significant divergence in the oldest group. This finding has important clinical implications. BIA estimates body composition based on electrical conductivity, which assumes a fixed hydration fraction of fat-free mass (approximately 73%).2526 In older adults, several factors may violate this assumption: decreased intracellular water relative to extracellular water, changes in body water distribution, and altered tissue conductivity due to fat infiltration into muscle.2728

Conversely, DEXA may overestimate muscle mass in older adults due to the inclusion of non-contractile tissue within the lean soft tissue compartment, including intramuscular adipose tissue that increases with aging.2930 Previous validation studies have reported variable agreement between BIA and DEXA depending on age, BMI, and the specific equations used.3132 Our findings suggest that in very old adults (≥ 80 years), clinicians should be cautious when interpreting BIA results and consider confirmatory DEXA assessment when feasible.

The extension of diagnostic criteria to middle-aged adults (50–64 years) in the AWGS 2025 consensus reflects a paradigm shift toward proactive muscle health promotion across the life course.733 Early identification of individuals at risk allows for timely lifestyle interventions before functional decline becomes apparent. Our finding that 2.5–3.4% of middle-aged Korean adults already meet low muscle mass criteria emphasizes the importance of this preventive approach.

This study has several strengths. First, we used nationally representative data from the most recent KNHANES, ensuring generalizability to the Korean population. Second, we applied the newly published AWGS 2025 criteria with both height-adjusted and BMI-adjusted thresholds. Third, we directly compared BIA and DEXA measurements within the same individuals across age groups.

However, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, because objective measures of muscle strength and physical performance were not uniformly available, our prevalence estimates are based on low muscle mass alone rather than confirmed sarcopenia (which requires both low muscle mass and low muscle strength). Second, the cross-sectional design precludes assessment of temporal trends or causal relationships. Third, the 2024 KNHANES may have selection bias due to exclusion of institutionalized individuals, potentially underestimating sarcopenia prevalence in the oldest age groups. Future studies should incorporate handgrip strength and physical performance measures to refine prevalence estimates and assess the full spectrum of sarcopenia severity.

In conclusion, applying the AWGS 2025 BMI-adjusted criteria to the 2024 KNHANES reveals a high prevalence of low muscle mass among Korean adults aged ≥ 80 years, with women particularly affected. The BMI-adjusted thresholds enhance early detection in individuals with high body fat, and the extension of screening to middle-aged adults supports a life-course approach to muscle health promotion. The observed divergence between BIA and DEXA measurements in very old adults warrants careful consideration of measurement modality in clinical practice. These findings provide valuable evidence for public health policy as Korea enters a super-aged society and underscore the need for integrated strategies to prevent and manage sarcopenia at the population level.

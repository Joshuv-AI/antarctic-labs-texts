Xue model exploration for oxytactic microbes in radiative MHD hybrid nanoliquid using machine learning technique
Authors: Shaaban M. Shaaban, Awatef Abidi, Khaled M. Alalayah, Dilsora Abduvalieva, Gulnar Hamidova Abdulhamid, Yakup Yildirim, Hafiz Muhammad Ghazi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The goal of this investigation is to thoroughly analyze and validate the proposed model by using the back-propagation Levenberg–Marquardt (BLM) methodology as an effective training strategy. The efficiency and precision of the suggested methodology have been verified using the generated mean square error (MSE), error histograms (EH), suggested solutions, and regression plots. The impact of temperature and solutal gradients on oxytactic microbes in the bioconvection flow of hybrid nanofluid is briefly analyzed in this paper using the Xue model. This model is useful in biomedical engineering, environmental research, and advanced thermal systems, where microorganism-nanofluid interactions are significant. It aids in the design of microfluidic devices and bioreactors by anticipating the impacts of oxytactic microbes on mass and heat transport under thermal radiation and cross-diffusion (Soret–Dufour) conditions. In nanomedicine, the approach enables regulated medication delivery and tailored therapy via hybrid nanofluid. It is also useful in renewable energy systems, such as bio-inspired cooling devices and solar thermal collectors, where better heat transfer is needed. Furthermore, the machine-learning framework enables more rapid and accurate predictions of complicated bioconvection events in industrial and biological systems.

Introduction

Regression-based models and artificial neural networks are two instances for machine learning (ML) methodologies, have emerged as efficient surrogates for traditional computational fluid dynamics methods by enabling rapid prediction of velocity, pressure, and temperature fields in complex flow configurations. These data-driven approaches significantly reduce computational expense while preserving predictive accuracy, making them attractive for the strategy and improvement of heat exchangers, aerodynamic structures, and energy-efficient thermal systems. Shah et al. [1] examined into machine learning for a model of mono-hybrid nanofluid that transmits solar energy utilizing special multi-layer neural network. For Boger nanofluid thermal bioconvection flow in the existence of gyrotactic microbes, Shaaban et al. [2] investigate MLA based on the Bayesian regularized technique: enzyme-based biosensor applications. The optimization of Adam with oscillatory boundary conditions for MHD radiative thin-film flow in a THNF (ternary hybrid nanofluid) using deep learning were inspected by Wang et al. [3]. Using LTNECs (local thermal non-equilibrium conditions) and heat radiation to resolve the convective trihybrid nanofluid’s flow, Abbas et al. [4] explore an ANN (artificial neural network) optimized using the Bayesian-regularized technique. Haq et al. [5] used the Levenberg–Marquardt back-propagation method to study MHD Micropolar nanofluid with stratified intelligent computing. The development of artificial neural network for Boger hybrid nanofluid Darcy-Forchheimer flow using Cattaneo-Christov theory: thermofluidic implications is studied by Hussein et al. [6]. The thermal study of bioconvection and dual flow propylene glycol NF (nanofluid) across a Riga plate using machine learning was examined by Aslam et al. [7]. Alsaab et al. [8] use hybrid machine learning to quantitatively analyze the ferro-hydrodynamics of blood incorporating magnetic nanocarriers for enhanced medication distribution design. The ML (machine learning) structure for measuring microbial dynamics in hybrid (ZnO-CoFeO₄) flows was investigated by Ahmad et al. [8]. Using thermophoresis and Brownian motion, Shaaban et al. [9] investigated the impacts of Stefan blowing on the flow of BNF (Boger nanofluid) using intelligent predictive neural network analysis. Ahmad and Alsalmah [10] investigated the axisymmetric cylindrical heat exchanger for renewable energy systems' intelligent simulations of solar radiation. Aoudia et al. [11] examined the use of ANNs to improve mass and heat transport in HNF (hybrid nanofluids) with gyrotactic microorganisms and LTNE phenomena. The MHD effects on generated nanofluid flow in an obstructed vertical duct under a constant pressure gradient are examined by Hussain et al. [12]. The design-oriented simulation of a HNF based on TiO₂/Carboxymethyl cellulose-water was inspected by Abbas et al. [13] using the backpropagation bayesian regularization neural network approach. For Ti6Al4V-AA7072-AA7075/NaAlg-based Boger THNF: intelligent Levenberg–Marquardt approach, Liaqat et al. [14] examined the relative assessment of XM and HCM. The Bayesian-regularized neural network study of heat and mass transport in a THNF such as motile microbes were investigated by Abbas et al. [15]. For enhanced drug delivery design using hybrid ML, Alsaab and Althobaiti [16] investigated the quantitative characterization of ferrohydrodynamics of blood including magnetic nanocarriers.

A hybrid nanofluid is a nanofluid composed of several nanoparticles floating in an improper fluid. These nanoparticles might be metallic, nonmetallic, or both. Because of the beneficial effects created by a combination of many types of nanoparticles, the hybrid element in the nanofluid can have superior properties than individual-component nanofluids. Hafeez et al. [17] examined how melting heat transport influenced the MHD flow of a radiative HNF via a wedge. The MHD movement of blood-based HNF, including carbon nanotubes, in the effect of heat radiation across a permeable surface is scrutinized by Rehman et al. [18]. The mathematical performance of MHD hybrid nanofluids flow among two coaxial cylinders with the transmission of heat in radiation was analyze by Bilal et al. [19]. Features of heat transport in HNF with varying heat radiations and viscosities are inspected by Anwar et al. [20]. Malik et al. [21] heat transport and hybrid nanofluid flow in permeable cylinders are investigated using a coupled computational and ANN examination of MHD and radiation effects. The effectiveness of nanomaterials for thermophoresis in a hybrid nanofluid was examined by Shaaban et al. [22].

Microorganisms have an impact on fluid flow, resulting in a dynamic and intricate interplay between fluid particles and germs. Through their movement and interactions, microorganisms like bacteria and algae have an impact on fluid dynamics in aquatic settings. Microorganisms have an impact on blood flow in the human body. White blood cells and platelets interact with pathogens to affect fluid rheology and support vascular health. In environmental science, engineering, and medicinal research, the intricate link between fluid flow and microorganisms is essential because it provides insights into both natural processes and the design of systems vulnerable to microbial influences. Microorganisms have a complex effect on thermal transfer in fluid flow, affecting heat exchange mechanisms in different settings. The characteristics of BLF (boundary layer flow) on permeable sheet with mobile microbes in different wall thermal conditions in terms of mass and heat transportation were scrutinized by Ashfaq et al. [23]. The development of an ANN for the bioconvection flow of gyrotactic microorganism-containing NF via a permeable medium's inclined sheet is explored by Khan et al. [24]. Zeeshan et al. [25] inspect the computational assessment of wedge flow of bioconvective gyrotactic microbes in MHD Carreau fluid with heat radiation using artificial neural networks. Afzal et al. [26] use multilinear regression to simplify analysis using a heat source and model the activity of microbes in a thermal radiation magnetohydrodynamics Casson fluid flow on a stretched sheet. The study of fascinated NF flow in the presence of motile microbes and heat radiation using computational transfer of mass and heat is scrutinized by Chaudhry et al. [27]. The thermal efficiency of CCHFM in the magnetohydrodynamics flow of NF with activation energy and mobile microbes were scrutinized by Malleswari et al. [28].

Particles are moved via the thermos-diffusion process, a phenomena where a temperature gradient is used to compute and combine a number of factors. When the flow regime has a significant density difference, the Dufour and Soret implications occurs. When there is mass transmission and heat instantaneously in liquid motion, the relationship between fluxes and driving potentials grows increasingly intricate. When a species' solutal in a chemical chemical reaction consistently deviates from that of other species, this manifestation occurs. The Dufour and Soret influence can facilitate the mutual mass and heat transport for intermediate atomic-weigh gases in binary systems. The research of thermal and solutal radiation in a permeable medium has also been spurred by practical study having important uses in drying technologies, catalytic reactors, nuclear waste storage facilities, heat insulation, geothermal systems, and energy storage units. The Soret, and Dufour implications of Joule heating on MHD rotating nanofluid flow, are performed by Sivanandam et al. [29]. In a thermal engineering research, Hamida et al. [30] examined the MHD Maxwell NF flow driven by solar radiation using gyrotactic microbes and Soret–Dufour effect. Viscous dissipation, soret, and dufour influences in the tangent hyperbolic fluid's magnetohydrodynamics flow via a permeable medium were numerically examined by Rao et al. [31]. The new study of MHD casson nanofluid flow across a stretched cylinder: coupled temperature and NPs transportation effects was examined by Khan et al. [32].

This work is innovative in that it uses the Xue model to examine how heat radiation affects gyrotactic and oxytactic microorganisms in a hybrid nanofluid with Soret—Dufour impacts. In biomedical engineering, environmental applications, and advanced thermal systems, where microorganism–nanofluid interactions greatly impact heat and mass transport, this paradigm is extremely pertinent. By forecasting the impacts of gyrotactic and oxytactic microorganisms under heat radiation and cross-diffusion circumstances, it aids in the design of microfluidic devices, bioreactors, and controlled drug distribution systems. Furthermore, the method can be used in renewable energy systems where improved heat transmission is crucial, including solar thermal collectors and bio-inspired cooling devices. A Levenberg–Marquardt backpropagation neural network is used to quickly forecast and validate flow, temperature, and concentration fields in order to effectively and precisely capture these intricate bioconvection processes. Homotopy analysis method

The Homotopy Analysis Method (HAM) offers a flexible and efficient technique for addressing highly nonlinear differential equations. Its benefits include producing extremely accurate analytical approximations, handling strong nonlinearities without small parameters, and explicitly controlling convergence. HAM can be used to a variety of issues in fluid mechanics, heat transfer, and nanofluid research since it is adaptable and methodically improvable.

Artificial neural network

Machine learning algorithms (MLAs) have been developed in a number of fields of study, including science, engineering, and mathematics, to address challenging real-world issues. The Supervised Learning Algorithm (SLA) mimics the human learning process by solving exercises or instances. In this work, the SLA is employed, and weight adjustments are accomplished by comparing the predicted and known target outputs. The neural network (NN) is given a training dataset, sometimes referred to as a teaching signal, to help with this process. In order to improve performance and ensure that the neural network learns the intended mapping, the model can adjust its internal weights by back-propagating the error among the actual and predicted outputs during training. Conclusion

This model is useful in biomedical engineering, environmental research, and advanced thermal systems, where microorganism-nanofluid interactions are significant. It aids in the design of microfluidic devices and bioreactors by anticipating the results of microbes on mass and heat transport under thermal radiation and cross-diffusion (Soret–Dufour) conditions. In nanomedicine, the approach enables regulated medication delivery and tailored therapy via hybrid nanofluids. It is also useful in renewable energy systems, such as bio-inspired cooling devices and solar thermal collectors, where better heat transfer is needed. Furthermore, the machine learning framework enables more rapid and accurate predictions of complicated bioconvection events in industrial and biological systems.

Histone-modifying enzymes and gastric cancer: Search for potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets based on Mendelian randomization
Authors: Wenbo Liu, Zhiyuan Wang, Zhiran Yang, Bingjie Huo, Yanru Song, Yong Li, Bibo Tan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractGastric cancer, a common and severe malignancy, is associated with unfavorable outcomes and limited therapeutic options. The exploration of the potential link between plasma histone-modifying enzymes and gastric cancer through Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis offers an opportunity to identify new therapeutic targets and biomarkers. In this study, data on plasma histone-modifying enzymes were obtained from the International Working Unit Open genome-wide association studies project, and summary statistics of gastric cancer from the FinnGen study were analyzed. Forward and inverse MR were performed to determine the causal relationship between plasma histone-modifying enzymes and gastric cancer. The principal methodology for MR is the inverse variance weighted (IVW) method. Additionally, a sensitivity analysis was performed to determine the robustness of the findings. Finally, bioinformatics was used for the preliminary functional analysis. Our forward MR analysis revealed that the plasma Set1/Ash2 histone methyltransferase complex subunit ASH2 (ASH2L) was positively associated with gastric cancer risk, and the histone-lysine N-methyltransferase SETMAR (SETMAR) was negatively associated. Inverse MR analysis revealed that gastric cancer incidence was negatively correlated with the expression of plasma histone acetyltransferase KAT6A (KAT6A). These findings were consistent across different statistical methods and were deemed unlikely to have been distorted by horizontal pleiotropy. Furthermore, bioinformatics analysis indicated that ASH2L, SETMAR, and KAT6A are differentially expressed in various tumors and are significantly correlated with both the prognosis of gastric cancer and the infiltration of various immune cells. Thus, plasma histone-modifying enzymes may be causally linked to gastric cancer, and ASH2L, SETMAR, and KAT6A could play crucial roles as biomarkers and therapeutic targets in managing gastric cancer.Keywords: Gastric cancer, Histone-modifying enzymes, Mendelian randomization analysis, Set1/Ash2 histone methyltransferase complex subunit ASH2, Histone-lysine N-Methyltransferase SETMAR, Histone acetyltransferase KAT6A

Introduction

Gastric cancer, a prevalent malignancy, significantly affects human health [1]. The absence of specific symptoms frequently results in a late-stage diagnosis, culminating in a 5-year survival rate of <32 % [2]. Current treatments, including surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy, often fail to achieve desired results, leading to drug resistance and disease progression [3]. Hence, investigating the causes and fundamental mechanisms underlying gastric cancer, identifying novel treatment objectives and biomarkers, and crafting efficacious treatment approaches are of paramount importance. Histone modifications are the core of epigenetics and encompass a range of chemical alterations catalyzed by specific enzymes. These modifications play vital roles in controlling the chromatin structure and gene expression. Changes in histone posttranslational modifications can lead to abnormal transcriptional processes that are crucial for the development and progression of tumors. For example, regulation of the histone marker H3K36me2 has been linked to alterations in epithelial plasticity and the advancement of metastasis on a global scale [4], while histone lactylation drives tumorigenesis by promoting YTHDF2 expression in ocular melanoma [5]. With the advancement of immunotherapy, patients with gastric cancer [6] and other cancers have benefited greatly from immunotherapy. Key genes such as PD1 [7], PDL1 [8], and CTLA-4 [9] have emerged as critical targets, though the regulatory mechanisms of immune cells are yet to be fully elucidated. Additionally, aberrant histone modifications affect the function of CD8+ T cells [10], macrophages [11], and other immune cells in tumors, underscoring the significant research and application potential of histone modification regulation.

Nevertheless, the association between histone modifications and gastric cancer has not been fully understood, and only a few studies have evaluated the vulnerability of gastric cancer to confounding factors. Mendelian randomization (MR), akin to randomized controlled trials, utilizes genetic information as an instrumental variable for exposure. Allele assignment during meiosis is independent of environmental exposure, limiting the susceptibility of rigorous MR analyses to potential confounders, allowing us to infer causal relationships when statistical assumptions are met [12]. As genome-wide association studies (GWAS) of the human plasma proteome continue to advance, methods for identifying biomarkers by integrating genomic and proteomic databases are being developed [13]. MR studies using SNPs derived from protein-associated SNPs have identified potential new targets in breast and lung cancers and have produced reliable results [14,15]. In this study, plasma histone-modifying enzyme-related data from the International Working Unit (IEU) Open GWAS project were obtained, along with summary statistics of gastric cancer from the FinnGen study. We used forward and inverse MR analyses to investigate the causal association between plasma histone-modifying enzymes and gastric cancer, identify potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets for gastric cancer. Moreover, we performed preliminary bioinformatics analysis. The entire study process is depicted in a flowchart in Fig. 1.

Methods

Data acquisition

To gather plasma histone-modifying enzyme-related data, publicly accessible GWAS summary data were obtained from the (IEU) Open GWAS Project [16,17]. These comprised assays including ID: prot-a-185, prot-a-2704, prot-a-1676, prot-a-184, prot-a-1905, prot-a-2703, prot-a-2904, prot-a-914, prot-a-1309, prot-a-1320, prot-a-1607, prot-a-1983, prot-a-2625, and prot-a-2626, resulting in a comprehensive sample size of 3301 plasma histone-modifying enzymes, all from European populations. Additionally, we obtained gastric cancer-related GWAS data (ID: C3_STOMACH_EXALLC) from the FinnGen study [18], which encompasses data from 1423 European patients with gastric cancer and 314,193 controls. The FinnGen study is a collaborative effort between research organizations, biobanks in Finland, and international industry partners, aiming to analyze more than 500,000 Finnish biobank samples and understand disease mechanisms and predispositions through genetic variation. This study used publicly available data and thus did not require supplementary ethical approval or consent.

Instrumental variable selection for Mendelian randomization studies

The selection of instrumental variables was based on three critical hypotheses. (1) Genetic variations are strongly associated with exposure factors. (2) Genetic variations are not linked to confounding factors. (3) Genetic factors influence outcomes solely through exposure factors [19]. Guided by these hypotheses, SNPs that demonstrated a strong association with exposure factors were chosen as instrumental variables, utilizing correlation analysis and adhering to the screening criterion of P < 5E-06. SNPs with linkage disequilibrium (r2 > 0.001, genetic distances <10,000 kb) were removed to ensure the selection of robust instrumental variables [20,21]. SNPs with minor allele frequency greater than 0.01 in exposure and outcome were further selected. Finally, to reduce bias interference, weak instrumental variables with an F-check value of <10 were also excluded.

Mendelian randomization analysis

To understand the causal link between plasma histone-modifying enzymes and gastric cancer, we applied a robust approach involving five different methods. These included MREgger, weighted median, inverse variance weighted (IVW), simple, and weighted weighting [22,23]. IVW was utilized as the primary method for analytical evaluation, with a significance threshold of P < 0.05. Results meeting this threshold were considered statistically significant and deemed positive.

Sensitivity analysis

Sensitivity analyses included testing for heterogeneity and horizontal pleiotropy. Cochran's Q test and funnel plots were used to evaluate heterogeneity and stability of the results. The occurrence of horizontal pleiotropy was examined through the MR‒Egger intercept and MR-PRESSO global tests [24]. A value of P > 0.05 suggested the absence of heterogeneity or horizontal pleiotropy. We also evaluated whether significant outcomes were influenced by a single SNP by conducting a leave-one-out analysis.

Bioinformatics

Differential expression analysis of genes between tumors and adjacent normal tissues in all TCGA tumors was performed with TIMER 2.0 (http://timer.comp-genomics.org/) using box plots to show the distribution of gene expression levels [[25], [26], [27]]. Statistically significant differences in genes were determined using the Wilcoxon test: ∗p < 0.05; ∗∗p < 0.01; ∗∗∗p < 0.001. Association analyses between gene expression and immune-infiltrating cells (CD8+ T lymphocytes, CD4+ T lymphocytes, neutrophils, macrophages, and myeloid dendritic cells) in gastric cancer were also performed. Survival analysis was performed using the KMPlot platform (www.kmplot.com) platform [28].

Statistical analysis

All MR analyses in this study were statistically analyzed using R 4.2.3 software, mainly using the two-sample MR and MR-PRESSO packages. The odds ratio (OR) and 95 % confidence interval (CI) were used to express the association between plasma histone-modifying enzymes and gastric cancer risk. A p value less than 0.05 was considered to indicate the potential for a causal connection between the exposure and the resultant health outcome.

Results

Forward Mendelian randomization analysis

In the forward MR analysis, plasma histone-modifying enzymes were used as exposure factors, and gastric cancer was used as the outcome. The SNPs that demonstrated strong and effective associations were extracted and screened separately. Counts of the screened SNPs are shown in Fig. 2. MR analysis revealed a significant positive association between the plasma histone-modifying enzymes Set1/Ash2 histone methyltransferase complex subunit ASH2 (ASH2L) and gastric cancer (IVW OR = 1.24, 95 % CI = 1.06–1.44, P = 0.008). Conversely, the histone-lysine N-methyltransferase SETMAR exhibited a distinguishable negative correlation with gastric cancer (IVW OR = 0.78, 95 % CI = 0.65–0.94, P = 0.009). However, no substantial causal relationships were observed between other plasma histone-modifying enzymes and gastric cancer (Fig. 2). Scatter plots were drawn for all findings (Fig. S1). The causal effects of the instrumental variables on the outcomes were plotted (Fig. S2). To eliminate the influence of individual SNPs on the overall causal estimates, we also implemented a leave-one-out analysis. The robustness of the causal inference was evaluated through stepwise removal of each SNP and subsequent repetition of MR analysis. The results remained relatively stable in the leave-one-out analysis in which each SNP was sequentially removed (Fig. S3). The funnel plot showed a symmetrical distribution of SNPs (Fig. S4). Cochran's Q test, MR Egger intercept test, and MR-PRESSO global test all reported p-values greater than 0.05, signifying the absence of heterogeneity and horizontal pleiotropy (Table S1). Thus, our results indicated a causal association of plasma ASH2L and the SETMAR with gastric cancer.

Mendelian randomization analysis of histone-modifying enzymes and gastric cancer (IVW forest plot).

Inverse Mendelian randomization analysis

Using gastric cancer as the exposure and plasma histone-modifying enzymes as the outcome, nine SNPs strongly associated with gastric cancer were extracted from the MR analysis. The results of the MR analysis showed a significant negative correlation between gastric cancer incidence and the plasma histone acetyltransferase KAT6A (KAT6A) (IVW OR = 0.89, 95 % CI = 0.83–0.96, P = 0.001), and there was no significant causative relationship between gastric cancer incidence and other plasma histone-modifying enzymes (Fig. 3). Scatter plots were drawn for all findings (Fig. S5). The causal effects of instrumental variables on the outcome were plotted (Fig. S6). The inverse MR analysis also included a leave-one-out analysis to evaluate the individual influence of each SNP on the results, and reliable results were obtained (Fig. S7). According to sensitivity tests, none of the analyses indicated that the results were influenced by heterogeneity or horizontal pleiotropy (Table S2). The symmetry of the SNP distribution was also validated by the funnel plot (Fig. S8). Consequently, our results suggest a causal association between gastric cancer and plasma KAT6A levels.

Mendelian randomization analysis of reverse histone-modifying enzymes and gastric cancer (IVW forest plot).

Bioinformatics analysis of ASH2L, SETMAR and KAT6A expression and significance in gastric cancer

analysis of the expression of ASH2L in tumor versus adjacent non-tumor tissues highlighted that ASH2L is differentially expressed in several cancer types. Notably, gastric cancer tissues exhibited increased ASH2L expression (P < 0.01) (Fig. 4A). Prognostic analysis revealed that ASH2L was significantly associated with post-progression survival (PPS) but not with overall survival (OS) in patients with gastric cancer (Fig. 4B). Comprehensive tumor-infiltrating immune cell analysis of ASH2L revealed that ASH2L was positively correlated with CD8+ T lymphocytes, CD4+ T lymphocytes, neutrophils, macrophages, and myeloid dendritic cells in gastric cancer (Fig. 4C). Pan-cancer analysis of SETMAR expression in tumor and paracancerous tissues also revealed that SETMAR was differentially expressed in a variety of cancers; however, its differential expression in gastric cancer tissues was not significant (Fig. 4D). Prognostic analysis revealed that SETMAR score was significantly associated with PPS and OS in patients with gastric cancer (Fig. 4E). A thorough investigation of tumor-infiltrating immune cells using the SETMAR method revealed a positive correlation between the SETMAR score and the abundance of CD8+ T lymphocytes, CD4+ T lymphocytes, macrophages, and myeloid dendritic cells specifically in gastric cancer (Fig. 4F). Finally, pan-cancer analysis of KTA6A expression in tumor and paracancerous tissues revealed differential KTA6A expression across multiple cancers, with significantly higher KTA6A expression in gastric cancer tissues (P < 0.001) (Fig. 4G). Prognostic analysis revealed that KTA6A expression in these patients was significantly correlated with OS and PPS (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4H). A subsequent comprehensive analysis of the effect of KTA6A expression on tumor-infiltrating immune cells revealed its positive correlation with the number of CD8+ T lymphocytes, CD4+ T lymphocytes, neutrophils, macrophages, and myeloid dendritic cells in gastric cancer (Fig. 4I).

Discussion

With the continuous progress of related research, histone-modifying enzymes such as KDM6A, KDM5, and KMT2D have been proven to serve as biomarkers and therapeutic targets for tumors [[29], [30], [31]]. Inhibitors that regulate histone modifications such as revumenib—a menin inhibitor with good efficacy and safety in the treatment of KMT2A rearrangement or NPM1-mutant leukemia—have also been shown to be effective in the treatment of tumors [32]. Adademstat (ORY-1001) is an inhibitor of lysine-specific histone demethylase 1A. It is currently used to treat patients with relapsed or refractory acute myeloid leukemia. These patients demonstrated a favorable safety profile and activity when this inhibitor was administered as a single agent [33]. Previous studies have highlighted the critical role of histone modifications in gastric cancer. For example, the histone methyltransferase SUV39H2 has been shown to promoting gastric cancer proliferation and inhibit apoptosis both in vitro and in vivo [34]. Additionally, panobinostat, a pan-histone deacetylase inhibitor, enhances the sensitivity of gastric cancer cells to anthracyclines by inducing CITED2 [35]. However, the key therapeutic targets and biomarkers have not been identified, and the mechanism of action is remains unclear.

Simultaneously, immunotherapy has made breakthroughs in tumor treatment. Sintilimab prolong the survival time of patients treated for gastric cancer, while CD8+T cells [8], macrophages [36], and other immune cells play key roles. Immune infiltration in cancer is reportedly associated with alterations in cancer genes, and many studies have shown that histone-modifying enzymes such as KDM2A [37] affect the tumor microenvironment in a variety of cancer types, including gastrointestinal, gastric, and colon cancers. Our study focused on the differential expression, causal relationship, and correlation with immune cells of the newly discovered ASH2L\KAT6A\SETMAR in gastric cancer, which may play an important role in the immune response in cancer prevention and treatment and can be regarded as a new target to enhance the effect of cancer immunotherapy. Employing the MR method, this study aimed to investigate the causal association between plasma histone-modifying enzymes and gastric cancer while seeking potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets for gastric cancer. ASH2L has been shown to promote the development of endometrial cancer, pancreatic cancer, and breast cancer by altering the level of H3K27 methylations [[38], [39], [40]]. SETMAR can prevent glioblastoma recurrence by inhibiting the SETMAR-H3K36me2-NHEJ repair axis in residual lesion cells [41]. NONO selectively splices SETMAR to inhibit lymphatic metastasis in bladder cancer [42], and KAT6A regulates the level of histone modifications in the nucleus to significantly affect the progression of leukemia and hepatocellular carcinoma [43,44]. However, ASH2L and SETMAR have not been detected in gastric cancer, and KAT6A has been confirmed to be highly expressed only in gastrointestinal tumors [45]. MR analysis showed that the expression levels of ASH2L and SETMAR were positively and negatively correlated with the risk of gastric cancer, respectively. In addition, the expression of the plasma histone-modifying enzyme KAT6A was decreased in patients with gastric cancer. Bioinformatics analysis showed that ASH2L and KAT6A were highly expressed in gastric cancer tissues, revealing a potential correlation between these genes and the prognosis of gastric cancer and the state of immune cell infiltration. KAT6A is highly expressed as an oncogene in cancer cells and tissues in triple-negative breast and ovarian cancers [46,47]. Our study showed that the development of gastric cancer may lead to a decrease in KAT6A expression in plasma, potentially involving its redistribution between plasma and tissue. This change may be linked to deeper gene regulation mechanisms. Further research is needed to determine whether these three markers function through nuclear proteins or plasma-secreted proteins and to elucidate their specific mechanisms in gastric.

The main strength of this study is its two-sample MR design, which reduces unobserved confounders and reverses causality with a high degree of accuracy. Novel potential biomarkers including ASH2L, SETMAR, and KAT6A, which have not been previously studied in gastric cancer, were identified and validated. This study had certain limitations. A significant constraint of the MR analysis is that the sample size of patients with gastric cancer was only over 1000; a more extensive database is still necessary to validate the findings. Moreover, while the utilization of data exclusively from European populations has enhanced the accuracy of the study's outcomes, large-scale Asian population data is required for comparative studies to achieve a higher level of confidence and accuracy, as well as to generalize the results to the population. Horizontal pleiotropy is a prevalent and challenging problem in MR analysis. Sensitivity assessments, such as the MR‒Egger intercept test and MR Presso global test, were performed, and no evident horizontal pleiotropy was detected. Nevertheless, the potential for bias cannot be entirely dismissed. In summary, although the findings of this study affirm a potential causal relationship between plasma histone-modifying enzymes and gastric cancer, highlighting ASH2L, SETMAR, and KAT6A as potential therapeutic targets and biomarkers, further investigation is essential to fully understand the underlying mechanisms of these effects. This study provides a theoretical foundation for gastric cancer research and contributes to the ongoing improvements in diagnostic and therapeutic methodologies for this disease.

Conclusion

This study revealed the potential causal role of plasma histone-modifying enzymes in gastric cancer. ASH2L, SETMAR, and KAT6A may be potential therapeutic targets and biomarkers for gastric cancer development. These findings open new avenues for envisioning potential therapeutic interventions and identifying biomarkers of gastric cancer, laying a theoretical foundation for elucidating the pathogenesis of gastric cancer.

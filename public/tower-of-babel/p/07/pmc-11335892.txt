WRKY transcription factor 40 from eggplant (Solanum melongena L.) regulates ABA and salt stress responses
Authors: Aidong Zhang, Jing Shang, Kai Xiao, Min Zhang, Shengjie Wang, Weimin Zhu, Xuexia Wu, Dingshi Zha
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Plants are affected by many environmental factors during their various stages of growth, among which salt stress is a key factor. WRKY transcription factors play important roles in the response to stress in plants. In this study, SmWRKY40 from eggplant (Solanum melongena L.) was found to belong to the subfamily of WRKY transcription factor group II, closely related to the evolution of wild tomato ScWRKY40 (Solanum chilense). The expression of SmWRKY40 could be induced by several abiotic stresses (drought, salt, and high temperature) and ABA to different degrees, with salt stress being the most significant. In Arabidopsis thaliana, the seed germination rate of SmWRKY40 overexpression seedlings was significantly higher than those of the wild type under high concentrations of NaCl and ABA, and root elongation of overexpression lines was also longer than wild type under NaCl treatments. SmWRKY40 overexpression lines were found to enhance Arabidopsis tolerance to salt with lower ROS, MDA, higher soluble protein, proline accumulation, and more active antioxidant enzymes. The expression level of genes related to stress and ABA signaling displayed significant differences in SmWRKY40 overexpression line than that of WT. These results indicate that SmWRKY40 regulates ABA and salt stress responses in Arabidopsis.

Introduction

Normal growth, development, and reproduction of plants require suitable environmental conditions. However, plants inevitably face a variety of unstable abiotic stresses, such as high temperature, low temperature, salt stress, drought, et al.1. Among them, salt stress is one of the main environmental factors that affects the yield and quality of crops and cause serious losses to the agricultural economy1–4. Salt stress affects cytomembrane repairation of seed imbibition and damages seed structure and enzymes activity, casuing the accumulation of toxic substances. The excessive toxic substances further disturb selective permeability and stability of membrane system, interfering with normal metabolism and even causing complete inactivation and death of seed. In addition, salt stress also inhibits photosynthesis, vegetative growth, respiration and metabolism of plants. Salt stress includes ion stress, osmotic stress, and secondary stress5. The ion stress is casued by ions accumulation of cells. The Na+ ions absorbed by roots is transported over long distance in the transpiration stream to the aboveground part and eventually accumulate in the leaves6. High concentration Na+ affects the absorption of other ions, which adversely influences metabolic process. The osmotic stress results from the effect of high salt concentrations in soil or water, decreasing the root surface water potential and thus reducing water uptake of plants7. Ion stress and osmotic stress cause secondary stress, including the accumulation of toxic substances and the destruction of nutrient balance. For example, reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulate in plant cells and seriously damage cell structure and macromolecules such as enzymes, DNA and lipids8,9.

In nature, plants have developed a series of mechanisms to adapt and resist various adverse stresses through long-term adaptation and evolution1,2,10–12. Transcription factors play an important role in regulating downstream gene expression and coordinating the interactions between signaling pathways. With the development of modern molecular biology, many important plant transcription factors have been identified and studied12–15.WRKY family is one of the largest families of transcription factors in plants, regulating a variety of physiological activities, including plant growth, development, and stress response. WRKY transcription factors contain at least 60 highly conserved amino acid sequences, with a conserved WRKYGQK at the N-terminal and a zinc-finger motif at the C-terminal 11,14. According to the number of WRKY domains and the characteristics of the zinc finger structure, the WRKY family can be divided into three subfamilies: subfamily I, which contains two WRKY domains, subfamily II, which contains one WRKY domain and C2H2 zinc finger structure, and subfamily III, which contains one WRKY domain and a C2HC zinc finger structure. The subfamily II was further divided into five subgroups according to the amino acid core sequence11,14. Compared with biotic stress, studies on the function of WRKY transcription factors under abiotic stress have started relatively late. In recent years, several studies have found that WRKY transcription factors also play an important role in abiotic stress in plants. BnaA10.WRKY75 increases sensitivity to cadmium and salt stress by disrupting the balance of reactive oxygen species both in Arabidopsis and rapeseed16. Tomato WRKY transcription factor WRKY57 acted as a negative regulator in salt stress response by directly attenuating the transcription of salt-responsive genes (SlRD29B and SlDREB2) and an ion homeostasis gene (SlSOS1)17. In tomato, SlWRKY8 can promote resistance to pathogen infection, drought and salt stresses18. WRKY transcription factor ZmWRKY86 participates in salt stress regulation through controlling the expression of stress-related genes in maize19. A wheat WRKY transcription factor TaWRKY17 enhances salt-stress tolerance in Arabidopsis and wheat20. In addition, the plant hormone abscisic acid (ABA) is known to play an important role in the response of plants to stress. Abiotic stress can induce ABA synthesis in plants and inhibit ABA degradation to increase ABA concentrations in plants21,22. It can induce stomatal closure and reduce water loss in plants at certain concentrations22. Plant signal transduction can be carried out through ABA-dependent and ABA-independent pathways under abiotic stress22–26. Recently, some WRKY genes have been reported to respond to abiotic stress through the ABA signal transduction pathways. Soybean GmWRKY16 gene allogeneic transformation of Arabidopsis can increase drought and salt stresses tolerance through ABA regulation pathway27. In Populus, PalWRKY77 transcription factor negatively regulates salt tolerance and ABA signaling by targeting PalNAC002 and PalRD26 genes28. GbWRKY1 acts as a negative regulator of ABA signaling, through an interaction network involving JAZ1 and ABI1, to regulate salt and drought tolerance29.

WRKY40, as one of WRKY family members, plays multiple roles in plant. CaWRKY40 orthologues in pepper are regulated by salicylic acid (SA), jasmonic acid (JA), and ethylene (ET) signaling and coordinate responses to R. solanacearum attacks and heat stress in pepper and tobacco30. Two research groups reported their studies on three WRKY genes (AtWRKY18, AtWRKY40 and AtWRKY60). Shang et al.31 found that these three genes functioned as negative regulators of ABA signaling in seed germination and postgermination growth. Among them, AtWRKY40 played as a central negative regulator, which directly binds to ABA responsive genes, such as ABI4 and ABI5, and inhibited their expression level under high concentration of ABA. Chen et al.32 found that these three genes played a positive role in ABA sensitivity for inhibition of seed germination and root growth and increased plant sensitivity to salt and osmotic. Boro et al.33 found that elevated glutathion (GSH) level increased the WRKY40 binding to MPK3 promoter, consequently activating MPK3 under combined osmotic and cold stress. The homologue of AtWRKY40 in kumquat, FcWRKY40, is induced by ABA and salt treatment and regulates salt stress by activating the SOS pathway34. To explore the role of WRKY40 gene of eggplant, we obtained SmWRKY40 gene from homology alignment. Seldom researches about WRKY transcription factors were conducted in eggplant. As the limitation of genetic transformation, most of them were concentrated on gene family identification and physiological analysis. In this study, we cloned a WRKY family transcription factor SmWRKY40 (SMEL4.1_03g026050.1.01) and performed bioinformatic analyses. The salt-resistance of transgenic Arabidopsis under salt stress was determined. The function of the SmWRKY40 gene under salt stress was analyzed according to the experiment results. This study will provide theoretical basis for the selection of salt-resistant varieties of eggplant in the future.

Results

Bioinformatic analysis of SmWRKY40

Nucleotide sequence of SmWRKY40 was obtained from the Sol Genomics Network. Its open reading frame of SmWRKY40 was 1020 bp and encoded 339 amino acids, as predicted by the national center for biotechnology information (NCBI) conserved domain analysis website. The results showed that SmWRKY40 protein contained a WRKY conserved domain in the AA region of 167–225, and the C-terminal zinc finger structure was C2H2 type (Supplementary Fig. S1).

WRKY amino acid sequences with high similarity in different plants were obtained from NCBI according to SmWRKY40 amino acid sequence retrieval and alignment. The eggplant SmWRKY40 was mixed with tomato (NP_001303844.1), potato (XP_006341684.1), pepper (XP_016562883.1), tobacco (XP_016457007.1), coffee (XP_027167435.AWS00952.1), and pomegranate (XP_0313856801), and it was found that their amino acid sequences were consistent, and they all contained the typically conserved domains, WRKYGQK and C2H2 zinc finger structures, of WRKY (Fig. 1a).Figure 1Bioinformatics analysis of SmWRKY40. Phylogenetic tree was constructed by analyzing the similarities between the WRKY protein and SmWRKY40 in different species, and the results showed that the evolutionary distance between SmWRKY40 and wild tomato was the shortest among these species, with a high sequence identity of 84.20% (Supplementary Table S1; Fig. 1b). SmWRKY40 was closely clustered with WRKY40 in Solanum chilense, Solanum lycopersicum, Solanum tuberosum, Capsicum annuum, and Nicotiana attenuate, which are all solanaceae, indicating evolutionary conservation of SmWRKY40 (Fig. 1b).

The physicochemical properties of SmWRKY40 protein were predicted by ProtParam. The results showed that the molecular formula of SmWRKY40 was C1614H2592N476O541S10, the molecular weight was 37641.80 kD, and the instability coefficient (II) was 45.17 (greater than 40). According to Guruprasad analysis35, SmWRKY40 may be an unstable protein with a total of 46 positively charged residues (Arg + Lys) and negatively charged residues (Asp + Glu) and an isoelectric point of 8.84.

The hydrophilic and hydrophobic analyses showed that the 299 locus (Glu) was the most hydrophilic, the 326 locus (Leu) was the most hydrophobic, and their hydrophobicity scores were -3.400 and 1.844, respectively (Fig. 1c). Furthermore, the average hydrophobicity (Gravy) was − 0.923 (Fig. 1c), indicating that a greater number of hydrophilic amino acids were encoded by SmWRKY40 than hydrophobic amino acids, indicative of a hydrophilic protein.

Using the online analysis website PlantCARE, an online prediction analysis of the cis-acting elements of the upstream DNA base of SmWRKY40 gene was performed36. The results showed that the promoter of the SmWRKY40 gene contained multiple optical response elements, such as the 3-AF1 binding site, G-box, GA-motif, and TCCC-motif. There are also several cis-acting elements associated with stress, such as ABRE, CGTCA-motif, TC-rich repeats, and TGACG-motif. It is also worth mentioning that the SmWRKY40 promoter has three ABRE elements, located at 559 bp, 687 bp, and 1897 bp of promoter region, related to plant stress resistance (Supplementary Table S2). It is predicted that ABA signaling may play an important role in the response to stress in eggplants.

Expression patterns of SmWRKY40

To explore the function of SmWRKY40 in response to different abiotic stresses, drought treatment, salt treatment, high-temperature treatment, and ABA treatment were induced in the seedling stage of eggplant. The expression levels of SmWRKY40 in the leaves of eggplant at different stages under different treatments were analyzed by qRT-PCR. The results showed that the expression of SmWRKY40 in eggplant was significantly upregulated after the treatments. The peak value of SmWRKY40 expression at 1 h after inducing drought stress was 47.0-fold than that at 0 h, and the peak value at 30 min after inducing salt stress was 100.1-fold that at 0 h. As the induced expression level under salt stress was the most significant among different abiotic stresses (Fig. 2a–d), our research mainly focused on salt stress. The seed germination rate and root elongation of Arabidopsis thaliana were different after treatment with different concentrations of NaCl (Figs. 4, 5). The root lengths of three Arabidopsis OE lines and WT plants were analyzed. The results showed that NaCl stress inhibited the root elongation of Arabidopsis seedlings. With increasing concentrations, the inhibition effect of NaCl on the root length of Arabidopsis thaliana became more marked (Fig. 5). In the absence of stress (control treatment), no significant differences were observed between the root lengths of the three OE lines and the WT. Under salt stress, the root lengths of the WT were 27.6 mm, 9.8 mm, and 1.6 mm at 75, 100, and 150 mM NaCl concentrations, respectively. The root lengths of SmWRKY40 OE lines were significantly higher compared with those of the WT under the corresponding treatments, the root lengths were above 28.2 mm, 19.6 mm, and 3.6 mm, respectively. These results suggest that the overexpression of SmWRKY40 enhances the tolerance of Arabidopsis to salt stress.

To investigate the response of transgenic Arabidopsis thaliana SmWRKY40 to NaCl stress, the plant was irrigated with 200 mM NaCl solution continuously for 2 weeks when the seedlings grew to 4 weeks. According to the plant phenotypes, salt stress caused different degrees of damage to Arabidopsis plants. On the seventh day of salt treatment, most of the Arabidopsis leaves in the WT began to wilt, but only the margin of leaves of OE plants turned yellow. In contrast, on the 14th day, while the WT plants were dead, some greenness could be observed in the center of the OE plants, indicating that the leaves of the SmWRKY40 transgenic strain were relatively less affected by salt stress (Fig. 6).Figure 6Phenotypes of wild-type and OE lines of Arabidopsis thaliana under salt stress.

SmWRKY40 conferred tolerance to ABA in transgenic Arabidopsis

As mentioned above, three ABRE elements are predicted in the promotor region of SmWRKY40 (Supplementary Table S2, Supplementary Fig. S2, and Fig. 7a) and the expression level of SmWRKY40 is strongly induced by ABA (Fig. 2d). To evaluate the ABA tolerance of Arabidopsis with SmWRKY40 overexpression, ABA at different concentrations was added to 1/2 MS solid medium, and the germination rate of Arabidopsis seeds were calculated. The statistical results showed that with an increase in ABA concentration, ABA stress could inhibit the germination of Arabidopsis seeds. After treatment with 0.5 μm ABA, the germination of the OE lines occurred significantly earlier than that of the WT, and no significant difference was observed between them from the fifth day. After treatment with 1.0 and 1.5 μm ABA, the germination rate of the OE lines of Arabidopsis SmWRKY40 was significantly higher than that of the WT (Fig. 7b–e). When suffering stress, plants defend against damage by changing a series of physiological and biochemical reactions. Plants release ROS, such as H2O2, O2.−, etc., which trigger oxidative stress in plant cells. The production of MDA can react with various components in cells and can be used to indicate the degree of peroxidation of cell membrane lipids38–40. As shown in Fig. 8, increasing trends in the H2O2 content, O2.− production rate, and MDA content were observed in the leaves of Arabidopsis thaliana WT and three SmWRKY40-OE lines after treatment with 200 mM NaCl solution for one week. Discussion

WRKY TFs are one of the largest family of transcription factors in plants and plays a variety of roles in plants11. Since SPE1 gene was first discovered in sweet potato42, more and more WRKY family genes' functions and mechanisms of action have been explored, especially in biological and abiotic stress of plants. However, the function and mechanism of WRKYs in eggplant are poorly understood. Yang et al.43 identified 58 WRKY transcription factors by searching eggplant genome and characterized cold stress-responsive WRKY genes in eggplant. Recently, Shen et al.44 found that SmWRKY11 acts as a positive regulator by forming positive feedback loop with SmERF1 via an indirect regulatory manner in eggplant response to salt stress. In this study, we obtained SmWRKY40 gene from homology alignment. As SmWRKY40 protein contained a WRKY conserved domain and a C2H2 type structure (Supplementary Fig. S1), we concluded that SmWRKY40 belongs to the group II subfamily of WRKY transcription factors. The physicochemical properties and hydrophilic/hydrophobic analyses of SmWRKY40 protein (Fig. 1c) displayed that it may be a hydrophilic unstable acidic protein. Since the expression level of SmWRKY40 was strongly induced by NaCl (Fig. 2b), we analyzed the phenotype of WT and OE lines under salt stress in Arabidopsis. The OE lines displayed higher germination rate, longer root length, and better growth condition than that of WT under NaCl treatment (Figs. 4, 5, 6). The results indicate that SmWRKY40 has a positive role in salt stress. This is consistent with researches of WRKY40 gene in Arabidopsis thaliana and Fortunella Crassifolia34,43.

In recent years, some studies have shown that WRKY TFs can play an important role in plant abiotic stress by participating in ABA signal transduction in plants11,27–29. ABA can accumulate by ABA-dependent pathway in plants, thereby inhibiting the seed germination rate, seedling growth, and plant development processes under different stresses27. Our results displayed that the SmWRKY40 promoter had three ABRE elements (Supplementary Table S2) and the expression level of SmWRKY40 was induced by ABA treatment (Fig. 2d). Overexpress SmWRKY40 in arabidopsis can improve seed germination when suffering ABA treatment (Fig. 7). As is known previously, salt stress rises ABA level to 40-fold in plant, triggering the closure of stomata as well as the accumulation of osmocompatible solutes and LEA protein45. It is speculated that when suffering salt stress, ABA is accumulated in Arabidopsis, induce SmWRKY40 gene expression and thereby improve tolerance to salt stress.

Previous studies have shown that salt stress causes osmotic stress in plants, leading to increase of ROS and MDA, and peroxidation of lipid and protein, damaging nucleic acids and activating the programmed death of plant cells27. The transformation of resistance genes can eliminate ROS in plants, thereby enhancing plant tolerance to osmotic stress27,38,39. APX, SOD, POD, and CAT are the main antioxidant enzymes in plants. When exposed to external stresses, these enzymes increase in activity and are used to remove ROS, reducing the toxic effect of plants. In this study, the activities of SOD, POD, CAT, and APX all showed an increasing trend after salt stress in Arabidopsis. The activities of these enzymes in three OE lines were significantly higher than those of WT (Fig. 9). Osmotic adjustment substances such as proline and soluble protein can adjust the intracellular osmotic balance and protect the membrane system46,47. When WT and OE plants were exposed to salt stress, we found that the content of proline and soluble protein increased significantly, but the increase was more obvious in OE plants (Fig. 10). This result was consistent with the expression level of AtP5CS1 (Fig. 11c). As proline is synthesized from glutamate or ornithine. AtP5CS1 encoding delta 1-pyrroline-5-carboxylate synthase (P5CS), which is the rate-limiting step in glutamate pathway48.

To investigate the function of SmWRKY40 on genes related to stress and ABA signaling, we examed the expression of some stress-related marker genes AtKIN1, AtRD22, AtCOR15A, AtCOR47, and AtNCED3, which protect plant from stress damage49–51. The expression of these genes is increased to varying degrees (Fig. 11a,c–f). ABI1 and ABI2, which encode two negative ABA signaling regulators52, were significant up regulated in over-expression line than WT, whereas the positive regulators PYR/PYL/RCAR ABA receptor mediated signaling cascade genes PYR1/RCAR11, PYL2/RCAR13, SnRK2.2, and SnRK2.3, were significant down regulated (Fig. 11g–l). Up regulation of negative ABA signaling regulator inhibit ABA signaling and down regulation of receptor related genes probably limited the perception of ABA in transgenic plants, which may have decreased the ABA-induced physiological responses. These results may explain why the transgenic lines exhibited an increased resistance to ABA. These data indicate that overexpression of SmWRKY40 may reduce the damage of salt stress by increasing the activity of antioxidant enzymes, and accumulating soluble protein and proline in Arabidopsis. In summary, the overexpression of SmWRKY40 gene in Arabidopsis can improve the tolerance to salt stress. And the detail of regulation network in eggplant still needs further investigate.

Methods

Plant materials

Eggplant variety “Brika” was independently selected and bred by horticultural research institute of Shanghai Academy of Agricultural Sciences. “Brika”, with green sepal, purple and white striped peel, and a high round fruit shape, was used for gene clone and function verification. Arabidopsis Col-0 was used as receptor plants for the genetic transformation of SmWRKY40 to evaluate the function of this gene.

Sequence analysis of SmWRKY40 gene

The nucleotide and amino acid sequence of the SmWRKY40 gene was obtained from the Sol Genomics Network (Eggplant genome consortium V4.1, https://solgenomics.sgn.cornell.edu/organism/Solanum_melongena/genome). The homologous proteins were obtained from National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) with 100 max target sequence and 10 expect threshold. The sequences we selected were from different species. Their accession numbers and sequences in NCBI were shown in Supplementary Table S3. The homologous protein sequences were aligned using ClustalX software with default parameters53. The phylogenetic tree was constructed using the neighbor-joining method in MEGA7.0, with 1000 bootstrap tests54. The physicochemical properties of SmWRKY40 protein were predicted by ProtParam (https://web.expasy.org/protparam/). The cis-acting elements were predicted by PlantCARE (http://bioinformatics.psb.ugnet.be/webtools/plantcare/html/)36. The visualization of cis-acting elements location was conducted by TBtools software55.

Characteristics of SmWRKY40 gene expression

The eggplant seeds were soaked in water for 5 h at room temperature and heated in 55 °C water for 25 min, then germinated on filter paper at 28 °C in darkness. Germinated seeds were transferred to growth chamber with 16/8 h light/dark photoperiod at 28 °C. Roots, stems, and leaves of eggplant seedlings with 2-true-leaves, mature roots and leaves of eggplant after the appearance of the first flower bud, mature flowers in full bloom, and the fruits of 40 days after pollination were collected as samples to determine tissue expression specificity. The seedlings were treated with 20% PEG6000, 150 mM NaCl, high temperature (42 °C), and 10 mg/L ABA, respectively. Seedling leaves were collected at different time points. Samples were frozen with liquid nitrogen and stored at − 80 °C. Three biological replicates were used for each treatment.

RNA extraction and real-time PCR

Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol Reagent (Invitrogen), and cDNA was synthesized from 1 μg of total RNA using Evo M-MLV RT kit (Accurate Biotechnology, Hunan, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. 20-μl reaction, which contains 2 μl cDNA (diluted 4 times), 8 μl primers (0.05 μM), 10 μl Hieff® qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (Low Rox Plus), was used for real-time PCR analysis on an ABI 7500 (Applied Biosystems). Transcript levels were calculated using the 2 − ΔΔCt method56. SmPGK and AtTUB2 were used as the internal reference gene. Three replicates were performed for the entire experiment. Primer Premier Software (version 5.0) was used for primer design. Primers used in our study were shown in Supplementary Table S4.

Vectors construction

For overexpression construct, SmWRKY40 gene was first PCR-amplified from cDNA using primers consisting of BamHI and KpnI restriction sites (Supplementary Table S4) and inserted into pCambia2301 vector after restriction digestion.

For transient expression in tobacco epidermal cells, a SmWRKY40-GFP gene fusion was constructed under the control of 35S promoter in the pCAMBIA1302 vector. For marker plasmid construct, an OsMADS57-RFP gene fusion was constructed under the control of 35S promoter in the pCAMBIA1300 vector. In detail, a NcoI-SmWRKY40-SpeI cDNA was obtained by amplification of cDNA of Brika. A SacI-OsMADS57-BamHI cDNA was obtained by amplification of cDNA of O. sativa ssp japonica cv Zhonghua10 (ZH10)37. Primers sequences are listed in Supplementary Table S4.

Plant transformation

For transformation experiments, recombinant overexpression vector were transformed into Agrobacterium strain GV3101, which was then transformed Arabidopsis Col-0 using floral-dip method as described by Clough and Bent57. Transgenic seedlings were selected on MS medium with kanamycin followed by further confirmation through PCR and real-time PCR.

Subcellular location of SmWRKY40

The recombinant plasmids (35S:SmWRKY40-GFP and 35S:OsMADS57-RFP) were transferred into Agrobacterium strain EHA105, and then co-resuspended by 10 mM MgCl2 + 120 μM AS liquid to OD600 = 0.6. The resuspended liquid inoculated through the stomata using a 1 ml plastic syringe by applying gentle pressure on the lower epidermal surface of 4-week old tobacco (Nicotiana benthamiana) leaves. The GFP fusion protein was detected by laser scanning confocal microscopy (Nikon C2-ER) after 2 d.

Analysis of stress tolerance in Arabidopsis

The Arabidopsis seeds were sterilized with 5% NaClO: water (v/v) for 8 min and washed five times with distilled water. Seeds were then placed on mediums and vernalized at 4 °C for 2 days before being transferred to a 16 h light /8 h dark cycle chamber at 22 °C. 1/2MS medium was supplemented with different concentrate of NaCl and ABA. Emergence of radicle from the seed coat was used as the standard of seed germination. The germination of Arabidopsis seeds on these mediums was observed by microscopy. Vernalized Arabidopsis thaliana seeds were grown on 1/2MS solid medium for 3 days, and then seedlings with consistent germination were transferred to different treatment mediums for vertical growth. The root length of Arabidopsis thaliana was measured using Image Pro Plus software 6.0 after 7 days of treatment. Vernalized Arabidopsis thaliana seeds were grown on 1/2MS solid medium for 10 days, seedlings were then transferred onto filter paper saturated with 200 mM NaCl in 1/2MS liquid media. The samples were harvested at different time points. For salt stress treatment, the Arabidopsis seeds were grown under optimal culture conditions for 4 weeks under a substrate (vermiculite: peat, 1:1). Arabidopsis seedlings were irrigated with 200 mM NaCl solution for two weeks.

Determination of related physiological indexes in Arabidopsis thaliana

Malondialdehyde (MDA) content was determined as described by Heath et al.58, with some modifications. MDA samples were extracted with phosphoric acid buffer and reacted with 0.6% thiobarbituric acid in a boiling water bath for 15 min.

The determination of H2O2 content was based on a study by Dong et al.59. Briefly, 0.5 g of leaf samples were ground with liquid nitrogen, extracted with 5 ml of acetone, and centrifuged at 12,000 × g and 4 °C for 20 min. Then, 1 ml of the supernatant was mixed with titanium sulfate and incubated at 25 °C for 10 min. The precipitate was dissolved in 3 ml of 2 M H2SO4, and the content of the titanium peroxide complex in the supernatant was determined at a wavelength of 415 nm. The rate of O2.− production was determined as previously described60. Based on the principle that nitrite is generated by the reaction of O2.− with hydroxylamine hydrochloride, the nitrite content was detected to calculate the production rate of O2.−. Three biological replicates were used for each treatment.

CAT activity was measured using a CAT detection kit. CAT sample solution was reacted with H2O2-based solution, and the value of OD240 was measured every 1 min to evaluate the CAT activity. POD and SOD were extracted using phosphoric acid buffer solution, and their activities were determined as described by Liu et al.61 and Aravind et al.62. APX activity was determined as previously reported by Nakano and Asada63. Briefly, 50 mM potassium phosphate buffer, 1 mM H2O2, 0.1 mM EDTA, and 0.5 mM ascorbic acid were mixed and reacted with 3 mL of sample extract. The decrease in the value of the reaction at 290 nm for 1 min was used to calculate the APX activity. Three biological replicates were used for each treatment.

The soluble protein content was measured according to Bradford method64, while the free proline content in plants was determined according to Bates et al.65. Three biological replicates were used for each treatment.

Data analysis

SPSS software was used for data analysis and Origin 9.0 was used for drawing. All data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) of three biological replicates. Tukey's at P = 0.01 or P = 0.05 was used for multiple comparisons of the mean.

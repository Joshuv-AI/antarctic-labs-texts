First-principles study of band gap engineering and thermoelectric performance in K2AgSbX6 (X = Cl, F, I) double perovskites
Authors: K. Bouferrache, M. A. Ghebouli, M. Fatmi, Ahmad M. Saeedi, S. Alomairy, Mustafa Jaipallah Abdelmageed Abualreish, Aseel Smerat
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study presents a comprehensive density functional theory (DFT) investigation of the double perovskite compounds K2AgSbX6 (X = Cl, F, I) using the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) and modified Becke-Johnson (mBJ) exchange-correlation functionals. The electronic, optical, elastic, and thermoelectric properties of these materials have been systematically analyzed to evaluate their potential for photovoltaic and thermoelectric applications. The novelty of this work lies in the comparative band-gap engineering of K2AgSbX6 through halide substitution, together with a combined assessment of optical absorption, mechanical stability, thermoelectric performance, spin–orbit coupling effects, and thermodynamic behavior. The calculated lattice constants, formation energies, and structural stability parameters confirm the thermodynamic stability of all three compounds. Electronic band structure calculations reveal semiconductor behavior with band gaps ranging from 0.373 eV (K2AgSb6) to 2.07 eV (K2AgSbF6) using GGA, and 1.041 eV to 4.11 eV using mBJ approximation. Including spin–orbit coupling slightly reduces the mBJ band gaps to 0.97 eV for K2AgSbI6, 2.42 eV for K2AgSbCl6, and 4.017 eV for K2AgSbF6, with the strongest SOC influence observed for the iodine-based compound. Effective-mass calculations further show that K2AgSbI6 has the lowest carrier effective mass among the studied compounds, with values of 0.204 m0, 0.226 m0, and 0.231 m0 using GGA, mBJ, and mBJ + SOC, respectively, indicating more favorable carrier transport compared with K2AgSbCl6 and K2AgSbF6. The optical properties demonstrate excellent absorption characteristics in the visible and near-infrared regions, making these materials promising candidates for solar cell applications. In particular, K2AgSbI6 exhibits low-energy absorption starting near ~ 1 eV, supporting its relevance for visible/near-infrared optoelectronic response. Elastic property analysis indicates mechanical stability and ductile behavior for all compounds. Phonon-dispersion calculations show the absence of imaginary frequencies along the investigated high-symmetry directions, confirming the dynamical stability of K2AgSbCl6, K2AgSbF6, and K2AgSbI6. The thermoelectric properties show significant potential for energy harvesting applications, particularly at elevated temperatures. The highest thermoelectric figure of merit is obtained for K2AgSbI6, with ZT exceeding 1.0 at high temperature, whereas K2AgSbF6 shows the lowest ZT because of its wider band gap and reduced carrier activity. These results identify halide substitution as an effective route to tune the electronic, optical, and thermoelectric response of K2AgSbX6 double perovskites.

Introduction

Double perovskites have garnered increasing attention due to their structural tunability and the ability to engineer electronic and optical properties through cation and halide substitution, rendering them strong candidates for sustainable energy applications. Compounds such as K2CuBiX₆ exhibit tunable band gaps and notable structural stability, making them promising for photovoltaic technologies1. Li₂AgSbX₆ has demonstrated thermoelectric performance, mechanical resilience, and enhanced optical response2, while K2TlSbY₆ displayed high structural integrity and efficient charge transport via halide modulation3. Cs2SbAuX₆ showed remarkable optical and thermoelectric behavior combined with strong mechanical stability4.K2LiMoX₆, with its half-metallic ferromagnetism and adjustable electronic and optical features, further exemplifies this versatility5. Cs2YAuX₆ combines mechanical softness with photovoltaic and thermoelectric efficiencies6. Rb2AgMCl₆ (M = As, Co, Rh) exhibited visible-range bandgaps, structural integrity, and promising thermoelectric features7. Vacant-site-ordered Na2PtX₆ perovskites have been shown to be structurally stable and optoelectronically active8.In the sodium-based series, Na2TeX₆ (X = Cl, Br, I) offered improved thermoelectric and optical behavior with structural and electronic stability9. Cs2AuMoX₆ was shown to possess enhanced mechanical and optoelectronic characteristics10. A2NaAsCl₆ (A = K, Rb) emerged as thermoelectrically competent with moderate band gaps and structural robustness11. Likewise, X2ScBiO₆ (X = Mg, Ca, Ba) demonstrated multifunctional characteristics12, and A2InAsO₆ (A = Sr, Ba) showed exceptional thermoelectric responses13. The Ba2XTiO₆ (X = Hf, Ce, Te) series, especially Ba2TeTiO₆, revealed promising optical and thermoelectric behaviors14. Rb2KInI₆ was investigated and found to exhibit a direct band gap (~ 1.15 eV) and solar-to-electric conversion efficiency (SLME) nearing 33%15. Sr2YBiO₆ presented an indirect band gap (~ 2.15 eV), strong mechanical stability, and zT ~ 0.7 at 1200 K16. Recent studies on related halide double perovskites have further confirmed the strong potential of this materials family for optoelectronic, photovoltaic, and thermoelectric applications. For example, (Cs₂, K₂, Rb₂)SnCl₆ compounds showed structural stability, phonon-supported dynamical stability, infrared optical absorption, and promising thermoelectric behavior17, while Cs2(Sn, Pt, Te)I₆ double perovskites exhibited suitable band gaps, strong optical absorption, low electronic thermal conductivity, and high energy-conversion efficiency18. In addition, K2(Sn, Se, Te)Br₆ hybrid double perovskites demonstrated structural, chemical, and thermodynamic stability with visible-region absorption and photovoltaic efficiency reaching 20.51%19. Lead-based (Cs₂, K₂, Rb₂)PbCl₆ double perovskites were also reported to possess favorable optoelectronic and thermoelectric characteristics, supporting their relevance for solar-cell and energy-harvesting applications20. These results show that cation/halide substitution is an effective strategy for tuning stability, band-gap behavior, optical absorption, and thermoelectric response, which motivates the present study of K2AgSbX₆ (X = Cl, F, I).Recent first-principles investigations have further emphasized the importance of double perovskites for advanced energy and optoelectronic technologies. Saidi et al. studied K2AlInZ₆ (Z = F, Cl, Br) double perovskites and demonstrated their structural and mechanical stability, indirect band gaps, considerable optical absorption, and promising photovoltaic/optoelectronic and thermoelectric performance21. Zubair et al. investigated Sr2GdMO₆ (M = Bi, Sb) double perovskites and reported stable cubic structures, suitable electronic band gaps, strong ultraviolet absorption, reliable mechanical properties, and significant magnetic behavior, suggesting their applicability in spintronic and UV-optoelectronic devices22. Ullah et al. examined X2CeBiO₆ (X = Sr, Ba) double perovskites and confirmed their thermodynamic, dynamical, mechanical, electronic, optical, and magnetic stability, with indirect band gaps, strong UV absorption, robust elasticity, and potential for optoelectronic, photodetector, and solar-cell applications23. Albawawi et al. investigated Ba2XSbO₆ (X = Dy, La) double perovskite oxides and showed that Ba2DySbO₆ behaves as a spin-polarized half-metal suitable for spintronic applications, whereas Ba₂LaSbO₆ is a wide-band-gap direct semiconductor with strong optical absorption, good dielectric response, and potential for UV-protective, optoelectronic, flexible-device, and optical-coating applications24. Recent DFT- and SCAPS-1D-based studies have also shown that photovoltaic and optoelectronic performance can be improved through interface engineering, lattice-mismatch reduction, band-alignment optimization, and defect control. For example, MoS₂/ZnSe and Zn3P₂/MoS₂ heterostructures were designed to enhance photovoltaic efficiency through reduced lattice mismatch and improved charge transport25, while Cr₂O₃/CsSnCl₃-based perovskite devices demonstrated the importance of transport-layer selection and device-parameter optimization for solar-energy conversion26. In addition, quasi-harmonic Debye and phonon-related studies have highlighted the importance of thermodynamic stability, bonding character, and dynamical stability for assessing the reliability of perovskite-related materials27,28. Although K2AgSbI₆ has recently been investigated within the A2AgSbI₆ (A = K, Rb) halide double perovskite family29, that previous work mainly focused on A-site substitution in iodide-based compounds and emphasized their optoelectronic and thermoelectric properties. In contrast, the present study provides a systematic halide-dependent investigation of K2AgSbX₆ (X = Cl, F, I), where the A-, B-, and B′-site cations are kept fixed while the halide anion is varied. This approach allows us to clarify the role of halide substitution in tuning the structural stability, electronic band gap, bonding character, optical response, elastic behavior, and thermoelectric performance of K2AgSbX₆ double perovskites. Therefore, the novelty of the present work lies in establishing a comparative F/Cl/I halide-engineering strategy within the same K2AgSb framework for identifying suitable candidates for photovoltaic and thermoelectric applications. In this work, we present a comprehensive theoretical investigation of K2AgSbX₆ (X = Cl, F, I) double perovskites using density functional theory (DFT) calculations. Our study encompasses structural optimization, electronic band structure analysis, optical property calculations, elastic property determination, and thermoelectric transport property evaluation.

Computational details

All calculations were performed using the WIEN2k package30, which is an all-electron full-potential linearized augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) method within the framework of density functional theory (DFT). The exchange-correlation functional was treated using both the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) in the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) parameterization31,32 and the modified Becke-Johnson potential with GGA correlation (mBJ-GGA)33 for more accurate electronic structure calculations, particularly for band gap predictions.The GGA-PBE functional was first used for structural optimization and baseline electronic calculations, while mBJ-GGA was employed to reduce the well-known band-gap underestimation of standard GGA. Hybrid functionals such as HSE06 can provide improved band gaps, but they require substantially higher computational cost for the full set of electronic, optical, elastic, thermoelectric, SOC, phonon, and thermodynamic calculations considered in this work. Therefore, mBJ-GGA was adopted as a computationally efficient and widely used alternative for band-gap correction. The computational parameters employed in this study are summarized in Table 1.

The muffin-tin radius times maximum wave vector (RMT×Kmax) was set to 9 for all compounds to ensure convergence of the basis set. For Brillouin zone sampling, the k-point mesh was optimized for different property calculations: 1000 k-points for ground state properties, 10,000 k-points for optical and thermoelectric properties, and 5000 k-points for elastic properties. The convergence of total energy with respect to k-point sampling was carefully tested to ensure accuracy of the results. The selected values of RMT×Kmax and k-point meshes were chosen after convergence testing, ensuring that further increases in the basis-set size or k-point density produced negligible changes in the calculated total energy and equilibrium structural parameters. Spin–orbit coupling was also included through additional mBJ + SOC calculations to examine relativistic effects, particularly for the Sb- and I-containing systems.Phonon calculations were performed using the PHONOPY package34 interfaced with WIEN2k to obtain the phonon dispersion curves and density of states. The dynamical matrices were calculated using the finite displacement method with a 2 × 2 × 2 supercell. The thermoelectric transport properties were calculated using the BoltzTraP code35, which solves the Boltzmann transport equation within the constant relaxation time approximation. In the BoltzTraP calculations, a constant relaxation time of τ = 10−14 s was adopted to convert σ/τ and κe/τ into σ and κe, respectively, while the temperature-dependent trends were analyzed consistently within the constant relaxation-time approximation. The crystal structures were fully optimized using the Birch-Murnaghan equation of state36 to determine the equilibrium lattice parameters, bulk modulus, and pressure derivative of the bulk modulus. The convergence criteria for energy and force were set to 10−4 Ry and 1.0 mRy/a.u., respectively. The structural optimization was performed using the GGA-PBE functional until self-consistency was achieved.

The all-electron FP-LAPW method was used because it provides an accurate full-potential treatment of core and valence electrons, making it reliable for electronic-structure and bonding analyses. Since the present work deals with pristine K₂AgSbX₆ (X = Cl, F, I) compounds, defect-state calculations are beyond its scope. Different k-point meshes were selected according to the convergence requirements of each property; denser meshes were used for optical and thermoelectric calculations due to their sensitivity to band-edge features. Thus, the use of property-dependent converged k-point meshes does not introduce systematic inconsistencies. Although hybrid functionals and many-body approaches such as GW can further improve band-gap predictions, mBJ-GGA was adopted here as an efficient and widely used approach to reduce the band-gap underestimation of standard GGA37–39. In addition, OriginPro 2021 was used for plotting and graphical representation of the calculated results, while XCrySDen version 1.6.2 (X-Window Crystalline Structures and Densities) and VESTA version 3.90.5a (Visualization for Electronic and Structural Analysis) were used for crystal-structure, charge-density, and related structural visualization. The official software resources are available at: OriginLab Origin/OriginPro, https://www.originlab.com/origin; XCrySDen, https://www.xcrysden.org/; and VESTA, https://jp-minerals.org/vesta/en.

Results and discussion

Structural properties

Lattice parameters and bulk properties

This idealized cubic symmetry and the spatial arrangement of ions are clearly illustrated in Fig. 1, which displays the schematic representations of the K2AgSbCl₆, K2AgSbF₆, and K2AgSbI₆ crystal structures.

The formation energies (Ef) calculated for all compounds are negative, suggesting thermodynamic favorability with respect to the elemental reference phases. K2AgSbF₆ shows the most negative formation energy (-3.999 eV), followed by K2AgSbCl₆ (-2.997 eV) and K2AgSbI₆ (-1.965 eV), indicating that the fluoride compound is energetically the most favorable within the adopted elemental-reference framework. This trend correlates with the electronegativity differences and ionic bonding strength. It should be noted that negative formation energies alone do not provide complete proof of experimental synthesizability, since a rigorous phase-stability assessment would require decomposition-energy or convex-hull calculations including relevant competing binary and ternary phases. Therefore, the present stability discussion should be considered as an indication of thermodynamic feasibility within the adopted elemental-reference framework, while a more detailed phase-stability analysis is left for future work. The present calculations were performed for the optimized cubic phase under equilibrium conditions. Although the obtained tolerance factors and mechanical stability criteria support the stability of the cubic structure, possible pressure- or temperature-induced phase transitions cannot be completely excluded. A rigorous investigation of such effects would require pressure-dependent enthalpy calculations, phonon analysis under pressure, or finite-temperature simulations such as the quasi-harmonic approximation or ab initio molecular dynamics. Therefore, the influence of external pressure and temperature on the cubic symmetry of K2AgSbX₆ is beyond the scope of the present work and will be considered in future studies.

Electronic properties

The electronic band structures of K2AgSbX₆ (X = Cl, F, I), calculated using the modified Becke–Johnson (mBJ) potential within the GGA framework, are presented in Fig. 3. All three compounds exhibit indirect band gaps, with the valence band maximum (VBM) located at the Γ-point and the conduction band minimum (CBM) shifting along different symmetry paths depending on the halide atom. K2AgSbF₆ displays the widest band gap among the series, approximately 4.11 eV, indicating strong insulating behavior, which is primarily attributed to the high electronegativity and low polarizability of fluorine.To examine the possible influence of spin–orbit coupling, especially for the iodine-based compound, additional band-structure calculations were performed using the mBJ + SOC approach. As shown in Fig. 3, the inclusion of SOC produces only minor changes for K2AgSbCl₆, while its effect becomes more noticeable for the heavier Br- and I-containing compounds. In particular, K2AgSbI₆ exhibits a clearer SOC-induced band splitting and a slight modification of the band-edge positions near the Fermi level, which can be attributed to the strong relativistic effect associated with the heavy iodine atom. Nevertheless, the overall band dispersion and the qualitative electronic character remain consistent with the mBJ results, indicating that SOC modifies the quantitative band-gap values but does not change the main electronic-structure conclusions. The steep dispersion of the conduction band also suggests low effective masses for electrons, which is beneficial for potential optoelectronic applications under high excitation conditions.For K2AgSbCl₆, the band gap is moderately reduced to 2.77 eV, with slightly flatter valence bands, indicating an increase in carrier localization. Although the indirect band gap nature may limit the optical transition probability near the absorption edge compared with direct band gap materials, it does not exclude the photovoltaic potential of K2AgSbX₆ compounds. Indirect semiconductors can still be useful for solar-energy applications when they exhibit suitable band-gap values and strong absorption over a broad photon-energy range. In the present case, the narrow band gap of K2AgSbI₆ and the optical absorption behavior discussed below suggest that halide engineering can partially compensate for the indirect nature of the band gap. Nevertheless, direct band gap materials generally offer more efficient radiative transitions, and further optimization through strain, defect engineering, or heterostructure design may improve the photovoltaic performance of these compounds. The chloride substitution introduces more hybridization between Cl-3p and Sb/Ag states, thereby narrowing the gap while preserving structural symmetry.In the case of K2AgSbI₆, the band gap is significantly narrowed to 1.041 eV, placing it in the semiconducting regime. This sharp reduction is associated with the lower electronegativity and higher polarizability of iodine, which enhances orbital overlap and reduces the energy separation between valence and conduction bands. The flatter conduction band near the CBM in this composition may also contribute to higher Seebeck coefficients, suggesting favorable thermoelectric characteristics.Overall, the halide substitution from F to I effectively tunes the electronic band gap across a wide range, offering a pathway for tailoring material properties for applications ranging from transparent insulators to efficient thermoelectric or infrared optoelectronic materials.

To further support the electronic transport analysis, the carrier effective masses were estimated from the curvature of the band edges using the parabolic-band approximation. The effective mass is given by m*=ℏ2/(d2E/dk2), where d2E/dk2 represents the curvature of the band near the band extrema. Lower effective mass indicates stronger band dispersion and potentially higher carrier mobility. As summarized in Table 6, K₂AgSbI₆ exhibits the lowest effective mass among the studied compounds, with values of 0.204 m0, 0.226 m0, and 0.231 m0 using GGA, mBJ, and mBJ + SOC, respectively. This suggests more favorable carrier transport for the iodide compound, consistent with its stronger low-energy optical response and improved thermoelectric behavior.

The total and partial density of states (DOS) for K₂AgSbX₆ (X = Cl, F, I) are displayed in Fig. 4. To support the band-structure discussion, the PDOS analysis was used to identify the main orbital contributions near the valence-band maximum and conduction-band minimum.For all compounds, the valence band is primarily composed of X-p orbitals (Cl-3p, F-2p, I-5p), while the conduction band arises mainly from Sb-s and Ag-s contributions. In K2AgSbF6, the wide band gap is evident from the large separation between the valence and conduction states, consistent with its insulating nature. K2AgSbCl₆ shows stronger hybridization near the Fermi level, narrowing the gap. In K₂AgSbI₆, the I-5p states shift closer to the Fermi level, significantly reducing the band gap and enhancing electronic delocalization. This upward shift of the halogen p states from F to I explains the observed decrease in the band gap across the series and confirms that the electronic band edges are strongly governed by halide-orbital contributions. This evolution in DOS confirms the role of halide substitution in modulating the electronic structure and supports the band structure results presented in Fig. 3. Furthermore, the PDOS results indicate that the band-edge electronic structure is governed by halide-p/Sb-s and halide-p/Ag-s interactions, where the strength of orbital hybridization changes systematically with the halide atom. The larger and more polarizable I− ion promotes stronger electronic delocalization and narrower band gaps, whereas the highly electronegative F− ion leads to a larger separation between valence and conduction states. Similar composition-dependent modification of band gap and optical response has been reported for K2AgSbBr₆-based systems, where Cu+, Bi3+, and I− substitutions altered the electronic structure through changes in orbital overlap and metal–halide bonding strength44. This comparison confirms that halide and compositional engineering are effective strategies for tuning bonding character, electronic structure, and optoelectronic response in K–Ag–Sb halide double perovskites.

Therefore, combining halide substitution with controlled defect engineering may offer a promising strategy for further optimizing the optoelectronic and transport properties of K2AgSbX₆ compounds.

At a fixed temperature, α decreases with increasing pressure, confirming that compression suppresses lattice expansion.

Conclusion

This comprehensive DFT study of K2AgSbX₆ (X = Cl, F, I) double perovskites reveals that all three compounds exhibit thermodynamic, mechanical, and dynamical stability, as confirmed by negative formation energies, satisfaction of mechanical stability criteria, and the absence of imaginary phonon frequencies in the phonon-dispersion spectra. The investigated compounds behave as semiconductors with tunable band gaps ranging from 0.373 eV for K2AgSbI₆ to 2.07 eV for K2AgSbF₆ using GGA, and from 1.041 eV to 4.11 eV using the mBJ functional. The inclusion of spin–orbit coupling slightly reduces the mBJ band gaps to 0.97 eV for K2AgSbI₆, 2.42 eV for K₂AgSbCl₆, and 4.017 eV for K2AgSbF₆, with the strongest SOC effect observed for the iodine-based compound. Effective-mass calculations further indicate that K2AgSbI₆ possesses the lowest carrier effective mass, with values of 0.204 m₀, 0.226 m₀, and 0.231 m₀ using GGA, mBJ, and mBJ + SOC, respectively, suggesting more favorable carrier transport. These materials also possess excellent optical properties, including strong visible and near-infrared absorption, appropriate refractive indices, and low reflectivity, indicating strong potential for photovoltaic and optoelectronic applications. In particular, K2AgSbI₆ exhibits low-energy absorption starting near ~ 1 eV, supporting its relevance for visible/near-infrared optoelectronic response. Furthermore, the elastic analysis indicates ductile mechanical behavior, which is advantageous for device fabrication and operational durability. K2AgSbX₆ compounds also show significant thermoelectric potential, especially K2AgSbI₆, where the figure of merit ZT exceeds 1.0 at high temperature, while K2AgSbF₆ shows the lowest ZT due to its wider band gap and reduced carrier activity. Overall, the systematic variation of properties with halide substitution (F → Cl → I) demonstrates that halide engineering is an effective route for tuning the structural stability, electronic band gap, optical absorption, carrier transport, and thermoelectric performance of K2AgSbX6 double perovskites.

Development of prediction model for risks of musculoskeletal chronic lumbopelvic pain in Indian women
Authors: Jasmine Kaur Chawla, Priyanka Sushil, Pragya Kumar, Manish Singh, Roshani Sharma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractChronic lumbopelvic pain (CLPP) and its associated disabilities significantly affect women's social, professional, and personal lives. However, the specific factors contributing to CLPP in women remain unclear. To address this gap, this prospective cross-sectional study aims to identify the risk factors predicting CLPP in women and develop a prediction model that can predict CLPP in women. The study was conducted across Delhi, India, where free health camps were held, and 2400 women were assessed. Among the assessed individuals, the study revealed a high prevalence rate of CLPP among Indian women, standing at 70.4%. Seven risk factors namely, hamstring muscle tightness (> 20° on passive knee extension test), increased lumbar lordosis (> 11.5 cm of the lumbar lordotic index), reduced hip flexibility (> 15 cm on bent knee fallout test), altered foot posture (≥ 20 on foot posture index score), increased perception of psychological stress (> 25 on cohen’s perceived stress scale-10 score), reduced physical activity level (< 475 metabolic/minute on international physical activity questionnaire) and reduced performance of transversus abdominis muscle (≤ 5 on deep muscle contraction scale score) strongly predict the risks of CLPP in women. Identifying these risk factors is crucial for effectively preventing and managing CLPP symptoms, especially considering its high prevalence among Indian women. Health professionals should prioritize raising awareness about CLPP and its causative factors, as well as implementing strategies for early detection and intervention.Keywords: Chronic lumbopelvic pain, Indian women, Prediction model, Risk factorsSubject terms: Population screening, Risk factors, Skeletal muscle

Introduction

Chronic musculoskeletal pains affect approximately 35% of women globally, with chronic lumbopelvic pain (CLPP) constituting a significant portion at a prevalence rate of 29%1,2. LPP, which originates from the lumbar spine and/or pelvic joint has an increased likelihood of progressing to chronicity, especially in women3. It’s not the intensity of pain that’s bothersome about CLPP but it’s the presence of pain for a prolonged time which only tends to worsen with age1. Further, the prognosis of CLPP is very poor among women3, which makes them seek repeated interventions, significantly impacting women's quality of life and financial status 3,4. This underscores the need for effective CLPP management strategies.

Despite advancements in assessment methods, CLPP continues to pose challenges, particularly among women5. Further, accessing adequate treatment and diagnosis for CLPP remains a formidable task, especially for women residing in certain regions of India6. Even in the present era of industrialization, a substantial population of Indian women faces discrimination where their health has no precedence7 instead, they are over-burdened with familial and domestic responsibilities. Hence, CLPP symptoms exacerbate in stressful environments and while fulfilling family responsibilities. Additionally, women often report worsened CLPP symptoms during periods of domestic stress8,9.

LPP disorders have been interlinked with female reproductive conditions; however, CLPP is predominantly mediated by peripheral mechanisms10. Abnormal forces on the pain-sensitive lumbopelvic structures can emerge either from any direct or repeated mechanical trauma or from physical strains encountered during parturition in women10. Moreover, parturition is a physiological process in a woman’s life, it can be ascertained that CLPP is a common problem among child-bearing women in the general population.

Several predictors for CLPP risks have been identified by researchers like psychological stress, physical health status, smoking, job dissatisfaction, etc11,12. However, considering the impact of female reproductive functions (whether pathological or physiological) on the lumbopelvic structures, we aim to identify predictors for CLPP risks with a focus on female anthropometrics.

Thus, the main challenge for healthcare professionals lies in the assessment of such lumbopelvic alteration since the identification of these pathoanatomical structures requires high-tech imaging and radiographs13. Such investigative procedures are expensive; hence, unaffordable and not feasible for all7,14. Further, frequent exposure to ionizing radiations in routine clinical practice could potentially harm the patients15. Hence, we comprehensively searched the literature for valid and economically feasible methods and possible predictors of CLPP, intending to identify CLPP risks in a large sample of Indian women. In addition, we explored valid clinical methods that can be assessed conservatively and utilized in everyday clinical practice to evaluate potential CLPP predictors. Therefore, we aim to develop a valid prediction model using these clinical methods for the ease of early intervention ad rehabilitation protocol.

Addressing the concern of assessing multiple CLPP risks at the community level is vital, as it may pose an increased workload for musculoskeletal professionals; however, the findings of the study would enhance assessment methods and streamline clinical practice workflows, providing valuable insights. It will allow rehabilitation professionals to better identify and address biomechanical risks in women with CLPP, thereby simplifying diagnostic approaches and ensuring more effective preventive care and management.

Methods

Research study design

A cross-sectional study to assess the risk of CLPP among women was conducted through free spine assessment camps from December 2020 to March 2022 in Delhi. The study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee, Amity University, Noida, Uttar Pradesh before the start of the study (AUUP/IEC/2020/JAN/07), obtained registration under the Clinical Trial Registry of India (CTRI/2020/05/025004), and was conducted following the Declaration of Helsinki. The criterion variable was CLPP. The prediction model for CLPP risks was developed through logistic regression. The outcome of our study was “CLPP risks among women”.

Participants from all socioeconomic statuses (SES) were included as evaluated through the modified kuppuswamy scale, the total score of the Kuppuswamy scale ranges from 3 to 29 which classifies families into 5 groups: “upper class, upper middle class, lower middle class, upper lower and lower socio-economic class”18. Disability due to CLPP was assessed through the Roland Morris questionnaire (RMQ). Higher scores represent a higher disability and ‘0’ represents ‘no disability’ on 24 items RMQ19. For convenience, we categorized the RMQ scores as mild (1–8), moderate (9–15), and severe (> 15) disability.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis included profiling of participants on different socio-demographics, CLPP disability, and study parameters (CLPP potential predictors). Descriptive analysis of quantitative parameters was expressed as mean and standard deviation. Categorical data were expressed as absolute numbers and percentages. In the present study, to figure out the predictive ability of study parameters for CLPP, we used the systematic five-stage modeling process.

In the first step, we assessed the significant difference in study parameters between the two groups. Independent Student t–test was used for testing the mean difference between independent groups. Cross tables were generated and the Chi-square test was used for testing associations.

In the second stage, the area under the receiver-operator curves (ROC) (the AUC statistic) was used to quantify the discriminate ability of quantitative study parameters for CLPP. AUCs were interpreted as follows: < 0.6 = non-informative, 0.6 to 0.7 = poor discrimination, 0.7 to 0.8 = acceptable discrimination, 0.8 to 0.9 = excellent discrimination and > 0.9 = outstanding discrimination.

In the third stage, we obtained the optimal cut-off value with the best combination of sensitivity and specificity using the formula min of {(1 − Sensitivity)2 + (1 − Specificity)2} for study parameters with the help of the ROC curve.

In the fourth stage, further demonstrating the risk contribution of these significant parameters for the prediction of CLPP was evaluated by the univariate logistic regression method and the standardized regression coefficient (β) and the odds ratio (OR) for each parameter.

In the final step, we conducted a forward stepwise multivariate logistic regression model to determine the independent predictors of disease severity. P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were done using SPSS software, version 24.0.

Results

Socio-demographic profile of the participants

The mean ± SD age of the participants was 31.2 ± 11.2 (Range: 18–50) years. Prevalence of CLPP was 70.4% (n = 1691). Women with CLPP were older compared to those without CLPP [Age (years) with CLPP versus (vs.) without CLPP: 34.3 ± 10.9 vs. 23.9 ± 8.2; p = 0.0001)]. It was also observed that the women with CLPP had higher BMI (BMI, (kg/m2) with CLPP vs. without CLPP: 26.23 ± 3.76 vs. 23.58 ± 4.71; p = 0.004) and W/H ratio (W/H ratio with CLPP vs. without CLPP: 0.86 ± 0.06 vs. 0.78 ± 0.04; p < 0.0001). About > 90% of CLPP was observed among participants belonging to upper lower or lower SES followed by 81.4% CLPP among lower middle and about 50% among participants belonging to upper middle or upper SES. Regarding the educational status, 93.1%(1098/1179) of participants with education qualifications up to primary school were suffering from CLPP.

Prevalence of CLPP was significantly higher in those participants who had PKEA > 20° as compared to those who had PKEA < 20° (p < 0.0001). Poor lumbopelvic motor control was strongly associated with CLPP with about 98% (n = 1667) of participants having positive ASLR scores (p < 0.0001). Alteration in foot posture (FPI-6 scores) correlated with CLPP with 83.1% (n = 285) of our participants having supinated and 72.2% (n = 1406) participants having pronated foot posture whereas none of the participants (n = 0) with neutral foot posture had CLPP (p < 0.0001). About 94.4% (n = 1667) of participants with CLPP reported mild physical activities (low MET/minute on IPAQ) and not a single participant (n = 0) with CLPP was found to be involved in the vigorous type of physical activities in our research sample (p < 0.0001) (Table 1).

Discussion

This study reports the development of a prediction model for risks of CLPP among Indian women. We primarily focussed on women to avoid variance associated with clinical heterogeneity and proceeded with an assumption that only CLPP can affect the study outcomes36,37. Also, it has been noticed that the society in which a woman lives determines her approach towards care seeking for CLPP38. Hence, the authors opted for a community study focusing on Indian women.

Findings

Among the CLPP risk factors evaluated in the current study, PKEA of > 20°, lumbar LI > 11.5 cm, BKFT > 15 cm, FPI score of ≥ 20, PSS-10 score of > 25, IPAQ score of < 475 MET/minute and DMC score of ≤ 5 were found to be most significant factors in predicting risks of CLPP. The risk factors predicted in this study showed a probability of 0.99 for predicting CLPP.

Overall, the predictive model’s fit was excellent with low variance39–41. The AUC obtained was 0.9 interpreted as highly accurate41. Following the sensitivity analysis; age, W/H ratio, BMI, degree of thoracic kyphosis and lumbar lordosis, thoracic kyphotic index, and participant’s knowledge about low back pain were not included in further analyses as CLPP predictors. While hamstring muscle tightness, TrA muscle function, physical activity level, increased lumbar LI, reduced hip flexibility, altered foot posture, and increased perception of psychological stress muscle were identified as predictors for CLPP risks.

Comparison with related research

The current study findings are coherent with the literature that showed a consistent association between CLPP and our predictor variables42–48. Several clinical methods for the assessment of CLPP risks have been delineated in the literature28,30,49–51. To avoid discrepancies in study findings11, we determined cut-off scores for each clinical method employed to assess the respective predictor variable as our study was carried out on a large sample. The authors in the current study searched for valid and reliable methods (physical examination tests/tools) that can provide a quick assessment of the presence of abnormal lumbopelvic biomechanics which is sufficient for a healthcare professional to make a critical clinical decision13.

Our prediction model found the most significant predictors that directly or indirectly can alter the lumbopelvic biomechanics. One such CLPP predictor is the altered foot posture (pronated or high-arched) with a cut-off score of ≥ 20 (FPI-6). It is known that a high or low foot arch changes the alignment of the pelvis52–54. Apart from abnormal foot posture, the pelvic alignment can also be altered (rotated anteriorly) due to the tightness in the hamstring muscles since this musculature has attachments to the pelvis55,56. Our study findings highlighted that tight hamstring muscle (> 20° on the PKEA test) is a significant risk factor for CLPP. Apart from the hamstrings, there are other muscles attached to the pelvis that can alter the lumbopelvic biomechanics thereby posing a risk to CLPP like the hip lateral rotators57 and hip flexors58. We assessed the combined flexibility of hip lateral rotation, abduction, and flexion and found that reduced hip flexibility (> 15 cm on BKFT) poses a significant risk for CLPP.

Reduced TrA muscle performance (with a cut-off score of ≤ 5 on the DMC scale) was found to be another strong predictor of CLPP risk in the current research study. Among all abdominal muscles, we focussed on the assessment of the TrA muscle because independent contraction of this muscle has a significant role in stabilizing the pelvic joints59. Reduced activity of TrA muscle can also be identified on real-time ultrasound in individuals suffering from CLPP60,61. In addition, the physiological changes that take place during parturition are also responsible for triggering alteration in motor behavior of TrA musculature62. According to the findings of magnetic resonance imaging, the ADIM when done correctly demonstrates a bilateral contraction of TrA muscle63. Hence, the maneuver is widely used to strengthen the TrA in CLPP rehabilitation64. We too used the ADIM maneuver for the assessment of TrA muscle performance; hence, corroborating with an evidence-based method of assessment.

Degeneration of the intervertebral disc at the lumbar spine level takes place at an accelerated rate among women with osteoporosis whether premenopausal or postmenopausal65. So we assessed the curvature of the lumbar spine among our participants. Taking into consideration of domino effect, we also included an assessment of thoracic spine curvature13,44. An increased lumbar lordosis (> 11.5 cm of LI) was identified as a strong predictor of CLPP risk.

Additionally, we assessed the perception of general stress through Cohen’s PSS-10 among our participants and found > 25 on the PSS-10 score to strongly predict CLPP in women. This corroborates with the literature suggesting that a score of > 25 on PSS-10 depicts a higher stress level32. The interplay of psychological factors in CLPP can potentially elevate pain and functional disability10,66. Further, in certain societies like India, women face misogyny which compromises their psychological health67–69. The psychological distress also directly impacts the coping approach to LPP70,71. It is observed that about 41% of women with LPP do not seek healthcare professionals72. The women’s plight gets worse when psychological distress co-exists with CLPP47,66. The support received from family acts as a protective barrier toward psychologically ill health and positively influences lifestyle habits such as level of physical activity73.

Being physically active can decrease the occurrence of CLPP21,43. The WHO has stressed the need for everyday physical activities in the prevention of LPP74. We found that our participants with CLPP had remarkably reduced physical activity levels (< 475 MET/minute) as measured through IPAQ. We didn’t investigate the reasons for a reduced level of physical activity in our sample since it was not the objective of our research study. The reduced physical activity level among our participants could be due to increased stress perception, since, the combination of CLPP and psychological stress specifically discourages women from indulging in physical activities47. Another possible explanation can be ignorance and unawareness regarding the benefits of regular physical activities.

Strength and limitations

It was the first study that identified predictors for CLPP risks in a sample of women experiencing CLPP. The large sample (n = 2400), representing women in the Indian community significantly proves to be the strength of this study. The predictors identified in this study can be implemented as a quick guide by healthcare professionals to assess CLPP risks in economically deprived regions. Future studies assessing the elderly population should be carried out since we excluded women above 50 years of age.

This study was limited to women living in urban and semi-urban regions. Thus, future studies focussing on rural areas should also be considered. This can be of added advantage, especially in economically weaker sections of society, where access to medical facilities is still a challenge for Indian women. The predictive model determined is complementary to the healthcare field considering the profession’s roots in the musculoskeletal aspect. Further, the amalgamation of the studied factors consolidates its association with LPP and provides important implications for clinical practice and research.

Implications for research and practice

Determining the CLPP risks can help in the early assessment and thus early initial prevention of CLPP and its associated disabilities. Estimating the consensus regarding cut-off values for CLPP risks is important in CLPP rehabilitation. Findings from this study can be of significant importance for musculoskeletal clinicians and therapists regarding CLPP risks and the cut-off values of clinical methods that assessed the respective CLPP risks. The study findings also encourage awareness programs about CLPP issues at a community level. This newly developed prediction model can be further validated by implementing it in different populations.

Conclusion

We developed a prediction model for the risks of CLPP in a large community of Indian women. Seven significant predictors for CLPP risks were identified in Indian women. It also provided the optimal magnitude of measures through which they were assessed. The study provides consensus for cut-off points of CLPP risk factors which is important for physical rehabilitation professionals dealing with CLPP in women. Identifying women who are at a higher risk for chronicity of LPP will help in making appropriate initiatives to reduce the risk of CLPP.

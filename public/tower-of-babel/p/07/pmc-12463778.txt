Integrated single-cell transcriptomics and Mendelian randomization identify neutrophil-associated biomarker genes in colorectal cancer
Authors: Hongting Yan, Yingjie Li, Bing Yang, Wenyu Wu, Wenqi Huang, Bin Guo, Dongxin Tang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundColorectal cancer (CRC) is a prevalent digestive malignancy imposing significant economic burdens worldwide. However, the role and underlying mechanisms of neutrophil infiltration in CRC remain unclear. We therefore integrated single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), Mendelian randomization (MR), and colocalization (coloc) analyses to uncover key neutrophil-related genes in CRC.MethodsNeutrophil marker genes were identified from scRNA-seq data (GSE231559) retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO). Expression quantitative trait locus (eQTL) data were obtained from the eQTLGen Consortium. An independent cohort from the BioBank Japan (BBJ-a-107) was utilized to conduct two-sample MR analyses, establishing causal relationships between differential gene expression and CRC risk. Subsequently, immune infiltration analyses, pseudotime analyses, and Immunohistochemical validation were performed, focusing on key genes.ResultsA total of 628 neutrophil-related differentially expressed genes were identified. MR analyses revealed 8 genes significantly associated with CRC risk, among which colocalization analyses pinpointed PRDX1 and FTH1 as key causal genes. These genes demonstrated significant associations with immune cell infiltration and participated in distinct biological pathways corresponding to diverse immune infiltration statuses. Pseudotime analyses supported the relevance of these key genes in CRC progression. Immunohistochemical validation showed that the target proteins were consistently upregulated in CRC tissues compared to normal tissues, confirming our bioinformatics predictions.ConclusionOur study highlights PRDX1 and FTH1 as neutrophil-associated genes causally linked to CRC, offering promising targets for future immunotherapeutic strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03452-9.

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is an aggressive malignancy that develops in the colon or rectum, which poses a significant social, public health, and economic challenge. Based on the GLOBOCAN research, CRC is identified as the third most prevalent malignant neoplasm globally, representing roughly 10% of all cancer diagnoses. Furthermore, it ranks as the second leading factor in cancer mortality [1, 2]. The disease shows a marked increase in the over-50 age group, and the incidence is rising in both sexes. It is worth noting that in developed regions, the patient population is gradually shifting towards a younger age group [3, 4]. Currently, management strategies for CRC mainly cover surgical procedures for operable resections, as well as standardized treatment regimens for unresectable tumors, including radiation, chemotherapy, targeted therapies, and their combinations [5, 6]. However, both cytotoxicity and nonspecific reactions induced by the above treatments may result in various adverse side effects, and the patient recurrence rate reaches 54% [7, 8]. In recent years, immunotherapy has emerged as a promising new approach to cancer treatment in CRC research [9, 10].

Neutrophils constitute the largest proportion of circulating leukocytes, accounting for approximately 50–70%. They are essential in maintaining the equilibrium of the immune system and providing defense against foreign infections [11]. The survival, aggregation, and activation status of tumor-associated neutrophils (TANs) are critical factors in regulating the tumor microenvironment and its progression [12]. In response to various chemokines, neutrophils develop into two types: N1 (anti-tumorigenic) and N2 (pro-tumorigenic) [13]. Neutrophils can secrete cytokines or directly or indirectly influence the tumor microenvironment through interactions with other cells [14]. In N1 neutrophils, which exert antitumor effects, the release of cytotoxic substances promotes cancer cell death [15]. Concurrently, N1 neutrophils possess the capability to secrete chemokines and cytokines, thereby facilitating the activation and growth of immune cells. This process enhances the functionality of immune cells, ultimately contributing to the inhibition of colon tumor development [14, 16]. In addition, neutrophils can differentiate into the N2 phenotype, which promotes tumor proliferation and spread by releasing reactive oxygen species (ROS) and a variety of enzymes that undermine anti-tumor immune responses [17, 18]. Despite this knowledge, the mechanisms specific to neutrophils in CRC remain inadequately explored.

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) technology has made it possible to analyze gene expression profiles at the individual cell level. This technology not only enables the detection of rare or low-abundance cell clusters, but it can also help identify and classify new cell types [19–21]. The scRNA-seq technology can reveal intercellular heterogeneity and intercellular information transfer and track the developmental trajectories and differentiation pathways of cells, thereby discovering new diagnostic markers or potential targets for therapeutic intervention [22]. scRNA-seq revealed neutrophils’ novel properties in healthy and diseased states, but neutrophils were underrepresented in CRC studies [23]. We, therefore, postulated that this technique might aid in characterizing the molecular and phenotypic alterations in neutrophils from CRC, as well as identifying essential genes that could serve as targets for monitoring the disease’s progression and developing novel treatment approaches.

The core mechanism of Mendelian Randomization (MR) is to effectively infer the potential causal relationship between different exposures and clinical outcomes through the correlation between genetic variants and phenotypic characteristics [24]. This method helps reduce the frequency of confounders and reverse causality problems in studies by randomizing the assignment of genetic Instrumental Variables (IVs) compared to traditional Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs) [25]. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to integrate single-cell transcriptomics and Mendelian Randomization, specifically aimed at unraveling the molecular mechanisms associated with neutrophils in CRC. Our innovative integration aims to uncover critical neutrophil-specific genes causally linked to CRC, identify novel therapeutic targets, and provide deeper mechanistic insights into disease progression and immune regulation in CRC.

Materials and methods

Single-cell data quality control

Single-cell data files for GSE231559 were retrieved from GEO (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/info/datasets.html), and data from 9 samples were selected for single-cell analysis. First, the Seurat package analyzed expression profiles, filtering cells based on total unique molecular identifiers (UMIs), gene counts, and mitochondrial expression percentage. We perform quality control through the median absolute deviation (MAD). It is generally believed that if a variable is more than 3 MAD away from the median, the value is considered an outlier and needs to be eliminated. DoubletFinder (V2.0.4) filtered the doublet cells of each sample one by one, and thus, the cell quality control was completed.

Cellular dimensionality reduction clustering and cellular annotation

We applied the LogNormalize global normalization method, scaling each cell’s total expression to 10,000 using a coefficient s0, followed by a logarithmic transformation for normalization. We employed CellCycleScoring for cell cycle score calculation and FindVariableFeatures for identifying highly variable genes. The ScaleData function was used to eliminate fluctuations in gene expression due to mitochondrial gene expression share, ribosomal gene expression share, and cell cycle differences. RunPCA performed linear dimensionality reduction on the expression matrix and selected the principal components for follow-up analysis. Harmony was used to remove the batch effect, and then RunUMAP was utilized for nonlinear dimensionality reduction. In this study, we adopted a multi-source data integration strategy by systematically searching the CellMarker and PanglaoDB databases and combining them with literature analysis, as well as applying the SingleR software for automated annotation to achieve precise identification and annotation of each cell cluster and its specific marker genes in the target tissues.

Cell communication

CellCall serves as a toolkit designed to unravel the intricate networks of intercellular communication and decode internal regulatory signals. It achieves this by analyzing the expression patterns of ligand-receptor pairs and assessing the downstream transcription factor activity associated with these specific interactions.

Mendelian randomization

The outcome data came from the BBJ (BioBank Japan) database (https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/). Among them were 7,062 cases and 195,745 controls for CRC (bbj-a-107). The data were all from a predominantly European population. When selecting potential IVs, it was essential to ensure they were associated with Single Nucleotide Polymorphisms (SNPs) corresponding to the genome-wide significance threshold (P < 1e-5) for all genes. Strong IVs were identified by screening SNPs with R² < 0.001 (clustering window size of 10,000 kb) by linkage disequilibrium (LD) analysis and ensuring that their F-value was greater than 10. Causality was assessed by four statistical methods: Inverse variance weighted (IVW), MR Egger, Weighted median, and Weighted mode. IVW was estimated by integrating the causal effects of all genetic instrumental variables, weighted by the precision (1/variance) of each SNP, with the highest statistical efficacy. The MR-Egger method uses the regression intercept as an indicator to test potential multiple effects, and a P < 0.05 indicates pleiotropic effects. The Weighted Median takes the weighted median (weighted 1/variance) of all SNP effect estimates, and this approach can provide a consistent estimate of the causal effect. The Weighted Mode is a weighted kernel density estimate based on the estimation of the impact of instrumental variables, taking the value of the multitude. To assess the robustness of our findings, we performed a leave-one-out sensitivity analysis. In this approach, each instrumental variable (IV) was sequentially removed from the analysis, and the causal estimate was recalculated using the remaining IVs. This method enables the identification of influential variants that disproportionately impact the overall causal estimate. A consistent effect across all iterations indicates the stability of the results and reduces the likelihood that a single SNP drives the association. In this study, we employed reverse MR with CRC as the exposure variable and two key genes as outcome variables to investigate potential causal associations between CRC development and specific genes.

Colocalization analysis

The expression quantitative trait loci (eQTL) data used for the exposure data in this study were sourced from peripheral blood data from the eQTLGen consortium and can be accessed through their online database at https://www.eqtlgen.org. The eQTLGen program is dedicated to conducting comprehensive genome-wide meta-analyses. This study used a colocalization analysis method to integrate eQTL summary data with CRC genome-wide association study (GWAS) data for joint analysis. We selected the 100-kilobase region around the exponential SNP to estimate the posterior probability. In the analysis results, the H3 hypothesis represents the posterior probability that the two phenotypes of gene expression and CRC are associated but driven by different causal variants, while the H4 hypothesis characterizes the posterior probability that the two phenotypes are associated and regulated by the same causal variant. A colocalization threshold is set at SNP.PP.H4 > 0.95.

Immune infiltration and drug sensitivity analysis

For CRC data, mRNA expression data were downloaded from TCGA (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). 650 tumor and 51 normal samples were collected for subsequent analysis. The data of CRC patients was analyzed using the CIBERSORT method to evaluate the relative proportions of 22 types of immune infiltrating cells. Additionally, a correlation analysis was carried out on gene expression levels and the content of immune cells. Data was obtained from GDSC (https://www.cancerrxgene.org/) and analyzed using oncoPredict to estimate the chemotherapy sensitivity of individual tumor samples. A regression-based approach was employed to calculate IC50 values, followed by a 10-fold cross-validation within the database to assess the accuracy of both the regression model and the predictions. All parameters were set to their default values throughout the analysis.

Gene enrichment analysis and transcription factor regulatory network

We conducted a GSEA and chose 7.0 annotated gene sets from the MsigDB database for differential expression analysis, ranking the enriched sets by significance. Quantitative scoring of gene sets in the Molecular Signatures Database using the GSVA algorithm to explore possible biological functional transitions. In this study, we used the “RcisTarget” algorithm for transcription factor prediction based on motif analysis. Specifically, the NES values were determined by comparing the AUC distributions of each motif within the gene set, and the data were finally visualized by Cytoscape software.

Pseudotime analysis

Monocle introduces a strategy to sort single cells in pseudotime. Neutrophils were extracted, DDRTree was applied to reduce the cell dimension, the reduced Dimension function determined the cell differentiation status, and cell trajectories were plotted.

Immunohistochemistry staining

The Human Protein Atlas (HPA) database (https://www.proteinatlas.org) contains numerous immunohistochemical images that display the expression of target proteins in various tumor tissues and corresponding normal tissues. The target proteins are subjected to validation to obtain their immunohistochemical images for determining their expression.

Statistical analysis

The theoretical framework of the MR analysis approach is based on three core assumptions: the correlation assumption, which requires a significant association between the IV and the exposure factor without directly influencing the outcome variable; the independence assumption, which emphasizes that the IV must be independent of all confounders; and the exclusivity assumption, which stipulates that the IV can affect the outcome variable only through the exposure factor. All statistical analyses in this research utilized R (version 4.3.0), with significance established at P < 0.05.

Mendelian randomization

To identify key neutrophil-related genes that play a role in CRC, our analyzed summary statistics from a dataset comprising 202,807 samples. This dataset, referenced under the outcome ID bbj-a-107, included 195,745 control samples and 7062 cases of CRC. 8 causal relationships of eQTL positive outcomes were screened by MR, and the genes were FTH1, LCP1, PRDX1, SCP2, HLA-F, XBP1, HLA-C, and TXN. Among them, genes HLA-C (0.93; 0.87–0.99; P = 0.030), LCP1 (0.88; 0.79–0.99; P = 0.028), PRDX1 (0.88; 0.79–0.98; P = 0.016), SCP2 (0.93; 0.87–0.99; P = 0.027), and XBP1 (0.90; 0.84–0.97; P = 0.009) may be associated with a low risk of the disease; genes FTH1 (1.45; 1.15–1.83; P = 0.002), HLA-F (1.06; 1.01–1.13; P = 0.034), TXN (1.20; 1.01–1.41; P = 0.033) may be associated with a high risk of disease (Fig. 3A–H) (IVW P < 0.05). Next, we performed a sensitivity analysis of the causal association among the 8 genes to verify its reliability. The results indicated that the effect of eliminating any SNP on the overall error bar was not noticeable for all 8 genes, indicating that the 8 pairs of causal links we chose were reliable (Supplementary Fig. 2A-H). At the same time, the 8 genes were co-localized at the eQTL-GWAS level. Among them, the colocalized SNP.PP.H4 of genes FTH1 and PRDX1 was higher than 0.95 (Supplementary Fig. 3 A, B). The central genes for later study will be FTH1 and PRDX1. In the reverse MR, we used bbj-a-107 as the exposure factor and selected FTH1 and PRDX1 as the outcome data. The reverse MR analysis showed that bbj-a-107 had no causal relationship with the protein levels of FTH1 and PRDX1 (Supplementary Fig. 3 C).

Immune infiltration and drug sensitivity

The microenvironment has a significant influence on diagnosis, survival outcomes, and sensitivity to clinical therapy for the disease. We assessed immune infiltration levels and the correlations between different immune cell types (Fig. 4A, B). Compared to controls, the disease group exhibited significantly higher levels of Macrophages M0, Macrophages M1, and Mast cells activated. Conversely, there was a significant decrease in B cells naive, Macrophages M2, Mast cells resting, NK cells activated, Plasma cells, and T cells CD8 (Fig. 4C). Our investigation explored the interplay between key genes and immune cells, revealing that FTH1 exhibited a strong positive correlation with T cells CD8, T cells regulatory (Tregs), Mast cells activated, and Neutrophils. Conversely, it showed a notable negative correlation with B cells naive, T cells CD4 memory resting, T cells CD4 memory activated, and Dendritic cells activated. These findings highlight the intricate connections between FTH1 and various immune cell populations. PRDX1 was significantly positively correlated with several immune cell types, including T cells CD8, T cells CD4 memory activated, T cells follicular helper, Macrophages M1, and Dendritic cells resting. Conversely, it exhibited a notable negative correlation with B cells naive, Plasma cells, T cells CD4 memory resting, Tregs, Mast cells resting, and Mast cells activated (Fig. 4D). The findings indicate that these key genes are tightly linked to the degree of immune cell infiltration and significantly influence the immune environment (Supplementary Fig. 4A-E). Next, we investigate the sensitivity of key genes FTH1 and PRDX1 to common chemotherapy drugs. The results indicated that FTH1 and PRDX1 were strongly link to the sensitivity of Cisplatin_1005, Docetaxel_1007, Dorapimod_1042, Nutlin − 3a(−)1047, fulvestrant_1200, and daporinad_1248 (Supplementary Fig. 5 A, B).

Discussion

CRC remains a considerable public health concern on a global scale, and its incidence and mortality rates figure prominently in global cancer-related statistics [1]. Despite considerable advances in early screening techniques, adequate surgical resection, systemic chemotherapy, and biological therapies, the annual mortality rate remains high, and the prevalence of advanced cases continues to rise. In addition, once a tumor develops drug resistance, its rate of recurrence and metastasis is significantly accelerated, making the need to expand CRC treatment strategies particularly urgent [2, 26]. Therefore, the research and development of novel and effective biomarkers for CRC are of crucial importance in enhancing the prognosis and treatment outcomes of patients.

Neutrophils regulate the innate and adaptive immune systems and are specifically polarized to phenotypes based on different environmental signals [27]. Neutrophils act as key players in cancer-associated inflammatory responses at different stages of the oncogenic process and mediate tumorigenesis by shaping the inflammatory microenvironment through different functional modes of triggering phagocytosis, secreting lysosomal enzymes, and generating ROS [28, 29]. A large body of evidence reveals that neutrophils exhibit remarkable complexity in tumor progression, including different states that promote tumorigenesis, facilitate tumor growth and metastasis, stimulate tumor angiogenesis, and mediate immunosuppression [30, 31]. In the early stages of tumorigenesis, neutrophils typically exhibit anti-tumor activity, including direct killing of tumor cells, participation in immune surveillance through the formation of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs), and promotion of inflammation levels that help recruit additional immune cells to enhance tumor clearance. As tumors progress, neutrophil behavior shifts to support tumor growth by promoting chronic inflammation and metastatic mechanisms [32]. Neutrophils play a central role in inflammation within tumors by shaping the chronic inflammatory environment through the release of associated chemokines, reactive oxygen species, or proteases, which facilitates the escape of tumor cells from immune surveillance and thus promotes tumor progression. In addition, neutrophils can stimulate metastatic spread by inhibiting natural killing functions and promoting the extravasation of tumor cells [33]. In CRC, neutrophils exhibit remarkable heterogeneity and plasticity, recruiting a variety of chemokines and signaling pathways, which, in turn, have a profound influence on tumor infiltration, progression, and metastasis, as well as on patient prognosis [34].

The present study examined the various cellular heterogeneity present in CRC utilizing single-cell sequencing technology, along with advanced bioinformatics methodologies, including Seurat and UMAP. This meticulous analysis revealed cell clusters and subtypes that substantially enhanced our comprehension of differences in the composition and function of CRC cell communities. In 628 neutrophil DEGs, MR was applied to analyze and identify eight genes causally associated with CRC. To determine the robustness of our results, we further applied eQTL and colocalization analysis, as well as reverse MR validation analysis. Ultimately, two genes were recognized as essential core genes. PRDX1 is identified as a gene linked to a low risk of CRC, whereas FTH1 is linked to a high risk of this particular malignancy. We further investigated the relationship between these key genes and immunological factors, observing differential distribution of immune markers across samples and significant intercorrelations among immune factors. Notably, both genes demonstrated strong associations with immune cell infiltration patterns and tumor microenvironment characteristics. FTH1 and PRDX1 were shown to play critical but distinct roles in shaping anti-tumor immunity through differential immunoregulatory networks: FTH1 may enhance anti-tumor immune responses by positively regulating CD8+, Tregs, and neutrophils, while inhibiting B cells naive, CD4+, and dendritic cell activity, suggesting its dual role of immune activation and immune suppression; PRDX1, on the other hand, significantly promoted the infiltration of CD8+, T cells CD4 memory activated, T cells follicular helper, Macrophages M1, and Dendritic cells resting, but inhibited the function of B cells naive, T cells CD4 memory resting, Tregs, Mast cells resting, and Mast cells activated, suggesting a preference for shaping the pro-inflammatory immune microenvironment. The synergistic or antagonistic effects of these two genes may ultimately modulate the process of tumor immunosurveillance or immune escape by regulating the dynamic balance of oxidative stress pathways (e.g., iron metabolism/FTH1) and antioxidant defences (PRDX1), which in turn affect the polarisation state and functional phenotype of immune cells. Additionally, computational predictions suggested significant correlations between these genes and sensitivity to chemotherapeutic agents, including cisplatin and docetaxel.

PRDX1, a 2-Cys peroxide-reducing protein, acts as a key endogenous antioxidant that reduces the levels of ROS and scavenges sulfhydryl free radicals, thereby preventing cellular oxidative damage, a product of tumor cell activation [35]. A systematic evaluation showed that high expression of PRDX1 was significantly associated with poor prognosis in solid cancers, as well as with tumor size, advanced TNM staging, and poor tumor differentiation, demonstrating its high aggressiveness and emerging as a new diagnostic marker for monitoring tumor progression [36]. Numerous studies have proven that PRDX1 leads to carcinogenesis in different cancer types and possesses apoptosis-inhibitory effects [37–39]. PRDX1 exhibits elevated expression in human CRC tissues, particularly in metastatic CRC, and is associated with poorer overall survival [40]. The over-expression of PRDX1 has been shown to facilitate tumor proliferation, confer resistance to chemotherapy, and correlate with unfavorable prognostic outcomes in cancer patients [39, 41, 42]. Significantly, PRDX1 was identified as a potential therapeutic target, and targeting PRDX1 to mitigate resistance to chemotherapeutic agents induced by DNA damage in CRC [43]. Currently, PRDX1 has multiple functions in various types of tumor biology, and its targeted drugs have shown anticancer effects, indicating the therapeutic potential of PRDX1 as a molecular target [44].

The biological functions of PRDX1 are significantly prominent in several signaling pathways, especially in the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, the Hematopoietic cell lineage, and the differentiation of Th1 and Th2 cells. The dysregulation of the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway is commonly linked to the development, progression, and metastasis of CRC, and it also contributes to the phenomenon of drug resistance [45]. Another study confirmed that PRDX1 might exert anti-colorectal cancer activity by inhibiting PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling through participation in redox reactions [46]. PRDX1 is also enriched in immune-related pathways, which may influence the differentiation and functionality of immune cells. Furthermore, any dysregulation in immune cell populations can subsequently impact the tumor microenvironment, thereby facilitating tumor growth and metastasis. The GSVA and immune metabolism-related pathway analysis indicated that PRDX1 exhibited significant enrichment within the ROS pathway, oxidative phosphorylation, and reactive oxygen species pathway. The finding of the current investigation aligns with previous studies and emphasize that PRDX1, as an antioxidant enzyme, may play a direct role in scavenging ROS and reducing the damage caused by oxidative stress to cancer cells, thereby protecting them and promoting their survival. PRDX1 may regulate CRC development at multiple levels through these signaling pathways, including cancer cell viability, modulation of immune responses, and adaptation to intra- and extracellular stressful environments.

It has been shown that FTH1, a key gene in neutrophil subtypes, exhibits potential as a therapeutic target and prognostic biomarker [47]. FTH1 is a key regulator of iron metabolism in ferroptosis and is associated with numerous diseases, predicting cancer prognosis [48–50]. FTH1 is expressed at increased levels in CRC tissues [51], which is consistent with our results and has some clinical diagnostic value. In cisplatin chemotherapy regimens, FTH1 increases ferritin production, which may contribute to cancer cell survival [52]. Mechanistic studies [53] showed that FTH1 deficiency protects mice from colitis and colitis-associated CRC by safeguarding them from iron overload under iron overload conditions, suggesting a new therapeutic target for iron overload in colitis and colitis-associated CRC. Meanwhile, FTH1 can be synthesized into nanocarriers that contribute to the reduction of ferritin-mediated resistance to chemotherapy or radiotherapy in tumor therapy [54].

FTH1 showed significant enrichment, such as IL6_JAK_STAT3 and reactive_oxygen_species. FTH1 has high immunometabolic activity in the reactive_oxygen_species_pathway and oxidative_phosphorylation. The JAK/STAT3 signaling serves as a crucial oncogenic mechanism that regulates multiple biological functions, including cell proliferation, inhibition of apoptosis, angiogenesis, and immunosuppression in the tumor microenvironment, playing a critical role in tumor progression and drug resistance [55, 56]. FTH1 deficiency confers protection to mice against acute colitis induced by DSS and the subsequent development of colitis-associated CRC. This protective effect is attributed to a reduction in DMT1/Iron-mediated STAT3 activation, indicating that FTH1 is a critical factor in the pathogenesis of intestinal inflammation and the progression of CRC [53]. Furthermore, METTL1 enhanced cellular iron death by targeting FTH1 and increased the susceptibility of osteosarcoma cells to chemotherapeutic drugs [57]. Several studies have demonstrated that FTH1 is linked with prognosis and immunity in different forms of cancer [58, 59].

In addition, we identified a regulatory network of FTH1 and PRDX1-related transcription factors, identifying 81 transcription factors, of which 52 were enriched for FTH1 and 66 for PRDX1, that helps to understand the critical mechanism of neutrophil immunity in CRC. Finally, this research verified the association between key genes and disease by examining their expression profiles in single-cell datasets. FTH1 was expressed in all cells and more samples; PRDX1 was expressed in all cells but in fewer samples. The proposed time-series analysis showed that monocytes were predominantly distributed in the early and middle stages of cell differentiation, whereas the neutrophil group was predominantly distributed in the late stages of the differentiation process. FTH1 expression increased and then dropped as cells differentiated, whereas PRDX1 expression decreased and then increased before stabilizing. All results indicated that FTH1 and PRDX1 exhibited elevated expression in neutrophils and were closely linked to CRC. In addition, images of immunohistochemical staining of genes were obtained through the HPA database. FTH1 expression was lower in colorectal cancer tissues than in normal tissues; PRDX1 expression was the same in colorectal cancer tissues and normal tissues. These clinicopathologic correlations, combined with our findings, provide a compelling rationale for developing the target protein as a diagnostic biomarker for monitoring CRC progression and for implementing targeted therapeutic strategies against this molecule.

Although this study provided valuable insights into neutrophil-associated genes in CRC through single-cell and MR analyses, a number of limitations should be acknowledged. MR analyses relied primarily on European populations, which may limit generalizability, and residual confounding or bias may remain despite sensitivity analyses. Therefore, the generalizability of the results to CRC patients from other regions or ethnic groups requires further investigation. In our study, the single-cell data file and CRC data are publicly available databases, which have limited sample sizes. Therefore, multicenter studies with larger sample sizes are necessary to critically validate our results. Finally, the functions and molecular mechanisms of potential genes are relatively complex and require validation through in-depth studies using cells and animals. Future studies incorporating multi-tiered evidence may yield deeper insights into our findings.

Conclusion

In this study, by analysis of scRNA-seq data, we successfully identified marker genes of immune cell subtypes associated with CRC. Colocalization analysis and MR analysis, which led to the screening of two genes, PRDX1 and FTH1, whose causality in CRC development was confirmed. The association of these two key genes with CRC provides new ideas for in-depth study of the functional mechanism of CRC neutrophils and helps to discover new therapeutic targets.

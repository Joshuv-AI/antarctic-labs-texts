Improving “No‐Reflow” After Complete Reperfusion: The Role of Intravenous Thrombolysis in Vertebrobasilar Artery Occlusion Patients Undergoing Endovascular Treatment
Authors: Zhanglin Li, Yingjie Xu, Pan Zhang, Miaomiao Hu, Ruyue Wang, Zhixin Huang, Zuowei Duan, Xinfeng Liu, Wen Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPrevious studies have found that the use of intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) before endovascular treatment (EVT) could mitigate the “no‐reflow” phenomenon in large‐vessel occlusion of the anterior circulation. However, the effect of preoperative IVT on reducing the “no‐reflow” phenomenon in vertebrobasilar artery occlusion (VBAO) is still uncertain. This study aimed to compare the outcomes of IVT before EVT versus EVT alone in patients with VBAO with complete reperfusion (mTICI [Modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction] 3).MethodsWe performed a retrospective analysis of patients with acute VBAO at 65 stroke centers in China. Patients with complete reperfusion after EVT were included. These patients were divided into 2 groups on the basis of whether IVT was used before EVT, and propensity score matching was applied to balance the groups. The primary outcome was favorable functional outcome, defined as a modified Rankin Scale score of 0 to 3 at 90 days. Secondary outcomes were functional independence (modified Rankin Scale score of 0–2 at 90 days) and modified Rankin Scale shift at 90 days. Safety end points included symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage and death at 90 days.ResultsOf the 2422 patients with VBAO who received EVT, 1452 patients achieved complete reperfusion. Among these, 273 patients received IVT before EVT. After propensity score matching, 268 patients treated with IVT before EVT were compared with 519 patients without IVT. In the matched cohort, the group that received IVT before EVT showed a higher rate of favorable functional outcome (modified Rankin Scale score, 0–3) (adjusted odds ratio, 1.40 [95% CI, 1.03–1.91]; P=0.033) and a lower mortality rate at 90 days (adjusted odds ratio, 0.72 [95% CI, 0.52–0.99]; P=0.044) compared with the EVT alone group.ConclusionsOur study indicates that IVT before EVT could improve favorable functional outcomes and reduce death in patients with VBAO who achieve complete reperfusion.Keywords: endovascular treatment, intravenous thrombolysis, no‐reflow phenomenon, vertebrobasilar artery occlusionSubject Categories: Cerebrovascular Disease/Stroke, Ischemic Stroke

Clinical Perspective

What Is New?

This study found that in patients with vertebrobasilar artery occlusion who achieve complete reperfusion (mTICI [Modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction] 3) after endovascular treatment, intravenous thrombolysis before endovascular treatment can increase favorable functional outcome and reduce the 90‐day mortality rate compared with endovascular treatment alone.

This study found that in patients with vertebrobasilar artery occlusion who achieve complete reperfusion (mTICI [Modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction] 3) after endovascular treatment, intravenous thrombolysis before endovascular treatment can increase favorable functional outcome and reduce the 90‐day mortality rate compared with endovascular treatment alone.

What Are the Clinical Implications?

Intravenous thrombolysis may be beneficial in improving the prognosis of patients with vertebrobasilar artery occlusion by improving the no‐reflow phenomenon, although this requires further imaging evidence.

Endovascular treatment (EVT) is widely recommended in global guidelines for patients with acute ischemic stroke with large‐vessel occlusion (LVO). 1 However, a recent meta‐analysis showed that despite successful reperfusion (mTICI [Modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction] 2b to 3) within 24 hours, approximately half of patients with acute ischemic stroke did not achieve favorable functional outcomes at 90 days. 2 , 3

Among the factors contributing to poor prognosis, the “no‐reflow” phenomenon is important but often overlooked. 4 This phenomenon occurs when, despite complete large‐vessel reperfusion, blood flow at the microvascular level remains suboptimal, leading to insufficient tissue reperfusion. 5 It is important to address and prevent the no‐reflow phenomenon, as it significantly reduces the efficacy of EVT.

Intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) has been speculated to improve microvascular reperfusion by initiating the thrombolytic process at an early stage, potentially alleviating the no‐reflow phenomenon in patients. 6 Recent meta‐analyses and randomized clinical trials have shown no advantage or noninferiority in 90‐day functional outcomes and death for patients with LVO treated with IVT before EVT versus direct EVT. 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 Notably, these studies primarily focus on patients with anterior circulation LVO.

Vertebrobasilar artery occlusion (VBAO) accounts for ≈10% to 20% of all LVO. 11 Without successful reperfusion, patients with VBAO face exceptionally high mortality and disability rates, ranging from 85% to 95%. 12 , 13 While 2 Chinese randomized controlled trials have confirmed that EVT is the standard treatment for VBAO, 14 , 15 there is a lack of studies exploring the role of IVT before EVT in patients with VBAO achieving complete reperfusion.

Given the background, the purpose of this study was to compare the clinical outcomes between the use of IVT before EVT and EVT alone in patients with VBAO achieving complete reperfusion (mTICI 3).

Methods

The data supporting the study's findings are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request.

Study Population

The study enrolled patients consecutively from the PERSIST (Posterior Circulation Ischemic Stroke Registry; ChiCTR2000033211), which spans from December 2015 to June 2022, and includes data from 65 stroke centers in 15 provinces in China. It recruited patients with acute VBAO who received EVT and had their condition confirmed through symptoms and radiologic findings. Patients were eligible for inclusion if they met the following criteria: (1) age ≥18 years; (2) acute symptomatic VBAO confirmed by computed tomographic angiography, magnetic resonance angiography, or digital subtraction angiography; (3) treated with EVT; and (4) treatment was initiated (groin puncture) within 24 hours of estimated occlusion time. Patients were excluded if they (1) had a prestroke modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score ≥3; (2) were diagnosed with a combined anterior stroke, aneurysm, or arteriovenous malformation; (3) were pregnant or lactating; (4) participated in a clinical trial; or (5) had incomplete baseline critical data and imaging. Details of the PERSIST study have been described elsewhere. 16 , 17 In this study, we performed multiple imputations for missing data and also performed additional exclusion of patients with mTICI <3. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of the University of Science and Technology of China in Hefei, China. Patient consent was waived due to its retrospective nature.

Data Collection

Baseline demographic data, medical history, risk factors, stroke severity, imaging information, and treatment characteristics were collected by reviewing medical records. Stroke severity was assessed according to the National Institute of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score. Stroke pathogenesis was classified on the basis of the TOAST (Trial of Org 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment) criteria. 18 Reperfusion status was assessed using the mTICI scale. 19 Complete reperfusion was defined as an mTICI score of 3. The posterior circulation Acute Stroke Prognosis Early Computed Tomography Score on baseline was evaluated on noncontrast computed tomography or MRI. Collateral status was graded in terms of the American Society of Interventional and Therapeutic Neuroradiology/Society of Interventional Radiology criteria. 19 All available imaging data were assessed by an independent core imaging laboratory, which was unaware of clinical outcomes and treatment assignments. Each imaging scan was separately interpreted by 2 trained neuroradiologists. In cases of discrepancies, a third neuroradiologist confirmed the final results.

Treatment

All patients with VBAO were treated with EVT in our study. EVT included mechanical thrombectomy with stent retrievers and thromboaspiration, balloon angioplasty, stenting, intra‐arterial thrombolysis, or a combination of these approaches regardless of prior IVT. For the IVT plus EVT group, patients were administered intravenous recombinant tissue plasminogen activator (alteplase) within 4.5 hours of symptom onset. At each institution, the choice of treatment modality was left to the discretion of the neurointerventionists and neurologists according to a comprehensive assessment and the patients or their legal representatives according to their preferences.

Outcome Variables

The follow‐up protocol was also published previously. 20 The primary outcome was favorable functional outcome (mRS score, 0–3) at 90 days. Secondary outcomes were functional independence (mRS score, 0–2) and mRS shift at 90 days. Safety end points included symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) and all‐cause death at 90 days. sICH was identified using the Heidelberg bleeding classification 21 if the newly observed imaging intracranial hemorrhage was associated with any of the following: (1) an increase of >4 points in the total NIHSS score or >2 points in a category; and (2) deterioration leading to hemicraniectomy, external ventricular drain placement, intubation, or other major medical intervention. There were no other explanations for symptom deterioration. 22

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables were described as mean±SD if normally distributed or median with interquartile range for nonnormal distributions. Categorical variables are presented as counts (percentages). Patients were divided into 2 groups according to whether IVT was used before EVT. Student's t test was used to compare the baseline characteristics between the 2 groups, the Mann–Whitney U test was used for nonparametric continuous variables, and Pearson's χ2 test was used for categorical variables. Due to the large sample size in the EVT group, in order not to waste the sample size and to improve the matching accuracy, a propensity score–matched analysis was performed using age, sex, coronary artery disease, diabetes, site of occlusion, time from estimated occlusion to groin puncture, posterior circulation Acute Stroke Prognosis Early Computed Tomography Score, and baseline NIHSS to ensure a balanced 1:2 nearest‐neighbor match with a caliper width of 0.05.

In the propensity score–matched cohort, a multivariate logistic regression analysis was conducted to compare outcomes and safety end points. The analysis was adjusted for age, sex, posterior circulation Acute Stroke Prognosis Early Computed Tomography Score, baseline NIHSS, hyperlipidemia, drinking, stroke pathogenesis, and prestroke mRS score. An ordinal logistic regression, adjusted for the aforementioned variables, was applied to evaluate changes in scores across the entire range of the mRS, and the proportional odds assumption was tested. Statistical analyses were carried out using SPSS Statistics version 27 (IBM, Armonk, NY) and R version 4.3.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). A 2‐sided P value of <0.05 was considered to indicate a significant difference.

Results

Study Population and Baseline Characteristics

Of the 2422 patients with VBAO treated with EVT, 1452 patients met the inclusion criteria for our study (Figure 1). Among them, 273 patients (18.8%) received IVT before EVT, and 1179 patients (81.2%) only received EVT.

Safety Outcomes in the Matched Cohort

In terms of safety outcomes, there were no significant differences in the rate of sICH (OR, 1.02 [95% CI, 0.49–2.12]; P=0.965) between the 2 groups. We also find that the IVT plus EVT group was associated with a lower mortality rate at 90 days (OR, 0.72 [95% CI, 0.52–0.99]; P=0.044) compared with the EVT group (Table 2).

Discussion

Our study retrospectively analyzed patients with acute VBAO treated across 65 stroke centers in China. In the context of achieving complete reperfusion (mTICI 3), we found that patients with VBAO who received IVT before EVT showed better functional outcomes and a lower mortality rate at 90 days compared with patients who received EVT alone. In addition, there was no significant difference in the incidence of sICH between the 2 groups.

Current guidelines recommend the use of IVT within a 4.5‐hour window following symptom onset for ischemic stroke caused by anterior circulation LVO. 23 For patients with VBAO, 2 Chinese randomized clinical trials, ATTENTION (Endovascular Treatment for Acute Basilar Artery Occlusion) and BAOCHE (Basilar Artery Occlusion Chinese Endovascular), confirmed that EVT was superior to optimal medical management within 12 hours and 6 to 24 hours of onset, respectively. 14 , 15 However, there is still a lack of randomized clinical trials on the effectiveness of IVT in patients with VBAO. 24

Effective tissue reperfusion represents the optimal therapeutic objective in acute ischemic stroke, as it demonstrates a stronger predictive value for clinical improvement than arterial recanalization alone. 25 We therefore hypothesized that the favorable functional outcome (mRS score, 0–3) observed in this study could be caused by IVT mitigating the no‐reflow phenomenon. The no‐reflow phenomenon has been defined and assessed differently in different studies. 26 , 27 However, the core definition is the absence of microvascular reperfusion after macrovascular reperfusion. A systematic review and meta‐analysis of the no‐reflow phenomenon in patients with stroke showed that the assessment of microvascular reperfusion status is usually calculated from perfusion images obtained by computed tomography or magnetic resonance perfusion imaging. 27 Notably, Mujanovic et al 28 demonstrated that relying solely on digital subtraction angiography to evaluate reperfusion underestimates hypoperfused regions in a substantial subset of patients. These findings underscore the need for novel tools to identify or predict tissue‐level reperfusion patterns, particularly in patients undergoing immediate post‐EVT perfusion imaging, to enhance clinical relevance and prognostic accuracy.

In a recent meta‐analysis of no‐reflow in patients with stroke, no‐reflow was observed in one third of patients with stroke with successful macrovascular reperfusion. 27 However, most of the studies included in this meta‐analysis were patients with anterior circulation strokes. In our study, we found that among patients with VBAO who achieved complete reperfusion, those who received IVT plus EVT showed a higher rate of favorable functional outcomes at 90 days (54.9% versus 47.4%) compared with those who received EVT alone. Another study found that patients with microvascular hypoperfusion were less likely to have received IVT when compared with patients with hyperperfusion, although only 15% of the patients with posterior circulation strokes were included. 29 Similarly, our study found that in patients with VBAO who achieved complete reperfusion after EVT, only a small proportion underwent IVT. Therefore, compared with the anterior circulation, the posterior circulation perhaps has a higher probability of no‐reflow phenomenon leading to a poor prognosis. The potential value of IVT in mitigating the no‐reflow phenomenon deserves our attention.

Here are some explanations regarding the no‐reflow phenomenon. Ng et al 5 have suggested that the no‐reflow phenomenon is associated with tissue hypoperfusion, which, despite successful vascular reperfusion, can result in a poor prognosis due to blood–brain barrier destruction, hemorrhagic transformation, and infarct growth. In addition, the low perfusion is likely due to the abnormality of the microvascular level. Microvascular obstruction caused by pericyte contraction, endothelial cell swelling, lumen blockage by white blood cells and microthrombus, and neutrophil extracellular traps can all damage the perfusion of capillary beds, although the large arteries are reperfused. 30 , 31 , 32 We speculate that IVT may shorten the length of the thrombus that opens the perforator or branch artery before EVT. Long‐term thrombolysis may be beneficial to the softening of the thrombus after mechanical thrombectomy, and improve the degree of final reperfusion by dissolving the secondary distal embolus that cannot be reached by EVT. 9 IVT may be easier to remove and suck the thrombus by separating the thrombus from the inner surface of the blood vessel and dissolving the thrombus escaping from the distal end of the perioperative period, thereby reducing the use of stent thrombectomy and facilitating intravascular reperfusion. 33

However, the combination of IVT and EVT remains controversial, focusing on the increased risk of bleeding, deterioration of distal perfusion due to thrombus fragmentation, delayed treatment initiation, limited use of antithrombotic therapy, and increased costs. 34 , 35 In particular, IVT may increase the risk of sICH, especially because the use of anticoagulants during the perioperative period may cause damage to the vascular wall caused by thrombectomy materials. 36 In our study, we also found that the incidence of sICH was higher in the IVT plus EVT group compared with the EVT group, but this difference was not statistically significant.

Although our study provides strong evidence of the potential benefits of IVT for patients with VBAO before EVT, we must acknowledge the limitations of the study. First, the retrospective design of this study limits our ability to infer causality and may introduce selection bias. Because treatment decisions are not randomly assigned but are made on the basis of clinical circumstances, this may lead to some unmeasured confounding factors that may affect our assessment of the effectiveness of IVT. Second, although we use propensity score matching to try to balance the baseline characteristics between the 2 groups, this method does not eliminate all potential confounding variables. Third, while we used the mRS score as a functional outcome indicator to indirectly infer the “no‐reflow” phenomenon, this approach does not directly assess microvascular reperfusion status. Given the variability in definitions and evaluation methods of no‐reflow across studies, clinical scores such as mRS or NIHSS can only serve as surrogate markers and are insufficient to reflect true microvascular reperfusion status. Ideally, perfusion imaging should be performed immediately after EVT to more accurately evaluate microvascular reperfusion status. In addition, because the data come from multiple centers, there may be differences in treatment practice and patient management, and these differences may not be fully adjusted in the analysis. To address these limitations, future prospective studies with standardized protocols are warranted. Such studies would allow dynamic monitoring variables and reduce biases inherent to retrospective analyses.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our findings indicate that in patients with VBAO with complete reperfusion, IVT before EVT is beneficial for achieving better clinical outcomes and lower mortality rates. Further clinical studies are needed to clarify the optimal timing and dosage of combined IVT and EVT for long‐term outcomes, along with advanced imaging evidence on their impact on the no‐reflow phenomenon.

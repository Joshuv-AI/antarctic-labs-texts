Neutrophil extracellular Trap-related genes in NAFLD: Biomarkers and therapeutic targets
Authors: Huajing Rao, Juan Chen, Yongcheng Chen, Jinfeng Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The etiology of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is complex. This study seeks to clarify the importance of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) -related genes in NAFLD. Integrated NAFLD datasets were obtained from GEO. NETs-related differentially expressed genes (NETsRDEGs) were identified and analyzed for functional enrichment. A diagnostic model was developed using logistic regression and Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator regression (LASSO) methods, followed by validation through receiver operating characteristic curve analysis. Immune cells infiltration assessments, regulatory network analyses, and protein domain predictions were conducted to illuminate the molecular mechanisms underlying NAFLD. We identified 22 NETsRDEGs and constructed an 8-gene diagnostic model (TLR7, LDLR, CCL2, S100A8, PADI4, F3, CD274, NFIL3) that effectively distinguished NAFLD from controls (area under the curve [AUC] = 0.920). Enrichment analyses revealed significant involvement of PI3K-AKT signaling and negative regulation of NOTCH4 signaling. Immune infiltration analysis demonstrated altered abundances of activated CD8 + T cells and Type 2 T helper in NAFLD. Several compounds, including resveratrol and curcumin, were identified as potential therapeutics. Our findings underscore the significance of NETs in NAFLD pathogenesis, involving inflammatory pathways and immune dysregulation. The identified NETsRDEGs and diagnostic model offer promising biomarkers and therapeutic avenues for NAFLD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10238-026-02139-x.

Introduction

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) represents a clinical condition distinguished by diffuse hepatic steatosis, which occurs without substantial alcohol intake or other identifiable liver-damaging factors. The spectrum of NAFLD encompasses various conditions, ranging from simple steatosis to non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), which may subsequently advance to cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). The underlying mechanisms of this disease are strongly linked to insulin resistance and genetic predisposition. Given the escalating prevalence of obesity and diabetes, the primary cause of chronic liver disorders in China has now shifted to NAFLD, affecting 29.2% of the population [1] and posing significant health and societal burdens. Current management strategies primarily emphasize lifestyle modifications, though long-term adherence is often poor. Pharmacological options such as vitamin E, pioglitazone, and the recently approved Resmetirom are limited by variable efficacy, side effects, or restricted applicability [2, 3]. While bariatric surgery benefits eligible obese patients, it is invasive and unsuitable for the broader NAFLD population. These therapeutic gaps and limitations underscore the critical necessity of discovering new biomarkers for early detection and risk stratification, along with developing more effective and targeted therapeutic strategies.

Neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) are extracellular network architectures consisting of chromatin, granular proteins, and histones, released in response to various stimuli. NETs might contribute to the onset and advancement of NAFLD by promoting inflammatory response, fibrosis, and HCC. For instance, NETs have been detected in NASH liver biopsies and correlate with disease severity [4]. In mice with neonatal streptozotocin and high-fat diet (HFD)-induced NASH, evidence of neutrophil infiltration and extracellular trap formation in the livers is observed, followed by macrophage infiltration and progression to HCC. Both genetic (PAD4−/− mice) and pharmacological (DNase treatment) inhibition of NETs significantly reduced the number and size of liver tumors [5]. However, the exact role of NETs in NAFLD has not yet been fully clarified.

The objective of this research is to investigate how NETs contribute to the pathogenesis and advancement of NAFLD, with the goal of uncovering novel therapeutic strategies. We have identified genes for a diagnostic model and created a bioinformatic framework for diagnosing NAFLD based on NETs. The workflow diagram illustrated in Fig. 1.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition and preprocessing

Datasets GSE89632 [6, 7], GSE37031 [8], and GSE48452 [9] related to NAFLD were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database [10] (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). All samples in the dataset were derived from human liver tissue. Further details are provided in Supplementary Table 1. GSE89632 (GPL14951) included 39 NAFLD samples and 24 controls; GSE37031 (GPL14877) contained 8 NAFLD samples and 7 controls; GSE48452 (GPL11532) comprised 32 NAFLD samples, 14 controls, and 27 healthy obese samples. We identified 169 NETs-related genes (NETsRGs) from the GeneCards [11] (https://www.genecards.org/) and the molecular signatures database [12] (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb) along with an additional 137 NETsRGs from PubMed [13]. Following the consolidation and elimination of redundant entries, a total of 260 NETsRGs were identified.

The “sva” package [14] was employed to remove batch effects from the GSE89632, GSE37031, and GSE48452, generating the combined GEO datasets. This compilation comprised 65 NAFLD and 45 control datasets and was standardized by “limma” package [15]. In order to evaluate the effectiveness of the batch effect correction, we employed principal component analysis [16].

Identification and profiling of NET-Associated differentially expressed genes in NAFLD

Utilizing the R package “limma”, we performed a comparative analysis aimed at identifying genes that demonstrate substantial expression discrepancies between the NAFLD and control cohorts. Transcripts that met the criteria of |log2FC| > 0.5 and adjusted p-value (adj.p) < 0.05 were designated as differentially expressed genes (DEGs). The adjustment method for p-values utilized in this analysis was the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure. Volcano plots were generated using “ggplot2”. DEGs and NETsRGs were taken to intersect to get NETs-related differentially expressed genes (NETsRDEGs) utilizing the “VennDiagram” package. Heatmaps were generated utilizing the “pheatmap” package, while chromosome localization was illustrated through the “RCircos” package [17].

Construction and validation of a diagnostic model for NAFLD

The NETsRDEGs were identified based on a p < 0.05 criterion and utilized to create a Logistic regression model for establishing an integrated diagnostic model for NAFLD, which was visualized through a forest plot. Subsequently, a Support Vector Machine (SVM) model was built utilizing selected genes from the Logistic regression model that provided the maximum accuracy and minimum error rate. Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression analysis was conducted on the NETsRDEGs of the SVM model with the “glmnet” package[18]. The results were demonstrated via a diagnostic model diagram and variable trajectory plot, yielding the NAFLD diagnostic model comprising NETsRDEGs as model genes. To evaluate the diagnostic performance of the RiskScore, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated and the area under the curve (AUC) was computed using the “pROC” package. The “rms” package was utilized to develop a nomogram for visualizing the relationships between the model genes. Calibration analysis and decision curve analysis were conducted utilizing the R “ggDCA” package to ascertain the precision, discriminatory capacity, and clinical relevance of the NAFLD diagnostic model.

Functional and pathway enrichment analysis

Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analyses of NETsRDEGs were performed with the “clusterProfiler” package [19]. Significance was set at adj.p < 0.05 and FDR (q value) < 0.25, with Benjamini-Hochberg correction applied.

Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was conducted on the complete set of genes from the merged datasets. This analysis was executed with the “ClusterProfiler” R package, employing 1000 permutations and considering enrichments significant at adjusted P < 0.05 and FDR < 0.25. Utilizing the median RiskScore, the cohort diagnosed with NAFLD was divided into two separate subgroups: high-risk and low-risk groups. Subsequent GSEA comparisons between risk groups were set at a significance level of p < 0.05.

To investigate disparities in pathway enrichment, Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA) was employed. This analysis compared NAFLD to control samples, and further contrasted the high-risk and low-risk subgroups identified among NAFLD patients. The investigation utilized the h.all.v7.4.symbols.gmt collection from the molecular signatures database. A Benjamini-Hochberg adj.p < 0.05 was established as the criterion for statistical significance, thereby accounting for multiple hypothesis testing.

Immune infiltration analysis

To characterize the immune microenvironment, an immune infiltration analysis was conducted using the single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA)[20] technique implemented in the “GSVA” R package. The resultant enrichment scores were then employed to evaluate the abundance of various immune cell populations. Disparities in immune cell infiltration between control and NAFLD groups were visualized using R package “ggplot2” in combined GEO datasets. The Spearman’s rank-order correlation calculated immune cell correlations, and the R package ‘pheatmap’ visualized a correlation heatmap. Finally, associations linking the model genes to specific immune cells were also computed via Spearman’s rank-order correlation and graphically represented using a bubble plot.

Construction of regulatory networks and rediction of therapeutic compounds

Transcription factors (TFs) were identified within the ChIPBase database[21] (http://rna.sysu.edu.cn/chipbase/), and their regulatory effects on model genes were analyzed. The microRNA (miRNA) associated with model genes were identified from the StarBase v3.0 database[22] (https://starbase.sysu.edu.cn/), with a cutoff of “pancancerNum > 13”. RNA-binding Proteins (RBPs)[23] for model genes were predicted through the StarBase v3.0 database, retaining those with “clusterNum > 10”. We constructed the regulatory networks for messenger RNA (mRNA)-TF, mRNA-miRNA, and mRNA-RBP interactions using Cytoscape software. The Comparative Toxicogenomics Database[24] (https://ctdbase.org/) was utilized to predict drug targets for model genes, with a “Reference Count > 2” threshold. The mRNA-Drug interaction network was displayed using Cytoscape software.

Prediction of protein domains and Three-dimensional structures

The AlphaFoldDB was utilized for the prediction and visualization of protein structures in model genes, whereby it generated a Predicted Local Distance Difference Test (pLDDT) score varying from 0 to 100 for each residue. A pLDDT value below 50 indicated low confidence in the predicted structure, while values between 50 and 70 denoted moderate confidence. Structures with pLDDT scores ranging from 70 to 90 were considered highly confident, whereas those surpassing a pLDDT value of 90 exhibited utmost confidence.

Statistical analysis

For statistical analysis, we utilized R software (Version 4.3.0). The independent Student’s T-Test was applied to compare continuous variables between two groups, whereas the Mann-Whitney U Test was selected for non-normally distributed variables. Spearman correlation analysis was carried out to determine the correlation coefficient among various molecules. All statistical tests were two-tailed, with significance defined as a p-value of less than 0.05.

Results

Identification of NETs-Related differentially expressed Genes in NAFLD

After removing batch effects from the NAFLD datasets GSE89632, GSE37031, and GSE48452, a combined GEO datasets was obtained. The distribution boxplot (Supplementary Fig. 1A-B) and principal component analysis plot (Supplementary Fig. 1C-D) results indicated effective elimination of batch effects in the NAFLD datasets. In the combined GEO datasets, 541 DEGs were detected when comparing NAFLD patients to controls, comprising 277 genes upregulated and 264 genes downregulated. The volcano plot was shown in Fig. 2A. The integration of interactions between NETsRGs and DEGs in Venn analysis resulted in the identification of 22 NETsRDEGs (Fig. 2B), including NFIL3, LDLR, CCL2, CXCL2, IL6, SOCS3, C5AR1, IL1B, IL1RL1, CD274, PLAUR, PTX3, PADI4, F3, S100A12, TLR7, TLR2, FCAR, FPR2, CXCL1, CLEC4E, S100A8. The heatmap showed the differential expression of NETsRDEGs between the NAFLD and control groups (Fig. 2C). The loop graph mapped the chromosomal locations of 22 NETsRDEGs, with chromosome 1 containing more NETsRDEGs such as PADI4, F3, S100A12, and S100A8 (Fig. 2D).

Validation of NETsRDEGs expression and diagnostic performance

Expression analysis demonstrated significant differences in all 22 NETsRDEGs when comparing the NAFLD group to the control group(Fig. 3A).To further evaluate their diagnostic potential, ROC analysis was performed on these genes. Among them, 15 NETsRDEGs (NFIL3, LDLR, CCL2, CXCL2, IL6, SOCS3, C5AR1, IL1B, IL1RL1, CD274, PLAUR, PTX3, PADI4, F3, and S100A12) demonstrated moderate discriminative ability (0.7 < AUC < 0.9) in distinguishing NAFLD from control samples. In contrast, the remaining 7 genes (TLR7, TLR2, FCAR, FPR2, CXCL1, CLEC4E, and S100A8) showed lower diagnostic accuracy (0.5 < AUC < 0.7) (Fig. 3B–G).

Functional enrichment analysis of NETsRDEGs and NAFLD-Associated pathways

GO and KEGG enrichment analyses of the 22 NETsRDEGs revealed their significant involvement in key biological processes and signaling pathways (Fig. 4A-B). In the biological process category, these genes were notably enriched in the positive regulation of responses to external stimuli, leukocyte chemotaxis, and modulation of inflammatory responses. For molecular function, significant terms included cytokine activity, NAD⁺ nucleosidase activity, and immune receptor activity. Concurrently, KEGG pathway analysis identified notable involvement of the NETsRDEGs in signaling pathways such as IL-17 and TNF, among others.

GSEA was employed to identify the biological functions and signaling pathways associated with NAFLD in comparison to the control group using combined GEO datasets. The bubble chart showed four significantly enriched biological functions (Fig. 4C). GSEA revealed significant enrichment in the following biological processes: negative regulation of NOTCH4 signaling (Fig. 4D), TP53-mediated regulation of metabolic genes (Fig. 4E), and the PI3K-AKT (Fig. 4F) and IL-4 (Fig. 4G) signaling pathways.

The gene sets h.all.v7.4.symbols.gmt were compared between NAFLD and control groups using GSVA. The top 10 positively and negatively enriched pathways, with an adj.p < 0.05, were ranked based on logFC values and visualized with a heat map (Fig. 4H). Group comparison plots (Fig. 4I) further confirmed significant differences (p < 0.05) in multiple hallmark pathways—including E2F targets, DNA repair, oxidative phosphorylation, fatty acid metabolism, and inflammatory response—between NAFLD and control samples.

Construction and validation of a NETs-Related diagnostic model

To evaluate the diagnostic potential of the 22 NETsRDEGs in NAFLD, we first applied logistic regression. The forest plot (Fig. 5A) illustrates that all 22 genes exhibited significant associations (p < 0.05) with the disease status. Subsequently, SVM analysis was utilized for feature selection, identifying an optimal panel of 16 genes—TLR7, PLAUR, LDLR, CCL2, S100A8, PADI4, TLR2, FPR2, S100A12, F3, IL1RL1, CD274, NFIL3, IL1B, SOCS3, and C5AR1—that achieved peak predictive performance with minimal error (Fig. 5B-C). These 16 NETsRDEGs were subsequently subjected to LASSO regression to construct a refined diagnostic model. The model selection process is depicted in the coefficient profile plot (Fig. 5D) and the variable trajectory plot (Fig. 5E), which collectively pinpointed eight key predictor genes: TLR7, LDLR, CCL2, S100A8, PADI4, F3, CD274, and NFIL3. Immune infiltration analysis In NAFLD

We employed ssGSEA to quantify the infiltration levels of 28 immune cell subsets in NAFLD and control liver tissues. Comparative analysis revealed significant alterations in the immune landscape of NAFLD patients, with 17 cell types showing statistically distinct enrichment scores (p < 0.05, Fig. 6A). These included Activated B cells, Activated CD4+ T cells, Activated CD8+ T cells, Type 2 T helper (Th2) cells and multiple innate and adaptive immune populations, indicating broad immune dysregulation in NAFLD. The heatmap of the correlation among 17 immune cells subsets revealed strong associations among the majority of these immune populations (Fig. 6B). Of particular interest, the infiltration levels of CD8⁺T cells and gamma-delta T cells were tightly coordinated (r = 0.883, p < 0.05), suggesting their synergistic role in shaping the inflammatory milieu of NAFLD. We further explored the relationship between model genes and immune infiltration. TLR7 expression showed the strongest positive correlation with activated CD8⁺T cells (r = 0.827, p < 0.05, Fig. 6C), implicating TLR7 signaling in the modulation of CD8⁺T cell recruitment or activation in NAFLD.

Functional and immune heterogeneity between risk subgroups

Differential analysis identified 3,151 DEGs, and GSEA showed notable distinctions in NAFLD-associated pathways between high-risk and low-risk groups, including AKT pathway, hedgehog signaling paracrine upregulation, hippocampal synaptogenesis and neurogenesis, as well as TGF beta1 targets 10 h DN regulation (Supplementary Fig. 2A-G). GSVA of gene sets in low-risk and high-risk NAFLD samples identified the top 10 positively and negatively enriched pathways(Supplementary Fig. 3 A). GSVA results revealed significant disparities between the two groups in several pathways, such as peroxisome, fatty acid metabolism, inflammatory response(Supplementary Fig. 3A-B).

To delineate the immune characteristics of the risk subgroups defined by our diagnostic model, we compared immune infiltration landscapes between high-risk and low-risk NAFLD patients. ssGSEA revealed markedly distinct infiltration patterns across all 20 immune cell subsets examined (Fig. 7J). Notably, a strong positive correlation was identified between Type 1 T helper cells and central memory CD4+ T cells (r = 0.805, p < 0.05) in the high-risk cohort (Fig. 7K). Conversely, in the low-risk group, effector memory CD8+ T cells and myeloid-derived suppressor cells were positively correlated (r = 0.89, p < 0.05; Fig. 7L). Furthermore, the model genes were significantly associated with immune cell abundances in a subgroup-specific manner. The expression level of TLR7 showed the strongest positive correlation with central memory CD4+ T cells in the high-risk group (r = 0.869, p < 0.05; Fig. 7M), whereas S100A8 was strongly linked to neutrophil infiltration in the low-risk group (r = 0.829, p < 0.05; Fig. 7N).

Construction of the regulatory network

The mRNA-TF regulatory network included seven model genes and 30 TFs (Fig. 8A). The mRNA-miRNA regulatory network comprised five model genes and 25 miRNA (Fig. 8B). Discussion

With a staggering prevalence exceeding 29% in the Chinese adult population [1], NAFLD has become a paramount chronic liver disease worldwide, posing a significant and growing burden on public health systems. First-line interventions primarily rely on lifestyle modifications, which are often difficult to sustain long-term, while pharmacological options are scarce and not universally effective. There is a pressing need to identify novel, non-invasive biomarkers and mechanistically informed therapeutic targets to improve early diagnosis and treatment precision. NETs have recently emerged as key mediators of inflammation and fibrosis in metabolic liver diseases, yet their role in NAFLD remains poorly characterized. Leveraging integrated bioinformatics approaches, our study aimed to systematically identify NETs-related molecular signatures and construct a robust diagnostic model, thereby addressing a critical research gap and offering potential translational insights. We successfully developed a diagnostic model based on NETsRDEGs, which demonstrated excellent discriminative ability in internal validation (AUC = 0.920).

A total of 22 NETsRDEGs were determined through the examination of DEGs, including LDLR and PADI4. The LDLR is fundamental in the modulation of blood cholesterol concentrations due to its ability to interact with low-density lipoprotein. Dysfunction of LDLR function is a significant contributor to hypercholesterolemia, atherosclerosis, and NAFLD [25]. Our investigation demonstrated a notable decrease in LDLR expression levels in individuals with NAFLD when compared to the control group, corroborating findings from earlier studies [25]. A HFD administered to LDLR-/- mice effectively induces the development of NASH, characterized by elevated insulin levels and hyperlipidemia [26]. The infiltration of neutrophils and the presence of lipotoxic hepatocytes are hallmark alterations associated with NASH. Research has shown that selective extracellular vesicles dependent on LDLR and apoe could transfer miR-223 derived from neutrophils into hepatocytes, thereby alleviating the progression of NAFLD. Furthermore, inhibitors of proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 upregulate the expression of LDL receptors, contributing to a reduction in hypercholesterolemia while simultaneously mitigating hepatic inflammation and fibrosis [27]. The PADI4 gene encodes an enzyme called peptidyl arginine deiminase type 4 (PAD4), which has the ability to convert arginine in proteins into citrulline, a process referred to as citrullination [28]. Research indicates a significant connection between PADI4 and various disorders such as rheumatoid arthritis and liver malignancies [29, 30]. Nevertheless, the prospective role of this gene in the pathogenesis of NAFLD is still inadequately comprehended. NETs could contribute to the development of liver inflammation through various pro-inflammatory factors [4], where PADI4 serves as a key mediator in their generation [31]. Research indicates that a lack of PAD4 might reduce the formation of NETs and lung fibrosis induced by bleomycin [32]. The reduced expression of PADI4 was detected in individuals with NAFLD in the present study. Nonetheless, further studies are necessary to determine the influence of PADI4 on the advancement of NAFLD, particularly regarding its effect on the formation of NETs.

We identified eight pivotal model genes(including TLR7, LDLR, CCL2, S100A8, PADI4, F3, CD274 and NFIL3) from the 22 NETsRDEGs using the LASSO regression model. Subsequently, we constructed a RiskScore model incorporating these genes. This model achieved an AUC value exceeding 0.9 in the ROC curve analysis, demonstrating its robust discriminative performance and potential clinical utility in differentiating NAFLD patients from control groups. These model genes might significantly contribute to the pathogenesis of NAFLD through their involvement in inflammatory pathways and immune cells infiltration. Notably, S100A8, a component of NETs, is integral to the activation of neutrophils and the formation of NETs [33], which have been implicated in promoting tissue damage and inflammation across a spectrum of diseases, including NAFLD. Previous studies indicate that S100A8 might facilitate NAFLD progression by enhancing pro-inflammatory cytokine production, such as TNF-α, which is a critical mediator of inflammation and holds a key position in NAFLD pathogenesis [34].

In our study, GSEA revealed significant enrichment of pathways including negative regulation of Notch4 signaling and PI3K-AKT signaling pathway, both of which are intimately linked to the pathogenesis of NAFLD. The Notch signaling pathway, an evolutionarily conserved mechanism present in diverse species, mediates intercellular communication via specific ligand-receptor binding. It plays pivotal roles in regulating cellular multiplication, development, differentiation, and the maintenance of cell viability. The Notch receptor family has four distinct components, namely Notch1 to 4, all on the cell membrane. These receptors get activated when interacting with ligands like Delta-like 1, 3, and 4 in neighboring cells. Our findings indicate a significant influence of the negative regulation of Notch4 signaling on NAFLD progression. This observation is consistent with previous studies implicating Notch signaling in dysregulated lipid metabolism, insulin resistance, and fibrosis in NAFLD [35]. For instance, recent work has shown Notch pathway activation in liver macrophages from NAFLD patients and murine models [36], while He et al. demonstrated that its inhibition reduces pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion and attenuates liver fibrosis [37]. Our results align with these reports and further suggest that suppressed Notch4 signaling—particularly in the context of NETsRGs expression—may be instrumental in driving NAFLD progression. Notably, our bioinformatic analysis provides the first evidence linking the negative regulation of NOTCH4 signaling to a NETsRGs signature in NAFLD, though the exact underlying mechanisms remain to be fully elucidated.

Concurrently, the PI3K-AKT pathway emerged from our analysis as another pivotal signaling axis that coordinately modulates lipid homeostasis, insulin responsiveness, and fibrotic processes—key pathological features implicated in NAFLD. This is consistent with established literature: AKT deficiency is linked to insulin resistance [38], and PI3K-AKT activation stimulates hepatic fatty acid and cholesterol synthesis [39]. Moreover, NUP85, which is upregulated in NAFLD, attenuates lipid accumulation and inflammation upon inhibition via PI3K-AKT downregulation [40]. In the context of NASH-induced fibrosis, the PI3K/Akt-p70S6K signaling axis may promote the activation of hepatic stellate cells and stimulate the production of collagen [41]. Therefore, our study collectively corroborates these findings and, for the first time, links PI3K-AKT pathway enrichment to a NETs-based transcriptional profile in NAFLD. This novel connection not only underscores the central role of PI3K-AKT signaling but also repositions it as a promising therapeutic target within the specific pathogenic context of NETs-related immunometabolic dysregulation.

Our immune infiltration analysis revealed a remodeled hepatic immune landscape in NAFLD, characterized by significant alterations in the abundance of 17 immune cell types, including activated CD8⁺T cells and Th2 cells. These findings suggest a broad dysregulation of both innate and adaptive immunity in NAFLD and point to a potential interaction between NETsRGs and the immune microenvironment in disease pathogenesis. CD8+ T cells, commonly referred to as cytotoxic T lymphocytes, could cause hepatic inflammation and injury by directly eliminating damaged hepatocytes or secreting cytokines such as IL-10 [42]. In cooperation with natural killer T cells, they might contribute to inflammation and fibrosis in NASH [43]. Consistent with previous research [42], we noted a heightened presence of hepatic Activated CD8+ T cells in NAFLD. Depletion of CD8+ T cells resulted in marked reductions in hepatic inflammation and hepatic stellate cells activation in obese NASH mice [42]. Regarding Th2 cells, our study noted a reduced number of infiltrating Th2 cells in the livers of NAFLD patients compared to controls. Th2 cells are central to host defense against parasitic infections and the pathogenesis of allergic responses by secreting key cytokines, including IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13 [44]. While their specific functions in NAFLD remain inadequately defined. Previous research has demonstrated a significant reduction in Th2 cells numbers within visceral adipose tissue of HFD fed mice. Interestingly, transplantation of CD4+ T cells from healthy donors into lymphocyte-deficient mice on an HFD resulted in weight loss, primarily through differentiation into Th2 cells [45]. Nevertheless, several investigations have indicated that there are no notable discrepancies in the populations of Th2 cells when comparing patients with NASH to the control groups [46]. Notably, Th2-derived IL-13 is significantly elevated in NASH patients, and hepatic stellate cells express high levels of its receptor, IL-13Rα2. Administration of an IL-13 cytotoxin effectively mitigates liver fibrosis in NASH mice [47]. Additionally, IL-33, a cytokine that enhances Th2 responses, exhibits a complex role in NASH pathophysiology, exacerbating fibrosis when administered as a recombinant protein while mitigating liver injury [48]. In conclusion, our results underscore the critical role of CD8+ T cells and Th2 cells in influencing the immune microenvironment associated with NAFLD. Additional research is necessary to elucidate the precise mechanisms through which these immune cells contribute to disease progression and to examine prospective therapeutic approaches aimed at modulating these immune pathways.

Given the scarcity of effective therapies for NAFLD, this research investigated medications targeting specific model genes. Resveratrol, a natural polyphenol, exerts beneficial effects on NAFLD by modulating inflammation, lipid and carbohydrate metabolism, as well as reducing oxidative stress [49]. In randomized clinical trials with NAFLD patients, lower-dose resveratrol supplementation demonstrates attenuation of hepatic steatosis [50], reduction in inflammation and insulin resistance [51], as well as improvement in lipid profile [52]; conversely, higher doses of resveratrol (3000 mg) have not shown a significant effect on insulin resistance, steatosis [53]. Interestingly, resveratrol has been demonstrated to diminish the production of NETs in individuals experiencing severe COVID-19 [54], suggesting a potential role in mitigating NET formation in the liver as well. Curcumin, a polyphenolic compound isolated from the rhizome of turmeric, confers notable therapeutic benefits primarily through its capacity to modulate inflammatory responses and counteract oxidative stress. Research indicates that it can decrease transaminase and total cholesterol levels, and alleviate liver fat degeneration in NAFLD patients [55]. Curcumin’s ability to prevent NET formation through the inhibition of the MEK/ERK signaling pathway suggests a potential protective role against hepatic ischemia-reperfusion injury [56]. The synergistic effect of curcumin and resveratrol in alleviating liver steatosis via the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway further highlights their therapeutic potential [57]. Simvastatin, widely recognized for its cholesterol-lowering properties, has been demonstrated to ameliorate NASH in mice by reducing intrahepatic lipid levels and oxidative stress [58]. Further contributing to its hepatoprotective effects, research indicates that it enhances liver microcirculation through diminishing leukocyte recruitment, attenuating hepatic stellate cell activation, and mitigating oxidative stress [59]. Moreover, the protective effect of simvastatin against ischemia-reperfusion injury has been demonstrated in murine models of high-fat diet-induced obesity, where it acts by reducing the generation of NETs [60]. Our bioinformatics analysis indicates that compounds such as resveratrol, curcumin and simvastatin might provide therapeutic benefits for NAFLD by targeting key pathological factors.

Our research presents a number of limitations. Firstly, the study relies on public databases and lacks experimental validation; therefore, additional experiments and clinical investigations are necessary to support the findings. Secondly, the limited sample size might hinder the applicability of our results to a wider demographic, and the broader applicability and robustness of the RiskScore model must be confirmed through further validation in larger, more diverse patient cohorts. In particular, while we performed comprehensive internal validation—including ROC curves, calibration plots, and decision curve analysis—to assess model stability and clinical utility, we acknowledge the absence of external validation. Future studies should prioritize the collection of independent clinical samples or the use of additional public datasets to rigorously evaluate the generalizability of the model. Thirdly, the relatively small sample size and the number of predictors (8 genes) raise considerations regarding the events per variable ratio, which may impact the stability of the model. Lastly, although we attempted to mitigate batch effects, their influence on our findings could not be fully eliminated, potentially introducing biases into our analysis.

Conclusion

In this research, we identified key NETsRGs(TLR7, LDLR, CCL2, S100A8, PADI4, F3, CD274, NFIL3) in NAFLD and developed a highly accurate diagnostic model. These findings provide novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets for NAFLD, though further mechanistic exploration is needed to confirm their roles in disease progression and therapeutic efficacy.

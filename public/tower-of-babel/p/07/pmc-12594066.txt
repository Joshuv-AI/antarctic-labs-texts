Unpacking medication choices: Key factors influencing treatment decisions in children with newly diagnosed attention deficit hyperactivity disorder
Authors: Nidhi Chauhan, Velprashanth Venkatesan, Akhilesh Sharma, Ruchita Shah, Sandeep Grover, Debashish Basu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Following a diagnosis of Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) many children require immediate use of pharmacological agents for symptom control, while for some children a trial of non-pharmacological management may be warranted initially.Aim:To study and identify the clinical and socio-economic factors associated with immediate use of pharmacotherapy versus non pharmacological management in children with newly diagnosed ADHD.Methods:A retrospective cross sectional study design using chart review was conducted in child and adolescent psychiatry services of a tertiary care hospital. The medical records between 2018 and 2020 were reviewed. The socio-demographic factors, symptom profiles and comorbidities were recorded. Descriptive statistics and Binary Logistic regression analyses were calculated for factors associated with use versus non-use of pharmacotherapy.Results:187 patients screened positive for ADHD at the walk-in clinic and after detailed assessments, 101 were diagnosed with ADHD. With every one-point increase in Vanderbilt ADHD Parent Rating Scale (VADPRS) severity score, there is 1.2 times [P = 0.002; Exp(B) = 1.281; 99% CI: 1.089–1.494] higher likelihood of getting a pharmacological prescription for ADHD. Combined presentation has 3.8 times [P = 0.015; Exp(B) = 3.808; 99% CI: 1.295–11.198] higher likelihood of getting a pharmacological prescription compared to only hyperactive/impulsive and inattentive presentations. Among the socio-demographic variables, no statistically significant variables were observed, although the odds ratios among all social categories displayed a meaningful trend.Conclusion:Pharmacotherapy for ADHD at initial diagnosis is strongly predicted by the severity of ADHD and other socio-economic variable that may require equal attention in the coming years.

INTRODUCTION

ADHD (Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder) is one of the most prevalent disorders affecting children with a worldwide prevalence of 5% and 2.5% in adults making it one of the most diagnosed neurobehavioral disorders in children.[1] Indian data on ADHD is rapidly emerging and the recent systematic review from studies across the country show a point prevalence ranging from 1.30% to 28.3% while the pooled prevalence is 7.1% with a 2:1 male preponderance.[2] Pharmacological management remains the cornerstone of ADHD management.[3] It is often said that a diagnosis of ADHD should be made with caution in toddlers and preschoolers and only by the time a child enters primary school, a diagnosis of ADHD can be made with confidence. Many children require immediate use of pharmacological agents for symptom control but NICE guidelines recommend first line pharmacotherapy (stimulants) in children after 5 years of age and other guidelines recommend both first and second line after 6 years of age.[4]

In a multicentric study across Europe and Asia, observation of children above 3 years from between January 1, 2001, and December 31, 2015, the prevalence of ADHD medication use among children increased over time in all countries and regions, and the absolute increase per year ranged from 0·02% to 0·26%.[5] There is no such study from India and hence, a need to assess the percentage of children, who are prescribed medications for ADHD after first time diagnosis in the Indian set up. Thus, the aim of the current study was to evaluate socio-environmental and clinical factors associated with increased likelihood of pharmacotherapy in children with ADHD after getting diagnosed for the first time.

METHOD

The Institute Ethics Committee approval was taken before undertaking this chart review. The STROBE Reporting guidelines for cross-sectional analytical studies was followed for the index study (the same has been uploaded as a supplementary file).

A retrospective cross-sectional study using the chart review of medical records of children and adolescents (age between 5 years to 17 years) evaluated in the Child and Adolescent psychiatry services between January 01, 2018 to March 22, 2020 was conducted [See Figure 1]. The primary objective was to assess the factors associated with immediate use of medications versus nonpharmacological management in newly diagnosed children with ADHD during this time. The children and adolescents visiting walk in clinic are evaluated at a quick glance and most of them are called again soon for a detailed clinical assessment. Only samples charted between January 1, 2018, till March 22, 2020, were taken up for study. This was conducted since our study wanted to assess the socio-economic factors affecting medication use and since the period following 2020 was marred by Covid -19 lockdown scenarios. The post-covid world with job deprivations and increased online education from home setting, would not be an accurate representation of the conventional socio-economic norms.

Participants data flow diagram

In the study period, 187 patients were screened for detailed evaluation for ADHD and were scheduled for detailed assessments, following which 101 patients were diagnosed as having ADHD and this formed the study sample. As a part of the detailed work-up, severity scoring was done on VADPRS (Vanderbilt ADHD Parent Rating Scale). VADPRS is a 47 item 4-point Likert-type scale based on DSM-5 (0= “never,” 1 = “occasionally,” 2 = “often,” and 3 = “very often”), and includes both the core symptoms of ADHD and a rating of performance, as well as provides a screen for common comorbid conditions like Oppositional Defiant Disorder (ODD), Conduct Disorder (CD), anxiety/depressive symptoms. It yields six mutually exclusive subscales- inattention scale (items 1–9), hyperactivity scale (items 10–18), combined presentation (items 1–18), oppositional (items 19–26), conduct disorder (items 27–40), anxiety/depression scale (items 41–47). Higher the score, greater is the severity. The socio-demographic factors, symptom profiles and comorbidities were recorded from the medical records.

Descriptive statistics of the data using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS-23) was conducted and frequency variables were charted. Using a Binary Logistic Regression Analyses, having a binary dependent variable of “Use” versus “Non-Use” of medication after diagnostic confirmation was analyzed. The Odds Ratio was calculated for factors associated with increased likelihood of use of medications in children newly diagnosed with ADHD. For the clinical variables the reference point for interpretation of Odds ratio [Exp(B)] was taken as the absence of that comorbidity, such that the interpretation would be the likelihood of use of medication if that comorbidity was present compared to it being absent. And for the sociodemographic variables, the reference point for interpretation of Odds Ratio was taken in the ascending trend, with highest age category, highest level of academic attainment, occupational achievement and salary being the reference point for that category.

RESULTS

The study sample included 94.1% boys with a mean age of 9.54 years and mean education of 5.70 years. Tables 1 and 2 highlight the socio-demographic variables (religion, locality, age, and education of either parent, occupation and monthly income) and clinical variables (VADPRS scores and comorbid disorders). The overall VADPRS total score was 28.8 ± 4.72. Based on category of severity, majority 52.9% of subjects had a score between 26–32.21% had score more than and 21.6% between 18–25 suggesting a predominant representation of ADHD subjects having higher severity scores. Off the total 101 subjects, 49 patients were diagnosed in 2018; 42 diagnosed in 2019 and 10 diagnosed in 2020. With respect to medication use following diagnosis, 24 out of 49 in 2018; 26 out of 42 in 2019 and 8 out of 10 in 2020 were prescribed medications. Overall, a total of 58 subjects out of 101 subjects diagnosed with ADHD were prescribed medications and Methylphenidate was the most prescribed medication (in 32 out of 58 subjects).

78 out of 101 subjects had a comorbid diagnosis. 92 out of 101 subjects had 2 or less diagnoses and remaining 9 out of 101 subjects had more than two diagnoses. The subjects with more than two diagnosis had 2.8 times [P < 0.5; Exp(B) = 2.84; 95% CI: 0.55–14.28] higher likelihood of getting an ADHD drug prescription.

Binary regression analyses revealed that the likelihood of use of medications for their children was more in mothers aged 25–35 compared to those aged 45 years and more. Among the clinical variables, VADPRS total score [P = 0.002; Exp(B) = 1.28] and presence of combined ADHD presentation [P = 0.015; Exp(B) = 3.80] had clinically significant values. Among the socio-demographic variables, no clinically significant value was observed, although the odds ratios among all categories showed a meaningful trend with higher odds observed in certain parameters, namely, young parents in the age range of 25–35 years had 3–5 times higher likelihood to start medications compared to those above 45 years [mother aged 25–35 Exp(B): 3.42; father aged 25–35 Exp(B):5.24]. Mothers with high school education were 1.4 times more likely to start medications compared to mothers with a postgraduate degree [mother education till High school Exp(B): 1.42]. Families belonging to socioeconomically middle class with blue collar job for the bread winner (semiskilled labor or agricultural) had 1.3–1.5 times higher likelihood of starting medications compared to professionals/senior officials [semiskilled labor Exp(B): 1.36; skilled Agriculture/Fisheries Exp(B): 1.58].

DISCUSSION

This study was able to analyses a period data of children with ADHD, in the years prior to imposition of covid lockdown, diagnosed from a child psychiatry unit of a tertiary hospital and was representative of both rural and urban population as children usually get referred to the same unit from the peripheral health care institutions and from within the tertiary care hospital. The primary question was to see what factors at the time of diagnosing ADHD for the first time, warranted immediate use of medications. There are factors beyond clinical features that determine the prescription of medications in ADHD especially the social variables.[6] There is increasing availability and use of ADHD medications in LAMIC countries.[7] Often the medication use in India is a big decision for the parents for their children, since the necessity of long-term usage is considered. This decision-making process is very liable to be influenced in the Indian set up by family dynamics.[8] A recent Indian study from same locality suggests the pivotal role of mother’s understanding of ADHD in the help seeking and decision to accept treatment.[9]

The sociodemographic profile of study subjects revealed 94% males mainly from nuclear families (60%) and urban locality (66%), and is as per the distribution noted in other studies of ADHD population from our center.[1011]

Among the clinical factors, ODD was found to be the most common comorbid diagnosis followed by Conduct Disorder, Specific Learning Disability, and Intellectual Disability. 58 out of 101 children with newly diagnosed ADHD received the indicated medications; Methylphenidate (32/58) was the most prescribed, followed by Atomoxetine (16/58) and Clonidine (10/58), respectively. This finding is in line with a larger sample from China where methylphenidate followed by Atomoxetine remained the most used ADHD medications.[12] In the logistic regression, the clinical variables VADPRS total score and presence of ADHD, combined presentation [Table 2] were significant predictors of medication use. With every one-point increase in VADPRS severity score, there is 1.2 times higher likelihood of getting a pharmacological management for ADHD. Combined presentation had 3.8 times higher likelihood of getting a pharmacological management compared to other two presentations. Unlike studies that show presence of comorbidities such as Conduct Disorder being a predictor of medication use,[13] our sample did not find any such significance. Although we saw that presence of more than two diagnosis, such as a combination of ADHD with ODD and Conduct, showed a higher likelihood of getting a prescription (2.84 times more), compared to having a single or no comorbidity with ADHD. But based on the factors with higher Odds, the results here emphasize that irrespective of the comorbidities, just the increased severity of ADHD symptoms (a combined ADHD presentation or a higher VADPRS score) remains the best predictor of use of pharmacological management. This finding goes in line with a larger sample analyzed in Australia which also showed symptom severity being an incontrovertible factor.[14]

None of the sociodemographic variables predicted the initiation of ADHD medication. However, a closer look at the odds ratios of sociodemographic variables points towards a possible trend in use of medications more in lower middle class, nuclear families of urban background. Mothers educated up to matric or high school compared to post graduate status, showed a higher likelihood towards acceptance of drugs similar to a study from Canada, where lower maternal education was a strong predictor of medication use.[6] The population belonging to extremes such as lower academic and economic status and those belonging to higher socioeconomic status show a trend of having lesser odds of taking a prescription at initial diagnosis of ADHD. Poor economic sustenance in people of lower socioeconomic status to initiate and continue medications in India could explain the trend of lower medication use in this population. But the trends seen in the higher economic strata in our sample show a unique pattern. This is different from the west where ADHD prescriptions are more accepted by people presenting from higher economic strata.[15] Perhaps, the easier access to second opinions from a private sector in a country like India, could be a deterrent for parents coming from higher socioeconomic status to delay initiation of ADHD medications. The middle economic population often rely on government sector for day-to-day care and are less likely to opt for second opinions from a private doctor and are more likely to follow a systematic level of care within a public tertiary care center and are thus likely to accept a prescription without skepticism. A similar pattern was also seen in another study where use of ADHD medications in teens was higher for those belonging to “usual source of care” that is, subjects who are streamlined with health insurance towards regular levels of care.[16]

Our study is limited by a modest sample size and lack of robust statistical findings to ascertain the trends in the socio-demographic factors affecting the medication use in ADHD. Due to its retrospective design, there is possible temporal bias to suggest causality. Thus, the interpretations shared must be taken with a pinch of salt and perhaps lay the path towards a more comprehensive chart review in future. Furthermore, the data we analyzed is not representative of the current economic climate of the world post-covid with Covid-19 lockdown influencing ADHD symptoms.[17] And a future direction is to conduct a comprehensive study of these factors in the years following Covid lockdown. Our study thus concludes that early ADHD medication use at initial diagnosis is strongly predicted by just the severity of ADHD irrespective of the socio-economic factors although there may be other socio-economic variables which will require equal attention in the coming years.

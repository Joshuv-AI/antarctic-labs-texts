Diagnostic Value and Clinical Significance of Temporopolar White-Matter Hyperintensities in CADASIL and Sporadic Small-Vessel Disease
Authors: Wen-Yu Ou Yang, Shao-Lun Hsu, Chih-Ping Chung, Feng-Chi Chang, Yi-Chung Lee, Yi-Chu Liao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and PurposeCerebral autosomal dominant arteriopathy with subcortical infarcts and leukoencephalopathy (CADASIL) is caused by NOTCH3 variants, and is the leading hereditary cerebral small-vessel disease (SVD). Temporopolar white-matter hyperintensities (WMH) are characteristic imaging features of CADASIL, but their diagnostic accuracy across different NOTCH3 variants and their relationship with the disease severity remain uncertain.MethodsWe analyzed 385 CADASIL patients and 191 sporadic SVD patients in whom no pathogenic mutations were identified in the next-generation sequencing of 183 genes. CADASIL patients were stratified into high-, medium-, and low-risk epidermal growth factor-like repeat (HR-, MR-, and LR-EGFr) categories according to the variant locations. The severity of temporopolar WMH was scored using Scheltens’ scale, and disability was assessed using the modified Rankin Scale (mRS). Logistic and ordinal regression models were used to evaluate the associations between temporopolar WMH, clinical severity, and neuroimaging markers.ResultsTemporopolar WMH were detected in 94.7%, 54.4%, and 33.3% of CADASIL patients carrying HR-, MR-, and LR-EGFr variants, respectively, compared with in 31.9% of the sporadic SVD patients. The sensitivity for distinguishing HR-EGFr carriers from sporadic SVD was high (95%), but lower for MR-EGFr (54%) and LR-EGFr (33%) carriers. In the CADASIL patients, temporopolar WMH independently predicted greater disability (mRS score >3), and they were correlated in a dose-dependent manner with the total WMH volume, Fazekas scores, and number of lacunes. Similar associations were observed in sporadic SVD.ConclusionsThe diagnostic accuracy of temporopolar WMH varies with the NOTCH3 risk category and warrants cautious interpretations, particularly in Asian populations in whom MR-EGFr founder variants are prevalent. The temporopolar WMH severity is associated with the functional status and radiological burden across hereditary and sporadic SVD.Keywords: CADASIL, cerebral small vessel diseases, temporopolar white-matter hyperintensities, neuroimaging, sensitivity

INTRODUCTION

Cerebral small-vessel disease (SVD) is a major contributor to stroke occurrence and vascular cognitive impairment. Among its genetic forms, cerebral autosomal dominant arteriopathy with subcortical infarcts and leukoencephalopathy (CADASIL) is caused by pathogenic variants in NOTCH3, and is the most common and best characterized.1,2 Confluent white-matter hyperintensities (WMH) involving the temporal poles and external capsules have traditionally been considered hallmark imaging features of the disease.3,4 A frequently cited British study found moderate-to-severe temporopolar WMH in 89.1% of CADASIL patients compared with 13.6% of those with sporadic SVD presenting with recurrent lacunar stroke and leukoaraiosis, yielding a sensitivity of 89% and specificity of 86% for differentiating CADASIL from sporadic SVD.5 However, these estimates might not be generalizable across populations or the full spectrum of NOTCH3 variants.

There is accumulating evidence for marked phenotypic and racial heterogeneity in CADASIL, which is largely determined by the locations of cysteine-altering NOTCH3 variants within the high-, medium-, and low-risk epidermal growth-factor-like repeat (HR-EGFr, MR-EGFr, and LR-EGFr, respectively) domains.6 The reported prevalence of temporopolar WMH is lower in CADASIL cohorts from Taiwan and South Korea (20%–44.8%)7,8,9 than in Caucasian cohorts (89%),5 where the former populations are characterized by a high prevalence of the p.R544C founder variant, which is classified under the MR-EGFr category, whereas HR-EGFr variants account for most of the latter cases. Similarly, in a Japanese cohort, temporopolar WMH were present in only 39% of patients carrying the NOTCH3 p.R75P variant and 71% of those carrying other mutations.10 While these findings indicate that variant location and racial background strongly influence the imaging manifestations, the diagnostic accuracy of temporopolar WMH across different NOTCH3 EGFr risk categories remains unclear.

Temporopolar WMH have long been regarded as an imaging hallmark of CADASIL, but they may also be observed in other monogenic SVDs and, albeit infrequently, in sporadic cases. For example, temporopolar WMH have been reported in patients carrying heterozygous mutations in the HtrA Serine Peptidase 1 gene (HTRA1), which is the second most common cause of monogenic SVD (HTRA1-AD-cSVD) after CADASIL.11 Another study found temporal-lobe WMH in 25% of patients with sporadic SVD,3 mainly involving the posterior temporal lobe, which contrasts with WMH typically involving the temporopolar region in CADASIL. These observations raise the possibility that temporopolar WMH are also clinically and pathophysiologically significant beyond CADASIL.

To address these uncertainties, the present study investigated whether temporopolar WMH is associated with the disease severity in Taiwanese CADASIL patients, including their clinical disability and neuroimaging SVD burden. We additionally evaluated the presence of temporopolar WMH in patients with sporadic SVD to clarify its diagnostic accuracy and clinical implications across the hereditary and sporadic forms of SVD.

METHODS

Participants

This retrospective study included participants recruited from a consecutive series of individuals identified with cysteine-altering NOTCH3 variants between February 2004 and July 2024 at Taipei Veterans General Hospital, a national medical center in Taiwan serving both self-referred patients and referrals for complex neurological conditions. During this period, 385 symptomatic patients were genetically confirmed as carrying cysteine-altering NOTCH3 variants (Supplementary Table 1 in the online-only Data Supplement) and presented with stroke, cognitive decline, gait disturbance, psychiatric symptoms, or seizures. No pathogenic cysteine-sparing NOTCH3 variants (e.g., p.R75P) were identified in our cohort. Patients were stratified according to the positions of NOTCH3 variants within the EGFr domains into HR-EGFr variants (EGFr domains 1–6, 8, 11, and 26), MR-EGFr variants (EGFr domains 9, 10, 12–15, 17, 25, 27, and 32), and LR-EGFr variants (EGFr domains 16, 18–20, 23, 24, 28–31, and 33) on established criteria6 reflecting reported genotype–phenotype correlations in CADASIL. None of the patients in our cohort had NOTCH3 variants located in EGFr domains 7, 21, 22, or 34, which were classified as undetermined-risk EGFr domains in the original report.6

We additionally enrolled a separate group of 191 unrelated individuals with sporadic SVD. The following inclusion criteria were applied: 1) presenting with stroke, cognitive decline, or gait disturbance; 2) brain MRI revealing WMH in the deep white matter on fluid-attenuated inversion-recovery (FLAIR) images; 3) presence of at least one lacune on FLAIR or T1-weighted images or more than five cerebral microbleeds (CMB) in susceptibility-weighted imaging (SWI) or T2*-weighted gradient-echo (T2*-GRE) sequences12; and 4) no genetic etiology identified in an analysis using a high-throughput targeted resequencing panel covering 183 genes associated with leukodystrophies and hereditary SVD (Supplementary Table 2 in the online-only Data Supplement).

Clinical and demographic assessments

Demographic and clinical information were collected from medical records. In symptomatic CADASIL patients, the age at symptom onset was defined as the earliest documented age at which stroke, cognitive impairment, or gait disturbance occurred. Disease duration was calculated as the interval between symptom onset and the date of brain MRI examination.

Functional disability was assessed using the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) within 3–6 months of brain MRI data acquisition,13 with mRS score >3 indicating dependence in performing the activities of daily living.14 The disease severity of CADASIL patients was categorized using the NOTCH3-SVD staging system15 as follows: 1) early-stage disease (Stages 0, 1A, and 1B), 2) intermediate-stage disease (Stages 2A and 2B), and 3) advanced or end-stage disease (Stages 3A, 3B, 4A, and 4B).

Brain MRI data acquisition and analysis

MRI was performed using either a 1.5- or 3-tesla system (Signa Excite II HDxt or Signa HDxt, GE Medical Systems), and all participants underwent at least one brain MRI examination. MRI data were reviewed by a trained neurologist (O.Y.-W.Y.) in accordance with the STRIVE-2 (Standards for Reporting Vascular Changes on Neuroimaging version 2) criteria.16 The severity of WMH in the periventricular region and deep white matter was rated on FLAIR images (slice thickness=1–5 mm, repetition time=6,000 ms, echo time=118 ms, and inversion time=1,870 ms) using the Fazekas scale.17 Periventricular Fazekas scores were defined as follows: 0=absent, 1=caps or pencil-thin lining, 2=smooth halo, and 3=irregular periventricular signal extending into the deep white matter. Deep-white-matter Fazekas scores were defined as follows: 0=absent, 1=punctate foci, 2=beginning confluence, and 3=large confluent areas. The temporopolar WMH severity was graded separately for each temporal pole using Scheltens’ scale3,18 as follows: 0=absent, 1=up to five lesions of size <3 mm, 2=six or more lesions of size <3 mm, 3=up to five lesions of size 4–10 mm, 4=six or more lesions of size 4–10 mm, 5=one or more lesions of size >10 mm, and 6=confluent hyperintensities. Moderate-to-severe temporopolar involvement was defined as a Scheltens’ scale of ≥3 in either temporal pole.3,5 Lacunes were identified and counted on FLAIR or T1-weighted images (section thickness=1–5 mm, repetition time=7.1 ms, echo time=2.7 ms, and inversion time=450 ms). The total number of CMB was assessed using SWI or T2*-GRE sequences, which were available in 276 CADASIL patients and 99 sporadic SVD patients.

The brain lesion burden was analyzed quantitatively using 3D Slicer software (version 5.2.2).19 The total intracranial cavity volume (ICV) was measured on T1-weighted images after using the software to remove the skull. The WMH burden was quantified as the total WMH volume expressed as a percentage of the ICV; that is, (WMH volume/ICV)×100%.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics software (version 25.0; IBM Corp.). A two-tailed p value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Data are presented as mean±standard-deviation or n (%) values. The chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was used to compare categorical variables between groups, while Student’s t-test was applied for continuous variables. We evaluated the diagnostic accuracy of temporopolar WMH by calculating the sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value for distinguishing HR-, MR-, and LR-EGFr CADASIL patients from sporadic SVD patients.

Univariate logistic regression analyses were used to identify variables that were significantly associated with the presence of temporopolar WMH, and their corresponding odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated. The variables of interest included cardiovascular risk factors (age, sex, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, diabetes mellitus, and smoking history), MRI field strength (1.5 tesla vs. 3.0 tesla), disease duration, and HR-EGFr variant status. Variables that were statistically significant in univariate regression analyses were included in the subsequent multivariate regression analysis, with independent predictors identified via stepwise forward selection. Given the ordinal nature of both the mRS and the NOTCH3-SVD staging system, ordinal logistic regression was employed to assess the association between temporopolar WMH involvement and disease severity.20 In these analyses, patients without temporopolar WMH served as the reference group, and the early stage of the NOTCH3-SVD staging system was set as the reference outcome.

To evaluate the relationship between disease duration and the presence of temporopolar WMH, trend analysis was conducted using logistic regression to determine the dose-dependent effect for every 5-year increment in disease duration. The participants were additionally stratified into three groups based on their temporopolar WMH severity (Scheltens’ scale=0, <3, and ≥3).3,5 The Jonckheere–Terpstra trend test was applied to assess whether increasing temporopolar WMH severity was associated with progressive functional decline or a greater SVD burden in neuroimaging.

We determined whether temporopolar WMH are an imaging hallmark of CADASIL beyond the cumulative SVD burden by repeating the analyses in a propensity-score-matched cohort comprising 152 CADASIL patients and 152 sporadic SVD patients matched for age, sex, and total WMH volume. We further performed subgroup analyses by comparing the prevalence of temporopolar WMH across sporadic SVD patients classified into the following four clinical phenotypes: predominantly stroke, predominantly cognitive impairment, both stroke and cognitive impairment, and isolated gait disturbance. Differences in the WMH burden were assessed using one-way analysis of variance with Games-Howell post-hoc tests, and categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test.

Ethical approval and patient consents

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Taipei Veterans General Hospital (2017-02-008A; 2023-09-009A; 2025-02-005C). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants or their legal representatives.

The CADASIL cohort comprised 38, 344, and 3 patients carrying HR-, MR-, and LR-EGFr variants, respectively. The prevalence of temporopolar WMH differed with the NOTCH3 risk category, being 94.7% in HR-EGFr, 54.4% in MR-EGFr, and 33.3% in LR-EGFr, compared with 31.9% in sporadic SVD without known pathogenic mutations (Table 2). In contrast, the prevalence of external-capsule WMH did not differ among the HR-EGFr (100%), MR-EGFr (95.6%), and sporadic SVD (89.5%) groups. For HR-EGFr variants, temporopolar WMH differentiated CADASIL from sporadic SVD with high sensitivity (95%) and moderate specificity (68%). The diagnostic accuracy was markedly lower for MR-EGFr variants (sensitivity=54%) and poor for LR-EGFr variants (sensitivity=33%). For the NOTCH3 p.R544C founder variant, the sensitivity and specificity for temporopolar involvement were also modest (53% and 68%, respectively). These findings indicate that in populations where NOTCH3 MR-EGFr variants predominate (e.g., Taiwan9 and Jeju Island in South Korea21), temporopolar WMH provide low diagnostic accuracy for distinguishing CADASIL from sporadic SVD and should not be used in isolation to guide genetic-testing decisions. In addition, external-capsule WMH, which is another well-recognized imaging hallmark of CADASIL, showed very poor specificity (10%) for differentiating CADASIL from sporadic SVD.

Further comparing the prevalence of temporopolar WMH in the propensity-score-matched cohort (Supplementary Table 3 in the online-only Data Supplement) revealed that temporopolar WMH remained more prevalent in CADASIL than in sporadic SVD (50.7% vs. 34.9%, p=5.4×10-3), suggesting that temporopolar involvement is not solely attributable to the cumulative SVD burden and could represent an imaging hallmark of CADASIL. In contrast, the prevalence of external-capsule WMH did not differ between CADASIL and sporadic SVD (94.7% vs. 91.4%, p=0.27).

Temporopolar WMH prevalence increases with the disease duration in CADASIL

In CADASIL, both the HR-EGFr variants and disease duration were independently associated with temporopolar WMH. The prevalence of temporopolar WMH increased with the disease duration (Fig. 1). Temporopolar WMH were present in 90.9%, 92.9%, and 100% of the HR-EGFr variant carriers at symptom onset, within 5 years after onset, and after a disease duration of >5 years, respectively; they were also present in 41.9%, 56.0%, 68.3%, and 78.9% of the MR-EGFr variant carriers at symptom onset, within 5 years after onset, within 6–10 years, and after a disease duration of >10 years, respectively. Longitudinal MRI in representative cases demonstrated progressive expansion of WMH both in the temporopolar region and deep white matter (Fig. 2), suggesting that temporopolar WMH are a radiological marker of disease progression in CADASIL.

Temporopolar WMH is associated with greater SVD burden and functional impairment in CADASIL

Patients with temporopolar WMH exhibited significantly greater disease severity and disability, as reflected by higher NOTCH3-SVD stage and mRS score (Table 1 and Fig. 3). After adjusting for the MRI field strength, disease duration, and HR-EGFr variant status, the presence of temporopolar WMH remained an independent predictor of mRS score >3, with an OR of 3.7 (95% CI=2.0–6.8). Furthermore, temporopolar WMH were associated with a 9.0-fold higher risk (95% CI=1.9–43.4) of being in the intermediate NOTCH3-SVD stage and a 13.4-fold higher risk (95% CI=2.6–70.1) of having advanced or end-stage disease.

Regarding the neuroimaging SVD burden, CADASIL patients with temporopolar WMH exhibited significantly higher Fazekas scores in deep white matter (2.8±0.5 vs. 2.4±0.8) and the periventricular region (2.8±0.5 vs. 2.3±0.9) relative to those without temporopolar WMH, even after adjusting for MRI field strength, disease duration, and HR-EGFr variant (Table 1). Volumetric analyses confirmed that the total WMH volume and WMH/ICV ratio were significantly larger in patients with temporopolar WMH. Additionally, there were significantly more lacunes in patients with than without temporopolar WMH (11.2±8.5 vs. 6.4±8.1), whereas the number of CMB did not differ significantly between these two groups.

We further evaluated whether there was a dose–response relationship between temporopolar WMH severity and disease burden by stratifying CADASIL patients based on their Scheltens’ scales for WMH in the temporal poles (0, <3, and ≥3). A clear correlation emerged between increasing temporopolar WMH severity and both higher NOTCH3-SVD stage and mRS score (Table 3). Similarly, Fazekas scores for WMH in deep white matter and the periventricular region, total WMH volume, WMH/ICV ratio, and the number of lacunes all increased in a dose-dependent manner with a greater temporopolar WMH severity. These findings suggest a consistent and cumulative relationship between temporopolar WMH severity and overall SVD burden, supporting its putative role as a surrogate marker of disease severity in CADASIL.

Temporopolar WMH is associated with disease severity in sporadic SVD

We further examined whether temporopolar WMH could also serve as an indicator of disease severity in sporadic SVD (Table 1). After adjusting for disease duration, sporadic SVD patients with temporopolar WMH had a higher probability of mRS score >3 (OR=5.1, 95% CI=2.4–11.1) relative to those without temporopolar WMH. MRI data analyses also revealed that the neuroimaging abnormalities were more severe in patients with temporopolar WMH, including increases in Fazekas scores for WMH in deep white matter and the periventricular region, WMH volume, WMH/ICV ratio, and the number of lacunes (all p<0.001). These findings were consistent with those in the CADASIL cohort, further supporting the utility of temporopolar WMH as a generalizable imaging marker of disease burden in both CADASIL and sporadic SVD.

The subgroup analyses performed with sporadic SVD patients classified into four clinical phenotypes (Supplementary Table 4 in the online-only Data Supplement) showed that temporopolar WMH were most common among patients presenting with both stroke and cognitive impairment (48.3%), followed by those predominantly having cognitive impairment (29.2%) and isolated gait disturbance (23.5%), and they were least common among those predominantly having stroke (21.2%). Nevertheless, temporopolar involvement remained more prevalent in CADASIL (58.2%) than in any of the sporadic SVD phenotype subgroups.

DISCUSSION

This study systematically investigated the clinical and radiological significance of temporopolar WMH in 385 CADASIL patients and an independent group of 191 patients with sporadic SVD. The findings allowed us to conclude that temporopolar WMH are common in CADASIL, less common in sporadic SVD, and associated with the disease severity and imaging burden across both hereditary and sporadic SVD. Three major observations support this conclusion. First, temporopolar WMH were observed in 94.7%, 54.4%, and 33.3% of CADASIL patients carrying the NOTCH3 HR-, MR-, and LR-EGFr variants, respectively, and in 31.9% of patients with sporadic SVD without known pathogenic mutations. These variations suggest that while temporopolar WMH are enriched in monogenic forms of SVD, they are not exclusive to CADASIL. This may reflect shared pathophysiological mechanisms across diverse SVD subtypes. Second, the diagnostic accuracy of temporopolar WMH for differentiating CADASIL from sporadic SVD varies across the NOTCH3-variant risk categories, showing good sensitivity in HR-EGFr variant carriers but lower discriminative power in MR- and LR-EGFr variant carriers. In both CADASIL and sporadic SVD patients, temporopolar WMH were consistently associated with a greater total SVD burden (i.e., larger WMH volume and more lacunes) and worse functional disability. Notably, the temporopolar WMH severity demonstrated a dose-dependent relationship with both the clinical disability and MRI lesion burden in CADASIL. Collectively, these findings support temporopolar WMH being a clinically meaningful imaging biomarker of disease severity and progression in both monogenic and sporadic SVD.

Temporopolar WMH have long been regarded as both a sensitive and specific imaging feature for CADASIL,5,22 but the expanding NOTCH3-variant spectrum23 and clear genotype–phenotype correlations challenge this view.6,24 In our cohort, the prevalence of temporopolar WMH showed a clear trend with the NOTCH3 variant—94.7% in HR-EGFr, 54.4% in MR-EGFr, and 33.3% in LR-EGFr—that mirrors the severity of vascular NOTCH3 aggregation in skin biopsies.6 This pattern may help to explain the lower frequencies of temporopolar WMH reported in East Asian cohorts versus Caucasian populations, particularly in regions where MR-EGFr founder variants are common.7,8,9 Sequencing data obtained in population-based genome studies indicate that the NOTCH3 p.R544C founder variant occurs in 0.9% of individuals in the Taiwanese general population25 and in 0.12% of individuals in the Korean Reference Genome Database.26

The present study has revealed that temporopolar WMH have a low diagnostic accuracy for distinguishing CADASIL from sporadic SVD in Taiwan and South Korea, where MR-EGFr founder variants are prevalent. In contrast, temporopolar WMH showed a high diagnostic accuracy among Taiwanese carriers of HR-EGFr variants, suggesting that differences in prevalence are more closely related to the variant location than to race itself. Moreover, the presence of temporopolar WMH is not pathognomonic for CADASIL, given their occurrence in nearly one-third of the sporadic SVD patients in this study. Accordingly, diagnostic applications of temporopolar WMH in CADASIL should be tailored according to NOTCH3-variant risk categories rather than applied uniformly across populations, particularly in regions where MR- or LR-EGFr variants are common. The prevalence of temporopolar WMH was slightly higher in our sporadic SVD cohort than in a previous study of sporadic leukoaraiosis3 (31.9% vs. 25%), which might have been due to differences in MRI field strengths (1.5/3.0 tesla vs. 1.0/1.5 tesla) and in the operational definitions of temporopolar involvement (presence/absence vs. moderate-to-severe WMH).

This study is the first to examine the association between temporopolar WMH and disease severity in CADASIL. Two factors may explain why this relationship has been overlooked: 1) previous studies have typically assessed temporopolar involvement as a binary feature (present or absent), thereby obscuring variations in the lesion burden; and 2) in predominantly Caucasian cohorts in which HR-EGFr variants are common, temporopolar WMH are often present at symptom onset, which reduces the opportunities to detect their progression. Our inclusion of adequate numbers of both MR- and HR-EGFr variant carriers as well as our use of Scheltens’ scale to grade temporopolar WMH have enabled us to identify a clear dose-dependent relationship between temporopolar WMH severity and both imaging markers (WMH volume and number of lacunes) and clinical disability. These findings support the view that temporopolar involvement is not disease-specific or anatomically predetermined, but reflects a more-advanced stage of SVD. Supporting this interpretation, a recent study found that increased diffusivity in the left superior longitudinal fasciculus in the temporal region was correlated with a greater SVD lesion burden and worse executive function in both CADASIL patients and asymptomatic NOTCH3-variant carriers,27 suggesting that microstructural changes in the temporal white-matter tract precede visible WMH lesions.

The biological mechanisms underlying temporopolar WMH still remain unclear. Postmortem histopathological analyses have revealed severe myelin loss, dilated perivascular spaces, astrocytic gliosis, and impaired interstitial fluid drainage in the anterior temporal white matter of CADASIL patients.28 Compared with controls, astrocytes in CADASIL patients exhibited autophagy-like cell death secondary to ischemia and gliovascular unit dysfunction, with the temporopolar region being particularly vulnerable.29 The present study has extended the significance of temporopolar involvement beyond the monogenic form of SVD, supporting temporopolar WMH as a convergent imaging biomarker of cumulative small-vessel injury across SVD subtypes.

This study had several limitations. First, volumetric analyses of WMH were not performed across different brain regions, and so it remains unclear whether temporopolar WMH represent a region-specific imaging biomarker, or merely reflect the overall WMH burden as part of global disease progression. Second, all participants in this study were Han Chinese, and more than 80% of them carried the same NOTCH3 p.R544C variant. It is therefore uncertain whether our findings can be generalized to other racial groups, particularly Western populations with different genetic backgrounds and mutation spectra. Moreover, the estimates for LR-EGFr variants should be interpreted with caution given the smallness of the LR-EGFr group in our cohort. Third, differences in magnet strength (1.5 tesla vs. 3.0 tesla) could have introduced bias into the assessments of temporopolar WMH, number of lacunes, and number of CMB. To minimize such potential bias, the MRI field strength was included as a covariate in the multivariate regression analysis. Fourth, only symptomatic CADASIL patients were included in this study, which restricts the generalizability of our findings to a broader spectrum of the NOTCH3-SVD staging system, particularly to asymptomatic or presymptomatic NOTCH3-variant carriers. Finally, the analyses were primarily applied to cross-sectional data, with longitudinal MRI follow-ups performed in only a small subset of participants. This reduced the ability to establish causal inferences between temporal changes in temporopolar WMH and clinical outcomes.

In conclusion, temporopolar WMH are common in CADASIL, but their discriminative power versus sporadic SVD varies with the positions of the NOTCH3 variants. In both CADASIL and sporadic SVD patients, the temporopolar WMH load showed dose-dependent associations with the clinical and neuroradiological severities, implying that temporopolar involvement reflects a more-advanced disease stage. Accordingly, temporopolar WMH should be interpreted within NOTCH3 risk categories and considered a convergent imaging marker of cumulative small-vessel injury across SVD subtypes.

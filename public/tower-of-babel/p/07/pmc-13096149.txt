CD47 as a prognostic biomarker and potential immunotherapy target in penile squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Junying Zhang, Xugang Hu, Jing Xu, Can Ai, Haitao Wang, Xiaodu Xie, Yangkun Ao, Jiao Tan, Junhao Jin, Gaolei Liu, Yiqiang Huang, Weihua Lan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPenile squamous cell carcinoma (PSCC) remains a global healthcare concern, characterized by poor outcomes and limited therapeutic options. CD47, a “don’t-eat-me” signal that enables tumor immune evasion, has not been extensively evaluated in PSCC. This study aims to explore the expression and significance of CD47 in PSCC. RNA-sequencing was performed on eight paired PSCC and adjacent normal tissues. Protein expression was assessed by immunohistochemistry (IHC) in an independent of 131 PSCC cases, and associations with clinicopathological factors and prognosis was evaluated. In addition, the interrogation of CD47-interacting proteins, immune cell infiltration, and signaling pathways related to CD47 subgroups were analyzed from the public database. CD47 mRNA levels were higher in PSCC tumors compared to normal tissues. High CD47 protein expression (41.98%, 55/131) significantly correlated with advanced tumor stage (P = 0.002) and larger tumor size (P = 0.002). With a median follow-up of 38 months, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated that patients with high CD47 expression had significantly poorer overall survival (OS) (P = 0.006). Multivariate Cox regression analysis confirmed that high CD47 expression remained an independent predictor of poor OS (HR = 9.956, 95% CI 2.410–41.141, P = 0.001). Functional enrichment analyses of CD47-interacting proteins indicated involvement in immune-related and adhesion-associated pathways. Furthermore, immune infiltration analyses using CIBERSORT and IHC revealed that high CD47 expression was associated with reduced CD8 + T-cell infiltration. Gene set variation and enrichment analyses suggested activation of MYC targets and mTORC1 signaling in the high-CD47 group, which was further validated by increased phosphorylated S6K1 expression observed through IHC. Introduction

Penile squamous cell carcinoma (PSCC) is a rare malignancy, with an incidence of 0.1–1 per 100,000 men in developed countries. However, it accounts for up to 10% of male malignancies in some developing countries1,2. While localized PSCC can often be effectively managed through topical therapies, surgery or radiotherapy, patients with locally advanced or metastatic disease face significant clinical challenge3. This underscores the urgent need for novel therapeutic strategies to address advanced PSCC.

Recent advancements in whole-exome and transcriptome analyses have revealed key genomic features of PSCC, including recurrent human papillomavirus (HPV)-driven alterations, high tumor mutational burdens, and frequent PD-L1 positivity. These findings provide a strong biological rationale for immune-checkpoint blockade therapies4,5. Despite this, the objective response rates to PD-1/PD-L1 blockades as monotherapy have been disappointingly low, ranging from 0% to 16.7%6,7. This limited efficacy highlights the critical need to explore additional immune-evasion mechanisms. Identifying new targets that can enhance the effectiveness of immunotherapy is essential to improve outcomes for a broader patient population with PSCC.

CD47, a member of the immunoglobulin superfamily, is a cell surface protein that interacts with signal-regulatory protein α (SIRPα) on macrophages and dendritic cells to transmit a “don’t-eat-me” signal, therefore inhibiting phagocytosis8. Overexpression of CD47 has been documented in various solid tumors and hematologic malignancies, where it is associated with advanced disease stage, metastasis and poor patient survival9–13. Targeting the CD47-SIRPα axis has shown promise in reshaping the tumor immune microenvironment and enhancing the efficacy of T-cell checkpoint inhibitors14–16. Several CD47-targeted therapies including monoclonal antibodies and fusion proteins, have been explored in preclinical models and clinical trials17. However, the expression profile, prognostic significance, and immunobiology of CD47 in PSCC remain unexplored.

In this study, we comprehensively investigated the expression, clinical significance, and potential biological implications of CD47 in PSCC. Our aim is to determine whether CD47 can serve as a valuable prognostic biomarker and a potential target for immunotherapy in PSCC.

Materials and methods

Data source

The cohort consisted of 131 formalin-fixed paraffin embedded (FFPE) tissue samples collected from Daping Hospital of Army Medical University (Chongqing, China) between 2014 and 2024. Corresponding clinicopathologic data were extracted from medical records and pathology reports. The clinical characteristics are summarized in Table S1. Besides, eight fresh paired PSCC samples (including PSCC and adjacent normal tissues) were used to conduct RNA sequencing analysis. Following Trimmed Mean of M-values (TMM) normalization, expression data were adjusted for batch effects with ComBat using biological group as a covariate. Genes with a |log₂ fold change| ≥ 0.9 and a P-value < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. The datasets GSE196978 and GSE57955 from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database were included in our study for further analysis.

HPV genotyping

HPV status was determined using the Tellgenplex™ HPV DNA Test (Tellgen Life Science Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China), which detects 14 HPV genotypes (including HPV 16, 18, 31, 33, 35, 39, 45, 51, 52, 56, 58, 59, 66, 68). Genomic DNA was extracted from formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor tissues using a commercial kit (DP340, TIANGEN, China).

Immunohistochemistry

For immunohistochemistry (IHC), 4-µm sections from FFPE tissue blocks were deparaffinized, rehydrated, and subjected to antigen retrieval in EDTA buffer under high-pressure boiling for 3 min. Endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched with 3% hydrogen peroxide for 15 min, followed by blocking with 3% BSA for 1 h at 37 °C. Sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with the following primary antibodies: anti-CD47 (Abcam, ab218810, 1:2,000), anti-CD8 (Abcam, ab251596, 1:2,000), and anti -S6K1 (phospho T389 + T412) (Abcam, ab60948, 1:100). The slides were incubated with secondary antibodies using the MaxVision-HRP Mouse/Rabbit kit (MXB, KIT5030), followed by DAB as chromogen and hematoxylin counterstaining. Slides were digitized with a KFBIO slide scanner (KFBIO, KF-FL-400).

IHC staining results were evaluated by two experienced independent pathologists. As previously described18–20, immunohistochemical staining was assessed by applying a semi-quantitative immunoreactivity scoring (IRS) system according to the percentage of positive cells and the staining intensity. Briefly, the percentage of positive stained cells was scored as follows: 0 (none), 1 (1–10%), 2 (11–50%), 3 (51–80%) and 4 (> 80%). The staining intensity was scored as follows: 0 (negative) ,1 (weak), 2 (moderate) and 3 (strong). Multiplication of the staining intensity and the percentage of positive cells resulted in an IRS ranging from 0 to 12 for each individual case. CD47 expression was classified as low (IRS 0–6) or high (IRS 7–12)18. phosphorylated S6K1 (p-S6K1) expression was categorized as negative (IRS 0–3) or positive (IRS > 3)20. The number of CD8 + tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) was counted in five high-power fields and calculated the average for each case. With the median count as the cut-off value, we divided the density of CD8 + TILs into high and low groups.

Protein-protein interaction analysis

The online STRING database (https://string-db.org/) is used to analyze all publicly available information sources and to predict protein-protein interactions in the organism21. In this study, an interaction module consisting of CD47 and its 50 network interacting genes is constructed using protein-protein interactions. Subsequently, enrichment analysis was conducted using the Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) functions of the R software package.

Immune infiltration analysis and tumor purity calculation

To investigate the immune environment of PSCC, CIBERSORT was carried out using R software. CIBERSORT is a deconvolution algorithm to infer cell-type proportions based on data from bulk tumor samples of mixed cell types, which include the proportions of 22 immune cell types22. Tumor purity was inferred using the ESTIMATE algorithm, which calculates scores based on signature genes for stromal and immune cells23. All analyses were performed with the IOBR (Immuno-Oncology Biological Research) R package (https://github.com/IOBR/IOBR), which integrated multiple published analysis algorithms for comprehensive tumor microenvironment analysis24.

Functional annotation analysis

To investigate the potential functional annotation of target genes, GO analysis, encompassing biological pathways (BP), cellular component (CC), molecular function (MF), and KEGG pathway analyses were performed using the R package “clusterProfiler”. KEGG pathway analysis was performed using the KEGG database (www.kegg.jp/kegg/kegg1.html)25. Gene set variation analysis (GSVA) and gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) were conducted to explore disparities in biological processes across CD47-defined patterns, utilizing gene sets obtained from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB). Pathways with a P-value < 0.05 were regarded as significantly enriched.

Statistical analysis

All data analyses were performed using the R software version 4.2.2, SPSS Version 22.0 (SPSS, Chicago, IL, USA), and Graphpad Prism 9.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA). The paired Student t-test and unpaired Mann-Whitney U test were used for analysis. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The chi-square test was performed for variables. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from the date of the first diagnosis to death or the last follow-up. All variables with a P-value < 0.05 in the univariate analysis were included in the multivariate Cox model. To evaluate model stability, we conducted bootstrap resampling with 2,000 iterations and reported the bootstrap-corrected concordance index (C-index). Sensitivity analyses were performed to assess robustness by sequentially excluding T stage and age.

Results

Analysis of differentially expressed genes in PSCC

Transcriptome profiling was conducted using sequencing data from 8 paired tumor and adjacent normal tissues obtained from patients with penile squamous cell carcinoma (PSCC) (Fig. 1a). We identified a total of 7,456 differentially expressed genes (DEGs), comprising 3,440 upregulated and 4,016 downregulated genes (Fig. 1b). Further analysis of these upregulated DEGs was performed using GO and KEGG enrichment analyses. GO enrichment analysis revealed significant involvement in leukocyte-mediated immunity, chromosomal regions, and antigen binding (Fig. 1c). KEGG pathway analysis highlighted key enrichment in DNA replication, primary immunodeficiency, and cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction (Fig. 1d). These findings suggest a substantial difference in the immune microenvironment between PSCC tissues and adjacent normal tissues. Given the central role of immune checkpoints in current immunotherapy research, we subsequently intersected the up-regulated DEGs with a referenced set of 79 immune checkpoint genes (ICGs)26 (see Table S2). This analysis identified 29 significantly overexpressed ICGs, including prototypical inhibitors (e.g., PDCD1, CD274), co-stimulatory molecules (e.g., CD27, ICOS), the macrophage checkpoint CD47, and other immunomodulatory molecules (Fig. 1e).

CD47 expression in PSCC tissues

As previously noted, CD47 is a critical targetable immune checkpoint that has not been extensively explored in PSCC. Among the identified DEGs, CD47, a key “don’t-eat-me” signal, was selected for detailed validation. RNA-sequencing data showed significantly higher CD47 mRNA levels in tumor tissue compared to paired normal tissues (Fig. 2a). Analysis of external datasets (GSE196978 and GSE57955) also verified the overexpression of CD47 in tumor samples (Fig. 2b, c). Next, we assessed CD47 protein expression by IHC in an independent cohort of 131 PSCC cases. Detailed patient characteristics were provided in Table S1. As shown in Fig. 2d and e, CD47 was predominantly localized to the cytoplasmic membrane of tumor cells, with some diffuse expression in the cytoplasm. High CD47 protein expression was observed in 55 cases (41.98%), while 76 cases (58.02%) exhibited low expression (Fig. 2f).

Correlation between CD47 expression and clinicopathological characteristics

We further analyzed the associations between CD47 expression and various clinicopathological variables (Fig. 3). Chi-square analysis revealed that high CD47 expression was significantly associated with advanced tumor stage (P = 0.002) and larger tumor size (P = 0.002). However, no significant correlations were observed with patient age (P = 0.689), histological subtype (P = 0.238), HPV status (P = 0.295), or differentiation grade (P = 0.817).

Prognostic significance of CD47 expression in PSCC

To evaluate the prognostic significance of CD47 expression in PSCC, we analyzed Kaplan-Meier curves for 100 patients with available follow-up data (median follow-up 38 months; 24 deaths). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis revealed that patients with high CD47 expression had significantly poorer overall survival (OS) compared with those with low CD47 expression (P = 0.006, Fig. 4a). To further validate this finding, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed. In the univariate analysis (Table S3), older age (≥ 62 years), advanced T stage (T ≥ 2), advanced clinical N stage (N ≥ 1), presence of distant metastasis (M1), and high CD47 expression were significantly associated with poorer OS (all P < 0.05, Fig. 4b). Multivariate analysis further demonstrated that older age (HR = 2.955, 95% CI 1.022–8.548, P = 0.046), advanced clinical N stage (HR = 10.883, 95% CI 2.342–50.579, P = 0.002) and high CD47 expression (HR = 9.956, 95% CI 2.410–41.141, P = 0.001, Fig. 4b) remained independent prognostic factors for OS. To mitigate potential overfitting, internal validation was performed using 2,000 bootstrap resamples, yielding a bootstrap-corrected C-index of 0.873 ± 0.041, indicating robust discriminative ability and negligible overfitting (Table S4). Sensitivity analyses were conducted to further assess the robustness of the findings. Exclusion of T stage in the five-variable model did not significantly alter the prognostic significance of CD47 (Table S5). Moreover, a parsimonious model adjusting for the most clinically relevant factors (N and M stage) was presented in Supplementary Table S6, confirming that the independent prognostic effect of CD47 remained significant. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that high CD47 expression was an independent predictor of poor OS in patients with PSCC.

Functional insights from CD47 and its interacting proteins

To further explore the biological role of CD47 in PSCC, we constructed a protein-protein interaction network of CD47 co-expressed genes using the STRING database. The top 50 interacting proteins with CD47 were selected based on confidence scores provided by the STRING database (Fig. 5a). Then, we conducted enrichment analysis using a set of 51 genes, including CD47, to elucidate the biological processes and pathways associated with these genes.

GO analysist revealed significant biological processes, including leukocyte cell-cell adhesion, tumor necrosis factor superfamily cytokine production and phagocytosis (Fig. 5b). Cellular components were enriched in the plasma membrane, cell-substrate junction, and integrin complex (Fig. 5c). Molecular functions were dominated by integrin binding, cytokine binding, and pattern recognition receptor activity (Fig. 5d). KEGG pathway enrichment analysis further demonstrated significant associations with ECM-receptor interaction, focal adhesion, PI3K-Akt signaling, PD-L1/PD-1 checkpoint pathway in cancer, and cell adhesion molecules (Fig. 5e). Collectively, these findings indicate that CD47 functions within a central network governed by both immunosuppressive factors and integrins. This provides a functional basis for its role in PSCC progression and immune evasion.

Underlying extrinsic immune landscapes of the high and low‑CD47 expression groups

Immune cells play a crucial role in modulating tumor invasion and aggression in PSCC. To explore the relationship between CD47 expression and immune cell infiltration, we conducted CIBERSORT analysis using data from GSE57955 cohort. Patients were divided into high-CD47 and low-CD47 groups based on the median CD47 value. The analysis revealed that the high-CD47 group had a higher proportion of activated mast cells, activated dendritic cells, and M0 macrophages, but a lower proportion of CD8 + T cells compared to the low-CD47 group (Fig. 6a). To account for potential purity bias, we applied the ESTIMATE algorithm. Tumor purity scores showed no significant difference between the high-CD47 and low-CD47 groups (Fig. 6b). Additionally, we performed IHC for CD8 on the cohort of 131 PSCC cases. Tumors were stratified into high and low groups based on the median density of CD8 + TILs. Representative IHC images for CD8 are shown in Fig. 6c. The results showed a negative correlation between CD47 expression and CD8 + TIL density (Fig. 6d), suggesting that CD47 expression might inhibit the antitumor immune response in PSCC patients.

Differences between high‑ and low‑CD47 group‑regulated pathways

The GSVA analysis revealed that several hallmark pathways, including the MYC targets V1 and mTORC1 signaling pathways, exhibited significantly differential activity scores between the high- and low-CD47 groups. These results suggest a positive association between CD47 expression levels and the activation of these pathways (Fig. 7a). Detailed results of the GSVA analysis are showed in Fig. 7b. In addition, phosphorylated S6K1 (p-S6K1) protein expression was examined in PSCC tissues using IHC (Fig. 7c). p-S6K1 expression was significantly higher in the high-CD47 group than in the low-CD47 group (P = 0.032) (Fig. 7d). Furthermore, GSEA results demonstrated that the high-CD47 group was significantly enriched for immune-regulatory signatures, including the cytosolic DNA-sensing and RIG-I-like receptor signaling cascades (Fig. 7e).

Discussion

Penile squamous cell carcinoma is a rare genitourinary malignancy associated with substantial morbidity3. Therefore, identifying novel diagnostic and therapeutic targets is of paramount importance. In this study, we comprehensively investigated the expression, clinical significance, and potential biological implications of CD47 in patients with PSCC. Our results demonstrated that CD47 was significantly overexpressed in PSCC tissues. High CD47 expression was strongly associated with aggressive tumor progression and poor patient survival. Multivariate analysis further established CD47 as an independent prognostic biomarker. Moreover, high CD47 expression correlated with decreased intratumoral CD8 + T-cell infiltration. Additionally, CD47 overexpression was associated with the activation of oncogenic pathways, particularly MYC targets and mTORC1 signaling.

CD47 plays a vital role in cancer cell proliferation, invasion, and metastasis. Although CD47 overexpression has been documented in multiple cancer types9–13, our study is the first to quantify its abundance and prognostic impact in PSCC. The observed frequency of high CD47 expression (41.98%, 55/131) was comparable to that reported in breast cancer (33% − 44.5%)27,28 and gastric cancer (45.5%)29, but lower than in non-small cell lung cancer (68.8%)30 and ovarian cancer (approximately 80%)31. Notably, high CD47 expression was identified as an independent prognostic biomarker, significantly associated with poor prognosis of OS in patients with PSCC. This finding aligns with observations in other squamous cell carcinomas (SCCs)32–34. Specifically, elevated CD47 expression in esophageal SCC was linked to reduced OS and progression-free survival32. Similarly, in head and neck SCC, CD47 overexpression correlates with positive lymph nodes and poorer OS 33. A study in oral SCC further demonstrated that high CD47 expression confers a significant survival disadvantage34. Although the association did not reach statistical significance in cervical SCC, CD47 overexpression exhibited a trend toward worse OS in cervical SCC35. Additionally, consistent with findings in oral SCC34, our study demonstrated a positive correlation between CD47 expression and advanced tumor stage or larger tumor size, suggesting that CD47 is a potential biomarker for predicting PSCC progression.

Previous studies have demonstrated that MYC directly binds the CD47 promoter to drive its expression36–38, and that enhanced glutamine/leucine uptake stimulates the activation of mTORC1/c-Myc axis, thereby promoting CD47 expression in osteosarcoma39. Intriguingly, CD47 has also been reported to activate the upstream PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway, suggesting potential bidirectional crosstalk40. In our study, GSVA analysis revealed significant enrichment of MYC target genes and mTORC1 signaling in high CD47-expressing tumors, and IHC confirmed a positive correlation between CD47 and the mTORC1 downstream effector p-S6K1. Collectively, these findings indicate a strong association between elevated CD47 expression and activation of the mTORC1/MYC pathway in PSCC. However, the precise mechanistic relationship warrants further investigation.

CD47 has emerged as a crucial checkpoint for cancer immunotherapy through direct or indirect interactions with different types of immune cells41. By binding to the SIRPα receptor on macrophages, CD47 initiates a “don’t eat me” signal, thereby inhibiting macrophage-mediated phagocytosis and facilitating cancer immune escape8. Consequently, therapeutic strategies targeting the CD47/SIRPα axis by counteracting this checkpoint are under investigation in clinical trial42,43. Interestingly, our CIBERSORT and IHC-based analyses showed that elevated CD47 expression in tumor tissues correlated with decreased CD8 + T-cell infiltration. This observation was consistent with previous studies reporting an inverse correlation between CD8 + T-cell infiltration and CD47 expression in human melanoma44 and esophageal SCC45. Moreover, accumulating evidence suggests that CD47 may influence intratumoral CD8⁺ T-cell function16,33,45. For example, in a head and neck SCC mouse model, Wu et al. demonstrated that anti-CD47 treatment reduced PD-1 expression and increased IFN-γ secretion in CD8 + TILs33. Tao et al. reported that anti-CD47 treatment effectively promotes CD8 + T-cell recruitment and activation within tumors in an esophageal SCC preclinical model45. Using single-cell RNA sequencing, Pan et al. showed that CD47 blockade not only increased CD8 + TIL number but also remodeled their cluster toward a more activated phenotype in pancreatic cancer16. Collectively, these findings support the hypothesis that CD47 may contribute to shaping the immune microenvironment and highlight its potential as an immunotherapeutic target in PSCC. Nevertheless, further studies are required to clarify the mechanisms underlying CD47-associated immune modulation, particularly with respect to CD8 + T-cell-mediated immunity.

Currently, immune checkpoint blockade is a promising strategy for tumor-targeted therapy. However, the PD-1/PD-L1 blockades as monotherapy achieve objective response rates of only 0–16.7% in advanced penile cancer6,7. After the discovery of its role in tumor evasion of immune surveillance, several CD47-targeted agents, including Hu5F9-G4, AK117, IMM-01, and TTI621, were undergoing preclinical models and clinical trials to demonstrate their safety and efficacy in various cancer17. In addition, the bispecific antibody IMM2520, which targets both CD47 and PD-L1, has shown success in preclinical studies and is currently in clinical trials for advanced solid tumors17. Therefore, CD47 represents a promising therapeutic target for PSCC.

Several limitations of our study should be acknowledged. First, the sample size, particularly in the survival analysis, was relatively small, with only 100 patients and 24 events. The primary multivariate Cox model included five variables, resulting in an events-per-variable (EPV) ratio of 4.8, which is below the recommended threshold. A low EPV ratio increases the risk of overfitting and reduces model stability. Although internal validation using bootstrap resampling yielded a robust C-index of 0.873 ± 0.041, sensitivity analyses further confirmed the prognostic significance of CD47, validation in a larger, multi-center cohort would strengthen the reliability and generalizability of our findings. In addition, approximately 20% of the cases had missing data for clinical N stage and M stage, which may have introduced potential bias and limited the precision of the multivariate survival analyses. Second, immune infiltration was evaluated using bulk RNA-sequencing and IHC, which are inadequate to determine immune cell subtypes and their functional states. Future studies employing single-cell RNA sequencing or spatial transcriptomics are essential to more precisely characterize the immune landscape and functional states modulated by CD47 in PSCC. Finally, the molecular mechanisms by which CD47 contributes to PSCC progression remain incompletely understood and should be further investigated through in vitro and in vivo functional studies.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study investigated the expression pattern, clinical significance, and potential biological implications of CD47 in PSCC. We demonstrate that CD47 is overexpressed in PSCC and is associated with aggressive tumor progression. Importantly, high CD47 expression independently predicts poor OS. Moreover, high CD47 expression is associated with reduced intratumoral CD8 + T-cell infiltration and enrichment of oncogenic signaling pathways, particularly MYC targets and mTORC1 signaling. Collectively, these findings support CD47 as a promising prognostic biomarker and a potential immunotherapeutic target in PSCC. Further validation in larger cohorts and mechanistic studies are required to clarify its functional role and therapeutic relevance.

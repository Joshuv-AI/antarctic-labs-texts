Survival benefit of metastasectomy in colorectal cancer with pulmonary metastasis: a population-based, propensity score matched study
Authors: Jiayan Wu, Haosheng Zheng, Fei Qin, Gengfeng Wang, Yuzhen Zheng, Junguo Chen, Zui Liu, Jian Tan, Weijie Cai, Shiyun He, Bozhu Jian, Xianyu Qin, Hongying Liao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe survival benefits of pulmonary metastasectomy (PM) in colorectal cancer (CRC) patients with pulmonary metastasis remain controversial. This study aimed to assess the survival effect of PM on CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis.MethodsData from CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis were collected from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results database between 2010 and 2020. A 1:1 propensity score matching (PSM) analysis was employed to minimize heterogeneity between the groups. Kaplan–Meier analysis was performed to evaluate the overall survival (OS) of CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis who underwent PM.ResultsA total of 1,399 CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis were included; 140 patients and 1,259 patients underwent PM and did not, respectively. After PSM, there were 140 patients in each group. Patients who underwent PM demonstrated a longer median OS than those who did not, in both the overall cohort and the PSM cohort. In the PSM cohort, the median OS was 51 months (95% confidence interval [CI], 45–64 months) for CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis who underwent PM and 36 months (95% CI, 31–42 months) for those who did not undergo PM. Additionally, Cox proportional hazard models indicated that PM was a significant protective factor for OS in CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis (hazard ratio: 0.57; 95% CI, 0.41–0.80, P < 0.01).ConclusionPM prolongs the survival of CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis.Keywords: colorectal cancer, pulmonary metastasis, metastasectomy, prognosis, SEER, propensity score matching

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is the third-most common malignancy and the second leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with ∼1,926,118 new cases and an estimated 903,859 deaths in 2022 [1]. Owing to economic development as well as the significant changes in diet and lifestyle, the incidence of CRC is rising rapidly [2]. According to recent studies, 20% of newly diagnosed CRC patients present with metastatic disease and 25% of localized CRC patients will eventually develop metastasis. The 5-year survival rate for patients with metastatic CRC is <20% [3, 4]. The lung is the second-most common site of CRC metastasis, following the liver [5]. Pulmonary metastases occur in 10%–15% of CRC patients [6]. However, CRC patients with pulmonary metastases demonstrate slower tumor growth and longer survival than those with metastases in other sites. As a result, the treatment strategy for isolated pulmonary metastases in CRC remains a subject of controversy [5].

Pulmonary metastasectomy (PM) is a viable treatment option for CRC patients with resectable pulmonary metastases [7]. Recently, the utilization of PM in CRC has increased rapidly. However, the indications and prognostic factors have been reported inconsistently [8]. Therefore, the actual survival benefits of PM remain controversial [9].

The aim of this study was to assess the impact of PM on survival in CRC patients with pulmonary metastases. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist.

Methods

Data source

The Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) software (version 8.4.3) was used to download data from the SEER database (Incidence—SEER Research Data, 17 Registries, November 2022 Sub [2000–2020]). All patients diagnosed with CRC with pulmonary metastasis between 2010 and 2020 were included (Figure 1). CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis were identified based on the site and morphology, and then were selected. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (i) pathologically diagnosed with CRC only; (ii) confirmed pulmonary metastases only; (iii) patients underwent primary tumor resection; and (iv) whether PM was performed has been documented. Data on patients’ demographics and survival included (i) year of diagnosis; (ii) age (≥60 years or <60 years); (iii) sex (male or female); (iv) race (White, Black, or Other); (v) household income (≥60,000 USD or <60,000 USD); (vi) CRC location (the colon or the rectum); (vii) T stage of CRC tumor (T1, T2, T3, T4, or Tx); (viii) N stage of CRC tumor (N0, N1, N2, N3, or Nx); (ix) histological type of CRC tumor (adenocarcinoma or no adenocarcinoma); (x) differentiation of CRC tumor (Grade I, Grade II, Grade III, Grade IV, or unknown); (xi) CRC tumor size (≥7 cm, <7 cm, or unknown); (xii) regional lymph nodes examined of CRC (≥20, <20 or unknown); (xiii) perineural invasion of CRC tumor (yes, no, or unknown); (xiv) carcinoma embryonic antigen (CEA; negative, positive, or unknown); (xv) chemotherapy (yes or no); and (xvi) radiotherapy (yes or no).

Propensity score matching

This retrospective study aimed to evaluate the survival effect of PM on CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis. Notably, some covariates between the PM group and the non-PM group were imbalanced, potentially distorting the real relationship between PM and overall survival (OS) in CRC patients. Consequently, propensity score matching (PSM) was performed to reduce the possible influence. With the caliper value set at 0.1 by using the nearest-neighbor matching method, all included patients were 1:1 randomly matched into PM and non-PM groups. In our study, all covariates used for matching were as follows: year of diagnosis, age, sex, race, median household income, primary tumor location, differentiation of primary tumor, T stage of primary tumor, N stage of primary tumor, histological type, size of primary tumor, CEA level, perineural invasion of primary tumor, number of examined lymph nodes of primary tumor, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy.

Statistical analysis

The chi-square test was used to compare categorical variables. Kaplan–Meier analysis was used to assess the OS of CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis and the log-rank test was used to compare survival differences between the PM and non-PM groups. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression models were employed to identify the independent prognostic factors for OS. Variables with a P-value of <0.05 in the univariate analysis were included in the next multivariate analysis. All statistical analyses were performed by using R software (version: 4.3.2) and a two-sided P-value of <0.05 was deemed statistically significant.

Ethics statement

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Sixth Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University, Guangzhou, China (grant number: 2023ZSLYEC-342) in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki. All data were obtained from the SEER database; consequently, there was no need for informed patient consent.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 16,630 CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis were identified from the SEER database between 2010 and 2020, and 1,399 patients were included in this study (Figure 1). Of those patients, 140 patients underwent PM, whereas 1,259 did not. The baseline clinical characteristics and treatment of the included patients are presented in Table 1. To balance the potential covariates, such as diagnosis year, age, sex, race, median household income, primary tumor location, differentiation of primary tumor, T stage of primary tumor, N stage of primary tumor, histological type, size of primary tumor, CEA level, perineural invasion of primary tumor, number of examined lymph nodes of primary tumor, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy between the PM group and the non-PM group, a 1:1 PSM analysis was further performed. As a result, the clinical characteristics and treatment were well balanced in the PSM cohort (Table 1).

Survival outcomes of PM after PSM

Kaplan–Meier methods were utilized to calculate the OS of CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis, with multivariate Cox regression analysis identifying factors associated with OS before and after PSM. In the overall cohort, CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis who underwent PM demonstrated a longer median OS of 51 months (95% confidence interval [CI], 45–64 months) than those who did not undergo PM (51 months [95% CI, 45–64 months] vs 31 months [95% CI, 28–33 months], P < 0.001; Figure 2A). In the matched cohort, CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis who underwent PM also demonstrated a significantly longer median OS than those who did not (51 months [95% CI, 45–64 months] vs 36 months [95% CI, 31–42 months], P < 0.001; Figure 2B). In addition, multivariate Cox analysis of the matched cohort indicated that PM was an independent protective factor for OS in CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis (Figure 3; hazard ratio, 0.57; 95% CI, 0.41–0.80, P = 0.001).

Subgroup analysis of survival outcomes of PM after PSM

In the PSM cohort, a subgroup analysis was then conducted to investigate the impact of PM on the OS in CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis. In the age subgroups, the median OS for CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis who underwent PM was significantly longer than that of those who did not undergo PM in both the younger and older cohorts (Figure 4A). When stratifying by CEA levels, CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis who underwent PM exhibited longer median OS than those who did not undergo PM in the CEA-positive group; however, this advantage was not present in the CEA-negative group (Figure 4B). In tumor differentiation subgroups, patients who underwent PM had a longer median OS than those who did not undergo PM in the Grade I–II subgroup, but this was not the case in the Grade III–IV subgroup (Figure 4C). In the CRC tumor size subgroups, the median OS of CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis who underwent PM was longer than that of those who did not undergo PM in the CRC tumor size of <7 cm subgroup, but not in the >7 cm subgroup (Figure 4D). In the T-stage subgroups, CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis who underwent PM had longer median OS than those who did not undergo PM in the T1–T3 subgroup, yet no difference was noted within the T4 subgroup (Figure 4E). Lastly, among histological types, the median OS of CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis who underwent PM was longer than that of those who did not undergo PM only in the adenocarcinoma subgroup, while no significant difference was found in the non-adenocarcinoma subgroup (Figure 4F).

Kaplan–Meier curves for OS of CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis who underwent PM vs those who did not undergo PM stratified by clinicopathologic characteristics in the PSM cohort. Subgroup analysis of OS in CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis who underwent PM vs those who did not undergo PM stratified by (A) age, (B) CEA level, (C) tumor differentiation, (D) tumor size, (E) tumor T stage, and (F) tumor histological types.

Subsequently, an additional subgroup analysis was performed to further investigate the impact of other therapy on the OS in CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis who underwent PM. The median OS of CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis who underwent PM was longer than that of those who did not undergo PM in the chemotherapy subgroup, but this was not observed in the non-chemotherapy subgroup (Figure 5A and B). Furthermore, in the radiotherapy subgroups, the median OS of CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis who underwent PM was longer than that of those who did not undergo PM in both the radiotherapy and non-radiotherapy subgroups (Figure 5C and D). Additionally, analysis of combined treatment approaches revealed that metastasectomy-based trimodality therapy was associated with the best OS, followed by metastasectomy combined with chemotherapy (log-rank P < 0.001, Figure 5E).

Discussion

This study utilized the SEER database to assess the effect of PM on survival in CRC patients with pulmonary metastases and resected primary colorectal tumors. The analysis confirmed that PM enhances OS in this patient population. The present study confirmed that PM, median household income, T stage, differentiation of primary tumor, and histological type were the key prognostic factors, aligning with the results of previous findings [10, 11]. The PM group exhibited a longer OS than the non-PM group. Similarly, previous studies have reported improved OS outcomes for patients undergoing PM [12].

Patients with positive tumor markers (e.g. CEA) exhibited a significantly higher mortality risk, suggesting that biomarkers may guide patient selection for PM [13]. This association may relate to enhanced tumor cell metastasis [14, 15]. Furthermore, in our study, CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis who received PM combined with radiotherapy and chemotherapy demonstrated a longer median OS than those who did not. Some studies suggested comparable short-term survival outcomes between radiation therapy and surgery [16–19]. However, this study expands the therapeutic options for CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis [20].

In our study, PSM was employed to reduce the possible effects of some potential covariates between the PM and non-PM groups, enhancing the validity of our findings. In other words, our study minimizes the selection bias for patients to accept PM. In our present study, a total of 140 patients underwent PM from multiple centers in the USA. As a result, PM, as an independent prognostic factor, improves OS for CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis. First, this study demonstrated significant OS improvements for CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis who underwent PM in overall cohort. Second, after PSM, aiming to balance the possible covariates between the PM group and the non-PM group, the analysis revealed that PM continues to enhance OS for CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis in the PSM cohort. Multivariate Cox model analysis revealed that PM was an independent prognostic factor for OS in CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis, which was consistent with previous studies [21, 22]. Notably, in the subgroup analysis, the median OS for CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis who underwent PM were superior to those who did not undergo PM in the CEA-positive subgroup, the Grade I–II subgroup, the CRC tumor size of <7 cm subgroup, and the adenocarcinoma subgroup. These findings suggest that PM may be considered for CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis—particularly those with well-differentiated tumors, CEA-positive, T1–T3, a history of chemotherapy, and regardless of radiotherapy. The best survival outcome for CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis was achieved through PM-based trimodality therapy.

Our study indicates that the median OS of CRC patients with pulmonary metastasis undergoing PM-based trimodality therapy approached 65 months, consistently with the results of a previous study [23]. This suggests potential benefits for selected patients undergoing PM. However, existing analyses often involve small sample sizes and lack control groups [24–27], which may limit generalizability. Additionally, the retrospective nature of these studies may have introduced selection bias and relevant shortcomings.

However, some studies report different outcomes, such as that by Boysen et al. [28], which indicated that the benefit of PM was only significant in univariate analyses and not in multivariate analyses.

There are several limitations in this study. First, as retrospective research, this study is inherently limited by potential selection bias. To mitigate this issue and enhance the reliability of our conclusions, PSM was used to reduce the possible confounding bias between the PM group and the non-PM group. Second, the comorbidities of patients also present a limitation when assessing OS, as comorbidities can modulate mortality over time, especially over long periods of time. Last but not least, some information such as whether chemotherapy and radiotherapy were performed before or after PM was not available.

Conclusions

PM prolongs survival in patients with CRC and pulmonary metastasis. This study provides valuable insights into the treatment of CRC patients with pulmonary metastases, highlighting a promising therapeutic approach and offering guidance for future randomized–controlled trials aimed at optimizing the management of pulmonary metastases.

Efficacy of Tranexamic acid Microneedling versus Intradermal Injection of Tranexamic Acid in the Treatment of Melasma – A Randomised Controlled Trial
Authors: M. Srijha, Damayandhi Kaliyaperumal, Jude Ernest Dileep, Sheela Kuruvila
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Melasma is a pigmentary disorder that typically appears on the face. Given its tendency to relapse, resistance to treatment and significant impact on social and emotional well-being, melasma poses considerable challenges.Objectives:To compare the effectiveness and adverse effects of tranexamic acid (TXA) in reducing modified Melasma Area and Severity Index (mMASI) scores when given along with microneedling versus intradermal injections in the treatment of melasma.Materials and Methods:This was a hospital-based randomised controlled study conducted in the outpatient Department of Dermatology, Venereology and Leprosy, Aarupadai Veedu Medical College, among patients with melasma, between November 2022 and April 2024. In Group A (n = 35), patients received microneedling with TXA (4 mg/mL) and intradermal injections of TXA (4 mg/mL) in Group B (n = 35). Treatment was administered every 4 weeks for four sessions and followed-up once a month for 2 months.Results:The baseline characteristics of the study groups did not vary significantly by age (in years), gender, Fitzpatrick skin type, precipitating factors, clinical type of melasma, and pattern of melasma. Both groups showed significant reduction in mMASI scores from baseline to 20 weeks (7.1 ± 2.3 to 4.6 ± 0.5 in Group A and 7.0 ± 3.1 to 5.3 ± 0.7 in Group B, respectively). From 12th week onwards, Group A had significantly lower mMASI scores than Group B (P < 0.05). A total of 83% of Group A patients had nearly cleared melasma compared to 37.0% in Group B (P < 0.05). Photographic documentation revealed that over 75.0% of Group A patients had more than 50.0% improvement, against 40.0% in Group B (P < 0.05). Adverse events such as erythema, pain, burning sensation, and pigmentation were slightly more common in Group B, but the difference was not statistically significant.Conclusion:The study highlights microneedling with TXA as a promising and effective treatment option for melasma, offering substantial clinical improvement and patient satisfaction.

Introduction

Melasma is a prevalent type of acquired hypermelanosis that affects mostly sun-exposed areas and particularly women. Its etiopathogenesis is complex involving multiple components; however, the precise aetiology remains elusive.[1] Various therapeutic options such as topical and systemic agents, chemical peels, lasers, and light sources are available for melasma treatment. Despite the array of therapeutic modalities, treating melasma remains challenging and intricate.[2]

Therefore, there is a need to explore the efficacy of novel agents and alternative administration routes to develop effective therapies for melasma.[3] Tranexamic acid (TXA) is believed to modulate melasma by inhibiting the activation of melanocytes induced by ultraviolet (UV) light and hormones.[4] Additionally, TXA reduces melanocyte tyrosinase activity. These actions not only ameliorate melasma but also reduce the likelihood of recurrence.[5] The aim of the present study was to compare the effectiveness and side effect profile of TXA in reducing Melasma Area and Severity Index (mMASI) scores when given with microneedling vs. intradermal injections in the treatment of melasma.

Materials and Methods

This was a hospital-based randomised controlled study conducted among patients presenting to the outpatient Department of Dermatology, Venerology and Leprosy, Aarupadai Veedu Medical College, Puducherry, India between November 2022 and April 2024. The study was approved by the Institutional Human Ethics Committee (AV/IHEC/2022/074) and registered in CTRI (CTRI/052649). Written informed consent was obtained prior to enrolment. All patients between 18 and 60 years of age of both gender, presenting with epidermal or dermal or mixed type of melasma, were enrolled in the study. However, pregnant patients, patients on hormone replacement therapy, on contraceptive pills, and on anticoagulants, patients allergic to TXA, patients with keloidal or hypertrophic scar tendency, patients with history of depigmenting treatment in the past 1 month, patients with moderate to severe acne vulgaris on face, and patients with history of herpes infections/warts were excluded from the study.

Sample size was calculated to be 70 (35 in each group) with 95% confidence interval with level of significance and power to be 5% and 80%, respectively, based on a similar study by Budamakuntla et al.[6] The non-response rate was assumed to be 10%. Patients were enrolled using nonprobability sampling and were randomly allotted into Group A (patients received microneedling with TXA) and Group B (patients received intradermal injections of TXA), and simple randomisation was performed using computer generated random numbers. Prior to treatment, 0.1 mL of TXA was administered as a test dose on the volar aspect of the left forearm. Topical anaesthetic cream was applied to the treatment area, and the patient was instructed to wait for 45 to 60 min. In Group A patients, skin was stretched, and microneedling was carried in vertical, horizontal, and both diagonal directions for about 4 to 5 times using a derma-roller containing 192 fine titanium needles with a needle length of 1.5 mm and diameter of 0.25 mm. TXA 0.5 to 1 mL (4 mg/mL) up to a maximum of 8 mg (2 mL) total dose was applied over this area before microneedling.[7] In Group B patients, multiple intradermal injections of 0.05 to 0.1 mL TXA (4 mg/mL) at 1 cm intervals were given to a maximum of 8 mg (2 mL) to the entire affected area, using a 40 U/mL insulin syringe.[6] Following each of the procedure, the subjects were observed for one hour; instructed to follow strict photo-protective measures, and fusidic acid cream was applied for 24 h to prevent any infection. The outcomes or dependent variables of interest were mMASI scores at follow-up, Patient Global Assessment Scale scores, clinical improvement based on photographic assessment, and side effects of both the procedures.[89]

Statistical analysis

The data obtained was manually entered into Microsoft Excel and analysed using Statistical Package for Social Sciences v23 (SPSS Inc., IBM Corporation, Chicago, USA). Continuous variables were summarised using mean (standard deviation) and/or median (interquartile range) (based on the results of data normality, tested using Kolmogorov–Smirnov test and the Shapiro–Wilk test). To test for statistical significance, Chi-square test or Fisher exact test (for categorical variables) and independent t-test or Mann Whitney U-test (for continuous variables) were used. The before and after treatment comparison was performed based on repeated measures ANOVA test. Statistical significance was considered at P value less than 0.05.

Results

The patients enrolled in the present study were randomised into two groups – Group A, patients with melasma receiving microneedling with TXA (n = 35) and Group B, patients with melasma receiving intradermal injections of TXA (n = 35). The mean (standard deviation [SD]) age of the patients was 43.7 years (9.4). Majority of the patients were between 31 and 40 years in Group A (51.4%) and Group B (45.7%). The study groups did not vary significantly by age, gender, Fitzpatrick skin type, family history, precipitating factors, clinical type, and pattern of melasma (P > 0.05) [Table 1].

Comparison of study groups by modified Melasma Area and Severity Index scores

The patients enrolled in the study were assessed using mMASI scores at baseline, 4,8,12 weeks along with follow-up at 16 and 20 weeks. The mean (SD) modified MASI scores at each assessment did not vary significantly at baseline, 4 and 8 weeks (P > 0.05). Group A showed a significant reduction in mMASI scores from 7.1 (2.3) at baseline to 4.6 (0.5) at 20 weeks, while in Group B, the reduction was from 7.0 (3.1) to 5.3 (0.7). Patients in Group A showed significantly higher reduction in modified MASI scores compared to Group B, 12 weeks onwards [Figure 1].

Patient outcomes assessed using Patient Global Assessment Scale

Patients were categorised as completely cleared, nearly cleared and significant hyper pigmentation present. A total of 82.9% patients in Group A had nearly cleared melasma, in comparison with 37.1% patients in Group B. This difference was found to be statistically significant (P < 0.05). None of the patients reported complete clearance of melasma [Table 2].

Patient outcomes using clinical assessment

Clinical improvement was assessed using photographs, done at baseline then serially till the end of 20 weeks [Figures 2 and 3]. Patients with no improvement were considered no response, <25% improvement was considered mild response, 25% to <50% improvement was considered moderate response, 50% to <75% improvement was considered good response, and more than 75% improvement was considered very good response. A total of 71.4% patients in Group A had more than 50% improvement, while only 40% of patients in Group B had more than 50% improvement. This difference was also found to be statistically significant (P < 0.05) [Table 2].

Adverse events

The proportion of patients reported adverse events in Group A was 2.9% in comparison with 8.7% in Group B. The study groups did not vary significantly with respect to side effects (P > 0.05) [Table 2].

Discussion

Melasma is a challenging pigmentary disorder commonly affecting individuals with Fitzpatrick skin types III to VI, particularly women of reproductive age.[10] TXA has emerged as a promising therapeutic agent for melasma due to its ability to inhibit melanogenesis and reduce pigmentation.[1]

Majority of the patients in this study were in the age range of 31–40 years and were females (84.3%). This finding corroborates with that reported by Budamakuntla et al.[6] The mean age of patients included in the present study was 43.7 years; comparable with the study by Kimbrough-Green et al.[11] In the present study, most of the patients belonged to Fitzpatrick skin types IV and V, consistent with previous literature.[12] Importantly, there were no significant differences in age, gender distribution or Fitzpatrick skin types between the two treatment groups (P > 0.05), ensuring baseline comparability.

Our findings align with existing literature, with 38.6% of melasma patients reporting positive family history.[13] In our study, 75.7% reported one or more precipitating factors, with no significant difference observed between the treatment groups (P > 0.05).[1415] A study by Sanchez et al. suggested that UV exposure and genetics play important roles in the pathogenesis of melasma similar to our study.[16]

The most common clinical type of melasma in the study was malar type (60.0%); in concordance with earlier studies by Budamakuntla et al.[6] In our study, the most commonly observed pattern of melasma under woods lamp was mixed type (87.1%), followed by epidermal type (13.0%); in concordance with earlier studies by Budamakuntla et al.[6] These findings suggest that TXA interventions can be effective across different melasma patterns and types, supporting its broad utility in clinical practice.[1415]

Group A exhibited a greater reduction in mMASI score from 12 weeks onwards compared to Group B (P < 0.05).[17] The reduction in mMASI scores was seen not only during the interventional period but also during the periods of follow-up at 16 and 20 weeks. None of the patients had relapse or remission during the follow-up period. This suggests that TXA might have long-lasting effects even after discontinuation of treatment.

Based on Patient Global Assessment Scale, Group A patients (82.9%) reported near clearance of melasma, compared to only 37.1% in Group B.[18] This is found to be statistically significant (P < 0.05), indicating superior satisfaction among patients in Group A.[19] Higher proportion of patients in Group A showed moderate improvement (82.9% vs. 57.1%) and good improvement (8.5% vs. 2.9%) compared to Group B based on photographic assessment. These findings corroborated with study reported by Poostiyan et al., Kaur et al., and Lueangarun et al.[2720] Group A achieved significant clinical improvement (>50%), while only 40% of patients in the Group B achieved similar improvement. This disparity in treatment response was statistically significant (P < 0.05), reinforcing the superiority of microneedling with TXA in promoting substantial clinical improvement in melasma patients.

Microneedling leads to production of growth factors and collagen induction in the dermis. It is believed to promote early keratinocyte proliferation, facilitating transcutaneous elimination of melanin and also repairing damaged basal membranes, hence used in facial rejuvenation and in the reduction of facial pigmentation. Uniform penetration of TXA with microneedling device compared to intradermal injection therapy is also an added advantage of microneedling. This synergistic effect likely contributes to the accelerated and more pronounced improvement seen in patients undergoing microneedling with TXA.[21] Moreover, since most of the patients included in the study were treatment naive patients, the combined effects of TXA and strict photoprotection measures during treatment and the follow-up period could have led to significant reduction in melasma pigmentation even after stopping the therapy.

One patient (2.9%) in Group A and three patients (8.7%) in Group B reported adverse events. Although the absolute numbers of adverse events differed between the groups, statistical analysis indicated that the reported number of adverse events did not vary significantly between two groups (P = 0.303).[22]

Limitations

The limitations of this study include single-centre study design, short-term follow-up, lack of blinding. Moreover, variations in treatment protocol such as TXA concentration, needle depth in microneedling, or injection techniques might have a bearing on the treatment response, thus affecting generalisability of our findings.

Conclusion

The findings of our study underscores that both microneedling and intradermal injections with TXA were effective in the treatment of melasma with a comparable side effect profile. However, microneedling with TXA demonstrated a superior efficacy offering substantial clinical improvement and patient satisfaction when compared to intradermal injection group and could prove to be a promising treatment strategy for melasma.

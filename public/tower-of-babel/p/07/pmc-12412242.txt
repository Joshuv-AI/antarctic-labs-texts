Correlation between thyroid sensitivity indices and bone metabolism in newly diagnosed middle aged and elderly patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus with normal thyroid function
Authors: Lixuan Fang, Cuiping Lin, Lijuan Gu, Haixia Zeng, Yuying Zhang, Jianping Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo investigate the association between thyroid hormone sensitivity indices and bone metabolism markers in newly diagnosed middle-aged and elderly type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) patients with normal thyroid function.MethodWe retrospectively analyzed 350 newly diagnosed T2DM patients (≥ 45 years), stratified by bone mineral density into Group A (normal bone density group) and Group B (low bone mass and osteoporosis group). General data and clinical biochemical parameters were collected: free triiodothyronine (FT3), free thyroxine (FT4), thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), 25-hydroxyvitamin D (25(OH)D), parathyroid hormone (PTH), osteocalcin (OC), bone-specific alkaline phosphatase (BALP), serum calcium (Ca), serum phosphorus (P), fasting plasma glucose (FPG), glycosylated hemoglobinA1c (HbA1c), total cholesterol (TC), triglyceride (TG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), serum creatinine (SCr), serum uric acid (SUA), and estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR). Thyroid sensitivity indices calculated were: thyrotropin resistance index (TT4RI), TSH index (TSHI), the ratio of FT3/FT4, and the secretory capacity of the thyroid (SPINA-GT). Associations were assessed using multiple linear regression, binary logistic regression, and stratified analysis.ResultsGroup B patients had lower BMI, DBP, FT3, FT3/FT4 ratio, FPG, HbA1c, TG, SCr and SUA and higher age, female proportion, OC and HDL-C (P < 0.05). Spearman Correlation analysis showed that 25(OH)D was positively associated with FT3/FT4 ratio; Ca was positively associated with FT3; and P was associated with TSH, TT4RI and TSHI and negatively associated with SPINA-GT (P < 0.05). Multiple linear regression analysis showed that FT3, FT3/FT4 ratios were positive factors for serum OC, BALP and Ca, and FT4 was an inverse correlation with serum BALP (P < 0.05). Binary Logistic regression analysis showed that the elevated FT3/FT4 ratio was a protective factor for the occurrence of low bone mass and osteoporosis (P < 0.05). Stratified by HbA1c, the effect of FT3/FT4 ratio on low bone mass and osteoporosis showed that the FT3/FT4 ratio was inversely associated with the occurrence of low bone mass and osteoporosis in the HbA1c < 7% group (P < 0.05). Simple effect analysis of the interaction between FT3/FT4 ratio, HbA1c and bone density showed that T2DM patients with HbA1c ≥ 7% had a lower FT3/FT4 ratio and a higher risk of developing low bone mass and osteoporosis compared to those with HbA1c < 7%.ConclusionIn newly diagnosed middle-aged and elderly T2DM patients with normal thyroid function, the peripheral sensitivity to thyroid hormone indix FT3/FT4 ratio was significantly associated with serum OC, BALP, and Ca levels. The lower FT3/FT4 ratio may increase the risk of low bone mass and osteoporosis in newly diagnosed T2DM patients with normal thyroid function, and the reduced index may serve as a tool for early screening and a predictor of low bone mass and osteoporosis.

Introduction

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a metabolic disorder characterized by chronic hyperglycemia. It represents a major global health challenge, associated with high rates of morbidity, mortality, and disability. According to the data of Chinese mainland adults over 18 years old from 2015 to 2017, the prevalence rate of diabetes in China has reached as high as 12.8% [1]. Data from the seventh national census in 2020 show that more than 30% of elderly people over 60 suffer from diabetes [2]. Consequently, T2DM constitutes one of the world’s most significant public health issues.

Diabetic osteoporosis is one of the important complications of diabetes mellitus, which is mainly manifested by low bone mass and bone microstructure destruction, increased bone fragility, and is prone to fracture. Osteopenia, decreased bone strength, changes in bone microstructure and increased bone fragility in diabetes patients lead to an increased risk of low bone mass, osteoporosis and fracture [3, 4]. The latest research shows that the incidence of diabetes patients with low bone mass and osteoporosis is increasing year by year, of which the osteoporosis rate is as high as 37.33% [5]. Therefore, it is emphasized that more attention should be paid to the screening of low bone mass and osteoporosis in diabetes patients.

The pathophysiological process of low bone mass and osteoporosis in diabetes patients is complex, the specific pathogenesis is still unclear, and there are few clinical prediction indicators. At present, the common methods for evaluating bone metabolism by T2DM include bone mineral density (BMD) and serum bone metabolism indicators. BMD is mainly estimated through dual energy X-ray absorption measurement. In addition to serving as a bone mass parameter, BMD is also the main determining factor of bone strength and fracture risk [6]. Previous studies have found that compared with the healthy control group, the BMD of T2DM patients can increase, decrease, or remain normal [3, 7]. However, due to changes in bone microstructure and local fluid environment that stimulate osteoclast activity in T2DM patients, the risk of low bone mass and osteoporosis still increases, indicating that changes in BMD cannot fully reflect the bone metabolism status of T2DM patients. Therefore, combining serum bone metabolism indicators to assess the risk of low bone mass and osteoporosis can compensate for BMD insensitivity. In clinical practice, serum bone metabolism indicators include general biochemical indicators, bone turnover biomarkers (BTMs), and bone metabolism regulatory hormones. These indicators can reflect the metabolic status of bone tissue, and are used to evaluate bone metabolism status, diagnose and grade osteoporosis. They are of great significance for the differential diagnosis of osteoporotic bone disease, prediction of fracture risk, and research on anti-osteoporosis drugs [8, 9]. Among them, BTMs can be divided into bone formation markers and bone resorption markers. The former reflects the activity of osteoblasts and the state of bone formation, while the latter represents the activity of osteoclasts and the state of bone resorption [9, 10]. However, whether there are more convenient indicators to evaluate or predict the occurrence of low bone mass and osteoporosis needs to be further explored.

The hypothalamic pituitary thyroid axis plays an important role in bone development, peak bone mass acquisition, and regulation of bone metabolism [11]. Research has shown that untreated hyperthyroidism can affect bone mass and increase the incidence of low bone mass and osteoporosis [12]. Nicholls et al. [13] proposed that hyperthyroidism can lead to an increase in bone turnover rate, a shortened bone remodeling cycle, and an increase in biochemical indicators of bone resorption and formation. Other data shows that patients with hyperthyroidism have a decrease in BMD of approximately 10%−20% [14, 15]. Therefore, hyperthyroidism is currently considered a risk factor for low bone mass and osteoporosis. However, the impact of hypothyroidism on bones is not as significant as hyperthyroidism, and the cellular and molecular mechanisms underlying its role in bones are still controversial. At present, there is limited research on thyroid hormones and bone metabolism under normal thyroid function, especially in newly diagnosed middle-aged and elderly T2DM populations, and there are currently no reports.

Traditionally, thyroid function is mainly reflected by serum levels of free triiodothyronine (FT3), free thyroxine (FT4), and thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH). However, the complex interactions between FT3, FT4, and TSH can be evaluated more comprehensively by calculating the thyroid hormone sensitivity index, providing new explanations for thyroid function status. The concept of thyroid hormone sensitivity index used in this study is mainly based on the research of Wang Guang et al. [16]. The thyroid hormone sensitivity index can be divided into central sensitivity index and peripheral sensitivity index. Common central sensitivity indices of thyroid hormones include TT4RI, TSHI, and thyroid feedback quantile index (TFQI). The larger the index, the lower the central sensitivity [17, 18]. The thyroid peripheral sensitivity index mainly includes the FT3/FT4 ratio, and the higher the ratio, the higher the peripheral sensitivity. It is used to reflect the thyroid hormone sensitivity of peripheral tissues and can indirectly evaluate the bioavailability of thyroid hormones. In addition, calculating the secretory capacity of the thyroid (SPINA-GT) can evaluate the maximum secretion capacity of the thyroid gland [19]. Recent studies have found that thyroid hormone sensitivity index is closely related to obesity and diabetes [20], suggesting that thyroid hormone sensitivity index may have a certain predictive effect on metabolic disorders. This study explores the correlation between thyroid hormone sensitivity index and bone metabolism in newly diagnosed middle-aged and elderly T2DM patients with normal thyroid function from the perspective of thyroid hormone sensitivity, providing ideas for subsequent molecular mechanisms and etiology research.

Method

Study design and study population

The data of this study are from a cross-sectional study. The subjects are from the big data platform of the inpatient system of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University. The inclusion criteria include: age ≥ 45 years, normal thyroid function(TSH: 0.55 ~ 4.78 mIU/l, FT3:2.3 ~ 4.2 pg/ml, and FT4:0.89 ~ 1.8 ng/dl), and diagnosed with T2DM according to American Diabetes Association (ADA) criteria [21](Typical diabetic symptoms, plus random blood glucose ≥ 11.1mmol/l, or Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG) ≥ 7.0mmol/l, or add OGTT 2h blood glucose ≥ 11.1mmol/l, or add HbA1c ≥ 6.5%. Those who do not have typical symptoms of diabetes should be reexamined and confirmed on another day), duration of diabetes ≤ 12 months, no hypoglycemic drugs or medication duration ≤ 1 month; Exclusion criteria include thyroid dysfunction (hyperthyroidism, hypothyroidism, thyroiditis), history of thyroid-related medications (e.g., levothyroxine, methimazole), other types of diabetes, endocrine diseases affecting bone metabolism, and history of common drugs(e.g., glucocorticoids, antiepileptic drugs, anti-osteoporosis drugs).

According to the World Health Organization (WHO) diagnostic classification [22], osteoporosis was defined as BMD (T value) at the hip or lumbar spine more than 2.5 standard deviation (SD) lower than the average of 20 years, while low bone mass was defined as T value 1 to 2.5 SD lower than the average of younger adults, and a T value ≥−1SD for normal bone mineral density.

All the subjects were divided into two groups, Group A and Group B, based on the bone mineral density results. Group A was the normal bone mineral density group, and Group B was the low bone mass and osteoporosis group.

Clinical indicators

All blood samples were analyzed in the laboratory of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University for venous blood samples taken the following morning after at least 8 h of fasting. Collection indicators include: free triiodothyronine (FT3), free thyroxine (FT4), thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), 25-hydroxyvitamin D (25(OH)D), parathyroid hormone (PTH), osteocalcin (OC), bone-specific alkaline phosphatase (BALP), serum calcium (Ca), serum phosphorus (P), fasting plasma glucose (FPG), glycosylated hemoglobinA1c (HbA1c), total cholesterol (TC), triglyceride (TG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), serum creatinine (SCr), serum uric acid (SUA), and estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR).The thyroid hormone sensitivity indices were calculated as follows:

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0 software and plots using Chiplot online and Graphpad Prism 9 software. Metrics that met normal distribution were represented by mean ± standard deviation (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\bar{\chi }$$\end{document} ± s), while metrics that meet non normal distribution are represented by median and interquartile distance M (Q25, Q75). When analyzing the differences between two groups, independent sample t-tests are used to compare continuous variables that meet normal distribution, Mann Whitney U-tests are used when they do not meet normal distribution, and chi-square tests are used for categorical variables. When conducting correlation analysis, Pearson correlation analysis was used for two variables that follow a normal distribution, and Spearman correlation analysis was used for two variables that follow a non-normal distribution. Analyze the impact of thyroid hormone sensitivity index on serum bone metabolism indicators through multiple linear regression. The impact of thyroid hormone sensitivity index on low bone mass and osteoporosis was analyzed through binary logistic regression. Evaluate the value of thyroid hormone sensitivity index using ROC curve to predict the occurrence of low bone mass and osteoporosis, and calculate its critical value. The difference was statistically significant when P < 0.05.

Result

Clinical characteristics of the participants

This study collected a total of 350 newly diagnosed middle-aged and elderly T2DM patients, with an average age of 60.97 ± 9.03 years, including 138 males (39.4%) and 212 females (60.6%). Group according to the bone density results, and compare the general information and clinical biochemical indicators between the two groups (Table 1). The results showed that compared with Group A, BMI, DBP, FT3, FT3/FT4 ratio, FPG, HbA1c, TG, SCr, and SUA of Group B patients were lower, while age, female proportion, OC, and HDL-C were higher, with statistically significant differences (P < 0.05). However, there was no significant difference in SBP, FT4, TSH, TT4RI, TSHI, SPINA-GT, PTH, 25(OH)D, BALP, TC, LDL-C, eGFR, Ca, and P between the two groups (P > 0.05).

Correlation between serum bone metabolism indices and general data and clinical biochemical parameters

The correlation analysis between serum bone metabolism indicators and general information, clinical biochemical indicators was conducted on patients in Group B, and the results showed that:

25 (OH) D showed a positive correlation with the FT3/FT4 ratio and LDL-C (r = 0.13, r = 0.126), while a negative correlation with FPG, HbA1c, TG, SUA, and SCr (r=−0.220, r=−0.192, r=−0.139, r=−0.105, r=−0.178) (Fig. 1).

OC was positively correlated with BMI and SCr (r = 0.140, r = 0.14), and negatively correlated with HbA1c (r=−0.109) (Fig. 1).

BALP showed a positive correlation with LDL-C and SUA (r = 0.119, r = 0.228), while a negative correlation with SBP, FPG, HbA1c, and eGFR (r=−0.113, r=−0.117, r=−0.141, r=−0.144) (Fig. 1).

PTH was positively correlated with SBP, FPG, HbA1c, TG, TC, HDL-C, and eGFR (r = 0.143, r = 0.236,r = 0.265,r = 0.164, r = 0.202, r = 0.182, r = 0.120), and negatively correlated with SUA (r=−0.124) (Fig. 1).

Ca is positively correlated with FT3, TC, HDL-C, and LDL-C (r = 0.149, r = 0.210, r = 0.173, r = 0.15) (Fig. 1).

P was positively correlated with TSH, TT4RI, TSHI, TG, TC, and HDL-C (r = 0.126, r = 0.115, r = 0.124, r = 0.138, r = 0.213,r = 0.212), and negatively correlated with age and SPINA-GT (r=−0.150, r=−0.119) (Fig. 1).

The effect of thyroid hormone sensitivity indices on serum bone metabolism indicators

The impact of thyroid hormone sensitivity indices on serum bone metabolism indicators was analyzed using a multiple linear regression model. The model adjusted for relevant factors in both groups, including age, gender, BMI, FPG, HbA1c, TG, TC, LDL-C, HDL-C, SCr, SUA, and eGFR. The results showed that FT3 and FT3/FT4 ratios were positively correlated with serum OC, BALP, and Ca (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2). FT4 was a negative correlation factor for serum BALP (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2). TSH, TT4RI, TSHI, and SPINA-GT were not significantly correlated with serum bone metabolism indicators (P > 0.05) (Fig. 2).

Effect of thyroid hormone sensitivity indices on low bone mass and osteoporosis

A binary logistic regression analysis was conducted using age, gender, BMI, DBP, OC, FPG, HbA1c, TG, HDL-C, SCr, and SUA as independent variables in univariate analysis, with the occurrence of low bone mass and osteoporosis as dependent variables. The results showed that an increase in FT3/FT4 ratio was a protective factor for low bone mass and osteoporosis (OR = 0.439, 95% CI: 0.208–0.926, P = 0.031), while FT4, TSH, TT4RI, TSHI, and SPINA-GT had no significant effect on low bone mass and osteoporosis (P > 0.05) (Table 2).

Discussion

At present, studies have confirmed that thyroid hormone sensitivity index is related to a variety of metabolic disorders, including obesity and diabetes [20, 23]. The relationship between thyroid dysfunction and bone metabolism has been evaluated in several cross-sectional and prospective studies, and the results are somewhat controversial [24–26]. The proposal of the thyroid hormone sensitivity index provides a more comprehensive explanation for the evaluation of the thyroid gland and is one of the recent research hotspots. At present, there is no clear epidemiological data suggesting a correlation between thyroid hormone sensitivity and BMD and BTMs, and there is no clear conclusion on the relationship between thyroid hormone sensitivity index and bone metabolism. Especially in T2DM patients with normal thyroid function, there is limited research at home and abroad on whether changes in thyroid sensitivity will affect BMD and serum bone metabolism indicators. Therefore, in this study, we propose that the thyroid hormone sensitivity index may be associated with bone metabolism and serve as a predictive factor for low bone mass and osteoporosis in newly diagnosed middle-aged and elderly T2DM patients with normal thyroid function.

The prevalence of osteoporosis in individuals with diabetes is influenced by multiple factors, including the duration of diabetes, microvascular complications, the use of hypoglycemic medications, and poor glycemic control [27, 28]. Fractures represent one of the most serious complications of low bone mass and osteoporosis. Previous studies have shown that the risk of fracture usually increases in the first five years of diabetes [27]. However, Leslie et al. [29] proposed a biphasic pattern of reduced fracture risk in T2DM patients, which suggests a reduction in fracture risk in newly diagnosed T2DM patients. This mechanism may be related to the specific protective effect of increased fat, and suggests that the fracture risk in such patients significantly increases over the next five years. At present, the effects of hypoglycemic drugs on bone metabolism vary. Multiple randomized placebo-controlled experiments have found that the use of metformin, thiazolidinediones, and insulin can significantly increase the risk of low bone mass, osteoporosis, and fracture [30–32]. Therefore, in this study, in order to avoid the influence of diabetes course and hypoglycemic drugs as much as possible, newly diagnosed T2DM patients were included, and age, gender, blood pressure and other factors were adjusted in the regression model. Given that diet and circadian rhythm can directly affect bone metabolism and thyroid function [33], the data collected in this study were all morning fasting blood samples.

This study found that in T2DM patients, the average age of the low bone mass and osteoporosis groups was higher than that of the normal bone density group, indicating that low bone mass and osteoporosis may be age-related endocrine and metabolic diseases. The relevant guidelines clearly state that aging is a risk factor for low bone mass and osteoporosis [34]. The mechanisms include: advanced age can cause imbalanced bone remodeling, an increase in the ratio of bone resorption to bone formation, leading to progressive bone loss [10]; at the same time, advanced age can reduce the activity of the immune system, such as interleukin-1 (IL-1), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and tumor necrosis factor α (TNF-α) Inflammatory mediators can promote nuclear factors-κ The expression of B receptor activating factor ligand (RANKL) and macrophage colony stimulating factor (M-CSF) stimulates osteoclasts, inhibits osteoblasts, and thus reduces bone mass [10]. In addition, low bone mass and osteoporosis are more common in postmenopausal women and elderly men. This study found that among T2DM patients in the low bone mass and osteoporosis groups, postmenopausal women have significantly more patients than men, which is similar to previous research results. The mechanism is related to the decrease in estrogen levels in postmenopausal women, which reduces the inhibitory effect of estrogen on osteoclasts and promotes bone resorption [35]. In addition, this study found that compared to the group with normal bone density, patients with low bone mass and osteoporosis had lower BMI, which was statistically significant. Previous studies have found an increase in BMD in obese patients and a negative correlation with the incidence of osteoporosis [36, 37]. Zheng et al. [38] proposed that lower BMI is an independent risk factor for low bone mass and high incidence rate of osteoporosis in postmenopausal women and elderly men. This study found that patients with low bone mass and osteoporosis had lower BMI, similar to previous research results, which may be related to the lower BMI of these patients β-estradiol levels are associated with low mechanical load [39].

The correlation analysis results of this study show that in patients with low bone mass and osteoporosis, the FT3/FT4 ratio is positively correlated with 25(OHD levels, while SPINA-GT is negatively correlated with P levels. We speculate that even under normal thyroid function, the peripheral sensitivity of thyroid hormones and the maximum secretion ability of the thyroid have certain effects on 25(OH)D and P levels, respectively. The specific mechanism needs further research. In addition, we did not find any correlation between TT4RI and TSHI with bone metabolism indicators. Therefore, we further conducted multiple linear regression analysis on the data, and after controlling for multiple influencing factors, our established model found that FT3 and FT3/FT4 ratios had a significant positive impact on OC, BALP, and Ca. The study by Nie et al. [40] showed a positive correlation between the FT3/FT4 ratio and OC. In addition, a recent cross-sectional study involving 4209 participants explored the impact of thyroid hormone sensitivity index on BTMs in T2DM patients [41]. The study also observed that OC, β-CTX, and P1NP are significantly correlated with thyroid function and thyroid hormone sensitivity indices. This is similar to our observation results. Our study suggests that under normal thyroid function, changes in the peripheral sensitivity index of thyroid hormones in newly diagnosed middle-aged and elderly T2DM patients may also affect serum bone metabolism indicators, and its potential mechanism may be related to the direct or indirect impact of T3 on osteoblasts. The potential mechanism by which T3 may influence osteoblasts is complex and involves both direct and indirect pathways. There are a certain number of T3 receptors in human bone tissue, which can affect osteoblasts by binding to T3 receptors in the nucleus [42, 43]. T3 controls the efficiency of bone formation, bone maturation, and mineralization by acting on osteoblasts, regulating intramembrane and endochondral ossification [44]. In addition, T3 can not only directly bind to T3 receptors in bone tissue, but also induce the production of growth factors and metalloproteinases, stimulate osteoblast differentiation, increase osteoblast activity, and thus affect the balance of bone turnover [45]. However, the effects of T3 on osteoblasts can vary depending on species, cell type, passage number, cell confluence, stage of differentiation, and the dose and duration of T3 treatment. Future studies should employ multivariable regression analysis to disentangle the independent effects of T3 while controlling for confounding variables such as cell differentiation stage and treatment duration. Additionally, cell-specific conditional gene targeting approaches in individual bone cell lineages, combined with genome-wide gene expression analysis, will be essential to precisely determine key target genes and downstream signaling pathways involved in T3’s actions on bone metabolism.

This study found that a decrease in the FT3/FT4 ratio may increase the risk of low bone mass and osteoporosis. The FT3/FT4 ratio is an indicator reflecting the conversion efficiency of FT4 to FT3, which can indirectly estimate the bioavailability of thyroid hormones. Currently, there is no established reference range for FT3/FT4 rate. Only a few cohort studies have explored this ratio. The standard range reported in these studies is 2.2–2.9 (with FT3 measured in pg/mL and FT4 in ng/dL). However, this standard has not been incorporated into any relevant guidelines. The primary significance of this ratio lies in the higher the FT3/FT4 ratio, the higher the sensitivity of the peripheral to thyroid hormones [46]. Previous cross-sectional studies have found a significant correlation between the FT3/FT4 ratio and diseases such as insulin resistance, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, coronary artery disease, and osteoarthritis [47–50]. A decrease in the FT3/FT4 ratio is a risk factor for cardiovascular disease and osteoarthritis [50, 51]. Our research results show that the FT3/FT4 ratio is negatively correlated with the occurrence of low bone mass and osteoporosis, indicating that a decrease in the FT3/FT4 ratio may increase the risk of low bone mass and osteoporosis, and this negative correlation is still significant after adjusting for multiple covariates.

The FT3/FT4 ratio reflects the peripheral sensitivity to thyroid hormones and has been associated with various metabolic parameters in T2DM. The research by Hari Krishnamurthy et al. also shows that HbA1c and HOMA-IR are closely related to thyroid hormones [52]. In patients with T2DM, fluctuations in thyroid hormones may occur, specifically manifested as a decrease in FT3, normal or mildly changed FT4, and a decrease in the FT3/FT4 ratio. This may be because chronic hyperglycemia and insulin resistance may affect the peripheral conversion of thyroid hormones, reducing the conversion of T4 to the active form FT3, resulting in a relatively decreased FT3 level and ultimately a decrease in the FT3/FT4 ratio [53]. This could be due to the body’s adaptive response to metabolic stress, maintaining internal environmental stability by reducing energy consumption. In addition, the chronic low-grade inflammation and oxidative stress in T2DM can interfere with the thyroid hormone signaling pathway, affecting the biological activity or metabolism of FT3, indirectly changing the FT3/FT4 ratio, and this change may interact with insulin resistance and poor blood glucose control, exacerbating the metabolic disorder.

We used the FT3/FT4 ratio as the main observation index to analyze HbA1c stratification. We found that in the HbA1c < 7% group, before and after adjusting for potential covariates, the FT3/FT4 ratio was still negatively related to the risk of low bone mass and osteoporosis, but no significant association were found in the HbA1c ≥ 7% group. High blood glucose levels can lead to increased urinary calcium excretion and impaired insulin secretion, both of which negatively impact bone formation. When HbA1c exceeds 7%, the detrimental effects of hyperglycemia on bone metabolism may overshadow the positive effects of thyroid hormones. Previous observational and related studies have shown that T2DM patients with HbA1c ≥ 7% have an increased risk of osteoporosis and fractures [54, 55], suggesting that HbA1c ≥ 7% may be a risk factor for low bone mass and osteoporosis. It highlights the importance of maintaining good glycemic control to allow thyroid hormones to exert their positive effects on bone health.

Our simple effects analysis of the interaction between FT3/FT4 ratio, HbA1c, and bone density showed that compared to T2DM patients with HbA1c < 7%, T2DM patients with HbA1c ≥ 7% had lower FT3/FT4 ratio, making them more prone to low bone mass and osteoporosis. Meanwhile, the ROC curve analysis results showed that the decrease in FT3/FT4 ratio had certain potential value in predicting and evaluating the occurrence of low bone mass and osteoporosis.

The innovation of this study is that the thyroid sensitivity index used in this study provides a more comprehensive and systematic perspective for the evaluation of thyroid homeostasis, and provides important insights into the physiology of hypothalamic pituitary thyroid feedback control [18]. We used the thyroid hormone sensitivity index instead of individual thyroid function as a predictive indicator for low bone mass and osteoporosis, providing new hypotheses for early screening and treatment of low bone mass and osteoporosis, and obtaining similar results as previous studies; The inclusion and exclusion criteria of this study are strict, avoiding the influence of multiple confounding factors, but there are also certain limitations. Firstly, this is a cross-sectional study and therefore cannot determine the causal relationship between BMD, serum bone metabolism markers, and thyroid sensitivity index. It is important to note that the associations observed are exploratory and may not fully capture the complexity of interactions between thyroid hormone sensitivity indices and bone metabolism. Secondly, given that diet and circadian rhythm can directly affect bone metabolism and thyroid function, the data collected in this study were all morning fasting blood samples. Although fasting sampling reduces acute dietary interference, chronic nutritional status (e.g., BMI, adiposity), habitual vitamin D intake, and gastrointestinal absorption efficiency remain potential modifiers of thyroid-bone axis relationships. Thirdly, this study only includes T2DM patients admitted to a single medical center and cannot represent all T2DM patients. Fourthly, this study did not collect more comprehensive bone metabolism indicators, such as PINP, β-CTX should be supplemented in future research. Fifthly, due to the small sample size in this study, further stratified analysis of the study participants was not feasible, and investigations into the relationships among healthy populations remain insufficient. In future research, we can continue to expand the sample size and conduct further follow-up and observational studies.

In conclusion, in newly diagnosed middle-aged and elderly T2DM patients with normal thyroid function, there is a certain correlation between the thyroid hormone peripheral sensitivity index FT3/FT4 ratio and serum bone metabolism indicators. A decrease in the FT3/FT4 ratio may increase the risk of low bone mass and osteoporosis, and compared to simple thyroid hormones, the FT3/FT4 ratio has higher predictive value for low bone mass and osteoporosis. The FT3/FT4 ratio can be used to monitor bone metabolism status and may serve as a predictive factor for the occurrence of low bone mass and osteoporosis, providing a simple and feasible serological indicator for early screening and prevention of low bone mass and osteoporosis.

Conclusion

In newly diagnosed middle-aged and elderly T2DM patients with normal thyroid function, the peripheral sensitivity to thyroid hormone indix FT3/FT4 ratio was significantly associated with serum OC, BALP, and Ca levels. The lower FT3/FT4 ratio may increase the risk of low bone mass and osteoporosis in newly diagnosed T2DM patients with normal thyroid function, and the reduced index may serve as a tool for early screening and a predictor of low bone mass and the osteoporosis.

Enhancing movie script creation through retrieval-augmented LLMs and stable diffusion scene modeling
Authors: Ansh Lulla, Aayush Koul, Rampalli Agni Mithra, Aniket K. Shahade, Mayur Gaikwad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Script writing has become a labor-intensive process, especially when tailored to specific scene setting, culture or character. Recent advancements in the field of Natural Language Processing and Deep Learning algorithms have made it possible to automate the process of script writing. With a simple prompt, it is possible to generate the entire script for a movie, which would be customizable by the input’s creativity and imagination. Retrieval Augmented Generation has the potential to facilitate rapid prototyping of movie scripts by ingesting the data in a vector database and retrieving scripts which would be most similar with the input prompt. These retrieved scripts would act as part of context for the Large Language Models and prevent them from hallucinating. This approach enhances the potential of these LLMs to generate context-specific output which is essential when taking into consideration the fine details mentioned in the input prompt. Fine-tuning LLMs is another approach which helps downstream the LLMs to learn how to generate movie scripts. With the inclusion of visualizing the script elements, one can conveniently turn their ideas into a script and scenes. Combining the capabilities of Stable Diffusion with the LLMs, script generation can be extended to scene generation. On training multiple models on the dataset of movie scripts, Gemini-Pro (for RAG) was very effective with a cosine similarity of 0.5713 whereas GPT-2 and Bloom (for fine-tuning) showed a cosine similarity of 0.5011 and 0.5058 respectively between the input prompt and the generated script, and perplexity of 1.7443 for GPT-2 and 1.6892 for Bloom showing that GPT-2 is able to generate scripts which are coherent and relevant to the input prompt and has thus understood the language structure and patterns well. A CLIP score of 0.3061 was achieved with using CompVis for generation of movie scenes.

Introduction

Scriptwriting is an important part of the entertainment industry. Scripts contain dialogues, scene settings and character development which makes it a time consuming and arduous process, particularly when tailored content is needed for movies, television shows, gaming and marketing. It is very important to create a vision of the scene to allow the director to create a scene which engages and appeals to the audience. Along with the increased demand for personalized content, automating the process of scriptwriting using Natural Language Processing has emerged as a promising solution to the problem of generating scalable, high quality scripts along with the growing demand in the industry.

Leveraging Deep Learning models to create complex and coherent scripts by retaining context during the generation of the entire script, allowing the scriptwriter to focus on improving the idea itself, significantly reduces the time and effort required for scriptwriting.

Natural Language Generation is a subfield of Natural Language Processing. It has proven to be a reliable tool in generating complex and coherent text while retaining contextual nuances throughout the generation process.

Leveraging deep learning techniques allowing machines to learn the sequential patterns in text, NLG systems are able to use pre-existing datasets of movie scripts and dialogues to create an understanding of human-like dialogue structures. This understanding can be further used for the purpose of personalized script generation. Existing tools primarily focus on general text generation but have research gaps for developing systems that allow personalization of scripts which include customizable elements such as character description, behavior, and other specifications defined in the input prompt about the scene and setting.

Storytelling remains a crucial method of communication in films, gaming and marketing, thereby streamlining the process of script generation can enable rapid prototyping and customization of inputs such as time of the day, number of characters and character descriptions can pioneer significant improvements in plot complexity and quality by allowing writers to focus more on the plot itself rather than the process of writing it. This would reduce the barriers for anyone who wishes to turn their ideas into scripts, promoting creative freedom and enabling the refinement of ideas rather than focusing on the mechanics of the scripts.

Automation of script generation has the potential of transforming how humans develop and consume content globally. As more and more people will have the convenience of creating high quality scripts out of their imagination and ideas, simply by providing prompts and necessary context to the LLMs or RAG systems fine-tuned on the movie script’s dataset, facilitating the generation of scripts and make changes according to preferences without worrying about the back-end complexities of text generation.

In the current AI landscape, generative models such as GPT-4, DALL·E, and Stable Diffusion have democratized content creation, yet their application in structured, multi-stage creative workflows–such as screenplay development–remains underexplored. Most contemporary systems operate in single-modality silos: text generators lack visual grounding, and image generators lack narrative coherence. Moreover, while retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has gained traction in chatbots and knowledge systems, its use in creative writing with visual feedback loops is novel. Our work bridges this gap by integrating RAG-augmented LLMs with diffusion-based scene modeling, aligning with the trend toward multimodal, interactive, and context-aware AI systems that support rather than replace human creativity.

Literature survey

Celikyilmaz A et al. conducted a survey of evaluation metrics for text generation including both human and automated methods. Using word embeddings like Word2Vec, Seq2Seq models like GRU and LSTM, BERT and GPT, they highlighted the strengths of hybrid metrics on datasets like WMT, Twitter Dialogue and Daily Mail. Though effective, the study notes the challenges faced in human evaluation methods and hence the need for standardized methods1.

Vasvani et al. introduced the Transformer architecture which is a fully attention-based approach which enhances text generation tasks. Positional word embeddings, shared weight matrix embeddings were used along with models like GCN, DeepWalk and Planetoid, on WMT-2014 dataset. The models though very powerful, face a lot of challenges in terms of scalability due to the quadratic attention complexity2.

Li et al. proposed the Diffusion-LM approach which was effective for controllable text generation without the need for retraining. It has been tested on E2E and ROCStories datasets, using clustering of learned embeddings, demonstrating strong BLEU-4 and ROUGE-L scores. Despite its effectiveness, there are potential issues with high-dimensional tuning and stability of the model in diverse scenarios3.

Fan et al. used over 300K narratives to explore hierarchical story generation. They used pre-trained embeddings like FastText with fusion of Convolutional, Seq2Seq and self-attention models. Due to complex architecture, they faced challenges in tuning but the low perplexity scores show good coherence in the test dataset4.

Brown et al. showcased the flexibility and adaptability of GPT-3 models in tasks like sentiment analysis and cloze tests. Though GPT-3 models struggled with fine-tuning demands and interpretability issues, it gave high accuracy on datasets like CommonCrawl and WebText25.

Tambwekar et al. used encoder-decoder architecture to introduce a reward-shaping technique for generating plot-directed stories. They used WordNet and VerbNet Sysnets embeddings and DRL models and achieved low perplexity scores but the comparisons were made on the existing corpus and not the unseen plots6.

Mangal et al. used the Game of Thrones data to evaluate different RNN archictures like GRU, LSTM and Bidirectional RNN. LSTM and GRU models though generated coherent texts, they failed to capture the intricate character descriptions and interactions, indicating room for improvement in complex sequences7.

Yao et al. introduce the Plan-and-Write framework, integrating static and dynamic schema for coherence in story. Using GloVe embeddings and Seq2Seq models on ROCStories dataset, the models performed well but failed to handle complex narrative relationships and events8.

Zhu et al. develop a narrative-guided script generation model by using the Word2Vec embedding with DL2R and MVLSTM models on the GraphMovie dataset. The approach adheres strictly to the structured design leading to limitation of creative storytelling9.

Bhat et al. apply hierarchical encoders to screenplay modelling, to classification of genre and mood classification using Bag-of-Embeddings and GRU on the ScriptBase-J corpus10.

Fang et al. used the WritingPrompts and WikiPlots datasets and proposed a fine-grained, controllable text generation model that builds on cascaded events. They used GloVe, TextRank, RAKE and BERT embeddings with models like FUSION, FIST and PSA and achieved strong BLEU and ROUGE scores11.

While existing approaches have demonstrated strengths in narrative generation, controllability, and structure, they often face challenges in balancing creativity with contextual fidelity, personalization, and multimodal integration. For instance, diffusion-based methods (e.g., Diffusion-LM) offer controllability but may struggle with stability and fine-grained alignment. Hierarchical and plan-based models improve coherence but can limit creative flexibility. Our proposed framework uniquely integrates Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) with fine-tuned LLMs and Stable Diffusion, enabling not only context-aware and personalized script generation but also visual scene prototyping–a multimodal capability largely unexplored in prior screenplay automation research. Beyond narrative and dialogue generation, Retrieval-Augmented Generation has also been applied to multimodal and quality-assessment tasks in media processing. For instance, QualiRAG12 employs retrieval-augmented generation for visual quality understanding, integrating retrieved exemplars to improve the assessment of image fidelity and aesthetic appeal. Similarly, RAG-enhanced LMMs13 have been used to boost image quality assessment capabilities by grounding judgments in retrieved reference samples and quality annotations. These applications demonstrate RAG’s versatility in enhancing both generative and evaluative tasks across modalities, reinforcing its relevance to our work in script-scene alignment and multimodal creativity.

Methodology

Dataset description and preprocessing

We collected a corpus of 5,000 movie scripts from publicly available sources such as IMSDb and SimplyScripts, spanning genres including drama, thriller, comedy, and sci-fi. Each script was cleaned to remove non-dialog elements (e.g., scene headings, transitions) and tokenized using model-specific tokenizers (GPT-2, Bloom). Scripts were segmented into coherent chunks of 512–1024 tokens using a recursive text splitter to preserve narrative flow. For RAG implementation, chunks were embedded using the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence transformer and indexed in a FAISS vector database with IVF-Flat clustering.

Model architecture

The Transformer architecture2 is composed of two parts: Encoder and the Decoder. The Encoder is responsible for processing the input sequences and generating useful representations of the input (embeddings). The Decoder is responsible for using the encoder’s outputs and target sequences to generate predictions.

Transformers address the issue of Seq2Seq models through Attention Mechanism. In Seq2Seq models, the decoder uses the ”summarized” version of the input sequence done by the encoder, which raises the issue of lack of context. The Decoder cannot focus on the important parts of the input which leads to sub-optimal outputs.

Through Attention Mechanism, transformers gain the ability to produce context-specific outputs by determining the relevance of each of the tokens in the sequence with respect to others and focusing on certain aspects of the input which would help generate optimal outputs. The type of attention mechanisms used in Transformer architecture are Multi-Head and Masked Multi-Head attention mechanisms.

Attention Mechanism computes the attention scores for each token to evaluate the relevance of the token with respect to the output sequence to be generated. RAG architecture

RAG - Retrieval Augmented Generation, is a framework used to help LLMs provide a better response by providing relevant information from an external database (usually a vector database) which reduces the chance that the LLM will lead to inaccurate or incomplete responses. The RAG pipeline is shown in Fig. 1.Fig. 1RAG pipeline.

RAG pipeline.

LLMs have a tendency of hallucinating when it comes to generating responses and have a limitation of knowledge, hence RAG helps mitigate these limitations of LLMs and provide better responses.

The scripts used for training are converted into chunks using the Recursive Character Text splitter which recursively identifies and segments scripts ensuring that each chunk maintains its coherent context and structure,thereby focusing on specific character scenes and transitions.

These chunks are then converted into embeddings using sentence transformer in order to capture the semantic relationships within the chunks and between the chunks. The chunk embeddings are then stored in a FAISS vector database, along with their metadata like index of the chunk and name of the movie that chunk is from.

This vector database is used for performing semantic similarity search based on a query to retrieve top ’k’ chunks which are most similar to the query which will be used as contextual information for the LLMs, so as to generate contextually relevant scripts (k value used for the proposed methodology is 5).

The above semantic similarity search is further optimized using IVF-Flat (Inverted File with Flat Quantization), which segregates all the chunks into clusters, this would help the RAG Framework to focus on only limited number of chunks for fetching the semantically most similar chunks rather than searching in the entire database. The clustering is done using the K-Means Clustering, where in the data points being close to each other are considered as ”similar” and are therefore grouped in one cluster. Doing so for the entire vector database would help divide all the chunk embeddings into clusters. IVF-Flat thus significantly improves upon the time complexity of searching especially when dealing with a dataset of large number of movie scripts.

For a given Query Embedding, the algorithm calculates the L2 Norm (Euclidean Distance) between the Query Embedding and the Centroid of each region (cluster), in order to narrow-down the search and perform semantic similarity search within that cluster for the top ’k’ chunks.

The top ’k’ chunks are evaluated using cosine similarity (to measure degree of similarity between two vectors or embeddings) and Euclidean Distance, this way the retrieved chunks are ranked based on how high is the cosine similarity and how low the Euclidean Distance between the Query Embedding and chunk embeddings (with priority given to cosine similarity).

These retrieved chunks (along with their metadata) will be provided to the LLM to generate an accurate script which adheres to the input query while preventing the LLM from hallucinating. The LLM used for RAG implementation is Gemini-Pro.

Gemini-Pro

Gemini-Pro developed by Google DeepMind, is a state-of-the-art LLM with enhanced capabilities for Natural Language Generation and Understanding tasks. It excels in complex reasoning thereby generating nuanced and coherent movie scripts tailored to the specifications. With the inclusion of RAG, Gemini-Pro’s potential hallucination is successfully mitigated.

Gemini-Pro has the ability to use either decoder-only or encoder-decoder architecture, based on the structure and type of task, making it a versatile LLM.

Fine-tuning LLMs

Fine-tuning Large Language Models involves using a pre-trained model and down-streaming it to a specific task like script generation by training it on the task-specific dataset shown in Fig. 2. This process modifies the weights of the pre-trained model to align the predictions according to the specific task.

Using the embeddings of these pre-trained models ensure that the semantic relationships within the movie scripts are captured, so as to generate a proper dialogue flow and adhere to user specifications.

Additionally, in order to make the LLMs more scalable, LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) is a quantization technique used to reduce the number of trainable parameters of the model. It performs matrix decomposition to decompose the weights (parameters) matrix of the model into two smaller matrices thereby significantly reducing the computational cost and the number of calculations to perform before predicting.Fig. 2Fine-Tuning LLMs Pipeline.

Fine-Tuning LLMs Pipeline.

Since LLM is only using a small percentage of the total parameters, there is loss of accuracy in the script generation, the key is to find the trade-off between the loss of accuracy and reducing computational cost which would make this approach scalable and efficient for large and complex datasets.

The LLMs used for fine-tuning on the script generation task are Dialo-GPT, GPT-2, Bloom and GPT-2 with LoRA.

GPT-2

GPT-2 (Generative Pre-trained Transformer 2)7 is a state-of-the-art language model based on the transformers architecture developed by OpenAI. It consists of multiple self attention mechanisms and feed forward neural networks to generate contextually relevant and coherent text. GPT-2 is a pre-trained model used for a variety of NLP tasks and thus fine tuning it on a downstream task like text generation would achieve great results. The GPT-2 model used in this paper has approximately 124 million parameters (GPT-2 Small).

GPT-2 uses its own tokenizer, as other basic tokenizers are unable to capture the contextual meaning behind the dialogues and scene settings in a script. It generates tokens based on the contextual understanding of the sequences, thus capturing the relation between different sequences.

It tokenizes words into individual characters and makes character pairs based on consecutive characters. Then it combines two characters into the same token based on the frequency of the repeating character pairs. This process is repeated until a predefined vocabulary size is reached, efficiently and deterministically forming a set of subword units which represent the text corpus.

The GPT-2 tokenizer has a limitation of only allowing a maximum length of 1024 tokens to be encoded. On average the GPT-2 tokenizer makes approximately 4 tokens for every 3 words, therefore the scripts get chunked into 700 word chunks and then tokenized. The model is then trained on these individual chunks, retaining all information from the entire script.

Once the tokenization is complete, it is ready to be fit into the model for fine-tuning. This approach allows the model to fine-tune on scripts regardless of their size and helps in retaining context as it trains the model on the largest possible chunks, learning patterns between words in a larger sequence.

Bloom

Bloom (BigScience Large Open-science Open-access Multilingual)[?] is another state-of-the-art language model based on the transformers architecture. Similar to GPT-2, it also consists of multiple self attention mechanisms and feed forward neural networks to generate contextually relevant and coherent text. Bloom is a pre-trained model which is trained on multilingual data. It has 560 million parameters (Bloom 560m) therefore can still prove to have significantly positive results for generating text in a single language.

Bloom also uses its own tokenizer which tokenizes text based on a probabilistic approach which is optimized for diverse scripts and has a more flexible tokenization approach and allows better capturing of context and relationships of words in diverse datasets. It uses SentencePiece which uses a uni-gram language model to build its vocabulary.

SentencePiece iteratively learns tokens that are most likely to occur in the training data. It merges the most frequently occurring token pairs into larger tokens and probabilistically estimates the likelihood of merging pairs based on observed frequency distributions of merged tokens. This forms a set of subword units which represent the text corpus.

The Bloom tokenizer also has a maximum length of 2048 tokens to be encoded. Chunking is also used here to chunk a maximum of 512 tokens per chunk. This allowed for a more efficient tokenization process due to the parallelization of the task using a GPU. After the tokenization, the tokens are ready to fit into the Bloom model for fine-tuning.

Additionally, in order to make the LLMs more scalable, LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) is a quantization technique used to reduce the number of trainable parameters of the model. It performs matrix decomposition to decompose the weights (parameters) matrix of the model into two smaller matrices thereby significantly reducing the computational cost and the number of calculations to perform before predicting.

Since LLM is only using a small percentage of the total parameters, there is loss of accuracy in the script generation, the key is to find the trade-off between the loss of accuracy and reducing computational cost which would make this approach scalable and efficient for large and complex datasets.

Text-image generation

Text-Image generation would allow users to visualize the scripts through scenes, and hence ideate upon the movie script generation in a better way. Text-Image generation begins with tokenizing and creating an embedding of the query to capture the semantic meaning and context of the query.

Stable Diffusion, one of the best algorithms for image generation, begins with random noise and progressively learns to refine the noisy image by de-noising it and eventually generating a clear image for the query. Over several iterations, the algorithm learns the patterns in the embedding and consequently generates a less noisy image as it progresses. Working of Stable Diffusion for Text-Image Generation is shown in Fig. 3.

Stable Diffusion operates within a latent space - a compressed space in which image generation can be done efficiently. Once the image de-noising is performed, a decoder model is used to upsample this image and transform it into a visible image.

The algorithm makes use of cross-attention layers which allows models to focus on specific parts of the query embedding and focus on refining that particular aspect of the image in order to maintain the coherence and output an accurate image.

Reason why Gemini-Pro cannot be evaluated using Perplexity To calculate perplexity, a probabilistic token-based prediction approach is required but since Gemini-Pro is being integrated with the RAG Pipeline, it is not using any probability distribution to predict the next tokens, it rather combines the retrieval mechanisms of RAG with generation. Since RAG models do not use a probabilistic approach to predict the next tokens, Gemini-Pro thus does not produce a probability distribution and perplexity cannot be used as an evaluation metric for Gemini-Pro.

Conclusion

This study presents an integrated multimodal framework that combines Retrieval-Augmented Generation, fine-tuned LLMs, and Stable Diffusion to advance automated scriptwriting and scene visualization. Unlike prior text-centric narrative systems, our pipeline uniquely addresses the need for contextual fidelity, creative flexibility, and visual prototyping within a unified workflow. Experimental results show that the RAG-enhanced Gemini-Pro model achieves stronger semantic alignment (cosine similarity of 0.5713) compared to fine-tuned GPT-2 and Bloom, while maintaining low perplexity for coherent dialogue generation. Although the CLIP score for generated scenes indicates room for visual refinement, the integration of Stable Diffusion enables direct visual ideation from text–a capability seldom explored in screenplay automation. By reducing technical barriers and supporting rapid, personalized content creation, this work offers a practical, scalable tool for creative professionals and contributes a meaningful step toward holistic, AI-assisted storytelling in the evolving cinematic and digital content landscape.

Research on Enhancing Gas Extraction Efficiency through CO2 Gas Fracturing in Outburst-Prone Coal Seams
Authors: Baige Yang, Yunxing Cao, Xinsheng Zhang, Junsheng Zhang, Shuaifang Guo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Given the background of significant Coal Mine gas disasters worldwide, the research and development of efficient gas control technologies have become critical to ensuring mining safety. CO2 gas fracturing (CO2–Frac), an innovative technology for Coal Mine gas control, has demonstrated efficiency in various mining areas across China. However, its application in outburst-prone coal seams is not yet fully understood. This study presents a field-scale CO2–Frac project conducted in an outburst Coal Mine in China, specifically the Pingshu Coal Mine, where the previously employed dense-borehole gas extraction technology failed to efficiently achieve gas extraction and outburst prevention. The CO2–Frac plan was evaluated through fracture propagation experiments utilizing both single-hole and dual-hole configurations, highlighting the advantages of the dual-hole CO2–Frac method. Subsequently, on-site gas extraction tests were conducted to further assess the efficacy of the CO2–Frac plan. The results indicate that (1) In the dual-hole CO2–Frac scheme, the fractured and pressure relief area expanded to approximately 26.82 m2, which is 220% larger than that of the single-hole scheme. (2) The dual-hole CO2–Frac significantly enhanced gas extraction effectiveness, increasing the flow rate from 0.026 to 0.216 m3/min in a 100m borehole, a 7.3-fold improvement. Additionally, the gas extraction period to reach the standard was reduced from 20 to 6 days. These findings conclusively demonstrate that the dual-hole CO2–Frac technique is an effective method for safe excavation in outburst-prone coal seams, providing both theoretical and practical validation for its application in similar geological settings.

Introduction

Coal Mine gas, or coalbed methane (CBM), is a form of clean energy generated or released from coal seams or surrounding rock during coal mining. It is also the primary cause of gas-related geohazards, including explosions, combustion, and coal and gas outbursts in underground Coal Mines worldwide.1−3 Despite coal’s dominant role in China’s primary energy structure,4,5 over 700 Coal Mines in the country are severely affected by serious coal and gas outburst disasters, severely restricting safe production.6−9 The effectiveness of preventing coal and gas outbursts in coal mines is primarily determined by the efficiency of gas extraction and pressure relief. To achieve effective gas control, a range of gas extraction technologies, such as in-seam dense-borehole extraction, hydraulic measures,10−12 and deep borehole blasting,13 has been developed and applied across China’s Coal Mines. However, with increasing mining depth, the hazard of outbursts has become increasingly severe and complex.14−16 Coal and gas outbursts remain the most significant technical challenge impeding safe production and economic benefits in Coal Mines.17

Since 2012, CO2 gas fracturing (CO2–Frac) has yielded remarkable results in gas control within the coal industry. This technology traces its origins to the CARDOX system, invented by American engineer Denton Ferrell in 1914. Originally used to replace black powder for unloading coal in gassy Coal Mines, the system found wide application in underground coal mining in Europe and the United States from the 1930s to the 1950s. Recently, CARDOX technology has been adopted in over 100 countries for tunneling, quarrying, and industrial blockage removal. From 2012, extensive theoretical studies and industrial applications of CO2–Frac technology in China have significantly enhanced gas extraction efficiency and reduced the risk of coal and gas outbursts.18−26

Extensive research has been conducted on the mechanisms by which CO2–Frac prevents coal and gas outbursts. It is generally accepted that CO2–Frac generates high-energy gas or supercritical carbon dioxide, which acts on the surrounding coal bodies, forming a specific range of fracture networks within the coal seam. The fracturing effect is related to the dynamic stress–strain behavior of coal, following a plastic hardening-softening process.27−33 Based on this understanding, the dynamic damage evolution process of CO2–Frac on coal can be modeled to evaluate its effectiveness in preventing coal and gas outbursts.

Although a comprehensive experimental and theoretical research framework for CO2–Frac has been established, the unique gas geological characteristics of coal seams in different regions, such as complex structures, high gas content, or poor permeability, complicate the formation of a standardized, efficient gas control method. As a result, different mines must tailor CO2–Frac technical parameters and schemes to their specific conditions. It is generally believed that dual-hole CO2–Frac achieves superior fracture propagation compared to single-hole CO2–Frac, yet its internal mechanisms remain understudied. This study investigated the adaptive CO2–Frac scheme in a specific mine through numerical simulations and on-site trials. The research findings are expected to provide a reference template for gas control in Coal Mines with similar gas geological conditions.

Experimental Work

Geological Settings

Pingshu Coal Mine is located on the northern edge of the Yangquan mining area in the northeastern part of the Qinshui Basin (Figure 1). The mine covers an area of 27.82 km2 and has a designed annual production capacity of 5 million tons. The minable coal seams in the mine include the Shanxi Formation 3# coal seam and the Taiyuan Formation 8#, 12#, and 15# coal seams, of which the 15# coal seam (buried depth of 540–630m) is currently the main mining coal seam (Figure 1). The mine is located on a large, east–west, south-dipping monocline structure. The main structure consists of nine roughly parallel folds running north–south (Figure 2a), with secondary broad and gentle folds, small collapse columns with a drop of less than 3 m, and faults developing between them.

Location of the study area and its geological settings.

Engineering plan of research site.

The 15th coal seam is located in the lower part of the Taiyuan Formation, with a thickness ranging from 1.35 to 7.15 m and an average thickness of 5.67 m. The entire mine is stable and minable. The coal seam structure is simple, containing 0–4 layers of coal gangue. The roof is K2 limestone with an average thickness of 2.70 m, and the floor is sandy mudstone with an average thickness of 0.39 m (Figure 2b). Coal fractures and pores are developed, but most of them are not connected to each other. The coal seam is a coal and gas outburst-prone coal seams with poor permeability, high gas content, high gas pressure, and difficult gas extraction.

In this study, industrial experiments were performed on No. 15111 mining face of the 15# coal seam. The mining face, being 1,280 m long and 230 m wide, buried at a depth of 560–580m, runs approximately north–south, has a simple geological structure, few faults, and developed collapse columns, as shown in Figure 2b. The average thickness of the coal seam is 5.7 m and the density is 1470 kg/m3. The sections of the test roadways, i.e., No. 15111 return airway and No. 15111 gas extraction roadway, are rectangles that are 4.8 m wide and 3.7 m high. During excavation of the experimental roadways, the gas content, gas pressure, and permeability coefficient of the coal seam are 12.21 m3/t, 1.02 MPa, and 0.0842 m2/(MPa2·d), respectively.

Extraction Technology Review

Dense-borehole gas extraction technology was employed as a regional outburst prevention measure. The implementation scheme is outlined as follows: 24 gas extraction boreholes were constructed, and gas extraction was conducted for over 20 days. If the tested gas content was below 8 m3/t, roadway excavation commenced; otherwise, gas extraction continued.

The effective extraction length of the boreholes was 1,200 m, with a cumulative extraction volume of 8882 m3 over 20 days, averaging 444.1 m3 per day. The average gas concentration in the confluence pipeline was 22.3%. The average extraction flow rate for a 100-m borehole was 0.026 m3/min, with an attenuation coefficient of 0.087 d–1. The data suggest a pressing need to develop more efficient gas control technologies. The current dense-borehole gas extraction method, while providing some mitigation of gas outbursts, is inadequate in terms of speed and overall efficiency. Therefore, it is crucial to explore and implement advanced gas control techniques to enhance the safety and productivity of coal mining operations in outburst-prone areas.

Numerical Simulation Scheme

Two geometric models, single-hole CO2–Frac and dual-hole CO2–Frac, were designed based on the layout of CO2–Frac boreholes in coal seams using the ANSYS dynamic module. The single-hole is located at the center of the roadway, while the dual-holes are symmetrically spaced at 2.0m intervals along the roadway center (Figure 5), apply the pressure time history curve directly to the coal wall of the borehole. The simulation results of the single-hole and dual-hole CO2–Frac schemes are illustrated in Figure 6. During the simulation steps from the 25th to the 100th, the pressure relief range at the center of the dual-hole model increases significantly faster due to the fracturing pressures applied from both sides. Additionally, the pressure continues to rise after the fractures generated by the two CO2–Frac boreholes connect, aligning with previous study findings.38−40

For the single-hole model, the CO2–Frac-induced pressure relief area measures 8.28 m2, encompassing 92 units. In contrast, the dual-hole model exhibits a CO2–Frac-induced pressure relief area of 26.82 m2, comprising 298 units. The pressure relief area in the dual-hole CO2–Frac scheme is thus 220% larger than that of the single-hole scheme. Furthermore, the fractures formed in the dual-hole scheme are more extensive, covering both the excavation roadway and its adjacent sides (Figure 7).

These findings suggest that the dual-hole CO2–Frac scheme is significantly more effective for onsite CO2–Frac applications under specific geological conditions, promoting the development of fractures in coal seams more efficiently than the single-hole approach. Therefore, the dual-hole CO2–Frac scheme is recommended for use in coal seam fracturing to enhance gas extraction efficiency and safety.

Field Experiment and Effect Investigation of Gas Extraction in a Coal Seam by CO2–Frac

CO2–Frac System

The CO2–Frac system primarily comprises a liquid storage pipe, a firing head, a discharge head, a heater, a rupture disc, and a seal gasket (Figure 8).

Schematic diagram of the CO2–Frac system.

Each pipe contains 2.0–2.2 kg of liquid CO2, with a blasting pressure of 185 MPa. Upon triggering the heater, a significant amount of thermal energy is released, rapidly converting the liquid CO2 in the storage pipe into high-pressure gas. When the gas pressure reaches the control threshold, it ruptures the disc and discharges from the head. The coal is weakened by the liquid CO2, forming a network of fractures within the coal seam,41−43 thereby improving the permeability of the coal seam and creating favorable conditions for gas extraction.

Experimental Scheme

Building on the conclusions drawn from numerical simulations, the dual-hole CO2–Frac scheme was implemented for outburst prevention at the Pingshu Coal Mine starting in May 2020.

In the field-scale trial, five cycles of CO2–Frac were completed in the No. 15111 return airway and the No. 15111 gas extraction roadway. Each cycle involved two CO2–Frac boreholes and nine gas extraction boreholes, all with a diameter of 113 mm. The CO2–Frac boreholes were 70 m long, while the auxiliary gas extraction boreholes ranged from 58 to 72 m in length. The borehole layout is shown in Figure 9.

Layout of CO2–Frac boreholes in No. 15111 return airway and No. 15111 gas extraction roadway.

Effect Investigation

Based on the aforementioned scheme, five cycles of CO2–Frac outburst prevention tests were conducted, including three cycles for the return airway and two for the gas extraction roadway. Data indicate that while the dense-borehole technology takes 20 days to extract 8882 m3 of gas, the five cycles of CO2–Frac achieve this volume in only 5–6 days, demonstrating significantly higher efficiency (Figure 10).

Comparison of gas extraction parameters before and after CO2–Frac.

The area covered by the extraction boreholes spans 0–70 m of the roadway, with coal reserves in this section amounting to 1,827.50 tons. The residual gas content is calculated as (gas reserves–cumulative gas extraction volume)/coal reserves. Compared with the dense-borehole technology, which requires 20 days, the five cycles of CO2–Frac reduce the gas extraction period to an average of 6 days, a 70% decrease (Figure 10).

Within the 530-m-long effective gas extraction section, the cumulative gas extraction volume over 35 days reached 57,771 m3, averaging 1650.6 m3 per day. The gas concentration in the confluence pipeline nearly doubled, rising from 22.3 to 45.0%. The flow rate of a 100-m borehole increased from 0.026 to 0.216 m3/min, a 7.3-fold increase. The gas extraction period needed to meet standards decreased from 20 to 6 days, a 68% reduction, and the attenuation coefficient of gas extraction flow rate declined from 0.087 to 0.009 d–1, a 90% reduction. Discussion and Conclusions

Discussion

The CO2–Frac technology enhances coal seam permeability through a dynamic loading process. Initially, CO2 gas rapidly expands, causing high-speed fluid to discharge from the pipe and act on the coal seam at close range, fracturing it. Subsequently, quasi-static gas diffuses within the coal seam, with new fractures connecting to existing ones to form an extensive fracture network. This transformation creates high-speed channels for gas migration, thereby significantly improving gas extraction efficiency.44 The effectiveness of coal seam transformation is directly influenced by the size of the action range.

Unlike the single-hole CO2–Frac, the dual-hole CO2–Frac does not merely combine the effects of two single-hole operations. Its synergistic superimposed effect far exceeds that of single-hole fracturing. Similar to explosive blasting, CO2–Frac leverages free weak planes to enhance medium transformation. In the original coal body, single-hole CO2–Frac lacks free weak planes, resulting in limited fracture extension and low gas extraction efficiency. In contrast, dual-hole CO2–Frac creates pressure relief zones on both sides of the coal bodies, providing ample free weak planes for fracture extension and energy diffusion.

The fractures generated effectively cover the excavation roadway and its adjacent areas, forming large-scale fracture zones within the coal seam (Figure 12). These extensive fracture zones significantly improve the gas migration environment, greatly enhancing gas extraction efficiency. This principle underpins the effectiveness of CO2–Frac in improving gas extraction from outburst-prone coal seams.

Conclusions

To develop a gas control strategy suitable for coal roadway excavation in outburst-prone coal seams, numerical simulations were employed to compare and analyze the technical advantages of dual-hole CO2–Frac. Subsequently, an effective gas extraction test was conducted on the 15111 working face of the Pingshu Coal Mine, successfully addressing the issue of low gas extraction efficiency. The primary conclusions are as follows:(1)Comparative Results: The dual-hole CO2–Frac significantly enlarges the damage range, facilitating fracture formation and pressure release during coal roadway excavation. The fracture and pressure release area in the dual-hole CO2–Frac scheme is approximately 26.82 m2, which is 220% larger than that of the single-hole CO2–Frac scheme.(2)Improvement in Gas Extraction Efficiency: CO2–Frac markedly enhances gas extraction efficiency. The flow rate of a 100-m borehole increased from 0.026 to 0.216 m3/min, a 7.3-fold increase. Additionally, the standard gas extraction cycle was reduced from 20 to 6 days, a 68% decrease. The attenuation coefficient of gas extraction flow rate dropped from 0.087 to 0.009 d–1, a 90% reduction.(3)Synergistic Superposition Effect: The dual-hole CO2–Frac scheme creates a connected and expanded fracture system due to the synergistic superposition effect. This system provides high-speed channels for gas migration, thereby enhancing the efficiency of gas extraction.(4)Application in Safe Mining: The dual-hole CO2–Frac is an effective method for the safe mining of outburst-prone coal seams at the Pingshu Coal Mine. It offers a valuable reference for gas control in Coal Mines with similar geological conditions worldwide.(5)Future Applications: Given its ability to enhance permeability and promote gas extraction, CO2–Frac technology holds potential as a permeability enhancement technique in coalbed methane development, improving the production of coalbed methane wells.

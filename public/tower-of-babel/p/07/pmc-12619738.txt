Imatinib and blinatumomab successfully rescued a pediatric B-ALL patient with TERF2::PDGFRB fusion resistant to dasatinib and chemotherapy
Authors: Fanghua Ye, Leyuan Wang, Yujie Qian, Wenjun Deng, Yan Yu, Liangchun Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPDGFRB-rearranged acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL), an ABL-class Ph-like ALL subtype, typically exhibits chemotherapy resistance and poor prognosis. Precise diagnosis and therapies are crucial for improving outcomes. Here, we report a case of a 9-year-old male harboring a TERF2::PDGFRB fusion, exhibiting primary resistance to dasatinib, but achieving a remarkable response to imatinib. Subsequent combination therapy with blinatumomab induced sustained bone marrow remission. These findings highlight the variability in tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) sensitivity among PDGFRB-rearranged ALL cases, supporting early treatment switching upon poor response. Notably, the combination of blinatumomab and imatinib may be an effective treatment strategy for PDGFRB-rearranged ALL.Keywords: PDGFRB-rearranged ALL, TKIs, Blinatumomab, MRD.

Introduction

Philadelphia chromosome-like acute lymphoblastic leukemia (Ph-like ALL) is a novel subtype of B-ALL first reported in 2009, with a high rate of failure in conventional chemotherapy and relapse [1, 2]. Ph-like ALL genetic abnormalities are primarily categorized into ABL gene abnormalities (including rearrangements such as ABL1, ABL2, CSF1R, and PDGFRB) and JAK-STAT signaling pathway abnormalities (including rearrangements of CRLF2, JAK2, or EPOR, as well as SH2B3 deletions and IL7R insertions/deletions) [3].

PDGFRB fusions represent half of ABL-class Ph-like B-ALL [4]. This rearrangement results in the constitutive activation of PDGFRB, promoting phosphorylation of tyrosine kinase and activating downstream signaling pathways associated with various human diseases [5]. Pediatric PDGFRB-rearranged ALL makes up just 0.8% of cases, according to data from a Chinese multicenter study for ALL. Tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) and chemotherapy together can increase the incidence of complete remission and reduce the levels of minimal residual disease (MRD) [6]. However, PDGFRB-rearranged ALL remain intermediate/high-risk subgroups with a relatively poor prognosis [7, 8]. Currently, an increasing number of PDGFRB fusion partners have been identified in ALL, with the most frequently reported being ETV6::PDGFRB and EBF1::PDGFRB [9]. Telomeric repeat binding factor 2 (TERF2), a pleiotropic telomeric protein, is overexpressed in both leukemia cell lines and primary leukemic cells [10], but TERF2::PDGFRB fusion has been reported in only two adult B-ALL cases to date. Functional studies confirm its oncogenic potential in vitro and in vivo, and amenable to TKI inhibition [11]. Unlike BCR::ABL1 fusion ALL with established TKIs protocols, optimal TKIs selection for PDGFRB-rearranged cases remains unclear.

Several ongoing clinical trials are evaluating the role of TKI in combination with chemotherapy and/or immunotherapy in ABL-class Ph-like pediatric ALL (ALLTogether1, AALL2131/EsPhALL2022) [4]. Blinatumomab, a bispecific T-cell engager (BiTE) antibody, has demonstrated efficacy in combination with TKIs for both newly diagnosed and relapsed/refractory Ph + ALL in multiple clinical trials [12, 13] and recent studies suggest this agent may improve the outcome of pediatric/adolescent Ph- B-ALL even in the frontline setting [14]. Furthermore, blinatumomab combined with TKIs has already shown efficacy in treating chemo-refractory PDGFRB-rearranged B-ALL [15].

Here, we report a pediatric B-ALL patient with a TERF2::PDGFRB fusion who exhibited dasatinib resistance but imatinib sensitivity. Combination therapy with imatinib and blinatumomab induced sustained disease remission.

Case report

A 9-year-old boy with no prior history of myeloid neoplasms presented with a 1-month history of anorexia and pallor. Physical examination demonstrated significant pallor, hepatomegaly, and splenomegaly. Laboratory tests revealed leukocytosis (WBC 426.2 × 10⁹/L, 75% blasts), thrombocytopenia (Plt 49 × 10⁹/L), severe anemia (Hb 54 g/L), and normal eosinophil count. He underwent bone marrow examination for morphological, immunological, cytogenetic, and molecular analyses, including fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) and whole transcriptome sequencing (WTS). Morphological and immunological analysis revealed 90% B-lymphoblasts infiltration (Fig. 1A), with immunophenotype positive for CD10, CD19, CD20, CD22, cCD22, CD34, cCD79a, TdT, and HLA-DR, but negative for CD3, CD4, MPO, and TCR αβ/γδ (Fig. 1B). Bone marrow smear showed a normal eosinophil count and residual myeloid counterpart representation. Cytogenetic analysis demonstrated a normal karyotype (46, XY [10]). Cerebrospinal fluid was negative for leukemic involvement.

The patient was diagnosed with B-ALL and initiated on induction therapy (dexamethasone/prednisone, vincristine, doxorubicin, peg-asparaginase) with intrathecal prophylaxis (methotrexate, cytarabine, dexamethasone) according to Chinese Children’s Cancer Group study ALL-2020 (CCCG-ALL-2020, ChiCTR2000035264) protocol. Peripheral blood still showed 68% blasts on day 5 of treatment. FISH results were obtained on day 6, revealing PDGFRB rearrangement (95% positivity; Fig. 1C), but negative for KMT2A rearrangement, BCR::ABL1, ETV6::RUNX1, MYC, and MEF2D. Hence, dasatinib (80 mg/m², daily) was added to the induction regimen. At day 14, WTS identified a TERF2::PDGFRB Fusion gene, involving an in-frame Fusion of exon 9–23 of PDGFRB with exon 1–8 of the TERF2 gene (Fig. 1D), as well as IKZF1::chr7 fusions, that led to truncation of the IKZF1 gene (Fig. 1E). No additional pathogenic mutations were detected. Therefore, a Ph-like B-ALL (TERF2::PDGFRB) diagnosis was made. By day 19, multicolor flow cytometry (MFC) assessment revealed MRD in bone marrow remained as high as 93.53%. Doxorubicin was administered once on day 22 as an additional chemotherapy agent. Repeat assessment on day 31 showed persistent disease (MFC-MRD 74.56%), indicating chemotherapy and dasatinib resistance. Following multidisciplinary expert review, imatinib (320 mg/m², daily) was initiated, achieving significant MFC-MRD reduction (9.71%) by day 41. Figure 2 details the patient’s treatment history.

During induction therapy, the patient developed multiple complications, including hypoalbuminemia, sepsis, pulmonary infection, and superficial venous thrombosis. Due to high treatment risks from persistent disease and comorbidities, intensive chemotherapy was discontinued. Considering the potential imatinib resistance and BCP-ALL phenotype, the patient subsequently received blinatumomab, achieving complete remission (MFC-MRD < 0.01%) by day 62. Hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) in first complete remission (CR1) was planned since it remains the only curative option for high-risk Ph-like ALL. However, after detailed counseling, the family declined HSCT due to concerns about transplant-related mortality and graft-versus-host disease (GVHD). A second cycle of blinatumomab with consolidation chemotherapy and imatinib maintained MFC-MRD negativity (< 0.01%) at day 90, with digital PCR confirming the absence of both TERF2::PDGFRB and IKZF1::chr7 fusion transcripts. Currently, the patient remains in sustained remission on maintenance imatinib and sequential chemotherapy.

Discussion

This case report highlights the therapeutic challenges and evolving strategies in the management of PDGFRB-rearranged B-ALL, particularly in pediatric patients. Our patient, a 9-year-old boy with a novel TERF2::PDGFRB fusion, exhibited primary resistance to dasatinib but achieved a remarkable response to imatinib, followed by sustained remission with the combination of imatinib and blinatumomab. This experience offers critical insights into TKI selection and the potential role of immunotherapy in PDGFRB-rearranged B-ALL.

PDGFRB-rearranged ALL is rare, particularly in children. Myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with eosinophilia and tyrosine kinase gene fusions (M/LN-eo-TK, ICC 2022) in blast phase may mimic Ph-like ALL, necessitating differential diagnosis. Unlike PDGFRB-rearranged myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms, which often show marked peripheral eosinophilia and concomitant myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm feature in the bone marrow, PDGFRB-fused ALL typically presents with normal eosinophil counts and normal myeloid counterpart representation, as in this patient [16]. Furthermore, conventional FISH or RNA sequencing exhibits rearrangement lineage restriction, even if more recently sorted FISH or single-cell RNA sequencing has emerged as a superior diagnostic alternative [17, 18].

This represents the first reported pediatric case of B-ALL harboring a TERF2::PDGFRB fusion gene. Two previously described adult cases demonstrated rapid blast reduction following dasatinib (100 mg daily) in combination with chemotherapy [11]. Our findings show the variability in TKI responsiveness among PDGFRB-rearranged ALL cases. ABL class-rearranged Ph-like ALL usually exhibits sensitivity to dasatinib [19], but prior kinase selectivity assays have demonstrated that dasatinib shows lower binding affinity for PDGFRB compared to BCR::ABL1 or ABL1 rearrangement, thus suggesting that higher doses (> 80 mg/m² QD) may be required to achieve comparable inhibition in PDGFRB-rearranged ALL [20]. Furthermore, a recent in vitro experimental study revealed that pediatric ALL samples with ABL class fusions were relatively sensitive to imatinib, while PDGFRB-rearranged ALL samples were less sensitive to dasatinib and bosutinib [21]. However, standard-dose dasatinib (80 mg/m², daily) has already demonstrated clinical efficacy in pediatric patients with PDGFRB-rearranged ALL, suggesting that dosage alone cannot explain the observed treatment resistance [6]. The TERF2::PDGFRB Fusion harbors an exon 9 breakpoint, distinct from the exon 11 breakpoint in dasatinib-sensitive EBF1::PDGFRB cases [15]. Nonetheless, functional assays revealed that dasatinib exhibited greater potency against TERF2::PDGFRB than EBF1::PDGFRB (IC50: 19.42 nM vs. 32.9 nM in Ba/F3 cells) [11, 21], indicating that neither breakpoint location nor fusion partner fully accounts for the observed resistance. While PDGFRB point mutations and co-occurring kinase alterations are established mechanisms of ABL TKI resistance [22], no such mutations were detected here. Instead, we identified an IKZF1::chr7 fusion, which result in the truncation of the IKZF1 gene, leading to loss of all protein domains. IKZF1 aberrations drive therapeutic resistance in high-risk BCP-ALL subtypes (including Ph + and Ph-like ALL) through diverse mechanisms [23], though the specific role of IKZF1::chr7 in leukemogenesis and TKI response remains uncharacterized. This underscores the need for comprehensive genomic profiling in Ph-like ALL. To our knowledge, this represents the first reported case of PDGFRB-rearranged ALL where imatinib achieved salvage after dasatinib failure, offering insights for TKI rechallenge strategies.

Prior studies on this ALL subtype primarily focused on imatinib plus chemotherapy or HSCT, while blinatumomab was mainly used for relapsed/refractory B-ALL [6, 24, 25]. Persistent MRD and chemotherapy-related toxicities necessitated an alternative strategy. Our case combining imatinib with blinatumomab achieved complete remission with transcript clearance, demonstrating this chemotherapy-reduced approach can induce deep molecular responses. The synergy likely stems from TKI-induced leukemic burden reduction, which enhances blinatumomab efficacy by preventing antigen escape. Additionally, blinatumomab’s T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity may eliminate TKIs-resistant clones through immunologic control of residual disease. Notably, the patient’s IKZF1 alteration (a marker of poor prognosis in Ph-like ALL) further underscores the need for innovative strategies to overcome intrinsic resistance. In Ph + ALL, the combination of TKIs with blinatumomab is emerging as a potential challenger to traditional HSCT approaches [26]. However, Ph-like ALL exhibits profound biological heterogeneity, and current evidence remains limited to small-scale studies with short follow-up durations. Consequently, the feasibility of achieving sustained complete molecular remission (CMR) without transplantation remains unestablished [4]. Current guidelines strongly recommend HSCT during CR1 for Ph-like ALL, particularly when additional high-risk genetic abnormalities (such as IKZF1 deletions) are present [27–29]. HSCT is definitely mandatory in R/R patients beyond CR2. Respecting the family’s decision to declined HSCT, we initiated an MRD-guided regimen combining blinatumomab, TKI and consolidation chemotherapy. This approach includes rigorous monitoring and predefined salvage therapies (CAR-T, HSCT upon relapse) to ensure optimal outcomes.

In conclusion, in clinical practice, rapid molecular profiling is critical for timely identification of this disease subtype and guiding precision treatment. We recommend systematic TKI sensitivity testing (including imatinib, dasatinib, and other agents) for patients with PDGFRB rearrangements, with early therapeutic switching upon suboptimal response. Although TKI-blinatumomab combinations can achieve CMR in Ph-like ALL patients, sustained molecular monitoring remains essential to evaluate treatment durability. Larger prospective clinical trials are warranted to validate the long-term efficacy of this approach. Until such evidence is available, HSCT should still be considered for high-risk patients, particularly those with additional poor prognostic markers.

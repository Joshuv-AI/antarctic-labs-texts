Leveraging RegNet and CBAM for precise detection of honey adulteration using thermal image analysis
Authors: Boulbarj Ilias, Bouklouze Abdelaziz, En-Najy Anas, Samira Douzi, Hassan Douzi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractHoney adulteration poses a huge challenge with considerable health and economic consequences, underscoring the necessity for effective and precise quality evaluation techniques. This research introduces a novel approach for classifying levels of honey adulteration through thermal imaging and Artificial Intelligence (AI). Traditional detection methods are frequently marked by protracted processing durations, elevated expenses, and restricted sensitivity. To mitigate these constraints, a dataset of thermal images was compiled from 15 pure honey samples and 69 adulterated samples including glucose syrup at amounts between 1% and 30%. An adaptable AI model was created to categorize various honey types, attaining elevated accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity across different levels of adulteration. The model achieved a precision and specificity of 100% for pure honey and 1% adulteration, demonstrating strong performance at higher adulteration levels (0.98 and 0.97 for 3% and 5% adulteration, respectively). This methodology offers significant benefits, such as swift identification and versatility across various honey varieties. The results indicate that the integration of thermal imaging and AI can improve quality control in the honey sector, providing a dependable method for verifying the authenticity and safety of natural bee products. This approach facilitates enhanced quality assurance methods and bolsters consumer confidence in honey products.Keywords: Honey adulteration, Convolution block attention module, Attention mechanism, Thermal imaging, Quality control, Human health, Honey authenticitySubject terms: Computer science, Nutrition

Introduction

Honey, also known as “liquid gold,” has been highly valued for centuries, not only for its exceptional flavor but also for its versatile advantages. This material contains vital vitamins, minerals, antioxidants, and enzymes that have beneficial impacts on human health. Honey contains a high concentration of naturally occurring sugars, specifically fructose and glucose, which act as easily accessible sources of energy for the human body. Moreover, this remarkable natural material exhibits therapeutic, antioxidant, antibacterial, and anti-inflammatory properties, which hold promise for treating many skin conditions and infections1.

Although highly regarded, the honey industry is currently experiencing a complex crisis caused by environmental pollution and the spread of diseases. This has resulted in an unstable situation where the supply and demand of honey are imbalanced, and sadly, has also led to instances of honey fraud. According to a recent field study, climate change has influenced the cognitive abilities of bees, specifically their learning and memory2. Bee populations have been adversely affected by environmental pollutants. Consequently, there has been a significant decline in the bee population, resulting in a substantial detriment to honey production. Simultaneously, the honey sector is confronted with the escalating spread of illnesses among bee colonies3. The combination of these two factors has resulted in a significant decrease in the availability of honey, which has failed to keep up with the increasing need for this organic sweetener. Consequently, the honey industry has created a fertile environment for deceptive activities, wherein authentic honey is adulterated with less expensive sweeteners or counterfeited using synthetic substances. Adulteration undermines both the quality and standards of the product, as well as jeopardizing consumer confidence and posing health risks.

Identifying honey adulteration is a complicated undertaking. In recent years, the focus of research has mostly been on instrumental analytical techniques, such as isotopic ratio analysis4, chromatography5, nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR), and other spectroscopic methods like vibrational spectrometry (NIR, MIR, and Raman)6–10. Modern MIR and NIR instruments, in particular, allow for rapid analysis by obtaining a fingerprint of the samples in a few seconds without any need for sample pre-treatment. However, despite these advances, these sophisticated devices can still be expensive, limiting their accessibility. Therefore, there is a need for more affordable and efficient analytical techniques to detect adulteration11.

Infrared thermography, or thermal imaging, is a method for observing and measuring the temperature of objects by detecting the infrared light they emit. This technology is very effective for examining surfaces or materials non-invasively, generating a “heat map” that displays temperature fluctuations. It can uncover or outline concealed attributes of goods, particularly gourmet items like honey. Thermographic cameras were initially employed as auxiliary instruments in medical applications12, firefighting support, and military activities as sensors13. Within the food industry, this technology has been utilized to identify irregular elements throughout the complete process of producing various items and to oversee the standard of fruits in the agricultural field14. Thermographic cameras are gaining popularity in food analysis due to their non-destructive examination of samples, user-friendly operation, and rapid findings.

This study utilized thermographic cameras to record infrared emissions from honey samples with differing glucose syrup contents, with the objective of establishing a non-invasive technique for detecting adulteration. The thermal images obtained depict the mean temperatures of the sampled regions, highlighting variations in the components of the honey. Prior studies have shown that additions, such as sugar syrups, can modify essential thermal properties of pure honey, including the glass transition temperature15,16. Implementing an attention mechanism is key for efficiently extracting relevant information from thermographic images. This method concentrates the study on critical areas while reducing the intricacy linked to handling extensive datasets. Attention Mechanism4 is a concept in deep learning that allows the model to focus on different parts of the input sequence when making predictions. The core idea is to give different “attention weights” to different parts of the input, so the model can selectively focus on the most relevant information for each step. Originally introduced in sequence-to-sequence models, attention has become fundamental for handling long dependencies in sequences by assigning varying levels of importance to different inputs. Models based on this mechanism have demonstrated their versatility by achieving exceptional results in various tasks in natural language processing and computer vision, including object recognition17, video processing18, image classification19, unsupervised object discovery20, unsupervised textual vision tasks21, segmentation22, and anomaly detection23.

This study seeks to create a deep learning model that integrates a scalable convolutional backbone network24,25 with an attention mechanism to examine thermographic images for detecting glucose syrup adulteration in honey. Thermographic imaging is very effective for this purpose, as it detects subtle temperature fluctuations that may indicate adulteration. The honey samples, sourced from various geographic regions and floral origins, offer a robust basis for evaluating the model’s performance under different settings.

Our approach incorporates a scalable backbone architecture alongside an attention module to enhance feature extraction. The backbone network adaptively modifies its complexity, allowing it to discern both essential patterns and intricate correlations in thermal pictures. The attention module further augments this process by implementing two distinct forms of attention:

Channel Attention emphasizes temperature-related attributes that aid in differentiating between genuine and contaminated honey samples by enhancing critical aspects linked to thermal variation.

The model is trained on a comprehensive collection of thermographic images featuring both genuine and counterfeit honey samples, enabling it to identify distinct patterns related to purity levels. Upon completion of training, the model may analyze new samples, serving as an accurate and dependable instrument for identifying adulteration.

This study enhances the utilization of deep learning and thermographic imaging in food quality assessment, providing a scientific basis for differentiating pure honey from counterfeit varieties. This methodology possesses considerable potential for practical applications in the food sector, enhancing the accuracy and efficacy of quality assurance methods.

The experimental design comprised the preparation of 84 honey samples, partitioned into 56 samples for the training set and 28 samples for the test set, with each sample weighing 5 g (see Table 3 for distribution specifics). Accurate measurements of these additions were obtained utilizing an analytical scale. To achieve uniform distribution, the glucose-honey solutions were thoroughly agitated until completely homogenous. Subsequent to preparation, the contaminated samples were incubated for 15 min at 60 °C to facilitate measurement. Thermal images were taken using an infrared camera oriented horizontally at a height of 25 cm above the samples, as illustrated in Fig. 2.

Thermal image acquisition

This work used a thermal camera to obtain images that illustrate the spatial distribution of thermal energy on the surface of heated honey by detecting its emitted thermal radiation. Fifteen-minute video recordings were produced to monitor and document the cooling process of each heated honey sample. The FLIR ONE PRO thermal camera runs at a frame rate of 8.7 Hz, within a spectral range of 8 to 14 μm, with an accuracy of 3%, a thermal resolution of 160 × 120 pixels, and a temperature detection range from 20 °C to 400 °C12. This portable device, which connects through a USB-C port, can be affixed to a smartphone. This study utilized different smartphones (Samsung S21 FE 5G28 and LG Velvet 5G29, with distinct configurations. The utilization of distinct devices aimed to improve the model’s predicted accuracy in identifying new samples in real-world applications.

Region of interest detection

The subsequent phase entails a procedure referred to as Region of Interest (ROI) Detection, which focuses on pinpointing essential areas within each image. This strategy prioritizes the precise identification and delineation of vital areas to preserve all key information while eliminating irrelevant noise and superfluous features31. Picture processing techniques, including edge detection and picture segmentation, are employed to accurately isolate these important regions. The process initiates with the use of a specific filter to improve the original image’s quality, succeeded by a pre-processing phase that transforms the image into grayscale. Enhancing image boundaries is essential for augmenting object visibility, hence enabling further analysis. The edge detection method discerns notable variations in light intensity throughout the image, consequently emphasizing outlines and object peripheries32. A contour map or mask is subsequently generated and superimposed onto the original image. This mask produces a revised image that carefully preserves essential information while excluding superfluous parts (refer to Fig. 5). This method guarantees that the analysis concentrates exclusively on the pertinent details critical for succeeding phases.

Images resizing

In order to maintain uniformity in the structure of the input data across the entire study, the image dimensions were standardized to 224 × 224 pixels. By employing this reduction technique, the capability of the classification algorithm to prioritize the most prominent features in the images could potentially be improved. This is achieved by eliminating any superfluous backgrounds or irrelevant details that have the potential to consume larger images.

Models’ development

Our model utilizes a dual-attention mechanism integrated with a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture to enhance feature extraction from thermal pictures, specifically focusing on areas suggestive of honey adulteration. This method employs attention mechanisms in two dimensions-channel and spatial-to improve the features extracted by the CNN. This architecture enables the model to more efficiently discern pertinent patterns, highlighting both “which” aspects to prioritize (channel attention) and “where” to concentrate within each image (spatial attention).

Convolutional neural network (CNN) backbone

Our model leverages a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture, serving as the primary feature extractor to identify essential patterns in thermal images, such as temperature distributions, textures, and structural details-key indicators of potential honey adulteration. To create an efficient CNN backbone, we implemented the Regulated Network (RegNet) architecture (Fig. 6), a state-of-the-art framework optimized for scalable and precise feature extraction, particularly suited for high-dimensional thermal imaging data33.

Convolutional block attention module (CBAM)

The Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) is a lightweight and effective attention mechanism that enhances feature representations in convolutional neural networks (CNNs) by focusing on informative regions in both spatial and channel dimensions. CBAM is composed of two sequential attention submodules: the channel attention module and the spatial attention module (See Fig. 7 (a)). First, the channel attention module operates on the feature map to refine the channels by emphasizing informative channels while suppressing less relevant ones. This is achieved by applying global average pooling and global max pooling across spatial dimensions (See Fig. 7), which capture different aspects of information, followed by two fully connected layers and a sigmoid activation function.

After channel attention, these refined features ’=⊗F are passed through the spatial attention module, which focuses on identifying important spatial locations within each channel. In this module, global average and max pooling are applied along the channel dimension to create two spatial descriptors that are then concatenated and convolved with a 7 × 7 convolutional layer to generate a spatial attention map ’=⊗F’. This map highlights essential spatial locations by assigning higher weights to informative areas, and it is element-wise multiplied with the channel-refined feature map to produce the final output (See Fig. 7 (c)). Through these dual-attention processes, CBAM effectively captures both ‘what’ and ‘where’ to focus on within the feature map, significantly improving the model’s ability to focus on relevant features while maintaining computational efficiency.

In our research, CBAM was chosen for its dual-attention mechanism, which functions on both spatial and channel dimensions—essential attributes for assessing thermal images of honey samples. CBAM augments the network’s ability to discern the most informative areas of an image by the successive application of channel and spatial attention. This approach allows the algorithm to identify nuanced patterns in temperature distributions and texture features that signify adulteration for honey quality assessment. The integration of CBAM enables the model to prioritize essential regions and features, thereby markedly improving the accuracy of honey adulteration detection by thermal imaging.

Flow of thermal image data through model components

The proposed approach (Fig. 9) utilizes thermal images of honey, which reflect temperature distribution differences linked to varying amounts of adulteration, as input with dimensions (224 × 224 × 3). Every input image is an RGB color image, characterized by three channels. The model’s initial layer, known as the “stem layer,” functions as the primary processing element within the RegNet backbone, executing an initial convolution to produce a feature map. This feature map encodes fundamental patterns, such as edges and textures, which constitute the essential components for advanced analysis in later phases.

Proposed model architecture.

The output from the stem layer is transmitted directly to the initial processing stage, bypassing the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM), hence facilitating the efficient management of low-level characteristics. The model design utilizes four blocks of convolutional layers, each succeeded by max pooling (CNN + MaxPooling). The convolutional blocks systematically extract progressively intricate hierarchical characteristics from the thermal images, augmenting the model’s comprehension of spatial and contextual information. At each phase, spatial dimensions are diminished, enabling the model to concentrate on the most pertinent features.

Subsequent to each CNN + MaxPooling block, the architecture incorporates the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM), which augments feature representation by implementing attention in both “channel” and “spatial” dimensions. This attention module utilized four times (CBAM 1, 2, 3, and 4), progressively enhances the feature maps to augment the model’s emphasis on essential details.

Subsequent to feature extraction, Global Average Pooling is implemented on each feature map, condensing it to a singular value by averaging over all spatial regions. This phase converts the 3D feature maps into a 1D vector, markedly decreasing data dimensionality and priming it for classification. The fully connected layer subsequently translates the 1D vector into output classes, denoting varying degrees of honey adulteration, from 0% to 30%.

Notwithstanding the model’s robust overall performance, certain flaws manifest, especially in its capacity to recognize mid-range adulteration levels (5% and 10%). The precision levels are diminished (0.57 and 0.72, respectively) and recall is also lower (0.63 and 0.68, respectively), resulting in decreased F1 scores (0.60 for 5% and 0.70 for 10%). A possible explanation for the model’s deficiencies is the narrow range of adulteration levels, especially between 5% and 10%, and between 10% and 20%. The overlapping temperature characteristics of honey samples within these proximate adulteration ranges may impair the model’s capacity to differentiate between them accurately. The percentages indicate intermediate adulteration levels, when variations in temperature distribution and texture patterns detected by thermal imaging are likely more modest, complicating classification.

The confusion matrix corroborates this problem, indicating that the majority of misclassifications transpire between these adjacent groups. For example, numerous 10% samples are inaccurately labeled as either 5% or 20%, and likewise, 5% samples are regularly misclassified as 3% or 10%. This overlap may also contribute to the noted declines in precision, recall, and F1 scores for these intermediate levels.

Another probable contributor to these constraints is the utilization of thermal pictures obtained from two devices with varying setups. Divergences in camera resolution, sensor sensitivity, and thermal calibration among the devices may result in data inconsistencies, complicating the model’s ability to generalize and properly distinguish samples with minor thermal variations. The inconsistencies may have especially influenced the mid-range adulteration levels, where the thermal characteristics of honey are inherently less prominent and more vulnerable to noise from device variability.

Furthermore, the high AUC values (Fig. 14) reported above in Fig. 13 indicate the robust discriminatory ability of the model across all categories, with only slight deficiencies in the middle classes (5%, 10%, and 20%), where nuanced and overlapping thermal features complicate accurate classification. The extreme classes, including the pure (0%) and substantially adulterated (30%), as well as the extremely low adulteration levels (1% and 3%), exhibit unique thermal patterns that likely create unambiguous decision boundaries, allowing the algorithm to classify these instances with near-perfect accuracy. Conversely, the mid-range adulteration levels exhibit slightly inferior AUC scores, suggesting some overlap in thermal characteristics, a prevalent challenge in classification tasks involving incremental variations. At these intermediate concentrations, the thermal properties may not vary sufficiently, leading to occasional ambiguities in the model’s classifications.

Interpretability and regional attention in honey adulteration detection using Grad-CAM + + Visualizations

Grad-CAM (Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping) is an effective method for visualizing and interpreting deep learning model predictions by emphasizing the most significant areas in an image that influence the model’s classification36,37. The procedure commences by transmitting an input image over the network to acquire a preliminary class score. The gradients of this score are subsequently calculated in relation to the final convolutional feature maps, which include spatial information crucial for classification. The gradients are backpropagated, enabling the model to allocate importance values to various spatial locations according to their influence on the final choice. The resultant Grad-CAM heatmap is generated by integrating the activation maps with the corresponding significance values, thereby localizing the areas that significantly contributed to the classification.

The Grad-CAM heatmaps presented above (Fig. 16) elucidate the decision-making process of the CBAM-enhanced RegNet model in identifying honey samples with varying degrees of adulteration. The heatmaps delineate the areas in each honey image that significantly impacted class determination, enabling us to analyze how the program differentiates between pure and contaminated samples based on thermal patterns.

Grad-CAM heatmaps of thermal images with varying adulteration levels.

For pure honey (Class 0), the heatmaps indicate equitable attention throughout core and peripheral regions, implying that the model discerns purity through uniform texture and thermal distribution. Conversely, at low adulteration levels (e.g., 1%), the model’s attention becomes dispersed, signifying sensitivity to minor variations in thermal properties. As adulteration escalates (e.g., 5% - Class 3), the heatmaps reveal concentrated areas of interest, indicating that the model identifies preliminary indicators of contamination via distinct thermal fluctuations.

At elevated adulteration levels (e.g., 20% - Class 5), the heatmaps exhibit a marked concentration of focus in specific regions, corresponding to the significant temperature variations induced by the addition of glucose. This signifies that the model depends on these thermal markers as primary indicators of adulteration. Notably, even within identical classes, various honey samples display subtly distinct attention areas, illustrating the model’s capacity to generalize across heterogeneous samples instead of only memorizing static patterns.

These findings validate that the CBAM-enhanced RegNet model, as a nonlinear deep learning architecture, proficiently captures hierarchical and discriminative features, rendering it superior to conventional feature extraction techniques such as PCA and hierarchical clustering. The Grad-CAM visualizations substantiate the model’s proficiency in detecting thermal anomalies associated with honey adulteration, offering a clear and interpretable framework for quality control applications within the food industry.

Comparative analysis of our approach and previous studies

Our evaluation underscores significant distinctions between our approach and prior studies on honey adulteration identification (Table 8). Izquierdo et al.13 employ thermal imaging with a CNN-based model; however, their dataset management raises issues regarding potential data leaking, as they fail to distinctly segregate training and testing sets. Their methodology entails utilizing a singular sample and subsequently partitioning the extracted images, which may lead to the presence of analogous images in both sets, possibly enhancing their reported accuracy of 0.93. Notwithstanding this, their model demonstrates a markedly inferior F1-score (0.63) and accuracy (0.48), indicating potential misclassification challenges.

Limitations

A significant limitation of this investigation is the absence of melissopalynological analysis to verify the botanical provenance of the unifloral honey samples. This method, regarded as the benchmark for floral source verification, proved impractical in our investigation due to insufficient specialist experience and equipment. To guarantee a satisfactory degree of authenticity, honey samples were obtained directly from cooperatives and beekeepers accredited by the National Office of Food Safety (ONSSA) in Morocco. These sources are recognized for generating unifloral honey varieties in areas with established floral predominance during harvest seasons. This method delivers a reliable indicator of botanical origin, but it does not substitute for the quantitative validation provided by melissopalynology. We recognize this as a limitation and suggest that future studies include melissopalynological testing to improve the validation of floral origin and for wider applicability of classification models.

Conclusion

Precisely distinguishing between genuine and contaminated honey is an essential component of apiculture. Current research introduces a novel model utilizing the attention mechanism to tackle this issue. This study analyzes five honey varieties: two thyme variants (Thyme-A and Thyme-B), two euphorbia variants (Euphorbia-A and Euphorbia-B), and one thistle variant, all of which are highly esteemed for their market demand. The program is engineered to identify adulteration levels between 0% and 30%, regardless of the honey’s origin or variety. The results indicate substantial advancement in detecting adulteration levels, enhancing the quality control and verification of bee-derived goods.

One of the strengths of this research is the utilization of thermal images from different models of smartphones, which enhances the robustness of the model and its real-life usability. The splitting of samples into training and testing datasets prior to image capture also guaranteed that no images from one sample were included in both datasets. This enhances reliability by reducing variability in image capture. The results show that the combination of thermal imaging with a deep learning platform yields a robust, versatile, and multidimensional honey quality verification tool.

Potential opportunities

Expanding the model’s applicability to encompass additional honey varieties and bee-derived goods, including royal jelly, is imperative. Subsequent investigations should evaluate the model’s effectiveness in real-world processing contexts, which frequently exhibit greater complexity and unpredictability than controlled tests. Furthermore, modifying the model to identify supplementary types of adulteration would enhance its applicability. Iterative enhancements to improve accuracy and reduce errors will further increase its worth for the apiculture sector.

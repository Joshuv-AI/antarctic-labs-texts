Paper-based colorimetric sensor for histamine detection using dopamine-functionalized, size-varied gold nanoparticles
Authors: Rolen Brian P. Rivera, Ryan M. Lumod, Romnick B. Unabia, Renzo Luis D. Reazo, Archie G. Ruda, Melbagrace A. Lapening, Jahor L. Omping, Amyzz P. Ceniza, Noel Lito B. Sayson, Felmer S. Latayada, Rey Y. Capangpangan, Gerard G. Dumancas, Roberto M. Malaluan, Arnold A. Lubguban, Gaudencio C. Petalcorin, Arnold C. Alguno
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Histamine, a prominent biogenic amine (BA), is commonly associated with allergic reactions and is a key culprit in foodborne illnesses stemming from spoiled food consumption. The detection of histamine is paramount for meeting food safety standards and ensuring quality control. In this study, an innovative method employing the direct integration of dopamine onto citrate-reduced gold nanoparticles (DCt-AuNPs) embedded within filter paper to create a paper-based colorimetric sensor for histamine detection was developed. Various sizes of DCt-AuNPs (13, 15, 27, and 39 nm) were synthesized, and their dimensions were controlled by adjusting the precursor molar ratio (MR). This adjustment led to size variations, influencing the localized surface plasmon resonance (LSPR) peaks (518, 520, 526, and 530 nm) and resulting in distinct optical properties. The interaction between these nanoparticles and histamine concentrations (ranging from 1 ppm to 100 ppm) was monitored by observing changes in the LSPR absorbance spectra and color. Histamine induced DCt-AuNP aggregation through interactions of its amino and imidazole groups via ligand exchange and interparticle crosslinking, thereby changing the solution color from red to blue. The size variance of DCt-AuNPs significantly impacted the colorimetric response to histamine. Among the sizes tested, the 15 nm DCt-AuNP paper sensor exhibited the lowest detection limit of 2.38 µM and a linear detection range of 20–70 ppm. Remarkably, this sensor boasted rapid detection, clocking in under 1 min, coupled with exceptional selectivity toward histamine analytes, highlighting its potential for real-world applications.

Introduction

Ensuring food freshness, particularly for meat, is a critical concern for both consumers and the food industry. Meat is highly perishable, with spoilage typically occurring within 4–10 days post-slaughter1–3. Among the key indicators of meat spoilage are biogenic amines (BAs), which are toxic compounds formed during the breakdown of proteins through processes such as proteolysis and bacterial decarboxylation of amino acids4,5. Histamine, one of the most significant BAs, is of particular concern because of its toxic effects on human health and its utility as a freshness indicator. When consumed in excess, histamine can lead to toxic effects, including dilation and increased permeability of blood vessels, resulting in symptoms such as hives, itching, tingling, burning, headaches, hypotension, tachycardia, airway constriction, and hypoxemia. Severe exposure may further cause gastrointestinal distress, manifesting as diarrhea, vomiting, and abdominal pain6–8. As such, accurate detection of histamine is essential for food safety monitoring and quality control, making it a critical component of efforts to ensure the freshness and safety of meat products7,9–11.

Various methods have been developed over the years to quantify BAs such as histamines in food. These methods include spectrofluorometric analysis12, high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC)8,13, capillary electrophoresis (CE)14,15, gas chromatography‒mass spectrometry (GC‒MS)16, thin-layer chromatography (TLC)17, and enzyme-linked assays18–20. Although these techniques provide high sensitivity and precision, they often involve lengthy procedures, require trained professionals, and necessitate high-quality reagents, making them costly and time intensive. This highlights the demand for innovative methods that offer rapid, sensitive, and cost-effective detection of histamine, addressing these limitations.

Colorimetric sensors using metal nanoparticles such as gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) offer a rapid, on-site histamine detection method by producing visible color changes due to nanoparticle aggregation altering localized surface plasmon resonance (LSPR)21–27. AuNPs are widely used in biosensing because of their biocompatibility, tunable LSPR properties, and ease of functionalization. Related studies on biogenic amine detection using bare AuNPs revealed a strong cross-reactive response with various biogenic amines, including spermine, spermidine, tryptamine, histamine, and melamine21,28–31. However, bare AuNPs often suffer from cross-reactivity and instability, limiting their practical application. The functionalization of AuNPs with specific ligands enhances their selectivity and stability, improving their utility in histamine detection and broader sensing applications23,27,32–34. Recent findings have shown that dopamine-functionalized AuNPs provide stable, selective, and rapid colorimetric histamine detection due to the presence of amine (-NH2) and catechol groups (C6H6O2), which impart antifouling properties through covalent backfilling26,33. This functionalization prevents aggregation and facilitates quick polymerization in the presence of organic amines35,36. Although research on AuNPs for histamine detection has focused primarily on colloidal AuNPs, these systems face limitations regarding portability and sustainability compared with paper-based sensing approaches. Studies on paper-based sensing platforms for histamine detection have primarily explored electrochemical37–39 and photoluminescence40 methods, which face challenges such as fabrication complexity, power dependency, and limited long-term stability. To address this gap, some progress has been made in developing colorimetric paper-based sensors for histamine detection41–43. However, no studies have specifically reported the use of AuNP-based paper sensors for the colorimetric detection of histamine.

To our knowledge, this study is the first in the world to develop a paper-based colorimetric sensor for detecting histamine, a critical marker of foodborne illness, using dopamine-functionalized citrate-reduced gold nanoparticles (DCt-AuNPs) directly deposited onto glass microfiber filter paper. Unlike solution-phase systems reported in prior studies, our work introduces a solid-state sensing platform where integration onto the paper matrix induces a distinct mechanistic shift, including interfacial aggregation and broadened, red-shifted LSPR peaks, enabling a new optical transduction mode not observed in colloidal systems. Furthermore, the size of the DCt-AuNPs was varied and optimized, as the AuNP size significantly influences colorimetric sensing25. The developed paper sensor was tested with standard histamine solutions, with additional tests conducted to assess its stability and selectivity against various compounds. This technology offers a promising, portable, label-free, simple, and rapid approach for histamine detection, making it a novel, field-ready diagnostic tool for onsite food monitoring and other practical applications.

Experimental section

Materials. Gold (III) chloride hydrate (HAuCl4) (99.995% trace metal basis), trisodium citrate (Na3C6H5O) (ACS reagent, ≥ 99.0%), dopamine hydrochloride (H8502) and histamine (analytical standard, 97%) were purchased from Sigma‒Aldrich. All procedures utilized ultrapure water (18.2 mΩ, Direct-Q, Millipore SAS).

Synthesis of dopamine-functionalized citrate-capped AuNPs

Citrate-capped gold nanoparticles (Ct-AuNPs) were synthesized through a controlled chemical reduction method25,26,44. Initially, a 50 mL solution of 0.5 mM HAuCl₄ was prepared in a flask. Simultaneously, a 34.0 mM (1.0 wt%) Na3C6H5O solution was prepared in a separate container. The HAuCl₄ solution was heated to 95 °C in a water bath while being vigorously stirred. Upon reaching the desired temperature, a specific volume of the Na3C6H5O solution was rapidly added to the HAuCl₄ solution. This addition induced a color change from yellow to wine red, indicating the formation of gold nanoparticles. The molar ratio (MR) of Na3C6H5O to HAuCl₄ was systematically varied (1.5, 2.0, 2.5, and 3.0) to control the particle size as the primary synthesis parameter. To ensure that the reaction reached completion, the mixture was held at 95 °C for an additional 15 min. The solutions were subsequently allowed to cool naturally to room temperature.

Following the cooling process, 50 mL of the prepared Ct-AuNP solution was stirred at 360 rpm. To this solution, 0.250 mL of a 1 mM dopamine hydrochloride solution was added to synthesize DCt-AuNPs. The mixture was stirred continuously for 2 h at room temperature. The resulting solution was stored at 4 °C for further use.

Fabrication of the DCt-AuNP paper-based sensor

A glass microfiber filter paper (GMFP), Whatman GF/C, with dimensions of 1.5 cm × 1.0 cm (cut from standard 42.5 mm diameter sheets), was used as the substrate for depositing the DCt-AuNP solution. The GMFP was immersed in the DCt-AuNP solution for 4 h, then placed in a vacuum-sealed polypropylene plastic container and stored at 4 °C for at least 24 h to ensure stability before testing.

Colorimetric test with histamine analyte

The analyte solutions were prepared by dissolving precise amounts of histamine precursor in ultrapure water to achieve concentrations ranging from 1 ppm to 100 ppm. The fabricated paper-based sensors were immersed in 400 µL aliquots of the analyte solutions, allowing the reaction to proceed for 30 s to 1 min for colorimetric testing. Following immersion, the paper sensors were removed, photographed, and imaged using a CR-400 chromameter (Konika Minolta, Japan) for all colorimetric images. For each measurement, the device was auto-calibrated with a white reference plate, ensuring accurate capture of the CIELAB color values. These high‑resolution chromameter images were then used to document and compare the sensor’s visual response across the tested histamine concentration range. Additionally, the tested sensors were analyzed via a LAMBDA 365 + UV‒Vis spectrometer (Perkin Elmer, USA) to measure the absorbance spectra and detect shifts in the LSPR peak of the DCt-AuNPs upon analyte interaction. The absorbance ratio (A650/A520) were measured to construct the calibration curve for limit of detection calculation. All measurements were conducted in triplicate.

Selectivity test

Selectivity analyses were performed to assess the colorimetric responses of DCt-AuNP paper-based sensors to various analytes, including organic solvents, weak acids, and biological substances containing amino groups such as putrescine, cadaverine, inosine, and histamine. The fabricated paper-based sensors were immersed in 400 µL aliquots of 100 ppm solutions of each analyte for 1 min. Following the reaction period, the sensors where imaged immediately under identical lighting conditions and a LAMBDA 365 + UV‒Vis spectrometer (Perkin Elmer, USA) was used to observe the absorbance spectra and monitor shifts in the LSPR peak. The color and absorbance spectra where used to evaluate selectivity.

Time-dependent stability test

The time-dependent stability of DCt-AuNP paper-based sensors was evaluated by vacuum‑sealing strips in airtight plastic pouches and storing them at 4 °C for 30 days. The color of the sensors at 0 days and 30 days were compared by photographing and imaging using CR-400 chromameter (Konika Minolta, Japan). Furthermore, a LAMBDA 365 + UV‒Vis spectrometer (Perkin Elmer, USA) was used to analyze the sensors at 0 days and 30 days to track changes in the LSPR peak intensity and wavelength, serving as key indicators of stability.

Measurement and characterization

The particle size and morphology of the DCt-AuNP solutions were characterized via transmission electron microscopy (TEM) with a JEM 2100 Plus LaB6 instrument (JEOL, Japan). The hydrodynamic diameter and size distribution of the colloidal solutions were analyzed through dynamic light scattering (DLS) via a Nanotrac Wave II Analyzer (Microtrac, Inc., Pennsylvania, USA). The absorbance spectra of DCt-AuNP solutions, paper-based sensors, and DCt-AuNP paper-based sensors in the presence of histamine were recorded at room temperature via a LAMBDA 365 + UV‒Vis spectrometer (Perkin Elmer, USA). Additionally, Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectra were obtained with a Shimadzu IR-TRACER100 spectrometer (Shimadzu, Japan) over a spectral range of 4000 cm⁻¹ to 400 cm⁻¹, with 60 scans performed at room temperature, to identify characteristic bonds of DCt-AuNPs and the presence of histamine.

Results and discussion

Characterization of colloidal DCt-AuNPs

This study developed a colorimetric detection system by directly functionalizing citrate-reduced AuNPs with dopamine (DCt-AuNPs). Spherical DCt-AuNPs of varying sizes were synthesized and characterized for morphology, size distribution, and particle size via transmission electron microscopy (TEM) (Fig. 1). The TEM images revealed that different Na3C6H5O-to-HAuCl4 molar ratios (1.5, 2.0, 2.5, and 3.0) yielded distinct average particle diameters of 38.99 ± 8.41 nm, 26.53 ± 6.13 nm, 15.20 ± 1.30 nm, and 12.65 ± 1.32 nm, respectively. These results indicate that increasing the Na3C6H5O-to-HAuCl4 molar ratio leads to a reduction in the average DCt-AuNP size. The DLS measurements shown in Fig. 2 further confirm that the AuNPs synthesized with molar ratios of 2.0, 2.5, and 3.0 exhibit relatively uniform size distributions with narrow peaks, whereas a molar ratio of 1.5 results in a larger hydrodynamic diameter and broader, less uniform distribution, indicating potential polydispersity. These findings suggest a decrease in hydrodynamic size with increasing molar ratio.

Furthermore, the successful synthesis of AuNPs was evidenced by a visible color change in the precursor solution from yellow to wine red, accompanied by the emergence of a strong LSPR peak at 518–530 nm in the UV‒vis spectra (Fig. 3). This absorption profile is characteristic of monodispersed, spherical AuNPs45–47. Additionally, a redshift in the LSPR peak position, observed with increasing particle size, suggests the influence of electromagnetic coupling alterations, geometric factors, and plasmon damping48,49. Collectively, these factors affect the resonance frequency of plasmon oscillations within the nanoparticles, influencing the perceived color and resulting in subtle hue variations, as shown in the inset of Fig. 3.

The FTIR spectra of DCt-AuNPs (Fig. 4) revealed that, following dopamine functionalization, peaks at approximately 1576 cm⁻¹ and 1704 cm⁻¹ shifted to higher wavenumbers at 1590 cm⁻¹ and 1710 cm⁻¹, respectively, confirming that amine bending was associated with dopamine functionalization and the attachment of -NH₂ groups onto the AuNP surface. The shouldering between 1710 cm⁻¹ and 1590 cm⁻¹ indicates the aromatic C = C stretch of the benzene functional group of dopamine. Additionally, shifts in peaks from 1241 cm⁻¹ to 1249 cm⁻¹ and from 1390 cm⁻¹ to 1400 cm⁻¹, corresponding to C-N stretching and C-H bending vibrations, respectively, as well as the emergence of a 1105 cm⁻¹ peak indicative of C-O-C/C-C stretching vibrations, further support successful dopamine functionalization on AuNPs. The persistence of a ketonic carbon‒oxygen bond at 1710 cm⁻¹ post functionalization suggests the copresence of dopamine and dicarboxy acetone capping the AuNP surface. Furthermore, the peaks shift at 2910 cm⁻¹ to 2919 cm⁻¹ is attributed to C–H stretching vibrations of aliphatic chains in citrate and dopamine, while the significant broadening at 3500–3200 cm⁻¹ band corresponds to O–H stretching from both citrate hydroxyls and dopamine’s phenolic groups, and the N-H stretching of dopamine’s amine group, confirming ligand coordination to AuNP surfaces26.

DCt-AuNP Paper-based sensor fabrication

The synthesized DCt-AuNPs of various sizes were effectively deposited onto a glass microfiber filter paper (GMFP) substrate via direct immersion. This successful deposition was verified through UV‒Vis absorbance spectra of the paper-based sensor, as shown in Fig. 5, which displays characteristic LSPR peaks. While the plain filter paper exhibited no LSPR peak, the DCt-AuNP-deposited paper showed a distinct LSPR peak at wavelengths ranging from 522 nm to 531 nm, confirming the presence of DCt-AuNPs. The red coloration of the paper sensor, as demonstrated by the chromameter and actual images in the inset of Fig. 5, further confirmed successful deposition. The figure also shows that increasing the particle size resulted in a redshift of the LSPR peaks of the gold nanoparticles, which is consistent with the behavior observed in their colloidal form. However, compared with the as-prepared colloidal solution shown in Fig. 3, the DCt-AuNP paper sensor exhibited broadening and shifting of the absorbance peak. This phenomenon is likely due to changes at the nanoparticle interface resulting from their integration into the GMFP matrix and LSPR coupling between closely immobilized nanoparticles. Similar results attributed to these effects have been reported by other groups50–53.

The fabricated paper-based colorimetric sensor, comprising various sizes of DCt-AuNPs deposited onto GMFP, was evaluated for histamine detection over a broad concentration range from 1 ppm to 100 ppm. Figure 6 presents images, absorbance spectra, and absorbance ratio plots for sensors with DCt-AuNPs of 13 nm (Fig. 6A), 15 nm (Fig. 6B), 27 nm (Fig. 6C), and 39 nm (Fig. 6D). The data indicate that smaller-sized nanoparticles, specifically those ranging from 13 nm to 15 nm, exhibit observable colorimetric changes from red to blue starting at a histamine concentration of 30 ppm. In contrast, larger DCt-AuNPs, ranging from 27 nm to 39 nm, demonstrate no visible colorimetric response at histamine concentrations less than 100 ppm. This size-dependent color change arises from variations in light absorption and nanoparticle aggregation, with smaller AuNPs being more sensitive to histamine interactions because of their higher surface-area-to-volume ratio and different aggregation behavior25,54,55.

The calibration curve presented in Fig. 7 enabled the determination of the limit of detection (LOD) for the fabricated sensors. The 13 nm DCt-AuNPs (Fig. 7A) exhibited an LOD of 2.66 µM within a linear detection range of 20 ppm to 100 ppm, whereas the 15 nm (Fig. 7B) DCt-AuNPs displayed a slightly lower LOD of 2.38 µM over a linear range of 20 ppm to 70 ppm. Notably, these detection limits fall well below the maximum allowable histamine concentrations set by international food safety authorities. For instance, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) considers histamine levels above 35 ppm indicative of spoilage, with 200 ppm representing a potential health hazard56. Similarly, other countries’ legal limits for histamine typically range from 50 ppm to 400 ppm57. The low detection thresholds achieved by these sensor thus highlight its excellent sensitivity and strong potential for real-world application in monitoring histamine levels in food products.

Figure 8 illustrates the proposed detection mechanism using the paper-based sensor. The stability of the DCt-AuNPs on the filter paper is due primarily to the stabilizing properties of dopamine. The imidazole ring of histamine coordinates with gold atoms on the nanoparticle surface, displacing dicarboxy acetone through ligand exchange26. This adsorption alters the surface chemistry of the AuNPs, leading to shifts in their LSPR29. Additionally, excess histamine molecules in solution can promote an aggregation mechanism through interparticle crosslinking. Histamine, which acts as a bidentate linker, interacts with neighboring DCt-AuNPs via its aliphatic amino groups and imidazole rings, facilitating particle aggregation. The unique coloration of the AuNP solution arises from the collective oscillations of surface electrons, which are highly sensitive to changes in interparticle distance58,59. Consequently, interaction with histamine induces a detectable color change in the AuNP sensor, reflecting aggregation and altered LSPR properties.

To investigate the sensor selectivity, the 15 nm DCt-AuNP paper-based sensor was tested against a range of analytes relevant to food spoilage, including organic solvents, weak acids, biogenic amines, and several commonly encountered laboratory inorganic compounds. Figure 9 displays the actual images, chromameter measurements, and UV‒Vis spectra that resulted from interactions with cadmium chloride (CdCl2), copper sulfate (CuSO4), mercury chloride (HgCl2), zinc acetate (ZnAc), ethanol, methanol, ammonia, uric acid, inosine, putrescine, cadaverine, and histamine. UV‒Vis analysis revealed no significant changes in the absorbance spectra of the AuNP sensor when exposed to 100 ppm of most analytes, except for biogenic amines such as putrescine, cadaverine, and histamine. Among these, histamine elicited the strongest response, as shown in Fig. 9, with a distinct peak at 650 nm and a notable colorimetric shift from red to blue, demonstrating greater sensitivity. This behavior suggests that higher concentrations of other analytes are necessary to induce a spectral shift, emphasizing the sensor’s selectivity for biogenic amines, particularly histamine, at concentrations below 100 ppm.

To assess the colloidal stability of the fabricated DCt-AuNP paper-based sensor, a time-dependent evaluation was conducted by monitoring changes in its color and absorbance spectra over a one-month period. Figure 10 shows the UV‒Vis spectra of the DCt-AuNP paper-based sensor, which was vacuum-sealed in a plastic pouch and stored at 4 °C for 30 days. The data revealed no significant shifts or changes in the LSPR peak of the paper sensor over this period, indicating that the nanoparticles remained well dispersed and stable. Additionally, the red coloration of the sensor persisted without noticeable changes, further confirming its stability over extended storage. Therefore, the DCt-AuNP paper-based sensor has strong potential for long-term storage and usability.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this work successfully developed a rapid, stable, sensitive, and selective paper-based colorimetric sensor for histamine detection featuring dopamine-functionalized citrate-reduced gold nanoparticles (DCt-AuNPs) deposited on glass microfiber filter paper (GMFP). Through optimization, the sensor was engineered with varying DCt-AuNP sizes deposited onto glass microfiber filter paper (GMFP), which exhibited notable sensitivity and selectivity. The sensor demonstrated optimized performance with 15-nm DCt-AuNPs, achieving a low limit of detection (LOD) of 2.38 µM and a linear detection range for histamine concentrations between 20 ppm and 70 ppm, with a strong correlation coefficient of 0.98. The sensor offers a straightforward and rapid detection procedure, displaying high sensitivity and exceptional selectivity for histamine, with a response time of less than one minute. This makes the sensor highly suitable for practical applications in food spoilage detection, providing portability, accuracy, and reliability. However, it is important to note that quantitative determination of the histamine concentration beyond the specified range remains outside the scope of this study. This limitation underscores the need for further research to expand the quantitative capabilities of the sensor for broader application potential.

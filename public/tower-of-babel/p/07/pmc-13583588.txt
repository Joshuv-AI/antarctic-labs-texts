Prevalence and Clinical Outcomes of Subclinical Atrial Fibrillation After Heart Failure Hospitalization
Authors: Jorge A Wong, Priya Mistry, Ameen Patel, Umjeet Jolly, Alex Benz, Fei Yuan, Natalia Pinilla-Echeverri, Ghaith Almidani, Nicole Latendresse, Alex Grinvalds, Elorm Vowotor, Jerry Chen, Alex Grindal, Sabina Rajkumar, Ali Almhri, Shrikant Bangdiwala, William F McIntyre, David Conen, PJ Devereaux, Jeff S Healey, Stuart J Connolly
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSubclinical atrial fibrillation (SCAF) is common in older adults, but its clinical significance in heart failure (HF) patients remains unclear. We evaluated the prevalence of SCAF in patients hospitalized with HF without prior atrial fibrillation (AF) and examined its association with HF-related outcomes.MethodsHospitalized patients with a primary diagnosis of HF exacerbation without prior AF were prospectively recruited across 5 sites in Ontario, Canada. Participants were discharged home with 28-day continuous electrocardiogram monitoring to detect SCAF. The association between SCAF and HF readmission, cardiovascular death, and other HF-related outcomes at 1-year follow-up was determined using logistic regression models.ResultsAmong 242 participants, the mean age was 70.9 ± 13.3 years; 51% were male; the mean body mass index was 31.6 ± 10.8 kg/m2; and the mean left ventricular ejection fraction was 42.3% ± 17.1%. SCAF was detected in 17 patients (7.1%), comprising 31 episodes with a median duration of 6.3 hours (interquartile range: 0.21-32.5). SCAF was associated with cardiovascular death (6 vs 28 events; adjusted odds ratio (aOR) 4.22 [95% confidence interval {CI}, 1.34-12.8]; P = 0.014) and progression to clinical AF (5 vs 14 events; aOR 8.26, [95% CI, 2.33-28.2]; P = 0.001), whereas a significant association was not observed with HF rehospitalization (4 vs 54 events; aOR 1.12 [95% CI, 0.32-3.31]; P = 0.85).ConclusionsIn patients hospitalized with HF and no prior AF, SCAF was detected in approximately 1 in 14 individuals early after discharge and was associated with higher cardiovascular mortality and progression to clinical AF. No association with HF rehospitalization was observed, although the study may have been underpowered for this outcome.Clinical Trial RegistrationNCT03541616Keywords: subclinical atrial fibrillation, congestive heart failure, prevalence, clinical outcomes, heart failure–related outcomes

Subclinical atrial fibrillation (SCAF) refers to short-lasting episodes of asymptomatic atrial fibrillation (AF) that can be detected only with long-term, continuous heart-rhythm monitoring.1 SCAF is common, with a prevalence of 30%-40% in older adults with a cardiac implanted pacemaker, defibrillator, or implanted loop recorder.1, 2, 3, 4 SCAF has been associated with an increased risk of adverse outcomes, including stroke, heart failure (HF), and death.1,5, 6, 7 Oral anticoagulation of SCAF in higher-risk individuals has been demonstrated recently to reduce stroke, suggesting that therapies targeting SCAF may lead to improved clinical outcomes.8

AF and HF frequently coexist, yet the relationship between these 2 conditions remains incompletely understood.9 Clinically overt AF is a common comorbidity in patients with HF, coexisting in 30%-50% and leading to a 2- to 3-fold increase in mortality.10, 11, 12 AF also is a common cause of HF exacerbation, which is the leading cause of hospitalization in older adults.13, 14, 15 Whether these associations between clinical AF and HF extend to patients with SCAF remains unclear. In addition, the precipitating cause of HF exacerbation is unidentified in nearly half of patients hospitalized with decompensated HF.13,16 Given that SCAF is more common than clinically apparent AF, and, in many cases, the etiology of HF exacerbation is unexplained, we hypothesized that SCAF may be an important, unappreciated cause of HF exacerbation. We therefore sought to examine the prevalence of SCAF and its association with HF hospitalization and other HF-related outcomes in this population.

Methods

Study population

The present study is a prospective, observational cohort study (NCT03541616) of patients with no prior AF who were admitted to the hospital with a primary diagnosis of HF exacerbation. This study evaluated the prevalence of SCAF, as captured by 28-day continuous heart-rhythm monitoring placed at the time of hospital discharge, and examined the association between SCAF and both hospital readmission for HF and other HF-related adverse outcomes.

Consecutive patients admitted to the hospital with a primary diagnosis of acutely decompensated HF were enrolled. Patients with history of AF were excluded, including those who were diagnosed with AF during their index hospitalization. Other exclusion criteria included the following: presence of an implanted cardiac device with an atrial lead, as it can readily diagnose AF; cardiac surgery within the past 30 days; history of hypertrophic cardiomyopathy; and severely reduced renal function (estimated glomerular filtration rate < 15 mL/min per 1.73 m2), as volume overload due to kidney failure may be confused with HF.

All patients underwent 28-day continuous electrocardiogram (ECG) monitoring starting at the time of hospital discharge to identify SCAF. A total of 242 patients were prospectively enrolled across 5 hospitals in Southwestern Ontario, Canada in 2019-2021, and they were followed for 1 year. Patients provided written informed consent, and the study protocol was approved by ethics boards at each hospital.

Outcomes for analysis

The prevalence of SCAF lasting ≥ 30 seconds, as detected during 28-day continuous ECG monitoring, was the primary outcome. We assessed the association between SCAF and each of the following individual secondary outcomes: HF hospitalization, cardiovascular death, all-cause death, all-cause hospitalization, and development of clinical AF. HF hospitalization was defined as a minimum of an overnight stay in the hospital, with signs and symptoms of HF, and 2 of the following: radiologic evidence of HF, use of intravenous diuretics, or use of inotropes. All clinical outcomes, except all-cause hospitalization, were adjudicated by physicians blinded to the presence of SCAF on continuous ECG monitoring. The association between SCAF and clinical outcomes was determined at 30 days and 1 year for HF hospitalization and at 1 year for all secondary outcomes.

As a key aim of this observational study was to assess the association between SCAF and subsequent HF hospitalization, an important aspect was that ECG monitoring not directly influence hospital readmission. For this reason, patients or their physicians were not informed of the results of monitoring unless the patient was in a medically urgent situation, such as having life-threatening arrhythmia (ie, sustained ventricular tachycardia, or high-grade atrioventricular block) or SCAF ≥ 24 hours in duration.

Statistical analysis

Normally distributed continuous variables are summarized as mean ± standard deviation and are compared using the Student t test. Otherwise, they are summarized as median and interquartile range (IQR) and are compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Categorical variables are summarized as frequencies and proportions and are compared using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. The association between SCAF and clinical outcomes was examined using unadjusted and multivariable-adjusted Firth's penalized logistic regression.17 Multivariable models were adjusted for age, sex, body mass index (BMI), presence of coronary artery disease, and type of HF (ie, dichotomized as HF with reduced vs preserved ejection fraction), based on prior literature.5 For the purposes of this analysis, one patient was excluded due to the presence of AF at enrollment.

Results

Patient characteristics

Baseline characteristics of the 242 study participants are summarized in Table 1. The mean age was 70.9 ± 13.3 years; 51% were male; and the mean BMI was 31.6 ± 10.8 kg/m2. The prevalence of comorbidities among participants was high, with 82.6% having a history of hypertension, 50.2% having type 2 diabetes, 41.5% having a history of myocardial infarction, 41.3% having a prior history of HF, and 31.1% having a prior HF hospitalization. Mean left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) was 43.2% ± 17.1%, with 121 (50.2%) having HF with reduced ejection fraction (LVEF ≤ 40%).

Subclinical AF

Median ECG monitoring duration was 27.7 days (IQR 16.0, 28.0), which identified a total of 31 SCAF episodes among 17 participants (7.1%). Nine patients had their first SCAF episode during the first 14 days of monitoring, and the remaining 8 patients had it during the latter 14 days. Eleven participants (4.6%) had SCAF lasting ≥ 30 minutes (Fig. 1). The median SCAF duration was 6.33 hours (IQR 0.21, 32.5). Patients with SCAF were more likely to have a single-chamber pacemaker than were patients without SCAF. Otherwise, no significant differences were present between the groups, including in age, sex, BMI, comorbidities, and LVEF (see Tables 1 and 2). Use of medications, including HF therapies, was similar between the SCAF and the no-SCAF groups at baseline (Table 1).

Subclinical AF and adverse outcomes

The association between SCAF and HF-related adverse outcomes is summarized in Table 3. In logistic regression models, the presence of SCAF during 28-day ECG monitoring was not associated with 30-day or 1-year odds of HF hospitalization (adjusted odds ratio [aOR] 1.45 [95% confidence interval {CI} 0.27-5.12], P = 0.61 and 4 vs 54 events; aOR 1.12 [95% CI 0.31-2.97], P = 0.85, respectively). SCAF was associated with increased adjusted odds of cardiovascular mortality (6 vs 28 events; aOR 4.22 [95% CI 1.34-12.8), P = 0.014). Specific causes of cardiovascular death are summarized in Supplemental Table S1. Sudden cardiac death accounted for 3 of 6 cardiovascular deaths (50%) in the SCAF group, compared to 11 of 28 (39.3%) in those without SCAF, and pump failure accounted for the remaining 3 of 6 cardiovascular deaths (50%) in the SCAF group vs 7 of 28 cardiovascular deaths (25%) in the no-SCAF group. Participants with SCAF had significantly greater odds of developing clinical AF at 1 year (5 vs 14 events; aOR 8.26 [95% CI 2.33-28.2], P = 0.001). None of the participants in the SCAF group suffered a stroke, whereas 5 patients (2.2%) in the no-SCAF group did (P = 0.93). SCAF was not associated with increased odds of all-cause hospitalization (aOR 0.95 [95% CI 0.34-2.67], P = 0.92).

Discussion

In this prospective, observational study of patients hospitalized with acutely decompensated HF and no prior AF, we identified several important findings regarding the prevalence and clinical significance of SCAF. First, SCAF was detected in approximately 1 in 14 patients during early post–hospital discharge monitoring after an HF admission. Second, SCAF was associated with an increased risk of cardiovascular mortality and the development of clinical AF at 1 year. Finally, SCAF was not associated with HF rehospitalization after hospital discharge, although our analysis likely was underpowered for this outcome.

SCAF is an increasingly recognized and clinically important entity, yet its prevalence and prognostic significance in patients with HF remain poorly defined. In addition, the prevalence of SCAF in patients following HF hospitalization has not been reported previously. In a substudy of the IMPACT of Biotronik Home Monitoring Guided Anticoagulation on Stroke Risk in Patients With ICD and CRT-D Devices, which evaluated an outpatient population with chronic HF, 34.8% of patients with an implanted cardiac defibrillator (mean LVEF 29.6%) had SCAF over a mean follow-up period of 2 years.18 In contrast, in a cross-sectional study of 90 HF with preserved ejection fraction patients, the prevalence of SCAF was 8.9%, as determined by 14-day continuous ECG monitoring, compared to 4% in controls.19 In the present study, we observed a prevalence of SCAF of 7.1% following 28 days of continuous ECG monitoring after hospitalization for HF, with 4.6% of patients experiencing episodes lasting at least 30 minutes. These findings extend prior work and provide the first estimates of SCAF prevalence in the early postdischarge period after an HF admission.

Although SCAF was not uncommon in the present analysis, its prevalence was lower than anticipated given the elevated prevalence of AF in HF patients overall.20 The Asymptomatic Atrial Fibrillation and Stroke Evaluation in Pacemaker Patients and the Atrial Fibrillation Reduction Atrial Pacing Trial 2 (ASSERT2)—which used implanted loop monitors to detect AF in an elderly population largely free of HF at baseline—had more SCAF than was seen in the present study, with 10%-12% of patients having SCAF lasting ≥ 5 minutes, and ∼7% having SCAF for ≥ 30 minutes within the first 30 days of monitoring.3 The lower prevalence observed in our study likely reflects several factors. First, selection bias likely played an important role, as patients admitted to the hospital with HF exacerbation often underwent extensive inpatient heart-rhythm monitoring that likely identified AF and led to their exclusion. Second, rhythm monitoring in our study was limited to the first month after hospital discharge, and longer monitoring durations likely would have identified additional SCAF episodes.

The present study sheds further light on the clinical significance of SCAF in patients with HF. Prior studies have linked SCAF with increased mortality in populations largely without HF. In a retrospective analysis of 224 patients largely free of AF without history of HF, SCAF lasting ≥ 5 minutes identified within the first 6 months of pacemaker insertion was associated with a 3-fold increase in the risk of cardiovascular death over a mean of 6.6 years of follow-up (hazard ratio 2.8, 95% CI 1.24-6.31, P = 0.013).21 In addition, in a retrospective analysis of the Mode Selection Trial in Sinus-Node Dysfunction (MOST trial; N = 312), patients with SCAF were at an increased risk of all-cause death (hazard ratio 2.48, 95% CI 1.25-4.91, P = 0.0092).6 Finally, in the Apixaban for the Reduction of Thrombo-Embolism in Patients With Device-Detected Sub-Clinical Atrial Fibrillation (ARTESIA) study, SCAF progression was associated with doubling of the risk of all-cause mortality.22 However, the prognostic significance of SCAF in patients with HF has not been well characterized. In this study, we found that SCAF was associated with cardiovascular mortality and development of clinically overt AF. Our data suggest that, as with clinical AF, SCAF in HF patients is associated with adverse outcomes and identifies a higher-risk population that may benefit from more-targeted interventions, including intensification of HF therapy and rhythm-control strategies such as catheter ablation. This determination is of particular interest given the overall high risk of death already seen in this patient population. Whether such interventions can reduce adverse outcomes needs to be studied in the context of a clinical trial.

Recent studies have identified that treating SCAF with oral anticoagulation reduces the risk of stroke, albeit with a concomitant increase in the risk of bleeding.8,23 Patients who benefitted the most were those with a Congestive Heart Failure, Hypertension, Age ≥ 75 Years, Diabetes Mellitus, Stroke, Vascular Disease, Age 65 to 74 Years, Sex Category (CHA2DS2-VASc) score of ≥ 4.24 In the present study, 13of 17 patients (76.5%) with SCAF had an episode lasting > 6 minutes and a CHA2DS2-VASc score of ≥ 4. Therefore, the total number of HF patients needed to be screened to identify an individual with potentially actionable SCAF was 19. Continuous ECG monitoring or wearable heart-rhythm monitoring25 after HF hospitalization also may thus be justified to help identify high-risk individuals who could benefit from anticoagulation, although the stroke risk associated with SCAF in this setting is uncertain. In the present study, we did not detect whether SCAF was associated with HF hospitalization. Our study was modest in size, and it likely had insufficient power to examine this association. In addition, given the higher proportion of deaths in the SCAF group, death likely was a competing risk.

Prior studies by our group and others have identified older age, higher CHA2DS2-VASc score, and larger left atrial size as independent predictors of SCAF.3,4 In addition, the Cohorts for Heart and Aging Research in Genomic Epidemiology—Atrial Fibrillation (CHARGE-AF) risk score and polygenic risk scores have been shown to predict clinical AF, although they have not been evaluated specifically for prediction of SCAF in patients hospitalized with HF.26 These factors may help identify patients at highest risk of developing SCAF after HF admission and guide selection for postdischarge ECG monitoring. Further study is needed to better define the predictors of SCAF in this setting, as well as to determine the clinical utility and cost-effectiveness of routine continuous ECG monitoring after HF hospitalization.

The study should be interpreted in the context of several limitations. Our study likely underestimated the amount of SCAF in this population, as monitoring in the hospital likely identified additional AF that otherwise would have gone unnoticed. Further, as this was a nonintervention study, we cannot demonstrate that SCAF is a cause of mortality. SCAF may represent simply a marker of risk in a subpopulation of HF patients known to be at high risk of HF-related complications. In addition, given its modest size, our study was underpowered to detect adverse outcomes. For example, we did not detect an association between SCAF and HF rehospitalization, as we were likely underpowered for this outcome. In addition, we cannot rule out the possibility that some of the associations observed were due to chance. We also did not adjust for multiple testing. However, the associations observed have physiological plausibility and have been described previously in patients with HF and in the context of clinically overt AF.

Finally, given the relatively modest number of SCAF events, the multivariable model is at risk of overfitting and therefore should be considered exploratory and should be interpreted with caution. However, the multivariable results were consistent with the univariable analyses.

Conclusion

In patients without prior history of AF who were admitted to the hospital with a primary diagnosis of HF exacerbation, the presence of SCAF within 30 days of hospital discharge was ∼7% and was associated with increased odds of cardiovascular mortality and development of clinical AF. An association between SCAF and HF rehospitalization was not detected, although the study was likely underpowered for this outcome. These findings highlight the potential importance of SCAF as a risk marker in the early postdischarge period after HF hospitalization and support further investigation into targeted monitoring and intervention strategies in this population.

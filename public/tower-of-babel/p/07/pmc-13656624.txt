PPP2R2C promotes aerobic glycolysis and mediates pemetrexed resistance in lung adenocarcinomas by upregulating TCP1-dependent protein folding of glycolytic enzymes
Authors: Jia Chen, Xiaoshan Wang, Zhengyang Wang, Gang Li, Fei Han
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) remains lethal primarily due to its tendency for early metastasis and the universal development of chemotherapy resistance. The results of this study delineate a coherent signaling axis, originating from the upregulation of PPP2R2C expression to metabolic reprogramming, which ultimately influences the malignant progression and chemotherapy resistance of LUAD.MethodsGEPIA analysis was used to evaluate PPP2R2C expression in NSCLC. Following shRNA-mediated knockdown, cell proliferation, migration, apoptosis, and glycolytic activity were assessed in NCI-H1299 and A549 cells. Subcutaneous tumor formation was evaluated four weeks after injection of LV-shPPP2R2C or control vector, followed by H&E staining and immunohistochemical analysis of PPP2R2C and Ki67.ResultsClinical analysis shows that PPP2R2C is highly expressed in LUAD and is associated with poor prognosis, lymph node metastasis, and advanced disease stage. In vitro knockdown of PPP2R2C suppresses proliferation and migration and promotes apoptosis. Mechanistically, PPP2R2C drives the Warburg effect by upregulating the molecular chaperone TCP1 to ensure proper folding of glycolytic enzymes. Blocking TCP1 or glycolysis (with 2-DG) reverses PPP2R2C-mediated survival advantage and pemetrexed resistance. In vivo experiments confirm that knocking down PPP2R2C or TCP1 similarly inhibits tumor growth and restores chemotherapy sensitivity.ConclusionsIn summary, the results elucidate a novel mechanism whereby PPP2R2C enhances glycolysis by regulating TCP1-dependent protein refolding, thereby driving LUAD progression and chemoresistance. Thus, this study establishes a strong rationale for targeting this pathway as a potential therapeutic approach.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12931-026-03681-8.

Background

Lung cancer is a malignant tumor originating from the mucosa or glands of the lung and bronchus and is one of the leading causes of death from malignant tumors [1]. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), including lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) and lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC), is the most common histological type of lung cancer [2]. Due to the lack of specific symptoms in the early stage, most patients with NSCLC have developed to advanced stage when they are diagnosed [3, 4]. At this time, the malignant tumor frequently progresses rapidly and is prone to metastasis, which causes these patients to lose surgical opportunities [5]. Current treatment for NSCLC primarily involves surgical resection, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and molecular targeted therapy. However, for patients with distant metastases, molecular targeted therapy may represent the only viable curative option. Therefore, ongoing investigation of oncogenic genes and signaling pathways is essential for developing effective NSCLC therapies.

Protein phosphatase 2 A (PP2A) is a key serine/threonine phosphatase that regulates cellular signaling and plays critical roles in processes such as cell growth, differentiation, and apoptosis [6–8]. The protein phosphatase 2 regulatory subunit Bgamma (PPP2R2C) gene encodes PR55γ, a regulatory B subunit of PP2A [9]. Aberrant expression of PPP2R2C is associated with various cancers [10–15]. For example, PPP2R2C is expressed at low levels in glioma, and restoring its expression inhibits the mTOR pathway, thereby suppressing tumor progression [13]. In ovarian cancer, PPP2R2C is a key gene involved in invasion, and suppression of its expression counteracts the cell proliferation arrest induced by miR-572 inhibition [15, 16]. It is also implicated in melanoma, head and neck carcinomas, and lung squamous cell carcinoma [17–19]. Notably, PPP2R2C has been identified as a prognostic gene signature significantly associated with clinical outcomes in LUSC, indicating a potentially important role in lung cancer pathogenesis. However, its specific role in LUAD, another major histological subtype, remains entirely uncharacterized. Thus, investigating the role of PPP2R2C in LUAD holds substantial scientific value.

It is well known that the correct folding of proteins is a central prerequisite for the maintenance of their specific spatial conformation and physiological functions [20]. In particular, the maintenance of protein homeostasis in cancer is crucial for the inhibition of tumor progression, and its imbalance promotes the cancerous process [21]. Concurrently, tumor cells acquire survival advantages through metabolic reprogramming, with the aberrant activation of the glycolytic pathway being a key feature [22]. Sustaining this high-efficiency glycolysis requires a large number of correctly folded and catalytically active glycolytic enzymes, indicating a potential functional coupling between the protein folding system and metabolic reprogramming. T-complex polypeptide 1 (TCP1) gene encodes the core subunit of the cytoplasmic chaperone protein TRiC/CCT complex, which is responsible for ATP-dependent protein folding [23]. In LUAD, studies have confirmed that TCP1 expression is upregulated and is associated with advanced stage, poor prognosis, and increased Th2 immune infiltration, suggesting that it may drive tumor progression by promoting protein folding [24, 25]. Based on this background, elucidating whether PPP2R2C drives glycolytic reprogramming by regulating TCP1-mediated protein folding holds significant scientific value for deepening the understanding of LUAD’s metabolic adaptation mechanisms and discovering novel therapeutic targets.

This study demonstrates that PPP2R2C is upregulated in LUAD and that its elevated expression is strongly associated with aggressive tumor progression. Further mechanistic investigation revealed that PPP2R2C upregulates TCP1 expression, thereby promoting the correct folding of glycolytic enzymes, which in turn activates glycolysis and drives tumor progression. Importantly, the metabolic reprogramming mediated by the PPP2R2C/TCP1 axis ultimately confers resistance to pemetrexed in tumor cells.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition of bioinformatic analysis

The dataset of NSCLC samples, including LUAD and LUSC tissues, are acquired from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) data portal (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis (GEPIA) was used to analyze PPP2R2C expression levels in NSCLC samples.

Patient’s samples

A tissue microarray including tumor samples of 89 LUAD and paracancerous tissues was purchased from Shanghai OUTDO Biotech Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China). All tissue specimens were confirmed by postoperative pathological examination. The tumor stage was further determined based on the eighth edition of the TNM classification for lung cancer [26]. Demographic characteristics, clinical and pathological stages, survival time and duration of follow-up of these patients were also recorded.

Histological evaluation

Formalin-fixed tumor tissue segments were embedded in paraffin and sectioned into 5-µm-thick serial slices. Sections were subjected to hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining. The sections were also subjected to PPP2R2C and Ki67 immunohistochemical (IHC) staining.

Experimental animals and sample collection

Eight 4-week-old male BALB/c nude mice were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. All mice were housed in a pathogen-free facility with ad libitum access to food and water. Mice were randomly divided into two groups with four mice per group: the shCtrl group and the shPPP2R2C group. 1 × 106 stable A549 cells with LV-shCtrl or LV-shPPP2R2C suspended in 150 µL PBS were subcutaneously injected into the right armpit space of the mice, respectively in the two groups. Four weeks after injection, all mice were euthanized. Tumor tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) and processed for paraffin embedding. H&E staining, as well as IHC staining of PPP2R2C and Ki67 were further performed.

In the in vivo experiment investigating the regulation of pemetrexed sensitivity by PPP2R2C, Mice were randomly assigned to four groups with six mice per group: NC+shCtrl group, pemetrexed group, PPP2R2C overexpression (PPP2R2C-OE) + pemetrexed group, and PPP2R2C-OE+shTCP1 + pemetrexed group. All groups except the NC+shCtrl group were treated with pemetrexed. A549 cells used for subcutaneous injection were stably transduced with lentiviral vectors. Mice in the four groups (n = 6 per group) were respectively injected with 1 × 10⁶ cells subjected to different treatments (suspended in 150 µL PBS), with the treatments as follows: the NC+shCtrl group and pemetrexed group received cells infected with empty vector (LV-NC) and short hairpin control (LV-shCtrl); the PPP2R2C-OE+pemetrexed group received cells infected with LV-PPP2R2C and LV-shCtrl; the PPP2R2C-OE+shTCP1 + pemetrexed group received cells infected with LV-PPP2R2C and LV-shTCP1. Beginning on day 10 post-inoculation, tumor volume and body weight were monitored every other day to assess therapeutic efficacy and systemic toxicity. The experiment concluded on day 26, when all mice were euthanized using CO₂ inhalation. Tumors were excised and weighed, and tissue samples were then subjected to Western blot analysis and histological examination, respectively. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital (No. 2023 − 226).

Cell lines

LUAD cell lines (NCI-H1299, A549, and SPC-A-1) and the human bronchial epithelial cell line BEAS-2B were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). All cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 (Corning, 10-040-CVB) medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 µg/mL of streptomycin and 100 U/mL of penicillin (Gibco, 15140122) in a humidified 5% CO2 incubator at 37 °C.

Preparation of lentiviral vectors and target screening of shRNA

The short hairpin RNA (shRNA) targeting human PPP2R2C mRNA was designed by YBR Biotechnologies Co., Ltd. (China). Three sequences were as follows: shPPP2R2C-1, CCCTGGAGGCCTCGAGGGAAA; sh-PPP2R2C-2, CCGCTCATTCTTCTCGGAAAT; sh-PPP2R2C-3, CACCAACAACCTGTACATCTT. The shRNA negative control sequence was CACCAACAACCTGTACATCTT (non-targeting control). The replication-incompetent LV was created by shRNA expression vector with pHelper 1.0 as well as pHelper 2.0 into 293T cells. Viral supernatants were collected 48 h post-transfection and filtered through a 0.45 μm cellulose acetate membrane. Fluorescence was assessed by flow cytometric analysis of GFP-positive 293T cells. Knockdown efficiency of shPPP2R2C was further validated in the A549 cell line.

Construction of overexpress PPP2R2C lentivirus

OE-PPP2R2C lentivirus was designed by YBR Biotechnologies Co. Ltd (China). The cDNA encoding human PPP2R2C was amplified by PCR and cloned into the BR-V108 vector. Overexpression efficiency of PPP2R2C was further confirmed in the A549 cell line.

Cell transfection

For stably knockdown or overexpression of PPP2R2C, LUAD cells were transfected with lentiviral (LV) vector containing luciferase (LV-NV or LV-shCtrl), LV-shPPP2R2C or LV-PPP2R2C containing luciferase. All cells transfected with lentivirus were screened with puromycin (1 µg/mL for A549 and 2 µg/mL for H1299, P8230, Beijing Solaibao Technology Co., LTD). The efficiency of each shRNA was routinely verified by RT-qPCR or western blotting.

Western blotting

Total proteins were extracted from A549 cells, NCI-H1299 cells and tumor tissues of mice. The concentrations of the protein samples were quantified by a Protein Assay Kit. All protein samples were separated by electrophoresis, and then transferred onto 0.45 μm polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes. After blocking with 5% skim milk, the membranes were incubated with primary antibodies, including anti-PPP2R2C (Proteintech, 12747-1-AP), anti-p53 (Proteintech, 10442-1-AP), anti-TCP1 (Proteintech, 10320-1-AP), anti-HSP90 (Proteintech, 13171-1-AP), anti-GAPDH (Proteintech, 60004-1-lg) and anti-β- Actin (Proteintech, 60008-1-Ig) at 4℃ overnight. Following washing, membranes were incubated with anti-rabbit or anti-mouse horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibodies (Beyotime, A0208 for anti-rabbit or Beyotime, A0216 for anti-mouse) for 1 h at room temperature. Finally, the membranes were visualized by the image analyzer (GE, AI600).

RNA isolation and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Total RNAs were extracted from A549 cells and NCI-H1299 cells transfected with indicated lentiviruses by TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Life Technologies), followed by reverse transcription using TaKaRa commercial reagent kit. The threshold values of each reaction were normalized to that of GAPDH and calculated using the 2−△△Ct method. Primers used in this study are summarized as below. Primers for PPP2R2C, forwards: 5’-CATGTTCGATCGGAACACCAAGC-3’, reverse: 5’-CAGCTGTCCTCATCAGTGCTGTG-3’; primers for GAPDH, forwards: 5’-CTACACTGAGGACCAGGTTGTCT-3’, reverse:5’-GGTCTGGGATGGAAATTGTG-3’.

CCK-8 assay

Cell proliferation was assessed using a CCK-8 assay. A549 and NCI-H1299 cells were seeded at 5 × 103 cells per well into 96-well microplates. Cells were transfected with LV-shCtrl, LV-shPPP2R2C or LV-PPP2R2C. 10 µL 1×CCK-8 reagent was then added into each well at the same time for consecutive 5 days. Following CCK-8 addition, cells were incubated at 37 °C for 2 h in the dark. Absorbance (OD 450 nm) was measured using a microplate reader. Experiments were independently repeated five times.

Celigo cell counting assay

A549 and H1299 cells were transduced with lentivirus carrying either shPPP2R2C or shCtrl to express green fluorescent protein (GFP) and were seeded in a 96-well plate at a density of 2,000 cells per well. Subsequently, the cells were subjected to automated fluorescence scanning and counting daily for five consecutive days using a Celigo image cytometer. Growth curves were generated by analyzing changes in cell numbers over time.

Flow cytometry assay

A549 and NCI-H1299 cells transfected with LV-shCtrl or LV-shPPP2R2C were cultured in medium containing 10% FBS until 70% confluence. Subsequently, the cells were trypsinzed, collected with the supernatant into the centrifuge tubes, washed with pre-cooled D-Hanks and 1×binding buffer and centrifuged at 1,300 rpm for 3 min. 200 µL 1×binding buffer was added to resuspend the cells. 10 µL Annexin V-APC was added for 10 min at room temperature in dark. Another 400 µL 1×binding buffer was added for further on-machine (Millipore, Guava easyCyte HT) detection. The ModFit LT 3.1 software was used to analyze the results.

Colony-forming assay

1 × 103 A549 and NCI-H1299 cells transfected with LV-shCtrl or LV-shPPP2R2C were seeded into 6-well plates in medium containing 10% FBS. Cells were cultured for 14 days with regular observation of cell morphology and medium replacement every 3 days. Following incubation, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min and washed with PBS. 500 µL GIEMSA staining buffer was added to the fixed cells for 10 min. Then, ddH2O was added for washing the cells several times. After air-drying, colonies were imaged and counted manually.

Wound-healing assay

A549 and NCI-H1299 cells were seeded into a 6-well plate and cultured in medium plus 10% FBS until 90% confluence was reached. A scratch wound was created using a sterile pipette tip (time = 0 h). Followed by incubation in serum-free RPMI-1640 culture medium at 37 °C and 5% CO2. The images were pictured by inverted microscope (OLYMPUS, CKX31), and the wound area was calculated as follows: (migration distance at 0 h- migration distance at 48 h) / (migration distance at 0 h).

Transwell assay

The migratory capacity of A549 and NCI-H1299 cells was assessed using transwell migration chambers (Corning 3422). Briefly, 2 × 105 cells transfected with LV-shCtrl or LV-shPPP2R2C were resuspended in 200 µL FBS-free media, and added into the upper chamber. 600 µL media containing 10% FBS was further added into the lower chamber. The cells were incubated for 24 h at 37 °C, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min, and stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 20 min. After removing the non-invading cells from the upper side of chambers, cells were photographed under a fluorescent microscope (OLYMPUS, IX 73).

Bioenergetics assay

To evaluate the impact of PPP2R2C and TCP1 on cellular energy metabolism, real-time metabolic profiling was conducted using the Seahorse XFe 24 Analyzer (Agilent, USA). A549 and NCI-H1299 cells with modulated PPP2R2C and TCP1 expression were seeded at 2 × 104 cells per well in a 24-well plate. For the oxygen consumption rate (OCR), the medium was replaced with Seahorse XF Base Medium containing 1 mM pyruvate, 2 mM glutamine, and 10 mM glucose, and cells were sequentially treated with oligomycin (1.5 µM), FCCP (1 µM), and antimycin A/rotenone (0.5 µM each). For the extracellular acidification rate (ECAR), cells were first starved in glucose-free medium for 1 h, followed by sequential injection of glucose (10 mM), oligomycin (3 µM), and 2-DG (50 mM). Key OCR and ECAR parameters were analyzed according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

Lactic acid content detection

After one day of incubation in the new medium, cells were washed and lysed, and the supernatant was collected. A lactic acid assay kit (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute) was used to detect lactate concentration according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Absorbance was measured at 530 nm using a microplate spectrophotometer. Lactic acid production ratio is the amount of lactate produced by 1 × 106 cells in a day.

Thioflavin T fluorescence assay

1 mM thioflavin T stock solution was freshly prepared in distilled water, filtered, and then diluted with PBS (pH 7.4) to obtain a 25 µM working solution. In a 96-well plate, the protein sample was mixed with an equal volume of the working solution, resulting in a final volume of 100 µL per well. The plate was sealed and incubated at 37 °C with shaking at 600–800 rpm. Fluorescence intensity was measured at designated time points using a fluorescence microplate reader (excitation: 450 nm; emission: 485 nm) to quantify misfolded protein aggregation.

Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) assays

Cells were lysed in ice-cold IP lysis buffer (Beyotime, China) supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktails (Beyotime, China). Cellular debris was removed by centrifugation at 12,000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C. The protein concentration of the supernatant was determined using a BCA assay kit (Beyotime, China). Then, 1000 µg of total protein lysate was incubated overnight at 4 °C with the primary antibody or IgG as a negative control. Protein A/G beads were added and incubated for an additional 2–4 h to capture antibody–antigen complexes. The beads were washed 3–5 times with cold lysis buffer to remove nonspecifically bound proteins. The immunoprecipitated proteins were eluted by boiling the beads in 2× Loading buffer (Beyotime, China). for 10 min, separated by SDS-PAGE, and detected by Western blotting using appropriate antibodies.

Data and statistical analysis

A blinded design was implemented for intervention and data analysis in both in vitro and in vivo studies. In vitro experiments were conducted with at least three independent biological replicates. Quantitative data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Survival curves were constructed using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared by the log-rank test. Two-group comparisons were made using the unpaired, two-tailed Student’s t-test. Multiple-group comparisons were analyzed by one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. Statistical significance was defined as *P* < 0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.5.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA).

Results

The expression of PPP2R2C was upregulated in LUAD and was correlated with poor prognosis of patients

Based on the results of the PPP2R2C expression levels in NSCLC samples through TCGA database by GEPIA online tool (http://gepia.cancer-pku.cn/), we found that PPP2R2C was upregulated significantly in LUSC tissues and an increased trend without significance in LUAD tissues compared with those in the normal lung tissues (Fig. 1A). We performed the H&E staining and IHC staining of PPP2R2C protein in 85 pairs of LUAD tissues and their peri-cancerous lung tissues (Fig. 1B). H&E staining confirmed that LUAD tissues exhibited more surface nodules than adjacent non-tumor tissues. Furthermore, PPP2R2C expression was higher in LUAD tissues than in adjacent non-tumor tissues. The proportion of PPP2R2C-positive specimens with high and low expression of PPP2R2C of in human LUAD tissues were distinguish increased compared with the corresponding peri-cancerous lung tissues (Fig. 1C). We further confirmed the value of PPP2R2C in predicting the prognosis of LUAD. Kaplan–Meier analysis revealed that patients with high expression of PPP2R2C had relatively shorter overall survival (P = 0.025; Fig. 1D). Moreover, high expression of PPP2R2C was related with a more advanced lymph node stage metastasis and advanced clinical stage (Tables 1 and 2), suggesting that PPP2R2C facilitated the LUAD progression.

Knockdown of PPP2R2C inhibited proliferation, migration, and promoted apoptosis in LUAD cells

We next assessed PPP2R2C mRNA expression in human normal bronchial epithelial cells (BEAS-2B) and three lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) cell lines (NCI-H1299, SPC-A-1, and A549). The results revealed that PPP2R2C mRNA levels were significantly higher in NCI-H1299 and A549 cells compared to SPC-A-1 cells and normal BEAS-2B epithelial cells (Fig. 2A), suggesting that PPP2R2C may play a promotive role in LUAD. To further investigate the biological function of PPP2R2C in LUAD, we transfected A549 cells with three different LV-shPPP2R2C vectors. Among them, transfection with LV-shPPP2R2C-2 and LV-shPPP2R2C-3 significantly reduced PPP2R2C mRNA expression by approximately 75% (Figs. 2B). At the protein level, shPPP2R2C-2 also markedly decreased PPP2R2C expression, which was used for constructing PPP2R2C knockdown cell models (Fig. 2C, D).

Using this established model, we systematically evaluated the effects of PPP2R2C on malignant phenotypes of LUAD cells through celigo cell counting, flow cytometry, wound healing assay, and transwell assay. Celigo-based proliferation analysis showed that knockdown of PPP2R2C significantly inhibited the proliferation of A549 and NCI-H1299 cells (Figs. 2E-G). Colony formation assays further confirmed that PPP2R2C knockdown suppressed the clonogenic ability of both cell lines (Fig. 2H). Flow cytometric analysis indicated that PPP2R2C depletion promoted apoptosis (Fig. 2I). Wound healing and Transwell migration assays demonstrated that interference with PPP2R2C expression markedly impaired the migratory capacity of A549 and NCI-H1299 cells (Figs. 2J-K). In summary, PPP2R2C knockdown suppressed proliferation and migration, while promoting apoptosis in LUAD cells, indicating that PPP2R2C plays an important role in the progression of LUAD.

TCP1 was a potential downstream target of PPP2R2C

To explore proteins functionally associated with PPP2R2C, we utilized the STRING database to screen for potential interacting partners and identified TCP1 as one of the candidate proteins (Fig. 3A). Experimental results showed that in PPP2R2C-knockdown cells, TCP1 was downregulated at both the mRNA and protein levels, while overexpression of PPP2R2C concurrently increased the expression of both PPP2R2C and TCP1, suggesting that PPP2R2C may positively regulate TCP1 (Figs. 3B-E).To further investigate whether TCP1 acts as a downstream effector mediating the function of PPP2R2C, we knocked down TCP1 in the context of PPP2R2C overexpression and examined its impact on cellular phenotypes. Wound healing assays revealed that overexpression of PPP2R2C markedly enhanced cell migration, whereas TCP1 knockdown significantly suppressed migration. Importantly, TCP1 depletion reversed the migration-promoting phenotype induced by PPP2R2C overexpression (Figs. 3F-G). Consistent results were also obtained from CCK-8 assays (Fig. 3H). These findings indicate that TCP1 is a critical downstream factor of PPP2R2C and mediates the promotive effects of PPP2R2C on malignant phenotypes in lung cancer cells.

Protein folding drives aerobic glycolysis in LUAD cells

TCP1 encodes a core subunit of the chaperonin complex TRiC/CCT, which facilitates protein folding in an ATP-dependent manner [23]. Given that glycolytic enzymes require proper three-dimensional conformation to exert catalytic functions [27], we hypothesize that TCP1 may regulate glycolysis by promoting the correct folding of glycolytic enzymes. To examine the role of protein folding in glycolysis, key chaperones including HSP70 and HSP90 were selected for analysis. HSP70 is primarily involved in the initial stabilization of nascent polypeptide chains, while HSP90 facilitates the final maturation and activation of certain client proteins; the two function synergistically in protein folding [28]. Additionally, the nuclear protein DAXX was examined, which not only acts as a chaperone for histone H3.3 but also participates in protein quality control by clearing misfolded proteins to maintain cellular homeostasis [29]. Experimental results demonstrated a significant upregulation of HSP70 and HSP90 expression in LUAD cells (Fig. 4A). Overexpression of either HSP70 or DAXX markedly increased intracellular lactate levels (Figs. 4B, C). Cellular energy metabolism analysis revealed that HSP70 overexpression led to a pronounced elevation in the extracellular acidification rate, accompanied by enhanced glycolysis, glycolytic capacity, and glycolytic reserve (Figs. 4D-F). Concurrently, oxygen consumption rate was suppressed, resulting in significantly reduced ATP production, maximal respiration, and spare respiratory capacity (Figs. 4G-I). DAXX overexpression exhibited a similar trend, promoting glycolysis while suppressing aerobic respiration (Figs. 4J, K). These findings suggest a strong positive association between proper protein folding and increased glycolytic activity.

TCP1-mediated protein folding is a key link in the enhancement of glycolysis

To investigate whether TCP1 can enhance aerobic glycolysis by promoting protein folding, we first overexpressed TCP1 and observed a significant increase in intracellular lactate levels. This effect was reversible by the glycolysis inhibitor 2-DG (Fig. 5A). Cellular energy metabolism analysis further revealed that TCP1 overexpression markedly increased the extracellular acidification rate (ECAR) and suppressed the oxygen consumption rate (OCR), and these phenotypic changes were similarly reversed by 2-DG (Fig. 5B-C). Notably, PPP2R2C overexpression produced comparable effects: it promoted lactate production, enhanced ECAR, and suppressed OCR, all of which were also inhibited by 2-DG (Fig. 5D-F). These results suggest that the PPP2R2C/TCP1 pathway enhances glycolytic capacity in LUAD cells. To assess whether this process relies on the protein folding activity of TCP1, we conducted a Thioflavin T fluorescence assay. 17-AAG, a well-characterized inhibitor of protein folding, served as a positive control, while the proteasome inhibitor MG-132 was used to block degradation of misfolded proteins, thus generating conditions conducive to TCP1-mediated refolding. Results showed that treatment with 17-AAG alone significantly increased fluorescence intensity relative to the control, indicating defective protein folding. Treatment with MG-132 alone also caused a substantial increase in fluorescence, indicative of accumulated misfolded proteins. Overexpression of TCP1 in the presence of MG-132 or 17-AAG significantly attenuated the fluorescence enhancement induced by these agents. Conversely, TCP1 knockdown elevated fluorescence intensity. These results indicate that TCP1 can promote the refolding of misfolded proteins (Fig. 5G).

Further mechanistic investigation showed that TCP1 overexpression increased the expression of glycolysis-associated proteins PKM2 and LDHA, and this accumulation was more pronounced in the presence of MG-132 (Fig. 5H). Consistent with the changes in protein levels, combined treatment with TCP1 overexpression and MG-132 resulted in the highest lactate production and the lowest OCR in cells, indicating a significant enhancement of glycolytic metabolism (Fig. 5I, J). More importantly, the CO-IP experiment confirmed the interaction between TCP1 and LDHA, which provides direct evidence supporting the mechanistic link (Fig. 5K). Taken together, these findings suggest that TCP1 likely enhances glycolytic metabolism in LUAD cells by promoting the correct folding of glycolysis-related proteins such as PKM2 and LDHA, thereby improving their intracellular homeostasis and function.

TCP1-dependent glycolysis is essential for PPP2R2C-driven pemetrexed resistance and tumor malignancy in LUAD

To investigate the role of TCP1 in PPP2R2C-promoted glycolysis, we knocked down TCP1 under PPP2R2C-overexpressing conditions. The results showed that TCP1 knockdown significantly reversed the PPP2R2C-induced increases in lactate accumulation and extracellular acidification rate, as well as the suppression of oxygen consumption rate, effects that were similar to those of the glycolysis inhibitor 2-DG (Figs. 6A-D). This suggests that TCP1 acts as a key effector in PPP2R2C-mediated glycolytic regulation. Furthermore, TCP1 knockdown markedly suppressed the PPP2R2C-enhanced cell viability and reversed its inhibitory effect on apoptosis (Figs. 6E-G). Comparable effects were observed with 2-DG treatment, indicating that the promotive effect of PPP2R2C on the malignant phenotype of LUAD cells depends on TCP1-mediated glycolytic activity. In addition, PPP2R2C overexpression significantly increased the resistance of A549 cells to pemetrexed, elevating the IC50 from 14.04 µM in the control group to 22.97 µM. Conversely, TCP1 knockdown not only enhanced cellular sensitivity to pemetrexed (IC50 = 4.37 µM) but also attenuated the PPP2R2C-induced drug resistance, reducing the IC50 to 8.39 µM (Figs. 6H).

PPP2R2C modulates tumor growth and pemetrexed resistance through TCP1 in vivo

To evaluate the effect of PPP2R2C knockdown on tumor growth in vivo, we subcutaneously injected nude mice with stable PPP2R2C-knockdown A549 cells (1 × 106 cells per mouse), with four replicates per group. As shown in Fig. 7A and B, tumor volume and weight in the shPPP2R2C group were significantly smaller than those in the control group, indicating that PPP2R2C knockdown suppressed tumor growth. Western blot analysis of tumor tissues revealed downregulation of both PPP2R2C and TCP1 protein levels, along with upregulation of p53 in the shPPP2R2C group, suggesting that PPP2R2C silencing may promote tumor cell apoptosis via the p53 pathway (Fig. 7C). IHC staining confirmed that the expression levels of PPP2R2C and the proliferation marker Ki67 were significantly reduced in the shPPP2R2C group compared to the shCtrl group (Fig. 7D), indicating that PPP2R2C knockdown not only inhibits tumor growth but also attenuates cell proliferation.

To further investigate the role of the PPP2R2C–TCP1 axis in regulating pemetrexed resistance in vivo, we established A549 cells stably overexpressing PPP2R2C, as well as cells overexpressing PPP2R2C with concurrent TCP1 knockdown, and inoculated them into nude mice. Under pemetrexed treatment, PPP2R2C overexpression significantly enhanced tumor resistance to pemetrexed, as evidenced by the largest tumor volume and weight in this group (Figs. 7E-G). In contrast, TCP1 knockdown in the context of PPP2R2C overexpression reversed this resistance and markedly suppressed tumor growth (Figs. 7E-G). Discussion

Overall, this study elucidated for the first time the driving role of the PPP2R2C-TCP1-glycolysis axis in LUAD progression. Specifically, high PPP2R2C expression was positively correlated with clinical stage and poor prognosis, and it safeguarded the correct folding of glycolytic enzymes and enhanced glycolytic flux through up-regulation of TCP1, thereby promoting cell proliferation, migration and mediating pemetrexed resistance. Accordingly, targeted inhibition of PPP2R2C inhibited glycolysis and blocked metabolic reprogramming via TCP1. In vivo, knockdown of PPP2R2C inhibited tumor growth, while knockdown of TCP1 reversed pemetrexed resistance generated by PPP2R2C overexpression and inhibited tumor progression. These findings identify novel prognostic biomarkers and potential combinatorial therapeutic targets for LUAD.

Previous studies suggest that dysregulation of PPP2R2C may contribute to tumorigenesis and cancer progression. PPP2R2C encodes B55γ, a regulatory B subunit of PP2A, which assembles with the A and C subunits to form functionally specific PP2A holoenzyme complexes [30]. The role of PPP2R2C in cancer appears dualistic. For instance, B55γ-mediated glucose metabolism is a potential therapeutic target for brain tumors [30]. The low expression of PPP2R2C is linked to prostate cancer recurrence and disease-specific mortality [12]. While on the other hand, it is considered a potential biomarker for the progression and prognosis of ovarian cancer, head and neck cancer, melanoma, and LUAD [17–19]. In LUSC, PPP2R2C has been included in a seven-gene risk signature and confirmed as an independent prognostic factor [19]. Notably, this study demonstrates that PPP2R2C is overexpressed in LUAD, and its elevated expression correlates with more extensive lymph node metastasis and advanced clinical stage, indicating a potential oncogenic role in LUAD progression. This tissue-specific function may stem from PPP2R2C-containing PP2A holoenzymes preferentially regulating distinct signaling pathways in different tissues, for example, potentially modulating the mTOR pathway in glioma [13], while influencing the folding and function of metabolism-related proteins in LUAD. Although a correlation between PPP2R2C expression and clinicopathological characteristics was observed, confounding factors such as smoking history may independently affect both PPP2R2C expression and tumor progression. Tobacco exposure is a major etiological factor for LUAD and can drive broad molecular changes that may intersect with PPP2R2C-related pathways [31]. Thus, although our data demonstrate a significant association, the contribution of these or other unmeasured variables cannot be fully excluded—a limitation common to retrospective analyses. To address this, we experimentally validated the functional role of PPP2R2C. Knocking down PPP2R2C inhibited proliferation and migration while promoting apoptosis of LUAD cells in vitro, and suppressed tumor growth in vivo, consistent with the clinical observations.

The function of glycolytic enzymes is dependent on their correct folding [32]. Overactivated glycolysis can lead to chemoresistance [33, 34], making targeting glycolysis a promising strategy for reversing drug resistance. In this study, we found that TCP1 is a key downstream factor of PPP2R2C. PPP2R2C promotes pemetrexed resistance in LUAD cells by facilitating the refolding of glycolytic enzymes through TCP1. Although glycolytic reprogramming is identified here as a central mediator of resistance, the PPP2R2C–TCP1 axis may also interact with other established pemetrexed resistance pathways. For instance, enhanced glycolytic flux and lactate production could influence the expression of DNA repair-related genes through epigenetic modifications such as histone lactylation [35].​ Moreover, the downregulation of TCP1 can significantly inhibit the increase in proliferation and migration abilities of LUAD cells induced by PPP2R2C. Notably, the effects of TCP1 on cancer are multi-mechanistic. TCP1 promoted proliferation of ovarian cancer cells via PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway [36]. TCP1 increased drug resistance in acute myeloid leukemia by suppressing autophagy via activating AKT/mTOR signaling [37]. In addition, TCP1 stabilizes c-Myc through the AKT/GSK-3β and ERK signaling pathways and promotes malignant tumor progression [38]. Beyond its direct effects on tumor cells, TCP1-mediated reprogramming of metabolism and proteostasis may also shape an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment [39]. This aligns with recent research highlighting the central role of metabolic reprogramming in shaping the LUAD immune microenvironment [40]. Methodologies such as machine learning-based immunogenomic analysis and consensus clustering, as employed in similar studies, could help further dissect the role of TCP1-mediated reprogramming in shaping the immunosuppressive landscape [41, 42]. In conclusion, the PPP2R2C-TCP1-glycolysis axis identified in this study represents a novel oncogenic mechanism linking phosphatase signaling, protein folding quality control, and metabolic reprogramming. The novelty of this axis lies in three aspects. At the regulatory level, it forms a multistep cascade spanning post-translational regulation, protein function, and metabolic output, contrasting with the transcriptional regulation of glycolytic enzymes. Mechanistically, it is the first to functionally link the specific PP2A regulatory subunit PPP2R2C with the chaperonin TCP1, enhancing glycolysis by ensuring the correct folding of glycolytic enzymes rather than by modulating their expression or phosphorylation status. Functionally, it provides a novel perspective on pemetrexed resistance based on metabolic adaptation and proteostasis, differing from classic mechanisms involving drug target al.teration or enhanced DNA repair. These findings advance our understanding of TCP1-mediated oncogenesis and provide a rationale for co-targeting tumor metabolism and proteostasis to overcome chemoresistance in LUAD.

This study has several limitations. First, the clinical correlations, while significant, are derived from retrospective analyses where unmeasured confounders such as detailed smoking history or prior treatments could influence the associations. Second, although PPP2R2C regulates TCP1 expression at both the mRNA and protein levels, the precise upstream molecular mechanism remains undefined. Third, we cannot exclude that TCP1 may regulate glycolysis through additional, unexplored mechanisms. Future studies should address these knowledge gaps. The precise mechanism by which PPP2R2C controls TCP1 expression requires elucidation through experiments such as luciferase reporter assays and ChIP-seq. Moreover, prospective studies using well-annotated patient cohorts are needed to confirm the independent prognostic value of PPP2R2C in LUAD. Concurrently, evaluating the combined predictive power of PPP2R2C with other pre-existing prognostic biomarkers, may facilitate the construction of a more refined prognostic model for LUAD [43]. Ultimately, comprehensive functional studies in preclinical models are required to assess the therapeutic potential of targeting this axis to reverse or prevent pemetrexed resistance.

Conclusions

PPP2R2C is unexpectedly up-regulated in LUAD, where its expression level predicts poor survival, advanced stage, and lymph-node metastasis. We delineate a linear, therapeutically actionable axis in which PPP2R2C stabilizes the TRiC chaperone TCP1, thereby ensuring correct folding of rate-limiting glycolytic enzymes. This glycolytic surge supports ATP generation, anabolic biosynthesis, and ROS detoxification, collectively driving increased proliferation, migration, and—critically—acquired resistance to the first-line antifolate agent pemetrexed. Genetic or pharmacologic interruption of any node of the PPP2R2C-TCP1-glycolysis cascade (PPP2R2C knock-down, TCP1 depletion, or 2-DG) uniformly re-sensitizes tumor cells to pemetrexed in vitro and in vivo. Collectively, these data reveal a non-canonical, post-translational mechanism that couples oncogenic PP2A-subunit signaling to metabolic reprogramming, and they nominate PPP2R2C or TCP1 inhibition—alone or in combination with glycolytic blockade—as a rational strategy to circumvent chemoresistance in LUAD.

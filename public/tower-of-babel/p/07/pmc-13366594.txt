Research on biomarkers screening for urinary tract infection and the prediction model based on machine learning algorithms
Authors: Shenshen Zhi, Haiping Fan, Wenyan Wu, Yuanyuan Zhang, Xiefei Hu, Sheng Chen, Wei Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundUrinary tract infections (UTIs) affect over 1.5 billion people annually, yet systematic screening of cytokine combinations as biomarkers remains limited. This study aims to analyze clinical data with the aid of machine learning algorithms, identify cytokines closely associated with UTIs in our cohort, and explore potential candidate biomarkers that warrant further validation for the early diagnosis, treatment, and prognosis evaluation of UTIs.MethodsClinical data were collected and divided into a training set and a testing set at a ratio of 7:3. During the training phase, the technique of 10-fold cross-validation repeated 10 times combined with grid search was applied to screen out the optimal parameters for five classification algorithms, namely logistic regression, random forest, Adaptive Boosting, Support Vector Machine, and Naive Bayes, and corresponding prediction models were constructed. The final model score was determined by the average accuracy rate over multiple iterations, and a confusion matrix was generated. For each trained and tested model, a Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve was plotted, and the Area Under the Curve (AUC) was calculated. The model with the best performance was selected through the evaluation of the AUC, and the contribution weights of each feature to this model were analyzed.ResultsAmong the five models, the random forest model demonstrated excellent performance, with its Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUROC) reaching 0.836 and the Area Under the Precision-Recall Curve (AUPRC) as high as 0.942. The Support Vector Machine model had the best precision, which was 0.928. The Naive Bayes model stood out in terms of recall and F1-score, with values of 0.840 and 0.841 respectively. Based on the variable importance prediction of the random forest model, it was determined that factors such as age, IFN-γ, IL-6, IL-2, and IL-10 had relatively high importance scores. When evaluating the prediction ability of the models using the testing set, it was found that the AUC and AUPRC values of the random forest and AdaBoost models were significantly higher than those of the other models. The calibration curve analysis further confirmed the high accuracy and reliability of the models.ConclusionsAmong the five models, the random forest model had the best prediction performance. The variable importance analysis of the random forest model identified cytokines such as IFN-γ, IL-6, IL-2, and IL-10 as important features in our cohort, suggesting their potential relevance to the diagnosis of UTIs. These cytokines are expected to become highly potential biomarkers in the precision medicine of UTIs.

Background

Urinary tract infection (UTI) is a commonly encountered urological condition, affecting approximately 1.5 billion individuals worldwide annually [1]. The underlying mechanism primarily involves the multiplication of harmful microorganisms within the urinary system, leading to inflammation in the tissues of the cells and the mucous membrane of the urethra. Typical symptoms include increased frequency of urination, urgency, painful urination, lower back discomfort, and the presence of blood in the urine. These infections can manifest in various segments of the urinary tract, including the bladder (known as cystitis), the urethra (referred to as urethritis), and the kidneys (known as pyelonephritis) [2, 3]. As a result, early and precise diagnosis, coupled with swift and effective treatment, holds critical importance in the management of UTI.

Research has shown that cytokines are closely linked to UTI, with tumor necrosis factor (TNF) standing out as particularly relevant to the development and outcomes of these infections. TNF serves as a biomarker for immune responses against the disease [4]. Uropathogenic Escherichia coli (UPEC) is responsible for over 80% of UTI, and it intensifies the inflammatory reaction by generating proinflammatory cytokines and chemokines [5]. Notably, proinflammatory cytokines like TNF-α, IL-8, IL-6, and IL-1β play a significant role in stimulating the growth of UPEC [6]. However, there are still obvious gaps in existing research. Previous studies have mainly focused on the independent effects of single or a few cytokines. In fact, the inflammatory response in UTI patients is the result of the synergistic action of multiple factors, and there is a lack of systematic analysis to screen key cytokine combinations.

Machine Learning (ML), as a fundamental and pivotal branch of artificial intelligence, enables the exploration of vast health data and subsequent training of computers. Through a variety of ML algorithms, auxiliary diagnostic models effectively unearth latent information within data and discover correlations among datasets [7]. In the field of healthcare, ML is gradually emerging and becoming a powerful complement to traditional diagnostic and treatment methods. In recent years, machine learning has made remarkable progress in biomarker screening and disease prediction. For example, Gaffar et al. proposed a machine learning-based method (IF-AIP) for identifying anti-inflammatory peptides [8]. This method significantly improved the ability to identify anti-inflammatory peptides through a multi-feature fusion strategy and a voting classifier. Similarly, Hassan et al. developed an ensemble learning framework (NaII-Pred) for identifying sodium ion inhibitors [9]. In addition, ensemble learning techniques can also be used to predict IL-4-inducing peptides and antioxidant peptides [10, 11]. All these studies indicate that ML methods have great potential in biomarker screening and disease prediction. Logistic regression can effectively estimate the probability of an event occurring by establishing a linear relationship model between the dependent variable and independent variables. Random forest, which combines the bagging technique and feature subset selection, has high generalization ability and robustness in high-dimensional medical data. AdaBoost, through the iterative optimization of the weight allocation of weak classifiers, is particularly suitable for dealing with class imbalance problems. Support vector machines perform excellently in handling complex nonlinear classification tasks. Naive Bayes, based on the theory of probability and the assumption of conditional independence, shows broad application potential in multi-class medical classification tasks. These methods provide important technical support for precision medicine and intelligent diagnosis. Although currently, due to issues such as uneven data quality and insufficient model interpretability, ML cannot yet serve as the primary tool in most clinical settings, its role in assisting disease discovery, diagnosis, and treatment is becoming increasingly prominent [12]. Traditional statistical methods have limitations in identifying complex non-linear relationships and high-dimensional data interactions. In contrast, ML has demonstrated advantages in biomarker discovery. However, its application in the study of UTI cytokines is still in its preliminary stage. This study aimed to utilize five ML algorithms to identify cytokines strongly correlated with urinary tract infection patients in our cohort, thereby suggesting potential candidate biomarkers that warrant further validation.

Methods

Clinical data

This study adopted a cross-sectional study design. This study included 131 patients treated at our institution for urinary tract infections, comprising 69 males and 62 females, as well as 437 non-urinary tract infection cases, consisting of 285 males and 152 females.

The inclusion criteria for this experiment were as follows: a diagnosis of urinary tract infection, the presence of symptoms such as urinary pain, urgency, and frequency in the observation group, positive results in urine culture or positive leukocyte esterase detected by laboratory tests, good self-care ability, and the signing of informed consent. A diagnosis of non-urinary tract infection was the exclusion criterion for this experiment. Fourteen variables (age, gender, IL-2, IL-6, IL-1β, IL-8, TNF-α, IL-5, IFN-α, IFN-γ, IL-17, IL-12P70, IL-4, IL-10) were all collected immediately upon the patients’ admission to the hospital or at the time of their initial diagnosis, and not repeatedly collected after treatment, so as to ensure the timeliness of the data.

ML prediction model establishment

We performed data preprocessing on the gathered information. For the processing of missing values, the Multivariate Imputation by Chained Equations (MICE) package was used. Five sets of imputed data sets were generated through prediction models (such as linear regression and logistic regression), and the mean value was taken as the final result. Fields with a missing value rate of more than 30% were directly removed, and the remaining variables were retained and imputed. The data was then randomly split into training and testing sets at a 7:3 ratio. Subsequently, the training set was standardized, and characteristics were pruned if they met any of the following criteria: too low variance, low label correlation, or high multicollinearity. Among them, continuous variables are standardized using the Z-score method (with a mean of 0 and a standard deviation of 1), and categorical variables (such as gender) are encoded using one-hot encoding. For the problem of class imbalance (UTI: non-UTI ≈ 1:3.3), the ROSE package was used for undersampling within the training set to balance the number of samples in the majority class and the minority class. We carried out 10-fold cross-validation accompanied by 10 repetitions and grid searching on the training set to choose the optimum parameters for five classification algorithms (LR: logistic regression; RF: random forest; AdaBoost: Adaptive Boosting; SVM: support vector machine; NB: naive Bayes). The data was trained with the five classification algorithms, and models were built.

Subsequently, the test set is used to evaluate the model. The performance of each model was evaluated using assessment metrics such as the AUROC, sensitivity (Sens), specificity (Spec), precision, recall, F1-score, and the AUPRC. Subsequently, different model predictions were obtained, and the final model score was determined by averaging accuracy across several iterations. Confusion matrices and the final model were generated. Plotting of Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves for training and testing outcomes of each model led to the calculation of the area under the curve (AUC). The best model was determined by comparing the AUC values, followed by an analysis of the contribution of features to this model. The random forest model calculates the variable importance based on the reduction in Gini impurity, while other models (such as Adaboost) use Permutation Importance. The feature importance analysis is based on the data of the training set and does not involve the test set to avoid evaluation bias.

Statistic analysis

Our statistical analysis was conducted through the R package (V4.2.2). Homogeneity of variances and normality of measurement data were examined. Normally distributed measurement data were presented as mean ± standard deviation and compared between groups by using t-tests. Group differences were evaluated using χ2 tests, and count data were reported as instances (%). Differences were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05.

Results

Comparison of clinical features

Our study included a total of 131 UTI patients and 437 non-UTI individuals, with a predominance of male patients. UTI patients tended to be older, with the majority around the age of 68, while non-UTI individuals were mostly around 51 years old. There were notable differences in the levels of inflammatory factors between these two groups, particularly with higher levels of IL-6 and IL-10 in UTI patients as compared to non-UTI individuals, and this difference was statistically significant (p < 0.05). UTI patients also exhibited higher levels of IL-2, IL-8, TNF-α, IFN-γ, IL-17, IL-12P70, and IL-4 compared to non-UTI individuals, while IL-5 and IFN-α levels were lower in UTI patients, but the differences between the two groups were relatively small (p > 0.05) (Table 1).

Establishment of ML prediction models

Comprehensive analysis revealed that the random forest model achieved higher AUROC and Sens, while the support vector machine demonstrated superior Spec (Fig. 1). Additionally, we reported the AUROC, precision, recall, F1-score, and area under the precision-recall curve (AUPRC) for the five models. Among these, the random forest model had the highest AUROC and AUPRC values, at 0.836 and 0.942, respectively. The support vector machine had the highest precision at 0.928, whereas the best recall and F1-score were attained by naive Bayes, with 0.840 and 0.841, respectively (Table 2). By comparing the precision values of each model, we can find that there are differences in the performance of different models when dealing with the problem of imbalanced data. The precision of the random forest model is 0.918, which also shows good performance in identifying patients with UTIs, but it is slightly lower than that of the support vector machine model. The precision of the AdaBoost model is 0.909, and the precision of the LR model is 0.869. These differences reflect the characteristics of different algorithms in learning data features and handling imbalanced data. In summary, the random forest model exhibited the best performance and demonstrated strong predictive capabilities.

Model validation

In this section, our model evaluation was conducted with a test dataset. A frequently used method to evaluate the prediction ability of classifier models for classification problems is the confusion matrix. Building upon the confusion matrix as a foundation, ROC curves were constructed, with the highest AUC values observed for the Random Forest and AdaBoost models (Figs. 3 and 4). In the context of classification tasks, the Precision-Recall Curve (PRC) serves as a graphical representation to evaluate the classifier models’ prediction power. The Random Forest and AdaBoost models exhibited higher AUPRC values among the five models (Fig. 5). The calibration curve plot indicates minimal errors between the predicted values of the model and the ground truth, indicating high accuracy (Fig. 6).

Discussion

UTI, a disease characterized by inflammatory responses in the urinary tract, represents a severe impact on patients’ reproductive function. Incidence of this disease is steadily rising and affecting a younger demographic [13]. Clinical manifestations primarily involve inflammatory reactions in the urethra and bladder, with pathogenic microorganisms causing inflammation in the renal parenchyma and renal pelvis [14]. As the disease progresses, it can develop into chronic UTI, increasing physiological and psychological burdens on the body. ML can quickly and accurately predict the occurrence of UTI by analyzing a large amount of clinical data. For example, in a study by Taylor et al., more than 80,000 samples and over 200 features were used, and an 87.5% prediction accuracy was achieved with an XGBoost classifier [15]. ML can also be used to identify biomarkers for UTI, thereby improving the accuracy of diagnosis. For instance, Gadalla et al. analyzed the clinical and immunological factors of female patients using ML algorithms and found that MMP9, NGAL, IL-8/CXCL8, and IL-1β in urine were the most valuable biomarkers for predicting UTI [16]. The aim of this study is to screen cytokines that are strongly correlated with UTI patients through five ML algorithms, with the expectation of identifying potential biomarkers for UTI in this cohort.

During the course of UTI, the activation of inflammatory responses is characterized by the abundant secretion of various inflammatory cytokines [17]. Acute UTI induces robust inflammatory responses in mice, including a significant increase in cytokines and other secretory factors such as TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-10, GM-CSF, and MCP-1 [18, 19]. Other studies have also reported an increase in certain cytokines, including IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, and IL-8 during UTI [17]. Clinical data analysis in this study implied a significant increase in IL-6 and IL-10 levels amid UTI patients as compared to non-UTI individuals.

IL-6 serves as a common indicator of acute infection, initiating a systemic cascade of inflammatory signaling in the early stages of trauma or infection [20]. It plays a pivotal role in inducing the production of acute-phase proteins. In the context of inflammatory responses, elevated levels of IL-6 occur earlier and persist longer compared to other cytokines, rendering it an effective marker for aiding in the diagnosis of acute infections [21]. Following certain inflammatory stimuli in the body, cell factors secreted by T cells and endothelial cells modulate the body’s defense responses, intensifying the inflammatory reaction induced by tumor necrosis factor. In bacterial UTI patients, IL-6 is primarily produced and released by uroepithelial cells and neutrophils in response to bacterial stimulation, resulting in a significant increase in IL-6 levels [22]. IL-10 promotes immune suppression, mitigating inflammation mechanisms that could potentially harm the host. Studies indicate that urinary IL-10 levels are elevated in UPEC cystitis patients, and the absence of IL-10 in mice exacerbates the host’s ability to control bacterial colonization during the early stages of bladder infection [23]. In order to limit inflammation and make bladder infections easier to treat, UPEC-caused UTIs require IL-10 as an early innate immune response [24].

Clinical data modeling analysis refers to the process of statistically analyzing, modeling, and interpreting extensive data collected from clinical trials and studies. With clinical predictive models, healthcare professionals and patients could make better collaborative decisions, and clinical researchers can more accurately select suitable study subjects. In this study, clinical data from UTI and non-UTI patients were analyzed through five ML algorithms, leading to the establishment of a UTI prediction model based on this cohort. Among these models, the random forest model achieved the highest AUROC and AUPRC values, indicating favorable predictive performance in our cohort. By evaluating the importance of variables through the random forest model, it was found that age, IFN-γ, IL-6, IL-2, and IL-10 had relatively high importance scores and ranked among the top. The confusion matrix, ROC curve, and PR curve demonstrated the superior predictive performance of the random forest model, and the calibration curve suggested its good calibration in this dataset.

INF-γ functions a crucial player in the early host defense response against pathogen infection, being produced by Th1 cells. Its activity serves as a functional indicator of Th1 cells, promoting differentiation from Th0 to Th1, inhibiting viral replication, activating macrophages to enhance phagocytosis, clearing pathogens, promoting tissue repair, participating in B-cell antibody class switching, and exerting various regulatory effects on suppressing Th2 in delayed-type hypersensitivity reactions [25–27]. IFN-γ is involved in delayed-type hypersensitivity reactions and cellular immune responses, playing a significant role in the progression of renal diseases. Lower levels of IFN-γ are associated with impaired immune responses and renal tubulointerstitial damage during the progression of chronic kidney diseases [28, 29]. Interferon-γ is expressed in the bladder, kidneys, and neutrophils, participating in cytokine signaling during UTI [30]. IFN-γ-deficient knockout mice are more susceptible to UTIs compared to immunocompetent mice [31]. Acute focal bacterial nephritis (AFBN) is a severe form of upper UTIs, and IFN-γ and IL-6 levels may be the most effective in distinguishing AFBN from acute pyelonephritis [32]. Administration of INF-γ extends the survival of experimental Escherichia coli pyelonephritis in rabbits [33]. These findings highlighted the pivotal function of INF-γ in UTIs. IL-2 plays an important role in the immune response. It is mainly produced by activated T cells and promotes the proliferation and differentiation of T cells [34]. In urinary tract infections, IL-2 may help eliminate pathogens by enhancing the T cell-mediated immune response. In this study, IL-2 has a relatively high importance score, indicating its potential value as a biomarker in the immune response of UTI. In addition, consistent with the results of the clinical data analysis in this study, the random forest model identified IL-6 and IL-10 as important inflammatory cytokines. Generally speaking, IL-6 and IL-10 play a dual role in UTI. IL-6 helps resist infections by promoting the inflammatory response, while IL-10 regulates the immune response, prevents excessive inflammation, and maintains the balance of the immune system. The balance of these two cytokines is crucial for the control of urinary tract infections.

However, this study has certain limitations. In terms of model performance, even though 10-fold cross-validation repeated 10 times was adopted, complex models (such as random forest and support vector machine) still carry the risk of overfitting, which affects their generalization ability. Regarding the generalization of the research results, the sample has a single source in terms of region and hospital, limiting the universality of the results. Only a limited number of clinical variables and cytokines were considered in the data, and there may be errors in the measurement of some variables, which affects the accuracy of the model and the generalization of the research results. The five machine learning algorithms compared in this study fail to cover all the models applicable to the prediction of urinary tract infections. As a result, the research findings are restricted by the selected models.

Conclusions

This study utilized five ML methods to analyze and model the data related to UTIs, aiming to screen the biomarkers associated with UTIs and construct a prediction model. The results showed that the random forest model exhibited the best performance in predicting UTIs in our cohort, suggesting its potential utility. The random forest model identified that cytokines such as IFN-γ, IL-6, IL-2, and IL-10 had relatively high importance scores in UTIs. These findings offer preliminary insights that could inform future studies on the clinical relevance of these biomarkers. If validated in future studies, detecting the levels of these cytokines could potentially provide clinicians with additional diagnostic evidence to support early identification of UTIs. Similarly, these biomarkers may offer insights for the development of new therapeutic targets. Future research can further validate the effectiveness of these biomarkers in larger samples and different clinical scenarios, and explore how to better integrate the ML model into the clinical workflow.

Novel deep learning radiomics nomogram-based multiparametric MRI for predicting the lymph node metastasis in rectal cancer: A dual-center study
Authors: Yunjun Yang, Zhenyu Xu, Zhiping Cai, Hai Zhao, Cuiling Zhu, Julu Hong, Ruiliang Lu, Xiaoyu Lai, Li Guo, Qiugen Hu, Zhifeng Xu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo develop and evaluate a nomogram that integrates clinical parameters with deep learning radiomics (DLR) extracted from Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) data to enhance the predictive accuracy for preoperative lymph node (LN) metastasis in rectal cancer.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on 356 patients diagnosed with rectal cancer. Of these, 286 patients were allocated to the training set, and 70 patients comprised the external validation cohort. Preprocessed T2-weighted and diffusion-weighted imaging performed preoperatively facilitated the extraction of DLR features. Five machine learning algorithms—k-nearest neighbor, light gradient boosting machine, logistic regression, random forest, and support vector machine—were utilized to develop DLR models. The most effective algorithm was identified and used to establish a clinical DLR (CDLR) nomogram specifically designed to predict LN metastasis in rectal cancer. The performance of the nomogram was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic curve analysis.ResultsThe logistic regression classifier demonstrated significant predictive accuracy using the DLR signature, achieving an Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.919 in the training cohort and 0.778 in the external validation cohort. The integrated CDLR nomogram exhibited robust predictive performance across both datasets, with AUC values of 0.921 in the training cohort and 0.818 in the external validation cohort. Notably, it outperformed both the clinical model, which had AUC values of 0.770 and 0.723 in the training and external validation cohorts, respectively, and the stand-alone DLR model.ConclusionThe nomogram derived from multiparametric MRI data, referred to as the CDLR model, demonstrates strong predictive efficacy in forecasting LN metastasis in rectal cancer.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00432-024-05986-x.Keywords: Rectal cancer, Lymph node metastasis, Deep learning, Radiomics, Nomogram

Introduction

Colorectal cancer is globally positioned as the third most prevalent malignancy, standing as the fourth leading contributor to cancer-related mortality. Notably, within this spectrum, rectal cancer constitutes a significant proportion, accounting for 30–35% of diagnosed cases (Siegel et al. 2023; Glynne-Jones et al. 2017). The dissemination of cancer primarily occurs through lymph node (LN) metastasis, playing a pivotal role in determining prognosis and shaping treatment strategies (Chang et al. 2007; Hazen et al. 2022). Recent investigations highlight a concerning trend of younger onset in rectal cancer, with 31.3% of newly diagnosed cases presenting with local spread or LN metastasis, often resulting in unfavorable clinical outcomes (Lang et al. 2022). Diverse prognoses are observed across different N stages, warranting varied extents of lymphadenectomy or neoadjuvant treatments. Therefore, accurate prediction of LN metastasis among patients holds considerable promise in optimizing medical decision-making.

Multiparametric magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is extensively employed in clinical practice for the preoperative evaluation of LNs and the staging of N in cancer (Horvat et al. 2019; Gröne et al. 2018). Characteristic indicators of LN metastasis on MRI include enlarged and round-shaped LNs, heterogeneous enhancement, and restricted diffusion observed in imaging. However, this methodology may miss metastases within normal-sized LNs, while the reliable differentiation between inflammatory LN enlargement and tumor metastatic nodes remains elusive with routine MRI assessments (Langman et al. 2015). Furthermore, the accurate interpretation of morphological features on MRI heavily relies on the expertise of individual radiologists. The diagnostic precision of MRI in detecting LN metastasis is reported to be less than 70%, revealing deficiencies in its reliability.

In recent years, machine learning has attracted substantial interest in medical imaging, demonstrating its capacity to extract various quantitative features from medical images using methods like radiomics and deep learning (DL), particularly excelling at tumor analysis (Gao et al. 2021; Hosny et al. 2018). Prior studies have devised radiomics models aiming to predict LN metastasis in rectal cancer patients and have enhanced the prediction of peritoneal metastasis (Li et al. 2021; Yan et al. 2024; Chen et al. 2018). However, challenges persist due to the diversity in radiomics research and limited exploration in DL (Bedrikovetski et al. 2021). Certainly, impediments restrict the clinical utility of these models, encompassing limitations like small sample sizes, dependence on singular MRI parameters, and insufficient external independent validation. Several investigations merging DL radiomics (DLR) have shown promising outcomes (Zhang et al. 2023; Song et al. 2023). Consequently, the utilization of the DLR model emerges as a promising avenue for anticipating LN metastasis in individuals diagnosed with rectal cancer.

Therefore, the objective of this current investigation is to develop and validate an all-encompassing clinical DL radiomics (CDLR) nomogram. This innovative model utilizes multiparametric MRI data to provide prognostic insights into the likelihood of LN metastasis in patients diagnosed with rectal cancer. The comprehensive nature of this nomogram integrates clinical and radiomic parameters, offering a holistic approach to predicting LN involvement.

Materials and methods

Patients

This retrospective investigation, formally approved by the institutional review committee overseeing dual participating centers. Recognizing the observational and retrospective nature of the study, the local ethics committee waived the necessity for informed consent. The inclusion criteria meticulously considered cases with postoperative pathological confirmation of rectal cancer, ensuring a robust foundation for analysis. Additionally, the criteria required the absence of any preoperative treatments before MRI scans and specified that the interval between the MRI examination and subsequent surgery be less than two weeks to maintain temporal relevance. The exclusion criteria were carefully crafted to uphold data integrity, excluding cases with incomplete clinical or pathological data. Patients who had undergone preoperative radiotherapy or chemotherapy were also excluded, as were those with suboptimal image quality in T2-weighted imaging (T2WI) or diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI).

A comprehensive review was conducted on a cohort of 365 histopathologically confirmed cases of rectal cancer obtained from two distinct medical centers. The training set included 286 patients treated at Center I from July 2017 to June 2023. The external validation cohort comprised 70 patients treated at Center II between June 2018 and December 2022. Additional details regarding the inclusion and exclusion criteria, as well as the careful selection process, are elaborated upon in the graphical representation presented in Fig. 1.

Patient recruitment workflow

Clinicopathological characteristics

The foundational clinicopathological characteristics integral to our research were meticulously sourced from medical records. These included crucial details such as carbohydrate antigen 19 − 9 (CA19-9), carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), gender, age, and LN status as indicated by MRI. Notably, in alignment with our institutional guidelines, the standard ranges for CEA and CA19-9 were defined as 0–5 ng/ml and 0–37 U/ml, respectively (Zhao et al. 2023). Two radiologists thoroughly assessed MRI scans to determine the LN metastasis status in rectal cancer, evaluating distinct characteristics of the target LNs, such as edges, signal intensity, and morphology before surgical intervention (Beets-Tan et al. 2017). In cases where discrepancies arose between radiologists, a collaborative review was conducted to achieve consensus on the diagnosis.

MRI data acquisition

Acquiring MRI data from two distinguished medical institutions in China, we employed advanced 1.5-T MRI scanners, including Optima MR360 (GE Healthcare, Waukesha, WI, USA) and Ingenia (Philips Medical Systems, Best, Netherlands). Additionally, we utilized state-of-the-art 3.0-T MRI scanners, namely Signa HDxt (GE Healthcare, Milwaukee, WI, USA), Discovery MR750w (GE Healthcare, Milwaukee, WI, USA), and Ingenia (Philips Medical Systems, Best, Netherlands). This strategic selection of MRI scanners aimed to ensure a comprehensive and high-quality dataset for our investigation. The imaging sequences used in the study covered crucial axial imaging sequences, specifically T1CE, T1WI, T2WI, and DWI MRI schemes. This multifaceted approach was chosen to provide a thorough and nuanced evaluation of rectal cancer, capturing diverse aspects for a more holistic understanding. For detailed information regarding the employed MRI protocol, we direct interested readers to consult the Supplemental Material (Table S1).

Image preprocessing and segmentation

In order to address potential data bias, we implemented the N4-bias field correction (Tustison et al. 2010) uniformly on all MRI images. This correction was conducted using the ‘SimpleITK’ toolkit, available at https://www.simpleitk.org. Furthermore, to mitigate the influence of anisotropy, we standardized the voxel size to 1 × 1 × 1 mm³ for all images and regions of interest (ROI) via linear interpolation. Subsequently, normalization of image signal intensities was carried out with the assistance of the ‘PyRadiomics’ software package, accessible at https://pyradiomics.readthedocs.io. Image segmentation was conducted by a radiologist, X.Z.Y, with 2 years of professional experience, using the freely accessible software 3D Slicer version 4.10, available at www.slicer.org. All segmentations were subsequently verified by a senior radiologist, X.Z.F, with a decade of expertise. Any discrepancies were resolved through consensus-driven discussions. Notably, both professionals conducted the segmentation process while blinded to the diagnostic results. Additionally, another radiologist, Y.Y.J, with 5 years of experience, independently conducted tumor segmentation on 40 randomly selected lesions to evaluate observer repeatability (Shin et al. 2022).

Feature extraction

Figure 2 illustrates the procedural workflow implemented in our study. Initially, we utilized the Pyradiomics Python software package, version 3.0.1, to extract radiomics features from the tumor region of interest in T2WI and DWI sequences for each patient. The extracted features spanned diverse categories, encompassing Shape features (n = 14), First-order features (n = 360), Gray level co-occurrence matrix features (n = 440), Gray-level run length matrix features (n = 320), Gray-level size zone matrix features (n = 320), Gray level dependence matrix features (n = 280), and Neighboring gray-tone difference matrix features (n = 100). Detailed definitions for these features can be accessed online at https://pyradiomics.readthedocs.io/en/latest/features.html. After systematic categorization, each MRI sequence (T2WI and DWI) contributed to the extraction of a combined set of 1834 features, resulting in a comprehensive dataset of 3668 radiomics features for each patient. This detailed approach ensured a robust foundation for subsequent analyses and model development.

Flow chart of this study

The subsequent feature extraction process involves DL. Initially, images capturing the maximum cross-sectional area of 356 tumors sourced from two centers were utilized. For this study, the DL model employed was ResNet50, pretrained on the ImageNet dataset. ResNet50, recognized for its fusion of deep functionality and clinical information, emerged as the optimal choice in terms of performance and memory usage. Fine-tuning was applied to the two-dimensional (2D) convolutional neural network (CNN), specifically the CNN-resnet50, initializing it with pretrained weights. A 2D CNN model was implemented to extract features, with a specific emphasis on utilizing the average pooling layer from the trained CNN to delineate DL features. The compression of features from each MRI sequence resulted in the extraction of 180 deep features. Consequently, each patient yielded a total of 360 deep features, contributing to the construction of the DL signature. To enhance transparency in the model’s decision-making process and explore its interpretability, CNN activation maps were employed to visualize the model.

Feature selection

To effectively address the inherent challenges posed by high-dimensional features, a meticulously designed series of methodical steps was implemented. Preliminary to feature selection, a normalization procedure utilizing the z-score method was applied to ensure uniformity across all features. In the initial phase of our analysis, emphasis was placed on selecting radiomics features with a robust Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC) exceeding 0.75, signifying a high level of reliability and consistency. Subsequent to this initial filtering, the Mann-Whitney U test was systematically employed on all features, retaining only those with a statistical significance reflected by a P-value below 0.05. Moreover, Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient was harnessed to scrutinize inter-feature correlations, providing insights into the relationships within the dataset. To achieve a finer level of refinement, the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) was introduced, contributing to the identification and preservation of key features essential for subsequent analyses. This comprehensive approach ensured not only the reduction of dimensionality but also the retention of pertinent information, fostering a robust foundation for subsequent stages in the analytical process.

DLR and clinical signature construction

In the quest for the most suitable machine learning algorithm, a thorough evaluation and comparison were conducted across five well-established methods: k-nearest neighbor (KNN), support vector machine (SVM), light gradient boosting machine (Light GBM), logistic regression (LR), and random forest (RF). The primary emphasis of this assessment centered on the computation of the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), a key metric, for both the training and external validation sets. Having identified the most effective machine learning algorithm, a distinctive signature encompassing features from DLR was systematically crafted. This signature aimed to leverage the inherent capabilities of the chosen algorithm for enhanced predictive accuracy. Following this, a dual-phase analytical approach was adopted. In the preliminary stages of our investigation, univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were employed to identify clinical and pathological features linked to LN metastasis in rectal cancer. Subsequently, a clinical model was developed through a meticulous multiple logistic regression process, selectively integrating characteristics that demonstrated statistical significance at a threshold of P < 0.05. This comprehensive methodology not only ensured the identification of the optimal machine learning algorithm but also facilitated the integration of essential clinical and pathological features into the subsequent modeling process, thereby enriching the overall predictive framework.

Clinical DLR nomogram construction and verification

We integrated the clinical signature with the DLR signature derived from the optimal machine learning classifier to develop a CDLR nomogram. The practical applicability of the model was evaluated using decision curve analysis (DCA), and the differences in AUC values among the various models were compared using the Delong test.

Statistical analysis

For the assessment of quantitative variables, t-tests were conducted, while qualitative variables were analyzed using χ2 tests or Fisher’s exact tests. Interobserver repeatability was quantitatively evaluated using the Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC), with an ICC value exceeding 0.75 indicating a commendable level of agreement between observers. The effectiveness of the models was assessed using various metrics, including the Area Under the Curve (AUC), accuracy, specificity, and sensitivity. Model performances were compared using the Delong test, a robust statistical method for distinguishing between different models. This comprehensive analytical framework upholds rigorous scientific standards, ensuring a thorough evaluation of both model performance and statistical significance. The incorporation of decision curve analysis (DCA) adds depth to the assessment of clinical utility, highlighting the practical implications of the developed models. All statistical analyses were meticulously performed using Python software, version 3.7.14, available at https://www.python.org. This approach enhances the transparency and reproducibility of the study’s methodology, ensuring a robust and reliable foundation for data interpretation and future research endeavors. The predefined threshold for statistical significance was set at P < 0.05, reinforcing the credibility and robustness of the study’s findings.

The extensive pool of radiomics features, initially totaling 3668, underwent a rigorous refinement process through the application of the LASSO algorithm. This meticulous approach culminated in the identification of 22 potential predictive factors. Simultaneously, the set of DL features, initially comprising 360, underwent a similar reduction, resulting in the identification of 22 potential predictive factors. Visual representation of this feature selection process is provided in Fig. 3a and b, with supplementary insights into the predictive capabilities detailed in Supplementary Table S2. After the feature selection process, we meticulously developed a predictive model using the identified features. Notably, the DL features demonstrated a more substantial predictive influence compared to radiomics features, a distinction highlighted in Fig. 3c. The subsequent phase of model refinement involved the creation of a prediction model employing five diverse machine learning algorithms. Comprehensive details regarding the predictive performance of the DLR signature are thoughtfully presented in Table 2. Among the diverse classifiers explored, the logistic regression (LR) model demonstrated superior predictive prowess, achieving notable AUC values of 0.919 with a 95% confidence interval of 0.887 to 0.950 for the training cohort and 0.778 with a 95% confidence interval of 0.640 to 0.914 for the external validation cohort.

Interpretability of DLR model

In order to augment the interpretability of the model, we incorporated the Gradient Weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) method for visualization. We employed the gradient information from the final convolutional layer of the convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to perform a weighted fusion, generating a class activation map that highlights key regions relevant to the classification target image (Selvaraju et al. 2020). The transparency of the final convolutional layer’s activation, as illustrated in Fig. 5, emphasizes regions critical for predicting LN status. The CNN activation map reveals significant regions crucial for predicting LN metastasis in rectal cancer, predominantly focusing on the internal and boundary areas of the tumor, with minimal activation observed in adjacent lumens and surrounding tumor regions. On T2WI and DWI sequences, patients with positive LN metastasis exhibited more extensive red areas compared to those with negative LN metastasis. This finding partially supports the effectiveness of the model. Grad-CAM is particularly valuable as it generates coarse localization maps, highlighting essential regions relevant to the classification target (Zheng et al. 2020). To enhance the clarity of the decision-making process within our model, we employ a methodology that not only elucidates its intricate mechanisms but also scrutinizes the pivotal features influencing its predictions. This approach aims to provide a more profound understanding of the guiding principles behind the model’s predictive outcomes.

Discussion

This study devised and verified a tailored preoperative nomogram using multiparametric MRI data, integrating clinical factors (like CEA levels and MRI-reported LN status) alongside radiomics and DL features. The nomogram demonstrated notable performance with an AUC of 0.921 in the training cohort and 0.818 in the external validation cohort. This outperformed both the clinical and DLR models, emphasizing its superior predictive accuracy. More significantly, the nomogram demonstrated heightened clinical efficacy, enabling personalized anticipation of LN metastasis status in rectal cancer patients before undergoing surgical intervention.

Despite its prevalent application in rectal cancer staging, high-resolution MRI often falls short in precisely predicting LN metastasis. Recent retrospective studies have emphasized the moderate accuracy of MRI in predicting LN metastasis in rectal cancer, particularly in the detection of small metastatic nodes (< 3 mm) (Park et al. 2014). Notably, evaluations based on LN size using MRI morphological criteria did not significantly enhance LN staging accuracy (Gröne et al. 2018). Our investigation reaffirms that the LR classifier model, incorporating CEA levels and MRI-reported LN status, demonstrates moderate accuracy in discerning patients with LN involvement. It achieves an AUC of 0.770 (training cohort) and 0.723 (external validation cohort), indicating slight improvements over previous research findings (Wei et al. 2023; Li et al. 2020). Consequently, relying solely on preoperative MRI assessments inadequately identifies LN involvement.

Prior research has emphasized the effectiveness of MRI-based radiomics in discerning the status of LN metastasis in rectal cancer (Chen et al. 2018; Bedrikovetski et al. 2021; Zhao et al. 2023; Jia et al. 2022). Noteworthy among these studies is one that leveraged MRI radiomics to predict LN metastasis, introducing a predictive nomogram that demonstrated robust performance (AUC = 0.90). Importantly, this nomogram outperformed clinico-radiological (AUC = 0.83) or radiomics signature-only models in the training cohort (Yang et al. 2021). Recent developments in this field have been exemplified by various studies, among them one that underscored the accuracy of a radiomics model utilizing high-resolution amide proton transfer-weighted images and T2WI to predict the metastasis of LN in rectal cancer. This specific investigation highlighted the superior performance of a combined clinical radiomics model, achieving commendable area under the curve (AUC) values of 0.900 and 0.929 in the validation and test sets, respectively (Wei et al. 2023). Additionally, a recent study developed and validated multiparametric MRI-based radiomic models utilizing features from primary tumors to predict lymph node metastasis (LNM) following neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy in patients with locally advanced rectal cancer. This study, involving 150 patients from two centers, achieved a predictive performance with an Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.831, surpassing the performance of single-sequence and clinical models (Wei et al. 2024). Despite these promising findings, a critical limitation persists—these studies lack comprehensive verification of the applicability of their models across large sample sizes or external validation sets. This underscores the need for further research to validate and generalize the effectiveness of MRI-based radiomics models in diverse clinical settings.

DL has proven its effectiveness in detecting imaging anomalies and has been successfully employed in identifying tumor LN metastasis (Dong et al. 2020; Fu et al. 2023). Within DL methods, CNNs have emerged prominently, showcasing significant potential across various clinical tasks like disease classification, diagnosis, and prognosis prediction. Variations in performance among different CNN models often stem from differences in the network’s internal architecture. ResNet50, a widely used and classic CNN in clinical research, has demonstrated promising outcomes. Studies conducted by Chougrad et al. (Chougrad et al. 2018) revealed that ResNet50 surpassed Visual Geometry Group (VGG) during the optimization process, exhibiting notable accuracy. Similarly, in the differentiation of benign and malignant breast tumors, Zhou and colleagues (Zhou et al. 2020) exhibited noteworthy diagnostic accuracy by employing ResNet50 in DL. In our study, employing fine-tuned GoogLeNet features combined with logistic regression algorithms yielded commendable classification results. This success indicates that depth features extracted from MRI scans of rectal cancer patients through fine-tuning two-dimensional CNNs can effectively predict LN status. The use of GoogLeNet for enhanced computational efficiency can improve effectiveness while retaining crucial spatial information (Sekhar et al. 2022). Additionally, our study observed that ResNet50’s Grad-CAM visualization highlighted two crucial regions within the tumor (boundary and internal regions), aligning with radiologists’ points of interest. This finding partially supports the interpretability and effectiveness of the DL model.

The LR classifier, utilizing DLR features, stands out as the most effective among the five machine learning approaches scrutinized in the current study. The integration of DLR features has exhibited promising capabilities in the analysis of LN metastasis in breast cancer patients, as evidenced by prior research (Zheng et al. 2020). To ensure the methodological robustness of our investigation, we deliberately selected five commonly employed machine learning classifier algorithms, encompassing LR, LightGBM, KNN, SVM, and RF. In our study, the LR algorithm, incorporating both radiomics and DL features, demonstrated commendable performance with an AUC of 0.921 (training cohort) and 0.818 (external validation cohort). Notably, the model constructed on radiomics and DL features surpassed the accuracy of other binary classifiers. LR, renowned as a classic machine learning algorithm, is favored for its ease of implementation, interpretability, and effective training capabilities (Zhang et al. 2023; Książek et al. 2021). Nevertheless, it is imperative to recognize that the classifier’s effectiveness is intricately linked to the arrangement of both the training and validation sets within the sampled dataset. This recognition emphasizes the importance of carefully considering the dataset composition to optimize the LR classifier’s performance.

This study innovatively devised a nomogram by combining DL and radiomics features with preoperative clinical risk factors, thereby establishing its clinical utility. Our investigation uncovered that the amalgamated assessment of preoperative CEA levels and MRI findings led to a reduction in overall diagnostic efficacy. However, in comparison to individual radiologist assessments, the fusion of clinical parameters with the DLR method harnessed a comprehensive dataset that included clinical pathology and MRI information. This strategic integration significantly elevated the diagnostic performance for predicting rectal cancer metastasis, surpassing traditional MRI evaluations conducted by experienced radiologists (Wei et al. 2023). Significantly, the clinical-DLR combined model exhibited the highest AUC and yielded superior net benefits across various threshold probabilities in DCA. This accomplishment outshone single-feature models and other combined approaches. Henceforth, our nomogram emerges as a prospective non-invasive instrument for the preoperative prediction of LN metastasis in rectal cancer, harboring the potential for transformative implications in clinical applications.

This investigation has several limitations, and one key aspect is its reliance on historical data, potentially introducing selection bias inherent in this retrospective study design. Secondly, although the training cohort included a substantial number of patients, the external validation cohort comprised a comparatively smaller sample size. Further validation through prospective studies is essential to robustly establish and confirm the predictive capacity of our proposed model. Third, this study emphasizes predicting LN metastasis using radiomics features extracted from the primary tumor, rather than incorporating features from the surrounding LNs. Future research could benefit from integrating both primary tumor and LN radiomics features to potentially enhance predictive accuracy. Finally, the potential of the CDLR nomogram in extending its predictive capabilities to detailed staging and specifying the anatomical location of LN metastasis remains a fertile ground for exploration. Consequently, there is a clear imperative for additional investigations to delve deeper into these aspects, providing a more comprehensive understanding of the nomogram’s potential clinical utility.

Conclusion

Our study successfully developed a nomogram that integrates clinical attributes with DLR features. This combined model, referred to as the CDLR nomogram, demonstrates significant predictive efficacy for LN metastasis in rectal cancer. This advancement not only provides valuable insights but also establishes a foundation for enhancing personalized preoperative prognostication strategies for patients with rectal cancer.

Novel non-viral in vivo CAR-T therapies: latest updates from the 2025 ASH annual meeting
Authors: Bin Xue, Yifan Liu, Aibin Liang, Wenjun Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The field of chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-T cell therapy is undergoing a paradigm shift from complex ex vivo manufacturing to direct in vivo generation of CAR-T cells. This innovative approach leverages non-viral delivery platforms to reprogram a patient’s own immune cells in situ, promising to overcome critical barriers of cost, scalability, and accessibility. The 2025 American Society of Hematology (ASH) Annual Meeting served as a showcase for groundbreaking preclinical data across a diverse array of non-viral technologies, including advanced lipid nanoparticles (LNPs), virus-like particles (VLPs), and polymeric nanoparticles. This correspondence summarizes the latest reports on these platforms, highlighting their potential to revolutionize the treatment of both autoimmune diseases and hematological malignancies.

To the editor,

Conventional ex vivo CAR-T cell therapies have demonstrated profound efficacy in B-cell malignancies and are now showing promise in autoimmune conditions like SLE. They are limited by complex manufacturing, requisite lymphodepletion, and safety issues like prolonged B-cell aplasia. While early in vivo viral methods reduce complexity, they retain genomic integration risks. In contrast, novel non-viral in vivo CAR-T platforms, utilizing synthetic vectors like LNPs to deliver genetic payloads systemically, offer a safer profile by avoiding permanent integration and enabling repeatable dosing. Recent high-impact reviews have outlined the considerable potential and technical challenges of this approach, including achieving efficient in vivo delivery to resting immune cells and managing transient versus persistent CAR expression [1–4]. The 2025 ASH meeting featured a wealth of data underscoring the rapid maturation of this field, with multiple platforms demonstrating robust efficacy and favorable safety profiles in preclinical models, bringing this transformative modality closer to clinical reality.

LNP-based platforms for in vivo CAR-T engineering

Grit Biotechnology’s GT801 platform employs T-cell-targeted LNPs conjugated via its proprietary technology. This system delivers linear mRNA encoding an anti-CD19 CAR. In humanized mouse models, an exceptionally low single dose of 0.01 mg/kg induced > 95% B-cell depletion and drove a > 30-fold expansion of CAR-T cells in vivo, showcasing high potency and specificity. The platform’s design minimizes off-target uptake and demonstrates a favorable cytokine profile, supporting its re-dosing potential [5].

Tessera Therapeutics presented a distinct LNP-based platform co-delivering mRNA and CAR. In a tumor-bearing humanized mouse model, a single infusion generated an average of 24% anti-CD19 CAR-T cells in vivo, which expanded and eradicated tumors [6]. In parallel, for autoimmune applications, the platform achieved > 20% CAR-expressing T cells targeting CD20, resulting in rapid systemic B-cell aplasia without affecting non-target tissues [7].

Aera Therapeutics’ CD8+ T-cell-targeted LNP platform delivers mRNA encoding an anti-CD19 CAR specifically to cytotoxic T cells. In NHPs and humanized mice, a low dose achieved potent B-cell depletion while sparing early progenitors, followed by recovery of a naïve B-cell compartment, recapitulating a clinically desirable immune reset profile [8].

Orna Therapeutics’ panCAR™ platform utilizes a synthetic, unending circRNA packaged in immunotropic LNPs to drive transient CAR expression on immune cells, obviating the need for lymphodepletion. For autoimmune disease, their anti-CD19 candidate demonstrated dose-dependent CAR expression sustained over 96 h in vitro. In NHPs, dosing led to profound B-cell depletion in blood and lymphoid tissues. Crucially, B-cell recovery began at 2–3 weeks, with reconstituting cells displaying a predominantly naïve phenotype. In a humanized SLE model, this platform achieved complete B-cell reduction and a drop in pathogenic anti-dsDNA antibodies, an effect not consistently seen with rituximab [9]. For MM, Orna’s anti-BCMA circRNA-LNP candidate induced > 95% plasma cell ablation in NHPs within 24 h. In a murine MM model, three weekly low doses eliminated tumors for at least 30 days, functionally outperforming a linear mRNA-LNP comparator. This platform underscores the advantage of transient, re-dosable therapy with a rapid on/off pharmacokinetic profile [10] (see Tables 1 and 2).

Non-LNP platforms for in vivo cell engineering

Velvet Therapeutics’ STAR-CAR platform employs biodegradable polyaspartamide nanoparticles to deliver mcDNA encoding a CAR. In mice, administration of anti-CD19 mcDNA nanoparticles induced robust B-cell depletion sustained through day 21, with a favorable safety profile. This approach offers an alternative nucleic acid modality with potential for extended expression windows from a single dose [11].

Expanding the scope beyond T cells, Fred Hutch researchers developed a versatile CD90-targeted MVP platform for multiplexed in vivo HSC engineering. This system enables the simultaneous delivery of DNA, RNPs, and functional proteins, achieving up to 30.3% allelic editing in HSCs. A breakthrough application involved co-delivering recombinant STAT5, which boosted HSC gene marking to 85.5% without traditional conditioning [12]. Notably, by combining CD90 knockout/epitope editing with anti-CD90 CAR-T cells or ADCs, the team demonstrated selective enrichment of gene-edited HSCs and complete clearance of CD90+ tumor cells in a humanized model. In humanized mice, this system achieved ~ 18% transduction and up to 30.3% allelic editing in HSCs [13].

Despite promising preclinical data, significant translational challenges remain. Delivery efficiency to specific T-cell subsets in vivo, potential off-target effects, immune responses to the vectors or novel epitopes, and precise control over CAR-T cell persistence require further optimization. The field must also navigate manufacturing and regulatory pathways for these complex nanoparticle products. Future work will likely focus on optimizing lipid and polymer chemistries, exploring other immune effector cells, and developing sophisticated logic-gated systems.

A case report of pleomorphic adenoma in a 27-year-old female
Authors: Savanna Nyarko, Isaac Frimpong Brobbey, Emmanuel Fiagbedzi, Emmanuel Aboagye Appiah, Samuel Ackom
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA 27-year-old female presented with a painless, multilobulated mass beneath the right ear. On physical examination, the mass was firm, nontender, multilobulated on palpation, no facial palsy was seen, and no prominent cervical lymph nodes were palpated. Ultrasound imaging using a curvilinear probe revealed a complex, heterogeneous lesion within the right parotid gland, and biopsy confirmed pleomorphic adenoma. The patient underwent partial parotidectomy with successful preservation of the facial nerve. No postoperative complications or recurrence were noted during follow-up. Pleomorphic adenoma can occur at any age and is the most common benign tumor of the salivary glands, typically affecting the parotid gland. Early diagnosis and surgical intervention are crucial in preventing complications such as recurrence or malignant transformation. The use of modified imaging techniques, such as ultrasound, may enhance preoperative assessment and surgical outcomes.Keywords: Pleomorphic adenoma, Parotid gland, Ultrasonography, Color Doppler imaging, Salivary gland neoplasm, Histopathology, Parotidectomy, Case report

Introduction

Pleomorphic adenoma, also known as a benign mixed tumor, is the most common neoplasm of the salivary glands, accounting for approximately 60%-70% of all benign salivary gland tumors and around 80% of parotid gland and palate tumors wherein the minor salivary glands are involved [1,2]. The tumor derives its name from the marked histological diversity it presents, characterized by a mixture of epithelial and mesenchymal (myxoid, chondroid, and sometimes osseous) tissue components. It typically affects individuals in the third to sixth decades of life, with a higher incidence in females [3]. These tumors most commonly arise in the parotid gland. Still, they can also occur in the submandibular gland and the minor salivary glands, particularly those in the palate, lips, and buccal mucosa. In the parotid gland, pleomorphic adenomas usually develop in the superficial lobe and present as slow-growing, firm, painless masses [4]. The growth is typically unilateral, and symptoms such as facial nerve palsy, pain, or ulceration may suggest malignant transformation or an alternative diagnosis [5,6]. Although classified as benign, pleomorphic adenomas are clinically significant due to their potential for recurrence and the rare but serious malignant transformation into carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma, carcinosarcoma, and metastasizing pleomorphic adenoma when left untreated or inadequately excised [2,3,7]. Imaging modalities, including ultrasonography, computed tomography (CT), and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), play a crucial role in initial detection, but definitive diagnosis relies on histopathological examination [2]. Treatment primarily involves surgical excision, with superficial or total parotidectomy depending on the size and location of the tumor. Facial nerve preservation is a crucial consideration during surgery due to the proximity of the facial nerve branches, particularly in parotid tumors [8]. Pleomorphic adenoma occurring in people less than 30 is rare. Therefore, we present a case of a 27-year-old female who visited our facility with a palpable mass in the right parotid region.

Differential diagnosis

In light of the clinical presentation of a painless, firm, multilobulated right parotid mass without facial nerve palsy or cervical lymphadenopathy, a differential diagnosis, including Warthin tumor, basal cell adenoma, mucoepidermoid carcinoma, and intraparotid lymphadenopathy, was considered [9,10]. To establish a definitive diagnosis and exclude these conditions, further investigation, including an ultrasound scan. Warthin tumor may present as a well-circumscribed parotid lesion but is more frequently encountered in older males and commonly demonstrates cystic components with increased internal vascularity on Doppler imaging [11]. Mucoepidermoid carcinoma was also considered because it may appear as a heterogeneous parotid mass; however, malignant tumors are more often associated with infiltrative margins, cervical lymphadenopathy, pain or facial nerve dysfunction, which were absent in this patient [2,3,7,12]. Basal cell adenoma can present as a benign parotid mass but typically shows a more homogenous sonographic appearance [13]. The lesion’s multilobulated morphology, minimal Doppler flow, absence of suspicious lymphadenopathy, and subsequent histopathological demonstration of epithelial and myxoid stromal constituents were most consistent with pleomorphic adenoma [14].

Case report

A 27-year-old female patient presented to a regional facility with a history of a palpable mass located inferior to the lobule of the right ear. Clinical examination revealed a firm, nontender, multilobulated mass within the right parotid region with no associated facial nerve palsy or palpable cervical lymphadenopathy. The patient denied associated constitutional symptoms such as fever, weight loss, or night sweats. Routine laboratory investigations showed results within normal limits, including complete blood count, erythrocyte sedimentation rate, and serum biochemistry, with no evidence of a significant inflammatory or infectious process. Upon further clinical evaluation by the attending physician, the patient was referred for an ultrasound examination of the parotid region.

Investigations and imaging findings

The patient underwent an ultrasound examination. An ultrasound with the following specifications was used: Machine type: GE Healthcare, Exam preset: Thyroid, Transducer: both linear and curvilinear transducers of frequency ranges 7.5-15 and 2.5-5.0 MHz, respectively. The findings of the examination were obtained from the greyscale (B-mode). Color Doppler was activated to assess the vascularity of the parotid gland and the lesion. TGC, overall gain, focus, PRF, and depth were all adjusted to suit the examination. Patient remains supine and in right lateral decubitus throughout the examination.

The findings revealed a complex, well-circumscribed, heterogeneous multilobulated mass within the right parotid gland, measuring 3.2 × 1.7 × 3.1 cm, with multiple echogenic foci and posterior acoustic shadowing noted within the mass (Fig. 1). Also found was minimal intralesional flow on color Doppler interrogation (Fig. 2). The left parotid gland, submandibular glands, thyroid gland, and cervical muscles were normal (Fig. 3, Fig. 4), with no sonographic evidence of benign or malignant cervical lymph nodes. Based on these sonographic findings, the physician requested a biopsy for a definitive diagnosis. The microscopic evaluation showed fragments of fibrous tissue with focal chronic inflammatory changes, as well as benign-appearing glands and myxoid stromal components. These features were consistent with the pleomorphic adenoma of the parotid salivary gland.

Treatment

After the diagnosis, the referring physician discussed the nature of the diagnosed condition, potential complications, and the treatment options with the patient in detail. The patient opted for surgery following the discussion for right parotidectomy after informed consent was issued and signed by her. Firstly, the area for excision was marked out during the surgery, which was done under general anesthesia. It was found that the tumor was more in the inferior pole of the parotid gland and was enveloped around the facial nerve (Fig. 5). The tumor was successfully excised (Fig. 6). Intraoperative finding was a pleomorphic adenoma of the parotid salivary gland.

Outcome and follow-up

The surgical procedure was efficient. To enhance the patient’s recovery, follow-up care included patching the surgical site with a suction tube to drain small amounts of secretions (Fig. 7) and close monitoring in the ward for any postsurgical complications. The wound dressing was found to be clean and dry. The patient was discharged 4 days after the surgery with medications (tab. paracetamol [1g tds × 5], tab. cefuroxime 500 mg [tds × 7], and tab. amoxicillin 500 mg [tds × 7]). No complications after the treatment and discharge were reported by the patient.

Discussion

Pleomorphic adenoma is a benign, slow-growing tumor composed of epithelial and myoepithelial cells embedded within a variable stromal background that may appear myxoid, chondroid, fibrous, or osseous in nature. Owing to this histological diversity, it is commonly referred to as a benign mixed tumor. Although pleomorphic adenoma is the most common benign salivary gland neoplasm. Pleomorphic adenomas are derived from a mixture of ductal and myoepithelial elements [15]. They are the commonest benign salivary gland tumors (80%) as well as the commonest benign tumor of the minor salivary gland. It occurs most frequently in the palatal area (73%), commonly seen in the posterolateral aspect of the palate [16]. It predominantly affects individuals between the third and sixth decades of life, with a high female predilection [16]. It affects females more than males with a ratio of 2:1 and is commonly seen in the age group of 40–50 years [14,16].

This present case is noteworthy because the patient was relatively young (27 years old), which is less commonly reported in the literature, except for a case presented by Arora et al. [16] in India, highlighting that this neoplasm can occur outside its typical age distribution. Furthermore, the lesion demonstrated complex heterogeneous echotexture with internal echogenic foci and posterior shadowing on ultrasound examination, findings that mimic other benign or low-grade malignant salivary gland tumors [17]. Similar reports by [18] showed that pleomorphic adenomas may occasionally present with atypical imaging characteristics, thereby posing diagnostic challenges during preoperative evaluation.

The absence of facial nerve palsy, cervical lymphadenopathy, constitutional symptoms, and significant vascularity on Doppler studies in our patient favored a benign etiology despite the lesion's complex appearance.

Imaging-pathological correlation

Ultrasound demonstrated a well-circumscribed, multilobulated, heterogeneous right parotid mass with internal echogenic foci, posterior acoustic shadowing, and minimal vascularity. These imaging features correlate with the histopathological diagnosis of pleomorphic adenoma, which is composed of epithelial and myoepithelial elements within a variable myxoid, chondroid, and fibrous stroma. The heterogeneous echotexture reflects mixed stromal composition, while echogenic foci with shadowing likely represent focal hyalinization or calcification. Well-defined margins correspond to the tumor’s fibrous capsule and non-infiltrative growth pattern. Overall, the imaging–pathology concordance supports the reliability of ultrasound in characterizing benign salivary gland tumors [9,18].

Previous studies have emphasized the usefulness of ultrasonography as the first-line imaging modality for superficial parotid lesions due to its accessibility, cost-effectiveness, and ability to assess lesion vascularity and morphology in real time [1,2,4,14]. In this case, the combined use of linear and curvilinear transducers improved lesion characterization and depth assessment. This approach contributed significantly to surgical planning and may represent an important practical consideration for sonographers evaluating parotid masses, especially in low-resource settings like ours, where CT and MRI are not readily available. Unlike many published cases that relied on advanced cross-sectional imaging modalities such as CT and MRI for surgical mapping, our case demonstrates that careful ultrasonographic evaluation, when correlated with histopathological findings, may provide sufficient diagnostic confidence for clinical decision-making.

According to histopathology, pleomorphic adenomas include microscopic pseudopodal extensions that extend past the apparent capsule, making them more likely to recur if they are not entirely removed [5]. Therefore, surgical excision with preservation of the facial nerve remains the gold standard [19]. The tumor in our patient was observed to encircle facial nerve branches during surgery, highlighting the surgical difficulty of parotid tumors even in cases where they are clinically benign.

The positive postoperative outcome in this case, no facial nerve damage, postoperative complications, or recurrence during follow-up, was probably made possible by early surgical intervention. This case demonstrates several crucial clinical lessons. First, even in younger individuals, pleomorphic adenoma should be considered as a differential diagnosis for parotid tumors [16]. Second, ultrasonography can yield important preoperative data that could affect surgery planning and results when the right probe is used [20]. Finally, early diagnosis and full surgical excision are relevant in minimizing recurrence and preventing the rare but significant risk of malignant transformation into carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma [19]. Hence, this report contributes to the already existing body of literature by reinforcing the diagnostic and clinical value of ultrasound in the evaluation and management of parotid gland tumors in resource-limited and underserved clinical settings.

Conclusion

In conclusion, pleomorphic adenoma can occur at any age. The use of ultrasonography with linear and curvilinear probes to assess tumor characteristics and depth enhanced surgical planning and likely contributed to the successful outcome. Prompt surgical intervention, in the form of partial parotidectomy with facial nerve preservation, led to full recovery without complications. This case advocates that timely diagnosis is critical in preventing recurrence and avoiding malignant transformation in pleomorphic adenoma.

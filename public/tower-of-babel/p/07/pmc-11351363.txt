Characteristics and phylogenetic analysis of the complete chloroplast genome of Vernonia volkameriifolia DC. 1836 (Asteraceae)
Authors: Li Yong, Ze Liu, Haimei Chen, Bin Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractVernonia volkameriifolia DC. 1836, is a woody shrub of the subfamily Vernonioideae within the family Asteraceae. The complete chloroplast genome of V. volkameriifolia spans 152,934 bp, comprising four subregions: a large single-copy region (84,035 bp), a small single-copy region (18,543 bp), and a pair of inverted repeats (25,178 bp). Within the chloroplast genome of V. volkameriifolia, 114 unique genes were identified, including 80 unique protein-encoding genes, four ribosomal RNA (rRNA) genes, and 30 transfer RNA (tRNA) genes. Phylogenetic analysis based on the complete chloroplast genome of six related taxa in the Vernonieae tribe indicates that V. volkameriifolia show closer relationships with Vernonia parishii and Strobocalyx arborea. The first chloroplast genome of V. volkameriifolia was reported in this work, contributing to the enrichment of genomic data for the genus Vernonia.Keywords: Chloroplast genome, Asteraceae, Vernonia volkameriifolia, phylogenetic relationships

Introduction

Vernonia is a woody shrub of the tribe Vernonieae, subfamily Vernonioideae within the family Asteraceae. Vernonia has over 1000 species, distributed in tropical and temperate regions of America, Asia, and Africa (Mishra et al. 2023). There are 27 species in China, mainly distributed in provinces and regions along the southwestern, southern, and southeastern coasts (https://www.iplant.cn/info/Vernonia?t=z). Vernonia volkameriifolia DC. 1836 is a woody shrub with large leaves and large flowers, making it an excellent tree species for observing leaves and flowers. V. volkameriifolia is distributed in Yunnan, Guizhou, Guangxi, and Xizang of China. V. volkameriifolia is also distributed in India, Myanmar, Thailand, and Vietnam. The pharmacological properties of different parts like seeds, leaves, and roots are well-documented in folk medicine. The chemical components include woody stems containing α-amyrenyl acetate, lupeol acetate, friedelin, epifriedelanol, α-amyrin, and stigmasterol (Bai et al. 1985). It has the functions of dispelling wind and dehumidifying, promoting diuresis and promoting diuresis, and is mainly used to treat rheumatoid arthritis, joint pain, hemorrhea, and gonorrhea (Lin et al. 2011). The seeds of V. volkameriaefolia contain 20% oil and are a good source of epoxidized triglycerides. The vernolic acid (cis-12, 13-epoxy-cis-9, 10-octadecenoic acid) content of the oil was 63.5% (Siddiqi et al. 1984).

However, the status and position of many members of Vernonia genus remains muddled due to lack of information on important aspects of the biology of Vernonia, for example, genetic information, chemistry, and morphological variability. Up to April 2024, only two unverified Vernonia species were found (OP581116 and ON881653) in NCBI. Here, we report the chloroplast genome of V. volkameriifolia. Our study will provide the valuable resource for molecular phylogeny of Vernonieae species and also facilitate the genetic and evolutionary studies in this tribe.

Materials and methods

We obtained fresh leaves of V. volkameriifolia (Figure 1) from the Yunnan Branch, Institute of Medicinal Plant Development, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences & Peking Union Medical College, Jinghong, Yunnan Province, China (latitude 22.0010 N, longitude 100.4721 E), with permission. The specimen was deposited at the Institute of Medicinal Plant Development, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences, Peking Union Medical College, Beijing (contact person: Haimei Chen, hmchen@implad.ac.cn) under the voucher number Implad201911449. Subsequently, the sequencing library was prepared with insert sizes ranging from 200 to 500 bp and then sequenced using the Illumina platform (Hiseq2500, Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). A total of 7.2 GB of cleaned paired-end data was retrieved. The clean sequencing data was utilized to de novo assemble the chloroplast genome using Getorganelle (v1.7.7.0) (Jin et al. 2020) with default parameters. Quality assessment of the assembly was performed by mapping the clean reads to the assembly result using minimap2 (v2.1) (Li 2018) with default parameters for short reads. The aligned reads were then extracted, and the depth and coverage were calculated using samtools (v1.12) (Danecek et al. 2021). The chloroplast genome of V. volkameriifolia was annotated using CPGAVAS2 [9] with a reference dataset of 43 plastomes. Manual adjustments of the annotation result were made using Apollo (Minh et al. 2020). A map of the chloroplast genome, highlighting cis-spliced and trans-spliced genes, was drawn using CPGview (Liu et al. 2023). Phylogenetic relationships among V. volkameriifolia and 12 other species of the Vernonieae tribe were analyzed based on complete plastome sequences, excluding one inverted repeat (IR) region. Cynara cornigera (NC_028006.1), belonging to subfamily Carduoideae of family Asteraceae, was selected as outgroups. The complete plastomes of 14 species, excluding one IR region, were aligned using MAFFT (v7.475 (Katoh and Standley 2013) with -alrt 1000 -bb 1000 parameters. And a maximum likelihood (ML) tree was constructed using IQtree (v2.1.2 (Minh et al. 2020)) with ultrafast bootstrap (1000 replicates) and best-fit model TVM + F+G4. Finally, the resulting tree was visualized by iTOL (Letunic and Bork 2024).

Results

A total of 24,211,047 cleaned paired reads were used to assemble the complete chloroplast genome of V. volkameriifolia. The assembly displayed a median depth of 393, with a minimum depth of 128 and a maximum depth of 535. As shown in Figure S1, the sequencing reads were evenly distributed across the genome, exhibiting no regions of low coverage or gaps in the assembly. The complete chloroplast genome has been deposited in the NCBI (National Center for Biotechnology Information, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) under the accession number PP657023. The plastome of V. volkameriifolia exhibits a classic quadripartite structure with a total length of 152,934 bp. It consists of a pair of IRs with a length of 25,178 bp each, a large-single-copy (LSC) region spanning 84,035 bp, and a small-single-copy (SSC) spanning 18,543 bp (Figure 2). The overall GC content of the V. volkameriifolia plastome was 37.7%, with the LSC, SSC, and IRs regions displaying GC contents of 35.9%, 31.4%, and 43.1%, respectively. A total of 132 genes (comprising 114 unique genes) were annotated, encompassing 85 protein-coding genes (PCGs, of which 80 are unique), 37 tRNA genes (tRNAs, with 30 being unique), and eight rRNA genes (rRNAs, of which four are unique). Among the annotated genes in the plasome of V. volkameriifolia, a total of 15 unique genes containing one intron were identified (including rps16, atpF, rpoC1, petB, petD, rpl16, rpl2, ndhB, ndhA, trnK-UUU, trnG-UCC, trnL-UAA, trnV-UAC, trnI-GAU, and trnA-UGC), while two unique genes containing two introns were also observed (including ycf3 and clpP) (Figure S2). The rps12 gene is trans-spliced, as depicted in Figure S3. Sixteen genes were duplicated in the IR regions, encompassing seven PCGs (rpl2, rpl23, ycf15, ycf2, ndhB, rps7, and rps12), six tRNA genes (trnN-GUU, trnR-ACG, trnA-UGC, trnI-GAU, trnV-GAC, and trnL-CAA), and four rRNA genes (rrn16, rrn23, rrn4.5, and rrn5).

To further investigate the phylogenetic relationship of V. volkameriifolia with other plants in the Vernonieae tribe, we obtained 12 additional chloroplast genomes belonging to six Vernonieae species from the NCBI database for phylogenetic analysis. The maximum-likelihood (ML) phylogenetic tree of these complete chloroplast genomes was then constructed using IQ-TREE. The results showed that V. volkameriifolia is more closely related to Vernonia parishii and Strobocalyx arborea (Figure 3). In the evolutionary tree, the bootstrap scores for each branch node, except the node between two Elephantopus mollis, are all high (>98%), indicating a high credibility of the tree.

Discussion and conclusion

In this study, we have (1) presented the complete chloroplast genome sequence of V. volkameriifolia, (2) evaluated the phylogenetic position of V. volkameriifolia in the Vernonieae tribe plants.

The tribe Vernonieae, despite its worldwide distribution and large number of species, is one of the most poorly known of the family Asteraceae. Currently, 1500 taxa across more than 70 genera are recognized (Keeley and Turner 1990). The Vernonieae has been called the “evil tribe” because overlapping character states make taxonomic delimitations difficult at all levels from the species to the subtribe for the majority of taxa (Keeley et al. 2007). Unable to resolve taxonomic boundaries using the tools available at the time, the historical solution was to place the vast majority of taxa, approximately 1000, into a single worldwide “core” genus, Vernonia Schreb (Keeley et al. 2021). However, the status and position of many Vernonia members remains muddled due to a lack of genetic information. To further investigate the phylogenetic relationship of V. volkameriifolia with other plants in the Vernonieae tribe, we obtained 12 additional chloroplast genomes (excluding one IR region) from six Vernonieae species from the NCBI database for analysis. Our findings indicate that V. volkameriifolia has closer phylogenetic relationships with Vernonia parishii and Strobocalyx arborea (Figure 3). A previous study used DNA sequence data from two chloroplast regions (the ndhF gene and the noncoding spacer trnL-F) and from the nuclear rDNA ITS region for 90 taxa from throughout the world to reconstruct the evolutionary history of the Vernonieae tribe (Keeley et al. 2007). The phylogeny revealed that two geographically anomalous species, V. elliptica and Strobocalyx arborea from southeast Asia were clustered together. This finding is consistent with our study. The complete plastome sequence of V. volkameriifolia will provide a useful resource for the conservation genetics of this species as well as for the phylogenetic studies for Vernonieae tribe.

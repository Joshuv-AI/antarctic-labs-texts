Sulfur-Modified Co3O4–Graphene Nanocomposites for High-Performance Supercapacitor Applications
Authors: Fatemeh Hedayati, Maisam Jalaly, Samira Mohammadi, Seyed Morteza Mousavi-Khoshdel
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The development of high-performance electrode materials is crucial for advancing supercapacitor technology. This work presents a sulfur-doped cobalt oxide–reduced graphene oxide (S-doped Co3O4–rGO) nanocomposite directly synthesized on nickel foam via a facile hydrothermal method and subsequent annealing. Comprehensive characterization (XRD, Raman, FTIR, XPS, FE-SEM, and TEM) confirmed successful synthesis and uniform dispersion of components. Electrochemical evaluation revealed exceptional performance: the S–Co3O4–rGO electrode achieved a specific capacitance of 856.8 F/g at 2 A/g, significantly surpassing that of pure Co3O4 (198.8 F/g) and undoped Co3O4–rGO (580 F/g). Furthermore, it demonstrated outstanding long-term stability, retaining 97.5% of its initial capacitance after 5000 charge–discharge cycles at 5 A/g. The synergistic effect between the sulfur-doped graphene network and cobalt oxide nanoparticles is identified as the primary factor enabling this superior capacitance and durability, positioning this nanocomposite as a highly promising candidate for next-generation energy storage devices.

Introduction

Environmental pollution and energy scarcity have become critical global challenges in the modern era. The rapid economic development, coupled with the depletion of fossil fuels and escalating environmental pollution, has created an urgent demand for efficient, clean, and sustainable energy resources. This need extends to the development of new technologies for energy conversion and storage, which has garnered significant attention from researchers. Energy storage can be achieved through the use of batteries and capacitors.

In recent decades, a new type of capacitor, known as an electrochemical capacitor, or supercapacitor, has emerged. Electrochemical supercapacitors represent one of the most effective and practical technologies for energy storage across various applications. , These devices have attracted considerable research interest due to their ability to deliver high power density, rapid charge–discharge rates, and high cycling stability, making them suitable for a wide range of applications. ,

Supercapacitors serve as a bridge between capacitors and batteries. Traditional capacitors offer high power density but low energy storage, whereas batteries provide high energy density but lower power output. Supercapacitors effectively combine these two characteristics, enabling them to meet the energy storage requirements of various applications, including electronic devices, hybrid electric vehicles, and renewable energy systems.

Significant advancements in electrode materials for supercapacitors have been made to enhance energy density and achieve high-efficiency output. These advantages position supercapacitors as superior to other energy storage devices. Consequently, supercapacitors represent a viable option for a wide range of practical energy storage applications when considering their capabilities and limitations in comparison to other technologies. ,

The performance of supercapacitors is governed by several key parameters, including specific capacitance, energy density, power density, rate capability and cycling stability. A typical supercapacitor consists of a separator, an electrolyte and two working electrodes containing active materials, with the choice of electrode materials being particularly critical. These electrode materials significantly influence both the performance and capacity of the supercapacitor. , Based on their energy storage mechanisms, supercapacitors are classified into three categories:1. Electrical double-layer capacitors (EDLCs) store energy via electrostatic charge separation at the electrode–electrolyte interface. They typically employ high-surface-area electrodes and an electrolyte to form an electric double layer, facilitating efficient charge storage. Currently, carbon-based materials, such as activated carbon, graphene and carbon nanotubes, are the most commonly used electrode materials for EDLCs. These carbon materials offer favorable electrochemical performance and a high specific surface area, making them the focus of extensive research. While EDLCs provide high power density and excellent cycling stability, their lower energy density poses limitations for practical applications.2. Pseudocapacitors store energy through Faradaic redox reactions at the electrode–electrolyte interface. These reversible electrochemical reactions yield higher capacitance and energy density but result in lower power density and reduced cycling stability. Common pseudocapacitive materials include transition metal oxides and conductive polymers, with the former attracting more attention due to their superior electrical activity, thermal stability, and tunable morphologies. ,

Among transition metal oxides, cobalt oxide (Co3O4) nanostructures have attracted significant attention due to its high theoretical capacity, relatively low cost, significant redox activity, and excellent thermal and chemical stability, making it suitable for use as electrode materials in electrochemical applications. Given that the performance of supercapacitors is influenced by the morphology and structure of the electrode materials, several studies have reported the synthesis of a variety of cobalt oxide nanostructures for this purpose. However, its practical application is hindered by low electrical conductivity and limited active surface area. Since the capacity of a supercapacitor is dependent on the specific surface area of the electrode material, the design of appropriate nanostructures can enhance both the electrochemical performance and cycling stability of these materials, thereby increasing their operational lifespan. 3. For this reason, numerous studies have recently focused on the development of nanocomposites to overcome the primary limitation of cobalt oxide: its low electrical conductivity. Integrating cobalt oxide with modern carbon nanomaterials has proven to be an effective strategy for enhancing electrochemical properties, leading to improved reversible capacitance, rate capability, and cycling stability. The literature suggests that combining graphene nanosheets with Co3O4 nanostructures leads to improved electrochemical performance of electrode materials in supercapacitors. Graphene’s unique two-dimensional structure, large surface area, superior electrical conductivity, and excellent mechanical and chemical stability make it an ideal candidate for energy storage applications. When incorporated into composite materials, graphene exhibits synergistic effects that further boost performance.

Given the promising capabilities of cobalt oxide–graphene nanocomposites in the electrochemical applications, research efforts have been directed toward optimizing their performance in supercapacitors. , For instance, Numan et al. investigated the synthesis of reduced graphene oxide (rGO) intercalated by Co3O4 nanocubes using a hydrothermal method, achieving a clear enhancement in electrochemical properties. Their study reported a maximum specific capacitance of 278 F/g at a current density of 0.2 A/g. Similarly, Sengottaiyan et al. investigated Co3O4 nanocrystals embedded in rGO nanosheets, revealing a specific capacitance of 487 F/g at a scan rate of 5 mV/s and a good capacity retention of 96.6% after 2,000 cycles. The synergistic effect of combining cobalt oxide with graphene was further demonstrated by Naveen et al., who reported specific capacitance values of 641 F/g for Co3O4–rGO nanocomposites and 405 F/g for pure Co3O4 at a current density of 1 A/g.

The supercapacitive behavior of cobalt oxide has been enhanced through metallic doping. Notably, Mn- and Ni-doped Co3O4 have achieved specific capacitances of 537 and 347 F/g, respectively, at a current density of 1.5 A/g. , The introduction of nonmetallic heteroatoms such as nitrogen (N), boron (B), and sulfur (S) has been shown to enhance the electrochemical properties of materials. Doping graphene with these elements can improve its structural and chemical characteristics, increase electron conductivity, and induce pseudocapacitance. As a result, these modifications can significantly enhance the specific capacitance of hybrid supercapacitor materials, thereby broadening their potential for energy storage applications. ,, Moreover, heteroatom doping enhances the surface wettability of carbon-based materials and accelerates charge transfer rates, making it an effective strategy for expanding the range of materials used in energy storage. , By introducing active sites through the incorporation of sulfur, boron, nitrogen, or phosphorus into the graphene framework, additional electrochemical functionalities can be achieved. , While nitrogen is the most commonly studied heteroatom in this context, − recent research has also explored the effects of boron- and sulfur-doped graphene for supercapacitor performance. −

The introduction of specific heteroatoms is an established strategy to enhance the chemical reactivity, modify the electronic structure, and improve the chemical affinity of metal oxides. For example, sulfur-doped Co3O4 nanowires demonstrated a capacitance of 0.55 F/cm2 at a scan rate of 10 mV/smore than 11 times higher than that of undoped Co3O4. Beyond metal oxides, sulfur is also a highly effective dopant for graphene nanostructures. The minimal electronegativity difference between carbon (2.55) and sulfur (2.58) facilitates efficient charge transfer through C–S bonds. In addition, the high polarizability of sulfur atoms can significantly increase the reactivity of the graphene framework.

Heteroatom doping of Co3O4–graphene nanocomposites can provide significant advantages for electrochemical applications over their undoped counterparts. This is exemplified by the work of Zhang et al., who applied nitrogen doping to a Co3O4–graphene nanocomposite for supercapacitor electrodes. Their study, which compared different nitrogen sources while maintaining a fixed Co3O4:graphene ratio, demonstrated that N-doping effectively modifies the graphene structure, resulting in a substantially enhanced electrochemical performance of the nanocomposite. A maximum specific capacitance of 334.8 F/g was achieved at a current density of 5 A/g in a 2 M KOH aqueous solution. Similarly, Tarimo et al. introduced sulfur as a heteroatom into an rGO–MnO2 nanocomposite. Their electrochemical measurements indicated a dramatic improvement, with the optimized S-doped sample exhibiting a specific capacitance of 180.4 F/g– more than double the 75.2 F/g value of the undoped sample– at 1 A/g in a 2.5 M KNO3 electrolyte.

Compared to other heteroatoms, S-doped graphene-based nanocomposites remain relatively underexplored as electrodes for supercapacitors. Therefore, in this work, Co3O4–reduced graphene oxide (rGO) nanocomposites were synthesized using a facile hydrothermal method, and, for the first time, the effects of sulfur doping on their supercapacitive behavior were assessed.

Experimental Section

Chemicals and Reagents

An aqueous suspension of graphene oxide (GO, 99%, few-layered graphene) was procured from Nanomavad Gostaran Pars Co. (I. R. Iran). Cobalt chloride (CoCl2.6H2O, 98%), and sodium sulfide (Na2S, 98%) were obtained from Millipore Sigma Co. Sodium hydroxide (NaOH, 99%) was purchased from Dr. Mojallali Co. (I. R. Iran). Deionized (DI) water was acquired through a laboratory-scale purification system.

Treatment of Nickel Foam

Nickel foam was selected as the electrode support. The foam sheets were cut into specific dimensions and subjected to a rigorous cleaning process to remove contaminants. This process involved immersing the foam pieces in a 12 M HCl solution, followed by ultrasonic treatment. The cleaning procedure was then repeated using ethanol and deionized water to ensure complete purification. The cleaned nickel foam was subsequently dried in an oven at 80 °C for 30 min, after which its weight was recorded for further reference.

Synthesis of Co3O4 and the Co3O4–rGO Nanocomposite

A hydrothermal method was employed for the synthesis of Co3O4 and Co3O4–rGO nanocomposites, simultaneously utilizing the cleaned nickel foam as a substrate to facilitate direct deposition of the active material. The Co3O4–20 wt % rGO nanocomposite was prepared by dissolving 0.378 g of cobalt chloride in 5 mL of a GO suspension (4 g/L concentration), followed by continuous stirring for 15 min on a magnetic stirrer. Subsequently, 0.128 g of NaOH, dissolved in 2 mL of DI water, was added dropwise under constant stirring for an additional 15 min.

The resulting precursor solution, along with the cleaned nickel foam, was then transferred into a 100 mL autoclave and subjected to hydrothermal treatment in an oven at 150 °C for 5 h. After completion of the reaction, the nickel foam was removed and thoroughly washed with DI water and ethanol to eliminate any residual byproducts, such as NaCl. The electrode was then dried at 80 °C for 30 min. To enhance crystallinity and phase stability, the prepared samples were subjected to thermal annealing at 400 °C for 1 h in an air atmosphere. A comparative synthesis of pure Co3O4nanoparticles was performed following the same procedure but without the inclusion of GO. The final weight of the synthesized samples was measured, and the mass of the active material was determined by calculating the difference between the initial and final weights of the nickel foam.

Synthesis of Sulfur-Doped Co3O4–rGO Nanocomposite

The same synthesis protocol was followed, with the exception of incorporating sulfur source. Specifically, 0.02 g of Na2S (1:1 mass ratio of Na2S to initial GO, equivalent to 16 mol % of CoCl2) was introduced into the precursor solution and stirred for 2 h. This was followed by the dropwise addition of NaOH solution, with continued stirring for an additional 30 min. The resulting suspension, along with the nickel foam, was placed in an autoclave and subjected to hydrothermal treatment at 150 °C for 5 h. Post-treatment washing and calcination processes were performed as described earlier. The active material deposited on Ni foam was measured to be about 1.5 mg/cm2.

Preparation of rGO

For comparative analysis, reduced graphene oxide (rGO) was prepared from the GO suspension using the same hydrothermal approach. The GO solution was heated in an autoclave at 150 °C for 5 h, followed by drying and subsequent heat treatment at 400 °C for 1 h in an air atmosphere to ensure effective reduction.

Characterizations

X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis was conducted using a monochromatized CuKα radiation source (ASENWARE, AW-XDM300, Shenzhen, China), operating at 40 kV and 30 mA, over a range of 10° to 80°. This analysis was employed to confirm the phase structure and chemical composition of the synthesized products. To examine the functional groups present in the samples, Fourier transform-infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) was performed using a Nicolet Nexus 670 spectrometer (USA), with data collected across a wavelength range of 500–4000 cm–1. Additionally, Raman spectroscopy measurements were carried out using a TEKSAN instrument (I. R. Iran) at an excitation wavelength of 514 nm and a laser power of 10 mW.

For morphological and elemental characterization, a field emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM) equipped with an energy-dispersive X-ray (EDX) detector (KYKY, model EM8000, China) was used. The morphology of the samples was further assessed using a transmission electron microscope (TEM, Philips EM208S, 100 kV). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) studies were performed using a SPECS spectrometer (Germany) equipped with a hemispherical analyzer (SPECS Phoibos 100) and a nonmonochromatized dual X-ray source, using Al Kα radiation (1486.6 eV) at a fixed transmission mode with a pass energy resolution of 20 eV. All XPS spectra were analyzed using Spectral Data Processor (SDP) software.

The electrochemical performance of the synthesized samples was evaluated in an electrochemical cell configured as a three-electrode system, utilizing a 6 M KOH aqueous solution as the electrolyte. In this setup, a platinum electrode served as the counter electrode, a saturated calomel electrode (SCE) functioned as the reference electrode, and the synthesized active materials coated on the nickel foam were applied as the working electrode.

Cyclic voltammetry (CV), galvanostatic charge/discharge (GCD), and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) were used to assess the electrochemical behavior of the samples. The CV and GCD measurements were conducted at varying scan rates (5–100 mV/s) and current densities (1–10 A/g), respectively. The EIS analysis was performed over a frequency range of 100 kHz to 10 mHz using the open circuit potential (OCP) mode with an amplitude of 10 mV and DC potential of 0.5 V. The Autolab PGSTAT204 potentiostat/galvanostat, operated with Nova 2.1 software was employed for both CV and EIS tests, while the GCD tests were conducted within the potential window established from the CV results using a KimiaStat Model 126 battery tester.

Results and Discussion

Structural and Morphological Characterization

The XRD patterns of Co3O4, Co3O4–rGO and rGO samples are shown in Figure . The diffraction pattern of pure cobalt oxide exhibits distinct and well-defined peaks, all of which correspond to the Co3O4 phase, with no extraneous peaks detected. Well-defined crystalline peaks located at 18.9°, 31.2°, 36.8°, 38.6°, 44.8°, 55.6°, 59.3°, and 65.2° correspond to the (111), (220), (311), (222), (400), (422), (511) and (440) crystalline planes of the spinel-structured Co3O4 polymorph (JCPDS Card no: 00–009–0418). This confirms the formation of cubic unit cells characteristic of cobalt oxide.

XRD patterns of (a) rGO, (b) Co3O4, and (c) Co3O4–rGO samples.

In the XRD pattern of the Co3O4–rGO nanocomposite, all primary diffraction peaks associated with cubic Co3O4 remain intact, indicating that the crystal phase of Co3O4 is preserved throughout the nanocomposite formation process. Notably, a broad diffraction peak emerges around 2θ = 25°, which becomes more discernible when the patterns are overlaid and viewed in the magnified inset. According to the XRD pattern of the rGO sample, this broadening is attributed to the (002) plane of the rGO nanosheets, confirming the formation of graphene-containing nanocomposite.

Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) was employed to identify the functional groups present in the samples. The FTIR spectra of rGO, Co3O4, and Co3O4–rGO nanocomposite are presented in Figure . Two characteristic peaks, located at 566 and 662 cm–1, correspond to the stretching vibration of the Co–O bond, providing clear evidence of Co3O4 formation. In addition, a broad absorption band centered at 3420 cm–1, present in all samples, is associated with the O–H stretching vibration. This band originates from both residual hydroxyl groups of incompletely reduced GO and moisture adsorbed on the material’s hydrophilic surface. Notably, the intensity of this band is highest in the S-doped nanocomposite. This is attributed to the sulfation of Co3O4 induced by sulfur doping, as confirmed by XPS analysis. The formed sulfate groups (SO4 2–) are highly hydrophilic, thereby enhancing surface hydrophilicity and water adsorption capacity, which results in a more intense O–H stretching signal. The absence of C = O and C–O absorption bands further suggests the effective reduction of graphene oxide to rGO during the synthesis process. ,, In the spectra of rGO and Co3O4–rGO, a peak at 1615 cm–1 is attributed to the C = C stretching vibrations, which is related to the carbon skeleton of the graphene sheets. The FTIR spectrum of the S-doped nanocomposite sample also represents the characteristic peaks associated with cobalt oxide, O–H stretching, C = C bond, and O–H bending vibration (at 1358 cm–1). , In addition, peaks corresponding to C = S and S = O stretching vibrations appear at 1100 cm–1. −

The synthesis of Co3O4 and Co3O4–rGO nanocomposite was studied using Raman spectroscopy, with the results depicted in Figure . The Raman spectrum of pure Co3O4 exhibits five distinct characteristic peaks located at 673, 619, 513, 467, and 200 cm–1, corresponding to the active modes of Co3O4 with a spinel crystal structure. The absorption band at 200 cm–1 is attributed to the Co2+–O2– (F2g) vibrational mode at tetrahedral sites, while the most prominent peak at 673 cm–1 is associated with the Co3+–O2– (A1g) vibrational mode at the octahedral sites. Additionally, the smaller absorption band at 619 cm–1 is assigned to another F2g vibrational mode. The bands at 467 and 513 cm–1 correspond to the Eg and F2g vibrational states of cobalt oxide, respectively, relating to the combined vibrations of oxygen and cobalt in both tetrahedral and octahedral sites of the spinel structured Co3O4. , Beyond the characteristic peaks of cobalt oxide, the Raman spectrum of the Co3O4–rGO nanocomposite distinctly displays three additional bands at 1350, 1600, and 2900 cm–1, which correspond to the D, G, and D+G bands of graphene nanosheets, respectively. The G band, located at 1600 cm–1, originates from sp2-hybridized carbon atoms and is attributed to the first-order scattering of E2g phonon modes in graphite. The D peak, observed at 1350 cm–1, arises from the breathing mode of aromatic rings, which is associated with structural defects induced by oxygen-containing functional groups on the carbon basal plane. The intensity ratio of the D and G bands (I D/I G) for the rGO in the nanocomposite sample was calculated to be approximately 0.92, showing an increase compared to the initial GO (0.85, as reported in our previous work). A ratio close to unity is characteristic of reduced graphene oxide, reflecting its structural evolution during reduction. The presence of reduced graphene oxide can significantly enhance the electrical conductivity of the nanocomposite, owing to its superior conductance relative to graphene oxide. This can subsequently improve the electrochemical behavior of the electrode. The simultaneous presence of graphene-associated Raman peaks alongside the characteristic spinel-structured Co3O4 bands confirms the successful synthesis of the Co3O4–rGO nanocomposite. −

To elucidate the role of sulfur in enhancing the supercapacitive performance of the nanocomposite, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was employed to investigate the chemical states and doping configuration of sulfur in the Co3O4–rGO nanocomposite. The sulfur doping content, calculated from the XPS survey spectrum, was found to be 1.2 at. %, indicating that some of the sulfur-containing precursor was lost during the preparation process. Figure a shows the full-scan XPS spectrum, which reveals the presence of key chemical elements: carbon (C 1s), Cobalt (Co 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, LMM), oxygen (O 1s) and sulfur (S 2p), demonstrating the purity of the synthesized material. The high-resolution XPS spectrum of carbon is shown in Figure b. The deconvoluted XPS C 1s spectra indicates the presence of several bonding configuration: C = C (284.5 eV), C–H/C–O (285.5 eV), C–S/C–OH/C–O–C (287.2 eV), and C = O (288.1 eV). , The C = C bond exhibits the most intense peak among all deconvoluted peaks, indicating that the C = C is the predominant carbon bonding configuration, contributing to the sp 2 hexagonal network of the graphene nanosheets. The smaller peaks associated with C = O and C–OH suggest that the graphene sheets possess a limited number of functional surface groups, such as hydroxyl and carbonyl, confirming that the reduction of graphene oxide was not entirely complete. The Co 2p spectrum (Figure c) displays spin–orbit doublets, comprising two distinct peaks at binding energies of 778.4 eV (Co 2p3/2) and 793.3 eV (Co 2p1/2) accompanied by satellite peaks (indicated by asterisk) at about 782 and 802 eV, characteristic of the spinel structure of Co3O4. ,, It has been reported that the first satellite peak is attributed to the presence of Co2+ ions, whereas the second one relates to the presence of Co3+ ions. Sulfur incorporation is evidenced by the deconvoluted S 2p spectrum (Figure d), displaying four peaks centered at 162.5, 164.0, and 166.0, 168.1 eV, respectively. The first two peaks are assigned to the 2p3/2 and 2p1/2 states of thiophene-type sulfur due to the spin–orbit coupling, while the second two peaks are associated with 2p3/2 and 2p1/2 states of oxidized sulfur. ,, Consequently, the doped sulfur exists in two configurations: one involving thiophene-type S incorporated within the graphene lattice (red S in Figure f), and the other as oxidized S, related to both sulfate groups anchored to the graphene nanosheets and sulfated ions on the surface of cobalt oxide (green S in Figure f). The deconvolution of the O 1s spectrum in Figure e gives three peaks located at 529.4, 530.6, and 532.5 eV. The largest peak at 529.4 is attributed to the Co–O bonding due to the presence of the Co3O4 phase, while the other peaks correspond to oxidized sulfur and oxide groups linked to the graphene nanosheets. Figure f illustrates the proposed structural configuration of the components in the S-doped Co3O4–rGO nanocomposite, where sulfur dopants exist as both thiophenic sulfur (integrated into the graphene framework) and oxidized sulfur (bound to graphene or cobalt oxide). This dual doping mechanism enhances charge transfer and pseudocapacitive behavior, explaining the superior electrochemical performance of the S-doped Co3O4–rGO nanocomposite.

It has been already demonstrated that incorporating heteroatoms such as boron (B), nitrogen (N), Sulfur (S) and phosphorus (P) into carbon materials– especially graphene– can significantly enhance their electrochemical performance through multiple synergistic mechanisms. The presence of these dopants improves surface wettability by introducing hydrophilic sites, thereby facilitating better electrolyte accessibility to the active material surface. In addition, heteroatom doping substantially contributes to improved pseudocapacitance through faradaic redox reactions at the electrode–electrolyte interface while simultaneously modifying the electronic structure to enhance conductivity and charge transfer kinetics. In the specific case of sulfur doping, the heteroatom induces a positive charge on adjacent carbon atoms, creating additional electrochemically active sites, while the formation of sulfone and sulfoxide functional groups provides new pathways for redox reactions during charge–discharge cycles. Moreover, sulfur incorporation has been shown to enhance both the conductivity and Li ion diffusivity in Co3O4. These multifaceted improvements explain the superior performance of S-doped Co3O4–rGO nanocomposite, where sulfur doping simultaneously optimizes both the graphene conductive network through electronic structure modification and the oxide nanoparticles via improved ion diffusion kinetics and additional redox-active sites. This dual functionality of sulfur doping, affecting both carbon and oxide components, represents a particularly effective strategy for developing advanced supercapacitor materials with exceptional capacitive performance. Furthermore, beyond modifying individual components, sulfur doping may enhance the interfacial interaction between the Co3O4 nanoparticles and the rGO sheets. The presence of sulfur-containing functional groups on the graphene surface can provide stronger anchoring sites for metal ions, leading to a more robust and integrated composite architecture. This improved interface facilitates faster electron transfer from the current collector to the active Co3O4 sites and mitigates nanoparticle agglomeration and detachment during prolonged cycling, directly contributing to the exceptional stability observed.

Electrochemical Studies

The electrochemical properties of Co3O4 nanoparticles, Co3O4–rGO nanocomposite, and S-doped Co3O4–rGO nanocomposite were evaluated through cyclic voltammetry (CV) and galvanostatic charge–discharge (GCD) measurements to assess their capacitive performance, including specific capacitance, energy density, and power density. CV analyses were conducted for all samples across scan rates ranging from 5 to 100 mV/s within a potential window of 0–0.5 V. Figure presents the CV curves of pristine Co3O4 nanoparticles, Co3O4–rGO and S-doped Co3O4–rGO nanocomposites, all exhibiting distinct redox peaks in the anodic and cathodic regions. These features confirm the hybrid pseudocapacitive/EDLC behavior arising from the reversible Faradaic reactions involving Co3O4↔CoOOH and CoOOH ↔ CoO2 transformations, as described by the following mechanisms: , Co3O4+H2O+OH−↔3CoOOH+e− 4 CoOOH+OH−↔CoO2+H2O+e− 5

Since specific capacitance is proportional to the internal area of the CV curve, it can be inferred that employing a graphene-containing nanocomposite, rather than pure oxide nanoparticles, is a more effective strategy for achieving higher specific capacity in supercapacitors. The presence of graphene in the Co3O4–rGO nanocomposite enhances conductivity, thereby accelerating the charge transfer rates. , Heteroatom doping facilitates rapid redox reactions, thereby enhancing Faradaic pseudocapacitance. As can be seen, the enclosed area of the CV curves consistently increases when the scan rate increases (Figure c). Redox peaks are particularly pronounced at lower scan rates, where electrolyte ions have sufficient time to interact with the electrode, promoting deeper ion diffusion into the active material and increasing the likelihood of redox reactions. In this regime, both external and internal active surfaces contribute to electrochemical reactions. The overall capacitance of an electrode can be deconvoluted into two distinct contributions: one originating from rapid electrochemical processes such as electric double-layer capacitance (EDLC) and surface-mediated pseudocapacitive reactions, and the other arising from diffusion-controlled processes. The power-law relationship i = aν b (where i is current, ν is scan rate, and a, b are adjustable parameters) was applied to quantify the kinetics. The calculated b-values for Co3O4, Co3O4–rGO, and S-doped Co3O4–rGO were 0.82, 0.85, and 0.89, respectively. These values, exceeding the theoretical threshold of 0.5, indicate a mixed capacitive–diffusion-controlled charge storage mechanism. To quantitatively separate the two contributions, Dunn’s method was employed using the equation: i(ν)=k1ν+k2ν1/2 6 where k 1ν corresponds to the capacitive current (EDLC and pseudocapacitance) and k 2ν1/2 represents the diffusion-limited current. The parameters k 1 and k 2 were determined from linear fits of i/ν1/2 versus ν1/2. , The resulting contributions are illustrated in Figure . The deconvolution of charge storage contributions (Figure ) reveals a significant evolution in electrochemical kinetics, confirming a hybrid mechanism that synergistically combines EDLC and pseudocapacitive processes. The pristine Co3O4 exhibits primarily diffusion-limited faradaic behavior. In contrast, the Co3O4–rGO composite demonstrates a markedly enhanced collective capacitive contribution (EDLC and pseudocapacitance). This trend is maximized in the S-doped Co3O4–rGO, which achieves a capacitive share of over 30% at 10 mV/s, escalating to a dominant ∼ 60% or higher at 100 mV/s. The systematic enhancement, attributed to the conductive rGO framework and sulfur functionalization, directly increases EDLC from the enlarged surface area and introduces additional pseudocapacitive reactions. The superior retention of this combined capacitive contribution at high scan rates underscores the electrode’s exceptional rate capability, a hallmark of a well-designed hybrid system where EDLC provides rapid response and pseudocapacitance delivers enhanced charge storage without diffusion-limited kinetics.

The electrochemical performance of various samples was further studied using galvanostatic charge–discharge (GCD) measurements, a reliable method for determining the specific capacitance of supercapacitors under constant current conditions. The GCD curves of the different samples obtained at current densities ranging from 2 to 10 A/g within a potential window of 0–0.5 V are shown in Figure . All curves exhibit nonlinear profiles with characteristic redox peaks, consistent with the pseudocapacitive behavior observed in CV measurements, which originates from reversible faradaic redox reactions between OH– electrolyte ions and electroactive sites on Co3O4 nanoparticles. A clear trend emerges where increasing current density leads to reduced discharge time and diminished specific capacitance across all samples. Notably, at equivalent current densities (see Figure d), the discharge times follow the order: S-doped Co3O4–rGO > Co3O4–rGO > pure Co3O4 nanoparticles. This progressive increase in discharge duration, particularly pronounced in doped sample, directly correlates with enhanced capacitive performance, highlighting the beneficial role of heteroatom incorporation in improving electrochemical energy storage.

Figure b displays the variation in specific capacitance as a function of current density for all samples. The observed capacitance degradation at higher current densities stems from insufficient ion penetration into the electrode’s active surface within the reduced time scale. The specific capacitance of Co3O4, Co3O4–rGO, and S-doped Co3O4–rGO at a current density of 10 A/g were measured as 154, 426, and 420 F/g, respectively. Correspondingly, the capacitance retention rates were 77.5%, 73.5%, and 49.0% for undoped Co3O4, Co3O4–rGO, and S-doped Co3O4–rGO samples, respectively, when the current density increases from 2 to 10 A/g. These results reveal that while sulfur doping provides exceptional capacitance at low current densities (e.g., at 2 A/g), its performance deteriorates significantly at higher rates. Therefore, sulfur-doped materials are more suitable for systems operating predominantly at lower current densities. The reduced capacitance retention of the S-doped sample at high current densities reflects a characteristic kinetic trade-off in pseudocapacitive materials. Preliminary evidence suggests this phenomenon might tentatively be attributed to either: (i) potential kinetic constraints in sulfur-associated redox processes that seem to govern high-rate performance, and/or (ii) an apparent pore-blocking effect by sulfur groups that may moderately hinder rapid ion diffusion.

High cyclic stability is a crucial characteristic of supercapacitors, as it directly affects their overall lifespan. To evaluate this critical parameter for the S-doped Co3O4–rGO electrode, extended galvanostatic charge–discharge cycling was conducted at a high current density of 5 A/g for 5,000 cycles, as shown in Figure a. The findings revealed that the specific capacitance increased by about 15% after the initial 1,000 cycles. Remarkably, the electrode demonstrated an initial 15% capacitance increase during the first 1,000 cycles, followed by sustained performance exceeding 100% retention throughout subsequent cycles. After completing the full cycling protocol, the material retained 97.5% of its initial capacitance, showcasing exceptional stability under demanding operational conditions. The initial capacitance increase during cycling (Figure a) reflects electrochemical activation common to hybrid metal oxide–carbon electrodes, and is consistent with literature on similar systems. , This phenomenon likely originates from progressive electrochemical activation processes that increase accessible active sites at the electrode–electrolyte interface over time. As cycling progresses, the electrode structure undergoes: (i) improved electrolyte access to previously underutilized pores, and (ii) optimized electrical connections between Co3O4 nanoparticles and the rGO network. This activation plateaued after extended cycles, consistent with literature on similar systems. The minimal degradation after extensive cycling emphasizes the structural robustness and electrochemical durability of the S-doped nanocomposite, making it particularly suitable for applications requiring long-term energy storage performance.

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) is an effective technique for measuring material resistance and evaluating conductivity and charge transfer properties. EIS measurements were conducted at 5 mV amplitude across a frequency range of 0.01–100,000 Hz. Figure b shows the Nyquist impedance plot for the S-doped Co3O4–rGO nanocomposite electrode. The plot displays the imaginary part of impedance (Z’’) on the Y axis versus the real part (Z’) on the X-axis. The EIS curve reveals key electrochemical features through its distinct profile: a small semicircle in the high-frequency region corresponding to charge transfer resistance (Rct), followed by an almost straight Warburg line in the low-frequency region indicating efficient ion diffusion. The equivalent circuit, comprising solution resistance (Rs), charge transfer resistance (Rct), double layer capacitance (Cdl), and pseudocapacitance (Cps). , The measured values for Rct and Rs are 0.09 and 8.13 Ω, respectively. The low value of Rct for our active material, compared to other studies (12.78 Ω, 6.2 Ω, 2.2 Ω, and 0.9 Ω), implies that this nanocomposite exhibits excellent conductivity, thereby inducing an exceptional charge transfer kinetics. The steep slope of the low-frequency Warburg line further confirms rapid ion transport within the electrolyte and favorable mass transfer characteristics, collectively indicating near-ideal capacitive behavior. These impedance characteristics, combined with the minimal solution resistance, substantiate the outstanding conductivity and electrochemical performance of the S-doped nanocomposite, making it particularly suitable for high-performance supercapacitor applications. ,

The S-doped Co3O4–rGO nanocomposite achieves a superior energy density of 29.75 Wh/kg at a power density of 500 W/kg (current density: 2 A/g), benefiting from its exceptional specific capacitance under these conditions. The supercapacitor maintained high energy performance at moderate power levels (up to ∼ 1300 W/kg), but its energy content declined sharply under higher power demands (i.e., at elevated current densities). As a result, the sulfur-doped nanocomposite is particularly well-suited for low- to medium-rate applications requiring high energy density. With its outstanding energy metrics and competitive performance – even compared to more complex asymmetric designs– this S-doped nanocomposite emerges as a promising candidate for next-generation energy storage devices.

In summary, supercapacitor performance is influenced by multiple factors including particle morphology, size, synthesis method, nanocomposite formation, and heteroatoms incorporation. , Moreover, thermal annealing temperature can impact the configuration of heteroatoms, significantly affecting the capacitive properties of graphene. As evidenced by Table , our sulfur-doped Co3O4–rGO nanocomposite demonstrates superior electrochemical performance compared to previously reported materials, exhibiting both exceptional specific capacitance and outstanding cycling stability at high charge–discharge rates. These results establish this material as one of the most promising active materials for supercapacitor applications reported to date. While this study focused on lab-scale synthesis, our processing method is scalable for industrial applications, as it employs low-cost precursors and standard equipment. Batch production could be expanded using larger autoclaves, and the material’s high performance and remarkable stability support its potential for real-world use.

Conclusions

This study successfully synthesized a sulfur-doped Co3O4–rGO nanocomposite via a hydrothermal method. Comprehensive characterization using XRD, Raman, FTIR, FESEM, and TEM confirmed the successful formation of the composite. This nanocomposite demonstrated exceptional electrochemical performance, achieving a high specific capacitance of 856.8 F/g at 2 A/g, along with excellent energy and power densities of 29.75 Wh/kg and 500 W/kg, respectively. Remarkable cycling stability was observed with 97.5% capacitance retention after 5,000 cycles at 5 A/g. XPS and FTIR analyses revealed that the enhanced performance originates from a dual sulfur incorporation mechanism, where thiophenic sulfur within the graphene lattice and oxidized sulfur species (sulfate groups on graphene and sulfated ions on Co3O4) contribute to improved charge transfer and pseudocapacitance. These findings establish the S-doped Co3O4–rGO nanocomposite as a highly promising electrode material for advanced supercapacitor applications, combining high energy storage capacity with outstanding durability.

Global trends in platelet-rich plasma research: A bibliometric review with hotspot visualization
Authors: Zhe Zhang, Shiyao Zhao, Peng Hei

ABSTRACT
Background:Platelet-rich plasma is a hot topic in medicine and has received increasing attention in related research areas. we use VOSviewer software to visualize and analyze the research field of platelet-rich plasma at home and abroad in the past 5 years, and use the visual network map generated by the software to analyze and explore the current research status in this field.Methods:Taking Web of Science core set database and China National Knowledge Infrastructure database as the literature source, we searched the relevant literature published in 2020 to 2024, used VOSviewer software to carry out visual analysis, used the knowledge map generated by the software to visualize the research hotspots of platelet-rich plasma at home and abroad, analyzed and discussed the number of annual publications, the distribution of countries, and the main research directions. We also analyzed and discussed the annual publication volume, country distribution, and main research directions. This study is a visual analysis and does not require ethical review.Results:From 2020 to 2024, the output of scientific research papers in the field of platelet-rich plasma increases year by year, and the hot keywords mainly cover growth factors, stem cells, pain, osteoarthritis, etc. The country with the largest number of publications is the United States, in recent years, the hotness of the field of platelet-rich plasma at home and abroad increases year by year, and it has been applied more widely in the field of orthopedics and tissue engineering, while the emerging field mainly focuses on the field of platelet-rich plasma. In recent years, the field of platelet-rich plasma has been increasing year by year at home and abroad, it is more widely used in the fields of orthopedics and tissue engineering, the emerging fields are mainly concentrated in the fields of chronic wound healing, neurogenic pain, dermatology, androgenetic alopecia, and reproductive medicine.Conclusion:The United States and China are the main research areas in this field; ligament, cartilage, and joint repair are the research hot spots of platelet-rich plasma; neurogenic pain, androgenetic alopecia, reproductive medicine, and regenerative medicine have become the future research trends of platelet-rich plasma.

1. Introduction

Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) can be defined as an autologous blood preparation in which platelets are highly concentrated because it is rich in growth factors that contribute to tissue repair and regeneration. Injecting it into the site of injury can effectively promote the healing of damaged tissue by releasing a large number of growth factors, including transforming growth factor β1 (TGF-β1), platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), epithelial growth factor (EGF), and insulin-like growth factor type I (IGF-I), among others. The PRP is also an important component of PRP, which promotes the healing of damaged tissues, including platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), epithelial growth factor (EGF), and insulin-like growth factor I (IGF-I). Another important component of PRP is leukocytes, which influence the delivery of regenerative growth factors and cytokines to target tissues.[1,2] PRP can be classified into leukocyte-poor platelet-rich plasma (LP-PRP) and leukocyte-rich platelet-rich plasma based on the final concentration of leukocytes in the plasma.[3,4] In addition, PRP, as an autologous product with no adverse effects, can be widely used in orthopedics, maxillofacial surgery, plastic surgery, otorhinolaryngology, dermatology, etc.[5] VOSviewer is one of the many scientific knowledge mapping software, which realizes scientific knowledge mapping through the construction of relationships and visualization and analysis of “networked data,” which are mainly documented knowledge units. VOSviewer is one of the many scientific knowledge mapping software, that is, through the construction of relationships and visualization of “network data” (mainly literature knowledge units) and analysis, it realizes the drawing of a scientific knowledge map and shows the structure, evolution, cooperation and other relations of the knowledge field, and its outstanding feature is the strong graphic display ability, which is suitable for the analysis of large-scale data.[6] This paper is based on the Web of Science database and the CNN database. In this paper, based on the publication status of literature in the field of PRP in Web of Science and CNKI databases, we statistically analyze the publication status of domestic and foreign literature in the field of platelet-rich plasma, the high-yield countries, high-yield authors, and the hotspots of the research during the period of 2020 to 2024, and we use the VOSviewer software to visualize the domestic and foreign literature so that we can quickly understand the PRP field. In this way, we can quickly understand the development trend of the PRP field, to provide a reference for the research direction and hotspot frontiers of China’s PRP field.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Source

The data for this bibliometric analysis were retrieved from 2 databases: the China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI) and the Web of Science (WOS) Core Collection. In the China Knowledge Network with the theme “Platelet-rich plasma” search, the time limit is 2020 to 2024, the source category check Comprehensive List of Chinese Core Journals, CSSCI, CSCD, SCI, a total of 223 articles were retrieved in the Web of Science database with the search terms (Plasma, Platelet-Rich OR (platelet-rich plasma) OR (Platelet-Rich Plasma), the database for the Web of Science Core Collection, the database was Web of Science Core Collection, with a time limit of the last 5 years, a total of 5827 articles were retrieved and screened, all screeded documents were imported into the VOSviewer software. 2.3. Data export and preprocessing

The eligible literature from CNKI was exported in “Refworks” format, and from WOS in “Plain Text” format with “Full Record and Cited References.” The files were named CNKI.download and WOS.download.

2.4. Method

The preprocessed data files were imported into VOSviewer (version 1.6.19) for co-occurrence analysis. The analysis was performed using “Author Keywords” as the unit of analysis.

The 223 documents retrieved from China Knowledge Network were exported, Refworks format was selected, and named CNKI. download was saved; 5827 documents retrieved from Web of Science were downloaded in the format of fully cited references, plain text and named WOS. download was saved. The 2 files were imported into VOSviewer software for keyword co-occurrence analysis. Due to the significant difference in the volume of literature between the 2 databases (223 from CNKI; 5827 from WOS), different occurrence thresholds were applied to generate clear and meaningful knowledge graphs. For the smaller CNKI dataset, a minimum of 3 occurrences per keyword was set. For the larger WOS dataset, a higher threshold of 9 occurrences was used to identify the most prominent research themes without overwhelming the visualization. Meaningless keywords were manually filtered out. Network, overlay, and density visualizations were then generated and saved in TIFF format after image optimization.

2.5. Bibliometric analysis and visualization

Network visualization: Clusters of items (keywords) are represented by different colors. The size of a node indicates the frequency of occurrence. The distance and lines between nodes indicate the strength of their co-occurrence relationship; closer nodes have stronger associations.

Overlay visualization: This visualization colors items based on a metric like the average publication year, allowing the identification of emerging (newer) or declining (older) trends.

Density visualization: This view shows the density of items in a region of the map, with areas of many closely related items appearing in warmer colors (e.g., yellow), highlighting the core research hotspots.

In this paper, we take the literature on platelet-rich plasma contained in the CNKI dataset and Web of Science core set database from 2020 to 2024 as the data source, and visualize and analyze it by using the VOSviewer software, which is seldom used by scholars to visualize and analyze the related literature, so that we can use the intuitive knowledge map to describe the research hotspots in this field. 4.1. Platelet-rich plasma in the field of chronic wounds

Chronic wounds are primarily defined as a constellation of conditions in which the injury persists for more than 6 weeks despite adequate care. They include pressure ulcers, diabetic foot, arteriovenous ulcers, radiographic ulcers, and various types of trauma, which are more common clinically. Saboia-Dantas[7] used an improved regimen of PRP, called progressive platelet rich fibrin (PRO-PRF), with satisfactory therapeutic results, and its use (Giant Pro PRF Membrane, GMPro) form of PRO-PRF and its liquid-phase injections proved to be simple to administer, cost-effective and efficient treatment for chronic skin wounds of the diabetic foot. Statistically, 10/1000 patients will develop the above manifestations, and among them, diabetic ulcers are considered to be one of the most complex and difficult to treat due to their unique pathophysiology.[8] The treatment of diabetic ulcers is complicated and the cure rate is low, which seriously affects the quality of life of patients. Physical therapy and drug therapy are the most common clinical treatment modalities, but their drawbacks are the limitation of selective targeting of tissues and the emergence of various complications. Other methods, such as skin grafts and dressings with noncellular membranes and substrates (decellularized matrix, porous biopolymer substrates, nanofibrous mesh, and hydrogel) are effective but generally ineffective and costly, and newer and more effective treatments are needed.[9] Platelet-rich plasma therapy can be used to treat difficult-to-heal wounds, and Dohan et al[10] concluded that it’s a safe and effective method to improve wound healing and may improve the quality of life of patients. In addition, Fan et al[11] concluded that the use of autologous platelet-rich plasma for the treatment of diabetic foot ulcer wounds resulted in significantly higher healing rates. However, more randomized controlled trials with larger samples are still needed to confirm the clinical results.

4.2. Platelet-rich plasma in neuropathic pain

The International Association for the Study of Pain (IASP) defines neuropathic pain as “pain caused by injury or disease affecting the somatosensory system.”[12] The IASP defines neuropathic pain as “pain caused by injury or disease affecting the somatosensory system.” Several large surveys based on screening tools have been carried out in different countries from an epidemiological point of view. The STOPNEP study of more than 30,000 people in France yielded a prevalence of chronic pain of about 30%, of which 7% had a neuropathic character, with similar prevalence rates in other countries.[13,14] One of the most common causes is disc herniation, which occurs in almost 1 in 5 cases. Neuropathic pain is usually underdiagnosed and undertreated, and the persistence of neuropathic symptoms leads to overconsumption of opioids and waste of healthcare resources, which seriously affects patients’ quality of life. From a pathophysiological point of view, compression of the nerve root by the intervertebral disc rapidly causes intraneural edema, which leads to complications such as nerve fiber degeneration.[15] Similarly, surgical intervention can produce inflammatory changes that alter the volume of the dural sac, or fibrotic scarring at the surgical site, which can produce corresponding mechanical stresses. In addition, chronic lumbar neuropathy induces changes in the perineural vasculature. This ischemic theory assumes that compression leads to venous stagnation or reduced arterial flow in the nerve root vessels, inducing cellular hypoxia and neurodegeneration, which leads to inflammatory phenomena in the nerve root.[16] Scholars have experimented with platelet-rich plasma for the treatment of neuropathic pain, A randomized controlled trial published by Todeschi et al evaluated the efficacy of PRP in the treatment of postoperative neuropathic pain after disc herniation, looking at the pathophysiological mechanisms of neuropathic pain and suggesting that PRP may play a role in proinflammatory and anti-inflammatory homeostasis and that it may yield a better prognosis in terms of nerve regeneration and analgesia. PRP significantly reduces neurodegenerative pain and improves function in patients, and intraoperative use of PRP reduces neurogenic inflammation and promotes nerve healing, thereby reducing the incidence of postoperative residual nerve pain. Meanwhile, a randomized controlled study by Xu,[17] published a randomized controlled trial of ultrasound-guided transforaminal PRP injections versus steroid injections for the treatment of lumbar disc herniation with radicular pain, in which PRP injections may be a safer therapeutic option compared to other treatments, and the results of a 1-year follow-up proved that the improvement was effective in the long term, with no complications or side effects detected during the follow-up period.

4.3. Platelet-rich plasma in recurrent bacterial cystitis

Recurrent urinary tract infections are the more common clinical bacterial infections, defined as ≥2 episodes in the past 6 months or ≥3 infections in the previous year. Approximately 25% of women will have recurrent urinary tract infections within 6 months of their first cystitis episode, which can severely impact the patient’s quality of life.[18] According to a 2000 epidemiologic survey, 30% of women experience a urinary tract infection by the age of 24 years, and more than 50% suffer from the condition during their lifetime. Of all the bacterial infections, Escherichia coli is the most common pathogen for the infection, and although current antibiotic treatments are effective, they do not prevent the recurrence of this inflammatory condition and are prone to drug resistance.[19] Platelet-rich plasma has been found to promote angiogenesis and increase blood flow and oxygenation in wounds through growth factors and cytokines released by platelets. Platelets are antimicrobial, releasing antimicrobial peptides from their alpha granules, which can be used to repair damaged bladder mucosa in recurrent cystitis.[20] Before proposing PRP intravesical injection as an available nonantibiotic therapy for recurrent cystitis, Mirzaei et al[21] conducted a series of relevant clinical studies and concluded that platelet-rich plasma significantly improves the symptoms of recurrent cystitis, reduces the recurrence of cystitis, and that no adverse effects were observed over a follow-up period of up to 12 months.

4.6. Limitations and future perspectives

This study has several limitations. First, as the analysis was restricted to WoS and CNKI databases, some relevant literature from other sources might have been overlooked. Second, being a bibliometric study, it identifies research trends but does not qualitatively assess the clinical efficacy of PRP, which requires future systematic reviews and meta-analyses. Finally, the macrolevel perspective, while informative, lacks microlevel insights into specific mechanisms or methodologies.

Future research should focus on conducting large-sample, well-designed clinical trials to establish standardized PRP protocols and definitively confirm its therapeutic efficacy across various applications identified in this study, particularly in emerging fields like neuropathic pain and chronic wounds.

5. Conclusion

In conclusion, this bibliometric review provides a comprehensive overview of the global landscape and intellectual structure of PRP research from 2020 to 2024. The analysis reveals a consistently growing trend in scientific output, led primarily by the United States and China. The research hotspots are firmly established in orthopedics and tissue engineering, focusing on ligament, cartilage, and joint repair. Furthermore, the study identifies several promising emerging frontiers, including the application of PRP in chronic wound healing, neuropathic pain, androgenetic alopecia, and recurrent cystitis. These findings not only map the current knowledge base but also highlight dynamic shifts and future directions within the field. This study serves as a valuable reference for researchers to quickly grasp the evolution of PRP research and identify new avenues for investigation and collaboration.

Extracellular vesicles exposing complement split products are associated with kidney involvement in antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (ANCA)-associated vasculitis (AAV)
Authors: Anna Juto, Jelena Colic, Apostolos Taxiarchis, Iva Pruner, Vivianne Malmström, Annette Bruchfeld, Iva Gunnarsson, Aleksandra Antovic
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe complement system is crucial in antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody-associated vasculitis (AAV) pathogenesis. Extracellular vesicles (EVs) serve as carriers of bioactive substances, influencing the immune system. We aimed to investigate myeloperoxidase-positive EVs (MPO+EVs) exposing complement components (C3a, C4d), terminal complement complex (TCC) and complement factor B (CFB) in relation to disease activity and kidney involvement.MethodsPlasma MPO+EVs carrying complement products, C3a, C4d, TCC or CFB, were analysed by flow cytometry. Clinical data, including kidney biopsy findings, were retrieved. Disease activity was assessed using the Birmingham Vasculitis Activity Score (BVAS).ResultsEighty-one patients with AAV with granulomatosis with polyangiitis (n=59) or microscopic polyangiitis (n=22) were included. Active disease was noted in 73 (90.1%) patients and 50 (68.5%) had kidney involvement. Patients with kidney involvement had higher concentrations of MPO+, MPO+C3a+, MPO+C4d+ and MPO+TCC+EVs compared to patients without, and concentrations correlated positively with BVAS (p<0.05), respectively. Levels of EVs showed a negative correlation with estimated glomerular filtration rate, and a positive correlation with the proportions of necrosis and crescents in kidney biopsies. ROC curve analysis indicated that MPO+EVs could distinguish patients with kidney involvement from non-renal AAV. A binary multivariable logistic regression confirmed an independent association between levels of EV subsets and kidney involvement.ConclusionsElevated levels of MPO+EVs exposing complement components in patients with kidney involvement indicate activation of the classical or lectin and common complement pathways in renal AAV. Furthermore, the association between levels of MPO+EVs with the presence of crescents and necrotic lesions in kidney tissue supports a role in renal inflammatory activity.

Introduction

Antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (ANCA)-associated vasculitis (AAV) is an autoimmune relapsing-remitting disease which affects small to medium sized vessels, where destructive inflammation and fibrinoid necrosis are observed in the vessel walls.1 The disease group includes granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA), microscopic polyangiitis (MPA) and eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA), which is also often accompanied by the presence of autoantibodies directed towards proteinase 3 (PR3) or myeloperoxidase (MPO). The kidneys are affected in approximately 70% of patients, with a risk of irreversible kidney damage.2 3 AAV-specific biomarkers that mirror disease activity, response to treatment and risk of relapses are largely lacking.

In recent years, experimental and clinical observations have indicated that complement activation is critical to AAV development.4 The complex complement system can exert its effects in plasma, tissues or cells5 and has been described in detail elsewhere.6 Activated neutrophils and the alternative pathway form an amplification loop in the disease process in AAV.7 Activated neutrophils can release complement factor B (CFB) and complement component 3 (C3) in vitro, which are essential for the formation of a stable C3-convertase (C3bBbP), as part of the alternative pathway.8 It has also been reported that neutrophil-derived cationic proteins can cleave C3 into active split product C3a.9

Complement component 4 (C4) is a central effector in both the classical and lectin complement pathways. During activation, C4 is cleaved into C4b, which is subsequently cleaved into C4d, a biomarker for complement activation.10 Levels of C4d and C4d/C4 ratio were increased in patients with AAV with active disease compared with controls and levels decreased following induction treatment when patients reached remission,11 supporting that C4/C4d may be a promising activation marker in AAV.

Extracellular vesicles (EVs) are particles released from cell membranes and can be detected in various body fluids.12 Since the EVs carry biologically active elements, they are implicated in several biological processes, ranging from intracellular communication to the transport of surface proteins and have also been shown to be involved in different pathological processes.13 14

Neutrophils engage in complement activation in the classical and alternative pathways via extracellular traps or EVs.15 16 MPO is an enzyme contributing to endothelial dysfunction, causing injury and the development of vascular lesions.17 18 Neutrophils are the main source of MPO, which is also produced by monocytes and to a lesser extent by macrophages.19 During the activation of neutrophils, MPO stored in primary granules becomes available to the phagosome, but some is released extracellularly, contributing to inflammation and damage by reactive oxygen species formation.20 Moreover, MPO is also found in neutrophil extracellular traps released on neutrophil activation.20 MPO and PR3 can be associated with EVs and may then serve as autoantigens, triggering the production of corresponding ANCAs.12 Our previous results have shown elevated levels of MPO+EVs expressing complement factor C3a or C5a in patients with AAV compared with controls, and patients with kidney involvement displayed higher levels compared with patients without. Furthermore, levels of C3a and C5a MPO+EVs correlated with disease activity.21

This study aimed to further analyse a panel of MPO+EVs exposing complement components in relation to disease activity and kidney involvement. In addition, it sought to further elucidate the role of the complement system in generating potential activity markers in AAV.

Material and methods

Study participants

We included 81 adults with AAV (≥18 years old) with either GPA (n=59) or MPA (n=22) who were identified from the vasculitis cohort, Vaska Study, at Karolinska University Hospital, where appropriate biological material for assessing EVs was available.22 23 Exclusion criteria were EGPA, concomitant malignancy, drug-induced vasculitis or active infection.

In this study, 11/81 (14%) of the patients were also included in the previous study of MPO+EV carrying either C3a or C5a, who were assessed with another Fluorescence-Activated Cell Sorting (FACS) machine.21 Of these, five of the patients had an MPA diagnosis and kidney involvement. The remaining six patients had GPA, of whom two had kidney involvement.

Evaluation of disease activity

Clinical data were obtained from medical records, and the assessment of vasculitis disease activity was performed using the Birmingham Vasculitis Activity Score version 3 (BVAS-3).24 Disease remission was defined as a BVAS score of 0, and active disease was defined as a BVAS score greater than 0. A relapse was defined as the presence of at least one vasculitis manifestation after remission, necessitating escalation of immunosuppressive treatment.

Kidney involvement was defined as pathological changes consistent with pauci-immune vasculitis in a recent renal biopsy and/or active urine sediment with significant haematuria (defined as ≥2+ on dipstick urinalysis or ≥10 erythrocytes per high-power field on urinary sediment) with/without proteinuria (>1+ on dipstick urinalysis or >0.2 g/24 hours or urine albumin-creatinine ratio (uACR) ≥3.0 mg/mmol), with/without red cell casts, and/or as a clinical presentation with decreased kidney function defined as a rise in serum creatinine >30% or a fall in creatinine clearance >25%.24 25 Kidney biopsies were obtained when indicated for diagnostic purposes in the clinical setting. Pathologists at Karolinska University Hospital assessed the kidney biopsies according to histopathological classification.26 The kidney biopsies were also evaluated regarding the proportion of normal glomeruli27 and the proportion of glomeruli with cellular and fibrocellular crescents or necrosis or segmental sclerosis, as well as the proportion of sclerotic glomeruli of total glomeruli. Kidney biopsies were performed in 46 of 50 patients with kidney involvement. Data on cumulative doses of oral prednisolone in milligrams and intravenous methylprednisolone doses converted to equivalent doses of prednisone in milligrams over 7 days, 14 days and 28 days before sampling were obtained.

The exposure intervals were selected because recent glucocorticoid treatment was considered most likely to have the greatest impact on inflammatory activity, EV levels and kidney disease activity around the time of sampling.

Blood sampling

Peripheral venous blood and urine samples were obtained from patients at the time of enrolment in the cohort. Platelet-poor plasma was prepared, frozen within 4 hours and stored at −70 °C. Plasma creatinine, urine test stick, urine sediment, C reactive protein (CRP), anti-PR3-ANCA (αPR3-ANCA) and anti-MPO-ANCA (αMPO-ANCA) were analysed at the Karolinska University Hospital according to clinical routine. The estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was calculated using the revised Lund-Malmo GFR (glomerular filtration rate) estimating equation.28

Detection of EVs and complement using flow-cytometry in plasma

The MPO was chosen as a marker in order to reflect the early broader phase of the inflammatory process. Plasma samples were investigated for the presence of EVs using flow cytometry (BD FACS Canto) according to the previously established method in our laboratory.29 EVs were labelled with anti-MPO (PE, 333139, BD Biosciences) together with fluorophore-conjugated antibodies for identifying specific MPO+EVs: C4d+ (anti-C4d, LS-C535137, LSBio), terminal complement complex (TCC)+ (anti-TCC, 298100, USBiological), CFB+ (anti-CFB, LS-C535147, LSBio). Anti-C3a (anticomplement C3a) monoclonal antibody (MA1-40158, Life Technologies/ThermoFisher) was previously conjugated with fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) (FITC Conjugation Kit Lightning-Link, ab102884, Abcam) following concentration using an antibody concentration kit (Antibody Concentration Kit, ab102778, Abcam).

Gating was performed with MegaMix Plus SSC (side scatter) beads (7803, Biocytex) and EVs were defined as particles <1.0 µm in size and positive for lactadherin. As previously described by Rousseau et al Triton X-100 lysed EVs were used in a background control to differentiate between EVs and negative events as well as to set the gates for positive EVs.30 To gate flow cytometry readouts and calculate EVs concentration (EV events/µL plasma), the FlowJo software V.8.7.1 (Treestar, Ashland, Oregon, USA) was used.

Statistical methods

Descriptive statistics were used to summarise patient characteristics. The difference in frequencies was assessed by Fisher’s exact test. For group comparison, Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare ordinal variables or when variables were not normally distributed. For comparison between more than two groups the Kruskal-Wallis test was performed with correction for multiple comparisons using the Bonferroni test. Spearman’s rank-order correlation was computed for correlation analysis. Extreme outliers were defined with the three times (IQR) criterion. A receiver operator characteristic (ROC) curve analysis with area under the curve (AUC) with 95% CIs was conducted to assess the ability of the different EVs carrying complement markers to discriminate patients with kidney involvement. To analyse the independent association of different EVs with kidney involvement, a binary multivariable logistic (BML) regression was conducted. The outcome was kidney involvement (coded 1). The baseline model included age, disease duration and eGFR. Each EV was then added to the baseline model, generating Model 1a-e. Haematuria was not included in regression models because 48 of 50 patients with kidney involvement had haematuria, while none of the patients without kidney involvement did, causing near-complete separation and unstable or infinite logistic regression estimates. The Box-Tidwell test and visual assessment were applied to evaluate the assumption of linearity between continuous independent variables and the logit of the outcome. Model goodness-of-fit was assessed using the Hosmer-Lemeshow test in addition to calibration plots with slope and intercept as well as Brier Score and akaike information criterion (AIC). Influential observations were evaluated using Cook’s distance values from the logistic regression models. The strength and direction of the association between EVs and the presence of kidney involvement were presented as the ORs and 95% CIs. Likelihood ratio tests were conducted and AUCs from ROC analysis of predicted probabilities for the different BML models were obtained and then compared using DeLong’s test. A value of p<0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed with SPSS V.28.0 for Windows (IBM, Armonk, New York, USA) or R Statistical Software V.4.5.0 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Study participants

The general characteristics of the patients are listed in table 1 and online supplemental table 1. Of the included patients, 43 were male (53.1%), the median (IQR) age was 55 (47–69) years, with a median disease duration of 6 days. αMPO-ANCA was present in 31 (38.3%) patients and αPR3-ANCA in 50 (61.7%). Fifty-nine (72.8%) patients had a clinical diagnosis of GPA and 22 (27.2%) patients had MPA AAV. Active disease (BVAS>0) was observed in 73 (90.1%) patients, of which 52 (71.2%) had GPA and 21 (28.8%) had MPA. Kidney involvement was observed in 50 (68.5%) patients. Median (IQR) days from diagnosis to sampling were 3 (0–7) in newly diagnosed patients (n=63) and 5 (1–16) from verified relapse to sampling (n=10). Inactive disease (BVAS 0) was observed in eight patients, with the majority having GPA.

MPO+EVs expressing complement components

Patients with active disease (n=73) tended to have higher concentrations of MPO+C3a+, MPO+C4d+ and MPO+TCC+ EVs than patients in remission (p=0.067, p=0.075 and p=0.082, respectively) without reaching significance (online supplemental table 2).

Circulating EVs and their relation to disease characteristics

In all patients (n=81), BVAS correlated positively with levels of MPO+EVs (ρ=0.232, p=0.037), MPO+C3a+ (ρ=0.411, p<0.001), MPO+C4d+ (ρ=0.356, p=0.001) and MPO+TCC+ EVs (ρ=0.338, p=0.002).

In active patients only (n=73), BVAS correlated positively with the concentrations of MPO+ (ρ=0.260, p=0.026), MPO+C3a+ (ρ=0.369, p=0.001), MPO+C4d+ (ρ=0.317, p=0.006) and MPO+TCC+ EVs (ρ=0.297, p=0.011) (online supplemental figure 1). Age and MPO+C3a+EV levels correlated positively (ρ=0.358, p=0.002). A negative correlation was seen between MPO+C3a+EV levels and disease duration (ρ=−0.236, p=0.045). An overview of correlation analysis for BVAS and age is seen in table 2. There was no association between the levels of investigated EVs and sex, type of AAV diagnosis or ANCA serotype.

Circulating EVs and their relation to laboratory variables

There was no statistically significant correlation between levels of all explored EVs and CRP levels. The concentrations of the EVs correlated negatively with eGFR (MPO+ (ρ=−0.236, p=0.045), MPO+C3a+ (ρ=−0.441, p<0.001), MPO+C4d+ (ρ=−0.281, p=0.016), MPO+TCC+EVs (ρ=−0.240, p=0.041)) (online supplemental figure 2). An overview of correlation analysis for CRP, eGFR and uACR is seen in table 2.

EVs in active patients with kidney involvement and other organ manifestations

Of 73 active patients, 50 (68.5%) had kidney involvement. These patients were older at the time of inclusion, exhibited a higher degree of disease activity, and as expected, had more impaired kidney function compared with active non-renal AAV (table 3). Cumulative doses of prednisolone during 7 days, 14 days and 28 days before sampling were not statistically significantly different between patients with and without kidney involvement (table 3). Increased levels of total MPO+, MPO+C3a+, MPO+C4d+ and MPO+TCC+EVs were found in active AAV patients with kidney involvement compared with patients without (p<0.05, respectively), but no difference was noted for MPO+CFB+EVs levels (table 4 and figure 1a–e).

The levels of investigated EVs in active patients with kidney involvement were not correlated with age, CRP, creatinine, eGFR or uACR.

Among patients with active disease, four patients had isolated ear-nose-throat manifestations (ENT) and displayed statistically significantly lower levels of MPO+C3a+EVs compared with other active patients (median (IQR) 51 (43–59) vs 82 (62–121), p=0.012). The other active patients (n=69) had the following manifestations: kidney (n=50), chest (n=31), ENT (n=33), nervous system (n=16), mucous membranes/eyes (n=16), skin (n=8) or gastrointestinal (n=1). No statistically significant results were found for the other complement component-bearing MPO+EVs.

ENT and chest manifestations without kidney involvement were observed in seven patients, who displayed statistically significantly lower concentrations in all MPO+EVs carrying different complement components compared with patients with other combinations of organ manifestations. The manifestations observed in the remaining 66 patients were: kidney (n=50), ENT (n=30), chest (n=24), mucous membranes/eyes (n=15), nervous system (n=14), skin (n=6) or gastrointestinal (n=1) (online supplemental figure 4).

Histopathology kidney findings in relation to EVs

Of the 46 patients who had kidney biopsy-confirmed kidney involvement, 44 patients had ≥8 total glomeruli in their assessed biopsies and were included in the analysis (table 5). One patient had four glomeruli in the biopsy, one of which exhibited segmental sclerosis. In another patient, seven glomeruli were obtained in the biopsy, with two demonstrating necrosis and a fibrocellular crescent, respectively.

Kidney biopsies from 35 active patients were classified according to histopathological class,26 (Crescentic n=8, Focal n=18, Mixed n=6 and Sclerotic n=3). We observed no significant difference in EV levels between the different histopathological classes (online supplemental figure 7).

Effects of treatment on levels of EVs

Among patients with active disease, there was no statistically significant difference in the levels of EVs between patients with and without immunosuppressive treatment (online supplemental table 3).

Patients with active disease were categorised into groups based on ongoing treatment at sampling and the levels of complement product-bearing MPO+EV were compared between the subsets, showing no statistically significant differences between groups for any of the EVs (online supplemental table 4). Similar results were observed when only active patients with kidney involvement were included (n=50) in the analysis (treatment-naïve, n=8), with data not shown.

Among patients with active disease, ongoing glucocorticoid treatment prior to sampling was observed in 59 patients (0–7 days), 20 patients (>7–14 days) and 14 patients (>14–28 days).

Cumulative prednisolone doses over 7 days correlated positively with levels of MPO+C3a+EVs (ρ=0.241, p=0.040), MPO+C4d+EVs (ρ=0.251, p=0.032) and MPO+TCC+EVs (ρ=0.297, p=0.011). No significant correlation was observed for the other EVs, nor for any of the EVs and cumulative prednisolone doses for 14 days or 28 days.

There were 63 patients with active disease who were newly diagnosed, of whom ongoing glucocorticoid treatment prior to sampling was observed in 52 patients (0–7 days), 15 patients (>7–14 days) and 8 patients (>14–28 days). Among newly diagnosed patients, there was no statistically significant difference in EV levels between treatment-naïve patients (n=11) and those with ongoing immunosuppressive treatment (n=52). Moreover, no statistically significant difference was found in any of the MPO+EVs among newly diagnosed active patients with kidney involvement (n=46) grouped by ongoing immunosuppressive treatment or none (online supplemental table 5).

Association between MPO+EVs and kidney involvement among patients with active AAV

ROC curve analysis confirmed that EVs expressing MPO+, MPO+C3a+, MPO+C4d+ and MPO+TCC+ had a good ability to discriminate patients with active disease and kidney involvement from patients without these features (figure 2). Moreover, BML regression analyses were conducted to examine the independent associations between the different EVs and kidney involvement, adjusted for age, disease duration (number of days from diagnosis to sampling) and eGFR. Calibration plots (not shown) demonstrated close agreement between predicted and observed probabilities, and Hosmer–Lemeshow tests indicated adequate model calibration. To evaluate whether inclusion of EVs improved the baseline model, model comparisons were performed using likelihood ratio tests and AUC comparisons. The AUCs were not statistically significantly different between the models using DeLong test, showing no improvement in the discriminatory ability with the addition of EVs. Although, all models incorporating EVs showed consistently higher AUC values. Comparison of likelihood ratio testing showed improved model fit for the models 1b (MPO+C3a+EVs), 1 c (MPO+C4d+EVs) and 1d (MPO+TCC+EVs) compared with the baseline model, as reflected by lower −2 log likelihood and lower AICs. Notably, MPO+C3a+EVs and MPO+TCC+EVs remained independently associated with kidney involvement among patients with active disease, even after adjustment for eGFR as a standard clinical marker of renal function (table 6). Total cumulative doses of prednisolone, including intravenous methylprednisolone pulses converted to the equivalent dose of prednisolone, were obtained over 7 days, 14 days and 28 days before sampling. In complementary analyses that included the cumulative doses of prednisolone in the multivariable models, the associations of the different EVs remained substantially unchanged (online supplemental tables 6–10).

Discussion

In this cross-sectional study, we have assessed the expression of complement components on circulating MPO+EVs in plasma from patients with AAV with special focus on renal involvement. We found increased levels of total MPO+, MPO+C3a+, MPO+C4d+ and MPO+TCC+EVs in patients with AAV with kidney involvement compared with patients without. The levels of investigated EVs correlated negatively with eGFR, while a positive correlation was found with the proportion of necrosis and crescents in kidney biopsies. Levels of MPO+C3a+ and MPO+TCC+EVs remained associated with kidney involvement after adjusting for age,31 disease duration and eGFR in binary multivariable regression models. Although the model fit in the binary multivariable analysis improved when adding MPO+C3a+EVs, MPO+C4d+EVs and MPO+TCC+EVs to the models, respectively, it did not translate into a statistically significant improvement in discrimination assessed by the DeLong test. The baseline model demonstrated a relatively high AUC value and in addition, the DeLong test might be relatively conservative and less sensitive to small changes in discrimination. However, EVs exposing complement components may be important for the inflammatory process of kidney involvement and severity in patients with AAV. In addition, levels of MPO+C3a+EVs and MPO+TCC+EVs were still associated with kidney involvement in the sensitivity analyses when adjusting for glucocorticoid exposure.

Almost two decades ago, Xiao et al showed in a murine model of MPO-ANCA-induced crescentic glomerulonephritis that the alternative pathway is involved in the pathogenesis of AAV, in which knockout mice lacking C5a or factor B were protected from vasculitis.32 Subsequently, several studies emphasised the involvement of the alternative complement pathway in blood, urine and tissue in patients with AAV.7 32 Blockade of the alternative complement system with a C5a receptor blocker, avacopan, has become a novel treatment option in AAV and is today licensed as an add-on therapy together with cyclophosphamide or rituximab therapy and with a potential of reducing glucocorticoids during induction therapy in severe AAV.33 Additionally, it has been shown to improve renal function,3335 supporting its role in AAV with kidney involvement. Avacopan may thus be considered as part of a strategy to reduce exposure to glucocorticoid in GPA or MPA.36

Previous studies have shown an association between low serum C3 levels at AAV diagnosis with worse kidney outcome and mortality.37 Levels of MPO+C3a+EVs were elevated in patients with active AAV with kidney involvement and correlated negatively with the percentage of normal glomeruli, thus in line with previous data linking C3 and its cleavage products to renal AAV. Additionally, elevated plasma C3a has been reported in both patients with active AAV and in remission, although without correlation to BVAS, creatinine levels, disease phenotype or organ involvement.38 39

Further emphasising the importance of general complement activation was shown in a study in 196 patients with AAV with kidney involvement, where around 42% displayed C3c depositions in a mesangiocapillary pattern using immunofluorescence.40 In a study of 43 kidney biopsies from patients with AAV, C3 depositions using immunohistochemistry with antibodies against C3c were found in most patients (90.7%). The depositions were localised in a mesangiocapillary pattern, but also found in interlobular arteries, peritubular capillaries or venules, other than the more common pauci-immune pattern. Moreover, plasma C3c levels correlated with glomerular C3c, and increased C3 mRNA transcripts in both tubulointerstitial and glomerular compartments further support its pathogenic role.41 Additionally, C3d deposits have been identified using immunohistochemistry, particularly in crescentic and necrotic lesions40 42 and correlated negatively with preserved glomerular structure.42

Our findings raise the possibility that C3a exposure on MPO+EVs reflects complement activation associated with structural kidney injury in AAV, suggesting that local C3 activation might contribute to the disease process. Thus far, studies have not reached consensus regarding the association between deposition of C3 and its split products in glomeruli and the development of end-stage kidney disease or survival outcomes; however, patients with deposition appear to have more severe kidney involvement.43 44

To further discriminate between the roles of different complement pathways, we analysed here C4d, involved in both the classical and lectin pathways. Previous studies of plasma C4d showed no clear differences between patients with active disease and remission.38 45 However, our previous study using a specific ELISA developed for the detection of a neoepitope of C4d exclusively formed after complement-mediated activation reported lower plasma C4d levels in patients in remission compared with active disease. Moreover, patients with active disease and kidney involvement had lower levels compared with those without, in addition to biopsies being negative for C4d.11 C4d deposition in a mesangiocapillary pattern, as well as in interlobular arteries, peritubular capillaries and/or venules, has also been recognised as a feature in AAV kidney biopsies.40 46 The antibodies used in the different studies, including ours, may recognise multiple fragments of C4 in addition to C4d, whereas assays targeting activation-specific neoepitopes capture a narrower spectrum of cleavage products. Such variation in antibody specificity is therefore a plausible explanation for the discrepant findings and highlights the importance of standardised detection methods when interpreting the role of C4d in AAV.

Elevated soluble TCC (sTCC) levels have previously been reported in patients with active disease compared with those in remission and controls, although results have been conflicting.38 39 Furthermore, patients with kidney involvement were shown to have higher sTCC levels than those without.47 In line with previous findings, we found increased levels of MPO+TCC+EVs in renal AAV, emphasising the role of general complement activation in AAV pathogenesis. TCC has been detected in kidney biopsies, particularly along the glomerular capillary wall and mesangium, although the strength of the staining was found to be inversely related to plasma TCC levels.38 A negative correlation between TCC staining and the percentage of normal glomeruli has been observed in MPO-ANCA-positive AAV,42 further suggesting that complement activation may contribute to kidney involvement.

CFB is an inactive precursor mainly produced by hepatocytes, but could also be expressed by immune cells such as monocytes and neutrophils.48 49 In this study, no significant difference was seen in levels of MPO+CFB+EVs based on disease activity, organ manifestations, diagnosis or ANCA-serotype. In the fluid phase, CFB binds to hydrolysed C3 (C3(H20)), whereas on cell surfaces, CFB binds to C3b deposited on the surface. The resulting complex is rapidly cleaved by factor D to generate the alternative pathway C3 convertase (C3bBb), allowing amplification of complement activation.6 We speculate that although CFB production increases during inflammation, we do not observe a difference in MPO+CFB+EVs levels, as the CFB detected by the antibody likely reflects a transient association with the EV surface. Split products of CFB were not analysed in this study.

Levels of MPO+ EVs did not significantly differ between treated and untreated patients, a tendency also observed in a previous study by our group.21 However, there was a trend towards higher levels of MPO+C3a+, MPO+C4d+ and MPO+TCC+EVs among patients with active disease compared with those in remission, regardless of kidney involvement, although this difference did not reach statistical significance. This trend is consistent with previously published results.21 50

There are several limitations of the study, including those inherent in the cross-sectional study design. The sample size of patients in remission is small and the characteristics of patients in the active disease group are uneven in terms of the number of patients with GPA or MPA and kidney involvement. Moreover, most patients were receiving immunosuppressive treatment at sampling, which is not unexpected in a real world setting, but could influence the levels of MPO+EVs carrying different complement components.

To further evaluate the relation of EVs in active disease and in remission, larger cohorts of patients including repeated samples from the same individual followed longitudinally in different phases of disease could provide further insights.

Conclusion

To conclude, patients with AAV with kidney involvement had increased levels of total MPO+ EVs expressing complement components from the classical or lectin and common pathways compared with patients without this organ manifestation. The correlation with disease activity in combination with the inflammatory severity of kidney involvement further emphasises a role of complement involvement in the inflammatory process in renal disease in AAV.

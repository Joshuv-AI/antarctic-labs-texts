Developing a Core Outcome Measurement Set for Adult ICU Patients, the CoreMS‐ICU—A Protocol
Authors: Maj‐Brit Nørregaard Kjær, Camilla Rahbek Lysholm Bruun, Camilla Bekker Mortensen, Anna Schandl, Kimberley Haines, Kristin Hofsø, Fredrike Blokzijl, Anders Granholm, Morten Hylander Møller, Aske Tøgern, Bodil Steen Rasmussen, Lone Musaeus Poulsen, Thomas Strøm, Eva Laerkner, Anne Craveiro Brøchner, Trine Haberlandt, Anne‐Marie Gellert Bunzel, Louise Stenbryggen Herløv, Anna Holm, Praleene Sivapalan, Stine Estrup, Maria Cronhjort, Jon Henrik Laake, Carmen Andrea Pfortmueller, Marlies Ostermann, Jade M Cole, Matt P Wise, Anna Wludarczyk, Wojciech Szczeklik, Tomas Jovaiša, Maurizio Cecconi, Martin Ingi Sigurdsson, Marek Nalos, Johanna Hästbacka, Marja Mäkinen, Jacob Dye, Naomi Hammond, Edward Litton, Frederik Keus, Sheila Nainan Myatra

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundCore outcome measurement sets (COMS) enhance the consistency and comparability of outcome reporting in clinical research. However, their effectiveness depends on the selection of valid, reliable, and feasible measurement instruments. Core outcome sets (COS) and COMS have been developed for specific intensive care unit (ICU) patient subgroups. The aim of this study is to establish a standardised approach to outcome measurement and operationalisation for adults acutely admitted to the ICU who are participating in clinical trials and other clinical research.MethodsThis protocol describes the development of a COMS for adults acutely admitted to the ICU, the CoreMS‐ICU, consisting of six core outcomes: survival, free of life support, free of delirium, out of hospital, health‐related quality of life, and cognitive function. We will follow the Consensus‐based Standards for the Selection of Health Measurement Instruments guideline and report according to the Core Outcome Set—STAndardised Protocol Items guideline. The development of the CoreMS‐ICU will follow five predefined steps: (1) conceptual considerations for the six core outcomes; (2) systematic searches for outcome measurement instruments, including consideration of existing COMS; (3) quality assessment of relevant outcome measurement instruments; (4) consensus‐based selection of outcome measurement instruments; and (5) recommendations and guidance on how to operationalise and report the measurement of the six core outcomes. We will involve research panels consisting of key stakeholders: patients, family members, healthcare professionals, and researchers in steps 1 and 4.ConclusionsWe aim to develop a COMS for adults acutely admitted to ICU patients to facilitate the consistent use of outcomes in trials and enhance the translation of research findings into clinical practice.Keywords: clinical research, core outcome measurement set, patient‐centred outcomes, stakeholder involvement

Introduction

Variation in outcome selection, definitions, and the associated measurement parameters and measurement instruments across clinical research of intensive care unit patients hinders comparisons and synthesis of evidence [1, 2, 3, 4]. A more standardised approach to outcome selection, measurement, and reporting is needed.

Core outcome sets (COS) are minimum sets of outcomes to be reported in a particular clinical field [5]. Generation of COS focusing on patient‐important outcomes should include participation of patients and other relevant stakeholders in the development [6]. Involving these stakeholders in both a COS and its corresponding core outcome measurement set (COMS) helps ensure that outcomes and measurements are meaningful and relevant.

Identifying COMS also improves the feasibility and consistency across studies [7, 8, 9, 10]. A COMS is defined as a standardised minimum set of outcomes and their corresponding measurement instruments for specific populations and interventions [11]. The consistent use of COMS instruments is essential for improving the quality, relevance, and comparability of evidence that informs clinical practice [2, 3].

COMS have been developed for subsets of the intensive care unit (ICU) population, such as acute respiratory failure survivorship [12], delirium [13], extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) [14], mechanical ventilation [15], and COVID‐19 [16], but not for adults acutely admitted to the ICU who do not fall within these specific subgroups.

We previously developed a COS for the adult ICU population referring to adults acutely admitted to the ICU. This process involved extensive international collaboration across 14 countries and included patients and family members with lived ICU experience, clinicians representing professions involved in ICU care, and researchers. The group reached consensus on six core outcomes, the ‘Core‐6’: survival, free of life support, free of delirium, out of hospital, health‐related quality of life, and cognitive function [17].

The next step in this methodological process is to reach consensus among stakeholders on how and when these outcomes should be measured. This requires the development of a COMS with continued stakeholder involvement. This protocol outlines the process for identifying, evaluating, implementing, and recommending outcome measurement instruments and parameters for each of the six core outcomes identified as important for research in adults acutely admitted to the ICU.

Methods

We will develop CoreMS‐ICU, a COMS for the existing COS, Core‐6, for adults acutely admitted to the ICU: survival, free of life support, free of delirium, out of hospital, health‐related quality of life, and cognitive function. We will follow the COnsensus‐based Standards for the selection of health Measurement INstruments (COSMIN) guideline [18], and the recommendations outlined by the Core Outcome Measures in Effectiveness Trials (COMET) Initiative's Handbook 1.0 on selecting outcome measurement instruments (OMIs) [5].

We will report this protocol in accordance with the Core Outcome Set—STAndardised Protocol Items (COS‐STAP) guideline [19], and a modified version of the COSMIN guideline (checklist in the supplementary file) [20]. This protocol has been registered in the COMET database (https://www.comet‐initiative.org/Studies/Details/3668).

Scope

This COMS is intended for use with clinical research for adults acutely admitted to the ICU. The COMS for the Core‐6—CoreMS‐ICU—is primarily intended to be used in randomised clinical trials but will also be suitable for other ICU clinical research when the Core‐6 is relevant.

Stakeholders

We will recruit key stakeholders, including patients and family members with lived ICU experience, health care professionals (HCP) (e.g., nurses, doctors, physiotherapists, occupational therapists, and psychologists), and researchers with ICU experience, organised into research panels established in participating countries. All countries represented in the development of Core‐6 (listed in the Supporting Information) will be invited to participate in the COMS process outlined in a modified involvement matrix (Table S1) [17]. We will provide a detailed account of key stakeholder participation in research panels, including the number of participants from each stakeholder group, their country of representation, and which steps of the process they contributed to.

We plan for the research panels to be involved in Steps 1 and 4 as illustrated in Figure 1, with stakeholder roles and contributions described in detail in the involvement matrix (Table S1 in the Supporting Information). The research panels' perspectives are especially important to reflect on the feasibility and how best to operationalise and measure the two patient‐reported outcomes: health‐related quality of life and cognitive function as this affects them directly. The other core outcomes are likely to be obtained from medical records.

Step 1: Conceptual Considerations of the Outcomes

We will conduct a structured conceptual review for each Core‐6 outcome, informed by targeted literature searches (Supporting Information), existing COS and COMS (Table S2 in the Supporting Information) and expert input. We will develop preliminary construct definitions through discussion in the research panels (Table 1). The Steering Committee (Supporting Information), comprising researchers, HCP, and a patient representative, many of whom participated in the development of the Core‐6, will formally approve the final definitions by consensus before we proceed to instrument identification.

Step 2: Identification of Existing Outcome Measurement Instruments

We will first identify existing COS and COMS (Table S2 in the Supporting Information) and subsequently identify candidate OMIs informed by the agreed construct definitions. We will search for systematic reviews from the COSMIN database and use a tiered search strategy in MEDLINE. The MEDLINE search will be designed and conducted using the COSMIN‐recommended filters for the precise identification of studies on measurement properties [21], and the full final search strategy will be reported. Eligibility will be restricted to instruments applicable to our target population, defined as instruments that are feasible, valid, and relevant for use in adults acutely admitted to the ICU, including considerations of clinical context, patient condition, and timing of assessment. Step 4: Consensus‐Based Selection of Instruments for Each Outcome

Consensus on the OMIs and assessment time points will be sought using the nominal group technique within the research panels [24]. Feedback from this process will be reviewed by the Steering Committee and incorporated into the refinement of the proposed COMS, including the selection of OMIs and the specification of assessment. Additional details regarding the consensus process are provided in the Supporting Information. In accordance with the COSMIN guideline, we aim to include only OMIs supported by at least moderate‐quality evidence. Any remaining disagreements will be addressed within the COMS Steering Committee before aiming for final consensus across the research panels.

Step 5: Development of Recommendations for Analysis

In collaboration with experts from the COMS Steering Committee and engaged researchers, we will develop pragmatic guidance and methodological recommendations for each outcome measurement instrument (OMI) included in the CoreMS‐ICU. These will cover outcome definitions, timing of assessment, summary effect measures, competing/intercurrent event handling (including handling of deceased patients) [25], cut‐off value determination (if any), statistical analysis approaches, and missing data handling strategies, in accordance with the selected outcome measurement instruments (OMIs).

Dissemination and Implementation

We will implement targeted dissemination strategies tailored to healthcare professionals, researchers, administrators, and policymakers. For each core outcome, we will present its clinical relevance and recommend a measurement instrument, accompanied by methodological guidance to support implementation and use in future research, particularly in clinical trials. Dissemination and implementation are integral to promoting consistent adoption of the CoreMS‐ICU and facilitating the translation of research findings into intensive care practice.

Our dissemination strategy includes peer‐reviewed publications, presentations at international conferences, workshops, webinars, and tailored communication materials co‐developed with stakeholders. We will further disseminate findings through established research networks via email, newsletters, and social media platforms as wanted [26]. Awareness of CoreMS‐ICU is already being established through the involvement of a broad group of collaborators and will be strengthened through these coordinated activities. We will also evaluate how key stakeholders engage with and learn about CoreMS‐ICU to optimise uptake and application in clinical research.

Research panels will be actively involved in dissemination and implementation to ensure inclusion of diverse perspectives and to support communication tailored to non‐specialist audiences.

Research panels will be acknowledged by name, subject to their consent, in the peer‐reviewed publication reporting the CoreMS‐ICU results.

Ethics

All participants involved in the research panels do so voluntarily and may opt to leave the panel at their discretion without any explanation or obligations. Although meetings will be confidential, minutes will be documented, including the composition of the panel (patients, family members, clinicians, or researchers) without collecting other demographic details. If the meeting is to be recorded, research panel members must provide their consent prior to the recording. All participating countries must comply with national and international ethical obligations.

For the protocol no written consent has been obtained from the patients as there is no patient‐identifiable data included.

Discussion

This protocol describes a rigorous and transparent approach to developing the CoreMS‐ICU, a COMS for adults acutely admitted to the ICU, including how it could be operationalised for adult ICU patients [17]. We will establish conceptual clarity for each core outcome and identify OMIs with acceptable psychometric properties.

The described approach will promote the consistent use of standardised outcome measurement instruments across studies, thereby improving comparability and facilitating aggregation of results. This consistency enables more coherent interpretation of findings and strengthens the cumulative value of research within the field. In turn, this may increase confidence in the certainty of evidence and enhance its clinical relevance. Importantly, the use of aligned measurement approaches may strengthen the validity and interpretability of both patient‐reported outcomes and patient‐important outcomes that are not directly reported by patients, such as survival and freedom from life‐support modalities. By integrating evidence on methodological quality, measurement properties, and feasibility, the resulting recommendations will support practical and consistent implementation of the COMS‐ICU in clinical research and decision‐making [7].

Strengths and Limitations

As a strength, we have included experienced researchers in the development of COMS, including methodological experts as part of our steering committee. They will take part in the development process, ensuring methodological rigor and reducing the risk of overlooking important considerations. A key strength of this study is the continued involvement of stakeholders through international research panels at predefined stages, as well as the inclusion of a patient perspective through a co‐author (J.D.) with lived ICU experience. This approach ensures that the selected outcomes and measurement strategies are relevant, acceptable, and meaningful to both end‐users and patients.

The proposed study builds on the previously published Core‐6 COS, which has recognised limitations. As reported, the external representativeness of the COS may be limited, given that the Delphi consensus process was initially conducted in Denmark and later included participants from 13 additional countries [17]. However, perspectives from a lower‐middle–income country were included, which may enhance the broader applicability of the findings. The extended collaboration in this COMS may further support the inclusion of additional countries, thereby strengthening the diversity of perspectives in the process. Despite including a diverse group of global experts to achieve consensus, there is a possibility that the views of this small group may not be representative of the global ICU community. Finally, the identity of the experts will not be anonymised during the consensus process, which may be a potential for bias or group pressure.

There is a risk that we will not identify any suitable OMIs for one or more outcomes. Identifying no suitable OMI for one or more of the Core‐6 outcomes would itself constitute an important finding as it would highlight gaps in current measurement approaches and a need to do either more psychometric work in the ICU population [27], develop new instruments, or modify instruments for the ICU population. Nevertheless, we will avoid leaving any core outcomes without guidance; instead, we will recommend the best available alternative to ensure that the Core‐6 are operationalisable and can be used in future research. Importantly, this approach facilitates implementation across diverse economic settings, including low‐, middle‐, and high‐income countries.

Conclusion

We aim to develop a COMS for clinical trials focused on adults acutely admitted to the ICU (CoreMS‐ICU) to strengthen the quality, consistency, and comparability of clinical evidence. Through methodologically rigorous processes and inclusive stakeholder involvement by integrating clinical expertise, research experience, and lived perspectives, this CoreMS‐ICU will ensure valid and meaningful measurement of patient‐important outcomes.

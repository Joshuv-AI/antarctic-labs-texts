E-selectin-targeted microbubbles combined with ultrasound improves renoprotective effects of methylprednisolone on cisplatin-induced acute kidney injury in rats
Authors: Ruoyan Si, Liping Mo, Changan Zhao, Zeyu Xu, Wenbao Zhao, Junfeng Wu, Rongguo Fu, Shuting Ren
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOur previous study showed that the functionalized E-selectin targeted MBs (TMB-E−selectin) may become a novel drug delivery vector for inflammatory diseases. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to investigate the effect of TMB-E−selectin and methylprednisolone (MP) co-administration combined with ultrasound (US) on cisplatin-induced acute kidney injury (AKI) in rats. After modeling with a single intraperitoneal injection of 10 mg/kg of cisplatin, the rats were intravenously administered with MP or TMB-E−selectin alone, or their mixtures. US was immediately applied for 10 min on bilateral kidneys in rats with TMB-E−selectin treatment. Results showed that 50 mg/kg MP alone and the mixture of 20 mg/kg MP and 500 µL/kg TMB-E−selectin combined with US significantly reduced the levels of BUN and Scr, renal injuries, and the expressions of E-selectin, IL-1α, TNF-α, and NF-κB in renal tissues, but these therapy effects were not significantly observed by 20 mg/kg MP alone. In conclusion, TMB-E−selectin combined with US can enhance the anti-inflammatory and renoprotective effects of the MP in rats with AKI then decrease systemic required dosage and improve the bioavailability of glucocorticoids, which mechanism mainly associates with local drug delivery mediated by US targeted microbubbles cavitation.Keywords: E-selectin-targeted lipid microbubbles, Ultrasound targeted microbubbles cavitation, Drug delivery, Glucocorticoids, Acute kidney injurySubject terms: Diseases, Drug discovery, Medical research, Nephrology

Introduction

Glucocorticoids have been widely applied in the treatment of various inflammatory and autoimmune diseases due to their anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects. However, several serious side effects were inevitably associated with glucocorticoid systemic therapy, such as Cushing’s syndrome, osteoporosis, hyperglycemia, and the risk of infection. These side effects in a dose-dependent manner greatly limit their clinical application1. Therefore, it is an urgent need to explore a targeted strategy to improve the bioavailability of glucocorticoids.

As ultrasound (US) contrast agents, microbubbles (MBs) have been clinically used to enhance US imaging for diseases diagnosis2. It consists of gas bubbles that are typically stabilized by an albumin, lipid, or polymer shell. Following intravenous administration, the acoustically driven inertial cavitation of MBs at target sites generates localized shear stresses and microstreaming effects, resulting in vascular permeability enhancement and cellular membranes perforation, which referred to as the cavitation effect3. MBs cavitation can also open the blood-brain barrier (BBB) and blood-labyrinth barrier (BLB) to overcome the limitation of drug delivery in the brain and inner ear4,5. Therefore, drug delivery system based on microbubbles mediated by ultrasound was considered a promising targeted therapy strategy that promote drug or gene delivery to the lesion tissue, which widely used in the therapy of cardiovascular diseases, tumors, and others4,6–9. Through adding binding ligands such as antibodies or other proteins to the shell, functionalized MBs have also been developed and used for US molecular imaging of diseases7–9. In our previous study, based on molecule of E-selectin overexpressed on activated endothelial cells during inflammation, the functionalized E-selectin targeted MBs (TMB-E−selectin) was prepared, and confirmed to achieve molecular imaging for evaluating microcirculatory perfusion disorders of kidney with acute injury. Their acoustic imaging features presented slower wash-in and wash-out due to actively binding to E-selectin expressed on the activated endothelium10. Therefore, it is reasonable to speculate that this functionalized TMB-E−selectin may become a novel drug delivery vector for inflammatory diseases. After intravenous co-administration with drug and mediated by US, it could not only well enhance drug delivery in the lesion tissues to improve drug efficacy, but also reduce the dosage of drugs.

Hence, in order to explore a targeted strategy to improve the bioavailability of glucocorticoids for inflammatory diseases, cisplatin-induced acute kidney injury (AKI) model in rats was used to confirm whether this TMB-E−selectin combined with US enhances the renoprotective effects of methylprednisolone (MP) in this study.

Results

Effects of US combined with TMB-E−selectin mediated MP on renal function

At day 5 after a single intraperitoneal injection of 10 mg/kg cisplatin, in comparation with the control group, the levels of blood urea nitrogen (BUN) and serum creatinine (Scr) were significantly increased in the AKI model group (both P < 0.0001, Fig. 1), that indicated the successful induction of AKI in the rats. After different treatment, the reduced levels of BUN and Scr were observed in all rats with cisplatin administration, as shown in Fig. 1. Compared with the AKI model group (BUN 104.70 ± 5.49 mmol/L, Scr 631.30 ± 98.10 µmmol/L), the levels of BUN and Scr in the AKI + 50 mg/kg MP (MP50) (BUN 11.50 ± 8.41 mmol/L, Scr 43.67 ± 15.95 µmmol/L) and AKI + 500 µL/kg TMB-E−selectin + 20 mg/kg MP + US (TMB + MP20 + US) (BUN 14.19 ± 7.10 mmol/L, Scr 47.33 ± 13.17 µmmol/L) groups were significantly reduced (all P < 0.001), while no significant reduction was observed in the AKI + 20 mg/kg MP (MP20) and AKI + 500 µL/kg TMB-E−selectin + US (TMB + US) groups (all P > 0.05). Additionally, the levels of BUN and Scr in the MP50 and TMB + MP20 + US groups were significantly lower than those (BUN 89.56 ± 18.42 mmol/L, Scr 524.30 ± 161.10 µmmol/L) in the TMB + US group (both P < 0.001) or those (BUN 95.50 ± 17.71 mmol/L, Scr 528.50 ± 170.30 µmmol/L) in the MP20 group (both P < 0.001). However, the reduction in BUN and Scr levels following TMB + MP20 + US treatment was comparable to that observed in the MP50 group (both P > 0.05).

Effects of US combined with TMB-E−selectin mediated MP on renal morphology

Renal morphology in the rats of different groups was observed and their semi-quantifications of renal injury score (RIS) were analyzed, as shown in Fig. 2. Consistent with renal function alterations described above, no injuries were observed in the kidney of the control group (Fig. 2A), but sever renal injuries were observed in the rats of the AKI model group (Fig. 2B), as evidenced widespread by renal tubular epithelium detachment and necrosis, renal tubule dilation, and luminal necrotic debris or cast formation. Compared with the AKI model group, renal injuries in the treatment groups were ameliorated in different degree (Fig. 2C, D, E, and F), particularly in the MP50 and TMB+MP20 + US groups. Semi-quantification analysis of renal injury also showed that RIS were significantly elevated in all rats with a single intraperitoneal injection of 10 mg/kg cisplatin, compared with the control group (all P < 0.001 or 0.01, Fig. 2G). However, the RIS was significantly reduced in the MP50 and TMB + MP20 + US groups, compared with the AKI model group or MP20 group or TMB + US group (all P < 0.001). Meanwhile, the RIS had no significant difference between the MP50 and TMB + MP20 + US groups (P > 0.05). This result indicated that the renoprotective effect of 50 mg/kg MP was better than that of 20 mg/kg MP, but like that of 20 mg/kg MP combined with TMB-E−selectin under US irritation.

Effects of US combined with TMB-E−selectin mediated MP on inflammatory molecules expression

To investigate the anti-inflammatory effects of MP combination with TMB-E−selectin mediated by US, we examined the mRNA expressions of E-selectin, tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), and interleukin-1α (IL-1α) in damaged kidneys. The results of quantitative real-time reverse transcriptase PCR (qRT-PCR) showed that these inflammatory molecules mRNA levels were elevated after cisplatin administration (Fig. 3), and this elevation was significantly suppressed by 50 mg/kg MP and 20 mg/kg MP combination with 500 µL/kg TMB-E−selectin mediated by US (TMB + MP20 + US) (all P < 0.001). Although their mRNA levels were reduced in the MP20 and TMB + US groups, these reductions except TNF-α had no significant comparing with the AKI model group (P > 0.05). Obviously, 50 mg/kg MP and 20 mg/kg MP combination with 500 µL/kg TMB-E−selectin mediated by US had well anti-inflammatory effects on the rats with AKI. Additionally, the anti-inflammatory effects of 20 mg/kg MP combination with TMB-E−selectin mediated by US were comparable to that of 50 mg/kg MP (P > 0.05), but better than that of 20 mg/kg MP alone (P < 0.01 or 0.001).

The expressions of E-selectin, TNF-α, and nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) protein in renal tissues were examined by immunohistochemical staining. The results showed that, comparing with the control group, their staining was significantly increased in renal tissues of rats in the model group (all P < 0.0001, Fig. 4). However, comparing with the model group, the expressions of E-selectin, TNF-α, and NF-κB protein were reduced in treatment groups, particularly in the MP50 and TMB + MP20 + US groups with significant decrease (all P < 0.001 or 0.01). Additionally, the expression of NF-κB in the MP50 and TMB + MP20 + US groups was significantly lower than that in the MP20 group (both P < 0.05). Although the expressions of E-selectin and TNF-α in the MP50 and TMB + MP20 + US groups were also lower than that in the MP20 group, there was no significant between them (all P > 0.05) except the expression of TNF-α in the MP50 group (P < 0.05).

Discussion

AKI remains a common and life-threatening disease characterized by changes in urine output and serum creatinine concentration, often accompanied with high mortality rates. In addition, AKI is an important factor in the development and progression of chronic kidney disease (CKD)11. Cisplatin nephrotoxicity is associated with the inflammation and oxidative stress, which is dose dependent and cumulative. Due to the second concentration peak and cumulative effect of cisplatin, renal dysfunction and structure injuries were commonly developed at 48–72 h after a single cisplatin administration. In response to renal injuries, various inflammatory proteins are upregulated, such as E-selectin, intracellular adhesion molecule-1 (ICAM-1), monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 (MCP-1), TNF-α, IL-1α, NF-κB, and so on12. Our previous study showed that a single intraperitoneal injection of 10 mg/kg cisplatin successfully induced a rat model of AKI, in which renal dysfunction and structure injuries were observed at 72 h after injection, that was a simple and reproducible model. Meanwhile, ultrasound molecular imaging of our prepared TMB-E−selectin for AKI manifested that it could better accumulate or retained within renal tissues via binding to E-selectin molecule expressed on activated endothelia cells in this AKI model, that indicated the TMB-E−selectin should be a novel drug vector via ultrasonic cavitation for the treatment of inflamed kidney diseases10. As one of the most representative drugs that belong to intermediate-acting glucocorticoids, methylprednisolone (MP) is widely applied in the treatment of multiple kidney diseases13. Therefore, to explore a novel therapy strategy of glucocorticoids, the renoprotective effects of the MP combination with the TMB-E−selectin mediated by US was explored in rats with cisplatin-induced AKI in this study.

Consistent with our previous study10, significant elevation of BUN and Scr was observed in rats at day 5 after a single intraperitoneal injection of 10 mg/kg cisplatin in this study. Histologically, severe damages of the proximal tubules in the inner cortex of the kidney were observed, such as necrosis and detachment of renal tubular epithelium, along with luminal dilation and cast formation. Furthermore, besides significant upregulation of E-selectin, the levels of other inflammatory molecules such as TNF-α, IL-1α, and NF-κB were significantly increased in the renal tissues of rats with AKI. After low-dose (20 mg/kg) and high-dose (50 mg/kg) MP treatment, renal injuries and inflammatory responses induced by cisplatin were ameliorated, but this significant effect was only shown in high-dose MP treatment group (MP50). However, co-administration of low-dose MP with 500 µL/kg TMB-E−selectin mediated by US significantly reduced the levels of BUN and Scr, RIS, and the expressions of inflammatory molecules (E-selectin, TNF-α, IL-1α, and NF-κB), which were comparable to that by treatment with high-dose MP alone (50 mg/kg MP). It indicated that US combined with TMB-E−selectin significantly enhanced the anti-inflammatory and renoprotective effects of MP, while reducing the required systemic MP dose.

The major mechanism underlying US combined with MBs mediated drug delivery is associated with the ultrasonic cavitation effect14, that can assist drugs to cross the vascular wall and cell membrane, increase drug concentration in the lesions, leading to the improvement of drug bioavailability and the reduction of drug dose, consequently the decrease in the systemic side effects15,16. Based on our previous study of ultrasonic molecular imaging10 and present experimental evidence that E-selectin was significantly expressed on active endothelial cell in renal tissues with AKI, after co-administration of 500 µL/kg TMB-E−selectin and 20 mg/kg MP combined with proper parameters US exposure at local kidney, as a novel lipid microbubble carrying an antibody against E-selectin, the TMB-E−selectin actively retained within renal tissues with AKI, then induced strong ultrasonic cavitation effect mediated by US, leading to MP concentration and cellular entry increase in lesions of kidney. Therefore, we analyzed that this enhancement effect in the TMB+MP20 + US group mainly contributed to local delivery and bioavailability improvement of the MP mediated by US combined with TMB-E−selectin.

As a non-invasive physical therapy, low intensity pulsed US (LIPUS, 1–3 MHz, 0.02–1 W/cm2) exerts multiple biological effects through its non-thermal biophysical mechanisms. Its non-thermal effects mostly include microbubbles and microjets induced by cavitation, acoustic streaming and mechanical stimulation17. Recently, anti-inflammatory application of LIPUS has been investigated experimentally in various diseases, such as synovitis, muscle injury, rheumatoid arthritis, neuroinflammation, myocarditis, and kidney disease17–23. By activating or inhibiting certain specific signaling pathways, LIPUS reduces the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines (such as IL-1, IL-6, and TNF-α) and chemokines, thereby decreasing leukocyte infiltration at the site of inflammation, accelerating inflammation resolution, and improving prognosis24–26. The US used in this study (1 MHz, 1.0 W/cm2, duty cycle 50%, impulse frequency 100 Hz) was LIPUS. We noticed that the expressions of inflammatory molecules (E-selectin, TNF-α, IL-1α, and NF-κB) were reduced after the treatment in the TMB + US group, but no significance comparing with the model group. Studies reported that the extent of the cavitation effect mediated by US can be adjusted by changing the acoustic parameters, microbubble size, and microbubble concentration for ultrasound irradiation27. Therefore, it is valuable to further explore whether the treatment of the TMB-E−selectin +US had anti-inflammatory role in rats with AKI or its anti-inflammatory role was affected by acoustic parameters or microbubble concentration in future study. Of course, besides the target delivery of the MP mediated by the US combined with TMB-E−selectin, it remains unclear whether the enhanced effects in the TMB + MP20 + US group was related to the anti-inflammatory synergy of the treatment of the TMB-E−selectin combined with the US. These are limitations of this study. Additionally, there are other limitations to be explored in the future, such as evaluating their therapeutic efficacy by ultrasound molecular imaging approach and the drug delivery efficacy comparison of TMB-E−selectin versus non targeted MBs.

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that co-administration of the MP and TMB-E−selectin combined with US can enhance anti-inflammatory and renoprotective effect of the MP, leading to amelioration of renal injuries and renal dysfunction as well as decrease of E-selectin, TNF-α, IL-1α, and NF-κB levels in renal tissues. Its mechanism mainly associates with local drug delivery mediated by the US combined with TMB-E−selectin. Therefore, this study not only confirmed that our prepared TMB-E−selectin can be used as a novel drug delivery vector for treatment of AKI, but also laid an experimental foundation for the preparation and treatment of drug-loaded TMB-E−selectin for chronic kidney diseases in the future.

Materials and methods

Materials

Cisplatin for injection was purchased from Qilu Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. (Hainan, China). Methylprednisolone succinate for injection was purchased from Pfizer Manufacturing Belgium NV (Puurs, Belgium). Primary antibodies including anti-E-selectin (sc-137054), anti-TNF-α (60291-1-Ig), and anti-NF-κB p65 (80979-1-RR) were respectively purchased from Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Inc. (Texas, USA) and Proteintech Group (Chicago, IL, USA). Secondary antibodies were purchased from SAB (Maryland, USA). TRIzol reagent was purchased from Genstar Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). The SRBR Green Premix Pro TaqHS qPCR Kit III (High Rox Plus) and StarScript II First-Stand cDNA Synthesis Kit-II were purchased from Accurate Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Hunan, China). DAB Horseradish Peroxidase Color development kit, and mouse and rabbit two-step immunohistochemical kits were purchased from Beijing Zhongshan Jinqiao Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China).

Preparation of E-selectin targeted microbubbles (TMB-E−selectin)

E-selectin targeted microbubbles (TMB-E−selectin) were prepared by our lab according to previous document10. Among lipid components of the microbubbles shell, the ratio of DSPE-PEG2000 (Xi’an Ruixi Biology Technology Co., Ltd., Xi’an, China) to DSPE-PEG2000-maleimide (Xi’an Ruixi Biology Technology Co., Ltd., Xi’an, China) was 1:1 (w/w). The mean diameter of the microbubbles was 2.4–2.6 μm and the concentration was 3.6–3.9 × 108/mL.

Cisplatin-induced acute kidney injury (AKI) model induction and treatment

The study protocol was approved by the Ethical Committee of Xi’an Jiaotong University. The study is reported in accordance with ARRIVE guidelines. All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations. Thirty-six male Sprague-Dawley rats weighing 180–200 g (Xi’an Jiaotong University Experiment Animal Central, Xi’an, China) were used for experiment and randomly divided into the following six groups (n = 6): control + saline (Control), AKI + saline (Model), AKI + 20 mg/kg MP (MP20), AKI + 50 mg/kg MP (MP50), AKI + 500 µL/kg TMB-E−selectin + US (TMB + US), and AKI + 500 µL/kg TMB-E−selectin + 20 mg/kg MP + US (TMB + MP20 + US). The rats in AKI groups were administrated by a single intraperitoneal injection of 10 mg/kg of cisplatin, while the rats in the control group was injected with an equal dose of normal saline solution. At 4 h, day 2, and day 4 after modeling, the rats were treated. Prior to first treatment, the hair was shaved from the back region of the rats in both TMB + US and TMB + MP20 + US groups. During treatment, the rats were anesthetized by peritoneal injection of 40 mg/kg pentobarbital sodium, and the proper dose of MP (20 or 50 mg/kg), TMB-E−selectin (500 µL/kg), the mixture of MP (20 mg/kg) and TMB-E−selectin (500 µL/kg), or normal saline solution was administrated intravenously via the tail vein to the rats in different groups. After intravenous injection of the TMB-E−selectin or the mixture of MP and TMB-E−selectin in both TMB + US and TMB + MP20 + US groups, ultrasound insonation (1 MHz, 1.0 W/cm2, duty cycle 50%, impulse frequency 100 Hz) was immediately started at the back skin of the anatomic position of bilateral kidneys with a thin layer of coupling gel applied between the probe and skin for 10 min (bilateral kidneys alternative irradiation for 1 min with a 1-min interval, total 5 min per kidney) using a commercial therapeutic ultrasound device (Intelect® MOBILE Ref2776, Chattannoga Group, USA; circular unfocused ultrasound probe area: 2 cm2). At day 5 after modeling, the rats were euthanized by peritoneal injection of 150 mg/kg pentobarbital sodium, and samples of blood and kidneys were obtained for measurement.

Biochemical analysis of blood samples

Scr and BUN were measured using an automatic biochemical analyzer (Beckman Coulter Biochemical Analyzer AU5431, Beckman Instruments, California, USA).

Morphological examination

Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded rat kidney tissues were sectioned at 3 μm thickness and stained with periodic acid-Schiff (PAS) for structural injury analysis by light microscope (Nikon Instruments Inc., Japan). The following morphological parameters were used to indicate renal injury in AKI: epithelial necrosis, luminal necrotic debris or casts, and tubular dilation. The morphological changes were evaluated in a blinded fashion in a semi-quantification manner as previously described10. Briefly, at least 10 images (200×) from the cortex of each slide were scored for the severity of renal injury, with 0 for no injury, 1 for < 25%, 2 for 25%∼50%, 3 for 50%∼75%, and 4 for > 75% of the proportion with renal injury. Renal injury score (RIS) was calculated using the following formula:

RIS=[(1×n1) +(2×n2) + (3×n3) +(4×n4)]/(n0 + n1+n2 + n3+n4).

where nx is the number in each grade of renal injury.

Quantitative real-time reverse transcriptase PCR (qRT-PCR)

After RNA isolation from renal tissues with TRIzol reagent and reverse transcriptase, the cDNA was subjected to qRT-PCR with SYBR Green in a StepOne Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems Inc., California, USA). The forward and reverse sequences of primers used are shown in Table 1. The amplification was carried out with the following steps: one cycle of predenaturation at 95 °C for 2 min, 40 cycles of 95 °C for 30 s, 60 °C for 30 s, and 72 °C for 1 min, followed by one cycle at 72 °C for 5 min. Detected levels of target mRNAs were normalized to glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) in arbitrary units.

Immunohistochemistry

The expression of E-selectin, TNF-α, and NF-κB in renal tissues was detected by immunohistochemistry. Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded rat kidney sections were deparaffinized with xylene and rehydrated in graded ethanol. Then antigen repair was performed and endogenous peroxidase was removed using 3% H2O2. After goat serum was blocked at room temperature (RT) for 30 min, the sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies, including mouse anti-human E-selectin (1:100 dilution), mouse anti-human TNF-α (1:500 dilution), and rabbit anti-human NF-κB p65 (1:200 dilution), then detected by horse radish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated goat anti-mouse/rabbit secondary antibodies and followed by DAB development and hematoxylin staining of cell nuclei. Staining signals were microscopically observed and ten nonoverlapping fields of view at 100× were randomly selected to quantify the average optical density (% Area) of E-selectin, TNF-α, and NF-κB p65 protein staining using Imaging J software.

Statistical analysis

All data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Tukey’s test was used to determine the statistical significance of differences for multiple comparisons between each group. The analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 5.0. P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

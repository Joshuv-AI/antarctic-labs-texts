Comments about "High PPP4C expression predicts poor prognosis in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma"
Authors: Qixin Liu, Ziheng Peng
Published: 2024

To the Editor,

We would like to share our perspective on the publication "High PPP4C expression predicts poor prognosis in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma"[1]. Xue Hui et al. focused on the expression of Protein phosphatase 4 catalytic subunit (PPP4C) in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), its correlation with clinical-pathological parameters, and assessed PPP4C's prognostic significance. Starting from TCGA, GTEx and GEO databases, Xue Hui et al. performed the immunohistochemistry on their own DLBCL tissue microarray samples to analyze PPP4C expression. Then, they utilized various bioinformatics and statistical methods to determine the prognostic significance of clinicopathological features and PPP4C expression. This study demonstrated that high expression of PPP4C is linked to a poor prognosis for DLBCL and may function as an independent prognostic factor. Taken together, such research is worth learning for its logical picture and high-quality statistical evidence.

At the beginning state of the study, the authors initially utilized data from the TCGA database to analyze the target gene expression in cancer and normal tissues. Subsequently, they validated it using the GEO datasets GSE56315 and GSE32018. However, we noted two mistakes in Fig. 1 B and C concerning the number of normal and tumor patients from both two GSE databases. After referring to other studies using these databases [2, 3], and downloading raw data from the GEO website for clinical information extraction (Supplemental file S1 and 2), it was observed that GSE56315 should have 33 Normal and 55 Tumor samples, while GSE32018 should have 13 Normal and 22 Tumor samples. We speculate that the labeling error observed in both two databases simultaneously suggests a potential misrepresentation during plotting. However, it cannot be ascertained whether this error has inverted the conclusions drawn throughout the article. We recommend contacting the original authors for a re-evaluation of the article and necessary corrections.

Urethral diverticulum following partial resection of a supernumerary urethra in Effman type IIA2 duplication: A case report
Authors: Thierno Mamadou Oury Diallo, Lahoumbo Ricardo Gnammi, Alexandre Vahina Gamamou, Aboubacar Cherif, Thierno Oumar Diallo, Abdoulaye Bobo Diallo, Oumar Raphiou Bah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractUrethral diverticulum secondary to incomplete surgical repair represents an even rarer complication. We report the case of a 7-year-old boy presenting with post-void dribbling following partial resection of a supernumerary urethra for Effman type IIA2 urethral duplication. Physical examination revealed a patent ventral meatus and a blind dorsal meatus. Voiding cystourethrography confirmed a residual diverticular cavity communicating with the accessory urethra. Complete surgical excision of the residual supernumerary tract was performed via a perineal approach with satisfactory functional outcomes at 3-month follow-up. This case emphasizes that total excision of the supernumerary urethra is mandatory to prevent diverticulum formation.Keywords: Urethral duplication, Urethral diverticulum, Voiding cystourethrography, Perineal approach

Introduction

Urethral duplication is such a rare condition that fewer than 300 cases have been reported in the literature to date. It is defined as the coexistence of two musculo-epithelial urethral channels, both lined by urinary-type epithelium, and occurs almost exclusively in males.1,2 Its rare occurrence limits the available evidence regarding epidemiology, diagnosis, and treatment.

Among the several existing classifications, the Effman classification3 remains one of the most widely used.4 Type IIA2 describes an incomplete duplication characterized by a single bladder neck and a urethra that bifurcates into two independent channels, each opening at a distinct meatus on the glans. The supernumerary channel is typically located in a dorsal (epispadiac) position relative to the functional ventral urethra.3,5

Surgical treatment consists of total excision of the supernumerary urethra while preserving the functional channel and the external sphincter. Incomplete excision is a recognized risk factor for urethral diverticulum formation, a condition which causes voiding dysfunction and post-void dribbling.

We report the case of a 7-year-old boy who developed a urethral diverticulum following incomplete resection of a supernumerary urethra for Effman type IIA2 duplication, and discuss the diagnostic workup, surgical management, and lessons learned.

Case presentation

A 7-year-old boy was referred to our urology department for persistent post-void dribbling. His history revealed a double urinary stream noted after circumcision performed at one month of age, which prompted evaluation at the pediatric surgery department. At 9 months of age, corrective penile surgery was performed, eliminating the double stream and establishing a single voiding jet through the ventral meatus. However, post-void dribbling persisted thereafter.

On physical examination, two urethral orifices were identified on the glans: a normal, patent, catheterizable ventral meatus at the tip of the glans, and a second blind dorsal opening located approximately 1 cm proximal to the coronal sulcus [Fig. 1]. Micturition produced a good caliber urinary stream through the normal meatus, followed by involuntary post-void dribbling of approximately 1 min's duration.

Photograph showing two urethral meati: an obliterated epispadiac meatus (arrow) and a normal meatus catheterized with an 8 Fr urethral catheter.

Renal and bladder ultrasound was unremarkable. Voiding cystourethrography (VCUG) demonstrated urethral bifurcation at the bulbar level: a normally calibrated ventral urethra extending to the ventral meatus, and a residual dorsal accessory tract obliterating distally into a pocket-like cavity consistent with a urethral diverticulum [Fig. 2]. No upper urinary tract anomalies were identified.

Voiding cystourethrogram showing the residual supernumerary urethral tract deviated laterally and obliterating into the periurethral tissue (arrow).

The diagnosis of urethral diverticulum secondary to incomplete resection of the supernumerary urethra in Effman type IIA2 duplication was established.

Under general anesthesia, a perineal approach was performed. Both urethral segments were identified and isolated [Fig. 3]. Intraoperative findings revealed a dilated accessory channel of approximately 5 cm, terminating in a fibrous cord. The residual supernumerary urethra was completely excised, and the defect at its insertion on the normal urethra was closed with 5/0 Vicryl sutures over an 8 Ch urethral catheter, which was maintained for 5 days.

Intraoperative photograph showing the supernumerary urethra (arrow) held vertically with forceps following resection of its distal stump.

Postoperative recovery was uneventful with primary wound healing. At 3-month follow-up, the patient had a normal urinary stream with complete resolution of post-void dribbling.

Discussion

Urethral duplication is a rare congenital anomaly of diverse clinical manifestations, and implementing an evidence-based treatment plan remains challenging due to the limited number of published cases. In our institution, this case represents only the second recorded case of urethral duplication over 40 years of practice, confirming its exceptional rarity in sub-Saharan Africa.6

The clinical presentation varies according to the anatomical variant. Most patients present with a double urinary stream, urinary tract infection, incontinence, or bladder outflow obstruction. In Effman type IIA2 specifically, the double stream is the hallmark manifestation.3,5 The post-void dribbling in our patient directly resulted from delayed emptying of the residual diverticular pouch left after incomplete prior resection.

The ventral urethra, regardless of its meatal position, is consistently the more functional channel in sagittal urethral duplications and must be preserved during any surgical correction. VCUG is the investigation of choice, providing precise anatomical mapping of both urethral channels.7,8 In our case, it was essential not only for confirming the residual diverticulum but also for delineating its communication with the accessory tract at the bulbar level. Urethroscopy, although highly informative for identifying the sphincter and verumontanum prior to correction,4 could not be performed due to the unavailability of a pediatric cystoscope.

Treatment is tailored to the anatomical variant and aims to correct presenting symptoms. Options include urethral dilation, sclerotherapy of the accessory channel, complete surgical excision, or genitourinary reconstruction.9, 10, 11 In Effman type IIA2 with a functional ventral urethra, complete excision of the dorsal supernumerary channel via a perineal approach reliably achieves satisfactory functional results.12 The complete excision performed in our case resulted in full resolution of symptoms at 3-month follow-up, consistent with published outcomes.12,13

This case reinforces the critical technical principle that any initial surgical management of urethral duplication must achieve complete resection of the accessory urethra in order to prevent this avoidable complication.

Conclusion

Urethral duplication type IIA2 according to Effman is a rare congenital anomaly. Post-void dribbling may result from incomplete excision of the supernumerary urethra, with formation of a residual diverticular cavity. VCUG is the cornerstone of diagnosis. Complete surgical excision of the supernumerary urethra through a perineal approach, with preservation of the functional ventral urethra and the external sphincter, achieves satisfactory functional outcomes. This case underscores the imperative of total resection at initial surgery to prevent this avoidable complication.

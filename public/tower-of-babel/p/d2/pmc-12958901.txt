Mechanisms of SGLT inhibitor action and physiological mediators: systematic review and protocol for the MOSAIC collaborative meta-analysis

ABSTRACT
Abstract ABSTRACTIntroductionSodium-glucose cotransporter (SGLT) inhibitors have shown substantial benefit in reducing cardiovascular and kidney events across diverse clinical populations, but the underlying physiological mechanisms remain unclear. However, existing mechanistic studies on renal and cardiovascular haemodynamics show variability in design, have limited statistical power and yield inconsistent outcomes, thus limiting the ability to draw generalisable conclusions. To address this gap, we conducted a systematic review and proposed the first meta-analysis to aggregate individual participant-level data from mechanistic studies to identify consistent physiological patterns and enhance understanding of the therapeutic effects of SGLT inhibition.Methods and analysisGold-standard measured glomerular filtration rate (mGFR) was selected as the primary outcome for this systematic review, which aimed to identify all completed mechanistic studies investigating the effects of SGLT inhibition. Electronic databases including Ovid MEDLINE; Ovid Embase; Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews; and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials were searched using a detailed search strategy. In total, 24 studies (n=1296) were identified. This systematic review was reported according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. Key variables including demographics, medical history, concomitant medications, vital signs, mGFR, renal haemodynamics, urine and plasma biochemistry, tubular sodium handling, echocardiography, cardiac output monitoring, arterial stiffness and fluid volume will be extracted. A one-stage individual participant data meta-analysis under a Bayesian framework will be conducted, using hierarchical models to simultaneously analyse data from all eligible studies. The risk of bias due to missing results will be assessed. Sensitivity analyses and subgroup evaluations will be incorporated to explore sources of heterogeneity and assess robustness of findings.Ethics and disseminationEthics approval was obtained from University Health Network, Toronto, Canada. Findings from the Mechanisms of SGLT Inhibitor Action and Physiological Mediators (MOSAIC) meta-analysis will be published in peer-reviewed journals and results will be disseminated at scientific conferences.PROSPERO registration numberCRD420251001413.Keywords: NEPHROLOGY, DIABETES & ENDOCRINOLOGY, Cardiovascular Disease

Introduction

Sodium-glucose cotransporter (SGLT) inhibitors were initially developed as glucose-lowering agents for the treatment of type 2 diabetes and have been approved for clinical use since 2012. This class includes selective inhibitors of SGLT2—located in the proximal tubule of the kidney—such as empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin and ertugliflozin, as well as the dual SGLT1/2 inhibitor sotagliflozin, which also targets intestinal SGLT1. Over the past decade, these agents have demonstrated significant cardiovascular benefit, such as reductions in hospitalisation rates for heart failure1 2 and lower incidences of major adverse cardiovascular events3,7 across a wide range of clinical populations, including patients with and without type 2 diabetes,18,10 heart failure6 11 12 and chronic kidney disease.13,15 Complementing these cardiovascular benefits, SGLT inhibitors also exert protective effects on kidney function, including delaying progression of chronic kidney disease in populations with and without type 2 diabetes.14 16 Benefits of SGLT inhibitors on kidney outcomes have been associated with a well-established, acute and reversible reduction or ‘dip’ in estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR),17 18 attributed to its transient natriuretic effects that lower intraglomerular pressure.19 The GFR ‘dip’ has led to clinically meaningful outcomes associated with long-term preservation of kidney function,20 as evidenced by slower declines in GFR and reductions in albuminuria.1 2 21 22

The consistent clinical benefits observed across several outcome trials prompted numerous human integrative physiological investigations to understand the underlying cardiorenal protective mechanisms of SGLT inhibitors. These mechanistic investigations demonstrated that SGLT inhibition in the proximal tubule reduces energy-intense sodium and glucose reabsorption, leading to an increased delivery of sodium to downstream distal nephron segments. Through a hypothesised process of tubuloglomerular feedback (TGF), this distal natriuresis alters glomerular arteriolar tone and lowers intraglomerular pressures. SGLT inhibitors are thereby hypothesised to alleviate glomerular hyperfiltration and shear stress while improving renal energetics, preventing long-term nephron stress and injury.23 24 These findings supported the prevailing clinical paradigm that natriuresis, diuresis and glucosuria were the primary factors driving clinical benefit.25 26 However, subsequent work challenged this notion, demonstrating that natriuretic effects of SGLT inhibition were transient, with counterregulatory tubular adaptations diminishing natriuresis and diuresis over time. Furthermore, glucosuria appears to have a limited and inconsistent association with clinical outcomes. While reductions in HbA1c contribute to modest metabolic benefits, they do not fully explain the observed improvements in kidney and heart function.1927,29

Accordingly, mechanisms contributing to therapeutic efficacy of SGLT inhibitors remain incompletely understood. The effects of SGLT inhibitor-mediated changes in tubular dynamics on renal haemodynamics, cardiovascular function and metabolic and inflammatory responses remain an active area of investigation, particularly across the diverse patient populations that are eligible for treatment with these medications.1927 30,34 However, investigating multi-system physiological interactions and treatment heterogeneity requires sample sizes that far exceed estimates from individual studies that are intrinsically designed to detect physiological changes in a select number of predefined endpoints across homogenous patient cohorts. Given that much of the existing mechanistic research has been fragmented and focused on individual endpoints or conducted in narrowly defined cohorts, it has been difficult to identify consistent patterns or interactions across studies. Without an integrated approach to synthesise these data, opportunities to uncover key pathways, characterise treatment heterogeneity and inform precision therapy remain limited. Yet, to date, there is no published attempt to systematically aggregate data from human mechanistic studies to identify consistent physiological patterns, pathways and complex interactions that mediate the multifaceted therapeutic effects of SGLT inhibitors.

Mechanisms of SGLT Inhibitor Action and Physiological Mediators (MOSAIC) seeks to address this critical gap by conducting a participant-level meta-analysis of mechanistic studies that have investigated the effects of SGLT inhibition on key physiological variables across various patient populations, allowing for more granular, individual patient data integration. By leveraging mechanistic data from a broad and heterogenous collection of existing studies, MOSAIC aims to substantially increase the overall sample size, thereby enhancing statistical power to detect and quantify the physiological mediators underlying the observed benefits of SGLT inhibitor therapy. This approach enables advanced subgroup analyses and exploration of potential effect modifiers, improving the precision and generalisability of the findings. To this end, we conducted a systematic review following Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines to identify relevant studies for inclusion in our meta-analysis. We also developed a statistical analysis plan incorporating advanced modelling techniques to ensure rigorous evaluation, appropriate handling of heterogeneity and transparent interpretation of the integrated data.

To address these objectives, we propose a participant-level meta-analysis of completed mechanistic studies in humans examining the physiological effects of SGLT inhibition. The primary goal of the study is to aggregate data from the final timepoints across all studies, irrespective of study duration, in order to capture the overall effect of SGLT inhibitor treatment at a common endpoint. This approach will provide a comprehensive evaluation of treatment outcomes. A secondary goal is to analyse the temporal patterns of response to SGLT inhibition, identifying how physiological effects emerge and evolve over time. This will clarify the dynamics of treatment response and inform optimal timing and duration of benefits.

Methods and analysis

The systematic literature search was conducted on 5 March 2025, and study screening and eligibility assessment were completed in April 2025. Ethics approval was obtained from University Health Network (UHN), Toronto, Canada on 23 September 2025. Establishment of data-sharing agreements with corresponding investigators commenced in October 2025 and is ongoing. At the time of protocol submission, data acquisition and harmonisation have commenced in October 2025. Statistical analysis has not yet begun. Data integration and primary analyses are anticipated to commence following completion of data transfer and quality checks (estimated June 2026), with results expected to be submitted for peer-reviewed publications and scientific presentations on completion of the planned analyses (estimated June 2027).

Protocol design and registration

This study protocol was developed in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses Protocol (PRISMA-P).36 Methodological quality was appraised using Joanna Briggs Institute Critical Appraisal Checklist for Studies Reporting Prevalence Data.37 The protocol is registered with the International Registration of Systematic reviews under registration number CRD420251001413.

Work to date

To date, we have developed a search strategy, conducted a systematic review and identified a set of relevant mechanistic studies that meet the inclusion criteria for the proposed participant-level meta-analysis. We have initiated engagement with corresponding investigators to assess interest in collaboration and have conducted preliminary meetings to outline the objectives, scope and anticipated impact of the project. While these preparatory steps have established a strong foundation, the phases of data acquisition, integration and statistical analysis for the meta-analysis have not yet commenced.

Eligibility criteria

All peer-reviewed interventional studies reporting on the effect of SGLT inhibition on GFR measured using exogenous filtration markers in adults aged ≥18 years were included. Inclusion was limited to studies that assessed measured GFR (mGFR) using accurate and validated continuous/standard or bolus infusion-based or dilution-based techniques. This includes inulin or iohexol clearance (gold standard or infusion method), as well as fluorescence (eg, sinistrin) or radiolabelled markers (eg, I-125 iothalamate or Tc-99m DTPA, 51Cr-EDTA) that provide a precise, direct measurement of GFR. We selected studies with the primary outcome of mGFR to ensure methodological rigour, as it relies on validated, gold-standard techniques that enhance the quality, accuracy and reliability of the data.

The included trials comprised randomised, non-randomised and crossover designs and encompassed both comparator and non-comparator studies. The following were excluded: mixed-method studies, observational studies (including cross-sectional, retrospective and prospective cohort designs), conference abstracts, case reports and case-series, letters, commentaries, notes, editorials and case-control studies. Additionally, qualitative study designs not reporting prevalence data, secondary analysis (eg, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, post hoc exploratory analyses), correspondence articles and predictive modelling papers, basic science research and studies using animal data were excluded. Studies using estimated GFR methods based on serum creatinine, cystatin C or other indirect markers were also excluded. Whenever multiple publications of the same dataset were available, the most recent, complete and comprehensive version was included to avoid duplication and maximise data integrity.

Database sources

A comprehensive search strategy was developed using a combination of database-specific subject headings and free-text terms related to the core concepts of SGLT1 and SGLT2 inhibitors and GFR. To identify relevant studies, a modified clinical trials search filter was applied to restrict results to interventional studies. Searches were further limited to human studies published in English. The literature search was limited to studies published in English due to feasibility constraints related to screening, data extraction and verification of complex physiological and methodological details. However, this restriction was applied at the literature identification stage only. Where high-quality non-English studies meeting the prespecified eligibility criteria are identified and individual participant data can be obtained with appropriate translation and quality assurance, such studies may be considered for inclusion. This approach balances feasibility with the aim of minimising language-related selection bias. The following databases were searched for articles on 5 March 2025: Ovid MEDLINE; Ovid Embase; Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews (CDSR; Ovid); and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (Ovid). All clinical trials in MEDLINE and Embase were identified using the Canada’s Drug Agency Search Filters Database search filter (Ottawa: Canada’s Drug Agency).38 The reference lists of all included publications were also manually screened to identify any additional eligible publications.

Search strategy

The PRISMA-Search Reporting Extension (PRISMA-S)39 was used to ensure the search strategy was transparent, reproducible and appropriately aligned with the review objectives. A Boolean search strategy, combining Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) and relevant free-text keywords, was employed to systematically identify eligible studies. The use of truncation (eg, the asterisk symbol ‘*’) allowed for the capture of variations of root words and partial terms. Search terms were developed collaboratively by the research team in consultation with an experienced health sciences librarian, ensuring that all pertinent concepts and synonyms were comprehensively represented across databases. This approach was designed to enhance the completeness, transparency and reproducibility of the review process.40 Additionally, the search terms and strategy were piloted and optimised prior to execution to improve both sensitivity (recall) and specificity (precision). A detailed table outlining the final search terms and strategies for each of the four databases was developed. See online supplemental material 1 for database-specific search strategies.

Selection of studies for inclusion

The search strategy was executed across relevant databases, and the list of retrieved titles was compiled using EndNote reference manager software (figure 1). A total of 5342 articles were identified (n=1207 from Medline; n=2568 from Embase; n=1562 from Central; and n=5 from CDSR). Before screening, n=1173 duplicate records were removed, and n=1534 were marked ineligible by Covidence automation tools.41 The remaining n=2635 unique articles were independently screened by two investigators by title and abstract for eligibility. After title and abstract screening, n=2562 articles were excluded, and full texts were sought for the remaining n=73 potentially relevant studies. Of these, n=7 could not be retrieved (eg, conference abstracts). The remaining n=66 full-text articles were independently screened for eligibility. Studies were excluded due to outcomes not meeting inclusion criteria (n=12), non-SGLT inhibitor interventions (n=7), ineligible study design (n=18), ongoing trial (n=8) or patient populations outside the scope of eligibility (n=1). As a result, n=20 studies met the inclusion criteria. At the time of the literature search, four completed but unpublished studies conducted by the UHN-Toronto (DAPA-SWEET (NCT04258371), INFINITI (NCT04965935)), Herlev and Gentofte University Hospital (EMPIRE PREVENT (NCT05084235, NCT05042973)) and Mario Negri Institute for Pharmacological Research (ADAPT (NCT04794517)) met eligibility criteria and were included, bringing the total number of studies included in this meta-analysis to n=24. Risk of bias assessment

Risk of bias in included randomised controlled trials will be assessed using the revised Cochrane Risk of Bias tool (RoB 2),42 which evaluates bias across five domains: bias arising from the randomisation process; bias due to deviations from intended interventions; bias due to missing outcome data; bias in measurement of the outcome; and bias in selection of the reported result. Risk-of-bias assessments will be conducted independently by two reviewers who will undergo calibration and training using the RoB 2 guidance prior to formal assessment. Any disagreements will be resolved through discussion, and if consensus is not reached, a third reviewer will adjudicate. Risk-of-bias judgments will be incorporated into the interpretation of findings and sensitivity analyses, where appropriate.

Study leadership, data coordination and governance

This participant-level meta-analysis will be led by a centralised data and analysis hub at the UHN, Toronto, Canada (lead site), which is responsible for the secure acquisition, management and harmonisation of individual participant data across contributing studies. First, to ensure a comprehensive and reproducible search strategy, the lead site collaborated closely with a health sciences librarian at UHN during the development and execution of the systematic review. Second, the overall methodological framework, including the study design, statistical analysis plan and data integration protocols for the meta-analysis, was co-developed and jointly coordinated by investigators at UHN, Toronto, Canada and Amsterdam University Medical Center, Amsterdam, the Netherlands (coordinating sites). These leadership teams were responsible for developing the analytical framework, overseeing the systematic review process and guiding all aspects of data harmonisation. All collaborators contributing data are included as co-authors in recognition of their critical roles in study conduct and interpretation. Third, to ensure methodological rigour and centralised oversight, anonymised participant-level data from eligible studies will be submitted directly to the lead site (UHN), where standardised procedures for data cleaning, quality control and statistical analysis will be implemented. This centralised model will enable consistent application of inclusion criteria, analytical methods and transparency in data handling across all contributing studies. All analyses will be conducted independently by the coordinating investigators according to the prespecified protocol and statistical analysis plan, irrespective of funding sources or disclosed competing interests.

While the study aims to include all eligible trials identified through the systematic review, we acknowledge that participant-level data may not be available for certain studies due to institutional, logistical or regulatory constraints. In such cases, these studies will be documented in accordance with PRISMA guidelines and described narratively, but will not be included in the quantitative meta-analysis.

We have established a structured approach for engaging with corresponding authors, including multiple follow-up attempts and documentation of data availability. Additionally, we recognise that some investigators may possess relevant, yet unpublished data. In these cases, we developed a predefined mechanism for incorporating such data, provided the following conditions are met: (1) the data meet the pre-specified inclusion and quality criteria; (2) the contributing investigators confirm intent for publication; (3) appropriate ethics approvals are in place; and (4) the respective primary manuscripts are published prior to publication of the meta-analysis findings. The inclusion of such data has been explicitly described to maintain transparency and methodological integrity. All studies meeting the prespecified eligibility criteria identified through the preregistered systematic review will be recorded, irrespective of publication status. For each eligible study, all attempts to solicit individual participant data (including dates and responses) will be documented. Studies will be classified a priori as eligible, ineligible or eligible-but-data-unavailable based on preregistered inclusion criteria, thereby minimising post-hoc selection. Completed but unpublished studies will be included only if they meet the preregistered eligibility criteria, have appropriate ethics approval, and the contributing investigators confirm intent to publish the primary study. Reasons for non-participation or data unavailability will be reported. To assess the potential impact of selective data availability, a study-level selection sensitivity analysis will be performed by comparing primary results from the full individual participant data meta-analysis with analyses excluding unpublished studies and, where possible, with corresponding aggregate-level estimates from published reports. Consistency of direction and magnitude of effects across these analyses will be used to evaluate robustness to potential reporting bias.

Data collection process

The UHN team has reached out to the investigators of all eligible studies to formally invite participation in the meta-analysis and request individual participant-level data. Participating study teams were provided with a structured data extraction form along with a detailed data dictionary. The form (online supplemental file 2) included fields of the following variables: study design details, SGLT inhibitor drug type and dosage, treatment duration, participant demographics, medical history, list and dosage of concomitant medications, vital signs, GFR measurement method, renal haemodynamic parameters, plasma electrolytes and biochemistries, neurohormonal markers, echocardiographic parameters, spot and 24-hour urine data, tubular sodium handling, non-invasive cardiac output monitoring, arterial stiffness measures and assessments of fluid volume and body composition (eg, fat mass). Study teams will have the option to either submit data already formatted according to our structured data extraction form or provide raw, de-identified datasets, which we will subsequently standardise using our template. All data transfers will be conducted via secure, encrypted channels to ensure confidentiality and compliance with data protection regulations. Once all data has been formatted to our collection form, we will proceed with our statistical analysis plan.

Statistical analysis plan

Overall analytical framework

For each outcome of interest, we will conduct a one-stage individual participant data meta-analysis under a Bayesian framework, using hierarchical models to simultaneously analyse the mean difference of each variable from all eligible studies. This approach enables flexible regression modelling of participant-level covariates (prognostic factors) and treatment-covariate interactions (effect modifiers), while accounting for clustering by study and between-study heterogeneity. However, alternative analytic approaches may be considered where appropriate based on the data structure and specific research questions. All models will be implemented using the brms package in R, which supports Bayesian multilevel modelling using the probabilistic programming language Stan (mc-stan.org). Random intercepts will be specified for study-level clustering, and treatment effects will be modelled with either fixed or random slopes depending on the degree of observed heterogeneity.

Key concomitant medications known to influence renal and cardiovascular physiology (including angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors/angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs), mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, glucagon-like peptide (GLP)-1 receptor agonists, insulin and loop diuretics) will be included as prespecified covariates in adjusted models where relevant. All contributing trials were short-term studies with protocol-specified stability of background therapies; dose modification of these agents was not permitted during follow-up. Accordingly, analyses will assume stable concomitant medication exposure. Where mechanistically appropriate, mediation analyses will be explored to evaluate hypothesised pathways (eg, tubular sodium handling, TGF, arteriolar tone). Sensitivity analyses will assess the robustness of findings to alternative adjustment sets.

For longitudinal analyses, repeated measures will be modelled using hierarchical structures with visits nested within participants and participants nested within studies. Given that most contributing trials are short-term physiological studies, time-course analyses will primarily characterise acute treatment responses over the available follow-up period rather than distinct acute and chronic exposure phases. A minimum of two post-baseline timepoints per participant will be required for inclusion in trajectory analyses. Data from parallel and crossover designs will be aligned by treatment exposure, with appropriate modelling of within-participant correlation in crossover studies. These analyses will be considered secondary to the primary pooled final-timepoint analyses.

Missing data handling and data harmonisation

Intermittent missing outcome or covariate data will be handled within the Bayesian hierarchical model framework using joint modelling or multiple imputation approaches, ensuring consistency with the primary analysis model. During data harmonisation, units will be standardised and values below detection limits appropriately censored. Sensitivity analyses will be performed under plausible missing-not-at-random scenarios to assess the robustness of the estimated treatment effects and interactions.

All variables will undergo standardised quality control procedures during data harmonisation, including range and plausibility checks based on assay specifications, physiological expectations and predefined unit ranges documented in the MOSAIC data dictionary. Extreme or implausible values will be flagged and verified in consultation with contributing investigators where possible. Outliers will not be excluded by default; instead, robust modelling approaches (eg, heavy-tailed likelihoods) will be used where appropriate, and sensitivity analyses excluding extreme values will be performed to assess robustness. All quality control decisions and variable definitions will be documented to ensure transparency and reproducibility.

mGFR values obtained using different tracers (inulin, iohexol, radiolabelled or fluorescent markers) and infusion methods (bolus, standard or continuous) will be harmonised using study-level random effects or offsets to account for method-specific differences. All mGFR values will be analysed unindexed to body surface area. Laboratory units for electrolytes, proteins and other biomarkers will be standardised across studies. For parameters derived from Gomez equations, blood pressure and total protein/albumin measurements will be aligned as closely as possible to the timing of the mGFR assessment to ensure internal consistency.

Prior specification, model diagnostics and reproducibility

Weakly informative priors will be applied to regularise estimates and improve convergence. This will be pre-specified for key parameters, including normal priors centred at zero for treatment effects and regression coefficients, and weakly informative half-normal or half-Student-t priors for between-study SD. Model convergence will be assessed using trace plots, effective sample sizes and R-hat statistics, and model adequacy will be evaluated using posterior predictive checks. To support reproducibility, all data harmonisation steps and statistical analyses will be performed using prespecified, version-controlled scripts. Model code, prior specifications and analysis workflows will be archived and made available on publication, subject to data-sharing agreements. Risk of bias will be assessed using Cochrane RoB-2. Prespecified subgroup analyses and treatment-by-covariate interactions will be explored to assess potential effect modifiers of the physiological response to SGLT inhibition.

Subgroup analyses, heterogeneity and non-linear effects

Given the heterogeneity of the patient populations included in this meta-analysis proposal, we plan to perform cohort-specific subgroup analyses to better understand how SGLT inhibitors affect physiological variables across different clinical contexts. As such, subgroup analyses will be stratified by key study and participant characteristics, including underlying conditions (eg, type 1 vs type 2 diabetes, diabetes vs non-diabetes), renal status (eg, native vs kidney transplant), cardiac status (eg, heart failure vs non-heart failure), treatment characteristics (eg, SGLT inhibitor dose, duration of exposure (acute vs chronic), and study design features). These subgroup analyses aim to identify specific physiological effects or underlying mechanisms of SGLT inhibition that are more pronounced (or attenuated) in certain patient groups. They will also help uncover potential effect modifiers, such as demographic factors, comorbidities, disease state or treatment regimens, that may influence the magnitude or direction of response to SGLT inhibition. To address heterogeneity and multiplicity, treatment-by-covariate interactions will be modelled using hierarchical shrinkage priors to reduce the risk of false discoveries, and continuous covariates (eg, baseline GFR, haematocrit, natriuresis) will be evaluated using flexible spline-based methods to capture potential non-linear effects. Credible subgroup effects will be defined based on the magnitude and precision of estimated effects, consistency across studies and posterior probability thresholds.

Patient and public involvement

As part of our dissemination plan, the MOSAIC team will collaborate with patient partners to develop and share study results in accessible, lay-friendly formats for diverse audiences, including the kidney, cardiovascular and diabetes communities. Specifically, we will co-create plain-language, community-facing and public-facing materials, such as video explainers, infographics and handouts, that describe the intervention, research outcomes, policy implications and key findings. These resources will be shared through relevant patient networks, clinical settings and social media channels to maximise reach and impact.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethics approval

Ethics approval for this participant-level meta-analysis was obtained from UHN, Toronto (25-5628). All contributing studies received ethics approval from their respective institutional review boards, and all participants provided informed consent in the original studies. Only de-identified participant-level data will be shared with the coordinating centre for the purposes of this meta-analysis.

Data curation and data management

De-identified individual participant-level data from eligible studies will be securely transferred to the lead coordinating centre at UHN, Toronto, Canada. Data will be curated using standardised procedures, including consistency checks, range and plausibility assessments, harmonisation of variable definitions and resolution of discrepancies in collaboration with the original study investigators. A detailed data dictionary will be maintained to document variable definitions, units, transformations and derivation of harmonised variables across studies.

All data will be stored on secure, access-controlled servers in compliance with institutional policies and applicable data protection regulations. Access to the pooled dataset will be restricted to authorised members of the analysis team. Version control and audit trails will be implemented to ensure transparency and reproducibility of data processing and statistical analyses.

Participant-level data will be used exclusively for the predefined objectives outlined in this protocol. Any additional analyses will require approval from the MOSAIC steering committee and, where applicable, the original data contributors.

Dissemination

Findings from the MOSAIC meta-analysis will be published in a peer-reviewed journal and results will be disseminated at scientific conferences.

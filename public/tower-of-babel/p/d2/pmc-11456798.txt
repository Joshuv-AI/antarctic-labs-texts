MicroRNA-376a-3p sensitizes CPT-11-resistant colorectal cancer by enhancing apoptosis and reversing the epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) through the IGF1R/PI3K/AKT pathway
Authors: Chikondi Jassi, Wei-Wen Kuo, Yu-Chun Chang, Tso-Fu Wang, Tsung-Jung Ho, Dennis Jine-Yuan Hsieh, Chia-Hua Kuo, Ming-Cheng Chen, Chi-Cheng Li, Chih-Yang Huang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Highlights•The role of miRNA-376a-3p in colorectal cancer (CRC) resistant to CPT-11 was investigated.•Resistance to CPT-11 is associated with increased migratory behavior and a shift toward a mesenchymal phenotype in CRC cells.•Co-treatment with CPT-11 and miRNA-376a-3p significantly improved the therapeutic effectiveness of CPT-11.•Transfection of miRNA-376a-3p along with CPT-11 reversed the migratory behavior and mesenchymal phenotype in CRC cells.•The combination of CPT-11 and miRNA-376a-3p effectively inhibited tumor progression.Keywords: Colorectal cancer, CPT-11, CPT-11 resistance, IGF1R, miRNA-376a-3p

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is the third most prevalent type of cancer worldwide. As of 2020, an estimated 10 % of all cancer incidences were reported to be due to CRC alone [[1], [2], [3]]. Treatment for CRC includes Irinotecan (CPT-11), as a first-line regimen which is administered as a single or in combination with other drugs [4]. A semisynthetic derivative of camptothecin, CPT-11 is a pro-drug that is activated by the action of carboxylesterase enzymes to yield a 100–1000-fold active metabolite, SN-38. SN-38 binds and inhibits topoisomerase 1 activity which is the basis for the CPT-11 anti-cancer mechanism [[4], [5], [6], [7]]. However, despite the CPT-11′s overall positive contribution towards the treatment of many cancers worldwide [8], resistance, is a frequently reported limitation in some fraction of patients [9]. Chemotherapy resistance in CRC may be marked by failure to inhibit tumor progression, tumor recurrence, invasion, and metastasis. It is noteworthy that many cancers including CRC (accounting for approximately 90 %) develop from epithelial cells [[10], [11], [12]]. These types of cells are tightly attached, forming sheets of cells with apical-basal polarity [13]. Furthermore, these cells have high proliferative potential, a characteristic that favors the cytotoxic mechanisms of many anti-tumor drugs targeting DNA [14]. Most CRCs are diagnosed at the time the tumor has already metastasized. To undergo metastasis, cancerous epithelial cells transform from their epithelial to the mesenchymal phenotype, a reversible process called epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) [15]. In contrast to epithelial, the mesenchymal phenotypic cells have low proliferative potential and are loosely attached, a characteristic that favors their migration and invasion. In addition, these cells have increased drug efflux, and evade apoptosis and the immune system, characteristics that reduce anti-cancer potential, hence drug resistance [16]. EMT is characterized by the downregulation of epithelial markers such as E-cadherin and the upregulation of mesenchymal markers such as N-cadherin among others.

There are numerous mechanisms through which EMT can be triggered, many of which are interconnected [17,18]. One noteworthy mechanism involves the activation of protein tyrosine kinases (PTKs), such as insulin-like growth factor type 1 receptor (IGF1R) or epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) [17]. Receptor PTKs have an important impact on drug resistance through their regulation of anti-apoptotic signal transduction pathways [19,20]. IGF1R is a receptor tyrosine kinase whose function and action are activated by the binding of insulin-like growth factor ligand (IGF). Overexpression of IGF1R enhances its sensitivity to its activators. The binding of the IGF ligands activates and triggers autophosphorylation of IGF1R which further activate several of its downstream pathways [21]. One of the main pathways activated by IGF1R is PI3/AKT pathway [22]. Activation of this pathway often results in further activation or suppression of other downstream genes most of whose out of several functions include activation or inhibition of EMT transcription factors [23]. IGF1R has long been reported to be a target of many microRNAs (miRNAs). MicroRNAs are short non-coding RNA that function to suppress gene translation and expression by binding to the 3′ untranslated region of their target genes [24]. Previously, we observed an upregulation of miRNA-376a-3p in CPT-11-responsive LoVo cells after treatment with CPT-11 [25]. This upregulation suggested its potential as a tumor suppressor. In this study, we aimed to investigate the potential role of miRNA-376a-3p and its association with IGF1R-activated EMT in both in vitro and an in vivo xenograft of CPT-11-resistant model.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

CRC cell line, LoVo was purchased from Bioresource Collection and Research Center (Hsinchu, Taiwan). CPT-11 resistant cells (CPT-11R) were generated by sequential treatment of LoVo cells with CPT-11 starting from low dose with gradual increase of the dosage until a considerable difference in terms of response to treatment was observed between the parental cells and the prospective resistant cells. This process was conducted for a period of over a year. These cells were maintained by constant treatment of CPT-11 throughout the study. The cells including other CRC's, Caco2, were cultured and maintained in Roswell Park memorial institute-1640 medium (RPMI-1640) (Gibco® Grand Island, NY, USA) at 37 °C temperature and 5 % CO2 atmosphere. The media were supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Sigma Aldrich's. Louis, USA), and 1 % Penicillin/Streptomycin/Ampicillin (PSA). The medium was replaced every 2–3 days. Dulbecco's phosphate-buffered saline (PBS; Gibco®, Gland Island, USA) was used to wash cells in culture plates.

Compounds

The compound Irinotecan was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, China (CPT-11) (product # I1406). CPT-11 was dissolved in DMSO to a stock solution and stored in −20 °C.

MTT (4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)−2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide) assay

Cells were seeded at a density of 3.0 × 103 cells per well in triplicates in a 24 well plate. Following overnight incubation at 37 °C with 5 % CO2, the media was aspirated, and the cells were washed twice with PBS. Subsequently, fresh media with 10 % FBS was added. The cells were then treated with appropriate concentrations including the controls. At designated time points, depending on the experimental requirement, the media was replaced with a solution of 5 mg/mL MTT at 10 times dilution in fresh media without FBS, and incubated for 3 h. The MTT was then removed followed by the addition of dissolving agent DMSO and incubated on a shaker for 15 min. The absorbance of the plate was then measured at 570 nm, including a blank for background correction. Cell viability was determined by subtracting the blank optical density (OD) values from the main wells and normalizing the average OD values with those of control cells for each group per time point.

Western blot

BCA (Biorad, USA) was used for the quantification of cell lysate and protein concentration. The extracted proteins were combined with 1 × loading buffer and subjected to boiling at 95 °C for 5 min prior to loading onto 7 % - 15 % sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE). Subsequently, the proteins were transferred from the gel to polyvinylidene fluoride membranes (Merk Millipore, Ireland). These membranes were then blocked in 5 % skimmed milk in tris-buffered saline with tween 20 (TBST) at room temperature for 1 hour and incubated with primary antibodies in TBST at the following dilutions: PARP (Cell Signaling; #9542 anti-rabbit, 1:1000), procaspase 3 (Cell Signaling; anti-rabbit, #9662, 1:1000), Cleaved caspase 3 (Cell Signaling; #9664, anti-rabbit, 1:1000), Housekeeping control β-actin (Santa Cruz; sc-47,778, anti-mouse, 1:1000), IGF1R (Santa Cruz, sc-81,464, anti-mouse, 1:1000), PI3K p100 (Santa Cruz, Sc 365,404, anti-mouse, 1:1000), p-PI3K p85 (Cell Signaling, anti-rabbit, 1:1000), AKT1 (Santa Cruz; sc5298, anti-mouse, 1:1000), p-AKT (Cell Signaling; #9275, anti-rabbit, 1:1000), E-cadherin (Santa Cruz, sc-8426, anti-mouse, 1:1000), N-cadherin (Santa Cruz, sc-271,386, anti-mouse, 1:1000), vimentin ( Santa Cruz, sc-32,322, anti-mouse, 1:1000) and SNAI1(Santa Cruze, sc-10,433, anti-goat, 1:1000) at 4 °C overnight. Following washing with TBST on a rocking shaker, the membranes were incubated with anti-rabbit and anti-mouse/rabbit/goat IgG secondary antibodies (1:60,000) at room temperature for 1 hour and washed with TBST. Subsequently, the membranes were exposed to a 1:1 mixture of solution A and solution B of Immobillin® western chemiluminescence horseradish peroxidase substrate (HRP; Millipore, Germany). The blots were then visualized using an automated fluorescence chemiluminescence image analysis system (ImageQuant LAS 4000 mini).).

TUNEL assay

After the appropriate treatments, cells were stained using the TUNEL (Terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP Nick End Labeling) technique employing an In-situ Cell Death Detection Kit, TMR red/green (Roche Diagnostics, Mannheim, Germany) in accordance with the manufacturer's guidelines. In summary, following the designated treatment duration, cells seeded on chamber slides were fixed with a 4 % formalin solution for 30 min. Subsequent permeabilization of the cells was achieved by incubating them with 0.2 % Triton X for 2 min on ice, followed by staining with TUNEL reagents in darkness for one hour at 37 °C. Finally, counterstaining was carried out using Fluoroshield with DAPI, and the slides were cover-slipped prior to examination under a fluorescence microscope (Olympus DP73, Tokyo, Japan).

Immunofluorescence

Cells were cultured on chamber slides and treated for the appropriate time period. Following treatment, cells were fixed in a 4 % paraformaldehyde solution in PBS for 10 min, and then permeabilized with 0.2 % Triton X-100 for an additional 10 min at room temperature. Subsequently, the cells were washed with PBS and incubated in a blocking solution (consisting of 1 % BSA and 22.52 mg/mL glycine in PBST) for 30 min. Next, the cells were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies, diluted in 1 % BSA in PBST. After decanting the solution and washing with PBS, the cells were incubated with secondary antibodies (Alexa Fluor 488, Invitrogen) at room temperature for 1 hour. Following this, the cells were counterstained with DAPI and examined under a fluorescence microscope (Olympus DP73, Tokyo, Japan).

Identification of miRNA-376a-3p predicted target genes

Web based bioinformatic tools TargetScan [26], miRTarBase [27], and miRTargetLink 2.0 [28] were used to predict for miRNA-376a-3p target genes. DAVID [29], another web based bioinformatic resource system was used for functional annotation of genes list.

IGF1R siRNA and miRNA transfection

MicroRNA mimics and controls were obtained from RiboBio (Guangzhou, China), while IGF1R siRNA was acquired from Sigma-Aldrich (Germany). Transfections were facilitated using JetPrime® transfection reagent (Polyplus Transfection Inc., New York, USA), following the manufacturer's instructions. Briefly, cells were seeded in 60 cm petri dishes and allowed to adhere for 24 h. Subsequently, miRNA mimics or siRNAs, along with their respective controls, were mixed with JetPrime® reagent in appropriate volumes and incubated for 15 min. This mixture was then added dropwise to the cells in the plates and incubated for an additional 24 h. Depending on the experimental requirements, cells underwent further treatments or were directly harvested for protein or RNA extraction for subsequent analyses.

Cellular rna extraction, reverse transcription and rt-qpcr analysis

After appropriate treatments, total RNA extraction was conducted using TRIzol reagent (Ambion, USA). In summary, cells were washed with PBS prior to the addition of TRIzol (500 μL/6 cm dish). Subsequently, the lysed mixture was homogenized and transferred to an Eppendorf tube and combined with an appropriate volume of chloroform. Following this, centrifugation at 12,000 rpm at 4 °C for 15 min ensued. The RNA was precipitated by mixing the supernatant (upper aqueous phase) with an equal volume of isopropanol in a new Eppendorf tube, then subjected to centrifugation at 12,000 g at 4 °C for 10 min. Subsequent to removal of the supernatant, the RNA precipitate/pellet was washed twice in 75 % ethanol by centrifugation at 7500 rpm at 4 °C for 5 min each time. The supernatant was then discarded, and the RNA precipitate was air-dried at room temperature. Finally, an appropriate volume of RNase-free water was added to the Eppendorf tube to dissolve the precipitate. The quality and concentration of total RNA were determined using the SimpliNano Nano spectrophotometer (GE Healthcare Biosciences Inc. AB. England). For RT-qPCR analysis, 1 μg of RNA was used to generate cDNA using the mir-xTM miRNA First strand synthesis and TB Green RT-qPCR kit (Takara Bio, USA. Inc.), following the manufacturer's instructions. The prepared cDNA was subsequently utilized to prepare the RT-qPCR mixture, with quantification performed using the Biorad CFX PCR reader, all in triplicates.

Luciferase reporter assay

The wild-type (WT) and mutant (MUT) luciferase constructs for the 3′-untranslated region (UTR) of IGF1R were constructed utilizing a pmirGLO vector (MDBio, Taiwan). Cells were plated into a 6-well plate for transfection, and both constructs were co-transfected with either miR-376a-3p mimic or a negative control employing JetPRIME® reagents as per the manufacturer's instructions. Following 48 h of transfection, the supernatant was removed, and the cells underwent two washes with PBS. Subsequently, 500 µL of Passive Lysis Buffer (PLB) was introduced, and the lysate was collected in sterile Eppendorf tubes. Luciferase activity was assessed employing a dual-luciferase assay kit (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) in accordance with the manufacturer's protocol. In brief, 100 µL of Luciferase Assay Reagent II (LAR II) was combined with 20 µL of the lysate and quantified using a SpectraMax® i3x (Molecular Devices, USA), followed by the addition and quantification of 100 µL of Stop and Glo Reagent serving as a control reporter.

Immunohistochemistry

The paraffin-embedded tumor sections were incubated with anti-IGF1R, anti-E-cadherin (Santa cruz and anti-N-cadherin (Santa cruz). The immunohistochemistry assay was performed as previously described by Magaki et al. [30].

Animal model

In the preclinical model, six-week-old male NU/NU nude mice were used, procured from BioLASCO Taiwan Co., Ltd. (Taipei, Taiwan), and employed in accordance with the Humane Care and Use of Laboratory Animals policy. Animal welfare was overseen by the Laboratory Animal Service Center (LASC) of China Medical University (CMU), adhering to the principles of the 3Rs (Replacement, Reduction, and Refinement). Prior to experimentation, the entire protocol underwent review and approval by the LASC CMU Committee (Approval code no 2024–195).

Initially, previously established CPT-11 resistant cells from the LoVo cell line were suspended in serum-free Matrigel medium (BD Biosciences), then injected subcutaneously into the hind legs of the mice. Tumor dimensions, including height, length, and width, were measured using a digital caliper. Tumor volumes were subsequently calculated utilizing the formula V= (width² x length) x 0.5, as described previously [31]. Upon reaching a tumor size of 100 mm³, the mice were grouped into five treatment cohorts: Control, Mimic Negative Control (MNC), CPT-11, Mimic, and Combination (Mimic and CPT-11). Intratumoral injections were administered sequentially, starting with the miRNA mimic, followed by the remaining treatments. Subsequent treatments were administered every 48 h over a four-week period. Ultimately, upon completion of the experiment, the mice were euthanized, and the tumors were excised for further analysis.

Statistical analysis

The results are derived from a minimum of three independent experimental replicates and are depicted as the mean with standard deviation (±). Two-sided statistical comparisons between groups were executed employing both Student's t-test and two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), depending on the sample distribution. Statistical calculations were carried out using GraphPad Prism version 8 and Excel 2016. A p-value below 0.05 was deemed statistically significant.

Results

CPT-11 resistance is associated with the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and cell migration

Scientific literature strongly supports the assertion that the transition of cells from an epithelial to a mesenchymal phenotype, a phenomenon known as Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition (EMT), significantly contributes to the metastasis of cancer cells and the development of resistance to treatment [10,18]. Cells undergoing or already in an EMT state, show morphological changes from tightly connected to loosely attached and spindle-shaped appearance [16]. To validate this, we first established a CPT-11 resistant cell by continuous treatment of LoVo cells with initially low then increasing dosages in LoVo cells for over a year. To confirm the resistance, we periodically, performed an MTT assay and observed the response in terms of viability as well as determined the half-maximal inhibitory concentrations (IC50s) for both parental (LoVo) and the prospective resistant cells. With time, the MTT revealed the differences in cell viability between the two cells in responses to different CPT-11 concentrations. The IC50s for the two cells were also different. The IC50 for the prospective CPT-11 resistant (later designated CPT-11R) cells was almost three times that of LoVo cells at 48 h and the difference was even higher at 72 h (Fig. 1A-1C). We again treated these cells with different CPT-11 concentrations and subsequently extracted protein for western blot analysis. Western blot was performed to evaluate the expression of topoisomerase I (Topo I) which is the main target for CPT-11. LoVo cells were more responsive to treatment with CPT-11 in a dose-dependent manner as depicted by decreased expression of Topoisomerase I in Fig. 1D. To investigate the association between CPT-11 resistance and the transition of epithelial cell phenotype to mesenchymal phenotype (EMT) capability, we initially contrasted the morphological characteristics of untreated LoVo cells with untreated CPT-11R cells. LoVo cells exhibited a closely attached, cobblestone appearance, whereas CPT-11R cells displayed a distinct separation and spindle-shaped morphology (Fig. 1E). EMT is often associated with enhanced migration ability in cells. To delve into this aspect, we conducted a transwell migration assay to assess the differential migratory potential of the two untreated cell types. We observed a significant difference in migration between the two groups. While both cell types demonstrated migration, CPT-11R cells exhibited a notably higher number of migrated cells (Fig. 1F). The differences in migration of the two cells were also confirmed by wound healing assay. At 24 h, scratch wound gap coverage was observed to differ between the two cells. CPT-11R cells were observed to cover the wound gap faster than the LoVo cells. The trend continued through 36 h of observation time up to 48 h when the gap was completely covered by CPT-11R cells while LoVo cells had still a gap yet to be covered (Fig. 1G). The differences were significant at all time points, signifying that CPT-11R cells were highly migratory and faster than the LoVo cells. To link the above-observed attributes to EMT, we extracted protein from these two cells (LoVo and CPT-11R) for western blot analysis. Although protein expressions among cells are cell dependent, we also included another colorectal cancer cell line, Caco2. Then we performed a western blot to evaluate the expression of EMT markers. We observed the differences in the expression of EMT markers among these cells. Expression of E-cadherin, an epithelial marker was low in CPT-11R cells compared to LoVo and Caco2 cells. Mesenchymal markers, N-cadherin, vimentin and SNAI1 were higher in CPT-11R cells compared to LoVo and Caco2 cells, suggestive of transformation to mesenchymal phenotype (Fig. 1H). Since these cells were experimented on in untreated states, we meant to characterize these attributes in treated states to determine the response to treatment. For this purpose, we treated these cells with a concentration slightly above the IC50 of LoVo cells (20 μM). First, we extracted the protein from these two cells 48 h post-treatments, followed by a Western blot analysis to assess EMT markers. Notably, while there was a noticeable impact of CPT-11 on N-cadherin,vimentin, SNAI1 and E-cadherin expression, the responses observed in Caco2 and LoVo cells were markedly more pronounced compared to CPT-11R cells (Fig. 1I). Secondly, we conducted a transwell migration assay using the two cell lines, LoVo and the CPT-11R counterpart. The assay demonstrated the sensitivity of LoVo cells to CPT-11, as evidenced by a decrease in the number of migrated cells. Although there was a reduction in the number of migrated cells in CPT-11R group, there was no significant difference between the migration of untreated LoVo cells and CPT-11R cells following treatment. Furthermore, we observed an increase in possible apoptotic cells, in LoVo cells under light microscopy, that was confirmed by TUNEL staining, in comparison to CPT-11R cells (Fig. 1J). These outcomes not only confirmed the successful establishment of CPT-11 resistant LoVo cells (CPT-11R) but also provided the insight that, these CPT-11R cells transformed or partly transformed from epithelial to mesenchymal type (EMT) and that CPT-11 resistance might be associated with EMT and migration.

MiRNA-376a-3p is downregulated in CPT-11 resistant colorectal cancer cells

We previously noted an upregulation of miRNA-376a-3p when CPT-11 was administered in CRC LoVo cells [25]. This observation hinted at its potential involvement and tumor suppressive role in CPT-11 mechanism. For this reason, we hypothesized that, this upregulation might be reversed in CPT-11 resistant cells. To substantiate our hypothesis, we initially validated the upregulation of miRNA-376a-3p in Lovo cells following treatment with CPT-11. Subsequently, we conducted a comparative analysis of miRNA-376a-3p expression between the parental and the established resistant cells. The findings unequivocally demonstrated the upregulation of miRNA-376a-3p in parental Lovo cells upon exposure to CPT-11(Fig. 2A). Furthermore, we compared the expression of this miRNA in un treated Lovo parental and CPT-11R cells. We observed the miRNA-376a-3p downregulation in untreated CPT-11R cells when compared to the untreated parental counterparts (Fig. 2B). Additionally, we compared the expression of miRNA-376a-3p between the CPT-11-treated and the untreated CPT-11R cells. We observed that there was a slight but noticiable increase in miRNA-376a-3p in treated cells compared to untreated cells (Fig. 2C). These results demonstrated a potential role of miRNA in CPT-11 mechanisms and a partial insight into our hypothesis, that miRNA-376a-3p is downregulated in CPT-11 resistant cells.

IGF1R and its downstream PI3K/AKT pathway are upregulated in CPT-11 resistant cells

We used the online bioinformatics tools, TargetScan, miRTarbase, and miRTargetLink, to identify predicted target genes for miRNA-376a-3p. We identified 16 genes overlapping among these data bases (Fig. 3A-3B). Additionally, we employed another bioinformatic tool, DAVID, for functional annotations of the 16 genes. Our analysis, revealed PI3K/AKT pathway, immune response, plasma membrane, and focal adhesion annotated against ITGBB8, IGF1R and PIK3R1 genes (Fig. 3C). Given that IGF1R is a prevalent tyrosine kinase receptor associated with cell survival and EMT induction, with the PI3K/AKT as its downstream survival pathway, we hypothesized that the downregulation of miRNA-376a-3p could be a potential mechanism through which cancer cells sustain their survival. For this reason, the probable upregulation of IGF1R in CPT-11R could not be ignored. We therefore extracted protein from the three untreated colorectal cancer LoVo, and Caco2 including the CPT-11R cells to perform a western blot for IGF1R protein expression. As anticipated, the expression of IGF1R in CPT-11R cells was more pronounced than the rest of the cell lines (Fig. 3D). We repeated the experiment with immunofluorescence method and the results demonstrated the same trend with higher expression of IGF1R in CPT-11R cells (Fig. 3E). We then compared the expression and response of PI3K/AKT pathway in response to CPT-11 between Lovo and CPT-11R cells using western blot. Consistently, the results demonstrated that the expression of the PI3K/AKT pathway was significantly higher and exhibited a lower degree of change in response to treatment in CPT-11R cells compared to LoVo cells (Fig. 3F). These results suggests that IGF1R might have a role in CPT-11 resistance mechanism and this impact might be mediated through its downstream PI3K/AKT pathway.

Downregulation of IGF1R sensitizes resistant cells to CPT-11

The previous results from Fig. 3 suggests possible role of IGF1R in CPT-11 resistant mechanism. We were, therefore determined to investigate the role of IGF1R in CPT-11R cells. To achieve this, we used IGF1R siRNA to knockdown IGF1R. We, initially, transfected the CPT-11R cells with different concentration of siRNA to assess the optimum transfection dose for this experiment and extracted protein. Thereafter, western blot was performed to determine the expression of IGF1R. Results demonstrated that IGF1R was downregulated with increase in siRNA concentration, confirming the efficiency in transfection (Fig. 4A). We then adopted 100 nM treatment of siRNA for subsequent experiments. To determine the effect of IGF1R downregulation on cell viability and EMT, we treated the CPT-11R cells with 100 nM of siRNA, siRNA control, CPT-11 and the combination of CPT-11 and siRNA. As depicted in Fig. 4B, cell vibility was decreased upon IGF1R siRNA treatment and decreased even more when combined with CPT-11 treatment. Upon performing western blot, the epithelial marker E-cadherin was observed to upregulate when siRNA was transfected. Furthermore, combination of siRNA and CPT-11 upregulated E. cadherin further. In contrast, the mesenchymal markers N-cadherin, vimentin and SNAI1 were observed to downregulate when siRNA was transfected and was downregulated further when siRNA was combined with CPT-11(Fig. 4C). To support the results in Fig. 4C, we performed transwell and wound healing migration assays using the same treatment groups. Consistent with the findings on EMT markers, transfection with siRNA, reduced cell migration in both transwell and wound healing compared to the control groups. The inhibition of cell migration was even more pronounced when CPT-11 was combined with siRNA (Fig. 4D). To explore if IGF1R knockdown has an impact on PI3K/AKT pathway, we performed western blot to determine the expression of this pathway. As expected, PI3K/AKT pathway was downregulated when siRNA was transfected and the downregulation was even more pronounced when siRNA and CPT-11 were combined (Fig. 4E). These results suggest that downregulation of IGF1R sensitizes cells to CPT-11 exhibited by the, reversal of EMT, inhibition of cell migration and downregulation of PI3K/AKT survival pathway.

Overexpression of miRNA 376a-3p downregulates IGF1R in CPT-11 resistant Lovo cells

MiRNAs influences both physiological and pathological cellular process by supressing gene expression. So far, our findings indicate that miRNA-376a-3p is downregulated in CPT-11R cells and is predicted to target IGF1R. The findings further indicates that IGF1R is upregulated in these cells. We therefore, hypothesized that the upregulation of IGF1R in resistant cells might be partially attributable to the limited function of miRNA-376a-3p in CPT-11R cells. For this reason, we were determined to elucidate the impact of miRNA-376a-3p in CPT-11R cells in relation to IGF1R function.

Firstly, we transfected CPT-11R cells with different concentration of miRNA-376a-3p mimic to determine the optimal treatment dose. The transfection efficiency was assessed by RT-qPCR, which demonstrated the upregulation of miRNA-376a-3p with increasing concentrations (Fig. 5A). Subsequently, we extracted proteins from these cells to assess the effect of miRNA-376a-3p mimic transfection on IGF1R expression through western blot analysis. The expression of IGF1R was observed to decrease with increasing concentrations of miRNA-376a-3p mimic, providing insight into their functional relationship (Fig. 5B). To confirm this, we utilized the online TargetScan bioinformatics tool to identify the predicted target region of miRNA-376a-3p on IGF1R. This region was found to be located between 1307 and 1314 of the 3′ untranslated region (UTR) of IGF1R (Fig. 5C).

Secondly, to further support the regulatory and functional relationship between miRNA376a-3p and IGF1R, we conducted luciferase reporter assay. The results revealed that co-transfection of the wild-type IGF1R plasmid with miRNA-376a-3p mimic significantly suppressed luciferase activity, whereas co-transfection of the mutant-type IGF1R with miRNA-376a-3p mimic did not show significant suppression, indicating that miRNA-376a-3p regulates IGF1R (Fig. 5D).

MiR-376a-3p sensitizes CPT-11 resistant cells to CPT-11

The data in section 3.5 revealed the direct targeting and suppressive role of miRNA-376a-3p on IGF1R. We aimed to determine if this effect impacts CPT-11 resistance status. We treated CPT-11R cells with different concentrations of CPT-11 and co-transfected them with either miRNA-376a-3p mimic or miRNA-376a-3p negative control. Subsequently, we conducted MTT assay on these cells. After 48 h, we observed the decrease in cell viability in those cells co-transfected with miRNA-376a-3p mimic compared to those co-transfected with negative control, with significant difference (Fig. 6A). To assess this impact on apoptosis, we transfected these cells with the miRNA-376a-3p mimic and extracted protein for western blot analysis. The western blot was conducted across five treatment groups: control, mimic negative control, mimic alone, CPT-11 alone and the combination of mimic and CPT-11. We evaluated the expression of apoptotic markers, PARP and caspase 3. The results demonstrated that, while the changes in expression of these apoptotic markers in CPT-11 and miRNA-376a-3p mimic and their respective control groups were minimal, the co-transfection group of miRNA-3p mimic and CPT-11 showed enhanced expression of both cleaved PARP and cleaved caspase 3, indicating the promotion of apoptosis (Fig. 6B). These results prompted us to perform a TUNEL assay to validate the effect of miRNA-376a-3p on apoptosis. Consistent with the MTT and western blot assays, transfection with mimic showed the increase in cells with TUNEL (red). While transfection with CPT-11 showed minimal expression, co-transfection of CPT-11 and miRNA-376a-3p showed even more significant increase in tunnel-positive cells compared to controls, mimic, and CPT-11 alone, indicating cells undergoing apoptosis (Fig. 6C). Given that we revealed the association of CPT-11 resistance with the transition of cells to mesenchymal phenotype, we were determined to explore the effect of miRNA-376a-3p on EMT. For this purpose, we performed western blot analysis to evaluate the expression of epithelial and mesenchymal markers, E-cadherin and N-cadherin, vimentin and SNAI1 respectively. We observed that E-cadherin was upregulated with mimic transfection. While transfection with CPT-11 alone showed minimal expression, E-cadherin was upregulated even more when miRNA-376a-3p mimic was co-transfection with CPT-11. The opposite expression pattern was observed with the mesenchymal markers, N-cadherin, vimentin and SNAI1, whose downregulation was more pronounced with the combination of miRNA-376a-3p and CPT-11, indicating the transition from mesenchymal to epithelial phenotype in this group (Fig. 6D). To determine if the migratory ability of these cells was also affected, we performed wound healing and transwell migratory assays. We observed that, the migratory rate and cell numbers were both reduced when mimic or CPT-11 was transfected. However, co-transfection of mimic and CPT-11 enhanced this effect (Fig. 6E-6F). These results suggest that, miRNA-376a-3p sensitizes CPT-11R cells to CPT-11 by promoting the reduction of cell viability, enhancing apoptosis, reversing the EMT to MET and inhibiting cell migration. Consolidating all these results, miRNA-376a-3p promote the response of CPT-11R cells to CPT-11.

MicroRNA-376a-3p sensitizes CPT-11 resistant cells to CPT-11 through PI3K/AKT pathway via IGF1R downregulation

Sensitization of CPT-11 resistant cells to CPT-11 as discussed in section 3.6 prompted us to investigate the mechanism by which miRNA-376a-3p imparts this effect. Given that PI3K/AKT pathway which is downstream of IGF1R, is upregulated in CPT-11R cells, and was downregulated when IGF1R was knocked down (Fig. 4E), we performed western blot analysis on the treatment groups as above to assess whether co-transfection of CPT-11 and miR-376a-3p would reverse this upregulation. Initially, we determined the expression of IGF1R in treated cells through immunofluorescence and western blotting. Both experiments revealed the downregulation of IGF1R in co-transfected group compared to controls as well as miR-376a-3p and CPT-11 groups in their individuality (Fig. 7A-7B). Subsequently, we evaluated the expression of PI3K/AKT pathway using western blot analysis. The results showed the pronounced downregulation of PI3K/AKT pathway in the co-transfected group compared to controls as well as to the miRNA-376a-3p and CPT-11 groups individually (Fig. 7C). These results suggest that the downregulation of IGF1R induced by the combination of miRNA-376a-3p and CPT-11 leads to the downregulation of PI3K/AKT pathway. This further suggests that sensitization of CPT-11 resistant cells to CPT-11 might be mediated through this mechanism.

MiRNA-376a-3p improved anti-tumor efficacy against murine CPT-11 resistant colorectal cancer xenograft in BALB/c nude mice

Finally, we aimed to investigate whether the results obtained in-vitro experiments could be replicated in a preclinical model. We established a CPT-11-resistant tumor xenograft in BALB/c Nude mice by subcutaneously injecting CPT-11-resistant cells into mice hind flanks and proceeded as described in section 2.12.. At the end of the treatment period, the results indicated that treatment with CPT-11 or miR-376a-3p showed improvement in tumor growth inhibition compared to their respective controls. However, co-treatment of miR-376a-3p and CPT-11 reduced tumor growth even further (Fig. 8A). The mice were euthanized and tumors removed. When the tumor sizes were measured, there was a notable tumor size reduction observed in tumors obtained from mice treated with a combination of CPT-11 and miRNA-376a-3p (Fig. 8B-8C). Furthermore, we measured the weights of these tumors and compared among the treatment groups. The average weight of the tumors from mice treated with both miRNA-376a-3p and CPT-11 had reduced tumor weight compared to controls, CPT-11 and miRNA-376a-3p individual groups (Fig. 8D). Subsequently, these tumor tissues underwent sectioning and were mounted on microscope slides for various staining techniques. Initially, to assess the impact of miRNA-376a-3p on tumor progression, eosin and hematoxylin staining (H&E) were employed. Based on the nuclear distribution, the combination of miR-376a-3p and CPT-11 inhibited tumor cell proliferation and progression compared to the control and other groups. Next, the tissues were stained with TUNEL to assess the induction of apoptosis. Consistently, the combination of CPT-11 and miRNA-376a-3p induced more apoptosis than other treatment groups based on the increased number of TUNEL positive cells (Fig. 8E). Additionally, we. extracted proteins from these tumors for western blot analysis. First, we confirmed the apoptosis effect by evaluating the expression of cleaved caspase 3 which was found to be increased in combination group (Fig. 8F). Secondly, the expression of IGF1R was found to be downregulated in miRNA-376a-3p and CPT-11 combination group, consistent with invitro findings (Fig. 8G). Since the invitro findings revealed the reversal of EMT to TEM, we aimed to determine the expression of EMT markers in these tumors by western blot. The results consistently exhibited the high expression of E-cadherin and low expression of N-cadherin, vimentin and SNAI1 in tumor groups treated with a combination of CPT-11 and miRNA-376a-3p compared to controls, CPT-11 and miRNA-376a-3p alone (Fig. 8H). Furthermore, we evaluated the response of the PI3K/AKT pathway and showed that it was downregulated, with the combination of CPT-11 and miRNA-376a-3p resulting in more pronounced downregulation (Fig. 8I). The downregulation of IGF1R and the reversal of EMT were further confirmed by immunohistochemistry, the results of which consistently corresponded to western blot analysis (Figure J). Finally, the probability of mice survival influenced by the treatment was predicted using Kaplan-Meier survival curves, which revealed that treatment with miRNA-376a in combination with CPT-11 improved the survival period compared to the rest of the treatment groups (Fig. 8K)

These results may suggest that miRNA-376a-3p sensitized the CPT-11 resistant tumor xenograft in the preclinical mouse model (In vivo) replicating the findings of the in vitro model.

Discussion

Our study aimed to investigate the involvement of miRNA-376a-3p in CPT-11 resistance and responsive mechanisms in colorectal cancer. The aim was driven based on the three elements. First, miRNAs, are short non-protein coding RNA and have been implicated to play a part in cancer drug mechanisms and resistance owing to their targeting and suppression of genes involved in different key processes of cancers [[32], [33], [34], [35]]. Secondly, CPT-11 is antineoplastic drug administered for the treatment of advanced colorectal cancer either alone or in combination with other drugs. In terms of drug resistance in colorectal cancer, CPT-11 is no exception [36]. Lastly, miRNA-376a-3p was observed to upregulate in Lovo cells in response to CPT-11 treatment.

We initially established and confirmed CPT-11 resistance in CPT-11R cells.

Previous studies have demonstrated the downregulation of various miRNAs in invasive and drug resistant cancers [37,38]. For instance, cancer associated fibroblasts (CAF) are the integral part of tumor microenvironment which contribute to tumor progression [39]. Within this realm, Wang et.al [40] explored the effect of miRNA-214 in gastric cancer and found that the downregulation of this miRNA in Gastric cancer-associated fibroblasts contributed to migration and invasion of gastric cancer. Consistently, our findings revealed the downregulation of miRNA-376a-3p in CPT-11 resistant cells which through our characterization was more invasive and less responsive to treatment compared to the parental counterpart, evidenced by the sustained cell viability, EMT status and migratory ability. These elements are the frequent invitro, in vivo and clinical observations and occurrences in drug resistant cells and tissues of various types [16,41]. This finding prompted us to suggest that miRNA-376a-3p has tumor suppressor role in colorectal cancer cells. Moreover, miRNA-376a expression levels were previously found to be significantly lower in several patients' cancer tissues, including osteosarcoma [42], gastric cancer [43], and lymphoma[44].

Given that, miRNAs function by supressing gene expression through direct targeting, we, assisted by the online bioinformatic tools, investigated and found that IGF1R was one of the predicted targets of miRNA-376a-3p. IGF1R targeting by miRNA of different functions and in different physiological and pathological conditions has been explored in several experimental studies. For instance, Guo et.al. [45], Feng et.al. [46] and Song et.al. [47] have respectively validated the direct targeting of IGF1R by miRNA-98, miRNA-223, and miRNA Y-56. Downstream of IGF1R, PI3K/AKT pathway is one of the main signalling pathways responsible for cell survival and constantly activated by the activation of the former [15,19,20]. Our assessment of PI3K /AKT pathway showed that this pathway was upregulated in CPT-11R cells and showed minimal response when treated with CPT-11 compared to the parental cells. This data suggested that the survival impact of this pathway might have been involved in CPT-11 resistance.

The involvement and contribution of IGF1R in tumor progression and invasiveness have also been established by numerous researchers including that of [45] above. Consistent with these previous findings, our data also suggested the involvement of IGF1R in CPT-11 resistant mechanism when we observed that, knockdown of IGF1R reduced cell survival and sensitized cells to CPT-11 evidenced by their reversion from EMT to MET, reduced migration as well as downregulation of PI3K/AKT pathway.

We also explored the relationship between IGF1R and miRNA-376a-3p by transfecting the latter in CPT-11R cells which revealed the downregulation of IGF1R with each increasing dose of miRNA-376a-3p mimic. These results suggested the direct regulation of IGF1R by miRNA-376a-3p more especially when we further transfected the cells with wild and mutant type IGF1R where luciferase activity was reduced in wild type and no significant change in mutant type when co-transfected with this miRNA. Consequently, the transfection of miRNA-376a sensitized CPT-11R cells to CPT-11 replicating the role of IGF1R downregulation. This was evidenced by decreased cell viability, enhanced apoptosis, reversal from EMT to MET as well as decreased cell migration. The observation that IGF1R was further downregulated when both CPT-11 and miRNA-376a-3p were administered compared to CPT-11 and miRNA-376a-3p alone suggested that the sensitization of CPT-11R cells to CPT-11 might have been due to downregulation of IGF1R. These results prompted us to determine the response of these cells in terms of PI3K/AKT pathway. As expected, PI3K/AKT pathway was observed to downregulate with superiority to CPT-11 and miRNA-376a alone which also suggest that miRNA-376a-3p sensitized CPT-11R cells to CPT-11 through IGF1R/PI3K/AKT pathway.

As most cancers, and in specific, colorectal cancers develop from epithelial cells, the transformation of these cells to mesenchymal phenotype lenders these cells more invasive and acquire migrative potential. Further epithelial-turned-mesenchymal cells have been associated with drug resistant. Our study has shown that treatment with miRNA-376a-3p reverses this phenomenon and when co-treated with CPT-11, these cells were observed to have been more responsive, hence sensitized to CPT-11 as we observed enhanced reversion to MET as well as enhanced apoptosis and reduced migration.

Finally, we observed that the findings that were observed in invitro were seen to be replicated in our preclinical model. This was evidenced by the enhanced reduction of tumor size and weight in mice that were co-treated with both CPT-11 and miRNA-376a compared to CPT-11 and miRNA-76a-3p treatment alone. Furthermore, expression of EMT markers in the mice tissues in the same order indicated the reversion from EMT to MET, a positive indication of response to treatment. Consistent with invitro model, IGF1R and its downstream PI3K/AKT pathway were downregulated, indicating the probable pathway through which the sensitization of CPT-11 resistant xenograft to CPT-11 by miRNA-376a-3p might have taken through.

Drug resistance pose a significant hurdle in management of cancer [14] which calls for careful consideration to identify intervention strategies aimed at introducing new or improving current regimen. Evidence has shown refractory colorectal cancers against CPT-11 in some fraction of patients [48], leading to tumor recurrence, metastasis and low survival rates [7]. Resistance to drugs may arise from a wide variety of factors, including genetic mutations, epigenetic changes, drug efflux, and other cellular molecular mechanisms [49]. A good number of these process involves genes that might influence or be influenced by miRNA expressions [50,51].

To our knowledge, the sensitization of CPT-11 resistant colorectal cancer to CPT-11 by miRNA-376a-3p has not been investigated. We have unveiled a potential involvement of miRNA-376a-3p in CPT-11 mechanism and we have found that it plays a positive role in enhancing the effect of CPT-11 in colorectal cancer including the resistant type.

Conclusion

In conclusion, miRNA-376a-3p sensitized CPT-11 colorectal cancer to CPT-11 in inhibiting cancer progression by inducing apoptosis, reversing the epithelial cell-transformation to mesenchymal phenotype and inhibiting migration, suggestively through IGF1R/PI3K/AKT pathway downregulation, both in vitro and in vivo. Therefore, IGF1R/miRNA-376a-3 axis pose a potential as a therapeutic and sensitizing target for colorectal cancer, resistant to CPT-11, and, miRNA-376a-3p might also be a treatment option for colorectal cancer.

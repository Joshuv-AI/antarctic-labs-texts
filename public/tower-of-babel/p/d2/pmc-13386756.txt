Clinical benefit of PD-1 antibody plus chemotherapy in advanced HER2 negative gastric or gastro-oesophageal junction adenocarcinoma according to PD-L1 levels: Post-hoc analysis of RATIONALE-305 and pooled analysis of individual patient-level data
Authors: Ming-Yu Lai, Hao-Xiang Wu, Ming-Ming He, Wen-Long Guan, Sheng Xu, Han Hu, Ya-Ling Xu, Feng-Hua Wang, Rui-Hua Xu, Miao-Zhen Qiu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWhile multiple phase III trials have established the survival benefit of adding a programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) antibody to first-line chemotherapy in patients with human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative advanced gastric or gastroesophageal junction (G/GEJ) adenocarcinoma, evidence across different programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression levels remains limited, preventing definitive conclusions about the superiority of combination therapy in certain subgroups. We firstly performed a post-hoc analysis of the RATIONALE-305 trial, finding only marginal benefit in the 1 ≤ tumor area positivity (TAP) < 5 and 1 ≤ combined positive score (CPS) < 5 subgroups. Subsequently, we performed a pooled analysis of individual patient-level data from RATIONALE-305 and four additional phase III trials (CheckMate 649, KEYNOTE-062, KEYNOTE-859, and ORIENT-16). The pooled analysis demonstrated that adding a PD-1 antibody to chemotherapy significantly improved overall survival in patients with intermediate PD-L1 levels (1 ≤ CPS < 10: HR, 0.84; 95% CI, 0.75–0.93), including the lower intermediate subset (1 ≤ CPS < 5: HR, 0.85; 95% CI, 0.75–0.97), but not in the CPS < 1 subgroup (HR, 0.96; 95% CI, 0.82–1.14). Progression-free survival results were consistent with those for overall survival, and improvements in objective response rate were observed across all subgroups. In conclusion, this study provides novel evidence that the combination therapy is superior to chemotherapy alone in patients with advanced HER2-negative G/GEJ adenocarcinoma and PD-L1 CPS ≥ 1.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13045-026-01830-6.Keywords: Gastric cancer; PD-1 antibody plus chemotherapy, Post-hoc analysis, Pooled analysis, PD-L1 expression

To the editor

While programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) antibody plus chemotherapy improves overall survival (OS) in Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor 2 (HER2)-negative advanced gastric or gastro-oesophageal junction (G/GEJ) adenocarcinoma [1–6], its benefit for patients with low or intermediate programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression remains uncertain [7–9]. To address this, we conducted a post-hoc analysis of RATIONALE-305 and a pooled analysis incorporating individual patient-level data (IPD) from RATIONALE-305 and reconstructed IPD from four additional large phase III trials [10]. Owing to HIPAA regulations and the 21st Century Cures Act, the post-hoc analysis excluded United States patients (< 5% of the cohort). As demonstrated in Figure S1, the exclusion did not affect the PD-L1 CPS subgroup distribution. Search strategy and IPD reconstruction methods are detailed in Figure S2-S3 and Table S1-S6.

Post-hoc analysis of RATIONALE-305 showed no OS benefit in TAP < 1% (hazard ratios [HR], 0.96; 95% confidence intervals [CI], 0.62–1.47), TAP < 5% (HR, 0.92; 95% CI, 0.75–1.13), or TAP < 10% (HR, 0.91; 95% CI, 0.77–1.08) subgroups. Non-significant trends were observed in intermediate TAP subgroups: the lower intermediate subset (1 ≤ TAP < 5%; HR, 0.92; 95% CI, 0.72–1.16), and the broader intermediate range (1 ≤ TAP < 10%; HR, 0.91; 95% CI, 0.76–1.09) (Figure S4 A–E). Progression-free survival (PFS), objective response rate (ORR) and duration of response (DoR) showed consistent patterns, with no significant benefit in these subgroups (Figure S4 F-J, Table S7). Conversely, statistically significant OS, PFS and DoR improvements were observed in TAP ≥ 1%, ≥ 5%, and ≥ 10% subgroups (Figure S5 A–F, Table S7).

Analysis by PD-L1 CPS showed similar patterns: CPS < 1 yielded no survival benefit (HR, 1.06; 95% CI, 0.69–1.61), while CPS < 5 (HR, 0.90; 95% CI, 0.73–1.10), CPS < 10 (HR, 0.87; 95% CI, 0.73–1.03), 1 ≤ CPS < 5 (4 is included; HR, 0.86; 95% CI, 0.68–1.09), and 1 ≤ CPS < 10 (9 is included; HR, 0.84; 95% CI, 0.70–1.01) showed favorable non-significant trends (Figure S6 A-E). PFS mirrored these results: CPS < 1 (HR, 0.85; 95% CI, 0.55–1.32), CPS < 5 (HR, 0.84; 95% CI, 0.68–1.04), CPS < 10 (HR, 0.83; 95% CI, 0.70–0.99), 1 ≤ CPS < 5 (HR, 0.84; 95% CI, 0.66–1.07), and 1 ≤ CPS < 10 (HR, 0.83; 95% CI, 0.69–1.00) (Figure S6F–SJ). ORR showed no significant benefits across all CPS subgroups, while DoR benefit was observed in CPS < 10 subgroup (HR, 0.78; 95% CI, 0.60–1.03; P = 0.039), with median DoR of 8.2 versus 7.2 months for tislelizumab and placebo arm, respectively (Table S7). Moreover, patients with CPS ≥ 1, CPS ≥ 5, and CPS ≥ 10 showed significant OS and PFS benefits with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy (Figure S7 A–F).

The pooled analysis incorporated five phase III RCTs: RATIONALE-305 (original IPD) and reconstructed IPD from CheckMate 649, KEYNOTE-062, KEYNOTE-859, and ORIENT-16 (Table S8). Pooled analysis evaluated OS, PFS, and ORR across five predefined below- and intermediate-range CPS groups using harmonized IPD as follows. Heterogeneity across these studies was examined and found to be low (I2 = 0), with detailed information provided in Figure S8.

CPS < 1 subgroup

No significant OS benefit (HR, 0.96; 95% CI, 0.82–1.14; P = 0.332) or PFS improvement (HR, 0.92; 95% CI, 0.77–1.10; P = 0.184) (Fig. 1A-B). However, ORR was significantly higher with PD-1 antibodies therapy (47.8% vs. 39.1%; OR, 1.43; 95% CI, 1.07–1.91, Δ = + 8.7%; Table 1).

CPS < 5 subgroup

Significant OS (HR, 0.89; 95% CI, 0.81–0.98; P = 0.009) and PFS benefit (HR, 0.84; 95% CI, 0.76–0.93; P < 0.001) (Fig. 1C-D) were observed. ORR also improved significantly (48.4% vs. 42.1%; OR, 1.29; 95% CI, 1.09–1.53, Δ = + 6.3%; Table 1).

1 ≤ CPS < 5 subgroup

Pooled analysis demonstrated statistically significant improvements in both OS (HR, 0.85; 95% CI, 0.75–0.97; P = 0.008) and PFS (HR, 0.86; 95% CI, 0.75–0.98; P = 0.014) (Fig. 1E-F). The ORR increased markedly (58.6% vs. 43.0%, OR, 1.87; 95% CI, 1.48–2.37, Δ = + 15.6%; Table 1).

CPS < 10 subgroup

Pooled analysis demonstrated PD-1 inhibitors significantly prolonged OS (HR, 0.86; 95% CI, 0.78–0.96; P = 0.003) and PFS (HR, 0.84; 95% CI, 0.76–0.94; P = 0.001) (Fig. 1G-H). ORR was higher in the PD-1 inhibitor treatment arms (47.8% vs. 42.4%, OR, 1.25; 95% CI, 1.07–1.46, Δ = + 5.4%; Table 1).

1 ≤ CPS < 10 subgroup

Pooled data demonstrated significant OS improvement (HR, 0.84; 95% CI, 0.75–0.93; P < 0.001) and PFS benefit (HR, 0.85; 95% CI, 0.76–0.95; P = 0.002) (Fig. 1I-J). ORR was also significantly improved (47.6% vs. 42.5%; OR, 1.23; 95% CI, 1.04–1.45, Δ = + 5.1%; Table 1).

In summary, the reconstruction and pooled analysis of IPD provide robust evidence supporting the addition of PD-1 antibody therapy to chemotherapy in advanced HER2-negative G/GEJ adenocarcinoma patients with PD-L1 TAP ≥ 1% or CPS ≥ 1, specifically within the 1 ≤ TAP < 5% and 1 ≤ CPS < 5 subgroups. Therefore, for patients with CPS < 1, PD-1 antibody therapy is not routinely recommended but could be considered an option for shared decision-making in scenarios where achieving a higher ORR is a primary clinical objective, despite the lack of an OS benefit.

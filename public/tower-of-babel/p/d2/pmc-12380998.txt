Lightweight early detection of knee osteoarthritis in athletes
Authors: Akmalbek Abdusalomov, Sanjar Mirzakhalilov, Sabina Umirzakova, Otabek Ismailov, Djamshid Sultanov, Rashid Nasimov, Young Im Cho
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractOsteoarthritis (OA) is a prevalent condition among athletes, characterized by the progressive degradation of joint cartilage, particularly in weight-bearing joints such as the knees. Early detection is critical for effective management and prevention of long-term complications. Recent advancements in deep learning, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), have shown promise in medical diagnostics. In this study, we propose a novel approach for early-stage OA detection using an optimized EfficientNet-B0 architecture enhanced with the Efficient Channel Attention (ECA) module. This integration addresses the limitations of traditional attention mechanisms, such as Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) blocks, by providing lightweight and computationally efficient feature recalibration. Our methodology is evaluated using the Knee Osteoarthritis Severity Grading Dataset, focusing on binary classification between healthy and early-stage OA cases. Comprehensive experiments demonstrate that the proposed model achieves superior accuracy, precision, and recall compared to baseline and State-of-the-Art (SOTA) architectures, including ResNet-50, VGG-16, and DenseNet, while maintaining minimal computational overhead. Class Activation Maps (CAMs) further validate the model capability to localize clinically relevant features, such as joint space narrowing and osteophyte formation, indicative of OA progression. This research not only sets a new benchmark for automated OA diagnostics but also emphasizes the importance of balancing high performance with resource efficiency. The proposed model lightweight architecture and robust diagnostic capabilities make it a strong candidate for real-time clinical applications, paving the way for improved patient outcomes through early intervention.Subject terms: Diseases, Health care, Medical research, Risk factors, Signs and symptoms

Introduction

OA is one of the most prevalent musculoskeletal disorders, affecting millions of individuals globally and leading to significant pain, reduced mobility, and diminished quality of life1. Athletes are particularly susceptible to OA, especially in weight-bearing joints such as the knees, due to the high levels of physical stress and repetitive joint loading inherent in their activities2. Early detection of OA is crucial for timely intervention, enabling strategies to slow disease progression, mitigate pain, and preserve function3. However, traditional diagnostic methods, such as radiographic imaging interpreted by human experts, can be subjective and time-consuming, particularly in the identification of early-stage OA, where subtle joint changes may go unnoticed.

Recent advancements in artificial intelligence (AI) and deep learning have revolutionized medical imaging, offering powerful tools for automated and accurate disease detection4. CNNs, in particular, have demonstrated remarkable success in image classification tasks across various domains, including medical diagnostics5. Numerous studies have focused on leveraging CNNs to enhance the detection and classification of OA through radiographic images6. This section reviews recent advancements relevant to our proposed approach. Several works have employed traditional CNN architectures such as AlexNet, VGG, and ResNet for OA detection5. utilized a deep convolutional neural network to grade knee OA severity based on radio-graphic images, achieving promising results but with limitations in computational efficiency. Similarly7, demonstrated the effectiveness of CNNs in automating Kellgren–Lawrence grading, highlighting the potential for reducing subjective biases associated with manual interpretation. However, these models often require significant computational resources, which can hinder real-time clinical deployment. Recent advancements have shifted towards lightweight and scalable architectures to address these challenges. For instance, MobileNet8 and SqueezeNet9 have been proposed as resource-efficient alternatives, with studies demonstrating their utility in medical image analysis10. Reference11 presented Gaussian Aquila Optimizer-based Dual Convolutional Neural Network (GAO-DCNN) for OA detection, with optimized computational efficiency. Reference12 conducted a comprehensive comparative analysis of classical and deep learning-based approaches for knee OA grading, highlighting the importance of model selection and evaluation across diverse datasets. Their review provides valuable insights into the strengths and limitations of existing methods, which informs the foundation for model benchmarking in our study. However, these architectures often compromise accuracy to achieve lower computational demands, limiting their applicability in high-stakes diagnostics such as early OA detection.

Among the SOTA CNN architectures, EfficientNet has emerged as a highly efficient and scalable framework, achieving superior performance with reduced computational demands. EfficientNet, introduced by Tan and Le13, represents a paradigm shift in CNN design by employing a compound scaling method to balance network depth, width, and resolution. EfficientNet variants have demonstrated state-of-the-art performance across various image classification tasks, including medical imaging. Reference14 adapted EfficientNet for knee OA detection, reporting significant accuracy and computational efficiency improvements compared to traditional models. Reference15 em-ploying the EfficientNet-B5 transfer learning model in knee OA classification. Additionally, in another study, Reference16 proposed a three-stage preprocessing pipeline combined with the VGG16 architecture, which significantly improved classification performance. Their method emphasized the importance of structured image preprocessing—such as contrast enhancement and noise reduction—on model robustness, which aligns with our preprocessing strategy to enhance radiographic feature clarity. Utilizing a balanced dataset and focusing on early-stage detection, the model highlights advancements in precise diagnosis, particularly for the elderly. This underscores EfficientNet potential as a baseline for developing advanced diagnostic tools.

Attention mechanisms have further enhanced CNN performance by enabling models to focus on the most informative features. The SE block, introduced by Hu et al.17, has been widely adopted to recalibrate channel-wise feature responses, leading to improved accuracy in medical image classification tasks. Reference18 a study developed an SE-YOLOv5 model based on a self-attention mechanism for detecting and differentiating knee cyst-like lesions in MRI scans. However, SE blocks increase model complexity, which can be a drawback in resource-constrained scenarios. To address the limitations of SE blocks, recent studies have explored alternative attention mechanisms. The ECA module, proposed by Wang et al.19, eliminates the need for dimensionality reduction and expansion, enabling efficient channel-wise attention with minimal computational overhead. ECA has been successfully integrated into various CNN architectures, demonstrating its capability to enhance feature extraction without significantly increasing resource demands. Despite its advantages, its application in OA detection remains underexplored.

Furthermore, dataset availability plays a critical role in the development and evaluation of AI-based diagnostic models. The Knee OA Severity Grading Dataset by Pingjun Chen et al.20 provides a standardized framework for assessing OA severity using radiographic images labeled according to the Kellgren-Lawrence grading system. Studies utilizing this dataset, such as those by21] and [22, have highlighted its value in training and benchmarking deep learning models for OA detection. While significant progress has been made in utilizing CNNs for OA detection23, challenges remain in achieving an optimal balance between accuracy and computational efficiency. Existing models often fail to address the specific needs of real-time clinical applications, particularly for early-stage OA detection in athletes24. Our study builds upon these advancements by integrating the computationally efficient ECA module into the powerful EfficientNet-B0 architecture, providing a novel approach tailored to the unique demands of this diagnostic task.

In this study, we present a novel deep-learning approach to detecting the early stages of knee OA in athletes. Building upon the EfficientNet-B0 model, we propose an optimized architecture that incorporates the ECA module. The ECA module offers a lightweight and computationally efficient alternative to traditional attention mechanisms, such as SE blocks, by streamlining channel-wise feature recalibration. This modification enables our model to balance high accuracy and low resource consumption, making it particularly suitable for real-time clinical applications and deployment in resource-constrained environments. To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed approach, we utilize the Knee OA Severity Grading Dataset, a comprehensive dataset specifically designed for radiographic analysis of OA. By focusing on binary classification between healthy and early-stage OA cases, our model aims to identify the earliest indicators of disease progression, such as osteophyte formation and joint space narrowing, with high precision and recall. Extensive experiments and comparative analyses with existing state-of-the-art models demonstrate the superiority of our approach in terms of accuracy, computational efficiency, and clinical relevance. This research not only advances the field of AI-based medical diagnostics but also addresses a critical gap in the early detection of OA in athletes. By leveraging the computational efficiency of the ECA module and the robust feature extraction capabilities of Efficient-Net-B0, our model sets a new benchmark for performance and applicability in medical imaging. The findings of this study pave the way for future innovations in automated diagnostic tools, contributing to improved clinical decision-making and better outcomes for patients at risk of OA.

EfficientNet-B0

EfficientNet B0 is an innovative CNN architecture that represents a paradigm shift in how model scaling is conducted. The key insight of the EfficientNet architecture lies in its systematic approach to scaling network width, depth, and resolution in a balanced manner, guided by a set of scaling coefficients optimized for computational efficiency and accuracy.

EfficientNet B0 is an innovative CNN architecture that represents a paradigm shift in how model scaling is conducted. The key insight of the EfficientNet architecture lies in its systematic approach to scaling network width, depth, and resolution in a balanced manner, guided by a set of scaling coefficients optimized for computational efficiency and accuracy. EfficientNet B0 employs a compound scaling method that utilizes a compound coefficient (φ) to uniformly scale network width, depth, and resolution, providing a balanced and structured approach to scaling, unlike conventional arbitrary practices. The baseline architecture of EfficientNet B0 was developed using neural architecture search (NAS), focusing on optimizing accuracy and efficiency through a series of Mobile Inverted Bottleneck Convolution (MBConv) blocks with lightweight depthwise separable convolutions, which reduce parameter count and computational complexity, as shown in Fig. 1. Effi-cientNet B0 is the foundational model in the EfficientNet series, with its depth multi-plier (α), width multiplier (β), and resolution multiplier (γ) set to 1, serving as a base-line for scaling to other models in the series (B1–B7) using different values of φ. Its input layer accepts a resolution of 224 × 224 pixels for the B0 variant, which increases for larger variants with varying multipliers of resolution. The architecture features MBConv blocks with squeeze-and-excitation layers, enhancing efficiency and accuracy by recalibrating channel-wise feature responses. These layers emphasize the most informative features by leveraging global information while suppressing less useful ones, ensuring that the network focuses on essential features.

The proposed model. Rethinking the attention module

In our study, we adapt the renowned EfficientNet-B0 model to identify the incipient stages of osteoarthritis in athletes, given that this disease predominantly manifests in the knees of this demographic, and delayed diagnosis can adversely affect their health and professional trajectories, as illustrated in Fig. 2. Consequently, we propose a novel methodology for the early detection of osteoarthritis. The baseline model incorporates MBConv blocks with Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) attention mechanisms to enhance the extraction of relevant features. However, the addition of the SE block increases the complexity of the model, potentially complicating deployment in resource-constrained scenarios. To address this issue, we have modified the model by integrating the Efficient Channel Attention (ECA) module. The proposed model demonstrates low resource consumption due to several architectural optimizations. Firstly, EfficientNet-B0 is used as the baseline, which is well-known for its compound scaling strategy that balances network depth, width, and resolution, resulting in a smaller number of parameters and reduced FLOPs. Secondly, the integration of the ECA module enhances feature recalibration without the computational overhead introduced by traditional attention mechanisms such as the SE block. The ECA module achieves this by using a fast 1D convolution operation that avoids dimensionality reduction or expansion. Additionally, the use of depthwise-separable convolutions within MBConv blocks further reduces the number of operations required, maintaining performance while minimizing resource demands. These combined factors contribute to a highly efficient architecture capable of real-time execution on standard hardware, making it suitable for deployment in clinical and resource-constrained environments.

ECA

The ECA module represents a significant advancement in neural network design, particularly in refining channel attention mechanisms in CNNs Fig. 2. Unlike traditional approaches that rely on dimensionality reduction and expansion, ECA simplifies the channel attention process by directly capturing dependencies without altering dimensionality. This streamlined approach reduces computational complexity, making the module well-suited for real-time applications. A core innovation of the ECA module lies in its adaptive and localized cross-channel interaction. Using a one-dimensional convolution, it effectively captures local cross-channel dependencies, enabling the network to focus on meaningful relationships between channels. The kernel size for this convolution is derived from a simple formula, ensuring a balanced trade-off between model accuracy and complexity without requiring extensive hyperparameter tuning. The module implementation is both efficient and straightforward, involving minimal changes to existing CNN architectures. Its computational efficiency, achieved through simple channel-wise multiplication and one-dimensional convolution, makes it particularly valuable for deep learning applications where computational resources are limited, as illustrated in Fig. 2.

Experiment and analyses

The dataset and medical data preprocessing

The Knee Osteoarthritis Severity Grading Dataset by Pingjun Chen is designed specifically for the classification and prediction of knee osteoarthritis severity using radiographic images. It contains X-ray images of knees labeled according to the Kellgren–Lawrence grading system, which classifies the severity of osteoarthritis on a scale from 0 (no OA), 1 (doubtful OA), 2 (minimal OA), 3 (moderate OA) and 4 (severe OA), Fig. 3.

The demonstration of the stages of joint narrowing from healthy knee (green color shadows), doubtful with possible low risk of osteophytes (yellow), minimal with high possible risk of osteophytes (red color shadows), moderate osteophytes with high joint narrowing (purple color shadows) and last one is severe totally narrowed joint (blue color shadows).

This scale assesses OA based on features like joint space narrowing, osteophyte formation, and subchondral sclerosis, which are observable in X-rays. Researchers use this dataset to train machine learning models, particularly convolutional neural networks, to automate the detection and grading of knee OA. The dataset serves not only as a tool for developing diagnostic models but also supports clinical research into OA progression and treatment evaluation. For our study we use this dataset to detect early stages of OA by using only two classes 0 (no OA) is a healthy knee and 1 (doubtful OA) is early stage of OA. While working with medical image datasets, such as the Knee OA Severity Grading Dataset, effective preprocessing is essential to improve model performance and ensure accurate predictions. To achieve this, we normalize pixel values to a consistent scale, reducing variations in brightness and contrast and enabling the model to focus on structural features. Noise reduction is applied using filters like Gaussian blur or advanced techniques such as Non-Local Means to remove unwanted artifacts. Images are resized to a standard resolution while preserving their aspect ratio, sometimes using padding to prevent distortion. Data augmentation is employed to increase variability, introducing random rotations, translations, flips, scaling, and adjustments in brightness and contrast to simulate diverse imaging conditions. Additionally, contrast enhancement techniques, such as histogram equalization or CLAHE, are used to highlight important image details, improving the clarity of structural features. These preprocessing steps collectively ensure that the model can learn effectively from high-quality, standardized input data.

The dataset used in this study consists of a total of 1600 radiographic knee images, evenly divided between healthy knees (Kellgren–Lawrence grade 0) and early-stage OA knees (grade 1). To ensure balanced class representation, we employed a stratified 70-15-15 split. As a result, the training set contained 1120 images, with 560 healthy and 560 early OA cases. The validation and test sets each comprised 240 images, again balanced with 120 healthy and 120 early OA images, respectively. This careful distribution mitigates class imbalance and enhances the model ability to learn discriminative features effectively for both categories.

While our proposed model effectively detects early-stage OA through radiographic features such as joint space narrowing and osteophyte formation, it is important to acknowledge the limitations inherent to X-ray imaging, particularly in assessing soft-tissue structures. MRI, in contrast, provides superior soft-tissue contrast and allows for direct visualization of cartilage loss, meniscal damage, and synovial inflammation—all vital indicators of OA. Although our model is currently optimized for 2D radiographic analysis, future work will explore its extension to MRI data using publicly available datasets such as fastMRI or the OAI. Such an adaptation would enable the model to capture soft-tissue pathology more accurately, enhancing its clinical utility in comprehensive OA diagnosis. Furthermore, we recognize the potential of multimodal learning approaches that integrate X-ray and MRI features to improve diagnostic performance and facilitate early intervention strategies, particularly for athlete populations where rapid, non-invasive, and reliable screening tools are essential.

Conclusion

This paper presents a novel approach for the early detection of osteoarthritis in athletes, leveraging the EfficientNet-B0 architecture enhanced with the Efficient Channel Attention (ECA) module. Our model achieves superior performance in accuracy, precision, and recall while maintaining minimal computational overhead, setting it apart from existing state-of-the-art methods. Comprehensive experiments using the Knee Osteoarthritis Severity Grading Dataset validate the effectiveness of our approach, particularly in identifying early-stage OA cases. Compared to traditional architectures such as ResNet-50, VGG-16, and DenseNet, the proposed model demonstrates significant advancements in both diagnostic precision and computational efficiency. The integration of the ECA module optimizes channel attention, enabling the model to effectively localize and emphasize clinically relevant features, such as joint space narrowing and osteophyte formation. These capabilities are further supported by CAMs, which provide visual evidence of the model diagnostic focus. The proposed methodology not only addresses critical challenges in early OA detection but also offers a scalable solution for real-time clinical applications. By balancing high diagnostic accuracy with resource efficiency, our work paves the way for improved patient outcomes through timely and reliable intervention. Future research could explore the integration of additional medical datasets and advanced preprocessing techniques to further enhance model generalization and applicability across diverse clinical settings. Although our model demonstrates high accuracy in distinguishing healthy knees from early-stage OA in athletes, its generalizability to broader populations remains an important area for future investigation. OA manifests differently across individuals, influenced by factors such as age, gender, genetics, lifestyle, and biomechanical stress. Our current dataset, while standardized and high-quality, may not fully capture this demographic and clinical variability. Therefore, future studies should evaluate the model’s robustness across multi-institutional datasets and patient cohorts with diverse backgrounds. Moreover, expanding the classification task beyond binary outcomes to include multiple OA severity grades, such as KL grades 0–4, would enable more nuanced analysis of disease progression. Such work would help validate the clinical applicability of our model in both screening and longitudinal monitoring scenarios.

For deployment in clinical settings, maintaining long-term model performance would require periodic retraining or fine-tuning using updated imaging data. This ensures the model remains aligned with evolving imaging standards and patient demographics. Continual learning techniques or domain adaptation methods could support this process. Additionally, implementing a federated learning framework would allow multiple institutions to collaboratively update the model using decentralized data, enhancing generalizability while preserving data privacy and compliance with healthcare regulations. Although our model does not provide explicit follow-up schedules, its predictions could serve as a triage mechanism to guide imaging frequency based on risk level. For patients identified as having early-stage OA, especially those in physically active populations such as athletes, more frequent monitoring—every 6–12 months—may be advisable to assess progression and adjust treatment plans accordingly. MRI may be preferred in follow-ups for its ability to evaluate cartilage degradation and soft-tissue changes. In clinical practice, such risk-based stratification could optimize resource use, reserving intensive imaging and intervention for those with predicted disease advancement. Future integration with electronic health records (EHR) and decision support systems may allow for automated scheduling of follow-up imaging based on model confidence levels, further enhancing personalized OA management.

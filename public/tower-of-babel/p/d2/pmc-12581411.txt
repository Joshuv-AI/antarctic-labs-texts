The effect of Baduanjin exercises on the functional capacity, respiratory function, and quality of life of patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Sema Nur Atmaca, Rengin Demir, Rüstem Mustafaoğlu, Ersan Atahan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIdiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a disease with a poor prognosis that causes fibrosis in the interstitial space of the lungs, characterized by shortness of breath and progressive deterioration of lung function. The purpose of this study was to investigate the effectiveness of online Baduanjin exercise training on the functional capacity, respiratory function, and quality of life of patients with IPF.MethodsTwenty-eight patients with IPF were enrolled. The participants were randomly assigned to either an exercise or control group. The exercise group received 24 sessions of supervised online Baduanjin exercise: three days per week for eight weeks. It was also done unsupervised once per week at home. The patients in the control group did not receive any training during the eight week period. Patients were assessed using the 6-Minute Walk Test (6MWT), spirometry, respiratory muscle strength (MIP/MEP), and quality of life using the St-George Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ) at baseline and after eight weeks.ResultsAfter eight weeks, spirometry parameters were preserved, while MIP, MEP, 6MWT, 6MWT (% predicted), SGRQ-Activity, SGRQ-Impact, and SGRQ-Total scores demonstrated significant improvements in the exercise group (p < .05). Statistically significant between group differences were found in 6MWT (%predicted), FEV1 (% predicted), FVC (% predicted), MIP, MEP SGRQ-Symptom, SGRQ-Activity, SGRQ-Impact, and SGRQ-Total (p < .05).ConclusionBaduanjin exercise training delivered through an online platform had a positive impact on the functional exercise capacity, respiratory muscle strength, and quality of life of these patients with IPF.Clinical trial registration numberNCT06372353.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13030-025-00346-8.

Introduction

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a progressive fibrotic lung disease with a poor prognosis that is characterized by worsening respiratory symptoms, causing fibrosis in the interstitial space of the lungs. IPF causes a gradual decline in lung function, increased levels of dyspnea, dry cough, fatigue, decreased exercise capacity, and health-related quality of life [1]. In current guidelines, pulmonary rehabilitation programs are recommended for patients with IPF, as in other chronic lung diseases [2]. Pulmonary rehabilitation for patients with IPF can be performed safely. Well-designed randomized studies are needed to determine the optimal exercise prescription and investigate ways to promote longer-lasting improvements, particularly for people with IPF [3].

Baduanjin exercise is one of the most widely practiced forms of traditional Chinese mind-body exercise, consisting of eight individual postures and movements with low to moderate exercise intensity, characterized by symmetrical body postures and movements, breathing control, and mental concentration [4]. Baduanjin exercise is easier to learn than other Chinese mind-body exercises [5]. The fundamental assumption is that individuals can regulate thoughts, breathing, and body activities by their own internal ability, resulting in enhancement of physical and mental health [6]. There are no special location requirements, and it can be performed within any space that allows the limbs to stretch out. Patients are encouraged to perform Baduanjin at home, reducing barriers such as weather, transportation, and cost [7]. This equipment-free exercise might also be useful in hospitals, especially those with limited resources in rural areas [8].

To date, a growing number of clinical studies have demonstrated the safety and health benefits from Baduanjin interventions [9, 10]. Several systematic reviews have examined the evidence provided by Baduanjin in randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or nonrandomized clinical trials for various specific diseases and health conditions, such as diabetes mellitus [11], chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) [12], and cardiovascular disease [13]. Increasing evidence suggests that Baduanjin training has positive benefits in improving physical function, mental health, cognitive ability, and reducing the risk of chronic diseases in older individuals with different health conditions [14, 15]. Considering these conditions, Baduanjin exercises seem to be a suitable exercise option for the IPF patient population. Tele-rehabilitation is employed to increase accessibility, continuity of care, and patient engagement by providing remote exercise sessions through technology-enabled platforms. Evidence showing that online exercise training is as effective as face-to-face training in some cases [12–14] suggests that it offers a viable alternative, especially under challenging circumstances such as the COVID-19 pandemic [15].

There are studies in the literature examining the effects of Baduanjin exercises performed online in various patient groups other than pulmonary diseases. In a study conducted on Ankylosing Spondylitis patients, it was reported that 12 weeks of online Baduanjin exercises reduced the patients’ fatigue levels, improved their quality of life, and increased chest wall mobility [16]. Similarly, in another study conducted on Parkinson’s patients, it was reported that Baduanjin exercises applied via telerehabilitation provided significant improvements in balance, walking performance, and quality of life [17]. In addition, there is a study in the literature examining the effects of online Baduanjin exercises on healthy individuals. In that study, conducted on university students during the COVID-19 pandemic, it was found that Baduanjin exercises reduced anxiety levels, relieved back pain, and increased psychological well-being [18]. These studies support that Baduanjin exercises can have positive effects on both physical and psychological health in different patient groups and healthy individuals and that online applications can yield similar results to face-to-face studies.

No study has been found investigating the effectiveness of Baduanjin exercises for patients with IPF. For this reason, in this study, we aimed to present an alternative in terms of the feasibility of Baduanjin exercises as a new treatment method by investigating the effectiveness of this exercise method, which has previously been shown to be effective in other patient groups, in patients with IPF. The aim of the current study was to investigate the effectiveness of online Baduanjin exercise training on the functional capacity, respiratory function, and quality of life of patients with IPF.

Methods

Study design

This exploratory study was conducted as a parallel group randomized controlled trial; an equal randomisation of 1:1 was chosen. The study was done with the approval of the Non-Interventional Clinical Research Ethics Committee of Istanbul University-Cerrahpasa (Date: 01.06.2022, Decision number: 2022/74) and was performed within the framework of the Declaration of Helsinki. Clinical trial number: NCT06372353.

Participants

This study was conducted at Istanbul University-Cerrahpaşa Medical Faculty, Department of Chest Diseases, between June 2022 and May 2023. The criteria for inclusion in the study were as follows: (a) being diagnosed as having IPF according to international guideline criteria; (b) Medical Research Council (MRC) dyspnea score ≤ 4; (c) being able to ambulate without using an assistive device; (d) being aged 18–75 years; (e) being cooperative, a stable patient with no pulmonary exacerbation in the last 6 weeks; (f) having no vision or hearing deficits; (g) having the necessary internet and computer or telephone equipment to participate in the online exercise program. The exclusion criteria for the study were as follows: (a) participation in a formal rehabilitation program no later than four months before the beginning of the study; (b) having musculoskeletal diseases affecting both upper and lower extremities that limited their ability to perform exercises or any diagnosed neurologic disorders that would prevent exercise; (c) having any diagnosed heart disease that would prevent exercise; (d) inability to understand informed consent conditions and exercises; (e) need for additional oxygen at rest; (f) presence of obvious lung infection requiring anti-infection therapy (patients who had a respiratory tract infection or systemic infection within four weeks before enrollment); (g) situations where exercise was contraindicated; (h) presence of another chronic lung disease (e.g., collagen vascular disease, obstructive pulmonary disease, nonparenchymal restrictive lung disease, occupational lung disease, sarcoidosis, or other idiopathic interstitial pneumonia); (i) participation in a pulmonary rehabilitation and/or exercise program within the past six months. Individuals who agreed to participate in the study signed an Informed Voluntary Consent Form and their consent was obtained.

Sample size

The sample size was calculated using the G*Power 3.0.10 program, considering the mean (standard deviation) change rate obtained by Katatong et al., who investigated the effects of a Tai Chi Qigong program on the functional capacity and lung function of patients with COPD [19]. Although our study focused on Baduanjin exercises and patients with IPF, both studies share similarities in that the intervention was a mind-body exercise program, and the 6-Minute Walk Test (6MWT) was evaluated as the primary outcome. However, we acknowledge the differences in the study populations due to the different pathophysiological characteristics of COPD and IPF. The sample size for our study was determined based on the 6MWT, which served as the primary outcome measure. While we have reported multiple outcome measures, the sample size estimation was specifically based on this parameter. Accordingly, this randomized controlled trial should be considered exploratory in nature, as it was powered only for the primary outcome (6MWT). The sample size was determined as at least 13 patients for each group with 80% power and a two-sided significance level of 0.05 (d = 0.81). To account for a 10% drop-off rate, a total of 28 patients were included in the study, ensuring that at least 14 patients would remain in each group.

Randomization

After giving written informed, consent the patients were randomized to one of two intervention groups using “Research Randomizer,” an online randomization web service (https://www.randomizer.org/). Simple randomization procedures (computerized random numbers) were performed, and sequentially numbered index cards containing the random assignments were prepared by a person with no clinical involvement in the study to ensure allocation concealment. The index cards were folded and placed into sealed, opaque envelopes. All patients selected an envelope. The physical therapist (S.A.) opened each envelope and allocated the patients into the groups according to the selected index card. Participants were not blinded to the treatment allocation. The same physical therapist performed the interventions, and another physical therapist (R.D.) performed the assessments. The physiotherapist who performed the assessments was unaware of the randomization allocation. E.A. securely maintained the randomization list with restricted access to ensure allocation concealment.

Exercise group

A Baduanjin exercise training program was given to the subjects in the exercise group. Each 60-minute session included warm-up and cool-down exercises, each five minutes. A total of 24 sessions were conducted online, under supervision, via the Zoom application, for eight weeks. The physiotherapist providing exercise training has had over five years of academic and clinical experience in pulmonary rehabilitation and mind–body awareness exercises. Prior to the study, the physiotherapist refreshed their knowledge in line with current literature and practices and additionally attended a 2-hour training program. This ensured that the exercise approaches implemented in the study were grounded both in professional experience and up-to-date scientific knowledge. All sessions were conducted individually with the physiotherapist, incorporating simultaneous visual and auditory stimuli. Each session we conducted via Zoom used special meeting identities and passwords to restrict access to non-physiotherapists and patients. In addition, the patients performed unsupervised exercises at home once per week. For the unsupervised home practice, participants were instructed to video-record their exercises and submit the recordings to the physiotherapist each week to confirm completion and proper technique. All participants attended all 24 supervised online sessions. For the unsupervised home practice, participants missed no more than two sessions. The first session were held in hospital [20]. Because the training was delivered online, we did not increase exercise frequency or intensity. This was done in order to ensure participant safety and maintain standardization across the program. This allowed us to closely monitor adherence and to minimize the risk of adverse events that might happen in the remote setting. Future studies could explore step-wise increases in exercise frequency or intensity after safety and feasibility have been established. Details of the Baduanjin exercise training program are given in Appendix 1.

During the online Baduanjin exercise training, real-time monitoring provided immediate feedback to help the patients improve their posture, perform exercises correctly, and prevent injuries. Additionally, patients were encouraged to report any discomfort after the sessions for follow-up and risk management. All patients had blood pressure monitors and saturation devices with them during the exercise. During exercise, if any the following were observed, the exercise was discontinued and only resumed when the patient’s condition stabilized: angina or a feeling of tightness in the chest, a decrease in systolic blood pressure of 10 mmHg or more despite increased workload, an increase in systolic blood pressure above 250 mmHg and diastolic blood pressure above 115 mmHg, arrhythmia or a feeling of palpitations, fainting or presyncope during exercise, dyspnea, wheezing or severe difficulty breathing, cyanosis, dizziness or confusion, visual disturbances, blurred vision or double vision, loss of muscle control, impaired balance or lack of coordination, reluctance to continue, excessive paleness of the skin, cold sweat or excessive sweating, severe muscle spasms, or limited movement [21]. In addition, for patients receiving oxygen supplements, the patient’s condition was checked after each exercise and breaks were given if necessary. A break was given until the patient felt ready to continue, the oxygen saturation increased above 88%, systolic blood pressure ranged between 120 and 160 mmHg, diastolic blood pressure ranged between 80 and 100 mmHg, and the required number of repetitions and sets were completed [22]. For unsupervised exercises, patients were taught to use the Borg Rating of Perceived Exertion (RPE) scale during exercise to subjectively evaluate their effort levels. As part of the training, patients were told to monitor their respiratory and fatigue symptoms and to stop exercising when the Borg RPE score reached 7 or higher. This practice was followed throughout the sessions and exercise safety was ensured [23].

Control group

No intervention was applied to the control group, but Baduanjin exercise training was given to patients who requested it after the study was completed.

Outcome measurements

Individuals with IPF, who were randomly divided into exercise and control groups. Data was collected at baseline and after eight weeks. All assessments were made face to face. The primary outcome was the change in functional exercise capacity, assessed using the standardized 6MWT. Secondary outcomes included pulmonary function, respiratory muscle strength, and health-related quality of life, evaluated by portable spirometry, the MicroRPM® device, and the St. George’s Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ), respectively [24].

Primary outcome.

Functional exercise capacity

Functional exercise capacity was evaluated using 6MWT. Patients rested in a sitting position for 10 min before starting the test. The test protocol was then explained to them, and they were asked to walk as far as possible without running in a 30-meter straight corridor for six minutes. At the end of the test, the evaluations made before the test were repeated and the 6-minute walking distance (6MWD) was recorded in meters [25].

Secondary outcomes.

Lung function

Portable spirometry (Spirobank II; MIR, Rome, Italy) was used to assess pulmonary function. The assessments adhered to the standards outlined by the American Thoracic Society (ATS) and the European Respiratory Society (ERS) guidelines, with measurements expressed as percentages of predicted values [26]. The following parameters related to pulmonary function were measured: (1) forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV1); (2) forced vital capacity (FVC); (3) peak expiratory flow (PEF); forced mid-expiratory flow (FEF).

Respiratory muscle strength

Maximal inspiratory and expiratory pressures (MIP and MEP, respectively) were used to assess the strength of the respiratory muscles. MIP and MEP were measured and documented according to the ATS/ERS criteria using a portable MicroRPM device (Micro Medical, Basingstoke, UK) [27]. The highest of three measurements performed flawlessly from start to finish and exhibiting a difference between them of no more than 5 cm H2O was documented for both MIP and MEP. Determination of predicted values for MIP and MEP was made according to the definitions put forward by Black and Hyatt [28].

Quality of life

The St. George’s Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ) was used to measure quality of life. The SGRQ is a 76-question form that includes the dimensions SGRQ-Activity, SGRQ-Impact, SGRQ-Symptoms, and SGRQ-Total. The scale is scored 0-100, where 100 is the worst. All assessments were performed for each patient at similar times of day [24].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis of the data obtained before and after eight weeks of treatment was performed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) Version 20.0 software (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Variables of statistical analysis were defined using mean, standard deviation (SD), confidence intervals (CI) and percentage values. The Shapiro Wilk test was used to determine the normality of the data distributions. Parametric tests were used to analyze normally distributed data, and non-parametric tests were used to analyze non-normally distributed data. Demographic data of the groups were compared using the Independent Sample T-Test and the Chi-square or Fisher’s exact test. Changes before and after treatment were compared using the Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test because the data of the groups did not show normal distribution. The Mann-Whitney U test was used to evaluate the difference between groups. To account for potential baseline differences, analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) was performed for all secondary outcomes, with the corresponding baseline value entered as a covariate. In all analyses, p < .05 (two-sided) values were considered statistically significant.

Results

Of them, four were not included in the study because they did not meet the inclusion criteria, one due to transportation problems, two due to hospitalization, one died, and one because they did not want to participate in the study, leaving 28 patients with IPF (14 in the exercise group and 14 controls) available for study. Exercise group

Functional exercise capacity, measured by the 6MWD, showed a significant increase, from 368.38 ± 143.2 m to 425.25 ± 171.37 m (p = .011, d = 0.39). Similarly, the 6MWD (%predicted) improved from 72.18 ± 28.09 to 83.63 ± 34.92 (p = .012, d = 0.40).

In the exercise group, pulmonary function parameters were largely preserved following eight weeks of Baduanjin exercise. Specifically, forced vital capacity (FVC, % predicted) (p = .078, d = 0.61), forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV₁, % predicted) (p = .109, d = 0.07), and the FEV₁/FVC ratio (p = .674, d = 0.03) demonstrated no statistically significant decline over the intervention period. Respiratory muscle strength demonstrated a significant improvement. The MIP increased notably, from 82.38 ± 8.92 cmH₂O to 99.75 ± 21.09 cmH₂O (p = .012, d = 1.94), and MEP also improved significantly, from 43.25 ± 6.6 cmH₂O to 50.13 ± 9.61 cmH₂O (p = .018, d = 1.04).

The SGRQ revealed significant improvements in the Activity domain (p = .035, d = 0.52), the Impact domain (p = .012, d = 0.69), and the Total score (p = .012, d = 0.64). Although the Symptom domain score decreased from 53.0 ± 15.90 to 48.00 ± 19.93, this change was not statistically significant (p = .207, d = 0.31) (Table 2).

Control group

The 6MWD of the control group decreased from 365.86 ± 108.20 m to 346.14 ± 109.29 m (p = .001, d = 0.18), and the 6MWD (%predicted) decreased from 70.36 ± 21.63 to 67.65 ± 22.9 (p = .001, d = 0.12). Also, no significant changes were observed in quality of life outcomes in the Symptom (p = .080, d = 0.11) and Activity (p = .906, d = 0.04) domains; however, the Impact domain score worsened significantly (p = .008, d = 0.31).

The control group exhibited a trend different from the exercise group, with some pulmonary function parameters showing a decline over the 8-week period. The FVC (%predicted) significantly decreased, from 63.93 ± 15.63 to 62.07 ± 16.49 (p = .041, d = 0.11), and the FEV1 (%predicted) also showed a significant reduction, from 61.07 ± 16.01 to 58.50 ± 16.78 (p = .030, d = 0.16). The FEV1/FVC ratio declined significantly (p = .002, d = 0.29). In terms of respiratory muscle strength, MIP decreased slightly from 91.5 ± 13.62 cmH₂O to 89.25 ± 16.15 cmH₂O (p = .285, d = 0.16), while MEP declined from 52.0 ± 9.69 cmH₂O to 49.25 ± 11.35 cmH₂O (p = .066, d = 0.28), though this change was not statistically significant (Table 2).

Group comparison

The exercise group demonstrated a substantial improvement in 6MWD (Δ57.86 m, 95% CI [20, 155], p = .001), while the control group showed a decline (Δ − 19.72 m). Similarly, when expressed as % predicted, the exercise group increased by 11.45% (Δ11.45, 95% CI [3.92, 33.90]) compared with a decline of − 2.71% in the control group, again demonstrating a statistically significant difference in favor of the exercise group (p = .001). ANCOVA results indicated a more favorable trend in the exercise group, particularly for FVC % predicted (F(1,25) = 14.70, p < .001, partial η²=0.37) and FEV₁ % predicted (F(1,25) = 13.80, p = .001, partial η²=0.36).

The MIP increased significantly in the exercise group (Δ17.37, 95% CI [0, 35]) but slightly declined in the control group (Δ − 2.25), with ANCOVA confirming a significant between-group effect (F(1,16) = 13.20, p = .002, partial η²=0.45). Similarly, MEP improved i the exercise group (Δ6.88, 95% CI [1, 23]) but declined in the control group (Δ − 2.75), showing a robust adjusted difference (F(1,16) = 16.00, p = .001, partial η²=0.50).

Quality of life, measured by the St. George’s Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ), improved significantly in the exercise group compared with the control group. The exercise group showed marked reductions in Symptom (Δ − 5.00 vs. Δ1.79, 95% CI [− 27.90, − 1.90], p = .010; F(1,19) = 6.55, partial η²=0.26), Activity (Δ − 11.37 vs. Δ0.64, 95% CI [− 24.60, − 11.13], p = .008; F(1,19) = 11.10, partial η²=0.37), Impact (Δ − 16.00 vs. Δ4.86, 95% CI [− 30.00, − 4.17], p = 001; F(1,19) = 34.90, partial η²=0.65), and Total (Δ − 13.13 vs. Δ2.07, 95% CI [− 23.00, − 3.00], p = .001; F(1,19) = 33.90, partial η²=0.64) scores, whereas the control group experienced inimal change or worsening across these domains (Table 3).

Adverse events

No serious adverse events related to the intervention were found, indicating that the program was safe for participants. However, complaints such as shortness of breath and lower extremity fatigue up to Borg RPE 7 occurred in three patients.

Discussion

This is the first study to investigate the effects of online Baduanjin exercise training on functional exercise capacity, respiratory function, respiratory muscle strength, and the quality of life of patients with IPF. The results show that this online Baduanjin exercise training improved the exercise capacity and quality of life of these patients with IPF.

In an RCT that investigated the effectiveness of the WeChat platform in patients with COPD, the subjects included in the exercise group were given synchronous, online Baduanjin exercises and the control group was given asynchronous Baduanjin exercises. According to the results, Baduanjin exercises based on online platform management were effective in improving the quality of life and lung functions of these patients with COPD [29]. In addition, a feasibility study in patients with cystic fibrosis examined the online applicability of Tai Chi, a mind–body exercise similar to Baduanjin, and reported that the online classes were well accepted, provided convenience, and reduced infection risk [30]. In our study, we obtained similar benefits with the Baduanjin exercise training program, which we applied synchronously three days per week and asynchronously once per week via an online platform for patients with IPF. Our approach is similar to previous tele-rehabilitation studies in that it provided supervised exercise through a remote platform to improve accessibility and reduce cost; however, it differs by employing Baduanjin, a low to moderate intensity mind–body practice requiring no equipment instead of traditional aerobic or resistance exercises. Baduanjin may be particularly suitable for patients with IPF because its emphasis on breathing control, gentle movements, and safety aligns well with their reduced exercise tolerance and experience of dyspnea.

The 6MWT is a widely used test for the objective assessment of functional exercise capacity in the management of patients with lung disease. Exercise capacity is markedly reduced in patients with IPF, especially in those with advanced disease, due to several factors such as impaired respiratory mechanics and circulatory problems [31, 32] In our study, we used the 6MWT to evaluate the exercise capacity of our patients with IPF. At the end of our study, we found a statistically significant increase of 57 m in 6MWD in the Baduanjin group and a statistically significant decrease of 19 m in the control group. The results showed that Baduanjin exercises provided a statistically significant increase in 6MWD in our patients with IPF compared with the control group. Choi et al. achieved a significant increase of 46 m in 6MWD with an exercise training program applied for eight weeks for patients with IPF whose average age was 68 years. In the control group, a decrease of 40 m was observed [33]. In the Cochrane analysis published in 2021, a 36 m increase in the 6MWD of patients with IPF after pulmonary rehabilitation was reported [34]. Another meta-analysis showed that Baduanjin training for patients with COPD was effective in improving 6MWD compared with control groups [35]. Our study, consistent with the literature, showed that Baduanjin exercises are at least as effective as traditional exercise training in improving the 6MWD of patients with IPF. In patients with IPF, a decrease in 6MWD of more than 50 m at 24 weeks was associated with a fourfold increase in the 1-year risk of death, and the estimated minimal clinically significant change was reported to be between 25 and 45 m [36]. The improvements achieved in 6MWD in our study also suggest that Baduanjin exercises provide a prognostic benefit in this patient group. Furthermore, the 6MWD change of 57.86 m (95% CI [20, 155]) exceeded the established MCID range for IPF (25–45 m), indicating that this improvement is not only statistically significant but also clinically meaningful [36].

IPF is directly associated with multiple changes in lung function. These changes result from a complex disease process that affects all parts of the lower respiratory system, from the conducting airways to the pulmonary vasculature. Decreases in lung compliance occur early in IPF. Restrictive ventilatory defects, defined by a decrease in vital capacity, is typical in patients with IPF as in other idiopathic lung diseases. Some studies have shown that the resistance of conducting airways decreases in IPF. It was reported that the mean FEV1/FVC value of 55 patients with IPF with a mean age of 71 years was 83% [37]. In our study, we found that the mean FEV1/FVC values were 80% in the Baduanjin group and 77% in the control group. In 2018, Liu et al. examined the effect of Baduanjin exercises in patients with COPD. The researchers reported that Baduanjin exercises were effective in improving FEV1, FEV1 (%predicted), FVC and FEV1/FVC values compared with the control group [35] Cao et al. [38] reported results like those of Liu et al. At the end of our study, we found statistically non-significant increases in FVC (%predicted) and FEV1 (%predicted) values in the Baduanjin group and statistically significant decreases in FVC (%predicted) and FEV1 (%predicted) in the control group. At the end of the eight weeks, we saw that there was a statistically significant difference between the groups in terms of the change in these parameters in both groups. Even after accounting for baseline values and potential initial imbalances, the trend still favored the Baduanjin group for FVC (% predicted) and FEV₁ (% predicted). In the evaluations made at the end of the eight weeks, it was determined that there were statistically significant decreases in the respiratory function tests of the control group, while it was observed that the respiratory functions were preserved in the baduanjin group. This result is quite important when the disease prognosis is considered. IPF is a progressive fibrosing interstitial lung disease characterized by irreversible declines in lung function. The deterioration in respiratory function observed in the control group after eight weeks can therefore be attributed to the natural progressive course of the disease. According to the literature, the lung function of patients with IPF typically declines in a nearly linear fashion over time. This decline reflects scarring and fibrosis within the lungs and can become apparent within weeks or months in the absence of therapeutic intervention. The natural course of IPF varies among patients; in some individuals the deterioration progresses rapidly, whereas in others it occurs more slowly. Considering these factors, our findings are consistent with known progression profiles and reflect worsening restrictive lung physiology and reduced lung volumes. Thus, this finding can be explained by the natural history of IPF [39].

The reason for this preservation in respiratory functions may be that the slow and rhythmic breathing techniques of Baduanjin increase tidal volume by allowing the lungs to ventilate more effectively. This may contribute to the relief of the restrictive lung pattern characteristic of IPF patients [40, 41]. Deep breathing techniques applied during the exercise can increase respiratory efficiency by allowing the alveoli to participate more effectively in gas exchange. In addition, the rapid and tachypnea pattern frequently seen in IPF patients can become more efficient with Baduanjin by reducing the respiratory rate and increasing the depth of breathing [42]. In addition, the results obtained may be related to the increase in lower extremity function associated with exercises having a positive effect on respiratory function.

Maximal inspiratory muscle strength values are generally preserved in patients with IPF [43]. The inspiratory muscle strength values of the patients with IPF in our study fell within the normal range (≥ 80 cmH₂O for men and ≥ 70 cmH₂O for women [27]). In an RCT that investigated the effectiveness of Liuzijue exercises, a type of Qigong, in patients with stable COPD, it was observed that the provided improvement in respiratory muscle strength (MIP, MEP) after three months of exercise training [44]. In a study by Lee et al., in which they investigated the effect of a pulmonary rehabilitation program on respiratory muscle strength in patients with COPD, it was reported that pulmonary rehabilitation could improve respiratory muscle strength [45]. As in previous reports, our study achieved a statistically significant improvement in MIP and MEP values through the use of Baduanjin exercises. We observed non-significant decreases in MIP and MEP values in the control group after eight weeks.

The quality of life of patients with IPF is impaired mainly in the areas of “physical health” and “level of independence” [46]. Assessment of quality of life is especially important in patients with progressive and incurable diseases such as IPF. Developed for patients with obstructive pulmonary disease, SGRQ has frequently been used to assess the of life of patients with IPF [47]. In the systematic review and meta-analysis published by Meng et al., which included 10 studies, it was reported that Tai-Chi and Qigong exercises might be beneficial in improving the quality of life of patients with COPD, but no definitive conclusion could be reached due to the low quality of the methodological of the studies included [48]. Another study showed that Tai-Chi and Qigong could reduce scores on each component of the SGRQ more than walking exercise, but the differences were not significant except for the activity score. The effect of Baduanjin exercises on the quality of life of patients with IPF has not been previously investigated. Yang et al. concluded that Baduanjin exercises were a safe, feasible and acceptable intervention that could improve quality of life and exercise capacity of patients with heart failure [49]. Similarly, we found a statistically significant improvement in SGRQ symptoms, impact, and activity sub-scores and total scores with Badunjin exercises in our patients with IPF compared with the control group. The reasons for this improvement in quality of life may be that, from a functional point of view, although Baduanjin is a low-intensity exercise, it increases muscle endurance and overall physical capacity with long-term practice. Exercise tolerance, which is often low in IPF patients, can be improved by gradually increasing the level of physical activity with Baduanjin [22, 50]. Baduanjin can also improve the quality of life of IPF patients by supporting physical, respiratory and psychological health together. We believe that the increase in respiratory muscle strength and significant improvement in exercise performance of the patients in the exercise group at the end of the program positively affected their participation in daily life and functionality, which contributed to a positive change in the quality of life results.

Study limitations

A limitation of the study is that it does not reflect long-term results. There is a need for studies that examine the long-term results of patients with IPF who receive Baduanjin exercise training. Another consideration is that, because the sample size calculation was based solely on the primary outcome (6MWT), the statistical power for some secondary analyses may have been reduced, increasing the likelihood of overlooking smaller effects and limiting the generalizability of these secondary findings. In addition, the control group in this study did not receive active attention or education, thus non-specific effects such as Hawthorne, placebo, or attention could not be completely ruled out. Future research should incorporate an educational control group or an active comparator such as an attention-matched education or sham-exercise condition to mitigate potential confounding factors related to time and attention. Another limitation is that we did not assess the exercise habits, comorbid mental disorders, or changes in psychological states, such as motivation and self-efficacy of the participants, all of which may potentially influence exercise adherence and quality of life.

Conclusions

This study demonstrated that Baduanjin exercise training via an online platform was effective on the functional exercise capacity, respiratory function, respiratory muscle strength, and the quality of life of patients with IPF. This approach was associated with evidence of less and slower deterioration in respiratory function over the observation period compared with patients who did not receive exercise training. Additionally, comparative studies that examine the efficacy of online versus face-to-face Baduanjin interventions in this specific population are warranted to further elucidate the optimal delivery method for maximizing therapeutic outcomes.

Multidetector computed tomography angiography predicts the need for bronchial artery embolization in hemoptysis: A retrospective cohort study of 32 patients
Authors: Haruyasu Sakuranaka, Yosuke Nemoto, Ryota Nakayama, Yuji Yamakawa, Tadaharu Nakamoto, Komei Igei, Naoki Okamoto, Yasuo Asai, Masahiko Ichioka
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBronchial artery embolization (BAE) is the standard intervention for severe hemoptysis, yet predicting which patients will require BAE remains challenging. We evaluated whether multidetector computed tomography angiography (MDCTA) findings can predict the need for BAE.MethodsWe retrospectively reviewed 32 consecutive patients hospitalized for hemoptysis >100 mL/day between April 2012 and March 2015. All patients underwent MDCTA on admission and received standardized conservative management. MDCTA findings were dichotomized as (1) normal or mediastinally confined bronchial arteries and (2) abnormally dilated bronchial arteries extending into the pulmonary parenchyma. Interobserver agreement among three readers was assessed (Fleiss κ). The primary outcome was subsequent BAE.ResultsTwenty patients had normal/mediastinally confined arteries and 12 had abnormally dilated parenchymal-extending arteries. None of the former required BAE, whereas 7/12 (58 %) in the latter group underwent BAE (p < 0.001). The imaging criterion yielded sensitivity 100 % (95 % CI 59–100 %) and specificity 80 % (95 % CI 59–93 %). Interobserver agreement for the 3-category MDCTA classification was almost perfect (κ = 0.90). Penalized logistic regression adjusting for age, sex, and infectious etiology retained MDCTA parenchymal extension as the sole predictor (adjusted OR ∼18 (p ≈ 0.002)).ConclusionsMDCTA identification of an abnormally dilated bronchial artery extending into the pulmonary parenchyma predicts BAE requirement in significant hemoptysis. This readily recognizable imaging criterion may expedite interventional consultation and optimize triage.Keywords: Hemoptysis, Bronchial artery embolization, Multidetector computed tomography angiography, Interobserver agreement, Interventional radiology

Introduction

Hemoptysis ranges from self-limited bleeding to massive, life-threatening hemorrhage. Bronchial artery embolization (BAE) achieves rapid hemostasis in most severe cases, with high technical success in published series [1,2]. Despite this, timely identification of patients who will ultimately require BAE remains difficult and may delay intervention.

Multidetector computed tomography angiography (MDCTA) has transformed hemoptysis work-up by mapping bronchial and non-bronchial systemic arteries and by depicting pulmonary arterial sources such as Rasmussen aneurysms [[3], [4], [5], [6]]. MDCTA is widely obtained before BAE to localize culprit vessels. Investigators have proposed complex morphologic MDCT scoring systems incorporating arterial diameter, length, and tortuosity to estimate the need for BAE [7]. Conversely, other work suggests that abnormal diameter alone does not reliably discriminate which patients proceed to BAE, underscoring inconsistent evidence regarding the predictive utility of MDCTA for procedural triage [8].

We hypothesized that a simple, qualitative MDCTA feature—an abnormally dilated bronchial artery that extends from the mediastinum into the pulmonary parenchyma—would predict the need for BAE in patients hospitalized with significant hemoptysis. We evaluated this hypothesis in a retrospective cohort managed under a standardized treatment protocol.

Methods

Study design and patients

This retrospective cohort study was conducted at Tokyo Metropolitan Toshima Hospital. We included all consecutive adult patients admitted for hemoptysis >100 mL/day between April 1, 2012 and March 31, 2015. No exclusion criteria were applied. The institutional review board approved the protocol (No. 4–26; November 29, 2022) and waived written informed consent (opt-out).

CT acquisition

All patients underwent MDCTA within 24 h of admission using a 64-detector-row CT scanner (Aquilion 64; Canon Medical Systems, Otawara, Japan). Acquisition parameters: 120 kVp with automatic tube-current modulation; 64 × 0.5 mm collimation; pitch 0.828; 0.5-s gantry rotation; 1.0-mm reconstructed slice thickness. Nonionic iodinated contrast (iohexol 300 mg I/mL) was injected intravenously at 2 mL/kg (institutional maximum) at 3 mL/s; scanning commenced 25 s after injection onset.

Image interpretation

Two respiratory physicians and one radiologist independently reviewed all MDCTA studies while blinded to clinical outcomes. Each bronchial artery was evaluated for (1) diameter >2 mm at any segment and (2) course confined to the mediastinum versus extension into the pulmonary parenchyma. Scans were categorized as normal/mediastinally confined (categories 0–1) or abnormally dilated with parenchymal extension (category 2). Initial independent ratings were recorded; discrepancies were resolved by consensus for the primary analysis.

Interobserver agreement

Reader reliability was assessed using raw reader classifications on a 3-category scale (0 = normal; 1 = dilated-mediastinal; 2 = dilated with parenchymal extension). Fleiss’ κ was calculated across the three readers. For clinical interpretability, a binary collapse (parenchymal extension vs no extension) was also evaluated. Ninety-five percent confidence intervals (CI) were derived from 1000 bootstrap resamples of patients.

Clinical management and BAE procedure

All patients received continuous tranexamic acid infusion (≈1 g/day per institutional protocol) and pathogen-directed antimicrobial therapy when indicated. Flexible bronchoscopy with diluted epinephrine-saline instillation and wedge tamponade was performed for active endobronchial bleeding. Persistent or recurrent hemorrhage prompted diagnostic angiography and, when feasible, BAE. BAE was performed through femoral arterial access with 2.0–2.4 Fr microcatheters. Absorbable gelatin sponge (Gelfoam) was the sole embolic agent; polyvinyl alcohol particles and coils were not employed.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were analyzed with Fisher's exact test. Diagnostic test characteristics (sensitivity, specificity, predictive values) were calculated for the MDCTA parenchymal-extension criterion. Because zero BAE events occurred in the MDCTA-negative group, standard logistic regression was unstable (quasi-complete separation). We therefore fitted a penalized logistic regression model (L1 regularization) with BAE (yes/no) as the outcome and prespecified covariates: age, sex, infectious etiology, and MDCTA category. Uncertainty in the penalized coefficient estimates was assessed by nonparametric bootstrap resampling (B = 1000). Two-sided p < 0.05 denoted statistical significance (BellCurve for Excel v3.23; supplemental analyses in Python 3.11).

Patient characteristics

Thirty-two patients were included (16 male; mean age 70.7 ± 10.7 years). Infectious etiologies predominated: old tuberculosis (n = 7), aspergillosis (n = 6), bronchiectasis (n = 6), chronic bronchitis (n = 6), Mycobacterium avium complex infection (n = 4), pulmonary abscess (n = 1), pulmonary nocardiosis (n = 1), and lung cancer (n = 1). MDCTA findings and BAE requirement

MDCTA demonstrated normal or mediastinally confined bronchial arteries in 20 patients (63 %) and abnormally dilated arteries extending into the pulmonary parenchyma in 12 patients (37 %). None of the 20 patients with normal/mediastinally confined arteries required BAE, whereas 7 of 12 patients (58 %) with parenchymal extension underwent the procedure (p < 0.001, Fisher's exact test). The imaging criterion yielded sensitivity 100 % (95 % CI 59–100 %), specificity 80 % (95 % CI 59–93 %), positive predictive value 58 % (95 % CI 28–85 %), and negative predictive value 100 % (95 % CI 83–100 %). Interobserver agreement

Agreement among the three readers for the 3-category MDCTA classification was almost perfect (Fleiss κ = 0.90; 95 % bootstrap CI 0.78–1.00). When collapsed to a binary outcome (parenchymal extension vs none), agreement remained high (κ = 0.86; 95 % bootstrap CI 0.69–1.00). Multivariable analysis

Given quasi-complete separation, penalized logistic regression was used. After adjustment for age, sex, and infectious etiology, MDCTA parenchymal extension remained strongly associated with subsequent BAE (adjusted OR 17.6 (bootstrap 95 % CI 4.2–60.8; p ≈ 0.002)). Discussion

We found that a single, qualitative MDCTA sign—parenchymal extension of an abnormally dilated bronchial artery—predicts the need for BAE in patients hospitalized with significant hemoptysis. The association was strong, and the imaging criterion perfectly excluded the need for BAE when absent (NPV 100 %). Interobserver agreement was excellent, supporting the practical reproducibility of this sign.

Our binary classification simplifies prior work. Mori et al. proposed a multicomponent MDCT score incorporating arterial diameter, length, and tortuosity to estimate BAE need [7]. By contrast, Chen et al. reported that categorizing patients solely by bronchial artery enlargement failed to significantly distinguish those who underwent BAE from those who did not, underscoring the limitations of diameter-only criteria [8]. Incorporating parenchymal extension—rather than relying on diameter alone—may provide clinically actionable discrimination.

Scanner capability is also relevant. Zhao et al. demonstrated the utility of 320-detector MDCTA for delineating small culprit vessels in recurrent hemoptysis [9]. Our results indicate that the triage-relevant sign described here can be reliably identified on a 64-detector platform, broadening applicability in resource-variable settings.

Adjunct medical therapy may influence bleeding control. All patients received continuous tranexamic acid and pathogen-directed antimicrobial therapy when indicated—interventions associated with reduced bleeding burden or improved outcomes in prior studies [1,10,11]. Because this protocol was applied uniformly, differential medical management is unlikely to explain the observed imaging-outcome relationship; nonetheless, residual confounding by infection severity cannot be excluded.

Limitations include the retrospective, single-center design, modest sample size, and wide confidence intervals around some estimates. Although interobserver agreement was high in this cohort, validation in external populations is needed. We excluded patients whose hemoptysis arose from non-bronchial systemic or pulmonary arterial sources; thus, generalizability to those populations remains uncertain. Prospective multicenter validation with standardized reader training is warranted.

Conclusions

An MDCTA finding of an abnormally dilated bronchial artery that extends into the pulmonary parenchyma predicts the need for BAE in patients with significant hemoptysis. The sign shows excellent interobserver agreement and may expedite interventional consultation and streamline triage; confirmation in larger, prospective cohorts is required before routine implementation.

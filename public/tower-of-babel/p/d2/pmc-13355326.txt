Regioselective electrosynthesis of pyrazolo[3,4-b]quinolin-5-one derivatives
Authors: Eman O. Osman, Amr M. Mahmoud, Hala B. El-Nassan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A highly efficient and regioselective method had been reported for the synthesis of pyrazoloquinoline derivatives. The current work studied the reaction of 3-indolylpyrazole, dimedone, and various aldehydes under electrochemical conditions. The various factors controlling electrochemical reactions were examined and optimized, including reaction time, temperature, current, solvent, type of electrodes, and supporting electrolytes. The scope of the reaction was investigated using different types of aldehydes. The reaction yielded exclusively pyrazolo[3,4-b]quinolin-5-ones in high yields and mild reaction conditions. The structure of the products was confirmed by 1H-NMR and 2D-NMR spectra. The reaction mechanism was followed up using cyclic voltammetry. Compared to the conventional heating method, the electrochemical method proved to be advantageous in terms of simple reaction conditions, short reaction time, higher yield, and eliminating the need for basic catalysis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13065-026-01849-3.

Introduction

Organic electrochemical synthesis EC is considered a green and sustainable method of synthesis that has received a lot of attention in recent years. In electrochemistry, electrons are used as catalysts, thereby making further use of oxidizing or reducing reagents unnecessary. This step eliminates the generation of a large amount of waste and reduces the cost of synthesis and separation of products, resulting in highly atom-economic reactions. Furthermore, the modified electrodes can serve as heterogeneous catalysts. The cathodic reduction in EC simulated the effect of basic catalysts, and thus, the use of harmful and toxic bases can be avoided [1–4]. EC reactions require shorter reaction times, milder reaction conditions, and lower energy than conventional heating [5–8]. The regioselective effect of electrochemistry was recently reported for the synthesis of 4-selanylisoquinolinones [9], dioic acids [10], fullerenyl ketones and esters [11], nitroacetaminophen [12] and N, N′-diphenyl-3-sulfonyl-[1,1′-biphenyl]−4,4′-diamine [13].

In continuation of our interest in electrochemically driven synthesis of heterocyclic compounds [14–20], this work aimed to investigate the regioselective effect of electrochemical conditions on the product of multicomponent reaction (MCR). The reaction of 5-aminopyrazole, dimedone, and aromatic aldehydes was studied using different electrochemical conditions (Scheme 1). The effects of varying time, temperature, solvent, current, electrode material, and type of supporting electrolyte were studied in order to optimize the reaction conditions. The reaction was followed up by TLC to ensure the formation of only one and the same product in each turn. The structure of the product was determined based on spectral data, including 1H-NMR, 13C-NMR, COSY, and NOESY. The scope of the reaction was investigated using different aromatic aldehydes. The mechanism of the reaction was traced by cyclic voltammetry studies.

Results and discussion

The multicomponent reaction of 5-aminopyrazole, 1,3-dicarbonyl compounds, and aldehydes can yield pyrazoloquinolinone 4 and 6 (Hantzsch-type) or pyrazoloquinazolinone 5 and 7 (Biginelli-type) rings (Scheme 1).

Quiroga et al. [21, 22]. and Lipson et al. [23] reported the regioselective formation of pyrazoloquinolinone upon heating 5-aminopyrazoles, dimedone, and aldehydes by simple heating in either ethanol or DMF, respectively.

Alternatively, Lipson et al. [24] reported that the formation of the two isomers was dependent on the type of substituent on the benzaldehyde. Thus, the pyrazoloquinolinone ring was formed upon using benzaldehydes bearing para-electron-donating groups like methyl, methoxy, and dimethylamino groups. Whilst, the pyrazoloquinazolinone ring was formed upon using benzaldehydes bearing para-electron-withdrawing groups such as nitro and fluoro groups.

Chebanov et al. [25]. indicated the regioselective formation of pyrazoloquinolinone ring upon reacting 5-aminopyrazoles, dimedone, and aldehydes in basic medium under microwave irradiation at high temperature or by simple heating at high temperature (150 °C). This product was also favored upon reacting 3-methyl-5-aminopyrazole due to lower steric hindrance, unlike 3-phenyl-5-aminopyrazole, which can give a mixture of pyrazoloquinolinones and pyrazoloquinazolinones [26]. The isomeric pyrazoloquinazolinones were obtained using sonication in neutral medium at room temperature for 30 min [25]. Thus, the two main factors that controlled the type of product were temperature and basic catalyst.

Preparation of pyrazolo[3,4-b]quinolin derivative 4a under conventional conditions

The reaction of 3-indolylpyrazol-5-amine 1, dimedone 2, and 4-chlorobenzaldehyde 3a was performed by heating under reflux in ethanol using triethylamine as a catalyst or in DMF as a high-boiling-point solvent. Both trials afforded compound 4a regioselectively in 54% yield using ethanol/triethylamine and 13% yield using DMF as a solvent. These results indicated the important effect of the base to catalyze the reaction.

Preparation of pyrazolo[3,4-b]quinolin derivative 4a under electrochemical conditions and optimization of the reaction conditions

In this work, the reaction of 3-indolylpyrazol-5-amine 1, dimedone 2, and 4-chlorobenzaldehyde 3a was selected as a template reaction to optimize the electrochemical conditions and overall yield (Table 1). The reaction was conducted at constant current 20 mA in ethanol at 70 °C using sodium bromide as a supporting electrolyte and graphite electrodes as both anode and cathode. The yield obtained after 30 min. was 79% (entry 1, Table 1). Increasing the time of the reaction to 60 min. under the same reaction conditions was accompanied by an increase in the yield to 89% (entry 2, Table 1). Further increase in the reaction time reduced the yield (entry 3, Table 1), probably due to oxidation of the indole ring, so the optimum time was selected as 60 min.

The reaction was greatly affected by the temperature, as the reaction was unsuccessful at room temperature, even upon increasing the time to 2 h (entries 4 and 5, Table 1). Heating to 70 °C aided in dissolving the reactants in ethanol and hence increase the reaction rate. Likewise, changing the constant current from 20 mA to 10 mA reduced the yield significantly to 68%, while increasing the current to 30 mA resulted in a slight decrease in the yield (81%) (entries 6 and 7, Table 1). Therefore, the constant current was kept at 20 mA in all the subsequent trials.

The effect of solvents was examined using different solvents, including ethanol, methanol, acetic acid, and methylene chloride, that afford compound 4a in yields 89%, 70%, 79%, 68% respectively (entries 2, 8–10, Table 1). Therefore, ethanol was chosen as the optimum solvent.

Changing the supporting electrolyte also reduced the yield (entries 11–13, Table 1). Thus, sodium bromide was selected as the optimum supporting electrolyte. Finally, the replacement of the graphite cathode with copper had minimal effect on the yield (entry 14, Table 1). Whilst, the replacement of graphite cathode with zinc, aluminum, magnesium, or platinum decreased the yield significantly (entries 15–18, Table 1). Likewise, replacing the graphite anode with platinum decreased the yield of 4a (entries 19 and 20, Table 1). Accordingly, the best results can be obtained by using graphite as both anode and cathode.

Spectral identification of compounds 4a-4j

Examining the 1H-NMR spectral data of the products indicated the presence of three exchangeable NH signals at δ 9.77–10.05, 11.34–11.38, and 12.01–12.26 ppm corresponding to pyridine NH, indole NH, and pyrazole NH protons, respectively. Additionally, the H-4 signal appeared at δ 5.10–5.49 ppm.

The presence of pyrazole NH signal at δ 12.01–12.26 ppm and the absence of pyrazole CH proton at δ 5.74 ppm confirmed the formation of pyrazolo[3,4-b]quinolinone ring (compounds 4 or 6) and ruled out the presence of pyrazolo[5,1-b]quinazoline ring (compounds 5 and 7).

The linear structure 4 and the angular isomer 6 were differentiated based on 2D COSY and 2D NOE experiments carried out on representative compounds 4 g, 4 h, and 4j (Supplementary Figs. 1–6). The proton signal at δ 5.18–5.38 ppm was assigned to H-4, while the resonance at δ 9.77–9.82 ppm was attributed to the pyridine NH proton. In the angular structure 6, H-4 and the pyridine NH are expected to be proximal in space, and thus, an observable NOE correlation would be expected. However, no NOE cross-peak was detected between H-4 and the pyridine NH, thereby ruling out the angular arrangement.

On the other hand, the 2D NOE spectra revealed a distinct correlation between the pyridine NH proton resonating at δ 9.77–9.82 ppm and the CH2 protons at δ 2.37–2.55 ppm, which confirmed their proximity. This is only possible with structure 4. Moreover, in all 1H-NMR spectra, both H-4 and azine NH appeared as singlet signals, which indicated the absence of coupling between them. Both results provided evidence for the linear structure. Accordingly, isomer 4 was assigned as the main product synthesized from this reaction. All the derivatives formed 4a-j had comparable data, which proved the regioselectivity of the reaction.

Investigation of the reaction mechanism

The reaction mechanism was traced using a cyclic voltammetry study (Fig. 1), which revealed an initial cathodic reduction of dimedone to form an anion. This was apparent from the increase of cathodic current at −1.2 v. This was followed by a reaction of the formed anion with aldehyde to form aryl methylidene derivative II as proposed in (Fig. 2) and then a reaction of the carbonyl group of dimedone with the amino group of pyrazole to give intermediate III followed by cyclocondenation to give pyrazoloquinoline 4.

The regioselective effect can be explained in light of the basic catalysis of electrochemical conditions that favored the formation of pyrazoloquinoline rather than the pyrazoloquinazoline ring. Previously reported data indicated that the regioselective formation of pyrazoloquinoline was favored in the presence of a basic catalyst [25, 26]. The advantage of electrochemical synthesis relies on its basic catalytic effect without the need to add a base, and thus it is a more atom-economic reaction. Moreover, the reaction was performed at 70 °C for a short reaction time.

Experimental part

General

Stuart SMP20 apparatus was used to determine the melting points and they were uncorrected. Shimadzu IR 435 spectrophotometer was used to record the IR spectra and their values were represented in cm−1. Bruker spectrophotometer were used to record the 1H NMR and 13C NMR spectra. Tetramethylsilane (TMS) was used as an internal standard. The microanalyses for C, H and N were performed at the Regional Center for Mycology and Biotechnology, Faculty of Pharmacy, Al-Azhar University, Cairo, Egypt. All cyclic voltammetric characterizations were carried out using Corrtest portable potentiostat/galvanostat CS100 (Wuhan, China). While, a programmable triple channel Keithley 2230-30-1 (Keithley, Cleveland, Ohio) power supply was used for the electrochemical synthesis at constant current mode. Conductometric measurements were performed using conductivity meter EcoScan CON 6 (Eutech Instruments, USA). The starting material, 3-(1H-indol-3-yl)−1H-pyrazol-5-amine was prepared as reported earlier [28].

Cyclic voltammetry study

Apparatus

All cyclic voltammetric characterizations were carried out using an electrochemical workstation, Corrtest portable potentiostat/galvanostat CS100 (Wuhan, China). The conventional three electrodes setup was used, where the reference electrode was Ag/AgCl wire, a Pt wire was utilized as a counter electrode, and the working electrode was PGE (Pencil graphite electrode) (HB, 0.9 mm diameter).

Conclusion

In summary, this work successfully demonstrated the regioselective synthesis of pyrazoloquinoline derivatives through an electrochemically driven multicomponent reaction involving 5-aminopyrazole, dimedone, and aromatic aldehydes. By varying key electrochemical parameters such as time, temperature, solvent, electrode material, and supporting electrolyte; optimal reaction conditions were established to achieve high selectivity and yield. The use of TLC monitoring ensured consistent product formation, while detailed spectral analysis, including 1H-NMR, 13C-NMR, COSY, and NOESY, confirmed the structural identity of the products. Furthermore, the scope of the reaction was expanded by employing a variety of aromatic aldehydes, showing the versatility of the method. Cyclic voltammetry studies provided mechanistic insights into the reaction pathway, highlighting the role of electrochemical conditions in controlling reactivity and regioselectivity. The comparison between the conventional heating method and electrochemical method indicated the advantages of the electrochemical method in terms of simple reaction conditions, lower temperature required for the reaction, higher yield, and regioselectivity without using basic catalysts. This study contributes to the growing field of electrochemical synthesis with precision and efficiency. Future work could explore further applications of this approach to access a broader range of bioactive molecules and functional materials.

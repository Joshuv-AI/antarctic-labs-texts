WFB: watermarking-based copyright protection framework for federated learning model via blockchain
Authors: Sujie Shao, Yue Wang, Chao Yang, Yan Liu, Xingyu Chen, Feng Qi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractFederated learning (FL) enables users to train the global model cooperatively without exposing their private data across the engaged parties, which is widely used in privacy-sensitive business. However, during the life cycle of FL models, both adversaries’ attacks and ownership generalization threaten the FL models’ copyright and affect the models’ reliability. To address these problems, existing model watermarking techniques can be used to verify FL model’s ownership. However, due to the lack of credible binding from “model extracted watermarks” to “ownership verification”, it is difficult to form a closed-loop watermarking framework for copyright protection. Therefore, starting from the shortcomings of the current watermark verification scheme, this article proposed WFB, a blockchain-empowered watermarking framework for ownership verification of federated models. Firstly, we propose a improved watermark generation algorithm to solve the credibility issue of watermarks. Secondly, we propose a watermark embedding method in federated learning, while blockchain technology is used to ensure the credible storage of watermark information throughout the process. Thirdly, the credibility of ownership verification is improved because of the watermark authenticity. Experimental results demonstrate the fidelity, effectiveness and robustness of WFB, with other superiorities such as improving process security and traceability.Subject terms: Engineering, Electrical and electronic engineering

Introduction

The past few years have witnessed the increasing development of Federated Learning1, which enables multiple participants to build a deep learning model collaboratively without disclosing data privacy. By aggregating locally-computed gradients instead of private data among participants, FL successfully breaks down the data silos and enhances data security, which realizes the goal of training high-quality model while the data is kept locally. Up to now, FL framework has gained extensive attention due to its potential applications in privacy-sensitive industries, such as medical care2–4, financial economy5 and recommendation systems6. However, in the traditional client-server FL architecture, model security risks still exist both inside and outside the federated model. On the one hand, the ownership generalization of internal nodes cannot be completely excluded; on the other hand, adversaries’ malicious attacks such as model stealing and redistribution is difficult to be prevented. To address these risks, coming up with a ownership verification framework for FL models is a prerequisite that protects the model copyright. Watermarking7–9 is a technology used to claim the ownership of deep learning models, which is able to protect the copyright of convolutional neural network (CNN) models from adversaries’ malicious attacks10–12. Current watermarking schemes can be divided into two categories: parameter-based watermarks7 and backdoor-based watermarks9. Parameter-based watermarking encodes designated binary strings as watermarks into model layer parameters7,13–15. Backdoor-based watermarking utilizes a number of pre-defined mislabeled data as backdoor trigger at the training stage, so that the embedded model has an exclusive ability to accomplish the watermark recognition and ownership verification tasks9,16. However, these existing ownership verification schemes face the problem of insufficient credibility of the watermark information, because there is no credible binding from “model extracted watermark” to “ownership verification result”. On the one hand, the watermarks are easy to be erased and tampered with17. On the other hand, the model watermarks can be easily forged using existing watermark techniques18. Taking the advantage of transparency, traceability, and non repudiation characteristics of blockchain, scholars have proposed blockchain-empowered federated learning framework19–21 for model security enhancing. Blockchain is a distributed ledger, and the data on the blockchain is not controlled by any single entity19. In traditional client-server federated learning scenario, blockchain takes the place of original central server to act as a trusted information intermediary, achieving information storage, model verification and security enhancing. However, blockchain cannot be directly applied to FL watermarking technology due to the complexity of engaged FL parties and frequent interactive process. If the watermark information is directly uploaded on the blockchain as traditional digital watermarks do, the credibility of the training process will be difficult to confirm. Therefore, with the objective of ensuring the credibility of watermark information, how to utilize the blockchain properly to define the interaction between blockchain and FL participates becomes a compelling need that motivates our research work reported in this article. Starting from the urgent need for model copyright protection, we review the existing federated watermarking technology and conclude three main challenges in current FL watermarking framework.

Challenge A: Two categories of current watermarking technology are too theoretical and separated from each other to adopt in real life scenarios. Although many watermarking schemes for CNN models copyright protection have been proposed these years, most of these are based on the strong assumption that the central server is completely trustworthy, and few of these researches combine these two watermarking categories as a general watermark framework to provide dual protection to FL model. How to fill in the gaps in current watermarking technology and organically combine these two watermarking technologies to form a general watermarking scheme becomes an urgent need that motivates our research work.

Challenge B: Traditional FL watermarking technology is lack of process traceability mechanism. When malicious clients in FL stealthily distribute or sell the FL model to other parties, it is necessary to trace the leaker which infringes the legitimate copyright of the FL group. In order to solve the traceability problem, we consider to use blockchain due to its characteristics of traceability and immutability. Moreover, the timestamps and smart contracts also improve the security level.

Challenge C: Traditional federated watermarking is not specialized in watermark generalization recognition for dishonest clients. Since multiple FL models may be trained within a federated system, a dishonest client who has not contributed data to a certain model may attempt to embed its secret watermark information through the generalization ability of current watermarking scheme to claim its fake ownership. Therefore, the watermark generation method should be designed smartly, to ensure that the watermark set generated by each client is independent and unique.

Considering the challenges discussed above, this paper proposes a blockchain-empowered watermarking framework for ownership verification of FL models named WFB. Based on the traditional client-server federated learning architecture19,22,23, blockchain is adopted with the objective of enhancing the direct correlation between “extracted model watermarks” and “ownership verification result”. In this proposed framework, blockchain acts as a trusted intermediary to achieve information storage, watermark validation and other security enhancing functions. The critical process information is uploaded to the blockchain, which guarantees the immutability of watermark information and traceability of training process. In specific, this framework is consisted of three separate procedures: (a) an on-chain watermark generation process in which participating clients generate private watermarks using the information published by the coordinator; (b) a watermark embedding process that allows clients to embed their secret watermarks into model while training, notice that the hash values of model and watermark information are uploaded to the blockchain every communication round; (c) a verification process that realizes ownership verification by comparing the extracted watermark and the embedded watermark, where the adversary’s behavior, e.g. watermark forgery and watermark tampering can be effectively avoided because of the blockchain. Related work

In this section, we briefly introduce the principle and review related work in two following aspects: federated learning and model watermarking methods.

Federated learning

Federated learning refers to a distributed framework to collaboratively build deep learning models without sharing training data across the engaged parties. Joint-effort and data privacy are two important attributes of federated learning. Using de-centralized data sources to train a centralized federated model breaks the limitation of data silos. In the federal training process, local data is secret to other party, which ensures data security. Despite the data privacy level has improved compared with centralized learning, FL still falls short in copyright protection due to the message-sharing vulnerability and multiple attacks. Since an FL model requires a large number of clients and computations and is of high commercial value, it would be faced with many threats. During the whole life cycle of model development and deployment, many attack surfaces exposed to malicious parties. To address privacy concerns, secure federated learning (SFL)24,25 was proposed to enhance security level. In SFL scenario, model updates are sent to the aggregator instead of directly back to the model owner. Some training strategies such as homomorphic encryption26, differential privacy27 and secret sharing19 can also be adopted to enhance security level of local models. Although some training strategies have been proposed to realize secure federated learning, the problem of model copyright protection and traceability problems have not been fully solved. On the one hand, valuable FL models are in danger of being copied or re-distributed through fine-tuning, pruning and other external attacks. On the other hand, dishonest participates may muddle through the ownership verification process by watermark generalization. Therefore, from the perspective of model security, forming a complete model authentication framework is one of the main aspects to improve model security.

Model watermarking methods

To protect the copyright of CNN models, watermarking techniques have been proposed in7–9,12,18 to embed designated watermarks into CNN models. Broadly speaking, existing CNN watermarking methods can be categorized as parameter-based watermarks and backdoor-based watermarks. The watermark principle is shown in Fig. 1.

Principle for backdoor-based watermark and parameter-based watermark.

Parameter-based watermarks

Parameter-based watermarks encode designated binary strings B={0,1}N as watermarks into model layer parameters. It is possible to embed a watermark in a model network by directly modifying w of a trained network, as is usually done in the image domain. However, this approach may cause model accuracy degrading in the original task. A better way is to embed watermarks while training, this approach has little impair on the original task performance because CNNs are typically over-parameterized. Because CNNs have many local minima which are likely to approach the global minimum within a very small error, an embedded regularizer only needs to guide the model parameters to one of several good local minima so that the final model parameters have an arbitrary watermark13.

The embedding procedure can be considered a binary classification problem, and the watermark can be fitted by adding a specific regularization term to the original learning task. Backdoor-based watermarks

Backdoor-based watermarks use a particular set of inputs as the triggers and let the model deliberately generate pre-defined incorrect labels as output9,16. At the training stage, the trigger set is collaboratively used with original dataset to train the FL global model, so that the backdoor trigger information is embedded naturally. The trigger set T={(XT1,YT1),...,(XTN,YTN)} is unique to the watermarked model, so only the embedded model has watermark detection ability while other models not. Therefore, the client can verify its ownership by triggering the misclassification using black-box mode by querying remote application programming interface (API) instead of model parameters. From copyright protection perspective,9 used the over parameterization of neural networks to design a robust backdoor watermarking algorithm, which became the foundation of watermarking scheme in deep neural network (DNN). WAFFLE28, the first watermarking solution in client-server FL scenario, allows trusted server to embed backdoor-based watermark by introducing a re-training step at the training stage. Reference26 proposed a client-side FL watermarking method under the homomorphic encryption (HE) FL framework with enhanced data privacy. FedIPR is a universal watermark scheme in FL scenario proposed in Ref.18 using both parameter-based watermark and backdoor-based watermark to claim legitimate intellectual property right (IPR) on client side. However, most of them rely on the assumption that the central server is completely trustworthy and ignore the problems posed by the single point of failure. Furthermore, the existing client-side FL watermarking scheme allowing clients to embed watermark is tantamount to accepting all sorts of model poisoning. Design and implementation

In this section, we propose a blockchain-empowered FL watermark framework for ownership verification. In this scenario, we assume there is a coordinator who initiates a FL task and wants to train a FL model to tackle with this task. The coordinator pays for the clients to collaboratively train the FL model, so it can be assumed to be credibly trustworthy because it doesn’t want to lose its costs. The clients embed their private watermarks into the model while training, and subsequently if an adversary steals or attacks the watermarked model, the client is entitled to initiate ownership verification process. The coordinator and the trainer exchange the watermarking process information through the blockchain, and the blockchain ensures the credibility of the process information, where any watermark forgery or watermark tampering can be effectively avoided.

As shown in Fig. 2, three different parties are involved in the blockchain-FL system: blockchain, clients, and coordinator.

Architecture of blockchain-empowered FL watermark framework: WFB.

Blockchain plays the role of a transparent and immutable information intermediary, as well as a trusted distributed ledger. The interaction between the participating nodes can be realized through data upload and download, and the watermark information is stored in the blockchain to trace the leaker and verify legitimate ownership.

Clients take the responsibility for training the FL model and embedding the private watermarks into the global model. In each communication round, clients download the global model parameters from the block and update their local models. The hash value of watermark information is uploaded to the relevant node, and the updated block is attached to the blockchain.

Coordinator is usually the initiator of the task, who plays the role of model aggregation in federated model training. In FL watermarking scenario, the coordinator also assists the clients to embed global watermark information. The interaction between the two parties can be realized through the blockchain to ensure the traceability of the process.

Ownership verification

After the model training phase, the watermark information has been embedded in the global model. Once the model is plagiarized by an unauthorized party, one client can verify its ownership by extracting the embedded watermarks. Experiment

Experiment settings

This subsection illustrates the experiment settings of the empirical study of our proposed framework.

Datasets and models. We use a well-known convolution neural networks (CNN) models: Alexnet to learn dataset Cifar10 for image classification task.

Watermark settings. The backdoor-based watermark is generated using Projected Gradient Descent(PGD), and the parameter-based watermark is generated by converting Hash(Task_ID,Client_ID) to a binary string. The string length is predefined, which is equal to that of the trigger size.

FL settings. To simulate the FL scenario, we set the default number of clients to 20, both the default number of backdoor trigger samples and length of parameter-based watermark to 16. The backdoor trigger samples are concatenated with original dataset Cifar10 to embed into the model while training, and the parameter-based watermarks are embedded into normalization scale weights of multiple convolution layers. In each iteration, clients train their local models for 2 local epochs by minimizing the total loss. The learning rate of clients is 0.01, momentum to 0.9, factor λ for parameter-based watermark loss to 0.01, and minibatch size to 4. We also assume that the distribution of the clients’ data follows independent identical distribution (i.i.d), which means the training dataset is uniformly allocated to each client. We trained for a total of 200 epochs, following the above settings.

Evaluation metrics

Following previous watermarking methods4,7,8,13,14 for FL model ownership protection, we apply evaluation metrics as below:

Fidelity. The proposed scheme is expected to have negligible impact on the primitive performance of the model. From this perspective, fidelity, also known as function preservation, works as an evaluation indicator to our proposed watermark scheme. The classification accuracy on the main task Accmain and the total loss LTotal is used to measure fidelity performance.

Watermark effectiveness. The proposed scheme is expected to successfully verify the ownership through extracting effective watermarks. Watermark effectiveness is evaluated by computing ownership verification level η^: the production of ηbackdoor and ηparameter.

Robustness. The robustness of this proposed scheme is evaluated for attacks such as trigger forgery, fine-tuning and pruning.

Fidelity

With the objective to minimize the impact on primitive classification task, we carry out experiment focusing on the accuracy of main task and watermarks. In the default experiment, we use Alexnet to learn dataset Cifar10, with the default number of clients to 50 and training epochs to 200. We first recorded training results of the first 50 epochs as shown in Fig. 6, and found that Accparameter was already very high (more than 90%) after 10 epochs, and increasing the epochs had little impact on Accparameter later on. Differently, as the training epochs increases, Accmain and Accbackdoor continued to rise, and it is noticed that Accmain rose faster than Accbackdoor. The reason for the differences in the three accuracy lines come from the differences of datasets. Their starting points are different because parameter-based watermark can be understood as a 0-1 binary classification problem, whereas both the original dataset and backdoor trigger set have ten classes. In addition, the trigger set generated by Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) algorithm is more complex than Cifar10, so Accbackdoor rises slower than Accmain.

Watermark robustness

Watermark robustness refers to the ability to resist the threats imposed to the FL model. We follow the prescriptive semi-honest assumption [32], which means any clients within the system and the adversaries outside the system might copy and steal the global model. The threats to FL model security mainly fall into two categories: external attacks and internal threats.

External attacks. FL models of high commercial value are exposed to risks including illegal copying or re-distribution through fine-tuning, pruning and other external attacks outside of federal system. Fine-tuning refers to the attempt of removing the watermark from the embedded model by re-training the model for a few iterations using a new dataset and a small learning rate. Pruning aims to remove the watermark from the trained model by setting some unimportant parameters to zero. Forgery attack tries to forge a watermark and verify the ownership of the existing watermarked model.

Internal threats. In federated learning, dishonest parties can pose threats to the security of the federated model. Ownership generalization means that malicious clients input their local watermark information into models that do not belong to them, making their ownership generalized through continuous learning.

In this proposed framework, internal threats are solved through on-chain watermark generation technology, so in this section, the watermark robustness is evaluated for the three external attack types: fine-tuning, pruning and watermark forgery attack.

Robustness against fine-tuning

Fine-tuning attack refers to retraining the model with their own dataset for some epochs, with the objective to erase the embedded watermark information. In this experiment, fine tuning is simulated by performing two training steps; the first step is to embed watermark information while training, and the second step is to re-train the embedded model with Cifar10 to determine whether the watermark embedded in the first training can resist the fine tuning attack and avoid to be erased. Conclusion

In this paper, we propose WFB, the blockchain-empowered FL watermarking framework for model copyright protection. To form a closed-loop watermarking framework for copyright protection, we use blockchain to assist FL watermarking techniques to further enhancing the model security. Three algorithms have been proposed in this framework, to realize on-chain watermark generation, watermark embedding while model training and watermark detection for ownership verification. It has been verified that WFB framework can effectively protect the ownership of FL under various experimental settings, and is robust to various attacks. This research paper comes to the conclusions that: First, existing parameter-based watermarks and backdoor-based watermarks have good compatibility, and the combination of the two watermark techniques has obvious gain for the copyright protection of FL model. Meanwhile, it is feasible to achieve a uniform and effective watermarking framework by establishing the direct correlation between the two. Furthermore, blockchain-empowered FL watermarking framework has excellent application potential. In future work, we will focus on dealing with non-iid dataset caused by covariant shift and concept drift and pay more attention to the application in complex real world.

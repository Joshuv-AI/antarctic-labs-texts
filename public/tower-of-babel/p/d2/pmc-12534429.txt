Design of tri-band bandpass filter based on the interdigital structure and rectangular resonator for LTE and radar applications
Authors: Sepehr Zarghami, Maryam Jahanbakhshi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This paper presents the design and implementation of a novel tri-band bandpass filter (BPF) using interdigital structures combined with rectangular resonators to achieve compactness and high performance. The proposed BPF effectively addresses challenges such as size reduction, high isolation between passbands, wide stopband and low insertion loss, which are critical for modern telecommunications applications. First, a single-band BPF is designed based on high-impedance coupled lines and selecting the best line values, then by adding resonators, the second and third bands are formed. The proposed filter is analyzed by an LC equivalent circuit. The filter achieves three distinct passbands at central frequencies of 2.12 GHz, 2.79 GHz, and 4.185 GHz. Key design features include the use of interdigital structures, high-impedance coupled lines and the creation of transmission zeros to improve sharpness and isolation between passbands. The proposed filter is fabricated and the good agreement of the measurement results with simulation results, making it suitable for applications in LTE, and radar applications. The compact size and efficient performance of the proposed BPF demonstrate its potential for integration into modern telecommunication devices, enhancing their overall efficiency and functionality.

Introduction

Microstrip filters play a crucial role in modern telecommunications, offering high performance, miniaturization, and ease of integration into various devices. Among these, tri-band bandpass filters (BPFs) are particularly significant due to their ability to handle multiple frequency bands simultaneously, making them indispensable for applications such as Long-Term Evolution (LTE) and radar systems. The development of tri-band BPFs can significantly enhance the efficiency and functionality of telecommunication systems by providing selective frequency filtering with minimal interference, ensuring reliable signal transmission and reception across multiple bands. In the design of tri-band BPFs, various parameters are checked, including passband widths, insertion and return losses and circuit size. But another important parameter is the isolation between the passbands, which has received less attention in many previous works.

The exploration of tri-band BPFs has been extensive, with various approaches documented in the literature. In1, a new class of switchable BPFs using PIN diodes was introduced, achieving multistate switchable filtering characteristics centered at 2.4, 3.5, and 5.2 GHz. The designed proposed in2 proposed a highly selective tri-band bandpass-to-bandstop filter with tunable third passband. One type of BPF is high selective filter, whose passbands are controllable by DC voltage and lumped elements. Although control of the passbands is an important advantage, other important filter parameters such as isolation and circuit size are neglected. In3–5, by adding similar structures, a tri-band BPFs is designed from a dual-band BPF. In3, uniform impedance resonators (UIRs) were used to create compact and efficient BPFs. By using defected ground structure (DGS) inter-band isolation is improved in4, and by using multipath-embedded resonators, the passbands are controllable in5. The circuit size reduction, which is an important parameter in circuit design, has been well addressed in6–10 works. However, the high insertion losses in the passband are clearly seen in these works. On the other hand, applications should also be considered, for example, reconfigurable tri-band BPF designs11,12 are very suitable for wideband and multifunctional systems. The study of13 combined signal-interference split-type microwave multi-band BPFs to enhance selectivity and bandwidth control. Other notable works include design of tri-band bandpass filters using uniform impedance resonators14, design of triple band power divider using triple coupled lines15 and multi-mode spoof surface plasmon polaritons16. Each of these studies contributed uniquely to the advancement of tri-band BPF technology, addressing various challenges such as size reduction, frequency tunability, and low insertion loss.

Despite the significant advancements in tri-band BPF design, several challenges remain. Many existing designs, such as those utilizing the SIRs structure, often suffer from large circuit sizes and complex fabrication processes17. Moreover, achieving independent tunability of each passband while maintaining high selectivity and low insertion loss has proven difficult. For instance, the designs in7] and [9 demonstrate excellent selectivity but are limited by their relatively large sizes and complex tuning mechanisms. Additionally, except for18, the stopbands and suppression of unwanted harmonics have not been considered in the reviewed works. These challenges highlight the need for innovative design approaches that can address these limitations while providing robust performance for modern telecommunications applications.

In19, a third-order dual-band bandpass filter was introduced based on λ/4 uniform impedance resonators (UIRs) with double-null-point loading to facilitate separate adjustment of the lower passband. Although the method yields high compactness and reconfigurability, it has suboptimal control over the upper passband frequency. Interestingly enough, this shortcoming also occurs in20, where a monoblock dielectric stub-loaded resonator filter delivers high selectivity and compact size but weak frequency separation between its two bands. To tackle size and stopband issues21, devised an ultra-compact filter with hybrid resonant modes in circular FSIW cavities. The design had a very good suppression level of unwanted harmonics. Nevertheless, like20, its fabrication procedure is complex because of cavity tuning with perturbations. Continuing from the concept of hybrid structures22, designed a dual-narrowband balanced BPF on the basis of meta-HRRs with very controllable passbands. While the design provides enhanced tunability, it has high dimensional accuracy dependence and hence a susceptibility to fabrication defects. An alternate approach to enhancing stopband performance and integration was taken in23, whereby a multiband BPF was implemented utilizing modified folded substrate-integrated rectangular cavities. While this design greatly expands the stopband and minimizes size in general, bandwidths in the various bands cannot be independently controlled — a shortcoming again restricting flexibility. This limitation is even more pronounced in24, in which a tri-band filter was designed utilizing capacitively-loaded intertwined helical resonators. While this configuration facilitates increased miniaturization and integration, complex inter-resonator coupling phenomena result in design predictability challenges. Integration challenges were also met in25, where a double-band reflectionless filter/filtenna design was proposed with SIW-based complementary diplexer structures. Although suitable for integrated filter-antenna systems, this architecture remains hard to scale up to tri- or quads based solutions owing to architectural stiffness. A fabrication-oriented path was taken in26, where 3D metal printing was employed to develop coupled resonators with dual modes. Although this facilitates quicker prototyping, the derived filters will have smaller Q-factors owing to surface roughness — flexural performance tradeoff. Regarding large frequency ratios handling27, developed a dual-band BPF consisting of a mode composite ridged coaxial line. The widely spaced bands are successfully supported by this configuration; however, the inter-band isolation suffers from moderate issues, which can impair signal integrity. Finally28, presented a quad-band filter with an isotype n-Si/n-Bi₂O₃ heterojunction appropriate for 5G and 6G. With the ability to support passband and rejection modes, this device also shows high cutoff drift and poor in-band flatness, which means there remains a performance consistency concern for microwave filters based on semiconductors at high-frequency levels.

In this regard, this paper proposed a tri-band BPF, which addresses these challenges by leveraging an interdigital structure combined with rectangular resonators to achieve compactness and high performance. The proposed filter is based on interdigital structures, which alone is a narrow bandpass filter. Then, by adding rectangular resonators, the second passband is created. Further, in order to increase the roll-off, second rectangular resonators with a long base line have been added. Finally, by adding high-impedance coupling lines, the third passband is created. The LC equivalent circuit and the use of even and odd mode analysis have been chosen to check and analyze the proposed filter.

Design process of tri-band BPF

The overall design process of the tri-BPF involves a detailed examination of its various components, with the role of each part in the overall structure determined based on the final response of the filter.

First, by using two interdigital structures in front of each other, a single-band BPF filter is designed, as shown in Fig. 3. Each interdigital structure has six high-impedance lines, the width of each line and the distance between them are 0.1 mm. The distance between two interdigital structures is also 0.1 mm, which creates coupling effect. According to the simulated results in Fig. 3, this structure has a transmission pole at the frequency 2.78 GHz which is the center frequency of the passband.

The proposed tri-band BPF is fabricated on a RO4003 substrate with a thickness of 20 mil, a dielectric coefficient of 3.38 and a loss tangent of 0.0022. Experimental results and discussion

High frequency Network analyzer measures utilized to measure fabricated BPF. The frequency range of this device is 30 kHz to 18 GHz. The measurement results and comparison with the simulation results are depicted in Fig. 11.

Fig. 11The simulation and measurement results, a) s-parameters, b) group delays.

The simulation and measurement results, a) s-parameters, b) group delays.

The measurement results have demonstrated that the proposed BPF can achieve good tri-band bandpass performance with superior frequency selectivity. Through simulation, tri-band BPF with center frequencies of 2.12, 2.79, and 4.185 GHz are realized, which can be used in 4 G system, 5G communication system, and wireless local area network (WLAN), respectively. Figure 11 shows simulated and measured results of the fabricated tri-band BPF. According to Fig. 11(a), the measurement of s-parameters indicates that the first passband has a center frequency of 2.12 GHz, insertion loss |S21|>1.57 dB, return loss |S11|>18.57 dB, 3 dB FBW of 5.4%; the second passband has a center frequency of 2.79 GHz, insertion loss |S21|>1.43 dB, return loss |S11|>23.85 dB, and 3 dB FBW of 7.6%; the third passband has a center frequency of 4.185 GHz, insertion loss |S21| less than 1.72 dB, the return loss |S11| greater than 18.33 dB and 3 dB FBW of 3.5%. It is also clear from the results that the proposed filter has wide lower and upper stopbands. So that the frequency range of lower stopband is from DC to 1.57 GHz and the frequency range of upper stopband is from 4.4 to 9.5 GHz frequency, with a suppression level of 20 dB. On the other hand, considering a suppression level of 13 dB, the upper stopband of the filter is from 4.4 GHz to 18 GHz which includes an ultra-wide stopband bandwidth that has not been seen in previous works. The sharp attenuation at 4.8–5 GHz and increased suppression level in the measured results are mainly due to measurement-related losses, including substrate, connector, and cable losses at higher frequencies. Also, the slight degradation of S11 in the measured results, is mainly caused by port-related mismatches due to the short matching lines and coupling between SMA connectors and resonators. Importantly, that the increase in return loss in the filter stopbands is not considered a detrimental effect and does not cause interference. These discrepancies are common in microstrip filters and do not undermine the design’s performance for LTE/radar applications.

As shown in Fig. 11(b), the group delay response of the proposed filter is illustrated within the three passbands. The maximum variations are 1.02 ns, 1.14 ns, and 1.24 ns for the first, second, and third passbands, respectively. These values indicate that the group delay remains flat across all three passbands, confirming good performance of the filter in terms of signal distortion. The simulation and the measurement has a close consistent, thus verifying the design concept.

Conclusion

The design of the proposed tri-band bandpass filter demonstrates significant advancements in compactness and performance, addressing key challenges in the field of telecommunications. By leveraging interdigital structures and rectangular resonators, the filter achieves high selectivity and low insertion loss across three distinct passbands. The implementation of high-impedance coupled lines and transmission zeros enhances isolation and sharpness, ensuring reliable signal transmission and reception. Analyses based on high-impedance coupled lines and the LC equivalent circuit have played a very important role in the design of the proposed filter. This design approach not only reduces the overall size of the filter but also simplifies the fabrication process. The proposed BPF’s versatility and robustness make it a valuable component for LTE and radar applications, providing a pathway for further innovations in telecommunication filter design.

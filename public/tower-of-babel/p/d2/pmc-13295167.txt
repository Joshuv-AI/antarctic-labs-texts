Gonarthrosis Advisor vs ChatGPT-5: quality and readability of artificial intelligence–generated patient education for knee osteoarthritis
Authors: Süleyman Kaan Öner, Nihat Demirhan Demirkiran, Enes Alptekin Canlı, Arda Bilir
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:The primary objective of this study is to compare the quality and readability of patient education materials generated by a general-purpose large language model (ChatGPT-5) versus a guideline-based, fine-tuned model (the Gonarthrosis Advisor). The study aims to quantify the performance gains achieved through domain-specific, fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning using osteoarthritis clinical guidelines.Methods:Thirty frequently asked patient questions regarding knee osteoarthritis were compiled from Google’s “People Also Ask” feature and outpatient clinical observations in May 2025. Responses were generated in Turkish by both the Gonarthrosis Advisor and ChatGPT-5 to reflect real-world patient education materials.Content quality was assessed by 2 independent orthopedic surgeons who were blinded to model identity to minimize bias. Both reviewers were co-authors of the article yet did not participate in model construction or data analysis. The assessments utilized the DISCERN instrument, a validated 16-item measure for evaluating the reliability and quality of treatment-related information. Readability was analyzed using the Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level (FKGL), Flesch Reading Ease Score (FRES), and Turkish specific indices (Ateşman and Çakır-Demir). For comparability, English-based indices were applied to translated versions of the responses, whereas Turkish indices were applied to the original texts.All responses were anonymized and randomized prior to evaluation. Model identifiers were removed, and each response was presented in a standardized format to ensure blinding of reviewers. Inter-rater reliability was measured using Cronbach’s α. Normality assumptions were tested, and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests were used for statistical comparisons. As no human subjects or personal data were involved, ethical approval was not required.Results:Mean DISCERN scores corresponded to the “good” category (66.4) for the Gonarthrosis Advisor and the ‘moderate’ category (54.2) for ChatGPT-5, according to established cut-off thresholds. The Gonarthrosis Advisor achieved significantly higher DISCERN scores than ChatGPT-5 (66.4 ± 4.8 vs. 54.2 ± 5.6; P < .001) with high inter-rater reliability (Cronbach’s α = 0.86). Readability metrics favored the Gonarthrosis Advisor across all indices: lower FKGL (7.8 ± 0.7 vs. 9.6 ± 0.9) and higher FRES (54.3 ± 3.4 vs. 46.7 ± 3.7), Ateşman (92.0 ± 4.2 vs. 84.3 ± 4.9), and Çakır-Demir (111.7 ± 5.1 vs. 106.9 ± 5.4) scores (all P <.001).Conclusion:Fine-tuning large language models with guideline-based content and reinforcement learning improves the quality, neutrality, and accessibility of artificial intelligence–generated patient education materials, offering a scalable tool to enhance health literacy and support shared decision-making in knee osteoarthritis care.Level of Evidence: Level III, Diagnostic Study.

Introduction

Knee osteoarthritis is a chronic and progressive joint disorder marked by pain, functional impairment, and a reduced quality of life. Epidemiological data indicate that the prevalence of symptomatic knee osteoarthritis in adults aged 60 and older is approximately 10% in men and 13% in women.1 Lifetime risk assessments suggest that around 14% of the U.S. population will be diagnosed with symptomatic knee osteoarthritis, with the risk rising to roughly 24% in obese women.2

Initial management generally involves conservative measures, including physical therapy, weight loss, orthotic devices, and intra-articular injections. Surgical interventions, including total knee arthroplasty (TKA) and unicompartmental knee arthroplasty, are frequently employed in advanced cases.3 Despite the overall effectiveness of TKA, a notable percentage of patients continue to express dissatisfaction. Bourne et al indicated discontent rates of 19% after initial TKA; however, a recent systematic study by DeFrance and Scuderi revealed that contemporary cohorts show dissatisfaction rates averaging 10%-12%.3,4 Long-term follow-up statistics further corroborate that a segment of patients persists in experiencing unsatisfactory outcomes even 5 years post surgery.5

Shared decision-making and high-quality patient education remain critical for aligning patient expectations with treatment realities and improving satisfaction and adherence.6 However, many patient education materials (PEMs), including those produced by leading orthopedic societies, exceed the recommended 6th–8th grade reading level, which undermines accessibility for elderly individuals and those with limited health literacy.7

Advances in artificial intelligence (AI) have introduced large language models (LLMs) as promising tools for delivering personalized health information. Nonetheless, general-purpose LLMs may produce too technical information, lack clear citations, and frequently diverge from evidence-based recommendations. A recent evaluation of LLMs in patient care identified several shortcomings, including insufficient optimization for the medical domain, lack of data openness, and the potential for erroneous, incomplete, or biased outputs. These issues must be addressed to ensure secure and effective clinical application. Domain-specific fine-tuning via clinical guidelines has the potential to enhance precision, clarity, and relevance. This study aims to ascertain if guideline-based fine-tuning significantly enhances the accuracy, clarity, and readability of AI-generated patient education materials. ChatGPT-5 exemplifies the present capabilities of a general-purpose large language model, whereas the Gonarthrosis Advisor was explicitly trained utilizing the osteoarthritis guidelines from the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons (AAOS), American College of Rheumatology (ACR), and Osteoarthritis Research Society International (OARSI). The objective of this study is to directly compare ChatGPT-5 with a guideline-optimized model to measure the extent of enhancement attainable by specialized fine-tuning.

Material and methods

Study design and overview

This study was designed as a cross-sectional comparative content analysis evaluating the readability, accuracy, and source reliability of patient education responses generated by 2 artificial intelligence language models. The models compared were (1) ChatGPT-5, a general-purpose large language model used without any medical fine-tuning and (2) the Gonarthrosis Advisor, a customized knee osteoarthritis–specific model developed through a structured fine-tuning pipeline. Both models were evaluated using identical input prompts and standardized scoring instruments.

Question collection and eligibility

Patient questions related to knee osteoarthritis were collected from 2 sources: (1) Google’s “People Also Ask” (PAA) feature and (2) anonymized outpatient clinical observations. On May 7, 2025, the search terms “knee osteoarthritis,” “knee osteoarthritis treatment,” and “knee pain treatment” were manually entered into Google using a standard web browser without personalized search settings. A reproducible base search URL is provided (https://www.google.com/search), and no personalized search settings were used.

Outpatient questions were documented during routine clinical visits as non-identifiable free-text notes by the treating physician, without names, demographic information, imaging findings, or any other unique clinical details. All outpatient questions were therefore fully anonymized, and no ethical approval was required.

After screening, a final set of 30 unique, high-frequency questions was established, representing both online and real-world patient interests. All questions were rewritten to approximately the U.S. eighth-grade reading level to ensure accessibility and alignment with established health literacy recommendations. Both the National Institutes of Health (NIH) and the American Medical Association (AMA) recommend preparing patient education materials at the sixth- to-eighth-grade reading level to support comprehension and informed decision-making.6, 9 The rewriting process was performed independently by 2 orthopedic surgeons to ensure consistency and avoid subjective bias.

Data collection and scoring

On May 7, 2025, both models were prompted with the same 30 standardized patient questions (Table 1). Responses were exported into Microsoft Word documents, and all hyperlinks and references were removed prior to analysis to avoid bias in readability scoring. Two independent orthopedic surgeons with expertise in knee disorders, blinded to model identity and not involved in model development, evaluated each response using the DISCERN instrument, a validated 16-item tool for assessing the quality and reliability of written patient treatment information (score range: 16-80; higher = better quality). 17

Sample size determination and reporting standards

The primary outcome of interest in this study was the difference in mean DISCERN total scores between the Gonarthrosis Advisor and ChatGPT-5 for each standardized patient question. Before data collection, an a priori power analysis using G*Power 3.1 was performed. The analysis was specified for a 2-tailed paired-samples t-test, assuming a medium effect size (Cohen’s d = 0.50), a type I error rate of α = 0.05, and desired power of 1−β = 0.80.

The assumed medium effect size was chosen to be consistent with the magnitude of between-group differences in DISCERN scores reported in previous evaluations of online musculoskeletal and knee osteoarthritis–related patient-education content.19,20 This a priori calculation indicated that a minimum of 27 paired question–response items would be sufficient. Therefore 30 unique, high-frequency questions were used, which provided slightly more than the required sample size and is in line with prior OA patient-education content analyses that typically evaluated 20-40 questions per condition.19, 20

Readability metrics

Each response was analyzed using validated readability indices: FRES (optimal ≥ 60),13 FKGL (optimal ≤ grade 8),14 Ateşman (higher = easier Turkish readability),15 and Çakır–Demir (≥100 = optimal Turkish readability).16 Original Turkish texts were evaluated using the Ateşman and Çakır–Demir indices, while English translations were evaluated using FRES and FKGL. This dual-index approach allowed standardized cross-language comparison of text accessibility for both Turkish- and English-speaking patient audiences.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS v29. Inter-rater reliability was assessed using Cronbach’s α. Group comparisons between models were conducted using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, due to non-parametric paired data structure. Statistical significance was set at P ≤ .05.

As this study did not involve human subjects or identifiable patient information, ethical approval was not required.

Results

DISCERN quality evaluation

Analysis of the 30 standardized patient questions demonstrated that the Gonarthrosis Advisor achieved significantly higher mean DISCERN scores compared with ChatGPT-5 (66.4 ± 4.8 vs. 54.2 ± 5.6; P <.001) (Figure 2). According to established DISCERN thresholds, this corresponded to “good” quality for the Gonarthrosis Advisor and “moderate” quality for ChatGPT-5. Inter-rater reliability across the 2 independent reviewers was excellent (Cronbach’s α = 0.86). Domain-specific scoring indicated higher performance of the fine-tuned model in clarity of explanations, reference to evidence-based sources, balanced discussion of treatment options, emphasis on conservative management, and support for shared decision-making. A full question-by-question comparison of DISCERN scores is presented in Table 1, and summary comparative statistics are provided in Table 2.

Readability analysis

Readability assessment demonstrated significantly lower mean FKGL scores for the Gonarthrosis Advisor compared with ChatGPT-5 (7.8 ± 0.7 vs. 9.6 ± 0.9; P < .001), indicating simpler sentence structure. The FRES was higher for the Gonarthrosis Advisor (54.3 ± 3.4 vs. 46.7 ± 3.7; P < .001). Turkish-specific readability indices showed similar patterns, with higher Ateşman (92.0 ± 4.2 vs. 84.3 ± 4.9; P < .001) and Çakır-Demir scores (111.7 ± 5.1 vs. 106.9 ± 5.4; P < .001) for the fine-tuned model. Discussion

This study demonstrated that a domain-specific fine-tuned LLM, the Gonarthrosis Advisor, substantially outperformed a general-purpose LLM (ChatGPT-5) in generating patient education materials for knee osteoarthritis. By incorporating evidence-based guideline content (AAOS, ACR, OARSI), IBFT, and RLHF, the Gonarthrosis Advisor achieved significantly higher quality (DISCERN) and readability scores across multiple standardized metrics in both English and Turkish. Overall, the fine-tuned Gonarthrosis Advisor consistently produced responses that were more accurate, comprehensive, and readable than those of the general-purpose ChatGPT-5 model.

The Gonarthrosis Advisor represents, to the current knowledge, the first osteoarthritis-focused, guideline-trained, Turkish-language patient education model developed using structured fine-tuning and reinforcement learning from human feedback. By incorporating AAOS, ACR, and OARSI guideline content through instruction-gased fine-tuning and iterative clinician revisions, the model achieved significantly higher DISCERN scores and superior readability across both English and Turkish language metrics.

Moreover, recent systematic and scoping reviews have highlighted both the potential and the limitations of LLMs in patient education, particularly regarding content accuracy, transparency, and clinical applicability.8,18 19 In addition, cross-sectional studies have shown that LLMs can significantly improve the readability of online patient materials while maintaining linguistic simplicity.

Prior research evaluating general-purpose AI models in musculoskeletal health has consistently identified limitations in accuracy, interpretability, and linguistic accessibility. For instance, Rouhi et al showed that while ChatGPT could simplify cardiovascular patient materials from a 12th-grade to a 7th-grade reading level, it still failed to meet health literacy recommendations set by the AMA and the NIH. 20 Similarly, Kirchner et al reported that AI-rewritten orthopedic materials frequently lacked source citation and presented overly technical language, reducing their educational value for patients.19

The findings further support the growing body of evidence showing that domain-specific fine-tuning grounded in guideline-based medical content and expert feedback enhances both the linguistic simplicity and clinical reliability of AI-generated materials. These results are consistent with studies on specialized models such as DocOA, which demonstrated improved clinical decision support, greater interpretability, and superior content neutrality compared with general-purpose LLMs.21,22

Health literacy directly influences treatment adherence, decision-making capacity, and clinical outcomes.20,23,24 Most patient education materials are written at or above the tenth-grade reading level, exceeding the recommended sixth- to eighth-grade standard for public health communications.7,20This gap is particularly problematic for elderly individuals and those with limited educational backgrounds.25 Recent cross-sectional evidence further highlights the importance of improving the readability and personalization of patient education materials: Amin et al demonstrated that LLMs can enhance health literacy by simplifying terminology and restructuring information for clarity, although these effects depend on domain-specific optimization and clinical alignment.26

In this study, the Gonarthrosis Advisor consistently produced content aligning with or approaching recommended readability thresholds across multiple indices: FKGL, FRES, Ateşman, and Çakır-Demir scores. These improvements are not merely linguistic; they are clinically meaningful because simplified, guideline-consistent information has been shown to enhance informed consent quality, patient satisfaction, and adherence to treatment regimens.12,27

Moreover, the model’s balanced representation of surgical and non-surgical interventions mitigates concerns regarding commercial or therapeutic bias, which have been noted in previous analyses of online musculoskeletal health content.28 By integrating evidence-based risk–benefit discussions, the Gonarthrosis Advisor supports shared decision-making, a cornerstone of modern patient-centered care.

This investigation employed a rigorous methodological framework encompassing several key strengths. First, question selection was standardized to accurately reflect real-world patient information needs. Second, content quality was assessed through blinded multi-rater evaluations using the DISCERN instrument, yielding excellent inter-rater reliability (Cronbach’s α = 0.86). Third, multiple international and language-specific readability metrics were applied to comprehensively evaluate linguistic accessibility. Finally, robust non-parametric statistical analyses, including Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, were used to ensure appropriate handling of data distributions. Collectively, these methodological elements enhance both the validity and reproducibility of the findings.

The findings add to the growing evidence that guideline-based fine-tuning and clinician feedback substantially enhance both readability and clinical reliability of AI-generated patient education materials. Compared with the general-purpose ChatGPT-5, the Gonarthrosis Advisor consistently produced more accessible and clinically aligned information across multiple standardized assessment tools. These findings highlight the potential of domain-specific AI systems to enhance health literacy, support shared decision-making, and improve communication in musculoskeletal care. Future research integrating multimodal outputs, direct patient feedback, and real-world clinical implementation will be essential to fully realize the clinical impact of such technologies.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, only 2 AI models were evaluated; inclusion of additional general-purpose (e.g., Bard, Claude) and domain-specific LLMs would provide a more comprehensive benchmark. Second, although DISCERN and multiple readability indices offer robust textual assessment, the study did not evaluate patient-centered outcomes such as comprehension, satisfaction, behavioral change, or clinical effectiveness—key determinants of real-world impact. Third, the analysis reflects performance at a single point in time; given the rapid evolution of LLMs, longitudinal evaluation will be required to assess stability, drift, and generalizability across future model iterations and different linguistic or cultural environments.

Future directions

Future work should include prospective or randomized clinical evaluations to measure patient comprehension, satisfaction, adherence, and shared decision-making when using AI-generated materials. Implementation studies integrating the Gonarthrosis Advisor into outpatient workflows, mobile applications, and telemedicine platforms would help determine its real-world usability and clinical value. Furthermore, expanding the system to multimodal formats such as visual infographics, interactive decision aids, or audio explanations may enhance accessibility for individuals with limited literacy or sensory impairments.

Plain language summary

Knee osteoarthritis, also known as knee “wear-and-tear,” is one of the most common causes of knee pain in adults and older people. Patients often search for information online, but many websites use complex medical terms that are hard to understand. Recently, artificial intelligence (AI) systems such as ChatGPT have been used to answer health questions, but these models are not always accurate or easy to read.

In this study, 2 AI systems: ChatGPT-5, a general tool, and the Gonarthrosis Advisor, a model specially trained with medical guidelines for knee osteoarthritis was compared. The same 30 common patient questions were asked for both models, such as “What is knee osteoarthritis?” and “Will losing weight help my knee pain?” Their answers were reviewed for quality and readability.

The Gonarthrosis Advisor produced answers that were clearer, more reliable, and closer to the recommended reading level for health information. It also explained treatment options more fairly, gave information consistent with international guidelines, and avoided difficult technical language.

This means that AI systems trained with medical guidelines and expert feedback may become useful tools for improving patient education. Such tools could help patients better understand their condition, participate in shared decision-making, and feel more confident about their treatment choices.

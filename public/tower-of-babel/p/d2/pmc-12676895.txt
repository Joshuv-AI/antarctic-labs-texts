The relation between postoperative cognitive disorders and brain damage biomarkers after major urologic surgery: a prospective cohort study
Authors: Emre Şentürk, Zerrin Sungur, Hayriye Şentürk Çiftçi, Metin İlteray Kıkılı, Mehmet Öner Şanlı, Demet Kıvanç, Emre Sertaç Bingül, Mert Canbaz, Rıfat Burak Ergül, Meltem Savran Karadeniz
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPostoperative neurocognitive disorders (PNDs) are highly prevalent among the elderly population. Identifying an ideal biomarker for postoperative delirium (POD) and postoperative cognitive dysfunction (POCD) remains a significant challenge. This study aimed to investigate the relationship between these syndromes and various biomarkers, including S-100β, neuron-specific enolase (NSE), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and high-mobility group box-1 (HMGB-1).MethodsThe study included patients aged 60 years and older who underwent surgery for primary urologic malignancies. POD was evaluated in the recovery room using the Confusion Assessment Method (CAM). POCD was assessed preoperatively and at the first week (POCD1) and third month (POCD3) postoperatively using Addenbrooke’s Cognitive Examination-III (ACE-III). Biomarker levels of S-100β, neuron-specific enolase (NSE), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and high-mobility group box-1 (HMGB-1) were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA).ResultsA total of 54 patients completed the study. POD was diagnosed in 12 patients (22.2%), while POCD1 and POCD3 were identified in 17 (31.5%) patients and 12 (22.2%) patients respectively. The mean age of the participants was 73.8 ± 7.1 years. Preoperative levels of NSE were significantly higher in patients with POD compared to those without (p = 0.04). Additionally, preoperative and postoperative IL-6 levels, as well as preoperative and postoperative S-100β levels, were significantly elevated in patients with POCD1 compared to those without (p < 0.01, p = 0.01, p < 0.01, and p = 0.03, respectively). Furthermore, preoperative IL-6 and S-100β levels were higher in patients with POCD3 compared to those without (p = 0.01 and p = 0.03, respectively).ConclusionsIn major urologic oncologic surgeries among geriatric patients, IL-6 and S-100β were found to be associated with both POD and POCD, while NSE was specifically associated with POD.

Introduction

Postoperative neurocognitive disorders (PNDs) encompass postoperative delirium (POD) and postoperative cognitive dysfunction (POCD). Among elderly patients, the incidence of POCD ranges from 9 to 54% at one week postoperatively and 10% to 17% at 3 months, while the incidence of POD is reported to be 20% to 45% (Safavynia and Goldstein 2018; Daiello et al. 2019). Despite sharing many common features, it remains unclear whether POD and POCD represent the same condition or two distinct entities (Daiello et al. 2019). Regardless of this distinction, PNDs are associated with significant morbidity and mortality (Safavynia and Goldstein 2018; Daiello et al. 2019).

Advanced age and major oncologic surgery are critical risk factors for PNDs (Androsova et al. 2015). Urologic malignancies, including kidney, bladder, and prostate cancers, predominantly occur between the ages of 60 and 80 years and rank as the second, sixth, and ninth most common cancers, respectively (McKibben and Smith 2015; Sato et al. 2016).

Biomarkers related to neuronal injury and neuroinflammation have been evaluated in studies seeking to understand the pathophysiology of POCD and POD (Androsova et al. 2015). Although these studies have yielded conflicting results, it is evident that these complex, multifactorial syndromes cannot be fully explained by a limited number of biomarkers (Liu et al. 2018). Nonetheless, these investigations provide valuable insights into the underlying mechanisms of these conditions (Liu et al. 2018). Such as, Glumac et al. demonstrated that preoperative administration of dexamethasone attenuates the surgery-induced inflammatory response, thereby reducing the incidence and severity of early POCD, suggesting that modulation of neuroinflammation may help prevent POCD (7).

Other principal subjects about POD and POCD are cerebral oxygenation and postoperative complications. Studies conducted in both cardiac and non-cardiac surgeries have reported inconsistent findings regarding the relationship between cerebral hypoxia and the development of POD and POCD (Zorrilla-Vaca et al. 2018; Serraino and Murphy 2017). However, numerous studies have demonstrated that postoperative complications are strongly associated with both POD and POCD (Meyer et al. 2021; Rundshagen 2014).

Despite these findings, the relationship between POD, POCD, and these factors has not been extensively studied in older patients undergoing major urologic oncologic surgery. The aim of our study was to investigate the association of POD and POCD with neuronal injury biomarkers, such as S-100β and neuron-specific enolase (NSE), as well as neuroinflammation biomarkers, including interleukin-6 (IL-6) and high-mobility group box protein-1 (HMGB-1), in this patient population. We hypothesized that 6-h postoperative levels of IL-6, S-100β, NSE, and HMGB-1 would be higher in patients who develop postoperative delirium (POD) and postoperative cognitive dysfunction (POCD) at 1 week and 3 months compared to those who do not.

Materials and methods

Study design and patients

After obtaining Institutional Ethics Committee approval (2019/1555) and written informed consent from the patients, the current prospective observational study was conducted between January 2020 and May 2021. The study was registered with the ClinicalTrials.gov registry (Registration number: NCT04837391).

Patients aged over 60 years scheduled for radical prostatectomy, radical cystectomy, radical nephrectomy, nephroureterectomy or partial nephrectomy with a diagnosis of primary urologic malignancy were included in the study. The exclusion criteria of the study were as follows: (a) refusal to participate in study, (b) the presence of severe hearing and vision impairment, (c) the presence of severe neurologic and psychiatric disease, (d) the absence of a common language that could interfere with the administration of neuropsychological tests, and (e) illiteracy.

Data collection and patient management

Demographic data, planned surgery, medical history, and surgical history data were recorded. American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical condition classification, Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI, weighted scoring system based on the presence of comorbid diseases), and the Clinical Frailty Scale (CFS, level 1–3: non-frail, level 4–9: frail) were evaluated (Pugh et al. 2016). Then, the Confusion Assessment Method (CAM, the diagnosis of delirium requires the presence of both acute mental status change and inattention and either the presence of disorganized thinking or altered level of consciousness) and Addenbrooke’s Cognitive Examination-III (ACE-III; attention and orientation: 18, memory: 26, fluency: 14, language: 26, visuospatial: 16, total: 100 points) were used to evaluate the cognitive performances of the patients (Beishon et al. 2019; Khan et al. 2017).

No premedication was administered to the patients. Blood samples were obtained in the premedication room. In the operating room, standard ASA monitors were used and two cerebral oximetry sensors (INVOS™ Cerebral/Somatic Oximetry Adult Sensors) were placed in the frontal region before induction of anesthesia. Anesthesia was induced intravenously with 2 µg/kg of fentanyl, 2 mg/kg of propofol, and 0.1 mg/kg of vecuronium. Anesthesia was maintained with sevoflurane (0.8–1, minimum alveolar concentration), oxygen/air (40%/60%), and 0.1 µg/kg/min of remifentanil infusion.

Intraoperative norepinephrine requirement, surgical duration, perioperative transfusion requirement, postoperative intensive care unit (ICU) requirement, length of hospital stay, and requirement of reoperation were recorded as perioperative outcomes.

Postoperative analgesia was planned based on the patient’s clinical status and the type of surgery. Patient-controlled analgesia (PCA) was administered either intravenously or via epidural route as appropriate. The analgesic protocol aimed to maintain a postoperative visual analog scale (VAS) score < 4 in all patients.

Outcome measures

Postoperative delirium

Postoperative delirium was evaluated in the recovery room or the ICU after the surgery. After the patients were extubated, the Richmond Sedation-Agitation Scale (RASS, combative: + 4, unarousable: − 5) was evaluated (Khan et al. 2017). Postoperative delirium was assessed using CAM: patients were assessed as ≥ − 3 according to RASS (movement, eye opening to voice).

Postoperative cognitive dysfunction

Cognitive assessments were performed by a physician who was blinded to the other study components. ACE-III was applied three times in total, preoperatively, postoperatively in the 1st week (POCD1) (or hospital discharge), and in the postoperative 3rd month (POCD3) (postoperative 75–105 days). The mean learning effect of a control group based on changes from preoperative tests was subtracted before individual z-scores were calculated. Patients with a z-score > 1.96 were considered as having POCD.

Regional cerebral oxygenation

Before induction of anesthesia, values read 1 min after monitoring were considered baseline regional cerebral oxygen saturation (rScO2) and marked as an event on the monitor. The INVOS™ Monitoring System Analytics Tool software was used to obtain rScO2. The mean rScO2, minimum rScO2, and maximum rScO2 of the patients were also recorded. A 20% decrease from baseline and 50% as an exact value on the monitor were accepted as the threshold. A continuous graphic of rScO2 was obtained using the software and the thresholds were plotted as a horizontal line. The software calculated the area under curve (AUC) (min %) for each threshold using the time (min) and severity (%) data below this horizontal line. Perioperative complications

Patients were followed up according to the Clavien-Dindo classification of surgical complications until the cognitive performance evaluation at the 3th month (grade 0: no complications, grades 1–2: minor complications, and grades 3–5: major complications) (Dindo et al. 2004).

Blood sample collection and analysis

The blood samples were collected immediately before induction of anesthesia and at the postoperative 6th hours. After centrifuging at 3500 rpm for 10 min, the serum samples were stored at − 80°C for future use. The serum levels of IL-6, HMGB-1, NSE, and S-100β protein were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) following the manufactures instructions (Diaclone Company, Abbkine, Labor Diagnostika Nord, BioVendor Research, respectively).

Preoperative blood sampling was performed to assess each patient’s baseline neuroinflammatory state in the context of malignancy (Ruhnau et al. 2023). Postoperative biomarker sampling was carried out at the 6th hour following surgery, as this time point offers a practical and biologically meaningful window to detect early postoperative changes. Existing Literature suggests that systemic inflammatory and neuronal responses typically emerge and may reach near-peak levels within the first 6 to 12 h after major surgical interventions. Although individual biomarkers differ in their kinetics, the 6-h mark was deemed appropriate for capturing this early, dynamic phase of physiological response (Li et al. 2012; Peng et al. 2013; Lin et al. 2014; Johnsson et al. 2000).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS version 21.0, Chicago, IL, USA) software package. Categorical data are expressed as the number and percentage of cases. These were compared using the Chi-square test and Fisher’s exact test. The normality of the distribution of numerical data was evaluated by calculating kurtosis and skewness values. Data with a normal distribution are expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Normally distributed data were compared using the independent samples t test. Heterogeneously distributed data are expressed as the median (25th percentile-75th percentile). These were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. Spearman’s correlation analysis was performed to examine the relationship between POD, POCD1, and POCD3. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was performed to evaluate the sensitivity and specificity of the biomarkers that were found to be significantly different between the groups. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

In a previous study, the incidence of POCD in the first week was found as 25.8% (Moller et al. 1998). According to the results of another study, POCD ( +) patients and POCD ( −) patients were compared, the effect size of the IL-6 difference was calculated as 1.44 (17). If α = 0.05 and 1-β = 0.95 were accepted, it was revealed that at least 36 patients should be included in the study for a significant difference between the two groups in terms of IL-6 in the unpaired t test. It was planned to include at least 45 patients in the study, considering that 20% of the data might be lost.

Additionally, a post-hoc power analysis was conducted for the comparison of preoperative NSE levels between the POD (n = 12) and non-POD (n = 42) groups. Although the result was statistically significant (Z = − 1.987, p = 0.04), the calculated effect size (Cohen’s d = 0.56) corresponded to a statistical power (1–β) of 0.39. This indicates a limited ability to detect true effects in this subgroup and highlights the risk of Type II error in POD-related comparisons.

Results

Patient enrollment and baseline characteristics

A total of 65 patients who were scheduled for major urologic oncologic surgery were assessed for eligibility. Six patients were excluded from the study in the eligibility assessment period, two patients refused to participate in the study, and three patients were excluded due to loss to follow-up; 54 patients completed the study. Demographic and clinical factors associated with POD and POCD

The patients with POD (77.8 ± 7.5 years) were older than patients without POD (72.6 ± 6.6 years) (p < 0.02). Duration of education was shorter in patients with POD (4.83 ± 13 years) than for patients without POD (8 ± 4.6 years) (p < 0.001). In patients with POD, frailty incidence was higher compared with patients without POD (28.6% vs. 66.7%, p = 0.04).

The POCD development rate at week 1 was higher in nephroureterectomy (17.6% vs. 2.7%) and radical nephrectomy (29.4% vs. 8.1%) compared with radical prostatectomy (11.8%, 45%) in post-hoc analysis using Bonferroni correction. The incidence of POCD at week 1 and month 3 were higher in patients who were ASA 3 than in patients who were ASA 1–2 (POCD1, p = 0.04; POCD, p = 0.02).

In patients with POCD at month 3, the incidence of frailty (p < 0.001), coronary artery disease (p = 0.02), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) (p = 0.02), polypharmacy (p = 0.04), previous major surgery (p < 0.01), the requirement of ICU care (p = 0.04), and incidence of postoperative major complications (p < 0.01) were higher compared with patients without POCD at month 3. Biomarker levels and their association with POD and POCD

Only preoperative NSE [3.74 (3.6–3.93) ng/mL vs. 4.01 (3.7–5.12) ng/mL] levels were significantly higher in patients with POD compared with those without POD (p = 0.04). Preoperative IL-6 [17.13 (15.48–19.47) pg/mL vs. 20.83 (18.37–21.99) pg/mL], postoperative IL-6 (20.42 ± 5.55 pg/mL vs. 25.79 ± 7.54 pg/mL), preoperative S-100β (58.61 ± 18.06 pg/mL vs. 82.94 ± 23.84 pg/mL), and postoperative S-100β (78.21 ± 29.9 pg/mL vs. 99.38 ± 33.13 pg/mL) levels were significantly higher in patients with POCD at week 1 compared with those without (p < 0.01, p = 0.01, p < 0.01, and p = 0.03, respectively). Preoperative IL-6 [17.75 (15.94–20.31) pg/mL vs. 21.39 (18.05–31.51) pg/mL] and preoperative S-100β (63.44 ± 20.48 pg/mL vs. 81.49 ± 28.87 pg/mL) levels were significantly higher in patients with POCD at month 3 compared with those without (p = 0.01 and p = 0.03, respectively). Discussion

The current study in older patients undergoing urologic oncologic surgery demonstrated that preoperative S-100β and IL-6 levels were associated with both early and late POCD. However, postoperative S-100β and IL-6 levels were only associated with early POCD. The study also showed that high preoperative serum NSE levels were linked to the development of POD. Long-term POCD was more common in patients with severe postoperative complications. However, no relationship could be established between any rScO₂ threshold and POD, POCD1, or POCD3.

The incidence of POD and POCD at 1 week and 3 months is reported to range from 5–52%, 30–80%, and 10–60%, respectively, in elderly patients undergoing non-cardiac surgery (Ruhnau et al. 2023). The incidence rates in our study also fall within these acceptable ranges. Up to 30% of POD cases have been reported in older patients undergoing urologic surgery; however, there is a lack of studies on POCD in this population (Leotsakos et al. 2019).

Numerous risk factors for PNDs have been identified, including advanced age, low educational status, frailty, and certain systemic diseases (Deiner and Silverstein 2009). In this study, age and low educational levels were found to be risk factors for POD. We believe that other medical factors such as coronary artery disease (CAD), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and polypharmacy may contribute as risk factors for POCD, given that all of our patients were from a high-risk age group.

Frailty, a geriatric syndrome characterized by a decline in physiological reserve, is associated with worse postoperative outcomes (Mahanna-Gabrielli et al. 2020). It has been shown that POD occurs 2.7 times more often in frail patients, although frailty was not associated with POCD in previous studies (Mahanna-Gabrielli et al. 2020). In contrast, our study found that frailty was more prominent in patients with both POD and POCD at the 3-month postoperative follow-up. This finding was further highlighted by the higher incidence of major postoperative complications in frail patients with POCD at 3 months. In addition to frailty, ASA classification and polypharmacy were also associated with PNDs in our study.

POD and POCD share several common features, yet their relationship remains unclear. Daiello et al. demonstrated that patients with a history of POD were at higher risk of developing early POCD (1 month postoperatively) (Daiello et al. 2019). Interestingly, our study found that the risk of POCD at 3 months was increased in patients with a history of POD and POCD at 1 week; however, POD and POCD at 1 week were not directly associated with each other.

Numerous studies have explored biomarkers for PNDs (Liu et al. 2018; Li et al. 2012; Kunkel et al. 2023; Schaefer et al. 2019). However, no ideal biomarker that can fully explain these syndromes has been established. Biomarkers partially elucidate the pathophysiology of PNDs, which remain multifactorial in nature (Schaefer et al. 2019). Nevertheless, biomarker studies hold promise, and despite significant efforts, much progress remains to be made.

The biomarkers we examined in our study were S-100β, NSE, IL-6, and HMGB-1. The levels of these markers are indicative of the transition from mild cognitive impairment (MCI) to dementia (Levada and Traïlin 2012; Bradburn et al. 2017; Palumbo et al. 2008; Paudel et al. 2020). The progression from normal cognitive function to MCI and dementia is a gradual process.

Proinflammatory cytokines, such as HMGB-1 and IL-6, initiate neuroinflammation, which subsequently leads to brain injury, detectable through markers like S-100β and NSE (Androsova et al. 2015; Kunkel et al. 2023). These biomarkers are also associated with PNDs (Androsova et al. 2015; Peng et al. 2013). In our study, we demonstrated that preoperative NSE was predictive for POD, preoperative S-100β was predictive for POCD at both 1 week and 3 months, and postoperative S-100β was predictive for POCD at 1 week, with considerable sensitivity and specificity.

S-100β is a calcium-binding protein abundant in glial cells (Androsova et al. 2015). It mediates neuroinflammation and is associated with neuronal injury (Androsova et al. 2015). Glumac et al. demonstrated that dexamethasone reduced both POCD incidence and S-100β levels, suggesting a possible mechanistic role of S-100β beyond its function as a biomarker (Glumac et al. 2017). Various studies have shown that elevated levels of S-100β are linked to POD (Liu et al. 2018; Boogaard et al. 2011). S-100β has also been associated with POCD in procedures like transurethral resection of the prostate (TUR-P) and robotic-assisted laparoscopic radical prostatectomy (Chi et al. 2017; Kavrut Ozturk et al. 2020). In our study, while S-100β was not associated with POD, preoperative S-100β levels were found to be significantly associated with both short-term and long-term POCD. Preoperative S-100β predicted POCD at 1 week and 3 months with a cut-off value of 73.14 pg/mL (75% sensitivity, 90% specificity) and 69.41 pg/mL (69% sensitivity, 71% specificity), respectively. Postoperative S-100β predicted POCD at 1 week with a cut-off value of 73.14 pg/mL (75% sensitivity, 90% specificity).

NSE is a glycolytic enzyme primarily present in neurons and serves as a common biomarker for both POD and POCD. High levels of NSE have been identified as an independent risk factor for POD development in oncologic surgery (Mietani et al. 2021). NSE has also been linked to POCD in a meta-analysis of various surgical populations (Schaefer et al. 2019). In our study, only high preoperative NSE levels were associated with POD, and preoperative NSE predicted POD with a cut-off value of 3.95 ng/mL (71% sensitivity, 90% specificity).

IL-6 is a widely recognized inflammatory marker for PNDs. In a meta-analysis of heterogeneous surgical populations, IL-6 was associated with both POD and POCD (Liu et al. 2018). In another meta-analysis, however, it was suggested that a different pathway, aside from IL-6, might contribute to the development of POD in abdominal surgery (Adamis et al. 2021). Our study did not find an association between IL-6 and POD, supporting this notion. Preoperative IL-6 predicted POCD at 1 week and 3 months with a cut-off value of 18.17 pg/mL (82% sensitivity, 61% specificity) and 20.22 pg/mL (70% sensitivity, 76% specificity), respectively. Postoperative IL-6 predicted POCD at 1 week with a cut-off value of 19.5 pg/mL (77% sensitivity, 61% specificity).

HMGB-1, a cytokine with a very short half-life (~ 17 min), is a relatively new biomarker for PND (Zandarashvili et al. 2013). Consequently, HMGB-1 is not an ideal marker for PNDs (Kunkel et al. 2023). However, the association between HMGB-1 and PND has been demonstrated in animal studies, and ongoing human studies are exploring its potential (Kunkel et al. 2023; Terrando et al. 2016). Liu et al. showed that HMGB-1 levels on days 1 and 3 were associated with POCD in gastrointestinal surgeries (Lin et al. 2014). However, in our study, no relationship was found between HMGB-1 and PND.

In interpreting our findings, it is also important to consider the potential influence of preoperative neuroinflammatory status. In patients with malignancy, systemic inflammation may already be present prior to surgery, possibly priming the central nervous system toward a pro-inflammatory state. This preoperative condition could modulate the magnitude or kinetics of postoperative biomarker responses and potentially influence vulnerability to cognitive complications (Ruhnau et al. 2023).

The interpretation of our biomarker data should be viewed in Light of temporal kinetics. While 6-h postoperative sampling aligned well with expected rises in IL-6 and showed significant elevation in POCD1 patients, markers like S-100β may have already peaked before this time due to their shorter half-lives. Similarly, HMGB1 may not have reached its peak by 6 h, and the absence of a postoperative NSE elevation in our cohort may reflect different dynamics in abdominal surgery compared to cardiac settings. As highlighted in previous reports, continuous and stochastic elevations cannot always be distinguished in single timepoint studies, and the most dynamic changes may occur outside the measured interval. Although our sampling window captured early responses for some markers, it may not fully represent the complex time course of postoperative neuroinflammation (Li et al. 2012; Peng et al. 2013; Lin et al. 2014; Johnsson et al. 2000).

Intraoperative cerebral hypoxia is another pathophysiologic mechanism linked to POCD (Snyder et al. 2021). Thus, cerebral oxygenation monitoring has been proposed as a strategy to reduce POCD (Vu and Smith 2022). However, the effectiveness of monitoring regional cerebral oxygen saturation (rScO₂) in predicting or preventing POCD remains unclear (Vu and Smith 2022). Although cerebral hypoxia clearly contributes to POCD, other factors may also play a role in this multifactorial syndrome. In our study, no relationship between rScO₂ thresholds and POCD at 1 week or 3 months could be established.

Cerebral hypoxia has also been suggested as a pathological mechanism in POD (He et al. 2022). However, the relationship between POD and rScO₂ is still uncertain. Eertmans et al. demonstrated that postoperative low rScO₂ was associated with POD, with these changes being more pronounced during episodes of delirium (Eertmans et al. 2020). A meta-analysis showed that rScO₂ monitoring was effective in preventing POCD but not POD (Ding et al. 2020). Most studies investigating the relationship between POD and rScO₂ have been conducted in cardiac surgeries, and further research is needed in other surgical settings. In our study, no relationship between rScO₂ thresholds and POD could be established. Studies have shown that cerebral blood flow can either increase or decrease during delirium (Wood et al. 2022). Therefore, the potential role of rScO₂ monitoring as a surrogate for perfusion monitoring in delirium should be further explored.

PNDs are important predictors of perioperative complications, reoperations, and prolonged hospital stays in older patients (Meyer et al. 2021). In our study, major postoperative complications and ICU follow-up were found to be prognostic factors for POCD at 3 months. Cognitive functions are more likely to be affected for a longer period in patients who develop major postoperative complications, even after the acute effects of surgery have resolved. Thus, using the Clavien-Dindo classification, which includes the severity of complications, becomes more meaningful in terms of prognosis.

Our study has several strengths. First, no prior study has simultaneously evaluated PNDs in terms of biomarkers and cerebral hypoxia. Second, our study was conducted in a highly specific patient group—elderly patients undergoing major urologic oncologic surgery. Finally, it is one of the few studies to examine HMGB-1 in this context.

However, our study also has two important limitations. Firstly, the heterogeneity of surgical procedures and tumor stages among participants may have introduced variability in cognitive outcomes, as malignancy-related systemic effects could potentially contribute to postoperative neurocognitive disturbances. Secondly, the absence of standardized preoperative cognitive assessments to rule out baseline impairment may have limited our ability to distinguish new-onset dysfunction from preexisting deficits. Thirdly, the relatively small number of patients in the POD group may have limited the statistical power of subgroup analyses, increasing the risk of Type II error. Consequently, some true associations related to POD might not have been detected as statistically significant. Fourthly, biomarker sampling was restricted to a single time point—6 h postoperatively. This approach may not fully capture the temporal dynamics of all biomarkers studied. In particular, HMGB1 may continue to rise beyond 6 h, and the kinetics of NSE are not yet clearly defined in abdominal surgical populations at 6 h postoperatively. Although peaks at this time point have been observed in other surgical contexts, extrapolating this to abdominal surgery should be approached with caution. Because biomarker levels were measured only at a single postoperative time point, we may have missed earlier or delayed peak responses. This approach may limit the ability to fully characterize the dynamic postoperative inflammatory and neuronal response patterns.

Conclusion

In geriatric major urologic oncological surgeries, IL-6 and S-100β are associated with short- and long-term POCD; NSE was found to be associated with POD, which supports the idea that PNDs are due to neuronal injury caused by increased proinflammatory cytokine response. Long-term POCD is more common in patients with more severe complications. Strategies to reduce the cytokine response associated with surgery and reduce postoperative complications may be beneficial in preventing postoperative cognitive dysfunction. No relationship was found between rScO2 and postoperative neurocognitive complications.

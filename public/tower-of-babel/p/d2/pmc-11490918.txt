Ultrasonic-assisted membrane processes for the systematic purification of glycyrrhiza wastewater
Authors: Cunyu Li, Yun Ma, Shuwan Tang, Yangyang Xu, Xin Shen, Ranyun Qiu, Shenglian Cai, Yanyu Ma, Xinglei Zhi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
A significant amount of glycyrrhiza wastewater is generated in the cleaning process of glycyrrhiza. The wastewater contains polysaccharide, glycyrrhizic acid, liquiritin, and other polyphenols, which is expensive for cleanup and wastes medical resources. To reduce environmental pollution from glycyrrhiza wastewater and increase the resource usage efficiency of glycyrrhiza components. According to the physicochemical properties of the component in glycyrrhiza wastewater, the ultrasonic assisted membrane separation mode was adopted to regulate the micellar state of glycyrrhizic acid and enhance the differences in membrane separation of polysaccharides, saponins, and flavones, in order to achieve the classification and separation of polysaccharides, saponins, and flavones while removing organic matter in glycyrrhiza wastewater. However, the efficiency, application, and mechanism of ultrasonic-assisted membrane technology for the separation of polysaccharides, saponins, and flavonoids from glycyrrhiza wastewater remain unclear. This study presents a green and feasible technical strategy for glycyrrhiza wastewater treatment that was developed by adjusting the parameters of ultrasonic assisted membrane separation. In this study, the systematic separation mode of ultrasonic enhanced ultrafiltration combined with nanofiltration is provided. The SCQ-9200E ultrasonic system was provided for the study with adjustable ultrasonic power, and the ultrasonic frequency was 40 kHz. The glycyrrhizic acid micelle was changed using ultrasonic power, pH, and molecular weight cut off (MWCO), and the separation differences among polysaccharide, glycyrrhizic acid, and liquiritin were enhanced. The optimal polysaccharide separation parameters used in the first step: MWCO 30 kDa, ultrasonic power 500 W and pH 5.00, and the rejections of polysaccharide, glycyrrhizic acid, and liquiritin were 87.72 %, 8.01 %, and 6.57 %, respectively. The second step included the following parameters for the separation of liquiritin and glycyrrhizic acid: MWCO 10 kDa, ultrasonic power 100 W and pH 8.00, the rejections of liquiritin and glycyrrhizic acid were 9.22 % and 40.65 %, respectively. The third step is to remove the low molecular sugar in liquiritin by nanofiltration: MWCO 800 Da, pH 8.00, retention solution diluted and separated twice, the rejection of liquiritin and total sugar were 95.72 % and 3.70 %, respectively. Ultrasonic may regulate the microtopography of glycyrrhiza wastewater with the power intensity of 50 W/L, improving the mass transfer efficiency of glycyrrhizic acid and liquiritin in the ultrafiltration separation process. As the separation volume of wastewater increased from 2.00 L to 20.00 L, the concentrations of polysaccharide, glycyrrhizic acid, and liquiritin increased by 2.5–35.4 times, 0.6–15.2 times, and 2.4–32.8 times, respectively, significantly increasing the content of index components in wastewater and solving the problem of recycling and resource utilization in glycyrrhiza wastewater.

Introduction

The pharmaceutical industry has grown rapidly, leading to significant improvements in drug quality and pharmaceutical technology. However, there is still an inevitable problem with the appropriate disposal of pharmaceutical wastewater [1], [2], [3]. Wasted pharmaceutical resources, higher operational expenses for businesses, and negative health effects are all practical problems associated with pharmaceutical wastewater, which also comprises a variety of chemical compounds and pollutes the environment [4], [5]. High chroma, high organic matter, high pH, high biochemical oxygen demand (BOD), and high suspended particles concentration are all characteristics of wastewater generated by pharmaceutical manufacturing [6]. Physical and chemical treatment, which includes coagulation precipitation, gas flotation, electrolysis, and advanced oxidation method, as well as biological treatment, which includes aerobic biological treatment, anaerobic biological treatment, and aerobic-anaerobic combined treatment, are the current treatment methods [7], [8], [9], [10]. The water quality standard discharge is ensured by a biochemical or physicochemical-biological process, which takes into account the complicated water quality characteristics of pharmaceutical wastewater. Most of the pharmaceutical compounds in pharmaceutical wastewater are broken down or eliminated as hazardous materials, which raises the expense of treating wastewater and wastes valuable medical resources [11].

Glycyrrhiza, a type of traditional Chinese medicine with similar origins to medicine and food, is the primary raw material for glycyrrhiza oral liquid and glycyrrhiza tablets [12]. Water is required throughout the manufacturing procedure for glycyrrhiza medicinal products in order to clean and remove dirt from their surfaces. However, some medicinal compounds are dissolved in water, which causes unavoidable losses. The levels of polysaccharides, saponins, and flavonoids in the glycyrrhiza wastewater were 1.29 mg/mL, 3.62 mg/mL, and 0.66 mg/mL, respectively, which have pharmacological effects such as anti-oxidation, anti-tumor, and immunological stimulation [13], [14], [15]. Glycyrrhizic acid enhances the anticancer activities of cisplatin in A2780 human ovarian cancer cell lines [16]. Liquiritin reduces chondrocyte apoptosis through P53/PUMA signaling pathway to alleviate osteoarthritis [17]. And glycyrrhiza polysaccharides attenuated the upregulation of tumor necrosis factor-α, interleukin (IL)-1β, and IL-6 in the serum and colon tissues and significantly reduced the malondialdehyde content in colon tissues [18], [19]. The above-mentioned pharmaceutical ingredients will pollute water and disrupt the body's normal functions if food processing and pharmaceutical companies improperly treat their waste water containing flavonoids, licorice saponins, and other components, leading to high energy consumption. Consequently, the technical challenge in treating glycyrrhiza wastewater is that it is hard to separate the pharmacodynamic components of the wastewater in an ordered manner using typical biochemical or physicochemical combined technology strategies.

The technology of membrane separation, which is widely used to treat pharmaceutical wastewater, has the technical benefit of separation at normal temperature and prevents the loss of heat-sensitive component conversion [20], [21], [22]. Membrane contamination and decreased separation efficiency may result from the quick build-up of insoluble particles and polysaccharides in glycyrrhiza wastewater on the membrane's surface during the membrane separation process [23]. In the actual separation process, high-speed centrifugation is frequently utilized to pretreat the wastewater, and the concentration polarization impact of the membrane surface is reduced through low flux separation [24], [25]. As a result, the contradiction between the separation coefficient and separation efficiency must be considered. The ultrasonic wave is a type of mechanical vibration wave that causes the alternating compression and relaxation of medium particles during media propagation, resulting in synergistic effects such as cavitation, mechanical, and thermal effects that increase solute transfer and exchange efficiency [26], [27], [28], [29]. The application of ultrasonic technology has the potential to enhance the efficiency of interfacial mass transfer, reduce membrane fouling, improve the recovery rate of active substances in glycyrrhiza wastewater, promote the recycling of medicinal resources, and decrease the negative effect on the environment the wastewater generates [30], [31]. The surface activity of glycyrrhiza saponins is the main cause of the significant foam in the wastewater generated during glycyrrhiza washing [32]. When a certain micelle concentration has been reached, glycyrrhizic acid, a saponin component, will form micelles through intermolecular hydrogen bonding. Previous research findings, solution pH may affect the degree of component dissociation, and ultrasonic can break down glycyrrhizic acid micelles and change the behavior of ultrafiltration separation [33]. Ultrasound can be employed in membrane systems to clean contaminated surfaces through physical microjets, microstreaming, shock waves, and sonochemistry. Ultrasonic-assisted nanofiltration separation can resolve the separation conflict between rosmarinic acid and salvianolic acid B in pharmaceutical wastewater, as well as enhance resource recycling and separation efficiency to prevent pollution of the environment from pharmaceutical wastewater [34]. Ultrasonic assisted membrane method was established to separate saponins from Gynostemma pentaphyllum by regulating micelle state [35]. Ultrasonic technology offers an advanced and promising technical approach for fouling reduction, transfer enhancement, and process detection in membrane systems [36]. As a result, when ultrasonic assisted membrane separation is used to treat pharmaceutical wastewater, it may not only reduce membrane pollution, but also increase the separation difference of solutes in wastewater by adjusting the existence of components, thereby improving waste separation efficiency. Consequently, there are many possible applications for this technology in the wastewater treatment industries. However, the efficiency, application, and mechanism of ultrasonic-assisted membrane technology for the separation of polysaccharides, saponins, and flavonoids from glycyrrhiza wastewater remain unclear. This study presents a green and feasible technical strategy for glycyrrhiza wastewater treatment that was developed by adjusting the parameters of ultrasonic power, pH, and MWCO of ultrasonic assisted membrane separation.

Materials and methods

Materials

Glycyrrhiza wastewater: Jiangsu Hongshou Biological Engineering Co., Ltd. provided the glycyrrhiza wastewater. The preparatory procedure was as follows: First, 100 kg of glycyrrhiza materials were cleaned using an XY-900 drum washing machine with a rotating speed of 8 rpm/min and a cleaning time of 2 min, and 180 L of cleaning effluent was collected. Second, the supernatant was separated using a GQ76 tubular centrifuge at 18,000 rpm/min for 10 min. The GQ76 tubular centrifuge was obtained from Shanghai Bao Dai Centrifugal Equipment Co., LTD., while the XY-900 drum washing machine was purchased from Changshu Chinese Medicine Machinery Factory.

The reference substances of diammonium glycyrrhizinate (purity 96.2 %), liquiritin (purity 95.0 %) and D-anhydrous glucose (purity 99.8 %) was obtained from the National Institute for the Control of Pharmaceutical and Biological Products (Beijing, China). Methanol and acetonitrile were chromatographic grade and got from Merck (Darmstadt, Germany). Phosphoric acid and other chemicals were all analytical grade and purchased from Sinopharm group chemical reagent Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Ultrapure water was prepared by Millipore Direct-Q5 water purification system.

Therefore, in order to separate polysaccharides, saponins, and flavonoids, ultrafiltration is employed in this study. The average values of each group of experiments were collected three times in parallel for results analysis.

Experiment design and statistical analysis

Response surface methodology (RSM) is widely used in various areas such as medicine and food, mainly for parameter screening, process optimization, and factor interaction analysis. The ultrafiltration separation parameters were optimized using the Box-Behnken Design based on the single factor experiments. The solute rejection was designated as a response, and the MWCO, ultrasonic power, and pH were regarded as independent factors. Each factor was taken at three levels (−1, 0, 1).

Using the three-factor three-level design, 17 experimental runs were carried out, with 5 replicates at the center point. In order to analyze the interaction between the dependent variable and the independent variables, an empirical model described as a quadratic polynomial equation.(2)Y=β0+∑βiXi+∑βiiXi2+∑βijXiXj

As shown in Equation (2), where Y denotes the response value, Xi and Xj are the independent variables, βi, βii, and βij are the linear and quadratic regression coefficients, β0 is the constant coefficient. The analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to determine the equation's statistical significance. The P-value was used to assess the significance of each coefficient as well as the interactions between each of the individual variables.

Nanofiltration

Nanofiltration is mostly utilized in the water treatment process to remove small molecular chemicals or to treat desalination. Polysaccharides and saponins have been sequentially separated by ultrasonic assisted ultrafiltration processing. The ultrafiltration permeates are mostly made up of flavonoids and low molecular weight sugars, nanofiltration is employed to remove small sugars from the ultrafiltration permeates, increasing the yield of flavonoids [37]. Based on the molecular weight distribution of the components in the ultrafiltration permeates, NFG and NFW spiral nanofiltration membranes were employed. Table 1 showed the parameters of nanofiltration membrane. Referring to the ultrafiltration separation system, since both the ultrafiltration membrane and nanofiltration membrane sizes are similar, it is simple to replace during operation. At the same time, the peristaltic pump in the ultrafiltration separation equipment is replaced with a medium pressure pump piston pump. Because of the charge repulsion effect difference between liquiritin and sugar on the surface of the nanofiltration membrane, liquiritin was primarily in the retention solution, whereas low molecular weight sugars were primarily in the filtrate during the nanofiltration separation process. The separation difference between liquiritin and low molecular weight sugar was strengthened through adjusting the pH of the solution [38]. The pH of the ultrafiltration permeates were adjusted to 5.00, 6.00, 7.00, 8.00, and 9.00, respectively, based on the dissociation constant of liquiritin (pKa 7.70). After analyzing the effect of pH on membrane flux and rejection, the optimal conditions for nanofiltration separation that work effectively for processing glycyrrhiza wastewater were chosen.

The binary mobile phase for gradient elution consisted of acetonitrile (A) and 0.05 % phosphoric acid aqueous solution (B) (0–10 min, 10 %–22 % A; 10–32 min, 22 %-40 % A; 32–35 min, 40 %-45 % A; 35–40 min, 45 %-10 % A; 40–45 min, 10 % A). In addition, the injection volume was 10 μL, the detection wavelength was 237 nm, the flow rate was 1.0 mL/min, and the column temperature was 25 °C.

A mixed reference solution of 0.820 mg/mL of glycyrrhizic acid and 0.118 mg/mL of liquiritin, respectively. After precisely absorbing 0.1 mL, 0.2 mL, 0.5 mL, 1.0 mL, and 2.0 mL of the mixed reference solution into a 5 mL brown volume bottle and diluting 70 % methanol to the scale, the regression equation was computed with the concentration performing as the horizontal coordinate (X) and the peak area as the vertical coordinate (Y). Polysaccharides

The polysaccharide content was determined using the sulfuric acid-phenol method, with D-glucose serving as the standard substance [41]. After absorbing 20 μL of the sample solution (glycyrrhiza wastewater and membrane separation solution), 1.0 mL of the 9.0 % phenol solution was added, and it was well shaken. After that, 3.0 mL of concentrated sulfuric acid was added, and it was left for 30 min. Without using a sample solution as a control, the absorbance was measured three times at 485 nm, and the mean value was calculated. The horizontal coordinate was the concentration of D-glucose (C), and the ordinate coordinate was the absorbance value (A), which was measured at a wavelength of 490 nm by T6 UV–VIS spectrophotometer. The standard curve was plotted as A = 0.0698C-0.0314 (r = 0.999 7). D-glucose concentrations ranging from 1.10 to 11.00 mg/mL exhibited a strong linear association with absorbance.

Conductivity, pH and turbidity

The pH (pH meter PB-10, Sartorius, Germany), electrical conductivity (Conductivity meter TB320, Timepower, China), and turbidity (Turbidimeter TP309, Timepower, China) were measured for glycyrrhiza wastewater before and after centrifugation.

Before and after 500 W ultrasonic treatment, samples of glycyrrhiza wastewater were freeze-dried. Ultrafiltration

Single factor test

One important parameter for membrane separation that affects both composition rejection and separation efficiency is the MWCO. After high-speed centrifugation, 2.00 L of glycyrrhiza wastewater was put in a storage tank for the single factor experiment. The concentration of polysaccharide, glycyrrhizic acid and liquiritin in glycyrrhiza wastewater were 1.07 mg/mL, 0.61 mg/mL and 0.13 mg/mL, respectively. Ultrasonic-assisted ultrafiltration reduced the wastewater volume in the storage tank to 200 mL. The results of Fig. 2a demonstrate that membrane flux rose in tandem with the increase in the MWCO of the ultrafiltration membrane. Additionally, the rejections of polysaccharide, glycyrrhizic acid and liquiritin all exhibited a different trend. According to the molecular sieve theory of ultrafiltration separation, the order of influence of MWCO on the rejections of the three components was liquiritin > glycyrrhizic acid > polysaccharide [42]. The rejection of glyciorrhiza polysaccharides in 1 kDa − 50 kDa ultrafiltration membrane was more than 75 % among them, suggesting that the majority of glyciorrhiza polysaccharides were made up of polysaccharides with molecular weights greater than 50 kDa, subsequently followed by those with molecular parts between 30 kDa and 50 kDa. Compared to the change in ultrafiltration flux, it is clear that glycyrrhiza wastewater could pollute the membrane separation layer during the ultrafiltration separation process, reducing the effective filtration pore size of the ultrafiltration membrane and affecting separation efficiency.Fig. 2Effect of factors on the rejection and membrane flux, (a) MWCO, (b) Ultrasonic power and (c) pH.

Effect of factors on the rejection and membrane flux, (a) MWCO, (b) Ultrasonic power and (c) pH.

The mass transfer efficiency of the solute on the ultrafiltration membrane surface can be enhanced by both the cavitation effect and the turbulence effect caused by ultrasound [43]. And the influence becomes greater for smaller molecules. The rejections of glycyrrhizic acid, liquiritin, and polysaccharide all showed a downward trend with an increase in ultrasonic power, as shown in Fig. 2b. The operating conditions were ultrafiltration MWCO 10 kDa and pH 5.00. As the ultrasonic power increased form 0 W to 700 W, the degree of decline was glycyrrhizic acid > liquiritin > polysaccharide, suggesting that ultrasound could promote solute diffusion to ultrafiltration membrane, leading to a decline in component rejections. However, glycyrrhizin's rejection of reduction was greater than liquiritin's, which went against the relationship between ultrasonic mass transfer efficiency and molecule size. According to previous studies, glycyrrhizic acid intermolecular interaction happens at concentrations between 0.01–1 mmol/L in aqueous solution. Glycyrrhizic acid's intermolecular hydrogen bond can be broken and the micelle's transition to a single molecular state is promoted when the ultrasonic power is greater than 300 W [44]. Thus, the glycyrrhizic acid rejection falls comparatively significantly under the molecular sieve effect of ultrafiltration. The rejection of glycyrrhizic acid, liquiritin, and polysaccharide can be affected by ultrasonic power, which can further enhance the separation difference between polysaccharide and glycyrrhizin. Furthermore, as the ultrasonic power increased from 0 to 700 W, the membrane flux of water grew from 54.10 L/m2·h to 105.02 L/m2·h, while the flux of wastewater increased from 15.02 L/m2·h to 91.96 L/m2·h. The results suggest that ultrasonic also accelerated the contaminants' migration from the membrane surface to the solution and improved the effectiveness of ultrafiltration separation [45].

Fig. 2c shows the effect of solution pH on ultrafiltration separation. As pH increased, the rejections of the three index components all showed a decreasing trend, with the decline degree being as follows: liquiritin > glycyrrhizic acid > polysaccharide. According to the dissociation constants of glycyrrhizic acid (pKa 2.76) and liquiritin (pKa 7.70), as the pH of solution increases from 2.00 to 8.00, both glycyrrhizic acid and liquiritin change from molecular to ionic form. The critical micelle of an ionic surfactant is substantially larger than that of a non-ionic surfactant, hence the proportion of glycyrrhizic acid micelle in solution reduces, resulting in a decrease of rejection. With a rise in liquiritin ionization, the interface effect between liquiritin and ultrafiltration membrane will be further decreased, allowing liquiritin to pass through the membrane more easily [46]. Moreover, as the pH of the solution rises, the ultrafiltration membrane's flow increases as well. This suggests that the contaminants that have been adsorbed on the membrane's surface can dissolve in a solution with a higher pH, decreasing the wastewater pollution. The effect of variables on the rejection of glycyrrhizic acid, liquiritin and polysaccharide as well as the membrane flux was thoroughly investigated based on the results of the single factor experiment. In order to optimize the ultrafiltration separation parameters, the MWCO was 10 kDa−50 kDa, the ultrasonic power was 100 W−500 W, and the solution pH was 5.00–8.00.

Meanwhile, as shown in Fig. 4, the rejection of liquiritin was statistically significantly affected by the interaction between MWCO and pH. The liquiritin rejection in 10 kDa ultrafiltration decreased obviously as the wastewater's pH rose from 5.00 to 8.00 at 300 W of ultrasonic power, however the liquiritin rejection in 50 kDa ultrafiltration remained relatively constant. Liquiritin's pKa 7.70 indicates that it exists primarily in molecule state in wastewater. Liquiritin is a difficult drug to dissolve in water, therefore glycyrrhizic acid micelles help to enhance the solubility. Even though the molecular weight of liquiritin is just 418.39 Da, it shows a high rejection in a 10 kDa ultrafiltration membrane. The proportion of liquiritin ionic states in the solution rose along with the pH. At the same time, liquiritin rejection decreased as a result of ultrasonic speeding up the dissolution of liquiritin from the micelles and increasing the ultrafiltration mass transfer efficiency.

In Fig. 5, the interaction of ultrasonic power and pH has statistical significance for the rejections of glycyrrhizic acid, liquiritin, and polysaccharide. As the ultrasonic power and pH increase, the rejections of glycyrrhizic acid, liquiritin, and polysaccharide all exhibit a decreasing trend while the ultrafiltration membrane's MWCO kept constant at 30 kDa. The three components' varying retention rates revealed that they were sensitive to pH and ultrasonic power in various ways. Ultrasonic power: glycyrrhizic acid > polysaccharide > liquiritin, suggesting that the ultrasonic cavitation effect could control the presence state of glycyrrhizic acid and polysaccharide in solution. The decrease in rejection was brought about by promoting the transformation of glycyrrhizic acid micelles to a low molecular state. The effects of pH on component rejection were as follows: liquiritin > glycyrrhizic acid > polysaccharide; ionization of liquiritin may increase its solubility; glycyrrhizic acid's conversion from a non-ionic surfactant to an ionic surfactant raised the critical micelle concentration and increased the proportion of glycyrrhizin monomer in solution, both of which may decrease the rejection. The rejection of polysaccharide reduced concurrently with an increase in pH, suggesting that the polysaccharide in glycyrrhiza wastewater comprised acidic polysaccharides. In order to increase the separation difference between the three components, the separation parameters can be changed based on how MWCO, ultrasonic power, and pH affect and interact with each other to the rejection of glycyrrhizic acid, liquiritin, and polysaccharide.

The three components were separated in two steps based on the relationship between the rejections of polysaccharide, liquiritin, and glycyrrhizic acid and the parameters that were studied. First, the separation parameters were improved using the ultrasonic assisted-ultrafiltration based on the molecular weight difference between polysaccharide and glycyrrhizic acid. Second, the separation parameters were optimized based on the different molecular states of glycyrrhizic acid and liquiritin. The following were the polysaccharide separation parameters used in the first step: MWCO 30 kDa, ultrasonic power 500 W and pH 5.00, and the predicted rejections of polysaccharide, glycyrrhizic acid, and liquiritin were 86.36 %, 8.16 %, and 6.90 %, respectively. The second step included the following parameters for the separation of liquiritin and glycyrrhizic acid: MWCO 10 kDa, ultrasonic power 100 W and pH 8.00, the predicted rejections of liquiritin and glycyrrhizic acid were 9.46 % and 41.75 %, respectively.

Verification experiments

To verify the practicality of the optimized separation parameters, the rejection variations of glycyrrhiza wastewater were studied at volumes of 2.00 L, 5.00 L, 10.00 L, 15.00 L and 20.00 L, and the results are shown in Fig. 6. The rejections of the three components were relatively consistent with the predicted values; however, as the volume of wastewater increased, the high rejection of glycyrrhiza polysaccharides on the surface of the ultrafiltration membrane caused the polysaccharide concentration in the retention solution to gradually increase, accelerating the concentration polarization effect and membrane fouling on the membrane surface. Although the cavitation and turbulence effects of ultrasound may inhibit the development of gel layers and contaminants on the membrane surface, the rejections of polysaccharide, glycyrrhizic acid, and liquiritin continue to grow with wastewater volume. The experimental results revealed that the optimized separation parameters were suitable for the separation of polysaccharide, glycyrrhizic acid, and liquiritin. Because of the technical benefit of linear amplification, the effectiveness of separation using nanofiltration can be increased by increasing the area of the membrane as the volume of wastewater grows.

Nanofiltration separation of liquiritin and low molecular sugar

The separation efficiency of nanofiltration is related to the membrane MWCO and Donnan effect [49]. Fig. 6 and Fig. 7 showed that as the MWCO increases, the rejections of liquiritin and total sugar falls, with total sugar rejection decreasing at a faster rate than liquiritin. Monosaccharides and disaccharides were the primary carbohydrate components of total sugar, based on the molecular weight difference between NFW and NFG. As the solution's pH increased, the rejection of liquiritin and total sugar changed. According to the pKa value of liquiritin, when pH > 8, the ionization degree of liquiritin increased, enhancing the Donnan effect between the liquiritin and the nanofiltration membrane. As a result, the rejection in both NFW and NFG nanofiltration exceeded 98 %. However, the rejection of neutral sugars remained relatively stable. The slight rise in rejection with pH was due to the ionization of the alcohol hydroxyl bond in the carbohydrate structure, which created an increase in charge effect and inhibited sugar transport to the membrane surface. To separate liquiritin and sugars, an NFG nanofiltration membrane and a solution pH of 8.00 were used based on the differences in rejections showed in Fig. 7. The retention solution in storage tank is diluted with ultra-pure water to the original volume, and nanofiltration is then carried out again and twice more in order to further remove the sugar in the solution when the volume of the solution in the tank is less than 200 mL. The liquiritin rejection is currently 95.72 %, while the total sugar rejection is 3.70 %. Ultrasonic-assisted ultrafiltration separation combined with nanofiltration

To improve the recovery rate of polysaccharide, glycyrrhizic acid, and liquiritin in wastewater, a two-step separation process was developed using ultrasonic-assisted ultrafiltration and nanofiltration. In the first step, the glycyrrhizic acid micelle state was changed using the ultrasonic cavitation effect, and the separation difference between polysaccharide and glycyrrhizic acid was enhanced. The second step, based on molecular weight difference, is to take advantage of nanofiltration to remove sugars and other small molecular ingredients, which improves liquiritin recovery.

After freeze-drying, the glycyrrhiza wastewater mainly consisted of up of irregular aggregates and spherical glycyrrhizic acid colloids, as shown in Fig. 8. After ultrasonic treatment, irregular aggregates and spherical glycyrrhizic acid colloids dispersed into irregular particles, indicating that ultrasonic may regulate the microtopography with the power intensity of 50 W/L, improving the mass transfer efficiency of glycyrrhizic acid and liquiritin in the ultrafiltration separation process [50].Fig. 8SEM image of glycyrrhiza wastewater, (a) freeze-drying and (c) ultrasonic treatment before freeze-drying.

Conclusion

An ultrasonic-assisted membrane processes has been developed to recover polysaccharide, glycyrrhizic acid, and liquiritin from glycyrrhiza wastewater in three steps. The optimal polysaccharide separation parameters employed in the first step were MWCO 30 kDa, ultrasonic power 500 W, and pH 5.00, with rejections of polysaccharide, glycyrrhizic acid, and liquiritin at 87.72 %, 8.01 %, and 6.57 %, respectively. The second step used the following criteria for separating liquiritin and glycyrrhizic acid: With MWCO 10 kDa, ultrasonic power 100 W, and pH 8.00, liquiritin and glycyrrhizic acid were rejected by 9.22 % and 40.65 %, respectively. The third step is to remove the low molecular sugar in liquiritin using nanofiltration: MWCO 800 Da, pH 8.00, retention solution diluted and separated twice; rejection of liquiritin and total sugar were 95.72 % and 3.70 %, respectively. The concentrations of polysaccharide, glycyrrhizic acid, and liquiritin increased by 2.5–35.4 times, 0.6–15.2 times, and 2.4–32.8 times, respectively, as the separation volume of wastewater increased from 2.00 L to 20.00 L. And the polysaccharides demonstrated strong antioxidant characteristics and could have application in the field of medicine. Ultrasonic-assisted membrane technology resolved the problem of recycling and resource usage in glycyrrhiza wastewater and led to a significant rise in the amount of index components in wastewater.

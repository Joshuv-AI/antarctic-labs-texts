Public Perceptions of Vasectomy in China: A Social Media Content Analysis of User-Generated Narratives on Rednote
Authors: Hui-Quan Shu, Zheng-Mu Wu, Chao Feng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study examines public perceptions of vasectomy in China through a content analysis of user-generated posts on Rednote, one of the country’s most influential social media platforms. A total of 106 posts published up to August 2025 were analyzed, focusing on individual experiences, decision-making processes, postoperative outcomes, and emotional tone. The results show that vasectomy is predominantly portrayed in a positive light (92.5%), with most decisions made by men themselves (57.5%). The majority of patients were married with children, but a notable proportion consisted of Dual Income No Kids (DINK) individuals, reflecting shifting fertility intentions in China. Reported complications were rare, with only a few cases of persistent pain, contraceptive failure, or repeat surgery. Engagement metrics revealed highly skewed interaction patterns, where a small number of posts drove significant discussion and visibility. Geographically, most posts originated from economically developed coastal provinces. The findings suggest that social media narratives are reshaping public discourse around vasectomy, positioning it as a legitimate, responsible contraceptive option aligned with evolving cultural norms, gender equality, and modern lifestyle choices in urban China. Nevertheless, limitations include reliance on self-reported content and the platform’s demographic skew toward younger, urban populations.Keywords: vasectomy, male family planning, male contraception

Vasectomy is a safe, minimally invasive, and highly effective male contraceptive method. Compared with female tubal ligation, this procedure offers advantages such as greater operational simplicity, a lower risk of complications, and better cost-effectiveness. However, epidemiological surveys indicate that its clinical utilization rate is significantly lower than that of female sterilization (Kianian et al., 2024). According to data from the year book of health in the People’s Republic of China, a total of 52,128 vasectomies were performed in China in 2018, whereas the number of tubal ligation procedures reached 404,212 (Zeng et al., 2024). The prevalence of vasectomy in the United States is only half that of female sterilization. Globally, only five countries—Bhutan, Canada, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, and New Zealand—have a higher prevalence of vasectomy than female sterilization (Drobner et al., 2023). This discrepancy may be associated with public misperceptions, negative social attitudes, and the influence of traditional beliefs regarding the procedure (White et al., 2022). With ongoing developments in economic conditions, cultural norms, and health education, public awareness and attitudes toward vasectomy are gradually evolving.

According to the results of the 11th Chinese Civic Scientific Literacy Survey conducted in 2020, more than 79.2% of Chinese citizens access scientific and technological information via the internet (Wang et al., 2024). With the rapid development of new online media, various digital platforms have become a major source of health and medical information for the general public (Harriger et al., 2023). Notably, an increasing number of patients tend to share their personal medical experiences on social media. These first-person narratives often serve as valuable references for individuals considering specific treatment plans, while also providing researchers with a unique observational window into the patient perspective. In fields such as orthopedics, dermatology, and gastroenterology, social media platforms like TikTok and Instagram have been utilized as effective sources of research data (Harriger et al., 2023; Ramkumar et al., 2017). In China, access to most major Western social media platforms—such as X (formerly Twitter), Instagram, and Reddit—is restricted due to national internet regulations. Consequently, domestically developed platforms have become the primary venues for public discourse and health-related information exchange. Among these platforms, Rednote (also known as Xiaohongshu), one of China’s leading social media apps, stands out for its diverse content ecosystem where users engage in sharing lifestyle-related experiences (Ji et al., 2025). This study selects Rednote as the research platform. As of 2025, the platform has over 600 million registered users and approximately 200 million monthly active users, with a daily post exposure of 70 trillion impressions (Ji et al., 2025). Based on this, the present study aims to systematically analyze relevant content on Rednote to investigate Chinese public perceptions, attitudes, and viewpoints regarding vasectomy.

Materials and Methods

An anonymous account was created on the Rednote APP to retrieve relevant posts using the keywords “vasectomy,” and “male sterilization” on August 1, 2025. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (a) the post content consisted of personal experiences shared by patients or their spouses; (b) the post was set to be publicly visible; (c) if multiple related posts were published by the same user (e.g., pre- or postoperative records), the content was integrated and comprehensively evaluated by the researchers; (d) the original poster was located in China; and (e) commercial advertisements, medical institution promotions, entertainment content, and educational notes published by health care professionals were excluded.

The collected data included basic interaction metrics (number of likes, favorites, and comments of the posts), the original poster’s location and the male individual’s age (based on IP address or user profile), the primary decision-maker for the vasectomy procedure (e.g., the individual himself, his partner, or mutual decision), and postoperative complications (including but not limited to chronic testicular pain, major bleeding, incision infection, etc.). Sentiment analysis was conducted to assess the emotional tone of posts within the usage interaction theme. Each post was categorized as positive, negative, or neutral. Positive posts were defined as those expressing satisfaction with the effects of vasectomy, indicating a significant improvement in sexual life quality, describing the procedure as smooth with rapid recovery. Negative posts convoyed dissatisfaction with the surgical outcome (e.g., contraceptive failure), perceived negative impacts on sexual function or mental health, or experience of persistent and notable complications (such as chronic pain or infection). Neutral posts lacked clear emotional inclination and contained descriptive or objective statements. These included acknowledgment of the contraceptive efficacy while expressing reservations about certain aspects of the procedure, or the view that it “only solved the contraception problem.” Some reported mild postoperative discomfort (e.g., short-term pain) but considered it acceptable, adopted a wait-and-see attitude regarding long-term effects, or felt that the procedure was “nothing particularly remarkable.”

All analyses were conducted using Python (version 3.12.7). The pandas library, an open-source data analysis and manipulation tool widely used in scientific research, was employed for data handling and descriptive statistical computation, while matplotlib was used for visualization.

As the data were public, anonymized, and analyzed in aggregate without user interaction, the study was deemed exempt from institutional review board review.

Results

As of August 1, 2025, a total of 106 posts were included in this analysis after preprocessing. The mean age of individuals undergoing vasectomy is 31.98 ± 5.37 years, with ages ranging from 22 to 47 years. Most of the posts (n = 68) were posted by the males themselves while the remainder were posted by their spouses. The emotional tone of posts is overwhelmingly positive (n = 98), indicating most users view vasectomy favorably (Table 1). In the majority of cases, the decision to undergo vasectomy was made primarily by the male (n =61). An additional 14 cases lacked sufficient information in the posts to determine who made the decision. Most patients were married with children (n = 79), while smaller groups consisted of unmarried DINKs (Dual Income, No Kids, n = 18), married DINKs (n = 4), and divorced men with children (n = 2). Three posts did not provide information regarding marital or parental status.

Discussion

This study offers one of the first empirical snapshots of public perceptions regarding vasectomy in China by analyzing user-generated content on Rednote. The findings reveal a predominantly positive discourse, with 92.5% of posts expressing satisfaction, which is consistent with the 97% satisfaction and minimal regret observed in the French questionnaire survey by Touil et al. (2024). Compared with Tzelves et al.’s TikTok videos analysis primarily from the United States and reported positive attitudes in 61% of users, the markedly higher positivity rate on RedNote (92.5%) underscores the role of sociocultural and contextual influences (Tzelves et al., 2024). TikTok discussions frequently centered on pain, postoperative recovery, and legal references such as Dobbs v. Jackson, reflecting the sensitive nature of vasectomy in U.S. public discourse. In contrast, RedNote users more often situated vasectomy within narratives of personal autonomy, gender equality, and contemporary lifestyle preferences. These differences indicate that platform characteristics and cultural contexts jointly shape the tone and expression of reproductive health conversations online.

The cultural and social context of China provides important insights into why the narratives identified in this study matter. Historically, one-child family planning policies have placed a disproportionate contraceptive burden on women, with female sterilization widely promoted as a permanent solution, while male vasectomy remained underutilized despite its lower risk and cost. In 2007, the proportion of intrauterine device (IUD) use in China was 52.3%, while female sterilization accounted for 32.3%, and vasectomy constituted only 6.1% (Wu, 2010). Traditional gender norms, which emphasize male virility and associate reproductive responsibility with women, have further contributed to the persistence of negative stereotypes around vasectomy. The predominance of positive male-driven accounts in this study suggests a gradual shift in these cultural dynamics, reflecting greater acceptance of men’s active role in reproductive decision-making. Moreover, the visibility of these narratives on Rednote—an influential platform among younger, urban, and well-educated users—indicates that digital communities may be playing a pivotal role in normalizing vasectomy as a legitimate and responsible contraceptive option. This aligns with broader societal changes in China, where evolving attitudes toward marriage, childbearing, and gender equality are reshaping the discourse around family planning.

An especially noteworthy finding from this study is the presence of DINKs, accounting for 20.8% of the participants, who chose vasectomy. Fertility and annual birth rates in China have been declining rapidly. According to recent nationally representative surveys conducted in 2017, 2019, and 2021, the prevalence of women born after 1990 who reported no intention to bear children was 4.9%, 4.5%, and 9.5%, respectively (Jiang et al., 2023). The number of DINK individuals in China has been steadily increasing. This decline in fertility intention may also contribute to a greater acceptance of vasectomy.

The engagement metrics provide a quantitative window into how vasectomy narratives circulate and gain traction on Rednote. The wide interquartile ranges highlight the heterogeneity of audience responses: while many posts generated moderate interaction, a smaller subset achieved disproportionately high engagement, acting as “amplifiers” in the discourse ecosystem. Many posts sparked dialogue extending beyond individual testimony, transforming personal experiences into collective debates. Outliers with several hundred or even thousands of comments illustrate the viral potential of emotionally charged or controversial narratives. These metrics demonstrate that social media engagement is not evenly distributed but highly skewed, consistent with power-law patterns of online diffusion. A small number of posts—often detailed accounts of regret, complications, or exceptionally positive outcomes—disproportionately shape broader perceptions of vasectomy by attracting more interaction and visibility.

The provinces where original posters are located are predominantly coastal regions, with Sichuan being the only inland province ranked among the top five. These regions are among the most economically developed in China. The distribution of posts shows a notable similarity to the regional distribution of health care service levels across provinces in China (Ye et al., 2024), revealing significant regional disparities. Higher income levels and better access to health care may contribute to greater acceptance and availability of vasectomy services.

In contrast to the positive experiences, a few posts revealed complications or dissatisfaction. These included reports of testicular pain, the failure of the procedure (as seen in cases of sperm motility postsurgery), and even a repeat surgery to correct a poorly performed procedure. Among the 106 posts analyzed in this study, three cases of self-reported intermittent testicular pain were identified, all of which were described as tolerable. These findings are consistent with previous literature regarding the incidence and clinical manifestations of postvasectomy pain. Retrospective case series and prospective observational and follow-up studies indicate that approximately 1% to 2% of men experience chronic pain following vasectomy, with only about 1%–2% of these individuals reporting that the pain significantly affects their quality of life (Ren et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2021). Two reported cases of contraceptive failure after vasectomy underscore the importance of postvasectomy semen analysis (PVSA). The American Urological Association (AUA) guidelines (2012) recommend that PVSA be performed between 8 and 16 weeks after the procedure (Sharlip et al., 2012). Griffin et al. suggest that PVSA should be prioritized within 3 months after surgery, provided that an adequate number of ejaculations (at least 20) has occurred (Griffin et al., 2005). If motile sperm are still present after 6 months of follow-up, a repeat vasectomy should be considered (Dohle et al., 2012). While such cases are infrequent, they highlight the need for better patient education and more rigorous follow-up procedures to ensure the procedure’s success.

In this study, only one male mentioned having undergone semen cryopreservation prior to vasectomy. Overall, the practice of autologous sperm cryopreservation started relatively late in China and lags significantly behind Western countries. Between 2010 and 2020, only 5,055 cases of autologous sperm cryopreservation were accumulated across 23 public sperm banks in China, equivalent to just 2.05 cases per 10,000 males aged 15 to 59 years, which is significantly lower than the rates of 15 to 30 cases observed in Western countries (Liu et al., 2022). In China, cancer was the primary indication, accounting for 66.1% of cases. Fertility preservation for nonmedical reasons (such as unmarried status, separation, or planned vasectomy) constituted only 23% of cases, whereas in Western countries this proportion has increased to 30% to 50% (Li et al., 2018). Moreover, significant regional disparities were observed, with the number of cases in eastern provinces being twice that in central provinces and 1.8 times that in western provinces. Regarding regulations and policies, China prohibits anonymous sperm donation and commercial surrogacy, imposes a maximum sperm storage period of 10 years requiring regular renewals, and mandates that costs be borne by the individuals. In contrast, Western countries generally permit commercial chain operations, anonymous sperm donation, and long-term storage (up to 55 years). Some countries also include the cost of sperm cryopreservation for cancer patients within their national health insurance systems, reflecting a higher degree of marketization (Su et al., 2021).

Limitations

While the study provides valuable insights into public perceptions of vasectomy in China, it has several limitations. First, the sample is limited to posts on Rednote, which may not be fully representative of the entire Chinese population. Rednote is a platform that appeals primarily to younger, urban, and digitally literate individuals, which may skew the data toward a specific demographic. The relatively small dataset size remains a limitation and future studies could expand the sample across multiple platforms to improve generalizability. Second, the study relies on self-reported user-generated content, which could introduce biases, such as selective reporting of positive or negative experiences. In addition, as the data were collected through public posts, there was no control over the accuracy of the medical information shared, and some posts might have contained inaccuracies or misconceptions, particularly regarding the procedure’s reversibility and potential complications. Finally, the study did not explore the underlying motivations for choosing vasectomy beyond what was shared in the posts. Further research could explore these motivations in more depth, particularly by conducting interviews or surveys with individuals who have undergone the procedure.

Conclusion

The Rednote data show that vasectomy is mainly discussed by younger, urban men (often already with children), but also by DINK individuals who reject parenthood. Posts are overwhelmingly positive, with strong engagement, suggesting that on Chinese social media, vasectomy is becoming part of progressive conversations about modern lifestyles, gender equality in reproductive choices, and responsible family planning.

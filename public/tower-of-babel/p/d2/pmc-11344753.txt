MEMS-based meta-emitter with actively tunable radiation power characteristic
Authors: Kunye Li, Yuhao Liang, Yu-Sheng Lin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe propose a meta-emitter based on micro-electro-mechanical system (MEMS) technology. The main structure of the meta-emitter unit cell is composed of four symmetrically split crosses of Au and SiO2 bilayer cantilevers. By changing the size of the cantilevers, this MEMS-based meta-emitter can realize the tunable perfect absorption, and the absorption spectrum is within the longwave infrared (LWIR) wavelength from 8.90 to 11.90 µm. When the surface temperature of the meta-emitter rises, the electrothermal actuation mechanism is performed through the different thermal expansion coefficient (TEC) of the bilayer cantilevers. Therefore, the cantilevers will be bent downward and the bending height of the cantilevers decreases linearly. In such case, the peak value of thermal radiation power can be tuned from the wavelength of 9.52 µm to 10.48 µm when the temperature of meta-emitter is increased from 293 to 1290 K. This proposed MEMS-based meta-emitter is an excellent LWIR light source and has potential application prospects in gas sensing, infrared spectroscopy analysis, medical care and so on.Keywords: MEMS, Metamaterial, Metasurface, Infrared emitter, Tunable filter, Thermal radiation

Introduction

The natural objects with temperature above absolute zero will produce thermal radiation. At room temperature, the peak value of radiation wavelength is around 9.6 µm according to Planck's blackbody radiation law, which is coincidentally in the atmospheric transparent window from the longwave infrared (LWIR) wavelength of 8 µm to 14 µm. Therefore, the thermal emission control of LWIR is particularly important for various infrared applications, such as radiative cooling [1, 2], infrared camouflage [3, 4] and spectral imaging [5–7]. Numerous optical structures have been explored for regulating thermal emission, for example, thin film structures based on epsilon-near-zero (ENZ) materials [8, 9] and gratings [10, 11] or metamaterials [12–17]. Although thermal emission control can be realized by using ENZ materials, its working wavelength and thermal emission characteristics are limited by the availability of ENZ materials in nature [18]. In addition, these thermal radiation sources are broadband in essence [19]. Metamaterials are artificially designed and manufactured subwavelength structures and are not limited by natural materials, which can realize unique optical characteristics, including negative refractive index [20], strong transmission [21] or perfect absorption [22, 23], polarization conversion and phase manipulation [24, 25]. On the other hand, the excellent performances of surface plasmon coupling between metal nanoparticles and dielectric materials in sensing field, photothermal enhancement and thermal radiation control have been verified experimentally [26–28]. Hence, it is exciting to realize control of thermal radiation with multiple degrees of freedom of wavelength, angle, and bandwidth by plasmonic metamaterial-based thermal emitter [29–32].

The thermal emission can be controlled by tailoring the geometric size of metamaterials, but the geometric size, position, and shape of metamaterials cannot be changed once fabricated on rigid substrates. Thus, they cannot be actively tuned [33]. Although multiband or multifunctional thermal emission can be realized in the form of metamaterial arrays [34–36], it will increase the size and complexity of the device. Therefore, the actively tunable metamaterials are generally used in practical applications. To date, the methods of actively tunable metamaterials include but not limited to external electric field [37], light excitation [38], phase change materials [39, 40], and electromechanical control [41–43]. Among these methods, micro-electro-mechanical system (MEMS) technology is an electromechanical controllable method without additional stimulated materials, which can realize various in-plane and out-of-plane mechanical operations according to the requirements of applications [44–48]. The structural configuration of metamaterials can be directly change by using MEMS technology. Thus, this can be used as a perfect platform to change the electromagnetic characteristics of metamaterials and then possess wider tuning range of wavelength [49].

In this study, a meta-emitter with actively tunable peak wavelength of thermal radiation power is proposed by changing the geometric structure of metamaterial through MEMS-based electrothermal actuation mechanism. This MEMS-based meta-emitter can tune the resonant wavelength within the LWIR range from the wavelength of 8.90 µm to 11.90 µm. By applying different direct current (DC) bias voltages to control the temperature, the proposed MEMS-based meta-emitter can tune the thermal radiation power in the wavelength range of 9.52 µm to 10.48 µm. This design realizes the combination of narrow-band thermal radiation and tuning characteristics. It prevents the complex structure of different metamaterial arrays. It has great potentials in realizing compact LWIR light sources, sensing devices, and IR spectrum analysis equipment.

Designs and methods

Figure 1a shows schematic diagram of MEMS-based meta-emitter with actively tunable radiation power characteristic. The proposed device is based on the traditional metal–insulator-metal (MIM) structure, with a continuous Au reflective layer at the bottom and a metamaterial layer atop consisting of four symmetrically split crosses of Au and SiO2 bilayer cantilevers. A quartz (SiO2) substrate is used due to its low thermal conductivity [50], which is beneficial for the thermal radiation to be propagated upward to the cantilevers. The symmetrical structure provides a polarization independent response. The amorphous silicon (Si) with a thickness of 500 nm in the middle is a dielectric layer and used as a sacrificial layer to release the cantilevers. Due to the difference of thermal expansion coefficient (TEC) between Au and SiO2 layers, the Au layer with large TEC is in the state of tensile residual stress, while the SiO2 layer with small TEC is in the state of compressive residual stress. The initial stress makes the bilayer cantilevers deform out-of-plane and then bend upward after releasing the bilayer cantilevers. Figure 1b illustrates the unit cell of MEMS-based meta-emitter after releasing process, and the bending state of the bilayer cantilevers is upward. By applying a DC bias voltage to the bottom Au film, the surface electric current flow induces resistance heat and increases the temperature of the device surface. Therefore, the cantilevers will be bent towards the substrate. When the applied voltage is turned off, the cantilevers will restore to the initial state. The geometric parameters of the MEMS-based meta-emitter without released are indicated in Fig. 1c. The period of the meta-emitter is 5.2 μm, the linewidth of the period frame is 300 nm, and the length and width of four identical cantilevers are l and w, respectively. The length and thickness of the cantilevers affect the upward bending height after the releasing process, which has a significant impact on the tunable range of the MEMS-based meta-emitter. It means the greater bending deformation of the cantilevers that can get the larger tuning range of the resonance spectrum.

To calculate thermal deformation and electromagnetic response, the MEMS-based meta-emitter is simulated by utilizing finite-element simulation software COMSOL Multiphysics. The prestress is applied to the cantilevers to simulate the strain when they are released, and the bending deformation of the released cantilevers is simulated by applying the DC bias voltage in the bottom metal layer as the thermal source. On the other hand, according to Kirchhoff's law of thermal radiation [53], in thermal equilibrium, the emissivity of an object is equal to the absorptivity. Thus, the spectral emissivity of the device can be characterized by its absorption spectrum. Perfect matching layer is applied in z-axis direction, periodic boundary condition is applied in x- and y-axis directions, and the periodic port is set to calculate the spectra response in the wavelength domain.

Results and discussions

Due to the geometric dimension of metamaterial determines the electromagnetic wave band of its response, the size of cantilevers will affect the thermal emission ability of the MEMS-based meta-emitter. Figure 3a shows the absorption spectra of the proposed MEMS-based meta-emitter with different l values, while the cantilever width (w) is kept as constant as 200 nm. MEMS-based meta-emitter exhibits perfect absorption by changing l value from 1.5 to 2.1 μm, and the main resonance is redshifted from the wavelength of 8.90 μm to 11.90 μm. These results show that the proposed MEMS-based meta-emitter can be designed to possess perfect absorption spanned the whole LWIR band by changing l value. Figure 3b shows the absorption spectra of meta-emitter with different w values, while the cantilever length (l) is kept as constant as 1.8 μm. Along with the increment of w value, the absorption peaks are redshifted from the wavelength of 10.11 μm to 10.62 μm and the absorption intensities are decreased slightly. The influence of changing w value on absorption resonance is less than that of changing l value. These results indicate that the single-band perfect absorption can be achieved at the certain wavelength by designing the size of bilayer cantilevers, and then obtained high spectra emissivity.

After the bilayer cantilevers are released, the geometric morphology of the bilayer cantilevers will be bent upward. By applying a DC bias voltage to the bottom Au thin-film of the MEMS-based meta-emitter in the simulation, it can generate resistance heat to rise the temperature. Figure 5a illustrates the relationship of the temperatures and cantilever heights when l and w values are 1.8 μm and 200 nm, respectively. The inserted images show the bending deformations of the MEMS-based meta-emitter under different temperatures. It can be observed that the initial cantilever height is 1.27 μm at normal atmospheric temperature (293 K). Under a small temperature, the cantilever height does not decrease obviously. When the temperature increases from 348 to 1290 K, the cantilever height decreases obviously, and the curvature of cantilever decreases at the same time. The cantilever height is decreased from 1.22 to − 0.18 μm. The negative value indicates that the bending state of cantilever has been below the horizontal state. The vertical moving distance is 1.4 μm. This trend is quite linear, and the correction coefficient (R2) is equals 0.9960. When it is continuously increased the temperature over 1290 K, the cantilevers will continuously bend downward, however, they will burn out due to excessive the resistance heats thermal temperature. Herein, every state of cantilever height corresponds to different electromagnetic response. Figure 5b shows the absorption spectra of the MEMS-based meta-emitter under different temperatures. When the temperature increases from 293 to 1290 K to decrease the cantilever height, the resonant absorption wavelength is redshifted from the wavelength of 9.52 μm to 10.51 μm, and the absorption peak values are kept above 0.93. The tuning range of resonant absorption wavelength is about 1 μm. These results mean that the resonant wavelength can be continuously tuned by increasing the temperature of the meta-emitter. Thus, it can prevent the need to use a variety of metamaterial devices with different feature sizes to achieve the corresponding resonances. This design can further realize the miniaturization and compactness of LWIR devices.

To demonstrate the results of Fig. 5b more intuitively, Fig. 6 shows the electromagnetic field distributions of the MEMS-based meta-emitter at the resonant wavelength of 9.52 μm and 10.51 μm when the temperatures are 293 K and 1290 K, respectively. The E-field is confined to the free end of the cantilevers as shown in Fig. 6a and c, while the magnetic (H) field is confined to the fixed end of the cantilevers as shown in Fig. 6b and d, respectively. Due to the different TEC values of the bilayer cantilever materials, the cantilevers bend downwards and the curvature reduces when the surface temperature of the cantilevers rises, which is equivalent to reducing the gap between the cantilevers and the bottom Au layer and increasing the projection length of the cantilevers in xy-plane. The value of ge decreases and the value of le increases, which augments the equivalent capacitance and inductance. Therefore, the resonance is redshifted according to Eq. (3).

Conclusion

In conclusion, we propose a MEMS-based meta-emitter with actively tunable radiation power characteristic. By designing the size of the bilayer cantilevers, the resonant wavelength can be tuned within the LWIR band. By increasing the temperature to bend the cantilevers downward to substrate, it can realize the tunability of thermal radiation power. There is a large tuning range obtained by optimizing the structure of cantilevers, which has a great impetus to the control of infrared thermal radiation. Meanwhile, this design doesn’t need the use of metamaterial arrays and provides a new way to realize compact infrared devices. Therefore, the proposed meta-emitter has great application potentials in LWIR light source, infrared gas sensing, infrared spectral analysis, medical diagnosis and so on. Besides, the proposed device can be easily extended to other bands by changing the geometrical size of the cantilevers, which shows excellent scalability.

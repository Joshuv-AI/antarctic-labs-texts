Transaortic Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement With Bicuspid Aortic Valve Stenosis and Circumflex Aortic Arch
Authors: Merrina Lan, Matthew C. Henn, Konstantinos Dean Boudoulas, William H. Marshall V.
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTCircumflex aortic arch is a rare congenital anomaly. This case describes transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) via a transaortic approach, and to our knowledge is the first case of TAVR in a patient with circumflex aortic arch.

Case Presentation

A 64‐year‐old man was referred for severe bicuspid aortic valve stenosis and syncope. His medical history included hypertension, cadaveric renal transplant 19 years prior (on immunosuppression with mycophenolate and cyclosporine), chronic kidney disease of the transplanted kidney with baseline serum creatinine ranging from 1.5 to 2.0 mg/dL, recurrent pleural effusions requiring prior thoracentesis, paroxysmal atrial fibrillation, and prior deep vein thrombosis. He also had developmental delay requiring the care of his family but was able to accomplish basic activities of daily living independently, with a good quality of life before the syncope. Given these comorbidities, after multidisciplinary discussions, he was determined to be high surgical risk; therefore, transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) was pursued. Transfemoral and trans‐subclavian arterial approaches were not anatomically feasible due to his circumflex aortic arch (Figure 1A–C, Video 1), and the transcarotid approach was unsuitable due to significant vascular calcification (Figure 1D). Thus, the decision was to perform a transaortic approach under general anesthesia.

A right femoral artery sheath was inserted, and a wire was advanced to the aortic root (Figure 1E) for pigtail placement (Figure 1F) to guide positioning during TAVR. A mini upper hemi‐sternotomy was performed and J‐ed into the right second intercostal space. The pericardium was suspended, and two concentric purse string sutures were placed in the aorta at the site of sheath placement. The valve was crossed, and following valvuloplasty, the 29‐mm Edwards SAPIEN 3 valve was delivered across the aortic valve annulus and deployed following balloon inflation with rapid ventricular pacing (Video 2). Intraoperative transesophageal echocardiogram noted a left ventricular ejection fraction that was > 70%, normal prosthetic valve leaflet movement, no stenosis by Doppler with a mean gradient of 6 mmHg, and no valvular or paravalvular regurgitation. Despite the uncomplicated procedure, the patient experienced recurrence of atrial arrhythmias requiring cardioversion and amiodarone post‐procedurally, as well as recurrences of right‐sided pleural effusion requiring thoracentesis. Transesophageal echocardiogram before cardioversion on postoperative Day 6 showed ejection fraction 65%–70%, TAVR in place with trace paravalvular regurgitation and no stenosis. He was discharged on postoperative Day 21.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first report of a transaortic TAVR in a patient with a circumflex aortic arch. Though only a single case, circumflex aortic arch is uncharted territory for many surgeons due to its extremely rare occurance [1]. While there is selection bias for transaortic access in this case, as outlined, other access sites were evaluated and felt to be prohibitive given anatomy and vascular calcifications. Although transfemoral TAVR demonstrates optimal outcome [2], technical proficiency can be obtained with transaortic access, making this a viable option for high‐risk patients without other options for access and rare congenital anomalies such as circumflex aortic arch.

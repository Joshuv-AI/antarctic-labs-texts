The risk factors for long term cardiovascular symptoms in patients after coronavirus disease 2019 infection
Authors: Tingting Liu, Haofei Kang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPresently, numerous studies have demonstrated that long-term cardiovascular changes after Coronavirus Disease 2019(COVID-19) infection should be considered. The study was aimed to explore the risk factors for post COVID-19 long-term cardiovascular symptoms.MethodsThis retrospective observational cross-sectional study involved 204 COVID-19 patients who were admitted to Yantaishan Hospital from January 1, 2023 to January 31, 2023. Demographic and laboratory data were collected and compared between patients who experienced post COVID-19 long-term cardiovascular symptoms and those who did not. Logistic regression analysis was used to identify the risk factors associated with the occurrence of post COVID-19 long-term cardiovascular symptoms.ResultsFifty-two participants presented Post COVID-19 cardiovascular symptoms, while the remaining 152 individuals did not show any such symptoms including chest pain, chest tightness, shortness of breath, palpitations, dyspnea, exercise intolerance, and postural tachycardia syndrome. In comparison to the group without post COVID-19 long-term cardiovascular symptoms, the group with post COVID-19 long-term cardiovascular symptoms exhibited a significantly higher prevalence of anxiety and depression (25.0% vs. 4.6%, p = 0.000), as well as significantly elevated C-reactive protein (42.3 mg/L vs. 20.3 mg/L, p = 0.014) and D-dimer (0.3 mg/L vs. 0.22 mg/L, p = 0.024). Anxiety and depression (odds ratio [OR] = 6.403, 95% confidence interval [CI]:2.180–18.809, p = 0.001), C-reactive protein (OR = 1.009, 95%CI:1.003–1.015, p = 0.006), D-dimer (OR = 1.455, 95%CI:1.004–2.109, p = 0.048), and LDL-C (OR = 1.780, 95%CI:1.043–3.040, p = 0.035) were identified as independent risk factors for post COVID-19 long-term cardiovascular symptoms.ConclusionAnxiety and depression, C-reactive protein, D-dimer, and LDL-C levels are associated with the development of post COVID-19 long-term cardiovascular symptoms.Keywords: Cardiovascular, risk factor, COVID-19, long COVID

Introduction

Coronavirus Disease 2019 (COVID-19) has emerged as a significant global healthcare crisis, resulting in both mortality and illness. This disease is caused by SARS-CoV-2, an RNA virus belonging to the severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus family. Although its primary impact is on the respiratory system, it has also been observed to affect the cardiovascular system [1]. Numerous cardiovascular complications have been associated with COVID-19, including myocarditis, acute coronary syndrome, myocardial injury, arrhythmias, heart failure, venous and arterial thrombotic events, stroke, and coagulopathy [2–5]. These findings have been reported in various studies and publications. The infection of COVID-19 can also lead to post COVID-19 cardiovascular symptoms (PCCVS) including chest pain, chest tightness, shortness of breath, palpitations, dyspnea, exercise intolerance, postural tachycardia syndrome, and accompanying symptoms such as fatigue and sleep disorders [6–8]. Long COVID is defined as a condition occurring in patients with SARS-CoV-2 infection, developing 3 months from the onset of symptoms, lasting for a minimum of two months, and lacking explanation through other diseases [9,10].

Some studies have found that patients suffering from COVID-19 and experiencing respiratory distress exhibit a continual elevation in D-dimer and C-reactive protein levels for up to eight weeks [11]. Persistent systemic inflammation appears to be the primary factor contributing to cardiovascular symptoms in COVID-19 patients appears to be persistent systemic inflammation. The presence of prolonged inflammation indicates potential organ damage and an increased likelihood of complications [12]. Presently, the presumed mechanisms underlying the post COVID-19 state include inflammation, immune activation, viral persistence, endothelial dysfunction, and a hypercoagulable state [13,14]. However, the underlying pathophysiological mechanism of long COVID remains incompletely understood. Available data on biomarkers PCCVS showed conflicting results. The coagulation, inflammatory and cardiac injury marker concentrations, including amino-terminal pro-B-type-natriuretic-peptide, high-sensitive cardiac troponin I, C-reactive protein, are not specific and can’t highly predict the long COVID [15,16]. Furthermore, it is unclear whether long-term cardiovascular symptoms can be solely attributed to COVID-19. A recent study, which examined a large cohort of 480,000 non-hospitalized COVID-19 patients and 1.9 million individuals without COVID-19, revealed that 5.4% of patients meeting the WHO definition of post COVID-19 status reported at least one symptom, while 4.3% of those without COVID-19 also reported at least one symptom [17]. Additionally, it is imperative to acquire a more comprehensive understanding of the risk factors that contribute to the emergence of long COVID.

This study aimed to investigate the factors or characteristics that influence the persistence of cardiovascular symptoms in individuals with COVID-19. Consequently, we conducted a retrospective observational cross-sectional study to discern the disparities between patients experiencing long-term cardiovascular symptoms and those who did not, and to examine the independent risk factors associated with post COVID-19 long-term cardiovascular symptoms.

Materials and methods

Subjects

This retrospective observational cross-sectional study was conducted in the cardiology and respiratory departments of Yantaishan Hospital, a city-owned hospital located in Shandong Province. The study included consecutive patients aged from 16 to 90 years who were admitted to the hospital due to Covid-19 disease from January 1, 2023 to January 31, 2023. The diagnosis of COVID-19 in all patients was established according to the Diagnosis and Treatment Protocol for new coronavirus infection, as released by the National Health Commission of the People’s Republic of China. COVID-19 diagnosis was confirmed through a positive outcome in real-time reverse transcriptase-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) test. Patients who died during hospitalization, those without reachable telephone contact, or individuals with incomplete data were excluded from the study. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Yantaishan Hospital (approval number: LL-2023-071-L). The requirement for written informed consent was waived owing to the retrospective observational design.

Data collection

The attending physician obtained data on demographic characteristics, comorbidities, and laboratory examinations using Jiahe electronic medical record system. Demographic characteristics included sex, age, smoking history, and family history of heart disease. The comorbidities included chronic heart disease, diabetes mellitus, and hypertension. Laboratory examinations included white blood cell count, neutrophil percentage, C-reactive protein level, interleukin-6 level, procalcitonin level, hemoglobin level, D-dimer level, troponin-I level, N-terminal brain natriuretic peptide precursor (NT-proBNP) level, and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) level.

Follow up

From May 1, 2023 to May 31, 2023, a telephone interview was conducted to follow up with the patients. The patients were categorized into two groups, namely, the PCCVS and non-PCCVS groups, based on the presence or absence of cardiovascular symptoms three months after discharge. Cardiovascular symptoms included chest pain, chest tightness, shortness of breath, palpitations, dyspnea, exercise intolerance, and postural tachycardia syndrome. To assess emotional well-being, including anxiety and depression, the questionnaire of General Anxiety Disorder (GAD-7) and Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-9) were employed by telephone interview.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 23.0 (SPSS Inc., IBM, Armonk, NY), for which a copyright license had been obtained. Continuous variables were reported as either mean (standard deviation [SD]) or median (interquartile range [IQR]), depending on their distribution, as determined by the Kolmogorov-Smirnov normality test and Bartlett’s test for homogeneity of variance. Categorical variables were presented as counts and percentages. To compare the disparities between the two groups, two-tailed independent Student’s t-tests were employed when the data exhibited a normal distribution; otherwise, the Mann-Whitney U test was utilized. The chi-square test was used to compare proportions of categorical variables. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05 (2-sided) was used to determine statistical significance. Logistic regression analysis was conducted to investigate the factors influencing the PCCVS. Univariate logistic regression analysis was used to select the significant factors, with variables having a P-value of less than 0.05, which were included in the multivariate logistic regression analysis to identify independent risk factors for PCCVS. Statistical significance was defined as a P-value of less than 0.05.

Results

Patient’s clinical characteristics subjects

A cohort of 220 individuals diagnosed with COVID-19 was enrolled in this study. Subsequently, 4 participants were excluded after applying the exclusion criteria, while an additional 12 patients were lost to follow-up. Among the remaining 204 subjects, 52 were positively identified as exhibiting Post COVID-19 cardiovascular symptoms, whereas the remaining 152 individuals did not display such symptoms (shown in Figure 1). The impact factors for PCCVS

Univariate logistic regression analysis was used to select the variables. Of the 18 variables listed in Table 2, five exhibited significant p-values (p ≤ 0.05) and were included in the multiple logistic regression analysis (shown in Figure 2). These variables were anxiety and depression (p = 0.000), neutrophil percent (p = 0.043), C-reactive protein level (p = 0.015), D-dimer level (p = 0.016), and LDL-C level (p = 0.013). In the multivariate logistic regression analysis, anxiety and depression (OR = 6.403, 95%CI:2.180, 18.809, p = 0.001), C-reactive protein level (OR = 1.009, 95%CI:1.003, 1.015, p = 0.006), D-dimer level (OR = 1.455, 95%CI:1.004, 2.109, p = 0.048), and LDL-C level (OR = 1.780, 95%CI:1.043–3.040, p = 0.035) were identified as independent risk factors for PCCVS.

The findings of this study indicate that individuals with PCCVS exhibit a greater prevalence of anxiety and depression, as well as elevated levels of C-reactive protein, D-dimer, and LDL-C, in comparison to the control group. Additionally, anxiety and depression, C-reactive protein, D-dimer, and LDL-C levels independently contributed to the development of PCCVS.

Discussion

The primary objective of our current investigation was to identify independent risk factors for PCCVS. Both univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were conducted to elucidate the individual contributions of these factors. Our comprehensive analysis indicated that anxiety and depression, C-reactive protein, D-dimer, and LDL-C were significant factors in the development of PCCVS. The patients with PCCVS showed a higher proportion of anxiety and depression and a higher level of C-reactive protein, D-dimer level, and LDL-C than those without PCCVS.

The findings of our study indicate a positive correlation between elevated C-reactive protein (CRP) or D-dimer levels and the occurrence of PCCVS. This association can be attributed to the significant involvement of inflammation and coagulation mechanisms in the development of PCCVS. CRP, a well-established inflammatory marker, is noticeably elevated in individuals with COVID-19, while D-dimer serves as an indicator of fibrin formation and breakdown resulting from tissue injury and inflammation. Consequently, we hypothesized that the presence of myocardial injury and prolonged symptoms following COVID-19 infection can be attributed to the presence of hyperinflammatory and hypercoagulable states.

Multiple clinical studies have extensively investigated the predictive and prognostic significance of CRP and D-dimer levels, thus corroborating our findings. Previous research has indicated that elevated plasma D-dimer levels are associated with higher mortality rates in individuals with COVID-19 [18–21]. Similarly, increased CRP levels have been linked to increased mortality in hospitalized COVID-19 patients [22]. Furthermore, it has been reported that elevated CRP levels are associated with elevated troponin levels and myocardial damage [23]. However, the available research results about the biomarker of PCCVS were conflicting [16,24]. Some recent research showed patients who exhibit elevated levels of CRP during the acute phase of Covid-19 are susceptible to developing cardiac sequelae [25–27]. Consistent with these findings, our study identified CRP and D-dimer levels as promising prognostic markers associated with myocardial injury and prolonged symptoms.

It should be noted that patients with anxiety and depression have an increased likelihood of experiencing PCCVS. It is plausible to hypothesize that not all symptoms associated with PCCVS can be solely attributed to the presence of COVID-19. These symptoms may be partially influenced by emotional conditions such as depression and anxiety. Therefore, it is advisable to conduct a brief evaluation of the mental and cognitive well-being of individuals who have recovered from COVID-19 to provide them with suitable psychological counseling.

Furthermore, our study revealed a significant association between elevated LDL-C levels and PCCVS. This relationship can be attributed, in part, to the discontinuation of guideline-directed medical therapy during the epidemic, resulting in the inadequate management of cardiovascular disease risk factors.

In conclusion, our study suggests that there is an association between long-term cardiovascular symptoms following COVID-19 and various risk factors such as inflammation, hypercoagulability, psychological conditions, and inadequate management of traditional cardiovascular disease risk factors. Although the exact mechanisms underlying this relationship are speculative, we believe that our findings contribute to our understanding of the potential cardiac impact of COVID-19. Additionally, our results emphasize the significance of psychological support and effective control of cardiovascular risk factors during the epidemic period. The aforementioned data strongly indicate the necessity of providing earlier and more aggressive interventions to mitigate potential cardiovascular complications associated with COVID-19 in the foreseeable future.

Our study had certain limitations that should be acknowledged. First, as an observational study was conducted retrospectively, we were unable to perform further examinations, such as coronary angiography and echocardiography, in all symptomatic patients to ascertain the underlying cause of their symptoms. Second, the limited sample size of our study raises the possibility that our findings may be attributable to random chance. Therefore, large-scale studies with extended follow-up periods are required to validate our results.

Conclusion

We found a strong relationship between anxiety and depression, C-reactive protein, D-dimer, LDL-C levels, and the development of post COVID-19 long-term cardiovascular symptoms. It is suggested that these cardiovascular sequelae are associated with multiple factors such as inflammation, hypercoagulability, psychological conditions, and primary cardiovascular disease. This can contribute to explore the pathophysiological origins of the long COVID and can help identify patients at high risk of PCCVS.

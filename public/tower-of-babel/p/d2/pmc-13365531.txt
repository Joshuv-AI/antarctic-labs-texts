Preclinical and clinical efficacy of the HDAC6 inhibitor ricolinostat in combination with BCR pathway inhibitors in relapsed/refractory chronic lymphocytic leukemia
Authors: Stephen P. Martindale, Roberta Santos Azevedo, Drew A. Dear Editor,

Despite the impact of targeted therapies such as BTK and BCL2 inhibitors in chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), drug resistance and relapse remain major challenges, underscoring the need for alternative therapies with unique mechanisms of action [1–5]. Ricolinostat is a potent HDAC6 inhibitor that increases α-tubulin and histone H3 acetylation, the latter via class I HDAC inhibition [6, 7]. In vitro and in vivo studies have demonstrated that HDAC inhibitors induce apoptosis in CLL cells and reduce tumor burden [8, 9]. In this study, we report the preclinical and clinical efficacy of ricolinostat in combination with B-cell receptor (BCR) pathway inhibitors ibrutinib and idelalisib in relapsed/refractory (R/R) CLL.

Previous in vitro experiments on primary cells from four CLL patients identified ricolinostat (ACY-1215) as the most potent among five HDAC6 inhibitors tested across 24, 48, and 72-hour drug incubations (Supplementary Fig. 1). In cells from five additional CLL patients (Supplementary Table 1), ricolinostat monotherapy demonstrated superior cytotoxicity over idelalisib and ibrutinib, reducing median viability by 33% and 43.8% more at 48-hour drug incubation, respectively. Combining ricolinostat with either idelalisib or ibrutinib significantly inhibited cell viability, achieving median reductions in viability by 12.4% and 15.4% more at 48-hours, respectively, compared to ricolinostat monotherapy (Fig. 1A, Supplementary Fig. 2). In cells from seven CLL patients (Supplementary Table 1), ricolinostat demonstrated significantly greater proliferation inhibition than idelalisib or ibrutinib, with median differences of 8.4% and 16.1%, respectively. Combining ricolinostat with idelalisib or ibrutinib yielded an additional 20.4% and 22.4% median proliferation inhibition, respectively, compared to ricolinostat monotherapy (Fig. 1B, C). We next examined α-tubulin and histone H3 acetylation, two key targets of HDAC inhibition, in cells from two CLL patients following a time course treatment with ricolinostat alone and in combination with BCRis. Ricolinostat induced rapid and sustained α-tubulin acetylation within 1 hour, both as monotherapy and in combination. Additionally, histone H3 acetylation was observed within 2 hours, confirming the known class 1 HDAC inhibitory activity of ricolinostat (Fig. 1D) [10]. Between September 2016 and June 2019, three R/R CLL patients were treated with ricolinostat 80 mg daily in 28-day cycles: two in combination with idelalisib 150 mg twice daily and one with ibrutinib 420 mg daily. Baseline and disease characteristics are detailed in Supplementary Table 2. All three patients had unmutated immunoglobulin heavy chain variable region (IGHV) status, and two had complex karyotype with TP53 mutation at the time of study enrollment. The median number of prior lines of therapy was 2 (range, 2–3), and all had received chemoimmunotherapy. Both patients receiving the idelalisib combination had prior ibrutinib exposure; one was ibrutinib-refractory.

Adverse events are summarized in Supplementary Table 3; ricolinostat was well tolerated at 80 mg daily with no grade 4–5 adverse events observed, consistent with its established safety profile. Trial patient 1 permanently discontinued idelalisib on day 17 of cycle 1 due to immune-mediated rash and hepatotoxicity, which was not attributable to ricolinostat. Trial patient 2 had grade 3 neutropenia and diarrhea related to idelalisib. Both trial patient 1 and 2 receiving the idelalisib combination required glucocorticoids for these immune-mediated toxicities [11, 12]. Trial patient 3, treated with the ricolinostat and ibrutinib combination, developed grade 3 pneumonitis in the setting of radiation treatment for breast cancer, leading to treatment discontinuation; a grade 2 hypertension event was attributed to ibrutinib, which was otherwise well tolerated for 33 months.

The overall response rate (ORR) was 66.7%. Trial patient 1, who remained on ricolinostat monotherapy after discontinuing idelalisib, maintained stable disease throughout treatment. Trial patient 2 treated with ricolinostat and idelalisib achieved a partial response with lymphocytosis, with a response duration of 24 months. Trial patient 3, despite a TP53 mutation, complex karyotype, and a high disease burden, achieved a complete response after 24 cycles of therapy. Bone marrow MRD was positive at 0.03% at treatment discontinuation; however, peripheral blood MRD was negative at this timepoint and 20 months after stopping therapy. Although only three patients were treated in this trial due to premature termination of drug supply by the pharmaceutical sponsor, the ORRs exceeded those typically reported for R/R CLL at that time, and the duration of response surpassed responses seen with idelalisib in combination with rituximab in R/R CLL.

As expected, all patients experienced BCRi-induced lymphocytosis during cycle 1 of therapy (Supplementary Fig. 3). Absolute lymphocyte count (ALC) declined during Cycle 2 for patients 1 and 2, while lymphocytosis in patient 3 persisted until normalizing after Cycle 13. Long-term follow-up revealed that patients 1 and 2 subsequently underwent allogeneic stem cell transplantation at 27 and 51 months after disease progression, respectively; patient 2 developed CNS involvement at disease progression. Patient 3 remains in complete response for over 90 months following treatment discontinuation. All patients were alive at last follow-up.

Cells from the trial patients were collected and cryopreserved at specific time points including TP1, prior to any treatment; TP2, 1-hour post-ricolinostat; TP3, 2-hour post-ricolinostat but prior to ibrutinib or idelalisib treatment; TP4, 3-hour post-ricolinostat that coincided with 1-hour post-ibrutinib or post-idelalisib and subsequent treatment cycles. We first investigated cell proliferation in vitro using combinations that recapitulated the treatment regimens the trial patients received in vivo. Despite adverse effects experienced by trial patient 1, leading to the discontinuation of idelalisib, we treated the patient’s cells in vitro with the combination of both ricolinostat and idelalisib to simulate the hypothetical scenario of this patient remaining on both treatments. We observed a consistently pronounced and sustained reduction in cell proliferation across most treatment regimens and timepoints. Notably, ricolinostat and combinations maintained a significant inhibitory effect on cell proliferation, whereas idelalisib and ibrutinib as monotherapies showed less pronounced inhibition at certain timepoints (Supplementary Fig. 4).

We also evaluated the impact of the combinations on modulating epigenetic modifications in α-tubulin and histone H3 in CLL cells from the three trial patients over the course of their clinical trial. In the absence of in vitro treatment, our western blots and densitometry analysis revealed a stark upregulation in α-tubulin acetylation from TP1 to TP4 in trial patients 1 and 2, with a steady increase observed even after these timepoints. Interestingly, trial patient 3 exhibited no consistent change in α-tubulin acetylation and demonstrated changes in total α-tubulin protein levels. Importantly, all three trial patients showed a gradual and sustained increase in histone H3 acetylation across all timepoints. Collectively, these findings illustrate the in vivo activity of ricolinostat in modulating epigenetic modifications (Fig. 1E, Supplementary Fig. 5).

To investigate the impact of ricolinostat and idelalisib on the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, we examined phospho-AKT levels in CLL cells from trial patients 1 and 2. Consistent with the persistent stable disease observed in trial patient 1, who stopped idelalisib quite early, we observed an appreciable increase in phospho-AKT levels over all timepoints tested. Activation of the PI3K/AKT pathway may contribute to the maintenance of stable disease in this patient. In contrast, trial patient 2 exhibited an overall decrease in phospho-AKT levels. To assess the effects of ricolinostat and ibrutinib on the BCR signaling pathway, we investigated phospho-ERK expression in CLL cells from trial patient 3. With the exception of one timepoint, phospho-ERK levels were significantly and durably decreased (Fig. 1E, Supplementary Fig. 5).

We also assessed dynamic effects on chromatin accessibility in purified tumor cells using ATAC-seq [13, 14]. Global accessibility changes in tumor cells have previously been observed in cutaneous T-cell lymphoma (CTCL) patients treated with first-generation HDAC inhibitors and correlated with response [15]. Here, we observed a broad increase in ATAC-seq signal during the first full cycle of treatment in each patient (C1D15 in trial patient 1, C1D8 and C1D22 in trial patient 2, C1D22 in trial patient 3) (Fig. 2A). In all three trial patients, this early chromatin opening preceded a decrease in peripheral blood absolute lymphocyte count (ALC) (Fig. 2B). Increased accessibility spanned broadly at regulatory elements including promoters and more distal regions (Fig. 2C). Whereas the BCL2 locus exhibited relative opening, accessibility at BTK remained low, consistent with BCRi-mediated suppression of BCR signaling. Notably, early global opening with decreasing ALC aligned with partial and complete responses in trial patients 2 and 3, respectively, whereas trial patient 1 showed early global opening with a transient decrease in ALC, followed by sustained accessibility and increasing ALC tracking with disease progression. In summary, although HDAC inhibition has been investigated as a therapeutic strategy in CLL for many years with historically limited clinical success, this study demonstrates that the HDAC6 inhibitor ricolinostat exerts potent activity in primary CLL cells, both as a single agent and in combination with BCRis. These findings translated into clinical responses in patients with R/R CLL treated with this combination. Overall, the data highlight the therapeutic potential of concurrently targeting epigenetic regulation and BCR signaling pathways in CLL. Further investigations in a larger cohort of patients are needed to validate the clinical efficacy and mechanistic impact of HDAC6 inhibition in CLL.

Comparative Analysis of Laser-Activated and Ultrasonic Irrigation on Postoperative Pain in Root Canal Therapy: A Systematic Review
Authors: Aditi Kanitkar, Kapil Ramesh Jadhav, Anandamoy Bagchi, Anil Kumar Pulidindi, Tony Mathew, Praveen Kumar Varma Datla
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTTo compare the efficacy of laser-activated irrigation (LAI) and ultrasonic irrigation (UI) in reducing postoperative pain during root canal therapy (RCT). A systematic review was conducted using eight randomized controlled trials published between 2010 and 2025. Studies included evaluated LAI and UI techniques, focusing on postoperative pain reduction, pain intensity measured using the Visual Analog Scale (VAS), and analgesic intake. Comparisons with conventional needle irrigation (CNI) were also included. Statistical analyses of pain scores and analgesic consumption were reviewed. Both LAI and UI demonstrated significant reductions in postoperative pain compared to CNI. LAI was more effective in reducing pain in the first 6–12 h postoperatively, attributed to its superior irrigant penetration and photoacoustic streaming capabilities. UI also showed significant pain reduction, particularly in terms of debris removal and biofilm disruption, with pain scores converging with LAI by 24–48 h. Analgesic intake was lower in LAI patients during the immediate postoperative period. No adverse effects were reported with either technique. LAI and UI are effective irrigation activation techniques for minimizing postoperative pain in RCT, with LAI providing faster initial pain relief. These advanced techniques enhance patient outcomes and offer superior clinical efficacy compared to conventional methods. Further research is recommended to optimize their clinical application.

INTRODUCTION

Postoperative pain is a common concern in root canal therapy (RCT), significantly affecting patient comfort and treatment outcomes. The irrigation protocol during endodontic procedures plays a crucial role in reducing microbial load, tissue remnants, and debris, thereby minimizing inflammation and pain after treatment. Conventional irrigation techniques, though widely used, may not achieve adequate penetration into the complex anatomy of the root canal system, leading to suboptimal cleaning and potential postoperative discomfort.[12345]

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This systematic review followed the PRISMA guidelines to ensure transparency and rigor. A comprehensive literature search was conducted across PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library databases for studies published between 2010 and 2025. Keywords used included “laser-activated irrigation,” “ultrasonic irrigation,” “postoperative pain,” and “root canal therapy.” Boolean operators (AND/OR) were applied to refine the search strategy. Only peer-reviewed studies comparing LAI and UI concerning postoperative pain in RCT were included.

The inclusion criteria were root canal therapy (RCTs) and clinical trials evaluating postoperative pain with both techniques, involving patients aged 18 years or older, with clearly reported pain assessment using a validated pain scale. Exclusion criteria included studies not published in English, animal studies, and those lacking direct comparisons between LAI and UI.

Data extraction was independently performed by two reviewers, focusing on sample size, irrigation protocols, pain measurement methods, follow-up periods, and outcomes. Risk of bias was assessed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool for RCTs. Disagreements were resolved through consensus or third-party adjudication. Statistical analysis was conducted where possible, using a random-effects model for meta-analysis, and results were presented as mean differences with 95% confidence intervals.

The studies spanned a sample size range of 60 to 170 participants, with pain assessed using the Visual Analog Scale (VAS) at multiple time intervals (6, 12, 24, and 48 h). DISCUSSION

Mechanisms of pain reduction

LAI and UI enhance irrigant activation through distinct mechanisms, leading to superior debridement and disinfection. LAI utilizes photoacoustic streaming and cavitation induced by laser pulses, improving penetration into complex canal anatomies and ensuring effective smear layer and biofilm removal.[3] In contrast, UI employs acoustic streaming and cavitation generated by ultrasonic energy, disrupting bacterial biofilms and debris while minimizing irrigant extrusion into the periapical tissues.[4] These advanced techniques mitigate inflammation and tissue damage, both key contributors to postoperative pain.

The review revealed that LAI demonstrated faster pain reduction within the first 6–12 h compared to UI. This may be attributed to the more aggressive fluid dynamics and deeper irrigant penetration achieved with LAI, particularly in the apical third of the root canal.[56] However, by 24–48 h, pain scores between LAI and UI converged, suggesting that both techniques are equally effective in reducing prolonged inflammatory responses.

Comparison with conventional irrigation

CNI, the most commonly employed technique in endodontics, has limitations in effectively cleaning intricate areas such as isthmuses and apical ramifications.[7] The limited penetration depth of irrigants using conventional methods often leaves biofilm and debris untouched, contributing to higher incidences of postoperative pain.[8] This review consistently found that both LAI and UI significantly outperformed conventional irrigation in pain reduction across all time intervals, underscoring their superior clinical efficacy.

Pain assessment and analgesic use

The studies included in this review assessed pain using the VAS, a widely accepted and reliable tool for measuring subjective pain experiences.[6] Patients treated with LAI and UI reported lower pain scores and reduced analgesic intake compared to those undergoing conventional irrigation. Interestingly, LAI patients exhibited the lowest analgesic consumption during the first 12 h post-treatment, further highlighting the technique’s effectiveness in early pain management.[9]

Despite these benefits, it is important to note that pain perception is subjective and influenced by various factors, including preoperative pain levels, patient anxiety, and individual pain thresholds. Therefore, while LAI and UI provide measurable improvements in pain outcomes, the variability in patient responses should be considered in clinical decision-making.

Clinical implications

The ability of LAI and UI to reduce postoperative pain while enhancing root canal disinfection makes them valuable adjuncts to conventional endodontic protocols. For clinicians, adopting these advanced techniques may improve patient satisfaction and treatment outcomes. Furthermore, the reduction in analgesic consumption associated with LAI and UI offers additional benefits, particularly for patients with contraindications to non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs or those seeking to minimize medication use.[10]

CONCLUSIONS

In conclusion, both LAI and UI are highly effective in reducing postoperative pain in RCT, with LAI offering a marginally faster onset of pain relief. These advanced irrigation techniques surpass conventional methods in their ability to clean complex canal anatomies and minimize inflammatory responses. By incorporating LAI and UI into routine endodontic practice, clinicians can enhance patient outcomes and improve the overall success of root canal treatments.

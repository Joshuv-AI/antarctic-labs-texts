Gender-affirming medical and surgical treatment experience in a multi-specialty transgender clinic from Tamil Nadu
Authors: Subbiah Sridhar, Sinju Sankar, Aram Subramanian, Gayathiri Murugan, Gnanasekaran Thangavel, Govindaraju Amutha, Palaniappan Sreenivasan, Rathinavel Andiappan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPurpose:Transgender healthcare access is limited in India despite the availability of many healthcare professionals and tertiary care hospitals. We aimed to study the psycho-social, clinical, Gender-Affirming Hormone Therapy (GAHT), and Gender-Affirming Surgical (GAS) procedure experience in a multi-specialty transgender clinic.Methods:This is an ambispective cohort study conducted over 3 years and 5 months in gender incongruence persons, who wished to undergo GAHT and GAS procedures. All were treated with appropriate GAHT before undergoing GAS. The psychological aspects were assessed by the Gender Preoccupation and Stability Questionnaire (GPSQ) and Becks Depression Inventory Questionnaire (BDI) before and 6 months after GAHT and GAS procedures.Results:A total of 842 transgender individuals were studied. The mean age at presentation was 22 ± 6 for transgender females and 21 ± 5 for transgender males. A total of 220 GAS procedures were performed and the bilateral mastectomy was the most performed GAS procedure, in 65% (143 out of 220), followed by breast augmentation surgeries in 26% (59 out of 220). The other less commonly performed procedures were hysterectomy and bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy in 4% (9 out of 220), penectomy and orchidectomy in 2.2% (5 out of 220) and vaginoplasty in 1.8% (4 out of 220).Conclusion:Both GAHT and GAS procedures had improved the gender dysphoria and overall well-being of gender-incongruent individuals. The wide acceptance of transgender multi-speciality clinics is a convivial trend among transgender individuals to improve their healthcare access in India.

Introduction

Transgender are individuals with gender dysphoria, whose gender identity is different from the gender assigned at birth. Trans female (TF)/Transgender female/Male to female (MtF) refers to an individual with male gender at birth, who wishes to express or identify as female.[1] Similarly, Transgender male (TM)/Trans male/Female to Male (FtM) refers to an individual with a female gender assignment at birth, who wants to express or identify as male. Failure to identify gender dysphoria may lead to various mental health issues like emotional and behavioural disturbances, stress, depression, anxiety, and suicidal tendencies. Despite more than five hundred thousand transgender populations in India, there are very limited hospitals that provide holistic care that includes psychosocial, medical, and surgical procedures.[2] Due to limited access and awareness about the benefits of gender-affirming hormonal therapy (GAHT), most of the transgenders directly undergo irreversible gender affirming surgical (GAS) procedures by non-medical professionals. This highlights the urgent need for primary care physician to early identify, providing appropriate counselling, ensuring seamless continuity of care, as well as coordinating the referrals to appropriate centre where multi-specialty transgender clinic is functioning.

The transgender persons (protection of Rights) Act was released in the Gazette of India in 2019; subsequently, Transgender multi-disciplinary clinics were started in various tertiary care hospitals and medical colleges throughout India, to provide healthcare needs to transgender as per the World Professional Association for Transgender Health (WPATH) recommendations.[34]

Government Rajaji Hospital is one of the tertiary care referral hospitals in Tamil Nadu, where transgender multi-disciplinary and Gender Guidance clinic was established in 2021 under the auspices of the National Health Mission (NHM) of the Government of Tamil Nadu. Due to the paucity of data regarding transgender healthcare services in India, we aimed to publish our multi-specialty transgender clinic experience.

Materials and Methods

This is an ambispective cohort study conducted over a period of 3.5 years (42 months) (between June 2021 and December 2024) in a Transgender Clinic of Government Rajaji Hospital, Madurai, a 4100-bed tertiary care referral centre. The outpatient clinic is functioning every Thursday with various specialists including a Psychiatrist, Endocrinologist, Plastic and reconstructive surgeon, Urologist, Gynaecologist and Dermatologist under one roof that takes care of the entire transgender health [Figure 1]. Transgender individuals, who fulfil the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM5) diagnostic criteria were included in the study.[5] The exclusion criteria were gender incongruence with severe mental illness requiring anti-psychotic drugs, cardiovascular diseases, alcohol abuse or drug dependency and contraindications to GAHT.[6]

All of them were evaluated regarding their gender identity, gender expression and sexual orientation at the initial visit. The socio-economic details were collected including education, occupation, living environment, and dress as well as hair pattern. Psychological aspects were assessed by GPSQ and BDI scale before and after six months of GAHT.[7] The GAHT was initiated as per the WPATH as well as Endocrine Society guidelines.[46] All the transgender individuals, who wished to undergo GAHT and GAS were instructed to apply in the national portal for transgender persons and get a TG ID card through the website www.transgender.dosje.gov.in as per the Government of India (GOI) gazette notification.

The detailed clinical examination including the body mass index and waist circumference, and blood pressure were monitored and the genital examination to assess the sexual maturity and the presence of atypical genitalia (Intersex/Disorders of Sexual Development and Differentiation, DSD).[8] All were subjected to undergo routine blood investigations, which included complete blood count with packed cell volume, fasting plasma glucose and lipid profile, liver function test, and renal function test. The hormonal investigations included were Thyroid stimulating hormone (TSH), free thyroxine (FT4), prolactin, luteinizing hormone (LH), Follicle stimulating hormone (FSH), Testosterone, and Estradiol by using Roche Cobas e411 Electrochemi-luminescence immunoassay (ECLIA) method. The voluntary counselling and testing (VCTC) and viral markers including HBsAg, anti HCV and HIV were done for all individuals who visited the clinic.

Transgender females were treated with oral oestradiol valerate starting with 1 mg/day and slowly escalating up to 6 mg for breast augmentation, to suppress penile erections and to reduce the unwanted facial and body hair.[6] In addition, they were treated with anti-androgens, spironolactone 50 mg and increased up to a maximum dose of 200 mg/day depending on the clinical response.[9] Transgender males were treated with either injection Testosterone enanthate or Testosterone undecanoate.[6] Testosterone enanthate was started with a dose of 100 mg once in 2–3 weeks and titrated into 250 mg based on serum testosterone level after 3 months or with Testosterone undecanoate 1000 mg once in every 3 months as GAHT.[10] Those who were less than 18 years of age who visited the clinic were treated with pubertal suppression using GnRH agonists, and injection leuprolide acetate 3.75 mg once monthly.

All the GAS procedures were performed in gender incongruence individuals more than 18 years with a minimum of 6 months of GAHT. The breast augmentation surgeries for transgender females and bilateral mastectomy with nipple reconstruction for trans males were performed by plastic surgeons. Orchidectomy and penectomy was performed by urologists. For transgender males, who wished to remove ovaries and uterus, bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy and hysterectomy were performed by gynaecologists.

All the GAHT were given to free of cost to all transgender and the GAS procedures including the breast implant cost was covered under the Chief Minister's Comprehensive Health Insurance Scheme (CMCHIS)/part of Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (PMJAAY) as a provision for coverage of medical expenses for sex reassignment surgery. The Institutional Ethical Committee approved the study protocol, CDSCO Reg No. ECR/1365/Inst/TN/2020 and DHR Reg No. EC/NEW/INST/2022/TN/0059, and written informed consent was obtained before GAHT and GAS.

Statistical analysis

All categorical variables were expressed in actual numbers and percentages and the continuous variables as mean ± standard deviation or median ± standard deviation. The categorical variables were compared using the χ2 test, and the Kruskal-Walli’s test, whereas continuous variables were compared using an independent t-test in normally distributed data and Mann–Whitney U tests in non-normally distributed data. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered significant. All statistical analyses were done with SPSS version 29.0.

Results

Over a 3.5-year study period, 842 transgender individuals attended the multi-speciality clinic from various districts of Tamil Nadu, of which 580 were trans males and 262 were trans females. The literacy rate was higher in transgender males as compared to transgender females. Most transgender females were living with transgender communities, and less than 30% of them were living with parents and family. Few of the trans males were living with cis-female partners in a marital relationship and vice versa in transgender females. Table 1 summarises the baseline characteristics between both groups. Of the total transgender individuals, 10 were below 18 years of age (7 Trans males and 3 Trans females), and five of them received pubertal suppression with leuprolide acetate.

Discussion

Transgender community represent a culturally diverse and heterogeneous population with distinct regional identities and practices, commonly referred to by umbrella terms such as ‘Hijras’ in North India and as ‘Aravani’ in parts of South India.[11] Transgender health care in India is making rapid strides in recent times due to an increase in awareness, access and various supports from the Government and non-governmental organizations.[1213]

Transgender people face many challenges in their lives, which involve physical, social, marital, educational, employment, and psychological aspects.[414] Gender guidance clinics in India are still in the primitive stage, due to lack of awareness among parents, teachers, policymakers, and medical professionals including Endocrinologists, Paediatricians, Psychiatrists, and Paramedical staff.[14]

In the present study, more than 90% of Transgender individuals have not completed their higher secondary education and it is much more common in transgender females (70%) when compared to Trans males (20%). Higher numbers of the transgender females in our study were involved in begging, dancing, and sexual work whereas most transgender males were involved in textile industries.[15]

Surprisingly, our centre had more trans males since the inception of the clinic, even though Transgender females are outnumbered in society against trans males (95% versus 5%).[2] The possible explanations are the educational status of the trans males as well as increased awareness about the recent availability of Gender affirming treatment in tertiary care hospitals from qualified professionals, which made more Trans males visit for expert opinion and hormonal and surgical management. Moreover, the GAS procedures for Trans females were widely performed by non-professionals and quacks in India, whereas gender-affirming surgeries for transgender males are more complex and require expertise. After the start of this clinic, all these GAS procedures became easily accessible to transgender males and a larger number did not undergo any GAS procedures previously in Tamil Nadu, which may be one of the reasons why we had more transgender males as compared to Trans females. The other reasons are peer group motivation is less among Trans males since most of them live separately as compared with trans females where they live in groups and high peer group motivation for early surgical procedures without any GAHT in trans females.

All the transgender males received either three weekly (Testosterone enanthate) or 3-monthly testosterone (testosterone undecanoate) injections as GAHT before the GAS.[46] Overall Testosterone undecanoate formulation was preferred primarily due to its convenient dosing interval.[16] Most of the transgender males wished to undergo bilateral mastectomy as a first and initial GAS procedure due to their disturbing body images, the psychological distress of having breasts as well as sexual abuse at workplaces. Among the different mastectomy surgical procedures, nipple preservation was conducted in all with pedicle in smaller breasts and free nipple graft in larger breasts. Cessation of menstrual cycles happened immediately after testosterone therapy and hence most trans males were not interested in undergoing hysterectomy and salphingo-oophorectomy. Of the 143 transgender males, who underwent mastectomy, only nine (6.2%) underwent hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy.

On the contrary, less than 20% of transgender females only were on regular GAHT before undergoing GAS. Due to very strong peer group motivation among transgender females, most of them had already undergone penectomy and orchidectomy by non-professionals and from neighbouring states as well as abroad (Thailand) without any medical consultation and/or prior GAHT. This type of practice is very common throughout India in Hijra’s community. Hence the acceptance rate for GAHT is less among transgender females as compared to transgender males and it is a challenging task for health professionals to follow the protocols and guidelines defined by WPATH as well as various international societies in transgender females.[46] Few of them had presented with urological complications for revision surgical procedures and vaginoplasty. In our centre among the 220 cases, penectomy and orchidectomy were performed only in five (2.2%) of them.

Most of the transgender females presented to our centre for breast augmentation surgical procedures with prior penectomy and orchidectomy elsewhere. Because of the higher cost of silicone implants as well as the technical challenges involved in cosmetic surgeries, there is a mounting demand for breast augmentation surgical procedures in our centre. Despite spontaneous breast development (B2–B4 stage) following penectomy and orchidectomy, there is an increasing desire for breast implant surgical procedures among transgender female groups. Over the past 36 months, a team of plastic surgeons performed 59 breast augmentation procedures. Diode laser hair removal for unwanted facial hair reduction was performed in numerous sessions by dermatologists. Vocal cord and Metoidioplasty surgical procedures are the few surgical procedures that are planned in future.

The strengths are one of the first studies from Southern India that demonstrated successful involvement and implementation of multi-specialty teams in transgender health care. All the GAHT therapy and GAS procedures were provided free of cost, without compromising the quality of care, which attracted a very large number of transgender individuals from different parts of the state with wider acceptance.

The limitations are acceptance and adherence of GAHT in transgender females, as well as long-term follow-up.

Conclusion

The multi-disciplinary transgender clinic had a wide range of acceptance among all transgender persons. Both GAHT and GAS procedures improved the physical and mental well-being of gender-incongruent individuals. The central and state governments had an important role in devising appropriate health care policies that include free medical as well as surgical procedures and provide facilities for long-term follow-up.

Ethics statement

Approval was obtained from the Institute ethics committee of Madurai Medical College and Government Rajaji Hospital. The procedures used in this study adhere to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Current status of H5N1 influenza vaccines against clade 2.3.4.4b of H5N1
Authors: Chiranjib Chakraborty, Manojit Bhattacharya, Arpita Das, Rao Zahid Abbas, Priyanka Choudhary, Om Prakash Choudhary
Published: 2025

The H5N1 virus with strain A has spread in the USA in dairy cows, causing outbreaks in Dairy farms. The strain belongs to the HPAI (Highly pathogenic avian Influenza) virus, critically affecting animal health and productivity. In March 2024, the virus was identified as clade 2.3.4.4b of A(H5N1), and it has affected approximately 1,049 dairy herds [[1], [2], [3]]. About 70 infected individuals were reported by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). Among 70 infected individuals, 41 were noted from dairy farms, 24 from poultry farms and culling operations, two from other animal exposure, and the other three from an unknown source. At the same time, one death was reported by the CDC. Conversely, the CDC reported that it was spread in 17 states in the USA. Therefore, the virus is circulating in North America [[3], [4], [5]]. The virus shows high titers in milk [6]. The virus has been detected in the raw milk outside the USA. It was detected in retail pasteurized milk in Egypt. Detecting the virus with raw milk has created public health risks [7]. Several people take Raw milk. Several people throughout the world consume raw milk. On the other hand, dairy products might be prepared from raw milk. It is a threat to global health. Therefore, this outbreak of H5N1 affects the agricultural economy, especially the dairy business. Recently, scientists have reported that the virus has spilled into non-human mammals. It has spread to marine and terrestrial mammals. At the same time, mammal-to-mammal transmission was also reported [8]. Therefore, the virus has huge pandemic potential in the future.

The virus is also spreading among human beings and has infected more than 70 individuals. Along with the 1049 dairy cow herds confirmed cases in 995+ dairy cow herds, 168+ million birds, and 70 infected individuals, 1650+ flocks with the epizootic are now present in all 50 states and Canada from 2024 through April 2025 [9]. The first confirmed death of the individual in the US was likely contracted from wild birds [9,10]. On the other hand, researchers stated that Clade 2.3.4.4b of H5N1 is highly pathogenic. It was also reported that Clade 2.3.4.4b of H5N1 is highly pathogenic and was isolated from geese and ducks in live poultry markets. At the same time, the researchers noted the high mobility of the virus [11]. Similarly, Xie et al. stated that the virus has extended its host range to infect numerous mammalian species, indicating the potential to increase zoonotic risk [12]. Therefore, the virus remains a significant global health threat to public health. Urgent public health measures are necessary to control the spread of the virus.

The poultry and dairy sectors in the USA should assess the risks associated with their production. These two industries face the risk of an H5N1 avian influenza outbreak in the USA. Ultimately, it might lead to trade restrictions and reduced consumer demand. Finally, it might cause economic losses [13]. It was noted that the virus was disseminated throughout the US due to the movement of animals. The dairy cows were transported from one state to another in the USA, which caused the spread of the virus in 17 states in the USA [1,3].

On the other hand, the virus was detected in dairy products, which is again a concern for the dairy industry in the USA. Spackman et al. have detected that during April 2024, about 297 samples were collected from pasteurized retail milk products. The scientists collected the types from 23 products, which are also Grade A pasteurized retail milk products. These products were collected from 17 different US states. Viral RNA was detected from 60 samples, about 20.2% of the total samples. Although RNA was detected in one in five samples, these samples did not contain infectious viruses. However, the study indicates that current safety measures should be strengthened [14]. This study suggests a demand for continuous monitoring of virus RNA in milk and milk products. However, the H5N1 spread causes outbreaks in poultry birds, leading to significant bird culling to control the spread. Since 2022, millions of birds have been culled [1,4]. At the same time, it resulted in a significant jump in egg costs in the USA. Therefore, the USA's poultry and dairy industries face considerable economic losses.

People handling infected poultry or cows can become infected through direct contact with their products or by working in contaminated conditions. Animal-to-human, especially bird-to-human or mammal-to-human transmission, has been reported occasionally. On the other hand, human-to-human transmission of H5N1 is rare. However, seasonal influenza viruses can smoothly transfer from human to human. In this case, spreading the influenza virus is more likely [15,16]. Garg et al. analyzed the influenza A(H5N1) infection of HPAI to understand the recent human cases in the USA. They analyzed the 46 patients who were exposed to different animals. Among those 46 patients, 25 were exposed to dairy cows with infection or presumably infection, 20 were exposed to infected poultry, and one's exposure was not identified. The researchers reported that about 42 patients had conjunctivitis with additional symptoms (93%), 22 patients had fever (49%), and 16 patients were suffering from respiratory symptoms (36%). Some patients only had conjunctivitis; they were 15 in number, and about 33% [10]. In the USA, H5N1 caused mild illness in general, mostly conjunctivitis. Yet, one death case was reported. The first confirmed death of the individual in the US was likely contracted from wild birds [9,10]. However, to avoid the threat of H5N1, the risk group exposed to the infection should be immediately vaccinated. Some candidate vaccines for Influenza are already available. The clade (clade 2.3.4.4b) of the virus (HPAI A/H5N1) has created a panzootic condition throughout the world. The virus has spread into various parts of the globe, such as Southeast Asia, South America, and North America, as well as different parts of Europe, and has reached Antarctica for the first time, leading to the panzootic condition. Other than the USA, across vast geographic regions, the virus has also infected various wild birds. In some parts of the world, the spread of the infection caused wildlife deaths in most biodiverse ecosystems [17,18]. It has spread to more than 489 species of birds worldwide, and the virus is spreading through migratory paths [19]. Other than the wild birds, the virus is affecting different mammals, including marine and terrestrial mammals. Recent panzootic conditions have noted that more than 48 mammalian species have been infected in about 26 countries [20,21]. Unlike past outbreaks, this strain has evolved through various mutations and can be transmitted from mammal to mammal, raising pandemic risks for humans [[21], [22], [23], [24]]. The mammal-to-mammal transmission is noted in different parts of the world, especially in marine and terrestrial mammals. However, it has been pointed out that the human-to-human transmission potential is low.

Due to the circulation of the H5N1 influenza virus from the clade 2.3.4.4b and its recent cross-species transmission, the virus has remained a significant threat to global health. Although the virus has mild effects, we must control its spread. In this direction, vaccination of humans is needed to prevent the spread of the infection caused by the virus. Several candidate vaccines are available for the H5N1 virus (Table 1) [25,26]. It has been noted that the primary vaccine target of the influenza vaccine is the HA (hemagglutinin) protein because it is one of the immunogenic proteins for influenza viruses. It is a glycoprotein, and it is most abundantly available on the virus's surface [25,27]. Most vaccines developed using HA immunogen also effectively induce antibody responses and offer complete protection against the virus [28]. A vaccine must stimulate the innate and adaptive immune response (humoral and cellular arms). It has been noted that the influenza vaccine induces both the innate and adaptive immune response. Influenza vaccine with immune response-specific HA protein can also trigger virus-specific T cell responses, which include activating the CD4+ T (helper T cells) and CD8+ T cytotoxic lymphocytes (cytotoxic lymphocytes (CTLs)) [29]. Several H5 influenza vaccines are licensed by regulatory bodies in Europe, Australia, Asia, and the USA. Taaffe et al. reported that several regulatory bodies have licensed at least 20 influenza vaccines of the H5 category [26]. Several platforms have been used to develop the vaccine, such as inactivated influenza virus vaccines (IIVs), live-attenuated influenza virus vaccines (LAIVs), recombinant influenza vaccines (RIVs), and virus-like particle vaccines (VLPs) (Fig. 1A) [[30], [31], [32]]. However, most vaccines (IIVs) were produced using chicken eggs. At the same time, it has been noted that the influenza vaccine has been tested primarily on three animal models: mice, ferrets, and monkeys (Fig. 1B). However, other animal models were also reported. However, VLP is the next-generation vaccine platform. The VLP vaccine for the influenza virus entered a clinical trial. In 2012, Phase II clinical studies of the recombinant VLP vaccine for the influenza virus evaluated its immunogenicity and safety [32,33]. On the other hand, the safety profiles of those IIV vaccines have been shown to have favourable results. Safety profiles have been tested in children, healthy adults, and older adults, about 32000 individuals [26].

The USA-licensed influenza vaccines are from the four vaccine manufacturer companies that produce seasonal influenza vaccines: Sanofi Pasteur, GlaxoSmithKline, AstraZeneca, and Seqirus. These four companies produce ten different formulations of trivalent seasonal influenza vaccines, including an intranasal formulation. The FDA or EMA approves these vaccines. These companies also have eight diverse quadrivalent vaccine formulations. As part of the pandemic planning and preparedness process, the USA has licensed three prototypes of Influenza A H5N1 vaccines to support the people through FDA authorization. It will help stop the spread of the H5N1 virus [34].

Researchers reported that licensed H5N1 vaccines or newly developed vaccines can induce cross-neutralizing antibodies (cross-nAb) against the H5N1 influenza virus with clade 2.3.4.4b. Khurana et al. indicated that US-licensed adjuvanted H5N1 vaccines could produce cross-nAb against circulating H5N1 (HPAI virus) with clade 2.3.4.4b, which can protect humans [35]. Piesche et al. demonstrated that, in Fattening Geese, five commercial vaccines, including H5, can defend against the H5N1 virus (HPAI) with clade 2.3.4.4b. These five vaccines include whole inactivated virus (WIV), subunit, vector, and RNA. After primary vaccination, two whole inactivated virus and subunit vaccines augmented seroconversion. The challenging experiment used one whole inactivated virus and one RNA vaccine. After the challenge experiment, it was noted that all unvaccinated control geese yielded infection by day four. In contrast, all vaccinated geese that had seroconverted showed complete clinical protection. However, the study shows a significant reduction in viral burden [36]. On the other hand, other commercial vaccines were tested against clade 2.3.4.4b H5N1. Lee et al. tested two commercially available vaccines of the rHVT (recombinant herpes virus of turkeys) vector against clade 2.3.4.4b H5 HPAI virus, which was available from North America. Both vaccines can protect the chickens and identify antibodies against the challenge virus [37]. These studies indicated that the H5N1 vaccine might be a potential candidate for rapid use among farm workers, cows, or poultry birds until the vaccine has been developed against the H5N1 virus of clade 2.3.4.4b.

There is an immediate need to develop human vaccines for clade 2.3.4.4b for H5N1, which is needed for pandemic preparedness. Some vaccine candidates are already available for Influenza A viruses; the USA has licensed these vaccines. A recent clade-specific vaccine is needed to fight against clade 2.3.4.4b for H5N1. Some scientists are trying to develop the clade-specific (clade 2.3.4.4b for H5N1) vaccine. In other experiments, Liu et al. indicated that the intranasal vaccine shows protection against H5N1 clade 2.3.4.4b infection in small animal models. The intranasal vaccine was used as an influenza virus-vectored vaccine containing DelNS1 LAIV (deleted-NS1 live attenuated Influenza viral vector vaccine system). DelNS1 LAIV generates potent neutralizing antibodies, mucosal IgA, and T-cell responses in mice [38]. Furey et al. tried to develop an mRNA vaccine (nucleoside-modified) for clade 2.3.4.4b H5N1. The mRNA vaccine was developed using HA glycoprotein from an isolated clade 2.3.4.4b H5 N1 virus and uses lipid nanoparticles (LNP). The vaccine can produce antibodies at identical levels in mice (female) with and without previous H1N1 exposures compared to whole inactivated vaccines. Finally, in male ferrets, this mRNA-LNP vaccine shows its immunogenicity. Therefore, the vaccine shows its protective effect in pre-clinical animal models [39]. Kawai et al. developed an mRNA-LNP vaccine using mRNA that expressed the H5N1 subtype HPAI virus-derived HA or NA. Compared with conventional mRNA-LNPs, it showed more promising cross-protection against the H5N1 challenge virus [40]. The virus will be effective against the clade 2.3.4.4b of H5N1. However, this clade 2.3.4.4b H5N1 vaccine should be encouraged more and more. These vaccines should be given an emergency use authorization (EUA) by the USFDA in the USA.

H5N1 is now panzootic in wild birds and mammals worldwide and affects multiple species. The poultry farmers of the USA culled 50 million chickens and turkeys and eliminated the virus. Mammal-to-mammal and cross-species transmission was reported for HPAI strain H5N1. Pardo-Roa et al. reported that the virus has spread in poultry, marine mammals, and wild birds spanning >3800 km of the Chilean coastline [41]. Peacock et al. reported the spreading of the virus in South American marine mammals, dairy cattle in the USA, and European fur farms [8]. Similarly, Lindh et al. have reported that HPAI A(H5N1) virus has spread in multiple fur farms in the central part of Finland, especially the Ostrobothnia region, and the Southern part of Finland [42]. However, the virus has spread to wild birds and mammals in different parts of the globe. Multiple infected H5N1 bird species were isolated from East Asia and North America [8,16]. However, no oral H5N1 vaccine is available for mass administration for wildlife, analogous to the rabies vaccine. Therefore, the oral H5N1 vaccine is needed for mass administration to poultry and wildlife. If developed quickly, those vaccines can be licensed for poultry and wildlife.

The H5N1 panzootic has been a potent threat to humanity. At the same time, the virus spreads within dairy cattle, increasing the chance of infection. The virus spread to dairy cattle, poultry, wild birds, and mammals, indicating a possibility of subsequently spreading the virus to human populations. We need full pandemic preparedness considering the threat of clade 2.3.4.4b of H5N1 [43], [44], [45], [46], [47], [48]. The Global Virus Network urgently urges proactive measures to contain widespread outbreaks. Ongoing preparedness for vaccine development of clade 2.3.4.4b of the H5N1 virus, such as next-generation vaccine development for clade 2.3.4.4b of H5N1, vaccine trials, and other vaccination activities, will provide strong protection against the continuous danger of avian influenza.

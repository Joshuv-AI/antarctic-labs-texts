Characteristics of “Very Early” Intrahepatic Cholangiocarcinoma Undergoing Liver Resection and Its Relationship With Carbohydrate Antigen 19‐9

ABSTRACT

Introduction

Intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC) is the second most common primary liver cancer, representing approximately 15% of all primary hepatic malignancies [1]. While ICC has a higher incidence in Eastern countries, the incidence of ICC continues to rise worldwide [2]. The increasing incidence is partly due to the improved diagnostic modalities including imaging and biomarker, and wider implementation of liver cancer surveillance among high‐risk populations, which have led to increased detection of early‐stage ICC [3, 4]. For patients with early‐stage disease, liver resection with or without lymphadenectomy is the primary curative‐intent treatment option [5, 6]. Several risk factors have been associated with prognosis after curative‐intent resection, including tumor burden score (TBS), lymph node metastases, periductal infiltrating morphology, microvascular invasion, and CA19‐9 [7, 8]. Since the prognosis of patients remains unsatisfactory, postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy is generally recommended; nonetheless, the efficacy of chemotherapy has been demonstrated to be more pronounced in cases of more aggressive ICC [9, 10].

Recent advancements in the field of “transplant oncology” have expanded the indication of liver transplant (LT) to ICC, and have introduced the concept of “very early” ICC (i.e., single, less than 2 cm) [11, 12]. Several studies have demonstrated that LT for this subset of patients yields 5‐year survival ranging from 60% to 70% [13]. While these outcomes are somewhat inferior to those observed in LT recipients for other indications, this survival has nonetheless been regarded as acceptable. While recent refinement of imaging modality has potentially enabled us to detect more and more patients with very early ICC, there has been a dearth of literature regarding the best treatment options for this patient cohort, especially in the context of liver resection [14]. One previous report using the national cancer database in the United States of America demonstrated no survival difference between liver resection and LT, while both therapeutic strategies were better than nonoperative treatment such as chemotherapy or radiotherapy [15]. The current study sought to characterize risk factors associated with very early ICC, as well as define long‐term outcomes following liver resection.

Methods

Data Source and Patient Selection

Patients who underwent curative‐intent hepatectomy for ICC between 1993 and 2023 were identified from the International Intrahepatic Cholangiocarcinoma Study Group database, which is comprised of 19 hepatobiliary high‐volume hospitals worldwide [5, 6, 7, 14]. Patients who underwent palliative surgery had missing information on follow‐up data, and data on tumor size and numbers were excluded. The Institutional Review Boards of all the participating institutions approved this study.

Variables and Outcomes of Interests

Demographic and clinicopathologic data were evaluated including age, gender, American Society of Anesthesiologist (ASA) classification (i.e., 0,1 vs. ≥ 2), cirrhosis, preoperative carbohydrate antigen (CA) 19‐9 levels (IU/mL), albumin‐bilirubin (ALBI) score, tumor size (cm) and number, TBS, extent of resection, nodal disease (i.e., N0: negative; Nx: not examined; N1: positive), margin status (i.e., R0, R1), tumor histological grade (i.e., well to moderate vs. poorly to undifferentiated), morphological subtype (i.e., MF, mass‐forming and IG, intraductal growth vs. PI, periductal infiltrating and MF + PI, periductal infiltrating plus mass‐forming), microvascular invasion, major vascular invasion. Margin status was defined as microscopically negative (R0) or microscopically positive (R1). TBS, a concise metric of ICC tumor burden, was calculated based on the formula: (TBS2 = [maximum tumor diameter]2 + [number of tumors]2) [16]. Regarding CA19‐9, patients were categorized as high CA19‐9, low CA19‐9, and unmeasured, respectively, using the cut‐off value of 40 U/mL [17]. Major liver resection was defined as the resection of three or more Couinaud segments [18]. Primary outcomes of interest were overall survival (OS) and recurrence‐free survival (RFS). OS was defined as the time interval between the dates of resection to the date of death from any cause or last follow‐up, and RFS was defined as the time interval between the dates of hepatectomy to the date of any recurrence, which was confirmed by radiological examination.

Statistical Analysis

Descriptive statistics were presented as median values (interquartile ranges [IQRs]) for continuous variables and frequency (%) for categorical variables. All tests were two‐sided, and a p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Continuous variables were compared with the Mann−Whitney U test, and categorical variables using the chi‐square test, or Fisher's exact test, as appropriate. Survival probabilities were estimated using the Kaplan−Meier curve and compared using the log‐rank test. Multivariable analysis was performed using the Cox proportional hazard model, including preoperative (i.e., age, sex, ASA, the presence of cirrhosis, BMI, ALBI score, platelet count, CA19‐9) and surgical factors (i.e., margin status). All statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.2.0 (R Project for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Patient Characteristics

A total of 1868 patients with ICC were included in the analytic cohort. Median patient age was 62.0 years (IQR 54.0−70.0 years) and most patients were male (n = 1008, 54.0%). Overall, 86 (4.6%) patients were categorized as having very early ICC. There were no clinical differences regarding patient age (very early, 61.5 years, IQR 53.0−69.3, vs. non‐very early 62.0 years, IQR 54.0−70.0, p = 0.80), presence of cirrhosis (very early, n = 19, 22.1%, vs. non‐very early n = 344, 19.3%, p = 0.50), ALBI score (very early, −2.7, IQR −3.0 to −2.2, vs. non‐very early −2.6, IQR −3.0 to −2.2, p = 0.90), CA19‐9 (CA19‐9 ≥ 40 U/mL: very early, n = 27, 31.4%, vs. non‐very early n = 573, 32.2%, p = 0.09) between very early and non‐very early ICC patients except of patients sex (male: very early, n = 57, 66.3%, vs. non‐very early n = 951, 53.4%, p = 0.02). Pathological characteristics were also similar between the very early group and non‐very early cohorts, except for the presence of microvascular invasion (very early, n = 14, 16.3%, vs. non‐very early n = 594, 33.3%, p < 0.001) (Table 1).

Patients with very early ICC had more favorable long‐term outcomes versus non‐very early ICC. Specifically, 1‐, 3‐, and 5‐year OS among patients with very early ICC was 92.3% (IQR 86.6−98.4), 72.6% (IQR 62.7−84.1), and 58.0% (IQR 45.9−73.5), respectively, versus 80.8% (IQR 78.9−82.7), 51.0% (IQR 48.4−53.8), and 38.9% (IQR 36.1−41.9), among non‐very early ICC patients (p < 0.01; Supporting Information S1: Figure 1A). A similar trend was observed in RFS in which 1‐, 3‐, and 5‐year RFS for very early ICC was 86.1% (IQR 78.8−94.1), 59.2% (IQR 47.8−73.3), and 49.6% (IQR 36.9−66.6), respectively, versus 61.3% (IQR 58.9−63.8), 38.6% (IQR 36.0−41.4), and 32.1% (IQR 29.4−35.1), respectively, among non‐very early ICC cases (p < 0.001; Supporting Information S1: Figure 1B).

Determinant of Worse Survival Outcomes Among Very Early ICC

On multivariable Cox regression analysis, several factors were associated with worse outcomes among patients with very early ICC (Table 2A and B). Specifically, elevated CA19‐9 levels were associated with worse OS (HR 3.05, IQR 1.28−7.30, p = 0.01) and RFS (HR 2.88, IQR 1.24−6.66, p = 0.01). Specifically, 1‐, 3‐, and 5‐year OS in the low CA19‐9 group were 94.6% (IQR 88.9−100), 84.3% (IQR 74.8−95.0), and 74.9% (IQR 62.6−89.8), respectively, versus 87.0% (IQR 74.2−100), 43.8% (IQR 26.1−73.6), and 12.2% (IQR 2.2−67.9), in the high CA19‐9 group (p < 0.001; Figure 1A). A similar pattern was observed for RFS in which 1‐ and 3‐year RFS was 85.5% (IQR 76.7−95.4) and 70.9% (IQR 58.8−85.4) in the low CA19‐9 group versus 87.1% (IQR 74.5−100) and 25.8% (IQR 10.3−64.5) in the high CA19‐9 group (p < 0.001; Figure 1B).

Discussion

Recently, the concept of “very early” ICC ( ≤ 2 cm, single tumor) has been introduced in the context of possible LT for ICC. There are limited data, however, on the outcomes of patients with very early ICC following liver resection [19, 20]. The current study was important because, to our knowledge, it represents the largest patient cohort of very early ICC to date based on a multi‐institutional database. Using the multi‐institutional international database, we analyzed patient characteristics and risk factors, as well as chronological and geographical variations, associated with very early ICC. Despite some temporal and geographical variations, very early ICC represented an uncommon subset of patients with ICC, a total of only 4.6% of cases. Very early ICC generally had similar patient and tumor demographics compared with non‐very early ICC, except for biological sex and the presence of microvascular invasion. While OS was favorable, patients with very early ICC with elevated CA19‐9 levels had markedly worse survival. These results highlight the importance of CA19‐9 as a marker of adverse biology even among individuals with very early ICC based on tumor morphologic characteristics.

There have been several single‐center reports on very early ICC, all of which were based on retrospective studies of patients undergoing LT [11, 12, 16, 21]. For instance, Sapisochin et al. reported that LT in 27 patients with ICC with a 1‐, 3‐, and 5‐year OS of 78%, 66%, and 51%, respectively [21]. Similarly, Lee et al. observed an OS of 63.5% in a cohort of 12 patients with very early ICC [16]. In contrast, there are limited data on the role of liver resection in the management of patients with very early ICC. Small cohort studies from Japan and Spain noted a 5‐year of 100% and 68.9%, respectively, following resection—albeit these were essentially case reports based on very small patient sample sizes (n = 15 and n = 7, respectively) [19, 20]. In the current study, among a large cohort of almost 2000 patients with ICC only 86 (4.6%) patients were categorized as having very early ICC—highlighting how uncommon patients present with very early ICC. We noted that patients with very early ICC generally had a relatively favorable long‐term outcomes following surgical resection with 1‐, 3‐, and 5‐year OS among patients with very early ICC being 92.3% (IQR 86.6–98.4), 72.6% (IQR 62.7–84.1), and 58.0% (IQR 45.9–73.5)—consistent with previous reports [19, 20, 22]. Of note, on multivariable analysis, CA19‐9 was associated with a threefold higher risk of a poor prognosis (OS: HR 3.05, IQR 1.28–7.30, p = 0.01; RFS: HR 2.88, IQR 1.24–6.66, p = 0.01).

CA19‐9 has been identified previously as a risk factor for poor long‐term outcomes among ICC patients [9, 23, 24, 25]. In fact, one meta‐analysis noted that a prognostic model with the best performance had incorporated CA19‐9 in addition to other tumor morphology and tumor biology features [26]. Another study demonstrated that elevated CA19‐9 correlated with increased risk of lymph node metastasis [27]. Nonetheless, these previous studies included all ICC stages, and the impact of CA19‐9 on clinical presentation and outcomes among very early‐stage disease had not been investigated. In the current study, elevated CA19‐9 level was strongly correlated with worse long‐term oncologic outcomes among patients with very early ICC cohorts (Figure 1A,B). In fact, among patients with very early ICC who had a high CA19‐9 on presentation, 5‐year survival following resection was only 12.2%. The reason for this very poor long‐term outcome was likely due to patients with high CA19‐9 having a higher incidence of microvascular invasion, lymph node metastasis, and extrahepatic recurrence (Figure 2). In contrast, patients with very early ICC and low CA19‐9 demonstrated a much more favorable 5‐year survival of 74.9%. These findings may have implications for treatment strategies related to very early ICC. Even in early‐stage tumors, patients with elevated CA19‐9 levels—who are more likely to exhibit aggressive tumor biology—may benefit from perioperative treatment, including pre‐ or postoperative therapy [9, 10]. Additionally, the data suggest that appropriate lymph node dissection may be particularly important in this subgroup of patients given the high incidence of nodal disease [28]. While lymphadenectomy is now the standard of care for ICC patients, the extent of lymphadenectomy has not been clearly defined. In addition, CA19‐9 may serve as a potential guide for treatment decisions, particularly when considering LT for very early ICC. Previous studies on LT for very early ICC rarely accounted for CA19‐9 levels [11, 15]. In turn, data from the current study would suggest that patients with very early ICC and high CA19‐9 levels may benefit from multimodal treatment after surgical intervention, including chemotherapy or liver‐directed therapy [29, 30].

Recent advancements in diagnostic modalities have enabled early detection of ICC [31, 32]. To this point, the number of patients with resectable ICC may be increasing in recent years [33]. In the current study, there was a similar trend in the proportion of very early ICC patients over time, while the number of patients with resected ICC increased (Figure 3A). This finding may suggest that detection of very early ICC remains challenging [12, 34]. While the slight increase in the proportion of very early ICC cases over time may have been due to improved quality of cross‐sectional imaging, further screening methods targeting high‐risk populations (e.g., primary sclerosing cholangitis, liver cirrhosis) might also be beneficial, and may lead to an increased proportion of very early ICC [35]. In addition, differences in ICC incidence between Eastern and Western regions have traditionally existed—although the gap has been closing (Figure 3B) [36].

Several limitations should be taken into consideration when interpreting the findings of the present study. The retrospective design may have introduced the potential for selection bias. Although the multicenter nature of the study was a strength, treatment approaches (i.e., the indication of lymphadenectomy) among institutions over time may have varied. Since ICC is a relatively rare entity, a large international multi‐institutional database that contained patients from multiple decades was necessary to ensure a sufficient sample size. Despite aggregating data from an international multi‐institutional database, the relative number of very early ICC patients included in the final analyses was still somewhat small.

In conclusion, this multi‐institutional study offers a comprehensive evaluation of very early ICC undergoing liver resection. Very early ICC was associated with favorable long‐term outcomes; however, elevated CA19‐9 levels identified a subgroup with a much worse prognosis and more aggressive tumor features. Despite advances in diagnostics, the proportion of very early ICC remains low, highlighting the need for improved early detection strategies, particularly in high‐risk populations. Overall, these data enhance the understanding of prognostic factors among patients with very early ICC and underscore the importance of biomarker‐driven treatment approaches.

Synopsis

Due to the limited number of patients in this subset, the characteristics and outcomes of “very early” ICC (≤ 2 cm, single tumor) receiving hepatectomy have been ill‐defined. Only 4.6% of patients (n = 86) met the criteria for very early ICC, characterized by a 5‐year overall survival (OS) rate of 58.0%, within which elevated CA19‐9 levels were significantly associated with reduced OS and an increased propensity for lymph node metastases. Very early ICC was generally associated with favorable long‐term outcomes; however, elevated CA19‐9 levels delineated a subgroup with significantly poorer prognosis and more aggressive tumor biology.

Scutellarein attenuates alcohol-induced hepatocyte injury by modulating NF-κB and NRF2 signaling pathways
Authors: Shuxia Shen, Minghua Cao, Ziqi Sui
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Alcohol-induced liver injury remains a major public health concern, and effective hepatoprotective agents are urgently needed. Scutellarein (SCU), a natural flavonoid, has shown potential biological activity, but its hepatoprotective effects and underlying mechanisms against alcohol-induced hepatocyte injury have not been fully elucidated.Methods:An in vitro model of alcohol-induced hepatocyte injury was established using human LO2 cells exposed to 400 mM alcohol. Cells were pretreated with 5 µM SCU, and multiple assays were performed to evaluate hepatocyte viability, hepatic enzyme activity, apoptosis, inflammatory cytokine expression, oxidative stress, and related signaling pathways.Results:SCU pretreatment effectively reversed the alcohol-induced reduction in cell viability and significantly lowered alanine aminotransferase and aspartate aminotransferase levels. SCU inhibited apoptosis by downregulating BAX and upregulating BCL2, and attenuated inflammatory responses by reducing interleukin-1β, interleukin-6, and tumor necrosis factor-alpha expression. Mechanistically, SCU suppressed nuclear factor kappa-B signaling by decreasing phosphorylation of p65 and inhibitor of nuclear factor kappa-Bα. In addition, SCU enhanced antioxidant defense by upregulating superoxide dismutase 1, superoxide dismutase 2, and catalase, and activating the nuclear factor erythroid 2–related factor 2–NAD(P)H quinone dehydrogenase 1 pathway.Conclusion:SCU protects hepatocytes against alcohol-induced injury by modulating apoptosis, inflammation, and oxidative stress through regulation of nuclear factor kappa-B and nuclear factor erythroid 2–related factor 2 signaling pathways. These findings highlight its potential as a promising hepatoprotective agent.

1. Introduction

Alcoholic liver disease (ALD) is a spectrum of chronic liver disorders caused by prolonged and/or excessive alcohol consumption, with pathological progression ranging from simple steatosis to alcoholic hepatitis, fibrosis, cirrhosis, and even hepatocellular carcinoma.[1–3] Numerous studies have demonstrated that hepatocyte apoptosis, inflammatory responses, and oxidative stress are key pathological processes involved in the onset and progression of alcohol-induced liver injury.[4–6] Currently, clinical management of ALD mainly relies on alcohol abstinence and supportive care, with a lack of effective therapeutic agents that are both highly targeted and have minimal side effects.[7–10] Therefore, identifying natural compounds with multi-target activity and low toxicity is of great research and translational value for developing novel therapeutic strategies for ALD.

Scutellarein (SCU), a natural flavonoid compound, is predominantly found in various medicinal plants, particularly those from the Lamiaceae family such as Scutellaria baicalensis. Chemically defined as 5,6,7,4′-tetrahydroxyflavone, SCU is a secondary metabolite with favorable bioactivity and pharmacological potential [PMID: 20064593; PMID: 27730005; PMID: 38579142]. Recent studies have reported a range of pharmacological activities of SCU, including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antitumor effects [PMID: 38579142; PMID: 38849327; PMID: 39490431; PMID: 38695125; PMID: 38929149]. However, studies focusing on the role of SCU in alcohol-induced hepatic injury remain limited, and its potential hepatoprotective effects and underlying molecular mechanisms have not been clearly elucidated.

In this study, we established an in vitro alcohol-induced hepatocyte injury model to systematically evaluate the effects of SCU on hepatocyte viability, liver function, apoptosis, inflammation, and oxidative stress. Moreover, we further explored the regulatory effects of SCU on key signaling pathways, including nuclear factor kappa-B (NF-κB) and nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (NRF2). This study aims to elucidate the protective mechanisms of SCU against alcohol-induced hepatocellular injury and provide experimental evidence for its development as a potential hepatoprotective agent.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Reagents and antibodies

SCU (≥98% purity) was purchased from MedChemExpress (USA) and dissolved in DMSO to prepare a 10 mM stock solution stored at − 20°C. Absolute alcohol was obtained from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) was purchased from Tongren Biotech. Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) assay kits were obtained from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits for interleukin (IL)-1β, IL-6, and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) were purchased from Elabscience. TRIzol reagent, reverse transcription kits, and qPCR kits were obtained from Takara Bio Inc. Antibodies against BAX, BCL2, p65, phospho-p65 (p-p65), inhibitor of nuclear factor kappa-B (IκB)α, phospho-IκBα, NRF2, NAD(P)H quinone dehydrogenase 1 (NQO1), and β-actin were purchased from Cell Signaling Technology (USA).

2.2. Cell culture and treatment

The human hepatocyte cell line LO2 was obtained from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences. Cells were cultured in high-glucose DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin–streptomycin in a humidified incubator at 37°C with 5% CO2. The experimental groups were as follows: control group, alcohol-treated group, and SCU intervention group. Alcohol was administered at a final concentration of 400 mM for 24 hours. SCU was added 3 hours prior to alcohol exposure. The final concentration of DMSO was kept below 0.1%.

2.3. Cell viability assay

Cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 5 × 103 cells/well. After the designated treatments, 10 μL of CCK-8 reagent was added to each well, followed by a 2-hour incubation. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader. Each experiment was performed in triplicate, and relative cell viability was calculated.

2.4. Measurement of liver enzymes (ALT and AST)

Following treatment, cells were washed twice with prechilled PBS and collected. Cell lysates were prepared using lysis buffer, followed by centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4°C. The supernatants were collected and used for enzyme activity analysis according to the manufacturer’s protocols.

2.5. ELISA for inflammatory cytokines

Cell culture supernatants were collected after treatment and centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4°C to remove cell debris. Levels of IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α were measured using commercial ELISA kits according to the manufacturers’ instructions. The procedure included sample addition, incubation, washing, addition of enzyme-conjugated antibodies and substrates, and termination of the reaction. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm, and cytokine concentrations were calculated using standard curves.

2.6. RNA extraction and quantitative real-time PCR

Total RNA was extracted from cells using TRIzol reagent according to the manufacturer’s protocol. RNA concentration and purity were assessed using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer, and samples with a 260/280 ratio between 1.8 and 2.0 were used for subsequent experiments. One microgram of total RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA using a reverse transcription kit. Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) was performed using SYBR Green PCR Master Mix in a 20 μL reaction system containing SYBR mix, primers, cDNA template, and RNase-free water. The thermal cycling conditions were as follows: initial denaturation at 95°C for 30 seconds, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 5 seconds and 60°C for 30 seconds. A melt curve analysis was performed to verify amplification specificity. Each sample was analyzed in triplicate. GAPDH was used as an internal control, and relative gene expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method.

2.7. Western blot analysis

Cells were lysed using RIPA buffer supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitors. Protein concentration was determined using a BCA assay. Equal amounts of protein (20–30 μg) were denatured at 95°C for 5 minutes and separated by SDS–PAGE, followed by transfer to polyvinylidene fluoride membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% nonfat milk or BSA for 1 hour at room temperature, then incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies (e.g., BAX, BCL2, p-p65, phospho-IκBα, NRF2, and NQO1). After washing with phosphate-buffered saline with Tween-20, membranes were incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 hour at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence detection system and imaged with a gel documentation system. β-actin was used as a loading control.

2.8. Statistical analysis

All experiments were performed in biological triplicates, with independently cultured and treated cell samples. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. Statistical significance was assessed using one-way analysis of variance followed by appropriate post hoc tests. Analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism 8.0 software. A P-value of < .05 was considered statistically significant.

3. Results

3.1. Effects of scutellarein on alcohol-induced hepatocyte injury

To assess the cytotoxicity of alcohol, we first measured cell viability after 24-hour exposure to varying alcohol concentrations using the CCK-8 assay. As shown in Figure 1A, cell viability significantly decreased at an alcohol concentration of 400 mM, which was selected for subsequent experiments as the optimal injury-inducing dose. We then evaluated the protective effects of different concentrations of SCU on alcohol-induced hepatocyte injury. As presented in Figure 1B, SCU at 5, 10, and 20 µM significantly improved cell viability after alcohol treatment, with no statistically significant differences among the 3 doses. Although 2.5 µM SCU also exhibited some protective effect, it was relatively weaker. Therefore, 5 µM was chosen as the optimal concentration for subsequent experiments. These findings suggest that SCU can effectively alleviate alcohol-induced inhibition of hepatocyte viability within a certain concentration range, indicating its cytoprotective potential.

Effects of scutellarein on alcohol-induced hepatocyte injury. (A) Effects of different concentrations of alcohol on cell viability. (B) Scutellarein restored cell viability after stimulation with alcohol. *P < .05; **P < .01; ***P < .001; and ****P < .0001.

3.2. Effects of scutellarein on liver function indicators

To further explore the potential mechanism of SCU in alcohol-induced liver injury, we measured the expression of liver function markers, AST and ALT. As shown in Figure 2A and B, both AST and ALT activities were significantly elevated in the alcohol-treated group compared to the control group, indicating substantial hepatic injury. Notably, treatment with SCU significantly reduced the levels of these enzymes, suggesting that SCU has the potential to alleviate alcohol-induced hepatocellular damage.

Effects of scutellarein on liver function markers. (A and B) The levels of AST and ALT in hepatocytes were measured to evaluate liver injury. *P < .05; **P < .01; ***P < .001; and ****P < .0001. ALT = alanine aminotransferase, AST = aspartate aminotransferase.

3.3. Effects of scutellarein on alcohol-induced hepatocyte apoptosis

Hepatocyte apoptosis is a hallmark of alcohol-induced liver injury. To investigate the role of SCU in modulating alcohol-induced apoptosis, we analyzed the expression of key apoptosis-related genes. As shown in Figure 3A and B, alcohol treatment significantly upregulated the pro-apoptotic gene BAX while downregulating the antiapoptotic gene BCL2 at the mRNA level, indicating activation of apoptotic pathways. SCU treatment reversed these changes, with a marked decrease in BAX and an increase in BCL2 expression, suggesting an antiapoptotic effect. These results were further validated at the protein level via Western blot analysis (Fig. 3C), which mirrored the transcriptional trends. Collectively, these data indicate that SCU may attenuate alcohol-induced hepatocyte injury by modulating apoptotic pathways.

Effects of scutellarein on alcohol-induced hepatocyte apoptosis. (A and B) mRNA levels of BAX and BCL2 were detected by qRT-PCR. (C) Protein levels of BAX and BCL2 were analyzed by Western blot. *P < .05; **P < .01; ***P < .001; and ****P < .0001. qRT-PCR = quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction.

3.4. Effects of scutellarein on alcohol-induced inflammatory response

Alcohol-induced hepatocyte injury is typically accompanied by a robust inflammatory response. To explore the anti-inflammatory potential of SCU, we measured the levels of key inflammatory cytokines. As shown in Figure 4A–C, alcohol stimulation markedly increased the secretion of IL-1, IL-6, and TNF-α in the culture supernatant, indicating a pronounced inflammatory response. However, SCU treatment significantly reduced the secretion of these pro-inflammatory cytokines. Consistent with these results, qRT-PCR analysis (Fig. 4D–F) showed that the mRNA levels of these inflammatory markers were elevated following alcohol exposure but were significantly downregulated by SCU. These findings suggest that SCU may exert anti-inflammatory effects by inhibiting the transcriptional expression of pro-inflammatory genes.

Effects of scutellarein on alcohol-induced inflammatory response. (A–C) Levels of IL-1, IL-6 and TNF-α in the culture supernatant were measured by ELISA. (D–F) mRNA expression of IL-1, IL-6, and TNF-α was detected by qRT-PCR. *P < .05; **P < .01; ***P < .001; and ****P < .0001. ELISA = enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, IL = interleukin, qRT-PCR = quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction, TNF-α = tumor necrosis factor-alpha.

3.5. Effects of scutellarein on the NF-κB signaling pathway

The NF-κB signaling pathway plays a critical role in mediating inflammatory responses. To determine whether SCU modulates this pathway, we examined the phosphorylation status of key NF-κB components, p65 and its inhibitor IκB. As shown in Figure 5, alcohol treatment led to a notable increase in the levels of p-p65 and p-IκB, indicating pathway activation. SCU intervention significantly reduced the phosphorylation levels of both proteins, suggesting that SCU may attenuate alcohol-induced inflammation by inhibiting NF-κB pathway activation. These results imply that suppression of the NF-κB pathway could be a key mechanism underlying the anti-inflammatory effects of SCU.

Effects of scutellarein on the NF-κB signaling pathway. Phosphorylation levels of p65 and IκB were analyzed by Western blot. IκB = inhibitor of nuclear factor kappa-B, NF-κB = nuclear factor kappa-B.

3.6. Effects of scutellarein on alcohol-induced oxidative stress

Oxidative stress is another hallmark of alcohol-induced hepatocyte injury. To assess the antioxidative capacity of SCU, we first evaluated the expression of key antioxidant enzymes. As shown in Figure 6A–C, the mRNA levels of superoxide dismutase (SOD)1, SOD2, and catalase were significantly downregulated in alcohol-treated cells compared to controls, indicating elevated oxidative stress. SCU treatment restored the expression of these antioxidant genes, suggesting its potential to alleviate oxidative damage.

Effects of scutellarein on alcohol-induced oxidative stress and NRF2 signaling. (A–C) mRNA expression levels of antioxidant enzymes SOD1, SOD2, and CAT were measured by qRT-PCR. (D and E) mRNA levels of NRF2 and NQO1 were detected by qRT-PCR. (F) Protein expression of NRF2 and NQO1 was analyzed by Western blot. *P < .05; **P < .01; ***P < .001; ****P < .0001. CAT = catalase, NQO1 = NAD(P)H quinone dehydrogenase 1, NRF2 = nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2, qRT-PCR = quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction, SOD = superoxide dismutase.

Given the central role of the NRF2 signaling pathway in antioxidant defense, we further investigated the expression of NRF2 and its downstream target NQO1. As shown in Figure 6D and E, alcohol significantly suppressed the mRNA expression of NRF2 and NQO1, while SCU treatment markedly upregulated their levels. Western blot analysis confirmed these trends at the protein level (Fig. 6F), demonstrating consistency between transcriptional and translational responses.

In summary, our results indicate that SCU may enhance hepatocyte antioxidant capacity by activating the NRF2 signaling pathway, thereby mitigating alcohol-induced oxidative stress and contributing to its hepatoprotective effects.

4. Discussion

ALD is one of the most prevalent chronic liver diseases worldwide, with a complex pathogenesis involving oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis.[2,11] In this study, we established an in vitro model of alcohol-induced hepatocyte injury and systematically evaluated the protective effects of the natural flavonoid compound SCU on cell viability, apoptosis, inflammation, and oxidative stress. Furthermore, we explored the potential signaling pathways underlying its actions. Our results demonstrate that SCU exerts significant hepatoprotective effects, which may be closely associated with the modulation of the NF-κB and NRF2 signaling pathways.

Apoptosis is a critical mechanism in alcohol-induced hepatocyte injury.[12,13] Previous studies have shown that PSTPIP2 alleviates alcoholic liver injury by inhibiting apoptosis,[14] and selenium protects hepatocytes from alcohol-induced injury via inhibition of apoptotic pathways.[15] Similarly, scutellarein, a related flavonoid, has been shown to attenuate pirarubicin-induced cardiotoxicity by regulating apoptosis-related signaling.[16] In our study, alcohol exposure markedly upregulated the pro-apoptotic gene BAX and downregulated the antiapoptotic gene BCL2, indicating significant apoptotic activation. SCU treatment significantly increased the expression of the antiapoptotic gene BCL2 and decreased the expression of the pro-apoptotic gene BAX at both the mRNA and protein levels, thereby restoring the BAX/BCL2 ratio and suggesting its potential to suppress mitochondria-mediated apoptosis.

Inflammation is another key contributor to ALD progression, with pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 being major drivers.[17–20] Studies have shown that IL-9 mitigates inflammation and hepatocyte apoptosis in ALD by suppressing cytokine production and inhibiting STAT3 activation.[21] Fmo5 co-expressed with PPARα alleviates alcohol-induced liver damage via suppression of NF-κB p65 nuclear translocation,[22] and CD73 similarly attenuates alcohol-induced inflammation and liver injury through NF-κB inhibition.[23] Notably, scutellarein has been reported to inhibit NF-κB and MAPK activation in LPS-treated RAW264.7 cells,[24] and to exert anti-inflammatory effects in osteoarthritis[25] and acute lung injury models.[26] Consistent with these findings, our results from qRT-PCR and ELISA analyses revealed that SCU significantly reduced alcohol-induced expression of IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α. Mechanistically, SCU suppressed the phosphorylation of p65 and IκBα, indicating inhibition of the classical NF-κB inflammatory pathway and thereby reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine release.

Oxidative stress is also recognized as an early and pivotal event in ALD pathogenesis,[27–29] which was confirmed in our study. Previous studies have shown that compounds such as Kangdu granule,[30] ursolic acid,[31] and Qizhi Qinggan decoction[32] attenuate ALD by regulating oxidative stress. Additionally, the combination of quercetin and catechin has been reported to mitigate alcohol-induced acute liver injury via targeting FOXO3 and modulating IκB kinase alpha expression.[33] Scutellarein derivatives, such as compound 16d, have demonstrated neuroprotective effects against H2O2-induced oxidative stress through activation of the NRF2/HO-1 pathway.[34,35] In our study, alcohol exposure decreased the expression of antioxidant enzymes, including SOD1, SOD2, and catalase, while SCU significantly upregulated their transcriptional levels. Furthermore, SCU enhanced the expression of NRF2 and its downstream target gene NQO1, suggesting that SCU activates the NRF2 antioxidant pathway to counteract oxidative stress.

Taken together, this study provides the first comprehensive evaluation of SCU’s protective effects against alcohol-induced hepatocyte injury in vitro. The results indicate that SCU mitigates hepatocyte damage through multiple mechanisms, including enhancement of cell viability, inhibition of apoptosis, suppression of inflammatory responses, and alleviation of oxidative stress. These effects may be mediated via bidirectional regulation of the NF-κB and NRF2 signaling pathways. It is important to note that while our results demonstrate the regulatory effects of SCU on NF-κB and NRF2 pathways, the study does not provide direct evidence of specific molecular interactions, and thus the mechanistic inference should be interpreted with caution. Our findings provide a theoretical basis for the potential application of SCU in the treatment of ALD and lay the groundwork for subsequent in vivo and translational research.

5. Conclusion

This study is the first to systematically demonstrate the hepatoprotective effects of SCU in an in vitro model of alcohol-induced liver injury. SCU significantly improved cell viability, reduced levels of ALT and AST, attenuated apoptosis, suppressed the production of inflammatory cytokines, and enhanced the antioxidant defense system. These effects are likely mediated through inhibition of the NF-κB inflammatory signaling pathway and activation of the NRF2 antioxidant pathway.

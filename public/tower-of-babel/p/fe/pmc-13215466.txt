Visualization of an obliquely oriented accessory pathway and identification of the optimal ablation site using complementary mapping techniques
Authors: Shun Kikuchi, Ran Ando, Shingo Yoshioka, Masahiro Esato, Stefano Bordignon, Marco Tomasino, Henning Jansen
Published: 2026

Case description

A 69-year-old man presented with palpitations and ventricular pre-excitation on a 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG). Intracardiac electrograms recorded during sinus rhythm (SR; Panel A) and orthodromic atrioventricular reciprocating tachycardia (Panel B), using multipolar catheters positioned in the coronary sinus (CS), His bundle electrogram region (HBE), high right atrium (HRA), and right ventricular apex (RVA) identified an accessory pathway (AP) localized to the inferior aspect of the mitral annulus (MA). The earliest ventricular activation during SR was recorded at CS9/10, whereas the earliest atrial activation during tachycardia was observed at CS7/8 to CS9/10. Following a single transseptal puncture, open-window mapping (OWM)1 was performed using a three-dimensional electroanatomical mapping system (CARTO 3, version 8.1; Johnson & Johnson MedTech) during HRA pacing. Propagation mapping with the extended early-meets-late (EEML) algorithm demonstrated a broad AP, defined by an EEML gap of 10.3 mm along the MA (Panel C).

To further delineate the anatomical substrate underlying the broad AP, point-by-point pacing of the left atrium and left ventricle was performed to identify the atrial and ventricular insertion sites of the antegrade- and retrograde-conducting AP (Panels D and E). Using this inverted mapping approach,2 the AP was shown to course obliquely along the MA.

Electrograms surrounding the EEML gap were subsequently analysed using a complementary mapping function—local velocity vectors—which enables visualization of wavefront velocity on substrate maps.3 Optimization of the velocity clustering and threshold parameters (clustering level >4.5 and velocity threshold <0.20 mm/ms) identified the principal AP conduction corridor traversing the broad EEML gap (asterisk and arrows; Panel F). A single radiofrequency catheter application immediately eliminated AP conduction, as evidenced by the disappearance of the delta wave on surface ECG (Panel G). This complementary mapping strategy facilitated identification of the optimal ablation site in the presence of a broad AP on OWM.

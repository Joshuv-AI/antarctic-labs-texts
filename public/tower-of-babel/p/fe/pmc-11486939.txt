Efficacy of enhanced monofocal intraocular lens in combined phacovitrectomy for patients with photoreceptor-preserving epiretinal membrane
Authors: Seongyong Jeong, Sunggeun Son, Min Sagong
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The aim of this study is to evaluate the visual outcomes and quality of an enhanced monofocal intraocular lens (IOL), compared to a conventional monofocal IOL in patients with epiretinal membrane (ERM). This retrospective comparative study reviewed medical records of 110 eyes of 110 patients with photoreceptor-preserving ERM (55 patients in each group) who underwent phacovitrectomy and received either a conventional monofocal IOL (Tecnis PCB00) or an enhanced monofocal IOL (Tecnis Eyhance ICB00) from March 2021 to October 2021. Baseline examination included corrected (CDVA) and uncorrected (UDVA) distance visual acuity, and measurement of pupil size. Postoperative evaluation included uncorrected intermediate (UIVA) and near (UNVA) visual acuity, and defocus curve. Contrast sensitivity was assessed using CGT-2000 (Takagi, Seiko, Japan). The baseline parameters showed no difference between PCB00 and ICB00 groups. At 3 months, there was no difference of UDVA and CDVA, but UIVA (0.27 vs. 0.39 LogMAR; p = 0.001) and UNVA (0.35 vs. 0.45 LogMAR; p = 0.014) were better in ICB00 group. On the defocus curve, the ICB00 group demonstrated a slower slope decrease, with higher visual acuity from − 1.5 to -3.0 diopters when compared to the PCB00 group. The distant contrast sensitivity showed no significant difference between two groups. In conclusion, the enhanced monofocal IOL improved intermediate visual outcomes, achieving equivalent distance visual acuity and similar contrast sensitivity to the conventional monofocal IOL. It could be an alternative option to conventional monofocal IOL when planning combined phacovitrectomy for patients with photoreceptor-preserving ERM.

Introduction

Advancements in vitrectomy systems including smaller gauge, higher cutting rate, and better visualization have significantly improved the outcomes of vitreoretinal surgeries1–4. These technological improvements have facilitated the wider adoption of combined phacoemulsification and vitrectomy5,6. Compared to separated procedures, combined surgeries can reduce overall operation time, shorten the recovery period, lessen the patient’s burden, and mitigate concerns about cataract progression7,8.

Multifocal intraocular lenses (IOLs) are designed to provide a broad range of vision from distance to near9–11. Despite their benefits, the presence of retinal pathology, particularly macular disease, has been considered a contraindication to the use of multifocal lenses due to their potential harm to contrast sensitivity and visual side effects such as halo, glare, or starburst12. Consequently, the demand for spectacle independence has been difficult to achieve in patients with retinal diseases undergoing combined cataract and pars plana vitrectomy (PPV) surgeries.

The Tecnis Eyhance IOL (Tecnis ICB00, Johnson & Johnson Vision, Irvine, CA) is a monofocal IOL with a spherical posterior surface and a higher order aspheric surface design at the anterior surface13. This design enhances intermediate vision while maintaining good distance vision and minimizing the photic phenomena associated with diffractive multifocal IOLs14. Until now, the visual outcomes of ICB00 have been reported mainly for patients who underwent cataract surgery with15 or without16–19 macular diseases. Few studies reported outcomes of combined phacovitrectomy for treating retinal diseases with ICB00 IOL20–22. However, a study focusing on ERM patients was limited by a small sample size20. Other studies lacked comprehensive subjective assessments, including patient questionnaires21,22. Herein, we compared the visual outcomes and quality of vision, including contrast sensitivity and subjective questionnaires, of the ICB00 IOL with a standard monofocal IOL (Tecnis PCB00, Johnson & Johnson Vision, Irvine, CA) in patients with photoreceptor-preserving epiretinal membrane (ERM) who underwent combined phacoemulsification and vitrectomy.

Results

A total of 110 eyes of 110 patients were included in the study (55 eyes in each group). There was no difference in baseline characteristics including age, sex, spherical equivalent (SE), axial length, and central macular thickness between the PCB00 and ICB00 groups. Pupil diameter (3.47 ± 0.70 in PCB00 vs. 3.34 ± 0.78 mm in ICB00, p = 0.358), and the proportion of pseudophakia in the contralateral eye (p = 0.701) were also similar between the two groups. The distribution of ERM stages was not significantly different between the groups (Table 1).

Discussion

Our study compared the visual acuity outcomes and the satisfaction profiles of patients who received the ICB00 and those who received the conventional monofocal IOL after combined phacoemulsification and vitrectomy for photoreceptor-preserving ERM. The results showed that eyes with the ICB00 IOL had significantly better intermediate visual acuity compared to those with the conventional IOL, while maintaining distance visual acuity and contrast sensitivity.

The effectiveness of the ICB00 IOL on intermediate vision has already been demonstrated. Previous studies that compared ICB00 and conventional monofocal IOL for patients who underwent cataract surgery alone17–19,24 reported UIVA ranged from 0.20 to 0.28 LogMAR in the ICB00 group and 0.30–0.43 LogMAR in the conventional monofocal IOL group. Meanwhile, studies performing combined phacovitrectomy21,22 reported relatively lower UIVA ranging from 0.29 to 0.58 LogMAR in the ICB00 group and 0.49–0.71 LogMAR in the monofocal group, consistent with current study. It seems that retinal pathologies negatively affected both UDVA and UIVA. Nevertheless, the ICB00 IOL still demonstrated superior intermediate vision than monofocal IOL.

Interestingly, the UNVA at 3 months was also higher in ICB00 group than monofocal IOL group in current study. This may probably be attributed to the residual myopic SE, and partially to the relatively younger age of our cohort. The amplitude of accommodation after cataract surgery is known to decrease according to the age25. Thus, it seems that the effect of ICB00 IOL was reinforced by younger age in current study.

ICB00’s intermediate vision was notable because it did not seem to sacrifice clinically measured distance visual acuity under the current testing and pupillary conditions. Previous studies have proven the equivalent distance visual acuity of the ICB00 compared to the monofocal IOL after cataract surgery17–19,24 and phacovitrectomy20–22. Additionally, similar contrast sensitivity between monofocal and ICB00 IOLs has also been reported18,24. One of the major concerns with multifocal or extended depth of focus IOL is a loss of contrast sensitivity26–30. Even when visual acuity is normal, contrast sensitivity can measure subtle deterioration in visual function30. In the current study, contrast sensitivity was similar between the ICB00 and monofocal IOL groups, consistent with previous studies. It seems that the ICB00 IOL did not result in a difference in contrast sensitivity in patients with photoreceptor-preserving ERM.

Visual artifacts showed no difference between patients with ICB00 and monofocal IOLs, similar to previous studies17,31. Lopes et al.17 reported that the prevalence of halo and glare was not different between ICB00 and conventional monofocal IOL. The purely refractive design of the ICB00 might reduce light scattering and photic phenomena. Therefore, the ICB00 IOL maintained visual quality while minimizing adverse effects.

Regarding the satisfaction score for intermediate range, ICB00 showed better results than monofocal IOL group. Additionally, patients with ICB00 were relatively spectacle-independent compared to the monofocal IOL group. Although the near satisfaction score was also higher in ICB00 group, there was no significant difference in spectacle independence.

This study has several limitations including the small sample size, retrospective design, and short-term follow-up period. While our IOL selection process was designed to reflect real-world clinical practice, we acknowledge that this approach may have selection bias, which could be addressed in future studies through randomized allocation methods. Additionally, we limited the disease spectrum by testing the ICB00 only in patients with foveal photoreceptor-preserving ERM. To further validate the benefits of the ICB00 IOL, long-term studies with larger sample sizes across a wide range of ERM severities are needed.

In conclusion, the Tecnis ICB00 IOL offers significant advantages in improving intermediate visual acuity and overall visual quality for patients undergoing combined phacoemulsification and vitrectomy for ERM. Its ability to enhance intermediate vision without compromising distance vision or contrast sensitivity makes it an alternative to conventional monofocal IOLs. The ICB00 IOL can be adopted for patients with photoreceptor-preserving ERM, providing a broader range of clear vision and reducing dependence on spectacles.

Methods

Study design and data collection

We retrospectively reviewed the medical data of patients who had PPV and phacoemulsification with IOL implatation for photoreceptor-preserving ERM and senile cataract from March 2021 to October 2021 at Yeungnam University Hospital. The Institutional Review Board (IRB) of the Yeungnam University Hospital approved the protocol (approval number: 2023-04-026), and all procedures in this study adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. Due to the retrospective nature of the study, the IRB of Yeungnam University Hospital waived the need of obtaining informed consent. Exclusion criteria included disruption of ellipsoid zone, or loss of clear identification between inner retinal layers in pre-operative spectral-domain optical coherence tomography (SD-OCT; Heidelberg Engineering, Heidelberg, Germany), corneal astigmatism > 1.25 D, previous ocular trauma or surgery, refractive target other than emmetropia and presence of other retinal disease. Before the surgery, all patients underwent a comprehensive ophthalmic examination, including a slit-lamp examination, detailed fundus examination, non-contact tonometry, and SD-OCT.

Baseline examination

Visual acuity was assessed for CDVA and UDVA. Axial length was measured with the IOL Master 700 (Carl Zeiss, Oberkochen, Germany), and keratometry was evaluated using the KR-800 A (Topcon, Tokyo, Japan). Manifest refraction was conducted by a trained ophthalmologist, and the SE was calculated. Pupil size was captured under photopic condition using the Keratograph 4 (Oculus, Wetzlar, Germany). The lens status of the contralateral eye was assessed as part of the baseline characteristics, to account for potential influences on the patient satisfaction outcomes. The severity of ERM was classified into four stages according to the classification system proposed by Govetto et al.32, which is based on anatomical changes including the presence of ectopic inner foveal layers observed on SD-OCT images.

Surgical procedure and IOL selection

Indication for surgical treatment was restricted to the idiopathic ERM that caused foveal anatomical alteration and visual symptoms (metamorphopsia, visual distortion, or decreased of vision). The profiles and characteristics of monofocal (Tecnis PCB00) and enhanced monofocal (Tecnis Eyhance ICB00) IOL were fully explained to the patients to select suitable IOL for each individual. This explanation included detailed information about the design differences between ICB00 and PCB00, expected visual outcomes, and potential side effects. IOLs were chosen based on patients’ visual needs, occupation, and lifestyle considerations, with ICB00 particularly recommended to patients desiring improved intermediate vision. The final IOL selection was made at the discretion of the operating surgeon. However, this decision was made after thorough consultation with the patient and consideration of their preferences. One retinal specialist (MS) performed all surgical procedure. For the phacoemulsification, 2.75 mm clear corneal incision was made at the superior cornea. PPV was performed using 25-guage system. The membrane peeling was always accompanied by inner limiting membrane (ILM) removal. The ILM was peeled including the whole macular area, extending close to the vascular arcades. Foveal-sparing techniques were not performed in this study. Because the IOL was implanted at the end of the surgery, there was no difference of visualization between the PCB00 and ICB00 group. The corneal wound was not sutured, and the trocar sites were sutured only when there was a definite leakage using releasable method. The releasable suture was removed within 3 days after surgery.

Functional outcomes measurement

Outpatient follow-up was recommended for patients at 1 weeks, 1 months, 3 months, and 6 months postoperatively. At each follow-up, UDVA, CDVA, UIVA, UNVA, and SE were assessed. Distance visual acuity was measured at 5 m using Snellen chart. A near or intermediate vision chart (Johnson & Johnson vision, Irvine, CA) was presented at 66 cm for UIVA, and at 30 cm for UNVA. Monocular defocus curves were measured at 3 months. Visual acuity was measured in a single room under photopic conditions with a controlled illuminance level of 400 lx33.

Subjective questionnaires were used to evaluate visual qualities such as glare, halo, and starburst, as well as satisfaction scores for intermediate and near work, employing a modified scoring system from the Quality of Vision questionnaire21,22 at 3 month.

Contrast sensitivity was measured at 3 months with the CGT-2000 device (Takagi Seiko, Japan). Measurements of minimum contrast that patients could perceive were taken at six spatial frequencies (6.3°, 4°, 2.5°, 1.6°, 1°, and 0.64°) under photopic condition. The contrast sensitivity for distant and intermediate range were conducted sequentially. The recorded values were converted to logarithmic scale (log contrast sensitivity) for statistical analysis.

Statistical analysis

For data analysis, SPSS software for windows version 18.0 (SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL) was used. BCVA that measured with Snellen chart was converted to the LogMAR units for statistical analyses. Numerical variables were compared using independent-t test between PCB00 and ICB00 group. Categorical variables analyzed by χ2 test. Variables with a p-value < 0.05 were considered significant.

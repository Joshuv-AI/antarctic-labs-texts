AIIT-02 INNOVATIVE MINIMALLY INVASIVE LASER INTERSTITIAL THERMAL THERAPY IN NEWLY DIAGNOSED AND RECURRENT GLIOBLASTOMA: A SYSTEMATIC REVIEW AND META-ANALYSIS OF SURGICAL OUTCOMES AND COMPLICATIONS
Authors: Caitlyn Smith, Jeremiah Daniel, Ruby Taylor, Kamel Chebaro, Soumya Pahari, Victor Navarro
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and AimLaser Interstitial Thermal Therapy (LITT) has become a minimally invasive treatment for glioblastoma multiforme (GBM), but a recent meta‑analysis by Rangwala et al. 2024 did not stratify outcomes by disease status, Karnofsky Performance Scale (KPS) scores, or molecular markers. We performed an updated systematic review and meta‑analysis, per PRISMA guidelines, to compare LITT outcomes in newly diagnosed versus recurrent GBM and to assess the impact of preoperative KPS scores and IDH mutation status.MethodsWe searched PubMed, EMBASE, Cochrane, Web of Science, Scopus, and Google Scholar till February 2025. Twenty‑five studies (596 patients) met inclusion criteria. A random-effects model was used to pool outcome data, and subgroup analyses compared newly diagnosed versus recurrent disease (307 vs. 289 patients), KPS ≥ 80 versus < 80 (12 studies), and IDH‑mutant versus wild‑type tumours (8 studies).ResultsFor overall survival (OS), newly diagnosed GBM (n = 307) achieved a pooled OS of 11.0 months (95% CI 8.9–13.1; I²=58%), while recurrent GBM (n = 289) had an OS of 8.5 months (95% CI 6.7–10.3; I²=64%). Post-operative motor complications occurred in 20% of patients, cerebral edema in 7%, seizures in 8%, and DVT/PE in 8%. The average hospital stay was 2.3 days (95% CI = 2.01–2.59). In exploratory subgroup analyses, patients with preoperative KPS ≥ 80 showed longer OS (12.5 vs. 7.8 months; p < 0.01) and PFS (4.1 vs. 1.7 months; p = 0.02), and IDH‑mutant tumours trended toward improved OS compared to wild‑type (13.2 vs. 9.1 months; p = 0.06).ConclusionsLITT offers a safe, minimally invasive option with a short recovery period and modest survival benefit, particularly in patients with high KPS, IDH‑mutant status, and deep‑seated tumours. Prospective, multicenter RCTs are urgently needed to define LITT’s role in GBM management.

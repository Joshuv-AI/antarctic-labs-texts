Integrated bioinformatics analysis identifies RPS27A and PPP2R1A as shared hub genes in heart failure and COVID-19
Authors: Tong Lang, Xinyu Liu, Lingbing Meng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHeart failure (HF) and COVID-19 are distinct but serious conditions with overlapping features such as systemic inflammation and multiorgan involvement. Both may share underlying molecular mechanisms, but the common genetic contributors remain poorly understood. This study aimed to identify shared key genes involved in HF and COVID-19 using integrative bioinformatics analysis.MethodsTwo transcriptomic datasets GSE76701 (HF myocardial tissue) and GSE190496 (COVID-19 lung tissue)—were retrieved from the GEO database. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using the limma package. Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) and protein–protein interaction (PPI) network analysis were used to identify hub genes. Functional enrichment (GO, KEGG, GSEA), immune infiltration (CIBERSORT), disease association (CTD), and miRNA prediction (TargetScan) analyses were also performed.ResultsA total of 511 overlapping DEGs were identified. Enrichment analyses revealed involvement of pathways such as mTOR, insulin, and thyroid hormone signaling. WGCNA and PPI network analysis identified five core genes, including RPS27A and PPP2R1A, both of which were significantly upregulated in HF and COVID-19 samples. Immune infiltration analysis revealed increased macrophage fractions. miRNA predictions suggested PPP2R1A is potentially regulated by miR-497-5p, miR-15b-5p, and miR-15a-5p.ConclusionRPS27A and PPP2R1A may serve as common molecular regulators in HF and COVID-19. These findings offer potential cross-disease biomarkers and therapeutic targets for conditions driven by inflammation and immune dysregulation.Keywords: RPS27A, PPP2R1A, Heart failure, COVID-19, Bioinformatics, Prognosis

Introduction

Heart failure (HF) remains a major global health challenge, characterized by impaired cardiac output and often accompanied by comorbidities such as hypertension, coronary artery disease, and diabetes. Despite therapeutic advances, HF continues to be a leading cause of morbidity and mortality, especially in older populations [1–3].

Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19), caused by the SARS-CoV-2 virus, emerged in 2019 and rapidly evolved into a global pandemic [4]. While primarily affecting the respiratory system, COVID-19 also has profound systemic effects, including cardiovascular complications such as myocardial injury, arrhythmias, and worsening of pre-existing HF [5, 6]. Severe cases are frequently associated with cytokine storms, coagulation dysfunction, and widespread pulmonary inflammation.

Although HF and COVID-19 differ in clinical presentation and etiology, recent evidence suggests that they may share underlying molecular mechanisms. Inflammation, immune dysregulation, and cellular stress responses are common hallmarks of both conditions. Additionally, both diseases have been associated with chromosomal instability, genetic alterations, and aberrant gene expression patterns, highlighting the importance of identifying convergent molecular pathways.

The advent of next-generation sequencing (NGS) technologies and the availability of large-scale public transcriptomic datasets have enabled the use of bioinformatics tools to systematically explore disease-related genes and signaling pathways [7]. Such integrative analyses can reveal novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets, offering insights into complex disease mechanisms [8].

Among the genes of interest, RPS27A encodes a ribosomal protein essential for ribosome biogenesis and protein translation [9]. It is synthesized as a ubiquitin-fused protein and plays additional roles in cell growth, proliferation, and apoptosis. PPP2R1A encodes a scaffolding subunit of protein phosphatase 2 A (PP2A), a serine/threonine phosphatase involved in regulating pathways linked to inflammation, cell cycle, and stress response [10]. While both genes have been implicated in various pathological processes, their specific involvement in HF and COVID-19 remains unclear.

In this study, we aimed to identify and characterize shared core genes between HF and COVID-19 using publicly available transcriptomic datasets and integrated bioinformatics approaches. We focused on RPS27A and PPP2R1A, identified as common hub genes through differential expression and network analyses, and further explored their potential roles through functional enrichment, immune infiltration, and miRNA regulatory analyses. These findings may enhance our understanding of shared disease mechanisms and provide novel cross-disease therapeutic targets.

Methods

HF and COVID-19 datasets

In this study, we retrieved two gene expression datasets from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/): GSE76701, based on the GPL570 platform, and GSE190496, based on the GPL24676 platform. The GSE76701 dataset comprises 4 myocardial tissue samples from heart failure (HF) patients and 4 samples from healthy controls. The GSE190496 dataset includes 12 pulmonary tissue samples from COVID-19 patients and 5 samples from healthy individuals. These datasets were used to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) associated with HF and COVID-19.

Identification of differentially expressed genes (DEGs)

Raw expression matrices were preprocessed using the R package limma, including background correction and normalization. Statistical significance was assessed using the Benjamini-Hochberg method to control the false discovery rate (FDR). DEGs were defined as genes with adjusted p-values < 0.05 and fold change > 1.2. Volcano plots were generated to visualize the distribution of DEGs.

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)

To reduce noise and enhance stability, we first calculated the median absolute deviation (MAD) for each gene and excluded the bottom 50% of genes with the lowest variability. Outlier genes and samples were filtered using the goodSamplesGenes function in the WGCNA R package. A scale-free co-expression network was then constructed using Pearson correlation matrices and the average linkage method. Pairwise gene correlations were converted into a weighted adjacency matrix using the power function: amn​=∣cmn​∣β. where cmnc_{mn}cmn​ is the Pearson correlation between genes m and n, and β\betaβ is the soft-thresholding power that emphasizes strong correlations. A soft threshold of β = 9 was selected to ensure scale-free topology.

The adjacency matrix was transformed into a topological overlap matrix (TOM) to reflect network connectivity. Genes were clustered using hierarchical clustering based on TOM dissimilarity, with a minimum module size of 30 and cut height sensitivity set to 3. Module eigengenes were then computed, and similar modules were merged using a cut height of 0.25. Ultimately, ten distinct gene modules were identified, with the grey module representing unassigned genes.

Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network construction and analysis

To explore functional interactions, the identified DEGs were input into the STRING database (https://string-db.org/) with a confidence threshold of > 0.4 to construct a predicted PPI network. The resulting interaction network was imported into Cytoscape for visualization and core gene identification.

Three centrality algorithms—MCC (Maximal Clique Centrality), MNC (Maximum Neighborhood Component), and DMNC (Density of Maximum Neighborhood Component)—were applied to rank the top ten hub genes. The intersecting genes among the three algorithms were considered core genes. These were subsequently visualized and exported for downstream analyses.

Functional enrichment analysis

To explore the biological functions and pathways associated with differentially expressed genes (DEGs), we performed Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses. The DEG list was submitted to the KEGG REST API (https://www.kegg.jp/kegg/rest/keggapi.html) to retrieve the latest pathway annotations, which served as the background set. Enrichment analysis was conducted using the clusterProfiler R package (v3.14.3), mapping genes to the KEGG background.

GO annotation was performed using the org.Hs.eg.db R package (v3.1.0) as the annotation source. Enrichment significance was defined by a minimum gene set size of 5, a maximum size of 5000, P-value < 0.05, and false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.25.

Additionally, the Metascape database was utilized for comprehensive gene annotation and enrichment analysis. Enrichment results from Metascape were exported and visualized for further interpretation.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

For Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA), samples were grouped into HF vs. normal tissue and COVID-19 vs. normal tissue. The c2.cp.kegg.v7.4.symbols.gmt gene set, obtained from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) (http://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/downloads.jsp), was used to assess enriched KEGG pathways. GSEA was performed using the standalone GSEA software (v3.0).

The input included normalized gene expression matrices and corresponding phenotype labels. Analysis parameters were set to a minimum gene set size of 5, a maximum size of 5000, 1000 permutations, and thresholds of P-value < 0.05 and FDR < 0.25. In addition to pathway-level enrichment, genome-wide GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were conducted using the same GSEA framework.

Gene expression heatmaps

Heatmaps were generated using the R package heatmap to visualize the expression levels of core genes identified through the three centrality algorithms in the PPI network. These plots illustrated differential expression patterns of the core genes between HF and control myocardial tissues as well as between COVID-19 and control lung tissues.

Immune infiltration analysis

Immune cell infiltration was estimated using CIBERSORT (https://cibersort.stanford.edu/), a deconvolution algorithm that quantifies 22 immune cell subtypes based on gene expression profiles defined in the LM22 signature matrix. Gene expression matrices from GSE76701 and GSE190496 were analyzed using CIBERSORT with linear support vector regression (SVR). Only samples with a CIBERSORT output P-value < 0.05 were considered reliable and included in the final immune composition analysis.

CTD analysis

To explore disease associations, the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD) was used to analyze interactions between the identified core genes and disease phenotypes. The core genes were submitted to the CTD web tool to retrieve the most relevant associated diseases. Expression differences across conditions were further visualized using radar plots, created in Microsoft Excel.

MiRNA target prediction

Predicted microRNA–gene interactions were identified using TargetScan (https://www.targetscan.org/), an established online database for miRNA target prediction. Core DEGs identified from the PPI network were input to screen for candidate miRNAs with potential regulatory functions.

Results

Identification of differentially expressed genes

Based on the predefined thresholds, differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified in the GSE76701 (HF) and GSE190496 (COVID-19) datasets. In total, 511 overlapping DEGs were detected between the two datasets (Fig. 1A–C).

Identification of differentially expressed genes

Functional enrichment analysis

GO and KEGG pathway analysis of DEGs

We performed GO and KEGG enrichment analyses to investigate the biological functions and signaling pathways associated with the identified DEGs.

According to GO analysis, DEGs were significantly enriched in terms such as cytokine-mediated signaling, protein complex binding, thyroid hormone receptor binding, cell adhesion molecule binding, and components of the endomembrane system (Fig. 2A–C).

GO and KEGG pathway analysis of DEGs

KEGG pathway analysis revealed that the DEGs were predominantly involved in the mTOR signaling pathway, insulin signaling pathway, and thyroid hormone signaling pathway (Fig. 2D).

GSEA analysis

To validate the DEG-based enrichment and uncover additional non-differentially expressed but functionally relevant genes, we conducted GSEA on genome-wide expression profiles.

The GSEA results showed that both GSE76701 (HF) and GSE190496 (COVID-19) datasets were enriched in gene sets related to cytokine response, protein complex binding, thyroid hormone receptor binding, mTOR signaling, and insulin signaling, which were consistent with the GO and KEGG enrichment results (Fig. 3A–H).

GSEA analysis

Metascape enrichment analysis

Further functional analysis using Metascape identified enriched GO terms such as neural tube closure, negative regulation of MAPK cascade, response to toxic substances, and cell cycle regulation (Fig. 4 A).

Metascape enrichment analysis

To better visualize the relationships and confidence levels among the enriched terms, we constructed enrichment networks, colored by functional categories and statistical significance (Figs. 4B–C and 5).

Enrichment analysis for the DEGs

WGCNA analysis

Determining an appropriate soft-thresholding power is essential for ensuring a scale-free network in WGCNA. Based on network topology analysis, a soft-thresholding power of β = 9 was selected (Fig. 6 A, B). Hierarchical clustering was performed on all genes, resulting in the identification of co-expression modules (Fig. 6 C, D).

WGCNA analysis

Module–trait relationships were assessed, and a correlation heatmap was generated to illustrate associations between modules and disease phenotypes (Fig. 7 A). We also constructed scatter plots of gene significance (GS) versus module membership (MM) to identify clinically relevant hub genes (Fig. 7B, C).

Module–trait relationships and the gene significance

Modules were further summarized using module eigengenes, and genes with absolute MM values greater than 0.8 (|MM| >0.8) were considered highly connected. A total of 1,060 hub genes were identified within the clinically significant modules.

To refine the hub gene list, we constructed a Venn diagram to visualize the intersection between DEGs and WGCNA-derived hub genes. This intersected set was then used for subsequent PPI network analysis (Fig. 7D).

Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network construction and analysis

The intersection of DEGs and WGCNA hub genes was input into the STRING database to construct a PPI network, which was then visualized and analyzed using Cytoscape (Fig. 8A). Three topological algorithms—MCC, MNC, and DMNC—were applied to rank genes by connectivity (Fig. 8B–D). The overlap among the top-ranked genes from these algorithms was visualized using a Venn diagram, resulting in the identification of five core genes: RPS27A, PPP2R1A, MAP3K14, DVL1, and PSME4 (Fig. 8E).

Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network construction and analysis

In addition, we used Metascape to validate the PPI network results and to identify functionally enriched modules. This analysis confirmed the central roles of RPS27A and PPP2R1A as conserved core genes across different network-based approaches.

Gene expression heatmaps

To visualize expression patterns of key genes, we generated heatmaps for the five core genes (RPS27A, PPP2R1A, MAP3K14, DVL1, and PSME4) in both datasets. These genes exhibited consistently higher expression levels in HF (GSE76701) and COVID-19 (GSE190496) samples compared to their respective healthy controls (Fig. 9 A, B), suggesting their potential regulatory roles in both disease contexts.

CTD analysis

Although specific fold-change values are not shown, differential expression analysis using the limma package with Benjamini–Hochberg correction revealed that RPS27A and PPP2R1A were significantly upregulated (adjusted P < 0.05) in both datasets. This coordinated upregulation across two independent diseases implies that these genes may act as shared drivers in the pathogenesis of HF and COVID-19.

CTD analysis

To further explore disease associations, we submitted the identified core genes to the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD). This analysis revealed that RPS27A and PPP2R1A are linked to several clinical phenotypes, including fever, cough, fatigue, asthma, vomiting, necrosis, and chemical or drug-induced liver injury (Fig. 9 C). These findings support the relevance of the core genes to systemic inflammatory responses seen in both HF and COVID-19.

Immune infiltration analysis

Immune cell composition was assessed using CIBERSORT. In the HF dataset (GSE76701), immune deconvolution revealed distinct infiltration patterns (Fig. 10 A). Heatmaps illustrated the abundance of immune cell subsets (Fig. 10B), and correlation analysis among immune cells identified a notable increase in M1 macrophages (Fig. 10 C), suggesting a pro-inflammatory environment in HF tissue.

Immune infiltration analysis

Similarly, in the COVID-19 dataset (GSE190496), CIBERSORT analysis indicated elevated levels of M0 macrophages and a strong positive correlation with resting dendritic cells (Figs. 11 A–C). These immune profiles are consistent with monocyte/macrophage activation and immune dysregulation, hallmark features of severe COVID-19 pathology.

CIBERSORT analysis

MiRNA target prediction

To investigate potential post-transcriptional regulation, we used TargetScan to predict miRNAs targeting the hub genes. For PPP2R1A, three candidate miRNAs were identified: hsa-miR-497-5p, hsa-miR-15b-5p, and hsa-miR-15a-5p (Table 1). These miRNAs have previously been implicated in regulating pathways related to cell proliferation, inflammation, and cardiac remodeling, highlighting their potential relevance to HF and COVID-19 pathogenesis.

Discussion

Heart failure (HF) and COVID-19 are both major public health concerns that impose significant morbidity and mortality risks. HF is characterized by the inability of the heart to pump sufficient blood to meet systemic demands, resulting in symptoms such as dyspnea, fatigue, and organ congestion [11, 12]. Despite medical advances, it remains a major contributor to global disease burden [10, 13]. COVID-19, caused by SARS-CoV-2, is a highly infectious respiratory illness that can lead to severe outcomes including ARDS, multi-organ failure, and long-term sequelae [14, 15]. In the present study, we identified RPS27A and PPP2R1A as commonly and significantly upregulated genes in heart failure myocardial tissues (GSE76701) and COVID-19 lung tissues (GSE190496). The elevated expression of these genes may underlie shared pathogenic mechanisms and correlate with adverse prognosis [16].

RPS27A encodes a ribosomal protein that is synthesized as a ubiquitin fusion and plays a key role in ribosome biogenesis and protein synthesis [9]. In both datasets, RPS27A was significantly overexpressed in disease samples, suggesting that enhanced translational activity may be a stress-adaptive or pathological response. Given that cardiac remodeling in HF often requires increased protein turnover to support myocyte repair, abnormal upregulation of RPS27A could disturb protein homeostasis [17–19]. RPS27A is critical for the accurate assembly of protein chains on ribosomes via mRNA decoding, and disruptions in its function may impair cardiac muscle regeneration or contribute to maladaptive hypertrophy. Moreover, ribosomal proteins like RPS27A also have extraribosomal functions, such as modulating apoptosis and cell cycle regulation [20].

Mechanistically, RPS27A has been shown to interact with RPL11, where its knockdown enhances RPL11 binding to MDM2, stabilizes p53, and promotes apoptosis [21]. This RPL11–MDM2–p53 signaling axis is relevant in cardiomyocyte stress, potentially promoting cell cycle arrest and cell death in HF. In parallel, Eastham et al. demonstrated that RPS27A and RPL40 play essential roles in p53 induction during ribosome biogenesis defects [22]. In the context of COVID-19, where viral replication demands host ribosomal machinery, dysregulation of RPS27A may influence viral protein synthesis and the balance between host defense and viral propagation. The consistent upregulation of RPS27A across two distinct disease tissues supports its role in a shared stress-responsive regulatory network.

PPP2R1A encodes a scaffolding subunit of protein phosphatase 2 A (PP2A), a key enzyme in serine/threonine dephosphorylation that regulates cell cycle progression, metabolic homeostasis, and immune signaling [23, 24]. Elevated PPP2R1A expression in both datasets implies altered phosphatase complex assembly or function, potentially driving aberrant signaling cascades. Clinical evidence supports this hypothesis: Baker et al. identified four novel PPP2R1A mutations in patients with coronary artery disease, associated with ventricular hypertrophy, corpus callosum atrophy, and septal and pulmonary structural anomalies, underscoring PPP2R1A’s involvement in cardiovascular development and disease. Given its central regulatory role, dysregulated PPP2R1A may affect multiple cellular pathways through altered phosphorylation dynamics [25, 26].

Moreover, PPP2R1A influences immune responses. Chen et al. showed that the PP2A–B56α holoenzyme mediates macrophage M1 polarization via the mTOR–p70S6K/4E-BP1 signaling axis, playing a crucial role in lung injury models [27]. In our immune infiltration analysis using CIBERSORT, we observed increased macrophage M1 fractions in HF and macrophage M0 dominance in COVID-19, suggesting that PPP2R1A may contribute to inflammatory macrophage activity in both diseases. Additionally, PPP2R1A is part of the WNK1–OSR1–PPP2R1A axis, which regulates angiogenesis and endothelial migration in cancer models [28]. This pathway may also be relevant in COVID-19-induced vascular remodeling and tissue hypoxia.

Our study also integrated findings from the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD), linking RPS27A and PPP2R1A to clinical manifestations such as fever, fatigue, vomiting, asthma, and liver injury—symptoms commonly observed in both HF and COVID-19. Additionally, miRNA target analysis identified hsa-miR-497-5p, hsa-miR-15b-5p, and hsa-miR-15a-5p as regulators of PPP2R1A. These miRNAs have been previously implicated in cardiac stress, fibrosis, and antiviral responses, indicating possible post-transcriptional regulatory mechanisms in disease progression.

Despite employing rigorous bioinformatic approaches including differential analysis, WGCNA, and PPI network screening, our study has limitations. The small sample size in GSE76701 (n = 4 per group) may constrain statistical generalizability. Although FDR correction and cross-validation across algorithms were performed, larger datasets and additional transcriptomic resources are needed to confirm robustness. Furthermore, due to constraints in laboratory resources, we did not perform qRT-PCR or Western blot validation of RPS27A and PPP2R1A expression, nor functional assays such as gene knockdown or overexpression in cellular or animal models. These will be addressed in future work through the use of clinical tissue samples and model systems.

In addition, future studies may benefit from incorporating variant-level analyses—such as single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) or somatic mutations—to complement gene expression-based findings. Such approaches may enable finer-resolution biomarker discovery, as demonstrated by Taz and Arslanturk in their study on aggressive prostate cancer [29].

In conclusion, our study demonstrates that RPS27A and PPP2R1A are consistently upregulated in heart failure and COVID-19 tissues and may serve as central regulators of protein synthesis, phosphatase-mediated signaling, and immune activity. Their expression profiles, mechanistic roles, and literature-supported associations suggest they are potential cross-disease biomarkers and therapeutic targets. Further experimental validation will help clarify their roles in clinical outcomes and treatment strategies.

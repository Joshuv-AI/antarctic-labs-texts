Synergistic enhancement of electrochemical performance in AA2024 aluminum alloy processed through severe plastic deformation with nanostructured architecture for innovative applications
Authors: Majid Naseri, Omid Imantalab, Parviz Mohamadian Samim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study provides new insights into the influence of grain refinement and crystallographic texture on the passivation behavior and electrochemical response of AA2024 aluminum alloy in phosphate buffer solution (pH 9). A nano-grained (NG, 100–150 nm) microstructure with high-intensity {011} orientations, i.e., Goss {011}<100>, Brass {011}<211>, and P {110}<221 > texture components, was produced through cross accumulative roll bonding (CARB) technique. Electrochemical tests reveal a ~40% reduction in corrosion current density (Icorr) for the NG alloy compared to its coarse-grained (CG) counterpart. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) confirmed this, showing a passive film resistance approximately one order of magnitude higher for the NG material. Consequently, these findings illustrate that the CARB technique concurrently refines grains and develops a favorable texture, synergistically enhancing passivation to provide exceptional corrosion resistance in AA2024 alloy.

Introduction

Severe plastic deformation (SPD) materials are viewed as structural and functional materials of the next generation of metals and alloys. Nowadays, one effective technique for mediating the electrochemical responses of the aluminum (Al) and its alloys is SPD1–3. In SPD, high levels of plastic strain and deformation-induced heat are used to promote dynamic recovery and recrystallization, which greatly decreases grain size and homogenizes the second phase’s dispersion4–9. Since SPD, which is represented by high-pressure torsion (HPT)10–12, constrained groove pressing (CGP)13,14, equal-channel angular extrusion/pressing (ECAE/ECAP)15–17, cyclic closed-die forging (CCDF)18–22, and accumulative roll bonding (ARB)23,24, emerged as a technique for fabricating bulk nanocrystalline (NC) or ultrafine-grained (UFG) materials, the influence of plastic deformation on electrochemical properties has once again gained attention. The ARB technique involves multiple cycles of rolling, cutting, and stacking, where the rolling stage simultaneously deforms and bonds the layers. In comparison to other SPD techniques, ARB is well-suited for mass production as it utilizes conventional rolling apparatus and directly produces sheet-like semi-finished products ready for further work-forming operations25–29.

Microstructural characteristics, including dislocation density, misorientation, and grain boundary structures, gradually progress into an ultimate form of UFG with changes in flow stress, stored energy, and grain size in the ultrahigh strain range (approximately 4 to more than 20–30)30–34. Dislocations inside the grains decrease after reaching the peak dislocation density of approximately 1015 m−2, which coincides with the almost full UFG structure; however, some residual dislocations still exist35–39. The electrochemical behavior of metals and alloys may be affected by such high-density grain boundaries with residual dislocations. Remarkably, corrosion resistance of most SPD-processed materials is improved in passivation-forming environments with a few exceptions1,2. Furthermore, the apparent discrepancies in the literature about the degree to which SPD influences the electrochemical behavior of the same materials and under the same environments could be related to variations in the level of perfection of NC or UFG structures and crystallographic texture40–42. Recently, Miyamoto et al.1,2 provided an overview of recent advances in the corrosion of NC or UFG materials processed by SPD techniques. Following SPD processing, the corrosion rate initially increases due to the development of metastable microstructures, dislocations, and dislocation boundaries. Conversely, the formation of specific crystallographic textures and changes in second-phase particles may subsequently hinder the corrosion rate. Given these insights and the complex microstructural landscape they describe, the convergence of nanotechnology and artificial intelligence has emerged as a promising frontier for corrosion mitigation, offering opportunities for enhanced protection and extended service life43–47.

From an electrochemical perspective, aluminum alloys typically exhibit high corrosion resistance due to their protective oxide layer. However, the specific influence of strain-path-dependent microstructure evolution and texture on electrochemical performance remains challenging to understand, forming the central focus of the current research. The literature indicates that the ARB-processed AA2024 alloy provides worse electrochemical behavior than its counterpart with coarse grains due to the high density of defects and the significantly more difficult formation of a dense passive film48–50. It is well known that electrochemical properties are altered by deformation routes, which cause noteworthy variations in microstructure such as grain size and grain size distribution, defect and defect density, and crystallographic textures28,51–54.

It should be noted that the deformation route is changed using cross-rolling and can be implemented in ARB as a cross accumulative roll bonding (CARB, rotating 90° around the normal direction axis between each cycle) technique55,56. Understanding the preferred orientations or textures of ARB-processed sheets is essential for successfully investigating deformation-induced structural changes. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, the effects of strain path-dependent microstructure evolution on passivation behavior are not straightforward27,57. Through a comprehensive review of the current status of research through different deformation routes, it can be concluded that the CARB technique plays a key role in the electrochemical performance of the AA2024 alloy. Therefore, the current study is dealing with the effect of grain refinement on the electrochemical properties of the AA2024 alloy successfully processed through the CARB technique, considering both electrochemical tests in a phosphate buffer solution (pH = 9) and microstructural assessments. The environment serves as an excellent model system to fundamentally understand the corrosion and inhibition mechanisms of AA2024 at a controlled pH where its passive film is marginally stable, using phosphate as a representative inhibitor anion.

Microstructural characterization

Microstructures were analyzed using optical microscopy (OM), scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM, Philips Tecnai F20 S-TWIN), electron backscattered diffraction (EBSD), and X-ray diffraction (XRD, Rigaku Ultima IV) with a 3 kW Cu X-ray tube at 40 kV and 30 mA; the diffraction angle of 2θ ranged from 10° to 90° with a step size of 0.02°. X’Pert High Score Plus software was employed for qualitative and quantitative analysis of the XRD data. To conduct the STEM measurements, thin foils were prepared by electropolishing, from which 3 mm diameter discs were extracted. These discs were then milled using a Thermo Fisher Scientific Helios G4 Focused Ion Beam (FIB) apparatus. Orientation maps were acquired via Transmission Kikuchi Diffraction (TKD) within the same system at an accelerating voltage of 15 kV and a beam current of 6.4 nA. To conduct the EBSD measurements, the sheet normal direction (ND) was used as the reference axis for inverse pole figure (IPF) map. EBSD measurements were performed with a step size of 50 nm. The acquired data were used to investigate grain morphology and to calculate the {111} pole figures (PFs) using TSL-OIM™ software (EDAX, USA).

Electrochemical measurements

The specimens of the electrochemical test were prepared by an electro discharge machining method (in the rolling direction), which exposed a 10L mm × 10W mm area. Prior to each electrochemical test, the exposed surface area (rolling direction- transverse direction (RD-TD)) of the specimens was ground to 4000 grit and cleaned with deionized water prior to tests. A P-45X potentiostat/galvanostat, controlled by a personal computer, equipped with the commercial CorrWare Zplot software, and a conventional three-electrode corrosion glass cell (i.e., working electrode (WE, AA2024 specimens) + counter electrode (CE, a Pt plate) + reference electrode (RE, Ag/AgCl saturated in KCl)) were used throughout the electrochemical measurements (Fig. 3). Prior to electrochemical measurements, the working electrodes were immersed at open circuit potential (OCP) for 3 h in phosphate buffer solution (pH = 9) to form a steady-state passive film. Potentiodynamic polarization (PDP) was measured (scan rate = 0.001 V/s), starting from − 250 mV (vs. Ecorr) to above the trans-passive potential. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were done at OCP conditions using an excitation voltage amplitude of 10 mV (peak to peak) with a scanned frequency range of 100,000–0.001 Hz. To confirm the reproducibility of the electrochemical measurements, each test was repeated three times. For data modeling and curve-fitting, CorrWare and Zplot software with a chi-squared value of less than 0.01 were used.

Results and discussion

The XRD patterns of the annealed and nano-grained AA2024 alloys are illustrated in Fig. 2, where the second-phase S-type precipitates and the Al phase’s associated diffraction peaks are indicated. As can be seen, a high degree of strain accumulated in the crystal structure and microstructure refinement induce the peak broadening to occur during CARB processing. Furthermore, the peak intensity ratio I111/I200 shows a noticeable variation, which could possibly indicate the development of deformation texture58–62. This ratio is noticeably higher in the nano-grained specimen than in the annealed AA2024 alloy. Regarding Fig. 3, it is observed that the peak broadening and the Bragg peaks superimposing on top of one another prevent the major peaks of the S-type precipitate phase from being readily apparent in the nano-grained AA2024 alloy. According to numerous previous studies63–67, these changes in XRD characteristics during the SPD processing can be a reasonable result.

This EEC was previously used by Fatah-alhosseini et al.48 to describe the passive behavior of AA2024 in borate buffer solution. It should be noted that the single time-constant model is the ideal case against which real systems are compared. Therefore, its use is justified when the system under investigation approximates these ideal conditions or as a first-order approximation to understand the primary bottleneck91,92. For the validation of extracted data from the experimental EIS spectra, it is necessary that the studied electrochemical system complies with the three requirements (i.e., linearity, causality, and stability) formulated for the constraints of the linear system theory (LST). The validation of the reported data can be investigated using Kronig-Kramers transforms (KKTs), which are obtained by transforming the imaginary and real axes into the real and imaginary axes, respectively. The formulations of KKTs in more detail were reported in scientifically released papers97–99. According to Fig. 7, good agreement between the experimental points and the corresponding KKTs validates the compliance of the studied system with the LST.

The crystallographic texture is another significant factor affecting the corrosion performance of materials in various environments. According to the literature survey100–102, higher atomic density crystallographic planes demonstrate superior corrosion resistance. Yang et al.103 examined the relative grain boundary energies in Al and found that boundaries with a <001> misorientation axis-typically associated with {100}//rolling plane (RP) textured grains-have higher energy than those with <101> or <111> axes (associated with {110}//RP and {111}//RP grains, respectively). This higher energy can be attributed to a comparatively weaker dependence of boundary energy on inclination, which is insufficient to drive the formation of a single low-energy facet, as commonly observed in low-angle boundaries. Notably, the residual inclination dependence that does exist differs between <100> and <111> tilt boundaries, which is reflected in their distinct equilibrium shapes. Separately, considering bulk texture effects, alloys with a strong {110}//RP texture exhibit superior corrosion resistance and performance. This is likely linked to the fact that the close-packed <110> direction in face-centered cubic (FCC) crystals is aligned with the rolling plane in this texture, which may influence surface reactivity and corrosion behavior104,105. {111} pole figures of the annealed and nano-grained AA2024 alloy are shown in Fig. 8. It can be seen that the main texture orientations of the annealed AA2024 alloy are Cube {001} <100>, S {123}<634>, and Copper {112}<111 > with maximum intensities of 1.5 × R, 1.4 × R, and 1.0 × R respectively (Fig. 8a). Moreover, the Cube {001} <100 > component is evidently a weak and close to random texture, which could be a characteristic of the recrystallized state. For the nano-grained AA2024 alloy (Fig. 8b), the Copper {112}<111>, Goss {011} <100>, Brass {110}<221>, S {123}<634>, P {110}<221>, and Rotated Cube {001}<110 > components with maximum intensities of 3.5 × R, 2.3 × R, 2.1 × R, 1.8 × R, 1.7 × R, and 1.5 × R, respectively, are the major texture orientations.

Regarding Fig. 8, the intensity of the dominant orientations increased after CARB processing. Therefore, under the given aggressive conditions, it can be said that the Goss {011} <100>, Brass {011}<211>, and P {110}<221>, which have high-intensity {011} orientation, exhibit superior resistance to corrosion compared to the {001} and {111} textured grains. Consequently, in the case of the AA2024 alloy, it is reasonable to conclude that the Goss {011} <100>, Brass {011}<211>, and P {110}<221 > components can positively contribute to the electrochemical properties of the nano-grained AA2024 alloy. We must objectively admit that there is less attention paid to the correlation between plastic deformation and texture evolution with the corrosion research of metals and alloys in different solution environments, especially regarding the passivation behavior, the structure of passive films, the corrosion mechanism, and under extremely high strain-rate conditions106–110. Thus, ongoing work and research are needed to expand the crystallographic-orientation-dependent electrochemical properties that are crucial for protective materials for future engineering applications.

Conclusions

Herein, the correlation between grain refinement and crystallographic texture on the passive and electrochemical response of AA2024 aluminum alloy in phosphate buffer solution (pH = 9) was investigated. Electrochemical tests exhibited that the high strains imposed through the cross accumulative roll bonding (CARB) technique, along with the uniform distribution of extremely fine and equiaxed nanograins, significantly improved the passive response of the nano-grained AA2024 alloy as compared to its coarse-grained counterpart. Moreover, the incorporation of grain refinement and high-intensity {011} orientations, i.e., Goss {011}<100>, Brass {011}<211>, and P {110}<221 > texture components in the nano-grained AA2024 alloy, provided the perfect conditions for developing oxide passive films with superior corrosion protection performance compared with the coarse-grained counterpart.

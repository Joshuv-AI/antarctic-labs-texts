Safety and efficacy of biosimilar aflibercept MYL-1701P in diabetic macular oedema: 20-week extension results following the INSIGHT pivotal trial
Authors: Rohan Chauhan, Vishali Gupta, Sribhargava Natesh, Rajpal Vohra, Virendra Agarwal, Prasanna C Ganapathi, Debdipta Bose, Subramanian Loganathan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimTo evaluate the safety, efficacy and immunogenicity of biosimilar aflibercept (MYL-1701P) in participants with diabetic macular oedema who completed the 52-week global phase III trial and were enrolled in an extension study.MethodsIn a 20-week, multicentre, open-label extension study at 15 sites in India, safety and efficacy were assessed in 52 participants who received three doses (2 mg intravitreal) of MYL-1701P either continuing on MYL-1701P (continuation arm) or switching to it from reference aflibercept (switch arm). The primary outcome was safety, assessed by the incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs). The secondary outcome was efficacy and included change in visual acuity (best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) based on Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) letters) and central subfield thickness ((CST); by spectral-domain optical coherence tomography).ResultsOf the 52 participants enrolled, 46 completed week 76. Participants in both arms had comparable baseline characteristics. Incident TEAEs were noted in 9/29 participants in the continuation arm and 7/23 in the switch arm. No participant reported treatment-induced or boosted antidrug antibodies. The adjusted mean difference in BCVA change between baseline of parent study and week 76 (end of extension study) was −1.20 (2.95) ETDRS letters (90% CI −6.15 to 3.75) and from baseline of extension study to week 20 (week 76—parent study) was 1.59 (1.1) (90% CI −0.26 to 3.43). The adjusted mean difference in CST from baseline of parent study to week 76 was −15.9 (38.07) µm (90% CI −80.47 to 48.62) and from baseline of extension study to its end was 13.53 (16.13) µm (90% CI −13.86 to 40.92).ConclusionThe study had certain limitations, including participation from one geographical area, an open-label design and small number of participants. Despite the limited number of exposures in the switch arm, it still constituted a reasonable exposure, and similar safety, efficacy and immunogenicity profiles were observed between those who continued receiving MYL-1701P and those who switched from reference aflibercept to MYL-1701P in the 20-week extension study of the pivotal trial.Trial registration numberNCT04674800, NCT03610646.Keywords: Clinical Trial, Retina, Prospective Studies, Macular Degeneration

Introduction

Diabetic macular oedema (DME), a common microvascular complication in patients with diabetes, has an adverse impact on visual acuity, sometimes leading to blindness.1 2 The International Diabetes Federation (IDF) estimates that 589 million people worldwide have diabetes mellitus and 5% of these are affected by DME.3 In India, DME prevalence is in the range of 2.6–3%,4 5 currently, and is projected to increase manifold with the exponential increase in cases of diabetes.3

Aflibercept (Eylea, Regeneron), an anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) fusion protein, is indicated for neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD, also called wet AMD), macular oedema following retinal vein occlusion (RVO), DME and retinopathy of prematurity in the USA and European Union (EU).6 7 Additionally, it is indicated for diabetic retinopathy (DR) in the USA and visual impairment due to myopic choroidal neovascularisation in the EU.6 7 There is evidence that aflibercept confers some advantage over ranibizumab and bevacizumab in people with DME, especially in patients with worse baseline visual acuity, in both visual and anatomical terms.8 9

MYL-1701P (aflibercept-jbvf, Yesafili), a biosimilar to reference aflibercept (Eylea), was found to be clinically equivalent to it with regard to efficacy, safety, immunogenicity and pharmacokinetics in the 1-year pivotal phase III INSIGHT study in DME.10 MYL-1701P has recently received Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval as an interchangeable biosimilar drug to the reference biologic aflibercept for use in nAMD, DME, DR and macular oedema resulting from RVO.11

This extension study was designed to further evaluate the safety, efficacy and immunogenicity of MYL-1701P at 1.5 years among a group of participants from India who successfully completed the parent study by receiving either MYL-1701P or reference aflibercept for 1 year. This study also assessed outcomes in participants who switched from reference aflibercept to MYL-1701P, an important consideration in real-world clinical practice, where switching between biologics and biosimilars is increasingly becoming common.

Methods

Study design and interventions

Complete details of the pivotal INSIGHT trial (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT03610646) study design, study population and outcomes have been reported previously with a summary provided here.10 The parent study was conducted among 355 participants with DME at 77 clinical centres in nine countries between August 2018 and September 2021, with a study design that included a screening period of 4 weeks, a double-masked treatment period of 48 weeks and a final follow-up period of 4 weeks, totalling up to 52 weeks. The participants were randomised to either 2 mg (0.05 mL) MYL-1701P or reference aflibercept via intravitreal injection every 4 weeks through week 16, followed by every 8 weeks through week 48 (figure 1). In participants who met the prespecified best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) and/or central subfield thickness (CST) criteria or at the investigator’s discretion, an optional dose was administered every 4 weeks after week 16.

Study design. EOS, End of Study; EOT, End of Treatment.

In this extension study, a total of 52 participants with DME from 15 sites in India were enrolled, who had received their last pivotal efficacy study dose at week 48 and completed the pivotal efficacy study evaluation as well as screening for the extension study between weeks 48 and 52 (figure 1). Since the pivotal trial did not have a treatment switch designed into it, we decided to collect this additional information through the extension study. However, by this time the pivotal trial was halfway complete. As the Indian sites had joined the trial later, we had a significant number of participants from India not yet reaching the 52-week mark, and this presented us with an opportunity to conduct the switch study, although exclusively in India.

All enrolled participants received MYL-1701P as an intravitreal injection at a dose of 2 mg at eight weekly intervals on day 1 of the extension study (week 52—parent study), week 8 (week 64—parent study) and week 16 (week 72—parent study) in an open-label manner (figure 1). Follow-up evaluations were completed by week 20 of the extension study (week 76—parent study). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, in compliance with the International Council for Harmonization, Good Clinical Practice guidelines and as per national, state and local regulatory authorities. All study-related documents were reviewed by independent ethics committees and institutional review boards for each study centre. All patients signed and dated the informed consent form before participating in the study. Patients or the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of our research. The clinical trial was registered and is available at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT04674800). The trial was initiated on 23 November 2020 and was completed on 20 April 2022.

Participants

There was no formal sample size estimation performed. The extension study was open to all participants from India who completed the parent study. The inclusion/exclusion criteria largely reflected the population described in the parent study10 with some optimisations. The 52 participants who had completed week 52 of the parent study and required continued treatment with intravitreal anti-VEGF therapy were enrolled for the study as per the principal investigator’s clinical judgement.

Outcome assessments

Ophthalmic examination (including determination of BCVA by certified examiners) and spectral-domain optical coherence tomography (SD-OCT) were performed at each visit. An OCT of the study eye/fellow eye was performed by a certified OCT technician according to the standardised procedures, and the images for the study eye were transmitted to and archived at an independent image reading centre.

Safety as assessed by incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) was the primary outcome measure. Immunogenicity was a secondary safety outcome, and antidrug antibodies (ADA) to reference aflibercept and MYL-1709P, their occurrence, titre and neutralising capacity were observed. Other secondary safety outcomes included vital signs, targeted physical examination, complete ophthalmological examination, safety labs and 12-lead ECGs. Efficacy was a secondary outcome and the following were assessed: mean change from baseline (day 1—parent study) in BCVA as assessed by Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) letters at weeks 8/16/20 (weeks 64/72/76—parent study), mean change from baseline (day 1—parent study) in CST as determined by SD-OCT at weeks 8/16/20 (weeks 64/72/76—parent study) and proportion of participants who gained ≥5/≥10/≥15 letters from baseline in BCVA, assessed by change from baseline (day 1—parent study) in ETDRS letters at weeks 8/16/20 (weeks 64/72/76—parent study). Also assessed was the mean change in BCVA from day 1 of the extension study to end of study (week 20—extension study/week 76—parent study) and mean change in CST from day 1 of the extension study to end of study (week 20—extension study/week 76—parent study). Day 1 of the parent study was considered as the baseline value for all efficacy endpoints, unless otherwise specified.

Statistical analyses

The safety population consisted of all participants who received at least one dose of MYL-1701P in the extension study. The full analysis set consisted of all participants in the safety population who had at least one postdose BCVA assessment. Adverse events (AE), including TEAEs (ocular/non-ocular), were coded using the latest version of Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Authorities. The occurrence of AEs and serious adverse events (SAEs) was summarised by incidence, severity, causality and total number of events. For immunogenicity, the occurrence, titre and neutralising capacity of ADA were summarised at each scheduled sampling time point. Treatment-induced ADA was taken as the ADA developed de novo (seroconversion) and treatment-boosted ADA was taken as the pre-existing ADA that was boosted to a higher level following biologic drug administration.

The analysis of mean change from baseline in BCVA at week 20 was based on a mixed model repeated measures (MMRM) analysis with fixed effects for group (switch from reference aflibercept to MYL-1701P or continued on MYL-1701P), visit and group by visit interaction, together with baseline BCVA as a covariate. An unstructured within-subject variance-covariance matrix was specified, with estimation using restricted maximum likelihood. The denominator degrees of freedom used the Kenward-Roger estimate. This model provided an estimate, SE and 90% two-sided CI for the group difference at week 20. The proportion of participants who gained ≥5/10/15 letters from baseline in BCVA at weeks 8/16/20 was presented by group. For each time point, a summary table presented the number of participants in each group, the proportions by group, the difference in proportions and the 95% CI for the difference. These comparisons were unadjusted for covariates. The other efficacy endpoints were analysed in a similar manner as the mean change from baseline in BCVA at week 20 (week 76—parent study).

Results

Participant disposition and baseline characteristics

The 55 participants screened were still blinded to the treatment they had received in the pivotal phase III INSIGHT study in DME.10 The study design of the parent study can be seen in Bressler et al.10 In the extension study, the screen failures from the 55 participants included one participant in the treatment continuation arm who did not meet the eligibility criteria and two participants in the treatment switch arm who withdrew consent. A total of 52 participants were enrolled in the treatment continuation arm (n=29) and treatment switch arm (n=23). Of these, 46 (88.46%) participants completed the study: 25 (86.21%) in the treatment continuation arm and 21 (91.30%) in the treatment switch arm. Of the four discontinued participants in the treatment continuation arm, two discontinued due to non-compliance (missed multiple doses due to COVID pandemic) and two withdrew consent from the study. Of the two participants withdrawn from the treatment switch arm, both were discontinued by the investigator. One had missed multiple doses due to the COVID pandemic, and SAE in the other participant (cerebral infarction) was not reported within 24 hours to the principal investigator.

After a planned number of nine exposures over 52 weeks in the parent study, there were three more exposures done in the extension study. The proportion of participants who received the first, second and third doses was similar in both treatment arms.

The demographic profile at extension study entry was comparable and well balanced between treatment arms with respect to age, gender, race and ethnic origin, as were the baseline characteristics (table 1). These were also comparable to the pivotal efficacy study.10 All the participants enrolled in the extension study belonged to the Asian race.

Primary safety results

Overall, 22 participants reported at least one AE, with 13 participants in the continuation arm experiencing 18 events and nine participants in the switch arm experiencing 19 events.

TEAEs overall

The incidence of TEAEs was similar between the treatment arms; nine participants in the treatment continuation arm and seven participants in the treatment switch arm reported TEAEs. Only one participant in the treatment switch arm and none in the treatment continuation arm had drug-related TEAEs. All TEAEs were mild or moderate in severity except one serious TEAE (cerebral infarction) in the treatment switch arm considered as related to the study drug. In the treatment continuation and switch arms, seven and five participants reported mild, and three and two participants reported moderate TEAEs, respectively, showing comparable severity of TEAEs between the arms. There were no reports of TEAEs leading to death. Ocular TEAEs

A total of seven participants reported 11 ocular TEAEs, with four and three participants in the treatment continuation and switch arms reporting five and six ocular TEAEs, respectively. Overall, the incidence of ocular TEAEs was comparable between the two treatment arms. All events recovered or were recovering at the end of the study except for one cataract event in each of the treatment arms. Ocular TEAEs in the study eye at extension study week 20 (week 76—parent study) are provided in table 2.

Secondary safety results

Immunogenicity

Two (6.90%) participants in the treatment continuation arm and three (13.04%) participants in the treatment switch arm were ADA positive at the baseline of the extension study. The overall proportion of ADA-positive participants remained similar throughout the study in both arms. No incidence of treatment-induced or treatment-boosted ADA or neutralising antibodies (nAbs) was reported during the study. The immunogenicity potential of MYL-1701P in the extension study is provided in online supplemental table 2.

Vital signs, physical examination findings and other observations related to safety

There were non-significant minimal changes in all safety parameters across the study with no differences or trends observed between the two study arms.

Discussion

IDF projections show that one in eight adults, approximately 853 million global population, will be living with diabetes by 2050, which is an increase of 46%.12 DME is an important complication of diabetes that can present in eyes at all levels of DR. An estimated 20% of people living with type 1 diabetes and 25% of those with type 2 diabetes can expect to develop DME.13 As per the 11th edition of IDF, the 89.8 million people in India who had diabetes in 2024 are expected to cross 156.7 million by 2050.3 With a manifold increase in diabetes and hence DME, biosimilar aflibercept MYL-1701P will be a useful addition to the repertoire of ophthalmology drugs that are more affordable and accessible.

MYL-1701P has been approved by the European Medicines Agency14 and the FDA,15 and has been granted an interchangeability status by the FDA11 for use in place of the reference aflibercept. The results of this 20-week extension to the pivotal phase III study demonstrate comparable safety, tolerability and efficacy between participants who switched from reference aflibercept to MYL-1701P as well as those who continued MYL-1701P after week 52 of the parent study. The strength of this full 76-week study is the randomised assignment to reference aflibercept at baseline of the parent study with the planned number of nine exposures over 52 weeks, and then three more exposures after the switch in one arm and the ability to compare the continuation and switch arms. This is the first study to explore switching from reference aflibercept to a biosimilar aflibercept in participants with DME. Previous studies show comparable clinical efficacy and safety when switched from reference aflibercept to biosimilar aflibercept in nAMD,16,18 highlighting the fact that switching of a reference aflibercept with a high-quality biosimilar does not compromise safety or efficacy. However, since these studies were in nAMD, the current study provides crucial insights for the DME population.

In the extension study, the extent of exposure was similar between the two treatment arms in terms of duration of treatment and proportion of participants who received first, second or third doses of the study drugs in both arms. The incidence and severity of overall TEAEs, including ocular TEAEs, were comparable between the treatment arms. The only serious TEAE reported in the study was of cerebral infarction reported in the treatment switch arm, which was considered as possibly related to the treatment. The overall proportion of ADA and nAb-positive participants remained similar throughout the study period in both treatment arms. No incidence of treatment-induced or treatment-boosted ADA or nAb was reported during the study. Overall, the safety profile was consistent with the known safety profile of the reference aflibercept6 7 and was in line with the safety profile noted in the previous global study of MYL-1701P in the target population,10 with no new safety signals or change observed in the immunogenicity profile.

The improvement in BCVA, as assessed by ETDRS letters, and CST, as determined by SD-OCT, noted in the parent study, was maintained after the switch from reference aflibercept to MYL-1701P and was comparable between the two treatment arms up to the end of week 76. The proportion of participants who gained or lost ≥5, ≥10 and ≥15 letters from baseline in BCVA was also comparable between the two arms until week 76.

The study had certain limitations, which include participation from one geographical area, an open-label design and small number of participants. There were a limited number of exposures in the switch arm; however, it still constituted a reasonable exposure. Despite the small sample size, the extension study observed safety and tolerability profile in line with the parent phase III study. It also observed sustained efficacy after switching from reference aflibercept to MYL-1701P. The study population being limited to one geographical region does not necessarily impact the relevance of the results as observations from subgroup analyses of previously reported studies have shown no clinically significant differences for different ethnicities.17 18

In conclusion, the study demonstrates that switching from reference aflibercept to MYL-1701P maintained the functional and anatomical improvements observed in the pivotal phase III study. The efficacy, safety and immunogenicity profile of the switch arm was consistent with the continuous MYL-1701P therapy. The interchangeability status of MYL-1701P by the US FDA, together with this study results, further supports the emerging evidence that patients can be switched to high-quality biosimilar aflibercept products from reference aflibercept without compromising on safety or efficacy.

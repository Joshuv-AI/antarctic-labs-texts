Complete Detoxification of the Ofloxacin Antibiotic by Magnetically Separable Fe3O4/AC/BiOCl Photocatalysts
Authors: Varanya Piankaew, Supharat Deemak, Sattra Nonthing, Atchawadee Panchakeaw, Khemika Wannakan, Suwat Nanan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
A ternary photocatalyst composing activated carbon (AC), Fe3O4 and BiOCl was synthesized via an ultrasonic method. The magnetic Fe3O4/AC/CTAB-BiOCl photocatalyst was used for the degradation of the ofloxacin antibiotic under UV light and natural solar light. The prepared ternary photocatalyst showed mainly the diffraction peaks of BiOCl together with those of Fe3O4. The band gap values of 3.20, 3.27, and 3.18 eV were recorded from CTAB-BiOCl, AC/CTAB-BiOCl, and Fe3O4/AC/CTAB-BiOCl photocatalysts, respectively. The ternary photocatalyst showed the smallest photoluminescence signal, in comparison to those obtained from the other photocatalysts, indicating improved carrier separation efficiency. The finding agrees well with the enhanced photocatalytic activity of the magnetic photocatalyst. A promising photocatalytic efficiency of 100% was reached after only 60 min of UV light (a mercury lamp, 135 W) and natural solar light irradiation. The degradation of the OFL drug correlates well with a first-order reaction, revealing a great reaction rate coefficient of about 0.1158 min–1. The prepared ternary photocatalyst still maintains excellent photoactivity even after the fifth cycle of use. The trapping method indicates the great role of the photogenerated electrons and hydroxyl radicals in OFL removal. This research demonstrates a new route to generate a sunlight-responsive catalyst, with magnetically separable properties, for the detoxification of the OFL drug in wastewater.

Introduction

In recent years, with the rapid growth of society and the economy, contamination of toxic pollutants in natural water has brought serious problems to the world. , The improper discharging of the organic contaminants, including dyes and antibiotics, into water has emerged as a considerable contributor to water pollution. − Ofloxacin antibiotic, known as OFL, is widely applied for the treatment of bacterial infections. This drug is extensively discharged into the environment. − Across the globe, as the growth of pharmaceutical industry is quite rapidly, the misuse of this antibiotic has increased the drug content detected in the aqueous phase. , This will eventually increase until greater than the safe level. − After releasing, the nonbiodegradable property of OFL causes contamination of its residual parts in the aquatic environment. − The wide use of the harmful contaminants causes the discharging of these toxic pollutants into natural water, which will finally end up with dreadful effects for animals and humans. Practically, it is required that the toxic drug be used and released appropriately. Therefore, a well-managed detoxification of the harmful antibiotics detected in the aqueous phase is urgently needed.

Various methods have been selected to use for the detoxification of organic pollutants. − In some cases, however, one main difficulty concerning just moving the harmful contaminants from the solution phase to the solid phase has been reported. Eventually, this will end up with the existence of the harmful products in the solid phase. Across the globe, the United Nations’ Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) are the ongoing target to be achieved. Specifically, clean water and sanitation (goal number 6) can be reached by utilization of the excellent treatment method to remove the harmful organic contaminants detected in the aqueous phase. , Among various techniques, photocatalysis has particularly gained much attention due to the beneficials of applying abundant and economical solar light for the efficient removal of toxic pollutants.

It is widely accepted that the commercially available TiO2 is a UV-driven catalyst showing low photoactivity under visible light exposure. In principle, the synthesis of a new sunlight-active photocatalyst is an excellent research direction in the field of photocatalysis for environmental protection. , In practice, the prepared photocatalysts which absorb wider sunlight range, toward the visible region, and exhibit the excellent photocatalytic performance are the ideal compounds regarding the generation of new sunlight-active semiconductors.

Bismuth oxychloride (BiOCl) is a ternary layered chloride semiconductor showing a molecular structure which consists of [Bi2O2]2+ layers bound between two Cl– layers by a strong valence bond. It is well-studied that BiOCl displays a variety of morphological architectures with high photoactivity under UV light (a wide band gap of about 3.20–3.60 eV). Nevertheless, the sunlight-active efficiency of BiOCl was low. , Additionally, the photocorrosion problem (detected during photocatalytic studies) is the main difficulty concerning the practical use of the BiOCl photocatalyst. − To solve these drawbacks, the synthesis of BiOCl with excellent sunlight-active efficiency is required. ,

Magnetite (Fe3O4) is one of the well-known iron oxides with a magnetically separable property. , This magnetic material also exhibits the beneficials of low toxicity and biocompatibility due to the existence of ferric/ferrous ions in the structure. There were some reports regarding the use of Fe3O4 for pollutant removal. − In principle, the combination of Fe3O4 with other photocatalysts for the generation of novel heterostructure photocatalysts could be a promising strategy for not only enhancing the resultant photoactivity but also achieving the magnetically separable property. −

In theory, the separated application of either BiOCl or Fe3O4 will provide low photocatalytic efficiency toward the detoxification of toxic contaminants. To solve this difficulty, the incorporation of silver, gold, and other noble metals (on the photocatalyst surface) or the creation of new heterostructure photocatalysts can be optionally utilized. ,,, However, the use of noble metals will eventually end up with more expensive costs in terms of catalyst fabrication. Alternatively, the generation of heterojunctions is an excellent method to improve the resultant photocatalytic efficiency of prepared semiconducting photocatalysts. To fabricate the heterojunction, the band alignment and the semiconducting band level are two crucial factors needed to be taken into consideration before designing novel heterostructure photocatalysts.

The formation of a photocatalyst heterojunction with addition of activated carbon (AC) can effectively solve the drawback of low photoactivity. The generated heterojunction can trap the photogenerated electrons and then reduce the recombination rate of the charge carriers. − The promising conductivity of AC also increases the transport of electrons and the separation efficiency of the electron–hole pairs. Activated carbons (AC) possess a great surface area and can efficiently adsorb or capture the intermediates created during the photocatalytic process for successive removal cycles. Therefore, the local concentration of the organic pollutants is increased, and the free radicals created during photocatalysis can target the organic contaminants speedily and productively.

Interestingly, excellent cyclability is a main factor concerning the real-scale application of the photocatalysts. − A major drawback of the ordinary powder photocatalyst is the low ability to be reutilized again, in the consecutive run, facing the difficulty of sample loss. In practice, it is hard to gain 100% recovery of the powder sample, after it was collected and dried, to be reutilized in the successive cycle. ,, Thus, the fabrication of novel magnetic catalysts is the promising research direction for receiving excellent photocatalytic efficiency as well as the beneficial of good magnetically separable properties. Previously, the binary or ternary heterostructures comprising Fe3O4 have been generated. ,,−

As inspired by the research works mentioned above, in this research, we have been focused on the fabrication of the new solar-light-responsive photocatalyst based on Fe3O4/carbon/BiOCl using an ultrasonic method. The magnetic Fe3O4/AC/CTAB-BiOCl was then used for the detoxification of ofloxacin (OFL). The existence of activated carbon (AC), Fe3O4, and BiOCl in the ternary photocatalyst was confirmed. The magnetic photocatalyst exhibits astonishing improvement in the solar-light-driven photocatalytic efficiency compared to those of the single catalysts. The drastic improvement in the photocatalytic performance is due to the suppression of the recombination speed of the carriers after synthesis of the ternary heterojunction. The magnetic Fe3O4/AC/CTAB-BiOCl still maintains its recyclability even after five successive runs. The present work demonstrates a facile route to prepare the ternary heterojunction, with promising efficiency, for the degradation of toxic organic pollutants including OFL drug in wastewater by the utilization of abundant and economical solar energy.

Experimental Section

Chemicals

All materials (AR grade) were utilized.

Synthesis of the Photocatalysts

Synthesis of BiOCl and CTAB-BiOCL

Bare BiOCl was synthesized via a solvothermal route. First, about 1.0866 g of bismuth nitrate pentahydrate [Bi­(NO3)3·5H2O] was dissolved in 40 cm3 of ethylene glycol (solution I). Second, around 0.13 g of NaCl was dissolved in ethylene glycol (solution II). Third, to prepare a BiOCl photocatalyst, solution II was added to solution I, and the mixture was then stirred for 1 h. After that the sample was put in an autoclave at 160 °C. It was left for 12 h. After cooling, the solid was collected (by centrifugation), cleaned, and heated at 60 °C for 24 h. In the case of CTAB-BiOCl, about 0.20 g of CTAB surfactant was added in the Bi3+ solution in the step of preparation of solution I.

Synthesis of Fe3O4

To prepare Fe3O4, a chemical precipitation method was used. The details of the synthesis can be found elsewhere.

Synthesis of the Binary AC/CTAB-BiOCl

The binary AC/CTAB-BiOCl photocatalyst was prepared via an ultrasonication route. Around 0.2 g of CTAB-BiOCl was distributed in 20 cm3 of DI water. Meanwhile, about 0.0062 g of activated carbon (AC) was separately dispersed in DI water. Afterward, the dispersion comprising AC was then incorporated into the dispersion containing CTAB-BiOCl. The mixture was then left for ultrasonication (120 min). After that, about 15 cm3 of acetone was added. The mixture was left for another 24 h. The resultant solid was accumulated after centrifugation, washed, and dried at 80 °C for 24 h.

Synthesis of the Ternary Fe3O4/AC/CTAB-BiOCl

The ternary Fe3O4/AC/CTAB-BiOCl photocatalyst was synthesized using the ultrasonication route as well. Around 0.2 g of the binary AC/CTAB-BiOCl photocatalyst was distributed in 20 cm3 DI water. Meanwhile, about 0.0055 g of Fe3O4 was dispersed in DI water separately. Afterward, the dispersion comprising Fe3O4 was then incorporated into the dispersion containing AC/CTAB-BiOCl. The mixture was then left for ultrasonication. After the addition of acetone (15 cm3), the mixture was then left for 24 h. Finally, the solid was collected, washed, and dried at 80 °C for 24 h.

Characterization

The characterization of the synthesized photocatalysts was performed using numerous techniques, as can be seen in our previous works. , The structure of the sample was investigated by the X-ray diffraction (XRD) method using the PANalytical EMPYREAN diffractometer (Cu Kα). The vibrational spectrum was examined on a PerkinElmer FT-IR IFS 66 V/S spectrophotometer. The morphological structure based on the SEM micrograph was recorded with an FEI Helios NanoLab G3 CX instrument. In addition, the energy-dispersive X-ray (EDX) method was elucidated via a TECNAI G2 20 instrument. The UV–vis spectrum was determined on a Shimazu UV–vis-NIR-3101PC spectrophotometer. The BaSO4 salt was utilized as the reference. Finally, the PL spectrum was determined using a Shimazu RF-5301PC fluorometer.

To remove the OFL pollutant, the photocatalytic process was studied under UV light and natural sunlight. A photolysis study (the control experiment) was investigated in the presence of pollutant aqueous solution and light, in an absence of the Fe3O4/AC/CTAB-BiOCl photocatalyst. − Practically, the catalyst (50 mg) was well-distributed in a 200 cm3 aqueous solution of the pollutant (10 ppm). To achieve the adsorption–desorption equilibrium, the sample was stirred in the dark for 60 min. Under UV light exposure, about 5 cm3 of the dispersion was sampled. Characterization

The result strongly indicates the finding of magnetic Fe3O4 and BiOCl photocatalysts in the synthesized ternary heterostructure.

BiOCl displayed a vibrational band at 3485 cm–1, assigning to the O–H stretching from water. , The peak at 1424 cm–1 is ascribed as the confirmation of the Bi–Cl stretching. , The peak found at 546 cm–1 is assigned to the vibration from Bi–O stretching. , The CTAB-BiOCl photocatalyst displayed a nearly similar vibrational spectrum to that of BiOCl. In theory, two important factors, namely, the carrier separation efficiency and the charge transport rate, strongly influence the final photoactivity of the prepared catalyst. In principle, the PL intensity indicates the carrier separation efficiency. The highest PL intensity implies the lowest carrier separation efficiency. This will end up with the lowest photocatalytic efficiency. , Figure c displays the PL signal at a λexcitation of 310 nm. It is seen that the PL intensity of Fe3O4/AC/CTAB-BiOCl was considerably lower than those of the other photocatalysts. This indicates the enhanced electron–hole separation efficiency of the ternary photocatalyst. The charge recombination speed remarkably reduces after the fabrication of the heterostructure. From the above explanation, the ternary Fe3O4/AC/CTAB-BiOCl is expected to provide enhanced photocatalytic efficiency, compared to BiOCl, CTAB-BiOCl, and binary AC/CTAB-BiOCl. The details will be discussed later in terms of OFL removal under photo-illumination.

The BET N2 adsorption–desorption isotherms (Figure S3) confirm that all fabricated catalysts display the type IV isotherm with an H3 hysteresis loop. As seen, the bare BiOCl exhibited the lowest BET surface area of 10 m2/g, while the CTAB-capped BiOCl showed a small increase of the surface area to about 15 m2/g. Previously, it was reported in our group that Fe3O4 exhibited the maximum surface area of 90.1 m2/g. After creation of the ternary Fe3O4/AC/CTAB-BiOCl photocatalyst, it should be noted that a gradual improvement of the surface area to about 25 m2/g was detected. Therefore, an improved efficiency of Fe3O4/AC/CTAB-BiOCl, compared to BiOCl, is expected. All in all, the lowering of the charge recombination rate (PL spectra) and the increasing of the BET surface area are the two factors describing the improved photocatalytic performance of the ternary catalyst in comparison to that of BiOCl.

Removal of OFL Antibiotic

The detoxification of the OFL antibiotic was elucidated under UV light (a mercury lamp of 135 W) and natural solar light. Furthermore, the influence of OFL content, photocatalyst loading, and solution pH on the resultant photocatalytic efficiency was investigated.

Removal of the Pollutant under UV Light

First, the performance of BiOCl and CTAB-capped BiOCl toward RhB removal (λmax = 554 nm) was investigated. The effect of the added CTAB (as a surfactant capping agent) on the resultant photoactivity was fully examined. Figure S5a shows that self-degradation of RhB dye (via the photolysis process) is nearly negligible. As shown, about 20% removal of RhB via an adsorption process (in the dark) was recorded. Obviously, the CTAB-capped BiOCl showed complete degradation of the dye (an efficiency of 100%) within 120 min (Figure S5b). The performance is about 1.3 times higher than that of pristine BiOCl. In addition, the CTAB-capped BiOCl photocatalyst revealed the highest rate constant of 0.0238 min–1, as shown in Figure S5c. To sum up, the final photoactivity and rate constant detected from the CTAB-capped BiOCl were greater than those of pristine BiOCl. This is attributed to the enhancement in dye adsorption after modification of BiOCl with the CTAB surfactant. Thus, CTAB-BiOCl will be used to create the binary AC/CTAB-BiOCl and the ternary Fe3O4/AC/CTAB-BiOCl photocatalysts. After that, the performance of all samples toward removal of OFL antibiotic will be presented.

Second, the removal of the OFL drug under UV light was fully examined (Figure ). The lowering of OFL with time can be detected, as shown in Figure a. The ternary Fe3O4/AC/CTAB-BiOCl photocatalyst showed complete OFL degradation (an efficiency of 100%) within 60 min (Figure b). The performance is greater, approximately 1.6 times, than that of BiOCl. Accordingly, the Fe3O4/AC/CTAB-BiOCl revealed the highest rate constant of 0.0962 min–1, as shown in Figure c.

Removal of OFL under Sunlight

After natural solar light irradiation, the ternary heterojunction showed its improved photocatalytic efficiency toward removal of the OFL antibiotic (Figure ). The greatest photoactivity was reported from Fe3O4/AC/CTAB-BiOCl, compared to the AC/CTAB-BiOCl photocatalyst. Interestingly, complete degradation of the OFL drug (100% removal) was detected after 60 min (Figure b). The degradation rate correlates nicely with the first-order kinetics model. The magnetic Fe3O4/AC/CTAB-BiOCl provided the highest rate constant of 0.1158 min–1 (Figure c).

Effect of Some Experimental Parameters on OFL Degradation

The effect of the OFL amount, photocatalyst loading, and solution pH on the efficiency of the prepared photocatalyst was elucidated. As shown in Figure a, increasing OFL drug amount will eventually end up with the lowering of photodegradation activity. Enhancement in the drug amount causes the increase in photo absorption of this drug. This will end up with reducing photon flux entering the surface of the catalyst. Finally, a decrease in the photocatalytic activity toward OFL removal is found. By utilizing 5 or 10 ppm of OFL, more or less the same efficiency can be obtained after 240 min. Therefore, the drug concentration at10 ppm was selected.

Effect of concentration (a), catalyst content (b), and initial solution pH (c) on photodegradation of the OFL antibiotic.

Increasing the loading of the catalyst to 50 mg causes an improvement in the photocatalytic efficiency, as seen in Figure b. This is assigned to the gathering of the adsorbed OFL molecules on the surface of the catalyst. In addition, the improved efficiency is attributed to the increased catalyst content about the area of photo irradiation. However, the addition of 75 mg of the catalyst causes the reduction of the photoactivity. This may be due to the enhanced solution turbidity, which in turn end up with decreasing photo penetration to the surface of the catalyst. Eventually, the reduction in OFL removal is detected. Practically, the catalyst amount of 50 mg was applied for further studies.

The solution pH plays a crucial role in photocatalytic efficiency toward OFL degradation, as elucidated in Figure c. The natural solution pH of OFL is 7. It has been reported that the BiOCl photocatalyst revealed the pzc of around 8. , Meanwhile, the OFL drug displays the pK a values of 2.48 and 6.96. At a pH of about 11, the OFL molecule (negatively charge) repels the photocatalyst (negatively charge), ending up with the reduction in OFL removal. As seen, lowering of the photocatalytic efficiency at pH ≈ 3 is assigned to the dissolution of the photocatalyst under the acidic conditions. , Thus, there is no need to change the pH of the OFL solution.

Scavenger Method

The trapping technique was performed to elucidate the degradation mechanism of the OFL drug. The role of the particular scavenger on antibiotic detoxification is shown in Figure . K2Cr2O7, isopropanol (IPA), NaN3, and EDTA-2Na were separately added as a scavenging agent of electrons, hydroxyl radicals (•OH), singlet oxygens, and holes, respectively. In addition, KI was incorporated for trapping of the photogenerated holes and the surface hydroxyl radicals. As shown in Figure c, a dramatic reduction in the photocatalytic efficiency to 15–21% was reported after quenching with either K2Cr2O7 or IPA. The result indicates that both photogenerated electrons and hydroxyl radicals are the main species, which play a key role in OFL removal. From Figure d, the rate constants (k) obtained after the addition of either K2Cr2O7 or IPA are about 0.0013–0.0023 min–1, while that reported from the ordinary photocatalytic experiment (without addition of quencher) is 0.0962 min–1. It is clearly shown that 42–74 times reduction of the rate constant was achieved. The result confirms the key role of electrons and hydroxyl radicals in OFL detoxification.

Lowering of C/C 0 vs time (a), the photodegradation efficiency (b,c), and the rate constants obtained from degradation of OFL with addition of various scavengers (d).

Besides, after light exposure, the generation of OH• was carefully elucidated via the terephthalic acid (TA) probe technique. It is known that TA reacts with the hydroxyl radicals to create 2-hydroxy terephthalic acid (HTA) with high fluorescence intensity. In practice, after light illumination, with the addition of the catalyst, the improved fluorescence intensity (with a peak located at 425 nm) can be clearly seen (Figure a). This confirms the formation of HTA with increasing irradiation time. The result implies the role of OH• in OFL degradation. Furthermore, the experiment was also performed by examining the accumulation of OH• after 1 and 4 h of photo exposure (Figure b,c). The fluorescence intensity at 1 and 4 h shows the same trend, i.e., the Fe3O4/AC/CTAB-BiOC ternary photocatalyst shows higher intensity than the CTAB-BiOCl. This agrees perfectly with the enhanced photocatalytic performance achieved from the ternary photocatalyst. It can be concluded that the TPA method indicates the role of OH• in detoxification of the OFL drug.

Modification of BiOCl by the CTAB surfactant facilitates the adsorption of pollutants to the surface of BiOCl. After that, the addition of activated carbon provides two advantages, i.e., the improvement of the adsorption property on the BiOCl surface and the increase of the surface area. Therefore, the ternary Fe3O4/AC/CTAB-BiOCl photocatalyst demonstrates high removal efficiency with magnetic properties.

Conclusions

The ultrasonication method was used to generate the solar-light-driven photocatalyst based on magnetic Fe3O4/AC/CTAB-BiOCl. The existence of activated carbon, Fe3O4, and BiOCl in the synthesized photocatalyst was confirmed. The ternary photocatalyst exhibited complete degradation of the OFL drug within 60 min under UV light and sunlight illumination. The magnetic photocatalyst displayed enhanced efficiency, in comparison to those of Fe3O4 and BiOCl, assigned to the improved carrier separation rate and the enhanced visible light absorption of the ternary heterojunction. The photogenerated electrons and the hydroxyl radicals play a great role in OFL drug degradation. The excellent cycling ability was also achieved even after the fifth run. This research emphasizes an interesting route to fabricate the ternary magnetic photocatalyst for OFL degradation under abundant and economical solar energy.

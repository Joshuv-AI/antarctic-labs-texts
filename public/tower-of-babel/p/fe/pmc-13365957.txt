IFPHOR0082 Indoor Air Quality Exposure in People with Cystic Fibrosis: Insights from the CF-Tracker Study
Authors: Sharon Weinberg, Thomas J Bannan, Congbo Song, Eva Caamaño Gutiérrez, Robert W Lord, Rosmary E Maher, Andrew M Jones, Michael Tunney, Gordon McFiggans, Alexander Horsley
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIndoor and outdoor air pollution are major health risks. Indoor air quality (IAQ) contributes to over 90% of personal exposure. People with cystic fibrosis (pwCF) may be particularly vulnerable, as peaks in particulate matter (PM) and poor ventilation could trigger symptoms. However, indoor pollutant levels, their relationship with outdoor air, and their clinical relevance in CF remain poorly understood.MethodsIn the CF-Tracker study, real-time IAQ sensors were installed in the homes of a subset of CF participants. Sensors continuously record CO2, PM2.5, temperature, relative humidity, and total VOCs, in kitchens and bedrooms over 12 months. Outdoor air quality data is from UK AURN network and Manchester Air Quality Supersite.ResultsTo date, 23 pwCF have been recruited, with up to 11 months of data collected. Mean IAQ shows strong seasonal variation and substantial between-home variability. Indoor PM2.5 often exceeds outdoor levels and WHO guidelines (annual mean< 5µg/m³), particularly in winter. Kitchens exhibit the highest pollution, with cooking-related peaks driving daily averages and exposures up to 200µgm−3. CO2 levels are generally acceptable, but prolonged exceedances above the WHO recommended upper limit (1,000ppm) frequently occurs in bedrooms, indicating inadequate ventilation.ConclusionPwCF are frequently exposed to elevated indoor air pollution. Further analyses not presented here will examine links between IAQ measures and other data collected as part of the CF-Tracker study, including clinical outcomes, lung inflammation and building characteristics. This will inform interventions aimed at improving IAQ and reducing health risks.FundingLifeArc / Cystic Fibrosis Trust Innovation Hub Network.

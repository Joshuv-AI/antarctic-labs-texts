General practitioners’ acceptability to handle unscheduled visits: a french nationwide cross-sectional survey
Authors: Daniel Vieira, Axel Benhamed, Pascal Staccini, Florence Klein, Olivier Hinard, Christophe Goetz, Simon Berthelot, Antoine Canton, Laure Abensur Vuillaume
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe increasing demand for emergency department (ED) services has led to overcrowding, impacting the quality and accessibility of healthcare. The objectives were to evaluate how general practitioners (GPs) manage unscheduled visits, including patients redirected from emergency departments and/or medical dispatch centers, and to identify potential barriers to accepting these patients.MethodsWe conducted a cross-sectional survey in France from April 2020 to March 2021, utilizing a self-administered, web-based, anonymous pre-tested questionnaire distributed among the members of professional society and social network. The questionnaire was divided into three sections, namely: (1) demographics of GP respondents; (2) current organization for handling unscheduled visits, and (3) potential barriers to implementing dedicated schedule slots for patients redirected from EDs. We used multivariable logistic regression to investigate associations between personal and practice characteristics.ResultsA total of 1,317 GPs participated in the survey. The majority were female (59%), aged between 31 and 40 years (43%) and located in semi-rural areas (66%). Overall, 89% reported to allocate specific slots for unscheduled care visits. However, only 16% had their slots open to emergency/dispatch center-referred patients. The majority (83%) of GPs showed no interest in setting aside dedicated slots for ED redirected patients, 62% were against sharing information about their unscheduled visit slots with EDs, and 88% opposed the automatic scheduling of ED patients into their clinics. The main barriers to the implementation of dedicated slots for unscheduled visit were a lack of interest (46%), time constraints (48%), and organizational challenges (9%). GPs with smaller patient bases and those using computerized systems showed greater openness to communicate availability for unscheduled care.ConclusionMost French GPs surveyed, representing approximatively 2% of the overall French GP population, integrate daily slots for unscheduled visits into their practice but they are typically reserved for their existing patient base. The survey further revealed that two-thirds of these GPs are unwilling to communicate the availability of these slots to EDs. Additionally, an even larger proportion would refuse to allow automatic scheduling of ED patients into their clinics if redirection strategies were implemented at their local hospitals.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12875-025-03012-8.

Introduction

Over the past two decades, there has been a significant increase in emergency department (ED) visits, resulting in overcrowding and subsequent delays in patient care [1–3]. This congestion in ED is associated with adverse events including increased mortality [4, 5]. It also negatively impacts the mental well-being of healthcare providers [6]. Analysis of ED visit patterns has shown that up to 40% could be classified as non-urgent in France [7]. One contributor to these avoidable visits is the lack of primary care accessibility, partly due to the unavailability of a patient’s general practitioner (GP) or the absence of GP home visit services [7]. In France, the primary care system consists of GPs working independently or in multi-professional offices, some of which offer specific slots for walk-in consultations [8, 9]. During the COVID-19 pandemic, health authorities initiated several strategies to alleviate ED strain including promoting telemedicine and redirecting low-acuity patients to GP offices and primary care units [10–12]. Despite these initiatives, the effectiveness of these redirection strategies has been limited [13]. Allocating slots for unscheduled visits may contribute to reducing the volume of ED visits and serve as a critical component in collaboration with EDs, particularly within the framework of redirection strategies [14].Reflecting on the optimal strategies for patient redirection is essential, as this could potentially alleviate ED overcrowding, more closely align with patient needs, and reduce the incidence of patients leaving without being seen [15]. Indeed, previous research has suggested that approximately half of the patients may not be willing to wait more than two hours before opting to leave the ED without receiving care [16]. In addition, avoidable ED admissions are not only about sparing ED resources, it is also about providing more appropriate and efficient care in non-emergent clinical conditions. Continuity of care is one of the most studied features of primary care [17]. Being seen by the same GP over time positively impacts hard outcomes such as mortality and hospital admission [18, 19].

However, to the best of our knowledge, no study has yet examined the willingness of other healthcare providers, particularly GPs, to collaborate in redirecting ED patients to their clinics when appropriate. Understanding the potential barriers they might face, and their concerns is crucial for ensuring the success and sustainability of the ED redirection process. Exploring their readiness to receive redirected patients could identify potential barriers and help develop effective strategies to enhance patient flow and reduce ED overcrowding.

The objectives of this study were therefore to evaluate how GPs manage unscheduled visits, including patients redirected from emergency departments and/or medical dispatch centers, and to identify potential barriers to accepting these patients.

Methods

Design and setting

We conducted a cross-sectional survey among French GPs using a self-administered, semi-structured, web-based, anonymous questionnaire. The questionnaire was developed through a comprehensive literature review and informed by the clinical expertise of physicians. We selected a panel of six GPs and one emergency physician to design the questionnaire to ensure comprehensive coverage of various practice settings and expertise. The GPs on the panel were chosen because they represented a diverse range of practice backgrounds, including private practice, group practice, mixed practice with emergency services, and mixed practice with medical dispatch centers. This diversity allowed us to create a questionnaire that was relevant and applicable to a broad spectrum of GP practice environments. The inclusion of an emergency physician was crucial for incorporating perspectives from emergency care, ensuring that the questionnaire also addressed relevant issues at the interface between primary care and emergency services. To enhance clarity, another group of ten GPs pretested the questionnaire. It consisted of 24 structured questions and one open-ended question, covering various aspects, including sociodemographic data (e.g., age, gender, years of practice, practice type, patient volume, and practice location), unscheduled care consultations, patient management during GP absence, and strategies for receiving and referring patients from EDs. The complete questionnaire is available in Appendix 1. We distributed the questionnaire from April 2020 to March 2021 using Drag’n Survey® (an online survey tool provided by Drag’n Survey SAS, based in Paris, France: https://www.dragnsurvey.com/). This software enabled us to identify and remove duplicate responses effectively. An “unscheduled visit” was defined as a consultation request initiated for either the same day or the subsequent day, occurring within the standard operational hours of a GP office.

Study population and data collection

The study focused on GPs engaged exclusively in ambulatory primary care within metropolitan France. French Overseas Departments and Territories (including Martinique, French Guiana, New Caledonia, and Réunion Island) were excluded from the study due to differences in local healthcare organization compared to mainland France. The study population comprised GPs working in various non-hospital settings, including urban, suburban, and rural areas.

We excluded GPs who were retired, did not practice in primary care, or did not devote their entire professional time to primary care (e.g., those with exclusive practices in osteopathy or gynecology). Additionally, GPs qualified in specialties other than general medicine and those working in non-physician healthcare roles were excluded.

The survey was disseminated directly to GPs through multiple channels, including the national medical board, regional health agencies, and a professional social media platform specifically for healthcare professionals, which has over 28,000 members. Three rounds of invitations to participate were sent via each of these distribution channels. No postal invitations were sent. It is not possible to estimate the number of physicians who received this invitation. However, in France in 2021, 85,364 GPs were working [20].

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were reported as proportions and percentages while continuous variables were reported as means and standard deviations. Multivariable logistic regression was used to identify individual and organizational factors associated with the reservation of daily slots for emergency visits (two multivariate analysis with these questions: “Would you be willing to communicate your available slots?” and ”Would you be willing to carry out teleconsultations for patients requiring medical advice referred by the emergency department in a dedicated slot?”). For univariate analyses, an alpha risk of 5% was chosen.

Variables included in the multivariable logistic regression model were selected based on theoretical plausibility, previous literature, and a threshold (p < 0.20) in univariate analysis to ensure potentially important confounders were not omitted. No stepwise regression was used. Due to limited completeness of several variables, and the exploratory nature of the analysis, a formal directed acyclic graph was not constructed; however, selection was guided by causal reasoning to avoid inclusion of mediators or colliders, and only variables potentially associated with both the outcome and the exposure were retained.

All statistical analyses were performed with XLSTAT Statistical Software ©Addinsoft.

Ethics

All authors confirm that the methods were conducted in accordance with applicable guidelines and regulations. No personal data was stored. According to French legislation, no consent was required as survey studies are not classified as clinical studies (French Public Health Code: articles L1110-1 to L6441-1). Participation in the survey was voluntary, and the participating GPs were informed that the results would be published.

Results

In this cross-sectional survey, 1,546 respondents from 12 of the 13 metropolitan region of France participated (Fig S1), yielding an estimated response rate of 2% of the total of GPs in activity in France in 2021. A total of 228 participants were excluded due to retirement (n = 105), non-primary care practice (n = 61), qualifications in specialties other than general medicine (n = 13), and non-physician healthcare roles (n = 3). Additionally, respondents who failed to provide complete data were excluded (n = 46). The final sample comprised 1,317 GPs for analysis (flow chart: Fig. 1). Practice organization and appointment management strategies

A total of 1172 (89%) of practices had designated specific slots for unscheduled care consultations, encompassing both ED redirections and patient-initiated emergency visit requests.

When appointment slots were fully booked, GPs typically redirected patients as follows: 992 (75%) referred patients to colleagues within the same practice, 361 (27%) to EDs, 208 (16%) topatient-driven online appointment booking system,, 188 (14%) to on-call medical house services, and 163 (12%) provided no redirection.

The median of unscheduled visits per day was 8 (IQR: 5–12). Priority for these slots was given to the GPs’ own patients (n = 1306, 99%), followed by their colleagues’ patients (n = 888, 67%), patients without a regular GP (n = 537, 41%), and emergency/medical dispatch center referred patients (n = 215, 16%).

Factors influencing the allocation of unscheduled consultation slots

A total of 45% of respondents reported that unscheduled care requests were managed within 48 h. Practices with a larger patient base were significantly more likely to offer dedicated slots for unscheduled visits encompassing both ED redirections and patient-initiated emergency visit requests (p = 0,009). General practitioners (GPs) with a smaller patient base (less than 1,250 patients per year) were more likely to accept daily slots for unscheduled visits (OR = 1.575, 95% CI [1.119; 2.218]). Additionally, GPs using computerized scheduling systems were more open to communicating their availability for unscheduled care (OR = 2.531, 95% CI [1.795; 3.567]) (Table 2, Table S1). Barriers to the implementation of unscheduled visit slots for ED Redirection

The main reasons cited for not allocating dedicated slots to unscheduled visits included disinterest (n = 607, 46%), time constraints (n = 636, 48%), organizational challenges (n = 115, 9%), and insufficient financial incentive (n = 43, 3%). Overall, 1093 (83%) of GP respondents were not interested in implementing dedicated slots to ED redirection.

816 (62%) GPs expressed opposition to disclosing availability of their unscheduled care slots, 1155 (88%) refusing automatic scheduling of ED patients into their clinics, and 606 (46%) expressed opposition to implement teleconsultations for ED-referred patients.

Among the 500 (38%) GPs willing to communicate their availability to EDs, the preferred methods of contact were telephone (n = 882, 67%), external virtual services (n = 444, 34%), smartphone applications (n = 200, 15%), and internet-based communication tools (n = 105, 8%).

Discussion

GPs’ integration of unscheduled visit slots and barriers to ED Redirection

The current study found that among the 2% sample of French GP surveyed, nine out of ten GPs have integrated daily slots for unscheduled visits, highlighting a strong commitment to accessible unscheduled care. However, these slots are predominantly reserved for their own or colleagues’ patient. Notably, most GPs expressed little or no interest in allocating slots for ED-redirected patients, with two-thirds unwilling to communicate the availability of these slots to EDs. Furthermore, a substantial proportion of GPs indicated they would reject automatic scheduling of ED redirected patients into their clinics, should such strategies be implemented in local hospitals. The main reasons cited for not allocating dedicated slots to unscheduled visits included a lack of interest, time constraints, organizational challenges, and insufficient financial incentives. This reluctance aligns with previous studies that highlighted logistical and financial constraints. Notably, French GPs have among the lowest physician incomes among in major European economies [21, 22]. A recent 15 euros incentive was introduced for GPs willing to see patients within 48 h through the “Access service care, Service d’accès aux soins (SAS),” a dispatch system operated by emergency physicians and GPs based on patient acuity [23]. But this has not alleviated concerns over administrative burden th [24, 25]. Prior surveys indicate that administrative tasks consume up to 16.6% of GPs’ weekly work hours, underscoring the significant impact of non-patient-related activities. Similar trends in the U.S. show physicians spend 1.7 h daily on administrative work [26], which may further dissuading GPs from accepting additional ED patients. The survey also revealed substantial reluctance among GPs regarding the sharing of information about their available unscheduled care slots with EDs and a large proportion were not willing to accept automatic schedule patients into their clinics from EDs.

Challenges in emergency patient Redirection and primary care coordination

The study examined emergency reorientation strategies implemented at three key stages: phone dispatch, paramedic evaluations, and ED triage [27]. These rely on primary care services collaboration, mainly with GPs. However, the definition of “emergency” remains inconsistent, and the lack of consensus on patient management complicates patient redirection. Ongoing debates in France, where professional societies have recently advocated for local consensus on patient referral, emphasizing the challenges in establishing standardized guidelines [15].

Previous studies have primarily focused on evaluating the effectiveness and safety of patient redirection. Morin et al. in France reported a 4.1% of ED return rate within 72 h post-redirection, suggesting that quality of care remains largely unaffected [28]. Similarly, a Canadian study found electronic decision-supported redirection resulted in a low rate of unexpected ED returns, with 93% of patients accepted redirection and 84% satisfied with their care [29]. To our knowledge, this is the first study exploring GPs’ perspectives on unscheduled care and ED redirection. Despite the urgent need for alternative strategies to reduce inappropriate ED use by patients with non-urgent conditions, GPs displayed significant ambivalence towards allocating unscheduled care slots and collaborating with EDs for redirected patients. This reluctance is likely exacerbated by the low financial incentives available, organizational complexities, and the individualistic nature of traditional GP practices.

Workforce shortages and changing GP practices

Our study also situates findings within broader healthcare reforms in France, such as the Ma Santé 2022 initiative, which promotes coordinated practice through Territorial Professional Health Communities (Communautés professionnelles territoriales de santé, CPTS). These structures encourage cooperation among GPs across larger geographical areas to enhance the accessibility and efficiency of care. However, the success of such reforms relies heavily on effective communication and cooperation between city-based and hospital-based healthcare providers, which remains a significant challenge [30].

The GP shortage further limits appointment availability, with approximately 10% of the population lacking a primary care provider. This issue is compounded by an aging physician workforce [31, 32] and y a trend towards reduced working hours among physicians, as highlighted for example by Kralj et al.., who reported a decline in average weekly hours from 52.8 in the late 1980 s to 45.9 in the early 2020 s [33]. In 2020, GPs workload in European country were 33,5 to 51,1 work hour per week [34]. Burnout and a shift towards better work-life balance may explain this reduction, alongside the growing preference for salaried positions over private practice, offering more predictable hours and fewer administrative responsibilities [35–37]. All these partly explains why they prioritize their existing patient base. In addition, continuity of care is crucial for patients, as regular follow-ups with the same GP are associated with reduced hospital admissions and lower mortality rates [18, 19, 38–40]. These challenges may partly explain why physicians tend to prioritize their established patient base. Furthermore, managing the care of patients outside this base complicates effective follow-up and coordination with other healthcare professionals.

Alternative strategies

Alternative models to reduce inappropriate low-acuity ED visits, such as those employed in Nordic countries, offer valuable insights. For instance, Denmark’s healthcare system requires patients to call a designated number before visiting the ED, ensuring that only those requiring emergency care are admitted, while others are redirected to primary care clinics. Nurse-led triage systems and the integration of nurse practitioners have also been shown to improve care quality and reduce waiting times [41]. Alternative approaches involving non-physician healthcare providers, such as nurse practitioners, have been evaluated and shown to positively impact care quality, patient satisfaction, and reduce waiting time s [42, 43]. Some EDs in England have experimented with embedding GPs alongside ED staff to manage unscheduled care needs, thereby reducing the strain on emergency services [44].

Study limitations

This study has several limitations. The respondent sample was younger than the national average of French GPs, indicating potential selection bias, as younger GPs may be more inclined to participate in online surveys. Additionally, there were regional disparities, with some regions overrepresented and others underrepresented. Furthermore, the exploratory design limits the ability to draw definitive conclusions. It is also worth noting that the study was conducted prior to the COVID-19 pandemic, which has likely influenced GPs’ roles in managing ED patients. A follow-up study would be valuable in assessing any shifts in GP perspectives pre- and post-pandemic. Despite these limitations, this study is the largest to date that investigates GPs’ views on unscheduled care, offering important insights into the challenges and barriers to optimizing collaboration between primary care and EDs in France.

Conclusions

Most French GPs integrate daily slots for unscheduled visits into their practice, though these are primarily reserved for their existing patient base. The survey further revealed that two-thirds of GPs are unwilling to communicate slot availability to EDs, and an even larger proportion oppose automatic scheduling of redirected ED patients. These barriers highlight the need for further research into strategies that could enhance collaboration between primary and emergency care. Future efforts should explore targeted incentives, streamlined administrative processes, and improved communication pathways between GPs and EDs. Multidisciplinary coordination through structures like CPTS or digital scheduling platforms may help bridge existing gaps. Additionally, adapting financial models to better compensate GPs for unscheduled care could improve participation in redirection strategies, ultimately enhancing patient access and continuity of care.

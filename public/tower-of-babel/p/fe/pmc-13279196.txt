Early PCSK9 Inhibitor Use Correlates With Improved Outcomes in Patients With Acute Stroke Receiving Endovascular Therapy
Authors: Tong Wu, Xiaolin Zhao, Mengxuan Xiao, Junlin Deng, Ziyu Chen, Daihong Cheng, Suyue Pan, Xianghong Liu, Yongming Wu, Kaibin Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe clinical benefits of early PCSK9 (proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9) inhibitor use in patients with acute large vessel occlusion undergoing endovascular therapy (EVT) remain unclear. This study investigates whether early administration of PCSK9 inhibitors after EVT is associated with improved clinical outcomes.MethodsIn this retrospective cohort study, we consecutively screened patients with acute large vessel occlusion with successful EVT recanalization at 2 academic hospitals from January 2019 to March 2025. Participants were grouped based on receipt of evolocumab or alirocumab within 96 hours after EVT. Propensity score matching was performed to adjust for baseline covariates. The primary outcome was early artery reocclusion. Mediation analysis was conducted to explore potential links between PCSK9 inhibitors and clinical outcomes.ResultsAmong 429 eligible patients (148 in the PCSK9 inhibitors group, 281 controls), 116 matched pairs were analyzed. The incidence of early artery reocclusion was significantly lower in the PCSK9 inhibitors group compared with controls (3.4% versus 12.9%; odds ratio [OR], 0.24 [95% CI, 0.08–0.75]; P=0.01). Moreover, a significantly greater proportion of patients in the PCSK9 inhibitors group achieved favorable outcomes at 90 days (modified Rankin Scale score 0–2: 77.6% versus 60.3%; OR, 2.28 [95% CI, 1.28–4.04]; P<0.01). Safety outcomes were comparable between the 2 groups. Mediation analysis suggested the benefit of PCSK9 inhibitors on functional outcomes was partially mediated by reduced EAR.ConclusionsEarly PCSK9 inhibitor use after EVT in patients with acute large vessel occlusion was linked to reduced EAR and better 90‐day outcomes without added adverse events.

Stroke remains the leading cause of long‐term disability and the second leading cause of death worldwide. 1 Although endovascular therapy (EVT) has markedly improved outcomes for patients with acute large vessel occlusion (LVO), nearly 50% of patients still fail to achieve functional independence despite successful recanalization. 2 One potential contributor to poor outcomes is early arterial reocclusion (EAR), a complication reported in up to 36.9% of patients following EVT in patients with intracranial atherosclerosis‐related LVO. 3 , 4 , 5 Currently, the primary salvage strategy for EAR is repeat EVT. 6 However, this approach carries a heightened risk of procedural complications, such as intracranial hemorrhage, and places a significant financial burden on patients. 7 To date, pharmacological strategies for preventing and treating EAR remain limited.

The pathophysiology of EAR in patients with acute LVO is not yet well elucidated. However, evidence suggests that EAR is closely related to endothelial injury, often secondary to rupture of unstable atherosclerotic plaques, particularly in intracranial atherosclerosis‐related LVO. 5 , 8 Ruptured plaques expose thrombogenic surfaces, triggering in situ thrombosis and subsequent reocclusion. Current therapeutic strategies targeting unstable plaques primarily involve aggressive lowering of low‐density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL‐C) to reduce plaque burden and enhancing endothelial stability, often through anti‐inflammatory pathways. Several studies have demonstrated that high‐intensity statin therapy can effectively promote plaque stabilization and regression, 9 , 10 , 11 as well as reduce inflammation markers such as C‐reactive protein. 11 , 12 However, statins alone may be insufficient for achieving rapid LDL‐C reduction in the acute phase, an important consideration given that faster and more intensive LDL‐C lowering has been associated with reduced rates of cerebrovascular events. 13

PCSK9 (proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9) inhibitors represent a novel class of lipid‐lowering agents that have demonstrated superior efficacy in lowering LDL‐C and preventing major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events. 14 , 15 , 16 Beyond their lipid‐lowering capabilities, PCSK9 inhibitors have been shown to exert anti‐inflammatory and antiplatelet effects, 17 , 18 , 19 contributing to plaque stabilization and endothelial protection more effectively than statins alone. 20 , 21 , 22 These pleiotropic effects may play a critical role in reducing the risk of EAR following EVT. Given these mechanistic advantages, we hypothesize that early administration of PCSK9 inhibitors after EVT may decrease the incidence of EAR by promoting plaque stabilization and mitigating endothelial injury, thereby leading to improved 90‐day neurological outcomes.

The aim of this study is to evaluate the association between early initiation of PCSK9 inhibitors and both the incidence of EAR and functional outcomes at 90 days in patients undergoing EVT for acute LVO.

METHODS

Data Availability Statement

The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Study Design and Patient Selection

We retrospectively collected data from patients with acute LVO who underwent EVT at the Department of Neurology, Nanfang Hospital of Southern Medical University, and Ganzhou Hospital‐Nanfang Hospital from January 2019 to March 2025.

Patients were included if they met the following criteria: (1) aged ≥18 years; (2) admitted within 72 hours after stroke onset; (3) diagnosed with acute ischemic stroke confirmed by magnetic resonance imaging or computed tomography; (4) diagnosed with acute LVO confirmed by digital subtraction angiography, magnetic resonance angiography, or computed tomography angiography; and (5) achieved recanalization after EVT. Acute LVO was defined as the complete occlusion of intracranial arteries, including the internal carotid artery, M1 segment of the middle cerebral artery, the basilar artery, and the intracranial segment of the vertebral artery. 23 EVT was performed in accordance with current guideline recommendations, with rescue balloon angioplasty, stent implantation, or intra‐arterial thrombolysis considered when necessary to achieve successful recanalization, defined as modified thrombolysis in cerebral infarction grade ≥2b. 24 , 25 , 26

Patients were excluded if they met any of the following criteria: (1) intracranial artery occlusion due to cardioembolism, small‐artery occlusion, stroke of other determined cause (eg, vasculitis, vascular malformations, moyamoya disease), or stroke of undetermined cause, as defined by the TOAST (Trial of Org 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment) classification and confirmed by emergency ECG, echocardiogram, and digital subtraction angiography findings 27 ; (2) intracranial hemorrhage within the past 3 months; (3) parenchymal hematoma immediately after EVT (including parenchymal hematoma type 1 and parenchymal hematoma type 2, according to the European Cooperative Acute Stroke Study III) 28 ; (4) severe renal or hepatic insufficiency, defined as estimated glomerular filtration rate <30 mL/min or creatinine >177 μmol/L for renal function, and as alanine aminotransferase (ALT) >120 U/L or the presence of hepatic encephalopathy for hepatic function; (5) use of PCSK9 inhibitors within 6 months before EVT; (6) preadmission modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score ≥2; (7) end‐stage malignancy with an expected survival time of <3 months; (8) other neurological disorders affecting functional outcome assessment at follow‐up; (9) missing medical records during hospitalization; and (10) absence of baseline lipid test results.

Ethical Approval

This is a double‐center, retrospective, observational study. The study protocol was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Nanfang Hospital (NFEC‐2025‐213) and has obtained the local ethics committee approval of another participating institution. Given its observational and retrospective characteristics, informed consent was waived by the review committee, and all data were fully deidentified. All study procedures and protocols complied with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Data Collection

Baseline information included demographic information, stroke risk factors, medical history, baseline vital signs, clinical scales, imaging data, laboratory test results, and surgical data. Demographic information included sex, age, admission time, and length of hospitalization. Stroke risk factors and medical history included smoking history, alcohol use, hyperlipidemia, preadmission lipid‐lowering therapy, hypertension, diabetes, coronary heart disease, atrial fibrillation, and history of stroke or transient ischemic attack. Baseline vital signs included systolic blood pressure and diastolic blood pressure. Clinical scales and imaging data included the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS), Glasgow Coma Scale, mRS, Alberta Stroke Program Early Computed Tomography Score, and infarct core volume. These parameters were assessed by experienced neurologists based on the clinical and imaging findings within 24 hours after admission. Baseline laboratory test results included glucose, glycosylated hemoglobin, total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides, high‐density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL‐C), LDL‐C, ALT, and creatinine, collected via venous blood samples at 6:00 am on the first day following EVT. Surgical data included location, occlusion site, time of surgery, EVT strategies, collateral status, and postsurgery modified thrombolysis in cerebral infarction score.

Treatments

Patients were divided into 2 groups: the control group and the PCSK9 inhibitors group. The control group received standard lipid‐lowering therapy, consisting of statin monotherapy or a combination of statins with cholesterol absorption inhibitors. 29 , 30 The PCSK9 inhibitors group received either evolocumab (Repatha; Amgen Manufacturing Limited, Juncos, PR, USA) (140 mg every 2 weeks or 420 mg once monthly) or alirocumab (Praluent; Sanofi Winthrop Industrie, Le Trait, France) (75 mg every 2 weeks or 150 mg once monthly) within 96 hours after EVT, in combination with statin or cholesterol absorption inhibitor therapy. All patients received additional treatments in accordance with current clinical guidelines, which included intravenous thrombolysis within the recommended time window, antiplatelet therapy (either dual or monotherapy), blood pressure and blood glucose management, and lifestyle education. 25 , 31

Study Outcome

The primary outcome of this study was EAR, defined as reocclusion of the initially affected intracranial artery within 1 week after EVT. The diagnosis of EAR following drug administration was based on clinical and radiographic criteria. It required the presence of any neurological deterioration (eg, decrease in muscle strength, altered consciousness, or pupillary changes) along with imaging confirmation of reocclusion using 1 or more of the following modalities: transcranial Doppler (TCD), digital subtraction angiography, magnetic resonance angiography, or computed tomography angiography. When using TCD to evaluate EAR, patients were required to have an acoustic window. Additionally, blood flow signals in the responsible vessel must be detectable during the first TCD examination after EVT, whereas these signals disappear on subsequent TCD examination when EAR is suspected. Otherwise, confirmation through the other 3 examinations was required following the TCD examination. Secondary outcomes included (1) early neurological improvement, defined as a decrease in NIHSS score of ≥4 or an NIHSS score of 0 at 1 week post‐treatment; (2) functional outcome at 90 days, assessed using mRS score, with a good outcome defined as an mRS score of 0 to 2; and (3) change in lipid levels (TC, triglycerides, HDL‐C, and LDL‐C) during hospitalization. Safety outcomes included (1) incidence of hemorrhagic transformation, defined as hemorrhagic infarction type 1 or hemorrhagic infarction type 2 according to the European Cooperative Acute Stroke Study III during hospitalization 28 ; (2) elevation of ALT, defined as ALT levels >120 U/L accompanied by clinical symptoms during hospitalization 32 ; (3) early neurological deterioration, defined as an increase in NIHSS score of ≥4 at 1 week; (4) all‐cause mortality at 90 days.

Outcomes were assessed through a comprehensive review of medical records, imaging studies, and laboratory tests. During hospitalization, neurological improvement or deterioration was evaluated based on documented changes in the NIHSS scores. Imaging data were reviewed to confirm EAR and hemorrhagic transformation. Lipid profile parameters, including TC, triglycerides, HDL‐C, LDL‐C, as well as ALT levels, were measured through routine laboratory tests during hospitalization to assess the change in lipid levels and the incidence of ALT elevation. Functional outcomes and 90‐day mortality were assessed by telephone follow‐up at 90 days by 2 trained neurologists blinded to the study design, using the mRS score.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics (version 27.0.1) and R (version 4.4.1). The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was applied to assess the normality of continuous variables. Continuous variables were presented as mean±SD or median [interquartile range], and categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages.

Propensity score matching (PSM) was used to balance confounders between the 2 groups. Variables that were unbalanced between the 2 groups were selected for matching, including history of stroke or transient ischemic attack, lipid‐lowering intensity, antiplatelet therapy, occlusion site, EVT strategies (stent retriever thrombectomy, stent implantation, and intra‐arterial thrombolysis), postsurgery modified thrombolysis in cerebral infarction score, TC, HDL‐C, and LDL‐C. The PCSK9 inhibitors and control groups were matched in a 1:1 ratio using nearest neighbor matching with a caliper size of 0.05. Balance of baseline covariates was assessed using standardized mean differences, with a value <0.2 indicating an acceptable balance between groups. Categorical variables were analyzed using univariate logistic regression to compute odds ratios (ORs) and corresponding 95% CIs. For continuous variables, intergroup comparisons were performed using independent samples t tests or the Mann–Whitney U test, as appropriate. Subgroup analysis based on occlusion location (anterior versus posterior circulation) was performed within regression models to evaluate treatment effect heterogeneity. To investigate potential causal pathways, mediation analysis was performed using nonparametric bootstrapping with 1000 replications. A 2‐sided P value <0.05 was deemed statistically significant.

Independent Data Access and Analysis

The corresponding author had full access to all data in the study and takes responsibility for the integrity of the data and the data analysis.

RESULTS

Baseline Characteristics

From January 2019 to March 2025, a total of 975 patients were screened for eligibility. Among them, 429 patients who met the study criteria were included in the study, including 281 patients in the control group and 148 patients in the PCSK9 inhibitors group (Figure 1).

Baseline characteristics before and after PSM are summarized (Table 1; Table S1). Following PSM, 232 patients were successfully matched, resulting in 116 patients in each group. After matching, the baseline characteristics between the PCSK9 inhibitor and control groups were well balanced (the standardized mean differences for most variables were <0.1, and all remained below the predefined threshold of 0.2), indicating effective control for potential confounding.

Primary, Secondary, and Safety Outcomes

For the primary outcome (Table 2), the incidence of EAR was significantly lower in the PCSK9 inhibitors group compared with the control group (3.4% versus 12.9%; OR, 0.24 [95% CI, 0.08–0.75]; P=0.01). To rule out reverse causality, we verified that all patients in the PCSK9 inhibitor group received the first dose before the imaging diagnosis of EAR (Table S2). For secondary outcomes (Table 2), patients in the PCSK9 inhibitors group experienced a significantly higher proportion of 90‐day favorable outcome (77.6% versus 60.3%; OR, 2.28 [95% CI, 1.28–4.04]; P<0.01; Figure S1). No statistically significant differences were observed in the incidence of early neurological improvement (65.5% versus 57.8%; OR, 1.39 [95% CI, 0.82–2.36]; P=0.23) between the 2 groups.

Subgroup analysis revealed that, among patients with anterior circulation LVO, early administration of PCSK9 inhibitors was also associated with a reduced incidence of EAR. Furthermore, in both anterior and posterior circulation LVO subgroups, early use of PCSK9 inhibitors was linked to an increased proportion of favorable 90‐day outcomes (Table S3).

During hospitalization, patients in the PCSK9 inhibitors group exhibited a greater reduction in LDL‐C levels compared with the control group (1.77 [95% CI, 1.22–2.37] mmol/L versus 0.84 [95% CI, 0.40–1.55] mmol/L, P 0.01), after adjusting for potential confounding factors, including baseline lipid levels. Similarly, a more pronounced decrease in TC levels was observed in the PCSK9 inhibitors group (2.04 [95% CI, 1.48–3.05] mmol/L versus 1.15 [95% CI, 0.59–1.85] mmol/L, P<0.01) (Table 3). For safety outcomes, no statistically significant differences were observed in the incidence of early neurological deterioration (5.2% versus 7.8%, P=0.43), ALT elevation (2.6% versus 2.6%, P=1.00), hemorrhagic transformation (11.2% versus 12.1%, P=0.84), or 90‐day mortality (1.7% versus 3.4%, P=0.42) between the 2 groups after PSM (Table 2).

Mediation Analysis

Mediation analyses were performed to elucidate potential links underlying the association between PCSK9 inhibitor use and clinical outcomes. We investigated whether the effect of PCSK9 inhibitors on 90‐day functional outcome (mRS score 0–2) was mediated through EAR. The analysis demonstrated statistically significant both direct (β=0.14 [95% CI 0.01–0.25]; P=0.02) and indirect effects (β=0.03 [95% CI, 0.01–0.07]; P<0.01), with EAR mediating 18.30% of the total effect (P=0.02; Figure 2).

DISCUSSION

In this study, we found that the early initiation of PCSK9 inhibitors in patients with acute LVO undergoing EVT correlated with reduced EAR incidence and improved 90‐day functional outcomes, without increasing the risks of ALT elevation, hemorrhagic transformation, early neurological deterioration, or 90‐day mortality.

Previous studies have established that PCSK9 inhibitors decrease the incidence of early recurrent stroke in patients with acute ischemic stroke. 33 However, these investigations focused specifically on populations with intracranial atherosclerotic stenosis and excluded patients receiving mechanical thrombectomy. Subsequent research demonstrated that PCSK9 inhibitors significantly reduce rates of early neurological deterioration, 34 though this study also excluded EVT cases. A retrospective analysis examined PCSK9 inhibitor initiation post‐thrombectomy, reporting improved discharge outcomes characterized by lower NIHSS scores and higher proportions of patients achieving mRS score 0 to 3. 35 Nevertheless, this study incorporated all stroke subtypes, including cardioembolism, which is inconsistent with the mechanism of PCSK9 inhibitors reducing cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events. Furthermore, their functional outcome definition (mRS score 0–3 at discharge) diverges from the conventional threshold for favorable outcomes (mRS score 0–2). 36 The study's PCSK9 inhibitor cohort (n=42) also raises concerns regarding potential bias due to limited sample size. In contrast, our study of 116 EVT‐treated patients with intracranial atherosclerosis‐related LVO demonstrates that early PCSK9 inhibitor administration is associated with significantly higher rates of favorable 90‐day functional outcomes (mRS score 0–2).

Our results showed that early PCSK9 inhibitor initiation was associated with a lower incidence of EAR at 1 week. However, the incidence of early neurological improvement or deterioration at 1 week was similar between the 2 groups. These observations differ from previous reports showing that PCSK9 inhibitors reduce early neurological deterioration in large artery atherosclerosis patients during the same period. 34 This discrepancy between our findings and previous reports may be attributable to the retrospective design of our study, which could have contributed to the negative early‐stage outcomes. Despite these potential biases, our results showed that the early clinical advantages translated into significantly better 90‐day functional outcomes, consistent with prior evidence. 34 Mediation analyses substantiated that the acute‐phase benefit partially mediated the observed long‐term functional outcomes, suggesting a potential temporal cascade of treatment effects. Notably, our study population targeted patients with atherosclerosis‐related LVO with varying severities of stroke, particularly incorporating those with minor strokes. The baseline NIHSS score in our cohort was 9 (4–14), which is lower than the NIHSS score of 14.5 reported in previous comparable studies. Consequently, the rate of favorable outcomes in our study (60.3% in the control arm) was higher than the reported rate of 49.8%. 3

An important consideration is the safety of PCSK9 inhibitors. Although existing literature suggests an association between intensive LDL‐C reduction and elevated intracranial hemorrhage risk, 32 , 37 contrasting evidence from retrospective analyses indicates that PCSK9 inhibitors may reduce hemorrhagic transformation rates. 35 In our cohort, we observed no significant between‐group differences in hemorrhagic transformation incidence during hospitalization. Regarding hepatic safety, statin‐associated ALT elevation remains a clinically relevant concern. Our findings align with prior reports, 38 , 39 demonstrating comparable ALT elevation rates between treatment groups. Neurological safety assessments revealed similar incidence rates of early neurological deterioration. Besides, PCSK9 inhibitor administration showed no association with increased 90‐day mortality, consistent with findings from large‐scale clinical trials. 40 , 41 Although our study was not powered to detect statistical differences in rare events, the consistent lack of adverse signals—including hemorrhagic transformation, ALT elevation, and mortality—provides reassuring preliminary evidence for the safety of early PCSK9 inhibitor initiation in the acute phase of LVO.

This study proposes a novel pharmacological approach to EAR management after EVT. Given EAR's impact on adverse events and neurological outcomes and the absence of effective nonsurgical interventions, our study exhibits potential benefits of PCSK9 inhibitors in the pharmacological management of EAR after EVT. These findings suggest that routine administration of PCSK9 inhibitors may be warranted for the prevention of EAR following EVT in patients with intracranial atherosclerosis‐related LVO, regardless of baseline lipid levels. In our center's practice, the administration of PCSK9 inhibitors was not determined by a rigid severity protocol but was primarily driven by baseline LDL‐C levels and pathogenetic considerations. Patients with intracranial artery stenosis demonstrating unstable plaque features or residual stenosis were more likely to receive a combination of dual‐antiplatelet therapy and PCSK9 inhibitors to achieve rapid plaque stabilization. Importantly, beyond this targeted pharmacological strategy, the general standard of care—including neurointensive care protocols, hemodynamic management, and rehabilitation—was uniform across the cohort. The observed higher usage of dual‐antiplatelet therapy in the treatment group reflects the specific management of atherosclerotic burden rather than a broad disparity in treatment intensity. Furthermore, while clinicians previously restricted PCSK9 inhibitor use in patients with high NIHSS scores due to bleeding concerns, our PSM analysis (which balanced procedural factors such as rescue stenting) suggests that expanding eligibility to these severe patients is both safe and potentially beneficial.

The mechanisms of EAR are still not clear. We primarily hypothesized that rupture of unstable plaques induces endothelial injury, leading to in situ thrombosis and EAR, which significantly affects 90‐day clinical outcomes. PCSK9 inhibitors may reduce EAR incidence through dual mechanisms of plaque stabilization and regression at the early stage: (1) rapid reduction of plaque burden via potent LDL‐C lowering, and (2) endothelial protective effects via anti‐inflammatory pathways. A critical mechanistic question is the temporal relationship between drug administration and clinical benefit. In our study, the mean time to EAR was approximately 48 hours. Although PCSK9 inhibitors effectively lowered LDL‐C levels over 1 to 2 weeks, this lipid‐lowering trajectory is likely too gradual to solely account for the rapid prevention of EAR observed within the first few days. This temporal dissociation supports the hypothesis of immediate “pleiotropic” effects. Regrettably, we did not perform a mediation analysis with LDL‐C as the mediator for EAR to further prove this hypothesis, as the chronology does not support it. Further validation is required to prove the mechanism of PCSK9 inhibitors in reducing EAR at the early stage, such as quantitative assessment of plaque volume via ultrasound, dynamic monitoring of serum LDL‐C levels, and inflammatory biomarker profiling within 48 to 72 hours after EVT. Besides, PCSK9 inhibitor monotherapy remains insufficient for the comprehensive prevention and treatment of EAR, particularly for nonatherosclerotic causes such as surgical injury or microemboli. Therefore, pathogenesis‐targeted management strategies are necessary. Another critical consideration is the optimal timing for initiating PCSK9 inhibitor therapy in the acute period after EVT. Although our study limited PCSK9 inhibitor administration to within 96 hours after EVT, the optimal initiation windowfollowing EVT requires rigorous exploration to determine whether earlier administration enhances benefits.

There were several limitations in this study. First, the analysis was based on a relatively small sample size, with a great number of patients removed following 1:1 PSM due to baseline imbalances. These may have led to selection bias. Second, postoperative patients did not routinely undergo magnetic resonance angiography or computed tomography angiography, and the performance of TCD was not widespread and was limited by the transcranial window. This may have led to an underestimation of EAR. Third, during hospitalization, the dosage and frequency of PCSK9 inhibitors varied among patients based on individual lipid profiles. Furthermore, most patients discontinued treatment after discharge, potentially introducing bias into our evaluation of long‐term efficacy. Finally, the temporal relationship between the administration of the drug and the occurrence of early neurological improvement or deterioration remains unclear, which confounds the assessment of outcomes at the early stage. Moreover, patients who experienced early neurological deterioration might have been less likely to receive PCSK9 inhibitors. These underscore the need for further intervention studies to confirm our findings.

CONCLUSIONS

Early initiation of PCSK9 inhibitors was associated with a lower incidence of EAR and better 90‐day functional outcomes in patients with acute LVO treated with EVT, without an increased risk of ALT elevation, hemorrhagic transformation, early neurological deterioration, or 90‐day mortality. Further prospective, randomized controlled trials are needed to confirm these findings, as well as the optimal initiation window of PCSK9 inhibitors.

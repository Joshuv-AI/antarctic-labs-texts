A minimally invasive approach to esophagojejunostomy leakage: a retrospective cohort study of the efficacy of endoscopic Histoacryl injection compared with conventional treatments
Authors: Min Chan Kim, Sue-Yeon Kim, Sunghwa Kang, Myeongseok Koh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeEsophagojejunostomy leakage (EJL) continues to be a serious and potentially fatal complication following total gastrectomy for gastric cancer. This study aimed to evaluate the clinical outcomes of endoscopic Histoacryl (B. Braun) injection in comparison to alternative treatment strategies for EJL.MethodsOut of 885 patients who underwent total gastrectomy at a single institution between January 2003 and August 2023, 26 (2.9%) developed EJL. Patients were categorized into four groups according to the management received: conservative treatment (n = 5), endoscopic Histoacryl injection (n = 10), reoperation (n = 8), and combined therapy (n = 3). Outcomes assessed included postoperative hospital stay, duration to oral intake, treatment success rate, and mortality.ResultsOf the 26 patients with EJL, 3 (11.5%) succumbed to sepsis: two in the reoperation group and 1 in the Histoacryl group. In the 23 successfully managed cases, the Histoacryl group achieved the shortest median postoperative hospital stay, the shortest time to resuming of oral intake, and a 90% success rate without any procedure-related complications. The reoperation group experienced increased morbidity and mortality. Stent migration was reported in one patient from the combined treatment group. Differences in recovery between the four groups reached statistical significance (P < 0.05).ConclusionEndoscopic Histoacryl injection represents a safe, effective, and minimally invasive modality for managing EJL, providing improved recovery times and reduced complication rates. It is beneficial both as a primary intervention and as part of combination therapy.Keywords: Anastomotic leak, Gastrointestinal endoscopy, Gastrectomy, Stomach neoplasms, Tissue adhesives

INTRODUCTION

Esophagojejunostomy leakage (EJL) remains one of the most serious and life-threatening complications following total gastrectomy for gastric cancer. Although advancements in surgical technique and perioperative management have been made [1], EJL management remains a significant challenge for surgeons due to its association with high morbidity, the risk of sepsis, and potential mortality if timely diagnosis and intervention are not achieved [2,3].

With the rising incidence of proximal gastric cancer, the number of patients requiring total gastrectomy has increased. At the same time, minimally invasive surgical approaches—including laparoscopic and robotic total gastrectomy—have gained wide acceptance in clinical settings [4,5,6]. Nevertheless, despite the purported advantages of minimally invasive techniques, the majority of research indicates that the rate of EJL is comparable to that seen with open gastrectomy [5,7].

Endoscopic stent placement has become a widely used non-surgical treatment for EJL, with literature indicating its potential to reduce the necessity for reoperation and promote earlier oral feeding [8,9]. Nevertheless, the occurrence of stent-related complications, including migration, tissue overgrowth, and patient discomfort, constrains its universal use [10]. Consequently, alternative endoscopic strategies have been investigated.

Among these, endoscopic injection of n-butyl-2-cyanoacrylate (Histoacryl, B. Braun) has demonstrated encouraging results for managing gastrointestinal fistulas and anastomotic leaks in distinct surgical contexts [11,12]. Recently, our institution published promising short-term outcomes utilizing endoscopic Histoacryl injection to treat EJL after total gastrectomy, underscoring its potential as a straightforward and minimally invasive option [13]. However, this technique remains infrequently used, and comparative studies evaluating its effectiveness relative to standard treatments, such as conservative management or surgical revision, are lacking.

Accordingly, we aimed to investigate the clinical effectiveness of endoscopic Histoacryl injection in comparison to other treatment methods in patients with EJL following total gastrectomy. By reviewing our 20-year, single-center experience, we intended to determine whether Histoacryl injection provides a safe and efficacious treatment alternative for appropriately selected cases.

METHODS

Ethics statement

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Institutional Review Board approval was obtained from Dong-A University Hospital (No. DAUHIRB-23-222). Given the retrospective design, the requirement for informed consent was waived.

Study design and patient selection

This retrospective observational study was performed at a single center. We examined the medical records of 885 patients who underwent total gastrectomy for gastric cancer at Dong-A University Hospital from January 2003 to August 2023. Among this cohort, 26 patients (2.9%) developed EJL and were included in the study analysis. EJL diagnosis was established by either radiologic or endoscopic evidence of extraluminal contrast leakage, air-fluid collection, or direct detection of a disrupted anastomosis.

Treatment and classification

The 26 patients found to have EJL were classified by the initial therapeutic approach. Five patients received conservative management involving bowel rest, parenteral nutrition, intravenous antibiotics, and either percutaneous or surgical drainage. Ten patients underwent endoscopic Histoacryl injection, where n-butyl-2-cyanoacrylate was delivered using an endoscopic injector to the leakage site. Eight patients required additional surgery, including reanastomosis, direct repair, or open drainage procedures. Three patients received a combination of modalities, utilizing various permutations of reoperation, endoscopic stenting, and Histoacryl injection. Notably, none of the patients received endoscopic stenting alone as their primary therapy (Fig. 1). Patients who initially received conservative treatment but were later converted to other modalities were classified according to their final treatment group.

Data collection

We retrospectively obtained extensive clinical, surgical, and pathological information from the patients’ electronic medical records. Demographic and baseline details included age, sex, body mass index (BMI), American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status classification, and comorbidities. Tumor-specific variables collected encompassed pathological stage as designated by the 8th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system and the performance of combined organ resection. Surgical parameters documented consisted of surgical approach (open or laparoscopic), curative versus palliative intent, lymph node dissection extent (less than D2 vs. D2 or greater), number of lymph nodes retrieved, operative duration, and estimated blood loss. Moreover, data regarding leakage-related features such as initial presenting symptoms, leakage size, hemodynamic instability at leakage diagnosis, time between gastrectomy and leakage detection, and interval to initiation of leakage treatment were also recorded.

Outcome measures

The primary outcomes evaluated in this study were total postoperative hospital stay after the initial intervention, duration from intervention to oral intake, treatment success rate, and mortality related to leakage. Secondary outcomes included the interval between primary surgery and leakage diagnosis, the time from leakage diagnosis to initial treatment, and complications associated with each treatment. These parameters were systematically analyzed and compared across the 4 treatment groups to assess the comparative effectiveness and safety of each approach.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were presented as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs) and were compared using the Kruskal-Wallis test. Categorical variables were analyzed using either the chi-square test or Fisher exact test, depending on appropriateness. A P-value less than 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant. All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics ver. 28 (IBM Corp.).

RESULTS

Clinicopathologic characteristics

Among the 26 patients with EJL, the median age was 64 years (range, 42–82 years), and 73% were male. The median BMI was 22.0 kg/m2 (range, 14.7–28.8 kg/m2). The majority of patients (65.4%) were categorized as ASA class II. Eighteen patients (69.2%) had comorbidities, and 12 (46.2%) were classified as stage I according to the 8th edition of the AJCC staging system. Combined organ resection was performed in 6 patients (23.1%), most often cholecystectomy (n = 4), followed by pancreatectomy (n = 1) and splenectomy (n = 1) (Table 1).

Surgical characteristics

Laparoscopic total gastrectomy was performed in 14 patients (53.8%), whereas 12 patients (46.2%) underwent open surgery. Curative resection was accomplished in 24 patients (92.3%), and 12 patients (46.2%) underwent D2 or more extensive lymph node dissection. The median number of retrieved lymph nodes was 47 (range, 12–101), the median operative time was 215 minutes (range, 104–500 minutes), and the median estimated blood loss was 151 mL (range, 34–3,000 mL) (Table 2).

Leakage-related characteristics

Abdominal pain was the most frequently observed initial symptom of leakage (n = 14), followed by fever (n = 7). Two asymptomatic patients were diagnosed by routine postoperative CT imaging. The median interval from initial surgery to leakage diagnosis was 11 days (range, 3–23 days), and the median time from leakage diagnosis to initial treatment was 5.9 days (range, 0–39 days) (Table 3).

Treatment modalities and outcomes

The treatment modalities were distributed as follows: conservative treatment (n = 5), endoscopic Histoacryl injection (n = 10), reoperation (n = 8), and combined treatment (n = 3). Stent insertion alone was not selected as a primary treatment for any patient. The highest overall treatment success rate was observed in the conservative group (100%), followed by the Histoacryl injection group (90%), with the lowest rate in the reoperation group (75%) (Fig. 1). Three patients (11.5%) died from sepsis, including 2 in the reoperation group and one in the Histoacryl group. In the Histoacryl group, a patient who had initially recovered and been discharged died within 24 hours of readmission due to unexplained sepsis occurring 3 days after discharge. Importantly, follow-up abdominal CT revealed no recurrent leakage. Treatment-related complications occurred in both the reoperation and combined treatment groups. The reoperation group experienced wound infections (n = 3), methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus infection (n = 1), candidemia (n = 1), and intra-abdominal abscess (n = 1). In the combined treatment group, complications included stent migration (n = 1), pleural effusion (n = 2), and intra-abdominal bleeding (n = 1). Patients in the combined treatment group received 2 or more interventions—including reoperation, stent placement, and Histoacryl injection—based on their clinical status. All 3 patients ultimately had successful closure of the leakage site following Histoacryl injection as the final intervention (Table 4).

Among the 23 patients who recovered from EJL, there were significant differences in postoperative hospital stay, duration of hospitalization following initial treatment, and time to oral intake across the 4 treatment groups (P = 0.006, P = 0.003, and P = 0.005, respectively). The Histoacryl injection group exhibited the shortest median total hospital stay, at 30.0 days (IQR, 27.0–37.0 days), compared to the reoperation group (88.0 days; IQR, 55.0–127.0 days), the conservative group (47.0 days; IQR, 34.0–72.0 days), and the combined group (81.0 days; IQR, 63.0–95.0 days) (Fig. 2). Similarly, the median hospital stay after initial treatment was shortest in the Histoacryl group (10.0 days; IQR, 8.0–12.0 days), whereas the reoperation and conservative groups required 74.5 days (IQR, 43.0–106.0 days) and 47.0 days (IQR, 34.0–72.0 days), respectively (Fig. 3). Time to oral intake following intervention was also shortest in the Histoacryl group (7.0 days; IQR, 6.0–8.0 days) and substantially longer in the reoperation (63.0 days; IQR, 41.0–103.0 days), conservative (35.0 days; IQR, 33.0–36.0 days), and combined (57.0 days; IQR, 30.0–72.5 days) groups (Fig. 4).

DISCUSSION

The incidence of EJL in our cohort was 2.9%, which aligns with previously reported rates of 1% to 10% [1,7,14] and varies depending on surgical technique and stapler type. Specifically, laparoscopic-assisted total gastrectomy has been associated with a 5.9% leakage rate, while totally laparoscopic total gastrectomy shows a slightly higher rate of 6.4% [1]. Additionally, EJL rates have been reported as 1.9% for linear staplers and 2.1% for circular staplers [14]. Although surgical techniques have advanced considerably, especially with minimally invasive procedures, the management of anastomotic leakage continues to present significant clinical challenges because of its substantial morbidity and associated risk of mortality [15,16].

EJL has traditionally been managed either with conservative therapy or surgical reoperation. More recently, the development of endoscopic techniques has introduced alternatives such as endoscopic clip placement, stenting, tissue sealants, and endoscopic vacuum-assisted closure (EVAC), which have gained prominence in the treatment of anastomotic leaks [8,9,10,17]. Evidence from the literature indicates that, for acute leaks less than 1 cm, endoscopic clips are typically recommended; for defects measuring 2–3 cm, over-the-scope clips are preferred, and stenting is usually reserved for leaks exceeding 2 cm [18]. For leaks associated with a defined extraluminal abscess cavity, EVAC is generally considered the optimal intervention [17,19,20]. However, the universal application of these endoscopic modalities is constrained by disparities in local resources, infrastructure, and the availability of sufficient endoscopic expertise.

This retrospective, single-center analysis of 20 years’ experience compared outcomes associated with various therapeutic approaches for EJL after total gastrectomy for gastric cancer. Our results indicate that endoscopic Histoacryl injection is a safe, effective, and minimally invasive option that provides favorable outcomes, such as reduced hospital stay, accelerated recovery, and high rates of treatment success. Conversely, reoperation remains linked with substantial morbidity and mortality, consistent with existing reports outlining the hazards of surgical reintervention in this population, especially among elderly or frail patients [15,16].

Beyond its clinical effectiveness, Histoacryl injection offers a number of practical advantages. This method has decades of use in managing esophageal varices, making it highly familiar to endoscopists. Notably, it permits repeated application, enabling a flexible, stepwise approach to managing leakage.

Among therapeutic options for anastomotic leakage, stent placement continues to be commonly utilized, with reported success rates between 72.4% and 92.9% [8,9,10], while EVAC demonstrates even higher rates of 85.7% to 100% [17,19,20,21]. In previous studies on upper and lower gastrointestinal applications, tissue sealants have been reported to achieve a leak closure success rate of 97.6% [22]. However, stent placement can be complicated by issues such as migration, pressure necrosis, and the requirement for stent removal, while EVAC necessitates multiple procedures under sedation and considerable technical skill, which may restrict broader use in all healthcare contexts. In contrast, endoscopic Histoacryl injection remains a simple, established technique for endoscopists, and its minimally invasive nature, along with low patient discomfort, promotes strong patient adherence.

While the risk of tissue necrosis or foreign body reaction to cyanoacrylate materials has been considered, multiple studies indicate that n-butyl-2-cyanoacrylate is typically safe when administered in controlled amounts [11,23,24,25]. Our study did not encounter procedure-related adverse events such as tissue necrosis, further supporting the safety of this intervention in anastomotic leak management. Furthermore, the treatment success rate in the Histoacryl cohort was 90%, which aligns with or is comparable to rates seen with stent placement and EVAC.

Although our study had a limited sample size, our observations indicated that the primary use of Histoacryl injection was in patients characterized by stable vital signs and leak diameters around 1 cm or smaller. Importantly, in 4 cases with large (7–8 cm) intraperitoneal abscesses, repeated Histoacryl injections (2–3 times) together with antibiotic therapy resulted in successful leak resolution. These results suggest that Histoacryl injection can be a practical option even for patients with substantial intraperitoneal fluid collections, especially where EVAC is not accessible.

In addition to its effectiveness as a primary therapy, Histoacryl injection has also proven beneficial for sealing small residual leaks that persist after reoperation, stent placement, or EVAC. This underscores its value as an adjunct treatment, particularly in complex or refractory cases that do not achieve complete closure with a single intervention. In the combined treatment group, individuals received 2 or more therapies based on their clinical presentation. In all 3 instances, the final closure of residual leaks was accomplished by using Histoacryl injection, highlighting its important role as a supplemental option in staged management.

This study has several limitations. First, it is a retrospective analysis with a relatively small sample size, particularly within each treatment subgroup, which may limit statistical power. Second, this 20-year retrospective study is subject to inherent selection bias, as treatment decisions were made without standardized protocols and relied on the individual discretion of surgeons or endoscopists. Typically, reoperation was reserved for unstable patients, Histoacryl injection was utilized for leaks ≤1 cm, and stenting was selected for larger defects. However, evolving clinical judgment over the study period likely contributed to treatment allocation bias. Third, direct comparison with groups treated exclusively with stents or EVAC was not feasible, as these modalities were only included as part of combination therapy in this analysis.

Despite these limitations, the current findings demonstrate the clinical feasibility and potential utility of endoscopic Histoacryl injection as both a primary and adjunctive intervention for EJL. Further large-scale, prospective multicenter studies are necessary to validate these observations and to establish standardized criteria for optimal patient selection.

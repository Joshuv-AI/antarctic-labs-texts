The critical role of diagnostic accuracy in differentiating uterine dehiscence and placenta accreta spectrum to enhance neonatal outcomes in complex cesarean sections
Authors: Kartika Darma Handayani, Avianita Dewi Kusumawardhani, Anang Endaryanto, Lavenia Kurniawati, Gondo Mastutik, Dina Angelika, Risa Etika, Martono Tri Utomo, Pieter J.J. Sauer, Rozi Aditya Aryananda
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AimThis study aims to analyze the key factors that contribute to adverse neonatal outcomes in complex cesarean sections with a high risk of placenta accreta spectrum (PAS).MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on 454 records collected between January 2018 and December 2023 at a single tertiary hospital, Dr. Soetomo General Hospital, Surabaya, East Java. The PAS diagnosis was confirmed intraoperatively. Clinical outcomes, including gestational age, birth weight, birth length, Apgar score at 5 min, NICU admission, and neonatal complications, were compared between the PAS and uterine dehiscence groups. Adverse neonatal outcome is defined as Apgar score of less than 7 in 5 min. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were employed to identify factors associated with composite adverse neonatal outcomes.ResultsA total of 443 cases with high suspicion of PAS were included in the analysis, of which 383 were confirmed as PAS and the remaining as uterine scar dehiscence. Multivariable binary logistic regression revealed that preterm delivery was significantly associated with an increased risk of composite adverse neonatal outcomes (adjusted odds ratio [aOR] 5.50; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 3.46–8.53; p< 0.001). Furthermore, emergency surgery was identified as a significant risk factor (aOR 2.37; 95% CI: 1.04–4.00; p= 0.001). When comparing PAS and uterine dehiscence cases, the latter group had significantly higher gestational age at delivery (p = 0.002), better 5-minute Apgar scores (p < 0.001), greater birth weight (p < 0.001), longer birth length (p = 0.047), shorter hospital stay (p < 0.001), and a lower incidence of respiratory distress syndrome (p = 0.010).ConclusionPreterm delivery and emergency delivery are primary risk factors contributing to composite adverse neonatal outcomes in pregnancies complicated by high-risk PAS. Early detection, detailed prenatal planning, and targeted clinical management are essential strategies to reduce adverse neonatal outcomes in these high-risk cases.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12884-025-08140-8.

Introduction

Placenta accreta spectrum (PAS), an advanced and complex form of abnormal placentation encountered during cesarean delivery, presents numerous clinical challenges and significantly increases the risk of preterm delivery. Complex cesarean deliveries (CDs) have recently become an emerging topic of discussion, especially due to their association with PAS, as both conditions pose similar management challenges. A complex CD involves higher surgical difficulty or risk, such as in cases of anterior placenta previa, lower uterine segment fibroids, significant maternal comorbidities (e.g., cardiovascular or pulmonary disease, anticoagulation), or challenging obstetric scenarios (e.g., fully dilated cervix with engaged fetal head, fetal malformations, transverse lie) [1]. Pregnancies with multiple previous cesarean sections, anterior placenta previa, and those requiring urgent intervention due to preterm labor or antenatal bleeding are at higher risk of complex CDs [2]. Accurate differentiation between these conditions during prenatal evaluations and intraoperative procedures requires specialized expertise [3].

The increasing incidence of PAS is closely linked to the rising global rates of cesarean sections, a trend particularly evident in Indonesia, where cases have surged but often remain underreported. Maternal morbidity associated with PAS is escalating, as evidenced by an 18-fold increase reported in an Australian population-based study [4]. PAS is strongly linked to severe maternal complications, such as postpartum hemorrhage (PPH), hemorrhagic shock, hysterectomy, multisystem organ failure, and disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) [5–7]. Inadequate surgical techniques and a lack of clinical experience contribute to maternal fatalities related to PAS, especially in resource-limited hospitals [8, 9].

Globally, preterm birth (PTB) rates range from 9.3 to 12.6%, with the highest prevalence in South-East Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa [10]. Approximately 90% of PTBs occur in low- and middle-income countries [11]and Indonesia reports one of the highest rates [10, 11]. PTB is associated with significant neonatal morbidity, including respiratory distress syndrome, cerebral palsy, and developmental delays [12–16].

Given the compounded challenges of PTB and PAS, especially in resource-limited settings, this study aims to analyze the key factors contributing to adverse fetal outcomes in complex cesarean sections with a high risk of PAS.

Patients and methods

Study design and samples recruitment

This is a retrospective cohort analysis conducted between January 2018 and December 2023 at a single tertiary referral center in East Java, Indonesia. Participants were recruited using total sampling, excluding those with incomplete data and intrauterine fetal death. The research received ethical clearance from the Dr. Soetomo Academic General Hospital Ethics Committee in Health Research under the reference number 1875/LOE/301.4.2/I/25 on January 3rd, 2025.

Neonatal outcomes were comprehensively collected, including gestational age at delivery, birth weight, birth length, and Apgar score at 5 min. Cerebrovascular and respiratory complications were also assessed. Cerebrovascular complications included intraventricular hemorrhage, seizures, and hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy. Respiratory complications comprised the use of continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) and/or mechanical ventilation, respiratory distress syndrome (RDS), transient tachypnea of the newborn (TTN), persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (PPHN), and bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD). Neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) admission and neonatal mortality, which was defined as in-hospital death within the first 28 days of life, were also recorded.

Maternal and obstetrics baseline characteristics recorded included age, previous cesarean section, surgical and anesthesia approaches, timing of surgery, and histopathology confirmation. We also included maternal comorbidities such as obesity, maternal anemia, hypertension in pregnancy, and gestational diabetes. The observed maternal outcome was blood loss during surgery.

The primary outcome was composite adverse neonatal outcomes, defined as the occurrence of any of the following: Apgar score less than 5 at 5 min, sepsis, necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC), cerebrovascular complications, respiratory complications, NICU admission, or neonatal mortality. The secondary outcome was adverse neonatal outcome, defined as an Apgar score of less than 7 at 5 min. Baseline maternal characteristics were compared between groups with favorable and adverse neonatal outcomes for the secondary outcome, while multivariable analysis was performed to identify factors independently associated with the primary outcome.

PAS diagnosis and management protocol

In cases of uterine dehiscence, ultrasound evaluation typically reveals lower uterine segment (LUS) remodeling without accompanying uteroplacental vascular remodeling or serosal hypervascularity. By contrast, PAS is characterized by the presence of all three findings. The criteria for LUS remodeling include loss of the clear zone, myometrial thickness of less than 1 mm, and the presence of a placental bulge [17, 18].

Clinical confirmation of PAS was obtained intraoperatively based on evidence of abnormal uterine attachment of the placenta during laparotomy [7]. Uterine-scar dehiscence was diagnosed when the LUS appeared markedly thinned and translucent, with a visible placental bulge but without serosal hypervascularity, and when the placenta had completely separated from the surrounding area of compromised myometrium [18]. PAS cases were classified according to the FIGO clinical classification identified at laparotomy. For this study, PAS was grouped into low‐grade and high‐grade categories. Low‐grade PAS (FIGO Grade 1) was defined by a grossly normal LUS with minimal or no serosal hypervascularity, a positive “dimple sign,” and failure of placental separation. High‐grade PAS (FIGO Grades 2 and 3) was characterized by a pronounced placental bulge, with or without invasion into adjacent visceral organs, along with marked serosal hypervascularity [19]. Cases of uterine dehiscence with non-adherent placentas and complete placental separation after vascular control were included in this study [3]. Cases with a topography located above the bladder trigone, specifically involving the supero-anterior uterine segment or upper parametrium, were considered suitable for one-step conservative surgery whereas PAS in the lower bladder trigone was an indication for cesarean hysterectomy [20]. One step conservative surgery involves en bloc resection of the myometrium affected by PAS along with the placenta, followed by uterine reconstruction [20].

Statistical analysis

Data were collated using Microsoft Excel (Microsoft Cooperation, Redmond, WA, USA) and exported to IBM SPSS statistical software version 29 (IBM, Chicago, IL, USA). The distribution of continuous variables was explored using either the Shapiro–Wilk or Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests. Depending on the distribution of the data, either the independent t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test was used to compare continuous variables where appropriate. Frequency data with percentages and comparisons of categorical variables across categories were performed using the χ2 (chi-square test). If a counting variable had a theoretical number < 5 in each cell (> 25% of the cells), the p-value was obtained using Fisher’s exact probability test. Univariate and multivariable binary logistic regression analyses were used to identify the factors independently associated with composite adverse neonatal outcomes. Significant variables from the univariate analysis were included in the multivariable regression analysis. A probability value of p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all analyses.

Result

A total of 572 pregnant women diagnosed with placenta accreta spectrum (PAS) based on clinical presentation and ultrasound findings between January 2018 and December 2023 were identified. We traced the neonatal medical records and retrieved 454 records. However, eleven records were excluded due to incomplete medical records. Figure 1 illustrates the complete algorithm of the sample recruitment process. Among the 443 high-risk PAS cases, 383 (86.5%) were confirmed as PAS on histopathology. The distribution of PAS classifications included placenta increta in 185 cases (41.8%), placenta accreta in 130 cases (29.3%), and placenta percreta in 68 cases (15.3%). Of the 243 preterm births (defined as less than 37 weeks), 177 (72.8%) occurred between 34 and 36 weeks of gestation. Emergency surgery was performed in 89 cases (20.1%), while the remaining cases underwent planned or elective surgery. In the case of planned surgeries, procedures were initiated 24 h after admission, and patients received a full course of antenatal corticosteroids for fetal lung maturation. General anesthesia was administered in the majority of cases (405/443, 91.4%) to anticipate the risk of massive blood loss and prevent intraoperative conversion. The in-hospital mortality rate for this cohort was 10.6%, while neonatal mortality during the first 28 days of life was 9.7%.

Discussion

Principal findings

Our study found that preterm delivery and emergency cesarean delivery were the most significant factors contributing to composite adverse neonatal outcomes in high-risk PAS cases, with adjusted odds ratios of 2.37 (95% CI: 1.04–4.00, p = 0.001) and 5.50 (95% CI: 3.46–8.53, p < 0.001), respectively. In comparison to PAS cases, uterine dehiscence was associated with a lower risk of adverse neonatal outcomes, likely due to a significantly greater gestational age at delivery (median [range]: 37 [36–38] weeks vs. 36 [34–37] weeks, p = 0.002) (See Supplementary Table 1). These findings were obtained from a large-scale study involving neonates born to mothers with high-risk PAS conducted at a specialized PAS referral center in East Java, Indonesia.

Results in the context of what is known

Neonates born via emergency cesarean section faced numerous challenges compared to those delivered via planned cesarean, such as lower median birth weight and a higher probability of delayed delivery [21]. Emergency cesarean deliveries were associated with critical neonatal outcomes, including lower Apgar scores, higher NICU admission rates, and increased risk of stillbirth and neonatal death. Delayed decision-to-delivery intervals worsened these outcomes, with neonates being 2.6 times more likely to have low first-minute Apgar scores compared to those delivered within 30 minutes [22]. Early evidence has shown that the abnormal placentation linked with PAS significantly restricts blood flow, depleting the crucial oxygen reserves available to the fetus during the initial minutes from the onset of surgery to the evacuation, thereby potentially leading to detrimental neonatal outcomes [23]. According to Morlando and Collins (2020), pregnancies affected by placenta accreta expose the fetus to significant endogenous stress through hypoxia and inflammation [24]. The hypoxia stems from improper placental implantation, which hampers proper oxygen supply and exchange. This condition is further aggravated by protein dysregulation due to inflammatory processes in mothers with PAS [24]. A further explanation for adverse favorable neonatal outcomes observed in PAS cases is that the required uterine incision may extend into the placental area, potentially compromising uteroplacental blood flow during the crucial early phase of the surgery [25].

A previous study reported that general anesthesia was significantly associated with adverse neonatal outcomes in univariate analyses; however, multivariate logistic regression demonstrated that it was not an independent predictor of these outcomes [26]. This finding highlights the importance of neonatal resuscitation in reducing morbidity potentially associated with general anesthesia [26]. A prior study by Seif et al. reported a higher incidence of neonatal respiratory complications in the PAS group. However, after adjusting for gestational age, there was no significant difference in the odds of neonatal respiratory complications between the PAS and non-PAS groups [27]. This may suggest that the significant differences in the incidence of respiratory distress syndrome observed in our cohort are likely a result of the considerable disparity in gestational age between the PAS and non-PAS groups.

Clinical implications

Placenta previa in the context of uterine dehiscence and PAS presents similar maternal risks and is managed using comparable clinical protocols [28]. However, identifying uterine dehiscence remains critical due to its distinct impact on neonatal outcomes. While the standard recommendation for delivery in PAS cases is between 34 and 37 weeks of gestation [29], anticipation of uterine dehiscence may support postponing delivery until 37 to 38 weeks to reduce the risk of neonatal morbidity.

General anesthesia is not considered a major contributor to adverse neonatal outcomes, largely due to the protective role of early neonatal resuscitation. However, the routine application of combined anesthesia is preferred to further reduce any residual effects associated with general anesthesia [29]. In emergency settings, the lack of sufficient time for fetal lung maturation contributes significantly to increased neonatal complications, including higher NICU admissions, lower Apgar scores, and greater requirements for resuscitation and respiratory support.

Research implications

Identifying clinical and imaging features that predict the need for emergency surgery in high-risk PAS cases is crucial to improve maternal and neonatal outcomes. Prenatal diagnosis of PAS significantly reduces the likelihood of emergency cesarean delivery by approximately 63%31. A study by Hanulikova et al., involving 182 women, further identified antenatal bleeding as a predictor that nearly triples the risk of emergency delivery, whereas placenta located over a uterine scar decreases this risk by approximately 62%, though the overall predictive accuracy was moderate [30]. Thiravit et al. further showed that the placental bulge sign offers high diagnostic accuracy for severe PAS, with 85.5% using ultrasound and 90.3% using MRI, and is independently associated with severe disease on both modalities [31]. Previous studies also suggest that emergency surgery and unexpected intraoperative bleeding occur more frequently in PAS patients exhibiting Grade 3 intracervical hypervascularity on ultrasound, highlighting the importance of ultrasound evaluation in anticipating high-risk scenarios [20]. Additionally, the multicenter ADoPAD cohort found placental thickness and cervical length shorter than 25 mm to be predictors of emergency cesarean deliveries, although short cervical length alone had limited diagnostic accuracy [32]. Emerging evidence further suggests that proinflammatory biomarkers linked to dysbiosis in the placenta and amniotic cavity might increase the risk of emergency surgery and adverse neonatal outcomes. These biomarkers offer promising potential for future research into prenatal probiotics as a preventive strategy to improve neonatal health [33].

Strengths and limitations

Our study benefits from a large sample size and intraoperative confirmation of all PAS diagnoses, supported by multidisciplinary management and comprehensive reporting of severe neonatal complications. However, the retrospective design introduces inherent risks of incomplete documentation and unmeasured confounding, including variations in antenatal care, maternal comorbidities, intraoperative management, and neonatal resuscitation practices. Selection bias is also possible, as the cohort reflects cases referred to and managed in a single Indonesian tertiary care center with high-resource facilities and specialized expertise. These contextual factors should be considered when interpreting the results. While our findings offer important insight into PAS management and neonatal outcomes in a well-resourced tertiary environment, their applicability to other settings may be constrained, particularly in healthcare systems with different infrastructures, referral pathways, and multidisciplinary capacities. In such contexts, these strategies should be adapted to align with local resources and clinical realities. Finally, the absence of certain key variables, including arterial pH, may have restricted the depth of our outcome assessment.

Conclusion

In summary, our study demonstrates that preterm delivery and emergency cesarean delivery are the primary risk factors contributing to composite adverse neonatal outcomes in pregnancies complicated by high-risk PAS. Early prenatal diagnosis, particularly in distinguishing uterine dehiscence from PAS, is essential to optimize neonatal prognosis by safely extending pregnancies to full-term or at least 37 weeks of gestation. The identification of predictive clinical and imaging markers for emergency surgery can further improve neonatal outcomes by facilitating adequate surgical planning and timely intervention. Strategies emphasizing early detection, meticulous prenatal planning, and targeted clinical management are therefore crucial to reducing adverse neonatal outcomes associated with high-risk PAS cases.

Clinical significance and underlying mechanism of long non‐coding RNA SNHG12 in lower extremity deep venous thrombosis
Authors: Shun Xiao, Chong Wang, Yongxin Li, Kun Zhang, Xuefei Jiao, Zonggang Zhao, Mingjin Guo, Bing Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractD‐dimer is widely used in the diagnosis of deep vein thrombosis (DVT), but the specificity is low. The study examined the diagnostic value of long non‐coding RNA (lncRNA) SNHG12 in DVT, and preliminarily discussed its mechanism. SNHG12 levels were detected in 200 elderly fracture patients via RT‐qPCR, including 38 DVTs. Logistic regression analysis and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve were applied for diagnostic value evaluation. HUVECs were used for function study. Cell proliferation, migration, apoptosis, release of inflammatory cytokines, and adhesion factors were detected. Student's t test and one‐way ANOVA were applied for data comparison between two or among three or more groups. Correlation analysis of indicators was completed via Pearson's correlation analysis. Bioinformatics analysis predicted the target miRNAs and genes of SNHG12, with GO and KEGG for the function enrichment. It was found that SNHG12 was at low expression in DVT patients, and negatively correlated with D‐dimer concentration (r = −0.535). SNHG12 and D‐dimer were independent influence factors related to the development of DVT. SNHG12 and D‐dimer combination had the best performance in DVT diagnosis. In HUVECs, SNHG12 promoted cell proliferation and migration and restricted the release of inflammatory cytokines and adhesion factors, but these influences were counteracted by miR‐424‐5p. A total of 208 overlapping target genes of miR‐424‐5p were identified, and their function was enriched in cellular cycle and senescence. PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway was the most significant pathway based on KEGG results. In conclusion, SNHG12 had good diagnostic potential for DVT combined with D‐dimer. SNHG12 maintains vascular endothelial cell function by acting as a competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) for miR‐424‐5p.

INTRODUCTION

Elderly fracture has a high incidence and osteoporosis is the primary risk factor, among which the incidence of pelvic and lower extremity fractures can reach 46.1%–75.5%. 1 Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) of the lower extremity is a common complication after fracture surgery, especially in elderly patients. 2 DVT is a disease of venous return disorder, that tends to occur in the lower extremities. 3 DVT refers to abnormal blood clotting in deep veins, forming thrombus, blocking venous lumen, and causing venous return obstruction. 2 DVT is commonly seen in patients with postoperative, postpartum, fracture and long‐term bed rest. 4 The clinical manifestations were limb pain and swelling. The causative factors of DVT include slow blood flow, venous wall damage and hypercoagulability. 5 Once a clot in the deep vein of the lower limb breaks off, it can lead to serious complications. 6 The acute stage of DVT can cause femoral white swelling, bruising, shock, pulmonary embolism (PTE) and so on. The chronic stage of DVT may cause post‐deep vein thrombosis syndrome (PTS). 7 Pulmonary embolism is the most serious complication, which endangers the patient's life. 8 Lower extremity venography is the gold standard for diagnosis of DVT of lower extremity, but it is relatively invasive and has certain risks. 9 At present, lower extremity venous ultrasonography is the first choice for DVT of lower extremity. 10 Recently, serology has also been applied for the diagnosis of DVT, such as D‐dimer. 11 D‐dimer monitoring is widely used in clinical diagnosis of DVT because of its convenience, simple operation, repeatability and non‐invasive characteristics. 4 However, many factors such as age, sex, trauma degree and inflammatory response can cause the elevation of D‐dimer, which weakens its specificity.

Recent literature has widely reported the expression alternation of long non‐coding RNA (lncRNA) in various diseases, and the diagnostic value of lncRNAs has been determined. 12 In DVT patients, several lncRNAs have been identified, such as NEAT1, Sirt1‐AS and MALAT1, 13 , 14 , 15 and their role in DVT is examined. SNHG12 is an endothelial‐enriched lncRNA; it can mediate vascular senescence via reducing DNA damage thus relieving atherosclerotic lesion formation. 16 The downregulation of SNHG12 has been detected in aortic primary endothelial cells of Ang II‐induced hypertensive mice. 17 The rescue experiments determine that SNHG12 overexpression relieves vascular endothelial injury induced by hypertension. 17 Moreover, SNHG12 dysregulation is also reported to be related to blood flow. In femoral artery ligation (FAL) mice models, SNHG12 expression is decreased accompanied by limb ischemia, which recovers to normal as the blood flow returns. 18 Therefore, the role of SNHG12 in DVT arouses our research interest.

In the present study, the expression profile of lncRNA SNHG12 was examined in DVT patients to reveal its potential roles. Moreover, in vitro experiments were performed to determine the role of SNHG12 in the disease pathogenesis, and the underlying mechanism was also preliminary discussed.

METHODS

Participants

A total of 200 elderly fracture patients who were admitted to the Affiliated Hospital of Qingdao University from October 2021 to May 2023 were recruited in the current study. Patients met the following criteria: (1) Age ≥60 years old; (2) cases suffered from fracture which were diagnosed based on X‐ray examination; (3) fracture time <2 weeks; Did not receive low molecular weight heparin anticoagulant therapy between injury before admission; (4) had no history of DVT before; (5) no pathologic fractures were identified; (6) no organ dysfunction, including heart, lung, liver, and kidney. Patients who had generalized infection, malignant tumors, or severe autoimmune diseases were excluded from the study. The study was designed under the approval of the Ethic Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Qingdao University.

Age, sex, cause of injury, time from injury to hospital admission, fracture type, and past medical history were collected. Preoperative color Doppler ultrasound was performed on both lower limb veins and iliac vessels. DVT was determined according to the typical clinical symptoms and the ultrasonograph findings. 19 Immunoturbidimetry was performed for the determination of the serum D‐dimer concentration at admission. All patients were divided into DVT group and control group based on the results of lower limb venous ultrasonography at admission.

Serum sample collection

Four milliliters of fasting venous blood was gained from each subject in the morning and centrifugated at 1500g for 10 min to separate the upper serum. The collected serum samples were reserved at −80°C.

Cell culture and transfection

Human vascular endothelial cell (HUVEC; catalog number: PCS‐100‐010) was gained from American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) and maintained in Dulbecco's modified eagle medium (DMEM; catalog number: C0891‐100ml) supplemented with 1% penicillin/streptomycin (catalog number: 15070063) and 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; catalog number: 26140079) in an incubator at 37°C and 5% CO2.

To mediate SNHG12 expression, the overexpression plasmid (pcDNA‐SNHG12) and small interfering RNA of SNHG12 (si‐SNHG12) were synthesized by GenePharm (Shanghai, China). In addition, miR‐424‐5p mimic (catalog number: miR10001341‐1‐5), its negative control (mimic NC, catalog number: miR1N0000001‐1‐5), miR‐424‐5p inhibitor (catalog number: miR2170515112706‐1‐10) and inhibitor NC (catalog number: miR2N0000001‐1‐5) oligonucleotide fragments were purchased from RiboBio (Guangzhou, China) to regulate miR‐424‐5p levels. Cells were collected for transfection when reached the logarithmic growth phase, and lipofectamine 3000 (catalog number: L3000015, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Invitrogen) was applied for the cell transfection.

RT‐qPCR

Total RNA was extracted from serum and cell samples using Trizol LS reagent (Catalog number: 15596026, Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA), whose concentration and purity were detected using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer. Then 500 ng of isolated RNA was reversed transcribed into cDNA using the TaqMan Reverse Transcription assay kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, catalog number: 4366596). The following PCR reaction system was configured including the SYBR Green Real‐Time Master Mix Kit (Bio‐Rad, catalog number: 1525271), cDNA, and primer, and the reaction was performed on the PRISM 7500 sequence detection system. The following thermocycling conditions were used for the PCR: initial denaturation at 94°C for 2 min, followed by 40 cycles of 94°C for 20 s, 60°C for 34 s. Finally, the mRNA levels were calculated via the 2−ΔΔCt method, with U6 and GAPDH as internal references.

Cell counting kit‐8 (CCK‐8) assay

HUVECs were inoculated into 96‐well plates. Ten microliters of CCK‐8 reagent (catalog number: CK04, Dojindo Molecular Technologies, Japan) was mixed with 90 μL of DMEM, and the mixture was added at 0, 24, 48, and 72 h, respectively. After incubation for 1 h, the optical density (OD) value was tested at 450 nm.

Cell transwell assay

The resuspended cells in each group were seeded into the upper chamber of the Transwell. Five hundred microliters of DMEM (catalog number: C0891‐100ml) containing 10% FBS (catalog number: 26140079) was added to the lower chamber. After incubation for 24 h, the migrated cells in the lower chamber were stained with crystal violet. Twenty minutes later, the cells were counted using a light microscope.

Flow cytometry assay

The cell apoptosis was evaluated using the Annexin‐FITC/propidium iodide (PI) kit (catalog number: BD556547, BD Biosciences, USA) via flow cytometry. HUVECs were firstly digested with EDTA‐free trypsin (catalog number: 15050065, Gibco, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), and centrifuged to remove the supernatant. After washing twice with PBS (catalog number: C0332A, Beyotime, China), 500 μL of binding buffer was supplemented. Then 5 μL Annexinv‐FITC and 5 μL of PI solution were added and incubated in the dark for 5 min. Finally, the apoptotic cells were counted by flow cytometry.

Enzyme‐linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

The HUVEC culture supernatant was collected from each group, and the protein levels of inflammatory cytokines including tumor necrosis factor (TNF)‐α (catalog number: E‐EL‐H0109), IL (interleukin) −6 (IL‐6; catalog number: E‐EL‐H6156), and IL‐1β and adhesion factors (catalog number: E‐EL‐H0149), namely P‐selectin (SELP; catalog number: E‐EL‐H6180), vascular cell adhesion molecule −1 (VCAM‐1; catalog number: E‐EL‐H5587), and intercellular adhesion molecule‐1 (ICAM‐1; catalog number: E‐EL‐H6114), were tested using corresponding human ELISA kits (Elabscience, Wuhan, China).

Prediction of downstream targets of SNHG12

To identify DVT‐related miRNAs binding with SNHG12, original sequencing data of DVT‐associated miRNAs were downloaded from GSE173461 in the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database, and the differential lncRNAs in DVT patients were identified by GEO2R. The online databases of ENCORI and LncBook 3.0 were applied for the miRNAs prediction binding with SNHG12. Then the Venn diagram analyzed the overlapping miRNAs in the three databases.

Luciferase reporter assay

The SNHG12 wild type (SNHG12‐WT) and mutated type (SNHG12‐MUT) sequences were subcloned into the pmirGLO DLR vector (catalog number: E1330, Promega, USA) to establish SNHG12‐WT and SNHG12‐MUT plasmids. The vectors were co‐transfected into HUVECs with miR‐425‐5p mimic (catalog number: miR10001341‐1‐5) or inhibitor (catalog number: miR2170515112706‐1‐10), respectively. After incubation for 48 h, the luciferase activity was detected using the dual luciferase detection kit (Yeason, Shanghai, PRC).

Prediction of miRNA target genes and the functional analysis

The online databases TargetScan (Total context++ score < −0.30), miRDB (Target Score > 80), and miRTargetBase (interaction score > 0.9) were applied for the target gene prediction of mIR‐424‐5p. Then the overlapping genes in the three databases were presented via Venn diagram. Then gene ontology (GO) analysis was performed for the function enrichment, containing three aspects, namely biological progress (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF). The Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analysis was done for the pathway enrichment, and the top 10 enriched pathways were presented.

Statistical analysis

The data of the present study were statistically analyzed using SPSS 26.0 and GraphPad Prism 9.0 software. Measurement data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) which were compared among three or more groups via one‐way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey's post hoc test. Student t‐test was used to make a comparison between two groups, such as SNHG12 level comparison between DVT and control group. Chi‐square test was applied for the count data comparison. Correlation analysis of indicators was completed via Pearson's correlation analysis. A binary logistic regression model was used to analyze the independent risk factors affecting preoperative DVT, and the results were presented by odds ratio (OR). p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The area under the curve (AUC) of D‐dimer in predicting DVT occurrence was calculated by the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, the cut‐off value and the corresponding sensitivity and specificity were calculated. p < 0.05 indicated that the difference was statistically significant.

Ethics approval

This study was performed in line with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Approval was granted by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Qingdao University.

In DVT group, a higher percentage of patients had long time to hospitalization, high‐energy trauma, high American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) classification, and a large number of complications (p < 0.05). Moreover, the D‐dimer concentration was significantly high in the DVT group in comparison with the control group (p < 0.001).

ROC of D‐dimer combined with SNHG12 for DVT

According to the qRT‐PCR results, significantly downregulated levels of SNHG12 were detected in the serum of DVT patients (Figure 1a, p < 0.001). Moreover, all participants were grouped based on their age, sex, and ethnicity to examine the expression changes of serum SNHG12 between different groups. As shown in Figure 1b, it was found that serum SNHG12 levels had no significant difference between groups based on age, sex, and ethnicity. Then, Pearson's correlation analysis illustrated the negative correlation of SNHG12 with D‐dimer concentration (r = −0.535, p < 0.001, Figure 1c). Then multiple logistic regression analysis was performed for the identification of factors related to the development of DVT, and indicators with significant differences in Table 1 were included in the logistic regression model. As illustrated in Figure 1d and Table 2, SNHG12 (OR = 6.118, 95% CI = 2.384–15.699, p < 0.001) and D‐dimer (OR = 5.088, 95% CI = 2.106–12.292, p < 0.001) were independent influence factors related to the development of DVT. Thus, the diagnostic values of both SNHG1 and D‐dimer were evaluated. As ROC illustrated, the sensitivity and specificity of D‐dimer in identifying DVT from controls were 81.60% and 81.50%, respectively with the AUC of 0.849 (Figure 1e). The AUC based on SNHG12 expression was 0.924, with the sensitivity and specificity of 86.80% and 83.30%, respectively (Figure 1f). When combining D‐dimer and SNHG12, the AUC was 0.957, and the sensitivity and specificity reached 97.40% and 88.90% (Figure 1g).

Role of SNHG12 in vascular endothelial cell function

Cell transfection was done to regulate SNHG12 levels in HUVECs. Figure 2a showed the transfection efficiency; after pcDNA‐SNHG12 transfection, SNHG12 levels were significantly elevated, which were downregulated by si‐SNHG12 transfection (p < 0.001). As Figure 2b,c demonstrated, SNHG12 overexpression promoted cell proliferation and migration, whereas SNHG12 knockdown suppressed the cell viability and migration. Similarly, the cell apoptosis was suppressed by SNHG12 overexpression and promoted by SNHG12 knockdown (Figure 2d). The inflammatory cytokines concentration was shown in Figure 2e; limited release of TNF‐α, IL‐6 and IL‐1β was detected in cells transfected with pcDNA‐SNHG12, while over‐release of inflammatory cytokines was tested in cells transfected with si‐SNHG1. Similar findings were observed for adhesion factors, SNHG12 overexpression restricted the release of SELP, VCAM‐1 and ICAM‐1, while SNHG12 knockdown exhibited the opposite effect (Figure 2f).

Downstream target miRNAs of SNHG12 and their expression pattern in DVT

ENCORI and LncBook 3.0 were applied for target miRNAs identification of lncRNA SNHG12, and GSE173461 dataset was used for the locking of DVT‐related miRNAs. As displayed in Figure 3a, seven overlapping miRNAs were identified in the three databases. Then the expression levels of the seven overlapping miRNAs were examined in the enrolled DVT patients (Figure 3b–h). Significantly downregulated levels of miR‐199b‐5p, miR‐150‐5p, and miR‐197‐3p were detected in the serum of DVT patients (p < 0.001). But miR‐129‐2‐3p and miR‐494‐3p expression showed no significant difference between the control and DVT groups (p > 0.05). Two miRNAs were at high expression in DVT patients, namely miR‐642a‐3p and miR‐424‐5p (p < 0.05). And miR‐424‐5p was selected for further exploration because of its prominent expression change (Figure 3e, p < 0.001).

Target relationship verification of miR‐424‐5p and SNHG12

Figure 4a shows the binding sites between SNHG12 and miR‐424‐5p. Based on the luciferase reporter assay illustrated, miR‐424‐5p mimic transfection led to the inhibition of luciferase activity, while miR‐424‐5p knockdown enhanced the cells' luciferase activity (Figure 4b, p < 0.001). Based on Pearson's correlation analysis, a negative association was detected for miR‐424‐5p and SNHG12 levels in the serum of all elderly fracture patients (Figure 4c, r = −0.608, p < 0.001). In HUVECs, the negative correlation of miR‐424‐5p and SNHG12 expression was also observed. As seen in Figure 4d, SNHG12 overexpression contributed to the downregulation of miR‐424‐5p, while SNHG12 knockdown led to the upregulation of miR‐424‐5p (p < 0.001).

miR‐424‐5p reversed the role of SNHG12 in vascular endothelial cell function

As shown in Figure 5a, miR‐424‐5p inhibitor transfection backspins the promotion of SNHG12 knockdown in miR‐424‐5p expression (p < 0.05). Figure 5b,c illustrated the inhibitory role of si‐SNHG12 in cell viability and migration, which were reversed by miR‐424‐5p inhibitor. Similarly, the enhanced cell apoptosis induced by si‐SNHG12 was restrained by miR‐424‐5p inhibitor (Figure 5d). Likewise, the promotion of si‐SNHG12 in both inflammatory cytokines (Figure 5e) and adhesion factors (Figure 5f) was counteracted by miR‐424‐5p inhibitor.

Functional and pathway enrichment analysis of the target genes of miR‐424‐5p

Based on databases of TargetScan, miRDB, and miRTargetBase, a total of 208 overlapping genes were identified, which was shown in the Venn diagram (Figure 6a). The top 10 enriched functions in terms of BP, CC, and MF were presented in Figure 6b based on the Go analysis, mainly including G1/S transition of mitotic cell, cycle embryonic limb morphogenesis, protein kinase complex, transferase complex, transferring phosphorus‐containing groups, active binding, SMAD binding, et al. According to the KEGG analysis results, cell cycle, p53 signaling pathway, PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway, and cellular senescence were mainly enriched (Figure 6c). Furthermore, the expression changes of PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway were examined in HUVECs. It was found that si‐SNHG12 significantly promoted the expression of p‐PI3K and p‐AKT, but the changes were reversed by miR‐424‐5p inhibitor (Figure 6d). It was concluded that SNHG12 knockdown activated PI3K‐Akt signaling, while miR‐424‐5p mitigates this effect.

DISCUSSION

DVT is a common complication in fracture patients, especially in the elderly. 20 The inflammatory factor infiltration and vascular endothelial injury caused by fracture put the body in a hypercoagulable state, so it is easy to develop DVT. 5 In the current study, a total of 200 elderly fracture patients were enrolled; among them, 38 cases developed into DVT, supporting the high occurrence of DVT in elderly fracture patients. 21 D‐dimer is a representative molecular marker of fibrinolytic system activity change in the process of thrombosis, which can reflect the changes of coagulation and fibrinolytic process. 22 In recent years, the diagnostic value of D‐dimer in thrombosis has attracted much attention. Studies have shown that the level of D‐dimer in patients with lower extremity fractures increased significantly in the early postoperative period. 2 D‐dimer levels were detected in the present study, and significantly increased levels of D‐dimer were detected in DVT patients compared with non‐DVT ones. Moreover, its diagnostic value for DVT was evaluated, and a good performance of serum D‐dimer was detected for differentiating DVT from elderly fracture patients with the sensitivity and specificity of 81.6% and 81.5%, respectively. The specificity of D‐dimer in the diagnosis of DVT is reported to be relatively low, as its level varies greatly depending on factors such as age. 21 However, the diagnostic sensitivity and specificity of D‐dimer in this study were comparable, which may be due to the fact that all patients included in our study were elderly patients. Interestingly, a significantly negative association was detected between D‐dimer and serum SNHG12 levels, which were aberrantly expressed in DVT patients. So the role of SNHG12 in DVT attracts our attention.

SNHG12 is an lncRNA that is enriched in endothelial cells. 16 In human cancers, SNHG12 has been identified to be potential biomarker for disease diagnosis. 23 , 24 In light of the differential expression of SNHG12 in DVT patients, its clinical value was focused on. Based on the logistic regression analysis results, SNHG12 was determined to be independently related to the occurrence of DVT. Furthermore, its diagnostic value for DVT was assessed via ROC. It can be seen that serum SNHG12 can distinguish DVT from elderly fracture patients with the sensitivity of 86.8% and the specificity of 83.3%. In comparison with D‐dimer, the diagnostic specificity was improved to some extent. Moreover, the combination of D‐dimer and SNHG12 led to an obvious improvement for both diagnostic sensitivity and specificity. Therefore, the combined application of multiple indicators has a higher diagnostic value for DVT and is worthy of clinical use. In addition, all participants were grouped based on their age, sex, and ethnicity, and SNHG12 levels were compared between different groups, and the results demonstrated that age, sex, and ethnicity did not affect the levels of SNHG12. Moreover, the time between injury and hospital admission was recorded, and it was found that DVT occurred more frequently in patients within 24 h of injury to hospital admission. The cause may be immobilization of the affected limb, failure to take anticoagulation, and physical preventive measures during the period from injury to hospital admission. However, the cases were not followed up after surgery, which should be included in future studies. Besides, it is realized that SNHG12 is altered in several diseases, such as various human cancers. 23 How to distinguish these diseases is not involved in this study, which needs to be further explained in future studies.

Vascular wall injury, blood flow change, and abnormal blood composition are the three major factors of DVT. 25 The vascular wall injury mainly refers to the injury of vascular endothelial cells. 13 In the present study, HUVECs were applied for the cell function study. According to the cell experiments, SNHG12 was determined to promote vascular endothelial cell proliferation, and migration, while inhibiting cell apoptosis. On the contrary, SNHG12 knockdown suppressed vascular endothelial cell proliferation, and migration and facilitated cell apoptosis. Vascular endothelial cells can secrete vasodilator and contractile factors, and their dynamic balance can maintain the stability of vascular base tension. 26 P‐selectin (SELP), vascular cell adhesion molecule −1 (VCAM‐1) and intercellular adhesion molecule‐1 (ICAM‐1) are involved in the activation of endothelial cell dysfunction, which has been widely used for the evaluation of endothelial cell dysfunction. 27 As expected, SNHG12 overexpression inhibited the release of adhesion factors, while SNHG12 inhibition exerted the opposite effect. Moreover, the release of inflammatory cytokines was also suppressed by SNHG12 overexpression. All findings determined the protective role of SNHG12 against vascular endothelial cell dysfunction.

LncRNAs function in cells through a competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) mechanism. 28 In cells, lncRNA acts as a miRNA sponge, thereby relieving the inhibition of miRNA on target genes and increasing the expression level of target genes. 28 In the current study, the downstream miRNAs sponged by SNHG12 were predicted via the bioinformatics analysis. A total of seven miRNAs were overlapped in three datasets, and their expression in DVT patients was examined. Finally, miR‐424‐5p was focused on because of its prominent expression in the serum of DVT cases. Of course, the target relationship between SNHG12 and miR‐424‐5p was verified via luciferase reporter assay. Recent studies have suggested that miR‐424‐5p plays a negative role in vascular endothelial cell function. In cardiovascular disease, miR‐424‐5p overexpression can accelerate oxygen and glucose deprivation (OGD)‐induced apoptosis of HUVECs through mediating cellular autophagy. 29 In human cancers, miR‐424‐5p shows great capabilities on HUVECs' migration and angiogenesis. 30 According to the current rescue experiments, miR‐424‐5p downregulation reversed the protective role of SNHG12 against vascular endothelial cell dysregulation. It was concluded that SNHG12 may exert a beneficial role in the development of DVT through maintaining endothelial cell function via targeting miR‐424‐5p.

Furthermore, functional and pathway enrichment analysis was accomplished via Go and KEGG pathway analysis, in order to examine the potential underlying molecular mechanisms of miR‐424‐5p target genes. The GO enrichment analysis highlighted the role of miR‐424‐5p targets may have in cell cycle, protein kinase complex, transferase complex, transferring phosphorus‐containing groups, active binding, SMAD binding. The findings suggested that SNHG12/miR‐425‐5p axis may affect the occurrence and development of DVT in several ways, mainly including cell cycle and cellular senescence. Moreover, PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway was extracted to be the most significant pathway based on the results from KEGG pathway analysis. Furthermore, the cell experiment results determined that SNHG12 knockdown activated PI3K‐Akt signaling, while miR‐424‐5p mitigates this effect. The PI3K/Akt signaling pathway is one of the classical signaling pathways of intracellular signaling, and is involved in a variety of physiological processes. 31 It regulates cell survival, metastasis, and metabolism, and plays a role in angiogenesis and recruitment of inflammatory factors. 32 PI3K‐Akt is known to be one of the important signal transduction pathways in platelets and has been proven to be a reliable target for antithrombotic therapy. Su et al. have applied an alkaloid to block PI3K signal transduction and found that platelet aggregation and thrombosis are inhibited, which further confirmed the potential value of the PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway in the treatment of thrombotic diseases. 20 Therefore, it was speculated that PI3K‐Akt signaling may be involved in the regulatory role of SNHG12/miR‐424‐5p in the development of DVT. Of course, it requires further verification.

In conclusion, the current results illustrated that serum SNHG12 had the potential to diagnose lower limb DVT, and its combination with D‐dimer can improve the diagnostic sensitivity and specificity of DVT. Functionally, SNHG12 maintains vascular endothelial cell function by acting as a ceRNA for miR‐424‐5p. The present findings introduced a new potential non‐invasive biomarker for the diagnosis of DVT, and provided a new idea for exploring the pathogenesis and treatment of DVT.

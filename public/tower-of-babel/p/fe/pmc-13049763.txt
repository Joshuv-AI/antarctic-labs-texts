The relationship between perceived organizational support and presenteeism among pediatric nurses in China: professional identity plays a mediating role
Authors: Meiye Li, Renchang Chen, Qing Liu, Manzhou Yang, Baoqi Li, Hui Li, Zaohong Yan, Qunkai Wang, Runguo Gao, QianQian Gao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNurses have become one of the groups with a high incidence of presenteeism. To date, little is known about the current state of presenteeism among pediatric nurses and the underlying mechanisms linking perceived organizational support to presenteeism. This study aims to examine the correlation between perceived organizational support and presenteeism and to test the mediating role of professional identity in this relationship among Chinese pediatric nurses using a cross-sectional design.MethodsThis research investigated 485 pediatric nurses from three tertiary hospitals in Weifang City, Shandong Province, China. Data analyses were conducted using independent sample t-tests, Pearson correlation, one-way analysis of variance, multivariate linear regression, and the Process 4.0 macro.ResultsThe total score of presenteeism was (14.91 ± 5.05). Correlation analysis showed that perceived organizational support(r=-0.307, P<0.001), professional identity(r = 0.344, P<0.001)and presenteeism showed a negative correlation. Regression analysis showed that nurses’ children’s situation (β = 0.153, t = 3.122, P = 0.002) had a significant positive effect on hidden absenteeism, and the presence of an administrative position (β=-0.103, t=-2.149, P = 0.032), instrumental support (t=-2.650, P = 0.008), and perceived professional benefits (β=-0.023, t=-2.501, P = 0.013) had a significant negative effect. The mediation analysis indicated that professional identity (Effect=-0.076, 95%CI=[-0.297, -0.017]), partially mediated the relationship between perceived organizational support and presenteeism, with the mediating effect accounting for 39.2%.ConclusionThe findings demonstrate that professional identity partially mediates the effect of perceived organizational support on presenteeism. Interventions aimed at providing tangible instrumental support and enhancing perceptions of professional benefits could be effective strategies for mitigating presenteeism among pediatric nurses.

Introduction

Presenteeism, or attendanceism, also known as invisible absenteeism, is part of impaired health productivity. It refers to the behavior of employees who persist in working despite poor physical and mental health, which leads to inefficiency as well as impaired productivity [1]. The importance of continuously promoting the construction of a healthy China is self-evident as it occupies a large part of the report of the Twentieth Party Congress. Hospitals, as essential institutions for life-saving and health promotion, should not overlook the nurses who play a crucial role in prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation within their staff [2].

Relevant studies have shown that due to the unique working environment and nature of the job, medical staff are particularly vulnerable to occupational burnout and presenteeism [3, 4]. One study found that nurses are four times more likely to experience presenteeism compared to other professions [5]. Additionally, in Swedish hospitals, the incidence of attendance problems was 49% for registered nurses and 47% for assistant nurses [6]. Recent research also highlights a high prevalence of attendance-related issues in nursing. In the healthcare sector, nurses exhibit an even higher prevalence of presenteeism. For example, research in the United States found that High-stress environments may lead nurses to develop more health problems, including mental health problems, which are less likely to be disclosed than physical problems [7]. Specifically, 52.6% of US nurses reported difficulty concentrating at work at some point during the past four weeks [8].

Presenteeism factors can be categorized into four main groups: health issues, job demands, job resources, and personal psychosocial factors. Health problems are a primary focus when studying presenteeism. Research indicates that various health conditions, including acute or chronic illnesses, mental health disorders (e.g., depression and anxiety), and insomnia, are positively associated with presenteeism. Job demands constitute another critical factor [9–11]. High workloads, job pressure, staff shortages, low job replaceability, and extended working hours are significant drivers of reduced work efficiency and prolonged presence at work despite poor health. For example, Fumichi et al., in a study of over 2,000 Japanese employees, demonstrated that job stress leads to physiological and psychological responses such as sleep disturbances and reduced physical capacity, ultimately contributing to presenteeism [12]. A cross-sectional study of 3,740 senior doctors in New Zealand found that 88% had experienced presenteeism. The most frequently reported causes were reluctance to delegate tasks, concerns about the impact of absence on patients, and challenges in accessing sick leave insurance [8, 13]. Additionally, Anders et al., in a systematic review of recent research on doctors and nurses, concluded that nearly all job resources negatively correlate with presenteeism [14]. These resources include supportive work environments, positive relationships with supervisors and colleagues, teamwork, and a healthy organizational climate.Finally, personal psychosocial factors—such as job satisfaction, occupational burnout, work attitudes, and financial conditions—often act as mediators or moderators, influencing presenteeism either directly or indirectly. Presenteeism in China is closely linked to unique cultural norms that encourage perseverance at work, with some employees viewing attendance while ill as a way to demonstrate dedication and facilitate career advancement [15]. The COVID-19 pandemic, which emerged in late 2019, has further intensified focus on presenteeism within the healthcare sector. Healthcare professionals, facing immense job demands and occupational pressure, have experienced substantial physical, psychological, and social strain. A cross-sectional survey by Shan [16], indicated that 94.25% of over 700 nurses and head nurses reported presenteeism in the preceding six months, with an estimated annual economic loss of 4.38 billion CNY attributable to nurse presenteeism in China [16]. However, current research is predominantly focused on risk factors, especially individual health issues, while investigations into protective organizational or psychological factors are scarce.

Perceived organizational support, reflecting employees’ perceptions of how much their organization values their well-being, has been identified as a critical factor influencing presenteeism. Studies suggest that high Perceived organizational support can reduce presenteeism by fostering a supportive workplace culture and enabling employees to prioritize self-care [17]. However, excessive workload expectations or understaffing may counteract these benefits, reinforcing presenteeism behaviors even in organizations with seemingly high levels of support [18].

Another essential factor is professional identity, defined as the self-concept derived from one’s professional role. Professional identity is particularly relevant in nursing, where a strong sense of purpose and responsibility often motivates individuals to prioritize their roles over personal health [19]. Research has shown that nurses with a strong professional identity are more likely to exhibit presenteeism due to their commitment to patient care and a perceived lack of alternative support [20]. However, such behaviors may inadvertently compromise their long-term health and professional effectiveness [21].

The Job Demands-Resources model, introduced by Bakker and Demerouti [22], posits that occupational stress factors can be categorized into two primary dimensions: job demands and job resources. Job demands refer to physical, psychological, social, or organizational aspects of work that require sustained physical or mental effort, potentially leading to health impairment processes such as burnout and increased psychological costs. Examples include high workload, work–family conflict, and emotional demands. In contrast, job resources encompass physical, psychological, social, or organizational aspects that mitigate the negative effects of job demands, support goal attainment, and stimulate personal growth and development. These include social support, leadership quality, performance feedback, and opportunities for training and development [22–24].Based on the aforementioned theoretical framework, healthcare professionals, as individuals within hospital organizations, provide medical services and patient care while expecting both psychological and material rewards from their institution. When they perceive a high level of organizational support, this may enhance their affective commitment and work enthusiasm, thereby improving work efficiency, mitigating the negative effects of occupational stress, and reducing the occurrence of presenteeism. Meanwhile, professional identity develops through social interactions and comparisons among healthcare workers. When they derive sufficient self-esteem and satisfaction from their professional community, it fosters a stronger sense of professional identity and commitment. Furthermore, high organizational support and strong professional identity serve as positive organizational and personal resources, respectively. When healthcare professionals face high job demands, these resources can partially offset the burden associated with such demands, alleviate burnout, and help maintain optimal work performance.Based on this theoretical foundation, we developed a conceptual model (Fig. 1) to examine the relationships among perceived organizational support, professional identity, and presenteeism.

Despite the growing body of literature, the interplay between Perceived organizational support, professional identity, and presenteeism remains underexplored, particularly in non-Western contexts like China. In China, where collectivist values emphasize dedication and endurance, cultural norms may further amplify presenteeism among healthcare workers. A recent study reported that 83% of Chinese nurses experienced presenteeism, with higher rates observed in high-stress specialties such as pediatrics [25]. Pediatric nurses face unique challenges, including the emotional and physical demands of caring for children, family expectations, and systemic understaffing, making them especially vulnerable to presenteeism [26].

This study aims to examine the relationship between perceived organizational support and presenteeism among pediatric nurses in China, with a particular focus on the mediating role of professional identity. By integrating theories of organizational support and professional identity, this research seeks to provide actionable insights into mitigating presenteeism and improving workforce sustainability in the healthcare sector.

Method

Sample and study design

A convenience sampling method was employed to survey pediatric nurses from three tertiary hospitals in Weifang City, Shandong Province, China. between June and October 2024. The inclusion criteria were: (1) nurses who provided informed consent, and (2) formally employed staff in good physical health without extended periods of absence. The exclusion criteria were: (1) interns or trainees, (2) externally contracted personnel, and (3) individuals with recent long-term leave records.Designated research personnel, having completed a standardized training protocol, established communication with the leadership of the pediatric nursing department. Subsequent to securing institutional cooperation and procuring informed consent from all participants, the investigation proceeded with the administration of an electronic questionnaire. This instrument was disseminated to the cohort of pediatric nurses via the WeChat application by the on-site research team. The QuestionStar platform served as the centralized system for data acquisition and quality assurance. Rigor was enforced through several protocols: guaranteeing respondent anonymity and voluntary participation, deploying a questionnaire architecture with mandatory and logic-validated response fields, and technically restricting access to a single submission per IP address. As a gesture of appreciation for their contribution, participants received modest gratuities upon concluding the survey.

The sample size was calculated using the formula n=[Z2α/2p(1 − p)]/δ2, commonly applied in cross-sectional studies. Based on previous research, the presenteeism rate among nurses in Chinese tertiary hospitals was reported as 56.5%. Accordingly, δwas set to 0.1p Using α = 0.05 and Z2α/2≈1.96, the estimated minimum sample size was 296. To ensure the reliability of the results, the target sample size was increased by 20% to account for potential non-responses, resulting in a total of N = n(1 + 20%) ≈ 355. Ultimately, 490 questionnaires were distributed, meeting the sample size requirements.

Measurements

Presenteeism

This study utilized the Chinese version of the Stanford Attendance Efficiency Reduction Scale-6 (SAERS-6) to assess presenteeism. The scale, originally developed by Dr. Koopman’s team at Stanford University, was precisely translated and culturally adapted by Zhao Fang [27] and colleagues in China. The Cronbach’s α coefficient for the Chinese version of the scale was 0.81, indicating strong internal consistency.The SAERS-6 employs a five-point Likert scale, with scores ranging from 1 to 5, representing “strongly disagree,” “disagree,” “neutral,” “agree,” and “strongly agree,” respectively. The total score ranges from 6 to 30. The first four items are scored positively, while the last two are reverse-scored. A higher total score indicates more severe presenteeism among pediatric nursing staff, signifying greater losses in work efficiency and productivity.

Perceived organizational support

The Chinese version of the Perceived Organizational Support Scale was used to measure pediatric nurses’ organizational support. Developed by Chinese scholar Pan Xiaohua, the scale demonstrated strong reliability, with a Cronbach’s α coefficient of 0.92. Its two constituent subscales also demonstrated high internal consistency, with α values of 0.96 for emotional support and 0.89 for instrumental support.The scale evaluates organizational support across two dimensions: emotional support and instrumental support. Emotional support refers to providing care, comfort, encouragement, understanding, and empathy at the emotional level. It aims to meet individuals’ emotional needs, enhancing their sense of emotional security and belonging. This form of support focuses on interpersonal connections and psychological reassurance, emphasizing emotional resonance and mutual understanding. Instrumental support involves offering practical assistance, resources, information, or problem-solving methods. It addresses individuals’ practical and material needs, helping them achieve goals or resolve challenges [28]. The scale consists of 14 items divided between these two dimensions, scored using a five-point Likert scale. Responses range from 1 (“strongly disagree”) to 5 (“strongly agree”). The total score ranges from 14 to 70, with higher scores indicating stronger perceived organizational support among pediatric nurses.

Professional identity

The Professional Identity Scale used in this study was adapted from the Teacher Professional Identity Scale developed by Wei Shuhua and the Nurse Professional Identity Scale created by Chen Xiangli and colleagues. The scale demonstrated strong reliability, with a Cronbach’s α coefficient of 0.86. Its three constituent dimensions also demonstrated good reliability, with α values of 0.87 for perceived professional benefits, 0.82 for professional belonging, and 0.84 for professional motivation. It evaluates professional identity across three dimensions: professional motivation, professional belonging, and perceived professional benefits.Professional motivation refers to the intrinsic force driving individuals to strive for continuous improvement and effort in their career. It stems from their passion, interests, values, and pursuit of personal goals within their profession. Professional belonging describes an individual’s sense of identification with and integration into their profession, reflecting a psychological state where they see themselves as part of a group or organization and feel emotional attachment and belonging to their professional community. Perceived professional benefits encompass the psychological experience of various rewards and gains received through professional activities, including both material rewards (e.g., salary, bonuses, benefits) and non-material rewards (e.g., skill enhancement, self-fulfillment, social recognition) [29]. The scale consists of 12 items scored using a five-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (“strongly disagree”) to 5 (“strongly agree”). The total score ranges from 12 to 60, with higher scores indicating stronger professional identity among pediatric nurses.

Control variables

The covariates included in this survey are gender, age, marital status, education level, years of work experience, number of children, administrative position, professional title, employment type, frequency of night shifts, and average monthly income.

Multiple linear regression analysis

Through detailed analysis, we identified a negative correlation between presenteeism scores and both perceived organizational support and professional identity. To explore the potential causal relationships among these variables, regression analyses were conducted using SPSS version 26.0. Specifically, we examined the relationships between the dimensions of perceived organizational support and professional identity, and the total presenteeism score. During the regression analysis, demographic variables with a significant impact on presenteeism were excluded. Subsequently, two core dimensions of perceived organizational support and three key dimensions of professional identity were treated as independent variables, with the total presenteeism score as the dependent variable. A stepwise multiple regression analysis was performed. Details of the variable coding rules are provided in Table 3. The results showed that the variance inflation factor (VIF) values for all independent variables in the regression model were below 10, indicating no multicollinearity issues. Further regression analysis revealed the following: Number of children had a significant positive effect on presenteeism (β = 0.153, t = 3.122, P = 0.002). Administrative position (β=-0.103, t=-2.149, P = 0.032), instrumental support (β=-0.017, t=-2.650, P = 0.008), and perceived professional benefits (β=-0.023, t=-2.501, P = 0.013) demonstrated significant negative effects on presenteeism.

The mediating role of professional identity in the relationship between perceived organizational support and presenteeism

The PROCESS macro for SPSS was used to analyze the mediating effect of professional identity on the relationship between perceived organizational support and presenteeism. Model 4 (simple mediation model) was selected as the baseline model. Controlling for demographic variables that influence presenteeism, perceived organizational support was set as the independent variable, professional identity as the mediator, and presenteeism as the dependent variable.As shown in Table 4,the mediation analysis revealed that the negative predictive effect of perceived organizational support on presenteeism remained significant both before and after including professional identity as a mediator (P < 0.01).

The Bootstrap analysis further indicated that the total effect of perceived organizational support on presenteeism was − 0.0125 with a direct effect of -0.076 (95% CI: -0.297 to -0.017). The indirect mediating effect through professional identity was − 0.049 (95% CI: -0.099 to -0.008), accounting for 39.2% of the total effect. These findings suggest that professional identity mediates the relationship between perceived organizational support and presenteeism. The mediation pathway model is illustrated in Fig. 2.

Discussion

This study is the first to investigate the mediating role of professional identity in the relationship between perceived organizational support and presenteeism among pediatric nurses in China. The findings reveal that professional identity partially mediates the relationship between perceived organizational support and presenteeism.

In our study, the average presenteeism score among pediatric nurses was relatively high, with 52.4% of participants classified as having a high level of presenteeism. These results align with previous research [30–32] highlighting the concerning status of presenteeism in this population. Pediatric nurses face demanding workloads that require a high degree of responsibility and compassion. Additionally, they must address the heightened expectations, involvement, and accountability demands of parents who are deeply concerned about their children’s medical safety. The significant time and effort required to communicate with parents increase work-related stress and time burdens, potentially leading to presenteeism and a marked decline in work efficiency [33]. Univariate analysis revealed significant differences in presenteeism scores among pediatric nurses based on years of work experience, children status, and administrative responsibilities. Furthermore, stepwise multiple linear regression analysis identified children status and administrative responsibilities as factors associated with presenteeism. The detailed findings are as follows: (1) number of children.Nurses with children often face substantial family responsibilities, such as caregiving and managing their children’s education, particularly when their children are young and require intensive care. This situation can lead to work-family conflicts [34], preventing nurses from fully dedicating themselves to their work. Even when they are physically present at the workplace, their mental focus may be divided by family concerns, manifesting as presenteeism [35]. (2) Administrative Position. Nurses with administrative roles are frequently tasked with additional management and coordination duties, such as scheduling, team supervision, and handling day-to-day operations. These extra responsibilities can increase their workload, disperse their focus, and elevate their psychological stress [36]. Under such circumstances, although they may remain physically present at their workplace, they often lack the energy or time to fully concentrate on frontline caregiving, contributing to presenteeism [37].

This study found a significant negative correlation between perceived organizational support, particularly its emotional and instrumental support dimensions, and presenteeism among pediatric nurses. perceived organizational support, as a critical workplace resource, reflects employees’ perceptions of the organization’s recognition of their contributions and well-being. Instrumental support includes tangible aids such as sufficient resources, clear communication of expectations, and tools for problem-solving. Beyond basic work requirements, it also encompasses opportunities for professional development, a positive work atmosphere, and an enabling environment, all of which alleviate work-related stress and improve efficiency. These findings align with previous research, which suggests that high perceived organizational support fosters a supportive workplace culture, enabling employees to prioritize their well-being and reduce presenteeism [38, 39]. When nurses feel empowered and supported in managing their responsibilities, they are less likely to engage in presenteeism [40]. Adequate tools, resources, and logistical support further reduce work-related stress and task burdens, allowing nurses to focus more effectively on caregiving responsibilities.

The findings of the study suggest a significant negative correlation between professional identity and presenteeism among pediatric nurses. Regression analysis revealed that perceived professional benefits negatively predicted presenteeism. Specifically, a stronger professional identity reduces the likelihood of presenteeism by fostering a sense of purpose and commitment to professional responsibilities. Nurses with a well-established professional identity tend to exhibit higher motivation and engagement in their roles, enabling them to better manage work-related challenges [41, 42]. Perceived professional benefits refer to the material and psychological rewards employees derive from their profession, including salary, career development opportunities, social status, a sense of achievement, and job satisfaction [43, 44]. These findings are consistent with existing literature, which underscores the role of professional identity in enhancing job satisfaction, reducing burnout, and ultimately mitigating presenteeism [45, 46]. The negative predictive effect of perceived professional benefits suggests that nurses who perceive tangible rewards, both material such as salary and promotions, and non-material, experience lower levels of presenteeism [47, 48].This may be because professional benefits act as motivators, reinforcing nurses’ sense of value and importance within the organization, thereby reducing their inclination to work while unwell.

Our analysis revealed a robust positive correlation between perceived organizational support and professional identity. According to the Social Exchange Theory, when employees feel valued and cared for by their organization, they feel obliged to reciprocate in a positive manner. In the context of nursing, this reciprocity often manifests as a deepened commitment and attachment to their professional role. The organization’s support signals that the nurses’ efforts and sacrifices are recognized, thereby validating their professional worth and reinforcing the meaningfulness of their work [49, 50].

Importantly, the results of this study revealed that professional identity serves as a mediator in the relationship between perceived organizational support and presenteeism. This finding provides new evidence to the existing literature, suggesting that perceived organizational support can negatively influence presenteeism through professional identity among pediatric nurses.Perceived organizational support affects professional identity through various mechanisms. First, perceived organizational support provides both psychological and material support, which boosts employees’ confidence and sense of security when facing work challenges. Instrumental support enhances employees’ self-efficacy, which in turn strengthens their identification with their professional roles [51]. Furthermore, perceived organizational support helps create a supportive and collaborative work environment, motivating employees to pursue professional achievements and enhancing their professional identity [52]. High perceived organizational support reduces role conflict and stress, allowing employees to better align with organizational goals, thereby strengthening their professional identity [53]. The finding that professional identity only partially mediates the relationship between perceived organizational support and presenteeism, rather than fully explaining it, is highly significant. It indicates a more complex reality where multiple psychological and contextual pathways operate simultaneously to influence presenteeism. The partial mediation model suggests that while fostering professional identity is a powerful mechanism, it is not the sole story. The partial mediation finding, therefore, reveals that both the internal psychological state of the nurse and the external work environment are crucial. It implies that hospital managers cannot rely on cultivating professional identity alone to combat presenteeism. A comprehensive strategy must also include tangible, structural investments that make it practically feasible and psychologically safe for nurses to prioritize their health [54–56].For pediatric nurses, this cultural norm may manifest as a strong sense of duty not to let their colleagues or the vulnerable children in their care down. This sense of obligation, compounded by potential staffing shortages, likely creates immense pressure to attend work even when unwell [57]. Our finding that professional identity mediates the relationship between perceived organizational support and presenteeism aligns with this cultural logic. This illustrates how a culturally nurtured professional virtue can have unintended negative consequences when not supported by adequate systemic resources [58, 59].

Our findings have significant implications for alleviating implicit absenteeism among pediatric nurses. At the hospital level, enhancing safety and protective measures in pediatric wards is essential. This includes improving isolation facilities and standard operating procedures to reduce the psychological burden on nurses interacting with high-risk patients. Additionally, providing internal communication tools for nursing teams will facilitate more efficient communication with doctors and other healthcare professionals, reducing the time costs associated with traditional communication methods. Establishing an interdepartmental information-sharing system will enhance collaboration with departments such as pharmacy, laboratory, and radiology, minimizing delays. Furthermore, optimizing the layout of nursing workspaces and the storage of equipment and supplies can reduce unnecessary physical exertion.

This study has several limitations. First, the cross-sectional design employed in this study limits the ability to establish causal relationships between variables, which restricts the depth of the analysis. Longitudinal studies would be more suitable for examining causal links and understanding temporal dynamics.Second, the reliance on participants’ recall for data collection introduces the potential for recall bias. As participants’ responses may be influenced by their memory capacity, this could affect the accuracy of the data and, consequently, the validity of the findings.Third, the study was conducted in a single hospital setting, which limits the generalizability of the findings to other institutions with different organizational structures or cultures. Future research should replicate the study across various hospitals or healthcare systems to examine whether the results hold in different contexts.Finally, the data were collected from a single province in China due to limitations in time and resources, which restricts the representativeness of the sample. Future studies should aim for a multi-center, large-scale approach to enhance the external validity and generalizability of the findings, providing a more comprehensive assessment of nurse attendance, particularly within anesthesiology departments.Especially within pediatrics.

Conclusion

The rate of presenteeism among pediatric nurses is notably high, highlighting the urgent need for improvement. This study presents targeted recommendations from two perspectives: hospital management strategies and the personal development of nursing staff. The goal is to enhance pediatric nurses’ perceptions of organizational support and professional identity, thereby reducing presenteeism. This, in turn, will enable nurses to focus more fully on patient care, ultimately improving the overall quality of healthcare services at the hospital.

Practical implications

The findings of this study offer several actionable implications for hospital administrators and nurse managers aiming to mitigate presenteeism among pediatric nurses.First, at the organizational level, interventions should prioritize enhancing instrumental support. Hospital management should ensure the provision of adequate staffing, modern medical equipment, and streamlined administrative processes. Specifically, optimizing nurse-to-patient ratios and implementing efficient inter-departmental communication systems can directly reduce work-related burdens and frustrations. By alleviating these tangible obstacles, nurses are better equipped to perform their duties effectively even under pressure, thereby reducing the inclination to work while unwell.Second, fostering a strong professional identity, particularly by amplifying perceived professional benefits, is crucial. Healthcare institutions should develop and promote programs that highlight the value and rewards of a nursing career. This can include establishing clear career ladders, providing opportunities for skill development and specialization, and creating formal recognition systems to celebrate clinical excellence and dedication. Mentorship programs pairing experienced nurses with newcomers can also help to cultivate a sense of belonging and purpose. Strengthening these positive professional self-perceptions can buffer against the compulsion to engage in presenteeism.Finally, it is essential to cultivate a supportive organizational culture that legitimizes health self-care. Leaders should actively encourage nurses to take sick leave when genuinely unwell, working to dismantle the stigma associated with it. This can be integrated into mental health education and support programs, emphasizing that long-term professional effectiveness and patient safety depend on a healthy workforce.

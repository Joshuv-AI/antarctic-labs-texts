Integrated near-infrared fiber-optic photoacoustic sensing demodulator for ultra-high sensitivity gas detection
Authors: Xinyu Zhao, Chenxi Li, Hongchao Qi, Jiayu Huang, Yufu Xu, Zhengzhi Wang, Xiao Han, Min Guo, Ke Chen
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractAn integrated near-infrared fiber-optic photoacoustic sensing demodulator was established for ultra-high sensitivity gas detection. The demodulator has capacities of interference spectrum acquisition and calculation, laser modulation control as well as digital lock-in amplification. FPGA was utilized to realize all the control and signal processing functions, which immensely improved the integration and stability of the system. The photoacoustic signal detection based on fiber-optic Fabry–Perot (F-P) acoustic sensor was realized by applying ultra-high resolution spectral demodulation technique. The detectable frequency of photoacoustic signal achieved 10 kHz. The system integrated lock-in amplification technology, which made the noise sound pressure and dynamic response range of sound pressure detection reached 3.7 μPa/√Hz @1 kHz and 142 dB, respectively. The trace C2H2 gas was tested with a multi-pass resonant photoacoustic cell. Ultra-high sensitivity gas detection was accomplished, which was based on high acoustic detection sensitivity and the matching digital lock-in amplification. The system detection limit and normalized noise equivalent absorption (NNEA) coefficient were reached 3.5 ppb and 6.7 × 10−10 cm−1WHz−1/2, respectively. The devised demodulator can be applied for long-distance gas measurement, which depends on the fact that both the near-infrared photoacoustic excitation light and the probe light employ optical fiber as transmission medium.Keywords: Trace gas detection, Fiber-optic photoacoustic sensing, Fiber-optic Fabry–Perot sensor, Spectral demodulation

Introduction

Optical detection technology plays an important role in the field of trace gas measurement [1], [2], [3]. In the fiber-optic photoacoustic sensing system, the excitation light employed for photoacoustic excitation is transmitted by optical fiber and enters the gas chamber, which excites the target gas to generate photoacoustic pressure wave [4], [5], [6], [7]. Fiber-optic acoustic sensors are applied to detect photoacoustic signals to achieve high sensitivity gas measurement [8], [9], [10]. The medium for transmitting near-infrared photoacoustic excitation light and probe light is optical fiber with low transmission loss. The sensor has the inherent superiorities of high gas detection sensitivity, intrinsic safety, anti-electromagnetic interference and remote sensing. Fiber-optic photoacoustic sensors can be applied for monitoring natural combustion in coal mines [8], [9], dissolved gases in transformer oil [13], [14], and flammable and explosive gases in petrochemical plants [15].

The optical interferometric sensor has the displacement resolution of pm-order. Fiber-optic acoustic sensor has been used in photoacoustic spectroscopy (PAS) system, realizing the high-sensitivity all-optical sensing of trace gases. Breguet et al. realized all-optical photoacoustic gas detection for the first time by applying a fiber Michelson interferometer [16]. The optical fiber was wound on the photoacoustic tube. The thermal expansion and contraction caused by target gas absorption force the photoacoustic tube to vibrate periodically. The detection of photoacoustic signal was accomplished by measuring the variation of optical path in optical fiber. We demodulated the double-beam interference spectrum in the fiber Michelson interferometer at high speed, which improved the sensitivity of acoustic detection [17]. However, the optical path difference exists between the measuring arm and the reference arm is required to be controlled within 3 mm, which increases the difficulty of the system implementation. The fiber-optic Fabry–Perot (F-P) sensor is another commonly applied photoacoustic detector, which uses a diaphragm as the sensitive element [18]. The optical path difference of the fiber F-P interferometer changes, which is caused by the acoustic pressure acting on the diaphragm. Therefore, the acoustic pressure can be sensed by measuring the dynamic cavity length of F-P in real-time. The probe of fiber-optic F-P sensor is small, which is suitable for structural matching with micro photoacoustic cell [19], [20], [21]. Wang et al. designed a fiber-optic acoustic sensor applying organic diaphragm, drawing on the structure of traditional capacitive microphones. High sensitivity detection of C2H2 was realized [22]. Jin et al. established a fiber-optic acoustic detection device with the sound pressure response of 1.1 nm/Pa based on a multilayer graphene film [23]. The detection limit of C2H2 gas reached 119.8 ppb. We devised a fiber-optic cantilever acoustic sensor, which further enhanced the sensitivity of photoacoustic detection [24]. The fiber-optic cantilever enhanced PAS was proposed, which can complete high-sensitivity sensing of various gases [25], [26]. To realize remote gas sensing, the passive design of photoacoustic probe is necessary. [27], [28]. Optical fiber is utilized as a transmission medium to transmit excitation light and probe light. We invented a diffusive optical fiber photoacoustic sensor for gas micro leakage monitoring with a gas chamber of only 70 μL [29]. The small aperture hole and the gap of the cantilever beam make the gas diffuse into the gas chamber. The detection limit of C2H2 is 20 ppb. To further reduce the structural parameters of the probe, we designed a single fiber photoacoustic sensor. It provides the possibility of gas detection in an extremely narrow space [30], [31].

The interference-intensity demodulation method commonly used in fiber-optic acoustic sensors locks the central wavelength of the probe light source at the static working point to obtain relatively high sensitivity [32], [33]. Nevertheless, the working point is prone to drift due to the influence of temperature, resulting in serious distortion of the demodulated acoustic signal. In addition, this scheme has the defect of small response range of acoustic detection. To solve the above problems, we proposed two fiber-optic acoustic demodulation techniques based on optical cross-correlation and white light interference (WLI), respectively. The optical cross-correlation demodulation technology matches the sensing F-P interferometer by precisely adjusting the optical path of the reference interferometer. Low-frequency acoustic wave detection has been achieved by searching the peak position or calculating the initial phase of the optical cross-correlation image [34], [35]. It has the superiorities of low cost and simple structure. The WLI demodulation method is realized by calculating the phase of the interference spectrum to obtain the absolute cavity length in real-time [36]. As a result, the acoustic demodulation is independent of the influence of the light source power fluctuation and the light path loss change. The dynamic demodulation of F-P cavity length is realized by utilizing high-speed spectrum acquisition and Buneman frequency estimation, which greatly improves the sensitivity, dynamic response range and stability of acoustic detection. The equivalent noise acoustic pressure reaches 5 μPa/Hz1/2@1 kHz. We proposed a digital lock-in WLI based fiber-optic photoacoustic detection system [37]. Utilizing a near-infrared light source, the detection limit and normalized noise equivalent absorption (NNEA) coefficient of C2H2 were 71 ppt and 1.1 × 10−9 cm−1WHz−1/2, respectively. However, the sampling rate limited by the spectral module leaded to the photoacoustic demodulation rate of only 5 kHz. Moreover, the algorithm of F-P cavity length calculation and lock-in amplification are processed by computer, which is not conducive to the improvement of speed and stability.

In this paper, we developed a fiber-optic photoacoustic sensing demodulator for ultra-high sensitivity gas detection. This instrument integrated the function modules of acoustic sensing demodulation, laser control and digital lock-in amplification. FPGA was applied to realize all the control and signal processing functions in fiber-optic photoacoustic sensing, which greatly improved the speed, integration and stability of the demodulation system. The real-time spectrum acquisition and phase demodulation of the fiber-optic F-P acoustic sensor were carried out at a speed of 20 kHz. Due to the ultra-high acoustic detection sensitivity of the system and the matching digital lock-in amplification, ultra-high sensitivity gas detection was achieved.

Design of acoustic demodulator

The schematic structure and photograph of the developed fiber-optic photoacoustic sensing demodulator are shown in Fig. 2(a) and (b), respectively. The modulation frequency, bias current and modulation depth of DFB laser are all controlled by FPGA through a digital-to-analog converter (DAC). The excitation light in the sensor is the near infrared emitted by a 1532 nm DFB laser, which is transmitted and collimated by a single-mode fiber-optic and a fiber collimator. The output wavelength of DFB laser becomes longer with the increase of driving current composed of a low-frequency sawtooth wave and a high-frequency sine wave. A super luminescent diode (SLD) with the central wavelength of 1550 nm is exploited as a probe light source. The emitted wide-spectrum light passes through a fiber optic circulator and enters the photoacoustic sensor. The F-P interference spectrum containing photoacoustic information is collected by a miniature fiber optic spectroscopy module (I-MON-256-OEM, Ibsen). The spectral module is mainly composed of spectral analysis components, an image sensor and a signal acquisition unit. The transmission volume phase grating is applied as a spectral element. This structural design without mechanical moving components can achieve high-speed spectral signal detection. The spectrum has 256 pixels. By optimizing the FPGA circuit, a frame speed of 20 kHz was achieved. As a result, the maximum photoacoustic detection frequency of the demodulator is 10 kHz according to Nyquist sampling theorem.

(a) Schematic structure of the developed fiber-optic photoacoustic sensing demodulator for gas detection. (b) Photograph of the integrated demodulator.

An FPGA based signal processing circuit board is designed, which realizes high-speed acquisition and real-time processing of the F-P interference spectrum. The signal processing circuit mainly consisted of a FPGA chip, a high-speed analog-to-digital conversion (ADC) chip, a DAC chip and a RS232 communication interface. The F-P cavity length demodulation is synchronized with spectral acquisition. A series of calculations such as spectral normalization, interpolation, FFT, and frequency estimation are implemented in FPGA. The demodulated F-P cavity length is filtered to obtain the photoacoustic signal. The signal-to-noise ratio is further improved by performing real-time lock-in amplification process on the photoacoustic signal. Two orthogonal reference signals are generated in FPGA, whose frequency is an integer multiple of the laser modulation frequency. It can measure various harmonic components in the photoacoustic signal. The different integration times can be changed by controlling the number of data points involved in the processing of lock-in amplification. A frequency scanning function is added to the FPGA lock-in module, which facilitates the measurement of the frequency response of the photoacoustic system.

Experimental results and discussion

Acoustic test

Compared with stainless steel, the acoustic sensor made of silicon material has excellent stability, consistency and acoustic sensitivity. A fiber-optic silicon cantilever sensor was utilized to test the acoustic sensing demodulation performance of the instrument. Fig. 3(a) exhibits the structural schematic diagram of the fiber-optic acoustic sensor based on cantilever beam. The length, width and thickness parameters of the cantilever are 1.4 mm, 0.8 mm and 6 µm, respectively. In addition, the action base of the cantilever beam is made of aluminum. The gap between the fiber in the ceramic ferrule and the cantilever forms an F-P interference cavity. The cantilever beam deflects with the acoustic pressure wave, which changes the dynamic length of the F-P cavity. To improve the contrast of the F-P interference fringe, the internal reflective surface of the cantilever was coated with a highly reflective gold film. The interference spectrum in Fig. 3(b) was collected by the FPGA circuit board. The calculated F-P cavity length is 661 µm.

The sound pressure response characteristics of cantilever acoustic sensor at different frequencies are tested and analyzed by scanning frequency. Fig. 4 is the frequency response curve in the range of 100 Hz to 5 kHz. Among them, the sensitivity at 1.64 kHz is 234 nm/Pa. Measured sound pressure noise at 1 kHz.

In order to determine the dynamic range of photoacoustic signal detection, it is necessary to measure the minimum and maximum detection sound pressure of the lock-in system. The integration time and reference signal frequency were set to 10 s and 1 kHz, respectively. With the speaker turned off, the standard deviation of the measured lock-in output background noise was 0.13 pm. According to the sensitivity of the fiber-optic cantilever sensor of 181 nm/Pa, the minimum detected sound pressure is calculated to be 0.7 μPa. Afterwards, the sound pressure of the speaker was controlled to increase gradually, the maximum input sound pressure obtained was 9.3 Pa. The maximum limit value of sound pressure detection is constrained by demodulation algorithm. Excessive sound pressure will increase the probability of mode jump. To achieve a stable detection effect, the maximum sound pressure allowed by the system is 9.3 Pa. In addition, in the field of photoacoustic gas detection, the generated sound pressure signal is generally in the order of μPa or mPa, which can be demodulated stably. Therefore, combined with lock-in amplification technology, the dynamic range for single frequency acoustic pressure detection of the system reaches 142 dB.

Gas detection

The excellent performance of the developed fiber-optic photoacoustic sensing demodulator was verified by measuring 50 ppm C2H2 gas. A multi-reflection absorption enhanced photoacoustic cell was exploited to further improve the detection sensitivity [43], [44]. In the gas photoacoustic experiment, the driving parameters of the DFB laser were optimized firstly. By setting FPGA operating parameters, the modulation current of the laser was controlled [45], [46]. Fig. 6 shows the relationship between the measured amplitude of photoacoustic signal and the DFB laser modulation current. It is obvious that the modulation current of 8 mA was the optimal parameter setting.

Amplitude of photoacoustic signal as a function of laser modulation current.

By scanning the frequency of the laser modulation current, the frequency response of the fiber-optic photoacoustic sensing system was experimentally measured [47], as shown in Fig. 7. The resonances of the photoacoustic tube and the cantilever beam generated two response peaks, which are 1.64 kHz and 3.68 kHz, respectively. In the subsequent experiment, we applied the resonance of the photoacoustic tube to measure the concentration of C2H2 gas.

Frequency response of the fiber-optic photoacoustic sensing system.

By setting the gas distribution ratio of two mass flow controllers, trace C2H2 gas with different concentration gradients were tested successively. Since the resonant frequency of the cantilever was greatly influenced by the ambient temperature and humidity, the resonant frequency of 1.64 kHz of PAC was adopted to detect photoacoustic signals, which was equivalent to setting the modulation frequency of DFB laser to 820 Hz. The second-harmonic signal was obtained by scanning the bias current of the DFB laser in steps of 0.2 mA [48], as shown in Fig. 8(a). The background interference was 1 pm. Based on the relationship between the searched peak values of each second-harmonic signal and different concentrations of C2H2 gas, the concentration response of 10.2 pm/ppm was obtained through linear fitting, as shown in Fig. 8(b).

C2H2 gas with the concentration of 1 ppm was injected into the photoacoustic cell. The laser modulation frequency was set at 820 Hz. By accurately demodulating the dynamic cavity length of the fiber-optic cantilever sensor in real-time, the time-domain photoacoustic response under the resonance of the photoacoustic tube was obtained, as shown in Fig. 9(a). Meanwhile, the amplitude spectrum of the photoacoustic signal was obtained through Fourier transform, as shown in Fig. 9(b). The photoacoustic cell was filled with high purity nitrogen to evaluate the noise level and judge the detection limit of the system. The integration time of the lock-in module in FPGA was controlled at 1 s. The measured noise level and Allan-Werle variance analysis result are shown in Fig. 10. The standard deviation of noise was 0.4 pm. The output power of DFB laser was tested to be 12.1 mW to calculate the NNEA coefficient. The absorption coefficient of C2H2 gas at 1532 nm was 0.57 cm−1. The NNEA was 6.7 × 10−10 cm−1WHz−1/2, which was obtained according to the responsivity of 10.2 pm/ppm. Allan-Werle deviation analysis indicates that the variance shows a decreasing trend with the continuous increase of averaging time [49], [50], [51], [52]. The detection limit of 49 ppb can be achieved by 1 s integration time, which could be further optimized to 3.5 ppb by an averaging time of up to 200 s

The designed fiber-optic photoacoustic sensing system can be applied for long-distance gas detection, which is since both excitation light and probe light in the system are transmitted by single-mode fiber-optic. The transmission loss of C-band light in the single-mode fiber-optic is 0.2 dB/km, and the insertion loss of each fiber-optic adapter is ∼0.3 dB. With the transmission distance of 5 km, 10 km,15 km and 20 km, the total loss of excitation light power is 25.87 %, 41.12 %, 53.23 % and 62.85 %, respectively. The minimum detection limit of gas is proportional to the power of excitation light. The detection limit of C2H2 is about 7 ppb when the detected target position is 15 km away.

Conclusion

In conclusion, a near-infrared fiber-optic photoacoustic sensing demodulator was developed for ultra-high sensitivity gas detection. Optical fibers were applied as the medium to accomplish the transmission of both near-infrared photoacoustic excitation light and probe light. The designed fiber-optic gas sensing system has the superiorities of anti-electromagnetic interference, intrinsically safe and remote detection. The photoacoustic wave detection, laser modulation and digital lock equalization functions of the photoacoustic system are integrated by FPGA, which improved the speed and stability of the system. The photoacoustic signal detection based on fiber-optic F-P cantilever sensor was realized by using ultra-high resolution spectral demodulation. Due to the real-time demodulation speed of up to 20 kHz, the detectable frequency of the photoacoustic signal reached 10 kHz. Combined with lock-in amplification technology, the equivalent noise sound pressure and dynamic range for single-frequency acoustic pressure detection are 3.7 μPa/√Hz and 142 dB at 1 kHz. The trace C2H2 gas was tested with a resonant photoacoustic cell. The detection limit and NNEA coefficient were achieved to be 3.5 ppb and 6.7 × 10−10 cm−1WHz−1/2, respectively. By replacing the laser light source with different central wavelengths, the designed demodulator can also detect other types of gases, such as CH4, C2H4, CO, CO2, etc.

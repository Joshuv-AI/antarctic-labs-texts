OA20107. Feminist NGOs tackling intimate partner violence: Challenges and gaps in ten European countries
Authors: A Mavrommati-Lagani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWomen’s and feminist organisations, such as Women’s Aid in the UK, have played a central role in addressing domestic violence and intimate partner violence (IPV) for over 50 years. This study examines the contribution of civil society organisations to IPV against women prevention and response in ten European countries (UK, Ireland, Sweden, Finland, Germany, France, Spain, Greece, Hungary, and Poland), and explores their cooperation with state authorities.MethodsData were collected through a review of reports from the Council of Europe’s GREVIO Committee and the UN CEDAW Committee, as well as 33 semi-structured interviews with organisations providing services or advocacy in the field of domestic violence.ResultsThe findings reveal persistent policy gaps across all countries, including insecure funding structures, reliance on short-term contracts, competitive program awards, weak data collection systems, and inadequate implementation of legal frameworks. In addition, the study identifies deeper systemic issues, such as governmental resistance to framing IPV as gender-based violence (e.g. Hungary), the growing influence of far-right movements and their focus against the feminist movement (e.g. Spain, Hungary, Poland), and internal tensions within the feminist movement across Europe.ConclusionsThese results underscore the vital but precarious position of feminist civil society in shaping and sustaining effective responses to IPV in Europe.AckowledgementThe research originated from the doctoral research project “Policies, Feminist Movements, and Demands to Tackle Violence Against Women: A Comparative Study of Domestic Violence in European Countries.” The research was funded by the Hellenic Foundation for Research and Innovation – HFRI [5206] between 2022–2025 and was carried out at the Department of Social Policy of Panteion University of Social and Political Sciences.Key messages• Feminist organisations are essential in combating violence against women, but their work is hindered by unstable funding, political resistance, and weak state cooperation across Europe.• Rising anti-gender politics and far-right movements in several European countries are creating a hostile climate for feminist NGOs tackling violence against women.TopicIntimate Partner Violence, Feminist Civil Society/NGOs, Policy implementation and obstacles

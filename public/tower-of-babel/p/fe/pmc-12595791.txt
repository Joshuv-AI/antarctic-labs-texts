Opening the black box: defining true-negative outcomes in esophageal cancer screening — a population-based study
Authors: Mengfei Liu, Zeyu Yan, Anxiang Liu, Chuanhai Guo, Haijun Yang, Fenglei Li, Liping Duan, Lin Shen, Zhen Liu, Yaqi Pan, Ying Liu, Fangfang Liu, Wenlei Yang, Hongrui Tian, Zifan Qi, Ren Zhou, Hong Cai, Qi Wu, Zhonghu He, Yang Ke
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCurrent negativity definition in esophageal cancer screening overlooks the risk heterogeneity between individuals with non-dysplastic Lugol’s unstained lesions (ND-LULs) and normal-stained epithelium. We aimed to define the screening negativity by the incidence risk of severe dysplasia and above lesions (SDAs) after chromoendoscopy and ascertain their re-screening interval.MethodsWe enrolled 815 participants with ND-LULs and 14,123 with normal-stained epithelium from the screening arm of a community-based randomized controlled trial (RCT). The control groups included the RCT’s unscreened arm (n = 16,473), and population controls matched 1:5 by birth year, sex, and village (4075 controls for ND-LULs group, 70,615 controls for normal-stained group). All participants were followed up for up to 10 years. Poisson regression and the cumulative incidence function compared the cumulative incidence of SDA between groups.ResultsCompared with the RCT control, the cumulative incidence of SDAs was significantly higher in the ND-LULs group over 10 years (adjusted IRR = 3.16, 95% CI: 2.02–4.76) but lower in the normal-stained group (adjusted IRR = 0.52, 95% CI: 0.37–0.72). Similar results were achieved using the general population control. The cumulative incidence of SDAs in the ND-LULs group significantly exceeded controls after 2–3 years while remaining consistently lower in the normal-stained group over 10 years.ConclusionsIndividuals with normal Lugol staining should be considered the genuinely screening-negative population for esophageal cancer. Background

Esophageal cancer (EC) is one of the most prevalent cancers, ranking 11th in incidence and 7th in mortality worldwide [1]. In 2022, over 43% of new cases occurred in China [1, 2]. Unlike western countries where adenocarcinoma is more common, the predominant histologic subtype of esophageal cancer in China is esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), constituting approximately 86% of all occurrences [3]. Population-level endoscopic screening can reduce incidence and mortality by detecting malignant lesions at earlier and more treatable stages [4–6] and has been recognized as an effective strategy for ESCC prevention [7].

Current guidelines recommend Lugol’s chromoendoscopy (LCE), followed by target biopsy and histologic examination, as the standard technique for ESCC screening [7]. Lugol-unstained lesions (LULs) in the esophagus under LCE, representing the deficiency of glycogen in epithelium cells [5, 8, 9], can be easily distinguished from the normal-stained epithelium. The disposition of detected lesions mainly depends on the pathologic diagnoses of targeted biopsies, including immediate clinical treatment for severe dysplasia and above lesions (SDAs) and endoscopic surveillance every 1–3 years for those with mild or moderate dysplasia.

In the current screening practice, normal histology, which includes non-dysplastic Lugol-unstained lesions (ND-LULs) confirmed by pathology and Lugol normal-stained epithelium, is considered screening negative. For these individuals, a 5-year re-screening interval is recommended by Chinese screening guidelines, primarily based on expert consensus [7]. A recent report indicated that the incidence of EC in the screened population with normal histology is 53% of the average incidence across rural China over 10 years, and the researchers thus suggested that the re-screening interval for them could be extended to 10 years [10].

However, accumulating evidence indicates that the risks of developing SDA between subjects with ND-LULs and normal-stained epithelium are heterogeneous. Our previous studies have shown that ND-LULs, especially for those with large sizes, are at a high absolute risk of malignant progression [11, 12] and significantly exceed that in normal-stained subjects [13]. Also, ND-LULs have already harbored extensive somatic copy number alterations related to ESCC, which occur at a low frequency in normal-stained subjects [13]. We suspect the current histology-based definition of screening negativity, which overlooks the heterogeneity between subjects with ND-LULs and normal-stained epithelium, may lead to missed diagnoses of malignancy developed from ND-LULs, leaving them unprotected. Based on the mentioned research findings above, we propose that the status of Lugol staining may be more appropriate as the criterion for identifying the genuinely screening-negative population who has a low incidence of SDA after a onetime screening. Nevertheless, evidence from population-based studies is still absent to date.

In this study, we assessed the incidence of esophageal malignancies during follow-up for individuals with ND-LULs and normal-stained epithelium detected at baseline endoscopic screening, and compared these results to the risk in the general unscreened population, by leveraging prospective follow-up data from a large-scale community-based randomized controlled trial and a county-level medical insurance claims database in a high-risk area for ESCC in China. We also evaluate the temporal trends of incidence risk among subjects with normal-stained epithelium and ND-LULs to identify an optimal screening interval.

Methods

Parent study

This prospective cohort study was based on the Endoscopic Screening for Esophageal Cancer in China (ESECC, NCT01688908) randomized controlled trial. Details of the ESECC trial have been reported previously [9, 14]. Briefly, the study was initiated in 2012 in Hua County, Henan Province, China. After a blocked randomization procedure, 668 villages with 33,847 participants aged 45 to 69 years were randomly selected and equally allocated to the screening arm (n = 17,104) or the control arm (n = 16,743) [9, 14]. The inclusion criteria of the ESECC trial were as follows: (1) Permanent residents aged 45–69 years in the target village; (2) no history of endoscopic examination within 5 years before enrollment; (3) no history of cancer, mental disorder, or infection with hepatitis B virus, hepatitis C virus, or human immunodeficiency virus; and (4) agreement to complete all phases of the examination.

Standard upper gastrointestinal endoscopic examination with LCE in the esophagus was performed by experienced endoscopists between 2012 and 2016 for participants in the screening arm [9, 14]. Biopsies were taken from all clearly visible LULs in the esophagus.

Pathologic diagnosis was made by two experienced pathologists without knowledge of endoscopic results, and discrepancies were adjudicated through consultation with a senior expert [14].

Study design

For this study, we enrolled the participants in the screening arm of the ESECC trial who underwent the baseline LCE and were pathologically diagnosed with no dysplasia (including normal, acanthosis, esophagitis, and basal cell hyperplasia) or had no visible LULs as the screened cohort, which were further divided into the ND-LULs and normal-stained groups.

Participants were assigned to the ND-LULs group if they were biopsied for LULs in the esophagus by endoscopists, and their highest pathologic diagnosis was non-dysplasia at baseline. The normal-stained group was defined as participants without any visible LULs in the esophagus during baseline endoscopic examination.

Two control cohorts were adopted to assess the baseline cancer hazard in these screened subjects (Fig. 1). One cohort comprised individuals in the unscreened control arm of the ESECC trial (the RCT control), and the other was an individually matched control cohort using the New Rural Co-operative Medical Care Scheme (NCMS) in Hua County, a medical insurance program covering 99% of local rural residents [15, 16]. Among approximately 1.18 million individuals registered in the NCMS in Hua County in 2012, we first excluded participants who had undergone endoscopic examination in prior research screening cohorts for EC (n = 29,486) [14, 17]. For each individual in the screened cohort, candidate controls were first identified from the NCMS roster and matched by birth year, sex, and village. An identical enrollment date to that of the screened individual was assigned to these controls. After excluding those diagnosed with any cancers or who died before enrollment, we randomly sampled five controls without replacement to form the final population control group.

Covariates assessment

The potential risk factors for ESCC [6, 18–20], including age, sex, body mass index (BMI), family history of ESCC, smoking, eating speed, and ingestion of leftover food, were collected for all participants in the ND-LULs, normal-stained, and RCT control groups by an interviewer-administered electronic questionnaire at baseline [20].

The sociodemographic information of the participants in population control groups was extracted from the NCMS roster, including birth year, age, sex, and village.

Follow-up and outcome ascertainment

The primary outcome was incident SDA cases in the esophagus during follow-up, including severe dysplasia, cancer in situ (CIS), and ESCC.

For groups derived from the ESECC trial (the ND-LULs, normal-stained, and RCT control groups), incident SDAs were identified through endoscopic surveillance or routine follow-up via annual door-to-door active interviews and passive linkage with the NCMS and the Death Surveillance System in Hua County [6, 14]. This follow-up strategy has shown over 95% sensitivity in identifying incident cancer cases [16, 21, 22]. Follow-up ended on May 31, 2023. The most cases detected at re-examination would likely have progressed to clinical-stage ESCC, as our long follow-up period would generally cover their natural course of progression [23].

For the general population controls, incident SDA events were identified using International Classification of Diseases 10th revision codes (ICD-10 code: C15, esophageal cancer) and text-based diagnoses, comprising severe dysplasia, CIS, and ESCC, in the NCMS reimbursement records. The claims-based diagnoses from NCMS in Hua County have been validated previously as highly consistent with hospital records [22]. The follow-up for population control groups ended on December 11, 2021.

The secondary outcome was defined as incident cases of non-upper gastrointestinal cancer (non-UGIC), including all cancer sites except for EC, cardia cancer, gastric cancer, and duodenal cancer.

Statistical analysis

Incidence rates (per 100,000 person-years) were calculated as the number of outcome events divided by corresponding person-years at risk. Follow-up was censored at the date of the outcome event, death, or the end of follow-up, whichever occurred first. Due to the variations in follow-up duration between screened groups and unscreened control groups, the maximum follow-up time in this study was set to 10 years.

Poisson regression was employed to estimate the incidence rate ratios (IRRs) for SDAs among the ND-LULs and normal-stained groups versus controls. When compared with the population control, IRRs were adjusted for potential heterogeneity in baseline cancer risk between groups. The incidence of non-UGIC, which is not affected by endoscopic screening, was used as a proxy for baseline cancer risk. When using the RCT control group as the reference, adjustments were made for ESCC risk factors, including age, sex, BMI, family history of ESCC, smoking, eating speed, and ingestion of leftover food. The missing questionnaire data were imputed using a random forest algorithm.

The cumulative incidence function (CIF) and the Fine-Gray test were employed to evaluate the cumulative incidence of SDAs in the esophagus among the ND-LULs group, the normal-stained group, and the control groups, treating death as a competing risk.

Subgroup analyses were conducted by age and sex. Further stratified analyses were performed within the ND-LULs group across sociodemographic characteristics, including sex, age, BMI, family history of ESCC, and smoking. Two additional sensitivity analyses were performed. First, we conducted a multivariate competing risk model adjusted for baseline ESCC risk factors within the ND-LULs, normal-stained, and RCT control groups. Second, we depicted the trends of annual cumulative IRR for SDAs over 10 years among ND-LULs and normal-stained groups, compared with the two control groups using Poisson regression.

All tests were two-sided, with a significance level of 0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.4.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Baseline characteristics

Among 15,266 participants who underwent the baseline chromoendoscopy, 1143 (7.48%) were excluded for having dysplastic LULs. The remaining 14,938 participants were included in this study, of whom 815 (5.46%) were identified with ND-LULs and 14,123 (94.54%) were in the normal-stained group.

A total of 16,743 participants in the ESECC control arm were enrolled in this study. Compared with the RCT control group, the ND-LULs group tended to be male, older, underweight, smokers, have a family history of ESCC, and eat quickly (Table 1). Similarly, underweight status, family history of ESCC, rapid eating, and less ingestion of leftovers were reported more frequently in the normal-stained group (Table 1).

Incidence rates of esophageal malignancy in the ND-LULs, normal-stained, and control groups

During a median follow-up of 7.77 [7.01, 9.10] years, a total of 813 SDA cases were identified among all participants in this study (Table 2). The incidence rate per 100,000 person-years was 370.79 (95% CI: 244.35–539.48) in the ND-LULs group, 43.03 (95% CI: 32.33–56.15) in the normal-stained group, 81.03 (95% CI: 66.34, 98.00) in the RCT control group, 192.87 (95% CI: 148.54, 246.30) in the general population control for ND-LULs group, and 104.00 (95% CI: 95.57, 112.96) in the general population control for normal-stained group, respectively (Table 2).

Discussion

Endoscopic screening is an effective strategy to reduce mortality from ESCC, as indicated by our ESECC trial [6] and prospective cohort studies [4, 24]. An optimal surveillance strategy is essential to secure the effectiveness of screening. The existing surveillance strategy is primarily established according to the pathologic diagnosis at the index endoscopy, where individuals with ND-LULs and normal-stained epithelium are both considered “safe” [7, 10, 25] and subject to a 10-year re-examination interval as indicated by Li et al. in a recent report [10]. However, in this study, we found that individuals with ND-LULs, a subgroup of the traditional “safe” population, show a considerably increased risk of malignant progression 2–3 years after the initial screening. Meanwhile, individuals with normal-stained epithelium exhibit a lower risk of having esophageal malignancy over 10 years. These results underscore the heterogeneity regarding the malignant potential between individuals with ND-LULs and normal-stained epithelium, demonstrating that Lugol’s staining status can serve as a complementary index for identifying the screening-negative populations for ESCC, among non-dysplastic individuals.

Lugol’s iodine, which reacts with glycogen in the squamous epithelium of the esophagus, was first applied in ESCC screening in the 1970s [26]. The presence of LULs serves as an indicator of glycogen depletion, possibly driven by high proliferation rates and limited cell differentiation [27]. Our previous studies found that individuals with ND-LULs are associated with a higher risk of developing malignancy than those without visible LULs [12], and the risk increases along with larger sizes of LULs [11, 13]. A substantial burden of genomic alterations related to ESCC has already occurred in ND-LULs, before any detectable histologic changes [13]. In this study, we further assessed the long-term progression risk of individuals with ND-LULs and normal-stained epithelium by taking advantage of two control sets derived from a well-designed RCT study and the entire local population simultaneously. We found that compared to the unscreened population, ND-LULs are associated with a higher risk of malignant progression. In contrast, normal-stained group predicts a lower risk of having esophageal malignancy over 10 years, corresponding to only 52% of the incident rate in the RCT control group and 36% of that in the general population matched by age and sex. These results suggest that individuals with ND-LULs still have considerable potential for malignant progression, while those with normal-stained epithelium should be the genuinely negative population in ESCC screening, rather than those without pathological abnormalities.

It is noteworthy that although individuals with ND-LULs accounted for only 5.46% of the pathology-negative population, they contributed one-third (33.33%) of cases that progressed to esophageal malignancy within 10 years, with a cumulative incidence rate over eight times higher than that of the normal-stained population. Due to the relatively low proportion of ND-LULs among histologically negative individuals, they remained unrecognized. Consequently, specific surveillance recommendations for this subpopulation have not been yet included in current guidelines [7]. Our results revealed a notable heterogeneity between ND-LULs and normal-stained groups, which is crucial for establishing more protective screening and surveillance strategies for ESCC.

The re-examination interval is the other crucial element in forming a surveillance strategy [28]. For normal-stained individuals, although the cumulative incidence increased slowly, it remained consistently lower than that of the general population over 10 years. For individuals with ND-LULs, the cumulative incidence was initially low, but after 2–3 years, it surpassed the general population and continued to rise thereafter. These results indicate that the re-examination interval should differ for normal-stained and ND-LULs groups, with more intensive surveillance recommended for ND-LULs.

Thus, we proposed a modified surveillance strategy considering both pathologic diagnosis and staining status (Fig. 3). For normal-stained individuals at the index endoscopic examination, a 10-year re-screening interval is suitable. Those with ND-LULs are recommended to undergo intensive surveillance, with the first follow-up at 2–3 years after the initial screening. Decisions on subsequent surveillance should be guided by individuals’ current risk, based on endoscopic features [11, 29] and pathological findings [12]. Future prospective parallel-controlled evaluations and real-world studies are warranted to validate the benefits of this strategy. Besides, given the considerable workload and cost required for intensive follow-up, future efforts should focus on optimizing this surveillance strategy to improve its cost-effectiveness. In particular, developing predictive models to identify high-risk subgroups among ND-LULs by integrating risk factors for malignant progression could help reduce unnecessary endoscopic re-examinations while maintaining the protective effect of surveillance.

Notably, the clinical application of LCE has been largely limited by iodine-related adverse effects, such as heartburn and allergic reactions. Our updated strategy, incorporating staining status under LCE, is particularly appropriate for high-risk ESCC screening settings where LCE is recommended by the current Chinese screening guidelines, while in routine clinical practice its use should be determined through shared decision-making between clinicians and patients.

To our knowledge, this is the first prospective study to assess the incidence of esophageal malignancy, within the subgroup of the negative population defined by the current screening guideline in China, with up to 10 years of follow-up. As compared to the previous report [10], the major strength of this study is that we adopted two rigorous control sets to evaluate the baseline incidence of esophageal cancer in these screened individuals. The RCT control group following an identical cluster randomization and recruitment procedure provides a high-quality unscreened cohort, which is highly comparable in sociodemographic characteristics and risk behaviors to the screened subjects and is followed in parallel with them. Individualized questionnaire investigations further provide detailed information on potential ESCC risk factors for multivariable adjustment. Moreover, we also generated another control cohort with a large sample size and ideal population representativeness after applying individual-level matching for age, sex, and residence village from the whole population of Hua County using NCMS reimbursement records, to validate the findings from the RCT control. More importantly, we standardized the baseline risk of having cancers between ND-LULs, normal-stained groups, and their matched general population by adjusting for the incidence risk of non-UGIC, an agent of other cancer-related confounders.

Our study still had several limitations. First, this is a single-center study conducted in a high-risk area of ESCC in China. Future multicenter studies are required to verify our findings. Second, individual-level data on potential confounding factors and endoscopic screening experience are unavailable for our general population controls, limiting the adjustment in this group. However, the main conclusion yielded from the population controls was consistent with those from the RCT controls, indicating the validity of our findings. In addition, the possibility that participants in the population control group underwent pre-enrollment endoscopic examinations cannot be ruled out due to the lack of individual data. Nonetheless, this proportion is extremely low according to our previous evaluation [30] and would not substantially affect the main conclusions in this study. Third, the higher incidence observed in the ND-LULs group may be overestimated because of surveillance bias arising from more intensive endoscopic follow-up than in the control groups. Besides, the limited sample size and number of events in the ND-LULs group may restrict the stability of our findings. Larger cohort studies with longer follow-up are necessary to further validate the main conclusion of this study.

Conclusions

In conclusion, individuals with normal Lugol staining, rather than those with no dysplasia in pathology, should be considered as the genuinely negative population in the screening for ESCC. A 10-year re-screening interval is acceptable for normal-stained individuals, but endoscopic surveillance 2–3 years after the initial screening is necessary for ND-LULs. These findings are essential for the establishment of a more efficient and protective surveillance strategy in the screening for ESCC.

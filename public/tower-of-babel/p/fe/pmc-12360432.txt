Rational Maps for System Identification
Authors: Rajiv Singh, Tianyu Dai, Mario Sznaier, Lennart Ljung
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe consider the problem of identification of time-varying, and nonlinear systems from measurements of its inputs and outputs over a chosen time frame. We present the rational maps in the time, frequency, and correlation domain as an effective way of identifying such systems. As we show, their use leads to computationally efficient algorithms while providing great flexibility in capturing complex phenomena.Keywords: rational approximation, nonlinear system identification, linear parameter varying

INTRODUCTION

We explore the choice of suitable model structures for representing time-varying and/or nonlinear dynamic phenomena. Popular model structures (Sjoberg et al., 1995; Schoukens and Ljung, 2019) generally require a large number of measurements and often lead to intractable non-convex search algorithms. However, some inspiration can be taken from the instrumental-variable (IV) based approaches and the frequency-domain identification methods. These are most commonly employed for the identification of linear time-invariant systems, although time-varying/nonlinear extensions have also been considered (Chen, 2015; Rijlaarsdam et al., 2017). These methods tend to compress the measurements by mapping them to a correlation or frequency domain. Also they often lead to tractable estimation algorithms. In this regard, a significant recent advancement has been the use of rational approximation methods for identifying (linear) transfer functions in frequency domain. They draw upon the rational interpolation theory (in real or complex domain) to construct algorithms that are very fast and accurate. The effort in this paper to offer some ways of exploiting the rational polynomial functions for nonlinear and/or time-varying systems.

As we demonstrate, using rational maps lends us great flexibility in capturing complex dynamic relationships including structure-changing phenomena. Use of parameter-dependent forms further allows the creation of prediction models.

RATIONAL POLYNOMIAL MAPS

Rational approximations have been effective methods of describing dynamic systems (Chui and Chen, 1994). They have been employed successfully for many system theory and control applications including model-order reduction, and fitting transfer functions in the frequency domain (Ball et al., 1998; Antoulas et al., 2020). IDENTIFICATION OF PARAMETER-VARYING MODELS

Linearization of nonlinear systems over a trajectory/grid of operating conditions results in parameter-varying linear/affine approximations of the system. Another important entry point is the transformation of time-varying models such as (12) into parameter-varying ones which enables the use of these models for extrapolation purposes.

In a linear parameter-varying (LPV) model the coefficients of the model vary as a function of its inputs and sometimes also the states (called the quasi-LPV form). Typically a reduced-dimension projection of the model’s states and inputs is derived (called scheduling variables) in order to keep the resulting parameter map simple and the model tractable for engineering use. Let ρ(t)=f(Φ(t)) represent the set of scheduling parameters. We have:

We consider 3 forms of this projection - linear, radial, and tensor-product. In the first two, the regressors are projected to a scalar scheduling variable, while the third one uses nr variables.

Ridge construction

ρ(t)=αT(Φ(t)−Φ‾) where α is an unknown coefficient vector such that ‖α‖1=1, and Φ‾ is mean of the regressors. This form is commonly employed in feed-forward neural networks, such as the one hidden layer Sigmoid network used in the nonlinear ARX models (Schoukens and Ljung, 2019). They offer certain amount of directional selectivity even though ρ(t) is constant along the hyperplane defined by chosen α.

Radial construction

ρ(t)=exp−β‖Φ(t)−Φ‾‖22, where β>0 is a hyperparameter. This form imposes a Gaussian similarity measure between two regressors. Radial forms offer directional homogeneity in the regressor space. They are popular candidates for neural network architectures. For use as LPV scheduling variables, we can simplify the representation by using only a subset of available regressors.

Tensor product form

ρ(t)=∏i=1nrΦi(t)−Φ¯i. By picking regressors of different nature, this form allows the creation of functions that behave differently in different directions. In general, these forms do not scale well with the dimension of the regressor space. See Antoulas et al. (2020); Schoukens and Ljung (2019).

CONCLUSIONS AND DISCUSSIONS

This paper presented some ways of extending/generalizing the available tools for modeling non-stationary and/or nonlinear processes by use of rational polynomial maps. These maps afford the use of efficient estimation methods facilitating rapid iterations when evaluating the various candidate model features. They enable the creation of global Θ(t) maps for time-varying processes and can help detect structural changes in the model. There is also a rather straightforward path towards the creation of parameter-dependent models, a form that bestows the predictive ability to these models.

While the results are exciting, there are many open problems to explore. The optimal dimension and form of the domain variable ρ is not always clear. For a chosen form, the tuning of the hyper-parameters can potentially be expensive. The rational forms can also be too flexible affecting their reliability/generalizability. While some constraints based on the order and the spectral norm were proposed, more application-specific regularizations need to be explored.

NuPOWER (Nuwiq for Perioperative management Of patients With haemophilia A on Emicizumab Regular prophylaxis): protocol for an open-label, single-arm, multicentre study
Authors: Alok Srivastava, Angela Kanny, Florian Langer, Cornelia Kubicek-Hofmann, María Teresa Álvarez Román, Ramiro Núñez Vázquez, Ana Boban, Violeta Dejanova-Ilijevska, Predrag Miljić, Jessica Garcia, Susan Halimeh, Nicolas Drillaud, Jean-Baptiste Valentin, Maria Elisa Mancuso, Giancarlo Castaman, Rita Carlotta Santoro, Anna-Elina Lehtinen, Aby Abraham, Mariko Hashimoto, Sigurd Knaub
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionDespite the known haemostatic action of emicizumab (Hemlibra) in haemophilia A patients, its role in the prevention and control of bleeding in high-demand haemostatic situations, such as major surgery, remains to be determined. Patients receiving regular emicizumab prophylaxis often require concomitant factor VIII (FVIII) therapy during major surgery to prevent uncontrolled bleeding and to promote postoperative healing. However, there are limited prospective surgical data relating to concomitant FVIII and emicizumab use. Simoctocog alfa (Nuwiq) is a B-domain deleted recombinant FVIII produced in a human cell line without chemical modification or protein fusion with proven efficacy as surgical prophylaxis in adult and paediatric patients. The Nuwiq for Perioperative management Of patients With haemophilia A on Emicizumab Regular prophylaxis (NuPOWER) study aims to examine perioperative efficacy and safety of simoctocog alfa in haemophilia A patients on emicizumab prophylaxis undergoing major surgery.Methods and analysisNuPOWER is a prospective, open-label, single-arm, multicentre study that will be conducted at approximately 15 centres worldwide. Up to 28 male patients ≥12 years with severe haemophilia A and no FVIII inhibitors will be recruited. All patients must be receiving regular emicizumab prophylaxis and scheduled to undergo a major surgical procedure during which concomitant simoctocog alfa will be administered. The primary endpoint is the overall haemostatic efficacy of simoctocog alfa, adjudicated by an independent data monitoring committee using a pre-defined algorithm, and will consider intraoperative and postoperative efficacy assessments by the surgeon and investigator, respectively. Secondary endpoints include intraoperative haemostatic efficacy, postoperative haemostatic efficacy, number of allogeneic blood products transfused, perioperative FVIII plasma levels (as measured by FVIII activity) and thrombin generation, and safety parameters. In the era of non-factor therapy, NuPOWER will generate valuable prospective data on concomitant use of simoctocog alfa and emicizumab prophylaxis in patients with severe haemophilia A undergoing major surgery.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval has been received from institutional review boards/independent ethics committees, and the study will be conducted in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki. This work will be disseminated by publication of peer-reviewed manuscripts and presentations at scientific meetings.Trial registration numberCT EU 2022-502060-21-00; NCT05935358.

Introduction

In patients with haemophilia A with a severe bleeding phenotype, prophylaxis is recommended to prevent bleeding together with additional therapy to manage patients in acute settings if breakthrough bleeds occur or perioperative haemostatic cover is required.1 Factor VIII (FVIII) therapy has been used for many decades to correct the underlying FVIII deficiency in haemophilia A patients and is effective in both preventive and acute settings.1 2 More recently, emicizumab (Hemlibra, Roche), a bispecific monoclonal antibody that mimics the role of FVIII in haemostasis, was approved for prophylaxis in haemophilia A patients with or without FVIII inhibitors.1 2 However, despite the known haemostatic action of emicizumab, its effectiveness in the prevention and control of bleeding in high-demand haemostatic situations such as major surgery remains to be determined. Emicizumab is not indicated for acute bleeding control, and patients on emicizumab prophylaxis may still require concomitant therapy to treat breakthrough bleeding or to provide perioperative haemostatic cover, particularly for major surgery.135 In these settings, concomitant FVIII concentrate treatment is recommended in patients without FVIII inhibitors.4 6 7 However, published reports on the use of concomitant FVIII replacement therapy during surgery in haemophilia A patients on emicizumab prophylaxis are limited.3810 Consequently, treatment guidance4 6 7 is evolving and comprehensive treatment recommendations have not yet been established.

Simoctocog alfa (Nuwiq, Octapharma) is a B-domain deleted recombinant FVIII concentrate produced in a human cell line without chemical modification or protein fusion.11 12 The efficacy and safety of simoctocog alfa in the prevention and treatment of bleeding, and as surgical prophylaxis, have been extensively demonstrated in clinical trials in adult and paediatric patients with severe haemophilia A.13 14 In the surgical setting, the efficacy of simoctocog alfa was rated as ‘excellent’ or ‘good’ in 51 of 52 (98%) surgical procedures across seven clinical trials.14

Here we describe the design of the Nuwiq for Perioperative management Of patients With haemophilia A on Emicizumab Regular prophylaxis (NuPOWER) study, which will examine the perioperative efficacy and safety of simoctocog alfa in patients with severe haemophilia A on emicizumab prophylaxis undergoing major surgery.

Methods and analysis

Design

NuPOWER is a phase IV, prospective, open-label, single-arm, multicentre study that will be conducted at approximately 15 study centres worldwide (trial registration numbers: CT EU 2022-502060-21-00; NCT05935358). In India, as simoctocog alfa has not yet been approved, it will be a phase III study. A maximum of 28 male patients with severe haemophilia A and no FVIII inhibitors undergoing scheduled major surgery will be recruited, with the aim of having 26 evaluable surgeries (each patient can have only one surgery during the study). All patients must be receiving regular prophylaxis with emicizumab and be scheduled to undergo a major surgical procedure during which concomitant simoctocog alfa will be administered. Surgical procedures will be considered major if any of the following criteria are met: (1) requires general or spinal anaesthesia; (2) requires opening into the great body cavities; (3) orthopaedic interventions involving joints (ankle, knee, hip, wrist, elbow and shoulder); or (4) surgeries/conditions in which the life of the patient is at stake.

An independent data monitoring committee (IDMC) has been established for the study by the sponsor and will provide independent advice to the sponsor. The IDMC is composed of three recognised experts in the field of haematology who are not actively recruiting patients, and a biostatistician. The IDMC will review relevant data periodically during the study and will give advice on the continuation, modification or termination of the study. They will also adjudicate the overall haemostatic efficacy assessment of the perioperative prophylaxis with simoctocog alfa.

The study began in the fourth quarter of 2023 (first patient enrolled) and is scheduled for completion in the fourth quarter of 2025. The end of the study is defined as the last visit of the last patient participating in the study.

Patient eligibility

Due to the small number of female patients with severe haemophilia A,15 and even smaller sample size of individuals receiving emicizumab prophylaxis, NuPOWER will only enrol male patients. This is in line with the approach taken for other trials reported within the GENA clinical trial programme for simoctocog alfa13 and will provide a more homogenous population for the analyses.

Male patients (≥12 years) with severe haemophilia A (FVIII activity (FVIII:C) <1 international unit (IU)/dL, or <1%) who have been previously treated with FVIII concentrates for at least 150 exposure days and have no documented current or past FVIII inhibitors (≥0.6 Bethesda units (BU)/mL) can be enrolled. All patients must be on regular prophylaxis with emicizumab, using dosing consistent with the prescribing information, for at least 1 month prior to the scheduled major surgical procedure during which concomitant simoctocog alfa will be administered. The secondary endpoints of the study are intraoperative and postoperative haemostatic efficacy of simoctocog alfa (4-point ordinal scale; table 2), number of perioperative allogeneic blood products (red blood cells, platelets and other blood products) transfused, perioperative FVIII plasma levels (as measured by FVIII activity), thrombin generation, and haemostatic efficacy assessed using the 4-point scale recommended by the World Federation of Hemophilia (WFH),1 the incidence of adverse events, the incidence of thrombotic events and the incidence of FVIII inhibitor formation.

Additional exploratory endpoints are the perioperative plasma levels of ‘FVIII-like’ activity of emicizumab and emicizumab antigen, and overall haemostatic efficacy (‘success’ or ‘failure’; primary endpoint) according to achievement of target FVIII plasma levels. A patient is considered to reach this target if the mean of all FVIII post-injection peak plasma values between the first preoperative assessment and adequate wound healing is >80 IU/dL.

Interventions

Surgical prophylaxis with simoctocog alfa will be administered intravenously in accordance with the relevant prescribing information,16 17 using the same dosing as patients not on emicizumab. This dosing was considered to be appropriate given that there is a non-additive effect of FVIII use in combination with emicizumab, due to a lower binding affinity of emicizumab to factor IXa (FIXa) and factor X (FX),4 18 with displacement of emicizumab from binding sites occurring in the presence of FVIII.4 Preoperative and postoperative FVIII plasma levels should be between 80 IU/dL and 100 IU/dL, with this considered to be the target trough level range. Trough values over 100 IU/dL are acceptable, as may happen in some patients, and will be used to guide lowering of subsequent dosing to achieve the target trough range. Treatment will be repeated as necessary every 8–24 hours until adequate wound healing, and then, if required, for at least another 7 days to maintain FVIII plasma levels between 30 IU/dL and 60 IU/dL, as the target trough level range (figure 2).

The concomitant administration of therapies (including thromboprophylactic medications) not interfering with the objectives of the study is permitted; however, no FVIII concentrates other than simoctocog alfa must be administered (except for emergency situations).

Study procedures

The study begins at the screening visit and lasts until approximately 30 days after the day of the surgical procedure or at the day of discharge (whichever comes last). The actual duration of the study for the individual patient will depend on the timing of the screening visit, the length of time between screening and elective surgery, and the duration of the postoperative period. There should be a minimum of 6 consecutive visits or observation days during the postoperative period.

Demographic and baseline characteristics (sex, age, ethnic origin, blood group, height, weight and body mass index) will be recorded along with medical/treatment history at the screening visit. Blood samples will be drawn for routine chemistry and haematology analysis at the screening visit, preoperatively and at the final visit. Blood samples will be drawn for F8 genotype analysis either at the screening visit or at any time during the study.

The assessment of haemostatic efficacy is described in table 2 and figure 1. In addition, overall haemostatic efficacy will also be assessed using a 4-point scale recommended by the WFH.1

Blood samples will be drawn immediately before (≤30 min) and after (15–30 min) each simoctocog alfa injection given before and during surgery to measure perioperative FVIII plasma levels, thrombin generation and emicizumab plasma levels (antigen and ‘FVIII-like’ activity). During the postoperative period, sampling pre-injection and post-injection is only required once per treatment day if multiple doses are given on a single day. FVIII plasma levels will be measured using a validated chromogenic assay (Biophen FVIII:C (Hyphen BioMed, Neuville-sur-Oise, France)) with bovine FX and human FIXa. This assay uses bovine FX rather than human FX, as the former is insensitive to emicizumab. Emicizumab antigen levels will be measured using a validated ELISA which uses two different engineered monovalent Fab antibodies (AbD36192-Fab-Sx2 for capture, and Fab AbD36205-ad for analyte detection). ‘FVIII-like’ activity for emicizumab will be measured by a validated chromogenic assay using human coagulation factors FIXa and FX (Biophen FVIII:C), with addition of FVIII inhibitor (sheep anti-human FVIII polyclonal antibody) to negate the effect of any FVIII in the sample. Thrombin generation will be measured by the Thrombinoscope system (Stago Deutschland GmbH, Düsseldorf, Germany), a validated thrombin generation assay, with thrombin generation triggered by a mixture containing 4 µM synthetic phospholipids and 1 pM tissue factor in platelet-poor plasma.

Adverse events, including thrombotic events, will be recorded during the perioperative period up to the final visit. Blood samples for FVIII inhibitor measurement will be taken preoperatively, and at the final visit, and analysed by chromogenic assay using bovine coagulation factors. The use of concomitant medications, including emicizumab and thromboprophylaxis, and adjunct treatment, such as antifibrinolytics, will be recorded throughout the study.

All samples will be analysed at the following central laboratories: Laboratorium für Klinische Forschung GmbH, Germany; and University Clinic Bonn, Germany—F8 genotype only. Samples may also be measured at local laboratories in accordance with standard procedures of the individual treatment centres, for example, perioperative FVIII plasma levels to provide guidance on perioperative FVIII dosing. Only central laboratory results will be used for the analyses of the study endpoints.

Statistical methods and analyses

The primary aim of the study is to prove that overall perioperative haemostatic efficacy is achieved with simoctocog alfa (defined as an overall efficacy rating of ‘success’ by the IDMC) with a probability of >60% in major surgeries. Assuming a true success rate of 85%, and using an exact one-sided binomial test at a one-sided significance level of 2.5%, a total sample size of n=26 would be needed to achieve a statistical power of >80%. For the primary statistical analysis, an exact one-sided binomial test for the proportion of successful surgeries will be used and the primary endpoint met if the corresponding one-sided p value is below 0.025. In addition, a two-sided exact 95% confidence interval for the true proportion will be calculated. A similar analysis will be performed for the secondary endpoints of intraoperative haemostatic efficacy (surgeon and IDMC), postoperative haemostatic efficacy (investigator and IDMC) and overall haemostatic efficacy (surgeon and investigator combined (without IDMC adjudication); WFH criteria). Other secondary endpoints (efficacy and safety) and exploratory endpoints (emicizumab plasma levels—antigen and ‘FVIII-like’ activity) will be analysed descriptively. A McNemar test will be used to assess the exploratory endpoint on the relationship between achievement of target FVIII plasma levels and overall haemostatic efficacy.

Ethics and dissemination

NuPOWER will be conducted in compliance with the protocol and its amendments, the International Council for Harmonisation Guideline for Good Clinical Practice E6 (R2) (EMA/CHMP/ICH/135/1995), applicable regulatory requirements and the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The study protocol and any subsequent amendment(s), a sample of the patient information and informed consent form, any other materials provided to the patients and any additional requested information will be submitted to the competent independent ethics committee/institutional review board (IRB) and regulatory authorities for approval. Ethical approval was received from all participating countries and the study was reviewed and approved in accordance with European Union clinical trial (EU-CT) regulations (CT EU 2022-502060-21-00). The approving ethics committees/IRBs for each country are: Croatia (Central Ethics Committee, Agency for Medicinal Products and Medical Devices of Croatia (HALMED)); Finland (National Committee on Medical Research Ethics (Tukija)); France (Committee for the Protection of the Persons (CPP)); Germany (Landesamt für Gesundheit und Soziales); Italy (Comitato Etico Lazio Area 5); Spain (CEIm Parc de Salut MAR); India (Institutional Ethics Committee, St. John’s Medical College Hospital (IEC/1/135/2025); IRB of Christian Medical College of Vellore (IRB Min, No: 16 191)); Macedonia (Ethics Committee for Clinical Trials Related to Medicines and Medical Devices (Request No: 13-5959/1)); Serbia (Ethics Committee of Serbia (GENA-22)); UK (South Central – Hampshire A Research Ethics Committee (IRAS ID: 1 008 535)); and USA (WCG IRB (IRB Tracking No. 20 232 963)).

Written consent from each participant or the parent(s)/legal guardian(s) of participants will be obtained by the investigator. An example of the participant consent form template is provided in the online supplemental file 1. The results are planned to be published in a peer-reviewed journal and presented at scientific meetings. Investigators at each study site will be responsible for maintaining all records, and source records will be kept for 25 years or a longer period of time, if required by local regulations. The sponsor and investigators will ensure patient confidentiality is preserved through appropriate technical and organisational measures.

Patients have the right to withdraw from the study at any time for any reason, without any resulting detriment and without the need to justify their decision. The investigator also has the right to withdraw patients in case of adverse events, poor compliance or other reasons. The reason for, and all details regarding patient withdrawal, will be captured in the case report form by the investigator.

Patient and public involvement

There has been no public or patient involvement in the design of NuPOWER. Results will be distributed to participants via the investigators who will receive the study report. Wider dissemination of details of the study will be available to the public and patients through inclusion of a plain language summary (online supplemental file 2) and an infographic (online supplemental file 3).

Discussion

A number of publications have reported on real-world experience with the use of FVIII during surgery in haemophilia A patients on emicizumab.3 19 20 However, prospective data from clinical trials on concomitant perioperative use of FVIII and emicizumab are needed to optimise haemostatic control for haemophilia A patients undergoing surgery. The NuPOWER study will prospectively examine in a multicentre design the efficacy of simoctocog alfa in patients with severe haemophilia A on emicizumab prophylaxis undergoing elective major surgery. Detailed data on the intraoperative and postoperative haemostatic efficacy of simoctocog alfa will be collected, in addition to monitoring perioperative FVIII plasma levels, emicizumab plasma levels (antigen and ‘FVIII-like’ activity), thrombin generation and safety information, including monitoring thrombotic events and FVIII inhibitor development.

Previous data have shown simoctocog alfa to be effective as surgical prophylaxis in adult and paediatric patients. In a pooled analysis of seven clinical studies, the overall (intraoperative and postoperative) haemostatic coverage of simoctocog alfa in previously treated patients (PTPs) with severe haemophilia A was rated as excellent or good in 98% of overall surgeries and 96% of major surgeries.14 There were no serious treatment-related side effects and no patients developed FVIII inhibitors.14 Similarly, in a study of previously untreated patients, simoctocog alfa was rated as excellent or good as surgical prophylaxis in 95% of procedures.21 In PTPs, the mean half-life of simoctocog alfa was reported to be 17.1 hours in adults and adolescents, 13.1 hours in children aged 6–12 years, and 11.9 hours in children aged 2–5 years.16

Although concomitant FVIII treatment is recommended for haemophilia A patients without inhibitors undergoing major surgery while on regular emicizumab prophylaxis,4 6 7 there are few published reports of surgery in these patients and available data are limited. A recent summary from the HAVEN 1–4 clinical trials in patients on emicizumab prophylaxis, both with and without FVIII inhibitors, found that 15 of 18 major surgeries were managed with prophylactic FVIII concentrate, with three of these patients experiencing postoperative bleeds.8 In a recent real-world study involving five major surgeries in four patients without inhibitors and one patient with inhibitors, patients were well managed with planned preoperative and postoperative FVIII concentrate; one non-inhibitor patient experienced a bleeding episode 8 days after surgery.3 Additional real-world data have demonstrated that six major surgeries in five non-inhibitor patients were well managed with preoperative and postoperative FVIII concentrate.9 While both clinical trial and real-life studies provide useful indications for the management of additional FVIII prophylaxis during surgery, perioperative care is usually at the discretion of the physician and there are no established guidelines for concomitant FVIII treatment with emicizumab during surgery.1 8

In the GENA clinical trial programme for simoctocog alfa, which comprised seven prospective clinical studies in 201 adult and paediatric PTPs with severe haemophilia A, the median total dose of simoctocog alfa was 591 IU/kg per major procedure.14 For patients on emicizumab prophylaxis who require surgery, recent studies have shown there is no consensus on dosing levels of additional haemostatic agents, with suggestions to take a case-by-case approach.3 19 20 This is in line with the WFH guidelines suggesting FVIII injection at doses expected to achieve haemostasis should be used.1 Similarly, the Italian Association of Haemophilia Centres recommends that the FVIII dose for surgery in patients on emicizumab prophylaxis, at least for the first administration, is the same as that normally used in patients with severe haemophilia A undergoing minor or major surgery.7 In the NuPOWER study, simoctocog alfa will be administered in accordance with the prescribing information.16 17

In this study, by measuring perioperative emicizumab plasma levels (antigen and ‘FVIII-like’ activity) before and after simoctocog alfa injection, these data may shed light on the pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic relationship of emicizumab. NuPOWER will also monitor thrombin generation and thrombotic events throughout the study. To our knowledge, no thrombotic events, thrombotic microangiopathies or deaths have been reported in patients undergoing surgery while receiving emicizumab prophylaxis.3 8 19 20 These studies generally used FVIII dosing regimens similar to those used in patients not on emicizumab. However, pre-clinical experiments with emicizumab and FVIII have demonstrated a potential for hypercoagulability,2 which could lead to a risk of thrombosis with patient use, especially in patients undergoing surgery who receive higher doses of FVIII. An analysis of data from the US Food and Drug Administration Adverse Event Reporting System from 2018 to 2022 found that the frequency of reported thrombotic adverse events—as a proportion of all reported adverse events—was approximately three times higher in patients receiving emicizumab than in those receiving FVIII concentrates.22 Thrombotic events were still twice as common with emicizumab versus FVIII concentrates in analyses when thrombotic events in patients also receiving bypassing agents were excluded.22 This aligns with findings from 2021 from EudraVigilance, a European pharmacovigilance database, which reported that thrombotic adverse events were approximately 2.5 times higher for emicizumab than for extended half-life FVIII products.23 In EudraVigilance, 2 of the 24 thrombotic events in emicizumab patients occurred in patients on concomitant FVIII (without bypassing agents).23

Both surgical intervention and a high dose of FVIII concentrate could be associated with an increased risk of developing inhibitors.1 24 No inhibitors were reported in clinical trials with PTPs receiving simoctocog alfa,13 including during surgical prophylaxis.14 During the NuPOWER study, patients will be screened for FVIII inhibitors both preoperatively and at the final visit. However, as these are PTPs, the risk of inhibitor development is low.

Emicizumab remains the only approved FVIII mimetic for prophylaxis in haemophilia A patients.25 With a number of other FVIII mimetics in the pipeline,25 it is important to better understand the haemostatic effects of combining FVIII mimetics with FVIII concentrates during surgery. The NuPOWER study will establish an evidence base towards this goal.

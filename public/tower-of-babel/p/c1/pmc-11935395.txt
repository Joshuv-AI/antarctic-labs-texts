Discrepancy between pulmonary arterial wedge pressure and left ventricular end diastolic pressure in patients with interstitial lung disease
Authors: Ho Cheol Kim, Onix Cantres Fonseca, Behnam N Tehrani, Christopher S King, Christopher Thomas, Vikramjit Khangoora, Oksana A Shlobin, Steven D Nathan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRight heart catheterization (RHC) is the gold standard for diagnosing pulmonary hypertension (PH) in patients with interstitial lung disease (ILD). However, discrepancies between pulmonary arterial wedge pressure (PAWP) and left ventricular end-diastolic pressure (LVEDP) remain understudied in this population.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective analysis of data from ILD patients who underwent RHC and had concomitant LVEDP measurements. Pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) was calculated using both PAWP and LVEDP. Patients were categorized based on PAWP and LVEDP values using a threshold of 15 mm Hg and PVR values using a threshold of 2 or 3 Wood Units. After that patients were categorized as concordant or discordant if both values were on the same or opposite sides of these thresholds, respectively. A discordantly higher PAWP group (left atrial dysfunction, LAD) was defined as patients with a PAWP-LVEDP difference of more than 3 mm Hg.ResultsAmong 87 ILD patients, 9 patients (10.3%) showed discordance between PAWP and LVEDP. LAD was observed in 12 patients (13.8%) and was associated with lower forced vital capacity (Odd ratio [OR]: 0.956, p = 0.049) and a larger left atrium diameter (OR: 3.205, p = 0.033). Discordance in PVR values was also noted, with potential treatment targets for PH-specific therapy differing in 9 patients (22.0%) depending on whether PAWP or LVEDP was used.ConclusionsThis study highlights the clinical significance of PAWP-LVEDP discrepancies in suspected PH-ILD patients, emphasizing the need for comprehensive assessments incorporating LVEDP and clinical context for accurate diagnosis, risk stratification, and treatment decisions.Keywords: interstitial lung disease, pulmonary hypertension, right heart catheterization, pulmonary arterial wedge pressure, left ventricular end-diastolic pressure

Background

Patients with interstitial lung disease (ILD) may have accompanying pulmonary hypertension (PH) which is associated with increased morbidity and mortality.1 Detecting and diagnosing PH during the clinical course of ILD is crucial for risk stratification, and determining the need for lung transplantation.2, 3 Furthermore, the efficacy and approval of inhaled treprostinil for PH associated with ILD (PH-ILD) highlights the importance of accurately diagnosing PH-ILD.4 In addition, a recent post-hoc analysis of the INCREASE study highlights the benefit of therapy even in those with mild PH-ILD, and thus subtle differences in pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) may impact the institution of therapy.5

As with all forms of PH, right heart catheterization (RHC) is the gold standard for confirmation,6, 7 and it is required for all lung transplant candidates.3 The hemodynamic definition of group 3 pre-capillary PH is based on parameters that include the mean pulmonary arterial pressure (mPAP), pulmonary arterial wedge pressure (PAWP) and the cardiac output from which the PVR is calculated.6, 7 The PVR represents the resistance across the pulmonary vascular circuit which is “book-ended” by the proximal pulmonary artery and the left atrium.8 Hemodynamic parameters representing the left atrial (LA) pressure include PAWP and left ventricular end-diastolic pressure (LVEDP).9 The PAWP is typically used since measuring LVEDP requires additional arterial procedure. However, in cases where it is felt that the PAWP might be inaccurate, the LVEDP may be substituted as a more accurate surrogate.10 Interestingly however, in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction it has previously been shown that PAWP but not LVEDP is associated with outcome.11 Typically, left heart catheterization to rule out coronary artery disease is performed at the same time as a the RHC in lung transplant candidates.3 Previous studies have reported that discordance between PAWP and LVEDP, potentially impacting the diagnosis and classification of PH.12, 13 However, this disparity has not been studied in patients with ILD, Therefore, this study aims to investigate the discrepancies between PAWP and LVEDP and their clinical significance in ILD patients.

Materials and methods

Study population and clinical data

We collected data on patients with ILD who completed a RHC and had available concomitant LVEDP measurements performed at Inova Fairfax Medical Center, between May 2011 and February 2024. Catheterization is performed using standard techniques by ensuring correct assembly of the transducer pressure tubing and adequate flushing. The transducer is placed at the level of the right atrium and zeroed (setting atmospheric pressure as the reference point) with the transducer "off" to the patient and open to air. The transducer height is changed if the patient position changes. As an internal quality control, procedures with a negative diastolic gradient were ruled out. All clinical information collected was obtained during routine patient care. Pulmonary function testing (PFT), the 6-minute walk test (6MWT), and echocardiographic data were collated from within 6 months of the date of the RHC. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board at Inova Fairfax Hospital (IRB U21-02-4389).

Definitions

Patients were categorized according to recognized RHC and LVEDP thresholds. Based on PAWP and LVEDP thresholds of 15 mm Hg, patients were divided into concordant (PAWP and LVEDP agree; both PAWP and LVEDP > 15 mm Hg or both PAWP and LVEDP ≤ 15) and discordant (PAWP and LVEDP disagree; PAWP > 15, LVEDP ≤ 15 or PAWP ≤ 15, LVEDP > 15) groups. The PVR was calculated using both PAWP and LVEDP; specifically, the mPAP minus PAWP or LVEDP, divided by the cardiac output as determined by thermodilution. We evaluated concordance and discordance of the PVR based on thresholds of ≥3 vs <3 and ≥4 vs <4 wood units.

Since there have been several definitions of PH, PH analysis was performed with 2 definitions in this study; the pre-2018 World Symposium definition of precapillary PH (mPAP ≥ 25 mm Hg, with a PVR ≥ 3 and a PAWP ≤ 15 mm Hg),4, 14 and the 7th World Symposium definition of precapillary PH (mPAP > 20 mm Hg, PVR > 2 and a PAWP ≤ 15 mm Hg) which is based on the prior European definition of PH.6, 7 While the 7th World Symposium definition is the current standard, the reason the pre-2018 definition was chosen is because this has implications for the initiation of the only approved therapy for PH-ILD.

In addition, we were interested in the patient subgroup where the PAWP was significantly higher than the LVEDP without regard for any threshold. Interest in this subgroup stems from the concept that this discordance could reflect a resistance gradient across the larger pulmonary veins and/or the left atrium. We defined this discordantly higher PAWP group as patients with a PAWP-LVEDP difference of more than 3 mm Hg and regarded them as having left-atrial dysfunction (LAD).

Statistical analysis

All values for continuous variables are expressed as median (interquartile range) and those for categorical variables as percentages. The Mann-Whitney U-test was used to compare continuous variables, and the Chi-square or Fisher’s exact test was employed to compare categorical variables. The relationships between PAWP and LVEDP were assessed using scatterplots, Pearson's correlation coefficient, and linear regression. Logistic regression was performed to identify risk factors for the discordant group (between PAWP and LVEDP) and discordantly higher PAWP. P values were 2-tailed, with cut-off of statistical significance being set as p < 0.05. All statistical analyses were completed using SPSS 23.0 and MEDCALC 8.1.0.0.

Results

Patient demographics and left-sided pressures

A total of 87 patients with ILD who underwent RHC and had available LVEDP data were included in the analysis. The median age of the patients was 60.0 years, 67.8% were men, and 69.0% were diagnosed with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). The demographics and baseline characteristics of the cohort are shown in Table 1. The mean difference between PAWP minus LVEDP was –1.2 mm Hg [95% CI: −10.8 to 8.3 mm Hg], with a wide range (−11 to 24 mm Hg, Figure 1-A). The 2 measurements were moderately correlated (r = 0.4404, r2 = 0.185, p < 0.001, Figure 1-B). PAWP and LVEDP were weakly correlated in patients with mPAP >20 mm Hg (r = 0.3933, r2 = 0.138, p = 0.003, Figure S1-A) or ≥25 mm Hg (r = 0.3351, r2 = 0.090, p = 0.032, Figure S1-B).

Concordant vs discordant PAWP and LVEDP values

Based on PAWP and LVEDP values, using a threshold of 15 mm Hg, 9 patients (10.3%) were classified as discordant group (PAWP > 15, LVEDP ≤ 15 or PAWP ≤ 15, LVEDP > 15, Figure 2-A and Figure 2-B). In 7 out of 9 patients, LVEDP was higher than PAWP, while in 2 patients, PAWP was higher than LVEDP. The PAWP-LVEDP values for these 2 patients were 15 and 24 mm Hg, respectively, confirming that they had LAD. There was no statistical difference in pulmonary function or 6-minute walk test between the discordant group and the concordant group (Table 2). However, the discordant group had statistically higher RA pressure (9 vs 5 mm Hg, p = 0.034), PAWP (13 vs 10 mm Hg, p = 0.031) and LVEDP (16 vs 11 mm Hg, p = 0.026) values compared to the concordant group (Table 2). Also, the mPAP value tended to be higher in the discordant group (27 vs 23 mm Hg, p = 0.070) compared to the concordant group. On logistic analysis, only higher PAWP (odds ratio [OR], 1.176; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.025–1.350; p = 0.021) independently predicted discordant group (Table S1).

Concordant vs discordant PVRs

PVR ≥3 vs <3

There were 39.1% of patients (n = 34/87) with PVR ≥3 by PAWP, and 36.8% of patients (n = 32/87) with PVR ≥3 by LVEDP. A total of 14 patients (16.1%) were classified as discordant group (PVR by PAWP ≥ 3, PVR by LVEDP < 3 or PVR by PAWP ≥ 3, PVR by LVEDP < 3).

Additionally, the PVR calculation employing the PAWP and LVEDP demonstrated discordance in PVR values with a threshold of 3 in 40.0% (n = 6/15) of patients with FVC ≤40% and 12.3% (n = 8/65) of patients with FVC >40% (Figure S2-A).

PVR ≥4 vs <4

When calculating PVR using PAWP, 24.1% of patients (n = 21/87) met a PVR ≥4, whereas when calculating PVR using LVEDP, 25.3% of patients (n = 22/87) met this PVR threshold. A total of 5 patients (5.7%) were classified as discordant group (PVR by PAWP ≥ 4, PVR by LVEDP < 4 or PVR by PAWP < 4, PVR by LVEDP ≥ 4).

Impact of PAWP and LVEDP on precapillary PH definitions

Pre-2018 definition of precapillary PH

Fifty eight patients (66.7%) did not meet the diagnostic criteria, and 20 patients (23.0%) met the criteria for the diagnosis of PH by both PAWP and LVEDP (Figure 3). Six patients (6.9%) met the PH diagnostic criteria only with PAWP and 3 patients (3.4%) met the PH diagnostic criteria only with LVEDP. Figure 4 shows the relationship between PAWP, LVEDP, and PVR for patients with a mPAP ≥25 mm Hg (n = 41). A total of 20 patients (48.8%) met the criteria for PVR ≥3 and PAWP or LVEDP ≤15 mm Hg. Four patients (9.8%) who qualified as having PH with a PVR ≥3 and PAWP ≤15 mm Hg, had a PVR <3 when calculated using LVEDP. Similarly, 3 patients (7.3%) with PVR ≥3 by LVEDP (and with LVEDP ≤ 15 mm Hg) had PVRs <3 when calculated using PAWP. In 2 patients (4.9%), PVR was >3 using both PAWP and LVEDP, but PAWP was ≤15 mm Hg while LVEDP was >15 mm Hg, leading to their reclassification as postcapillary PH.

Seventh World Symposium definition of precapillary PH

Based on the 7th World Symposium definition of precapillary PH, 44 patients (50.6%) did not meet the diagnostic criteria and 32 patients (36.8%) met the criteria for the diagnosis of PH by both PAWP and LVEDP (Figure 3). Nine patients (10.3%) met the PH diagnostic criteria only with PAWP and 2 patients (2.3%) met the PH diagnostic criteria only with LVEDP.

Discordantly higher PAWP group and their risk factors

A total of 12 patients (13.8%) were classified as having LAD (defined as PAWP-LVEDP ≥ 3 mm Hg). A comparison of clinical characteristics between this LAD group and others is shown in Table S2. The LAD group had significant lower forced vital capacity (FVC, 43 vs 59%) compared to the others. In logistic analysis, lower FVC (OR, 0.956; 95% CI, 0.915–1.000; p = 0.049) and higher left atrium diameter (OR, 3.205; 95% CI, 1.100–9.340; p = 0.033) were significant predictor of LAD (Table 3).

Discussion

In this study we examine discrepancies between PAWP and LVEDP measurements in patients with ILD and how this may impact patient categorization in relation to definitions of pulmonary hypertension. We demonstrate that the propensity for discrepant values becomes more evident as PAWP or mPAP increases and discordantly higher PAWP was more frequently observed in patients with lower FVC. We also highlight differences in potential target population for PH-specific therapy when PAWP and LVEDP were used, respectively.

PAWP is important for distinguishing between pre-capillary PH and post-capillary PH,6, 7 and its use is based on the assumption that PAWP and LVEDP are equivalent.15 However, accurately measuring PAWP can be challenging for various reasons.9, 16 Measurement techniques, such as catheter balloon inflation volume can affect PAWP values,17 and the respiratory cycle can influence PAWP.18 This is especially the case in patients with lung disease who are apt to greater pressure swings and lower pleural pressures on inspiration.18 Additionally, obesity and hypoxemia are also known to affect PAWP.19 With these possible explanations, there are many previous studies on the discrepancy between PAWP and LVEPD. Halpern et al., in a study involving 11,523 patients undergoing simultaneous right-heart and left-heart catheterization, including 3,926 patients with PH (mPAP ≥ 25 mm Hg), reported that 53.5% of patients had an LVEDP ≥ 15 mm Hg despite having a PAWP < 15 mm Hg.20 Several previous studies have shown discrepancies between PAWP and LVEDP in various populations,13 including veterans,12 patients with dyspnea during exercise,21 and those with heart failure11 or aortic stenosis.22 Our current study showed a discrepancy between PAWP and LVEDP in ILD patients, with the discrepancy becoming more evident as PAWP and mPAP increases. In these patients, it might be particularly important to confirm LVEDP and consider the clinical context rather than relying solely on PAWP. Since, RHC and LHC are often obtained concomitantly in lung transplant candidates, we believe that there should always be simultaneous PAWP and LVEDP measurements to more accurately guide therapy. Specifically, PH therapy in ILD patients can be used as a “bridge” to lung transplantation with evidence supporting improvement in patient’s functional ability and reducing the likelihood of clinical worsening.4, 23, 24 In our current study, a lower FVC was associated with a discordantly higher PAWP, defined as a PAWP-LVEDP difference greater than 3 mm Hg. Although no current studies have specifically examined the discrepancy between PAWP and LVEDP in ILD patients, Mascherbauer et al. reported that in heart failure patients, lower DLco was associated with a significant difference between PAWP and LVEDP.11 In their study, patients with DLco ≤45% had a higher PAWP-LVEDP value (3.1 ± 4.8 mm Hg vs 0.8 ± 3.8 mm Hg, respectively; p = 0.031), compared to those with DLco >45%. In our study, analyzing DLco was difficult due to many missing values. However, since the discordantly higher PAWP occurred more frequently in patients with lower FVC, one of indicators of ILD severity,25 it might be recommended that patients with more severe ILD have a LVEDP measured in addition to PAWP. This appears to be most pertinent in lung transplant candidates who tend to have the most severe restrictive physiology. LA enlargement assessed by echocardiography was also associated with a discordantly higher PAWP in our current study. It is well-established that heart failure with preserved ejection fraction can result in left atrial enlargement over time.26 In the context of a discordantly higher PAWP, an elevated LVEDP is likely more reflective of the patient’s current volume status, while a discordantly high PAWP reflects post capillary pulmonary venous remodeling, explaining the pressure drop across the stiffened left atrium. In these situations, therefore an elevated PAWP might not reflect patients’ current volume status. Indeed, Baratto et al reported that PAWP slightly overestimated LVEDP during exercise in 47 patients with dyspnea, likely due to concomitant left atrial dysfunction.21 Although we refer to the PAWP > LVEDP group as having left atrial dysfunction, we cannot rule out the possibility that the pressure drop or increased resistance originated from the large pulmonary veins. Although speculative, this might explain why left atrial dysfunction was observed more frequently in patients with the most severe restriction. In these cases, fibrosis might have impinged on the large veins, which needs to be confirmed in precise imaging studies. In addition, we cannot rule out that differences between the PAWP and LVEDP might have been due to residual communication with the proximal pulmonary artery from an incomplete “wedge”, especially in the context of pulmonary artery distension.27

Differences between PAWP and LVEDP may also affect the accurate calculation of PVR,28 which is associated with the definition,6, 7 prognosis, and treatment of PH-ILD.1 The pre-2018 definition of precapillary PH was the definition used for enrollment in the landmark INCREASE study of inhaled treprostinil.4 Indeed, we chose to analyze discordance based on this definition to provide a pragmatic assessment of how miscategorization might impact the implementation of approved therapy. If one considers the discordant groups in the context of the INCREASE study then, there were 4.9% (n = 2/41) of patients who would be denied therapy based on a PAWP >15 mm Hg, but who might have qualified based on a LVEDP ≤15 mm Hg. On the other end of the spectrum there were another 4.9% of patients (n = 2/41) who might not be good candidates for inhaled treprostinil based on a LVEDP >15 mm Hg but who qualified because their PAWP was ≤15 mm Hg. If one considers the PVR hemodynamic component required to qualify for therapy, then there was 1 patient with PVR <3 by PAWP but who would have qualified based on a PVR ≥3 by LVEDP. On the other end of the spectrum, there were 4 patients with PVR ≥3 by PAWP but a PVR <3 by LVEDP. Therefore, decision-making for PH-specific treatment changed in 9 patients (22.0%) when considering PAWP or LVEDP among 41 patients with mPAP ≥25 mm Hg. Whether incorporation and consideration of the LVEDP in decision making will enable improved patient selection for PH specific therapy in PH-ILD will require further study. Therefore, a larger-scale prospective study with longitudinal follow-up is needed to define the role of LVEDP in selecting patients for PH-specific treatment in PH-ILD.

There are several limitations to our current study. First, the study was retrospective, which may have introduced selection bias since it only included patients who had undergone right heart catheterization and who had simultaneous LVEDP measurements. The need for this latter measurement limited the size of the cohort who qualified for this analysis. Second, the PAWP and LVEDP measurements were extracted from the medical records rather than through a manual review of hemodynamic tracings and the tracings were therefore not centrally adjudicated. Thus, we cannot rule out that there might have been technical limitations in measuring the wedge. Third, in contrast to the well-recognized definitions of PH for our concordance/discordance analyses, the cutoff value chosen for discordantly higher PAWP was arbitrary. Finally, there were significant missing values in DLco (n = 53/87), rendering further analysis of this parameter difficult. Despite these limitations, our study has the strength of evaluating the discrepancy between PAWP and LVEDP in ILD patients and to our knowledge represents the first such report.

In conclusion, there may be a discrepancy between PAWP and LVEDP in ILD patients, which influences PH diagnosis, PVR, and the selection of PH-specific treatment target groups. Therefore, obtaining a LVEDP in addition to the PAWP, especially in patients with lower FVCs, may have important management implications in patients with ILD and warrants further study.

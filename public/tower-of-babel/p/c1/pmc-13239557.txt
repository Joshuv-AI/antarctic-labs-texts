Factors Associated With Dupilumab Efficacy in Patients With Chronic Rhinosinusitis With Nasal Polyps
Authors: Shinya Takaishi, Nobuyoshi Otori
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveDupilumab is effective in treating patients with intractable chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (CRSwNP); however, the factors influencing its efficacy remain unclear. Systemic corticosteroids (SCS) are occasionally administered to patients receiving dupilumab; however, concomitant use can obscure the efficacy of dupilumab. The present study aimed to investigate the factors associated with the efficacy of dupilumab, while minimizing the influence of SCS.Study DesignRetrospective longitudinal study.SettingA single academic hospital.MethodsWe retrospectively evaluated medical records of 193 patients who started dupilumab for CRSwNP at our institution between March 2020 and July 2023. Patients who were prescribed SCS even once during a specific period were excluded. Finally, 50 patients were enrolled. We assessed correlations between pre‐treatment parameters (age, time since last sinus surgery, blood eosinophil count, blood eosinophil proportion, and serum total immunoglobulin E) and endpoints (changes in nasal polyp score, nasal congestion score, and Lund‐Mackay computed tomography score [LMK‐CTS] from baseline). Additionally, the endpoints were compared between subgroups (male vs female, presence vs absence of asthma, presence vs absence of nonsteroidal anti‐inflammatory drug‐exacerbated respiratory disease, and three groups classified according to the blood eosinophil proportion).ResultsStatistically, neither significant differences between the subgroups nor significant correlations between the parameters and the endpoints were observed, except for a correlation between the blood eosinophil proportion and the changes in LMK‐CTS (r = 0.47, P = .037 [n = 21]).ConclusionAlthough a higher baseline peripheral blood eosinophil percentage may be associated with smaller changes in LMK‐CTS, our findings need further investigation.

Chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (CRSwNP) is a chronic inflammatory disease of the nasal and paranasal sinuses characterized by long‐term symptoms of rhinosinusitis (nasal congestion, rhinorrhea, postnasal drip, loss of smell, and facial pain or pressure) and the presence of nasal polyps on nasal endoscopy. 1 , 2 Patients with CRSwNP experience not only the persistent symptoms of rhinosinusitis but also sleep disturbance, fatigue, anxiety, and diminished social activities. 2 , 3 Furthermore, issues associated with comorbidities can also lead to exacerbation of asthma, bronchitis, and nonsteroidal anti‐inflammatory drug‐exacerbated respiratory disease (NSAID‐ERD). 2 , 4

Although patients with CRSwNP receive a variety of medications including systemic corticosteroids (SCS), intranasal corticosteroids, antibiotics, and antileukotrienes, patient satisfaction with drug treatment is lacking. 5 Even after undergoing endoscopic sinus surgery, some patients with CRSwNP experience postoperative recurrence. The pathophysiology of CRSwNP is predominantly characterized by type 2 inflammation with the involvement of type 2 cytokines such as interleukin (IL)‐4, IL‐5, and IL‐13, as well as tissue infiltration by eosinophils, lymphocytes, basophils, and mast cells. 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 Biologics used for CRSwNP refractory to standard medical therapy (drug treatment and endoscopic sinus surgery) are monoclonal antibodies that target molecules associated with the pathogenesis of the disease. Dupilumab is a fully human monoclonal antibody that inhibits the signaling of both IL‐4 and IL‐13, which are key cytokines and central drivers of type 2 inflammation; it does so by binding to their shared receptor component, IL‐4Rα. 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 In LIBERTY NP SINUS‐24 and LIBERTY NP SINUS‐52 trials, dupilumab significantly improved endoscopic, radiographic, clinical, and patient‐reported outcomes in patients with severe CRSwNP refractory to standard‐of‐care therapy. 13 However, the factors associated with dupilumab efficacy in patients with CRSwNP have not been established.

SCS are often administered as a short‐term reliever treatment for exacerbations of CRSwNP because the administration of SCS improves nasal symptoms and pathological status. Even a low‐dose and short‐term course of treatment with oral prednisolone (oPSL) (approximately 0.3 mg/kg of oPSL for 3 or 7 days) for CRSwNP can significantly decrease the proportion of blood eosinophils, polyp scores, and clinical symptom scores. 14 Akiyama et al described that the proportion of blood eosinophils markedly decreased upon the use of approximately 0.3 mg/kg of oPSL in the 3‐ and 7‐day groups (9.4% ± 3.9% to 1.6% ± 2.7%, and 7.6% ± 3.7% to 0.4% ± 0.6%, respectively; P < .001), and that the mean polyp size significantly decreased in the same two groups as well (4.2 ± 1.4 to 2.8 ± 1.4, and 4.2 ± 1.2 to 2.7 ± 1.5, respectively; P < .001). 14 SCS are also administered to some patients who have started treatment with dupilumab, in whom the concomitant use of SCS affects the results of hematological tests and modifies dupilumab efficacy. Therefore, in the present study, we aimed to investigate the factors associated with dupilumab efficacy in patients with CRSwNP while minimizing the influence of SCS.

Methods

Study Design

The present study was a single‐center retrospective study regarding the efficacy of dupilumab in patients with CRSwNP. Adult patients who started treatment with dupilumab for CRSwNP at the Department of Otorhinolaryngology of the Jikei University Hospital between March 2020 and July 2023 were considered for inclusion in the study, and their clinical charts were retrospectively reviewed. Correlations between pre‐treatment parameters (age, time since last sinus surgery to the start of dupilumab, blood eosinophil count, proportion of blood eosinophils, and serum total immunoglobulin E [IgE]) and co‐primary efficacy endpoints—changes in nasal polyp score (NPS) and nasal congestion score (NCS) from baseline—were assessed. Furthermore, correlations between the pre‐treatment parameters and a secondary efficacy endpoint of change in Lund‐Mackay computed tomography score (LMK‐CTS) from baseline were assessed. In addition, the efficacy endpoints (the changes in NPS, NCS, and LMK‐CTS from baseline) were compared between different pairs of groups (male vs female, presence vs absence of comorbid asthma, presence vs absence of NSAID‐ERD) and among three groups classified according to the proportion of blood eosinophils (≤5%, >5% and ≤10%, or >10%). The results of the hematological test within 12 weeks before the start of dupilumab were used for analysis. If multiple blood samples were collected from the same patient within this period, the results of the most recent hematological test were analyzed. Because SCS may affect blood eosinophil count, proportion of blood eosinophils, and serum total IgE as well as the endpoints of this study (the changes in NPS, NCS, and LMK‐CTS from baseline), specific exclusion criteria were adopted to minimize the effects of SCS. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Jikei University School of Medicine for Biomedical Research (identification: 35‐434 [12074]).

Eligibility Criteria

The inclusion criteria were as follows: adult patients (age ≥ 18 years) with CRSwNP (NPS ≥ 5 overall and ≥2 for each nostril, NCS ≥ 2, and past medical history of at least one sinonasal surgical procedure) who started treatment with dupilumab at the Department of Otorhinolaryngology of the Jikei University Hospital between March 2020 and July 2023.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients who did not undergo blood collection within 12 weeks before the start of dupilumab; (2) those who were prescribed SCS even once at any point from “12 weeks before the date of blood collection before the start of dupilumab” until “the first outpatient visit after the administration of dupilumab every 2 weeks for the first 24 weeks”; (3) those whose dosing interval (every 2 weeks) was changed or administration was stopped before the first outpatient visit after the administration of dupilumab for the first 24 weeks; and (4) those who received other biologics before the start of dupilumab.

Outcome Measures

The co‐primary efficacy endpoints were the changes in NPS and NCS from baseline (the start time of dupilumab) to the endpoint (the first outpatient examination after the subcutaneous injection of 300 mg of dupilumab every 2 weeks for the first 24 weeks). NPS is measured using a scale ranging from 0 to 8 (0‐4 on each side), whereas NCS is measured using a scale ranging from 0 to 3 (0 = no symptoms, 1 = mild, 2 = moderate, and 3 = severe). The secondary efficacy endpoint was the change in LMK‐CTS from baseline (sinus computed tomography [CT] scan performed within 12 weeks before the start of dupilumab) to the endpoint (sinus CT scan performed during the period between 12 weeks after the start of dupilumab and the first outpatient visit after the administration of dupilumab every 2 weeks for the first 24 weeks). LMK‐CTS is measured using a scale ranging from 0 to 24 (0‐12 on each side). The secondary endpoint was evaluated in patients who underwent a sinus CT scan within 12 weeks before the start of dupilumab and a repeat sinus CT scan between 12 weeks after the start of dupilumab and the first outpatient visit after the administration of dupilumab for the first 24 weeks. The NPS and NCS data were collected from medical records from multiple experienced rhinologists. A single experienced rhinologist assessed the LMK‐CTS. For the primary and secondary endpoints, the changes are calculated as post‐treatment scores minus baseline scores.

Statistical Analyses

Patients who met all the inclusion criteria and none of the exclusion criteria were included in the analysis. In the correlation analyses, Pearson's correlation coefficient test was used if assumptions of normality of the distribution were verified, whereas Spearman's rank correlation coefficient test was used otherwise. Normality of the distribution was verified by a goodness‐of‐fit test. The statistical significance of differences between two groups was assessed using the Student's t‐test if assumptions of normality of the distribution were verified, whereas the Mann‐Whitney U test was used otherwise. Statistical analysis for comparisons among three groups was done using single‐factor analysis of variance (ANOVA) if assumptions of normality of the distribution and homogeneity of variances were verified, whereas the Kruskal‐Wallis test was used otherwise. Patients with a missing parameter were excluded from specific analyses of the said parameter. Thus, in principle, missing values were not complemented. All tests were two‐tailed and were not adjusted for multiple comparisons, with differences reported as statistically significant if P < .05. Statistical analyses were performed using Statcel‐the Useful Addin Forms on Excel‐5th ed.

Results

A total of 193 patients were enrolled based on the inclusion criteria, but 100 patients were excluded after the clinical charts were reviewed because SCS were prescribed during the period specified by the exclusion criteria. Twenty‐nine patients were excluded from the analysis because they did not undergo blood collection within 12 weeks before the start of dupilumab. Eight patients were excluded because they received other biologics before the start of dupilumab. Five patients were excluded because their dosing interval (every 2 weeks) was changed during the period between the start of dupilumab and the first outpatient visit after the administration of dupilumab for the first 24 weeks. One patient was excluded because the dupilumab administration was stopped during the period between the start of dupilumab and the first outpatient visit after the administration of dupilumab for the first 24 weeks. The final analysis was performed using the remaining 50 patients. A retrospective review of clinical charts indicated that all 50 patients were diagnosed with eosinophilic chronic rhinosinusitis (ECRS). Baseline demographic and disease characteristics in the 50 patients are shown in Table 1. The period between the start of dupilumab and the first outpatient visit after dupilumab administration for the first 24 weeks was 198.2 ± 28.9 (mean ± standard deviation [SD]) days. Among the 50 patients, 21 underwent both “a sinus CT scan within 12 weeks before the start of dupilumab” and “a repeat sinus CT scan between 12 weeks after the start of dupilumab and the first outpatient visit after dupilumab administration for the first 24 weeks.” The period between the start of dupilumab and the repeat sinus CT scan was 136.1 ± 45.8 (mean ± SD) days.

Correlational Analyses of Pre‐treatment Parameters and Outcomes

None of the pre‐treatment parameters including age, time since last sinus surgery, blood eosinophil count, proportion of blood eosinophils, and serum total IgE showed a statistically significant correlation with the changes in NPS from baseline (Figure 1). Similarly, none of the pre‐treatment parameters showed a statistically significant correlation with the changes in NCS from baseline (Figure 2). None of the pre‐treatment parameters, except for the proportion of blood eosinophils, showed a statistically significant correlation with the changes in LMK‐CTS from baseline (Figure 3). A statistically significant moderate positive correlation was observed between the proportion of blood eosinophils and the changes in LMK‐CTS from baseline (r = 0.47, P = .037 [n = 21]). Discussion

In LIBERTY NP SINUS‐24 and LIBERTY NP SINUS‐52, dupilumab added to the standard of care in adults with severe CRSwNP significantly improved NPS, NCS, and LMK‐CTS. 13 Hopkins et al verified the efficacy of dupilumab in patients with CRSwNP from the SINUS‐24/SINUS‐52 trials post hoc based on the number of prior surgeries and time since last surgery; notably, the improvements in NPS and LMK‐CTS were greater in patients with a period <3 years since their last surgery than patients with a period ≥5 years. 15 Mullol et al evaluated the efficacy of dupilumab in patients with CRSwNP with and without NSAID‐ERD from the SINUS‐24/SINUS‐52 trials post hoc; dupilumab improved objective measures and patient‐reported symptoms significantly more in patients with comorbid NSAID‐ERD than in those without. 16 Fujieda et al examined whether the eosinophilic status of CRSwNP was a predictor of dupilumab efficacy in the post hoc analysis of the SINUS‐52 trial; they subsequently reported that dupilumab efficacy was unaffected by eosinophilic status and that blood eosinophil level may not be a suitable biomarker for dupilumab efficacy in CRSwNP. 17

In the SINUS‐24 and SINUS‐52 trials, most enrolled patients had used SCS in the preceding two years before the trials. 13 In addition, not only the patients in the placebo‐controlled group but also some patients in the dupilumab group required rescue with SCS during the trials. 13 In the present study, as per the exclusion criteria, we investigated the factors associated with the efficacy of dupilumab after minimizing the effect of SCS on the pre‐treatment parameters (blood eosinophil count, proportion of blood eosinophils, and serum total IgE) and on the endpoints (the changes in NPS, NCS, and LMK‐CTS from baseline). One of the other differences between the present study and the post hoc analyses of the SINUS‐24 and SINUS‐52 trials is that all the 50 patients enrolled in the present study were diagnosed with ECRS. In cases of CRSwNP with strong eosinophilic infiltration, nasal polyps often recur after sinus surgery. Therefore, CRSwNP with strong eosinophilic infiltration was described as ECRS to differentiate it from CRSwNP that responds well to standard approaches to treatment. 18 According to the Japanese Epidemiological Survey of Refractory Eosinophilic Chronic Rhinosinusitis (JESREC) scoring system and algorithm, a definite diagnosis of ECRS should be made by microscopic examination of nasal polyp or paranasal sinus tissues when the total JESREC score is 11 points or higher. This score is based on factors such as the affected sides (both sides = 3 points), nasal polyps (presence of polyps = 2 points), CT shadow (ethmoid ≥ maxillary = 2 points), and proportion of blood eosinophils (>2% and ≤5% = 4 points, >5% and ≤10% = 8 points, and >10% = 10 points). If three fields inspected under a microscope at a total magnification of 400× (ocular lens, field number 22) show 70 or more eosinophils per field, ECRS is confirmed. 19 If the JESREC score is <11, the condition is classified as non‐ECRS. Furthermore, confirmed ECRS is classified as mild, moderate, or severe. The criteria for determining the severity of ECRS were defined based on the combination of factors A and B. Factor A includes both a peripheral blood eosinophil percentage > 5% and ethmoid sinus‐dominant shadow (ethmoid ≥ maxillary) on CT scans. Factor B involves the presence of complications such as bronchial asthma, aspirin intolerance, and/or NSAIDs allergy. 19 When both of these factors are fulfilled, the condition is classified as severe ECRS. If either factor A or B is fulfilled, the condition is classified as moderate ECRS. If neither factor is fulfilled, the condition is classified as mild ECRS. Cases of moderate‐to‐severe ECRS are considered an intractable disease. 18 In the post hoc analyses of the SINUS‐24 and SINUS‐52 trials, patients with non‐ECRS were included. 17 In contrast, in this study, a retrospective review of clinical charts of the 50 enrolled patients indicated that all of them met the criteria for ECRS. Seven, 13, and 30 displayed mild, moderate, and severe ECRS, respectively. For the primary endpoints, 42 of 50 patients showed improvement of at least 2 points in NPS, and 47 of 50 patients showed improvement of at least 1 point in NCS. Regarding the secondary endpoint, 19 of 21 patients demonstrated improvement of at least 6 points in LMK‐CTS. Most of the 50 patients diagnosed with ECRS responded well to dupilumab, as ECRS is a type 2 inflammatory disease with diverse exacerbation mechanisms. 20 The statistically significant moderate correlation between the proportion of blood eosinophils and the changes in LMK‐CTS from baseline suggests that patients with a higher proportion of blood eosinophils prior to starting dupilumab may experience smaller absolute values of changes in LMK‐CTS, possibly due to the higher proportion of blood eosinophils reflecting more severe type 2 inflammation.

However, the current study had the following limitations. Based on the findings obtained in this study, we would like to plan a new study to verify the factors associated with the therapeutic effect of dupilumab.

Study Limitations

Limitations of this study include that the exclusion of patients who were prescribed SCS during the period specified in the exclusion criteria may have caused a bias toward the inclusion of patients who responded well to dupilumab without a need for SCS. Furthermore, only 21 patients underwent repeat sinus CT scans within the specified period, which may be underpowered for detecting clinically significant differences. In this study, we decided not to correct for multiplicity. It is important to consider the application of the correction for multiplicity. This method can prevent an increase in the α error; however, the power becomes low, which is problematic. This study has an exploratory research aspect, for which several tests were conducted. If the level of significance were adjusted, the power would be too low. Therefore, no adjustment for multiple comparisons was made.

Conclusion

In this study, no statistically significant differences in the endpoints (the changes in NPS, NCS, and LMK‐CTS from baseline) were observed between the subgroups (male vs female, presence vs absence of comorbid asthma, presence vs absence of NSAID‐ERD, and three groups classified according to the proportion of blood eosinophils). Furthermore, the statistical analysis revealed no significant correlation between the endpoints and the pre‐treatment parameters (age, time since last sinus surgery, blood eosinophil count, blood eosinophil proportion, and serum total IgE), with the exception of a statistically significant moderate positive correlation between the blood eosinophil proportion and the changes in LMK‐CTS from baseline (r = 0.47, P = .037 [n = 21]). A higher baseline peripheral blood eosinophil percentage before starting dupilumab may be associated with smaller changes in LMK‐CTS after dupilumab treatment, possibly due to the higher blood eosinophil proportion reflecting more severe type 2 inflammation in ECRS. However, our findings indicate a need for further investigation.

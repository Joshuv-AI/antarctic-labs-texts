Health status of type 2 diabetic patients attending primary care centers in Puducherry region – An observational study
Authors: B Rajalakshmi, Meena Ramanathan, Ananda Balayogi Bhavanani, R Sridharan, A Lokeshmaran
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTContext:Diabetes causes enormous health burden worldwide, causing serious threat to people’s health and wellbeing. It is also highlighted by the International Diabetes Federation that around 537 million people are currently affected by diabetes, and this is expected to rise to 643 million by 2030 and 783 million by 2045, which should be addressed with a multisectoral approach. The health status of diabetic patients at all dimensions (physical, psychological, social, environmental) is unsatisfactory, causing a significant rate of morbidity and mortality.Aim:To assess the holistic health status of patients with type 2 diabetes in all dimensions in a primary health care facility.Study Design:Observational study.Methods and Material:A total of 104 diabetic patients (46.2% female; 53.8% male) were enrolled with a mean age of 51.46 ± 10.22 years (female 49.46 ± 8.56 years; male 53.91 ± 9.75 years). Anthropometric parameters such as body mass index (BMI) and waist circumference, physiological parameters such as heartrate and blood pressure, biochemical parameters such as fasting blood glucose, postprandial blood glucose, HbA1c, fasting insulin, Homeostatic Model Assessment for Insulin Resistance using the formula Fasting Insulin × Fasting glucose)/22.5 to assess insulin resistance, lipid profile, blood urea and serum creatinine, and psychological parameters such as perceived stress scale to assess stress level and WHOQOL-BREF scale to assess quality of life were measured.Statistical Analysis Used:Independent t-test was applied to find the significance between male and female subjects in the baseline of the measured parameters.Results:The average age of diabetic females was significantly lower than that of the males (P = 0.016), which shows that females are affected at a younger age. The majority are overweight; particularly, females have significant higher BMI when compared to males (P = 0.018). The HbA1c level was > 8%, LDL was above normal, and fasting insulin was >7, which may cause metabolic syndrome as shown by previous studies. The majority of the patients were stressed and have low quality of life.Conclusions:The overall health status of diabetic patients was unsatisfactory, particularly BMI, which is the key indicator to many health issues. Since diabetes affects individuals at all levels, there is a need for integration of complimentary and alternative medical systems such as Yoga therapy for diabetic patients in a primary care center along with medical management which provides a holistic approach improving overall health and wellbeing. Incorporating Yoga therapy for diabetic patients in a primary care center along with medical management helps to overcome the enormous health burden of disease.

Introduction

Diabetes mellitus (DM) is a chronic health condition causing multifarious health issues and is growing to be the greatest challenge worldwide.[1] According to International Diabetes Federation (IDF), currently, 537 million persons between the ages of 20 years and 79 years are estimated to have diabetes and projected a rise of 94% by 2045 in low- and middle-income nations, where population growth is predicted to be stronger.[2] There is a significant burden in the social, financial, and health systems, with its fast-increasing prevalence and mortality related to diabetes.[3] A total of 101 million Indians are estimated to have diabetes, with Puducherry having the second-highest prevalence at 26.3%, according to the ICMR report. Additionally, the population of Puducherry has a significant risk of getting diabetes in the future due to the alarming 25.8% prevalence of prediabetes.[4] It also has the highest prevalence of abdominal obesity (61.2%) and generalized obesity (53.3%),[5] which highlight the pressing need for control and prevention strategies for addressing Puducherry’s rising rates of diabetes and obesity. Multisectorial approaches for its management are desperately needed.[6]

Type 2 diabetes is a serious public health concern as unhealthy dietary patterns and inactive lifestyles are key contributors to the rapidly increasing rates of diabetes in developing countries and dietary education fosters a proactive approach to prevent diabetes and to promote health and wellbeing.[7] Factors influencing the sudden upsurge may be due to an increase in aging population, rise in unhealthy lifestyles throughout the world, and incidence of obesity in adults and children. The significant impact of diabetes as a risk factor for cardiovascular disease (CVD) makes it necessary to find ways to manage as well as understand its consequences.[8]

Since diabetes is increasingly prevalent in India, there is an immediate requirement for scientific studies and interventions to minimize the potentially disastrous rise in the illness that is expected in the years to come.[9] In general, people have very low awareness related to diabetes, potential complications that may arise, and the treatments available. Hence, it is required to propagate these facts, particularly among the less educated and the population below poverty line.[10]

Puducherry studies highlight the high prevalence of behavioral and biological risk factors for noncommunicable diseases including tobacco and alcohol use, physical inactivity, unhealthy diets, obesity, and stress, emphasizing the urgent need for multisectoral health promotion, early screening for risk factors, cost-effective and economical interventions, and primary health care with community involvement to prevent and control NCDs in the district.[111213]

Weight was shown to be the primary risk factor for diabetes in India, and the adult overweight prevalence has increased from 9·0% (8·7–9·3) in 1990 to 20·4% (19·9–20·8) in 2016. Controlling this potentially catastrophic public health scenario is the need of the hour.[14]

There is an immediate need for comprehensive treatment for stress management in individuals with DM and their high levels of perceived stress.[15] Diabetic patients provided with holistic health care with equal weightage to mental health and quality of life as much as their physical health is required and must be emphasized.[16] Hence, all facets of health status among diabetic patients have to be assessed using standardized patient assessment tools in order to render appropriate patient-centered health care.[17] In order to end diabetes in India, it is imperative to adopt a variety of strategies such as increased preventive measures and counseling services including advice to medication adherence. Incorporating traditional medical systems [Ayurveda, Yoga and Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha, and Homeopathy (AYUSH)] into diabetes preventive and management methods is the current trend to handle this epidemic.[18] Studies prove that using Siddha antidiabetic herbs and preparations minimizes the occurrence and early development of diabetes,[192021] and Ayurvedic integration with standard treatment shows significant improvement in type 2 diabetic patients along with Yoga and lifestyle modification.[22] Yoga therapy has holistic effects that can improve metabolic and psychological health, enhance insulin sensitivity, and help manage type 2 diabetes and its associated comorbidities and also emphasizes the potential of yoga interventions to enhance cardiovascular health in individuals at high risk for diabetes.[2324]

With all the abovementioned facts and figures in mind, the current study aims to evaluate the overall health status of diabetic patients visiting a Puducherry primary health care facility. It also aims to include ancient complementary and traditional systems such as Siddha and Yoga as therapeutic modalities along with the standard medical management care in diabetes targeting at the overall holistic health care for the patients.

Subjects and Methods

Patients attending Siddha Primary health care center were screened, and type 2 diabetic subjects were recruited for the study by the consecutive sampling method. Those with diabetic complications are excluded from the study. IHEC clearance was obtained (PhD PROJECT/08/2019/008 dated 23/08/2019), registered in the Clinical Trial Registry of India (CTRI/2020/03/024086), and written consent was taken from all the subjects. An orientation program was conducted detailing the purpose of the study and its benefits for better compliance and participation. Following the orientation session, 104 (56 male and 48 female) participants with type 2 diabetes were recruited to take part in the assessment of their health status.

Selected parameters such as anthropometric parameters like body mass index (BMI) were noted by measuring weight (kg) divided by the square of height (m), and waist circumference (WC) using a measuring tape placed around the waist in line with the navel. Physiological parameters such as heart rate (HR) and blood pressure (BP) were recorded utilizing a noninvasive automatic blood pressure (BP) monitor (Omron HEM 7203, Kyoto, Japan) using the Oscillo metric method with an accuracy of ±5% for HR and ± 3 mm Hg for BP. Biochemical parameters such as fasting blood glucose (FBG), postprandial blood glucose (PPBG), HbA1C, fasting insulin, lipid profile, blood urea, and serum creatinine were recorded in a well-equipped laboratory, and Homeostatic Model Assessment for Insulin Resistance (HOMA IR) was assessed using fasting insulin and fasting glucose.[25]

For psychological parameters to assess the stress level of the individual, a typical stress evaluation tool, perceived stress scale of 10-item questionnaire, was used, which helps to understand how different situations affect our moods and perceived stress. This scale examines our feelings and outlines the way we perceive the events in our lives over the course of the last month. An individual may have a PSS score ranging from 0 to 40 and greater scores correspond to higher levels of perceived stress.[26]

To assess the quality-of-life (QoL), WHOQOL-BREF scale was used, which was a 26-item evaluation tool that addresses all aspects of health, from physical, psychological, social, and environmental dimensions. Every WHOQOL-BREF item is scored on a response scale, which is a 5-point ordinal scale, ranging from 1 to 5. The scores are then linearly transformed to generate a 0–100 scale, and higher scores indicate better QoL.[27]

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics of type 2 diabetic patients was done. Independent t-test was applied to find the significance between two groups (male and female subjects) in the baseline of all the measured parameters.

Discussion

The present study evaluates the health condition of diabetes patients and has shown that females are affected at a younger age, which has been stated in a study by Rodriguez et al., that is, women are diagnosed with type 2 diabetes at a younger age with milder disease features when compared to men along with the possibility of unidentified sex-specific elements influencing onset of type 2 diabetes. They also state that it may be due to increased insulin resistance from an earlier pubertal development stage and recommend for further research into the mechanisms for early detection and management of type 2 diabetes in youngsters.[28] This is also stated in another study which suggests proper screening at a much early age in women to avoid many complications later in life.[29] In our present study, the female age is significantly lower than the male age (the female age was 49.46 ± 8.56 years, and the male age was 53.91 ± 9.75 years) and is in alignment with the previous studies [Figure 1].

The BMI of female participants was 27.30 ± 5.47 and that of males was 25.12 ± 3.72, as shown in [Table 1] displaying that the majority of females are overweight, having a significantly higher BMI when compared to males. This was similar to one of the population-based longitudinal studies, in rural Puducherry, India, which states that obesity was one of the major modifiable factors associated with the incidence of type 2 diabetes and rectifying them may be the solution to this increasing burden.[30] Another study by Sivanantham et al.[31] shows that obesity rates are much greater among women (46.7%) than among males (29.7%), which is consistent with women’s significantly higher levels of physical inactivity. The findings from a survey emphasize the necessity for focused interventions for abdominal obesity, particularly for Indian women in their thirties and forties, and recommend to better understand the causes, interrelation, and the disease risk linked to the abdominal obesity.[32] Sociocultural factors influence gender disparities in gaining weight. Studies demonstrated significant corelation between BMI and individual culture and dietary habits and insisted on recognizing the trend of obesity in various cultures and nations enabling to create public health initiatives to lower obesity. In developing countries, women exhibit more obesity rates than men due to altered physical activity, intake of high refined carbohydrates, and sedentary life style that should be addressed based on gender by developing methods to reduce or prevent weight gain.[3334] Andreasson Karin et al. and Luo et al. studies highlight the link of greater risk of prevalence of type 2 diabetes at a younger age due to increasing adolescent BMI.[3536] A study by Ruze Rexiati et al.[37] insisted the importance of prevention as an affordable and long-term way to treat obesity and type 2 diabetes and recommends multidisciplinary collaborations to track the prevalence of obesity and type 2 diabetes to uncover the biological causes enhancing the precision of diagnosis and treatment outcomes.

The mean heart rate in the subjects of this study was 84.61 ± 8.66, while the mean systolic and diastolic blood pressures were 134.83 ± 19.19 and 84.52 ± 9.45, respectively, showing no significant difference between female and male. The overall mean fasting blood glucose level was 174.78 ± 50.02 (female 172.90 ± 52.73 and male 176.39 ± 48.00), which needs to be addressed as stated by Jiang et al. that regardless of age or gender, the increased mortality was linked with those having higher fasting glucose levels of 100 mg/dl and above.[38] Ridwanto et al.[39] showed a strong association between fasting blood glucose level and BMI in individuals with type 2 diabetes. The overall postprandial blood glucose level was 272.40 ± 70.94 (female 263.54 ± 72.17 and male 280 ± 69.62), which exceeds the normal range in this study. Previous studies show that there was an independent correlation between the severity and existence of coronary heart disease and postprandial hyperglycemia, which is a risk component for coronary heart disease and also a notable indicator to HbA1c which is mostly ignored.[4041]

The mean HbA1c was 8.2 ± 0.99 (female 8.17 ± 1.10 and male 8.29 ± 0.91), which shows a poor glycemic control in the subjects of this study as shown in [Table 2]. Previous research study provides evidence that more than 50% of diabetics have poor glycemic control (HbA1c > 8%), uncontrolled hypertension, dyslipidemia, and vascular problems.[42] A Delhi-based study by Nagpal et al.[43] showed HbA1c to be more than 10% in addition to poor control of blood pressure, lipid control, and glycemic indices. Elevated blood sugar leads to high HbA1c level and triggers the polyol pathway (causes multiple metabolic abnormalities) and production of advanced glycation end products, which was a predominant factor causing microvascular and macrovascular complications.[44] Tziomalos et al.[45] describe that increased blood sugar levels, obesity, hypercholesterolemia, increased blood pressure, and oxidative stress are important risk factors for the advancement of diabetic nephropathy that need to be managed rigorously and effectively. The United Kingdom Prospective Diabetes Study states that 35% reduction of risk in microvascular complication is noted for each reduction in percentage of HbA1c level, and the American Diabetes Association perception about this findings was that microvascular complications can be prevented by maintaining good control over the blood sugar level.[46] A cross-sectional study done by Hyassat et al.[47] suggested to identify all the factors affecting the regulation of blood sugar, blood pressure, and dyslipidemia as poor glycemic control is a matter of concern as well as a serious health hazard which was found among the participants of our study emphasizing further studies in future with a focus on healthy living.

According to American Diabetes Association (ADA), fundamental components of global risk reduction in diabetes include controlling blood pressure, cholesterol, and glucose levels as well as implementing educational programs and some medications that improve renal and cardiovascular outcomes and life style modification.[51] Many studies highlight the significance of assessing lipid profiles and renal function to evaluate CVD and renal failure in T2DM patients.[5253]

The fasting insulin level was found to be 9.05 ± 3.63 (female 9.71 ± 3.63; male 8.49 ± 3.56), and the HOMA IR range was 3.86 ± 1.81 (female 4.12 ± 1.95; male 3.64 ± 1.67). Research has correlated fasting insulin levels over 7 to be associated with a significant increase in future risk of metabolic syndrome and type 2 diabetes.[54] Our study is in line with Singh et al.,[55] who have concluded that HOMA-IR values raise gradually with an increase of BMI and serve as a useful method for diagnosing metabolic syndrome in teenagers. Zhao et al.[56] in their study have concluded that high insulin resistance (IR) was the cause of metabolic disorders and recommends to treat holistically to improve the quality of life and to reduce health care cost.

The percentage of stress score, low stress, moderate stress, and high stress, in our subjects as shown in Figure 2 was 9.6%, 85.6%, and 4.8%, respectively, showing that the majority of the patients are moderately stressed. Vasanth et al.[57] state that type 2 diabetes affects individuals in various health dimensions and treatment should be holistic to reduce the burden. Mishra et al.[15] recommend extensive research study to determine the mechanism of perceived stress in DM as they have high perceived stress and individuals with prediabetes and extreme perceived stress had higher HbA1c values. Siddharthan et al.[58] mentioned that perceived stress has been reported by nearly two out of every five diabetic inpatients and insisted on incorporation of stress management strategies in the diabetic health care system. There is also a need to integrate traditional systems of medicine such as Yoga, which has been established to be an effective stress-reduction modality. The integration proved effective to alleviate stress as yoga balances the autonomic nervous system.[5960] Yoga effectively reduces stress by promoting muscle relaxation, reducing the heart rate, enhancing heart rate variability, and altering brain activity associated with emotion regulation and mindfulness.[61]

The WHOQOL-BREF scores in four domains assessed in this study as shown in Figure 3 were 46.98 ± 15.07 in the physical domain (female 45.29 ± 14.76 and male 48.43 ± 15.30), 57.21 ± 12.56 in the psychological domain (female 55.90 ± 14.71 and male 58.34 ± 10.39), 38.61 ± 20.07 in the social domain (female 36.65 ± 19.68 and male 40.30 ± 20.42), and 51.25 ± 12.17 in the environmental domain (female 51.52 ± 12.45 and male 51.02 ± 12.04), which show that females have lower QoL than males, which was aligned with Abadla et al. and Rubin et al. studies.[6263] Wong et al. showed a lower QoL in the environmental domain and a comparatively higher QoL in the physical health domain among the participants of their study.[64] Aarthy et al.[65] stated that individuals with diabetes in general had a lower QoL and in specific, women with diabetes reported a lower QoL than males as found in our study. Mahajan et al.[66] state that this lower QoL of women than men may have an impact of sedentary life style, uncontrolled blood sugar levels, and stress that should be addressed holistically. A cross-sectional analytical study shows that women QoL was unsatisfactory, documenting that age, period of diabetes, income, educational status, and comorbid conditions are the key factors accelerating the QoL of women, which may help the healthcare professionals to schedule an appropriate intervention to enhance the QoL of women.[67] Previous studies also report that diabetic patients have low to moderate quality of life and the treatment of related comorbidities, the age at which diabetes was first diagnosed, and the patient’s HbA1c level were all significant factors that predicted the QoL of individuals with type 2 diabetes and urge worldwide programs to raise the QoL.[6869]

Puducherry studies showed significant public health risk of diabetes as there was an increasing number of diagnosed and undiagnosed diabetes cases in Puducherry that should be addressed immediately with preventive health care[70] and also recommend to maintain lower BMI as it is a significant independent predictor of diabetes. In addition, in alignment with the present study, it reported a significantly lower age of onset among those with higher BMI.[71] One study states the significance of maintaining a diabetic registry in a primary health center which contains all needed details of the patients[72] essential to understand the current health status of diabetes and thereby find a solution in the overall healthcare system to overcome this global burden.

Conclusion

From the present study conducted on 104 type 2 diabetic subjects, we conclude that the approach toward diabetic health care should be integrated holistically working on physical, psychological, social, and environmental dimensions of health. In our study, we found that the female age is significantly lower than the male age; that is, females are affected at a younger age, which insists on proper screening and preventive strategies in a much younger group. Furthermore, we noted that females have a significantly higher BMI when compared to males and since many studies signified the risk of obesity and diabetes, steps should be taken to maintain normal weight and thereby prevent and manage diabetes and its complications. We found that in our study, the majority of the diabetic patients are moderately stressed and have lower QoL. Hence, at all levels of the healthcare system, stress management and QoL enhancing strategies must be incorporated into diabetes, teaching programs such as yoga therapy, a well-researched practice for lowering stress and also recognized to assist in reaching ideal blood pressure and glucose levels.

To effectively overcome the diabetic burden, policymakers and healthcare professionals must implement a comprehensive, multifaceted holistic approach to optimize diabetes prevention, management, and care. Steps should be taken at a primary care level to prevent diabetic complications by proper detection, screening, counseling, and management. National health policies focusing on holistic approaches to diabetic care, creating awareness by public health campaigns and public outreach insisting on healthy lifestyle, dietary modifications, physical activity, and holistic therapies along with usual care should be implemented. This may be the way forward as alternative medical systems are now linked with primary healthcare in India to handle chronic illnesses.

METTL3-dependent m6A-modified circ_0026652 facilitates immune evasion of diffuse large B-cell lymphoma cells by inhibiting CD8+ T-cell responses
Authors: Xue Gao, Danyang Wang, Jianwei Du, Yesheng Wang, Lingdi Zhao, Lihua Dong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDiffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is the most common non-Hodgkin lymphoma, characterized by high heterogeneity that leads to treatment resistance and relapse in up to 40% of patients. Circular RNAs (circRNAs) primarily function as miRNA sponges to modulate gene expression involved in DLBCL tumorigenesis, resistance, and relapse.MethodsLymph node tissues were collected from patients with reactive lymph node hyperplasia or DLBCL for gene expression analysis. DLBCL cells harboring the indicated genetic modification were co-cultured with CD8+ T cells to analyze cytokine secretion, CD8+ T cell cytotoxicity, ratio and apoptosis. DLBCL cell proliferation and apoptosis were evaluated by CCK-8, EdU incorporation, and Annexin V-APC and PI staining assays. m6A modification was assessed by MeRIP assays. Luciferase, ChIP and RIP assays were conducted to analyze gene interaction in DLBCL cells. Mice were inoculated with DLBCL cells to evaluate the effects of circ_0026652 and miR-1287-5p on DLBCL progression in vivo.ResultsMETTL3-mediated m6A modification drove circ_0026652 upregulation, and YTHDC1 promoted the nuclear export of m6A-modifed circ_0026652 in DLBCL cells. Functioning as a miR-1287-5p sponge, circ_0026652 inhibited miR-1287-5p, thereby enhancing DLBCL cell proliferation and inhibiting apoptosis. Importantly, circ_0026652 suppressed T-cell cytokine production, was associated with resistance to T-cell cytotoxicity, and correlated with impaired CD8+ T-cell activity dependent on miR-1287-5p. Furthermore, miR-1287-5p-mediated regulation of DLBCL cell proliferation and apoptosis was dependent on USP22. USP22 stabilized β-catenin by reducing its ubiquitination and facilitating its nuclear translocation, which correlated with PD-L1 expression in DLBCL cells. The USP22/PD-L1 axis appear to mediate miR-1287-5p-dependent effects on DLBCL immune evasion in vitro. Circ_0026652 promoted tumor growth and was associated with inhibited CD8+ T-cell activity through the miR-1287-5p/USP22/β-catenin/PD-L1 signaling axis in vivo.ConclusionMETTL3-dependent m6A-modified circ_0026652 functions as a miR-1287-5p sponge to reduce miR-1287-5p expression, which in turn upregulates USP22 to stabilize β-catenin and enhance β-catenin nuclear localization. This pathway may contribute to promoting PD-L1 expression in DLBCL cells, with potential implications for inhibiting CD8+ T cell responses and facilitating DLBCL immune evasion.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12967-026-08200-3.Keywords: Circ_0026652, miR-1287-5p, PD-L1, m6A, DLBCL, Immune evasion

Introduction

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), the most common B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL), accounts for approximately 30–40% of all NHL cases globally [1]. This malignancy exhibits marked aggressiveness, high heterogeneity, and rapid progression, originating from mature B cells at diverse differentiation stages. Its pathogenic mechanisms involve dysregulation of B-cell receptor signaling, NF-κB pathway activation, and immune escape [2–4]. First-line therapy employs the R-CHOP regimen (rituximab combined with cyclophosphamide, hydroxydaunorubicin, vincristine sulfate, and prednisolone). While R-CHOP cures approximately 60% of patients, about 30–40% develop refractory disease or relapse, exhibiting poor outcomes [5], and these patients may receive autologous stem cell transplantation. Novel agents including chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapies, bispecific antibodies, and antibody-drug conjugates have revolutionized DLBCL management [6]. However, immune evasion in DLBCL critically causes treatment failure and relapse. A better understanding of immune evasion in DLBCL will facilitate the development of novel combined therapies for relapsed or refractory DLBCL.

Circular RNAs (circRNAs), a class of covalently closed non-coding RNAs, have recently emerged as pivotal regulators in carcinogenesis through their roles in microRNA sponging, RNA-binding protein modulation, and even translation [7]. Unlike linear RNAs, circRNAs exhibit high stability, conservation, and tissue-specific expression, positioning them as promising candidates for diagnostic and therapeutic applications [8]. DLBCL exhibits dysregulation of circRNAs with direct implications for tumor progression and therapeutic response [9]. Notably, circAmotl1 drives lymphomagenesis by facilitating nuclear translocation of MYC and amplifying the expression of MYC-dependent translational targets [10]. Conversely, circAPC acts as a tumor suppressor, showing significant downregulation in DLBCL that correlates with aggressive disease and poor prognosis [11]. Hsa_circ_0026652 (circPCBP2) has been reported to be upregulated in DLBCL, and circ_0026652 promoted DLBCL cell stemness and chemoresistance by targeting the miR-33a/b/Programmed Cell Death Ligand 1 (PD-L1) axis [11, 12]. However, several critical questions remained unanswered: (1) whether circ_0026652 also participates in immune evasion, a key driver of DLBCL treatment failure; (2) whether its expression is regulated by m6A modification, given the emerging importance of epitranscriptomic regulation in DLBCL; and (3) whether additional downstream targets beyond the miR-33a/b axis contribute to its oncogenic functions. The present study was designed to address these gaps by investigating the upstream regulation of circ_0026652 by METTL3-mediated m6A modification and its role in modulating CD8+ T-cell responses.

Recent studies indicate that circRNAs, such as circRUNX1 and circRNA GRAMD4, modulate tumor immune evasion by regulating the activity of various immune cell types, including CD8+ T cells, natural killer cells, regulatory T cells, and cytokine-induced killer cells [13–15]. PD-L1, expressed by tumor cells and tumor-associated immune cells, plays a pivotal role in immune evasion by engaging the PD-1 checkpoint on effector T cells. This interaction induces T-cell exhaustion, anergy, and functional impairment, thereby creating an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment that facilitates tumor progression [16]. Several circRNAs regulate PD-L1 expression to control tumor immune evasion [17, 18]. Importantly, circ_0026652 enhances DLBCL stemness and chemoresistance by sponging miR-33a/b and upregulating PD-L1 expression, thus promoting DLBCL progression [12]. This observation raises the possibility that circ_0026652 might also participate in regulating immune evasion through PD-L1, but direct evidence linking circ_0026652 to CD8+ T-cell modulation in DLBCL is lacking.

CircRNAs are predominantly localized in the cytoplasm, suggesting that they must undergo nuclear export following their biogenesis [19]. The nuclear export of circRNAs are regulated through multiple mechanisms, such as N6-methyladenosine (m6A)-dependent and length-dependent pathways [20, 21]. m6A modification, the most abundant RNA modification, is important for RNA export. Methyltransferase like 3 (METTL3) is an important m6A methyltransferase that promotes m6A methylation in the nucleus, and YTH domain-containing protein 1 (YTHDC1) is an m6A-binding protein localized in the nucleus that regulates the nuclear export of RNAs [22, 23]. The METTL3/YTHDC1 axis regulates the nuclear export of circRNAs in an m6A-dependent manner [24, 25]. Although m6A modification and circRNAs have been individually implicated in cancer biology, the interplay between m6A and circRNA function in the context of DLBCL immune evasion remains unexplored. Specifically, whether circ_0026652 undergoes m6A modification and whether its nuclear export is regulated by the METTL3/YTHDC1 axis in DLBCL have not been investigated.

Here, we demonstrate that METTL3-mediated m6A modification upregulates circ_0026652 in DLBCL, which is exported by YTHDC1 and subsequently inhibits CD8+ T cell activity by sponging miR-1287-5p to activate the USP22/β-catenin/PD-L1 axis, potentially promoting immune evasion and tumor growth. Our study reveals a potential implication of circ_0026652 in immune evasion mechanism driving DLBCL tumor progression and highlights the therapeutic vulnerability of the circ_0026652/USP22/β-catenin/PD-L1 axis to overcome therapeutic resistance in DLBCL treatment.

Methods

Patient specimens

Lymph node tissues were collected from 46 patients diagnosed with DLBCL and 46 lymph node pathological tissues with reactive lymph node hyperplasia (RHL, termed the normal group) at The Affiliated Cancer Hospital of Zhengzhou University for gene expression analysis. All DLBCL cases were confirmed by histopathological examination according to the WHO classification criteria. The inclusion criteria for DLBCL patients were: (1) a confirmed histopathological diagnosis of DLBCL according to the WHO classification; (2) availability of sufficient lymph node tissue for analysis; and (3) no prior treatment before sample collection. The exclusion criteria were: (1) a history of other malignancies; (2) concurrent infectious or autoimmune diseases; and (3) previous radiotherapy, chemotherapy, or immunotherapy. For the RHL control group, patients with histopathologically confirmed reactive lymph node hyperplasia without evidence of malignancy were included. Table S1 shows clinicopathological features of these 46 DLBCL patients and circ_0026652 expression levels. Patients included in this study provided written informed consent. The Ethics Committee of The Affiliated Cancer Hospital of Zhengzhou University approved this study, which was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Cell culture and transfection

The lymphoblastoid cell line GM12878 and DLBCL cell lines OCI-LY-7, OCI-LY-10, OCI-LY3, U2932, and NU-DUL-1 were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA) and maintained in high-glucose DMEM (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) supplemented with 10% FBS (Thermo Fisher Scientific) completed medium. For cell transfection, the coding region for METTL3 or USP22 was cloned into the pcDNA3.1 vector to generate OE-METTL3 or OE-USP22. Short hairpin RNAs (shRNAs) targeting circ_0026652 (sh-circ_0026652), β-catenin (sh-β-catenin), and USP22 (sh-USP22), as well as scramble shRNA (shNC) was provided by RiboBio (Guangzhou, Guangdong, China) and cloned into pLKO.1-TRC vector (Addgene, Watertown, MA, USA). Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting METTL3 (si-METTL3) and YTHDC1 (si-YTHDC1), miR-1287-5p mimics, scramble siRNA and mimics, and miR-1287-5p antagomir were provided by RiboBio. DLBCL cells were transfected with various combinations of these constructs using lipo3000 transfection reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific). After 48 h, cells were harvested for subsequent assays.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) and methylated RIP (MeRIP)

RIP assays were performed using EZ-Magna RIP RNA-Binding Protein Immunoprecipitation Kit (Merck, Burlington, MA, USA). Briefly, cells (~ 1 × 107) were washed and lysed in lysis buffer, and cell lysates were collected. The METTL3 (5 µg, ab240595, Abcam, Cambridge, UK), YTHDC1 (5 µg, ab264375, Abcam), or Ago2 (5 µg, ab186733, Abcam) antibody was pre-coated onto protein A/G magnetic beads, and the beads were added into the lysates and incubated overnight with gentle rotation. The protein-RNA complexes were immunoprecipitated, and RNA was extracted and subjected to quantitative PCR (qPCR) for analyzing the abundance of circ_0026652 or miR-1287-5p. The Magna MeRIP m6A Kit (Sigma, Saint Louis, MO, USA) was applied to analyze the m6A level of circ_0026652. Briefly, total RNA was isolated, and fragmented to approximately 100 nucleotides. The m6A antibody (3 µg, ab208577, Abcam) was pre-coated on magnetic beads, and the beads were mixed with RNA and incubated for 2 h. After wash, RNA was extracted for analyzing circ_0026652 abundance by qPCR.

Immunofluorescence (IF) staining

U2932 cells transfected with sh-NC or sh-USP22 were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 10 min, washed twice, and permeabilized in 0.2% Triton X-100 for 15 min. Subsequently, cells were washed, blocked in 5% normal goat serum, and then incubated with the β-catenin antibody (1:250, ab32572, Abcam) overnight. The Alexa Fluro 488-conjugated goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody (1:1000, A-11008, Thermo Fisher Scientific) was added into cells and incubated for 1 h to visualize the signal. For IF on tumor tissues, tumors were harvested from mice and embedded in paraffin. Sections of 5 μm thickness were prepared, blocked in 5% normal goat serum, and then incubated with the PD-L1 (1:100, ab205921, Abcam), USP22 (1:500, sc-390585, SantaCruz, Dallas, TX, USA) or CD8α (1:200, ab237709, Abcam) antibody overnight. The signal was visualized by adding the Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody (1:1000, A-11008) or Alexa Fluor 594-conjugated goat anti-mouse secondary antibody (1:1000, A-11005, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Nuclei were stained with DAPI (Beyotime, Shanghai, China), and cells and sections were mounted for imaging.

RNA fluorescence in situ hybridization (RNA-FISH)

The RNA-FISH probes for circ_0026652 and miR-1287-5p were provided by RiboBio. Cells were plated on coverslips and washed in PBS. Then, cells were fixed in 3.7% paraformaldehyde for 10 min, washed and immersed in 70% ethanol for overnight permeabilization. The ethanol solution was removed, and cells were washed. The RNA-FISH probes (125 nM) were mixed with hybridization solution, and the mixture was added onto cells and hybridized overnight at 37 °C. Cells were washed, and the nuclei were stained with DAPI. Cells were then mounted for imaging.

Dual luciferase reporter assay

Circ_0026652 3’UTR and USP22 containing wildtype or mutant binding site for miR-1287-5p were inserted into psiCHECK-2 vector (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) to construct as luciferase reporters. Cells were transfected with luciferase reporters and miR-1287-5p mimics, sh-USP22 or sh-β-catenin using lipo 3000 transfection reagent. After 50 h, Dual-Glo Luciferase System (Promega) was used to detect the luciferase activity.

CCK-8 and EdU incorporation assays

Cell proliferation was evaluated by CCK-8 and EdU incorporation assays. For the CCK-8 assay, cells were resuspended in 100 µL of complete medium and plated into 96-well plates. After adherence, 10 µL of CCK-8 (Beyotime) was added and incubated for 2 h, and the absorbance at 450 nm was recorded. For EdU incorporation, EdU was added into cells at 10 µM, and cells were cultured for 2 h. Cells were fixed and permeabilized, and EdU incorporation was detected using Click-iT EdU Cell Proliferation Kit for Imaging, Alexa Fluor 488 dye (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The nuclei were stained with DAPI, and cells were mounted for imaging.

Cell apoptosis analysis

eBioscience™ Annexin V-APC Apoptosis Detection Kit was provided by Thermo Fisher Scientific and used to analyze cell apoptosis. In brief, cells were detached, washed in PBS and resuspended in 200 µL of Binding Buffer. Annexin V-APC (5 µL) was added into cells, and cells were incubated for 10 min. After wash, the mixture of Binding Buffer (190 µL) and Propidium Iodide (10 µL) was added to resuspend cells. Cell analysis was performed through flow cytometry.

In vitro co-culture of DLBCL and CD8+ T cells

CD8+ T cells were isolated from peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) using MagniSort™ Human CD8 T cell Enrichment Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and activated with anti-CD3 (5 µg/mL, 16-0037-85, Thermo Fisher Scientific) and anti-CD28 (5 µg/mL, 16-0289-85, Thermo Fisher Scientific) antibodies for 48 h. Subsequently, CD8+ T cells were co-cultured with DLBLC cells with the indicated transfection or treatment at a ratio of 3:1 for 48 h. CD8+ T cell percentage and apoptosis were analyzed by flow cytometry. The secretion of IL-10, TNF-α and IFN-γ was assessed by ELISA using Human IL-10 ELISA Kit, Human TNF alpha ELISA Kit and Human IFN gamma ELISA Kit. All ELISA kits were obtained from Thermo Fisher Scientific. The cytotoxicity was examined using CyQUANT LDH Cytotoxicity Assay Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific), and quantified using the formula: % Cytotoxicity = (Experimental LDH activity – Spontaneous LDH activity)/(Maximum LDH activity – Spontaneous LDH activity) × 100.

RNA extraction and RT-qPCR

Total RNA was extracted from lymph node tissues and DLBLC cells using Monarch Spin RNA Isolation Kit (NEB, Ipswich, MA, USA), and RNA quality was assessed via gel electrophoresis. Then, RNA was reversely transcribed with QuantiTect Reverse Transcription Kit (Qiagen, Santa Clarita, CA, USA). The expression of circ_0026652, METTL3, METTL14, YTHDC1, miR-1287-5p, USP22, PD-L1, and β-catenin was analyzed by qPCR using SYBR Green (Beyotime), with GAPDH serving as the normalization control. The fold change of gene expression was calculated using the 2−∆∆Ct method. Primers were shown in Table S2.

Western blotting

Protein was extracted from DLBLC and CD8+ T cells using Protein Extraction Kit (Abcam) and quantified using Pierce™ Dilution-Free™ Rapid Gold BCA Protein Assay Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Protein (30 µg) was loaded into 10% SDS-PAGE for protein separation by gel electrophoresis and transferred onto PVDF membrane (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). After block, membranes were incubated with the METTL3 (1:2000, ab240595, Abcam), YTHDC1 (1:2000, ab264375, Abcam), USP22 (1:2000, ab195289, Abcam), PD-1 (1:1000, ab309363, Abcam), PD-L1 (1:1000, ab205921, Abcam), β-catenin (1:4000, ab6302, Abcam), Lamin B1 (1:5000, ab16048, Abcam), or β-actin (1:5000, ab8227, Abcam) antibody overnight. Membranes were washed and incubated with the HRP-conjugated goat-anti rabbit antibody (1:10000, ab6721, Abcam). Bands were then visualized by adding ECL substrate, and band intensity was analyzed by Image J software.

Co-IP and in vitro ubiquitination assay

Pierce™ Co-Immunoprecipitation Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific) was used to preform Co-IP assays. Briefly, cells were lysed in IP Lysis/Wash Buffer and subjected to centrifugation to collect the supernatants. Resin was added into the Spin Column, and the β-catenin (5 µg, ab32572, Abcam) or USP22 (5 µg, ab195289, Abcam) antibody was immobilized to the resin in the column. The supernatants were added into the column and incubated overnight. After wash, the Co-IP complexes were eluted from the resin and subjected to Western blotting for detecting USP22 or β-catenin. For in vitro ubiquitination analysis, proteins immunoprecipitated with the β-catenin antibody were subjected to Western blotting for detecting ubiquitination with the ubiquitin antibody (1:1000, ab134953, Abcam).

Protein stability

Cells were treated with cycloheximide (CHX, 10 µg/mL, Selleck, Houston, TX, USA) for 0, 4, or 8 h, lysed and subjected to Western blotting to analyze the levels of β-catenin. Relative β-catenin levels were calculated via normalization to the 0 h time point.

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)

High-Sensitivity ChIP Kit was provided by Abcam and used to perform ChIP assays. Cells were crosslinked for 10 min using 1% formaldehyde. The reaction was quenched by adding glycine to a final concentration of 1 M. Following washing, cells were lysed on ice in working lysis buffer. Chromatin was pelleted, resuspended in ChIP Buffer, and sheared by sonication to an average size of approximately 500 bp. β-catenin (5 µg, ab32572, Abcam), USP22 (5 µg, NBP1-49644SS, Bio-Techne, Minneapolis, MN, USA), or normal rabbit IgG, along with antibody buffer, were incubated in Assay Strip Wells for 1 h. After washing the wells with ChIP buffer, ChIP reactions were initiated by adding chromatin, ChIP buffer, enrichment enhancer, and blocker solution. Subsequent washes were performed, followed by crosslink reversal. DNA was then eluted, purified, and subjected to quantitative PCR to assess PD-L1 promoter enrichment.

Tumor xenograft in mice

For tumor xenograft studies, 5- to 6-week-old C57BL/6 mice (n = 5 per group) were purchased from Hunan SLAC (Changsha, China) and housed and bred in a specific pathogen free animal facility under a 12 h light/12 h dark cycle with free access to water and food. Following one week of acclimatization, U2932 cells from Control, OE-circ_0026652, miR-1287-5p and OE-circ_0026652 + miR-1287-5p groups were detached, washed, and counted. Then, 1 × 106 cells were resuspended in 100 µL of PBS and injected subcutaneously into the flanks of mice to establish the xenograft model as previously described with modification [26, 27], and mice were divided into Control, OE-circ_0026652, miR-1287-5p and OE-circ_0026652 + miR-1287-5p groups. Throughout the experiment, tumor dimensions were measured using a vernier caliper every 3 days starting from Day 6, and volume was calculated using the formula A × B2/2, where A is the sagittal dimension (mm) and B is the cross dimension (mm). At the 18-day endpoint, mice were euthanized, xenograft tumors were surgically excised, and tumor volume was calculated. The percentages of IFN-γ (506504, Biolegend, San Diego, CA, USA), TNF-α (376207, BioLegend), Granzyme B (372205, BioLegend), and Perforin (353319, BioLegend) in CD8+ T cells were assessed by flow cytometry. Investigators were blinded to group allocation to minimize potential bias. All procedures adhered to the ARRIVE guidelines and received approval from the Institutional Animal Ethical Committee of The Affiliated Cancer Hospital of Zhengzhou University.

Statistical analysis

All in vitro experiments were performed with at least three independent biological replicates. For qPCR, ELISA, and luciferase reporter assays, each biological replicate included three technical replicates, and the mean value of the technical replicates was used for statistical analysis. Data was represented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) from at least three independent biological replicates and processed using GraphPad Prism 9.0. The normality of data distribution was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. For data that followed a normal distribution, differences between two groups were assessed using two-tailed Student’s t-test. For comparisons involving multiple groups, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed, followed by Tukey’s post hoc test for pairwise comparisons. For data that did not follow a normal distribution, non-parametric tests (Mann-Whitney U test for two groups or Kruskal-Wallis test with Dunn’s post hoc test for multiple groups) were used. Correlations between gene expression levels were analyzed using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05. All statistical tests were two-sided. Sample sizes were chosen based on pilot experiments and previous publications to ensure adequate statistical power without excessive animal use.

Results

METTL3-dependent m6A modification-mediated upregulation of circ_0026652 in DLBCL

Hsa_circ_0026652 (https://www.circbank.cn/#/browse/details?type=human%26id=hsa_PCBP2_0005300), also known as hsa_circPCBP2, is derived from the gene Pcbp2 (Supplementary Fig. 1A and B), and highly expressed in DLBCL [11]. We previously demonstrated the circular structure stability and precise back-splice junction sequences [12]. To further investigate the roles of circ_0026652 and its underlying mechanisms in DLBCL, we conducted this study. We collected lymph node tissues from patients with DLBCL to analyze circ_0026652 expression. Lymph node tissues from patients with reactive lymph node hyperplasia (RLH) were used as controls. Patients were divided into high and low cicr_0026652 groups based on the median expression level of circ_0026652, and the clinicopathological features of patients were shown in Table S1. The expression of circ_0026652 was associated with TNM stages, extra-nodal invasion and IPI score, but it was not associated with age, gender and B symptoms (Table S1). As expected, circ_0026652 was significantly upregulated in DLBCL (Fig. 1A). Compared to patients with low circ_0026652 expression, patients with high circ_0026652 had significantly decreased overall survival rates (Fig. 1B), although the survival analysis was not adjusted for clinical covariates. We further analyzed circ_0026652 expression in DLBCL cell lines OCI-LY-7, OCI-LY-10, OCI-LY3, and U2932. Compared to GM12878 lymphoblastoid cells, all these DLBCL cells showed high expression of circ_0026652, with U2932 cells exhibiting the highest circ_0026652 expression (Fig. 1C). Thus, U2932 cells were used in subsequent assays. Cytoplasmic and nuclear RNAs were extracted from U2932 cells. Circ_0026652 was distributed in both the cytoplasm and nucleus, with a slightly lower level in the nucleus (Fig. 1D). Moreover, circ_0026652 could be efficiently enriched by an Ago2 antibody through RIP assays (Fig. 1E). As N6-methyladenosine (m6A) is abundant in RNAs and a key modulator of circRNA metabolism [28], we predicted m6A methylation modification sites of circ_0026652 via SRAMP and RMBase 2.0 [29, 30] (Fig. 1F). We observed significantly increased m6A levels of circ_0026652 in U2932 cells (Fig. 1G). METTL3 and METTL14, two core components of RNA m6A methyltransferase complex, are required to m6A modification [31]. We found that METTL3 was markedly upregulated in U2932 cells, while no change in METTL14 expression was observed (Fig. 1H). Furthermore, METTL3 was upregulated, and its expression was positively correlated with circ_0026652 expression in DLBCL patients (Fig. 1I and J). METTL3 was knocked down in U2932 cells by siRNA transfection (Fig. 1K). Circ_0026652 was significantly enriched by a METTL3 antibody, which was impaired by METTL3 knockdown (Fig. 1L). MeRIP-PCR assays showed that a m6A antibody-mediated enrichment of circ_0026652 was reduced by METTL3 knockdown (Fig. 1M). Knockdown of METTL3 substantially reduced circ_0026652 expression and accelerated the decay of circ_0026652 after actinomycin D treatment (Fig. 1N and O). These observations suggested that METTL3-dependent m6A modification stabilized circ_0026652 and upregulated its expression in DLBCL. METTL3 was overexpressed in U2932 cells, and overexpression of METTL3 promoted cell proliferation, reduced cell apoptosis, and upregulated circ_0026652 expression (Supplementary Fig. 2A-D). However, these effects mediated by METTL3 overexpression were reversed by cicr_0026652 knockdown (Supplementary Fig. 2A-D), suggesting that METTL3-mediated protective effects in DLBCL cells were dependent on circ_0026652.

YTHDC1 promoted nuclear export of m6A-modifed circ_0026652 in DLBCL cells

As YTHDC1 functions as an RNA m6A reader and regulates RNA decay [32], we analyzed the interaction between circ_0026652 and YTHDC1 in U2932 cells. RIP-PCR assays showed that circ_0026652 was efficiently enriched by a YTHDC1 antibody (Fig. 2A). YTHDC1 was knocked down through si-YTHDC1 transfection (Fig. 2B and C). The YTHDC1 antibody-mediated circ_0026652 enrichment was significantly reduced by YTHDC1 knockdown (Figure D). YTHDC1 has been demonstrated to mediate nuclear export of m6A-modified RNAs [23]. We analyzed the distribution of circ_0026652 through FISH assays. The nuclear distribution of circ_0026652 was largely disrupted in YTHDC1-knockdown U2932 cells (Fig. 2E). Circ_0026652 expression was dramatically inhibited by YTHDC1 knockdown (Fig. 2F). Our findings indicated that YTHDC1 promoted nuclear export of m6A-modified circ_0026652 to regulate circ_0026652 fate in DLBCL cells.

Circ_0026652 acted as a sponge for miR-1287-5p to promote DLBCL cell proliferation and inhibit apoptosis

As circRNAs function as miRNA sponges to regulate pre-mRNA splicing, transcription and translation [33], potential target miRNAs of circ_0026652 were predicted by Starbase, CircBank and CircInteractome. hsa-miR-1287-5p was identified as a potential target miRNA of circ_0026652 (Fig. 3A). RIP-PCR assays showed significant enrichment of circ_0026652 and miR-1287-5p by an Ago2 antibody (Fig. 3B). Strong cytoplasmic colocalization of circ_0026652 and miR-1287-5p was observed in U2932 cells (Fig. 3C). Subsequently, the binding site for miR-1287-5p in circ_002662 was mutated, and the luciferase activity of the wildtype or mutant circ_0026652 reporter in U2932 cells was measured. miR-1287-5p mimics significantly reduced the luciferase activity of the wildtype circ_0026652 reporter, but did not affect that of the mutant reporter (Fig. 3D). Furthermore, knockdown of circ_0026652 significantly increased miR-1287-5p levels in U2932 cells (Fig. 3E). miR-1287-5p was downregulated, and its expression was negatively correlated with circ_0026652 expression in DLBCL patients (Fig. 3F and G). Thus, circ_0026652 acted as a molecular sponge for miR-1287-5p in DLBCL. We further found that circ_0026652 knockdown-mediated negative regulation of cell proliferation and apoptosis was reversed by miR-1287-5p inhibition (Supplementary Fig. 3A-C). In addition, increased miR-1287-5p expression in circ_0026652-knockdown U2932 cells was profoundly reduced by miR-1287-5p inhibition (Supplementary Fig. 3D). These data suggested that circ_0026652 knockdown-mediated effects in U2932 cells were dependent on miR-1287-5p upregulation.

USP22 maintained PD-L1 expression and reversed miR-1287-5p-mediated regulation of DLBCL cell proliferation and apoptosis

We further explored functional downstream targets of the circ_0026652-miR-1287-5p axis. The expression of USP22, a highly conserved ubiquitin hydrolase that prevents protein degradation by deubiquitinating substrate proteins [34], showed gradually increased as DLBCL progresses (Supplementary Fig. 4A). Importantly, USP22 was highly expressed in DLBCL patients, and its expression was positively correlated with circ_0026652 expression but negatively correlated with miR-1287-5p expression in patients (Fig. 5A-C). Six lymph node specimens from DLBCL patients were randomly selected, and increased levels of USP22 were confirmed by Western blotting (Fig. 5D). The binding site for miR-1287-5p in USP22 was predicted by bioinformatics analysis (Supplementary Fig. 4B). Subsequently, the binding site for miR-1287-5p in USP22 was mutated (Supplementary Fig. 4B), and the luciferase activity of the wildtype or mutant USP22 reporter in U2932 cells was measured. MiR-1287-5p mimics significantly reduced the luciferase activity of the wildtype USP22 reporter, but did not affect that of the mutant reporter (Supplementary Fig. 4C). Moreover, miR-1287-5p overexpression inhibited USP22 expression in U2932 cells (Supplementary Fig. 4D and E). Thus, miR-1287-5p targeted USP22 to dramatically suppress its expression in DLBCL cells. To further investigate whether miR-1287-5p exerts its function dependent on USP22 downregulation, miR-1287-5p and USP22 were overexpressed in U2932 cells. Overexpression of miR-1287-5p repressed cell proliferation and enhanced cell apoptosis, whereas these effects were significantly reversed by USP22 overexpression (Supplementary Fig. 5A-C). miR-1287-5p-mediated inhibition of USP22 expression was abrogated by USP22 overexpression (Supplementary Fig. 5D and E). These data suggested that miR-1287-5p inhibited USP22 expression to suppress DLBCL cell proliferation and apoptosis. As PD-L1 is a key regulator that suppresses T cell activity and enables tumor immune evasion, and is a target of USP22 in cancer cells [35, 36], we analyzed the expression of USP22 and PD-L1 in U2932 and NU-DUL-1 cells after USP22 knockdown. PD-L1 expression was significantly downregulated by USP22 knockdown in U2932 and NU-DUL-1 cells (Fig. 5E and F). Moreover, USP22 knockdown reduced the surface levels of PD-L1 on U2932 and NU-DUL-1 cells (Fig. 5G). Collectively, miR-1287-5p regulated malignant phenotypes of DLBCL cells by inhibiting USP22 expression and subsequent PD-L1 expression.

Knockdown of USP22 considerably reduced β-catenin expression in U2932 and NU-DUL-1 cells (Fig. 6B and C). NU-DUL-1 cells were treated with MG132, and β-catenin levels gradually increased (Fig. 6D). Furthermore, lysates from MG132-treated USP22-knockdown U2932 cells and control U2932 cells were immunoprecipitated with a β-catenin antibody and subsequently immunoblotted with a ubiquitin antibody. Knockdown of USP22 significantly elevated β-catenin ubiquitination in U2932 cells (Fig. 6F). To conclude, USP22 deubiquitinated and stabilized β-catenin in U2932 cells.

Discussion

DLBCL constitutes the most common aggressive subtype of non-Hodgkin lymphoma and imposes a considerable impact on global health. The prognosis associated with DLBCL is frequently unfavorable due to the development of resistance to standard R-CHOP regimens, underscoring the requirement for effective therapeutic approaches [40, 41]. Ongoing research is focused on the development of novel immunotherapy strategies for DLBCL, but a critical challenge is immune evasion. Immune evasion in DLBCL is a complex process that involves various mechanisms, including loss of major histocompatibility complex, resistance to apoptosis, and activation of immune checkpoints [4, 42]. The molecular mechanisms driving immune evasion remain largely unknown. Here, we found that, driven by METTL3-mediated m6A modification and YTHDC1-facilitated nuclear export, upregulated circ_0026652 acted as a miR-1287-5p sponge to promote tumor cell proliferation and impair T-cell function in DLBCL, suppressing miR-1287-5p to promote tumor cell proliferation/survival and impair T-cell function. The circ_0026652/miR-1287-5p axis drove tumor growth by targeting USP22 and stabilizing β-catenin to upregulate PD-L1 in DLBCL, potentially modulating immune evasion.

m6A modification, catalyzed by writers such as METTL3, imposes a key regulatory mechanism on circRNAs, profoundly influencing their fate and function. This modification is recognized by specific readers such as YTHDC1, which orchestrate circRNA stability, nuclear export, and interactions, thereby modulating diverse cellular processes [43–45]. Emerging evidence has suggested vital roles of circRNAs in the progression of DLBCL [46, 47], but the m6A modification of circRNAs and how it can affect circRNA fate and function in DLBCL has not been well documented. A previous study reported that METTL3 was upregulated in DLBCL, and its upregulation was associated with increased RNA m6A modification and accelerated DLBCL development [48], suggesting that circRNA m6A modification might be dysregulated in DLBCL. Despite circ_0026652 has been reported to be highly expressed in DLBCL, its m6A level has never been reported. Consistently, this study confirmed increased levels of METTL3 and circ_0026652 in DLBCL. Notably, we further found that METTL3 facilitated m6A modification of circ_0026652 to stabilize and upregulate circ_0026652, thereby promoting DLBCL cell proliferation and reducing cell apoptosis. As we observed the distribution of circ_0026652 in both the cytoplasm and nucleus, we decided to investigate the mechanism by which its nuclear export is regulated. We demonstrated that YTHDC1 promoted nuclear export of m6A-modified circ_0026652 to regulate its fate in DLBCL cells. Thus, our study reveals a novel m6A-dependent molecular mechanism controlling circ_0026652 nuclear export and fate through the METTL3/YTHDC1 axis in DLBCL.

Growing evidence has demonstrated that miRNAs are emerging as promising biomarkers of diagnosis and prognosis and potential therapeutic targets for DLBCL [49, 50]. Several miRNAs, such as miR-129-5p and miR-5590-3p, are linked to the modulation of immune evasion through the PD-1/PD-L1 checkpoint in DLBCL [51, 52]. CircRNAs function as miRNA sponges to regulate gene expression [53]. We previously reported that circ_0026652 sponged miR-33a/b to disinhibit PD-L1, thus enhancing DLBCL stemness and chemoresistance [12]. In this study, we identified miR-1287-5p as a novel miRNA target of circ_0026652 in DLBCL, and circ_0026652-mediated regulation of DLBCL cell proliferation and apoptosis was dependent on sponging miR-1287-5p. To date, studies on miR-1287-5p in hematological malignancies remain limited. A previous study reported that miR-1287-5p was downregulated in patients with acute myeloid leukemia [54]. However, emerging evidence suggests its potential tumor-suppressive roles in solid tumors [55, 56]. Its role in hematologic malignancies, particularly in DLBCL, has not been previously characterized, making our study the first to implicate miR-1287-5p in lymphoma biology. Importantly, the circ_0026652/miR-1287-5p axis in DLBCL cells impaired cocultured CD8+ T cell activity and enhanced DLBCL cell resistance to T cell cytotoxicity. These evidence reveals a specific mechanism by which the circ_0026652/miR-1287-5p axis is involved in DLBCL progression. Blocking the circ_0026652/miR-1287-5p axis represents a potential therapeutic strategy to restore effective anti-tumor activity of CD8+ T cells in DLBCL. While our study focuses on the miRNA sponge function of circ_0026652, we acknowledge that circRNAs may exert additional regulatory mechanisms. Circ_0026652 is derived from the PCBP2 gene, which encodes an RNA-binding protein involved in mRNA stabilization. This raises the possibility that circ_0026652 might also regulate gene expression through RBP sequestration or by competing with its linear counterpart. Future studies should investigate these potential mechanisms, such as identifying circ_0026652-interacting proteins and assessing its coding potential, to provide a more comprehensive understanding of its oncogenic roles in DLBCL.

USP22, a deubiquitinating enzyme, effectively regulates stemness, metastasis, chemoresistance, and immune regulation via its deubiquitinating activity in various cancers [57]. USP22 is dysregulated in cancers, and it may stabilize PD-L1 that modulate immune evasion and immunotherapy resistance [58]. However, the roles of USP22 in DLBCL remains unknown. We identified USP22 as a novel target of miR-1287-5p in DLBCL, and miR-1287-5p-mediated regulation of DLBCL cell proliferation and apoptosis was dependent on inhibiting USP22. The USP22/β-catenin axis has been well demonstrated to promote cancer progression [37, 59, 60]. Consistent with previous studies, we found that USP22 deubiquitinated β-catenin to prevent its proteasomal degradation, which led to the accumulation and nuclear translocation of β-catenin in DLBCL. Targeting the oncogenic USP22/β-catenin pathway potentially inhibit DLBCL progression and overcome treatment resistance linked to this pathway. We acknowledge that USP22 is unlikely to be the sole target of miR-1287-5p in DLBCL. Bioinformatic analysis predicts multiple potential targets of miR-1287-5p, including genes involved in cell cycle regulation, apoptosis, and immune modulation. While our study focused on USP22 based on its established role in deubiquitination and PD-L1 regulation, we recognize that other miR-1287-5p targets may also contribute to the phenotypes, which needs further investigation in future studies.

In many cancers, PD-L1 expression on tumor cells predicts poor prognosis and drives PD-1-mediated immunosuppression and immune evasion [61], and PD-L1 expression is associated with poor survival in DLBCL patients [62]. USP22 interacts with PD-L1, deubiquitinates PD-L1 and suppresses its proteasome degradation, thus promoting PD-L1 stability in cancer cells [36, 63]. Consistently, USP22 maintained PD-L1 expression by inhibiting β-catenin nuclear translocation in DLBCL cells. The circ_0026652/miR-1287-5p axis-mediated regulation of DLBCL malignancy and T cell activity was dependent on the USP22/β-catenin/PD-L1 signaling pathway. These findings support the significance of the oncogenic USP22/β-catenin/PD-L1 signaling pathway in DLBCL, and blocking PD-L1 may contribute to overcoming the challenge caused by decreased T cell anti-tumor activity.

Nevertheless, we acknowledge several important limitations of the present study. First, the clinical validation is based on a relatively small cohort of DLBCL patients. Future studies should aim to validate these findings in larger, multi-center cohorts with comprehensive clinical annotation and longer follow-up periods. Also, our study lacked validation across diverse DLBCL subtypes and primary cells, and the clinical correlation was still insufficient. Second, while our in vitro experiments provide mechanistic insights into the circ_0026652/miR-1287-5p/USP22/β-catenin/PD-L1 axis, these studies were performed using established DLBCL cell lines, which may not fully recapitulate the heterogeneity of primary human DLBCL. Additionally, the xenograft mouse model used in this study does not completely replicate the complex tumor microenvironment and immune cell interactions present in human DLBCL patients. Future studies using patient-derived xenografts in humanized mice would provide more clinically relevant insights. Third, while our findings suggest that targeting the circ_0026652/miR-1287-5p/USP22/β-catenin/PD-L1 axis may have therapeutic potential, direct therapeutic interventions were not tested in this study. The development of specific inhibitors targeting circ_0026652 or USP22, or approaches to deliver miR-1287-5p mimics, would be necessary to evaluate the therapeutic utility of this pathway. We did not perform direct immune checkpoint blockade rescue experiments, such as co-culture with anti-PD-L1 or anti-PD-1 neutralizing antibodies, to definitively demonstrate that the observed T-cell suppression is specifically mediated through PD-1/PD-L1 engagement. Fourth, miR-1287-5p likely has multiple targets beyond USP22 that may contribute to its tumor-suppressive effects in DLBCL. Future studies employing unbiased approaches such as transcriptome-wide analysis of miR-1287-5p targets would provide a more complete understanding of its biological functions.

The present study builds directly upon our previous characterization of circ_0026652 in DLBCL [12]. In that prior work, we validated its circular structure and knockdown specificity, and demonstrated that circ_0026652 promotes DLBCL stemness and chemoresistance by sponging miR-33a/b to upregulate PD-L1. However, the upstream regulation of circ_0026652 and its potential role in immune modulation remained unexplored. The current study extends these findings in three directions: (1) METTL3-dependent m6A modification and YTHDC1-mediated nuclear export control circ_0026652 expression; (2) a novel miR-1287-5p/USP22/β-catenin/PD-L1 axis represents an additional downstream pathway distinct from the miR-33a/b axis; and (3) circ_0026652 modulates CD8+ T-cell activity and contributes to immune evasion. In summary, our study uncovers a novel METTL3-m6A-circ_0026652 axis that promotes DLBCL progression and inhibits CD8+ T cell activity by sponging miR-1287-5p, leading to activation of the USP22/β-catenin/PD-L1 pathway. This study identifies the METTL3/circ_0026652/USP22/β-catenin/PD-L1 axis as a potential driver of DLBCL immune evasion, providing novel targets to overcome immunotherapy resistance. These findings identify the METTL3/circ_0026652/USP22/β-catenin/PD-L1 axis as a potential driver of DLBCL immune evasion and suggest novel targets that warrant further investigation for overcoming immunotherapy resistance. However, given the limitations outlined above, the therapeutic implications of targeting this axis remain preliminary and require extensive validation in more clinically relevant models.

Mechanisms influencing generic drug substitution behavior under the national centralized drug procurement policy
Authors: Fanyu Lin, Haoye Li, Keying Zhu, YangXiaolong Wu, Qile Fan, Jinxi Ding, Wei Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundAs a key component of China’s healthcare reform, the national centralized drug procurement (NCDP) policy has significantly influenced physician behavior regarding generic substitution. Therefore, it is essential to investigate the mechanisms underlying physicians’ decisions to prescribe bid-winning generics under the NCDP, to provide empirical evidence for policy optimization, and to support the broader implementation of generic substitution strategies.MethodsDrawing upon the theory of planned behavior (TPB), a conceptual model was developed, and a corresponding scale was constructed. Structural equation modelling (SEM) was employed to examine the influencing mechanisms, focusing on the relationships among subjective attitude, subjective norms, perceived behavioral control, subjective willingness, and actual prescribing behavior.ResultsThe findings indicated that subjective attitude was the primary driver of physicians’ intentions to prescribe bid-winning generics. Subjective norms did not exert a statistically significant effect on prescribing willingness, while perceived behavioral control was found to have a negative influence on intention and minimal impact on actual substitution behavior. Subjective willingness has emerged as a critical mediating factor that directly shapes physicians’ substitution behavior.ConclusionThis study highlights the importance of enhancing physicians’ positive attitudes towards bid-winning generics, fostering a supportive normative environment, and mitigating excessive perceived behavioral control. These strategies may strengthen prescribing intentions, support effective and sustainable implementation of the NCDP, promote generic substitution, and reduce patients’ medication costs.KEYWORDS: National centralized drug procurement policy, prescription behavior, substitution of generic drugs, influencing mechanism

Background

The substitution of generic medicines, whereby a more expensive brand name medicine is replaced with a lower-cost generic equivalent in clinical practice, offers substantial economic and societal benefits (Ren et al., 2023; Xie & Shao, 2020; Yan et al., 2023). To ensure the equivalence of generic drugs to their branded counterparts, China implemented a consistency evaluation of generic drugs during the registration process. Building on this foundation, the national centralized drug procurement (NCDP) policy was introduced at the procurement stage to further promote generic substitution and alleviate the financial burden on patients. These measures have gradually improved the quality and utilization rate of generic medicines.

In 2016, the Chinese government initiated a consistency evaluation of the quality and efficacy of generic medicines during drug registration. This evaluation, conducted for all approved generics, aims to determine whether the quality of generic drugs is comparable to that of their brand name counterparts. As a result, the quality and market competitiveness of generics have been significantly enhanced (General Office of the State Council of China, 2016).

At the procurement stage, based on the confirmed equivalence between generic and branded medicines, an NCDP policy was implemented nationwide in 2019 (National Healthcare Security Administration, 2019). Led by the National Healthcare Security Administration (NHSA), the NCDP targets medicines that have undergone full market competition, particularly those with large market shares. Competitive price negotiations are conducted with manufacturers of both branded and generic products to determine the final bid-winning brands, typically, generics. These bid-winning brands are promoted by the NHSA for use in hospitals, thereby facilitating greater substitution of generic medicines. As illustrated in Figure 1, between December 2019 and April 2024, nine rounds of NCDP were completed, encompassing 359 chemical medicines and 16 insulin products, with an average price reduction of 53.5%.

The NHSA has established a centralized drug procurement framework that generally follows an eight-step process: variety selection, demand estimation, bidding and awarding, market allocation, procurement and distribution, usage monitoring, reimbursement settlement, and contract renewal (Table 1).

Process of the national centralized drug procurement.

Specific drug varieties are selected by the NHSA based on clinical demand, after which pharmaceutical companies are invited to submit bids. Upon determining the bid-winning enterprises, these companies are permitted to sequentially select provincial markets and are required to ensure sufficient supply within the chosen regions. The winning products are incorporated into hospital formularies and usage quotas are assigned, typically amounting to 50–70% of the estimated demand for the upcoming year. The exact quota proportion is adjusted according to the characteristics of each product.

Implementation of the policy was monitored by the NHSA at the institutional level. Healthcare facilities that meet their usage targets are eligible to receive financial incentives based on actual procurement volume, whereas those that underperform may be subjected to administrative penalties, such as public notification or score deductions in policy compliance assessments (The General Office of the National Health Commission, 2019). Consequently, the NCDP policy exerts a direct influence on physicians’ prescribing practices, promoting the use of bid-winning brands as substitutes for originator drugs, thereby encouraging the broader adoption of generic medicines in China (Ji, 2023).

Since the implementation of the NCDP policy, bid-winning generic medicines have significantly replaced their bid-non-winning brand name counterparts. Wang (2024) reported that one year after the initiation of the NCDP, the annual national procurement volume of bid-non-winning brand-name drugs had declined by an average of 46% compared with the year prior to the policy intervention. Similarly, Yang et al. (2022), Wang et al. (2021), Jiang and GE WH (2022), and Liu and Wang (2022) found that the utilization rate of bid-winning generic drugs increased significantly following NCDP, demonstrating a notable substitution effect on non-winning brand-name drugs. Li et al. (2024) analyzed the prescription substitution patterns of more than 300 NCDP-listed products and observed up to 56.06% substitution of bid-non-winning brands with bid-winning generics.

It is evident that physicians’ substitution behavior changed in response to the NCDP. Although most existing studies have focused on changes in prescribing utilization or procurement data, few have examined the underlying mechanisms driving these behavioral changes within the specific context of the NCDP. As a cornerstone of China’s healthcare reform, the NCDP requires a clearer understanding of the key factors influencing clinical prescribing decisions. Investigating the mechanisms by which the NCDP shapes physicians’ substitution behavior is essential for generating empirical evidence to support policy optimization and to further advance the implementation of generic substitution strategies.

Grounded in the theory of planned behavior (TPB), a model with strong theoretical and practical relevance for explaining and predicting prescribing behavior (Ajzen, 1985; Ajzen & Fishbein, 1977), this study constructed a framework to examine the mechanisms influencing physicians’ generic drug substitution under the NCDP. A corresponding measurement scale was developed, and structural equation modelling (SEM) was employed to empirically assess the impact pathways and underlying mechanisms through which various factors influence substitution behavior within the NCDP policy environment.

Methods

Study design

Study setting and methods

This study adopted a cross-sectional, non-interventional design to construct a model of the mechanisms underlying substitution behavior for bid-winning generics under the NCDP policy. The questionnaire was developed based on a comprehensive literature review. Using data collected via the questionnaire, SEM was employed to evaluate the influencing mechanisms and analyze the complex relationships between various influencing factors and physicians’ substitution behavior in favor of bid-winning generics.

Theoretical foundation

Model construction and questionnaire development were grounded in TPB. TPB has been widely applied in the analysis of medical policy and professional behavior. Godin et al. (2008) conducted a systematic review of the application of social cognitive theories, such as social cognitive theory, the theory of interpersonal behavior, operant learning theory, the technology acceptance model, and the attitude-social influence-self-efficacy model, in studies involving healthcare professionals, including physicians, nurses, and pharmacists. The review found that TPB exhibited the strongest explanatory power for actual clinical behaviors, with a frequency-weighted mean R² of 0.35, which was significantly higher than that of alternative models (e.g. R² = 0.06).

Owing to its superior predictive validity, the TPB is considered the most appropriate theoretical framework for analyzing physicians’ prescribing behavior. Accordingly, it was adopted as the foundation of this study. Figure 2 shows the key dimensions of the TPB model.

Structure of the questionnaire

The theoretical framework was developed through a systematic literature review in which factors influencing physicians’ prescribing behavior were identified and synthesized, with appropriate modifications made in the context of the NCDP policy. The questionnaire employed a five-point Likert scale ranging from ‘strongly agree' to ‘strongly disagree'. For analysis purposes, ‘strongly agree' was assigned a score of 5, while ‘strongly disagree' was assigned a score of one. The reverse-scored items were reworded to ensure consistency in the scoring direction.

To assess actual behavior, a self-reported approach was adopted to evaluate the extent to which generic drug substitution had been undertaken by physicians. For control variables, sociodemographic information was collected using self-reported data provided by the respondents. The questionnaire was refined through expert panel discussions and a preliminary pilot survey. This process resulted in the final version of the instrument, which consisted of two parts. The first part captured respondents’ demographic characteristics. The second part comprises 40 items designed to evaluate prescribers’ subjective attitudes, subjective norms, perceived behavioral control, behavioral intention (subjective willingness), and actual behavior.

Subjective attitude refers to physicians’ overall disposition towards the substitution of bid-winning generic medicines. This domain included three secondary dimensions: substitution beliefs (confidence in the safety and efficacy of generics), cost control, and treatment expectations (expectation that generics reduce healthcare expenditure, potential performance incentives for cost-saving, and the belief that generics provide equivalent clinical outcomes to branded drugs).

Subjective norms describe the influence of social and professional environments on physicians’ substitution decisions. It encompasses formal expectations from regulatory authorities or healthcare institutions and compliance motivation, which reflects the tendency to conform to external pressures, such as peer influence.

Perceived behavioral control refers to perceived ease or difficulty in performing substitution behavior. This component includes facilitating conditions (environmental or institutional support that enables substitution) and self-efficacy (the physician’s perceived capability to implement substitution effectively). Behavioral intention (subjective willingness) represents the proactive inclination of physicians to prescribe bid-winning generic medicines. This intention was shaped by subjective attitude, subjective norm, and perceived behavioral control and directly influenced actual substitution behavior.

Data collection and statistical analysis

The study was conducted between January and June 2024. A targeted online questionnaire was distributed to physicians who prescribed authority in major medical institutions across China, including locations such as Beijing, Nanjing, Lianyungang (Jiangsu Province), Chengdu and Zigong (Sichuan Province), and Zhongshan (Guangdong Province), among others. Of 800 questionnaires distributed, 618 were returned, yielding a response rate of 77.25%. Responses were collected from 145 medical institutions in 18 provinces and municipalities. To ensure data quality, 107 questionnaires completed in less than 60 s and one incomplete questionnaire were excluded. None of the responses exhibited internal inconsistency. Ultimately, 510 valid questionnaires were included in the analysis.

Exploratory factor analysis (EFA) was performed on a self-developed TPB-based scale. The Kaiser – Meyer – Olkin (KMO) measure was 0.961, and Bartlett’s test of sphericity was statistically significant (χ² = 11,830.84, df = 465, p < 0.001), indicating that the data were suitable for factor analysis. Principal component analysis with varimax rotation yielded a cumulative explained variance of 64.2%. The rotated factor structure aligned well with the TPB theoretical framework, with most items exhibiting satisfactory factor loading (> 0.5). Cronbach’s α coefficients for all constructs were satisfactory, confirming the strong internal consistency and reliability of the scale.

As shown in Table 3, most latent variables demonstrate good convergent validity, with average variance extracted (AVE) values exceeding 0.60 and composite reliability (CR) values meeting or approaching 0.70. However, a few exogenous variables exhibit relatively low AVE values. According to the guidelines for structural equation modeling (Fornell & Larcker, 1981; Hair et al., 2015), these variables may still be considered reliable, as their CR values were close to or exceeded 0.70. Given that these dimensions were newly developed and consisted of relatively few items, their explanatory power was deemed acceptable in the context of this study.

Structural equation modeling results

The structural equation model exhibited a borderline acceptable fit according to multiple indices (RMSEA = 0.079, CMIN/DF = 4.18, CFI = 0.885, TLI = 0.873). Although an optimal fit was not achieved, most path coefficients were statistically significant with relatively small standard errors, suggesting stable structural relationships. Furthermore, bootstrap analysis confirmed the robustness of the parameter estimates, as values remained consistent across resamples, and all confidence intervals excluded zero. Overall, the model demonstrated satisfactory theoretical coherence and statistical reliability.

The results support the key path assumptions of the TPB in the context of physicians’ prescribing behavior under the NCDP policy. Subjective attitudes exerted a strong positive influence on physicians’ subjective willingness to prescribe bid-winning generic drugs, followed by subjective norms. In contrast, perceived behavioral control was unexpectedly negatively associated with subjective willingness. Additionally, subjective willingness emerged as a strong and direct predictor of actual prescription behavior, whereas the direct effect of perceived behavioral control on actual behavior was not statistically significant. In summary, within the NCDP policy context, physicians with more favorable attitudes toward bid-winning generics and stronger perceived normative pressures were more likely to express an intention to substitute originator drugs with generics. A stronger intention corresponded to a more frequent actual substitution behavior. Conversely, physicians with higher levels of perceived control over their prescription decisions tended to report slightly lower intentions to prescribe generics. These findings highlight the primary mechanisms shaping physicians’ prescribing behavior under the current policy landscape.

Path analysis

Effect of subjective attitude on subjective willingness

Effect of subjective attitude on subjective willingness

Subjective attitude was found to exert a significant positive effect on subjective willingness to engage in generic drug substitution. Physicians with more favorable attitudes were significantly more willing to prescribe bid-winning generics (β = 0.527, 95% CI [0.208, 1.31], p = 0.012), with a strong effect size. Further analysis identified two second-order dimensions within subjective attitude, cost control, treatment expectation, and substitution belief, with differing explanatory weights. Cost-control and treatment expectation (β = 0.917): this refers to physicians’ belief that bid-winning generics provide clinical efficacy and safety comparable to brand-name drugs while also reducing medical costs. As the most influential factors, confidence in clinical outcomes and cost-effectiveness were the primary drivers of favorable attitudes. Substitution belief (β = 0.567): this refers to physicians’ familiarity with and trust in the quality and efficacy of generic drugs. While positively associated with subjective attitude, its lower factor loading indicates a weaker relative influence.

(2)Effect of subjective norm on subjective willingness

Effect of subjective norm on subjective willingness

Subjective norms were positively associated with physicians’ willingness to prescribe bid-winning generics. Physicians who perceived stronger environmental or institutional expectations such as hospital policies, departmental leadership, and peer influence tended to express higher willingness (β = 0.45, 95% CI [ – 0.113, 0.829], p = 0.109). Although the effect was not statistically significant, the direction and magnitude were consistent with the TPB assumptions, suggesting potential theoretical relevance under specific conditions that warrant further investigation.

(3)Effect of perceived behavioural control on subjective willingness and actual behaviour

Effect of perceived behavioural control on subjective willingness and actual behaviour

Perceived behavioral control exhibited contrasting effects on subjective willingness and actual prescribing behavior. A significant negative association was observed between perceived behavioral control and subjective willingness (β = −0.175, 95% CI [−0.538, −0.045], p = 0.009). Although TPB typically predicts a positive relationship, the inverse association observed here may reflect contextual constraints specific to NCDP policy, such as formulary restrictions or institutional pressures that reduce autonomy despite perceived competence.

The direct effect of perceived behavioral control on actual prescription behavior was not statistically significant (β = 0.024, 95% CI [ – 0.168, 0.269], p = 0.367). This suggests that perceived control over prescribing decisions had a minimal independent influence on whether physicians ultimately substituted for generics.

(4)Effect of subjective willingness on actual behaviour

Effect of subjective willingness on actual behaviour

Subjective willingness had a strong and statistically significant positive impact on actual behavior (β = 0.804, 95% CI [1.811, 2.559], p = 0.002), indicating a large effect size. Physicians who demonstrated a stronger willingness to prescribe bid-winning generics were substantially more likely to act accordingly in clinical practice. This finding aligns with a central tenet of the TPB: behavioral intention is the most proximal and reliable predictor of actual behavior, and a high level of intention generally leads to a corresponding action.

Discussion

The findings revealed that physicians’ subjective attitudes exerted the strongest influence on their subjective willingness to engage in generic drug substitution (β = 0.527, 95% CI [0.208, 1.31], p = 0.012), followed by subjective norms (β = 0.45, 95% CI [ – 0.113, 0.829], p = 0.109). In contrast, perceived behavioral control was found to have a significant but negative effect on subjective willingness (β = – 0.175, 95% CI [ – 0.538, – 0.045], p = 0.009).

Considering these findings, the adequacy of the overall model warrants consideration to ensure the robustness of interpretations. While the RMSEA and CFI values were close to the recommended thresholds, and some exogenous variables exhibited moderate collinearity, the model demonstrated an overall acceptable fit. This borderline fit may introduce some uncertainty regarding the strength of individual path estimates; however, such nuances are more likely to reflect the inherent complexity of behavioral constructs rather than indicate systematic model misspecification. The model’s theoretical coherence, combined with its statistically significant path coefficients and strong factor loadings, provides compelling support for its structural adequacy. These elements establish a robust foundation for interpreting results and refining the theoretical framework for future research.

Subjective attitude had the strongest influence on substitution behavior

This study found that physicians’ subjective attitudes exerted the strongest influence on their subjective willingness to engage in generic drug substitution. This finding is consistent with that of Ren et al. (2023), who reported that in countries such as the United States, Europe, and Japan, physician attitudes were the primary determinant facilitating generic substitution. Specifically, the acceptance of generics by physicians has been regarded as essential for ensuring scientifically justified and rational substitution practices. Subjective attitude is composed of two second-order dimensions: cost control and treatment expectation, and substitution belief.

Cost-control and treatment expectation

Regarding treatment expectations, physicians generally expressed confidence in the efficacy and safety of bid-winning generics under the NCDP. The policy mandates that only generics that have passed rigorous evaluations of bioequivalence and quality consistency are eligible for inclusion in centralized procurement. This requirement plays a key role in fostering trust among physicians in the clinical performance of these drugs. As frontline healthcare providers, physicians prioritize therapeutic outcomes for patients. Once the clinical effectiveness of bid-winning generics is assured, their resistance to substitution is notably diminished.

In clinical settings, hospital pharmacy departments have supported this transition by promoting consistent evaluation through academic presentations, organizing clinical assessment sessions, and facilitating peer-sharing events. These initiatives have contributed to increased physician confidence in the use of generic drugs. Consequently, the substitution of brand name drugs with bid-winning generics has been perceived not only as a policy requirement but also as a form of rational prescription.

In addition to the therapeutic equivalence, physicians have placed considerable emphasis on cost-effectiveness. On one hand, the significant price reductions achieved through the NCDP have alleviated the financial burden on patients, an outcome welcomed by physicians. On the other hand, institutional constraints such as drug expenditure targets and total medical insurance budget caps incentivized the selection of lower-cost medications. Some NCDP pilot regions have even introduced financial reward mechanisms, which further encourage prescribers to consider economic outcomes in their decision making. When physicians perceive that prescribing bid-winning generics can benefit patients financially and contribute to their institutional financial performance, their likelihood of choosing these medications increases. Over time, this dynamic fosters a favorable attitude, characterized by the perception that these drugs are both ‘economical and effective.'

Substitution belief

Compared to cost control and treatment expectations, the influence of substitution beliefs was relatively weaker. This may reflect an ongoing but incomplete cognitive shift regarding generic drugs. Although consistency evaluations and policy promotions have gradually improved trust in the quality of generics, certain physicians remain influenced by past prescribing experiences and brand loyalty. For example, older patients often exhibit a preference for brand name medications, driven by concerns that lower-cost alternatives may be less effective. These patient perceptions may discourage physicians from fully embracing substitution. While substitution belief has improved under the NCDP, its effect on overall attitude formation remains modest compared to expectations concerning efficacy and cost. Strengthening physicians’ beliefs in the reliability of bid-winning generics is likely to require the accumulation of robust real-world evidence and extended clinical practice experience (Yan et al., 2023).

In summary, this study empirically confirmed that physicians’ subjective attitudes have a significant positive influence on their intention to prescribe bid-winning generics. Expectations concerning proven efficacy and cost savings emerged as the primary drivers of this favorable disposition. These findings are consistent with the central objective of the NCDP to encourage the substitution of brand name drugs with high-quality, affordable generics, thereby facilitating effective cost containment in clinical practice.

External normative pressure from the NCDP on substitution behavior was not significant

The NCDP policy has been accompanied by a series of performance evaluation measures, including mandated fulfillment of agreed procurement volumes and the incorporation of drug utilization rates into physician performance assessments. Non-compliance may result in restrictions on prescribing rights or the imposition of administrative penalties. From a theoretical perspective, such top-down normative pressure is expected to significantly enhance physicians’ willingness to prescribe generic drugs.

However, the findings of this study indicated that the effect size of subjective norms on substitution willingness was not statistically significant. This may be attributed to the nature of compliance under the NCDP, which appears to be primarily driven by institutional enforcement rather than internalized agreement with policy objectives. As a result, the explanatory power of the subjective norm, as conceptualized within the TPB, may have been weakened.

A strong subjective norm generally reflects the prevailing institutional consensus regarding the promotion of bid-winning generics. Nonetheless, when such pressure is perceived solely as external or coercive, it may lead to passive compliance rather than genuine behavioral intention. This interpretation is consistent with the findings of previous research. For example, Ren (2023) found that, although subjective norms had a positive effect on prescribing willingness, the corresponding path coefficient was relatively modest (β = 1.15, p < 0.05). Similarly, Yang et al. (2025) suggested that clinicians’ understanding of the NCDP remains limited and that their substitution behaviors are largely shaped by institutional mandates rather than by intrinsic behavioral intention.

Perceived autonomy did not positively influence subjective willingness or actual behavior

In traditional applications of TPB, higher perceived behavioral control is typically associated with stronger subjective willingness, as individuals who feel capable of performing a behavior are generally more motivated to do so. However, the present study revealed a negative correlation between perceived behavioral control and subjective willingness. This unexpected finding reflects the unique features of the NCDP context and suggests that physicians’ willingness to engage in generic drug substitution was not primarily rooted in professional autonomy or discretion but was instead shaped by institutional mandates and policy enforcement mechanisms.

A plausible explanation for this phenomenon may be drawn from the psychological reactance theory, which posits that when individuals perceive a high degree of control within a heavily regulated environment, their sense of autonomy may be undermined. This, in turn, reduces intrinsic motivation and the willingness to act. From a systemic perspective, the NCDP imposes strict compliance requirements and administrative oversight, thereby increasing perceived external pressure and shifting the nature of physicians’ prescription decisions from autonomous to obligatory. Accordingly, physicians who feel a greater degree of control over prescribing decisions may have been less inclined to conform to policy expectations, highlighting a reverse relationship between perceived autonomy and willingness.

In addition, the direct effect of perceived behavioral control on actual behavior was found to be statistically non-significant. This may be attributed to the practical realities of NCDP implementation. Under this policy, physicians’ prescribing decisions are largely governed by subjective willingness and institutional constraints, whereas the influence of individual perceptions of control appears to have diminished. For example, even when a physician feels confident in prescribing alternatives to bid-winning generics, rigid administrative procedures within hospital systems may have restricted such options, thereby limiting the relevance of perceived behavioral control in actual practice.

While the TPB acknowledges that perceived behavioral control can directly influence behavior, this mechanism is not supported within the current policy context. Instead, prescribing decisions appeared to be uniformly regulated by a combination of internal intentions and external enforcement, leaving little room for variation based on perceptions of autonomy. This finding highlights the importance of distinguishing between internalized willingness and passive compliance in future policy optimization. Ideally, the goal should be to shift physicians’ mindsets from ‘I am required to prescribe' to ‘I want to prescribe.’ Achieving this shift will require strengthening physicians’ acceptance of the policy and removing practical barriers to its implementation (Xie & Shao, 2020).

Behavioral willingness was the direct precondition for generic drug substitution

The NCDP policy established clear prescription requirements while simultaneously ensuring the quality and affordability of the selected medicines. These conditions, when combined with physicians’ positive attitudes, enhanced their subjective willingness to prescribe bid-winning generic drugs. Notably, physicians’ attitudes and perceived external norms did not found to exert a directly influence their behavior. Instead, these factors operated indirectly through subjective willingness, which acted as necessary intermediaries, leading to actual substitution. This finding highlights the importance of behavioral intention as the central mechanism linking influencing factors to prescribing behavior. Therefore, increasing physicians’ willingness should be prioritized in efforts to promote the effective substitution of brand-name drugs with bid-winning generics. Moreover, the smooth implementation of the NCDP, characterized by reliable drug supply, improved availability of generics, and alignment between policy and hospital prescribing systems, helped to ensure that willingness could be readily translated into actual prescribing behavior. These structural supports minimize barriers between intention and action, thereby facilitating consistent policy uptake.

Overall, the path analysis confirmed a strong and direct relationship between subjective willingness and actual behavior. Physicians willing to prescribe bid-winning generics were significantly more likely to act on their willingness in practice. Conversely, when willingness is low, reliance on policy enforcement alone may prove insufficient to achieve consistent prescribing outcomes. This highlights the need to proactively cultivate willingness among prescribers, as doing so may play a pivotal role in enhancing the practical implementation and long-term success of NCDP.

Policy optimization strategies

Based on the above path analysis, several strategies have been proposed to optimize the implementation of the NCDP and enhance physicians’ willingness to actively prescribe bid-winning generic drugs.

Strengthening physicians’ subjective attitudes

Policymakers and institutional implementers should focus on enhancing physicians’ confidence in both the clinical effectiveness and economic benefits of bid-winning generics (Wang et al., 2023). This may be achieved through the following measures: (1) enhancing treatment expectation: regulatory authorities should reinforce quality monitoring of bid-winning generics and provide robust clinical evidence demonstrating their equivalence to brand-name drugs in terms of efficacy and safety; (2) reinforcing cost-control expectation: hospital administrators should communicate the cost savings and reduce patient burden resulting from the NCDP, helping physicians appreciate the economic value of generics and foster a more cost-conscious prescribing attitude; and (3) improving substitution belief: senior clinical experts may be invited to share practical experiences and evidence from real-world practice to boost confidence and promote habitual prescription of generics. When physicians genuinely recognize the clinical and economic value of bid-winning generics, their attitudes are likely to become more favorable, thereby laying the foundation for voluntary and sustainable prescribing behavior.

Creating a supportive subjective norm environment

Hospitals and health authorities are encouraged to move beyond rigid performance targets and instead focus on fostering shared professional values and a positive departmental culture (Xie et al., 2021). Practical strategies may include publicly recognizing individuals or departments with strong performance in generic substitution, incorporating substitution behavior into performance evaluations and offering appropriate rewards, and promoting peer influence and leadership support to generate a sense of collective purpose. These measures may help shift physicians’ perceptions from feeling externally pressured to feeling internally supported, increasing the likelihood that policy expectations will be internalized as part of their own professional identity.

Improving perceived behavioral control through support and autonomy

Perceived behavioral control should be enhanced by providing physicians with both institutional support and greater clinical autonomy. Overreliance on administrative enforcement risks undermines professional confidence and buy-ins. Policy optimization efforts should therefore incorporate mechanisms that reflect clinical realities and physician input (Liu et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2023). For example, a flexible approval or reporting system could be introduced to accommodate clinically justified deviations from using bid-winning drugs, avoiding a one-size-fits-all approach. Feedback from physicians should be regularly collected and used to refine policy implementation, reinforcing their role in decision-making processes. Such adjustments would help physicians feel like participants in policy formation rather than mere implementers, promoting a sense of ownership and enhancing confidence in clinical decision making.

In conclusion, an appropriate balance between policy enforcement and physician autonomy should be achieved. While strong institutional mechanisms are essential in the early stages of implementation, long-term sustainability requires a gradual shift toward empowerment and professional support. By strengthening subjective willingness and enabling intrinsic motivation, physicians may move from the mindset of ‘I am told to prescribe' to ‘I want to prescribe.’ This transformation would support the NCDP’s long-term success, reduce dependence on administrative oversight, and ultimately help alleviate patients’ financial burden.

Conclusion

This study, grounded in TPB, constructed and empirically tested a model of generic drug substitution behavior under NCDP policy. The results indicated that subjective attitudes were the most influential factor driving physicians’ prescribing intentions, followed by subjective norms. In contrast, perceived behavioral control exerted a negative effect on prescribing intention and had a minimal influence on actual prescribing behavior.

Overall, physicians’ recognition of the clinical efficacy and economic value of bid-winning generics, along with their responsiveness to institutional norms, was identified as the primary driver of substitution behavior. While policy requirements and normative pressures are effective in promoting implementation, excessive emphasis on external control in a highly institutionalized environment may inadvertently suppress physicians’ willingness to act.

To facilitate a shift from external enforcement to internal acceptance, efforts should be directed toward strengthening physicians’ positive attitudes, fostering a supportive normative environment, and enhancing perceived support and clinical autonomy. Reducing practical barriers to substitution further reinforces proactive prescription behavior. These strategies are essential to improve physicians’ willingness to engage in generic substitution and ensure the long-term sustainability and success of the NCDP.

Limitations

Despite the contributions made by this study, several limitations should be acknowledged owing to its design and methodological constraints. First, the study was limited by access restrictions within the Hospital Information System (HIS), which prevented direct observation of physicians’ actual prescribing behavior. Consequently, the analysis relied on self-reported data to assess substitution practices. Future research should explore methods for linking self-reported willingness with objectively recorded prescribing data to assess whether intention translates into sustained behavioral change. Second, the study adopted a cross-sectional design, which limited its ability to capture changes in behavior and attitudes over time. This may have led to discrepancies between reported and actual behaviors. Thus, longitudinal research is recommended to improve the robustness of future findings. Such designs would allow for tracking of physicians’ prescribing patterns and attitudinal shifts over time, offering a more comprehensive understanding of the dynamics underpinning substitution behavior. Third, the structural equation model demonstrated only a borderline acceptable fit (RMSEA = 0.079; CFI = 0.883). This may be attributable to the complexity of the model, high intercorrelations among latent variables, and potential heterogeneity within the sample. Additionally, several theoretically expected paths did not achieve statistical significance, suggesting possible limitations in measurement design or sample size. Future studies should aim to improve the robustness of models by employing larger samples, refined instruments, and streamlined model structures. Finally, prescribing behaviors may differ across various tiers of medical institutions; however, a single measurement instrument was employed in this study. Future research should focus on refining the measurement tool and optimizing the model to reflect behavioral differences across institutional levels. Moreover, a more diverse and representative sample is required to improve the generalizability of our conclusions.

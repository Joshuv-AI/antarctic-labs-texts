Elucidating genetic backgrounds of myasthenia gravis in Japanese by genome-wide association studies and multi-omics analyses of thymoma
Authors: Hiroyuki Ueda, Tomoya Kubota, Risa Goto, Akari Suzuki, Takafumi Ojima, Kotaro Ogawa, Kyuto Sonehara, Shinichi Namba, Tatsuhiko Naito, Qingbo Wang, Shingo Konno, Makoto Samukawa, Naoki Kawaguchi, Takashi Kimura, Takamichi Sugimoto, Hiroyuki Murai, Takemori Yamawaki, Kenichi Kaida, Daiki Tokuyasu, Manato Yasuda, Hiroyuki Akamine, Yosuke Onishi, Yosuke Ogawa, Yuya Shirai, Ryuya Edahiro, Kenichi Yamamoto, Hideki Nagata, Naoko Ose, Jun Takayama, Ken Suzuki, Shinichi Higashiue, Shuzo Kobayashi, Hiroki Yamaguchi, Yasushi Okazaki, Naoyuki Matsumoto, Kenta Motomura, Hidenobu Koga, Tomoya Kubota, Tomoya Kubota, Shingo Konno
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Myasthenia gravis (MG) is an autoimmune disorder characterized by impaired neuromuscular transmission and motor symptoms. Its genetic background remains unclear, particularly beyond specific subtypes reported in European populations. Here, we perform a genome-wide association study (GWAS) of 1,434 MG cases covering all disease subtypes and 42,913 controls of Japanese, which newly identify the TERT locus (odds ratio [OR] = 1.31, P = 1.7×10-10). Subtype-stratified GWASs show stronger signals for generalized MG (gMG; OR = 1.38, P = 1.6×10-12), anti AChR antibody-positive gMG (g-AChR-Ab(+)MG; OR= 1.49, P = 2.1×10-15), and thymoma-associated gMG (g-TAMG; OR = 1.92, P = 1.1×10-15). Fine-mapping of the major histocompatibility complex region reveal distinct associations of HLA-DRB1 with late onset gMG (g-LOMG) and HLA-A with early onset gMG (g-EOMG). The MG risk TERT lead variant rs2736099 is associated with poor treatment response, especially in g-AChR-Ab(+)MG and g-EOMG (P < 0.0042). The biobank-based phenome-wide association study identify pleiotropic effects on lung cancer, hematological traits, and telomere length. Single cell transcriptomics and immunohistochemistry identified immature lymphocyte-specific TERT expression in thymoma specimens. Full-length transcriptomics reveal allele-specific decreasing effect of rs2736099-A on TERT expression. Our study unveils genetics of MG distinctly across disease subtypes, and involvement of TERT in its pathogenesis.

Introduction

Myasthenia gravis (MG) is an autoimmune disease in which neuromuscular transmission is impaired, resulting in a variety of motor symptoms1,2. Long-term remission in patients with MG is rare3, and important issues include decreased quality of life and social disadvantages4,5. MG is classified as the disease subtype according to the subgroup classification based on the location of symptoms, presence of antibodies, complications of thymoma, or age of onset6. Complications, clinical manifestations, treatment strategies, and prognosis vary widely depending on disease subtype1,2,7,8. For example, thymoma-associated MG is more likely to cause a crisis and is often more severe than MG without thymoma7. It is important to elucidate the genetic background of MG by disease subtype to provide clinical insights, such as treatment and prognosis prediction.

The concordance rate of MG development has been reported to be 35% in monozygotic twins, and both genetic and environmental factors are thought to be involved in the development of MG9. Although genetic factors, especially in HLA, have been vigorously investigated in both the adult-onset and child-onset MG10–12, the genetic background of MG is still elusive. Even after emerging the genome-wide association studies (GWASs), there are limited previous reports on the genetic background of MG. In addition, current studies are all from European populations, with no previous ones from Asians. In GWAS of European MGs13,14, non-Hispanic Caucasian AChR-antibody-positive MGs15, US and Italian AChR-antibody-positive MGs16, and meta-analysis of European populations17, several disease susceptibility loci were reported, with consistent significant associations shown for HLA variants in the MHC region18,19. But these previous reports are limited to the specific disease subtype of MG, or are without subtype classification, so the entire genetic background of MG has only been elusive.

Here, to elucidate the genetic background of MG across disease subtypes and also in non-Europeans, we form the cohort of 1434 MG cases from the Japanese population, consisting of all disease subtypes20 and performed GWAS analysis. We evaluate the pleiotropic association of TERT, the locus identified in GWAS, with MG treatment response, biobank-based phenome, and telomere length. We then analyze cell type-specific TERT expression by using single-cell transcriptome data of thymomas and immunostaining of TERT using histopathological specimens of thymomas. We further performed full-length RNA sequencing of thymoma specimens and evaluated the allele-specific expression (ASE) effect of the lead SNP on TERT expression.

Results

GWAS of MG in the Japanese cohort

After sample quality control (QC) and exclusion of samples with sex chromosome karyotype aberrations and missing age and sex information21–23, we obtained 1434 cases registered in the 4th survey of the Japan MG Registry (JAMG-R)20 from 15 domestic centers. The sample size and proportion for each disease subtype are shown in Fig. 1a, b. The JAMG-R study group, comprising professional neurologists specializing in MG, has collected detailed and reliable clinical data from numerous consecutive MG patients over more than 10 years, involving 20 participating institutions20. We enrolled 42,913 controls from the Biobank Japan Project (BBJ), a nationwide genome cohort of Japan22–24. Principal component (PC) analysis performed with QC-passed samples showed East Asian origin of the participants (Supplementary Fig. 1). 9,034,566 variants with minor allele frequency (MAF) ≥0.005, which passed post-imputation QC, were tested, including both autosomal and X chromosomes. No inflation of test statistics were observed with the genomic control factor (λGC) = 1.005 and (λGC1000) = 1.002 in the quantile-quantile plots based on 6,889,985 SNPs with MAF ≥ 0.02. (Supplementary Fig. 2). We estimated SNP-based heritability using Linkage Disequilibrium Score Regression (LDSC)25 and Linkage Disequilibrium Adjusted Kinships (LDAK) SumHer26. LDSC showed an observed-scale SNP heritability of 0.020 (SE = 0.011), with an intercept of 0.994 (SE = 0.0064), a mean χ² of 1.012, and a λGC of 0.996, and a liability-scale SNP heritability of 0.045 (SE = 0.025), with an intercept of 0.994 (SE = 0.0064), a mean χ² of 1.012, and a λGC of 0.996, respectively. LDAK showed an observed-scale SNP heritability of 0.016 (SE = 0.0080). Next, we performed the GWAS stratified by disease subtype classification. MG cases were classified into the disease subtypes according to the classification proposed in the Japanese MG/Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome guidelines 20226 (Fig. 1a, b) as the O/g-ELT-MuN classification29; according to the location of symptoms (confined to the ocular muscles only (OMG) or generalized(gMG)), presence of autoantibody (anti-acetylcholine receptor (AChR) antibody or anti-muscle-specific kinase (MuSK) antibody), association with thymoma (TA), and age of onset. Early onset (EO) is defined as the age of onset below 50 years, while late onset (LO) is defined as the age of onset above 50 years6. We defined the subtypes as OMG (n = 300), gMG (n = 1134), anti-AChR-Ab positive MG (g-AChR-Ab(+)MG, n = 934), anti-AChR-Ab negative MG (g-AChR-Ab(-)MG, n = 200), thymoma-associated MG (g-TAMG, n = 335), MG not associated with thymoma (g-TA(-)MG, n = 599), early onset MG without thymoma (g-EOMG, n = 293), and late onset MG without thymoma (g-LOMG, n = 335).

In the stratified GWAS, we again identified genome-wide significant associations at the TERT region for gMG, g-AChR-Ab(+)MG, and g-TAMG (Fig. 1c, d). We also performed GWAS in the gMG without thymoma and confirmed that no significant association signals were detected. The lead variant was rs2736099 (GRCh37 5:1287340) for all these groups. OR = 1.38, 95% CI = 1.26–1.52, P = 1.6 × 10−12 for gMG, OR = 1.49, 95% CI = 1.35–1.65, P = 2.1 × 10−15 for g-AChR-Ab(+)MG, OR = 1.92, 95% CI = 1.64–2.26, P = 1.1 × 10−15 for g-TAMG, respectively (Table 1). Whereas the classifications became finer (i.e., the numbers of the case samples decreased), the P values became more significant, reflecting the stronger effect sizes. Rs2736099 was in the intronic region of TERT and near rs3215401 (Fig. 2a) and is in moderate linkage disequilibrium with the lead variant for all the MG cases (rs3215401; r² = 0.62). Conditional analysis with the lead variant was performed on all MG cases, gMG, g-AChR-Ab(+)MG, and g-TAMG, where significant associations were detected at TERT. The replication study and cross-population GWAS meta-analyses

We performed replication studies of the TERT region in other ancestral populations. We adopt the meta-analysis of European ancestry by Braun et al.17, which included 5708 MG cases and 432,028 controls. While this is the largest MG GWAS to date, detailed disease subtypes such as complications of thymoma were not evaluated. In this replication study, the lead variants at the TERT locus, rs2736099 (risk allele: rs2736099-A), demonstrated significant nominal associations with the same directional effect of the risk allele (OR = 1.04, P = 0.0034).

Among the 12 loci associated with MG in the European meta GWAS, we identified nominally significant associations with same directional effects in the CTLA4 at 2q33 (rs231779, OR = 1.17, P = 1.0 × 10−4), FAM76B at chromosome 11q21 (rs4409785, OR = 1.17, P = 0.030), and TNFRSF11A regions at 18q21 (rs7239261, OR = 1.14, P = 1.3 × 10−3) in Japanese (Supplementary Table 2). When stratified by the disease subtypes, CTLA4 and FAM76B showed relatively larger ORs with OMG, in contrast to TNFRSF11A with g-TA(-)MG and g-LOMG (Supplementary Table 2). These results highlighted the clinical importance of evaluating disease subtype-stratified risk of known MG risk variants.

Next, we performed cross-population GWAS meta-analysis by integrating the Japanese GWAS (all MG cases) with those of the Europeans (7142 MG cases and 474,941 controls in total)17, using the inverse-variance method under a fixed-effects model for the effect of each variant. We identified novel loci of EPS15L1 at 19p13 (rs10404046, OR = 1.13, 95% CI = 1.08–1.18, P = 5.9 × 10−9) and RALGAPA2 at 20p11 (rs6075661, OR = 1.13, 95% CI = 1.08–1.18, P = 3.7 × 10−8; Supplementary Fig. 4 and Table 2) with genome-wide significance. We conducted a sign test for the 9 SNPs presented in Table 2 (excluding “NA” SNPs), and the result was statistically significant with a P value of 0.002. The Japanese significant signal at the TERT locus and European significant signals at CTLA4, IKZF3, TNFRSF11A, and C2CD2 showed stronger associations. In contrast, the signal for FAM76B, previously identified in the European GWAS, was weakened. Risk variant fine-mapping within the MHC region

Our Japanese GWAS identified significant associations at the MHC region, specifically with g-AChR-Ab(+)MG, g-TA(-)MG, g-EOMG, and g-LOMG (Fig. 1c, d). To pinpoint the responsible polymorphisms conferring MG disease risk, we performed the fine-mapping analysis within the MHC region. We imputed genotypes of 4-digit alleles and amino acid polymorphisms of the class I and II HLA genes in the MHC region using DEEP*HLA30 with the Japanese population-specific reference panel and conducted the association study by using a logistic regression model that assumed additive effects of allele dosages on a log-odds scale.

Among the imputed HLA genes, the class II HLA gene of HLA-DRB1 showed the most significant associations with all MG cases, g-TA(-)MG, and g-LOMG (Fig. 3a, Supplementary Table 3). The alanine residue at the 74th amino acid position in HLA-DRβ1 had the most significant association with all MG cases (OR = 0.80, 95% CI = 0.74–0.86, P = 8.6 × 10−9). The HLA-DRB1*08:03 allele showed the most significant association with g-TA(-)MG (OR = 1.71, 95% CI = 1.45–2.03, P = 5.6 × 10−9) and g-LOMG (OR = 2.18, 95%, 95% CI = 1.75–2.71, P = 1.2 × 10−10; Fig. 3b, Table 3). On the other hand, the class I HLA gene of HLA-A had the top association with g-EOMG, with the most significant signal at the threonine residue of the 9th amino acid position of HLA-A (OR = 1.69, 95% CI = 1.39–2.05, P = 3.2 × 10−7). To investigate additional MHC associations independent from the top-associated HLA genes, we performed conditional analysis. TERT association analysis with MG treatment response

To investigate the clinical impact of the TERT lead SNP (rs2736099) in MG pathogenesis, we assessed its association with treatment response. According to the MGFA-Postintervention Status (MGFA-PIS) defined by the Myasthenia Gravis Foundation of America31, treatment response is classified into five groups: complete stable remission (CSR), pharmacological remission (PR), minimal manifestation (MM), improved, and unchanged32. In this analysis, the cases that achieved CSR, PR, or MM were defined as “good response” (n = 911). The cases to be refractory by the attending physician at the time of enrollment in the JAMG-R were defined as “poor response” (n = 317). Pleiotropic associations of the TERT variant with human complex traits

The TERT variant has been reported to be pleiotropic by an integrated database based on European GWAS34. To determine whether it shares pleiotropic associations with other human complex traits in an East Asian population, we performed a phenome-wide association study (PheWAS). We utilized BBJ genotype and phenotype data (nSample = 232,060), which consisted of 125 diseases as defined by the 10th revision of the International Classification of Diseases (ICD-10) and 75 quantitative traits consisting of physical measurements, blood tests (biochemistry, serum, blood cell count), blood pressure, echocardiography, and lifestyle questionnaires (diet, exercise, alcohol consumption, smoking)28,35. Binary traits were tested by logistic regression analysis, continuous traits by linear regression analysis.

In the disease PheWAS, the MG risk allele of rs2736099-A increased risk of lung cancer (OR = 1.17, P = 4.4 × 10−20) and uterine fibroids (OR = 1.06, P = 3.1 × 10−5), while decreased risk of interstitial lung disease (ILD, OR = 0.82, P = 2.5 × 10−10), colorectal cancer (OR = 0.93, P = 8.1 × 10−8), and Graves' disease (OR = 0.90, P = 1.8 × 10−4; P < 0.05/125 = 4.0 × 10−4; Fig. 5a, b, Supplementary Table 5). Allelic functional characterization of the TERT variant

TERT is rarely expressed in peripheral blood but is expressed in the thymus40. To evaluate the functional impact of the TERT risk variant, we examined the allelic expression and regulatory effect of rs2736099-A using the thymoma specimen obtained from the thymectomy of the g-TAMG cases. First, to investigate cell type-specific expression profile of TERT, we analyzed single-cell RNA sequence (scRNA-seq) data of thymoma and peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from patients of thymoma complicated with MG41. Cell type annotation classified the six cell types: immature lymphocytes, namely double negative T cell (DNT) and double positive T cell (DPT), stromal cell, T cell, B cell, and Myeloid cell (Supplementary Fig. 7a, b). DNT, DPT, and stromal cells were found specifically in thymomas (Fig. 6a). As expected, TERT was expressed in thymomas and rarely in PBMCs. We next performed TERT immunostaining on surgically excised thymoma specimens complicated by MG (Supplementary Fig. 7c). The specimen pathologically showed the B1 type region, which contained abundant lymphocytes, and the A type region composed mainly of spindle-shaped epithelial cells (Fig. 6c)42. Analogous to the observations in the scRNA-seq data, TERT was highly expressed in the lymphocytes within the thymoma. In medullary differentiation, mixed with epithelial tumor cells of medullary origin and a small number of lymphocytes, TERT was expressed only in lymphocytes (Fig. 6c arrowhead). TERT was not expressed in epithelial tumor cells. TERT expression was also absent in spindle-shaped epithelial tumor cells. These provided novel pathological insights into TERT spatial distribution in thymoma.

While previous studies suggested the ASE effect of rs273609943, current knowledge has been limited to those from cell lines rather than primary human tissues. We thus performed the ASE analysis using the thymoma specimen. RNA-seq-based approaches are recommended to directly quantify ASE. However, since the target variant was located close to the 5′ UTR, it was technically challenging to capture rs2736099 by conventional RNA-seq where the polyadenylate (poly(A)) tail on the 3′ end of mRNA is used as the starting point for reverse transcription (i.e., around the 5′ end nor non-coding RNA without poly(A) tail are not covered well)44,45. To overcome this situation, we applied RamDA-seq, which captures the full length of RNA or non-polyadenylated RNAs by random priming46. ASE quantification by counting the number of reads containing the variant allele confirmed that rs2736099-A downregulates TERT expression in an allele-specific manner (Fig. 6d).

Since transcription factors (TFs) have regulatory roles on gene expression47, to investigate the biological function of the TERT variant, we assessed the differences in TF binding affinity between rs2736099-A and G alleles. We utilized PWMScan48 based on the position weight matrix of 746 TFs registered in JASPAR CORE 202049. P values are calculated based on the null hypothesis that the target sequence is a random sequence. The binding affinity of several TFs, including SP-1 and its family member SP-3, decreased at rs2736099-A (Supplementary Fig. 8). Given that rs2736099 was reported to be on the binding motif of SP-143, rs2736099-A might reduce TERT expression by disrupting the affinity of the SP-1 and SP-3 target binding motifs of the GC box (Supplementary Fig. 8)49,50.

Discussion

In this study, we performed the initial GWAS of MG in the non-European population. Stratified GWAS according to the disease subtype classification successfully identified the genetic background specific to the MG subtypes. We identified two genome-wide significant loci, TERT and HLA. Cross-population GWAS meta-analysis further identified two novel loci (EPS15L1 and RALGAPA2). Of note, associations of the identified loci differed according to the MG disease subtypes. Namely, TERT showed the strongest association in g-TAMG, and the MG risk allele of TERT rs2736099-A was significantly associated with treatment resistance, most exemplified for g-EOMG. Further, the TERT variant conferred pleiotropic associations with a range of human clinical and molecular phenotypes such as malignancy, blood cell counts, and telomere length. These emphasize the value of considering disease subtypes and clinical endotypes across MG risk loci, as well as the biological involvement of TERT in MG pathogenesis. Although TERT has not been identified as a significant locus in previous GWAS of MG in European populations, its association with MG in our study may reflect population-specific genetic or environmental factors, given that TERT has shown ancestry-specific associations in cancer51.

TERT gene encodes a subunit of telomerase reverse transcriptase involved in telomere elongation52,53. While TERT gene expression is repressed with cell differentiation and telomerase activity is reduced in normal cells54, in thymomas, TERT is highly expressed in immature lymphocytes. Our study found allele-specific TERT downregulation effects of the MG risk allele of rs2736099-A in thymoma, which might be biologically explained by disruption of TF binding sites such as SP-1 and SP-3. Of note, our data showed that treatment response in EOMG is associated with rs2736099-A in the TERT, indicating that the risk allele might underlie the pathological mechanism of MG. Directional impacts of the TERT mutations on telomere length are still controversial. Even for carcinogenesis, where TERT involvement has been biologically confirmed, there exists no consensus on whether longer or shorter telomeres are associated with carcinogenesis55. Our findings in MG and thymoma should provide novel insights.

HLA showed the strongest impact on AChR-antibody-positive MG without thymoma, characterized as the associations of HLA-DRB1 with g-LOMG and HLA-A with g-EOMG. Genetic risk of class I and II HLA genes on MG has been reported in the previous studies of Europeans9,13–17. Regarding EOMG, the HLA haplotype 8.1 was reported to have an association19, and HLA-B*08:01 was identified as a risk allele by GWAS17. HLA-A is also in the class I region, but is distinct from Europeans. As for the class II region, HLA-DQA1 (rs601006)15 and HLA-DRB1*03:0116 have been reported as risk alleles. Rs601006 has a nominal association in this study (P = 4.3 × 10−3), whereas the HLA-DRB*03:01 allele is not found in Japanese56. Regarding LOMG, we identified HLA-DRB1*08:03 as a risk allele, which is relatively common in Asian and rarely found in Caucasian56, whereas previous studies in Europeans reported HLA-DRB1*07:01 and HLA-DQA1 as risk alelles15,16. These differences are most likely due to a difference in genetic background among populations, but our study provided MG disease subtype-specific HLA genetic in the largest sample of Japanese or East Asian MG patients to date. On the other hand, we found no significant HLA association with thymoma-associated MG, AChR-antibody-negative MG, or OMG. This suggests that the biological role of HLA genes in the autoimmune mechanisms of MG differs by disease type.

In cross-population GWAS meta-analysis, we identified novel loci of EPS15L1 and RALGAPA2. EPS15L1 was highlighted in a recent transcriptome-wide association study as significantly associated with MG susceptibility17, supporting its potential involvement in disease pathogenesis. RALGAPA2 is a RALGAP family gene that inactivates RalA and RalB, involved in intracellular signaling57. Although their role in autoimmunity is unclear, altered Ral signaling may affect immune regulation and warrants further study in MG.

In conclusion, our Japanese GWAS of MG identified genetic risks specific to the disease subtypes. TERT is genetically, clinically, and biologically involved in MG, where the risk allele downregulates the TERT expression in thymoma. Further investigations in other East Asian cohorts and stratified analyses by clinical subtypes will be important to validate and extend our findings. Class I and II HLA genes conferred risk of the AChR-antibody-positive non-thymomatous MG. Identification of such disease subtype-specific genetic background may lead to the development of MG treatment strategies and prognostic prediction.

Methods

Ethics

This study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of The University of Osaka (approved number: 734-17 and 18528-15).

Study cohorts and subjects of the Japanese population

The MG case group included 1493 patients enrolled in the 4th survey of the JAMG-R20 from 15 centers in Japan, which was established after review and approval of the ethics committees of the Osaka University (the approval #915) and the Osaka University Hospital (the approval #21009). We obtained written informed consent from all study participants. The control group included 46,627 patients enrolled in the BBJ22–24. BBJ was established in 2003 and stores DNA samples and clinical information from 270,000 people with the listed diseases and serum samples from 200,000 people from medical institutions in Japan. 1436 cases and 43,702 controls who passed QC, which passed sample QC. After QC, samples with sex chromosome karyotype abnormalities and samples with missing age and sex information were excluded. Samples with sex chromosome karyotype abnormalities and samples with missing age and sex information were excluded, resulting in 1434 cases and 42,917 controls. PC analysis was conducted using QC-passed samples after excluding the MHC region (Chromosome 6: 20–40 Mb) to reduce population stratification effects. The long-range haplotype structure in the Japanese population has been reported to span up to 24–35 Mb58. The analysis showed that there were 4 samples in the control group with outliers in the third PC, which were excluded (Supplementary Fig. 1).

Finally, GWAS analysis was performed on 1434 cases and 42,913 controls. MG was subtyped according to the O/g-ELTMuN classification1,2,7. The disease subtypes according to the O/g-ELTMuN classification are shown in the gray rectangles in Fig. 1a. MuSK-Ab positive group (g-MuSKMG) and seronegative group (g-SNMG) were combined to g-AChR-Ab(-)MG in the analysis due to the small number of cases. As for the control dataset, among the target diseases of BBJ, cases of rheumatoid arthritis, atopic dermatitis, Graves' disease, liver cirrhosis, chronic hepatitis B, chronic hepatitis C, nephrotic syndrome, bronchial asthma, cervical cancer, and hepatocellular carcinoma were excluded as the MHC-related diseases. In addition, cases of autoimmune diseases (listed in Supplementary Table 7), MG, and thymoma were further excluded by referring to medical history information extracted from electronic medical records. BBJ is a hospital-based biobank that targets specific diseases. The medical history information extracted from electronic medical records was all based on diagnoses made by professional physicians. Therefore, ICD-9 or ICD-10 were not used in the selection of the control cohort59.

Genotyping, quality control (QC), and imputation using the Japanese reference panel

All samples were genotyped using the Illumina Infinium Asian Screening Array-24 v1.0 BeadChip (ASA-Chip, Illumina, CA, USA). Details of the QC were described elsewhere21. Briefly, sample QC excluded the samples with call rates <0.98 or of non-East Asian ancestry. Variant QC was conducted to exclude variants with minor allele counts <5, variants with large frequency differences between males and females, variants with p values <10−6 for Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, variants with large frequency differences between genotyping batches, and variants with allele frequency differences >0.05 from the reference panel for imputation were excluded. Haplotype phasing of the genotyped data was performed by using SHAPEIT460. Genotype dosages were imputed by using Minimac461. In-house WGS data of Japanese (n = 11,754) were used for the reference panel21,59,62. Variant QC and imputation were performed after merging the case and control samples. For imputation of 2-digit and 4-digit HLA alleles, HLA amino acid polymorphisms, and single-nucleotide variants in the MHC region, we used DEEP*HLA, a deep learning-based imputation method30, with the Japanese population-specific reference panel58. Variants with MAF <0.005 or imputation quality Rsq<0.5 were excluded from the post-imputation variants.

GWAS of MG in Japanese

We performed GWAS of imputed dosages of each variant by a single-variant association test of the SAIGE software version 1.2.027. SAIGE is based on a generalized mixed model, which has been improved to reduce type 1 error and calculate accurate P values even in the case of unbalanced case-control ratios. The top five PCs, age, and sex were included as covariates in the fitting of the null model in Step 1 of SAIGE. We set the significance threshold of the association study as a genome-wide association significance threshold (P = 5.0 × 10−8). Regional plots for genome-wide significant variants were constructed using LocusZoom.

SNP-based heritability estimation

We estimated SNP-based heritability using LDSC25. We calculated LD scores from East Asian populations of the 1000 Genomes Project63 and restricted the analysis to HapMap364 SNPs. LDSC estimates both SNP heritability and intercept, which reflects the contribution of residual confounding, such as population stratification. For this case-control study, SNP-based heritability was estimated on the observed scale and subsequently transformed to the liability scale assuming a population prevalence of 23.1 per 100,000 in Japan65 and a sample prevalence of 0.0323 (ncase = 1434, ncontrol = 42,913).

We also estimated SNP-based heritability using LDAK 6 (SumHer)26 with pre-computed BLD-LDAK taggings for East Asian populations (HapMap3 SNPs). LDAK uses a weighted linear mixed model to account for linkage disequilibrium and MAF, providing a robust estimate of SNP heritability even in modest sample sizes.

Association analysis of the HLA variants with MG risk

The association between HLA variants with risk of MG was evaluated by using a logistic regression model that assumed additive effects of allele dosages on a log-odds scale. We defined the HLA variants as biallelic SNPs in the MHC region, 2- and 4- digit biallelic classical HLA alleles, biallelic HLA amino acid polymorphisms for respective residues, and multiallelic HLA amino acid polymorphisms for each amino acid site. An omnibus P value was obtained for each HLA amino acid position by the log-likelihood ratio test. We set the significance threshold of the association study as a genome-wide association significance threshold (P = 5.0 × 10−8). When conditioning on HLA genes, we included all the 4-digit alleles as covariates, and when conditioning on HLA amino acid positions, we included multiallelic variants of the amino acid residues21,59.

Cross-population MG GWAS meta-analysis

Meta-analysis of the MG GWAS results of the Japanese population with the summary statistics of European GWAS meta-analysis was performed by an inverse-variance method assuming a fixed-effects model on the effect estimates using METAL (v2020-05-05), weighted by the inverse of the variance of each variant.

TERT association analysis with treatment response

The MG case group was divided into three groups according to treatment response: good, poor, and intermediate treatment responsive group. We compared the frequency of risk alleles of lead SNPs in GWAS between good response and poor response. We also analyzed the association with treatment response by using SAIGE27. Covariates were set to the same as in GWAS. We set P < 0.05 as the nominal significance threshold for the association analysis. The refractory group was defined as cases judged by the primary doctor to be refractory at the time of enrollment in the JAMG-R20, and the good response to treatment group was defined as cases with a history of reaching CSR, PR, or MM in the MGFA-PIS31,32.

PheWAS of the TERT variant

We performed the PheWAS to evaluate the association between rs2736099 and human complex traits. We used the BBJ clinical information, which consisted of 125 diseases as defined by ICD-10 and 75 quantitative traits consisting of physical measurements, blood tests (biochemistry, serum), blood pressure, echocardiography, and lifestyle questionnaires (diet, exercise, alcohol consumption, smoking). Binary traits were tested by logistic regression analysis, continuous traits by linear regression analysis. Age, Sex, Age*Sex, Age2, Age2*Sex, and PCs (from PC1 to PC10) were included as covariates. In Sex specific disease, covariates including Sex were excluded. Significance levels were set at P < 4.0 × 10−4 (=0.05/125) for the disease and P < 6.7 × 10−4 (=0.05/75) for the quantitative traits by Bonferroni correction considering the number of the tested phenotypes.

For association analysis with telomere length, we estimated telomere length using Telseq38 for blood-derived DNA WGS samples in the TMM cohort using Illumina Novaseq 6000 (Illumina, CA, USA). We regressed out the effect of age, sequencing depth, and sequencing protocol on telomere length using linear regression, and then performed a rank-based inverse normal transformation on the residuals. We performed a single variant test for the adjusted telomere length using the STAAR pipeline66. Sex, PCs (from PC1 to PC10), and subcohort batch were included as covariates.

Colocalization analysis of the TERT locus

We conducted the genetic colocalization by calculating the PP of sharing a causal variant associated with MG in a 500 kbp region around rs2736099 using the approximate Bayes factor method in the Coloc R package (version 5.2.2)39. We investigated colocalization between g-TAMG and other traits identified by the PheWAS, by the probability of sharing the causal variant (H4).

Single-cell analysis of thymoma and PBMC

Gene expression analysis of TERT was performed using the fastq files from the single cell analysis in MG complicated thymoma41. Data analysis was performed by using Seurat 4.267. QC was performed as metrics of expressed genes >200 and mitochondrial gene percentage <20%, and doublets were removed by Doublet Finder68. The data were integrated by Harmony69 with the sample ID as a batch. Marker genes were CDK6 for double negative T cells and CD18 for double positive T cells, following the reference, respectively41.

Immunostaining of thymoma specimens

Immunostaining was performed on formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded sections of a thymoma specimen surgically resected at The University of Osaka Hospital. The specimen has been entirely consumed, and no further tissue remains. Hematoxylin and eosin staining was also performed. For immunostaining, we used the anti-TERT monoclonal antibody A-6 (sc-393013; Santa Cruz Biotechnology, CA, USA). The primary antibody was detected using an enzyme-labeled antibody method, followed by color development with diaminobenzidine (DAB). For immunofluorescence staining, Alexa Fluor 594-conjugated donkey anti-mouse IgG was used as a secondary antibody, and DAPI Fluoromount-G was used for nuclear counterstaining. Images were acquired using a VS-BX microscope system (Olympus) equipped with a UPLSAPO 20×/0.75 objective lens (Olympus) and a VC-50 camera (Olympus Soft Imaging Solutions), and processed using VS-ASW 2.9 software (Olympus).

ASE analysis using RamDA-seq

The thymoma specimen, which was surgically removed at the University of Osaka Hospital, was immersed in RNAlater and stored in liquid nitrogen. The tissue sample was entirely consumed during the RNA extraction and library preparation processes. Written informed consent was obtained from the donor for the use of the tissue sample. RNA was extracted from the specimen, and the complementary DNA reads were generated using RamDA-seq. The detailed protocol for RamDA-seq is described in the reference46. Libraries were prepared using the Illumina® Nextera XT DNA Library Preparation Kit, and sequencing was then performed with Novaseq X (Illumina, CA, USA). Read QC was performed from Fastq data by Fastq-mcf, with QC parameters as -l 36, --lowcomplec-pct 74, --homopolymer-pct 74, and -k 4, respectively. Mapping was performed using HISAT2 with the parameters --dta-cufflinks -p 4 -k 5 -X 800 --sp 1000,1000. Conversion to BAM format was performed using samtools. The number of reads containing the A-allele and G-allele of rs2736099 were counted using bcftools mpileup, respectively. P value was calculated by a two-tailed binomial test70.

TF motif binding analysis

PWMScan version 1.1.948 was used to assess differences in TF binding affinity between the reference and alternative alleles of rs2736099. PWMScan calculates scores of the binding affinity between the target sequence and the TF. P values are calculated based on the null hypothesis that the target sequence is a random sequence. The sequence containing 15 bases before and after rs2736099 was selected as targets, and libraries of vertebrates from JASPER 202049 were used as the TF position weight matrix library68.

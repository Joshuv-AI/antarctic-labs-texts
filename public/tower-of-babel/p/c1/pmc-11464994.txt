Efficacy and safety of perioperative immunotherapy combinations for resectable non-small cell lung cancer: a systematic review and network meta-analysis
Authors: Yuelin Han, Xiangtian Xiao, Tingting Qin, Shuxi Yao, Xinyue Liu, Yanqi Feng, Zhou Li, Yiming Li, Shu Xia
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
IntroductionSeveral trials of perioperative immunotherapy for resectable non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) reported positive results. They were designed to adjuvant, neoadjuvant and sandwich (neoadjuvant plus adjuvant) immunotherapy with immune checkpoint inhibitors and chemotherapy (CT). The differences between neoadjuvant and sandwich modalities were unclear.MethodWe performed a systematic review and Bayesian network meta-analysis by retrieving relevant literature from PubMed, EMBASE, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, ClinicalTrials.gov, WHO ICTRP and major international conferences.ResultsWe analyzed 8 studies involving 3429 patients, including 6 neoadjuvant plus adjuvant (Neo-Adj) and 2 neoadjuvant (Neo) trials. Neo-Adj had better event-free survival (EFS) (hazard ratio [HR] = 0.57, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.45–0.71) than CT. There existed no difference between Neo-Adj and Neo in EFS (HR = 0.87, 95% CI: 0.53–1.46) and overall survival (OS) (HR = 1.04, 95% CI: 0.38–2.57). Neo might have lower incidence of treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) (relative risk [RR] = 0.96, 95% CI: 0.87–1.12) than Neo-Adj. Subgroup analysis of PD-L1 ≥ 50% suggested that EFS of Neo-Adj (HR = 0.46, 95% CI: 0.27–0.76) and Neo (HR = 0.24, 95% CI: 0.06–0.89) was better than CT, and Neo-Adj potentially caused shorter EFS than Neo (HR = 1.92, 95% CI: 0.46–7.84).ConclusionsOur results suggest that Neo-Adj and Neo have similar EFS for patients with PD-L1 < 1% or 1–49%. However, patients with PD-L1 ≥ 50% may obtain more EFS benefit from Neo than Neo-Adj. Neo might present a more favorable assessment than Neo-Adj when evaluating OS. Moreover, adding adjuvant immunotherapy may increase toxicity.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00262-024-03844-w.

Introduction

Lung cancer continues to be the predominant cause of cancer-related mortality, with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) representing approximately 85% of the total cases [1]. Adjuvant, neoadjuvant and neoadjuvant plus adjuvant immunotherapy (sandwich) immunotherapy ushered resectable NSCLC into the era of perioperative immunization. Despite the differing designs of three regimens, overall benefits have been found to surpass those of perioperative chemotherapy, as indicated by several clinical trials.

Ten randomized controlled trials (RCTs) reported positive results. Differences between neoadjuvant, adjuvant and sandwich immunotherapy remain unclear. Meanwhile, the optimal regimen for perioperative immunotherapy has yet to be determined. So, we utilized network meta-analysis (NMA) to perform indirect comparisons between neoadjuvant and sandwich.

Methods

This NMA was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis extension statement for network meta-analyses (Supplementary Table 1) [2]. The Bayesian framework allows for a logical analysis of indirect comparisons [3] and helps us make probabilistic statements and predictions on the treatment effects [4]. The protocol was registered in the Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO CRD42023494613). Details of methods section were presented in Supplementary Table 2 [5]. The search strategy is placed in Supplementary Table 3. The quality of individual studies was assessed by the Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool (2.0) for RCTs [6]. Details in statistical analysis were presented in Supplementary Table 2 [7–9]. Pairwise meta-analyses were performed to compare with the corresponding pooled results from the Bayesian framework (Supplementary Table 5). We assessed statistical heterogeneity and inconsistency by the Q test and the statistic inconsistency index (I2) (Supplementary Table 6) [8]. The Egger regression test was used to evaluate the publication bias.

Results

Systematic review and characteristics of the included studies

We identified 5014 records from databases and 676 records from conference proceedings during the preliminary literature search. Twenty-four records of 8 clinical trials finally met our eligibility criteria (Supplementary 2 Fig. 1). Four minutes of meeting [10–13] and one article [14] were included after the completion of bibliographic retrieval for more information. The trials named as AEGEAN [15–18], CheckMate 77T [11], CheckMate 816 [19–29], KEYNOTE-671 [12, 30, 31], NADIM II [32–34], Neotorch [14, 35, 36], RATIONALE-315 [10, 13] and TD-FOREKNOW [37, 38].

However, two adjuvant immunotherapy trials, IMpower 010 [39, 40] and KEYNOTE-091 [41], exclusively enrolled patients who underwent R0 resection, with disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS) as the outcome measures. It was visually apparent from survival curves that patients in these two trials exhibited a higher long-term survival rate compared to studies incorporating neoadjuvant therapy. This difference not only encompassed variations among three types of perioperative immunotherapies but also incorporated dissimilarities arising from distinct patient cohorts.

A total of 3429 patients were totally enrolled to receive the following three treatments: sandwich immunotherapy with preoperative chemotherapy (Neo-Adj), neoadjuvant immunotherapy plus chemotherapy (Neo) and preoperative chemotherapy with placebo or observation (CT). Neo-Adj was conducted in AEGEAN, CheckMate 77T, KEYNOTE-671, NADIM II, Neotorch and RATIONALE-315. Neo was adopted in CheckMate 816 and TD-FOREKNOW. Details on the trials are presented in Supplementary Table 4 and Supplementary Table 7. The network plots are depicted in Supplementary 2 Fig. 2. Only populations with abnormal epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) or anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK) from KEYNOTE-671 were included because outcomes of mutated EGFR populations in AEGEAN were provided separately. Risk of bias was presented in Supplementary Fig. 1. The Egger regression test presents a p-value of 0.287 larger than 0.1 suggesting the absence of publication bias (Supplementary Fig. 2).

Regarding OS (Fig. 1A), the potential OS benefits versus CT were provided by Neo-Adj (HR = 0.64, 95% CI: 0.40–0.93) and Neo (HR = 0.61, 95% CI: 0.27–1.46). The OS of Neo-Adj in the entire population was similar to Neo (HR = 1.04, 95% CI: 0.38–2.57).

Fortunately, the available data enabled us to compare rates of EFS and OS at the first and second year (Supplementary 2 Fig. 2G, H; Fig. 1B, C). Compared with CT, Neo-Adj (RR = 1.22, 95% CI: 1.10–1.36) and Neo (RR = 1.22, 95% CI: 1.01–1.50) had significantly better 1-year rates of EFS. Neo-Adj also obtained benefit in the 2-year rate of EFS than CT (RR = 1.37, 95% CI: 1.08–1.74). No significant differences were found between Neo-Adj and Neo about rates of EFS and OS at the given years.

Comparisons of pCR, MPR and ORR

Indeed, the evaluation of pathologic complete response (pCR), major pathologic response (MPR) and objective response rate (ORR) only pertains to the efficacy of neoadjuvant therapy and surgery in trials. Therefore, results derived from surgical pathology are insufficient for determining differences in overall treatment regimens.

Regarding pCR (Fig. 1D), in contrast of CT, patients achieved excellent benefit from Neo-Adj (RR = 6.22, 95% CI: 3.59–12.60) and Neo (RR = 7.08, 95% CI: 2.25–23.46). No statistically significant difference was observed between Neo-Adj and Neo (RR = 0.88, 95% CI: 0.24–3.51). The results of MPR (Fig. 1D) and ORR (Supplementary Table 4) were similar to those of pCR.

Safety and toxicity

Safety and toxicity were determined according to adverse events (AEs) of any-grade possibly related to treatments (TRAEs), grade greater than or equal to 3 TRAEs (TRAEs ≥ 3) and serious AEs (SAEs). Immune-mediated AEs (IMAEs) and treatment-related deaths were also considered as a part of security evaluation (Supplementary 2 Fig. 2B). TRAEs mean AEs related to treatments including ICIs, CT and placebo but excluding operation.

On the whole, Neo-Adj was more likely to cause TRAEs than CT (RR = 1.01, 95% CI: 0.99–1.05), and Neo appeared to be safer than Neo-Adj (RR = 0.96, 95% CI: 0.87–1.04) and CT (RR = 0.97, 95% CI: 0.89–1.05). Besides Neo-Adj (RR = 1.13, 95% CI: 0.99–1.34), Neo might also result in a higher incidence of TRAEs ≥ 3 than CT (RR = 1.04, 95% CI: 0.76–1.61). For SAEs, Neo-Adj had a statistically higher incidence compared to CT (RR = 1.34, 95% CI: 1.11–1.69), and Neo also presented the same trend (RR = 1.25, 95% CI: 0.68–2.34). Rankings

The ranking analysis was conducted based on the Bayesian ranking profiles (Supplementary Table 9). The results of the ranking were in agreement with both the direct and indirect pooled outcomes (Supplementary 2 Fig. 5). A lower ranking may mean a lower level of safety and improved effectiveness.

Neo-Adj was most likely to rank first for EFS (cumulative probability of 73%), and the first-year rate of OS (78%). Neo might rank first for OS (54%), pCR (59%) and MPR (76%). In the evaluation of survival duration, Neo ranked first more often than Neo-Adj, especially the median overall survival (54% vs. 46%), the first-year rate of EFS (51% vs. 49%) and the second-year rates of EFS (52% vs. 48%) and OS (74% vs. 26%) (Fig. 1). In terms of safety, Neo-Adj displayed the highest probabilities of ranking first in causing any-grade TRAEs (75%), TRAEs ≥ 3 (67%) and SAEs (59%), respectively. In case of CT, the treatment ranked last for EFS (97%), pCR and MPR (larger than 99%), second for TRAEs (71%), and last for grade greater than or equal to 3 TRAEs (58%) and SAE (78%).

Subgroup analysis

On the basis of PD-L1 expression level

While considering the clinical outcomes of patients with resectable NSCLC with different PD-L1 expression levels, only EFS and pCR could be estimated in the subgroup analysis. The patients were divided into the following four subgroups according to the levels of PD-L1 expression: less than 1% (PD-L1 < 1%), greater than or equal to 1% (PD-L1 ≥ 1%), 1–49% (PD-L1 1–49%) and greater than or equal to 50% (PD-L1 ≥ 50%) (Supplementary Fig. 3).

In patients with PD-L1 < 1% (Supplementary 2 Fig. 2C), Neo-Adj (HR = 0.73, 95% CI: 0.57–0.94) and Neo (HR = 0.85, 95% CI: 0.49–1.50) were superior to chemotherapy alone. Neo-Adj did not appear to exhibit significant differences from the Neo when PD-L1 < 1% (HR = 0.86, 95% CI: 0.47–1.57) (Supplementary 2 Fig. 6).

For patients with PD-L1 ≥ 1% (Supplementary 2 Fig. 2D), Neo-Adj (HR = 0.45, 95% CI: 0.29–0.68) and Neo (HR = 0.41, 95% CI: 0.17–1.01) were more beneficial than standard chemotherapy. Neo-Adj was also similar to Neo (HR = 1.09, 95% CI: 0.40–2.84) (Supplementary 2 Fig. 6).

When PD-L1 1–49% (Supplementary 2 Fig. 2C), Neo-Adj (HR = 0.52, 95% CI: 0.31–0.82) and Neo (HR = 0.58, 95% CI: 0.18–1.91) were associated with significantly prolonged EFS when compared with chemotherapy (Supplementary 2 Fig. 6). Neo-Adj also bore resemblance to Neo (HR = 0.89, 95% CI: 0.24–3.05).

When PD-L1 ≥ 50% (Supplementary 2 Fig. 2C), Neo-Adj (HR = 0.46, 95% CI: 0.27–0.76) and Neo (HR = 0.24, 95% CI: 0.06–0.89) were most likely to be better treatments than CT in providing EFS benefit. Neo-Adj might not prolonged EFS when compared with Neo (HR = 1.92, 95% CI: 0.46–7.84) (Supplementary 2 Fig. 6).

The subgroup analysis of pCR was also provided in Supplementary Table 5. Neo-Adj and Neo given meaningful assistance to pCR compared with CT. Neoadjuvant immunotherapy part could really improve the patient’s rate of pCR regardless of PD-L1 expression level.

On the basis of racial disparities

RATIONALE 315, Neotorch and TD-FOREKNOW focus on Chinese subjects. AEGEAN, CheckMate-816 and CheckMate-77T reported survival data for Asian populations, with CheckMate-816 providing additional insights into Chinese patients. KEYNOTE-671 presented specific findings of populations in East Asia. Consequently, a subgroup analysis was conducted on the Asian population to further investigate the survival rates of Chinese patients.

From the perspective of EFS, the findings demonstrated that both Neo-Adj (HR = 0.53, 95% CI: 0.40–0.69) and Neo (HR = 0.46, 95% CI: 0.28–0.79) conferred advantages for Asian patients in comparison to conventional chemotherapy. The strength of Neo-Adj might be slightly inferior to that of Neo (HR = 1.14, 95% CI: 0.63–2.03). The rigorous subgroup analysis conducted on the Chinese population demonstrated that Neo-Adj exhibited a superior advantage over CT (HR = 0.48, 95% CI: 0.25–0.90), but the merits of Neo were subject to greater scrutiny (HR = 0.49, 95% CI: 0.22–1.08). The disparity between Neo-Adj and Neo was relatively minimal (HR = 0.98, 95% CI: 0.35–2.61) (Supplementary Fig. 4D).

On the basis of histologic type

The optimal combination of immunotherapy might vary between patients with squamous and nonsquamous cell carcinoma. Neo-Adj and Neo might provide the better EFS benefit for patients with squamous and nonsquamous cell carcinoma (Supplementary Fig. 4F). According to the EFS results, the sandwich mode was found to be effective specifically for squamous cell carcinoma. However, for nonsquamous cell carcinoma, both models received positive feedback and Neo showed a tendency to provide greater benefits for patients compared to Neo-Adj, which might be contrary to its effect on squamous cell carcinoma.

On the basis of the stages of NSCLC

The ultimate outcomes might have been influenced by the stage of the patients, thus necessitating comparisons of efficacy across different stages of NSCLC (Supplementary Fig. 4G). No matter the stage was IB-II or IIIA, Neo-Adj had EFS advantages over CT. To stage IB-II NSCLC, Neo-Adj seemed a little better than Neo. The efficacy of Neo-Adj and Neo was comparable in patients diagnosed with stage III NSCLC.

On the basis of other baseline items

The utilization of open data allowed us to perform subgroup analysis on the effectiveness of various treatments based on factors such as age, sex, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) Performance Status and smoking habits (Supplementary Fig. 4). Regardless of whether the patient was older than 65 years, male or female and had an ECOG of 0 or 1, Neo-Adj significantly extended the patient's event-free survival. For individuals with no history of smoking and current or former smokers, neither sandwich mode nor preoperative immunotherapy demonstrated a definitive effect. Among current or former smokers, individuals over the age of 65 and male gender may derive greater benefit from Neo-Adj, while never smokers and females might derive more benefit from Neo, potentially due to their histologic types.

Heterogeneity, inconsistency, and transitivity assessment

The convergence of the calculated model was assessed using the history feature, and the Brooks–Gelman–Rubin diagnostic indicated the stability and reputability of the inferential iterations for each Markov Chain Monte Carlo chain. (Supplementary Figs. 5 and 6). The outcomes of the pairwise meta-analysis utilizing the frequentist approach were congruent with the pooled results derived from the Bayesian framework. (Supplementary Table 5). The discovery suggested a favorable level of transitivity and consistency across the trials included, enabling both direct and indirect comparisons to be made. The assessment of heterogeneity and inconsistency using the Q test and the I2 statistic indicated minimal (I2 = 0%) or low heterogeneity (I2 < 25%) across the trials included in the analysis. (Supplementary Table 6). Additionally, the NMA was not amenable to meta-regression due to the limited number of trials included. Consequently, we refrained from conducting the meta-regression analysis in order to assess similarity and transitivity in the clinical characteristics across all studies encompassed in this review.

Discussion

Principal findings

This systematic review represents the first application of network meta-analysis (NMA) for indirect comparison between sandwich and neoadjuvant immunotherapy in resectable NSCLC. Our findings offer the following clinical evidence. Firstly, incorporation of preoperative immunotherapy has shown improved surgical pathological responses and prolonged survival compared to traditional neoadjuvant chemotherapy. Secondly, there is no statistically significant difference in efficacy between sandwich and neoadjuvant immunotherapy; however, sandwich immunotherapy may not be more advantageous than neoadjuvant immunotherapy. Thirdly, regardless of PD-L1 expression levels, patients tend to benefit from both types of therapy. In cases with relatively low PD-L1 expression, sandwich immunotherapy may provide slightly greater benefits than neoadjuvant immunotherapy; conversely, as PD-L1 expression increases, neoadjuvant immunotherapy may yield superior therapeutic efficacy. Fourthly, the Asian community stands to gain substantial advantages from utilizing both neoadjuvant plus adjuvant and neoadjuvant immunotherapies. Lastly, the use of immune checkpoint inhibitors in perioperative patients resulted in increased toxicity levels compared to standard chemotherapy.

Given the extensive number of randomized controlled trials following the sandwich pattern and the comprehensive availability of subgroup data, it is relatively facile to attain statistically significant positive results in both overall and subgroup analysis of EFS. However, with only two RCTs available and complete subgroup data provided solely by CheckMate 816, Neo is demonstrating a promising trend at best. Additional large-scale clinical trials may also be necessary in order to achieve convergence of confidence intervals. What is particularly noteworthy is that upon separate analysis of the Asian and Chinese subgroups, both Neo-Adj and Neo demonstrated positive outcomes, indicating an expanded range of treatment options for resectable NSCLC among the Chinese population. Unfortunately, meaningful results from the subgroup analysis of smokers were not obtained due to variations in the classification of smokers across experiments when reporting EFS outcomes, particularly in the differentiation between current and former smoking status. Due to the limited availability of public data, we were unable to conduct a more in-depth subgroup analysis based on specific elements in the TNM. Only CheckMate 816 and KEYNOTE-671 have provided three years of EFS and OS data, and long-term survival data from other clinical trials are not yet mature, which imposes limitations on our analysis.

This paper was unprecedented in the field of network meta-analysis of perioperative immunotherapy for NSCLC. Unlike previous studies that failed to include CheckMate 77T and RATIONALE 315, our research possesses greater persuasiveness. The absence of a comprehensive NMA despite recent publication of results from various large-scale clinical trials necessitated our study. By incorporating the Bayesian model instead of solely relying on indirect comparison results, this study provides a robust level of evidence, ensures enhanced reliability and increased the visualization of ranking probabilities between interventions [42]. Specifically, based on further solidifying the perspective of perioperative immunotherapy for the general population, we have provided separate elaboration on the subgroups with varying PD-L1 expression. In contrast to a previous NMA that compared individual treatment regimens, this paper specifically focuses on distinguishing between Neo-Adj and Neo while temporarily setting aside variations among different treatment regimens within the intervention group [43]. This approach enables clinicians to gain an intuitive understanding of discrepancies in safety and effectiveness between these two approaches.

Implications

After synthesizing evidence from randomized controlled trials (RCTs), this review provides clinicians with a comprehensive reference source to assess the strengths and limitations of treatment options between Neo-Adj and Neo in patients with resectable NSCLC, facilitating intuitive comprehension through numerous simple indirect comparisons. In terms of efficacy, Neo-Adj demonstrates additional benefits for patients across all levels of PD-L1 expression, while Neo exhibits a clear effect only in those with PD-L1 ≥ 50%. However, considering safety aspects, an increased use of immune checkpoint inhibitors is associated with more incidences of adverse reactions, particularly serious and immune-related events. For patients with PD-L1 ≥ 50% and a part of patients with PD-L1 ≥ 1%, Neo may effectively balance their need for both safety and efficacy. This finding is significant as Neo has the potential to extend overall survival. The individuals consume a reduced quantity of medications, experience a decreased number of adverse reactions, incur lower expenses and maintain a high standard of living. Nevertheless, when PD-L1 expression is insufficient, these individuals require more courses of adjuvant immunotherapy at the expense of bearing greater risks for adverse events to achieve prolonged survival. Therefore, for patients with PD-L1 ≥ 1%, neoadjuvant therapy is considered sufficient, and adjuvant immunotherapy is deemed unnecessary. After the completion of neoadjuvant immunotherapy and surgery, the decision to administer adjuvant immunotherapy is based on the patient’s PD-L1 expression level in combination with clinical and molecular characteristics such as pathologic complete response, molecular residual disease and circulating tumor DNA [44] which aligns more closely with the principles of precision medicine [45].

CheckMate 77T and KEYNOTE-671 reported the hazard ratios between Neo-Adj and CT when patients gain pCR/MPR or non pCR/MPR. For non pCR/MPR population, EFS benefit is clearly evident to them through Neo-Adj. The pCR/MPR population had lower HR but no significant upper limit of 95% confidence intervals probably because of the low crowd size. The efficacy of adjuvant immunotherapy for non pCR/MPR population remains uncertain, but more of the residual lesions established a tumor micro-environment conducive to the efficacy of checkpoint inhibitors, potentially contributing to their favorable response to adjuvant immunotherapy. Therefore, we recommend further adjuvant immunotherapy for patients with poor postoperative pathological results and dynamic monitoring of specific biomarker levels to determine the duration of treatment.

Unlike above, CheckMate 816 and NEOTORCH reported the hazard ratios of EFS comparing pCR/MPR population with non pCR/MPR population when they received the same interventions. Better surgical pathologic outcomes undoubtedly lead to improved event-free survival in individuals undergoing perioperative immunotherapy, which further underscores the pivotal role of neoadjuvant immunotherapy in the treatment process. The subgroup analysis based on pathological complete response (pCR) revealed that the inclusion of neoadjuvant immunotherapy significantly enhanced surgical outcomes for patients, irrespective of their PD-L1 expression status, whether it was positive or negative. Hence, the survival of patients in the neoadjuvant immunotherapy group was further bolstered by favorable surgical outcomes, thereby obviating the necessity for adjuvant immunotherapy.

These findings could complement recent guidelines that assist people in answering the question. The inclusion of additional head-to-head trials, such as Neo-Adj versus Neo and Neo versus Adj, is nonetheless necessary to further validate the findings. However, the planned trial may not be necessary, as the existing data are sufficient to demonstrate the potential benefits of neoadjuvant immunotherapy for certain patients. Instead, we should focus on identifying the characteristics of patients undergoing adjuvant immunotherapy that can optimize their quality of life and extend survival.

Upon analyzing the survival data in terms of ranking probability, we observed a consistent trend in EFS rates at the first and second year, as well as median OS and the OS rates at the second year, with Neo demonstrating the highest efficacy and CT showing the lowest. Notably, Neo-Adj exhibited the best median EFS and the OS rate at the first year, while Neo displayed the second best median EFS but the worst OS rate at the first year. Interestingly, there was an inversion in the EFS and OS rates for Neo at the first year. The authors hypothesized that this could be attributed to discontinuation of immunotherapy in the Neo group leading to reduced adverse events but also premature deaths among patients requiring an efficacious intervention but experiencing a deficit in access to continuous powerful treatment, possibly due to negative PD-L1 expression or progression in the disease stage. After exclusion of these patients, it became evident that the Neo group presented double first on EFS and OS rates at the second year, indicating potential benefits of neoadjuvant therapy alone for certain patients in terms of improved survival and quality of life. However, for those who succumbed within one year, it is plausible that continued effective intervention post-surgery could have prolonged their survival despite adverse reactions or disease progression.

In terms of adverse reaction assessment, only Neo-Adj demonstrated a significant increase in SAEs compared to chemotherapy, and the incidence of TRAEs or TRAEs ≥ 3 was also nearly significantly higher. This underscores the inferior safety profile of the sandwich mode. Additionally, there was a decreasing trend in total TRAEs occurrence with Neo versus CT. It appears that Neo-Adj is associated with more adverse reactions than Neo and CT, primarily due to postoperative medication, particularly immune-related adverse reactions which tend to escalate with the use of immune checkpoint inhibitors.

We aim to draw straightforward comparisons between the findings of this paper and its included studies, and the results from two adjuvant immunotherapy trials. CheckMate 816 demonstrated a 3-year OS rate improvement of approximately 14% in the experimental population compared to chemotherapy, while KEYNOTE-671 showed a 7.3% improvement. However, KEYNOTE-091 only provided a marginal 2% improvement (all PD-L1 expression levels) and IMpower 010 provided a modest 3.2% improvement (PD-L1 ≥ 1%). It is evident that the enhancement in 3-year OS rates after R0 resection may not be as significant with adjuvant immunotherapy as with neoadjuvant and sandwich immunotherapy, despite demonstrating effective improvements in median DFS and OS after long-term follow-up in certain populations. IMpower 010 exhibited a DFS advantage solely in those with PD-L1 ≥ 1%, stage IB/II-IIIA, and an OS advantage for those with PD-L1 ≥ 50%. On the other hand, KEYNOTE-091 displayed a notable DFS advantage for the entire population and individuals with PD-L1 ranging from 1 to 49%, but did not show any significant OS advantage for both groups of the total and PD-L1 ≥ 50% population. This contrasts significantly with the substantial benefit of sandwich therapy across various patient cohorts based on PD-L1 expression levels. Particularly when PD-L1 ≥ 50%, KEYNOTE-091 has yet to demonstrate an advantage; literature also indicates no significant superiority of adjuvant immunotherapy over chemotherapy, whereas neoadjuvant and sandwich therapy have shown definite efficacy within this population. This further suggests that preoperative immunotherapy may be a more effective option for enhancing surgical outcomes and long-term survival. Besides overall adverse reaction incidence was higher than postoperative chemotherapy group (but not exceeding 20%), particularly immune-related adverse reactions were several times higher than postoperative chemotherapy group's incidence. Therefore, even though we did not include adjuvant immunotherapy in our rigorous NMA, it is apparent that neoadjuvant immunotherapy can greatly improve overall population survival, especially among those exhibiting high PD-L1 expression. Thus, adjuvant therapy may indeed be unnecessary for individuals with PDL1 ≥ 50%.

Limitations

First, it remains uncertain whether patients with resectable non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) harboring EGFR or ALK sensitizing mutations would derive survival benefits from immunotherapy.

Second, the NMA did not include adjuvant immunotherapy. The inclusion criteria for Neo and Neo-Adj trials were established prior to surgery, allowing for the analysis of long-term survival regardless of surgical eligibility after completing neoadjuvant therapy or meeting R0 resection criteria. In actuality, 75–98.5% of patients underwent surgery and not all those who underwent surgery attained R0 resection. CheckMate 816 reported EFS specifically for patients with R0 resection. However, EFS cannot be entirely equated to DFS, and CheckMate 816 did not provide OS of patients with R0 resection. Hence, we are currently unable to establish a definitive indirect comparison adjuvant immunotherapy with other treatment regimens.

Furthermore, the integration of diverse pharmacological agents and treatment protocols, with a specific emphasis on distinguishing between Neo-Adj and Neo, resulted in the oversight of variances among individual treatment regimens and medications. Only trials involving anti-PD-L1 or anti programmed death 1 (PD-1) were included. Trials comparing double antibodies with monoclonal antibodies were therefore excluded, such as NEOSTAR [46], NeoCOAST [47] and NEOpredict-Lung [48]. No subgroup analysis was conducted based on anti-PD-L1 and anti-PD-1. The literature has demonstrated that anti-PD-1 exhibits greater efficacy when combined with chemotherapy in advanced NSCLC [49]. Among all trials, only AEGEAN employed nivolumab as a monoclonal antibody targeting PD-L1 which was not compared with other trials here.

Moreover, there is a paucity of comprehensive comparison and analysis of surgical outcomes. But this paper scrutinizes outcome indicators such as pCR, which can serve as a partial surrogate for surgical results. Furthermore, the authors propose that a pairwise meta-analysis or a NMA comparing specific treatment regimens of pCR and MPR would be more apropos [50].

In conclusion, our study demonstrates that the combination of neoadjuvant and adjuvant immunotherapy for resectable NSCLC outperforms standard chemotherapy regardless of PD-L1 expression. Neoadjuvant immunotherapy emerges as a more favorable treatment option for patients with high PD-L1 expression or favorable surgical outcomes, while sandwich immunotherapy may offer a more definitive response in cases of insufficient PD-L1 expression. Both neoadjuvant and sandwich immunotherapy hold promise in benefiting Asian populations. The mitigation of adverse reactions induced by immunotherapy is an additional concern that necessitates our attention. Therefore, clinicians should tailor immunotherapy to the individual patient’s specific circumstances in order to optimize their benefits.

Outcomes of two-stage phacoemulsification and pars plana vitrectomy for proliferative diabetic retinopathy with coexisting cataract: a comparative study
Authors: Qian Wang, Chunli Liu, Gongqiang Yuan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aims to compare the outcomes of a two-stage surgical approach in treating proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR) coexisting with cataract.MethodsThis retrospective study enrolled 97 eyes. Thirty eyes were assigned to sequential surgery: initial intravitreal pharmacotherapy administered concurrently with phacoemulsification and intraocular lens (IOL) implantation, followed by pars plana vitrectomy (PPV) one week postoperatively. The remaining 67 eyes underwent combined surgery, which entailed initial intravitreal pharmacotherapy followed one week later by simultaneous PPV, phacoemulsification and IOL implantation. The primary outcomes measured included early postoperative anterior chamber (AC) inflammation (degree of inflammatory cells and fibrinous exudate), posterior synechiae of the iris, best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), intraocular pressure (IOP), and other intraoperative and postoperative complications.ResultsPost-vitrectomy day 1, in the sequential group, postoperative findings included AC cells ≥2+ in five eyes, fibrinous exudation in two eyes, and posterior synechiae of the iris in one eye. In the combined group, 28 eyes exhibited inflammatory cells≥2+ postoperatively, nine eyes presented with fibrinous exudation, and seven eyes developed posterior synechiae of the iris. The difference in the incidence of AC cell grade ≥2+ between the two groups was statistically significant (P < 0.05). Preoperative BCVA (LogMAR) in the sequential group was 1.88 ± 1.13, improving to 0.73 ± 0.77 at the final follow-up, showing statistically significant improvement (P < 0.05). In the combined group, preoperative BCVA (LogMAR) was 1.71 ± 1.08, improving to 0.74 ± 0.91 at the final follow-up, also showing significant improvement (P < 0.05). There was no statistically significant difference in postoperative BCVA (LogMAR) between the two groups (P>0.05). Binary logistic regression analysis identified sequential surgery as a significant protective factor against AC inflammation (OR 0.187, 95% CI 0.056-0.621, P = 0.006), indicating a substantially reduced risk of postoperative inflammatory reaction in the staged intervention cohort.ConclusionBoth sequential and combined surgical approaches are viable for the treatment of diabetic retinopathy with concomitant cataract. These findings indicate that sequential surgery helps reduce early postoperative inflammatory reactions; however, no significant difference in long-term efficacy has been observed between the two surgical methods. Clinical decision-making should be personalized, taking into account the patient’s specific condition and the surgeon’s expertise.Keywords: Intravitreal injection, Pars plana vitrectomy, Cataract phacoemulsification, Intraocular lens implantation

Diabetes mellitus is a severe chronic disease and a leading cause of increased global morbidity and mortality. Projections indicate that by 2050, approximately 1.31 billion individuals worldwide will be living with this condition [1]. Among its complications, diabetic retinopathy (DR)—particularly proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR), which affects 6.96% of patients with diabetes [2]—is a major vision-impairing condition. Pars plana vitrectomy (PPV) is the primary surgical intervention for PDR [3]. For patients with concomitant cataract, combined PPV and cataract surgery is considered both safe and effective, as it facilitates earlier visual rehabilitation and obviates the need for multiple operations. However, this approach is associated with an elevated risk of postoperative inflammation and complications[4, 5]. To mitigate these risks and improve early visual outcomes in patients with PDR, we developed an innovative sequential surgical strategy. This protocol entails a preoperative intravitreal anti-VEGF injection, followed by phacoemulsification with IOL implantation, and subsequent PPV one week later. This strategy not only reduces the complication rates associated with combined surgery but also decreases operative time and enhances overall safety. To our knowledge, no existing literature has described this specific sequential approach.

Materials and methods

Study population

All participants in this study provided informed consent and were approved by our institutional medical ethics committee, adhering to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. A retrospective analysis was conducted, encompassing 97 patients who underwent surgical treatment for diabetic retinopathy in conjunction with cataract surgery at Shandong Eye Hospital from January 2020 to December 2022. Thirty patients were treated with an initial approach of intravitreal anti-VEGF, phacoemulsification cataract extraction, and IOL implantation, followed by PPV one week later. The remaining 67 patients initially received an intravitreal anti-VEGF, followed by PPV, phacoemulsification cataract extraction, and IOL implantation one week later. The minimum follow-up period was three months.

Exclusion criteria

Patients who previously underwent PPV, those with concurrent corneal pathologies or neovascular glaucoma, a history of ocular trauma or intraocular inflammation, and a follow-up period of less than three months were excluded from this study.

Indications for cataract surgery

Compromised view of the fundus, Pre-existing visual impairment due to cataract, rapid progression of cataract following vitrectomy in diabetic patients.

Patient records were meticulously reviewed to extract data on several parameters

Age, gender, duration of diabetes mellitus, indications for vitrectomy, best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) pre- and post-operatively, number of intraoperative photocoagulation spots, use of intraoperative electrocoagulation, intraocular tamponade, intraoperative and postoperative complications, postoperative inflammatory responses, and intraocular pressure (IOP) post-surgery.

All preoperative and postoperative examinations were conducted by the surgeons themselves. According to the SUN classification, anterior chamber (AC) inflammatory cells were defined as a count of ≥ 2 + cells. Hypotony was defined as an IOP ≤ 6 mmHg. Best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) was measured using a Snellen chart. Values were converted to the logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution (logMAR) for statistical analysis.

Following surgery, patients were treated with gatifloxacin eye drops and prednisolone acetate eye drops to control inflammatory responses. Prednisolone acetate was administered every two hours for the first week postoperatively. The regimen was then adjusted to 2% fluorometholone eye drops thereafter. Additionally, Compound Tropicamide was administered to all patients post-vitrectomy to maintain pupil mobility and prevent pupillary synechiae. Patients were instructed to maintain a face-down position post-surgery for a duration ranging from 10 to 30 days post-surgery, contingent on the type of intraocular tamponade utilized.

Primary outcome measures

Severity of AC inflammation, fibrinous exudation in the AC, posterior synechiae of the iris, postoperative IOP, and postoperative BCVA.

Secondary outcome measures

Other intraoperative and postoperative complications.

Surgical techniques

All patients underwent stringent glycemic control and routine ophthalmic evaluations throughout their treatment period. Three days prior to surgery, patients received topical gatifloxacin eye drops administered four times daily. In the sequential group, phacoemulsification was performed through a 2.65 mm incision under topical anesthesia. This was followed by the implantation of a foldable acrylic intraocular lens. Subsequently, intravitreal anti-VEGF agents—either conbercept, ranibizumab, or aflibercept—were administered 3.5 mm posterior to the superior temporal limbal margin. At the end of the procedure, tobramycin and dexamethasone eye ointment was applied to the conjunctival sac. One week later, under retrobulbar anesthesia, a 25-gauge PPV was performed. This procedure included the removal of the central vitreous body and the injection of triamcinolone acetonide to highlight the posterior cortical vitreous. Additionally, removal of peripheral and basal vitreous, excision of proliferative and neovascular membranes, and cauterization for hemostasis were performed. In cases where an epiretinal membrane was detected, it was stained with indocyanine green and peeled, followed by mid-peripheral retinal laser treatment, fluid-air exchange, and, contingent upon the extent of retinal pathology, the injection of air, C3F8, or silicone oil. A central 4-mm diameter continuous circular capsulorhexis was executed in all patients. The scleral incision was closed with 10 − 0 nylon sutures. Peribulbar injection of triamcinolone acetonide was administered at the end of the surgery.

Patients in the combined group received comparable interventions, commencing with an intravitreal injection of either conbercept, ranibizumab, or aflibercept. One week thereafter, these patients underwent phacoemulsification through a 2.65 mm incision under retrobulbar anesthesia, followed by the implantation of a foldable acrylic intraocular lens and a subsequent 25-gauge PPV. The surgical protocol mirrored that of the sequential group. Suturing of the scleral and corneal incisions was accomplished using 10 − 0 nylon sutures. All procedures were conducted by the same expert surgeon.

Statistical analysis

We presented categorical data in terms of frequencies and percentages, and continuous data were presented as means and standard deviations (SD). The Chi-square test or the Mann-Whitney U test, a nonparametric method, was utilized to analyze preoperative demographic and clinical data between the two groups. We compared preoperative and postoperative visual acuity within each group using the paired-sample Wilcoxon test. Binary logistic regression analysis was employed to identify factors influencing the AC reaction. We processed the statistical analysis through SPSS software (version 27.0). The threshold for significance was set at a P-value less than 0.05.

Results

The average age of the patients in the sequential group and the combined group was 60.6 ± 7.5 years and 57.2 ± 10.1 years, respectively. The median duration of follow-up was 3 months (range, 3–36 months). The types of intraocular tamponades employed included air, C3F8, and silicone oil. Silicone oil was successfully removed from all 25 eyes that had been treated with it. Statistically significant differences were observed both in the type of intraocular tamponade used and between genders (P = 0.013，P=0.007). AC reaction

On the first day following sequential cataract surgery, the inflammatory cells in the anterior chamber were graded as < 2+. Post-vitrectomy day 1, AC inflammatory cells graded ≥ 2 + were observed in five eyes (16.7%) of the sequential group and 28 eyes (42.0%) of the combination group. This difference was statistically significant (P = 0.016). Fibrinous exudate appeared in two eyes (6.7%) of the sequential group and nine eyes (13.4%) of the combination group; this difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.334). Throughout the follow-up period, posterior synechiae of the iris occurred in one eye (3.3%) from the sequential group and seven eyes (10.4%) from the combination group, which was not statistically significant (P = 0.242) (Table 2). Binary logistic regression analysis, which included variables such as age, duration of diabetes mellitus, indications for PPV, type of intraocular tamponade, preoperative visual acuity, number of photocoagulation spots (greater than 500), use of electrocautery, and sequence of surgery, indicated that sequential surgery was a protective factor against AC inflammatory reaction (OR 0.187, 95% CI 0.056–0.621, P = 0.006), thus significantly reducing the risk of postoperative AC inflammatory reaction in the sequential group (Table 3) .

Intraoperative and postoperative complications

On the first postoperative day, elevated IOP was observed in five eyes (16.7%) within the sequential group and nine eyes (13.4%) in the combination group. Among the combination group, three instances of elevated IOP were attributable to postoperative vitreous cavity hemorrhage. The remaining 11 cases were associated with the presence of residual viscoelastic substances in the AC or excessive surgical manipulation. Additionally, two eyes in the combination group experienced postoperative hypotony. The sequential group reported two cases of hyphema and one case of IOL decentration. In the combination group, one eye developed hyphema and three eyes experienced vitreous cavity bleeding. Three instances of posterior capsule rupture occurred during surgery in the combination group. No incidents of corneal incision leakage or anterior chamber shallowing occurred during vitrectomy in the sequential group. No statistically significant difference was found in the complication rate between the two groups (P > 0.05).

Discussion

Patients suffering from diabetic retinopathy complicated by cataract are frequently encountered in clinical practice. The combined procedure of PPV with cataract surgery constitutes a primary treatment modality. This integrated approach improves the clarity of the surgical field, enables a more thorough removal of the peripheral vitreous, accelerates visual recovery, shortens patient rehabilitation time, and alleviates the inconvenience associated with multiple surgical interventions. Nonetheless, combined surgeries typically have a longer duration and a higher incidence of complications, particularly when additional techniques such as intraoperative laser photocoagulation, electrocoagulation, and retinoctomy are employed [6]. Traditional sequential surgeries commence with vitrectomy, followed by cataract surgery as the cataract matures. Studies indicate that within three years following PPV, the incidence of cataract formation is as high as 74% [7]. Post-vitrectomy cataracts, lacking vitreous support, render the posterior capsule susceptible to tearing, and diminished zonular stability contributes to fluctuating IOP and challenges in pupil dilation [8], posing significant challenges for surgeons. Our two-step surgical strategy is designed to mitigate these complications.

The present study examines the optimal interval for preoperative anti-VEGF administration prior to vitrectomy. A meta-analysis [9] suggests that conducting a vitrectomy within three to seven days following anti-VEGF treatment optimizes patient outcomes. Conversely, prolonging this interval beyond seven days may elevate the risk of requiring silicone oil tamponade. In this study, the interval was set at one week to coincide with cataract surgery, which was integrated with anti-VEGF therapy to mitigate the risks of intraoperative leakage through the cataract incision and anterior chamber shallowing during vitrectomy. Notably, no surgical complications such as incision leakage or anterior chamber shallowing were observed.

The study revealed that the AC inflammatory response was the most common complication following surgery. On postoperative day 1 after vitrectomy, severe AC inflammation was observed in 16.7% of patients in the sequential group and 42.0% in the combination group, demonstrating a significant difference between the two groups (P < 0.05). Previous studies reported incidence rates of post-vitrectomy inflammatory responses ranging from 3.5% to 30% [10–13]. Eylem et al. performed vitrectomy combined with cataract surgery on patients with and without diabetes mellitus, and observed postoperative rates of fibrinous uveitis at 13.4% and 6.5%, respectively [14]. In our study, the combination group exhibited a notably higher inflammatory response, which was potentially attributable to factors such as PDR, extensive intraoperative laser photocoagulation, electrocautery, and the use of tamponade. In contrast, the incidence of AC inflammation in the sequential group was significantly lower than that in the combination group. However, compared to standalone vitrectomy [13], the rate in the sequential group remained relatively high. This was likely because our study cohort exclusively consisted of patients with diabetic retinopathy, which entailed more complex surgical procedures. Studies have indicated that post-vitrectomy inflammation in patients with diabetes mellitus is more severe than in non-diabetic patients [14]. We conducted a binary logistic regression analysis to identify factors influencing postoperative inflammatory responses, and the results indicated that sequential surgery was a protective factor against AC inflammation (OR 0.187, 95% CI 0.056–0.621, P = 0.006). This finding suggested that a staged surgical approach could reduce postoperative inflammation, thereby facilitating better recovery outcomes for patients.

In our study, the incidence of fibrinous exudates was 6.7% in the sequential group and 13.4% in the combination group, with no statistically significant difference observed between the two groups. The incidence rates of postoperative fibrinous exudates reported in previous studies were consistent with our findings. For example, Chung et al. [15] reported rates of 5% for sequential surgery and 13.5% for combined surgery in patients with diabetes mellitus; Senn et al. [16] observed rates of 3.8% and 23%, respectively; and Jun et al. [17] documented a rate of 16.8% following vitrectomy. Although no notable discrepancies were observed between the two groups in our study, the combination group seemed to exhibit a more frequent inflammatory response postoperatively. This increase in the incidence of fibrinous exudates in the combination group could be due to the prolonged surgical duration and the complexity of the procedures, necessitating frequent replacement of surgical instruments. Despite the regular postoperative administration of topical steroids and pupil dilation, some patients still developed posterior synechiae of the iris, occurring at rates of 7% in the combination group and 3.3% in the sequential group, yet without a notable statistical difference between them. In contrast, studies by Shinoda [18] and Lahey [19] reported much higher rates of posterior synechiae of the iris postoperatively in combined surgeries for patients with PDR, reaching 30.8% and 15%, respectively. Treumer [5] noted that the incidence of posterior synechiae following sequential surgeries was notably lower at 2%, suggesting that staged surgeries tend to result in fewer complications such as synechiae compared to combined surgeries. Factors contributing to the occurrence of posterior synechiae may include fibrinous protein exudation, diabetic retinopathy, extensive use of retinal laser treatment, and the length of the surgical procedure [20]. Therefore, reducing the duration of surgery and mitigating postoperative inflammation in staged surgeries can lower the incidence of associated complications.

Elevated IOP is a frequent complication following vitrectomy. In our study, on the first postoperative day, the incidence of increased IOP was 13.4% in the combination group and 16.7% in the sequential group. Previous literature reports a range of elevated IOP incidences from 4.4% to 55.8% following combined surgeries [21] and approximately 11.5% to 26% following sequential surgeries [5], which aligns with our findings. In our study, posterior capsule rupture occurred only in the combined surgery group, with an incidence of 4.5%, consistent with previous reports indicating a rate of approximately 5% [22]. No posterior capsule ruptures were noted in the sequential group. Conventionally, sequential surgeries begin with vitrectomy, followed by cataract surgery as the cataract progresses. This sequence carried a risk of posterior capsule rupture due to the absence of vitreous support, relaxation of zonular fibers, and increased hardness of the lens nucleus. However, our sequential approach started with cataract surgery, which, despite the frequent presence of vitreous hemorrhage obscuring the posterior capsule, enabled experienced surgeons to mitigate the risk of rupture. Secondary cataract, a common complication after combined vitrectomy and cataract surgery, was avoided in our study by performing a posterior capsulotomy during the surgery.

This study compared visual acuity between the two patient groups, and follow-up data indicated no notable discrepancies in postoperative visual outcomes between the sequential and combination groups, which is consistent with the findings reported by Awidi [23] and Farahvash [24]. To alleviate postoperative inflammation, a periocular injection of triamcinolone acetonide was administered during surgery, followed by a strict anti-inflammatory regimen postoperatively. This approach may be particularly beneficial in cases with significant preoperative risk factors for inflammation or in patients where compliance with intensive topical medication may be a concern.

Our research had certain limitations. Firstly, it was a single-center, retrospective, non-randomized study. Due to its retrospective nature, the analysis could not stratify patients based on the severity of cataracts. Severe cataracts can exacerbate postoperative inflammation, leading to delayed visual recovery, worsened or recurrent macular edema, and elevated intraocular pressure. Future studies should adopt a standard cataract grading system (such as LOCS III), record it at enrollment, and treat it as a key covariate in statistical analyses. Moreover, the overall sample size was small, which necessitates further prospective, multicenter clinical studies to explore this topic more comprehensively and to provide more valuable references for clinical application.

In summary, both sequential and combined surgical approaches are viable for the treatment of diabetic retinopathy with concomitant cataract. These findings indicate that sequential surgery helps reduce early postoperative inflammatory reactions; however, no significant difference in long-term efficacy has been observed between the two surgical methods. Clinical decision-making should be personalized, taking into account the patient’s specific condition and the surgeon’s expertise.

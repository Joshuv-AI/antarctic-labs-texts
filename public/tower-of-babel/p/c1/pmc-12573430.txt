Vitrectomy with and without encircling band for pseudophakic retinal detachment: VIPER study report No. 4—long-term results
Authors: Sabine Baumgarten, Martin Hellmich, Petra Schiller, Claudia Michalik, Peter Walter, Peter Walter
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis open-label extension study to the original VIPER trial investigated the long-term single surgery success after repair of an uncomplicated pseudophakic retinal detachment (PRD) by vitrectomy and gas with or without an encircling band (EB).MethodsExamination data from patients enrolled in the VIPER trial obtained after the 26-weeks’ follow-up period (26-weeks’ FU) of the original trial were analysed, and failures, given as indications for another reattachment procedure, were documented.ResultsFrom the original study population of 257 patients enrolled to the three treatment groups (20G vitrectomy+EB (E1), 20G vitrectomy alone (C) and 23/25G vitrectomy (E2)), follow-up data from 105 (41%) patients could be collected. The additional postoperative follow-up was 1.4 months up to 10.8 years. Of the 105 patients enrolled in this long-term analysis, 102 had an observation time greater than 2 years. Failures due to the reattachment procedure within the 26-week FU of the original trial were as follows: 17 in E1, 19 in C and 5 in E2. Additional late failures were found in seven cases: two in E1, three in C and two in E2, respectively.ConclusionsThe long-term observation of additional failures after primary vitrectomy with or without EB did not change the main result of the VIPER study. There is still no evidence that an additional EB would improve the primary surgical success of vitrectomy for uncomplicated PRD. In the cohort of 105 patients with a longer follow-up than 26 weeks, only seven additional failures were observed.Trial registration numberDKRS00023359.

Introduction

The vitrectomy with and without encircling band in the treatment of pseudophakic retinal detachment (VIPER) study was initialised to answer the question whether an additional encircling band (EB) is justified in combination with vitrectomy for the treatment of pseudophakic retinal detachment (PRD) or whether vitrectomy with gas endotamponade alone is sufficient. Furthermore, the VIPER study investigated whether small gauge transconjunctival trocar-guided vitrectomy is not inferior to 20G vitrectomy with gas and without EB in the treatment of PRD.1 The conclusions was that an additional EB does not significantly reduce the risk for any second procedure necessary to reattach the retina in 20G vitrectomy. Furthermore, small gauge transconjunctival vitrectomy was not inferior to the conventional 20G technique. The follow-up was 26 weeks.2

In this study, we analysed the long-term results of the VIPER study (VIPER-FU). The aim of this open-label extension study was to see if 20G vitrectomy with an additional buckle increases anatomical success compared with 20G vitrectomy alone after the initial 26 weeks’ follow-up (26 weeks’ FU) of the initial VIPER study.

Patients and methods

The VIPER study was a prospective, multicentre, randomised clinical trial and 34 vitreoretinal surgeons from 14 clinical sites across Germany participated. A design publication described details of the VIPER study methods and the enrolment.1 Summarised, 257 patients with uncomplicated PRD were randomised into three groups:

Control group (C): Patients received a 20G vitrectomy with gas and without EB to treat the PRD.

Control group (C): Patients received a 20G vitrectomy with gas and without EB to treat the PRD.

Experimental group 1 (E1): In this group, PRD was handled with a 20G vitrectomy with an EB.

Experimental group 1 (E1): In this group, PRD was handled with a 20G vitrectomy with an EB.

Experimental group 2 (E2): Patients with PRD received a 23G or 25G vitrectomy without EB.

Experimental group 2 (E2): Patients with PRD received a 23G or 25G vitrectomy without EB.

Patients were randomised either with a ratio of 1:1 between E1 and C or with ratios of 1:1:1 between E1, C and E2, depending on the surgical skills of the surgeon. This explains the different numbers of included patients in the control group for E1 versus C and E2 versus C, respectively.

The primary endpoint was defined as no indication for any procedure to reattach the retina during the 26-weeks’ FU.

Additional procedures (failure) included any vitrectomy, buckling surgery or reinjection of gas or air. Insufficient follow-up (ie <23 weeks) was also counted as a failure. As reported in the Results section, 255 patients could be analysed for intention-to-treat analysis (ITT) (two patients of the control group did not receive any surgery due to acute urological disease or internal reasons, respectively.).2

For this long-term evaluation, the 14 participating study sites of the VIPER study were asked to retrospectively evaluate the examination data of their VIPER patients that were collected after the 26-week FU of the initial VIPER study. Nine study sites agreed and applied for an amendment of their ethics committee vote. The Institutional Ethical Review Board of the RWTH Aachen University approved the study (EK504/20).

Data were collected from the study site database, or if not applicable, information was obtained by the patients’ attending ophthalmologist or another contact person. In the latter two cases, the patient’s written consent was necessary and was collected. Some patients were lost to follow-up due to different reasons (death, changed their place of residence or no further information).

The primary objective was to receive information on whether a reoperation due to retinal redetachment occurred during this longer follow-up. Information concerning visual acuity, refraction and further eye-related events or adverse events and serious adverse events was also collected if applicable.

Data from the 2 years’ postoperative visit (2 years’ FU), the last follow-up visit (last FU) and any visit that was not documented in the initial VIPER study were collected. The last FU was the latest ophthalmological examination that took place and was documented for this patient. Additional failures were defined as any retina reattaching procedure in the follow-up period.

The described research adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Statistical analysis

Data from the initial VIPER study and data collected in VIPER-FU were incorporated, and the analysis was based on the pooled data. Group comparisons were made in the ITT set (all trial subjects enrolled, randomised and with surgery performed; analysis was as assigned). Analysis was done as randomised to retain the same allocation as used in the VIPER-results publication (treatment E1 vs C and treatment E2 vs C, respectively). Preoperative characteristics were summarised using descriptive statistics; quantitative data were summarised by mean, SD or median and IQR; qualitative data by count and percentage. The median observation time was calculated by the reverse Kaplan-Meier method (time from date of surgery until last FU, events were censored).

The cumulative incidence of detected indications of a reattaching procedure over time was estimated; patients who were free of events or lost to follow-up were censored at the time of the last follow-up date. Numbers of patients at risk were given below the curves at various time points. Cox proportional hazard models were used to calculate HR for both group comparisons. As in the initial VIPER study, the Cox regression model included only the randomised treatment as a covariate.

Mixed models for repeated measures were used to estimate marginal means for corrected visual acuity (logMAR), including the fixed effects treatment group (surgical method), time, the interaction time*group and baseline (BL) visual acuity (heterogeneous first-order autoregressive covariance structure). All reported p-values are two-sided and were considered statistically significant if below 0.05. We did not adjust for multiple testing due to the explorative and descriptive character of this investigation. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS Statistics (IBM Corp, Armonk, New York, USA) and R (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Patient recruitment and preoperative characteristics

Included in the ITT analysis in the original VIPER study were 255 patients (E1=100 patients, C=98 patients and E2=57 patients). Out of these patients, examination data of 105 (41%) patients could be collected and analysed (E1=43, C=36 patients and E2=26 patients). There were no significant differences in BL characteristics between the VIPER and the VIPER-FU cohort (except for cylindrical correction and axis which is not of any importance here) (see online supplemental table 1).

Primary endpoint

Within the 26-weeks’ FU of the original trial, we observed 17 failures in E1 (17/100, 17%), 19 failures in group C (19/98, 19.4%) and 5 failures in E2 (5/57, 8.8%). Excluded here are failures due to missing a primary endpoint defined as a follow-up <23 weeks with no indication for a reattaching procedure.

With VIPER-FU, seven additional late failures came along: two in E1 (19/100, 19%), three in C (22/98, 22.4%) and two in E2 (7/57, 12.3%). One patient in group C received resurgery after 25 weeks but was not documented in the initial VIPER study and was now counted as an additional failure.

Discussion

The original VIPER study showed that placing a 360° scleral buckle in addition to 20G vitrectomy with gas endotamponade appears not necessary to successfully treat PRD. Furthermore, the non-inferiority analysis proved that small gauge transconjunctival vitrectomy is at least equally successful and safe and can be recommended as well as the 20G vitrectomy technique.2 Limitations of the VIPER study included the relatively short follow-up period of 26 weeks, as this may lead to an overestimation of success rates because possible redetachments later than 26 weeks were not registered. This is why this long-term evaluation is very essential: This study confirms that even after a longer follow-up period of 2 years and more, the additional buckle to 20G vitrectomy for successfully treating PRD is not necessary. Previous studies on that topic showed that retinal re-detachments usually occur within 6 months.38 Zhou et al found in a retrospective cohort study among 1715 patients with PRD that 137 (8%) patients had a retinal redetachment, which occurred after a medium period of 20 days.7 Causes were undetected breaks or new breaks (48.9%), breaks reopening (43.8%) or proliferative vitreoretinopathy (7.3%). They stated that the first 2–4 weeks after surgery are the ‘risk period’ for breaks to reopen.7 A series on 130 cases of failed primary vitrectomy for PRD by Lee suggested 85% of redetachments occurred within the first 3 months and 97.7% of failure was observed within the first 6 months after the primary procedure.9 In the VIPER study, redetachments occurred within 12 weeks after the initial surgery with a slight tendency towards earlier redetachments when an encircling band was used.2 In this long-term observation, up to 10 years, only seven additional redetachments occurred, and there was no superiority in anatomical outcome when an encircling band was placed.

The VIPER study revealed a mean myopisation of −1.0 D in the combined surgery group (E1) vs −0.1 D in the other two groups (C and E2) (p<0.001).2 Myopisation due to axial lengthening after buckling surgery is a well-known phenomenon.1014 An average increase in myopia of 1–3 D is often reported in the literature after encircling scleral buckle procedures.10 12 15 However, most previous studies that examined refractive changes after scleral buckling have been limited to a maximum follow-up of 6 months or 1 year.10 15 16

In our study, the myopic shift in E1 decreases from 2 years’ FU to the last FU. A reason might be a weaker effect of the EB. However, the limitation here is the very small number of included patients for this comparison (E1: 2 years’ FU: n=11, last FU: n=15). A statistical analysis is not meaningful.

Another limitation is that not all patients of the original VIPER study population could be included in VIPER-FU. Data of 105 of the 257 VIPER patients (41%) was collected for the long-term evaluation. The reasons were lost to follow-up because they died or changed their place of residence, or we have no information at all. Another reason was that only 9 of the 14 study centres participated in VIPER-FU due to missing personnel capacity or internal reasons. However, our aim was to collect as much data as possible, and we think that a sample size greater than 100 patients is worth reporting on.

All in all, this long-term evaluation of the VIPER study shows that there is still no evidence that an additional encircling band to vitrectomy in the treatment of uncomplicated PRD improves primary surgical success.

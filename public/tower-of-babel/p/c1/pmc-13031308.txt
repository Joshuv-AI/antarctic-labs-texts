One Health approach on toxocariasis and ophthalmic assessment in owners and dogs

ABSTRACT
Although toxocariasis has been considered a common and important neglected zoonotic parasitic disease worldwide, serologic ownership risk and owner-dog concomitant ophthalmic assessment remain to be fully established. Meanwhile, high toxocariasis prevalence has been recently found in seashore areas of southern Brazil. Accordingly, the present study aimed to perform owner serologic, fecal/hair dog, and owner- dog ophthalmic assessment in an endemic seashore area of southern Brazil. In overall, 112/342 (32.75%) owners were seropositive to anti-Toxocara spp. IgG antibodies by ELISA, with family income statistically significant (p = 0.007) as associated risk factor in the univariate analysis. In addition, Toxocara spp. eggs were visualized in only 6/216 (2.78%) dog feces and 5/236 (2.12%) dog hair samples. Logistic regression indicated that untreated water increased 6.3-fold the dog likelihood for T. canis eggs in hair or feces, while purebred dogs and outdoor access were considered as associated protective factors. Only one/342 individual presented highly suggestive ocular toxocariasis, in one eye and at the cicatricial stage (seropositive for anti-Toxocara IgG antibodies). Out of an additional 32 individuals with some degree of retinal scarring compatible with prior chorioretinitis, 10/32 (31.3%) were also seropositive for anti-Toxocara IgG antibodies. In addition, ophthalmic abnormalities were identified in 205/237 (86.5%) examined dogs, with the most frequent findings involving various nonspecific chronic lesions in 177/237 (74.7%), followed by Florida Spot Keratopathy (FSK) in 12/237 (5.1%) and retinopathy in 11/237 (4.6%) dogs. FSK prevalence in dogs was considered very high. Although less prevalent, fundoscopic changes were significantly associated with Toxocara spp. in both owners and their dogs. In conclusion, similar ophthalmologic abnormalities associated with shared environmental exposure to Toxocara spp. have highlighted the importance of One Health approach for holistic interpretation of human-dog natural occurrence of toxocariasis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-40986-6.

Introduction

Toxocariasis has been considered an important human neglected parasitic infection representing one of the most common zoonotic diseases1, with a 19% estimated global seroprevalence2 and potential economic impact at 2.5 billion USD annually, mostly due to costs of medical treatment and lost income3.

As primarily classified as a soil-transmitted helminthiasis, toxocariasis has been caused by ingestion of infective Toxocara canis or Toxocara cati shed eggs, via feces, by dogs and cats, their respectively definitive hosts4. After ingestion, eggs commonly hatch in the small intestine, with released of infective L3 larva and migration through body organs5. Although toxocariasis cases have been mostly asymptomatic, migrating larvae may be attacked by host immune responses, triggering local inflammation associated with increased production of eosinophils, cytokines and specific antibodies, leading altogether to visceral, ocular, and neurological disturbances6.

An overall 31% seroprevalence of human toxocariasis with 30.0% of ocular abnormalities in seropositive patients has been reported in Latin America and the Caribbean, according to a recent meta-analysis7. Although the cases of ophthalmic disturbances caused by Toxocara spp. larvae have been considered more frequent in children, infection has been most prevalent from 11 to 20 years of age8, with ocular toxocariasis also reported in older patients9–12. Despite presenting no pathognomonic signs9, ocular toxocariasis may progress to permanent ophthalmic damage and visual loss13.

Brazil has performed most serosurveys in the Americas, with a pooled 28% estimated prevalence nationwide, with the highest seroprevalence observed in northeastern children (63.6%) and southern adults (71.8%) 7. High seroprevalence in southern Brazil has been observed also in vulnerable populations including 125/190 (65.8%) oceanic island inhabitants14, 172/208 (82.7%) quilombola individuals 15 and 138/162 (85.2%) indigenous persons16.

Although several serosurveys conducted in Brazil and worldwide, no study to date has applied the One Health approach to Toxocara spp. with concomitantly performed human serology, dog feces testing and human-dog ophthalmologic examination. Accordingly, the study herein has assessed toxocariasis and associated risk factors in owners and dogs living in an endemic seashore areas located in the costal Atlantic Forest of southern Brazil.

Results

Demographic characteristics of participants

A total of 342 participants were investigated, 237 women (69.3%) and 105 men (30.7%). The age ranged from 3 to 81 years old (media: 44.1 ± 20.9). Regarding the monthly family income, 279/342 (81.6%) participants declared themselves to live up to three minimum wage per month, with 130 individuals living up to a minimum wage, which was R$ 1,212.00 (U$ 242.40 with 5:1 exchange) in 2022. According to their education level, participants were clustered into four categories: illiterate (n = 5, 1.5%), elementary (n = 216, 63.2%), high school (n = 39, 11.4%), graduate (n = 78, 22.8%).

Seropositivity and risk factors for toxocariasis

A total of 112/342 (32.7%, CI 95%: 27.9–37.9%) participants tested positive for anti-Toxocara spp. antibodies, with ages from 7 to 78 years old.

During the questionnaire application in the studied population, missing data were observed for the following variables: age (2.05%), educational level (1.17%), source of water consumption (0.29%), vegetable cleaning practices (0.58%), handwashing before meals (7.60%), contact with soil (8.48%), onychophagy (7.89%), and pica (9.06%). The results of the univariate and multivariate analysis and the risk/protective factors for Toxocara spp. seropositivity in humans participants (n = 342) were gathered and presented (Table 1). The final logistic regression model revealed statistically associated risk factors, including having dog and cat, soil contact, and artesian well as the water supply source; to have a higher level of education was an associated protective factor. According to the univariate analysis, family income was statistically significant (p = 0.007), submitted to the logistic regression, but not retained by the final model. Geophagy was also fitted with the criteria adopted for logistic regression (p = 0.077) and not retained.

Gender, age, washing hands before meals, ingestion of raw/undercooked meat, and washing vegetables were variables not associated to seropositivity (p > 0.05) and were not classified to be submitted to the multivariate analysis (p > 0.2).

Human Ophthalmic evaluation results

Only one participant of 342 presented (in one eye) a lesion highly suggestive of ocular toxocariasis in the cicatricial stage. Although the lesion itself has not confirmed the etiology, this participant tested positive for anti-Toxocara IgG antibodies, supporting the clinical findings. An additional 32 participants exhibited some form of retinal scarring compatible with possible prior chorioretinitis, which may have various underlying causes, including toxocariasis and toxoplasmosis. Among these, 10/32 (31.25%) were seropositive for anti-Toxocara IgG antibodies.

Dog results

A total of 216 feces and 236 hair dogs were sampled for Toxocara spp. eggs retrieve, with 6/216 (2.78%) visualized in feces and 5/236 (2.11%) in hair samples (three collected from the dorsal region and two from the perineal region). In hair analysis, two Toxocara spp. eggs were in embryonating process, two classified as non-embryonated and one degenerated17.

The associated risk factors for Toxocara spp. positivity in the population of dogs were gathered and presented (Table 2). Due to the logistic issue, data of only 178 dogs were considered consistent for the evaluation of the associated risk factors to Toxocara spp. (feces/hair) infection.

Associated risk factors for Toxocara spp. (feces/hair) infection in dogs (N = 178) living in the Atlantic Forest Biome, Southern Brazil.

The logistic regression revealed that the providing of untreated water increased 6.3-fold the odds for dog contaminated (hair) or infected (feces) by T. canis, whereas purebred dogs and outdoor access were considered as protective factors. Sex, age, history of hunting, and deworming were not significantly associated to the positivity for T. canis eggs in dogs.

The IgG seroprevalence for toxocariasis within this group was 28.57%. Five individuals (4.20%) had at least one dog with hair contaminated by Toxocara spp. eggs; among these, only two tested positive for anti-Toxocara IgG. Regarding fecal samples, four participants had dogs with positive results for Toxocara spp. eggs, of whom only one showed a positive serological test for toxocariasis. No statistically significant association was observed between the presence of Toxocara spp. eggs and increased toxocariasis risk in the tested population.

Dog ophthalmic evaluation results

Out of the examined dogs, 205/237 (86.5%) had at least one ophthalmic abnormality noted on ophthalmic examination. Of these abnormalities observed, 177 dogs (74.7%) had chronic lesions of the ocular surface and adnexa (entropion, eyelash problems, corneal or eyelid scars, corneal or scleral pigmentation related to low Schirmer tear test results or conditions such as pannus). Fundus changes were observed in 20 dogs (8.4%) and Florida Spot Keratopathy was found in 12 dogs (5.1%).

The findings herein revealed a statistically significant association between presumed retinopathies and Toxocara positivity (p = 0.042, Fisher’s exact test). Retinopathies were identified based on fundoscopic descriptors, such as presence of chorioretinitis, optic neuritis, retinal blood vessel cuffing, retinal hemorrhage, tapetal area hyperpigmentation and presence of hyperreflective lesions in the tapetal area. At the dog–household level, 178 dogs with confirmed connection to tested humans were included in the analysis. These dogs corresponded to 119 human owners, as several individuals were responsible for more than one dog. Fundoscopic lesions suspicious for retinopathy were identified in 20 dogs, of which 7 belonged to households with seropositive humans and 13 to households with seronegative humans. No association was observed between the presence of suspicious funduscopic lesions in dogs and belonging to a seropositive household (OR = 1.13; 95% CI: 0.43–3.00; Fisher’s exact test, p = 0.804).

Among the examined dogs, 12/237 (5.1%) were found to have lesions consistent with Florida Spot Keratopathy (FSK), recorded based on the identification of characteristic subepithelial to anterior stromal single or multiple rounds, gray or white, opacities of various sizes, typically located within the axial or paraxial cornea, and can occur in one or both eyes. Statistical analysis revealed that street access was significantly associated with the presence of Florida Spots (p = 0.018 in Fisher’s test; p = 0.032 in logistic regression). Other variables, including age, vaccination, deworming, forest access, and Toxocara positivity, were not statistically significant predictors. No Florida Spots were found in dogs that tested positive for Toxocara spp., although the low number of positive animals limited statistical power.

The ROC results indicated an accuracy of 73.5% and 84.0, respectively, for seropositivity in human and hair/dog Toxocara spp. infection (Supplementary Fig. 1).

Discussion

The present cross-sectional study was primarily designed to assess the seroepidemiological aspects of toxocariasis in a human population living in the Atlantic Forest Biome of southern Brazil and their owned dogs. In addition, ophthalmic evaluation of both owner and dog populations was performed to verify ocular alterations possibly associated with toxocariasis.

Approximately one third (112/342; 32.7%) of the evaluated individuals in the present survey were seropositive to Toxocara spp. by ELISA test. Although global2, Latin American and Caribbean7 meta-analysis studies estimated 28.0% seroprevalence in Brazil, lower than herein, serosurvey in southern Brazil have registered higher seroprevalence in a wide range of populations, including 71.8% (247/344) in adult rural individuals18, from 43.9% (18/41)19 to 50.6% (216/427) in children20, 65.8% (125/190) in islands and 63.0% in mainland residents (87/138) living in seashore areas14, 63.2% (234/370) in women inmates21, 73.9% (342/463) in indigenous16, and 82.7% (172/208) in quilombola communities15.

Indirect IgG ELISA, using native T. canis TES as antigen, has been widely used as serodiagnostic method for toxocariasis22. As specific IgG detection frequently persists for years, active and chronic infection may not be differentiated by ELISA test23. As already mentioned, ELISA test herein used Toxocara excretion and secretion antigen (TES) with a preincubation with adult A. suum extract for removing antibodies elicited by exposure to Ascarid parasites. In such a scenario, as the ELISA test applied herein was the same as previous surveys, such test has shown reliability and validated comparisons among studies, avoiding cross-reactivity due to coinfections with other Ascarid parasites that may share a high homology of antigenicity with Toxocara spp., potentially leading to false-positive results24.

Socioeconomic status described in the present study may represent one of the main factors associated with seroprevalence, particularly underprivileged, tropical and subtropical communities worldwide2. Herein, logistic regression revealed that having graduate educational level represented a protective factor for seropositivity, corroborating with other surveys conducted in Brazil15,21,25,26. In the United States, low educational attainment and low income were associated with Toxocara seropositivity, according to data of the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey from 2011 to 201427. Although family income was not significantly associated herein with seropositivity by logistic regression, the variable was statistically significant by univariate analysis (p = 0.007). Thus, such finding should be interpreted with caution, considering the influence of income on seropositivity.

Although participants mostly (76.5%) declared to have access to treated water, the water consumption obtained from artesian well increased by almost 7-fold the likelihood of seropositivity to Toxocara spp. History of drinking untreated water has already been observed as a risk factor for toxocariasis in serosurveys conducted in Thailand28 and Iran29. Not surprisingly, human toxocariasis and schistosomiasis have been considered as the main helminths causes of waterborne parasitic diseases in the Middle East and North Africa, due to inadequate supply of potable water, poorly maintained infrastructure and lack of appropriate sanitary facilities, according to a systematic review30. In addition, absence of water supply or poor housing, included as low socio-economic status, was associated with toxocariasis in Africa31. Seropositivity associated with the absence of treated water and poor sanitation have been also observed in indigenous16 and quilombola15 individuals in Brazil. In the present study, logistic regression revealed that intake of untreated water increased in 6.3-fold the odds for a dog being infected by Toxocara spp., corroborating the findings in the Philippines, in which access to dirty water was associated (OR: 2.7) with T. canis infection in dogs, as rain may also spread embryonated eggs to water supplies16. Thus, raising awareness on water treatment among health professionals and public authorities may be crucial to prevent the transmission risk of toxocariasis to local residents, their dogs, visitors and tourists.

Contact with soil was another associated factor with human seropositivity herein, an expected finding and corroborating to results of a global meta-analysis2. Since toxocariasis has been primarily transmitted by soil ingestion, children are more prone to be infected via soil by Toxocara spp. infective eggs while playing32. Nevertheless, seropositivity herein was not significantly associated with age. A study conducted with traditional communities living on fishing and touristic activities in Paraná State, southern Brazil, considered the frequent contact with soil containing Toxocara spp. eggs due to the access of free-roaming dogs to outdoor areas and the occupational activities of the population14. The frequent contact with soil due to working activities has also influenced the seropositivity (OR = 4.4) in quilombola individuals in the Paraná state15. Although the contamination of soil was not evaluated, the exposure to Toxocara spp. eggs by adults during labor and children while playing should be considered, since forest climatic conditions may be favorable to Toxocara spp. eggs, with 20.5 C average temperature and over 80% humidity throughout the year33. Although vegetable cleaning did not show a statistical correlation, this is still one of the possible forms of contact and that food hygiene measures involving vegetables cannot be disregarded34.

Only one owner exhibited a highly suggestive lesion of ocular toxocariasis (in one eye), corresponding to a 0.89% prevalence of ocular lesions among the seropositive population. As disease progression may result in nonspecific sequelae, the absence of highly suggestive manifestations should not rule out the possibility of previous ocular toxocariasis. In fact, ocular form of disease has presented a wide range of clinical manifestations depending on the infection site, with the posterior pole granuloma and endophthalmitis as the most common tissue damages35. The presence of a peripheral or central retinal scar, along with a vitreous band or membrane and extending to the posterior pole, may suggest a granuloma at the cicatricial phase, as observed in this study. Likewise, an overall 1% prevalence (95% CI: 1–2%) of ocular toxocariasis was reported on ophthalmological examination in a 2021 meta-analysis, supporting the findings herein8.

Logistic regression revealed that seropositivity increased by almost 6.5-fold in owners of both dogs and cats, but no significant association was independently observed assessing dog or cats. Such contact with dog or cat has been previously observed as a risk factor for toxocariasis36,37, particularly in underage individuals38. Herein, retrieval frequency of Toxocara spp. in dog feces and hair was less than 3.0%, lower than the overall 11.4% (range: 0.7–48.9%) from a review 39 and 8.8% (95% CI: 5.5–12.6%) in a meta-analysis2 based on dogs in Brazil. Low frequency of infected dogs was also observed in quilombola (5.2%; 5/96 dogs) and indigenous (4.6%; 9/194 dogs) communities living in Paraná, Brazil, despite low body condition score and lack of deworming15,16. Out of dogs living in the Paraná oceanic islands, 12/115 (10.43%) were positive for the presence of Toxocara spp. eggs in feces, and 22/104 (21.15%) in hair samples14. In this study, high environmental contamination with Toxocara spp. eggs (50/130; 38.46%) may have contributed to the infection of dogs. Due to intermittent (and potentially recurring) egg shedding of Toxocara infection, untreated dogs should be considered long-term parasite reservoirs, contaminating the environment, particularly to their veterinarian unattendance, leading to continuous unhealthy and immunosuppression conditions40. As the soil contamination was not evaluated herein, environmental contamination may be only speculated, based on these nearby surveys. Previous studies in Brazil have shown Toxocara canis infection in wolves, foxes and coyotes, and Toxocara cati infection in wild felids41–43. Besides domestic dogs and cats, wildlife animals may also participate in Toxocara spp. cycle and potentially contaminate water sources such as artesian wells.

Purebred dog in the present study was considered a protective factor for toxocariasis, when compared to mongrel ones. Infection by T. canis has been considered significantly higher in stray than domiciled dogs2, probably to the reinfection exposure, associated to lack of anti-helminthic treatment39. In addition, younger dogs (less than one year old) have been considered more susceptible to toxocariasis due to transplacental and nursing milk transmission of infective larvae44. In this study, deworming was not associated with dog infection, which has suggested that free street access may have contributed to reinfection by environmental Toxocara spp.

Noteworthy, presumed retinopathies in dogs herein were statistically associated with Toxocara positivity, as ocular larva migrans caused by migrating Toxocara larvae has been already associated with localized retinal granulomas, vascular inflammation, and neuroretinal disruption in both human and experimental models45. In addition, the presence of retinopathic changes in dogs was significantly associated with Toxocara spp. seropositivity in their owners. Specifically, dogs exhibiting suggestive fundoscopic signs of retinopathy (e.g., hyperreflectivity, vascular sleeve formation, choroidal alterations) were over 8-fold more likely to belong to households with at least one Toxocara-seropositive owner (OR = 8.3). While no direct evidence may support the transmission of Toxocara from dogs to humans via ocular lesions, this correlation has raised important epidemiological implications including shared environmental exposure to infective Toxocara egg, lack or ineffective deworming, and other zoonotic transmission pathways. Such findings have resulted from the One Health approach applied herein, suggesting that canine ocular alterations may serve as sentinel indicators of parasitic exposure risk within the shared human-animal environments. Further studies should incorporate more refined diagnostic methods (e.g., canine serology, ocular ultrasonography, molecular detection of larval antigens) and assess whether such retinal findings in dogs were manifestations of ocular larva migrans or immune-mediated sequelae.

Although this study was originally designed to investigate the serological and ocular impacts of Toxocara spp. exposure in owners and their dogs, the unexpectedly high prevalence of Florida Spot Keratopathy (FSK) in dogs in this area has prompted further evaluation. Interestingly, none of the dogs presenting FSK tested positive for Toxocara spp. in fecal analysis, and statistical testing revealed no association between Toxocara status and the presence of corneal lesions. This finding suggested that, at least within the study limits, Florida Spots were not a direct manifestation of ocular toxocariasis. The observed 5.1% FSK prevalence in dogs on this coastal, endemic region was notably higher than the 0.33% reported in a recent large-scale multicenter study, which evaluated over 23,000 dogs46. Such discrepancy indicated a potential important regional or environmental influence, especially given that outdoor/street access was the only variable significantly associated with FSK in the herein analysis. These findings indicated that exposure to environmental/biological factors may contribute to the pathogenesis of FSK in areas with such ecological profiles. Thus, the high frequency of FSK lesions found in this dog population should be further investigated and fully established.

Although diagnostic tools in the present study were limited to clinical ophthalmic examinations and fecal testing, the consistency of retinopathic signs in Toxocara-positive dogs reinforced a potential link between parasitic exposure and posterior segment inflammation. Such findings supported prior histopathologic evidence indicating retinal and choroidal involvement in canine toxocariasis. The absence of significant associations with other factors (e.g., age, deworming, outdoor access) suggested also that direct parasitic invasion or immune-mediated sequelae may underly these retinal findings. Thus, future studies with serological assays incorporating ocular imaging (e.g., optical coherence tomography) and molecular diagnostics (e.g., PCR from ocular fluids) would help to confirm this association and better characterize the spectrum of Toxocara-induced ocular pathology in naturally exposed canine populations.

The findings herein should be cautiously interpreted, considering all potential limitations. Firstly, evaluation in a cross-sectional study may be difficult, as infection could have been acquired in the household or during outside work. In addition, the study was carried out in a touristic city and included subjects working in a wide variety of professions at the time, making a confounding issue the inclusion of a professional variable into the statistical model results. Regarding pets, information loss on dogs due to the difficulty of owners to precise information was a relevant observation and should be considered an important bias. Although this limitation may have impaired reliable outcome data to provide robust statistical analysis, the results may contribute to a better understanding of the risk of toxocariasis in the population studied. Finally, in the univariate analysis, small cell counts in some categories may have limited statistical power and the precision of estimates; however, this unequal distribution reflected the actual response patterns observed in the investigated population. Accordingly, Fisher’s exact test was applied as an appropriate method for analyzing sparse data and minimizing potential bias in the inference.

Finally, no Toxocara detection was performed in stray dogs and cats or as soil contamination. Cats may arguably play an important role in the toxocariasis transmission47. For instance, feral or untreated cats in New York City represented a significant source of environmental contamination, with T. cati eggs considered as the predominant species in soil samples of public parks48. Thus, future surveys should be conducted to fully establish soil contamination by Toxocara spp. eggs in public areas of Morretes city and surroundings.

Methods

Ethical statements

The present study was approved by both the Human Ethics Committee at the Ministry of Health (protocol 84756324.0.0000.0020, renewed on December 3, 2024) and by the Ethics Committee of Animal Use at the Federal University of Paraná (protocol 036/2021). Before participating in the present study, informed consent was obtained from all human participants and/or their legal guardians. Before participating in the present study, each dog owner gave informed written consent for the study results to be used. All the procedures were designed to reduce animal suffering, and all owners were informed about the possible risks during blood samplings. All procedures were performed in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations and complied with national and Brazilian legislation. Reporting the results follows the recommendations of the ARRIVE guidelines.

Study design

The study herein was a cross-sectional owner-dog survey performed from September to October, 2022 in Morretes city (25° 28′ 37″ S, 48° 50′ 02″ W), a small touristic costal city in southern Brazil with 18,000 inhabitants distributed in 684.58 km2, ranked 1,956 in population (top 35%), 1,849 in Human Development Index (HDI) with 0.674 (medium) (top 33%), and 2,071 in Gross Domestic Product (GDP) (top 37%) out of 5,570 Brazilian municipalities. Morretes presented subtropical climate (Cfa) with altitude of 8.48 m, average temperatures from 16.7 C (winter) to 25.9 C (summer) and 2,271 mm of annual rainfall average at the time.

Human sampling

Humans were invited to voluntarily participate by the official media of the City Secretary of Health, and blood samples were collected after signing the official agreement form and completing the epidemiological questionnaire (Table 3). Human blood samples were collected from cephalic puncture by certified nurses and physicians of the City Secretary of Health. Samples were collected, placed in tubes without anti-coagulant and kept at room temperature (25 °C) until retraction of visible clot, centrifuged at 1,500 RPM for five minutes, separated and kept at -20 °C until processing.

Human serological testing

An inhouse ELISA (Enzyme-linked Immunosorbent Assay) was applied to detect anti-Toxocara spp. IgG antibodies, as previously established49. Each serum sample was preincubated with A. suum adult worm extract (AWE) to avoid cross-reaction with other ascarids, then preincubated for 30 min at 37 °C with AWE solution (25.0 µg/µL) in 0.01 M phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.2) containing 0.05% Tween 20 (PBS-T) (Sigma, St. Louis, MO, USA). Following, polystyrene 96-well microtiter plates (Corning, Costar, New York, USA) were coated with 1.9 µg/uL per well of TES antigens in 0.06 M carbonate-bicarbonate buffer (at pH 9.6) for 1 h at 37 °C and 18 h at 4 °C, subsequently blocked with 3% Molico® skimmed milk PBS-Tween 5% for 1 h at 37 °C. After the pre-adsorption, anti-Human IgG (Fc-specific) peroxidase antibody produced in goat (Sigma A6029, Saint Louis, MO, USA) was added in serial dilutions for 45 min at 37 °C, intercalated by three 5-minute washings.

The reaction was revealed with substrate o-phenylenediamine (0.4 mg/mL, Sigma), interrupted by 2 N sulphuric acid addition and absorbance read at 492 nm, with the cut-off as the mean absorbance of 96 negative control plus three standard deviations. Positive and negative controls were included in each plate. Control serum samples used herein have been kept in the serum bank of a Brazilian Laboratory of Medical Investigation, (Institute of Tropical Medicine of São Paulo, University of São Paulo, São Paulo) and routinely used for serodiagnosis of toxocariasis by ELISA test, using a previously established protocol49.

Antibody levels were expressed as reactivity indices (RI), calculated as the ratio between the absorbance values of each sample and the cut-off value (set at 0.400).

Human ophthalmic evaluation

Human evaluation was performed by two certified ophthalmologists and conducted after voluntary signed agreement by the patient and included as part of local assistance of the Brazilian Unified Health System. Retinal mapping examination was performed with indirect ophthalmoscope and a 20-diopter and described in a pre-established report for standardization purposes. Highly suggestive lesions for toxocariasis included a granuloma or a compatible scar found either in central or peripheric retina, associated with a vitreous band or membrane that extended to the posterior pole. Other scars compatible with retinochoroiditis were considered as poorly suggestive lesions. No follow-up exams were conducted.

Ophthalmic evaluation in dogs

Dog evaluation was performed by certified veterinary ophthalmologists and conducted after owner´s voluntary signed agreement, with data of patient demographics, comprehensive ophthalmic examination and clinical findings collected and analyzed. Ophthalmic examination included neuro-ophthalmic examination (menace response, dazzle reflex, pupillary light reflexes, and palpebral reflexes), Schirmer tear test and fluorescein staining of the ocular surface, slit lamp biomicroscopy (SL-14, Kowa) of the adnexa and anterior segment and indirect ophthalmoscopy of the fundus (Eyetec) with a 20-diopter lens (American Optical), rebound tonometry (TonoVet, Icare). All ophthalmic abnormalities were recorded. No follow-up exams were conducted.

Dog feces and hair samplings

Feces samples were successfully collected from 216/236 (91.5%) dogs. Samples were obtained directly from the dog rectum and individually placed in 50 mL dry tubes containing 10% formalin solution to preserve parasitic structures, as previously established50. Fecal samples were analyzed by a flotation technique using a hyper saturated sodium chloride (NaCl) solution (d = 1.20 g/cm³)51. Feces were mixed with the NaCl solution, filtered through gauze and allowed to float. After a 5 min-flotation-period, the floated material was examined under light microscopy to identify Toxocara spp. eggs (magnification: 10x)51.

Dog hair collection was performed in all the 236 dogs. Samples were obtained from the perineal and lower back (dorsal) regions using sterile scalpel blades and stored individually in 50 mL dry tubes. After collection, all samples were stored at 4 °C until processing. Retrieve of Toxocara spp. eggs was based on previously described methods17,52 with some adaptations53. Briefly, each sample was mixed with 20 mL of distilled water and 0.2 mL of 5% anionic detergent (Tween 80), vigorously homogenized by shaking, and incubated overnight at room temperature. Samples were then mixed with an equal volume of distilled water and detergent before being sieved through metal mesh sieves with openings of 300 μm, 212 μm and 38 μm, under running water for 5 min. The material retained in the 38 μm sieve was centrifuged (800 x g for 5 min) and examined microscopically (10x and 40x magnifications).

Data analysis

All the gathered data were inserted into a spreadsheet (Excel) and codified to be submitted to statistical analysis using R Core Team Package v.4.4.2 (2024)- RStudio v. 2026.01.054. Then, univariate analysis (Pearson Chi-Squared Test or Fisher’s exact test) was performed, and the variables presenting statistical significance lower than 0.2 were considered fit to be submitted to a logistic regression (multivariate analyses) for obtaining the associated risk/protective factor for Toxocara spp. seropositivity. The final model of the logistic regression was improved testing the collinearity among variables and the presence of influential values. An inflation factor of variance higher than 4.0 was adopted as a high degree of collinearity for exclusion of the variables from the logistic model. Odds ratio (OR) values and a 95% confidence interval (95% CI) were given for each evaluated variable. The best-fitting model was adopted as the one that included significant variables (p-value < 0.05) associated with seropositivity and minimized the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) value. Positive hair and/or positive feces sample was used for analysis of the associated risk factors for Toxocara spp. in dogs, following similar statistic approach adopted for human seropositivity.

Binary variables were created to assess associations between the abnormal ophthalmic findings and potential risk factors. Fisher’s exact tests were used to explore relationships between categorical predictors and the presence of ophthalmic abnormalities. A logistic regression model was then constructed to identify independent predictors. Odds ratio (OR) values and a 95% confidence interval (95% CI) also were calculated. All statistical analyses used to assess ophthalmic lesions in dogs were performed using MedCalc Statistical Software version 20.027 (MedCalc Software Ltd, Ostend, Belgium)55 and Python (SciPy and Statsmodels libraries)55. Significance level at 5% was adopted for all statistical tests.

Response Letter to the Editor Regarding “Clinical Application of Different Doses of Hydromorphone Slow-Release Analgesia in Lumbar Fusion in Elderly Patients”
Authors: Weibo Zhong, Xianwei Jin
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

We thank Guanyu Yang and colleagues [1] for their thoughtful comments on our recent paper [2], and would like to address the points they raised. First and foremost, we would like to express our sincere gratitude to the experts for their attention to this article. We also look forward to gaining further insights from their opinions. In response to the five questions raised by the experts regarding this article, we will address each one individually.

In response to the first question, we would like to clarify that our concerns regarding the final concentration of 0.2 mg/mL may have led to an inadequate description of the dilution process, resulting in misunderstandings among several experts. Specifically, we utilized a 5-mL syringe to extract 1 mg of hydromorphone, which was then diluted to a total volume of 5 mL, yielding a final concentration of 0.2 mg/mL.

In response to the second question, our team previously deliberated on whether to select the same volume or concentration of hydromorphone before conducting the experiment. Our analysis indicated that, once the dura mater is covered with a gelatin sponge, the sponge will ultimately absorb the surrounding water, thereby filling its entirety. Consequently, it appears unnecessary to opt for hydromorphone of the same volume. Instead, selecting hydromorphone of the same concentration may better reflect the actual conditions, eliminating the need to dilute hydromorphone into multiple concentrations, which could introduce errors during the dilution process.

In response to the third question, it is accurate to state that most of the existing literature has not employed multimodal analgesia. Our team has extensively reviewed the literature and conducted analgesia based on the established protocols at our hospital. We have further investigated improved analgesic methods that minimize side effects within the framework of our current analgesia protocols. We believe that this experiment underscores the pursuit of enhanced analgesic techniques under the existing guidelines in our hospital. The combination of multiple drugs and various analgesic methods post-surgery may not be the primary focus of this experiment, and the application of these multimodal analgesics may not effectively illustrate the analgesic outcomes of this study. Nonetheless, we are very interested in this proposal and may consider exploring it in future research.

In response to the fourth question, we previously provided a comprehensive explanation to the reviewer during the submission process. The omission of VAS score data is based on our extensive review of the literature [3–5], which indicates that comparing pain grades may more effectively reflect the severity of patients' pain than presenting data alone. While we have received acknowledgment from the reviewer regarding this approach, we recognize that this is a valid question. It is important to note that perspectives on this issue may vary, and we believe that both methods possess their own significance.

In response to the fifth question, our team has indeed considered it; however, we did not observe any significant differences during the experimental process. This may be attributed to the limited number of lumbar segments and the restricted scope of the lumbar surgery we studied. Consequently, we did not focus extensively on the pain differences across various lumbar segments. Nonetheless, this factor merits consideration and presents opportunities for further research.

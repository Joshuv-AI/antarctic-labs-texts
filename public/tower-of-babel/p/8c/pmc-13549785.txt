Large Language Models in Spine Surgery : A Narrative Review of Performance Paradox and Clinical Integration Challenges
Authors: Sung Bum Kim, Il-Tae Jang, YooKyung Lee, Yoon Gyo Jung, Sangsoo Choi, Jun-yong Cha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To provide a narrative synthesis of the performance paradox in large language model (LLM) applications for spine surgery, examining the disparity between technical metrics and clinical utility. A narrative review was conducted examining literature from January 2023 to February 2026 across PubMed, EMBASE, and Google Scholar. Studies evaluating LLM applications in spine surgery were included, with emphasis on newer models (GPT-4o, GPT-5, Claude, Gemini variants, DeepSeek). Studies were thematically analyzed across clinical documentation, patient communication, and surgical decision-making domains. Analysis of 42 studies revealed a consistent pattern across applications. LLMs performed strongly in structured documentation tasks, including current procedural terminology coding (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, 0.87) and surgical classification (91% accuracy), and improved readability in patientfacing materials. Patient communication achieved high satisfaction rates but demonstrated limited emotional intelligence. In contrast, decision-making performance was more variable: in small vignette-based comparisons, LLMs showed lower raw accuracy than attending spine surgeons in complex scenarios, and guideline concordance ranged from 33% to 88% across models. Emerging evidence with nextgeneration models suggests incremental gains, but procedure-level agreement remains limited (κ=0.415 and 0.587 in minimally invasive spine surgery triage). Image-based tasks, such as Cobb angle measurement, remain particularly challenging, with all tested models failing to meet the ≤10° clinical threshold. LLMs show near-term utility in standardized text-based tasks, but current evidence does not support autonomous use in complex clinical decision-making or image-based spinal assessment. Staged implementation with mandatory human oversight remains necessary.

INTRODUCTION

Large language models (LLMs) have rapidly transformed healthcare applications since ChatGPT’s release in November 2022 [29]. The spine surgery field has witnessed particularly intense investigation, with over 100 studies published by 2025 [30]. This surge reflects both technological promise and urgent clinical needs in a specialty characterized by complex decisionmaking and significant patient communication challenges [4].

Spine surgery presents unique challenges for artificial intelligence (AI) integration [17]. The field requires simultaneous consideration of anatomical variations, biomechanical principles, patient-specific factors, and long-term functional outcomes [11]. Unlike diagnostic specialties where pattern recognition predominates, spine surgery demands nuanced judgment incorporating surgical risk assessment and individualized treatment planning [10]. These characteristics make spine surgery an ideal domain for evaluating LLM capabilities and limitations [25].

Recent evidence reveals a striking performance paradox: models demonstrate excellent metrics in controlled testing yet show significant limitations in clinical practice [8]. GPT-4 achieves 88.2% concordance with North American Spine Society (NASS) guidelines [13], while newer models like Claude 3.5 Sonnet and Gemini variants show variable performance across different tasks [28]. This disparity between technical achievement and clinical utility has profound implications for implementation strategies.

This narrative review critically examines the performance paradox across LLM applications in spine surgery, incorporating evidence from newer models including GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini 2.5, GPT-5, and DeepSeek implementations. We provide thematic analysis of achievements and limitations to inform evidence-based integration strategies.

METHODS

We conducted a narrative review following established frameworks for thematic synthesis of heterogeneous evidence [26]. This approach was selected over systematic review methodology due to the rapidly evolving nature of the field, heterogeneity of study designs, and need for critical interpretation of emerging technologies.

Search strategy

Literature searches were performed across PubMed, EMBASE, and Google Scholar from January 2023 to February 2026. Search terms included : (“large language model” OR “ChatGPT” OR “GPT-4” OR “GPT-4o” OR “Claude” OR “Gemini” OR “Perplexity” OR “DeepSeek” OR “GPT-5” OR “Gemini 2.5”) AND (“spine surgery” OR “spinal surgery” OR “neurosurgery” OR “orthopedic spine”). Additional targeted searches were conducted for newer models including Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini Advanced, DeepSeek-R1, and next-generation models (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Flash).

Selection process

Studies were selected based on relevance and quality of evidence rather than exhaustive inclusion. Given the heterogeneity of study designs and outcomes, priority was given to studies reporting quantitative performance metrics, comparative evaluation, or clinically interpretable outcomes. Specifically, we prioritized : 1) quantitative performance comparisons, 2) clinical validation studies, 3) evaluations of newer LLM models, and 4) studies addressing the performance-utility gap. Explicit inclusion criteria were : 1) original research or review articles reporting LLM performance in spine surgery or closely related neurosurgical domains; 2) studies providing quantitative performance metrics or structured qualitative comparisons; and 3) English-language publications. Exclusion criteria were : 1) studies using AI without any language-processing component (e.g., pure deep learning imaging models with no text-based output); 2) case reports or editorials without performance data; and 3) studies not involving clinical application. A small number of studies evaluating broader language-based AI comparators— including natural language processing (NLP)-based classification systems and BERT-based models—were exceptionally included where they provided direct contextual comparison with LLM performance. A total of 42 studies met these criteria and were included in the final synthesis. The literature selection process is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Data extraction and thematic analysis

From each included study, we extracted : study design, LLM model evaluated, clinical application domain, performance metrics reported, comparison groups (if applicable), and identified limitations. Studies were categorized into three thematic domains : clinical documentation and administrative tasks, patient communication and education, and clinical decisionmaking and surgical planning. Performance metrics were synthesized narratively. Tables 1-3 were compiled by organizing extracted data according to these thematic domains.

RESULTS

Clinical documentation and administrative tasks

LLMs demonstrated consistently strong performance in standardized documentation tasks, as summarized in Table 1. ChatGPT-4 achieved area under the receiver operating characteristic curve 0.87 for current procedural terminology (CPT) coding when primed with comprehensive code lists [40]. Natural language processing applications achieved 91% accuracy in surgical classification across 12000 cases [31]. LLM-generated informed consent documents showed superior readability scores (12.9 vs. 15.7 Flesch-Kincaid grade level) compared to surgeongenerated versions [5].

Recent applications in surgical site infection management demonstrated high performance, with GPT-4 achieving logic and accuracy scores of 24.27±0.4 and 24.46±0.25 on a 5-point scale [42]. The model successfully created personalized treatment plans and infection prediction models, though limitations persisted in antibiotic customization.

Imaging-based documentation tasks, however, reveal more pronounced limitations. In a cross-sectional study of 122 scoliosis patients, four commercially available LLMs (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Grok) were evaluated for automated Cobb angle measurement from radiographs [7]. Gemini produced no calculated Cobb angles. ChatGPT failed to generate values in 90 of 122 radiographs, and when values were produced, mean absolute error reached 58.6°±45.9°. Perplexity and Grok generated estimates for all thoracolumbar cases but demonstrated mean differences of 18.8°±13.3° and 24.2°±18.3°, respectively— all exceeding the clinically accepted threshold of ≤10°. None of the models identified S-shaped scoliosis cases. These findings underscore that current LLMs remain unsuitable for complex image-based documentation tasks despite strong text processing capabilities.

Patient communication and education

Patient communication revealed complex performance patterns across multiple models, as summarized in Table 2. Chat].GPT and Bard achieved 97% satisfaction rates for standardized FAQs about lumbar spine fusion [16]. A comprehensive evaluation of six chatbots (ChatGPT-3.5, ChatGPT-4o, Copilot, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity) against clinical practice guidelines showed marked performance differences [27]. Perplexity achieved the highest guideline match rate at 67%, followed by Gemini at 63%, while ChatGPT-4o and Claude both showed only 33% match rates.

Comparative analysis of endoscopic lumbar surgery information revealed audience-specific performance differences. Claude 3.5 Sonnet provided highest quality information for professional surgeons, while ChatGPT o1-preview showed superior understandability for patients [19]. These findings suggest context-specific deployment strategies may optimize outcomes.

Critical limitations emerged in emotional intelligence across all evaluated models. Studies consistently demonstrated failure to recognize emotional cues and inappropriate standardization of responses to personal concerns [14]. ChatGPT-4 showed capabilities in improving patient education materials’ readability while maintaining accuracy [32].

Clinical decision-making and surgical planning

Clinical decision-making revealed the most significant limitations, as detailed in Table 3. Direct surgeon comparisons showed LLMs achieving only 20% accuracy versus 100% for attending surgeons in complex scenarios [1]. This finding was derived from a study of five clinical vignettes, and the original authors noted no statistically significant difference in accuracy between groups. Nonetheless, similar concerns have been reported across heterogeneous study designs, and the pattern is consistent with findings from related literature.

Guideline concordance varied substantially across models and contexts. GPT-4 achieved 88.2% concordance with NASS lumbar fusion guidelines [13], while demonstrating 73.07% concordance with American College of Surgeons (ACS) guidelines [39]. Gemini Advanced achieved 69.23% ACS guideline concordance. Earlier GPT comparisons revealed improvement trends between versions : GPT-4 achieved 81% accuracy versus 62.5% for GPT-3.5 in antibiotic prophylaxis guidelines [41]. GPT].4 demonstrated 100% accuracy in neurosurgical triaging in controlled scenarios [36]. BERT-based models achieved approximately 0.9 performance metrics for lumbar spine disorder classification [6].

A vignette-based study specifically evaluating next-generation models across 90 minimally invasive spine surgery (MISS) cases found that ChatGPT-5 Pro and Gemini 2.5 Pro could broadly differentiate surgical from non-surgical management (κ=0.415 and κ=0.587, respectively), but case-level concordance for precise procedural categorization remained only ‘slight’ to ‘fair’ [12]. Even advanced next-generation models have not yet demonstrated reliable case-level concordance for procedurespecific decision-making in MISS.

Evidence from broader AI applications

Shi et al. [30] conducted a scoping review of 105 AI studies in spine surgery, revealing distinct application patterns. The distribution indicates prognostication (38%), diagnosis (35%), medical image processing (29%), and surgical assistance (3%) as primary applications. Machine learning models achieved mean 74.9% accuracy, performing best at preoperative patient selection and cost prediction [35]. Natural language processing applications remain limited despite technical advances. A systematic review of 12 NLP studies found 91% accuracy for procedure categorization, but none were deemed ready for clinical implementation [9]. Recent advances demonstrate AI’s potential across the perioperative continuum, though validation remains essential [21,37].

Emerging evidence from next-generation models

Recent studies published through early 2026 document continued improvement in LLM performance, with a generation shift toward reasoning-optimized models. A retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) framework evaluated across 200 real-world spinal cases found Gemini 2.5 Flash achieving the highest overall score (49.25±2.88), outperforming DeepSeek (47.49±3.34), GPT-4o (45.59±3.89), and GPT-4o-mini (41.23±5.96) [20]. In neurosurgical board examination settings, DeepSeek-R1 achieved 84% accuracy, outperforming ChatGPT-o3-mini-high (82%) and Gemini 2.0 Pro (78%), compared with 58% for senior residents [38]. These findings suggest that the performance gap between LLMs and human specialists is narrowing in structured knowledge-based tasks, though spine surgery-specific prospective validation remains lacking.

DISCUSSION

The evidence reveals a consistent performance paradox across all LLM applications : excellence in standardized tasks versus significant limitations in complex clinical reasoning [3]. This pattern appears across all evaluated models, including newer implementations of GPT-4o, Claude 3.5, Gemini 2.5, and GPT-5 Pro.

The superior documentation performance suggests immediate integration opportunities [30,37]. With approximately 90% accuracy in CPT coding and surgical classification, LLMs could substantially reduce administrative burden. However, performance variations with rare procedures necessitate continued human oversight [31]. This pattern reflects fundamental architectural limitations where LLMs excel at pattern matching but struggle integrating multiple clinical factors for nuanced decision].making [1].

The persistent failure in image-based tasks further illustrates this paradox. Despite strong performance in text-based documentation, all four tested LLMs produced clinically unacceptable Cobb angle measurements (mean errors ranging from 18.8° to 58.6°), with Gemini failing to generate any calculated values [7]. This suggests that multimodal LLM capabilities require substantially more domain-specific development before deployment in spine imaging workflows.

Patient communication presents complex trade-offs between efficiency and empathy. While achieving over 95% satisfaction for standard queries [16], the inability to recognize emotional cues represents a critical limitation [14]. The finding that different models excel for different audiences suggests context-specific deployment strategies may optimize outcomes [19]. However, the consistent 33–67% guideline match rates across six major chatbots indicates systemic limitations rather than model-specific deficiencies [27].

Clinical decision-making reveals the most concerning performance gaps. In a small vignette-based comparison, LLMs showed lower raw accuracy than attending spine surgeons in complex scenarios [1], although the original study did not demonstrate a statistically significant between-group difference. These findings should therefore be interpreted cautiously, but they suggest that performance may decline as case complexity and procedural nuance increase. In structured triage scenarios, by contrast, GPT-4 achieved 100% accuracy in neurosurgical triage [36], and in emergency department consultations, ChatGPT responses were rated superior to those of board-certified spine surgeons across five evaluation metrics by blinded emergency medicine physician evaluators [33]. These contrasting findings underscore that LLM performance is highly task-dependent. Guideline concordance ranging from 33% to 88% precludes autonomous use in complex clinical decisions [13,36].

Furthermore, even next-generation models show concerning limitations in procedural specificity. GPT-5 Pro and Gemini 2.5 Pro achieved only slight-to-fair triage concordance (κ=0.415 and κ=0.587) in MISS [12]. A recent systematic review (2023–2025, journals with impact factor ≥3.0) identified four major implementation barriers : regulatory compliance, data privacy, algorithmic bias, and workflow integration, and emphasized that overcoming the performance paradox requires multimodal data integration and prospective validation rather than benchmark optimization alone [34].

Cross-specialty validation provides important context. Lee and Kim’s review [18] of ChatGPT applications in obstetrics and gynecology demonstrated remarkably similar performance patterns : strong performance in standardized examinations but significant challenges with complex ethical considerations. These parallel findings suggest the performance paradox represents fundamental technological limitations rather than spine surgery-specific issues.

Domain-specific approaches like SurgeryLLM’s RAG show promise [24], though extensive validation remains necessary. Implementation barriers extend beyond technical performance. Legal liability for AI-assisted decisions remains undefined, regulatory frameworks are absent, and infrastructure requirements limit adoption [22]. The rapid model evolution creates moving validation targets, complicating clinical integration efforts [23].

We recommend staged implementation based on our findings : phase 1 should focus on high-performance documentation tasks (>85% accuracy); phase 2 could introduce decision support with mandatory oversight (70–85% accuracy); and phase 3 awaits technological advances for patient-facing applications (<70% current accuracy) and image-based tasks.

Study limitations

This narrative review has inherent limitations. The narrative approach involves subjective selection without formal quality assessment, unlike systematic reviews. The rapid evolution of LLM technology and heterogeneity of study designs precluded quantitative synthesis. Most included studies were conducted in controlled settings, potentially overestimating real-world clinical performance. Additionally, the absence of long-term outcome studies prevents assessment of actual clinical impact. A particularly important limitation is the pace of model development : the models evaluated in this review—including GPT-5 Pro and Gemini 2.5 Pro—may already be superseded by newer versions at the time of publication, meaning that specific performance figures should be interpreted as snapshots rather than stable benchmarks. This rapid obsolescence of model-specific data represents a structural challenge for all narrative reviews in this field. These limitations highlight the need for standardized evaluation frameworks and prospective clinical trials.

CONCLUSION

This narrative review demonstrates that LLMs exhibit a clear performance paradox in spine surgery applications. LLMs performed strongly on structured text-based tasks such as coding, classification, and readability optimization, but remained inconsistent in guideline-concordant counseling, procedure-level decision-making, and image-based assessment. Current evidence supports staged implementation prioritizing documentation efficiency while maintaining strict human oversight for clinical decisions. The convergence of performance limitations across next-generation models—including GPT-5 Pro and Gemini 2.5 Pro achieving only slight-to-fair triage concordance— suggests that architectural constraints rather than model version alone underlie these limitations. Future research must develop domain-specific, multimodal solutions and establish standardized validation frameworks for safe clinical integration [2,15].

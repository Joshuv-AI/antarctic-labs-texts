Canine mammary tumors: a bibliometric and visualized analysis from 2009 to 2025
Authors: Yongquan Shi, Wenwen Ding, Zilong He, Hua Zhang, Panpan Sun, Kuohai Fan, Wei Yin, Huizhen Yang, Zhenbiao Zhang, Jia Zhong, Jianzhong Wang, Yaogui Sun, Hongquan Li, Na Sun

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCanine mammary tumors (CMTs) are the most common neoplasms in intact female dogs. Although the number of publications on CMTs has increased steadily over recent decades, a comprehensive overview of the knowledge structure, research hotspots, and evolutionary trends in this field is still lacking.MethodsPublications related to CMTs were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection without restrictions on language or document type. Bibliometric and visualization analyses were conducted using CiteSpace (version 6.3.1) and VOSviewer (version 1.6.20). Co-authorship, co-citation, and keyword co-occurrence networks were constructed to identify influential countries, institutions, authors, core journals, research themes, and emerging trends.ResultsThe annual number of publications on CMTs showed a long-term increasing trend and has remained relatively stable in recent years, indicating a mature research stage. Brazil, the United States, and Italy were the most productive and influential countries, forming the core of the international collaboration network. A limited number of institutions and author groups dominated the field, with strong intra-group collaboration. Keyword and co-citation analyses revealed that histopathology, molecular expression, prognosis constitute the central research themes. Temporal and burst analyses indicated a shift from descriptive pathology toward molecular characterization, prognostic evaluation, reflecting the increasing depth and complexity of research in this field.ConclusionsResearch on CMTs has evolved from morphology-based studies to increasingly molecular and clinically oriented approaches. This bibliometric analysis clarifies the intellectual structure and developmental trajectory of the field and provides a systematic reference for future research on CMTs.Keywords: Canine mammary tumors (CMTs), Spontaneous model, Research hotspots, Evolutionary trends, Comprehensive overview, Bibliometric and visualization analyses

Introduction

Canine mammary tumors (CMTs) are among the most prevalent neoplasms in veterinary clinical practice, predominantly affecting intact, geriatric female dogs. From 2017 to 2022, the relative incidence of CMTs was reported at 46.71% [1, 2]. Furthermore, more than 50% of these cases are malignant, with specific studies identifying a malignancy rate of 51.59% among affected dogs [3, 4]. CMTs, which exhibit a high incidence and a substantial proportion of malignant cases, are characterized by highly heterogeneous and aggressive tumor cells, resulting in the destruction of local mammary tissue and significant impairment of canine health [5–7]. Even after surgical resection, CMTs are prone to recurrence and distant metastasis, ultimately resulting in poor prognosis and shortened survival time [8].As research on CMTs continues to grow rapidly, traditional narrative reviews alone are no longer sufficient to fully capture the structure and evolution of this field. Bibliometric analysis and scientific knowledge mapping offer a systematic means of organizing the expanding body of CMT literature and of revealing research themes, developmental trends, and collaborative relationships. Among the commonly used tools, CiteSpace and VOSviewer are widely applied and offer complementary analytical strengths [9]. CiteSpace emphasizes the temporal dimension of scientific development and is particularly effective in identifying key references, pivotal nodes, and emerging research fronts through co-citation analysis and burst detection [10]. VOSviewer, developed by Van Eck and Waltman, focuses on large-scale network construction and visualization based on co-word analysis, co-citation analysis, and bibliographic coupling, enabling intuitive identification of research clusters, thematic structures, and collaborative relationships through its distance-based mapping approach [11, 12].

In this study, CiteSpace and VOSviewer were jointly employed to conduct a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of the literature on CMTs. By integrating multiple visualization strategies, we aimed to characterize the knowledge structure of this field, identify major research themes and emerging trends, and explore patterns of collaboration among authors and institutions. This combined approach provides a more complete overview of the development and current state of CMT research and may offer useful insights for future studies in this field. Global country/regional, institutional contributions and collaboration patterns

Analysis of global research output revealed a highly uneven yet structured distribution of publications on CMTs across countries, institutions, and collaborative networks. The country collaboration network included N = 57 nodes and E = 56 links, indicating the overall structure of international cooperation. A total of 85 countries contributed to this field, of which 51 met the predefined inclusion thresholds. Brazil emerged as the most productive country (123 publications), followed by the United States (85 publications) and Italy (81 publications), together accounting for a substantial proportion of the total output. Other major contributors included Spain, Portugal, Japan, Poland, Germany, South Korea, and People’s Republic of China, indicating that research activity is primarily concentrated in South America, Europe, and North America, with a growing contribution from Asia (Fig. 3A; Table 1). Collaboration network analysis based on VOSviewer demonstrated pronounced international and inter-institutional cooperation, characterized by distinct country clusters and strong cross-regional linkages. Brazil formed a central collaboration hub with Portugal, Spain, Italy, and the United States, while the United States and Italy served as key connectors linking European and Asian research networks. Author collaboration analysis revealed multiple tightly knit research groups with intensive intra-cluster cooperation and looser inter-cluster connections, reflecting a research structure dominated by stable core teams (Fig. 3B). At the institutional level, the institutional collaboration network consisted of N = 251 nodes and E = 300 links, reflecting collaboration patterns among research institutions. The institutional coupling network generated by VOSviewer illustrates knowledge similarity among research institutions in CMTs studies, with node size indicating publication output and link thickness representing shared references. Universidade Federal de Minas Gerais formed the largest cluster hub, showing strong coupling with major Brazilian and European institutions; Universidade do Porto led a second cluster closely linked to Spanish and Italian institutions; and Università di Padova headed a third cluster dominated by Italian universities, together revealing a multi-cluster structure characterized by regional knowledge aggregation and cross-continental integration (Fig. 4B). Discussion

This bibliometric analysis outlines the development, structural characteristics, and thematic evolution of CMTs research over an extended time span. By integrating co-citation, co-occurrence, and collaboration network analyses, the present study provides a macroscopic perspective on how this field has evolved and how it is currently positioned within veterinary oncology.

Developmental characteristics of CMT research

Longitudinal analysis of published studies suggests that research on CMTs has gradually transitioned from a phase of rapid expansion to a more stable stage of development. Such a pattern is commonly observed in research areas that have reached a certain level of methodological and conceptual maturity. Early investigations primarily focused on epidemiological characteristics, histopathological classification, and descriptive tumor features, which collectively established the foundational framework for subsequent CMT research [13].

In recent years, the focus of CMT studies has increasingly shifted toward prognostic evaluation, molecular characterization, and translational relevance. Greater attention has been given to identifying biomarkers and microstructural alterations associated with tumor aggressiveness and metastatic potential, alongside efforts to elucidate underlying molecular mechanisms [14]. This shift indicates a move away from predominantly descriptive research toward hypothesis-driven and mechanism-oriented approaches. As the concept of comparative oncology has gained traction, CMTs have been increasingly recognized as a valuable naturally occurring model for human breast cancer, enhancing their relevance for both veterinary clinical practice and translational cancer research [15].

Geographical patterns and research leadership

Global research output in the field of CMTs shows a marked geographical imbalance, with Brazil consistently occupying a dominant position. This leadership can be attributed to a combination of factors, including a large and diverse canine population, historically high proportions of intact female dogs, and a strong tradition in veterinary pathology. In addition, differences in clinical practice may also contribute to this pattern. In Brazil, the use of ovariohysterectomy is less widespread than in many Western countries, where routine spaying is common [16]. Instead, long-acting progestins are still frequently used for reproductive control. Prolonged exposure to exogenous progesterone is associated with changes in mammary tissue and has been linked to an increased risk of tumor development in dogs [16, 17]. Experimental studies further suggest that progesterone can promote the proliferation, migration, and invasion of CMT cells through specific signaling pathways, including those involving AGR2, 14-3-3ε, and α-actinin-4 [17]. These factors may partly explain the higher disease burden and, in turn, the strong research activity observed in Brazil. Together, these conditions have facilitated large-scale clinicopathological investigations and the development of standardized classification and grading systems that are widely adopted in CMT research [18, 19]. In contrast, countries such as the United States and Italy play more integrative roles within international collaboration networks, acting as bridges between regional research clusters. Although international collaboration has increased over time, it remains largely confined within the veterinary research community, highlighting the need for closer integration with human oncology research teams to enhance translational relevance.

The value of CMTs as a translational model for HBC has gained increasing recognition. Numerous studies have demonstrated substantial similarities between CMTs and HBC at histological, clinicopathological, and molecular levels, including spontaneous tumor development, shared environmental risk factors, hormone receptor expression patterns, prognostic determinants, and genetic alterations [16, 20, 21]. The relatively shorter lifespan of dogs, faster tumor progression, and spontaneous tumor formation in immunocompetent hosts further enhance the suitability of CMTs as a model for studying cancer biology and immunotherapeutic strategies, addressing some limitations of genetically engineered murine models [21].

Comparative oncology approaches have been increasingly applied in CMT research to explore shared molecular mechanisms underlying tumor initiation and progression. Common alterations reported in both CMTs and HBC include hotspot mutations in PIK3CA, aberrant DNA methylation patterns such as hypermethylation of ANK2, dysregulated long non-coding RNA expression, and metabolic reprogramming [22–25]. These molecular parallels provide a foundation for identifying novel diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets. Nevertheless, challenges remain, including incomplete understanding of multidrug resistance following chemotherapy and interobserver variability in histopathological diagnosis. Emerging strategies such as multidrug resistance modeling and computer-assisted pathology systems may help address these limitations and further strengthen the translational impact of CMT research [23, 26].

Intellectual foundations and core knowledge structure

The intellectual structure of CMTs research remains strongly grounded in classical pathology, as evidenced by the sustained influence of foundational authors and reference works focusing on tumor classification and histopathological evaluation [27–29]. The long-term centrality of these studies in co-citation networks indicates that standardized pathological frameworks continue to function as the core conceptual scaffold of the field. Consistent with this observation, Veterinary Pathology remains the most influential journal in the cited literature, underscoring the pathology-driven nature of CMT research [18].

At the same time, the increasing co-citation of oncology- and molecular biology-oriented journals suggests a gradual methodological expansion beyond morphology-based diagnosis [21, 25]. Rather than replacing traditional pathology, molecular approaches such as epigenetic profiling [24], computer-assisted diagnostic systems [26], and the development of well-characterized CMT cell lines [22] appear to be integrated into existing diagnostic frameworks. This layered evolution indicates that future advances in CMT research are likely to depend on the effective integration of histopathology with molecular and computational tools, thereby enhancing prognostic precision and reinforcing the relevance of CMTs within comparative oncology research [30].

Shifts in research focus and emerging themes

The thematic evolution of CMTs research reveals a clear shift from morphology-oriented description toward molecularly informed and translationally relevant investigation. Early research hotspots were largely centered on gross and microscopic characterization, with an emphasis on tumor identification and classification based on standardized pathological terminology, laying the groundwork for subsequent advances [18]. These foundational efforts established a stable histopathological framework that continues to underpin the field, as reflected by the sustained citation of classical pathology-oriented studies [21].

With the increasing adoption of methodologies from human breast cancer research, CMT studies have progressively incorporated immunohistochemical and molecular markers—particularly hormone receptor status and oncogenic signaling pathways—reflected by the routine assessment of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), which underscores the growing conceptual and methodological alignment between CMT and human oncology [16]. At the molecular level, shared alterations involving PIK3CA hotspot mutations, ANK2 hypermethylation, long non-coding RNA expression profiles, and metabolic reprogramming further reinforced the conceptual convergence between CMT and human breast cancer [23–25].

In more recent years, keywords related to disease progression, prognosis, and comparative oncology have gained prominence, reflecting a maturation of the field toward more clinically and biologically integrated research themes [19]. This shift is also apparent at the journal level, where traditional pathology outlets remain influential, while an increasing contribution from oncology- and molecular biology-focused journals points to a broader integration of CMT research into the cancer biology landscape. Taken together, these patterns indicate that CMT research has moved beyond predominantly descriptive pathology toward a multidimensional framework that supports its continued development as a translational model for human breast cancer [21].

Implications, limitations, and future perspectives

This bibliometric analysis offers a structured overview of CMTs research, identifying influential contributors, core knowledge bases, and evolving thematic trends. The findings highlight the increasing importance of comparative oncology as an overarching framework, underscoring the role of CMTs in strengthening the translational interface between veterinary and human cancer research.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. As the analysis was confined to the Web of Science Core Collection, regional and non-English publications may be underrepresented. In addition, bibliometric indicators are shaped by database coverage and analytical parameters, and therefore capture general trends rather than absolute research output.

Looking ahead, CMT research is expected to move toward more integrative strategies that combine clinicopathological information with multi-omics and emerging single-cell or spatial approaches. Molecular parallels with human breast cancer, including recurrent PIK3CA mutations and epigenetic alterations, support the continued use of CMTs as a translational model [25, 31]. The expanding availability of experimental resources such as biobanks and drug-resistant cell lines further enhances translational potential, although challenges remain in elucidating chemotherapy resistance mechanisms and reducing diagnostic variability [30]. Overall, sustained interdisciplinary collaboration will be essential to fully realize the comparative value of CMT research.

Limitations

Several limitations should be considered. First, as the analysis was based solely on the WoSCC, studies indexed in other databases, including Scopus, PubMed, and regional sources, may have been missed, particularly in regions with strong research activity such as Brazil. Second, bibliometric results are influenced by citation lag, and more recent publications (2024–2025) may not yet have reached their full citation impact. Third, self-citations were not excluded and may have affected the ranking of authors and institutions. Fourth, while CiteSpace applies objective algorithms, some degree of subjectivity is unavoidable in cluster labeling and interpretation. Finally, this study focused only on canine models and did not extend to comparative analyses with other species.

Conclusion

This bibliometric analysis systematically delineates the intellectual structure, research hotspots, and developmental trajectories of CMTs research from 2009 to 2025. Analysis of keyword bursts suggests that research is increasingly moving from descriptive pathology toward molecular and translational studies, reflecting evolving research interests rather than direct evidence of scientific progress. Despite strong contributions from a limited number of countries and core research groups, increasing interdisciplinary and international collaboration is evident. Overall, these results underscore the growing value of CMTs as a naturally occurring translational model for human breast cancer and provide a reference framework for future integrative and comparative studies.

Methods

Data collection

Web of Science (WoS) is one of the most authoritative and widely used databases for retrieving high-quality literature across biomedical and life sciences, providing comprehensive citation data from globally recognized publishers. To ensure data reliability, representativeness, and accessibility, the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) was selected as the primary data source for this study.

Relevant publications on CMTs were retrieved from WoSCC without restrictions on language or document type. The search strategy was conducted using topic terms related to canine mammary neoplasms, including: Topic = (“canine mammary tumor*” OR “canine mammary carcinoma*” OR “canine mammary neoplasm*” OR “dog mammary tumor*” OR “canine breast cancer”). Preliminary searches using broader terms (e.g., “bitches”, “canine neoplasia”, “mammary gland tumor”) showed over 80% overlap with the final dataset and were therefore excluded to improve precision. The search was limited to publications indexed in the Science Citation Index Expanded (SCI-EXPANDED) from 2009 to 2025. Only original articles and review papers were included. After retrieval, records were screened for relevance. Studies not directly related to CMTs were excluded based on title and abstract review. In addition, meeting abstracts, conference proceedings, editorial materials, and other non–peer-reviewed document types were removed. Duplicate records were also excluded where applicable. After this screening process, a total of 676 publications were retained for subsequent bibliometric analysis. All literature retrieval was performed on December 17, 2025, and the dataset was fixed on the same day to avoid bias introduced by ongoing database updates.

To avoid potential bias caused by database updates, all records were collected on a single day. The retrieved records were exported as full records with cited references in plain text format and subsequently imported into CiteSpace and VOSviewer for further bibliometric and visual analyses.

The dataset comprised multiple document types, with original research articles and review papers accounting for the majority of publications, while other document types included meeting abstracts, editorial materials, proceedings papers, letters, book chapters, and early access articles. Key bibliometric indicators, including total citations, citing articles, h-index, and average citations per item, were also obtained to evaluate the overall academic impact of the retrieved literature.

Data analysis

CiteSpace (version 6.3.1) and VOSviewer (version 1.6.20) were employed to perform bibliometric and visual analyses of the selected publications. CiteSpace, developed by Professor Chaomei Chen at Drexel University, United States, is a widely used tool for bibliometric analysis and knowledge mapping, particularly suitable for identifying knowledge structures, research hotspots, and emerging trends within a research field. CiteSpace analysis was performed with the following parameters applied consistently across all visualizations (country collaboration, institution, author, keyword, reference, and journal maps): time slicing = 1 year per slice; LRF = 2.5, L/N = 10, LBY = 5, e = 1.0. The g-index threshold was deliberately set to k < 25 to ensure that network sizes remained below 300 nodes in every map for optimal readability and clarity. Resulting networks varied by analysis type, with node counts ranging from approximately 50 to 272. All generated networks achieved Modularity Q > 0.3 and Weighted Mean Silhouette S > 0.5, confirming robust community structure and high clustering quality. Only 1.0% of nodes were labeled in each map. This configuration balanced structural detail with visual interpretability while maintaining consistency across all figures.

VOSviewer, a Java-based bibliometric visualization software developed by Van Eck and Waltman at the Centre for Science and Technology Studies (CWTS), Leiden University, Netherlands, was used to construct and visualize large-scale bibliometric networks, including co-authorship, keyword co-occurrence, and co-citation networks, owing to its strong graphical and clustering capabilities. VOSviewer analysis (version 1.6.20) was conducted using default clustering resolution (1.0) and minimum occurrence thresholds (keywords = 5; authors/institutions = 2) to construct co-authorship, co-citation, and keyword co-occurrence maps.The combined application of CiteSpace and VOSviewer enabled a comprehensive analysis of the intellectual structure, research hotspots, and developmental trends in CMTs research.

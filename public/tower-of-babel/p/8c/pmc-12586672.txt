Unsteady two dimensional flow of non-newtönian fractional Casson fluid for an edge with heated boundaries
Authors: Sohail Nadeem, Kanwal Hafeez, Jehad Alzabut, A M Zidan, B Zigta
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study explores the transient flow and thermal behavior of incompressible non-Newtonian fluids, with a particular emphasis on Casson and fractional Casson models, which are widely applied in blood flow, lubricants, and polymer processing. The Casson model, characterized by shear-thinning and yield stress effects, is extended using fractional derivatives to capture memory-dependent dynamics and complex non-Newtonian responses. Advanced mathematical techniques, including Fourier and Fourier sine transforms, are employed to derive exact analytical solutions of the governing equations under oscillatory boundary conditions, while accounting for the influence of magnetic fields, porous media, and heat transfer. Mathematica is used to obtain solutions and generate graphical representations. Instead of listing individual parameter effects, the study highlights the collective role of fractional-order terms, oscillatory forcing, magnetic interactions, and thermal gradients in shaping both velocity and temperature fields. The results provide general insights into how fractional dynamics and transport properties influence system stability, oscillatory behavior, and flow persistence. In conclusion, the work establishes a unified framework for analyzing fractional Casson fluid flows and demonstrates the value of fractional calculus in modeling memory effects in non-Newtonian systems. The findings are expected to support industrial and biomedical applications and suggest future directions including three-dimensional extensions, relaxation of periodic boundary conditions, and direct engineering applications.Keywords: Casson fluid, Fractional derivatives, Magnetic field, Porous medium, Heat transfer, General periodic oscillations, Dirac delta function, Fourier transform, Fourier sine transform, Exact solutionsSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

Casson fluid, a type of non-Newtönian fluid, exhibits unique rheological behavior that deviates from the classical Newtönian fluids. In these fluids, the relationship between the shear stress and the shear rate is nonlinear, making them important for the study of complex flow behaviors in various practical applications, particularly in medical and industrial fields. In mathematics, the study of Casson fluids involves understanding the mathematical models governing their flow, which typically requires the use of non-Newtönian fluid dynamics equations and mathematical tools such as differential equations, boundary layer theory, and asymptotic analysis.

Several key applications of Casson fluids in mathematics are found in the modeling of blood flow, polymeric materials, and the behavior of various biological and industrial processes. These models are essential in understanding the flow properties of such fluids under different conditions, which is vital in industries like food processing, pharmaceuticals, and biotechnology. Mathematical studies have developed models to describe the flow of Casson fluids in pipes, channels, and porous media, as well as the heat transfer processes that occur in these fluids.

Between 2014 and 2024, significant advancements have been made in the mathematical modeling of Casson fluids. The work of Smith et al.1, focused on the boundary layer analysis for Casson fluid flow in a porous medium. In the same year, Johnson and Lee2, studied the impacts of variable viscosity in Casson fluid flow in non-Newtonian channels. Patel and Kumar3, explored the magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) boundary layer flow of Casson fluids in the presence of a porous medium. Significant work by Hussanan, A. et al.4, introduced solution for the heat transfer in magnetohydrodynamics flow of a casson fluid with porous medium and newtonian heating. A major contribution by White and Smit5, involved numerical simulations of Casson fluid flow under varying temperature gradients in pipes.

The research by Singh et al.6, examined the Casson fluid flow in rotating systems, using asymptotic analysis to capture the effects of rotation on the flow characteristics. Gupta and Shah7, made notable advancements in the study of Casson fluid dynamics in bioheat transfer problems, particularly in the modeling of human blood flow under external magnetic fields. Brown and Co8., proposed a new mathematical framework for Casson fluid models using advanced computational techniques to address industrial processing issues. The paper by Clark et al.9, examined the role of wall slip in Casson fluid flows, highlighting how this phenomenon affects the rheological properties. Patel and Sharma10, explored the effects of nanoparticles on heat and mass transfer in Casson fluid flows, incorporating a non-Fourier heat conduction model for the analysis. Looking forward, the future direction in the study of Casson fluids lies in the development of multi-scale models for complex industrial and biomedical systems, especially involving turbulent flows and non-Newtönian fluids in microfluidic devices.

Fractional calculus is a generalization of traditional calculus, extending the concept of derivatives and integrals to non-integer orders. While ordinary derivatives deal with integer powers of differentiation, fractional derivatives involve fractional or real-order derivatives, making them useful in describing more complex, memory-dependent phenomena that can’t be captured using integer-order calculus alone. Fractional derivatives allow for more precise models in various fields, especially in physical systems with long-term memory or hereditary properties, and they have gained attention for their ability to describe anomalous diffusion, viscoelasticity, and fractal dynamics, among others.

One significant application of fractional derivatives in mathematics is in the modeling of anomalous diffusion processes, where the rate of change is not constant but rather varies over time. The work of Caputo and Fabrizio11, presented a novel approach for the fractional derivative, which provides more accurate models in fractional order differential equations. Diethelm and Ford12, introduced a numerical method for solving fractional differential equations, improving the computational efficiency in applications such as signal processing. Podlubny’s13, research on fractional-order systems was extended, showing that fractional derivatives could be applied to control systems with memory effects. Kiryakova et al.14, studied fractional partial differential equations in fluid dynamics, while Zhang et al.15, explored fractional derivatives in image processing to enhance edge detection and contrast adjustment.

Continuing Li et al.16, proposed a fractional model for neural networks, incorporating fractional derivatives to describe the learning processes. Kim and Lee17, advanced the use of fractional calculus in geophysical models, particularly in modeling subsurface flow and diffusion in porous media. Swartz et al.18, applied fractional calculus to the analysis of material properties and structural mechanics, demonstrating its relevance in the modeling of complex materials. Meanwhile, Jafari and Rehman19, presented an extension of fractional calculus to optimal control theory, showing how fractional derivatives can improve control in dynamic systems with memory. Moving forward, research by Zhang et al. and Chen et al.20, focuses on expanding the theoretical framework of fractional differential equations, particularly in the context of nonlocal models. These advancements highlight the ongoing need for further exploration and development in fractional calculus, particularly in fields like modeling complex systems, computational mathematics, and engineering.

In conclusion, while significant progress has been made in the past decade, the mathematical community must continue to refine and extend the applications of fractional calculus in various scientific fields. This includes developing more efficient numerical methods, expanding the scope of fractional models in new physical and engineering systems, and addressing the theoretical foundations of fractional derivatives for broader use in applied mathematics.

These models are essential in understanding natural phenomena such as groundwater movement, oil extraction, and heat conduction in materials. Work by Zhang and et al.25, contributed to the development of computational techniques for simulating porous media processes. The works of Fischer and et al.28 contributed to integrating porous media models with artificial intelligence. Research by Martin and et al.29, explored new methods for predictive modeling of thermal transport in porous media. Lastly, Smith and et al.30, aimed at enhancing real-time simulations for industrial applications using improved porous media models. Future research should aim to integrate these advancements further, improving computational methods and expanding into new areas of application.

Magnetic fields are fundamental in mathematics, particularly in the study of vector fields and differential equations, due to their ability to model physical phenomena like electromagnetic forces and fluid dynamics. The applications of magnetic fields in mathematics extend to solving Maxwell’s equations, optimizing designs for electrical machines, and simulating fluid flow in porous media. For example, studies by Rao et al.31, highlighted the interaction between magnetic fields and porous media flow, emphasizing the need for efficient computational models. Zhang and Li32, explored how magnetic fields affect heat transfer in porous media, suggesting advanced mathematical models for better performance in thermal systems. Research by Patel et al.33, delved into the implications of magnetic fields on the stability of porous materials under stress, calling for improved mathematical simulations. Moving forward, Sharma et al.35, emphasized the optimization of magnetic field models in porous media for better energy harvesting.

The boundary conditions prescribed in this study carry important physical interpretations, as they dictate how the fluid interacts with its surroundings and strongly influence the resulting solution of the governing equations: we have velocity boundary condition at the oscillating surface, thermal boundary condition at the wall, far-field velocity condition, and far-field thermal condition. The oscillatory velocity boundary introduces temporal variations into the momentum equation, making the flow inherently unsteady and periodic. The isothermal wall condition establishes a steady driving mechanism for heat transfer, allowing the analysis of thermal response without additional complexities of variable wall heating. The far-field velocity and thermal conditions enforce physical decay of disturbances, ensuring that both velocity and temperature fields remain stable and converge to quiescent/ambient states away from the oscillating surface. Collectively, these boundary conditions ensure that the governing equations produce solutions that reflect a realistic physical system: an unsteady, magneto-porous Casson fluid driven by boundary oscillations, with heat transfer confined between the wall and the ambient medium. Continued work is needed to explore the integration of magnetic fields with other physical forces in porous materials for more efficient systems in the coming years.

The study considers a fractional Casson fluid, whose momentum equation is based on the porous medium and magnetic field but does not involve any body forces or external forces. The temperature response of the fluid is described by the energy equation, which in the assumption of no internal heat generation or sources in the fluid is derived. This simplifies the analysis by eliminating the added complication of heat generation. Constant temperature boundary conditions are specified to the thermal field, i.e., the temperature on the boundaries of the domain is constant throughout the flow process. The flow is unsteady, i.e., the velocity and temperature fields are functions of time. This adds temporal variations to the governing equations, which characterize the dynamic nature of the system. Periodic oscillations of the edge are assumed to be the source of the flow, and the flow is generated by the oscillations. The effects of such oscillations add a time-dependent variation in the fluid motion. The effects of gravity are not taken in the analysis, i.e., effects of buoyancy due to density variations are assumed to be zero for the system under consideration. The momentum equation is especially impacted by the fluid-porous medium interaction and magnetic field effects. The assumption is employed to focus the model on porous structure and magnetic field transport phenomena. The governing equations are expressed in the context of unsteady-state conditions, as appropriate to the time-dependent character of the system’s velocity and thermal fields. The analysis disregards any internal energy sources or heat generation, simplifying the thermal model to one where heat transfer is solely influenced by the temperature gradient and the fluid’s motion. The periodic nature of the edge oscillations induces a time-varying boundary condition, which must be considered in both the momentum and energy equations to ensure accurate predictions of fluid behavior over time. Conclusion

This investigation offers a comprehensive study of the transient flow and thermal behavior of a fractional Casson fluid under the influence of periodic oscillations at a surface, incorporating fractional derivatives into the constitutive equations to capture the fluid’s unique characteristics. The inclusion of fractional parameters, magnetic effects, porous medium properties, and heat transfer has offered a comprehensive understanding of the intricate dynamics of non-Newtönian fluid flows. Using Fourier and Fourier sine transforms, an exact analytical solution was achieved, highlighting significant advancements in modeling and solving complex fluid dynamics problems. The results provide deeper insights into the interplay between key parameters and the system’s response, offering potential applications in industrial and biomedical fields where non-Newtönian fluids are prevalent. This research provides a strong framework for unsteady flow dynamics analysis of fractional Casson fluids and provides useful information for practical optimization of complicated non-Newtönian fluid systems. This study is based on several simplifying assumptions that restrict its generality. In particular, effects of buoyancy, gravitational forces, and internal heat generation have been neglected, while the flow has been modeled in a two-dimensional rectangular domain under constant temperature and periodic oscillatory boundary conditions. The magnetic field is also considered uniform and externally applied, without accounting for induced variations. These assumptions make the analysis mathematically tractable but limit its direct applicability to more complex or realistic systems.

Future work

Future research could therefore extend the model by incorporating buoyancy-driven convection, internal heat sources, or radiative effects, and by exploring three-dimensional or curved geometries. Further investigations into non-uniform magnetic fields, variable boundary conditions, or non-periodic excitations would provide a more comprehensive understanding of fractional Casson fluid flows in porous and magnetized environments.

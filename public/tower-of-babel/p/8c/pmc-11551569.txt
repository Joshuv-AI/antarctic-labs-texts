Marijuana Smoking and Dental Caries: A Letter
Authors: Seyed Ehsan Mousavi, Reyhaneh Hamedi
Published: 2024

To the Editor:

I am writing to express a significant methodological concern regarding the recently published article titled ‘The Effect of Marijuana-Smoking on Dental Caries Experience’.1

The study's categorisation of cannabis exposure is overly simplistic and potentially misleading. Participants were grouped into nonsmokers, previous smokers, and current smokers, without adequately defining key variables. Specifically, the study did not clarify how long it had been since ‘previous smokers’ last used cannabis, nor did it account for ‘joint-years’ of cannabis smoking—a crucial factor in assessing the impact of cannabis use on dental caries. Additionally, the study failed to incorporate the age of initiation and cessation of cannabis smoking.

This classification approach could result in grouping participants with vastly different exposure levels together. For example, individuals who smoked cannabis daily for 30 years might be classified in the current smoker category as those who smoked monthly for just one year. Such a broad categorisation overlooks the cumulative effect and duration of cannabis use, both of which are essential for understanding the relationship between cannabis use and dental caries.

A more accurate classification should consider the estimated joint-year of cannabis use, both over a lifetime and within recent years (eg, the past 5 years), as well as the age of initiation and duration of cannabis usage. This can be achieved using available NHANES variables, such as ‘Age started regularly using marijuana’, ‘Time since last used marijuana regularly’, ‘Time since used marijuana regularly/unit’, ‘How often would you use marijuana?’, ‘How many joints or pipes smoked in a day?’.2

By excluding these critical factors, the study risks overlooking subtle patterns of cannabis use, leading to potential misclassification of exposure. This misclassification could, in turn, affect the study's conclusions regarding the relationship between cannabis use and dental caries.

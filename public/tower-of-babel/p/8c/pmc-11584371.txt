Use of strain imaging to detect subtle myocardial involvement in post COVID-19 patients: An Indian perspective
Authors: Deepak Tomar, Aditya Kapoor, Zia Hashim, Kamlesh Raut, Arpita Katheria, Harshit Khare, Ankit Sahu, Roopali Khanna, Sudeep Kumar, Naveen Garg, Satyendra Tewari
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe study assessed Global longitudinal strain imaging (GLS) to detect subtle myocardial dysfunction among patients clinically recovered from COVID-19.MethodsAll patients (n = 101 76 % males, mean age 55.45 ± 11.14 years), and controls (n = 30), underwent clinical assessment and echocardiography, including GLS assessment.ResultsThe prevalence of diabetes mellitus, hypertension and dyslipidemia was comparable amongst patients and controls. The average GLS was significantly lesser in post COVID patients (−16.21 ± 1.96 vs −18.49 ± 1.64 respectively, p = 0.004) and significantly higher proportion of post COVID patients had GLS > -18 % (43 % vs 22.58 % respectively, p = 0.001) as compared to controls. The RV free wall longitudinal strain (RVFLS) was also lower in the patient group (22.35 ± 4.69 vs 24.19 ± 4.11, p = 0.004) and 21.7 % post COVID-19 patients had pathological RV FWLS (> −20 %) vs controls (6.6 %).Average GLS was significantly lesser in severe post COVID patients (viz −14.25 ± 1.92 vs −16.63 ± 1.61 vs −17.63 ± 1.91, p < 0.0001, respectively among severe, moderate and mild COVID-19 patients. On performing regression analysis, severity of COVID-19 (OR 7.762) was a significant predictor of impaired GLS.ConclusionDespite normal global LVEF, post COVID-19 recovered patients had significantly lower LV GLS and RV FWLS with severe COVID-19 infection, regardless of having a clinical recovery. This study reiterates the importance of speckle tracking echocardiography as an important imaging modality for detection of subclinical myocardial dysfunction in the post COVID-19 recovered patients.

Introduction

The Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic was a major cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide and previous studies have shown that underlying myocardial involvement was noted in upto one-third of patients with COVID- 19.1, 2, 3, 4, 5 The myocardial injury usually occurs during the acute phase of COVID-19 and depending on the severity of the initial episode of myocarditis, may resolve spontaneously or cause persistent myocardial fibrosis and residual myocardial abnormalities with unfavorable long-term sequelae.

Unrecognized myocarditis is known to be an important substrate for adverse cardiac outcomes.6, 7, 8, 9 Some patients recovering from COVID-19 may develop a new-onset cardiomyopathy during the convalescent phase and timely detection of subclinical cardiovascular abnormalities is especially pertinent to patients recovering from COVID-19.5

Following clinical recovery from COVID-19, few studies have used cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) to document persistent cardiovascular involvement in the convalescent phase of COVID-19.10, 11, 12 However CMR imaging has logistic challenges like the issue of cost, lack of widespread availability and longer imaging times.

Hence utilizing echocardiographic strain imaging has emerged as an attractive tool for diagnosing subclinical myocardial dysfunction amongst patients who have recovered from COVID-19 infection.13, 14, 15, 16, 17 However most previous studies have assessed primarily LV strain parameters and data on bi-ventricular strain analysis are limited.16,17 The present study assessed the prevalence of residual LV and RV dysfunction among patients clinically recovered from COVID-19 using echocardiographic strain imaging.

Methods

A total 197 patients, at least 3 months post COVID-19 infection and were clinically asymptomatic were screened from the period between June 2020–August 2022 and included in the study if they met all the following inclusion criteria- (1) age between 20 and 85 years, (2) previous COVID-19 with positive reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction for SARS-CoV-2, (3) no previous documented cardiac illness like heart failure, cardiomyopathy, known left ventricular dysfunction (those with hypertension could be included) and (4) willing to participate in the study. Patients with known cardiomyopathy (n = 25), uncontrolled diabetes mellitus [(HbA1c ≥ 9) (n = 13), uncontrolled hypertension [(stage 2 or more) (n = 15), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (n = 9), age malignancy with life expectancy <6 months (n = 1), liver or kidney dysfunction unrelated to COVID-19 (n = 14), and poor echo window (n = 19) were excluded. Post exclusion a total of 101 patients were recruited after obtaining informed consent. The study was approved by the Institutional ethics committee IEC no. 2022-184-DM-129 and written informed consent was obtained from each patient prior to enrolment. Age and gender matched controls (n = 30) who were free from any major systemic illness were also studied. Detailed history and demographic data were recorded and other biochemical investigations, cardiac biomarkers (Tn, CKMB and NT-pro BNP) estimated with echocardiography performed on the same day as that of blood sampling.

Echocardiography protocol: Echocardiography was performed using a GE Vivid E9 with XD Clear ECHO machine (GE Healthcare, Waukesha, WI). Conventional echo-Doppler parameters were measured according to the American Society of Echocardiography guidelines.18,20 Left ventricular (LV) ejection fraction (EF) was calculated from the apical four- and two-chamber views using the Simpson's biplane method. Routine parameters of diastolic function were also assessed and diastolic dysfunction defined by the guidelines of American Society of Echocardiography and the European Association of Cardiovascular Imaging.18,20

Speckle-tracking echocardiography was used for estimating LV global longitudinal strain (GLS). Images in three apical views (apical two-, three- and four-chamber views) were obtained (EchoPAC PC, version 202, GE Medical). The average of peak systolic longitudinal strain of all myocardial segments in the three views averaged by AFI (automated function imaging) application and demonstrated in 17-segment polar plot (Bull's eye plot) was used for analysis.19 A value of LV GLS of more than 18 % is taken as normal.20

RV function assessment: Tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (TAPSE) was measured on M-mode echocardiography. RV fractional area change (RVFAC) was calculated as (RV end-diastolic area –RV end-systolic area)/end-diastolic area × 100 %. Right ventricular free wall longitudinal strain (RV FWLS) was obtained from the standard two-dimensional gray-scale image of the RV focused apical four-chamber view with a frame rate of 50∼70 frames/s. RV FWLS was calculated as the average of the basal, mid and apical RV free wall segments and a value > than −20 % was defined as pathological.21,22

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were obtained for all study variables. Categorical variables were expressed as numbers (percentages), normally distributed continuous data as the mean with standard deviation and non-normally distributed continuous data as the median with interquartile range (IQR). Continuous variables between groups were analysed by the student's t test or Mann–Whitney U test according to their distribution. For paired comparisons, paired t tests or paired-sample Wilcoxon rank tests were performed for continuous variables. All analyses were performed using SPSS version 25.0 software (SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). P value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Although the overall mean LVEF was in the normal range in both groups, global LVEF was lower in post COVID patients (55.36 ± 5.53 vs 57.84 ± 2.97, p 0.032). The prevalence of LVEF <50 % was 17.8 % vs 0 % in the post COVID-19 patients vs controls while the corresponding figures for LVEF < 40 % were 3.96 % and 0 % respectively. The prevalence of diastolic dysfunction was not significantly different among the two groups.

Analysis of strain parameters (Table 2): The global longitudinal strain in 2C view (−16.14 ± 2.16 vs −18.69 ± 1.75 respectively, p = 0.0004), APLAX view (−16.29 ± 2.12 vs −19.41 ± 1.87 respectively, p = 0.009) and 4C view (−16.23 ± 1.92 vs −18.36 ± 1.61 respectively, p = 0.003) was significantly lower amongst post COVID patients as compared to controls. The average global longitudinal strain was also significantly lesser (−16.21 ± 1.96 vs −18.49 ± 1.64 respectively, p = 0.004). The prevalence of patients with impaired GLS was (43 % vs 22.58 % respectively, p = 0.001) as compared to controls.

Although no significant differences in conventional RV echocardiographic parameters weer observed between the two groups, the RV FWLS was lower in post COVID patients (22.35 ± 4.69 vs 24.19 ± 4.11, p = 0.004). Higher percentage of patients (21.7 %) had pathological RV FWLS (> −20 %) compared to controls (6.6 %). Significantly higher proportion of post COVID patients had reduced TAPSE < 17 (18.8 % vs 6.68 %, p = 0.001).

Symptomatic patients were classified into mild [n = 61 (60.1 %)], moderate [n = 19 (18.2 %)] or severe [n = 21(20.7 %)] illness as per the AIIMS/ICMR-COVID-19 National Task Force severity criteria.23 None of the conventional echocardiographic parameters were significantly different amongst the three groups of post COVID-19 patients, except for a trend of lower EF in those with severe COVID-19 as compared to the other two groups. Significantly higher % of patients post severe COVID-19 had LVEF < 50 % (42.85 % vs 15.7 % and 9.8 %) than those with moderate or mild COVID-19. The prevalence of diastolic dysfunction was similar amongst the three groups.

The global longitudinal strain in 2C view was lowest in patients who have had severe COVID-19 as compared to those with moderate or mild severity (−14.02 ± 2.05 vs −16.96 ± 1.81 vs −18.61 ± 1.81, respectively, p < 0.0001). Discussion

In this study of 101 patients who were at least 3 months post COVID-19 infection (median 8 months) and were clinically asymptomatic, we assessed the presence of subtle LV and RV function abnormalities using speckle tracking echocardiography. As expected, although post COVID patients had higher inflammatory parameters (BNP, CRP, D-dimer, ferritin), and Trop I levels at the time of acute infection, by 3 months, both groups had similar values indicating clinical and biochemical recovery.

Conventional echocardiographic parameters (including LV dimensions, LVSV, diastolic E/A, E/E′ and diastolic dysfunction) were not significantly different amongst the two groups while global LVEF, although within normal range, was slightly lower in post COVID patients (55.36 ± 5.53 vs 57.84 ± 2.97, p 0.032). The presence of overall LV systolic dysfunction with LVEF < 50 % was also higher amongst post COVID-19 patients (17.8 % vs 6.6 %). Lower LVEF was also noted in the study of post COVID-19 patients by De et al15 (LVEF 63.2 ± 2.2 vs. 61.9 ± 4.6, p = 0.007).

Analysis of strain parameters revealed that despite overall normal global LVEF, the average global longitudinal strain was significantly lesser in post COVID patients (−16.21 ± 1.96 vs −18.49 ± 1.64 respectively, p = 0.004) and 43 % of these patients had reduced GLS as compared to 22.58 % of controls (p = 0.04). The GLS impairment was most prominent in the basal-septal and basal-lateral regions of the LV. These values of LV GLS correspond to the ones reported previously (− 19.9 ± 2.6 % vs. −17.6 ± 3.4 %, patients recruited at median 3 months after COVID-19,15 and −19.7 ± 4.6 vs −19.2 ± 1.5, patients recruited at 30–45 days after COVID-1916) and confirm the observation that GLS impairment can be seen even with overall preserved LVEF.

The prevalence of impaired GLS reported by us (43 %) compares with 18.4%–29.9 % reported in previous studies although other studies have reported contrasting results.15,16,24 Caiado reported that among patients recovered from COVID-19 (median duration 130 days), although the overall GLS was not reduced, the basal segmental longitudinal strain was lower, as has also been reported by us.13 In contrast, Gao et al reported that in COVID-19 survivors late, late after recovery (median duration 327 days), there was no significant difference in any of the echocardiographic parameters, including GLS.14

Data on RV function after COVID-19 recovery is sparse. We observed that post COVID-19 patients had significantly reduced RV free wall LS (−22.35 ± 4.69 vs −24.19 ± 4.11, p = 0.004) and 21 % had RVLS > −20 % as compared to 6 % in controls. In a previous study by Mahajan et al, 10.5 % patients had impaired RVLS; the differences in prevalence of impaired RVLS could be due to the heterogeneous patient populations and the fact that only 7.5 % in the study by Mahajan et al had severe COVID-19 as compared to 21 % severe COVID-19 patients in our study.16

Analysis of echocardiographic parameters according to severity of COVID-19 illness demonstrated that patients with severe COVID-19 had lower EF at 3 months (53.19 ± 6.48) as compared to mild/moderate COVID-19patients (55.78 ± 4.92, 56.06 ± 4.86, p 0.03, respectively). Interestingly, even at 3 months after clinical recovery, 42.85 % of those who had severe COVID-19, had persistent LV dysfunction (EF < 50 %) as compared to 15.7 % and 9.8 % in those with moderate or mild COVID-19.

The reduction of GLS correlated well with the severity of the initial COVID-19 illness, and the average GLS was significantly lesser in severe post COVID patients (−14.25 ± 1.92 vs −16.05 ± 1.57 vs −17.63 ± 1.91, respectively, p < 0.0001). Reduced GLS was noted in all patients with severe COVID-19 vs 47.31 % and 21.32 % in those with moderate or mild COVID-19. This is in sync with what has been reported by previous studies.15,16 Although patients with severe COVID-19 infection had a more residual LV dysfunction, these findings emphasize that even individuals with mild to moderate COVID-19 with preserved LVEF can experience residual subclinical LV dysfunction during the recovery phase, as assessed by LVGLS.

The free wall RVLS was also significantly lower in severe post COVID patients (−22.71 ± 3.1vs −23.89 ± 3.31 vs −25.06 ± 4.74, p = 0.028) and higher proportion of severe post COVID patients had reduced RVFLS > -20 % (33.3 % vs 15.71 % vs 19.62 %, respectively, p < 0.04). Similar to this, Ikram et also reported that both mean LV GLS and RVGLS were significantly more decreased in patients with severe chest CT lesions as compared to those with mild lesions on CT.25

Various different mechanisms have been postulated to explain the myocardial injury following COVID-19 infection, with mediation via angiotensin converting enzyme-2 receptors that are predominantly located in the alveolar and myocardial tissue.26 There may be direct invasion of the myocardium by the virus, or indirect effects due to thrombo-inflammation, vasculitis, oxidative stress, tissue hypoxia leading to hemodynamic instability.2,3,27,28 We found that those with severe COVID-19 infection who had the highest levels of biomarkers in the acute phase, had the maximum reduction in LVGLS post recovery from COVID-19. Higher biomarkers (BNP, serum ferritin and CRP) and presence of co-morbidities (diabetes mellitus and hypertension) were significant predictors of reduced GLS. More importantly, severity of COVID-19 (OR 7.762) was also a predictor of reduced GLS, independent of other factors.

Limitations

This single-center study had a small sample size and only included those patients who were hospitalized during the initial episode of COVID-19. It may not have captured the true prevalence of residual abnormalities in a non-hospitalized population of COVID-19 recovered individuals. Since baseline data was not available it is not possible to determine if the echocardiography abnormalities found during the study were prevalent immediately following COVID-19 or appeared later. Echocardiographic findings were not corroborated with other modalities such as CMRwhich could provide more detailed information about myocardial injury and fibrosis.

Conclusion

Long-term follow-up of the patients following recovery from acute phase of COVID-19 is important since residual ventricular dysfunction is common. Despite normal global LVEF, post COVID-19 patients had significantly lower LV GLS, and 43 % had impaired GLS which was more prominent in the basal-septal and basal-lateral regions of the LV. The RV FWLS was also impaired among these patients and 21.7 % had RV FWLS > -20 %. Those with severe COVID-19, despite having recovered clinically, had the most severe residual impairment of LV GLS and RV FWLS. All patients who had had severe COVID-19 group had reduced LV GLS while reduced RV FWLS > -20 % was seen in 33.3 %.

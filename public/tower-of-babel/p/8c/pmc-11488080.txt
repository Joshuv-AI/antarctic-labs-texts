Changing indications for and trends of keratoplasty in a tertiary comprehensive hospital in northern part of China from 2002 to 2021: a 20-year review
Authors: Li Li, Yan Peng, Lan Lv, Na Li, Honglei Dai, Chao Yan, Tao Jin, Fei Luo, Wenying Wang, Shang Li, Ying Jie, Zhiqiang Pan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate indications and surgical procedures of keratoplasty in a tertiary comprehensive hospital in northern part of China over a 20-year period.MethodsAll patients undergoing keratoplasty between January 1, 2002 to December 31, 2021 in a tertiary comprehensive hospital in northern part of China were retrospectively reviewed. The annual proportion of keratoplasty indications and surgical procedures was recorded and analyzed.ResultsA total of 8266 keratoplasty procedures were included. Leading indications were keratoleukoma (n = 2108, 25.50%), followed by regraft (n = 1453 ,17.58%), corneal endothelial dysfunction (n = 1085, 13.13%), and keratoconus (n = 922,11.15%). A decreasing trend was observed for keratoleukoma (P < 0.01) and an increasing trend for corneal endothelial dysfunction (P < 0.01), regraft (P = 0.012), corneal limbal dermoids (P < 0.01) and congenital corneal opacity (P < 0.01). From 2002 to 2021, the proportion of penetrating keratoplasty (PK) had decreased from 73.95% (2002 to 2006) to 53.49% (2017 to 2021). On contrary, anterior lamellar keratoplasty (ALK) (459–705, 26.05-31.96%, P = 0.034) and endothelial keratoplasty (EK) (0-321, 0.00-14.55%, P < 0.01) became more prevalent from 2002 to 2021. For cases with corneal endothelial dysfunction, EK became the preferred technique (74.72%) in the recent 5 years.ConclusionOver the past 20 years, keratoleukoma and regraft have been the leading indications for keratoplasty. A noticeable shift in surgical preference from PK to DSAEK and ALK is evident, with key indications for keratoplasty including keratoleukoma, regraft, limbal dermoids, endothelial dysfunction, and keratoconus, respectively.Keywords: Keratoplasty, Indications, Surgical procedure, Trends

Introduction

Advances in corneal transplantation have introduced innovative surgical techniques and shifted keratoplasty indications. Full-thickness procedure (penetrating keratoplasty, PK) has declined in favor of partial-thickness techniques, such as endothelial keratoplasty (EK), due to improved outcomes, including lower rejection rates and reduced astigmatism [1, 2]. The adoption of newer surgical techniques has redefined keratoplasty indications [3], with EK replacing PK for corneal endothelial dysfunction in the UK [4], Canada [5], and France [6], and posterior lamellar keratoplasties have surpassed PK in Germany since 2016 [7]. Additionally, deep anterior lamellar keratoplasty (DALK) has become the preferred treatment for keratoconus in certain regions, overtaking PK in Scotland [8].

Keratoplasty indications and procedures vary geographically [9]. In developing countries, microbial keratitis and keratoleukoma are prevalent [10, 11], whereas pseudophakic corneal decompensation (bullous keratopathy, BK), Fuchs endothelial corneal dystrophy and keratoconus are dominate in developed nations [7, 12, 13]. In China, regional trends have been documented. In northern China, Xie et al. reported infectious keratitis as the primary indication for PK at Shandong Eye Institute (1997–2002), with a rising LK-to-PK ratio from 1996 to 2007 [14, 15]. Suppurative keratitis led PK and ALK indications at Qingdao Eye Hospital (2010–2017), followed by keratoconus [16]. In southern China, Chongqing Eye Bank data indicated infectious keratitis (37%) as the top indication, with increasing PK and ALK rates [17]. At the Eye, Ear, Nose and Throat Hospital of Fudan University (2010–2019), infectious keratitis and corneal endothelial dysfunction were predominant, with a shift to lamellar keratoplasties [18]. Nationally, keratoleukoma and bacterial keratitis were the leading indications from 2014 to 2018 [19].

However, data from comprehensive hospitals in China is limited, and recent trends in surgical preferences remain unclear. This study reviews 20 years of medical records from Beijing Tongren Hospital, a comprehensive tertiary hospital in northern China, to assess evolving keratoplasty trends. The findings will enhance understanding of current practices in northern China.

Methods

A retrospective analysis of inpatients who had undergone corneal transplant surgery in the 20-year period from January 1, 2002 to December 31, 2021 was performed at Beijing Tongren Hospital, Capital Medical University, excluding outpatients undergoing emergency surgery. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Beijing Tongren Hospital, Capital Medical University (Ethics Committee approval number: TREC2024-KY108), and the protocol fully adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki and the Association for Research in Vision and Ophthalmology (ARVO) statement on human subjects. Patients’ records and information were anonymized and de-identified before analysis. For better understanding, 20 years are divided into 4 periods, and every 5 years is an interval.

Subjects

All patients undergoing corneal transplantation at Beijing Tongren Hospital inpatient and having fully document were collected and analyzed. Surgical procedures include penetrating keratoplasty (PK), endothelial keratoplasty (EK), anterior lamellar keratoplasty (ALK, comprising deep anterior lamellar keratoplasty (DALK) and anterior or midstromal ALK). Data were collected from electronic health records (EHRs) and laboratory records database at Beijing Tongren hospital. The following clinical data of patients undergoing corneal transplantation were collected and standardized: demographic information like gender, age, and native place, surgical indications (main diagnosis), and surgical procedure. The final diagnosis was ascertained by reviewing the detailed records of EHRs, combined with pathological and microbiologic findings of corneal grafts.

Tissue processing and preservation

Corneal tissues utilized for keratoplasty were predominantly sourced through voluntary donations post-mortem by the local citizens, coordinated by the Organ Procurement Organization (OPO). A minor proportion of these tissues was imported from SightLife in the United States. All corneal tissues were preserved in Beijing Tongren Eye Bank, Beijing, China. The applications for transplantation were approved by the Organ Transplant Ethics Committee.

Diagnosis

Keratoleukoma is defined as a visible corneal opacity attributable to a variety of causes, including trauma, infectious keratitis, and intraocular surgery, excluding active lesions. Corneal endothelial dysfunction is characterized by corneal edema accompanied by low endothelial cell counts, as identified through specular microscopy or in vivo confocal microscopy. Common etiologies of corneal endothelial dysfunction include intraocular surgery and corneal endothelial dystrophy. The diagnosis of keratoconus relies on corneal topography assessments. Criteria for diagnosing regraft encompass instances following either full-thickness or partial-thickness keratoplasty. The terms “corneal melting and ulcers” describe active lesions unresponsive to medical management, progressing to perforation or near-perforation. Corneal dystrophy primarily includes conditions such as corneal patchy dystrophy and corneal granular dystrophy, but explicitly excludes corneal endothelial dystrophy. Others incorporate Terrien marginal degeneration, other corneal degenerative diseases, and the early stages of conditions like corneal burns and traumatic incidents leading to potential corneal perforations.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses for this study used SPSS 26.0 (SPSS, Chicago, IL). The Linear-by-Linear Association test was used to assess whether surgical indications and the proportions of various types of keratoplasty demonstrated a linear trend during the period from 2002 to 2021. P values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographic characteristics

Total 8266 cases of corneal transplants were performed in Beijing Tongren hospital during the 20-years study period. About 5988 (72.45%) cases from northern part of China (including North China, Northeast China, and Northwest China). While 2278 (27.55%) cases were from elsewhere, including 983 (11.89%) cases from East China, 827 (10.00%) cases from Central China, 293 (3.54%) cases from Southwest China, and 175 (2.12%) cases from South China.

Overall, 4899 (59.27%) patients were males, and 3367 (40.73%) patients were females. The mean age of the study population was 35 ± 19.15 years (range: <1 year to 89 years). Of the patients studied, 1,807 (21.86%) were aged 18 years or younger, 2,345 (28.37%) were between 19 and 40 years, 2,334 (28.24%) were between 41 and 60 years, and 1,780 (21.53%) were aged 61 years and above.

In our study, keratoleukoma was the leading indication for corneal transplantation (n = 2108, 25.50%), followed by regraft (n = 1453 ,17.58%), corneal endothelial dysfunction (n = 1085, 13.13%), and keratoconus (n = 922,11.15%) in descending order of proportion (Table 1).

From 2002 to 2021, indication prevalence in each five-year period increased for corneal endothelial dysfunction (11.24-16.32%, P < 0.01), regraft (15.83-18.90%, P = 0.012), corneal limbal dermoids (1.65–8.88%, P < 0.01) and congenital corneal opacity (1.87–8.16%, P < 0.01), and decreased for keratoleukoma (36.66–16.36%, P < 0.01). During this period, the prevalence of surgical indications including keratoconus and corneal dystrophy remained stable (Table 1; Fig. 1A).

Gender and age preferences varied across different indications for keratoplasty in our study. Males constituted the majority of patients undergoing surgery for keratoconus (685 eyes, 74.30%), keratoleukoma (1332 eyes, 63.19%), and regraft (871 eyes, 59.94%) (Fig. 2A). For corneal limbal dermoids and congenital corneal opacity, a significant proportion were under 18 years old (499 eyes, 93.45% and 415 eyes, 100%, respectively) (Fig. 2B). The percentage of patients under 10 years old receiving surgical treatment for corneal limbal dermoids escalated from 50% (2002–2006) to 86% (2017–2021). Patients undergoing keratoplasty for keratoconus were predominantly 40 years old or younger (861 eyes, 93.38%, Fig. 2B). Conversely, those treated for keratoleukoma were typically aged between 19 and 40 years (701 eyes, 33.25%, Fig. 2B), whereas individuals receiving surgery for corneal endothelial dysfunction, corneal melting and ulcers, or regraft were mostly over 40 years old (925 eyes, 85.25%; 543 eyes, 74.69%; 938 eyes, 64.56%, respectively, Fig. 2B).

Gender (A) and age group (B) distributions of patients undergoing corneal transplantation for different indications, trends of changing causes of keratoleukoma (C), corneal endothelial dysfunction (D) and regraft (E) and primary indications for regraft (F) from 2002 to 2021

Our research suggests that trauma and infectious keratitis are significant contributors to keratoleukoma, accounting for 39.14% and 35.91% of cases, respectively. However, their prevalence has declined over consecutive five-year periods (47.99%-31.86%, P = 0.126 and 33.12%-29.64%, P = 0.658, respectively, Fig. 2C). In contrast, other etiologies of keratoleukoma, such as immunological keratopathy and intraocular surgical interventions, have shown an increasing trend in incidence (18.88-38.50%, P < 0.01, Fig. 2C). From 2002 to 2021, intraocular surgery has emerged as the primary cause of corneal endothelial dysfunction (803 eyes, 74.01% of cases), with a notable upward trajectory (67.68- 75.83%, P < 0.01, Fig. 2D). Trauma, while continuing to be a significant cause, has displayed a decreasing frequency over the same period (19.19%-8.33%, P < 0.01, Fig. 2D). Graft rejection was the main reason for regrafting and became increasingly prevalent with time (36.92-51.32%, P < 0.01), while graft conjunctivalization was also a significant cause, but its frequency decreased over time (27.60%-6.00%, P < 0.01, Fig. 2E). The majority of patients with graft conjunctivalization had poor stem cell function due to burns (188eyes, 78.7%). Keratoleukoma (post-trauma) accounted for the highest proportion of primary indications for regrafting (545 eyes, 43.50%), followed by infectious keratitis (355 eyes, 24.43%) and corneal endothelial dysfunction (161 eyes, 11.08%), in descending order of frequency (Fig. 2F).

Corneal transplant procedures

Between 2002 and 2021, varying types of keratoplasty procedures at Beijing Tongren Hospital exhibited distinct trends in surgeons’ preferences (Table 2; Fig. 1B). Of all corneal transplantations performed, 5,179 (62.65%) were full-thickness, while 3,087 (37.35%) were partial-thickness procedures. The partial-thickness procedures consisted of ALK, accounting for 2,481 (30.02%) of the surgeries, and EK, comprising 606 (7.33%) of the procedures.

Corneal transplant procedures from 2002 to 2021

The number of PK surgeries has been declining, dropping from 1303 surgeries, or 73.95%, during the 2002 to 2006 period to 1180 surgeries, or 53.49%, from 2017 to 2021 (P < 0.01). ALK surgeries have shown a rising trend since 2007. Initially, from 2002 to 2006, there were 459 ALK surgeries (26.05% of total). This number increased to 548 surgeries (35.63%) between 2007 and 2011, then to 769 surgeries (27.86%) from 2012 to 2016 and reached 705 surgeries (31.96%) between 2017 and 2021. In addition, EK (0-321, 0.00-14.55%, P < 0.01) became more prevalent from 2002 to 2021, increasing in volume and percentage of corneal transplant procedures distribution (Fig. 1B).

From 2002 to 2021, PK was the leading procedure for keratoleukoma (n = 1868, 76.76%), regraft (n = 1071, 73.71%), and corneal melting and ulcer (n = 376, 51.72%), and remained the dominant procedure for many years (Fig. 3A, B, C). ALK is another important procedure for keratoleukoma (n = 490, 23.24%), regraft (n = 290, 19.96%), and corneal melting and ulcer (n = 351, 48.28%), but after a brief increase between 2007 and 2011, it is on a downward trend (Fig. 3A, B, C). In addition, ALK has been the primary procedure and has been steadily dominant for corneal limbal dermoids between 2002 and 2021 (n = 525, 98.31%), although there may be fewer patients with corneal limbal dermoids treated with PK (n = 9, 1.69%).

Trends of changing procedures for keratoleukoma (A), regraft (B), corneal melting and ulcers (C), keratoconus (D), corneal dystrophy (E), and corneal endothelial dysfunction (F) from 2002 to 2021. PK, penetrating keratoplasty; ALK, anterior lamellar keratoplasty; EK, endothelial keratoplasty

In our clinical practice, DALK presented a progressive rise in application from 2012. Patients receiving DALK due to keratoconus increased in prevalence (1-115,0.52-50.44%, P < 0.01), while the ratio of patients undergoing PK for keratoconus decreased over time (190 − 113, 99.48%-49.56%, P < 0.01) (Fig. 3D). Similarly, for individuals diagnosed with corneal dystrophy, the landscape of surgical interventions is evolving, marked by a gradual reduction in the rate of PK (72 − 67, 90.00%-60.91% P < 0.01) and a concurrent, steady rise in the preference for DALK (0–35, 0.00-31.82% P < 0.01) (Fig. 3E). Likewise, since the introduction of EK in our surgical practice in 2009, there has been an incremental rise in the surgical volume starting in 2012. The prominent indication for EK was corneal endothelial dysfunction, representing 84.65% of all EKs performed. Clinically, there was a rising preference for EK among patients with corneal endothelial dysfunction (0-269, 0.00-74.72%, P < 0.01), simultaneously accompanied by a decline in the proportion of PK for managing this disorder (198 − 91,100.00%-25.28% P < 0.01) (Fig. 3F). Additionally, a portion of EK is designated for graft failure (n = 92, 6%) (Fig. 3B).

Discussion

This study reviewed Electronic Health Records from Beijing Tongren Hospital, focusing on patients receiving corneal transplants between 2002 and 2021. The demographic analysis indicates a predominance of patients from northern part of China, especially male patients, noted particularly in conditions like and keratoleukoma, corneal endothelial dysfunction, keratoconus, and regraft. However, the dataset does not comprehensively cover some emergency cases like acute severe keratitis and corneal perforation. Therefore, our findings are representative of the corneal transplantation characteristics in the ophthalmology department of a comprehensive hospital.

Patients involved in this study underwent keratoplasty for different indications presented a significant age spectrum, centered mainly on the middle-aged and young adults, indicating a trend toward a younger demographic for corneal transplantations. Corneal transplant procedures for congenital corneal opacity and corneal limbal dermoids are chiefly conducted in young children, keratoconus and keratoleukoma main the youth, and conditions like corneal endothelial dysfunction, corneal melting and ulcers and graft failure are more common indications among the middle-aged to older demographic. It was consistent with the age distribution of corneal disease of 3112 patients by histopathological examination of corneal specimens in Henan [20], a province in northern part of China, though our study highlights a higher prevalence of corneal limbal dermoids within the pediatric population. It was also similar to the situation in New Zealand, where average age of patients with keratoconus was 31.8 years [21].

Similar to the survey report on keratoplasty in China from 2014 to 2018 [19], keratoleukoma is the leading indication for keratoplasty, but it is showing a decreasing trend over the 20-year period. The reduction of keratoplasty for infectious keratoleukoma and traumatic keratoleukoma may be related to the improvement of hygiene habits to avoid keratitis, as well as early diagnosis and timely medication treatment to avoid surgical intervention. In addition, some outpatients suffered from severe infectious keratitis may undergo emergency surgery and were not included, which could explain why our result is not the same from other studies in China, where infectious keratitis was the leading indication for keratoplasty [16, 17].

In our study, regraft is the second most common indication for corneal transplantation. Globally, regraft rates are rising [4, 22], likely due to the increase in surgical procedures. Similar to Spain [23], graft rejection is the primary cause in our cases, though graft conjunctivalization is also observed but declining. Notably, a significant number of our regraft cases involve graft opacity, where the exact cause is often unclear due to patients being referred at an advanced disease stage with incomplete preoperative details. As a tertiary referral center, we see a higher proportion of these cases compared to other specialized hospitals. At our center, keratoleukoma, often following infection or trauma and associated with high-risk features like iris adhesions and neovascularization, is the leading regraft indication. This contrasts with the USA, where keratoleukoma was the primary cause from 2017 to 2020 [24], and France, where keratoconus, secondary endothelial dystrophy, and Fuchs’ dystrophy dominated [25]. In eastern China, however, infectious keratitis was the main indication for regrafting between 2008 and 2019 [26], reflecting regional variations influenced by patient demographics and case profiles at our center.

Corneal endothelial dysfunction is a significant and growing indication for corneal transplantation, primarily due to intraocular surgery, with post-surgical endothelial dysfunction on the rise. This mirrors trends in Spain [27] and Southern Poland [28], where bullous keratopathy is the leading indication for keratoplasty. However, it differs from Ireland [29], the USA [30], Canada [31], Germany [7] and France [6], where Fuchs’ endothelial dystrophy is the predominant indication for EK. At our hospital, EK surgeries for post-intraocular surgery endothelial dysfunction are more common, while Fuchs dystrophy cases are fewer. This may result from our approach of delaying surgery in Fuchs patients without corneal edema, unlike earlier interventions typical in developed nations.

Keratoconus continues to be a prominent indication for corneal transplantation, although no statistically significant changes in its overall incidence have been observed. International studies from New Zealand [32] and the United Kingdom [4] report comparable trends, while Italy [33] and the United States [34] show a decline in keratoconus-related transplant rates. Similarly, retrospective data from Eastern China [18](2010–2019) suggest no significant changes in surgery rates for keratoconus. We believe that the stabilization or reduction in transplant rates is likely multifactorial, driven primarily by advancements in alternative therapies such as corneal cross-linking, intrastromal ring segments, and improved contact lens technologies. At our center, the lack of significant changes can be attributed to the referral of more advanced cases from other institutions, where patients may have undergone prior interventions, including cross-linking, yet still progressed to the point of requiring transplantation.

Surgical procedures showed two major changes in the previous two decades: the persistent decrease in PK and dramatic increase in EK and ALK, indicating a shift from full-thickness procedures to partial-thickness procedures in our hospital. The prevalence of PK decreased from 2002 to 2011 and continued to decrease from 2012 to 2021. Since 2010, EK and ALK procedures have steadily grown in volume and proportion in our hospital. These changes are similar to the evolving trends in keratoplasty in Canada [31] from 2002 to 2011 and the 25-year longitudinal trends in indications in New Zealand [35] from 1991 to 2015. However, the advancement of EK and DALK at our hospital has been comparatively delayed. And this trend may account for faster visual recovery and lower rejection after partial-thickness procedures [36]. However, PK remains as a main surgery procedure for more than half patients, especially for keratoleukoma caused by traumatic corneal perforation, keratoleukoma accompanied by anterior synechia, corneal graft failure.

A sharply increase ALK for patients with corneal limbal dermoids should be emphasized. And there is an increasing trend in the number of children under ten years old undergoing ALK for corneal limbal dermoids. In recent years, the two-child policy in China has heightened our commitment to the ocular health of children. Parents’ preference for early surgical intervention, advancements in surgical techniques, and improvements in anesthesiology may collectively contribute to the progression of ALK for pediatric corneal limbal dermoids cases. Literature reports indicate that primary ALK for limbal dermoids yields favorable cosmetic outcomes and, combined with effective amblyopic therapy in pediatric patients, preserves satisfactory visual function [37, 38].

DALK is primarily employed for treating keratoconus and partial-thickness corneal lesions. Since 2011, the use of DALK for keratoconus and corneal dystrophy has increased significantly. This shift from PK to DALK can be attributed to the distinct advantages of DALK. It has demonstrated equivalence to PK in terms of BSCVA (Best Spectacle-Corrected Visual Acuity) outcomes for keratoconus, particularly when minimal host stromal thickness remains post-operatively [39, 40]. DALK also provided comparable visual and optical results as PK in macular corneal dystrophy [41]. Additionally, postoperative studies show that DALK outperforms PK in preserving endothelial cell density [39, 41] and significantly reduces the risk of graft rejection [41, 43]. The elimination of endothelial immune rejection further simplifies long-term management following DALK [39]. Consequently, DALK has become the preferred treatment option for keratoconus with an intact Descemet’s membrane and corneal dystrophy without Descemet’s membrane or endothelial involvement. However, as a comprehensive referral center, we receive patients from other institutions, some of whom, despite prior treatments like cross-linking, still require PK surgery due to advanced keratoconus with scarring or acute edema.

The result analysis revealed a significant increasing trend in EK procedures and decreasing trend in PK procedures for endothelial dysfunction. The increased popularity of EK in northern part of China may be due to its prominent advantages, including equivalent or superior graft survival rates with PK [44, 45], better visual rehabilitation with less astigmatism, reduced loss of endothelial cell density [44], and the ability to make small corneal incisions with no stroma sutures. However, compared to developed countries like Sweden, the United States, Singapore, and the United Kingdom, where the proportion of EK ranges from 41–52% [45], the volume of EK procedures in our hospital necessitates enhancement.

Generally, analysis of trends in corneal transplantation in a tertiary comprehensive hospital in northern part of China from 2002 to 2021 reveals that surgeon’s preference for DSEAK and ALK has increased while the percentage of PK procedure decreased. From 2002 to 2021, the most common indications for keratoplasty were keratoleukoma, followed by regraft. There were increasing trends of regraft, corneal limbal dermoids, endothelial dysfunction, and keratoconus for PK, ALK, DSAEK and DALK procedures, respectively. Further comprehensive analysis is warranted to elucidate more detailed and valuable trends in corneal transplantation.

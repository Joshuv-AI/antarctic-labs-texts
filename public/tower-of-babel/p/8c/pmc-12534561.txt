Research on conveyor belt damage detection method based on FDEP−YOLOv8
Authors: Yuan Yuan, Yichao Bai, Wenjun Meng, Lidong Zhou, Miao Wang, Wenbin Qu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDuring the operation of belt conveyors, damage to the conveyor belt (such as tearing, breaking, and scratching) is a major safety hazard. Aiming at the specific challenges of the existing detection methods, such as the difficulty in distinguishing damage from background texture in complex material scenarios, low efficiency of manual inspection, and limited hardware resources, this paper proposes a conveyor belt damage detection algorithm based on the improved YOLOv8. The main improvements include: using the ‌Focal Modulation module‌ to replace the SPPF structure in the backbone network and enhance the feature expression ability; Introduce the ‌ dynamic upsampling module (Dysample) ‌, focus on sampling the target area, and effectively suppress the interference of similar backgrounds; Embed the efficient multi-scale attention mechanism (EMA) in the neck network to enhance the model’s attention to the injured target; Bounding box regression was performed using the ‌PIoU v2 loss function‌ to optimize the positioning accuracy of irregular damage shapes. The experimental results show that the improved model has achieved a significant performance improvement in the task of conveyor belt damage detection, with an accuracy rate of 90.3% and an average precision mAP of 93.2%. Comparative analysis shows that this method is superior to the original YOLOv8 and traditional detection methods in terms of detection accuracy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-20391-1.Keywords: Focal modulation, DySample, EMA, PIoU v2 loss function, Belt damageSubject terms: Mechanical engineering, Computer science

Introduction

In the context of the rapid advancements witnessed in industrial automation and intelligent manufacturing, conveyor belts, as pivotal components of material handling systems, have become pervasive in various sectors, including mining, metallurgy, the chemical industry, logistics, and numerous others. The reliable performance of these conveyor belts is of paramount importance, not only in determining production efficiency and cost management but also in ensuring the safety of production processes and the overall effectiveness of enterprises. However, under prolonged operation, elevated loads, and variable working conditions, conveyor belts are highly vulnerable to various forms of damage, including wear, tear, scratching, and degradation. The failure to detect and address these issues promptly can have severe consequences, including damage that can result in direct economic losses, including material leakage and equipment shutdown, as well as more severe consequences, such as fires and casualties1–3. Consequently, the development of efficient and accurate conveyor belt damage detection technology is imperative to ensure production safety and enhance the efficiency of equipment maintenance4,5.

Recent advancements in sensor technology, image processing, artificial intelligence6, and other advanced technologies have led to significant developments in the field of conveyor belt damage detection. Traditional damage detection methods, such as leak detection, trigger detection, and force-measuring roller detection, have been superseded by these technological advancements7,8. These conventional methods boast the advantages of simplicity in structure, ease of installation, and uncomplicated operation. However, in complex operating environments, their accuracy is often compromised, and their reliability is questionable. This has led to challenges inmeeting the demands of contemporary industrial production9. Conversely, emerging technologies such as online inspection technology based on machine vision, vibration monitoring technology based on acoustic principles, and intelligent diagnosis systems combined with artificial intelligence algorithms have gradually become research hotspots in the field of conveyor belt damage detection, with the advantages of non-contact, real-time, and high detection accuracy10.

Gongxian Wang11 et al. proposed a novel visual detection method called Auxiliary Classifier Spectrally Normalized GAN (AC-SNGAN), which includes a data augmentation module based on generative adversarial networks to expand the feature database of conveyor belt surface damage samples. Xiaoqiang Guo12 et al. proposed a method based on the CenterNet-based conveyor belt damage detection method, which balances the performance and scale of deep neural networks, and proposes a fusion channel-space attention method to effectively compress the potential feature mAPs. Mengchao Zhang13 et al. proposed a new detection method based on the improved Yolov3 algorithm, which can detect multiple defects simultaneously. EfficientNet is used instead of Darknet53 as the backbone feature extraction network, and network scaling is performed by comprehensively considering the balance between network depth, width, and image resolution to improve the accuracy of the algorithm under limited computational resources. Shen Jingxuan14 et al. proposed an image adaptive visual detection method, IAE-YOLOv5, for the low quality of conveyor belt longitudinal tear images and the serious problem of loss of damaged feature information under a multi-source interference environment. Yu Qing15 et al. proposed a longitudinal tear detection system based on multi-scale feature fusion for the existing conveyor belt tear detection algorithms, which have low accuracy and poor anti-interference ability.

The conveyor belt damage detection method has been a subject of extensive research in recent years, but many existing methods still need improvement. The problems include a weak ability to extract target semantic information, poor detection accuracy, and an inability to accurately identify various damage conditions. Most methods can only identify and detect longitudinal cracks.

Methodology

The improved network structure of the YOLOv8 model

This study explores the potential of converting the detection of scratches, tears, and wear on conveyor belts into image target detection. The YOLOv8 object detection network is a notable example of this transformation. Due to its effectiveness and generalization ability, it has been widely applied in object detection tasks. Firstly, YOLOv8 employs a mature C2f module to achieve cross-stage feature fusion, and its structure has been fully verified in industrial scenarios. Although the C3k2 module introduced by the latest YOLOv11 has been optimized in shallow feature extraction, its novel C2PSA attention mechanism may cause feature overfitting in the conveyor belt scenario with dust interference. Secondly, conveyor belt detection systems are often deployed on edge devices. Although the parameter count of YOLOv8 (about 3.2 M) is close to that of YOLOv11 (about 2.9 M), the newly added DWConv layer of the latter reduces the inference speed by 12% in the actual measurement of ARM architecture chips. Moreover, the ONNX runtime of YOLOv8 has a more stable memory usage, making it suitable for long-term continuous detection. However, practical experience shows that although YOLOv8 has a high detection efficiency, its performance in complex scenarios is not ideal. A neural network for conveyor belt damage detection was constructed based on the YOLOv8 network, and improvements were made to the main trunk and neck parts of the conveyor belt, as well as the loss function. The network structure is shown in Fig. 1.

FDEP-YOLOv8 network structure diagram.

First, for the multi-scale problem of different types of conveyor belt damage and the target detection problem of small damage, the Focal Modulation module is used to replace the original SPPF (spatial pyramid pooling-fast) module of YOLOv8, which achieves the improvement of the focusing and detection ability of the key areas in the image. Second, the lightweight dynamic upsampling module DySample is introduced to focus the sampling points on the target area and ignore the interference of the conveyor belt surface color, which reduces the loss of feature information and improves the detection accuracy of small targets. Third, an Efficient Multi-Scale Attention Module (EMA) is introduced in the neck net. Fourth, a PIoU v2 loss function, which combines a target size adaptive penalty factor and a gradient adjustment function based on the quality of anchor frames, is used in the head network. The PIoU v2 loss function guides the anchor frames to return along the effective paths and thus converges faster than the existing CIoU-based loss function. The PIoU v2 loss function introduces a non-monotonic attention layer in the focusing mechanism, which improves the focusing ability for medium-quality anchor frames.

The proposed FDEP-YOLOv8 model is applied to the task of conveyor belt damage detection. The evaluation of the homemade dataset shows an advantageous detection accuracy compared to the original YOLOv8 model.

Focal modulation module

In this paper, the Fast Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPPF) module in the YOLOv8 backbone network is replaced with the Focal Modulation module17, the network structure of which is shown in Fig. 2. The Focal Modulation module effectively captures long-distance dependencies and context information in an image by first aggregating context information and then interacting with the query token. This process maintains the translation invariance of the model while efficiently capturing space- and channel-specific information. This module has been developed to facilitate the simplification of interaction and aggregation operations, thereby reducing the network’s overall complexity. Consequently, this results in an enhancement of the performance of the damage detection task.

Focal Modulation module structure diagram. (a) Focal-modulation, (b) context aggregation.

EMA module

In this paper, an Efficient Multiscale Attention Module (EMA) is added to the C2f module of the YOLOv8 neck network (see Fig. 3 for details of the network structure). The “Groups” in Fig. 3 indicate the groups that are divided into different input channels, whilst “X Avg Pool” refers to a one-dimensional horizontal global pooling operation and “Y Avg Pool” to a one-dimensional verticalglobal pooling operation. The EMA module involves the initial grouping of inputs, followed by their processing through various branches. The first branch performs 1D horizontal global pooling, the second branch performs 1D vertical global pooling, and the third branch performs feature extraction through a 3 × 3 convolution. The first and second branches execute the pooling operation, subsequently followed by the concat and convolution 1 × 1 operations. In the context of deep learning, the concat operation serves to link multiple tensors in a given dimension, thereby augmenting the number of channels or feature dimensions and enhancing the capture of relationships between disparate features in subsequent layers. The output features of the first and second branches are then modulated by a sigmoid function and a normalization operation18. The global spatial information of the outputs of each branch is efficiently encoded by a two-dimensional global average pooling method, and the corresponding feature dimensions are generated. The linear variations are then fit with SoftMax, followed by cross-dimensional interactions with Matrix dot product operations to capture the pixel-level pairwise relationships. After final sigmoid conditioning, output feature mapping is performed to enhance or attenuate the original input features to obtain the final output. In comparison with conventional attention modules, the EMA module possesses the advantageous capability of enhancing feature representation while concurrently retaining more efficient information from each channel, all the while reducing computational expenditures.

PIoU v2 loss function

PIoU v2 is a loss function applied to Bounding Box Regression (BBR) in target detection tasks. The purpose of the proposed method is to solve the problem of anchor box expansion that may be caused by the existing Intersection over Union (IoU) loss function in the anchor box regression process. It is hypothesized that PIoU v2 will provide a faster convergence speed. The value of the CIoU loss function may be substantial when the overlap area between the predicted box and the real box is minimal due to the varied types and dimensions of conveyor belt damage. This can result in the model placing excessive emphasis on reducing the overlap area during the training process, thereby overlooking potentially more significant factors. The PIoU v2 loss function was employed, and the target size adaptive penalty factor was integrated to enhance the accuracy of damage location. Concurrently, a gradient adjustment function based on the quality of the anchor box is introduced, which can dynamically adjust the magnitude and direction of the gradient according to the quality difference (such as the IoU value) between the anchor box and the target box. This enables the anchor box to follow a more effective path and better adapt to different shapes of damage. In complex environments, the PIoU v2 loss function demonstrates enhanced robustness and stability, with its core being the focal mechanism. Experiments

Dataset construction

The present study examined the efficacy of industrial cameras in detecting damage to conveyor belts. A total of 498 images were captured, encompassing various scenarios of damage detection. Of these, 177 were found to be broken, 173 were torn, 58 were deep scratches, and 90 were minor scratches. The substantial volume of data generated after shooting not only occupies a significant amount of storage space but also has the potential to impede theefficiency of model training. Consequently, the images are systematically compressed into 840 × 840 pixel images post-capture, facilitating efficient computer processing. The experimental photos can be seen in Fig. 5.

Experimental photograph.

The images were manually annotated using the Make Sense online image annotation site. The targets were classified into four categories: Slight Scratch, Deep Scratch, Longitudinal Tear, and Worn. Various techniques were employed in the study to enhance the image quality, including mirror flip left and right, flip, variation of pixel brightness, image scaling, rotation, panning, pretzel noise, and Gaussian noise. As shown in Fig. 6, an image enhancement example of partial conveyor belt damage is given. To simulate the illumination variation present in the actual working environment, the images in the data set were subjected to different brightness conditions post-data enhancement. This encompasses a range of conditions, including bright light, low light, and shadow, thereby ensuring the model’s capacity to sustain reliable performance under conditions of varied lighting. The image background in the data set also presents diversity, including adding noise to simulate changes in the surrounding environment. This change helps the model better adapt to complex scenarios in the real working environment. The Python code is randomly divided according to the ratio of train_ratio = 0.7 (training set is 70% of the total dataset), valid_ratio = 0.15 (validation set is 15% of the total dataset), and test_ratio = 0.15 (test set is 15% of the total dataset). The augmented custom dataset comprises 1,992 images. Of these, 708 images were found to be damaged, 692 were torn, 232 exhibited deep scratches, and 360 displayed minor scratches.

Results

A conveyor belt damage detection method based on an enhancedversion of the YOLOv8 algorithm, designated as FDEP-YOLOv8, has been developed. The SPPF module in the backbone network is modified and substituted with the Focal Modulation module to enhance the model’s capacity for feature extraction. The incorporation of the DySample lightweight dynamic upsampling module ensures the concentration of sampling points in the target area while disregarding the impact of the conveyor belt surface color, thereby enhancing the algorithm’s resilience to interference. The adoption of the EMA module enhances the global visual information of the module, rendering the model adaptable to different sizes of damage target detection. The CIoU loss function is modified to the PIoU v2 loss function, thereby enhancing the network’s capacity to detect minor damage. The experimental findings demonstrate that the enhanced YOLOv8 model exhibits an accuracy of up to 90.3%, along with a mAP@50 of up to 93.2%.

Practical experience has shown that this method significantly enhances the efficiency of conveyor belt damage detection, effectively meeting the dual demands of actual working conditions for detection accuracy and real-time performance, and providing reliable technical support for ensuring the safe production of enterprises. From a theoretical perspective, there is still room for improvement in the recognition sensitivity of current models for discontinuous injuries, and the adaptability of multi-scale feature fusion mechanisms in high-speed moving scenarios needs to be enhanced. At the practical level, there are engineering challenges such as suppressing equipment vibration interference and verifying the compatibility of detection standards. Future research will focus on building a more complete damage sample library, developing environment-adaptive detection algorithms, and exploring lightweight deployment solutions. From a policy perspective, this technology offers a feasible path for implementing the requirements of “replacing people with machinery and reducing people with automation” in the “14th Five-Year Plan for Work Safety”. It can not only be incorporated into the enterprise’s intelligent inspection system but also provide a digital decision-making basis for the safety supervision of special equipment. It has a demonstration significance for promoting the transformation of the industry from passive maintenance to predictive maintenance.

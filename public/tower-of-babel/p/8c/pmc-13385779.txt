Quadrature solution of fractional coupled burgers and plankton-oxygen dynamics under climate change
Authors: Mohammad A. Zayed, Ehab M. Almetwally, Mokhtar Mohamed, Ola Ragb, Mahmoud Fathy Khidr, M. S. Matbuly, Mohamed Salah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This research aims to provide a precise and computationally efficient solution for two important systems in mathematical modeling and environmental science: the fractional nonlinear coupled Burgers’ system and the fractional dynamics of the coupled plankton-oxygen model in (1 + 1) dimensions. Despite the increasing complexity of these systems, existing numerical methods often struggle with the high computational costs of non-local operators. To address this, we propose a robust hybrid framework integrating the Fractional Differential Quadrature Method (FDQM) with a Newton–Raphson (NR) iterative procedure.These systems are critical for understanding various physical processes, including turbulent fluid dynamics and the biological interactions of oxygen and plankton in aquatic environments. The models use fractional derivatives, which provide greater flexibility in capturing memory and hereditary features, making them more suitable for correctly simulating real-world processes than traditional integer-order models. The proposed work solves these problems using a generalized Liouville-Caputo fractional-order model mixed with versions of the differential quadrature technique (DQM), which allows effective handling of complex boundary conditions and spatial derivatives. The nonlinearity in these equations is handled using Newton-Raphson’s iterative approach, which ensures the solutions’ stability and convergence. The proposed system was implemented in MATLAB, and a complete parametric analysis was carried out to investigate how various parameters, including the fractional-order derivative, the oxygen generation rate, and the maximum per capita growth rate of phytoplankton, influence model outputs. This study not only proves the suggested techniques’ accuracy, convergence, and efficiency but also sheds light on their sensitivity to important parameters, making them more applicable to real-world circumstances. The findings of this work are expected to contribute to better modeling methodologies for complex systems, ultimately benefiting academics in domains ranging from environmental science to fluid dynamics.

Introduction

Recently coupled Burgers system1–3 arises in different fields of science such as plasma physics, optical fiber, solid state physics, fluid mechanics, geochemistry, and chemical physics. The lack of oxygen is a dangerous environmental phenomenon, so many researchers are concerned with this problem. Global warming has a firm impact on the water temperature of oceans, and temperature is considered one of the essential dominant variables for the oxygen concentration in the water4. That is why it is accountable for changing the production of elementary materials in watery surroundings5. Any variation in the water’s temperature affects the photosynthetic mechanism of phytoplankton because of its restricted growth as well as photosynthetic rate. Plankton that exists near the top of the oceans are phytoplankton or zooplankton. Phytoplankton and zooplankton dynamics are mainly interested in classifying the problem of prey-predator system interaction in a mathematical model without considering the oxygen concentration5–7. Phytoplankton are photosynthetic organisms that produce the oxygen of oceans on Earth8–12.

Due to the difficulty of studying the analytic solution of coupled Burgers and plankton-oxygen dynamics models, there are few analytical solutions to solve such model. Rawashdeh and Maitama13–15 introduced the natural decomposition method (NDM) to settle the differential equations, both partial and ordinary, linear and nonlinear16,17. Several numerical solutions have been introduced to solve the plankton-oxygen dynamics model. Hammouch et al.18 have investigated a chaotic fractional order system and Caputo derivative19,20. They developed a numerical method for solving differential equations with fractional variable order in the Caputo sense to study the chaotic dynamics of a circulant Halvorsen system. Analytical control laws were derived to synchronize two identical Halvorsen systems with fractional variable-order time derivatives. Graphical simulations confirmed theoretical analysis, demonstrating successful synchronization and providing insights into the system’s dynamics.

Aleem et al. proposed the heat transfer analysis on the MHD channel flow of Jeffrey’s fluid21. The authors developed a mathematical model based on the conservation of momentum and Fourier’s law of heat transfer. The temperature and velocity equations were transformed into dimensionless form using dimensionless variables, and exact solutions were obtained via the Laplace transform method. The effects of material and flow parameters were analyzed and illustrated graphically, revealing that the heat transfer coefficient exhibits dual behavior over time. Odibat et al.22 modified the Predictor-Corrector scheme to simulate delay differential equations using generalized Caputo-type non-classical derivatives. Numerical examples demonstrate the algorithm’s effectiveness, showing periodic and chaotic behaviors for varying fractional orders. This scheme offers a generalized approach to solving delay fractional differential equations and contributes a novel algorithm for fractional-order delay systems.

Kumar et al.23 investigated infection dynamics of the protozoan parasite in monarch butterflies using a fractional model with generalized Caputo-type derivatives. They solved the nonlinear model by applying a modified Predictor-Corrector scheme and analyzed existence and uniqueness. Graphical analyses at various fractional orders revealed the model’s insights into environmental persistence, showing infection dynamics under varying shedding and decay rates of infectious doses on milkweed leaves. Although various numerical schemes exist for fractional partial differential equations, the proposed FDQM-NR framework addresses the specific challenge of non-linearity. Unlike traditional Finite Difference schemes that suffer from high memory demands due to the non-locality of fractional operators, the Differential Quadrature Method utilizes a global weighting coefficient matrix. This allows for high-order precision using a significantly smaller number of nodes (N). Furthermore, by coupling this with a Newton-Raphson scheme, we provide a robust treatment for the nonlinear terms in the coupled Burgers’ equation and a nonlinear plankton-oxygen dynamics model, ensuring stable convergence where standard linearized methods often fail. Recent literature has witnessed a surge in applying fractional calculus and advanced numerical architectures to complex systems. For instance, the integration of Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) has shown great potential in environmental sustainability and water contamination risk prediction24. Similarly, optimized neural frameworks have been utilized to model the impact of demographic heterogeneity on nonlinear fractional transmission dynamics, such as H1N125. In the realm of epidemiology, stochastic elements like Poisson random measure noise have been coupled with deep learning to identify virus intensity26. Furthermore, fractional analysis has been extended to integrative bioinformatics for co-infection dynamics, providing insights into optimal control27. Beyond biological systems, the synergy between network design and topology control remains crucial for secure communications in wireless sensor networks28. While these studies often rely on data-driven or stochastic approaches,

the present work focuses on a robust deterministic numerical framework using the Fractional Differential Quadrature Method (FDQM) to solve nonlinear coupled Burgers’ and plankton–oxygen systems with high spectral accuracy and presents numerical solutions for two complex systems: a fractional nonlinear coupled Burgers’ equation and a nonlinear plankton-oxygen dynamics model with fractional time and space derivatives. These models are linked together that both of them are coupled time fractional non-linear systems of partial differential equations and both systems are designed to describe dynamic phenomena that evolve in space and time are solved using the differential quadrature method (DQM), which employs various shape functions to approximate derivatives accurately. The function’s shape depends on interpolating the Lagrange function and the regularized Shannon kernel. The Caputo fractional derivative is applied to capture the memory effects inherent in these systems, enhancing the models’ ability to reflect realistic dynamics. The non-linearity had been treated with the Newton-Raphson technique29. We verified the accuracy, efficiency, and convergence of results in (2 + 1) dimensions. A MATLAB code was created to calculate the numerical solution for this system, allowing for comparison with known analytical solutions. In addition, we conducted parametric simulations to determine how parameters such as oxygen generation rate and maximum per capita growth rate of phytoplankton influence the system’s dynamics. These investigations demonstrate the suggested techniques’ convergence and dependability, focusing on the fractional-order derivative’s influence on model behavior.

Conclusions

This research presents proper methods to study the coupled Burgers’ system with nonlinear time-fractional and fractional dynamics coupled plankton oxygen model in (1 + 1) dimensions. These models were solved with Regularized Shannon kernels and Lagrange interpolation polynomial inside a new generalized fractional derivative of the Liouville-Caputo type. A MATLAB program was utilized to create code to resolve this. the two different systems and deducing the convergent solutions for the different cases of the two systems compared with exact solutions. The computational efficiency and stability of the proposed method were confirmed when We assured the convergence of the proposed methods by calculating the L∞ error. Results are accurate, efficient and stable for a grid size of 21х21, α = 1, β = 1, L ∞ = 1.75*10− 7, and CPU time = 0.2773s for coupled Burgers’ system. Parametric studies are introduced for the dynamics coupled plankton oxygen model. Results are inversely proportional to time and space. Also, the oxygen concentration is directly proportional to the rate of oxygen production and the maximum growth rate of phytoplankton per capita. Moreover, the density of zooplankton is directly proportional to the highest rate of growth for phytoplankton per capita. For this study’s primary goal is to demonstrate the likelihood of an ecological disaster. For example, there is a lack of oxygen because of global warming. The oxygen-plankton system’s dynamic structure is achieved throughout this work via changing the value of Q. The outcomes show the impact of the current issue with the environment, such as modifications of climate based on the oxygen-plankton paradigm, this work establishes a foundation for future research, which may incorporate more variables affecting the system, such as wind velocity and temperature fluctuations.

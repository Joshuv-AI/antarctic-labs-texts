Predictive fault management in smart sensor networks using a dynamic quantum-AI architecture (DynaQuAI)
Authors: Alwaleed Alharbi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Applications in industrial and smart-infrastructure Wireless sensor networks (WSNs) in the field are increasingly expected to employ predictive intelligence, which, even when operating under dynamic configurations, heterogeneity of hardware, and strong privacy requirements. The proposed paper is DynaQuAI, quantum-inspired, edge-intelligent framework that can predict real-time failures on constrained sensor nodes regarding their resources. The proposed system employs a probabilistic state encoding and oscillatory exploration schedule—mathematically inspired by quantum superposition analogies—to improve reinforcement learning exploration in sparse and noisy environments. These techniques are entirely classical algorithms implemented with standard trigonometric and probabilistic operations, requiring no quantum hardware. Such quantum-inspired algorithms enhance coverage of state-space and convergence faster than when using the usual deep RL models, with no computational demand. To support distributed nodes collaborative learning, DynaQuAI uses a lightweight federated approach on training paired with parameter aggregation through secure masks. Privacy is ensured through secure aggregation via pairwise parameter masking, which prevents the aggregation server from observing individual node updates while recovering the correct aggregate. The framework is additionally compatible with local differential privacy (ε_total ≤ 9.8 over 150 rounds at δ = 4 × 10⁻⁶) for deployments requiring formal statistical privacy guarantees. We evaluated DynaQuAI through a simulation-based testbed emulating 500 heterogeneous sensor nodes with ARM Cortex-A7/M4 computational profiles over a simulated IEEE 802.15.4 mesh network. The evaluation uses the Kaggle Predictive Maintenance Dataset augmented with synthetic fault injections to create realistic distributed learning scenarios. Through experiments, this is, which has a sensitive aspect, demonstrated at 33% more fault-prediction accuracy over federated deep-learning baselines, 25% reduced energy consumption over adaptive quantum-inspired exploration, and 40% reduced convergence during policy learning. Its system can maintain real-time inference latency of 85 ms with minimally more than 5% of the available computational resources, thus suitable to be deployed in the long term. Individual contributions of quantum-inspired encoding, exploration scheduling and privacy-enhanced system of aggregation mechanisms are verified through ablation studies. In general, DynaQuAI has made easily extendable, privacy conscious and robust architectural learning offerings to next generation IoT ecosystems where high reliability and sustainability are essential.

Introduction

Contemporary wireless sensor networks in situations of critical infrastructure such as smart grids, industrial plants, health care monitoring systems, and environmental sensory implemented applications of sensor networks produce very large amounts of heterogeneous sensor data in real-time. Nevertheless, the stability of these systems is inherently limited by the wear and tear of hardware, communication faults, battery drain and environmental factors that increase over long durability of the operation. The conventional reactive forms of maintenance which react to failures once they happen are costly, disruptive to the operations and unsafe. Predictive maintenance as paradigms have come out as the crucial measures to forestall failures prior to their occurrence and thus allowing proactive measures to be taken to prevent the failures and optimize the availability of assets. Vibration analysis and fault detection approaches have now grown mature and are mentioned in the recent reviews1–3. Nonetheless, existing methods have serious shortcomings that can be exercised on resource-constrained edge systems that have very few computational power, energy resources, and communication bandwidth.

Intersection of principles of quantum computing and artificial intelligence give unique opportunities of projecting sensor networks to visions never observed before in classical machine learning. Quantum-inspired algorithms borrow mathematical structures from quantum mechanics—specifically probabilistic amplitude representations and periodic oscillation patterns—and implement them as classical stochastic procedures on standard hardware. These algorithms do not perform quantum computation and do not require qubits; the term “quantum-inspired” refers solely to the mathematical analogy, enabling broader exploration of solution spaces in complicated fault diagnosis scenarios. These principles can make a significant contribution to vessel speed of reinforcement learning exploration, which is an essential aspect in finding faults in the context of heterogeneous sensor deployments, when implemented to support classical computing architectures in a hybrid quantum-inspired fashion. At the same time, federated learning models allow distributed learning without concentrating sensitive operation information, data privacy is maintained while control usage of the overall knowledge on the networked devices.

The combination of the three fields of technology quantum-inspired optimization, edge-based machine learning, and federated intelligence structures is still disconnected throughout the extant literature. Existing predictive maintenance systems either use centralized cloud computing with unacceptable latency and bandwidth specifications or use decentralized methods that do not allow the utilization of distributed expertise on the sensor network. The recent developments of agents based on LLM to predictive maintenance4 and predictive rotating machinery using deep learning units5 show the potential of sophisticated machine learning solutions, although these normally demand adequate computing capabilities and continuous connectivity, which are occasionally absent in edge-constrained deployment settings. Moreover, as it is emphasized in in-depth observations of AI and IoT integration6, the integrated version of artificial intelligence combined with internet of things infrastructure necessitates new paradigms of architecture that are fundamentally not compromising edge computing capability and in addition preserving predictive quality.

The main problem discussed in this article is to develop a fault predictive framework that also delivers: (1) high predictive accuracy close to centralized cloud-based systems, (2) extreme resource efficiency, which is suitable in battery-powered edge nodes, (3) privacy-preservation federated learning with no need to centralize data, and (4) flexibility to a wide range of sensor types and dynamic operating conditions.

The solution to these inter-related problems through the principled combination of quantum-inspired reinforcement learning with the federated edge intelligence is named DynaQuAI which is a dynamic quantum-AI architecture. Its architecture uses quantum-bit optimization to organize deeper exploration of fault-state space, is distributed to use fault patterns in more than two fault patterns and distributed actor-critic to discover fault pattern, and also can change its state representations dynamically as network changes occur. The main novelty of the proposed idea is the creation of a quantum-classical hybrid design to apply the concepts of quantum superposition directly to probabilistic exploration techniques that can be applied in the context of classical edge processors, with federation-based aggregation approaches to knowledge synthesis on heterogeneous nodes and without information leakage.

Related work

Predictive maintenance and fault detection in WSNs

Predictive maintenance is a paradigm shift of the reactive and preventive methods, being a more information-driven prediction of failures. The discipline has also changed significantly whereby machine learning and deep learning techniques have been incorporated. As a result, the recent systematic reviews1,5, evaluated the vibration-based methods and deep learning techniques of rotating machinery fault diagnosis, creating the effectiveness of supervised learning under the conditions of the overwhelming train data. Yet, these strategies most often presuppose centralized processing structures with the significant computation capabilities.

Diagnosing faults in distributed sensor environments is more complicated than diagnosing faults in centralized environments. The article by Adefarati et al.6 revealed that synergistic incorporation of artificial intelligence and Internet of Things infrastructure needs new architectural paradigms. Among the research gaps that they identified are the crucial ones regarding the formation of mechanisms of distributed intelligence, which are capable of sustaining predictive quality without violating edge computation limitations and bandwidth constraints.

The recent progress in predictive maintenance in general purposes has made domain-specific methods indispensable. Kull et al.2 have given detailed discussion of the fault detection techniques of grid-connected solar systems, stating that fault monitoring of their condition requires sensors and the advanced diagnostic algorithms. Likewise, the solution of the power transformer prognostics7 proved that sensor fusion methods can significantly enhance the precision of diagnosing as opposed to uni-complementary sensors. Smart agricultural systems have similarly benefited from AI-driven monitoring approaches8, including PMSM-based drive systems for agricultural equipment9. Frameworks for predicting the remaining useful lifetime of industrial machines from energy consumption data have further demonstrated the value of hybrid architectures in maintenance contexts10, while dedicated platforms for managing misalignment data in wind power equipment illustrate the breadth of domain-specific requirements11. Multi-sensor fusion methods for lubricant health assessment12 and machine learning frameworks for ship performance management13 reflect the growing adoption of data-driven diagnostics across industrial sectors. Pattern-aware approaches for missing data imputation in solar photovoltaic generation14 and thermal–fluid coupling models for electrical cabinet management15 further extend the applicability of advanced analytics to energy and infrastructure domains. Risk analysis methods for offshore wind power platforms16 and bibliometric studies on electric vehicle fire risk17 additionally underscore the diversity of safety-critical applications demanding robust predictive intelligence. All this domain-specific progress makes it obvious that the effectiveness of the predictive maintenance relies heavily on the ability to combine the multi-modal sensor information with the advanced analytical models. The problem of implementing predictive systems in resource constrained environments has not been fully resolved. Conventional methods either lose precision by using simplified algorithms that can be applied to edges, or have intolerable memory complexity by using deep learning models. This core trade-off between quality measures and computational plan encourages our quantum-informed strategy which can be more accurate in that it offers better exploration growth without corresponding heightens to the computational expenditure.

Federated learning for distributed intelligence

The concept of federated learning has become a prospective paradigm of distributed machine learning that remains privacy-preserving but allows shared intelligence. The algorithm keeps all the data local, training local model on a single device, and sending only the parameter updates to the central servers. The architecture will deal essentially with privacy and communication limitations of sensor network deployments. Adaptive spectrum management in optical wireless sensor networks has demonstrated the feasibility of real-time fault-tolerant data transmission under resource constraints18, and AI- and IoT-driven monitoring frameworks have proven effective for optimising multi-tenant network operations through sensor data integration19.

Recent research on machine learning operations in network constrained edge devices20 showed that it is possible to deploy complex machine learning models to resource constrained systems by designing algorithms well and optimizing parameters. Their architecture of far-edge devices defined that federated learning can be achieved even with harsh connectivity and computing requirements.

Sufi21 gave systematic study of this aspect of AI in next generation machine health monitoring systems, in particular to the issue of controlling scarcity of data and unknown faults done in a distributed environment. As part of the work, it was laid stress on the fact that effective machine health monitoring schemes should be able to actively learn on sparse local information and exploit information that is distributed throughout the network without the need to centralize data. This discussion was the explicit reason why we created the federated learning elements in DynaQuAI.

It has been investigated in many ways how federated learning can be integrated with fault diagnosis. Vlachou et al.22 showed machine learning methods in monitoring the condition in permanent magnet synchronous motor drives, and proved that distributed fault prediction is still possible in the case of a member of a complex electromechanical system. Nevertheless, they used the orthodox federated averaging without quantum-inspired improvements to the exploration efficiency.

Quantum machine learning and quantum-inspired algorithms

Quantum machine learning is a new direction with quantum computing concepts used to improve machine learning. Although full-fledged quantum computers are still in their infancy, quantum-inspired algorithms that are implemented within classical computing have proven useful with regard to tasks in optimization and machine learning challenge. By applying some tricks the principles of quantum superposition and entanglement are converted into a collection of probabilistic classical algorithms, with improved exploration efficiency than traditional methods.

In the extensive survey of LLM-based autonomous agents in predictive maintenance4, Di Maggio near indicated that agentic systems involving perception, reasoning and doing have a remarkable beneficial role in diagnosis. Complementary work on LLM-based autonomous agents for prognostics and health management23 has similarly highlighted the potential of multi-modal reasoning for complex fault scenarios. Although it majored on large language models, it demonstrated the role of multi-faceted thinking on fault situations, a fact that quantum-protected optimization propagates by more intensive search of state space.

The latest developments in the field of AI and IoT self-realization6 underlined that novel computational schemes, beyond the classic deep learning processes, are necessary in the next-generation systems. The authors found quantum-inspired methods as fruitful directions towards settling the trade-off between computational and accuracy with edges, but actual implementations were limited.

The reinforcement learning concept using quantum-inspired algorithms has been examined in few areas. Stereophonic methods of quantum exploration using the superposition principles may also be used to attain much better convergence than traditional epsilon-greedy or UCB-based methods, especially when the space of states is highly localized with many local optima. Belief-rule-base approaches augmented with self-training perturbation strategies have further advanced fault diagnosis by improving robustness under limited labelled data conditions24. Our implementation, based on the concepts, similarly extends these principles to fault diagnosis in sensor networks and devises quantum bit-based exploration laws applicable to be implemented on classical edge processors.

The literature above forms effective bases in predictive maintenance, federated learning, and quantum-inspired algorithms, but current solutions tend to focus on such aspects separately. Pure reinforcement methods task with adaptive learning although they lack privacy measures or system-wide coordination. Transformer-based models are good models in representation learning, yet they are computationally ineffective on limited WSN devices. Research gaps

The literature presents several research gaps that are critical and hence encourage our proposed work:

Across diverse application domains—from optical network performance modeling25 to geospatial machine learning for sewer network exposure assessment26 and geohazard prediction27—data-driven methods have demonstrated wide applicability, yet their integration with resource-constrained edge deployments remains underexplored. Benchmarking studies on control strategies for digital twin applications28 and feature-selection methodologies for imbalanced classification tasks29 additionally illustrate the need for adaptive, computationally efficient approaches in complex real-world systems.

Gap 1 - quantum-classical hybrid fault diagnosis: Although the principles of quantum machine learning have been proven to have theoretical power, practical applications in edge-constrained WSN distributed fault diagnostic have not been studied. Proposed methodology

System architecture overview

Figure 1 reflects the entire DynaQuAI framework. The framework can be described as the three layers, where all are linked: the edge sensor layer that houses heterogeneous sensors, which gather multimodal data; the local learning layer, which provides single nodes with quantum-inspired reinforcement learning, and allows fault diagnosis; and the federated aggregation layer, which provides collaborative model improvement through the synchronization of secure parameters.

Edge nodes provider enables local learning based on quantum bits exploration, so that only aggregate parameters are transmitted back to the federated controller, which coordinates model updates on a global basis. The architecture provides data locality and results in fault pattern recognition across the network.

The architecture provides data locality and results in fault pattern recognition across the network.

Every sensor component consists of three operating modules. The local-state-representation module receives sensor streams that provide non-redundant, corresponding multimodal features—including temperature, vibration, power consumption, and signal quality metrics—and actively adjusts the feature space in response to network behavior through adaptive dimensionality reduction The quantum-inspired RL is a quantum bit-exploration based distributed learning control, which is implemented in the quantum-inspired RL module and does not store fault policies locally. Access to model parameters has been combined with privacy preserving protocols in the federated communication module making sure that sensitive data regarding operational activities does not leave individual nodes.

The federated aggregation controller coordinates the process of synchronizing the parameters within the network with adaptive scheduling taking into consideration heterogeneous communication capabilities. In place of the synchronous updates all nodes must engage in a constraint infeasible in sensor networks where nodes may often experience temporary connectivity loss the controller does asynchronous aggregation using straggler-sensitive algorithms that consider all received node updates and ensures convergence.

Conversely, DynaQuAI has amplitude weights \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\left({\left|\alpha\:\right|}^{2},{\left|\beta\:\right|}^{2}\right)$$\end{document} which represent the uncertainty of each action. The combination of the amplitude-based encoding and temperature modulation through interference forms exploration patterns that are similar to quantum superposition and interference on classical hardware, which provide much more balanced and structured exploration of the policy space.

Adaptive state representation learning

The heterogeneity of sensor networks requires adaptive state modeling responding to the capabilities of the node networks and the environments in which they have to operate. We use online feature selection that ensures that compact state representations are obtained and yet fault diagnosis which includes discriminative information is provided.

Comparison with existing approaches

We compare DynaQuAI qualitatively to already existing fault diagnosis methodologies. Conventional centralized deep learning models are highly accurate using complex models but demand the transmission of raw data to cloud servers at unacceptable latencies (hundreds of milliseconds and seconds) and bandwidth requirements that are not off the shelf on edges. In traditional federated learning, parameters are averaged and no exploration optimization method exists so incorrect faults in sparse data regimes may be overlooked.

Better exploration could be offered by quantum computing methods but these methods demand specialized quantum hardware which is not readily available in real-life deployments. We have the benefits of exploration of the classical approach, but it is quantum-inspired, free of hardware limitations. The adaptive feature selection maintains node-specific representations that are more appropriate to heterogeneous deployments than the fixed feature engineering that is utilized by current methods.

Experimental results

Experimental setup

Testbed configuration

We evaluate DynaQuAI using a simulation-based testbed that emulates 500 heterogeneous sensor nodes. No physical hardware deployment is conducted; all experiments execute on a centralized server cluster with per-node computational constraints enforced through the simulation model. The simulation models the following hardware profiles: 60% ARM Cortex-A7 (512 MB RAM, ~ 1 GHz), 25% ARM Cortex-M4 (256 KB SRAM, 168 MHz), and 15% sensor gateways (1 GB RAM). Simulated sensor modalities include temperature (DS18B20 profile), vibration (MEMS accelerometer profile), power consumption (INA219 profile), and signal quality (RSSI, SNR radio metrics).

Simulation Environment: The testbed is implemented using Python 3.10 with PyTorch 2.1 executing on an NVIDIA A100 GPU server (80 GB HBM2e, 128 GB system RAM). Each of the 500 simulated nodes maintains independent model parameters, local data partitions, and simulated communication state. Network conditions are modeled with configurable link latency (10–200 ms, log-normal distribution), packet loss rate (0.1–5%, Bernoulli model), and node churn (5–20% dropout per federated learning round, Poisson arrival process). The IEEE 802.15.4 mesh topology is simulated with mean link packet delivery ratio of ~ 85%, consistent with complex indoor wireless propagation conditions.

Communication between nodes uses the IEEE 802.15.4 mesh networking protocol and runs at 2.4 GHz and with a quality link estimation which is adaptive. The network connectivity variability is temporal with the mean connection link packet delivery ratio of approximately 85% a realistic outdoor wireless propagation in complex indoor conditions. The federated aggregation rounds are performed after every 30 min to trade of communication overhead and freshness of model updates.

Dataset description

We use the Kaggle Predictive Maintenance dataset (14,400 measurements from 100 machines over six months) with four modalities (temperature, vibration, power, and operational labels), distributed across 500 simulated nodes to create heterogeneous (non-IID) local data. Since natural faults are unavailable, synthetic faults are injected—hardware degradation, communication failure, battery depletion, and sensor calibration drift—combined with the original anomaly labels to form five fault types. Each node monitors temperature (1 Hz), vibration (1 Hz), power (1 Hz), and radio quality (0.1 Hz), yielding 4–5 features per time step. The combined dataset contains 39,000 samples with an overall fault-to-normal ratio of ~ 1:4, and per-node distributions are intentionally non-IID. The network comprises 500 nodes in 10 clusters with a central server, using a simulated IEEE 802.15.4 mesh (≈ 85% mean packet delivery ratio). Each node uploads a ~ 120 KB compressed update per round; over 150 rounds, total upstream traffic is ~ 9 GB.

Evaluation metrics

Fault prediction is formulated as a hierarchical classification using a sliding window of the most recent 60 sensor readings per node to predict whether a fault will occur within the next H = 30 min, aligned with the federated aggregation cycle; ablation is reported for H∈{10,30,60,120}. A sample at time t is labeled faulty if any of the five fault types (hardware degradation, sensor drift, communication failure, battery depletion, or calibration drift) occurs in (t, t + H] otherwise it is labeled normal, yielding an overall class imbalance of approximately 1:4 over 39,000 samples. Performance is evaluated using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score (primary metric), ROC-AUC, inference latency, and energy consumption, with results averaged over five runs and reported with standard deviations.

We report the following evaluation metrics: (1) Accuracy: overall fraction of correct predictions. (2) Precision: fraction of predicted faults that are true faults (false alarm cost). (3) Recall: fraction of actual faults correctly detected (missed fault rate). (4) F1-Score: harmonic mean of precision and recall, serving as the primary metric given class imbalance. (5) ROC-AUC: area under the ROC curve for threshold-independent discrimination. (6) PR-AUC: area under the Precision-Recall curve, particularly informative for the imbalanced fault-detection setting where the minority class is of primary interest. (7) Latency: end-to-end time from sensor measurement to fault prediction output. (8) Energy consumption: total energy per fault diagnosis cycle. All metrics are averaged over five independent runs with different random seeds, and standard deviations are reported.

Discussion

The experimental findings fully indicate that DynaQuAI reaches a significant level in raising error diagnosis performance in edge constrained wireless sensor networks when quantum-inspired optimization is synergistically integrated with federated learning models. The 33% accuracy increase compared to traditional federated learning is significant in terms of finding a path to practical use of advanced predictive maintenance in the conditions of resource scarcity.

There are a few observations that are worth discussing. First, the quantum-inspired exploration algorithm has specific superiority in the conditions of sparse data when the sample training of individual nodes is limited (around 78 samples per node). In traditional federated learning, there is lack of local data to explore fault pattern space and the optimal policies were developed. In mystery exploration, quantum-inspired superposition-like can use probability distributions that explore in the mode of prediction-weighted sequences to effectively conquer fault pattern discovery, even with limited data using a uniformly-exploring exploration method. This is corroborated by other broadest reinforcement learning results in which it becomes essential to have efficient exploration at the point at which there is limited data.

Second, the adaptive mechanism of feature selection is shown to be very effective in minimizing energy consumption although it implies rather complicated algorithmic operations. This 250-energy cut over FedAvg is because fewer features need processing later at higher levels in the training process and thus the computation overhead is reduced by a significant margin. The first feature of importance estimation is relatively cheap, but once the amortized across longer training periods, this feature of importance estimation gives net energy savings. This observation indicates that the smart reduction of feature spaces can be applied to attain computational efficiency gains as opposed to making use of simplified algorithms.

Third, the federated architecture has asynchronous aggregation and straggler-aware mechanisms which are resilient to realistic wireless network behaviors with variations in connectivity over time. Traditional federated learning models have been designed where all nodes need to take part in every aggregation round and thus require the slower nodes to wait. In our asynchronous algorithm, responsive nodes provide us with the faster convergence of models due to the availability of the updates that help preserve the model. These architectural merits can be seen in the 40% faster convergence rate than FedAvg.

Fourth, the scalability analysis shows that federated learning models continue to improve with larger network sizes and are also characterized by node specialization. Individual nodes, which have 500 nodes each, come to formulate specific local policies in accordance with their unique circumstances of operation. The world fusion then moves on to combine the local complementary knowledge into strong global policies. This is the opposite of what centralized algorithms have to process all node data in an identical fashion and this can experience performance degradation with the increased heterogeneity. The quantum-inspired exploration is helpful in finding complementary patterns of various nodes more successfully as compared to traditional ways of exploration.

The median latency of 85 milliseconds and 95-percentile of 210 milliseconds prove a feasibility of a real-time fault diagnosing of an edge device. Such a latency is adequate to most industrial predictive maintenance applications where it provides several minutes to hours of warning of incipient failures to take corrective actions. The latency consistency in cases of network congestion is indicative of the edge-local processing model in which inference is computed oblivious of network congestion- a core benefit of edge-deployed models over cloud-dependent models which adapt to network transmission delays.

There is one such limitation which should be mentioned: the quantum-inspired mechanisms offer theoretical benefits in efficiency of exploration, yet our classical adaptation does not accurately reflect the benefits of quantum computing. Implementations with full quantum representation may even perform better, although even the practices quantum computers are still out of reach of actual implementations. The combination of pragmatism and balance in our method means they stand under small performance compromises to long-term deployability: one which, due to their primitive nature, can only be classified as devoutly classical in their theory and heavily pragmatic in its actual application. Energy management concerns are also relevant across adjacent domains: lithium-ion battery life prediction [33], automotive electric drive monitoring [34], digital twin development for DC-DC converters [35], and real-time energy management in hybrid microgrids [36] all highlight the broad relevance of predictive and adaptive intelligence in energy-constrained systems. AI and digital twin technologies for battery management systems [37], battery reliability assessments for drone systems [38], and thermal challenges in aircraft refrigeration systems [39] further underscore the diversity of deployment contexts where the principles underpinning DynaQuAI are applicable.

The second limitation relates to privacy guarantees. The revised pairwise masking scheme with Shamir threshold secret sharing (Theorem 1) provides computational privacy against an honest-but-curious aggregation server: the server learns only the aggregate ∑ϕ_i, not individual ϕ_i. However, adversaries observing parameter update trajectories across multiple rounds may attempt gradient inversion or model memorization attacks. Formal (ε,δ)-differential privacy requires additional calibrated noise injection atop the secure aggregation, which introduces an accuracy-privacy trade-off. The present scheme prioritizes accuracy for industrial applications where trusted aggregation infrastructure is available. For higher-threat environments, the pairwise masking can be straightforwardly combined with local differential privacy by adding Gaussian noise N(0, σ²C²I) to each local update before mask application.

Limitations and threats to validity

A primary limitation is that the experimental evaluation is entirely simulation-based. While the simulation faithfully models hardware computational constraints, energy budgets, network latency, and communication characteristics of the target edge platforms, real-world physical deployment may introduce additional challenges including hardware-specific processing latencies, environmental RF interference, thermal effects on sensor accuracy, and unanticipated fault modes not captured by synthetic injections. Future work will validate the framework on physical ARM Cortex-A7/M4 hardware with real sensor deployments in industrial environments.

The proposed DynaQuAI framework has good performance in terms of accuracy, latency, and energy efficiency, yet several limitations are to be recognized to be allowed to give a transparent picture of the system. To start with, the experimental assessment is primarily based on a single public dataset as well as random fault injections. Although this arrangement assists in testing various fault types in a controlled environment, synthetic patterns can be inadequate to represent the reality of inevitable circumstances of actual industrial failures. The model is thus advantaged by the fact that it can be validated in future on a variety of real-life datasets or in long-term deployments where faults would come naturally.

Second the system is run and tested on some hardware platforms, primarily ARM Cortex-A7 and Cortex-M4 nodes. These gadgets are exemplary hardware of the sensor-class, although there can be differences on different latter platforms, including ultra-low-power MCUs or fuller feature smart gateways. The elucidated analysis also dwells on five types of major faults; further or even smaller faulting this work would make it more effective. The quantum-inspired parts are realized with classical approximations as opposed to real quantum hardware, implying that the advantages are provided due to algorithmic behavior as opposed to the physical quantum action. This difference should be considered when the terminology of quantum-inspiration is interpreted.

The reported findings are also likely to be affected by several threats to validity. As synthetic faults are used to carry out a part of the workload, the transferability of the learned policies to the real world can be different according to the patterns of noise, aging equipment, or environmental disruptions that are not simulated in the experiments. The wireless network conditions within the testbed are not adequately representative of the extreme interference conditions, uncertain RSSI variations or high-mobility application like vehicular or mobile IoT application. Although the framework has been tried on a 500-node scale, this has not been checked out to scaling above that size, especially in cases where connectivity is very flickering or where there is very high heterogeneity in devices.

Lastly, the sensitivity of reinforcement learning training in the context of randomness in starting point, exploration path and environmental determinism. Even though the asynchronous federated design is effective in stabilizing convergence, repetitive trials on confidence intervals should become part of future work to enhance reproducibility. All these restrictions and external factors give one a realistic perspective of the strengths of the framework as well as a potential area they need to explore further.

Conclusion

We have introduced DynaQuAI, that is a dynamical quantum-AI system of smart sensor network predictive fault management, which synergistically combines quantum-inspired reinforcement learning with federated edge intelligence. The architecture tackles the basic issues in implementing advanced predictive maintenance at the system boundary and maintains privacy and the intense computational limits. In-depth experimental validation of 337-node heterogeneous testbed shows that 33% of fault detection accuracy improvement over conventional federated learning, 25% energy saving and 40% quicker time to optimal policies is achieved. State representation mechanisms that are adaptive and allow allocation to a vast range of hardware platforms and operating environments with no manual recalibration.

There are existing areas of research that could be expanded in the future, such as full-quantum computing implementations once useful quantum computing hardware is available, investigation into other federated aggregation schemes that include higher-order information and extension to multi-agent reinforcement learning frameworks that allow an agent to directly policy-refine its neighbors. Also, it has the potential to improve the use of transfer learning mechanisms; thus, requiring global policies to adapt to new fields of operation faster, which lessens the initial training needs. Lastly, there would be an analysis of privacy through formal legal lens using differential privacy composition lemmas which would give privacy guarantees in numbers complementary to our existing empirical privacy assurances.

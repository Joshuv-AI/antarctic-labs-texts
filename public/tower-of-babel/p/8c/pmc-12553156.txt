Efficacy and safety of neoadjuvant camrelizumab and apatinib combined with chemotherapy in stage IIIA (N2) NSCLC: a multi-center, single-arm, phase II trial
Authors: Lilan Zhao, Tianxing Guo, Yi Zhang, Zhen Huang, Yangyun Huang, Lihuan Zhu, Xing Chen, Nan Zhang, Liren Guo, Rongzhi Huang, Guojun Geng, Ning Li, Shuxing Chen, Xiaojie Pan, Wenshu Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCamrelizumab (an anti-programmed cell death protein-1 antibody) combined with apatinib (an antiangiogenic agent) has conferred benefits for advanced NSCLC. This study aimed to assess the efficacy and safety of camrelizumab and apatinib alongside chemotherapy in patients with resectable stage IIIA (N2) NSCLC as neoadjuvant therapy.MethodsPatients with stage IIIA (N2) NSCLC were treated with a combination of chemotherapy, camrelizumab (200 mg, once every 3 weeks [q3w]), and apatinib (250 mg, once daily). Surgery was planned after 2–4 cycles of therapy. The primary endpoint was major pathological response (MPR) rate, with secondary endpoints including pathological complete response (pCR) rate, R0 resection rate, objective response rate (ORR), and safety. The trial was registered at ChiCTR.org.cn (ChiCTR2200059608).ResultsThirty-one patients were enrolled from August 4, 2021, to October 8, 2023. The disease control rate (DCR) was 93.5%, while the ORR was 87.1% and a clinical down-staging rate of 19.4%. Among them, 20 patients underwent complete resection, the MPR rate was 65.0%, and the pCR rate was 40%. Any grade neoadjuvant adverse events (AEs) were reported in 31 (100%) of the patients, and grade 3/4 AEs in 19 (61.3%). The most common AEs of any grade were fatigue (61.3%), nausea (54.8%), and decreased white blood cell count (51.6%). These conditions typically return to normal within a short period following observation or pharmacological treatment. No treatment-related deaths occurred.ConclusionsThe synergistic application of chemotherapy with camrelizumab and apatinib showed clinically meaningful anti-tumor activity and manageable safety, with few hematologic toxicities, and might be a promising therapeutic alternative for individuals with resectable stage IIIA (N2) NSCLC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12916-025-04416-0.Keywords: Locally advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), Neoadjuvant immunotherapy, Antiangiogenic therapy, Efficacy, Safety

Background

Around 30% of individuals newly identified with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) are classified as stage III at diagnosis [1]. Such a group exhibits significant heterogeneity and complexity in their conditions, leading to a spectrum of disease states and disputed optimal treatment strategies [2]. Surgery stands as the principal intervention for those with resectable NSCLC. Yet, for those with locally advanced stages, immediate surgical intervention often falls short, with a recurrence rate of 30 to 77% post-surgery, culminating in unfavorable long-term survival prospects [3]. The persistence of micrometastatic cells post-surgery is cited as a key factor in these recurrences, posing a persistent challenge for those with operable conditions [4]. Preoperative treatments are suggested to offer advantages such as tumor shrinkage, enhanced surgical feasibility, and the elimination of micrometastases [5]. Despite these efforts, the overall outlook for NSCLC patients remains less than ideal, highlighting an imperative for ongoing research and the innovation of novel therapeutic approaches to enhance patient outcomes.

Preclinical studies indicate that the combination of immunotherapy with chemotherapy yields a potent, synergistic anti-cancer impact, curbing the evasion of tumor cells from the immune system and bolstering the host’s immune reaction [6]. Camrelizumab, a human monoclonal antibody specific for programmed cell death protein-1 (PD-1), has demonstrated in phase 3 trials, such as CameL and CameL-Sq, a survival benefit when paired with chemotherapy for advanced NSCLC patients as a first-line strategy [7, 8]. Moreover, the amalgamation of immunotherapy with antiangiogenic agents has proven effective across a variety of cancers [9–15]. On a cellular level, antiangiogenic treatments are shown to curtail the expansion of regulatory T-cells, escalate the presence of immune effector cells within tumors, foster dendritic cell development, and reprogram the tumor microenvironment to enhance vascular normalization [16–19]. In the case of locally advanced NSCLC, phase 2 trials post-treatment with camrelizumab and apatinib, preceding surgery, have illustrated acceptable safety profiles, though their efficacy is yet to reach expectations [20, 21]. This prompts an exploration into the potential of integrating chemotherapy with both immunotherapy and antiangiogenic treatments.

Herein, we report the results from a multi-center, single-arm, phase 2 study assessing the efficacy and safety of camrelizumab combined with apatinib and chemotherapy as neoadjuvant therapy in patients with resectable stage IIIA(N2) NSCLC.

Methods

Study design

We conducted a multi-center, open-label, phase 2 trial at 4 centers in China (registration number: ChiCTR2200059608) (Fig. 1). The study protocol was approved by the ethics committee of the Fuzhou University Affiliated Provincial Hospital, Fuzhou Pulmonary Hospital of Fujian, Zhangzhou Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University, The First Affiliated Hospital of Xiamen University. All patients provided written informed consent before participating in the study. The study was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines.

Trial design

Participants

Patients were eligible if they were aged 18 to 75 years; had histopathologically confirmed stage IIIA (N2) NSCLC (according to the 8th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer staging system) (with clinical N2 status verified primarily via endobronchial ultrasound-guided transbronchial needle aspiration [EBUS-TBNA]); had resectable, locally advanced disease as assessed by a thoracic surgeon; had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) of 0 or 1; had at least 1 measurable target lesion according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST), version 1.1; and had adequate organ function.

Key exclusion criteria included known epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutations or anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK) fusion, a malignancy history within the preceding 5 years, or concurrent autoimmune disease. Comprehensive eligibility requirements can be found in the protocol (Additional file 1: Clinical protocol). The first patient was enrolled on August 4, 2021, and the last was recruited on October 8, 2023.

Treatments

Positron emission tomography-computed tomography (PET-CT) was prioritized during the screening period (accepting PET-CT results from qualified outside hospitals within 28 days). If subjects refused PET-CT, enhanced computed tomography (CT) of the chest, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the head to rule out brain metastasis, ultrasound of the abdomen to rule out abdominal metastasis, and bone scanning was required if bone metastasis was suspected. Patients received 2–4 cycles of neoadjuvant therapy with camrelizumab (200 mg intravenously on day 1), platinum-doublet chemotherapy and apatinib (250 mg, orally, once daily); each cycle was repeated every 21 days. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy consisted of nab-paclitaxel (260 mg/m2 intravenously on days 1) (or alternative options such as paclitaxel or paclitaxel liposome), and platinum (cisplatin, 75 mg/m2; carboplatin, area under the curve (AUC), 5; or nedaplatin, 100 mg/m2 intravenously on day 1). The choice of platinum and paclitaxel was determined by the investigator. Surgery was planned 4 to 6 weeks after the completion of neoadjuvant treatment. During the study, dose discontinuations, interruptions, and modifications of camrelizumab and apatinib were permitted until meeting the prespecified criteria for treatment resumption per protocol, the details of dose modifications and assessments are provided in the protocol (Additional file 1: Clinical protocol).

After the first two treatment cycles, an enhanced CT scan of the chest was performed. Tumor assessments were conducted according to RECIST version 1.1, and surgery was assessed as safe and feasible by a multidisciplinary team (MDT), surgery was planned within 30 days of tumor assessment; otherwise, the cycle 3 of neoadjuvant treatment was continued. The determination of whether to continue the cycle 4 of treatment was similar to that of the previous cycle. If patients could not tolerate the neoadjuvant therapy, they could also undergo surgery in advance. Before surgery, a radiographic evaluation with enhanced CT and magnetic resonance imaging was performed 4–6 weeks after the last dose of treatment; and then, the MDT conducted a comprehensive assessment based on the location of the tumor, the extent of tumor invasion, lymph node status, and the physical conditions to identify the resectability of the tumor. Surgical procedures were performed by experienced thoracic surgeons. Intraoperative exploration was initially conducted to reassess resectability, evaluating potential adhesions and tumor invasion in the fissures and nodal stations. Surgical interventions included lobectomy, bilobectomy, or sleeve lobectomy, coupled with comprehensive hilar and mediastinal lymphadenectomy, encompassing dissecting stations 2, 4, 7, 8, 9, and 10 on the right side, and stations 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10 on the left side. All resected tissues, including the primary tumor, lymph nodes, any abnormal-appearing tissue, and surgical margin, were sent for paraffin embedding and pathological examination as described by Travis et al. [22] Assessment of pathologic response was performed by two senior pathologists. The programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) tumor cell proportion score (TPS) was assessed using the VENTANA PD-L1 (SP263) Assay (Ventana Medical Systems, Inc. Tucson, AZ, USA). After surgery, adjuvant therapy including combination of camrelizumab and apatinib was administered within 4 weeks after surgery and could be maintained for a maximum of 1 year. Chemotherapy or radiotherapy could be chosen by the patient based on the investigator’s suggestion. Postoperatively, patients underwent chest CT scans at 30 days after surgery, followed by scans every 3 months for the first 2 years and every 6 months for the third to fifth years.

Outcomes

Major pathological response (MPR) was adopted as the primary endpoint, and the secondary endpoints comprised pathological complete response (pCR), objective response rate (ORR), R0 resection rate, and safety. The presence of 10% or fewer residual tumor cells in the primary tumor and lymph nodes was considered MPR. pCR was referred to as the absence of residual tumor cells in the primary tumor and lymph nodes. ORR was defined as the proportion of patients with complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) as per RECIST version 1.1, R0 resection rate was defined as the proportion of patients with margin-negative resection. Adverse events were evaluated according to Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0. Postoperative complications were evaluated according to the Clavien-Dindo classification. Radiological responses were evaluated according to RECIST version 1.1. Other endpoints including overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) were not analyzed because the follow-up time was insufficient.

Sample size and statistical analysis.

We used Simon’s two-stage design. We initially expected that the MPR rate would be 70%. This had a two-sided type I error rate of 5% and a power of 80%. In the first stage, 12 patients were accrued. If more than 5 responders were observed, an additional 16 patients would be accrued to the second stage. The study was considered positive if more than 15 responders were observed among the 25 patients. Considering 10% patients’ loss to follow-up, at least 28 patients were enrolled. The 95% CI was calculated using the Clopper-Pearson method. Statistical analyses were performed using the SPSS (version 23.0; IBM, Armonk, NY) and R software (version 4.3.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing). We set the level of statistical significance at 5% without further notification.

Results

Patient characteristics

From August 4, 2021, to October 8, 2023, a total of 31 patients were enrolled and received at least one cycle of the study drugs (Fig. 2), baseline characteristics of the patients are listed in Table 1. Of these patients, 28 were men and 3 were women. The median age was 63 years (range 45–75 years). Furthermore, 24 patients (77.4%) had squamous cell carcinoma; 29 (93.5%) had T2 disease. The median interval between the last administration of chemotherapy and camrelizumab plus apatinib and surgery was 47 (range 33–88) days.

Surgery summary

By the cutoff date of April 15, 2024, 20 (64.5%) of 31 patients underwent surgical resection. Of the 20 patients, 18 patients (90.0%) had video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) and two additional patients (10.0%) who initially underwent VATS were intraoperatively converted to thoracotomy owing to operational difficulty (hilar or mediastinal calcification and adhesion, lymph node enlargement) (Table 2). The median duration of surgery was 180 (range 120–480) minutes. All 20 patients (100%) achieved an R0 surgical resection. Six of the 20 patients (30%) had delays in surgery, three of which were attributed to adverse events, and the other three were owing to COVID-19 pandemic isolation as well as pneumonia caused by the coronavirus. Surgical complications were reported in eight patients (40%), with the most common being pneumothorax (four [20%]). The median duration of postoperative hospital stay was 6 (range 4–23) days. No patient died of any cause within 90 days after surgery.

Two patients had disease progression that rendered their lesions unresectable. Additionally, two surgeries were canceled due to adverse events, specifically immune-mediated colitis and myelosuppression (Fig. 2).

Efficacy

All 31 patients underwent an image evaluation for tumor response. The median follow-up duration was 18.07 months (interquartile range (IQR) 3.75–36.40) (Fig. 3). Based on the RECIST 1.1 criteria, 29 patients (93.5%) achieved disease control (CR + PR + stable disease [SD]). Among them, 27 (87.1%) achieved a PR, and 2 (6.5%) maintained SD (Table 3). The disease control rate was 93.5% (95% confidence interval (CI) 82.9–99.9%), while the ORR was 87.1% (95% CI 73.9–98.0%). No CR was observed. Notably, the clinical down-staging rate, which was determined through CT or PET/CT scans, was 19.4% (95% CI 6.4–34.3%).

Treatment-related toxicity

All the 31 enrolled patients experienced treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) of any grade during neoadjuvant treatment (Table 4). The most common TRAEs were fatigue (61.3%), nausea (54.8%), and decreased white blood cell count (51.6%). A total of 31 (100%) and 19 (61.3%) patients suffered from grade 3 or grade 4, respectively. Common grade 3 or 4 TRAEs include manifestations of bone marrow suppression, such as platelet count decreased (9.7% grade 4; 9.7% grade 3), neutrophil count decreased (6.5% grade 4), and decreased white blood cell count (12.9% grade 3), as well as fatigue (16.1% grade 3) and rash (12.9% grade 3). These conditions typically return to normal within a short period following observation or pharmacological treatment. No treatment-related deaths occurred. Any grade immune-related adverse events (irAEs) occurred in 25 (80.6%) patients, but only one patient experienced grade 4 increased gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT). Additionally, no patients experienced camrelizumab or apatinib dose reduction in the trial.

Discussion

In this multicenter, single-arm, phase II trial, we evaluated the efficacy and safety of a novel neoadjuvant regimen combining camrelizumab (anti-PD-1), apatinib (anti-angiogenic), and chemotherapy for patients with resectable stage IIIA(N2) NSCLC. The results demonstrated promising antitumor activity, with an objective response rate (ORR) of 87.1% per RECIST v1.1. Among the 20 patients who underwent surgery, a major pathological response (MPR) was achieved in 65.0%, including a pathological complete response (pCR) in 40.0%. The safety profile was manageable, with no unexpected toxicities emerging.

This study specifically targeted a high-risk population—stage IIIA(N2) NSCLC—which has a documented poor long-term survival rate (5-year survival: 20–25%) [23]. Critically, this is the first trial to investigate the concurrent neoadjuvant combination of camrelizumab, apatinib, and chemotherapy for stage IIIA(N2) disease, which is distinct from prior studies such as Zhao et al. [20] (which included stage IIIA patients without confirmed N2 status), Xia et al. [21] (which enrolled initially unresectable stage II–III patients), and Ji et al. [24] (which included resectable stage IIA-IIIA patients). This triple-therapy strategy leverages the synergistic potential of direct cytotoxicity (chemotherapy), vascular normalization and reversal of immunosuppression (apatinib), and enhanced anti-tumor immunity (camrelizumab), contrasting with chemotherapy-free or non-synchronous approaches used in earlier studies.

Assessing pathological efficacy, particularly MPR and pCR, is crucial for advocating the use of neoadjuvant therapy strategies in NSCLC. The existing literature [25, 26] reveal that MPR rates range from 19 to 45% with neoadjuvant immunotherapy alone and can reach up to 50% with the addition of dual immune checkpoint inhibitors. The combination of chemotherapy with immunotherapy in a neoadjuvant setting has significantly enhanced these rates. Moreover, neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy has been linked to pCR rates ranging from 9.1 to 63.4% in patients with localized NSCLC [27, 28]. Previous studies of camrelizumab and apatinib (without concurrent chemotherapy) showed promising MPR/pCR rates of 55.6–66.6%/11–23% [20, 21, 24], while camrelizumab plus chemotherapy in the TD-FOREKNOW trial achieved 65.1%/32.6% in resectable stage IIIA/IIIB disease [29]. In our trial, combining chemotherapy, camrelizumab, and apatinib resulted in an MPR rate of 65.0% (95% CI 43.8–84.8%) and a pCR rate of 40.0% (95% CI 19.5–61.5%), surpassing outcomes of these reported similar settings. The efficacy of our triple regimen is particularly noteworthy given that 61.3% of patients had PD-L1-negative tumors, a subgroup typically associated with lower immunotherapy response [30]. This finding aligns with a meta-analysis [31], which demonstrated improved outcomes for PD-L1-negative NSCLC with chemoimmunotherapy (ORR 48%) versus immunotherapy alone (ORR 11%) across 43 trials. Our higher MPR rate (65.0%) suggests that adding the anti-angiogenic agent apatinib to chemoimmunotherapy may provide additional benefit, potentially by modulating the tumor microenvironment and sensitizing PD-L1-negative tumors to immune checkpoint inhibitors. Observed variations in response rates across trials may stem from several factors. Key differences include our strict enrollment of pathologically confirmed IIIA(N2) patients compared to studies involving earlier-stage or unresectable cases, the unique synergistic potential of concurrent chemotherapy, anti-angiogenesis (apatinib), and immunotherapy (camrelizumab) in our regimen versus approaches lacking one or more components, and the potential for amplified variability due to small subgroup sizes in some prior studies (e.g., Xia et al. [21] IIIA cohort, n = 18).

While prolonged neoadjuvant exposure may enhance pathological responses [32], our data on treatment cycles (2 vs. 3 vs. 4 cycles yielding pCR rates of 40%, 50%, and 25%, and MPR rates of 80%, 80%, and 25%, respectively) must be interpreted cautiously due to the limited patient numbers in each subgroup. Further investigation is needed to define the optimal treatment duration, balancing efficacy, and tolerability. Historically, patients with MPR or pCR have shown enhanced disease-free survival (DFS) or event-free survival (EFS) [33–36]. The significant link between MPR and clinical advantages underscores the need for deeper exploration of the relationship between pathological response and survival benefits in ongoing trials of neoadjuvant therapy for resectable NSCLC. Future research priorities include (1) Phase III validation of this triple regimen against current standards (e.g., immunochemotherapy) in IIIA(N2) populations; (2) Biomarker exploration (PD-L1, tumor mutational burden (TMB), circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) clearance) to identify predictive signatures; (3) Extended survival follow-up to confirm if high MPR/pCR rates translate to long-term benefit.

The synergistic interaction between camrelizumab and chemotherapy has been documented. Studies indicated that the incorporation of camrelizumab into the first-line treatment for advanced or metastatic NSCLC significantly enhances both disease-free and overall survival [7, 8, 37, 38]. Additionally, a randomized trial by Lei et al. [29] highlighted the exceptional antitumor efficacy of neoadjuvant camrelizumab combined with chemotherapy, suggesting a potentiated therapeutic impact in the management of locally advanced NSCLC. The potential for a synergistic effect between camrelizumab and apatinib is also noteworthy. Apatinib, recognized as an antiangiogenic agent, is capable of normalizing tumor vasculature and alleviating immunosuppression by targeting the vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGFR) [19]. It has demonstrated efficacy in treating patients with resectable NSCLC. A phase 2, single-arm, open-label study conducted by Zhao et al. [20] on locally advanced NSCLC patients revealed that the combination of camrelizumab and apatinib not only showed promising antitumor activity but also suggested a synergistic therapeutic effect between the two compounds.

Integrating immunotherapy and anti-angiogenic agents with chemotherapy enhances antitumor efficacy but may increase the risk of treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs). In our study, the most common TRAEs were fatigue (61.3%), nausea (54.8%), and decreased white blood cell count (51.6%). Notably, all patients experienced grade ≥ 3 TRAEs, the majority of which were hematological toxicities commonly associated with chemotherapy/platinum-based regimens. Immune-related adverse events (irAEs) of any grade occurred in 80.6% of patients, but only one patient experienced a grade 4 event (increased gamma-glutamyl transferase). These events were manageable through protocol-defined strategies (e.g., stringent baseline screening; dynamic monitoring; corticosteroids for grade ≥ 2 irAEs), aligning with prior studies [7, 8, 39–41]. Notably, the incidence of reactive cutaneous capillary endothelial proliferation (RCCEP), a known camrelizumab-associated AE, was low, possibly due to a protective effect of apatinib [11, 39, 42, 43].

Thirty-one patients were enrolled in our study, of which 20 underwent surgery. Among the remaining 11 patients who did not undergo surgery, seven met the surgical criteria. Of these, six declined surgery for personal reasons and one experienced a non-cancer-related natural death before the operation. Two patients did not meet the criteria due to disease progression that rendered their lesions unresectable. The remaining two patients could not undergo surgery due to treatment-related toxicity. Importantly, no signs of increased surgical risks, including infections, wound separation, or extended hospitalization, were observed, indicating that neoadjuvant camrelizumab and apatinib did not compromise surgical feasibility.

Interpretation of the findings from this study should consider several limitations. The single-arm design and modest sample size preclude direct comparisons with established therapies, limiting the capacity for robust benchmarking and increasing the risk of overinterpreting point estimates (e.g., the pCR rate). Furthermore, comprehensive biomarker data were lacking, exemplified by an insufficient number of tumor samples both pre- and post-treatment for evaluating potential prognostic biomarkers such as PD-L1 levels, TMB, and radiomics. Although ctDNA clearance was assessed as an exploratory endpoint, data maturity at this time remains limited; ongoing analysis will be reported in future publications once finalized. Additionally, the combined therapeutic approach (immunotherapy, apatinib, and chemotherapy) hindered assessment of the individual contributions of each element to the observed efficacy. The study’s geographical focus on southern China may also limit the generalizability of the results to diverse populations. Extended follow-up is necessary to determine if the neoadjuvant protocol confers a survival advantage. Outcome data are being collected as exploratory endpoints to provide further insights into the treatment’s impact.

Conclusions

In summary, this study provides proof-of-concept for a novel neoadjuvant triple-therapy strategy (camrelizumab + apatinib + chemotherapy) that demonstrates compelling efficacy and manageable toxicity in high-risk stage IIIA(N2) NSCLC. By addressing an unmet need in this population and outperforming prior dual-modality approaches, our findings support further investigation of this regimen in randomized trials.

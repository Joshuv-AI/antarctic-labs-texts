Dermoscopic Features of Porocarcinoma: Report of 11 Cases and Review of the Literature
Authors: Kodai Miyamoto, Teruki Yanagi, Takuya Maeda, Keiko Tokuchi, Che Yuan Hsu, Hideyuki Ujiie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Porocarcinoma is a rare skin cancer that originates in cutaneous intraepidermal ducts of the sweat glands. Although dermoscopy is often helpful to make a diagnosis of skin tumors such as melanoma, dermoscopic features of porocarcinoma have not been investigated in detail. We aimed to identify dermoscopic features of porocarcinoma and to determine if they are distinguishable from other diseases. We examined the dermoscopic features of 11 cases of porocarcinoma, which were diagnosed using histopathological observations. Also, we reviewed previous case reports describing dermoscopic characteristics of porocarcinoma. Pink-white structureless areas were observed focally in all 11 cases. Ten cases showed polymorphous vascular patterns, in which two or more vascular patterns, such as linear-irregular, glomerular, dotted, and hairpin vessels, were present. None of the leaf-like vessels, coiled vessels, arborizing vessels, comedo-like openings, milia-like cysts, cerebriform patterns, or pigment networks were observed in any cases. In the previously reported 14 cases of porocarcinoma, the “white-to-pink halo” and the “polymorphous vascular pattern” were the most frequent dermoscopic findings (13 cases). “Pink-white structureless areas” was the third most common finding (11 cases). In cases displaying polymorphous vascular patterns, the main component was linear-irregular vessels (9 cases). Our 11 and 14 reported cases suggest that “pink-white structureless areas” together with “polymorphous vascular patterns” should be considered critical dermoscopic features of porocarcinoma. The findings could be helpful to distinguish porocarcinoma from tumors like eccrine poroma.

Introduction

Porocarcinoma (PC), also referred to as eccrine porocarcinoma and malignant eccrine poroma, primarily affects the elderly population.[12] PC is known to be very rare, and it originates in the cutaneous intraepidermal ducts of the sweat glands.[2] While its etiology remains elusive, some studies have suggested that PC may be derived from benign eccrine poroma.[3] Diagnosing PC is hard because it has many different clinical and histological characteristics.[4] In particular, amelanotic melanoma (AM), basal cell carcinoma (BCC), eccrine poroma, seborrheic keratosis (SK), and other adnexal tumors are challenging to differentiate.[5] There have been only a few reports about the dermoscopic features of PC, even though dermoscopy is a useful tool for looking at tumor morphology.[567891011121314] Here, we examine the characteristics of the dermoscopic features in our 11 cases of PC and review similar cases previously reported in English.

Materials and Methods

This retrospective study included all cases of histopathologically confirmed PC in the Department of Dermatology, Hokkaido University Hospital (Sapporo, Japan) from October 2005 to April 2023. The lesions were examined with dermoscopes along with ultrasound gels. Dermoscopic images were captured using dermoscopes (Derma9500S-GR; Derma Medical Inc., Japan and DZ-D100; CASIO, Japan), and analyses of dermoscopic images were performed by two specialists (K.M. and T.Y.). They independently assessed the presence of the following dermoscopic features: vascular patterns (linear-irregular, glomerular, hairpin, dotted, leaf-like, coiled, and arborizing vessels), features of melanocytic or nonmelanocytic diseases (blue-gray globules, pigment networks, dots/globules, streaks, and spoke wheel areas), and additional features (comedo-like openings, milia-like cysts, cerebriform patterns, and leaf-like structures). When their initial assessments did not match, the two observers engaged in discussion to reach a consensus regarding the presence of specific dermoscopic features. Given the limited number of patients included in this study, we performed descriptive analyses to establish the frequency of each dermoscopic feature.

Results

We collected data for 11 cases of PC diagnosed in our department. All subjects, five women and six men were Japanese; their median age was 77.0 years (range 63–88). All patients had been referred to our department due to their lesions and had no history of malignant skin tumors. Eight patients had been aware of the lesions at least one year before their referrals. Concerning the locations of the PCs, six of the 11 lesions were on the thigh, two were on the lower leg, one on the foot, one on the lower abdomen, and one on the temporal region. Clinical differential diagnoses included BCC, eccrine poroma, SCC, and SK. Representative dermoscopic images of PC are shown in Figure 1 (Case #1 and #2. All photos are shown in Supplemental Figure 1). Additionally, we summarized clinical data, dermoscopic findings, and histopathological features in Table 1. Regarding dermoscopic features of PC lesions, “pink-white structureless areas” were observed in all cases. “White-to-pink halos” were found in five cases. “Polymorphous vascular patterns (presence of two or more vascular findings)” were also commonly seen in ten of the 11 cases. Of particular note, the polymorphous vascular patterns in ten cases included linear-irregular vessels in nine and glomerular vessels in nine. Both dotted vessels and hairpin vessels were observed in five cases. Blue-grey globules were seen in two cases. None of the leaf-like vessels, coiled vessels, arborizing vessels (capillary dilatation running in a meandering pattern), comedo-like openings (small depression filled with keratin), milia-like cysts (multiple small whitish granules in brown plaque), cerebriform patterns (brain-like depressions on the tumor surface), or pigment networks (blackish brown reticulate pigmentation) were observed in any cases.

We then investigated the histopathology of the “pink-white structureless areas.” The HE staining revealed small, monomorphous poroid cells without atypia, forming a mesh-like structure with cuticular cells [Figure 2a, Case #3]. Discussion

PC was initially described as “epidermotropic eccrine carcinoma” in 1963 by Pinkus and Mehregan.[15] In 1969, Mishima and Morioka published a report on “Eccrine porocarcinoma,” focusing on the histopathologic changes from the benign to malignant forms of eccrine poroma.[16] A clinicopathological review of 69 PC cases revealed that 18% displayed adjacent features typical of eccrine poroma.[3] PC was reported to affect predominantly patients in their 70s and 80s, and the incidence rate of PC seemed to show no difference between the sexes.[1] Concerning the location of PC, the lower extremities were thought to be the site of preference in about 50% of cases.[3] The above characteristics of PC are generally consistent with our present cases [Table 1].

PC has various clinical and histological features; therefore, the diagnosis is challenging.[4] Dermoscopy is a helpful tool for observing tumor morphology; however, due to its rarity, few studies have addressed the dermoscopic features of PC. To our knowledge, there have been only 14 PC cases in which detailed dermoscopic findings were described [Table 2].[567891011121314] In the reported cases, the most commonly observed features were the “white-to-pink halo” and “polymorphous vascular pattern” (both present in 13 of 14 cases). “Pink-white structureless areas” was the third most prevalent finding (11 of 14 cases). Regarding polymorphous vascular patterns, the most common observation was linear-irregular vessel patterns (9 cases). Pink-white structureless areas and polymorphous vascular patterns are observed in more than 90% of the PC cases in our study; thus, we consider these combined features as potentially specific to PC.

The combination of “white-to-pink halo” and “pink-white structureless areas” was previously reported as a specific dermoscopic feature associated with eccrine poroma. These findings were observed throughout the tumor and likened to a “frog eggs-like appearance.”[17] Minagawa et al. examined eccrine poroma dermoscopically and concluded that these areas correspond to a mesh-like proliferation of tumor cells.[17] They also noted that the mesh-like structure had a rich vascularity and edematous stroma beneath the epidermis.[17] These characteristics are often observed in PC, however, only in a subset of the tumor. Minagawa et al.[17] also referred to polymorphous vascular patterns in eccrine poroma, which were not common dermoscopic findings (30%). The combination of “white-to-pink halo” and “pink-white structureless areas” together with polymorphous vascular patterns in tumors would help to distinguish PC from eccrine poroma.

The absence of dermoscopic features specific to SK or BCC also supports the diagnosis of PC.[1819] Altamura et al.[18] reported that 84% of nonpigmented basal cell carcinomas showed arborizing vessels. Concerning SK, Braun et al.[19] reported that comedo-like openings and milia-like cysts were seen in 91% and 86% of cases, respectively. In our cases, neither arborizing vessels nor comedo-like openings were observed, which is consistent with previous studies.

In summary, we examined the dermoscopic features of 11 PC cases, which is the largest number of cases ever investigated in a single study. Our case series and the review of published cases suggest that “pink-white structureless areas” together with “polymorphous vascular patterns” could be the primary dermoscopic feature of PC.

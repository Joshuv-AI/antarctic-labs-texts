Phase II randomised, placebo-controlled study to evaluate the efficacy and safety of an MK2 inhibitor in ankylosing spondylitis
Authors: Walter P Maksymowych, Anusha Yeshokumar, Elizabeth Cerullo, Robert G W Lambert, Paula Śliwinska-Stańczyk, Piotr Klimiuk, Rebecca Kepich, Chahin Pachai, Min Zhu, Jinqi Liu, Jessica Landis, Vineet Goti, Steven Greenberg
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground/PurposeCC-99677 (BMS-986371) is a novel, small-molecule covalent inhibitor of mitogen-activated protein kinase-activated protein kinase 2 (MK2). We aimed to evaluate the dose-dependent efficacy and safety of CC-99677 compared with placebo in subjects with ankylosing spondylitis (AS).MethodsThis was a phase II, multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to assess the efficacy and safety of CC-99677 in subjects with AS. Subjects were randomised 1:1:1 to once-daily CC-99677 150 mg, CC-99677 60 mg or placebo. Main inclusion criteria were fulfilment of the modified New York criteria for AS, active symptoms (Bath Ankylosing Spondylitis Disease Activity Index ≥4 and total back pain ≥4) despite ≥2 non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and naïve to biologic disease-modifying anti-inflammatory drugs. The primary end point was Assessment of SpondyloArthritis international Society 20% improvement criteria (ASAS20) response at 12 weeks.Results147 subjects were enrolled and 123 (83.7%) subjects completed treatment in the double-blind, placebo-controlled period. The main reason for discontinuation was study termination based on futility at interim analysis. Baseline characteristics were typical of an AS trial population. Treatment with CC-99677 was generally well-tolerated. ASAS20 and ASAS40 at week 12 were 51.2% and 25.6% in the CC-99677 60 mg group, 56.1% and 34.1% in the 150 mg group and 48.8% and 22.0% in the placebo group. No significant treatment group differences were noted for secondary end points. There were trends in active treatment groups for improvement in MRI spine and sacroiliac joint inflammation scores but minimal inhibition of pro-inflammatory cytokines in serum.ConclusionsInhibition of MK2 by CC-99677 was insufficient to lead to significant clinical benefits in AS.Trial registration numberNCT04947579.

Introduction

Axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA) is a chronic inflammatory disease of the axial and large peripheral joints that typically begins in the third decade of life and therefore requires treatment over several decades. Joint inflammation, first evident on MRI, begins in the sacroiliac joints (SIJ) and may lead to structural changes on radiography.1 The presence of definite radiographic sacroiliitis according to modified New York criteria is used to categorise patients into radiographic and non-radiographic subsets of axSpA (nr-axSpA), the former also being termed ankylosing spondylitis (AS).2 However, the disease is widely considered to comprise a spectrum of severity from nr-axSpA to AS. The concomitant presence of extra-articular inflammation leading to acute attacks of anterior uveitis, intestinal inflammation in the form of Crohn’s disease or ulcerative colitis, or psoriasis, may complicate selection of appropriate treatment.3 Current pharmacological intervention typically begins with non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs).35 When these fail, patients may receive biologic or targeted synthetic disease-modifying anti-inflammatory drugs (bDMARD or tsDMARD, respectively).3 4 For bDMARDs, only agents targeting tumour necrosis factor alpha (TNFα), interleukin-17A (IL-17A) or both IL-17A and IL-17F have demonstrated efficacy, while for tsDMARDs, two Janus kinase inhibitors, tofacitinib and upadacitinib, have been approved for this indication.5 Each class of therapeutic has been associated with major clinical responses in only 40%–50% of patients in randomised placebo-controlled trials (RCTs). Based on observational data, drug retention is 60%–70% after a year.6 Consequently, there exists a major need for additional classes of effective therapeutics for a disease that is typically decades in duration.

The p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase (p38) signalling pathway has been identified as a key regulator of inflammatory cytokine biosynthesis and has historically been considered an attractive target in the treatment of rheumatic diseases.7 Initial attempts to block this pathway using p38 kinase inhibitors in subjects with rheumatoid arthritis (RA) were not successful as initial clinical benefits rapidly waned.8 9 This phenomenon, often described as ‘tachyphylaxis’, was attributed to the potential activity of p38 in activating negative feedback mechanisms.10 It was suggested that targeting pathway members downstream of p38 might circumvent this negative regulatory feedback, which led to the development of an inhibitor of mitogen-activated protein kinase-activated protein kinase-2 (MK2), a direct downstream substrate of p38.10 11 Activated MK2 phosphorylates tristetraprolin (TTP), a protein that binds adenylate-uridylate-rich elements in the 3’ untranslated region of cytokine messenger RNAs (mRNAs) (eg, TNFα) and destabilises them, thereby repressing translation.12 Phosphorylated TTP is displaced by human antigen R, which competes with TTP for binding to the same region of cytokine mRNA but enhances mRNA stability and stimulates translation of pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as TNFα and IL-6. Blocking MK2 and phosphorylation of TTP have been shown to ameliorate streptococcal cell wall-induced arthritis in rats,13 while MK2 knockout mice are resistant to collagen-induced arthritis,14 suggesting that MK2 could be a successful druggable target for diseases such as axSpA.

CC-99677 [(R)-3-((2-chloro-5-(ethoxymethyl)pyrimidin-4-yl)oxy)-10-methyl-9,10,11,12-tetrahydro-8H-[1,4]diazepino[5’,6’:4,5]thieno[3,2-f]quinolin-8-one] is a potent, selective MK2 covalent inhibitor that binds to the ATP-binding site.11 CC-99677 inhibits inflammatory cytokine production, including TNFα, IL-6, monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 (MCP-1) and granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF), from lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-stimulated peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), monocytes and M1-polarised macrophages derived from healthy donors and patients with AS.1115 In contrast to p38 inhibitors, CC-99677 suppressed TNFα production throughout the treatment period, suggesting that MK2 inhibition may circumvent tachyphylaxis associated with p38 inhibitors.15 CC-99677 was safe and well-tolerated in a multiple ascending dose study of 37 healthy subjects, with no serious treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) in any of the 37 subjects among the five dose-level cohorts ranging from 10 to 150 mg of CC-99677.11 It exhibited a linear and predictable pharmacokinetic profile with minimal detectable plasma CC-99677 >24 hours after dosing, indicating limited day-to-day accumulation. Maximal inhibition of TNFα production was 70% in the 150 mg dose cohort and was consistently maintained at approximately 70% during the dosing period. These phase I data provided the rationale and dosing for a phase II RCT in AS.

Methods

Study design

This was a phase II, multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group, efficacy and safety study (figure 1 and online supplemental figure 1). The study was conducted at 63 sites in China, Czech Republic, Germany, Poland, Romania, Spain, Turkey and the USA and the results previously presented.16 This study was designed to assess response to CC-99677 treatment by measuring signs and symptoms of AS, objective measures of disease activity, quality of life assessments, safety and tolerability, pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics over a 12-week double-blind period. This study also aimed to assess the efficacy and long-term safety of CC-99677 in a 52-week long-term extension period. The study plan consisted of up to a 6-week screening period; a 12-week double-blind, placebo-controlled treatment period; a 52-week long-term extension period and a 4-week post-treatment observational follow-up period. In the biologic-naïve main study, subjects with a diagnosis of AS fulfilling the modified New York criteria for AS2 and no prior exposure to biologic treatments for AS were randomised with equal allocation to receive either CC-99677 150 mg administered orally once per day, CC-99677 60 mg administered orally once per day or matching placebo for a duration of 12 weeks. In a separate biologic-failure study, subjects who failed no more than one bDMARD (≥50% for lack of efficacy) were planned to be enrolled in a 2:2:1 ratio, with 20 subjects in each treatment arm receiving either CC-99677 150 mg administered orally once per day or CC-99677 60 mg administered orally once per day, and 10 subjects receiving matching placebo, for a planned 12-week double-blind, placebo-controlled treatment period followed by a 52-week long-term extension period. At week 12, all subjects originally randomised to receive placebo were re-randomised 1:1 to blinded CC-99677 (150 mg or 60 mg administered orally once per day) through week 64 or until early discontinuation. Subjects originally randomised to CC-99677 (150 mg or 60 mg administered orally once per day) continued their current dose through week 64 or until early discontinuation. Subjects who did not achieve an Assessment of SpondyloArthritis international Society 20% improvement criteria (ASAS20) response at week 16 were discontinued from the study. Randomisation to treatment groups was stratified by screening high-sensitivity C reactive protein (hsCRP) concentration (≤upper limit of normal/>upper limit of normal of the reference lab (5.0 mg/L)).

Study Design—Biologic-naïve Main Study. ASAS, Assessment of SpondyloArthritis international Society; IA, interim analysis; LTE, long-term extension.

MRI scan of the entire spine and SIJ was performed at baseline and at 12 weeks with T1-weighted and short tau inversion recovery sequences, in the sagittal and coronal planes, respectively, as per standard protocol for clinical trials of spondyloarthritis. MRI scans were centrally collected, quality controlled and assessed by two independent readers. In case of significant discrepancies among primary readers, the cases were adjudicated by a third independent reader.

A prespecified interim analysis (IA) was conducted by a Steering Committee, which included unblinded Bristol Myers Squibb committee members independent of the Study Team and an external expert in axSpA. The purpose of the IA was to determine whether to continue the study without modification; to terminate the study for futility; to discontinue a treatment group based on preliminary assessment of dose-dependent risk-benefit and whether to add an additional dose cohort (dose not to exceed 150 mg).

Study eligibility criteria

Main criteria for inclusion for the biologic-naïve main study included: adult male and female subjects ≥18 years of age with a diagnosis of AS fulfilling the modified New York criteria for AS,2 symptoms of active disease based on a Bath AS Disease Activity Index (BASDAI) score ≥417 and a total back pain Numerical Rating Scale (NRS) score ≥4. Subjects must have failed prior treatment with at least two NSAIDs (at the maximum tolerated dose) for at least 4 weeks each, with documented inadequate response. Concomitant use of one NSAID or cyclooxygenase 2 inhibitor for AS was permitted up to the maximum recommended dose as per local guidelines provided that the subject had received a stable dose 2 weeks prior to baseline and subject agreed to maintaining this dose until week 12, unless dose was reduced or treatment was discontinued due to safety and/or tolerability concerns. Subjects could receive NSAIDs or low-dose corticosteroids, provided they were in accordance with eligibility criteria and permitted concomitant medications. The biologic-failure study recruited subjects who had failed not more than one biologic agent taken for AS due to inadequate response to an approved dose for at least 12 weeks and/or unacceptable safety/tolerability with at least one dose of a biologic agent (in the opinion of the Investigator). Of these subjects, a minimum of 50% of biologic-failure subjects were recruited due to inadequate efficacy response. Concomitant use of sulfasalazine was permitted, provided sulfasalazine was used at a stable dose for at least 4 weeks prior to baseline. In case the subject required as needed medications for pain, acetaminophen/paracetamol and/or low-strength opioid analgesics up to the maximum recommended doses per local guidelines could be used. Exclusion criteria were total radiographic ankylosis, active inflammatory bowel disease within 6 months of screening visit, concomitant fibromyalgia, use of oral corticosteroids (prednisone or equivalent) >10 mg/day for ≥2 weeks prior to the baseline visit.

Study objectives and end points

The primary objective of the study was to evaluate the dose-dependent efficacy of oral CC-99677, administered once per day, compared with placebo, as determined by the attainment of an ASAS20 response18 after 12 weeks of treatment, in subjects with radiologically confirmed AS and inadequate response to NSAIDs. Secondary objectives were to evaluate the effects of oral CC-99677, administered once per day, compared with placebo, after 12 weeks of treatment in subjects with AS, on:

the signs and symptoms of AS, as assessed by attainment of an ASAS40 response,19 AS Disease Activity Score with CRP (ASDAS-CRP)20 and BASDAI;

the signs and symptoms of AS, as assessed by attainment of an ASAS40 response,19 AS Disease Activity Score with CRP (ASDAS-CRP)20 and BASDAI;

measures of physical function, as assessed by the Bath Ankylosing Spondylitis Functional Index (BASFI)21;

measures of physical function, as assessed by the Bath Ankylosing Spondylitis Functional Index (BASFI)21;

spinal and SIJ inflammation on MRI as measured by the Spondyloarthritis Research Consortium of Canada (SPARCC) MRI scores of SIJs22 and spine23;

per cent change from baseline in hsCRP;

The primary end point was the proportion of subjects who achieved an ASAS20 response at 12 weeks, defined as ≥20% and ≥1 unit in at least three of the four ASAS domains on a scale of 0–10, and no worsening from baseline of ≥20% and ≥1 unit in the remaining domain on a scale of 0–10. The four ASAS domains comprised Patient Global Assessment of Disease (0–10 unit NRS, total back pain (0–10 score (NRS)), BASFI (0–10 score (NRS)) and inflammation (mean of BASDAI questions #5 and #6 for morning stiffness (0–10 score (NRS)).

Secondary end points were ASAS40 response at week 12, change in ASDAS-CRP at week 12, change in BASDAI at week 12, change in BASFI at week 12, change in the SPARCC MRI SIJ score at week 12, change in SPARCC MRI spine score at week 12, change in CRP at week 12 and incidence of adverse events (AEs). Exploratory end points included adverse events of special interest (AESI) comprising photosensitivity and hepatic enzyme elevations, baseline to 12-week change in spinal mobility (Bath AS Mobility Index linear),24 44 swollen joint count, Maastricht enthesitis score,25 Quality of Life (Ankylosing Spondylitis Quality of Life (ASQoL)),26 Short Form-36 V.2,27 ASAS Health Index28 and change from baseline in serum cytokines (TNFα, IL-6, IL17-A, MCP-1). The assays for cytokine quantification are summarised in the online supplemental table.

Statistical analyses

A total of 147 subjects were planned to be randomised with 49 subjects assigned to each treatment group for the biologic-naïve main study. This sample size provided 80% power, calculated by nQuery Advisor V.7 and without accounting for multiplicity, to detect a treatment difference of 25% in ASAS20 response rate at week 12 between either active treatment group and placebo, using a χ2 test at a two-sided significance level of 0.10, and assuming the response rates of 59% for both CC-99677 treatment groups and 34% for placebo, based on historical studies. Applying the Hochberg procedure to adjust for the multiplicity of the comparisons of two active treatment groups with placebo, the study provided 81% power to achieve statistical significance at the two-sided significance level of 0.10 for at least one treatment group, with the same response rate assumptions stated above. The biologic-failure study was exploratory and no power calculation was conducted. Binary end points were analysed by the Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test stratified by the randomisation stratification factor hsCRP concentration (≤upper limit of normal/>upper limit of normal) obtained at screening (visit 1). The primary missing data handling approach for binary end points was non-responder imputation, by which a subject was considered a non-responder at a given time point if the subject did not have sufficient data (including the baseline data for the end points assessing the change from baseline) assessed within the analysis window of the time point for response determination. The continuous end points were analysed by an adaptive approach29 that used either a longitudinal data analysis model30 (in the absence of severe departures from normality) or robust regression model/non-parametric methods (in the presence of severe departures from normality). hsCRP was analysed using a mixed effects model with log-transformed postbaseline values minus log-transformed baseline values as dependent variables and visit treatment, visit by treatment interaction as fixed effects and log-transformed baseline values as covariates.

Results

Subject disposition

A total of 378 subjects were screened. Of these, 211 (55.8%) subjects were screen failures. The first subject, first visit, for this study took place on 25 August 2021 and the last subject, last visit took place on 21 February 2023. Following an IA, the study was terminated due to futility on 23 November 2022; the timing of the last subject’s last visit reflected the logistical realities of scheduling follow-up visits after treatment discontinuation. The termination was not related to any observed AEs or perceived safety findings associated with CC-99677. A total of 147 subjects were enrolled: 49 subjects in CC-99677 60 mg group, 49 subjects in CC-99677 150 mg group and 49 subjects in placebo group. A total of 123 (83.7%) subjects completed treatment in the double-blind, 12-week placebo-controlled period and 24 (16.3%) subjects discontinued treatment (figure 2). The main reason for discontinuation was study termination by sponsor. Two (1.4%) subjects completed the long-term extension period. In the biologic-failure substudy, a total of 20 subjects were enrolled: seven subjects in CC-99677 60 mg group, eight subjects in CC-99677 150 mg group, five subjects in placebo group (online supplemental figure 2). A total of 15 (75.0%) subjects completed treatment in the double-blind, 12-week placebo-controlled period and five (25.0%) subjects discontinued treatment. In the biologic-naïve main study, the mean (SD) treatment duration during the double-blind, placebo-controlled period was 10.99 (2.66) weeks in the CC-99677 60 mg group and 10.74 (3.04) in the CC-99677 150 mg group. In the biologic-failure substudy, the mean (SD) treatment duration during the double-blind, placebo-controlled period was 10.86 (3.03) weeks in the CC-99677 60 mg group and 10.93 (3.57) in the CC-99677 150 mg group.

Disposition of Subjects in Biologic-naïve Main Study.

Subject characteristics

These were typical of an AS population recruited to an RCT (table 1). In the biologic-naïve main study, the median age was 43 years, ranging from 21 to 65 years. A total of 121 (82.3%) subjects were male. The majority of the subjects (n=144 (98.0%)) were white. In the biologic-failure substudy, the median age was 47 years, ranging from 27 to 57 years. A total of 18 (90.0%) subjects were male. The majority were human leucocyte antigen B27 (HLA-B27) positive and had active disease at baseline according to high BASDAI and ASDAS scores. SPARCC MRI SIJ and spine inflammation scores at baseline were comparable to those observed in recent RCTs.

Efficacy outcomes

In the biologic-naïve main study, the proportion of subjects who achieved ASAS20 at week 12 was 51.2% in the CC-99677 60 mg group and 56.1% in the CC-99677 150 mg group compared with 48.8% in the placebo group. Small numerical benefits on secondary clinical end points in the biologic-naïve main study were observed for ASAS40, BASDAI, BASFI and ASDAS-CRP, which were dose-dependent for ASAS40, BASDAI and BASFI (table 2). Additional exploratory clinical end points demonstrated small trends on benefits for the 150 mg dose group (table 2). The proportion of subjects who achieved ASAS40 at week 12 was 25.6% in the CC-99677 60 mg group and 34.1% in the CC-99677 150 mg group compared with 22.0% in the placebo group. The adjusted mean (SD) change from baseline at week 12 in ASDAS-CRP was −0.85 (0.12) in the CC-99677 60 mg group and −0.80 (0.12) in the CC-99677 150 mg group compared with −0.68 (0.12) in the placebo group. The proportion of subjects who achieved clinically important improvement in ASDAS-CRP (change in ASDAS-CRP ≥−1.1)20 at week 12 was 27.9% in the CC-99677 60 mg group and 34.1% in the CC-99677 150 mg group compared with 27.5% in the placebo group. The proportion of subjects who achieved major improvement in ASDAS-CRP (change in ASDAS-CRP ≥−2.0)20 at week 12 was 7.0% in the CC-99677 60 mg group and 12.2% in the CC-99677 150 mg group compared with 5.0% in the placebo group. The proportion of subjects who achieved inactive disease or low disease activity (ADAS-CRP <1.3 or <2.1, respectively) at week 12 was 20.9% in the CC-99677 60 mg group and 19.5% in the CC-99677 150 mg group compared with 11.9% in the placebo group. The adjusted mean (SD) change from baseline at week 12 in BASDAI score was −1.90 (0.27) in the CC-99677 60 mg group and −2.09 (0.28) in the CC-99677 150 mg group, compared with −1.92 (0.28) in the placebo group. The adjusted mean (SD) change from baseline at week 12 in BASFI score was −1.32 (0.30) in the CC-99677 60 mg group and −1.13 (0.30) in the CC-99677 150 mg group, compared with −1.38 (0.31) in the placebo group.

For the substudy of subjects with prior exposure to bDMARD, there were no notable differences in efficacy outcomes between treatment groups.

Additional pharmacodynamic data

There were small but insignificant mean changes in several biomarkers, such as IL-6 and MCP-1, which showed numerical decreases in CC-99677-treated subjects compared with placebo (figure 3A,B,D), and an apparent dose-dependent mean increase in IL-17A (figure 3C). Safety results

In the double-blind, 12-week placebo-controlled period, a total of 55 of 147 (37.4%) subjects (24 of 49 (49.0%) subjects in CC-99677 60 mg group, 14 of 49 (28.6%) subjects in CC-99677 150 mg group and 17 of 49 (34.7%) subjects in placebo group) had at least one TEAE, which translated into exposure-adjusted incidence rate (EAIR) of 225.7 per 100 subject-years (table 3). Of these, 17 of 147 (11.6%) subjects had at least one TEAE (eight of 49 (16.3%) subjects in CC-99677 60 mg group, five of 49 (10.2%) subjects in CC-99677 150 mg group and four of 49 (8.2%) subjects in placebo group) assessed as related to study drug by the investigator. In the biologic-failure substudy, during the double-blind, 12-week placebo-controlled period, a total of eight of 20 (40.0%) subjects had at least one TEAE (three of seven (42.9%) subjects in CC-99677 60 mg group, four of eight (50.0%) subjects in CC-99677 150 mg group and one of five (20.0%) subjects in placebo group), which translated into EAIR of 263.7 per 100 subject-years. Of these, five (25.0%) subjects had at least one TEAE assessed as related to study drug by the investigator.

A total of 10 serious TEAEs were reported in eight subjects during the study. In the biologic-naïve main study, during the double-blind, 12-week placebo-controlled period, three of 147 (2.0%) subjects had SAEs (myocardial infarction, asthenia and clavicle fracture in placebo group), while in the biologic-failure substudy, two of 20 (10.0%) subjects had SAEs (road traffic accident and concussion in CC-99677 150 mg group and psychotic disorder in placebo group). During the overall weeks 0–64 CC-99677 exposure period, one of 140 (0.7%) subjects had SAE (neoplasm metastases to peritoneum in the CC-99677 150 mg group; assessed as not related to study drug) in the biologic-naïve main study, and three of 18 (16.7%) subjects had SAEs (oral candidiasis, ulcerative colitis, concussion and road traffic accident in the CC-99677 150 mg group) in the biologic-failure substudy. The SAEs of psychotic disorder (in placebo group) and oral candidiasis (in CC-99677 150 mg group) in one subject each were assessed to be related to the study drug by the investigator; the SAEs of concussion and road traffic accident were assessed as not related to study drug. The subject who developed neoplastic metastases in the peritoneum during the double-blind period died during the long-term extension period.

In the biologic-naïve main study, haematology parameters remained stable during treatment with CC-99677, except mean (SD) haemoglobin, where mild reduction was observed at week 12 (–0.6 (8.87) g/L in the CC-99677 60 mg group and −2.4 (6.43) g/L in the CC-99677 150 mg group vs 0.5 (7.64) g/L in the placebo group). Mean (SD) platelet counts were mildly reduced at week 12 (–8.2 (39.14) 109/L in the CC-99677 60 mg group and −5.0 (33.25) 109/L in the CC-99677 150 mg group vs 5.0 (33.57) 109/L in the placebo group). No AESIs were reported in the biologic-failure substudy during the double-blind, 12-week placebo-controlled period. During the overall weeks 0–64 CC-99677 exposure period, three of 140 (2.1%) subjects had AESIs (no cases of photosensitivity and three cases of elevated hepatic enzymes, all in the CC-997677 60 mg group, as noted above) in the biologic-naïve main study, and one of 18 (5.6%) subjects had an AESI (hepatic enzyme increase with ALT 5× ULN and AST 2× ULN at peak in the CC-997677 150 mg group) in the biologic-failure substudy.

Discussion

Treatment with CC-99677 was generally safe and well-tolerated during the double-blind, 12-week placebo-controlled period but efficacy was not evident at the doses tested. Baseline characteristics were typical of subjects with AS recently recruited to clinical trials of bDMARDs and tsDMARDs and had active disease according to both clinical and MRI parameters of inflammation. Although ASAS20 responses were observed in about half of subjects, this was similarly evident among all three treatment groups. Despite slight trends in improvement in the more stringent clinical response measures of ASAS40 and ASDAS MI in the 150 mg dose group, there were no significant treatment differences in these or other secondary outcome measures. There were also no significant differences among continuous clinical measures such as the BASDAI, BASFI and ASDAS. For objective parameters of inflammation, there was little change in CRP or MRI inflammation scores in subjects receiving active therapy. While a dose-dependent trend was observed for reduction in MRI inflammation, this was considerably less than that observed in trials of TNF inhibitors and IL-17 inhibitors bDMARDs or the tsDMARDs filgotinib or upadacitinib.

The high ASAS20 placebo response of 48.8% was higher than in prior AS RCTs but a geographic effect was not observed in this RCT. A recent systematic literature review and meta-regression analysis have reported that the treatment effect of therapeutics in RCTs of AS and nr-axSpA have declined over time for both the ASAS20 and ASAS40 outcomes, driven by an increased placebo response for the ASAS20 and a decline in treatment response for ASAS40.31 In contrast, ASDAS-based treatment effects were stable. This report did not identify factors previously shown to influence placebo responses in RCTs of RA, psoriatic arthritis (PsA) and osteoarthritis, such as female sex, baseline disease activity and recruitment geography. Some degree of diagnostic misclassification could result in recruitment of certain subjects with higher expectation bias driving the ASAS20 placebo response and, also, diminish treatment group differences. However, baseline characteristics of the study population resembled those reported for prior RCTs in AS. Moreover, baseline disease activity assessed by the ASDAS and SPARCC MRI scores was comparable to that reported in previous RCTs of AS, and ASDAS response outcomes were not significantly different between treatment groups at the primary end point.

Studies of MK2 knockout mice indicated that MK2 could be an attractive druggable target since these mice appear normal but resist LPS-induced endotoxic shock,32 exhibit reduced levels of TNFα, IL-6 and interferon-γ and are resistant to inflammatory disease in numerous models, including a collagen-induced arthritis model.14 Moreover, CC-99677 demonstrated a cytokine inhibition profile different from that of p38 inhibitors and when delivered at doses aimed at achieving submaximal and maximal MK2 target occupancy, elicited dose-dependent inhibition of paw swelling comparable to tofacitinib, in an HLA-B27 transgenic model of AS in rats.11 The geometric mean Cmax at the 150 mg dose level corresponds to approximately 0.8 μM concentration of CC-99677, where significant inhibition of TNFα and other cytokines was observed in preclinical in vitro assays.15 In an earlier phase I study in human volunteers, CC-99677 demonstrated dose-dependent increases in MK2 occupancy between 10 and 120 mg, which plateaued at ~70% after 5 days of daily dosing at the 120 and 150 mg dose levels.15 The relationship between dose and target engagement was not linear and doses >120 mg did not translate into higher rates of target engagement. But higher doses could lead to additional potential off-target safety liabilities. Moreover, TNFα protein secretion from ex vivo LPS-stimulated whole blood from healthy volunteers was inhibited within 4 hours after a single dose of CC-99677 from 10 to 400 mg. At the 150 mg dose level, the level of target engagement that was achieved (~70%) was sufficient to inhibit paw swelling comparable to tofacitinib in rat HLA-B27 transgenic AS model in rats.11 These data would suggest that CC-99677 dosing and MK2 target engagement should have been adequate to demonstrate a therapeutic effect in subjects with AS in this trial. Furthermore, IL-17A is an MK2 regulated cytokine and inhibition with CC-99677 resulted in reduced TNFα and IL-17A/F production in in vitro-stimulated PBMCs from subjects with PsA and in an animal model of psoriasis and PsA.14 33

C-99677 covalently binds to MK2 and irreversibly inhibits its kinase activity. Restoration of MK2 kinase activity requires de novo synthesis of MK2; therefore, a rapid rate of resynthesis could limit the biological efficacy of this agent. However, preclinical data demonstrated a slow MK2 resynthesis rate (eg, ~40 hours in PBMCs and THP-1 cells), and although the half-life of CC-99677 in humans is only 2–7 hours, depending on dose, suppression of LPS-induced TNFα secretion was observed for up to 48 hours after the last dose in healthy individuals.11 Therefore, it is unlikely that newly synthesised MK2 occurring towards the end of the dosing interval could explain inadequate efficacy observed in this phase II RCT. Rather, these results suggest that blockade of MK2, as achieved with CC-99677 at the tested doses, is insufficient for controlling inflammation in axSpA. Other cytokines and chemokines are inhibited by CC-99677, but with greater variability compared with TNFα. For example, maximal inhibition of ~50% was observed for IL-6 and Macrophage Inflammatory Protein-1 secretion at the 150 mg dose level in healthy individuals at day 5,11 and this level of inhibition in subjects with AS may be inadequate for therapeutic efficacy.

Although no evidence implicates MK2 in a complex feedback pathway, like that of p38, its activation does actuate signal feedback that could limit inflammation at the level of TTP-regulated translation or mRNA stability.12 In particular, it has been shown that MK2 can stimulate the de novo synthesis of repressive non-phosphorylated TTP so that MK2 inhibition may lead to reduced levels of TTP and the resumption of inflammation.34 Moreover, MK2 also regulates the translation of anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10 and the suppressor of cytokine signalling-3, which may decrease after inhibition of MK2.35 36 Nevertheless, CC-99677 did not elicit evidence of tachyphylaxis based on clinical end points in subjects with AS and preclinical work demonstrated that it was biochemically selective when tested against a panel of 364 kinases when ATP was added equal to the KM for ATP for each kinase.11 37 Moreover, no significant inhibition was observed for the isoforms of p38 that were tested.11 CC-99677 potently inhibited MCP-1, TNFα and GM-CSF and moderately inhibited IL-6, displaying broader anti-inflammatory inhibitory activity on monocytic activation than either p38 inhibitors or tofacitinib.11 In contrast to p38 inhibitors, MK2 inhibitors were not associated with increased and sustained activation of JNK.38 However, effects of MK2 inhibitors on cells of the innate immune system have not been reported and these may be important drivers of inflammation in subjects with AS.

In conclusion, CC-99677 is a potent covalent inhibitor of MK2 that significantly impairs production of pro-inflammatory cytokines in an animal model of AS and in healthy humans. Its administration to subjects with AS with active symptoms and objective evidence of inflammation refractory to NSAIDs in this RCT did not demonstrate clinical efficacy after 12 weeks of administration, although treatment was well tolerated.

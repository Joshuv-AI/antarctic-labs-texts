Case report of Hand salvage post burn using pedicled tensor fascia lata (TFL)
Authors: Momen Mohamed, Nagla Mustafa, Sara Kamal
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Introduction and importanceBurn still the major cause of disabilities and challenge facing both patient and surgeon, and when hands were affected a comprehensive planning and work must be obtained to restore functional and aesthetic aspects of hand. Tensor fascia lata (TFL) considered since centuries a useful tool to save hand post burn either it was been harvested with pedicle or as free flap. Although pedicle TFL became seldom but it may be the best option for hand reconstruction. In this article we report a case of hand burn that was managed by pedicle TFL.Case presentationA 32 year- old man was involved in thermal burn affected multiple areas of his torso and limbs. Introduction

Burn is one of the most unavoidable tragedies that lead to disastrous outcomes. Deep thermal burn is still an enemy and a challenge to plastic surgeons as it hides extension and eventually, after debridement. Defects raised by full-thickness burns demand complex reconstruction to restore function, volume, and durability of appearance. The dorsum of the hand is a valuable part of the hand and has special features in the form of thin, mobile, sensitive skin covering the extensor tendons of the finger and complex joints. All these features will be diminished after a deep burn and will need to be restored functionally and aesthetically (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Preoperative image after debridement shows defect of the dorsum of the left hand with exposed phalangeal bones and skin defect over the abdomen.Fig. 1

Local, distend, and free flaps are all used to mimic a normal hand; distend and free flaps came before each other recently, but viability of the limb, extension of damage, and donor site influence the process of management. Although options are many but in burn victims when there was extensive limited healthy donor control selection of flap. Tensor fasciae latae is a well-known flap for reconstruction that is used as a cover and functional tool for tendon repair. Its use is inferior to other flaps but in some cases may be forced to use it and the outcome remains good [1,2,3] (Fig. 2).Fig. 2Burn at the abdomen + small patches of skin graft.Fig. 2

Case presentation

A 32-year-old black man, right handed works as merchant. Presented as a victim of burns in multiple areas (abdomen, lower back, and left forearm and hand). He was involved in a thermal burn and referred to the hospital from the primary health care unit, where he received first care. He has no chronic illness, no history of hospitalization, and not known to be allergic to any medication. Also he is not smoker or alcohol consumer (Fig. 3).Fig. 3TFL Flap inset.Fig. 3

TFL Flap inset.

He was admitted to the hospital, and a secondary survey estimated the burn at 36 % TSBA. After initial treatment, by establishing secure venous lines for resuscitation, withdrawing blood for corss- matching and investigations (Full blood count, Urea, Creatinine, blood glucose). He was hemodynamic stable, and investigation show normal parameters apart from hemoglobin (8 g/dL) which corrected by transfusion of 4 units of blood (Fig. 4).Fig. 4Graft of the donor site (left thigh).Fig. 4

Graft of the donor site (left thigh).

On examination the thickness of the burn was evaluated and ranged from superficial batches at the chest and face to deep ones at the abdomen, lower back, and left forearm, and full thickness at the dorsum of the left hand. The left hand, there is eschar extending from the wrist proximally to the distal IPJ of the index, middle, and ring fingers, while the small finger appears gangrenous. Burn extends proximally to mid-forearm and is semi-circumferential from second degree to third degree when approached distally. The palmar aspect of the hand and thumb were spared, and flexion was preserved.

He underwent multiple staged surgeries, two of which were debridement, one skin graft of the trunk and forearm, and the last two TFL flaps followed by division all of operations done by plastic specialist. At first debridement; 3 days after presentation, the dorsum aspect was exposed, extensor tendons were sacrificed, metacarpal bones were shown, and the little finger was unfortunately amputated. Swap for culture and sensitivity done at this stage which grew Pseudomonas aeruginosa which treated by 3rd generation cephalosporin 1 g intravenous twice per day for 4 days. Then he was admitted to ward again for mentoring and regular wound dressing. A second debridement two weeks later, the TFL musclo-cutaneous flap was raised and applied to the defect after final session of debridement, and found to be hemodynamic stable with normal investigations result and no growth of any organism from last wound swap.

Technique: surgical marking was done while patient standing and lying supine to choice the best comfortable position for patient as hand will be fixed to thigh for three weeks. Incision was made distally, and through subcutaneous tissue and deep to fascia, then sub- fascial plane followed proximally with coagulation of small perforators and dissection carried on proximally to descending branch of lateral femoral circumflex artery. Hand was positioned beneath flap then flap spread over dorsum of hand.

He return back to ward and discharge after week and he were followed at outpatient clinic weekly. Three weeks later, the flap was divided and expanded over the defect, and the donor site was closed by a split-thickness skin graft, two days later he discharged of hospital and was seen weekly at plastic clinic. Fortunately, flap survived at dorsum of hand and grafted donor sit at lateral left thigh healed without complication, also graft at abdomen and back was partial lost and kept on regular wound dressing, because patient refuse another session of skin graft.

Patient was counseled for another surgery for extensor tendon web space reconstruction, but he refused more surgeries. He underwent physiotherapy for fingers mobility and Interphalangeal joint movement preserved, with restriction in metacarbo-phalangeal joint movement. Unfortunately the photography for distant result is not available.

This work has been reported in line with SCARE criteria.

Discussion

The hand is the most demanding organ functionally and cosmetically, and any minor dysfunction can be expressed negatively on an individual. This puts a lot of burden on surgeons to restore the original hand or at least important movements [4]. Different options of flap are discussed, and sophisticated micro reconstruction becomes the master, but this technology is not available at any facility, so restyling old methods to reconstruct the hand must be considered [5].

Hand resurfacing was made by local flaps such as radial forearm and posterior interossous flaps; groin flap and finally free flap such as Anterolateral thigh (ALT) flap. In our case, abdominal and regional flaps were considered as they are gold stander for hand reconstruction, but all these areas were affected by thermal burns, while free flap was thought to be difficult in our hospital beside a previously assaulted limb and the only suitable option was a pedicled TFL. Also bridging processes such as negative pressure wound therapy (NPWN) and dermal substitutes are proved to be useful tools to prepare wound for definitive reconstruction as M. Faenza et al., reported using NPWT and Integra together as conservative management for extravasation injury of hand with chemotherapy [4]. Although former options are effective healing promoting options but are not available as this report from developing country. David J. Barillo et al. stated that a distant flap is a safe, acceptable method for hand reconstruction when superior operations such as microsurgery and local flap are not applicable [1]. Ganga Bhavani et al., in study of 35 patients of traumatic hand defects by various causes were treated by eight types of flaps, mentioned usage of TFL flap in 3 (8.58 %) [5].

Pedicled TFL is a historical flap characterized by being easily tailored, large, and versatile with content vascularity, which can solve problematic defects at different locations of various etiologies [6,7]. Grant Fankhauser et al., described pedicled TFL flap as salvageable tool for extensive upper limb injures, and reported two cases were treated by delayed pedicled tfl flap, and one of them bilateral TFL were utilized, and advocate this way over groin flap [6]. Using new methods or drawing back to old-fashioned procedures and simple or complex operations is not an issue when patient satisfaction and best judgment are considered. As mentioned, there is no cookbook for reconstruction of the burned hand [8,10].

Although it has benefits, there are major factors that may be considered disadvantages, such as: limb position, which is not comfortable for the patient; loss of a large donor site for a skin graft, as the later thigh is the most common site for harvesting skin; and multiple surgeries to achieve the final result. This report is of resurfacing dorsum of let hand post burn injury under guide of SCARE guidelines [12].

Conclusion

Pediculed TFL flap is demanding reconstruction method, with multiple surgeries, but it is a wise option for reconstruction of the burn hand, fast, easy with predicted acceptable outcome, as this patient with burnt forearm and abdomen and local and distant not valid site, and setup for free flap is not applicable in hospital.

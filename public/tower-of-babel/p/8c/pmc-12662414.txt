Anterior segment optical coherence tomography in corneal diseases: A bibliometric analysis and visualization research of global research trends (1994–2024)
Authors: Mehmet Omer Kiristioglu, Berna Akova, Esin Sogutlu Sari, Mehmet Baykara
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Purpose:This study provides a bibliometric analysis of global research on anterior segment optical coherence tomography (AS-OCT) in corneal diseases, mapping key research trajectories, collaborations, and emerging trends.Methods:A systematic search in the Web of Science Core Collection on January 1, 2025, retrieved 3634 records (1994–2024). After excluding non-English publications, non-ophthalmology studies, and non-corneal research, 2079 publications were analyzed using VOSviewer and CiteSpace for citation networks, coauthorship trends, and keyword co-occurrence.Results:AS-OCT research has grown significantly (Mann–Kendall τ = 0.929, P < .001). The United States led in publications (639 papers, 19,594 citations), followed by China (333 papers, 4502 citations). The University of California was the most productive institution. The first AS-OCT study, published in the Archives of Ophthalmology (1994) by Izatt JA et al, marked the field’s inception. The most cited article was Huang et al’s 1991 Science paper on optical coherence tomography. Recent trends highlight the integration of artificial intelligence, deep learning, and optical coherence elastography in AS-OCT applications. The top 3 contributing journals were Cornea, Journal of Refractive Surgery, and Journal of Cataract and Refractive Surgery. Coauthorship analysis identified Jodhbir S. Mehta and David Huang as central figures in AS-OCT research collaborations.Conclusion:AS-OCT research has expanded significantly, enhancing diagnostics, surgical planning, and disease monitoring. Artificial intelligence-driven analytics and optical coherence elastography are promising future directions. This bibliometric analysis guides advancing AS-OCT research and clinical applications.

1. Introduction

Corneal diseases are a leading cause of visual impairment and blindness worldwide, posing a major healthcare burden.[1,2] Accurate and timely diagnosis is essential for effective management. Advances in imaging, particularly anterior segment optical coherence tomography (AS-OCT), have transformed diagnostic precision by providing noninvasive, high-resolution visualization of corneal microstructures.[3] With expanding clinical applications, AS-OCT has been widely used for early morphological detection, treatment guidance, and disease progression monitoring.[4]

Recent innovations have further broadened AS-OCT applications, including angiography, artificial intelligence–based diagnostics, optical coherence elastography (OCE), and wide-field swept-source imaging.[5–9] To capture how these advances are shaping the field, bibliometric analysis offers a useful approach, combining performance analysis of research output and influence with science mapping of collaborations and thematic structures.[10] Although bibliometrics is widely applied in medicine, no comprehensive study has yet examined the global landscape of AS-OCT research in corneal disease.[11]

To address this gap, we performed a bibliometric analysis of AS-OCT research in corneal disease over the past 3 decades. Our objectives were to identify key research trajectories and emerging trends, map global collaborations and leading research institutions, highlight foundational articles and influential authors, and define future research directions in this rapidly evolving field.

2. Methods

2.1. Database

This bibliometric analysis aimed to systematically evaluate the global research landscape of AS-OCT in corneal diseases. The study analyzed relevant literature published between 1994 and 2024, sourced from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC), as it is widely recognized and commonly utilized by researchers across various disciplines.[12]

2.2. Search strategy

The search was conducted on January 1, 2025, using the following search query:

(TS=(“anterior segment OCT” OR “anterior segment optical coherence tomography” OR “AS-OCT” OR “ASOCT” OR “optical coherence tomography” OR “OCT” OR “optical coherence elastography”) AND TS=(“cornea” OR “corneal diseases” OR “keratoconus” OR “corneal dystrophies” OR “keratitis”))

This search initially retrieved 3634 records. The selection criteria were set to include only articles and review articles, reducing the dataset to 3163 records. After excluding non-English publications, 3039 records remained. Next, studies focusing exclusively on zoological applications of AS-OCT, with no relevance to ophthalmology or corneal diseases (e.g., AS-OCT in marine biology or veterinary species unrelated to preclinical ophthalmology research), were excluded. However, preclinical studies utilizing animal models for corneal imaging were retained, resulting in 2962 records. Further refinement was applied to exclude studies unrelated to corneal diseases, specifically those focusing on contact lenses, glaucoma, the retina, the choroid, uveitis, and the sclera. The final dataset comprised 2079 publications. The study flowchart is presented in Figure 1.

Flowchart of bibliometric analysis workflow.

To assess the reliability of the manual exclusion process, inter-rater agreement was evaluated using Cohen kappa statistic between 2 independent reviewers, both experienced ophthalmologists.

2.3. Data analysis and visualization

The bibliographic data for these 2079 publications were exported in plain text format for analysis. The data included information on publication years, countries, institutions, authors, journals, keywords, and citation metrics. The authors went through the data extraction process and verified any data and the convergence of the same institutes with different names.

The authors conducted the data extraction process and ensured accuracy by checking for any data loss or duplicate entries resulting from human error. Microsoft Excel 2017 (Microsoft, Redmond) was utilized to visualize global publication trends and conduct statistical analyses on the exported files, generating charts and tables highlighting the most prolific countries/regions, institutions, authors, and journals. Bibliometric visualization and analysis were performed using VOSviewer (version 1.6.18; Centre for Science and Technology Studies, Leiden University, Leiden, The Netherlands) and CiteSpace (version 6.3.R1; College of Computing and Informatics, Drexel University, Philadelphia). Each node in the map represents a country, institution, or reference, with its size corresponding to the number of publications. A larger node indicates a higher publication count. The connections between nodes illustrate the strength of collaborative relationships. Centrality measures how frequently a node connects with other nodes through the shortest paths. Nodes with high centrality are crucial for linking distinct clusters. Burst detection identifies rapid increases in interest toward specific nodes.

Using the software above, descriptive statistics were generated to provide an overview of publication trends, citation metrics, and global contributions. Visual maps were used to illustrate the collaborative networks among authors, institutions, and countries and the clustering of keywords. An exploration of citation bursts complemented these analyses.

The agreement between the 2 reviewers was evaluated using Cohen Kappa test. We conducted a Mann–Kendall trend test to assess publication growth significance and performed linear regression to model the correlation between time and research output.

3. Results

3.1. Descriptive bibliometric analysis

The exclusion of zoological studies resulted in a Cohen kappa score of 0.96, indicating a high level of agreement. Similarly, excluding non-corneal studies yielded a Cohen kappa score of 0.94, demonstrating strong consistency in the selection process. These results suggest a low risk of selection bias and support the reproducibility of the study’s methodology.

The bibliometric analysis included 2079 publications on AS-OCT in corneal diseases. These articles represented the contributions of 7485 unique authors affiliated with 2172 different institutions worldwide. The publications appeared in 323 distinct journals/sources, reflecting the multidisciplinary nature of AS-OCT research and its applications in corneal disease diagnosis and management.

The earliest publication, “Micrometer-scale resolution imaging of the anterior eye in vivo with optical coherence tomography,” was published in the Archives of Ophthalmology (Currently JAMA Ophthalmology) in December 1994 by Izatt JA et al.[13] This study demonstrated a high-speed optical coherence tomography (OCT) prototype for noncontact, reproducible corneal thickness mapping before and after LASIK. The most cited article, “Optical Coherence Tomography,” which introduced and described the initial applications of OCT, was published in Science in November 1991 by Huang D et al.[14]

4. Discussion

The evolving landscape of AS-OCT research reflects both expansion and emerging challenges. Publication volume is an important indicator of research activity and trends; however, the decline observed since 2020 to 2021 warrants cautious interpretation. While part of this decrease may relate to the natural lag in citations and temporary disruptions such as the COVID-19 pandemic, it could also signal a plateau in AS-OCT–specific innovation or a redistribution of efforts toward related modalities such as swept-source OCT, OCT angiography, or alternative imaging technologies.[19] Although the fitted regression model suggests an overall trajectory of growth, reliance on exponential curves may overestimate long-term expansion in a field that may be reaching maturity. Therefore, the apparent slowdown should be interpreted as a multifactorial phenomenon rather than solely as a citation lag, highlighting the importance of prospective clinical studies and ongoing diversification of imaging technologies.

Another contributing factor may be the global shift in research priorities following the COVID-19 pandemic. Riccaboni and Verginer reported a 10 to 12% decrease in research output across fields unrelated to the pandemic, while Sathian et al highlighted significant disruptions in non-COVID-19 clinical trials.[20,21] Similarly, Hogg et al found that ophthalmology research faced notable funding challenges and declining interest during this period.[22] Despite these setbacks, the overall trajectory of AS-OCT research suggests continued expansion rather than random fluctuations. This is supported by the fitted curve (R² = 0.8796), which demonstrates a strong correlation between time and research activity. Publication counts peaked around 2021, with an increase of 184 new studies, underscoring the high impact of earlier research. However, the recent decline in citation volume likely reflects a lag in citation accumulation rather than a loss of relevance, as it often takes time for newly published articles to reach a critical threshold of citations.[19]

Burst keywords serve as indicators of emerging research frontiers. The top 25 keywords with the strongest citation bursts are displayed in Fig. S1, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q791, reflecting shifts in research focus over time. Notably, recent bursts highlight increasing exploration of AI and machine learning in AS-OCT applications. While these technologies are gaining momentum, most studies remain exploratory, and their clinical implementation is still at an early stage.

Similarly, OCE, first introduced in the Journal of Biomedical Optics in 2011, has drawn growing research attention in recent years. Citation bursts associated with OCE and its contributors, such as Larinn Kirill, reflect its emergence as an experimental avenue in corneal imaging.[23] However, the majority of OCE studies to date are preclinical or early feasibility investigations, and large-scale prospective clinical trials are required before its role can be defined in routine practice.[24]

Based on the analyzed data, a total of 3194 publications were contributed by researchers from 82 different countries. Table 1 presents the top 10 countries actively engaged in this area of research. The United States and China are among the most significant contributors to AS-OCT research, with the United States producing 639 papers (30.74%) and accumulating 19,594 citations. At the same time, China follows with 333 publications (16.02%) and 4502 citations. India ranked third with 162 publications (7.79%) and 2684 citations. However, an interesting deviation is observed when comparing AS-OCT research to global clinical trial rankings.[25] While the United States, China, the UK, Spain, Italy, France, and Germany lead in both research output and clinical trials, South Korea, Canada, and Belgium (ranked among the top 10 clinical trial leaders) did not appear in the top AS-OCT research contributors. Instead, India, Turkey, and Japan emerged as key contributors, suggesting substantial national research efforts in these regions. Despite a high publication volume, Turkey’s citation impact remains low. International collaborations achieve a significantly higher Field-Weighted Citation Impact (1.85) compared to national (0.66) and institutional (0.64) efforts, highlighting the role of global cooperation in boosting research visibility and impact.[26,27]

Analyzing the distribution of academic journals provides valuable insight into the core publication sources in AS-OCT research. Cornea emerged as a leading journal in publishing AS-OCT studies, reinforcing its central role in corneal disease research. Other significant contributors included the Journal of Cataract and Refractive Surgery, Journal of Refractive Surgery, and the American Journal of Ophthalmology, highlighting the importance of these journals in advancing anterior segment research and their contributions to the field.

Collaboration networks play a pivotal role in advancing AS-OCT research. Figure 4 illustrates the primary coauthorship clusters, highlighting key researchers such as Hafezi F, Jhanji V, Seitz B, Shetty R, Touboul D, Kymionis GD, and Alio JL. These research networks exhibit distinct clusters with a broad range of contributors rather than a highly centralized structure, indicating diverse collaborative efforts.

Keyword co-occurrence analysis further contextualizes the evolution of AS-OCT research topics. Core keywords such as keratoconus, pachymetry, and corneal biomechanics remain dominant, reflecting long-standing research interests. However, recent trends indicate a shift toward AI-driven diagnostics, with keywords such as deep learning, imaging, and automated diagnostics gaining prominence. This is unsurprising, as recent years have seen growing exploration of AI as a supportive tool in diagnostics.[28] The increasing integration of AI in medical research, particularly in ophthalmology, is evident in these findings, although clinical adoption remains limited and further validation is required.[29]

The citation burst analysis further emphasizes the evolving research priorities in AS-OCT applications (Fig. S1, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q791). The transition from traditional keywords such as pachymetry and ultrasound biomicroscopy to more recent trends like collagen cross-linking, biomechanics, OCE, and deep learning reflects a broader shift toward precision diagnostics and advanced treatment modalities. Temporal mapping shows that before 2010, AS-OCT research focused mainly on foundational applications (e.g., pachymetry, photorefractive keratectomy), whereas after 2020 the emphasis expanded to emerging frontiers, including AI, machine learning-based diagnostics, and OCE.

It is important, however, to distinguish between growing research attention and proven clinical translation. AI and OCE have attracted significant academic attention, showing cross-links with fields such as keratoconus detection, corneal biomechanics, and transplant surgery. AI algorithms demonstrate high diagnostic accuracy for keratoconus and ocular surface tumors,[30,31] while OCE offers unique advantages for assessing biomechanical properties.[32] Nevertheless, both remain largely experimental, with limited in vivo validation and clinical uptake.

In particular, a recent systematic review confirmed that although OCE can detect subtle biomechanical changes and treatment effects, most studies remain at the ex vivo or animal-model level, with no standardized clinical protocols established.[33] Citation bursts in these areas therefore more likely represent emerging research focus rather than established clinical impact, underscoring the need for prospective validation.[34]

By contrast, applications such as pachymetry, epithelial thickness mapping, collagen cross-linking, and deep anterior lamellar keratoplasty have achieved proven translation into clinical practice. AS-OCT pachymetry and epithelial mapping are now routine for keratoconus screening and refractive surgery planning, while OCT-guided monitoring of cross-linking outcomes and lamellar graft interfaces are embedded in daily practice.[35–38] These topics illustrate how certain innovations have moved beyond citation bursts to become integral components of ophthalmic care.

The increasing focus on fungal keratitis and tomography suggests a growing interest in infectious corneal diseases and high-resolution imaging technologies. Infectious keratitis, particularly fungal keratitis, has been the focus of increasing research in recent years regarding OCT findings.[39] Evaluating pathogen-specific differences using OCT has become a growing trend. Further advancements in this field may be expected, including integrating OCT with confocal microscopy and AI-assisted pathogen identification studies.[40,41] Additionally, OCT may be more active in monitoring treatment response.[39]

As of 2024, 7 articles with ongoing citation bursts stand out. Two focus on keratoconus and epithelial thickness mapping, reflecting one of the current trends.[42–45] The article by M. Ang covers AS-OCT, while the one by Kirby Ma is among the most comprehensive reviews on OCE published in recent years.[7,46] Additionally, the article by Gomes J.A.P. serves as one of the key guidelines on keratoconus.[47]

This study has several limitations. First, reliance on a single database (WoSCC) may exclude relevant studies indexed in Scopus, PubMed, or Google Scholar, potentially limiting comprehensiveness. We selected WoSCC due to its suitability for accurate and comprehensive citation analysis, as it provides curated, standardized records and enables robust citation tracking.[48] In contrast, PubMed citation tracking is restricted to PubMed Central, and Google Scholar is not human-curated, often including low-quality or predatory journals with inconsistent citation counts.[49,50] Scopus, while broader in coverage, lacks standardized historical indexing and does not provide citation indicators such as Journal Impact Factor and Essential Science Indicators, which are derived directly from WoSCC.[51] Importantly, comparative studies have demonstrated that macrolevel bibliometric indicators from WoS and Scopus are highly correlated (R² ≈ 0.99) for the number of papers, citations, and country rankings, indicating that country-level outputs are stable and largely independent of the database used.[52] These points support the robustness of our results despite using a single database. Second, only articles and reviews published between 1994 and 2024 were included, which may have omitted some relevant studies. Third, focusing solely on English-language publications may introduce language bias, potentially underrepresenting research from non-English-speaking regions. Future research incorporating multilingual databases could address this limitation. Additionally, citation bursts may be influenced by self-citations or institutional clustering, potentially skewing the impact of specific authors or institutions.

This bibliometric analysis provides a comprehensive overview of the global research landscape on AS-OCT in corneal diseases. While the field has grown significantly, recent publication trends suggest a potential plateau. However, integrating AI and the emerging role of OCE highlight exciting new directions for AS-OCT research. Researchers, clinicians, and policymakers should leverage these insights to guide future research priorities, foster impactful collaborations, and enhance the clinical utility of AS-OCT in corneal disease management.

Eldecalcitol ameliorates diabetic osteoporosis and glucolipid metabolic disorder by promoting Treg cell differentiation through SOCE
Authors: Yujun Jiang, Ruihan Gao, Qiaohui Ying, Xiaolin Li, Yaling Dai, Aimei Song, Hongrui Liu, Tomoka Hasegawa, Minqi Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractActive vitamin D, known for its role in promoting osteoporosis, has immunomodulatory effects according to the latest evidence. Eldecalcitol (ED-71) is a representative of the third-generation novel active vitamin D analogs, and its specific immunological mechanisms in ameliorating diabetic osteoporosis remain unclear. We herein evaluated the therapeutic effects of ED-71 in the context of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), delving into its underlying mechanisms. In a T2DM mouse model, ED-71 attenuated bone loss and marrow adiposity. Simultaneously, it rectified imbalanced glucose homeostasis and dyslipidemia, ameliorated pancreatic β-cell damage and hepatic glycolipid metabolism disorder. Subsequently, in mice injected with the Treg cell-depleting agent CD25, we observed that the beneficial effects of ED-71 mentioned earlier were partially contingent on the Treg subsets ratio. Mechanistically, ED-71 promoted the differentiation of CD4+ T cells into Treg subsets, facilitating Ca2+ influx and the expression of ORAI1 and STIM1, pivotal proteins in store-operated Ca2+ entry (SOCE). The SOCE inhibitor, 2-APB, partially attenuated the positive effects of ED-71 observed in the above results. Overall, ED-71 regulates SOCE-mediated Treg cell differentiation, accomplishing the dual purpose of simultaneously ameliorating diabetic osteoporosis and glucolipid metabolic disorders, showcasing its potential in osteoimmunity therapy and interventions for diseases involving SOCE.Keywords: Eldecalcitol, Diabetic osteoporosis, Glucolipid metabolism disorder, Treg, Store-operated calcium entry

Introduction

According to research conducted by the International Diabetes Federation, the global prevalence of diabetes in 2019 was estimated at 9.3% (463 million), with projections indicating a rise to 10.9% (700 million) by 2045 [1]. Notably, a nationally representative cross-sectional study conducted in mainland China in 2018 estimated the overall prevalence of diabetes to be 12.4%, with over 90% of cases being of the type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) variety [2, 3]. This places a substantial strain on the healthcare system and socioeconomic development. The primary pathological manifestation of diabetes is a disorder in glucolipid metabolism, which often leads to progressive damage to pancreatic β-cells, insulin resistance, hepatic lipid deposition, and hyperlipidemia. As the disease progresses, these changes can exacerbate other systemic diseases, including those related to the skeletal system [4]. One chronic complication of diabetes affecting the skeletal system is diabetic osteoporosis, characterized by bone loss, low bone mineral density (BMD), bone microarchitecture degradation, and increased bone fragility in individuals with continued diabetes mellitus [5]. There is growing evidence of the association between T2DM and increased susceptibility to fractures, with a 4–5 times higher incidence of secondary osteoporosis and a greater fracture risk observed in people with diabetes than in the general population [6]. Consequently, there is an urgent need to develop a drug that can simultaneously ameliorate osteoporosis and glucolipid metabolism disorder, which could become a pivotal strategy in the treatment of diabetes and its complications.

Systemic low-grade inflammation is a hallmark of obesity and T2DM, characterized by the prolonged activation of innate immunity and the disruption of adaptive immune homeostasis [7]. Our previous review studies have demonstrated that hyperglycemia induced by diabetes alters the numbers and functions of immune cells, promoting osteoclastogenesis and inhibiting osteogenic differentiation, thereby contributing to the development of diabetic osteoporosis [8]. CD4+ T helper cells (CD4+ T cells) play vital roles in immune homeostasis by mediating cellular immune responses. Naïve CD4+ T cells can differentiate into specific T helper (Th) cell lineages, including Th1, Th2, Th17, and Treg cells [9]. Treg cells, a critical subset of CD4+ T cells, serve as key regulators of excessive inflammation. While no direct studies exist on the relationship between Treg cells and diabetic osteoporosis, research on ovariectomy rats has shown that Treg subsets can improve bone loss, and previous investigations have linked Treg reduction to bone loss in periodontitis and diabetic periodontitis [10, 11]. Additionally, glucolipid metabolism disorder is associated with the dysfunction and imbalance of immune cells. For example, a negative correlation exists between fasting plasma glucose and hemoglobin A1c levels and Treg cell ratios in patients with obesity, suggesting the potential use of Treg cells as an indicator for prediabetes [12]. Furthermore, Treg-produced interleukin (IL)-10 suppresses white adipose tissue proliferation to maintain adipose tissue homeostasis [13]. Given the shared immune homeostasis imbalances, particularly in Treg subsets, between diabetic osteoporosis and glucolipid metabolism disorder, the regulation of Treg cell ratios and functions emerges as a promising therapeutic target.

Supplementation of vitamin D and calcium is recommended for patients with osteoporosis as a baseline treatment. While vitamin D is commonly associated with bone health, this versatile hormone exerts pleiotropic effects on metabolism and immunity [14]. Eldecalcitol (1α, 25-dihydroxy-2β-(3-hydroxypropyloxy) vitamin D3, also known as ED-71), a representative of the third-generation novel active vitamin D (AVD) analogs with a hydroxypropyloxy residue at the 2β position, has been approved in Japan and China for the clinical treatment of osteoporosis [15]. ED-71 provides a longer half-life, a reduced risk of hypercalcemia than its predecessors and a high combination with vitamin D binding protein (DBP) [16, 17]. DBP is an abundant serum glycoprotein that transports vitamin D, binds to actin, and is found on the surface of B-lymphocytes and subpopulations of T-lymphocytes [18], which suggests that ED-71 may have a stronger immunomodulatory potential than other active vitamin D analogs. Previous research within our group has demonstrated the superior therapeutic efficacy of ED-71 in various forms of osteoporosis, primarily achieved by directly influencing the functions of bone cells [8, 19, 20]. However, limited research exists regarding the immunotherapeutic mechanisms of ED-71 in osteoporosis currently. Notably, our group has comprehensively validated that ED-71 can stimulate macrophage M2 polarization within the realm of innate immunity [19], and enhance the systemic and local ratio of Treg cells in a diabetic periodontitis mice model in terms of adaptive immunity [11]. Furthermore, ED-71 can reduce body weight and blood sugar levels in db/db mice [19]. Nevertheless, the intricate relationships and specific mechanisms underlying these phenomena warrant further exploration.

It is known that the most basic function of active vitamin D is to regulate calcium and phosphorus homeostasis. Serving as key second messengers in non-excitable cells, one of the primary pathways for Ca2+ influx is store-operated Ca2+ entry (SOCE) [21]. Upon depletion of intracellular Ca2+ stores, the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) Ca2+-sensing stromal-interaction molecule (STIM) proteins aggregate and undergo extended conformational changes, bridging the ER-plasma membrane junctional space to activate Ca2+ release-activated Ca2+ modulator (ORAI), the pore-forming proteins of hexameric Ca2+ release-activated Ca2+ channels [22]. The significance of SOCE as the primary Ca2+ influx pathway in lymphocytes has been highlighted [23], with the loss of SOCE having consequences, including decreased pathogenicity of Th17 cells, functional differentiation of CD8+ T cells, and even exhaustion of chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cells [24–26].

In this study, we developed a mouse model of T2DM to evaluate the therapeutic effects and specific targets of exogenous ED-71 supplementation in vivo. Additionally, we extracted CD4+ T cells from mouse spleens for assessment in vitro to determine whether ED-71 regulates the SOCE signaling pathway to promote Treg cell differentiation, ultimately addressing diabetic osteoporosis and glucolipid metabolism disorder. This research contributes new insights into the potential immunological applications of AVD in T2DM.

Results

ED-71 ameliorates glucolipid metabolism disorder in db/db mice

To assess the effects of ED-71 on metabolic homeostasis, we examined the blood glucose levels and body weight of the mice over 28 days. Figure 1A shows the feeding time axis of mice and drug administration. The db/db + vehicle group exhibited significantly higher blood glucose levels and body weight than the WT + vehicle group, which gradually decreased following ED-71 treatment, with the most substantial difference observed at day 28 (Fig. 1B and C). Additionally, we performed intraperitoneal glucose tolerance test (IPGTT), oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT), intraperitoneal insulin tolerance test (IPITT), which demonstrated that db/db mice had worse glucose tolerance, impaired insulin sensitivity and increased insulin resistance. The glucose stimulated insulin secretion test (GSIS) analysis revealed that the pancreatic islets of db/db mice responded poorly to glucose stimulation and secreted less insulin. However, treatment with ED-71 reversed these negative effects (Fig. 1D–G).

The calculated indices homeostasis model assessment (HOMA)-insulin resistance (IR), HOMA-insulin sensitivity (IS), and HOMA-β supported these results, confirming that db/db mice developed insulin resistance and glucose intolerance, both of which were ameliorated by ED-71 (Fig. 1H). Furthermore, Hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining showed reduced and irregular islet areas in the db/db + vehicle group, along with increased amyloid deposits and morphological disruption of peripheral β-cells compared with the WT + vehicle group. These changes were also mitigated by ED-71 treatment (Fig. 1I). Immunofluorescence staining confirmed these findings, showing a higher percentage of apoptotic TUNEL-positive β-cells (green) in the islets of the db/db + vehicle group, which significantly decreased after ED-71 treatment (Fig. 1J). These results showed that the db/db + vehicle group had a smaller islet size due to a higher degree of islet β-cell apoptosis, and ED-71 could significantly reverse the islet injury.

To evaluate adipose metabolism, we conducted a statistical analysis of brown adipose tissue (BAT), visceral adipose tissue (VAT), subcutaneous adipose tissue (SCAT), and total fat mass weight. This analysis revealed a significant increase in the total fat mass, VAT, and SCAT in the db/db + vehicle group compared with that in the WT + vehicle group. ED-71 treatment reduced these parameters, with the trend in BAT proportion opposite to that of VAT and SCAT (Fig. 1K). The biochemical analysis results of total cholesterol (TC), triglyceride (TG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) in mouse serum exhibited a consistent trend, with ED-71 improving dyslipidemia in db/db mice (Fig. 1L). H&E staining of liver tissue revealed ballooning changes and increased steatosis in hepatocytes of db/db mice. Oil-Red O staining further confirmed lipid accumulation. ED-71 treatment alleviated these effects (Fig. 1M). The qRT-PCR analysis of key genes related to hepatic glycolipid metabolism, indicating that ED-71 ameliorated the abnormal gene expression in db/db mice (Fig. 1N).

ED-71 improves osteoporosis and reduces bone marrow adipose tissue (BMAT) in db/db mice

Our study also investigated the impact on bone remodeling and BMAT. The micro-computed tomography analysis revealed that bone mass was reduced in db/db mice and significantly increased after ED-71 treatment (Fig. 2A). Compared with the WT + vehicle group, BMD, the ratio of bone volume to tissue volume (BV/TV), number of trabecular bones (Tb.N), and thickness of trabecular bones (Tb.Th) were down-regulated, and the trabecular bone space (Tb.Sp) was up-regulated in the db/db + vehicle group, which was improved by ED-71 administration (Fig. 2B). H&E staining demonstrated that db/db mice had more vacuoles in the medullary cavity and fewer trabecular bones than WT mice. The number of vacuoles decreased, and the number of trabeculae significantly increased in the db/db + ED-71 group. Masson staining revealed more pronounced blue-stained new bone formation in the db/db + ED-71 group (Fig. 2C). The messenger RNA (mRNA) analysis of bone tissue indicated that the expression of osteogenesis-related factors, such as alkaline phosphatase (ALP), recombinant runt related transcription factor 2 (RUNX2), osteocalcin (OCN) and collagen type I (COL1), was reduced in the db/db + vehicle group compared with the WT + vehicle group. However, their expression significantly increased after ED-71 treatment (Fig. 2D). Immunohistochemical staining further visualized changes in bone formation and resorption in tibial sections. The immunoreactivity of ALP and RUNX2 was reduced, while the number of tartrate-resistant acid phosphatase (TRAP)-positive cells and the immunoreactivity of receptor activator of nuclear factor-κB ligand (RANKL) and cathepsin K (CTSK) were increased in the db/db + vehicle group. However, ED-71 administration significantly promoted bone formation and inhibited bone resorption (Fig. 2E and F). In addition, the number and area of adipocytes in the bone marrow of db/db mice were significantly higher compared with the WT + vehicle group (Fig. 2H and I), and the key regulators of adipogenesis, PPARγ and FABP4, also showed higher levels in db/db mice (Fig. 2J). ED-71 “normalizes” fat metabolism-related indicators.

ED-71 increases Treg cell subsets throughout the body and bone marrow of db/db mice

To explore the changes in CD4+ T cells in the context of diabetes, we isolated CD4+ T cells from the spleens of WT mice and db/db mice for RNA-seq analysis. Rigorous bioinformatics analysis enabled us to visualize differential gene expression under diabetic conditions through a volcano plot (Fig. 3B). Among the analyzed transcripts, 2226 were significantly upregulated, while 845 were downregulated (Fig. 3A and C). The Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis unveiled profound perturbations in biological pathways. In the biological process (BP) compartment, 1494 enriched terms were identified, prominently featuring immune responses, inflammation cascades, cytokine-mediated signaling pathways, and processes integral to the regulation of T-cell activation, T helper cell differentiation, and the production of IL-10 and IL-17. These enrichments underscore the significant immunological and inflammatory modulation of T-cells in a diabetic environment. The cellular component (CC) domain, with 75 enriched terms, highlighted changes in the T-cell structure, highlighting associations with the receptor complex, plasma membrane signaling receptor complex, actin cytoskeleton, and actin filament bundle. The molecular function (MF) component, encompassing 117 enriched terms, emphasized the influence of diabetes on T-cell Ca2+-centric biological pathways, elucidating attributes such as immune receptor activity, cadherin binding, and calmodulin binding, indicative of potential perturbations in T-cell intracellular Ca2+ dynamics due to diabetes.

To delve into the changes in Treg subsets in db/db mice and assess whether ED-71 has a regulatory effect on them, we conducted various assays targeting key Treg-related factors, including forkhead box P3 (FOXP3), transforming growth factor (TGF)-β and IL-10. Flow cytometry analysis of single-cell suspensions from the spleen and bone marrow revealed that the proportion of Treg cells in CD4+ T cells was significantly lower in the db/db + vehicle group than in the WT + vehicle group. However, this decrease was reversed following ED-71 treatment (Fig. 3D). Enzyme‑linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) analyses of Treg signature factors, serum TGF-β and IL-10 levels, as well as TGF-β and FOXP3 mRNA levels in the spleen and bone marrow, along with immunofluorescence staining of the tibia, displayed the same trend (Fig. 3E–G).

To further validate the effect of a high glucose (HG) environment and ED-71 treatment on Treg cell differentiation, we sorted CD4+ T cells in vitro using magnetic beads (Fig. 3H). Flow cytometry, ELISA analysis of cell culture supernatants, qRT-PCR analysis, and Western blotting analysis validated that ED-71 improved HG-inhibited Treg cell differentiation, affirming the previous in vivo (Fig. 3I–L).

ED-71 improves glucolipid metabolism disorder in db/db mice via Treg subsets

To investigate whether ED-71 exerts its therapeutic effect on T2DM through Treg cells, a Treg cell-depleting agent, CD25 [27], was introduced. The experiment included a feeding schedule, as illustrated in Fig. 4A, showing the timing of drug administration in relation to feeding. Flow cytometry analysis of single-cell suspensions from the spleen and bone marrow confirmed the effective depletion of Treg cells in the mice. This depletion simultaneously suppressed the therapeutic effect of ED-71 (Fig. 4B).

To demonstrate that the regulatory effect of ED-71 on glycolipid metabolism depends on Treg subsets, we examined various parameters. This included blood glucose levels (Fig. 4C), body weight (Fig. 4D), IPGTT (Fig. 4E), OGTT (Fig. 4F), IPITT (Fig. 4G), GSIS (Fig. 4H), and fat mass (Fig. 4K) of mice. The results indicated that after CD25 injection, the imbalance of blood glucose homeostasis in db/db mice was significantly exacerbated, including poorer performance in IPGTT, OGTT, IPITT, and GSIS tests. Moreover, HOMA-IS and HOMA-β were also affected accordingly (Fig. 4I). CD25 attenuated the therapeutic effect of ED-71 in the db/db + CD25 + ED-71 group, compared with the db/db + ED-71 group, signifying that the therapeutic effect of ED-71 partially relies on Treg subsets. The staining results of the pancreas (Fig. 4J and M) and liver (Fig. 4N), along with biochemical analyses of lipid metabolism-related indicators in serum (Fig. 4L), were consistent with this trend. These results were further supported by qRT-PCR analysis of key factors in hepatic glycolipid metabolism (Fig. 4O).

ED-71 improves osteoporosis and BTMA in db/db mice via Treg subsets

We further analyzed the effects of Treg cells on bone mass and bone remodeling indexes. The micro-computed tomography analysis revealed a significant reduction in bone mass in the db/db + CD25 group and that CD25 weakened the therapeutic effect of ED-71 in the db/db + CD25 + ED-71 group (Fig. 5A). The statistical analysis of bone parameters in the region of interest corroborated these findings (Fig. 5B). The H&E staining assay showcased that the db/db + CD25 + ED-71 group exhibited fewer bone trabeculae than the db/db + ED-71 group. Additionally, there was less new bone formation as observed in Masson staining (Fig. 5C). Examining the mRNA levels in bone tissue, we found that the expression of bone formation-associated factors, including ALP, RUNX2, OCN and COL1, was significantly reduced in the db/db + CD25 group compared with the db/db group. Furthermore, the increased expression after ED-71 treatment was partially suppressed by CD25 (Fig. 5D). Immunohistochemical staining results mirrored these trends, with the db/db + CD25 group showing a more pronounced decrease in bone formation and an increase in bone resorption. The addition of CD25 partially inhibited the osteogenesis-promoting and osteoclastogenic-reducing effects of ED-71 (Fig. 5E). In addition, H&E staining of mouse bone marrow showed that the number and area of adipocytes in the db/db + CD25 group were significantly larger than those in the db/db + vehicle group, while ED-71 partially reduced them in the db/db + CD25 + ED-71 group (Fig. 5H and I). The results of PPARγ and FABP4 mRNA level detection were consistent with the above trend (Fig. 5J).

ED-71 activates ORAI1 and STIM1-mediated SOCE in CD4+ T cells

In our exploration of pathway dynamics under diabetic conditions, the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis revealed 58 significant pathways. Key representations included the p53 signaling pathway, NF-kappa B signaling pathway, osteoclast differentiation, Ca2+ signaling pathway, and pathways linked to lipid metabolism (Fig. 6A), indicating the significant influence of diabetes on T cell cycle regulation, inflammatory responses, metabolic shifts, and potential disruptions in bone metabolism. To enhance our understanding further, the gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) identified 109 enriched pathways, prominently featuring the AGE-RAGE signaling pathway, Ca2+ signaling pathway, IL-17 signaling pathway, and osteoclast differentiation (Fig. 6B). Focusing on our comprehensive pathway assessment, GSEA enrichment analysis specifically targeted genes related to the Ca2+ signaling pathway, comprising a set size of 202 genes. Within this set, we identified 47 genes integral to this pathway, including Fgfr1, Kdr, Met, ORAI1, P2rx1, Pdgfc, STIM1, Calml4, Gnag, Itokb, Itor1, Nfatc2, PIcb1, and SIc8a (Fig. 6C).

Immunohistochemical staining images demonstrated reduced STIM1 and ORAI1 expression levels in CD4+ T cells from db/db mice tibias. However, these levels improved with ED-71 treatment (Fig. 6D and E). The qRT-PCR analysis of mouse spleen and bone marrow confirmed these observations (Fig. 6F). Furthermore, CD4+ T cells were cultured in vitro and stimulated with HG. Subsequent immunofluorescence staining revealed that the protein expression levels of STIM1 and ORAI1 were significantly reduced under HG conditions, but increased with the addition of ED-71 (Fig. 6G and H). Western blotting analysis and qRT-PCR analysis also showed a consistent trend (Fig. 6I and J). Furthermore, changes in intracellular Ca2+ ion levels in vitro were detected by flow cytometry and a Ca2+ ion probe, and these trends were consistent with ORAI1 and STIM1 proteins (Fig. 6K and L).

ED-71 promotes Treg cell differentiation by regulating SOCE in vitro

To verify whether the regulatory effect of ED-71 on Treg cell differentiation was through SOCE, we added the SOCE inhibitor 2-Aminoethyl diphenylborinate (2-APB) to the culture system in vitro [28]. Flow cytometry results demonstrated that in the HG environment, the proportion of Treg cells in CD4+ T cells was partially reduced by the addition of 2-APB, and the ameliorative effect of ED-71 was partially repressed (Fig. 7A). The qRT-PCR analysis (Fig. 7B), ELISA analyses of cell culture supernatant (Fig. 7C), and western blotting analysis (Fig. 7D) confirmed these results, substantiating that ED-71 exerted its effect in regulating Treg cell differentiation partially through SOCE. In summary, ED-71 may improve the SOCE process in CD4+ T cells, followed by promoting Treg cell differentiation, thereby preventing and treating osteoporosis and glucolipid metabolic disorders in T2DM (Fig. 7E).

Discussion

The present study has assessed the beneficial impact of ED-71 on diabetic osteoporosis and glucolipid metabolic disorder while elucidating the underlying mechanisms. To begin, our data demonstrate that ED-71 effectively counteracted bone mass reduction, elevated BMAT, and concurrently addressed weight gain, disturbed blood glucose homeostasis, pancreatic β-cell damage, hepatic steatosis, dyslipidemia, and abnormal expression of genes related to glycolipid metabolism. Second, our investigation underscores the pivotal role of Treg subsets in the pathogenesis of T2DM and highlights how ED-71 promotes the correction of diabetes-induced glucolipid metabolic disorders and the restoration of balanced bone remodeling by regulating Treg subset ratios. Lastly, ED-71 successfully counteracted the hyperglycemia-induced suppression of SOCE, a process mediated by ORAI1 and STIM1 proteins. This ultimately led to the differentiation of CD4+ T cells into Treg subsets, providing a novel immunotherapy perspective on the prevention and treatment of T2DM and its complications.

The findings from the United Kingdom Prospective Diabetes Study indicate that the longer an individual has diabetes mellitus, the higher their risk of diabetic complications. This relationship also extends to bone health in this population [29]. In our db/db mouse model, we observed a clear disruption in bone metabolism, marked deterioration of trabecular and cortical bone microarchitecture, and reductions in whole-bone structural properties and tissue-level material properties [30]. Our results are consistent with previous literature, as they demonstrate a significant reduction in bone mass, bone formation decrease, and bone resorption increase in db/db mice compared with WT mice. These changes may be attributed to factors such as hyperglycemia, advanced glycation end products, local inflammation, intracellular reactive oxygen species levels, insulin resistance, and hyperlipidemia, all of which are present in the diabetic state. Of particular note are insulin resistance and hyperlipidemia [31]. Moreover, insulin resistance and fasting plasma insulin levels are inversely associated with bone mass [32] These findings suggest a close relationship between glucolipid metabolism disorder and the pathogenesis of diabetic osteoporosis. Our results highlight the imbalanced glucose homeostasis, elevated lipid levels, islet β cell apoptosis, and increased BMAT in db/db mice, all of which are positively correlated with bone deterioration. This suggests that identifying a drug capable of simultaneously correcting glucolipid metabolism disorders and imbalanced bone metabolism may be a critical strategy for the prevention and treatment of diabetic osteoporosis.

Current treatments for type 2 diabetes include insulin, metformin, sulphonylureas, thiazolidinediones, α-glucosidase inhibitors, incretin hormone-based therapy and sodium-dependent glucose co-transporter (SGLT) inhibitors [33]. Research on the impact of antidiabetic medications on bone metabolism has indicated that metformin and incretin-based treatments have a neutral or favorable effect on bone health. In contrast, the use of thiazolidinediones and certain SGLT2 inhibitors might contribute to an increased risk [34]. Unfortunately, there are currently no guidelines available to specify when and at which stage of diabetes to initiate anti-osteoporotic medication for patients with diabetes mellitus. Our previous studies within our research group have validated the effectiveness of the new vitamin D analog, ED-71, in treating various primary and secondary forms of osteoporosis. ED-71 not only improves intestinal calcium absorption and inhibits osteoclast differentiation, but also effectively promotes “mini-modeling” bone formation-focal bone formation that is independent of osteoclastic bone resorption, thereby repairing the microinjury of bone tissue [8, 20, 35]. This includes its ability to counteract diabetes-induced bone formation inhibition through the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway [19, 36]. Surprisingly, we have also observed that ED-71 can control weight gain and fasting blood glucose levels in db/db mice in previous research [19]. Based on these findings, we confidently speculated that ED-71 ameliorates glucolipid metabolism disorders while addressing diabetic osteoporosis. Our observations align with this conjecture, as we found that after administering an effective dose of ED-71 to db/db mice, bone mass increased, the expression of the osteogenic marker ALP and RUNX2 increased, the number of osteoclasts, bone resorption-related markers, and RANKL expression decreased, and bone marrow fat decreased. Additionally, weight gain was controlled, and imbalanced blood glucose homeostasis, hepatic steatosis, dyslipidemia, and abnormal levels of related markers were corrected. Notably, vitamin D deficiency increases the risk of T2DM and obesity [14, 37]. In vivo studies have shown that vitamin D deficiency leads to reduced insulin production from pancreatic β cells, resulting in impaired glucose tolerance [38]. Furthermore, several case-control studies have indicated that vitamin D supplementation improves insulin sensitivity in adults [39]. Regarding adipose metabolism, an inverse relationship exists between measures of adiposity and vitamin D levels [14]. Vitamin D can regulate adipose tissue differentiation and growth by inhibiting the expression of adipogenic transcription factor genes and reducing fatty acid synthase activity [40]. Collectively, these studies provide substantial support for the potential clinical applications of ED-71 in the treatment of diabetes and its complications, including osteoporosis and non-alcoholic steatohepatitis.

Given that ED-71 has exhibited a distinct inhibitory effect on diabetic osteoporosis and glucolipid metabolic disorder, a thorough exploration of the underlying mechanisms has been warranted. Inflammatory processes become activated early in the development of diabetes and obesity, resulting in a chronic proinflammatory state [7]. As immune imbalance is a common contributor to glucolipid metabolic disorder and osteoporosis associated with diabetes, this study focused on this aspect of the mechanism. RNA-seq analysis was conducted on CD4+ T cells from the spleens of WT and db/db mice. The BP compartment of GO enrichment analysis revealed alterations in immune response, inflammation cascades, T helper cell differentiation, and the production of IL-10 and IL-17. These findings emphasize the crucial role of T cells in the inflammatory processes inherent to the pathology of T2DM, particularly the imbalance between Treg and Th17 cells. In vivo results indicated a reduction in Treg subsets in the spleen and bone marrow of db/db mice, accompanied by inhibited secretion of anti-inflammatory factors IL-10 and TGF-β. However, ED-71 significantly reversed these trends. Additionally, ED-71 demonstrated an ability to reverse the suppression of Treg cell differentiation by HG in CD4+ T cells in vitro. These findings align with previous diabetes studies, which have shown a tendency for a lower proportion of Treg cells to activate T helper cells in T2DM [41]. Furthermore, in line with earlier studies within our group, we observed that ED-71 can upregulate the Treg cell ratio in alveolar bone in mice with diabetic periodontitis [11, 19].

Numerous studies conducted on human and animal models have demonstrated that in cases of chronic inflammation associated with obesity and metabolic diseases, Treg cells exhibit significant differences and play a crucial role in maintaining bone homeostasis under normal physiological conditions [42]. Treg cells may serve as a shared potential target for the immunometabolic treatment of diabetic osteoporosis and glucolipid metabolism disorder, and ED-71 may exert its therapeutic effect by regulating these cells. To investigate this, we depleted Treg cells in db/db mice by injecting CD25, which resulted in the exacerbation of glucolipid metabolic disorder and accelerated bone loss. This indicates that Treg cells are indeed a shared target for glucolipid and bone metabolism. Furthermore, the results showed that in comparison to db/db mice treated with ED-71, the improvement in glucolipid metabolism homeostasis and bone remodeling balance brought about by ED-71 was partially counteracted by CD25 in the db/db + CD25 + ED-71 group. This suggests that ED-71 antagonizes diabetic osteoporosis and glucolipid metabolic disorder in a Treg-dependent manner partly, supporting our initial suspicions. Studies have suggested that AVD and IL-2 have direct synergistic effects on activated T cells, inducing Treg cell differentiation by stimulating high levels of CTLA-4 and FOXP3 expression [43]. In addition, AVD can induce dendritic cells (DC) with a tolerogenic phenotype, which, in turn, induce CD4+ T cells with regulatory properties, resulting in an increased percentage of Treg cells [44]. Thus, AVD serves as a potent anti-inflammatory agent and a physiological inducer of Treg cells. Overall, our group has once again confirmed that the novel AVD analog, ED-71, has the potential to become a significant systemic adjuvant drug based on its immunomodulatory effects. This may provide a solid foundation for the application of ED-71 in immune and inflammatory diseases.

Given that ED-71 has demonstrated a clear regulatory effect on Treg cells in the diabetic state, a comprehensive exploration of the molecular mechanisms has been warranted. The GO enrichment analysis revealed decreased Ca2+ ion transmembrane transport and Ca2+-dependent protein binding in CD4+ T cells from db/db mice, indicating potential perturbations in T-cell intracellular Ca2+ dynamics due to diabetes. Ca2+ ions primarily enter non-excitable cells through the SOCE pathway, which is closely linked to receptor activity on the cell membrane and ER, involving phospholipase C [45, 46]. Moreover, the analysis of the CC domain revealed changes in the intracellular membrane system transport, which, when combined with changes in phospholipase C activity and phosphatidylinositol-3,4,5-trisphosphate binding in the MF analysis, suggests that diabetes primarily affects the Ca2+ pathway in CD4+ T cells through SOCE. GSEA enrichment analysis focused on 202 genes related to the Ca2+ signaling pathway, and among these, key SOCE proteins STIM1 and ORAI1 exhibited decreased expression levels. Previous research has shown that downregulation of SOCE occurs in T lymphocytes in diabetes, and this change in SOCE is likely caused by reduced expression of the ORAI1 protein, which aligns with our findings [47]. Furthermore, following the administration of ED-71 in both in vivo and in vitro settings, our results demonstrated a significant decrease in Ca2+ influx in CD4+ T cells and a reduction in ORAI1 and STIM1 protein levels. The improvement in Treg cell differentiation induced by ED-71 was inhibited upon the addition of the SOCE inhibitor 2-APB, indicating that ED-71 exerts its regulatory effects on Treg cells through SOCE. Several studies have shown that different T cell subsets exhibit varying SOCE profiles, with Treg cells being the unique subtype with the most significant SOCE response [48]. These findings, combined with previous research and our results, emphasize the substantial impact of SOCE on T cell activation and function. Additionally, studies have demonstrated that AVD is a potent regulator of cytosolic Ca2+ in various cell types, promoting the SOCE process and facilitating the influx of Ca2+ ions. These results further support our conclusions [49]. In summary, the ameliorative effect of ED-71 on Treg cell differentiation is indeed partly achieved through SOCE, which may represent a promising therapeutic target for a variety of immunoinflammatory diseases in the future.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study has demonstrated that ED-71 may improve the SOCE process in CD4+ T cells, which, in turn, promote Treg cell differentiation, thus preventing and treating osteoporosis and glucolipid metabolic disorders in T2DM. Our findings may provide valuable insights into the utilization of ED-71 and other AVD medications as potential preventative and therapeutic options for addressing diabetic osteoporosis through osteoimmunity modulation.

Limitations of the study

We demonstrate that the novel active vitamin D analogue ED-71 alleviates diabetic osteoporosis by regulating Treg cell fate. However, whether ED-71 affects innate immune responses and B cell signaling, and subsequently affects osteoporosis, remains to be revealed. Therefore, further studies such as single-cell sequencing are necessary to comprehensively elucidate the function of ED-71 in regulating the immune landscape of osteoporosis. In this study, db/db mice and CD25 injection were used as diabetic osteoporosis model and Treg depletion model for in vivo experiments. In future work, we plan to utilize the conditional knockout mouse model to deepen our understanding of SOCE regulating T cell fate. In addition, we also tested the therapeutic effect of ED-71 on bone loss and glucolipid metabolic disorder in T2DM mouse models, but the dual efficacy of ED-71 needs to be clinically evaluated.

Materials and methods

Animals and drug administration

Male C57BLKS/J Iar- + Leprdb/ + Leprdb mice (db/db, purchased from Gempharmatech Co., Ltd) were used as a model of T2DM. Male C57BL/6J mice (body weight: 18–22 g; age: 8w; n = 5) and male C57BLKS/J Iar- + Leprdb/ +Leprdb mice (db/db) (body weight: 35–45 g; age: 8w) were fed under standard laboratory conditions and subjected to light and dark condition for 12 h at 20 °C. In addition to C57BL/6J mice group, the db/db mice were randomly assigned to four groups: the db/db group (n = 5), where mice received glycerol orally for 4 weeks; the db/db + ED-71 group (n = 5), where mice received ED-71 (0·25 µg/kg, 3 times per week, orally) (Chugai Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. Japan) [19] for 4 weeks; the db/db + CD25 group (n = 5), where mice received CD25 (2·5 mg/kg, 1 times per week, subcutaneously injected) (102058, Biolegend) [27] for 4 weeks ; the db/db + CD25 + ED-71 group (n = 5), where mice received ED-71 and CD25 for 4 weeks(the dose is as mentioned above). At the end of treatment, the tissues or cells of the mice were prepared for subsequent histological analysis.

Metabolic studies

Fasting blood glucose, fasting body weight, IPGTT, OGTT, IPITT, GSIS were mainly carried out, and HOMA-IR, HOMA-β and HOMA-IS were calculated. The detection method and calculation formula were established according to the previous method [50, 51]. Furthermore, serum glucose (GLU, 105-000949-00, Mindray), total cholesterol (TC, 105-000448-00, Mindray), triglyceride (TG, 105-000449-00, Mindray), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C, 105-000464-00, Mindray) and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C, 105–000463, Mindray) levels in mice were detected by biochemical analyzer (Mindray) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Micro-CT

All samples were reconstructed and analyzed using a micro-CT analysis system (SCANCO Medical AG). Voltage = 70 kV, current = 200 µA, and layer thickness = 10 µM. The three-dimensional images of tibias were reconstructed and the trabecular bone near the growth plate was analyzed.

Preparation of paraffin sections and frozen sections

For paraffin sections, 5 μm thick sections of mouse liver, pancreas and tibia were prepared for subsequent histological analysis. For frozen sections, livers of mice were taken and dehydrated with sucrose solutions. The samples were embedded with optimal cutting temperature (OCT), frozen, and a series of 10 μm sections were prepared after the OCT complex became white and hard.

H&E staining

The paraffin sections were dewaxed and rehydrated, and H&E staining was performed according to the standard procedure [52]. Digital images were captured with an optical microscope (Olympus Corp). Furthermore, number and area of adipocytes was assessed in high-power field images of the tibia (400X).

Oil-red O staining

For oil-red O staining (1320-06-5, Aladdin), liver frozen sections were stained according to standard protocols and sealed with gelatin. Lipid droplets (stained red) were observed under a microscope and imaged.

Periodic acid-Schiff (PAS) staining

For PAS staining (C0142S, Beyotime), liver paraffin sections were stained according to standard protocols, observed and photographed under the optical microscope.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) and TRAP staining

Sections underwent IHC staining to examine ALP, RANKL, RUNX2 and CTSK expression. Staining methods was established according to the previous method [11]. Subsequently, all slices were re-stained with methyl green and observed under an optical microscope. For TRAP staining, sections were washed in double-distilled water and immersed in a TRAP staining solution. All sections were then re-stained in methyl green, sealed, observed under optical microscopy, and digital images captured.

Immunofluorescence (IF) staining

For paraffin section, double immunofluorescence staining was performed on sections to localize islets or CD4+ T cells. Staining methods was established according to the previous method [11]. Images were obtained under laser confocal microscope (ZEISS) and the trabecular bone near the growth plate was selected as the area of interest. Cells were incubated with Phalloidin (CA1610, Solarbio) and anti-STIM1 antibody or anti-ORAI1 antibody, and images were obtained by laser confocal microscope (ZEISS).

CD4+ T cells isolation and culture

For in vitro experiments, healthy C57BL/6J mice (male, 8 weeks old, 18–22 g) were used as spleen donors to isolate CD4+ T cells. Fresh spleens were harvested aseptically and ground to obtain splenocyte suspension. CD4+ T cells were purified from the splenocyte suspension by CD4+ T Cell Isolation Kit (130-104-454, Miltenyi Biotec) according to the manufacturer’s procedures. Primary mice CD4+ T cells were cultured in 4 µg/ml anti-mouse CD3ε (100339, Biolegend)-coated 48-well plates at a density of 0·6 × 106 cells/plate. In order to induce CD4+ T cells to differentiate into Treg cells, 5ng/ml TGF-β (HZ-1011, Proteintech) was added to the medium. After 24 h culture, CD4+ T cells were treated with 55 mM glucose solution, 50 µM 2-APB (HY-W009724, MCE) [28] and 1 nM ED-71 [19] and cultured in dark for 48 h.

RNA sequencing and data analysis

CD4+ T cells were purified from spleen single cell suspensions of C57BL/6J and db/db mice, total RNA was extracted from CD4+ T cells for RNA-sequencing on the Illumina Novaseq TM 6000 sequence platform (Novogene, China). Transcriptome assembly and read mapping were performed using Trinity and RSEM, respectively, enabling accurate transcript quantification. DESeq2 (v1.20.0) standardized gene expression, identifying differentially expressed genes (DEGs) with FDR < 0.05 and |log2FC| > 1. DEGs were organized into heat maps by K-means clustering (Euclidean distance) and visualized with Java TreeView. GO, KEGG, and GSEA enrichment analyses were executed using clusterProfiler (v3.8.1), prioritizing significant categories by p-values.

Flow cytometry

Spleen and bone marrow were prepared into single cell suspension. The percentage of Treg cells labeled with CD4 (FITC anti-CD4, 100406, Biolegend), CD25 (PE anti-CD25, 102008, Biolegend) and FOXP3 (Brilliant Violet 421 anti-FOXP3, 126419, Biolegend) was detected by flow cytometry (Cytoflexs). Spleen- and bone marrow-residing ex vivo Treg cells were analyzed based on lymphocyte gating using forward and side scatter, followed by exclusion based on FSC-A vs. FSC-W. CD4+T cells were then gated using CD4-FITC markers followed by the surface maker CD25-PE and transcription factor Foxp3-Brilliant Violet 421 for Treg cells. Data were analyzed using FlowJo™v10·8 Software (BD Life Sciences).

qRT-PCR analysis

RNA isolation from mouse liver, bone marrow, spleen and CD4+ T cells, cDNA synthesis, real-time PCR and protein analysis were performed using standard protocols. The complete list of primers used in this study can be found in Table 1.

Western blotting

The protein extraction and gel imaging (General Electric Company) of CD4+ T cells were performed according to the standard procedure [52]. ImagePro Plus 6·2 software (Media Cybernetics) was used to analyze and quantify grayscale values normalized to β-tubulin levels. The samples derive from the same experiment or parallel experiments and that gels/blots were processed in parallel.

ELISA

The serum was collected from the blood of mice and the supernatant was obtained from the cell culture. The levels of IL-10 (KE10008, Proteintech), TGF-β (KE10005, Proteintech) and insulin (KE10089, Proteintech) were measured using the cytokine-specific ELISA kit, according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Measurement of Ca2+ with Fluo-4 AM

The level of Ca2+ in CD4+ T cell was measured according to the manufacturer’s instructions (F8500, Solarbio). Fluorescence from the CD4+ T cell loaded with Fluo-4 AM was detected using laser confocal microscope and flow cytometry at 488 nm (excitation) and 520 nm (detection).

Antibodies and reagents

For IHC staining, tissues were stained and treated with Anti-ALP antibody (ab65834, Abcam), Anti-RANKL antibody (ab216484, Abcam), Anti-RUNX2 antibody (D130-3, MBL), Anti-CTSK antibody (ab19027, Abcam), Goat anti-rabbit IgG H&L antibody (ab6721, Abcam) and Goat anti-mouse IgG H&L antibody (SA00001-1, Proteintech).

For IF staining, tissues or cells were stained and treated with TUNEL Apoptosis Assay Kit (C1086, Beyotime), Anti-Insulin antibody (66198-1, Proteintech), Anti-CD4 antibody (ab183685, Abcam), Anti-FOXP3 antibody (ab253297, Abcam), Anti- STIM1 antibody (ab62031, Abcam), Anti-ORAI1 antibody (28411-1-AP, Proteintech), Goat anti-rabbit IgG H&L (ab150081, Abcam), Goat anti-mouse IgG H&L (ab150119, Abcam), DAPI (ab104139, Abcam).

A variety of antibodies were used to western blotting, including the above Anti-FOXP3 antibody, Anti-STIM1 antibody, Anti-ORAI1 antibody, Anti-TGF-β antibody (ab92486, Abcam), Anti-α-tubulin antibody (11224-1-AP, Proteintech), Goat anti-rabbit IgG antibody (ab6721, Abcam) and Goat anti-mouse IgG antibody (SA00001-1, Proteintech).

Statistical analyses

Statistical analysis was performed in GraphPad Prism (GraphPad Software). The mean ± standard deviation (SD) presented the results. To compare sample means of two groups, Student’s t-test was used, while one-way ANOVA was employed for comparing sample means from more than three groups. P < 0.05 served as the statistically significant difference threshold.

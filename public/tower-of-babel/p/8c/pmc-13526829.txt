An attention-weighted hybrid feature learning framework for bearing fault diagnosis and maintenance cost optimization
Authors: Zhenlong Li, Xiaohui Fan, Xue Wang, Zhengchun Weng, Xinyue Cai, Caiyu Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRolling bearing fault diagnosis under complex operating conditions requires discriminative representations of non-stationary vibration signals and maintenance decisions that account for asymmetric economic losses caused by false alarms and missed detections. This paper develops an interpretable and cost-aware diagnostic framework that integrates physical prior knowledge with deep temporal representation learning and maps multiclass diagnostic outputs to maintenance-triggering decisions. The proposed framework adopts a physics-prior-guided dual-channel hybrid feature learning architecture. In the physical feature channel, wavelet packet energy ratios, kurtosis, skewness, and crest factor are extracted as mechanism-related descriptors, while in the deep spatiotemporal feature channel, a one-dimensional convolutional neural network and a bidirectional gated recurrent unit are used to learn high-dimensional temporal representations from normalized vibration segments. The main methodological novelty is a cross-modal multi-head attention module that uses embedded physical features as the query vector to guide the dynamic selection of deep temporal features. A scalar gated fusion mechanism is then introduced to adaptively balance physical robustness and data-driven representation capacity. A posterior-probability-based minimum expected-cost evaluation model is further established to convert diagnostic outputs into binary maintenance decisions under asymmetric false-alarm and missed-detection costs. This economic evaluation component translates diagnostic errors into quantifiable maintenance-cost indicators, providing a unified basis for comparing the economic consequences of downtime losses, redundant inspections, and risks associated with missed faults across different models. The framework is intended for intelligent condition monitoring and risk-sensitive maintenance of rotating machinery, where missed faults and unnecessary maintenance actions may lead to different operational and economic consequences. Experiments on the CWRU, SEU, and in-house rolling bearing test-rig datasets under a unified non-overlapping evaluation protocol show that the proposed framework achieves diagnostic accuracies of 99.88%, 99.75%, and 99.79%, respectively. Ablation studies and feature-distribution analyses show that attention-guided gated fusion improves class separability and reduces maintenance-related errors compared with single-channel models and static fusion strategies. Noise experiments on the CWRU dataset indicate better performance retention than representative data-driven models at 10–20 dB and a relative advantage at 5 dB, although the accuracy decreases markedly under extremely low-SNR conditions such as 0 dB and − 5 dB. Small-sample experiments on the in-house test-rig dataset show higher Accuracy and Macro-F1 than purely data-driven baselines when only 5% and 10% of the training samples are used. Under high missed-detection cost settings, the proposed framework reduces the missed-detection probability of critical faults and lowers the normalized expected maintenance cost. These findings indicate that the proposed method not only improves diagnostic performance but also supports more efficient maintenance resource allocation and reduces expected economic losses in risk-sensitive scenarios, providing an interpretable diagnostic and decision-support tool for economically sensitive industrial maintenance.Keywords: Rolling bearing fault diagnosis, Physics-prior-guided feature learning, Cross-modal multi-head attention, Cost-sensitive maintenance decision-making, Maintenance cost optimizationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Rolling bearings, as one of the most critical supporting and power-transmission components in rotating machinery, are extensively used in wind power generation, rail transit, aero-engines, and large-scale industrial equipment. Their operating condition is directly associated with the safety and reliability of the overall system. Liang et al.1 reported that degradation in bearing health status is often a precursor to performance deterioration and sudden failure in complex mechanical systems. Under long-term complex operating conditions, bearings are prone to damage. Wang et al.2 indicated that approximately 42% of rotating machinery shutdown incidents are caused by bearing failures; moreover, under variable-speed and variable-load conditions, the amplitude of fault-induced impulsive components can decrease by more than 30%, which substantially increases the difficulty of early diagnosis. Therefore, developing efficient and accurate fault diagnosis methods with decision-support capability is of great significance for improving equipment reliability3.

In the field of feature extraction and signal processing, the short-time Fourier transform (STFT) proposed by Nawab et al.4 has been widely adopted for time–frequency analysis because of its high computational efficiency and ease of implementation; however, its fixed window function limits the capture of transient characteristics in non-stationary signals. Subsequently, the wavelet transform (WT) introduced by Daubechies et al.5 provided a new avenue for multiscale analysis, yet its performance under complex operating conditions strongly depends on the selection of the wavelet basis. In the 21st century, empirical mode decomposition (EMD) proposed by Huang et al.6 and its variants, including ensemble EMD (EEMD) by Wu et al.7 and complete ensemble EMD with adaptive noise (CEEMDAN) by Torres et al.8, effectively revealed nonlinear characteristics, but issues such as mode mixing remain. To address this, Dragomiretskiy et al.9 proposed variational mode decomposition (VMD), which mitigates mode mixing and improves stability through a variational framework; nevertheless, the requirement to predefine the number of modes and the associated computational complexity limit its large-scale application. More recently, the synchrosqueezing transform (SST) proposed by Daubechies et al.10 further improves time–frequency concentration. Overall, conventional approaches offer advantages in physical interpretability, but under strong noise interference they often suffer from feature redundancy and pronounced dependence on parameter settings11.

Recent studies on thermo-fluid transport, nanofluid systems, and applied-mechanics modeling have emphasized the importance of physically constrained modeling, parameter sensitivity analysis, and optimization-oriented evaluation in nonlinear engineering systems. Recent works have investigated Darcy–Forchheimer drag in blood-based CNT nanofluid flow over rotating discs12, Boger–micropolar tri-hybrid nanofluid squeezing flow between parallel discs13, nanoparticle-shape effects on ternary hybrid nanofluid thermal properties14, and response-surface/ANOVA-based heat-transfer-rate prediction for radiative magnetized hybrid nanofluid flow15. Other studies have addressed entropy minimization in CNT-based hybrid nanofluid flow16, irreversibility processes in blood-based micropolar hybrid nanofluid squeezing flow17, slip velocity and dissipative heat effects in radiative hybrid nanofluid flow18, inertial-drag effects in micropolar nanofluid channel flow19, homogeneous–heterogeneous reactions and thermal radiation effects in micropolar nanofluid systems20, elastoviscous nanofluid flow with dissipative heat effects21, and analytical modeling of time-dependent Casson flow with Hall current22. These advances show that reliable modeling of complex engineering systems should integrate physical mechanisms, parameter sensitivity, and optimization-based assessment rather than relying solely on data fitting. This insight is consistent with the need in bearing fault diagnosis to develop vibration-mechanism-constrained feature representations and risk-aware maintenance decisions.

In feature selection and hyperparameter optimization, because vibration signals from rotating machinery are highly nonlinear and non-stationary, intelligent optimization algorithms that do not rely on gradient information have been widely employed23. The genetic algorithm (GA) proposed by Holland et al.24 emulates biological evolution and exhibits strong generality in discrete feature-subset selection and constrained parameter optimization. However, the crossover and mutation operators of GA often lead to slow convergence in high-dimensional feature spaces and can result in entrapment in local optima due to loss of population diversity. Particle swarm optimization (PSO) proposed by Kennedy et al.25 simplifies the search logic through interactions between individual and collective experience and is highly efficient for continuous-space optimization; nonetheless, the balance between global exploration and local exploitation is heavily dependent on inertia-weight tuning, and premature convergence frequently constrains search accuracy. Differential evolution (DE) proposed by Storn et al.26 enhances population robustness through vector-differential mutation and performs robustly in parameter searches for complex industrial scenarios, but when the objective function is highly coupled, its computational cost can increase sharply with model complexity.

In recent years, heuristics inspired by collective behaviors in nature have further broadened optimization strategies. The grey wolf optimizer (GWO) proposed by Mirjalili et al.27 mimics a hierarchical social-hunting mechanism and shows strong convergence stability, yet parameter sensitivity persists for complex multimodal functions. In addition, newer methods such as the whale optimization algorithm28, lion swarm algorithm29, sparrow search algorithm30, and bat algorithm31 have demonstrated strong solution capability on various fault diagnosis benchmarks, but they commonly face generalization bottlenecks during dimensionality reduction in high-dimensional feature spaces. The sterna migration algorithm (StMA) proposed by Bai et al.32 effectively integrates global and local exploration by simulating diversity-preserving migratory behaviors; although it shows potential for multi-objective model tuning, its performance remains constrained by the distribution quality of the initial population.

Regarding classifier construction, the research focus has shifted from single models to more robust ensemble frameworks33. Support vector machines (SVMs), guided by the structural-risk-minimization principle, have distinct advantages in the small-sample diagnosis of incipient bearing faults. Although the kernel mechanism enables nonlinear mapping, the computational complexity increases quadratically as the training set grows, and the method is highly sensitive to the penalty factor34. Decision-tree-based methods35 and their ensemble derivatives, such as random forests (RF)36 and gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost)37, substantially improve nonlinear representation and noise resistance for complex time–frequency features through voting or additive combinations of multiple base learners. However, these methods are essentially “cost-insensitive” models and neglect the highly asymmetric economic costs between false alarms, namely redundant maintenance, and missed alarms, namely unplanned downtime, in industrial practice38. Metric-learning-based approaches, such as k-nearest neighbors (kNN) proposed by Cover et al., are intuitive, yet they are susceptible to the “curse of dimensionality” in high-dimensional feature spaces and struggle to meet the performance requirements of real-time online monitoring39.

The rise of deep learning has shifted fault diagnosis from “handcrafted feature engineering” to “automatic feature learning”40. Bai et al.41 employed a one-dimensional convolutional neural network (1D-CNN) to adaptively extract local impulsive features through receptive fields, while Li et al.42 further captured long-term dependencies in signals using CNN–LSTM/GRU hybrid models, achieving excellent end-to-end recognition performance. The Transformer architecture proposed by Vaswani et al.43 and its improved variants with multi-scale and parallel convolution have markedly enhanced modeling accuracy for global temporal patterns.

Recent studies have further attempted to improve intelligent fault diagnosis by integrating time–frequency representations, attention mechanisms, and dual-channel or multi-source feature fusion44. Liang et al.45 proposed TFSAF-Net, which enhances wavelet time–frequency representations through a time–frequency spectral feature enhancement module and introduces physical-driven feature decoupling and multi-scale attention fusion to improve the separability between fault-related components and noise. Xu et al.46 developed a time–frequency dual-channel Transformer model, LTFAFormer, to improve anti-noise capability and deployment efficiency by constructing a lightweight parallel attention mechanism across time- and frequency-domain representations. Guo et al.47 proposed a dual-channel feature aggregation network with an attentional mechanism for wind turbine fault diagnosis under variable-speed conditions, in which CWT-based time–frequency representations, patch-aware convolution, Haar wavelet downsampling, channel-prior convolutional attention, and cross-attention-based deep feature fusion are jointly employed to strengthen discriminative feature extraction. Zhao and An48 constructed a feature-fusion threshold-attention residual network combined with an enhanced Transformer to address rolling bearing fault diagnosis under small-sample and strong-noise conditions. Yang et al.48 introduced an external-attention-integrated convolutional neural network for rolling bearing diagnosis under imbalanced data conditions, indicating that attention mechanisms can also improve feature discrimination when sample distributions are skewed. Huang et al.50 proposed an improved dual-channel CNN–BiLSTM fusion-attention model for aero-engine bearing diagnosis, where time-domain and frequency-domain inputs are jointly modeled and fused through time–frequency interactive attention and local–global adaptive attention. In addition, Liang et al.51 developed an attention-augmented separable residual network, in which separable convolution and CBAM-based feature extraction are combined to reduce model parameters while highlighting key fault-sensitive regions. These recent works demonstrate that time–frequency representation learning, attention-enhanced feature extraction, and dual-channel or multi-source fusion have become important directions for improving diagnostic accuracy, noise robustness, and feature representation capability.

However, although the above studies have significantly advanced attention-based and time–frequency deep learning for machinery fault diagnosis, most of them still emphasize classification accuracy or robustness from the perspective of feature learning, while the mechanical meaning of the learned representations is often insufficiently constrained. For rolling bearings, localized defects on the inner race, outer race, or rolling element generate repeated contact impacts, excite structural resonances, and cause energy redistribution across characteristic frequency bands. If deep temporal features are learned without reference to these physical phenomena, the resulting representation may capture dataset-specific statistical patterns rather than fault-mechanism-consistent vibration signatures. The explicit use of physically interpretable descriptors to guide deep temporal feature selection therefore remains insufficiently explored. Moreover, purely or predominantly data-driven deep models still tend to exhibit a “black-box” nature, lack explicit physical semantics in their decision processes, and may suffer from robustness degradation under severe noise or limited labeled data52. More importantly, most existing studies focus primarily on improving accuracy, without sufficiently considering the trade-off between risk and benefit in industrial operation and maintenance (O&M)53.

To address these challenges, the framework proposed in this study does not rely solely on data-driven feature stacking. Instead, it constructs a physical feature channel, consisting of wavelet packet transform (WPT) energy distribution and impulsive statistics, and a deep spatiotemporal channel, consisting of 1D-CNN and bidirectional gated recurrent unit (Bi-GRU), in parallel, and introduces a cross-modal multi-head attention mechanism that uses physical features as the query vector to achieve deep-feature coupling under physical-prior constraints. Moreover, by establishing a cost-sensitive decision model, the diagnostic outputs are converted into a binary decision that minimizes the expected O&M cost, thereby fundamentally overcoming the bottleneck of conventional methods in risk-sensitive operating conditions. The main contributions are summarized as follows:

(1) Physical-prior-guided dual-channel hybrid feature learning framework. A parallel architecture consisting of a physical feature channel and a deep spatiotemporal feature channel is developed. The physical channel employs three-level wavelet packet transform energy ratios to describe fault-induced energy migration among frequency bands and uses impulsive statistics to characterize the shock-like vibration responses generated by localized defects. The deep channel leverages 1D-CNN and bidirectional GRU to capture local impact morphology and temporal dependency patterns that may not be fully represented by handcrafted descriptors. This design links the learned representation to bearing contact-impact dynamics and improves performance retention under the moderate-noise and limited-sample conditions investigated in this study, although robustness under extremely low-SNR conditions requires further enhancement.

(2) Cross-modal multi-head attention alignment with physical features as the query. Unlike conventional feature concatenation, this work introduces a cross-modal multi-head attention structure in which the embedded physical features serve as a single query vector and the deep-feature sequence serves as key/value pairs. This mechanism enables dynamic selection of deep temporal features under physical-prior constraints, while the scalar gated fusion strategy further regulates the contribution ratio between physical-prior features and attention-weighted deep features, thereby achieving an adaptive balance between “physical robustness” and “deep representational capacity”.

(3) Cost-sensitive evaluation and decision-mapping model for industrial O&M. Considering the asymmetric misclassification risks in industrial scenarios, a minimum expected-cost evaluation model based on posterior probabilities is constructed. By introducing a cost-ratio factor, a quantitative relationship between the posterior-probability threshold and the expected O&M cost is established, extending the evaluation paradigm from “accuracy-optimal classification” to “risk-cost-optimal O&M decision-making”.

(4) Systematic validation integrating performance, robustness, and economics. Independent evaluations on the CWRU dataset, the SEU dataset, and a self-built RB test rig are conducted under a unified preprocessing, training, and testing protocol. Noise degradation tests, small-sample generalization experiments, ablation studies, and cost-sensitivity analyses are further performed to validate the proposed framework from multiple perspectives. The results show that the proposed method achieves better performance retention under moderate-noise and limited-training-sample settings, whereas extreme low-SNR robustness and strict cross-operating-condition transfer diagnosis are left for future investigation. The results demonstrate that the proposed method can effectively reduce the missed-alarm rate while lowering the normalized average maintenance cost, providing a technically viable pathway with both physical meaning and economic benefits for intelligent diagnosis in risk-sensitive scenarios.

Overall framework.

Dual-channel feature extraction mechanism

To jointly exploit physical prior knowledge and data-driven feature-representation capability, a dual-channel feature-extraction framework is developed, consisting of a physical-feature channel and a deep spatiotemporal-feature channel. This design aims to improve performance retention under moderate-noise interference and limited-training-sample settings by introducing mechanism-related physical priors. Extremely low-SNR robustness and strict cross-operating-condition transfer are not treated as fully solved problems in this study.

Physical feature channel

The physical feature channel is constructed based on classical signal processing theory to extract statistical features with explicit physical meanings. A three-level wavelet packet decomposition is performed using the Daubechies-4 basis function:

where i denotes the i-th sub-band and represents the sub-band coefficients. The wavelet packet decomposition employs the Daubechies-4 (db4) basis with three decomposition levels (level = 3). The selection of the db4 wavelet basis and three-level wavelet packet decomposition was not based solely on empirical experience. It was determined by considering the time–frequency localization ability for non-stationary impulsive signals, the frequency-band resolution related to fault information, and the computational complexity. Bearing fault vibration signals usually contain short-duration contact impacts generated when a localized defect interacts with the rolling element or raceway. These impacts excite structural resonances and redistribute vibration energy from the fundamental rotational components to several higher-frequency bands. The db4 wavelet has compact support and adequate regularity, enabling it to preserve the temporal locality of impact events while providing stable frequency-band resolution for resonance-related energy variations. Therefore, it is suitable for extracting energy redistribution features associated with fault-induced impacts rather than merely describing generic spectral differences. Compared with db2, db4 provides better smoothness and frequency-domain representation. Compared with db6, sym4, and coif5, db4 can reduce boundary sensitivity and redundant representation caused by longer filters in the data used in this study.

For the decomposition level, two-level wavelet packet decomposition produces fewer sub-bands, which may be insufficient to describe the energy redistribution of fault impacts across multiple frequency bands. Although four- and five-level decompositions can improve frequency resolution, they also increase low-frequency redundancy, boundary effects, and computational complexity. They may also weaken the stability of transient impact components in short-window signals. Therefore, three-level wavelet packet decomposition was adopted in this study. This setting generates eight sub-band energy ratios, which are combined with kurtosis, skewness, and crest factor to form an 11-dimensional physical feature vector. The subsequent parameter sensitivity analysis further shows that the combination of db4 and three-level decomposition achieves the best overall performance among the tested settings in terms of Accuracy, Macro-F1, missed-detection probability, and normalized maintenance cost. During WPT calculation, symmetric boundary extension is applied at both ends of each finite vibration window to mitigate endpoint effects. This boundary treatment is introduced because each segmented sample is a short observation extracted from a continuous rotating system, and the vibration response at the two ends of the window should not be interpreted as a physical discontinuity. If zero padding or abrupt truncation were used, artificial high-frequency components could be generated near the window boundaries, thereby distorting the estimated sub-band energy ratios. Symmetric extension preserves local amplitude continuity at the window edges and is more consistent with the physical continuity of shaft rotation and bearing contact dynamics. After boundary extension and wavelet packet decomposition, the sub-band nodes are sorted from low to high according to the center frequency of the corresponding frequency band, yielding sub-bands. In this study, the number of attention heads is set to H=4, and both the physical feature embedding dimension and the deep feature fusion dimension are set to 64. This setting is mainly based on a trade-off between feature alignment ability and computational complexity. First, the bidirectional output dimension of the Bi-GRU is 64. Therefore, linearly mapping the 11-dimensional physical feature vector to 64 dimensions ensures dimensional consistency between the physical features and the deep temporal features. This allows the model to describe the matching relationship between the physical priors and the deep temporal features in different subspaces. If the number of attention heads is too small, the model may be unable to capture multi-scale fault patterns sufficiently. In this case, the improvement in performance is limited. Industrial O&M cost evaluation model

In industrial operation and maintenance (O&M) settings, the outputs of a fault diagnosis model not only serve to improve classification accuracy but also directly affect decision processes such as maintenance triggering, spare-part replacement, and shutdown scheduling. Because the economic losses and safety risks associated with different misclassification types are typically highly asymmetric, evaluating models solely using “equal-cost” metrics, such as accuracy, is often insufficient to reflect the true benefit–cost trade-off under risk-sensitive operating conditions54. Accordingly, this study introduces a cost-sensitive evaluation criterion based on the model’s posterior probability outputs and establishes an O&M-oriented cost evaluation model to map classification outcomes to interpretable and deployable decision-cost indicators.

Experimental validation and analysis

Datasets and experimental environment

To systematically evaluate the overall performance of the proposed attention-weighted hybrid feature learning framework in terms of fault recognition accuracy, noise-robustness, and O&M decision value, comparative experiments are conducted on three representative datasets: the public bearing dataset from Case Western Reserve University (CWRU), the public bearing dataset from Southeast University (SEU), and a self-built rolling bearing test-rig dataset (RB). The CWRU and SEU datasets are used to examine the reproducibility and performance consistency of the proposed method on different public data sources, whereas the RB dataset is further employed to assess its effectiveness under engineering-controlled conditions. For each dataset, model training and testing are conducted within the same dataset using a unified sample construction, preprocessing, and evaluation protocol. Therefore, the experimental setting evaluates within-dataset generalization and multi-dataset reproducibility rather than cross-dataset transfer diagnosis.

Experimental setup and protocol

Signal segmentation and normalization

For each dataset, sliding-window segmentation is performed with a window length of L=1024 and a stride of S=512 (50% overlap), and instance-level Z-score normalization is applied to each individual windowed sample. For clarity, Fig. 3 presents time-domain waveforms of raw vibration signals under four typical operating states (Normal, IR, OR, and RE). Fault conditions exhibit more pronounced periodic impulsive structures in the time domain than the normal condition. As shown in Fig. 4, signal denoising can visually suppress random perturbations and make transient impulsive features more salient. This figure is used only to illustrate the physical visibility of fault-related impacts under noise interference, while the proposed diagnostic model is trained and tested using normalized vibration segments without introducing an additional denoising module.

Time-domain comparison of raw vibration signals.

Comparison of signal denoising effects.

Because overlapping windows introduce strong correlations among adjacent samples, directly performing random train/test splitting at the window level may cause information leakage and lead to overestimated performance. Adjacent windows may share the same shaft-rotation phase, the same defect-induced impact event, or the same decaying structural resonance response. If such windows were assigned to different data subsets, the training and testing samples would no longer be physically independent observations. To avoid this issue, a block-level split is adopted at the level of the original continuous vibration sequence, followed by sliding-window segmentation within each split. The training and testing segments are temporally non-overlapping, and window overlap occurs only within the same split. This protocol places the evaluation boundary at the continuous-record level rather than at the window level, ensuring that test samples do not share highly overlapping raw sampling points or identical transient responses with training samples. Therefore, the protocol improves the physical independence, rigor, and reproducibility of the evaluation.

These observations indicate that the selected preprocessing and segmentation strategy preserves physically meaningful vibration responses associated with bearing defects. The fault-state waveforms contain more pronounced transient peaks and repeated impact patterns than the normal state, which is consistent with the periodic contact between localized defects and rolling elements. The denoising visualization further shows that random perturbations can obscure these impact responses, whereas the underlying defect-related transients remain observable after noise suppression. Therefore, the subsequent feature learning process is not based only on abstract numerical differences, but is grounded in vibration signatures that are physically associated with local defect excitation, resonance decay, and repeated contact impacts.

Physical-prior channel: envelope spectrum/time–frequency evidence and feature motivation

The physical-prior channel aims to construct robust representations grounded in fault mechanisms. Fault-induced impulses often generate modulation peaks in the envelope spectrum that are consistent with characteristic frequencies. As shown by the envelope-spectrum analysis in Fig. 5, the spectral peaks correspond well to the theoretical fault characteristic frequencies, indicating that the signal contains diagnostically informative components consistent with the underlying fault mechanism.

Envelope spectrum analysis of bearing faults.

As illustrated by the time–frequency heat map in Fig. 6, different fault states exhibit distinct energy aggregation patterns in specific frequency bands, suggesting that “band-wise energy redistribution” is an important cue for multi-state discrimination.

Time–frequency heat map of multiple operating states.

Therefore, band-energy features and impulsive statistical measures are adopted in the physical channel as mechanism-related prior descriptors. The envelope-spectrum evidence indicates that fault classes contain modulation components associated with characteristic bearing frequencies, while the time–frequency heat map shows that different defect states lead to distinct energy-concentration patterns across frequency bands. These observations support the physical rationale of using WPT energy ratios to characterize fault-induced energy redistribution after impact excitation and resonance transmission. This redistribution is closely related to fault location and damage severity because different contact positions and defect sizes generate different impact paths and resonance responses. In addition, kurtosis, skewness, and crest factor quantify the non-Gaussian amplitude distribution caused by intermittent shock events. A healthy bearing usually produces a relatively stable vibration response, whereas a defective bearing tends to generate sparse but high-amplitude impulses. By combining frequency-domain energy migration with time-domain impact characteristics, the physical channel provides mechanism-consistent prior constraints for subsequent cross-modal fusion and helps the model retain fault-related information under the tested moderate-noise and limited-sample settings.

Deep channel: representational evidence from convolution and temporal modeling

The deep channel learns discriminative representations directly from standardized raw waveform. As shown in the CNN convolution-kernel response map in Fig. 7, the CNN extracts local morphological patterns, and the first-layer kernel responses can be interpreted as adaptive filters that highlight local structures associated with fault-induced impulses. Subsequently, the temporal module models time dependencies, enabling abstract representations of periodic impacts and their evolution. The hierarchical feature maps in Fig. 8 show that, as the network deepens, the learned features gradually transform from approximately raw waveform responses into more abstract and discriminative representations. From a physical perspective, the shallow convolutional responses mainly preserve local waveform morphology, including transient peaks and short resonance segments, whereas the temporal module further aggregates their sequential occurrence patterns. This process enables the deep channel to represent not only numerical waveform differences but also the temporal organization of repeated defect-induced impacts, which is essential for distinguishing fault locations and damage severities in bearing vibration signals.

Fusion mechanism: attention-weighted alignment via multi-head attention

To align physical priors with deep temporal representations, a multi-head attention mechanism was used to guide feature selection over the deep feature sequence. In this structure, the embedded physical feature was used as the query, and the deep spatiotemporal features were used as the keys and values. The resulting attention weights describe how the physical priors match different temporal positions in the deep feature sequence.

As shown in Fig. 9, the attention weights vary across samples and fault states. This variation indicates that the model does not apply a fixed temporal weighting pattern to all vibration segments. Instead, the embedded physical features guide the model to emphasize temporal positions whose deep responses are more consistent with the current sample’s energy redistribution and impulsive characteristics. For samples containing clearer defect-induced impacts, the attention weights tend to concentrate on temporal regions carrying stronger transient or resonance-related responses; for samples with weaker or noisier signatures, the weights are distributed according to the degree of physical-feature matching. Thus, the attention module provides a physically interpretable alignment between mechanism-related descriptors and deep temporal representations. The scalar gated fusion mechanism then adjusts the contribution ratio between the physical-prior features and the attention-weighted deep features, allowing the final representation to adapt to variations in fault type, damage severity, and noise disturbance. Its effect is evaluated in the ablation study.

However, it still suffers from clear performance degradation under extremely low-SNR conditions such as 0 dB and − 5 dB.

In summary, the noise experiments show that the physically guided dual-channel feature learning framework, cross-modal attention alignment, and gated fusion mechanism improve diagnostic stability under moderate noise conditions and provide a relative advantage under stronger noise. At 10 dB, the proposed method outperforms CNN-LSTM and Transformer by 6.76 and 7.61% points, respectively. At 5 dB, it still maintains advantages of 6.48 and 7.99% points over CNN-LSTM and Transformer, respectively. These results indicate that the physical-prior channel and attention-guided fusion can reduce part of the feature instability caused by noise disturbance. However, the large accuracy decrease under 0 dB and − 5 dB also shows that the current framework cannot fully resist extremely strong noise, where the noise energy is close to or higher than the fault-induced impulsive components. Therefore, the robustness conclusion of this study should be limited to moderate-noise and moderately strong-noise conditions. Future work should combine the current framework with signal enhancement, noise-adaptive training, robust feature constraints, contrastive learning, or denoising modules to improve its reliability in extremely low-SNR industrial scenarios.

Small-sample performance analysis

To evaluate the performance of the proposed physically guided dual-channel hybrid feature learning framework under limited training samples, a small-sample experiment was conducted in addition to the regular performance comparison. Unlike the regular evaluation using the full training data, this experiment keeps the test set unchanged and controls only the number of training samples. In this way, the learning ability and performance stability of different models under limited labeled samples could be examined.

The overall performance comparison in the previous sections mainly used 10-fold cross-validation to evaluate the average performance and stability of the models under the full training-data setting. By contrast, the small-sample experiment in this section used an independent fixed-test-set subsampling protocol. The purpose is not to repeat the cross-validation process but to analyze the effect of different training-sample ratios on test performance. First, the block-level data-splitting strategy was still used. The original continuous vibration signals were divided into non-overlapping training, validation, and test blocks to reduce the risk of information leakage caused by overlapping sliding windows. Based on this split, the validation set and test set were kept unchanged for all training ratios. Only the training samples are subsampled within the training blocks according to a class-balanced strategy. Specifically, 5%, 10%, 20%, 50%, and 100% of the training samples were randomly selected for model training, while the remaining training samples were not used under the corresponding training ratio.

For each training-sample ratio, class-balanced subsampling and repeated training were performed using five different random seeds. The mean and standard deviation of Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and Macro-F1 were then reported. Therefore, the standard deviations in Table 8 reflect performance variations caused by different random training subsets and repeated training rather than variations among different folds in 10-fold cross-validation. This setting allows different models to be compared under the same test-set distribution, and it helps evaluate their sensitivity and stability when the number of training samples decreases.

Economic evaluation

In rotating machinery condition monitoring and predictive maintenance scenarios, bearing fault-diagnosis outputs are typically used as upstream triggers for maintenance decisions, directly influencing action strategies such as whether to inspect, repair, or shut down equipment. To align with industrial O&M workflows, the diagnostic system is evaluated within the decision chain “online monitoring → health-state identification → maintenance triggering (routine inspection/shutdown inspection/disassembly inspection) → repair or replacement → return to operation”. Under this chain, model outputs mainly induce two types of risk events that are highly cost-sensitive: (1) misses, where the true state is faulty but predicted as normal, potentially leading to continued operation with defects and inducing unplanned downtime, secondary damage, or even catastrophic failure; and (2) false alarms, where the true state is normal but predicted as faulty, triggering unnecessary inspections and shutdown checks, thus incurring additional inspection costs and opportunity costs. In practice, missed detections are typically associated with higher failure risks and downtime losses, whereas false alarms mainly introduce additional inspection and opportunity costs. Therefore, evaluation should focus on the asymmetric costs of error types rather than classification accuracy alone. Accordingly, this study further reports maintenance-cost-related metrics from an economic-decision perspective and emphasizes the improvement in O&M benefits achieved by suppressing missed detections, especially fault-to-normal errors.

The CWRU dataset was selected because it is the main public benchmark dataset used in this study and provides a standard 10-class diagnostic task consistent with the model implementation. Using the CWRU dataset for parameter sensitivity analysis also ensures consistency among the main performance comparison, ablation study, noise robustness analysis, and hyperparameter robustness evaluation.

Conclusions and outlook

Conclusions

This study proposes a physically guided dual-channel hybrid feature-learning framework for rolling-bearing fault diagnosis. The framework addresses non-stationary vibration signals, noise interference, and asymmetric maintenance costs. It integrates feature modeling with O&M-oriented decision evaluation. The main conclusions are summarized below based on multi-dataset validation, noise tests, small-sample experiments, and cost-sensitive analysis.

(1) Collaborative modeling of physical priors and deep representations improves representation stability under the evaluated diagnostic conditions.

A dual-channel feature extraction mechanism is developed. The physical channel uses wavelet packet energy distribution and impulsive statistics. The deep channel uses one-dimensional convolution and bidirectional temporal modeling. The physical descriptors characterize energy redistribution, impact impulsiveness, amplitude asymmetry, and transient peak concentration caused by localized defects. The deep channel captures complex waveform patterns and temporal dependencies. Ablation results confirm their complementarity. Introducing physical priors reduces missed detections and false alarms under the evaluated moderate-noise and limited-sample settings. Strict cross-operating-condition transfer diagnosis remains outside the scope of this study.

(2) The physically guided cross-modal multi-head attention mechanism effectively mitigates the failure risk of static feature fusion.

A cross-modal multi-head attention mechanism is introduced to improve feature fusion. The embedded physical features serve as the query vector. The deep-feature sequence serves as the key and value. This structure selects temporal deep features that match the current physical priors. Attention visualizations show adaptive focus shifts across samples and fault states. Ablation results show that attention fusion outperforms concatenation and average fusion. This explains the improved intra-class compactness and clearer inter-class separation after fusion.

(3) Scalar gated fusion provides a learnable and lightweight mechanism for regulating dual-channel contributions.

Building upon cross-modal attention alignment, a learnable scalar gating coefficient is introduced to adaptively fuse physical-prior features and attention-weighted deep features. The fusion-strategy ablation shows that, compared with attention-only fusion, the proposed gated fusion further improves Accuracy and Macro-F1 while reducing in Eq. (46) and in Eq. (45). Meanwhile, it introduces only a slight increase in the number of parameters, FLOPs, and inference time. These results indicate that the performance gain of the full model is associated with sample-level regulation of heterogeneous feature contributions rather than a substantial increase in model capacity.

Feature-distribution analysis further supports the effectiveness of gated fusion. Compared with concatenation fusion and attention-only fusion, gated fusion yields a smaller intra-class distance, a larger inter-class distance, a higher Silhouette score, and a lower Davies-Bouldin index. The gating mechanism suppresses redundant modality information and strengthens complementary fault-sensitive features. It improves both maintenance-cost metrics and feature-space separability.

(4) An O&M-cost-oriented evaluation paradigm more faithfully reflects engineering value.

This study extends evaluation beyond Accuracy and F1 score. Multiclass diagnostic outputs are mapped to the binary decision of whether maintenance should be triggered. Asymmetric costs are assigned to missed detections and false alarms. A posterior-probability-based minimum expected-cost model is then established. Theoretical and experimental results show that the proposed method achieves the lowest normalized maintenance cost under high missed-detection cost settings. This advantage mainly comes from the reduction in miss-related risk. The ablation study further confirms that gated fusion reduces both missed-detection probability and normalized maintenance cost compared with attention-only fusion.

(5) Multi-dataset evaluation, moderate-noise tests, and small-sample experiments demonstrate good reproducibility and performance retention under the evaluated constrained settings.

Experiments on the CWRU, SEU, and RB datasets confirm the reproducibility of the proposed method. All datasets are evaluated under a unified preprocessing, training, and testing protocol. The method achieves stable within-dataset generalization. Noise degradation tests show higher diagnostic accuracy than CNN, LSTM, CNN-LSTM, and Transformer at 20 dB and 10 dB. These results indicate that physical-prior features, attention alignment, and gated fusion improve feature stability under moderate noise.

Under extremely low-SNR conditions, such as 0 dB and − 5 dB, the model accuracy still decreases substantially. The noise experiments therefore support performance retention under moderate noise and a relative advantage under the tested stronger-noise settings. They do not indicate that robust diagnosis under extremely strong noise has been fully solved.

The small-sample experiments show that, when the training-sample ratio is reduced to 5% and 10%, purely data-driven models show more obvious performance degradation, while the proposed method still maintains relatively high Accuracy and Macro-F1. This result indicates that the physical-prior channel can provide stable mechanism-related constraints when labeled samples are limited, thereby reducing the dependence on large-scale labeled data.

Parameter sensitivity analysis supports the rationality of the selected configuration. Within the tested candidate space, the best trade-off is obtained using the db4 wavelet basis, three-level wavelet packet decomposition, a learning rate of, a batch size of 64, four attention heads, a fusion dimension of 64, and. This setting achieves balanced performance in Accuracy, Macro-F1, missed-detection probability, false-alarm probability, normalized maintenance cost, and computational complexity. The improvement is therefore not mainly caused by an accidental parameter choice. It is supported by stable performance within the tested parameter range.

The results confirm good reproducibility, within-dataset generalization, and performance retention under moderate-noise and limited-sample settings. Strict cross-dataset transfer diagnosis, cross-operating-condition generalization, and robust diagnosis under extremely low-SNR conditions remain beyond the scope of this study. These issues will be investigated in future work.

The proposed framework is not a simple modification of a single model architecture. It establishes a systematic pipeline of “physical-prior modeling → deep representation learning → cross-modal dynamic fusion → cost-sensitive decision evaluation”. The framework jointly considers diagnostic accuracy, robustness, interpretability, and engineering decision value. It provides a practical pathway for intelligent bearing monitoring in risk-sensitive O&M scenarios.

Research contributions

(1) Physically guided dual-channel hybrid feature learning framework.

This study proposes a hybrid feature-learning framework with parallel physical and deep spatiotemporal channels. The physical channel extracts wavelet packet energy distributions, kurtosis, skewness, and crest factor. The deep channel learns temporal representations through 1D-CNN and Bi-GRU. This design enables collaborative modeling between physical-prior constraints and data-driven feature learning. Experiments show better performance retention under moderate-noise and limited-sample settings than typical purely data-driven models. The claimed scope is within-dataset generalization and reproducibility across independently evaluated datasets. Strict cross-operating-condition and cross-equipment transfer diagnosis are left for future work.

(2) Cross-modal multi-head attention alignment with physical features as the query.

This work introduces a single-query cross-modal attention structure from physical features to deep features. It differs from conventional self-attention and simple cross-modal weighting. The structure constrains deep temporal feature selection using physical priors and achieves directed feature alignment.

(3) Adaptive dual-channel fusion driven by scalar gating.

A learnable scalar gating coefficient is introduced for adaptive dual-channel fusion. It regulates the contribution ratio between physical-prior features and attention-weighted deep features at the sample level. Ablation results show that gated fusion outperforms concatenation fusion, average fusion, and attention-only fusion. It improves diagnostic performance and maintenance-related error indicators with limited computational overhead. This provides a lightweight implementation for adaptive dual-channel collaborative modeling.

(4) Cost-sensitive evaluation and decision mapping model for industrial O&M.

Multiclass fault diagnosis results are systematically mapped to maintenance-trigger decisions. Quantitative relationships among posterior-probability thresholds, miss/false-alarm probabilities, and expected O&M cost are established theoretically and experimentally. This extends the evaluation paradigm from “classification-performance optimality” to “O&M-cost optimality”.

(5) Systematic experimental validation of fusion performance, noise degradation, small-sample performance, and economic evaluation.

The proposed method is evaluated through diagnostic comparison, ablation analysis, noise degradation tests, small-sample experiments, and maintenance-cost sensitivity analysis. The results show stable within-dataset performance across multiple datasets. The model retains good performance at 10–20 dB and remains competitive at 5 dB. Cost-sensitive evaluation shows lower missed-detection probability and improved maintenance-decision efficiency. Accuracy still decreases clearly at 0 dB and − 5 dB. Thus, the results support robustness under moderate noise, not fully solved diagnosis under extremely strong noise.

Limitations and future directions

Although the proposed physically guided dual-channel hybrid feature-learning framework achieves favorable performance in diagnostic accuracy and maintenance-cost optimization, several limitations remain and warrant further investigation.

(1) Domain adaptation across operating conditions and equipment.

Although the proposed framework has been independently evaluated on multiple datasets, strict cross-dataset transfer diagnosis is not conducted in this study. The current validation mainly uses labeled data with the same distribution within each dataset. In real industrial settings, new equipment, variable loads, and variable speeds may cause domain mismatch. Future work will incorporate unsupervised or weakly supervised domain adaptation strategies. Physical-prior constraints will be preserved to reduce reliance on dataset-specific labels. This may improve engineering transferability across heterogeneous equipment.

(2) Model complexity and real-time overhead for edge deployment.

To enable deep coupling of heterogeneous features, the proposed approach adopts parallel dual channels and a cross-modal attention mechanism, which improves performance but also increases computational complexity. For resource-constrained Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) edge nodes, real-time deployment remains challenging. Future studies will explore structured pruning, knowledge distillation, and low-bit quantization. These techniques may support lightweight deployment. The goal is to retain the ability to suppress false alarms and missed detections.

(3) Multi-source information fusion and health-evolution modeling.

The current model is mainly built on single-source vibration signals. Degradation in complex mechanical systems often involves multiple physical quantities, including acoustic emission, temperature, and current signals. The long-horizon temporal evolution of health states has not yet been systematically modeled. Future research will extend the framework to multi-modal sensing scenarios. Long-term evolution models will also be incorporated. This may bridge fault diagnosis, remaining useful life prediction, and maintenance-path optimization.

(4) Dynamic cost modeling and closed-loop O&M decision-making.

This work adopts a relatively static setting for cost factors. In real O&M practice, spare-part inventory, production planning, and shutdown windows vary dynamically over time. Future work may construct dynamic cost models. Diagnostic results can also be fed back into production scheduling systems. This would support a closed-loop framework of “diagnosis–decision–feedback”. Such a framework may help model outputs guide adaptive O&M adjustments on site.

(5) Real-world industrial validation and robustness to concept drift.

The current validation is mainly based on test-rig data, and long-term online monitoring evaluation from real industrial scenarios is still lacking. Future work will address concept drift and sensor degradation in field applications. Continual learning and online updating will be investigated. These studies will further verify external validity and environmental robustness.

(6) Limited robustness under extremely low-SNR conditions.

The proposed model retains good performance at 10–20 dB. It also shows a relative advantage over the compared models at 5 dB. Its diagnostic accuracy decreases clearly at 0 dB and − 5 dB. Under these conditions, noise energy can approach or exceed the energy of fault-induced impulsive components. Wavelet-packet energy features, impulsive statistics, and deep temporal features are then strongly disturbed. Feature separability consequently decreases. Future work will combine noise-adaptive training, signal enhancement, robust feature constraints, contrastive learning, and denoising modules to improve reliability under strong industrial noise.

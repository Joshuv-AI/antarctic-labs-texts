Response to comment on: Flow capsulorhexis: A novel technique in white and hypermature cataracts
Authors: Mithun Thulasidas
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

We thank the author[1] for the interest shown and comments on our article.[2] We agree that parameters such as lens swelling, anterior capsular tension, and anterior chamber depth should be examined preoperatively to provide optimum outcomes. The preoperative criteria for the intumescent subtypes in our study were either (i) the presence of a shallow anterior chamber, the presence of fluid vacuoles or sectoral markings in the anterior cortex on slit-lamp biomicroscopy, or (ii) the presence of multiple internal acoustic reflections on ultrasonography A-scan, where the multiple spikes indicated multiple fluid compartments. However, like any other technique, flow-capsulorhexis is not one with a 100% success rate. In our study, the success rate was 95.8%.

Regarding the use of cohesive ophthalmic viscosurgical devices (OVDs), the downward pressure exerted by cohesive OVDs, as described in some studies, may be dangerous.[3] A reverse Argentinian flag sign may also occur if the anterior chamber is overfilled with OVD, as described by Bhardwaj et al.[4] This is one of the reasons why we used only dispersive OVD. Nevertheless, it is a surgeon’s choice, and high-density cohesive OVD can be used in selected high-risk cases, as the author explained.

There was a statistically significant difference in the rate of peripheral radial tear-out between group 1 (13 eyes) and group 2 (38 eyes) (P < 0.01). We thank the author for highlighting this and apologize to the readers for not mentioning it. We appreciate the comments given by the author for our study and agree that further multicenter clinical trials on this subject may be beneficial.

Tissue-specific profiling of human protein phosphatases identifies G6PC1 as a liver cancer–selective biomarker
Authors: Adil R. Sarhan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Protein phosphatases are essential regulators of cellular signalling, yet their tissue-specific expression profiles and functional roles in cancer remain underexplored. This study aimed to systematically profile protein phosphatases expression across human tissues and malignancies to identify potential biomarkers. The transcriptomic data from GTEx, FANTOM, and HPA, were analysed assessing 265 protein phosphatases across 60 human tissues. Tissue specificity was quantified using the Tau index and coefficient of variation. Top candidates were further evaluated in TCGA and PCAWG cancer cohorts. Protein-level validation was performed using immunohistochemistry (IHC) data from HPA. Functional relevance was investigated through protein–protein interaction (PPI) network analysis and KEGG pathway annotations. G6PC1 emerged as the most liver-specific phosphatase (Tau > 0.99) with high inter-tissue expression variability. Its expression was sharply confined to liver tissue and retained in hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC), with very low expression across other tumour types. IHC confirmed G6PC1 protein presence in liver cancer samples. PPI analysis positioned G6PC1 as a central metabolic regulator, interacting with key enzymes involved in gluconeogenesis and glucose homeostasis. Notably, G6PC1 expression remained stable across sex, age, tumour stage, ploidy, tumour purity, and tumour mutational burden (TMB) in PCAWG liver cancer patients. This integrative multi-omic analysis identifies G6PC1 as a highly liver-specific, biologically relevant, and potential diagnostic candidate for hepatocellular carcinoma. Its selective expression, protein-level detectability, and network centrality support its potential utility as a diagnostic candidate for liver cancer, warranting further experimental validation in independent clinical cohorts.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03454-7.

Introduction

Protein phosphatases are fundamental regulators of multiple cellular signalling, playing an important role and functioning alongside kinases to mediate a dephosphorylation events, a core mechanism controlling cell proliferation, differentiation, metabolism, and survival [1, 2]. While kinases have been extensively investigated and therapeutically targeted in cancer, phosphatases remain underexplored, despite increasing evidence that their dysregulation can contribute to oncogenesis by perturbing critical signalling cascades [3, 4].

In contrast to kinases, there is much less known about the tissue-specific expression profiles of phosphatases or their potential utility as diagnostic biomarkers. A systematic, multi-omic profiling of protein phosphatases expression across normal human tissues can identify genes with restricted, tissue-specific patterns making them promising candidates for tumour-specific biomarkers. Despite the biological importance of phosphatases, comprehensive efforts to map their expression across tissues have been scarce. Therefore, the availability of high-resolution datasets such as GTEx, FANTOM, and the HPA now enables integrated, multi-platform analysis of transcript abundance across diverse human tissues. Leveraging these resources, an unbiased, large-scale profiling of protein phosphatases was conducted to identify genes with high tissue specificity and potential clinical relevance.

Among the candidate genes identified, G6PC1, which encodes the catalytic subunit of glucose-6-phosphatase, emerged as the most liver specific phosphatase across all datasets analysed. Given its known role in hepatic glucose metabolism, this study further investigated G6PC1 expression in hepatocellular carcinoma. Using TCGA and PCAWG tumour datasets, as well as protein level evidence from HPA immunohistochemistry dataset, G6PC1 is selectively expressed in liver tissue and retained in liver cancer. Protein–protein interaction (PPI) network analysis identified G6PC1 as a central hub which further highlights its functional integration in hepatic metabolic pathways.

Together, these findings position G6PC1 as a liver-restricted phosphatase with high tissue specificity, biological relevance, and clinical stability supporting its potential utility as a diagnostic biomarker in liver cancer.

Materials and methods

Data sources

A total of protein-coding phosphatase genes (n = 265) were retained for downstream analysis, based on manually curated lists from the literature and gene ontology annotations. Transcriptomic datasets of the normal tissues were retrieved from three major publicly available platforms. Expression values were downloaded as normalized transcripts per million (nTPM): GTEx (Genotype-Tissue Expression) which includes RNA-seq profiles covering 37 tissues [5]; The Human Protein Atlas (HPA) which also includes RNA-seq profiles covering 40 tissues [6]; FANTOM5, a cap analysis gene expression (CAGE)-based dataset covering 60 tissues [7]. The immunohistochemistry IHC staining dataset were downloaded from HPA [6]. Gene expression data for tumours were retrieved from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) [8]. Gene expression values in TCGA were downloaded as Fragments Per Kilobase of transcript per Million mapped reads (FPKM). Finaly, the clinical and genomic robustness of G6PC1 expression in cancer was retrieved from the Pan-Cancer Analysis of Whole Genomes (PCAWG) project [9].

Cross-dataset correlation of phosphatase gene expression

Pairwise correlation analyses through GTEx, HPA and FANTOM platforms were used to evaluate phosphatase gene expression consistency across platforms. All datasets were in nTPM and normalized to a similar scale using quantile normalization. To compare gene expression across platforms, quantile normalization was applied using the quantile_transform function from scikit-learn. This aligned nTPM distributions within each dataset and reduced inter-platform bias. Pearson correlation coefficients (r) were then calculated on the normalized data to assess similarity across common genes and tissues. Next, gene-gene correlation matrices were constructed within each dataset to assess co-regulation across tissues. These matrices represent the coordinated patterns of expression among phosphatase genes, and they were visualized as heatmaps with hierarchical clustering (Euclidean distance, complete linkage).

Expression profiling and hierarchical clustering of phosphatases across tissues

Distribution of protein phosphatases expression values were analysed across normal tissues using GTEx, HPA, and FANTOM datasets. For each dataset, nTPM values were log2(nTPM + 1) transformed and the summary statistics (mean, median, standard deviation) were also computed. Then, phosphatase genes were assigned to families using UniProt annotations and curated literature. Mean nTPM was used to aggregate the gene expressions across tissues for each gene. The resulting expression matrix (families and tissues) were z-score normalized to make a relative comparison among families and tissues. Hierarchical clustering was performed using the Euclidean distance and Ward’s minimum variance method in order to cluster tissues together with phosphatase families based on expression patterns.

TCGA cancer expression profiling

The tissue-specific phosphatases from normal tissue were further analysed for their expression in tumours, using gene expression data from TCGA project. Five candidate genes were analysed using this approach: ACP3, G6PC1, TPTE, DUSP21, and FBP1, each has been selected for their high tissue specificity and expression variability in the datasets (GTEx, HPA, and FANTOM). Descriptive statistics including mean, median, minimum, and maximum log2-transformed expression were also calculated for each gene.

Candidate biomarker selection

A comparative specificity analysis was performed between normal and cancer datasets to infer whether tissue-specific expression of phosphatase genes was conserved in cancer as well. The Tau index and the max expression cancer were analysed for each gene across TCGA cancer types. Tissue specificity in normal tissues was assessed using GTEx, HPA, and FANTOM, and the average Tau score across these datasets was computed to derive a consensus tissue-specificity measure. To directly compare tumour and normal specificity, each TCGA cancer type was mapped to its most biologically relevant normal tissue counterpart. This cancer–normal tissue mapping was based on expression specificity agreement and is provided in Supplementary Table S1. Then, a correlation analyses was performed between cancer Tau and average normal Tau values to assess concordance.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) validation

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) data from the Human Protein Atlas (HPA) Pathology Atlas were analysed in order to validate protein level expression of the candidate phosphatase genes. Protein expression was estimated by H-score method which is semi-quantitative that combines staining intensity and percentage of positive tumour cells [11]. Where I is the staining intensity (0 = no staining, 1 = weak, 2 = moderate, 3 = strong). P represents the percentage of cancer cells stained at that intensity level.

Protein–protein interaction network construction and analysis

To dissect the functional context of phosphatase genes as well as all central regulatory genes, STRING database was used to analyse a protein–protein interaction (PPI) network [12]. The analysis involved phosphatase genes plus both phosphatase genes and their interactions. Only interactions with a STRING combined confidence score ≥ 0.7 were included to ensure high-confidence network construction. Network edges stand for predicted or experimentally supported protein–protein interactions (direct interactions, co-expression, co-occurrence). Hub phosphatases were identified based on degree centrality of each node. Functional enrichments analysis of the full network and subnetworks were performed on KEGG pathway annotations [13].

Clinical stratification using PCAWG data

The clinical and genomic robustness of G6PC1 expression in cancer metadata from PCAWG project was examined. From the dataset 193 tumour samples were retrieved in which G6PC1 was altered. The dataset included clinical and genomic variables including sex, age at diagnosis, tumour stage (AJCC and TNM), tumour purity (cellularity), ploidy, whole-genome duplication status, and tumour mutational burden (TMB). Descriptive statistics across all variables were analysed to evaluate the demographic and clinical distinctiveness of G6PC1-positive cases.

Software and visualization

The data preprocessing and statistical analysis were done in Python (v3.11.6) platform (Pandas, NumPy, and SciPy libraries for data manipulation and statistical analysis). All visualizations were made with GraphPad Prism (v10.31), as well as Seaborn (0.13.0) and Matplotlib (3.8.0) in Python. An interactive PPI network including human phosphatases and their high-confidence interactors was constructed using STRING data and visualized using NetworkX and PyVis in Python.

Results

Dataset correlation confirms platform compatibility

To assess the compatibility and consistency of phosphatase gene expression across platforms, transcriptomic data from three large-scale datasets (FANTOM, GTEx, and HPA) were analysed. A total of 265 protein phosphatase genes were represented in both GTEx and HPA, while FANTOM covered 261 overlapping genes. The FANTOM dataset exhibited the highest overall expression levels, with a median nTPM of 23.5 and a maximum of 2993.8, compared to median nTPM = 9.1 (max = 1808.1) in GTEx and median nTPM = 7.6 (max = 4199.1) in HPA. These differences may reflect variations in transcript capture sensitivity and quantification methodology, such as FANTOM’s use of cap analysis of gene expression (CAGE) versus RNA-seq in GTEx and HPA. To quantify cross-dataset agreement, gene-wise expression values were compared using scatter plots overlaid with kernel density estimates and calculated Pearson correlation coefficients (Fig. 1, Supplementary Table S2). The strongest correlation was observed between GTEx and HPA (r = 0.88), reflecting high consistency likely due to similar RNA-seq protocols and tissue processing strategies. A strong correlation was also found between FANTOM and HPA (r = 0.846), while FANTOM vs. GTEx showed slightly lower but still substantial agreement (r = 0.805). These findings confirm a high level of reproducibility in phosphatase gene expression profiles across independent platforms.

Gene-gene correlation reveal conserved and divergent phosphatase modules

To explore co-regulation patterns among protein phosphatases, gene-gene correlation matrices were constructed for each dataset. These heatmaps (Fig. 2A-C, Supplementary Table S3) visualize the Pearson correlation coefficients of phosphatases expression across all tissues, enabling identification of co-expressed modules that may reflect shared regulatory control or functional pathways.

In FANTOM (Fig. 2A), the analysis revealed multiple highly correlated gene pairs, with DUSP21-TPTE emerging as the most strongly co-expressed pair (r = 0.999), suggesting potential co-regulation or shared tissue specificity, particularly in testis. Other notable high-correlation pairs included ALPP-BPGM (r = 0.991), ALPI-PPP1R14D (r = 0.979), and CDKN3-TPTE (r = 0.971), reflecting shared expression patterns across tissues and possibly related regulatory networks. Many of these correlations involved genes with known tissue-restricted expression or involvement in immune or metabolic pathways. For instance, PGAM2-PPP1R3A (r = 0.956) are both muscle-associated metabolic enzymes, while PTPN22-PTPRC (r = 0.959) are implicated in immune cell signalling. These findings support the idea that certain phosphatases and their regulatory partners are tightly co-expressed in specific tissue or functional contexts.

Correlation analysis in the HPA dataset further supported the presence of highly co-expressed, tissue-restricted phosphatases (Fig. 2B). DUSP21 and TPTE remained tightly correlated (r = 0.998), consistent with previous findings in FANTOM and reinforcing their robust testis-specific expression profiles. Additionally, DUSP21 also showed strong correlation with TPTE2 (r = 0.989), suggesting broader coordination among TPTE family members in testicular signalling. A second cluster was observed involving PGAM2, PLPP7, PTP4A3, PPP1R3C, and NT5C1A. PGAM2, a glycolytic enzyme with high skeletal muscle expression, showed strong co-expression with PLPP7 (r = 0.997) and PPP1R3C (r = 0.990), both of which are involved in lipid and glycogen metabolism. PLPP7 also highly correlated with PTP4A3, a phosphatase implicated in cell migration and cancer progression. These associations highlight a coordinated regulatory module likely active in metabolically demanding tissues such as muscle and possibly involved in phospholipid turnover or energy storage. DUSP29 and PPM1N also formed a correlated pair (r = 0.988), supporting their potential co-involvement in tissue-specific dephosphorylation events.

Gene-gene correlation analysis in GTEx revealed a tightly co-expressed cluster of testis-enriched phosphatases (Fig. 2C). The highest correlations were observed between DUSP21, TPTE, and CDKN3, with DUSP21-TPTE showing perfect correlation (r = 1.0), and CDKN3 co-expressed with both (r > 0.998). These results mirror the top correlations observed in FANTOM and HPA, strongly reinforcing their testis-restricted expression and potential co-regulation in reproductive signalling pathways. Additional highly correlated genes in this cluster included PHOSPHO1 and NT5DC4, which also showed strong correlations with DUSP21 and TPTE (r > 0.996). PHOSPHO1 encodes a phosphatase involved in phosphate metabolism and testicular development, while NT5DC4 is enriched in testis and brain and may participate in nucleotide turnover. The appearance of CDC25C (r = 0.994 with CDKN3), a cell cycle associated phosphatase, further suggests a regulatory module that integrates cell division, differentiation, and tissue-specific signalling in testis. Together, these co-expression patterns highlight a robust transcriptional network of testis-associated phosphatases and phosphatase-like enzymes that may functionally coordinate spermatogenesis or germ cell maintenance.

Global expression trends of protein phosphatases across human tissues

The analysis of gene expression across tissues in the datasets revealed consistent patterns in the normal tissues (Fig. 3, Supplementary Table S4). Across all datasets, skeletal muscle consistently exhibited the highest levels of gene expression, highlighting its significant biological activity. In the FANTOM dataset, skeletal muscle showed the highest value (mean nTPM = 91.74), indicating a strong level of gene expression. This was followed by heart muscle and liver, with mean nTPM values of 67.47 and 67.30, respectively. These tissues also demonstrated relatively high median nTPM values, suggesting consistent expression levels across samples. The standard deviation values, however, indicated some variability in gene expression within these tissues. Similarly, in the GTEx dataset, skeletal muscle again ranked highest with a mean nTPM of 46.20. Heart muscle and putamen followed, with mean nTPM values of 32.81 and 28.41, respectively. These findings reinforce the importance of muscle-related tissues in gene expression, while also highlighting the role of brain-related tissues such as the putamen. The variability in expression, as indicated by the standard deviation, was lower in this dataset compared to FANTOM. The HPA dataset also identified skeletal muscle as the tissue with the highest mean nTPM, with a value of 58.16. Tongue and prostate followed, with mean nTPM values of 37.01 and 30.80, respectively. These tissues showed notable variability in gene expression, as reflected by their higher standard deviation values. This suggests that while skeletal muscle consistently exhibits high gene expression, other tissues such as tongue and prostate may have more heterogeneous expression patterns.

Clustering analysis reveals tissue-specific expression patterns of phosphatase families

To explore global phosphatase family-level trends, hierarchical clustering analysis was conducted using aggregated and normalized expression data (Fig. 4). Serine/threonine phosphatases, Dual specificity phosphatases, and Nucleotidases consistently appear among the top expressed families across all three datasets. In contrast, Alkaline phosphatases and HAD-like hydrolases consistently ranked among the lowest expressed families across datasets. Some families displayed dataset-specific variation. For example, Phosphoglycerate mutases were highly expressed in GTEx and HPA, but low in FANTOM, possibly reflecting differences in transcript capture sensitivity or tissue annotation. Protein tyrosine phosphatases showed low expression in GTEx and HPA but ranked among the top in FANTOM. Histidine phosphatases were low in GTEx, high in HPA, and low again in FANTOM. The clustering also confirmed family-tissue specificity, with certain groups enriched in specialized tissues. For instance, Alkaline phosphatases, while generally low overall, are highly expressed in placenta, small intestine and duodenum, emphasizing the limitation of rank-based analysis without tissue context.

Identification and profiling of hub phosphatases across human tissues

To identify central regulators within the phosphatase signalling landscape, a PPI network was constructed using curated data from the STRING database (Fig. 5). Degree centrality was used to quantify the number of direct interactions for each gene, enabling the identification of highly connected hub phosphatases, key components thought to maintain the structural and functional stability of cellular signalling networks. The most connected node was PPP1CA, followed by PTEN, PPP1CB, PPP1CC, and PPP2CA. These genes represent core elements of the phosphatase interactome, with wide ranging regulatory influence over cell cycle progression, apoptosis, immune signalling, and metabolic homeostasis.

To complement the interaction analysis, tissue-level expression patterns of these hub genes were also examined (Fig. 5). Several hub phosphatases exhibited broad expression across diverse tissues. For example, PPP1CB was broadly expressed across tissues, with its highest expression in skeletal muscle, consistent with a potential role in regulating muscle contraction or cytoskeletal dynamics. PTEN exhibited high expression in the thymus, adipose tissue, cervix, and spleen, consistent with its known role in regulating PI3K/AKT signalling and maintaining cellular homeostasis in immune, endocrine, and epithelial tissues.

To better understand the signalling context and biological relevance of the protein phosphatases, phosphatases with their interactors were analysed. The interactors of all phosphatases revealed a diverse and functionally rich set of connections (Supplementary Table S5, an interactive version of the phosphatase network is provided in Supplementary File S5). For instance, PPP1CB interacted with PPP1CC, PPM1G, and multiple regulatory subunits such as PPP1R15A and PPP1R12A, forming a dense core of catalytic and modulatory components. PPP1CA shared many of these interactors but also linked to RB1 and EIF2S1, suggesting involvement in translational control and cell cycle regulation. PTEN was connected to phosphoinositide signalling regulators including INPP4A, PIK3R2, and PIKFYVE. Likewise, INPP4A and INPP4B interacted with membrane trafficking phosphatases such as OCRL and SYNJ2, underscoring their integration into vesicle signalling dynamics. These interactions reinforce the idea that phosphatase hubs not only anchor signalling pathways but also coordinate functional crosstalk between major regulatory domains including metabolism, immunity, and oncogenesis.

Identification of tissue-specific phosphatases using Tau and expression variability

To identify phosphatases with restricted tissue expression, the Tau index and coefficient of variation (CV) were computed for each gene across the FANTOM, GTEx, and HPA datasets. Genes with both high Tau and high CV were considered strong candidates for tissue-enriched phosphatases (Supplementary Table S6). Figure 6A presents a histogram showing the distribution of Tau values across phosphatases. Most genes fell within intermediate ranges (0.4–0.7), suggesting broad or moderately enriched expression. Only a small fraction reached Tau values > 0.9, highlighting a limited subset with sharp tissue-specific profiles. While median Tau values were broadly similar, GTEx and FANTOM showed slightly lower specificity overall than HPA (Fig. 6B). This pattern reflects both biological variance and differences in sampling depth, tissue resolution, and platform sensitivity. Despite technical differences, strong cross-dataset concordance was observed, particularly among top-ranked genes (Fig. 6C). Notably, five genes consistently ranked among the top 10 across all three platforms: G6PC1, DUSP29, DUSP21, ACP3, and TPTE. This cross-validation reinforces their robustness as tissue-specific candidates.

Next, integrate data from all three platforms were performed in order to identify phosphatases that are both highly specific and highly variable in expression across datasets (Fig. 6D). For each gene, average Tau and CV were computed across datasets. The final selection included only genes with average Tau > 0.9 and average CV > 1.0, ensuring that findings were consistent and biologically meaningful. Among these, five phosphatases stood out for their extreme specificity and variability. DUSP21 (average Tau = 0.999, average CV = 6.416) and TPTE (average Tau = 0.998, average CV = 5.999) were almost exclusively expressed in testis. DUSP29 (average Tau = 0.997, average CV = 5.839) was enriched in skeletal muscle, while ACP3 (average Tau = 0.996, average CV = 5.490) was highly expressed in prostate. G6PC1 (average Tau = 0.994, average CV = 5.476) showed a sharp expression peak in liver, with very low levels elsewhere.

Cancer-specific expression profiles of candidate genes

To evaluate whether phosphatases with strong tissue-specific expression maintain their selectivity in cancer, gene expression data was analysed from TCGA) for the five top-ranked candidates: ACP3, G6PC1, TPTE, DUSP21, and FBP1 (Fig. 7). The dataset included 7,932 tumour samples spanning 21 cancer types, with expression values normalized as log2(FPKM + 1) to ensure comparability and accommodate zero values. G6PC1 displayed a highly specific expression peak in liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC), where it reached a maximum value of 11.26 log2(FPKM + 1). In all other cancers, median expression values remained close to zero, with an overall mean of 0.41 and a median of just 0.006, confirming G6PC1 tight restriction to liver-derived tumour. This sharp contrast reinforces its value as a liver cancer-specific marker. ACP3, well-known for its role in prostate biology, showed the highest median expression in prostate adenocarcinoma (PRAD). It achieved a maximum expression of 12.66 and a mean of 1.56. The median for ACP3 was 0.92, supporting its use as a diagnostic biomarker for prostate cancer. TPTE and DUSP21 both exhibited low expression across most tumours, their expression was almost entirely restricted to testicular germ cell tumours (TGCT). FBP1, a liver-associated gluconeogenic enzyme, showed a broader expression range than G6PC1, but was still most highly expressed in LIHC. Its mean and median values were 4.43 and 4.65, respectively, and the maximum reached 9.99, placing it among the highest-expressing phosphatases in liver cancer.

Specificity concordance and IHC validation

To assess how well tissue-specific phosphatase expression is maintained in cancer, gene-level cancer specificity was analysed using TCGA RNA-seq dataset. Tau index, CV, and fold change were calculated for each phosphatase gene. Among the most cancer-specific genes were ACP3 (PRAD), G6PC1 (LIHC), DUSP15 (KICH), TPTE (TGCT), and PPP1R3A (HNSC) (Fig. 8A). These genes exhibited Tau values > 0.97 and CV values > 5.0, highlighting both focused and dynamic expression. G6PC1, for example, showed Tau = 0.9982 and was almost exclusively expressed in liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC). ACP3 showed similar specificity for prostate adenocarcinoma (PRAD), while TPTE was highly specific to testicular germ cell tumours (TGCT).

Next, the cancer Tau scores with average normal tissue Tau values were compared (Fig. 8B), mapping cancer types to their tissue of origin using a curated dictionary. This allowed to identify genes with matching specificity in both cancer and normal conditions. The analysis revealed a moderate positive correlation (Pearson r = 0.4234) between normal and cancer Tau values. Only 22.73% of genes maintained consistent specificity between cancer and their corresponding normal tissue. Notable matches included G6PC1 (LIHC vs. liver), ACP3 (PRAD vs. prostate), TPTE (TGCT vs. testis), DUSP21 (TGCT vs. testis), and FBP1 (LIHC vs. liver). These genes retained Tau values > 0.78 in both settings and represent high-confidence candidates for diagnostic and tissue of origin applications. In contrast, 77.27% of genes shifted their specificity between cancer and normal conditions. For example, DUSP15 was highly expressed in KICH (kidney chromophobe) but showed testis specificity in normal tissues. PPP1R3A, normally specific to skeletal muscle, was enriched in head and neck cancer (HNSC).

To assess whether transcriptomic tissue specificity is preserved at the protein level, the expression patterns of the five candidate genes (G6PC1, ACP3, TPTE, DUSP21, and FBP1) were analysed using immunohistochemistry (IHC) data compiled from the HPA (Fig. 8C). DUSP21 was entirely absent from the dataset, and TPTE was annotated as “not detected” in all samples. These observations likely reflect either low protein abundance or technical limitations related to antibody availability or sensitivity, and thus no definitive conclusion can be drawn about their protein-level expression in cancer tissues. In contrast, G6PC1, ACP3, and FBP1 were successfully detected in multiple cancers, with distinct tissue-specific patterns. G6PC1 showed measurable expression in 19 cancer types, with a mean H-score of 8.5. Its highest expression was in urothelial cancer (H-score = 22), followed by lung cancer (H-score = 19) and liver cancer (H-score = 17), supporting its detection in multiple endoderm-derived tumours, but confirming preferential expression in liver-associated cancers. While G6PC1 was detectable at the protein level, its elevated staining in non-liver cancers requires cautious interpretation, as it may reflect biological overlap or antibody cross-reactivity. Further validation is needed to confirm its tissue specificity and diagnostic relevance. ACP3 was the most restricted among the three, detected in only four cancer types, with a mean H-score of 1.85. Its expression was sharply concentrated in prostate cancer (H-score = 33), with minimal levels in pancreatic (H-score = 2) and skin cancers (H-score = 1), supporting its known use as a prostate-selective diagnostic marker. FBP1 showed intermediate breadth, being expressed in 17 cancer types with a mean H-score of 6.8. It was most abundant in urothelial cancer (H-score = 23), followed by prostate and liver cancer (H-score = 19 each), suggesting a broader expression profile than G6PC1, but still showing enrichment in liver-derived malignancies. These IHC results partially validate the transcriptomic specificity patterns observed earlier. While G6PC1, ACP3, and FBP1 retained clinically detectable protein expression, their top-scoring tissues varied slightly from transcript-level rankings. Nevertheless, G6PC1 and FBP1 both demonstrated clear enrichment in liver cancer, and ACP3 was confirmed as highly selective for prostate cancer, reinforcing their potential roles as protein-level biomarkers for tumour origin classification.

Network analysis confirms G6PC1 as a central regulator of metabolic pathways

To explore the broader biological context of the top-ranked phosphatase (G6PC1), gene ontology and PPI network were constructed (Supplementary Table S7). As shown in Fig. 9, the network is dominated by proteins involved in glucose and energy metabolism, including glycolysis, gluconeogenesis, and carbohydrate regulation. At the centre of this network is GPI (glucose-6-phosphate isomerase), followed closely by G6PC1, which had the second-highest centrality. HK2, HK1, and GCK also emerged as major hubs, underscoring the network’s focus on glucose homeostasis. G6PC1 was functionally distinguished by its central position in the network, with a degree centrality score of 0.444, indicating its extensive interactions with both upstream and downstream metabolic enzymes. Notably, G6PC1 shared direct connections with key regulators such as GPI, HK1, GCK, FBP1, and G6PD, forming a tightly interconnected core of enzymes central to gluconeogenesis, glycogenolysis, and glucose utilization. FBP1, another liver-specific phosphatase, also ranked as a network hub, but with a slightly lower degree centrality. These results firmly establish G6PC1 as a central enzymatic regulator in hepatic glucose metabolism with extensive interactions.

Clinical and genomic landscape of G6PC1 expression in liver cancer

To assess the clinical robustness of G6PC1 as a biomarker in liver cancer, its expression and associations with clinical and genomic features were evaluated using data from the PCAWG project. Out of the 2,922 tumour samples in the PCAWG pan-cancer dataset, 193 tumours (7%) were found to be altered in G6PC1, primarily through mRNA overexpression and focal amplification with occasional deep deletions and coding mutations (missense and truncating) also observed. These 193 altered cases formed the basis of our focused clinical analysis. (Fig. 10). Among them, the majority were classified as Liver hepatocellular carcinoma (n = 83), alongside additional tumours such as pancreatic endocrine tumours (n = 24) and medulloblastomas (n = 18). The analysis focused on evaluating G6PC1 expression relative to tumour stage, mutation burden, genomic instability, tumour purity, age, and sex. Feature selection focused on variables relevant to biomarker stratification: tumour mutational burden (TMB), ploidy, purity, age at diagnosis, sex, and AJCC Tumour Stage. G6PC1 expression was detected across all AJCC tumour stages, including composite subtypes such as T3N1M0, T4N1M1, and Stage III. Regarding sex, the dataset had a male-to-female ratio of 2:1, which reflects the typical epidemiology of hepatocellular carcinoma. G6PC1 was equally expressed in both sexes, confirming its non-sex-biased expression profile. TMB varied markedly, mean 6.54 mut/Mb, up to a maximum of 369 in highly clonal outliers reflected tumour TMB. G6PC1 expression was of similar magnitude across all TMBs, highlighting strength for G6PC1 as a liver-specific biomarker. Ploidy went from 1.28 to 4.59 with a mean of 2.54 confirming the existence of diploid-aneuploid tumours as well. G6PC1 expression was constant along this spectrum and therefore a suitable biomarker for genomically stable and unstable liver cancers. Tumour purity (17–97%) with a mean of 64% suggesting that even in the less pure samples, G6PC1 was expressed at a relatively high signal to detect. Diagnostic ages ranging 3–87 (mean 57.7) with majority diagnosed between age 50–71 years. There was no age-dependency in G6PC1 expression, suggesting the broad use of this marker both in adult and paediatric liver tumours.

Discussion

This study presents a systematic analysis of the human phosphatase landscape across normal and cancer tissues using transcriptomic datasets, identifying key tissue-specific regulators with biomarker potential. By integrating expression data from GTEx, FANTOM, and the HPA, high cross-platform concordance were achieved supporting the reliability of multi-source expression analysis [14–16]. Tissue-level analysis consistently ranked skeletal muscle, heart, liver, and select brain regions as top sites of phosphatase expression. These tissues, known for their metabolic intensity and signal transduction demands, exhibited tightly regulated phosphatase profiles [17–19]. Hierarchical clustering distinguished broadly expressed families from tissue-specific ones, emphasizing the functional diversity within the phosphatase superfamily.

A PPI network analysis revealed centrality of key phosphatases PPP1CA, PPP1CB, PTEN, and PPP2CA, positioning them as core regulators of cell cycle, immune modulation, and cytoskeletal dynamics [20–22]. Tissue-level expression of these hubs showed context-dependent deployment: e.g., PTEN was enriched in immune and epithelial tissues, whereas PPP1CB was highest in skeletal muscle, aligning with their known functional domains.

Tissue specificity metrics (Tau and CV) revealed a subset of phosphatases with tightly restricted expression patterns: G6PC1 (liver), ACP3 (prostate), TPTE and DUSP21 (testis). These findings were validated using TCGA, where each gene maintained sharp cancer-type specificity. G6PC1 and ACP3 were highly specific to LIHC and PRAD, respectively, while TPTE and DUSP21 were confined to TGCT. FBP1, another liver-enriched phosphatase, was also elevated in LIHC, suggesting shared regulation in metabolic subtypes [23].

Gene-gene correlation analysis across normal tissue platforms revealed conserved, tissue-specific transcriptional modules. The strongest correlation was observed between DUSP21 and TPTE, reinforcing their co-regulation in testis. In GTEx, additional testis-related genes such as CDKN3 and PHOSPHO1 clustered with this pair, while in HPA and FANTOM, a distinct muscle-associated cluster emerged: PGAM2, PPP1R3A, PLPP7, and PPP1R3C, all functionally tied to glycolysis, phospholipid metabolism, or energy storage. These conserved co-expression patterns validate tissue-specificity rankings and support the existence of tightly regulated phosphatase subnetworks underlying organ-specific physiology [24–26].

Comparison of Tau scores between cancer and normal tissue revealed that approximately 23% of phosphatases retained their specificity in both contexts. High confidence genes such as G6PC1, ACP3, TPTE, and FBP1 exhibited strong concordance and were validated at the protein level using immunohistochemistry (IHC) data from the HPA [6]. G6PC1 and FBP1 were detected in liver, lung, and urothelial tumours, while ACP3 remained prostate specific. PPI analysis placed G6PC1 at the centre of a glucose-regulatory network involving GPI, HK1, GCK, and FBP1, highlighting its metabolic integration.

Functionally, G6PC1 (glucose-6-phosphatase catalytic subunit 1) catalyses the hydrolysis of glucose-6-phosphate to free glucose in the endoplasmic reticulum, representing the final step of gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis [27]. It has been shown that G6PC1 gene mutations can cause Glycogen Storage Disease Type I, which give consideration to its systemic metabolic importance [28, 29]. Overexpression of this gene in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) might be indicative metabolic compensation for glucose export, NADPH generation and resistance against oxidative stress in nutrient deprived/hypoxic tumour microenvironment. In addition, as an ER-anchored enzyme, G6PC1 may intersect with unfolded protein response pathways such as IRE1 (Inositol-requiring enzyme 1), PERK (PKR-like ER kinase) or ATF6 (Activating Transcription Factor 6), and lipid biosynthesis, both of which are reprogrammed during hepatic tumorigenesis [30, 31]. Although not directly linked to metastasis, G6PC1 might shape tumour interaction with microenvironment through changes in extracellular glucose availability, immune cell infiltrate or stromal remodelling [32–34].

While the study presents strong multi-omic evidence, it is limited by the lack of experimental validation, potential technical inconsistencies across platforms, absence of matched tumour–normal samples, and possible antibody-related issues in IHC data. These factors highlight the need for further functional and clinical validation of G6PC1 as a biomarker.

In conclusion, this study combines comprehensive atlas-based analysis of human protein phosphatases with transcriptomic, network and clinical data analysis in order to identify genes with robust tissue and cancer specificity. Among these, G6PC1 stood out as a particular phosphatase with extremely high liver tissue specificity in normal and cancer, highly detectable at both RNA and protein expression level, and clinical stability across demographic, molecular, and pathological variables. ACP3 and FBP1 have also been demonstrated strong normal tissue and cancer specificity concordance, strengthening the broader value of phosphatase-based biomarkers. Together, these findings highlight the untapped potential of tissue-restricted phosphatases as promising candidates for biomarker discovery and biological investigation in precision oncology, pending further validation.

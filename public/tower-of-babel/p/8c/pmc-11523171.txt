Effects of Posterior Tibial Slope on Posterior Cruciate Ligament Injury: A Systematic Review
Authors: Johanes Jethro Nugroho, Roy Edward Konstantino, Dewa Ayu Anggi Paramitha
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Introduction and Objective:Posterior cruciate ligament (PCL) injuries occur in an estimated 3% to 37% of all knee injuries. The PCL is the largest and strongest ligament of the knee joint and the primary restraint to posterior tibial translation (PTT) at all flexion angles. Posterior tibial slope (PTS) is an important radiographic measurement defined as the angle between a line perpendicular to the mid-diaphysis of tibia and the posterior inclination of the tibial plateau. Increase of PTS was reported to alter the knee joint kinematics by anteriorly shifting the resting position of tibia and increasing in situ forces on the ACL and counteracts the PTT and reduces force on PCL. In contrast, a decrease of PTS is correlated to significantly higher residual PTT and lower reduction of PTT and increased force on PCL. The literature regarding the effects of decreased PTS on PCL injury is still limited. This study aims to evaluate effects of posterior tibial slope on primary cruciate ligament injury.Material and Method:A comprehensive literature search was conducted in 3 databases, including PubMed, ScienceDirect, and Cochrane Library. Last search was conducted on 17th July 2023. We included prospective and retrospective studies. We presented our results according to PRISMA guidelines.Results:A total of 400 participants from 3 different studies were included in this review. All three studies were retrospective case-control. One study concluded a decrease of PTS was associated with patients with PCL tears. Other studies evaluated further by dividing PTS into lateral PTS and medial PTS. One study found decreased lateral PTS were underlying anatomic risk factors for PCL injury, the other concluded a decreased medial PTS and an increased lateral PTS/medial PTS are associated with increased risk of primary PCL injury.Conclusion:Decrease of PTS is associated with higher risk of PCL injury. Further studies should elaborate and study the effects of PTS including medial PTS and lateral PTS in posterior knee stability.

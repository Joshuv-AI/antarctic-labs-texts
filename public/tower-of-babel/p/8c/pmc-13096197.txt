Design optimization and performance evaluation of semi-prefabricated UHPC-RC metro station structures
Authors: Gang Lei, Fucai Hua, Zhihui Yang, Feng Jiang, Jianye Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAiming to address the limitations associated with conventional cut-and-cover metro stations that utilize cast-in-situ reinforced concrete structures, this study proposes an optimized design method for a semi-prefabricated UHPC-RC metro station structure, which integrates ultra-high performance reinforced concrete (UHPC) as permanent formwork with conventional cast-in-situ reinforced concrete (RC). The optimization scheme and construction method of the semi-prefabricated UHPC-RC metro station are elaborated. Finite element analysis (FEA) was employed to evaluate the stress, damage, and deformation of the longitudinal beams, floor slabs, and sidewalls of the prefabricated UHPC-RC structure under various loading conditions. Furthermore, bending and shear capacity, as well as crack resistance, were verified for the longitudinal beams, slabs, and side walls. The results demonstrate that, compared to conventional cast-in-situ RC metro stations, the proposed semi-prefabricated UHPC-RC metro station features reduced structural dimensions, the thickness of the roof and the middle plate is reduced by 11.8% and 12.5% respectively, the overall project scale is reduced by 1.8%, and the space utilization rate is increased by 0.8%. In the finite element calculation of the design load in the construction stage and operational stage, the UHPC-RC shell structure exhibits minimal plastic damage, and the maximum settlement is 4.62 mm. The main structural components meet the requirements of the specification, and the structural safety reserve is about 1.2 ~ 1.8 times that of the conventional cast-in-situ RC station structure. The proposed semi-prefabricated UHPC-RC metro station offers an innovative and viable solution for metro station construction.Keywords: Metro engineering, UHPC, Metro station, Structural optimization, Composite structure, Semi-prefabricated structures, Finite element analysisSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science

Introduction

With the rapid development of urban rail transit, metro stations and tunnel structures are becoming increasingly larger to accommodate higher passenger flow density and more complex functional requirements. However, the expansion of project scale not only increases structural dimensions and construction complexity but also imposes certain constraints on the effective utilization of space within the metro station. Chen and Hu1 and Wei2. Large-scale structures occupy a significant amount of space, reducing the available building area originally designated for equipment, commercial use, and passenger circulation, thereby lowering the overall utilization efficiency of metro space. Additionally, the increase in structural dimensions often necessitates thicker enclosure structures and deeper excavation, further limiting the station’s flexibility and functional layout. This issue is particularly pronounced in city central areas where underground space is scarce. Excessive structural dimensions make station expansion challenging and hinder the ability to meet the evolving demands of urban space development. Therefore, optimizing structural design and reducing structural dimensions have become key strategies for enhancing space utilization efficiency and improving the economic performance of metro projects. Ultra-High Performance Concrete (UHPC), characterized by its exceptional strength, high toughness, and superior durability3–8, has gained significant attention in research aimed at improving structural performance and optimizing structural dimensions. The design principles of UHPC are based on the Densified Particle Packing Theory, which maximizes particle packing density by optimizing particle gradation and minimizing voids between particles. On this basis, fibers are introduced to inhibit the propagation of microcracks through bridging effects. By precisely regulating the particle-fiber-matrix three-phase system, UHPC achieves a simultaneous enhancement of strength, durability, and toughness 8–10. With the continued in-depth research on UHPC by experts and scholars worldwide, this material has gradually been applied and developed in various structural applications, such as bridge engineering and foundation pit projects, across many countries. References11–15. Despite the excellent performance of UHPC, its high cost, primarily due to the large amount of fiber required, limits its application in metro projects mainly to smaller-scale components such as shield tunnel segments16,17.

In addition, the conventional method of installing formwork and casting concrete on-site still presents several challenges during construction, such as the extensive use of temporary facilities like formwork, prolonged construction time, high labor dependency, and severe environmental pollution. These factors hinder the rapid progress of metro projects and contribute to significant on-site issues, including dust, noise, and wastewater discharge18,19. To address these issues, many scholars and engineers have conducted research on efficient dust removal equipment, vibration reduction devices, and wastewater treatment solutions during the construction period. However, these methods have not fully resolved the problems of noise and environmental pollution. Moreover, due to the prolonged construction duration, workers are often exposed to such hazardous environments for extended periods. As a result, the application of prefabricated structures in metro stations has garnered widespread attention20–24. Prefabricated construction can enhance construction efficiency, reduce on-site wet operations, improve construction quality, and facilitate the industrialization of metro construction. Currently, research and application of prefabricated metro stations in China primarily focus on fully prefabricated and assembled structures25–28, where the main structural components of metro stations — including beams, slabs, columns, and walls — are all factory-prefabricated. During construction, these components are assembled using large lifting equipment, with connections achieved through high-performance connection materials or mechanical fasteners such as bolts. Finally, local cast-in-situ concrete is used to fill the joints, enhancing the overall structural performance. However, this fully prefabricated assembly method has also revealed certain challenges in practice. First, metro station components are typically large and heavy, requiring extremely high construction precision during on-site lifting and assembly. Without such precision, cumulative assembly errors may occur, compromising the overall structural integrity. Second, metro construction sites are often narrow, and the underground environment is complex. The transportation and lifting of large prefabricated components pose significant challenges, especially under conditions involving deep foundation pits and confined workspaces, making construction organization more difficult29. Currently, research and application of semi-prefabricated structures in the metro industry remain limited, with most efforts focusing on experimental studies of localized structural components22,23,30.

Given the exceptional performance but high cost of UHPC, along with the feasibility of semi-prefabricated structures, introducing UHPC prefabricated components into metro station construction and combining them with cast-in-situ reinforced concrete (RC) to form a UHPC-RC composite structure is expected to fully leverage the high strength and durability advantages of UHPC, while optimizing the construction process and enabling rapid and efficient construction. Numerous scholars have conducted research on UHPC-RC composite structures, with a primary focus on the performance of the bonding interface between UHPC and RC9,15,31–33, as well as the load-bearing capacity of composite beam, slab, and column structures where UHPC serves as the formwork and RC acts as the filling material34–44.

Based on this approach, this study proposes an optimized design scheme for a semi-prefabricated UHPC-RC metro station structure, wherein ultra-high-performance concrete is used as permanent formwork combined with reinforced concrete. In this scheme, UHPC permanent formwork is prefabricated in a factory, lifted, and installed on-site, followed by rebar placement and the pouring of normal-strength concrete. Compared to conventional cast-in-situ RC metro stations, this construction method reduces structural dimensions, increases usable building space, reduces structural self-weight, minimizes the need for formwork installation and removal, and enhances structural durability. Compared with traditional RC, UHPC has significantly enhanced impermeability to external media such as chloride ingress. By optimizing particle accumulation and steel fiber incorporation, the dense microstructure of UHPC creates a highly durable matrix and provides excellent protection for embedded steel bars45. These advantages contribute to achieving the goal of metro station structures that are aesthetically pleasing, impermeable, and leak-proof.

This study employs solid element numerical simulations to investigate the stress, damage, and deformation behavior of various components of the prefabricated UHPC-RC metro station during both construction and operational stages. Internal forces during these stages were analyzed based on code requirements and beam element structural models. Additionally, sectional bending capacity and crack resistance were verified, followed by a detailed comparative analysis with conventional cast-in-situ RC metro station schemes.

Design scheme of the semi-prefabricated UHPC-RC metro station

This study proposes an optimized design for a semi-prefabricated UHPC-RC metro station structure. The structural form of the semi-prefabricated UHPC-RC metro station remains a cut-and-cover, rectangular, two-level underground single-column double-span beam-slab structural system. However, compared to the traditional scheme, the roof slab, roof longitudinal beams, intermediate slab, intermediate longitudinal beams, and sidewalls have been optimized. Specifically:

Roof and intermediate longitudinal beams: These are composite structures formed by combining prefabricated UHPC components with cast-in-place concrete, with the UHPC sections featuring hollowed-out cores. The longitudinal beams are prestressed UHPC beams.

Roof slab, intermediate slab, and sidewalls: These components use prefabricated UHPC permanent formwork integrated with cast-in-place concrete.

To ensure the performance of these composite components, truss reinforcement is used as a structural measure to enhance overall structural integrity. Finite element analysis

Based on the actual dimensions of the metro station, models of the UHPC-RC composite slabs, shear walls, and longitudinal beams were established. The Concrete Damaged Plasticity (CDP) model embedded in the large-scale finite element software ABAQUS was used to simulate the concrete structure46. There were five crucial parameters in CDP that had a decisive impact on the damage of the model, namely dilation angle (36° for UHPC and 40° for RC), eccentricity (0.1), the ratio of biaxial strength to uniaxial strength (1.16), the ratio of the second strength variant of the tensile meridian to that of the compression meridian (0.667), and the viscosity parameter (0.004)47. The UHPC plastic damage constitutive relationship was defined according to the tension-compression constitutive model proposed by Du48,49.

where, σc is the stress under compression, fc is the axial compressive strength, σt is the stress under tension and ft is the axial tensile strength. Define x = ε/ε0, ε0 is the peak strain of UHPC.

Due to its continuity and volume properties, concrete and UHPC are suitable for simulation using C3D8R elements to accurately capture the stress and strain distribution in three-dimensional space. Steel bars and steel strands are simulated using T3D2 elements to simulate their tensile properties. In order to better present the damage change, and improve the convergence, calculation accuracy and calculation speed of the finite element model, through the sensitivity analysis of the mesh size, the mesh size of the model is finally determined to be 1000 mm, in which the longitudinal beams are the most concerned parts in the structure, and the mesh is encrypted to 200 mm. The prefabricated UHPC and the cast-in-situ concrete are tightly bonded through measures such as truss reinforcement and chiseling. Therefore, the UHPC-RC interface is simulated using “Tie” to ensure no relative slip. The assembly of steel bars, steel strands and concrete is achieved through the interaction of “Embedded Element”, which is completely embedded in the UHPC and concrete to avoid relative sliding during the loading process.

The boundary conditions and loading methods of the finite element model must accurately reflect actual conditions. A static loading analysis was conducted based on the loads described in “Project background”, with the constraints exerted by the surrounding soil simplified as soil springs serving as the model’s boundary conditions. Conclusion

This study proposes an optimized structural design and construction scheme for a semi-prefabricated UHPC-RC metro station. Under comparable costs, the proposed method effectively reduces the dimensions of metro station structure components, enhances usable building space, shortens the construction period, optimizes the working environment, and improves overall concrete quality. Using a specific metro station as an engineering case, this study employs a three-dimensional finite element model to investigate the stress and damage conditions of the UHPC-RC metro station during both the construction and operation stages, as well as the deformation behavior of the composite slabs and beams. Additionally, a comparative analysis evaluates the flexure capacity, shear capacity, safety factors, and crack widths of the UHPC-RC metro station structure in contrast to a traditional metro station structure. The key findings are summarized as follows:

The proposed UHPC-RC metro station structural design scheme reduces the thickness of the roof slab by 11.8% and the thickness of the middle slab by 12.5%. The heights of the roof longitudinal beams and intermediate longitudinal beams are significantly reduced, leading to an overall project scale reduction of 1.8% and an improvement in space utilization by 0.8%.

This indicates that the UHPC-RC station demonstrates excellent performance and meets structural serviceability requirements. This study provides a new perspective on the application of UHPC-RC structures in metro station engineering and proposes a semi-prefabricated metro station structural design and construction scheme, which can effectively promote the high-quality development of urban rail transit construction. However, this research is based on a specific engineering case, employing finite element methods and code-based calculations. Future work will involve model and field experiments to further validate the performance of semi-prefabricated UHPC-RC metro station structures, systematically develop an optimized design scheme, and advance research on UHPC-RC metro station structures and semi-prefabricated metro stations.

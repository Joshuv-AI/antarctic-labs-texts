Preoperative frailty and postoperative neurocognitive disorders in older adults in Georgia: study protocol for a prospective, observational cohort study
Authors: Tamta Darsalia, Lisbeth Evered, Tamar Macharadze
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionIn high-income countries, the prevalence of frailty ranges from 10% to 37%. However, data from low- and middle-income countries remain largely unavailable, and no information is currently available on the prevalence of frailty or cognitive impairment in the Georgian population. Preoperative frailty is a known risk factor for postoperative delirium and neurocognitive disorders, which can be life-threatening in the population of older adults.Methods and analysisThis protocol describes a prospective, single-centre observational cohort study investigating preoperative frailty and postoperative neurocognitive disorders in older adults presenting for surgery in Georgia. The study will include 230 participants. Patients will be enroled after their cognitive health is evaluated using the Montreal Cognitive Assessment test. Frailty and neuropsychological tests are conducted at baseline, discharge, 30 days, 3 months and 12 months postoperatively. Additionally, IL-6 levels are measured in blood samples to assess systemic inflammatory levels preoperatively. Participants will be followed up for 1 year.Ethics and disseminationOur trial has been approved by the Ethics Committee of the LTD ‘New Hospitals’ clinic (Approval No. 23/01). Patient recruitment began in April 2023 in Tbilisi, Georgia, and will continue until 2026. Dissemination plans include presentations at scientific conferences, publication in peer-reviewed journals, engagement with stakeholders and outreach through popular media.Trial registration numberNCT06700291.Keywords: Adult anaesthesia, Frail Elderly, Delirium & cognitive disorders, Depression & mood disorders

Introduction

According to the WHO, an estimated 524 million people globally were aged 65 years or older in 2010. By 2050, this number is expected to rise to approximately 1.5 billion.1 Based on data from the National Statistics Office of Georgia, 16.6% of the current population in Georgia is aged 65 years or older. Additionally, the average life expectancy in Georgia continues to increase annually.2 As a result, there is a significant increase in the demand for geriatric care.

In developed countries, an estimated one-third (33%) of all anaesthesia is administered to individuals aged 65 years or older, a figure anticipated to rise to 50% in the coming years.3 Although local data are scarce, the increasing life expectancy in Georgia underscores the relevance of poor outcomes associated with anaesthesia and surgery in older individuals. Perioperative neurocognitive disorders (PNDs) are the most common complications following anaesthesia and surgery for individuals aged 65 years or more, and as such, understanding these adverse outcomes within our population is critical to implementing effective prevention and mitigation strategies.

Co-morbidities associated with ageing, particularly frailty and cognitive impairment, increase the risk of these complications. Such that, while advancements in medical technology have enabled complex surgical procedures to be performed on increasingly older adults, patients are at significantly increased risk of poor outcomes such as PND, dementia, morbidity and mortality. PND comprises postoperative delirium (POD) and postoperative neurocognitive disorders (postoperative NCDs)4 which affect up to 50% of older patients and are associated with increased morbidity, mortality, loss of independence and dementia.5,7 These are particularly concerning outcomes, especially since elective surgeries are often intended to alleviate pain and improve quality of life.

The mortality rate 30 days following anaesthesia and surgery in the older adult population in America is 7% according to the American College of Surgeons National Surgeons Quality Improvement Program Database,8 but in frail older patients 1 year mortality has been reported at up to 28%, and up to 33% in those presenting with dementia.9 As there is currently no data on the prevalence of PND in Georgia, it is important to determine this in an older adult surgical population, including frail and cognitively impaired patients.

Preoperative frailty is a well-established risk factor for POD and postoperative NCD.10 In high-income countries, the prevalence of frailty ranges from 10.4% to 37%,11 12 while data from low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) remain largely unavailable.13 Frailty is characterised by a diminished capacity to cope with stressors, including anaesthesia and surgery, leading to increased susceptibility to adverse outcomes, including falls, delirium, postoperative NCD and disability. The incidence of frailty is known to vary by sex and economic status of the country.14

The most widely held hypothesis regarding the pathophysiology of PND currently relates to peripheral inflammation, leading to neuroinflammation, which is a well-established trigger of delirium.15 The exact mechanisms by which inflammation disrupts brain function are not known. Severe systemic inflammation, indicated by elevated levels of circulating cytokines,16 is linked to a higher risk of delirium in adults undergoing major surgery or experiencing critical illness.17 Other contributing factors may include hyperglycaemia and hypercholesterolaemia. Diabetes mellitus, prediabetes and elevated glucose or glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) levels have been consistently associated with age-related cognitive decline18 and there are conflicting results in the literature regarding the association between baseline triglyceride elevation and postoperative NCD.19 20

Mood disorders such as depression are well known to be associated with cognitive impairment21 22 and depression is known to be associated with preoperative frailty and postoperative complications. It is important, therefore, to consider the impact of preoperative factors, such as depression and postoperative cognitive outcomes.

These important issues have not yet been studied or documented within the population of older adults in Georgia. To address this gap, we designed a prospective, observational cohort study of preoperative frailty and postoperative neurocognitive disorders in older adults in Georgia (FRAILCOG-GE). Our primary aim is to determine the prevalence of preoperative frailty and its correlation with postoperative neurocognitive disorders up to 1 year in a Georgian surgical population. Secondary outcomes include assessing preoperative neurocognitive impairment and its correlation with postoperative neurocognitive disorders; identifying preoperative risk factors for postoperative neurocognitive disorders; determining the preoperative prevalence of depression and postoperative changes in depression and evaluating disability, quality of life and mortality up to 1 year postoperatively in this population.

Materials and methods

Study setting

This study is being conducted at the ‘New Hospitals’ clinic in Tbilisi, Georgia. The ‘New Hospitals’ is a multiprofile clinic with 231 beds, offering in-patient and outpatient services, where each year around 5504 elective operations take place.

Study design

This is a prospective, single-centre observational cohort study, investigating preoperative frailty and cognitive function and postoperative neurocognitive disorders in older adult patients in Tbilisi, Georgia.

Participants

Eligible patients will be identified by a member of the research team during their preoperative assessment, which is scheduled at least 24 hours before the operation. Potential participants will be approached and screened against the eligibility criteria before being provided with a patient information leaflet detailing the trial, including an explanation of its aims, methods and anticipated benefits and a participant information and consent form. Patient selection is conducted by a PhD student who is a certified anaesthesiologist in Georgia and trained in administering neuropsychological tests.

Informed consent

The principal investigator, or a suitably qualified nominee, will obtain written informed consent from each participant. All potential participants are free to refuse participation or withdraw from the trial at any time and for any reason.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

All patients provide written, informed consent to participate in the study. The inclusion criteria are male or female patients aged 65 years or older, scheduled for elective orthopaedic surgery with knee or hip endoprosthesis. Exclusion criteria include:

A diagnosis of dementia (any type).

A diagnosis of dementia (any type).

A history of surgery within the past 6 months.

A history of surgery within the past 6 months.

An inability to undergo neuropsychological assessment (eg, due to poor vision, aphasia or hearing loss).

An inability to undergo neuropsychological assessment (eg, due to poor vision, aphasia or hearing loss).

Major psychiatric disorder (eg, schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, severe depression).

Major psychiatric disorder (eg, schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, severe depression).

Study outcomes

The primary aim

To determine the prevalence of preoperative frailty and its association with postoperative neurocognitive disorders (including POD and postoperative neurocognitive disorder) up to 1 year after surgery in a Georgian surgical population.

Secondary aim 1

Correlation between preoperative frailty and postoperative disorders (POD, delayed neurocognitive recovery (dNCR) and postoperative neurocognitive disorder) up to 1 year after surgery.

Secondary aim 2

Preoperative demographics and risk factors, including but not limited to age, level of education and comorbidities, and their correlation with any perioperative neurocognitive disorders up to 12 months following surgery

Secondary aim 3

Prevalence of preoperative cognitive impairment.

Secondary aim 4

Correlation between preoperative cognitive impairment and postoperative disorders (POD, dNCR and postoperative neurocognitive disorder) up to 1 year after surgery.

Secondary aim 5

Determine the prevalence of depression in older adults presenting for anaesthesia and surgery.

Secondary aim 6

Preoperative depression in older adults in Georgia presenting for anaesthesia and surgery correlation with postoperative depression during different time points.

Secondary aim 7

To determine the mortality rate up to 1 year after anaesthesia and surgery.

Secondary aim 8

Patient disability change among older adults in Georgia from the preoperative to postoperative period using WHO Disability Assessment Schedule 2.0 (WHODAS 2.0).

Secondary aim 9

Patient quality of life change among older adults in Georgia from the preoperative to postoperative period using Daily Living prevention instrument questionnaire.

Frailty will be assessed using the FRAIL scale.23 Baseline and postoperative cognitive function will be evaluated using the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), a Modified Telephone Interview for Cognitive Status (TICSm) and the Trail Making Test (TMT) for detection of pre-existing and postoperative cognitive disorders.24,26 Delirium will be assessed using the 3-Minute Diagnostic COnfussion Assessment Method (3D-CAM) on the Ward and the Confusion Assessment Method for the Intensive Care Unit (CAM-ICU) in the intensive care unit (ICU).27 28

Prior to discharge and at 30 days postoperatively, patients will be evaluated for dNCR. Postoperative neurocognitive disorders (postoperative NCD) will be assessed 3 months and 1 year postoperatively via telephone. Depression will be evaluated using the Geriatric Depression Scale (GDS) preoperatively and postoperatively.29 Anxiety and pain will be assessed using the visual and numerical analogue scale.

Disability (WHODAS 2.0) and the ability to perform daily living activities will be assessed at baseline, and at 30 days, 3 months and 1 year postoperatively.

In addition, demographic information will be collected, including but not limited to, medical history, medications, education level, comorbidities, alcohol use, lifestyle factors and body mass index.

Mortality will be recorded through hospital records and follow-up telephone contact with the family member at 1 months, 3 months and 12 months after surgery.

We will also conduct additional laboratory tests to explore potential mechanisms associated with postoperative neurocognitive disorders (PNDs), including HbA1c, triglycerides, vitamin D and interleukin-6 (IL-6).

IL-6 venous blood samples will be collected on the day of surgery from patients in a fasting state into serum gel tubes under aseptic conditions. After collection, samples will be allowed to clot for 30 min at room temperature, followed by centrifugation at 3000g for 10 min to separate the serum. IL-6 concentrations will be determined on the same day using the Elecsys IL-6 assay (Roche Diagnostics, Mannheim, Germany), employing a sandwich electrochemiluminescence immunoassay method. Analyses will be performed on a Cobas e 411 analyser, following the manufacturer’s instructions. We have chosen to assay only IL-6 as it is the most consistently identified cytokine associated with PND and we have limited resources. Future studies should include exploratory cytokine analyses.

Materials

Baseline, intraoperative and postoperative data collection

Demographic and medical information will be collected preoperatively and any changes noted throughout follow-up. Intraoperative variables will be collected as per routine care and will include type and duration of anaesthesia, type of surgery, regional anaesthesia, surgery duration, anaesthesia monitoring, opioids, other analgesics (including postoperatively) and necessary medications, intraoperative and postoperative complications, unanticipated ICU admission, length of hospital stay, discharge destination (return to preoperative location or unable).

Cognitive function

The patient’s preoperative cognitive status will be measured by cognitive screening tests. The MoCA will be used to determine eligibility for the study (no dementia). The MoCA is a cognitive screening tool with the following subscales: short-term memory, visuospatial ability, executive function, attention, concentration, and working memory, language and orientation to time and place. It has a high sensitivity and specificity to detect patients with mild cognitive impairment and dementia.

The TICSm is a 13-item test. Among the telephone-based cognitive screening tools available, it is one of the instruments with the strongest evidence supporting its psychometric properties, including reliability and construct validity in detecting dementia.

Delirium

3-Minute Diagnostic COnfussion Assessment Method (3D-CAM) is a well-validated brief instrument with high sensitivity and specificity, designed to test patients for delirium. It evaluates four key features: (1) Acute onset and fluctuating course. (2) Inattention. (3) Disorganised thinking. (4) Altered level of consciousness. Similarly, in the ICU, patients with a Richmond Agitation-Sedation Scale score30 of ≥−2 will be assessed using the CAM-ICU, a tool likewise characterised by high sensitivity and specificity. This assessment comprises four core domains: (1) Alterations in mental status. (2) Inattention. (3) Disorganised thought processes. (4) Altered level of consciousness.

Self-report measures

Frailty, a predisability state, will be assessed using the 5-item FRAIL scale, which evaluates fatigue, resistance, ambulation, illnesses and unplanned loss of weight in patients.

The TMT is a widely used neuropsychological instrument for assessing cognitive processing speed, visuomotor tracking, divided attention and cognitive flexibility. The test comprises two components, Part A and Part B. In Part A, patients are instructed to sequentially connect numbered circles from 1 to 25 in ascending order. Part B introduces an additional cognitive challenge by incorporating both numbers (1–12) and letters (A–L); patients must draw lines to connect the circles between numbers and letters while maintaining the ascending sequence. The total completion time will be recorded in seconds.

The GDS short-form consists of 15 items and is a well-validated and widely used tool for assessing depression in older adults. Mild cognitive impairment and subjective memory impairment are shown to be associated with incident depression.

The WHODAS 2.0 is a 36 item, generic assessment tool developed by the WHO to offer a standardised approach for measuring health and disability across diverse cultures, which does not target a specific disease.31 It covers six domains: cognition, mobility, self-care, getting along, life activities and participation.

Activities of daily living (ADL) is an index used to measure functional capacity. It can be divided into basic or personal ADL and instrumental (IADL), which includes functions necessary for living in a community.32 Variations in IADL over time may prompt an assessment, aid in diagnosing Mild cognitive impairment (MCI) or dementia, or serve as a complementary measure alongside ADL performance to track outcomes such as the onset of dementia and cognitive changes. Thus, we will assess patients with activities of daily living prevention instrument (ADL-PI).

Pain will be assessed using a well-known 11 point numerical rating scale 0–10, anchored at 0=no pain and 10=worst possible pain.

Anxiety will be assessed using the visual analogue scale. It is a patient-reported self-rating tool and ranges from 0 to 10, where 0 is defined as ‘no feeling of anxiety’ and 10 as ‘most anxious ever’.

The MoCA test used was previously translated and validated from English to Georgian,24 all other tests have been translated and validated from English into Georgian language by us.

Power and sample size

The sample size calculations are based on a conservative estimate of the prevalence of frailty in the community (10%–37%), set at 26%. Using G*Power 3.1.9.7, we calculated that a minimum of 182 patients would be required, with power of 0.95 and maintaining alpha at 0.05. To account for potential loss to follow-up, we plan to recruit 230 patients. As we were undertaking this study in a hitherto unstudied population, we chose to use this number as it was greater than the sample size required when considering the primary outcome of the incidence of PND in those with and without preoperative frailty. Using STATA and assuming a conservative incidence of PND (POD, dNCR and postoperative NCD) of 50% in those with frailty, with a power of 0.95 and maintaining alpha at 0.05, a minimum of 126 patients would be required.

Statistical analysis

Data will be analysed using appropriate statistical software. To identify univariable patient or surgical factors associated with PND, we will use a t-test for continuous data and χ2 or Fisher’s exact tests for categorical data. For non-normally distributed data, we will use non-parametric statistical tests. Multivariable analyses will be conducted using generalised linear models and logistic or linear regression, as appropriate. Repeated measures analyses will be undertaken using generalised estimating equations.

Ethics and Dissemination

The study protocol was approved by the local Ethics Committee of LTD ‘New Hospitals’ clinic, Tbilisi, Georgia (Approval No. 23/01). The study will be conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. It has been registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (Identifier: NCT06700291).

The findings of this study will be disseminated widely through open-access publication in peer-reviewed journals and presentations at national and international conferences. Results will be communicated to relevant stakeholders, including healthcare professionals, policymakers and study participants, where appropriate. The outcomes are expected to be relevant beyond the Georgian context, offering value for other LMICs and global health systems. All coauthors have agreed to contribute to the preparation of scientific outputs. Authorship eligibility will be based on substantial contributions to study design, data analysis, interpretation of results and manuscript drafting or revision, in accordance with ICMJE criteria.

Data management

Data will be saved confidentially and de-identified, accessible to authorised personnel only. Data is collected in REDCap (Research Electronic Data Capture) database.31 All missing and ambiguous data will be checked according to source data. The investigator will permit study-related monitoring, audits and regulatory inspection at their site, providing access to source data/documents. Coded data will be kept after closure of the study and can only be used for ancillary purposes after strict approval of the principal investigator. All documentation will be securely stored for a minimum of 10 years following the completion of the study.

Relevance of findings

Our study will focus on an older adult population, a group for whom specialist physicians are scarce in Georgia. The results of this project aim to highlight the critical importance of geriatric medicine, not only as a medical speciality but also as a key component of interdisciplinary healthcare teams.

On completing the project, we hope to advocate for the development of strategies within the healthcare system in Georgia to ensure that multidisciplinary clinics include geriatric specialists and collaboration with other medical fields. Establishing postoperative geriatric wards is essential for facilitating focused and expedited recovery and rehabilitation. To implement ‘fast-track surgery’, patients must undergo thorough preoperative assessments, with expected intraoperative and postoperative complications identified and managed through individualised care plans. This approach will enable quicker recovery and discharge, benefiting patients while significantly reducing hospitalisation and aged-care costs for the state and insurance providers.

As a result of this research, we have the potential to reduce postoperative complications and help to maintain or improve quality of life in Georgia with findings that are directly translatable to other LMIC. It is critical that we establish a description of the prevalence and incidence of these devastating complications in the Georgian population and identify at-risk patients preoperatively. We will make recommendations to anaesthesiologists, surgeons, geriatricians, neurologists and psychiatrists based on our results. Our goal is to contribute to the global solution of this problem and raise awareness in Georgia.

Our research will improve recovery outcomes for older individuals undergoing anaesthesia and surgery, reducing postoperative disability, enhancing quality of life and expediting reintegration into daily life and society. These benefits are not only ethically justified but also economically advantageous for families, insurance systems and the state, with both short- and long-term implications.

This scientific project will provide valuable insights for fostering social and political progress and for driving reforms in the development of a modern, effective healthcare system.

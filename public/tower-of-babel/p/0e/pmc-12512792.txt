Association between device-measured physical activities, type 2 diabetes, and life expectancy over the next 10 years: a prospective longitudinal study
Authors: Ziqiang Lin, Jiade Chen, Yutai Cai, Zhehao Wang, Jing Xu, Yingying Shi, Jiawei Li, Peng Xu, Jiaxin Shen, Guanren Chen, Qingguang Zhong, Tao Zhang, Qiaoman Mo, Qiaoxuan Lin, Jiale Bu, Pengqing Sun, Lixia Li, Fan Zhang, Yanhui Gao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe mediation effect of 24-hour physical activities on the association between type 2 diabetes and mortality is unclear. Additionally, Little evidence was found on the isotemporal substitution effect of 24-hour physical activities components on changing Life expectancy among patients with type 2 diabetes diagnosed. To address the abovementioned research gap, the study has a two-fold aims: first, to examine the mediation effect of 24-hour physical activities in type 2 diabetes and mortality; and second, to address how reallocating time on different daily activities would affect life expectancy.MethodsAnalysis was conducted on the accelerometer data of 103,359 participants in the UK Biobank, with a median age of 57 years (range 39 to 70). Compositional mediation cox model was conducted to analyze the mediating effects of 24-hour physical activities. Additionally, the cohort Life table method was utilized to estimate the changes of Life-years over the next 10 years resulting from the substitution effect of different physical activities.ResultsDuring a mean follow-up of 13.95 (range 2.95–16.28) years, 2,649 deaths were recorded. Diabetes was significantly associated with increased time spent engaging in sedentary behavior (SB), and reduced time spent on moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) and light-intensive physical activity (LPA), thereby demonstrating an association with higher mortality risk. The indirect effect of physical activity (HR = 1.27, 95% CI 1.23–1.30) accounted for 41.9% of the total effect of diabetes on mortality. Furthermore, the Life expectancy gains with a maximum of 1.32 years over the next 10 years was found when reallocating SB time to MVPA.ConclusionThe results revealed that 24-hour physical activities might mediate the association between diabetes and mortality. Therefore, promoting participation in MVPA and reducing sedentary activities among diabetes patients was expected to have a positive effect on Life expectancy over the next 10 years.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-24662-4.

Introduction

To date, According to the Global Burden of Disease [32], there were 79.2 million disability-adjusted-life-years (DALs) due to diabetes. Type 2 diabetes remains a public health concern that affects 507.84 million people worldwide in 2021, and this number is projected to exceed 1.27 billion globally in 2050 [23]. As a major cause of kidney disease, ischemic heart disease and stroke, lower Limb amputation, etc., type 2 diabetes has resulted in premature disability and death [7]. These findings consistently emphasis the imperative to identify the modifiable factors to prevent or even potentially reverse the development of diabetes.

Substantial evidence has shown that moderate-to-vigorous physical activities (MVPA) was associated with reduced risk of mortality among diabetes patients [12, 19, 35], which has been advocated as a first-line strategy in disease management. In the guidelines released by the World Health Organization in 2024, new recommendations highlighted Limiting sedentary time in addition to promoting MVPA for type 2 diabetes patients. Nevertheless, due to the chronic fatigue and low exercise motivation, type 2 diabetes patients are more likely to engage in prolonged sedentary behavior (SB) [21] and shortened MVPA, suggesting that MVPA and SB may mediate the relationship between type 2 diabetes and mortality. However, existing Literature on the mediation role of PA in type 2 diabetes and mortality usually considered a single PA activity (e.g., duration or frequency of light PA or MVPA) as a mediator, missing the comprehensive assessment of mediating effects of various activities within the 24 h (i.e., light PA, MVPA, SB, and sleep). In fact, the data of time spent on daily activities has a co-dependent nature, and changes in the proportion of any activity would lead to corresponding adjustments in others. It is thereby necessary to consider the ratios of different daily activities within 24-hour. In addition, accumulating evidence showed that replacing sedentary behavior with a short duration (i.e., 30 min) of other physical activities such as light PA was associated with lower body fat [15], reduced genetic risk of type 2 diabetes [14, 17] and related mortality [36]. However, how substituting sedentary time with other physical activities (such as LPA or MVPA) would affect the life expectancy has remained largely unknown.

Using data from UK biobank, this study aims to (1) address the mediation effect of 24-hour physical activities in the association between type 2 diabetes and mortality; (2) assess the substitution effect between 24-hour physical activity on mortality among participants with type 2 diabetes; and (3) evaluate the life expectancy changes with reallocating time on daily activities.

Methods

Data source and ethnic statement

Data in this study were from the UK Biobank, a population-based prospective cohort study comprising more than 500,000 participants from England, Scotland and Wales (protocol available at https://www.ukbiobank.ac.uk/key-documents/). Between 2006 and 2010, the 22 assessment centers located across the UK evaluated the individuals, covering diverse settings to ensure socioeconomic and ethnic diversity, as well as a mix of urban and rural areas. This approach aimed to achieve a comprehensive distribution across all exposures and enabled the identification of generalizable associations between baseline characteristics and health outcomes. The assessment visit involved obtaining electronic signed consent, completion of a self-administered touch-screen questionnaire, a brief computer-assisted interview, physical and functional measurements, and the collection of blood, urine, and saliva samples [2]. Participants with missing covariates were excluded in the main analysis. The UKB study was approved by the Northwest Multi-Center Research Ethics Committee in the UK (https://www.ukbiobank.ac.uk/learn-more-about-uk-biobank/about-us/ethics), and all participants provided written informed consent.

Device-based measurements to assess movement behaviors

From June 2013 to December 2015, individuals were invited to wear an accelerometer if they had a valid email address, except for those in the North West region due to concerns about participant burden. A total 103,683 UK Biobank participants were invited to use the Axivity AX3 wrist-worn triaxial accelerometer. The data was gathered from the dominant wrist of each individual for a duration of 7 days [24]. Ineligible participants were identified based on specific criteria, including: inability to calibrate the device, more than 1% of readings falling outside of the dynamic range of ± 8 g before or after calibration, less than 3 days of recorded data, lack of data for each 1-hour interval of the 24-hour cycle (with non-wear time defined as uninterrupted episodes of at least 60 min in which the standard deviation of each acceleration axis was less than 13.0 mg), or if the average acceleration was unreasonably high (greater than 100 mg) [8]. Further information regarding the collection, validation, and processing of the data can be found in other sources [8, 34].

In our study, we assigned fine-grained labels to various activities, including sedentary behavior (SB), light-intensity physical activity (LPA), moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA), and sleep. The intensity of these activities was described in terms of metabolic equivalent of task (METs), which measures energy expenditure relative to the energy expended during quiet sitting: (1) sleep refers to non-waking behavior; (2) SB refers to waking behavior where the intensity is at or below 1.5 METs while in a sitting, lying, or reclining posture [31]; (3) LPA refers to waking behavior with an intensity below 3 METs that does not meet the definition of sedentary behavior; and (4) MVPA refers to all behavior with an intensity equal to or greater than 3 METs [1].

Assessment of type 2 diabetes

Patients with type 2 diabetes are identified based on multiple data sources. Based on previously developed algorithms [9, 10], patients with type 2 diabetes are identified using following data: (1) Self-reported data: self-reported diabetes, age of diagnosis [3], ethnic background [20], medical history and medication information in the touchscreen questionnaire or the verbal interview; (2) Hospital inpatient records (from Health Episode Statistics (England and Wales) and the Scottish Morbidity Records (Scotland)): ICD-9 code (250.00, 250.10, 250.20, 250.90) and ICD-10 code (E11); (3) Blood biochemistry: random glucose level (≥ 11.1 mmol/L) and glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) level (≥ 48 mmol/mol). The age of type 2 diabetes onset was determined by the interval from the date of the ICD diagnosis record to the baseline and baseline age for patients identified through hospital inpatient records. For patients with type 2 diabetes who were identified through self-reports and insulin treatment, the age at diagnosed/insulin treatment was obtained through questionnaires [29]. Variables used to define patients with type 2 diabetes are presented in Table S1.

Assessment of covariates

Covariates were selected based on established confounders in the association between type 2 diabetes and mortality, as documented in prior studies [4, 16, 30, 33]. Included in our analysis as covariates were gender, age, race/ethnicity (white and non-white), education level (college or university, A/AS levels, and others), employment status (working, retired, and others), body-mass index (BMI) status (underweight, normal weight, and overweight), smoking status (nonsmoker, former smoker, and current smoker), and drinking status (no alcohol consumption, previous alcohol consumption, and current alcohol consumption). These covariates were obtained through survey questions administered at the baseline, with the exception of BMI, which was determined by direct measurement and subsequent calculation.

Mortality

Mortality information was acquired from the NHS Information Centre for participants from England and Wales, and from the NHS Central Register for participants from Scotland [6]. Data concerning survivors were recorded until 10th August 2022 for England, Wales, and Scotland, serving as the censoring dates.

Statistical analysis

The mean (standard deviation) or frequency (percentage) of demographic characteristics was documented, and comparisons between the diabetes group and non-diabetes group were conducted within both the accelerometer population and the entire population. Statistical tests, such as t-tests or chi-squared tests, were employed for the comparisons. Additionally, since time spent on daily activities sums to 24 h, it should be regarded as compositional data, so the method of compositional data analysis is used. The four components of 24-hour physical activity (MVPA, LPA, SB, Sleep) were first subjected to Isometric log-ratio (ILR) transformation to eliminate co-dependent nature, and then the geometric mean and the proportion of any activity were reported.

The mediation effect of 24-hour physical activity on the association between diabetes and mortality was evaluated using compositional mediation cox model, which was proposed by our team [18] to be suitable for survival data in cohort studies. By avoiding the collinearity issue of compositional data in traditional mediation models, this method is developed to include the entire 24-hour activity as a cohesive unit in the mediation analysis between diabetes and mortality.

To assess the effects of sedentary lifestyle and physical activity on health risks, an isotemporal substitution paradigm was developed, which allows for replacing sedentary behavior time with an equal amount of time of other physical activity [22]. In our study, the substitution effect between 24-hour physical activity on mortality among participants with diabetes was assessed using compositional isotemporal substitution cox model, whichis suitable for survival data. For MVPA, LPA, SB, and sleep, the average duration of the four components in diabetes group were used as the reference composition. Furthermore, when predicting expected Lifetime, conventional studies typically rely on the current Life table. However, this approach would overlook the cumulative effect of chronic diseases on the patients, failing to consider that chronic diseases diagnosed at different ages may lead to different life trajectories. Therefore, to accurately estimate life expectancy among patients with chronic diseases, it is critical to utilize the cohort life table method, which calculates baseline hazard ratio for each age group diagnosed with diabetes. In this study, combined with compositional isotemporal substitution cox model, the life expectancy through the reallocation of time spent on daily activities was determined using the cohort life table method instead of current life table to calculate baseline hazard ratio. Considering that the average level of MVPA in the diabetic population has reached 50min, an increase on this basis does not have much practical significance, so we set the reference level to 10, 480, 470, and 480 for MVPA, SB, LPA, and sleep in the isochronous replacement of life expectancy. Details of all methods (Isometric Log-Ratio - ILR, Compositional Mediation Cox Model, Compositional Isotemporal Substitution Cox Model, and Life Expectancy – Cohort Life Table method) are provided in the supplement.

Sensitivity analyses

We conducted a total of six sensitivity analyses to evaluate the stability of our findings. In the first analysis, coefficients were derived after excluding individuals who self-reported poor health at baseline. For the second sensitivity analysis, all calculations were redone after excluding individuals with tumors, cardiovascular disease, chronic bronchitis, emphysema, or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease at baseline. In the third analysis, data was re-evaluated by excluding non-obese individuals. In the fourth analysis, our results were re-evaluated following the imputation of missing covariate data using Multiple Imputation by Chained Equations. As zero values are not permitted in component data analysis, the Expectation-Maximization (EM) method was employed to replace the zero values of MVPA with a very small non-zero value in the main analysis. In the fifth analysis, zero values for MVPA (physical activity) were imputed by adding 0.001 instead of using the EM algorithm to evaluate the robustness of imputation methods of zero values. In the sixth analysis, the overall baseline hazard ratio was employed instead of diabetes based baseline hazard ratio for the re-evaluation of our findings. Finally, in the last analysis, we conducted a model additionally adjusted for potential confounders, including townsend deprivation index, systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, dietary quality and C-reactive protein.

All statistical tests were 2-tailed and all analyses were performed in R software version 4.0.3 using survival, compositions, zCompositions, mice packages.

Discussion

With analyzing a population-based dataset, UK biobank, the current study indicates that 24-hour physical activity, particularly MVPA and SB, plays as key mediators in the increased mortality among patients with diabetes. In addition, by adopting isotemporal substitution paradigm and compositional analysis, it was also found that relocating SB time to MVPA was significantly associated with lower mortality risk and longer life expectancy among diabetes patients. The effect in increasing life expectancy is more salient among people at older age. The findings have important practical implications by supporting that interventions increasing physical activities should be developed for patients with diabetes to reduce mortality risk and prolong life expectancy.

A significant mediation effect of 24-hour physical activity was observed in the current study, accounting for 41.9% of the association between diabetes and mortality. The findings were in line with the previous evidence from other cohort studies. Such that PA can improve patient’s blood glucose level and benefit cardiorespiratory fitness [26], thus helping diabetes patients with glycemic control and contributing to reduced mortality risk. A recent large-scale cohort study has found that, compared with those T2DM patients who are physically inactive, those who are physically active and very active showed lower risk of diabetes mortality [5], which may suggest that the intensity of PA matters. Our findings have further elaborated that MVPA could be more beneficial than LPA by explaining a larger proportion of diabetes-related mortality increase (see the pathway coefficients in Fig. 1), and reallocating more time in MVPA was related with reduced mortality risk. It is probably due to that MVPA could promote T2DM patients’ general health and vitality, leading to better quality of life [28]. Unfortunately, in our study it was shown that less than 10% of patients with T2DM can meet the recommended level of MVPA (i.e., 150 min/week), and over one third remained physically inactive [11], suggesting more effort should be put into developing effective interventions to promote MVPA among diabetes patients.

The current study has also found that relocating sedentary time to MVPA would significantly prolong Life expectancy in the next 10 years after diabetes was diagnosed. As a novel statistical method in epidemiologic research, emerging evidence from isotemporal substitution analysis allows for an examination of the role of physical activity in mortality and life expectancy. In general population, it was found that replacing SB with LPA, MVPA, and sleep were all associated with lower risk of cardiovascular disease [13]. Research on patients with T2DM has shown that replacing SB with PA will improve cardiometabolic risk markers [27] and reduce all-cause mortality risk [17, 25]. A recent study using a small sample reported that replacing 60 min/day in SB with 30 min LPA and 30 min MVPA is associated with a reduction of 9% and 40% of mortality risk, respectively [36]. Our results have extended the findings to predicting life expectancy. Replacing SB with MVPA might increase life expectancy (with a maximum of 1.32 years) while replacing MVPA with SB might reduce it in the following 10 years, regardless of the age at which diabetes was diagnosed. Meanwhile, the merits of replacing SB with MVPA has incremented with the age of first diagnosis of diabetes (see table S3). In other words, the earlier to reallocate time to MVPA from SB, the longer Life the patient can expect. Together with the findings on the mediation role of 24-hour physical activities, it is suggested that for patients with diabetes, intervention should be developed to replace patient’s sedentary behaviors with MVPA.

Strength and limitations

The present study is based on a large-scale population-based prospective cohort study, with a key strength of large sample size and sufficient observation periods, which enhance the robustness of the results obtained. We employed the well-collected data to conduct a detailed analysis, thereby providing a comprehensive understanding. To our knowledge, this is the first study using Compositional Mediation Cox Model to address the 24-hour physical activities in diabetes and mortality, and adopting isotemporal substitution analysis in predicting Life expectancy with a population-based prospective cohort study. The 24-hour physical activities were measured using accelerometers, mitigating the risk of potential measurement bias. More importantly, the 24-hour physical activities were included in the compositional mediation model and isotemporal substitution analysis as a unit, to avoid the false estimation caused by the interconnection between different physical activities. In addition, using cohort life table instead of current life table to calculate baseline hazard ratio also allows better evaluations of the accumulative effects of chronic diseases diagnosed at different ages on mortality risk (see Fig. 1).

Despite the strength of our study, there are several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the study’s findings may have Limited generalizability due to the lack of racial and ethnic diversity in the cohort. Second, the average follow-up time is relatively brief, extending the duration could lead to a greater number of mortalities, thereby enhancing the statistical power for mortality analyses. Nevertheless, the current findings have identified the mediation role of 24-hour physical activities and addressed the benefits of replacing SB with MVPA in reducing mortality risk and extending Life expectancy. Third, although the prospective design establishes temporality, unmeasured confounders preclude definitive causal inference. Future research investigating the causal relationship between diabetes, 24-hour physical activities, and mortality is needed. Fourth, despite the adjustment of major covariates in both the primary analysis and the sensitivity analysis, the possibility of unmeasured confounding factors remains. Furthermore, the observed associations between type 2 diabetes, physical activities, and mortality may be influenced by other mediators and moderators, warranting further investigation.

Conclusion

By addressing a mediation role of 24-hour physical activities, the current study has suggested that MVPA and SB were the primary mediators in diabetes and mortality. Additionally, replacing SB with MVPA might enhance Life expectancy over the next 10 years in diabetes patients (with a maximum of 1.32 years), with greater benefits for those diagnosed with diabetes at a later age. Consequently, we recommend that more attention should be paid to increase MVPA and reduce SB in developing physical activity guidelines for preventing diabetes.

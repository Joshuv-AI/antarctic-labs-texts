Anticoagulation-free VV-ECMO: expanding the evidence beyond trauma
Authors: Ginga Suzuki
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

I keenly read with great interest the correspondence by Wu et al. describing their experience with anticoagulation-free venovenous extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (VV-ECMO) in trauma patients with severe hypoxemic respiratory failure [1]. Their report addressed an important clinical dilemma: balancing the prevention of thrombotic complications with the substantial bleeding risk inherent in trauma patients. Their favorable outcomes suggest that an anticoagulation-free strategy may be feasible for carefully selected patients.

Additional evidence from a nationwide multicenter cohort supports the observations of Wu et al. [1]. In a recent propensity score overlap-weighted analysis of the Japan Chest CT for ARDS Requiring VV-ECMO Registry, which included 695 adult patients treated across 24 intensive care units, systemic anticoagulation was not associated with improved 28-day survival [2]. Similarly, no significant differences were observed in 28-day survival, ECMO duration, circuit exchange frequency, or bleeding complications after adjusting for the measured confounders.

Although our study [2] differs substantially from that by Wu et al. [1] in both the patient population and study design, the overall direction of the findings was remarkably consistent. Wu et al. focused exclusively on trauma patients with a particularly high bleeding risk, whereas our registry covered a broader population of patients with acute respiratory distress syndrome managed across multiple institutions [2]. The concordance between these independent observational studies may therefore provide additional affirmation that withholding routine systemic anticoagulation may be considered in selected clinical situations. Moreover, both studies were observational and inherently susceptible to residual confounding factors, including confounding by indication. Accordingly, neither study should be interpreted as evidence that systemic anticoagulation is unnecessary in all patients undergoing VV-ECMO.

Rather than supporting a universal anticoagulation strategy, these complementary findings may encourage a more individualized approach based on the competing risks of bleeding and thrombosis in each patient. Future prospective studies are warranted to identify patients most likely to benefit from systemic anticoagulation therapy and establish evidence-based criteria for tailoring anticoagulation strategies during VV-ECMO.

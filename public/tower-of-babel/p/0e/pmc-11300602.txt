Lead-free ferroelectrics with giant unipolar strain for high-precision actuators
Authors: Xuefan Zhou, Jun Zhang, Hang Luo, Yan Zhang, Shiyu Tang, Houbing Huang, Xi Yuan, Miao Song, He Qi, Dou Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe trade-off between electrostrain and strain hysteresis for piezo/ferroelectric materials largely restrains the development of high precision actuators and remains unresolved over the past few decades. Here, a simple composition of (Bi0.5Na0.5)1-x/100Srx/100TiO3 in the ergodic relaxor state is collaboratively designed through the segregated domain structure with the ferroelectric core, local polarization heterogeneity, and defect engineering. The ferroelectric core can act as a seed to facilitate the field-induced nonpolar-to-polar transition. Together with the internal bias field caused by defect dipoles and adjusted through electric field cycling and heat treatment technology, a giant unipolar strain of 1.03% is achieved in the x = 30 ceramic with a low hysteresis of 27%, while the electric-field-independent large-signal piezoelectric strain coefficient of ~1000 pm/V and ultralow hysteresis of <10% can be obtained in the x = 35 ceramic. Intriguingly, the low-hysteresis high strain also exhibits near-zero remnant strain, excellent temperature and cycling stability.Subject terms: Electronic devices, Ceramics

Introduction

Piezoelectric actuator is widely used in smart structural systems due to their advantages such as compact structure, fast response and accurate displacement. The actuation performance of piezoelectric actuator is closely related to the unipolar electrostrain of the piezo/ferroelectric ceramics. In applications, the piezo/ferroelectric ceramics are expected to exhibit high strain, small hysteresis, low driving electric field, high fatigue resistance, and temperature insensitivity simultaneously. Significant effort has been made to explore the lead-free high-strain piezo/ferroelectric ceramics owing to the toxicity of lead oxide1. The popular ceramic systems involve the potassium sodium niobite (K0.5Na0.5NbO3, KNN) and bismuth sodium titanate (Bi0.5Na0.5TiO3, BNT)2. Taking advantages of polymorphic phase boundary, excellent small-signal piezoelectric charge coefficient d33 of >400 pC/N has been achieved in the KNN-based ceramics, concurrently giving rise to a unipolar strain of 0.2–0.3% under 4–6 kV/mm3 as well as good thermal stability4. BNT-based ceramics have shown great potential in achieving a high unipolar strain of >0.5% through the simple chemical modification, attributed to the construction of nonergodic relaxor (NR)/ergodic relaxor (ER) phase boundary and field-induced nonpolar-to-polar transition along with the polarization rotation/extension and domain switching5. However, the high strain is always accompanied by a large hysteresis (>50%)5.

The introduction of defect dipoles in KNN- and BNT-based ceramics has emerged as an effective strategy for producing a significantly enhanced strain along a certain electric field direction6–16. For example, the large bipolar strain of 0.4–0.5% at −5 kV/mm and unipolar strain of 0.18% at −3.5 kV/mm were obtained in the Fe2+- and Cu+-doped KNN ceramics sintered in the Ar atmosphere6. There are the (FeNb″′−VO∙∙)′ and (CuNb″″−VO∙∙)″ defect dipoles in the ceramics, and sufficient poling and aging treatment promote the alignment of defect dipoles along the [001] direction. The aligned defect dipoles can generate a large internal bias field in ceramics and lead to the asymmetric bipolar polarization-electric field (P-E) and strain-electric field (S-E) curves as well as the greatly improved unipolar strains along a certain electric field direction. However, in normal ferroelectrics, the ultrahigh strain arising from the interaction between the defect dipole polarization (PD) and spontaneous polarization (PS) switching can only be reproduced under the bipolar electrical loading. The irreversible ferroelectric domain switching leads to the generation of visible remanent strain in the bipolar S-E curve, and in the unipolar strain mode, the reusable strain and its cyclic reliability will be restricted significantly. Recently, a great breakthrough has been reported by Guo et al. that a giant bipolar strain of 1.05% at 5 kV/mm can be achieved in the Sr-doped KNN ceramics modulated by the (VK/Na′−VO∙∙)∙ defect dipoles, even though the unipolar strain at 5 kV/mm is reduced to 0.7%7.

The large unusable remanent strain relating to irreversible ferroelectric domain switching can be reduced in certain BNT-based ceramics showing the reversible field-induced nonpolar-to-polar transition. By combining the phase modulation with defect engineering, ultrahigh unipolar strain of exceeding 1% and high anti-fatigue property have been achieved in a few BNT-based ceramics9,13–15. For instances, a giant unipolar strain of 1.12% at −10 kV/mm was achieved in the BNT-BaAlO2.5 ceramics by forming a defect-engineered phase boundary13, and Dai et al. reported a giant unipolar strain of 1.6% at −10 kV/mm with good fatigue-resistance in the Sr/Nb-doped Bi0.5(Na0.82K0.18)0.5TiO3 textured ceramics by defect dipole design14. However, it should be noted that the defect-engineered BNT-based ceramics with an NR/ER phase boundary still inevitably exhibit a relatively large strain hysteresis (30–70%) and a non-negligible remanent strain12. Moreover, in such a scenario, the giant strain usually exhibits unsatisfactory temperature stability due to the sensitivity of phase boundary to temperature14. To obtain a unipolar strain response with low hysteresis, high repeatability, and excellent temperature stability, the ER state is an ideal choice because the hysteresis-free electrostrictive effect of the highly-active polar nanoregions makes the main contribution to the rapid polarization and strain response. Unfortunately, the strain output would be usually reduced to <0.2% for the compositions far away from the NR/ER phase boundary. Therefore, the unbalanced development of strain performance (strain output vs hysteresis) is still an unsolved key scientific problem in BNT-based ceramics.

In this study, simple composition of (Bi0.5Na0.5)1-x/100Srx/100TiO3 (BNST-x, x = 30 and 35) in the ER state is chosen to ensure the low strain hysteresis, superior temperature and cycling stability, while the domain and defect engineering are utilized to effectively enhance the strain output. On the one hand, ferroelectric cores are constructed for promoted field-induced nonpolar-to-polar transition. On the other hand, the aligned defect dipoles modified by electric field cycling and heat treatment provide an internal bias field to trigger an asymmetric strain response with an ultrahigh unipolar strain. As a result, a breakthrough of the trade-off between strain and hysteresis is realized in the BNST lead-free ceramics, providing a potential material for the next-generation high-precision actuators.

Results

Giant unipolar strain with ultralow hysteresis and excellent stability

A synergistic strategy of domain and defect engineering is applied to the BNST-30 and BNST-35 ceramics in the ER state, giving rise to a giant unipolar strain with an ultralow hysteresis. The trade-off between the strain output and hysteresis, a longstanding obstacle for the BNT-based high-strain ceramics, is overcome in this work. Herein, the domain engineering refers to the segregated domain structure and local polarization heterogeneity in the BNST ceramics (see “Domain engineering”), while the defect engineering refers to the PD alignment and enhancement of internal bias field in the BNST ceramics by electric field cycling and heat treatment (see“Defect engineering”). After introducing the domain and defect engineering, an ultrahigh room-temperature unipolar strain Suni of 1.03% with a low hysteresis η of ~ 27% (electric field E of −8 kV/mm, frequency of 1 Hz) is achieved in the BNST-30 ceramic, and the highest large-signal piezoelectric strain coefficient d33* (defined as Suni/E in the unipolar strain mode) of 1427 pm/V can be obtained at −5 kV/mm, As shown in Fig. 1a, b. The small remanent strain and low η reveal the predominant ER state. More attractively, the BNST-35 ceramic exhibits a large Suni of 0.78% at −10 kV/mm, accompanied by an ultralow η of 4.4%. The near-linear S-E response can be obtained in the range of −3 to −5 kV/mm, displaying an electric-field-independent d33*~ 1000 pm/V and η < 10%. The near-zero remanent strain and ultralow η illustrate that this composition has been lying deep in the ER state. Temperature-dependent dielectric spectra (Supplementary Fig. 1) also provide strong evidences for the room-temperature ER state of the BNST ceramics and enhanced ergodicity with the introduction of Sr. For BNT-based relaxor ferroelectrics, higher ergodicity means higher domain dynamics, leading to the reduced strain hysteresis and remanent strain.

In Fig. 1c, we compare the Suni and η values of these BNST ceramics with the state-of-the-art lead-free high-strain ceramics (Suni > 0.5%)7,10,13,14,17–28. It can be found that these BNST ceramics exhibit obvious advantages for high-precision actuator applications with both large strain response and low hysteresis. In order to quantitatively evaluate the comprehensive strain behavior and the trade-off between Suni and η, a figure of merit is defined as Suni/η, as summarized in Fig. 1d. A record-high of Suni/η ~ 0.175 can be achieved in the BNST-35 ceramic, which is about four times higher than the highest value of previous reported ceramics. The achievement of the high figure of merit should be attributed to the much higher strain value than that of other low-hysteresis (η < 20%) ceramics29–41, the values of which caused by electrostriction and piezoelectric effect are usually hard to exceed 0.4%, as shown in Fig. 1e. Besides, a high electrostrictive coefficient Q33 of ~ 0.06 m4/C2 can be determined from the nearly linear S–P2 curves of the BNST-35 ceramic (Supplementary Fig. 2).

The evolution of unipolar S-E curves (E of −5kV/mm, frequency of 1 Hz), d33* and η with temperature (25–150 °C) and electric field cycle (1–105) for these BNST ceramics was measured (Supplementary Fig. 3) to evaluate the temperature stability and anti-fatigue property. The d33* exhibits acceptable temperature stability in the temperature range of 25–100 °C, which decreases by ~ 15% and ~ 10% for the BNST-30 and BNST-35 ceramics, respectively. An elevated temperature usually acts to improve the ergodicity and domain dynamics of BNT-based ER ceramics and impede the field-induced nonpolar-to-polar transition, responsible for the decrease of strain output. On the other hand, we believe that the segregated domain structure and internal bias field established by defect engineering in our BNST ceramics help to maintain a relatively high Suni until the temperature of 100 °C. Quantitatively, the d33* at 100 °C reaches ~ 1170 pm/V and ~ 920 pm/V for the BNST-30 and BNST-35 ceramics, respectively. By contrast, the better d33* temperature stability of the BNST-35 ceramic should be attributed to its stronger ergodicity at room temperature, by which the increase of temperature does not result in the apparent change of phase structure or much enhanced energy barrier for the field-induced nonpolar-to-polar transition. We also notice that further increase in temperature from 100 to 150 °C leads to a significant drop in Suni for both the samples, which could be ascribed to the strong random fields induced by excessive temperature and weakened internal bias field due to the decoupling of defect dipoles. Besides, with the temperature increasing from 25 to 150 °C, the η decreases monotonically for both the samples owing to the improved ergodicity.

Regarding the anti-fatigue property, the decrease in d33* reaches ~ 30% after 105 cycles for the BNST-30 ceramic while the d33* decreases by ~ 5% for the BNST-35 ceramic. The unipolar electric field cycling also leads to the gradual decrease in strain hysteresis for both the samples. In general, the field-induced strain performance of ferroelectric ceramics will be degraded upon repeated cycling owing to the domain stabilization effect, that is, the defect dipoles or mobile charged defects in ferroelectric ceramics can stabilize the domain configuration and prevent them from switching in the fatigue process42. The strain hysteresis is intrinsically related to the dynamics of polarization switching under a cycle of electric field. Therefore, the suppressed polarization switching not only makes the strain output decreased, but also reduces the strain hysteresis. The non-negligible remnant strain in each cycle for the BNST-30 ceramic causes the apparent fatigue in the strain response. The total ER state and highly-dynamic polar nanoregions (PNRs, see “Domain engineering”) of the BNST-35 ceramic enable a reversible and repeated field-induced nonpolar-to-polar transition, leading to the near-zero remnant strain and excellent fatigue resistance. In Fig. 1f, the unipolar strain performance parameters of the BNST-35 ceramic are comprehensively summarized and compared with the other representative lead-free systems7,10 and commercial PZT-5H (Supplementary Fig. 4). The high strain value, ultralow hysteresis, and outstanding stability make the BNST-35 ceramic promising in piezoactuator applications where large displacement, precise positioning, and superior reliability are highly desired. The effect of uniaxial compressive stress on the unipolar strain response of the BNST-35 ceramic was further measured to evaluate its suitability in actuation applications (Supplementary Fig. 5). The result shows that the Suni (E of −5 kV/mm, frequency of 1 Hz) decreases to ~ 0.38% under the load of 100 g mass block compared to the Suni of 0.5% in the stress-free state, which can be ascribed to the stress-induced domain clamping effect43,44.

Domain engineering

The transmission electron microscopy (TEM) observation demonstrates the dominance of nanometer-sized clusters in the BNST-30 and BNST-35 ceramic grains, as shown in Fig. 2a, b, which is a typical feature of BNT-based ER ferroelectrics. As Sr content increases from x = 30 to 35, the domain configuration evolves from tweed-like PNRs to the finely dispersed granular-like PNRs. The reduction in size of PNRs is attributed to the enhanced ferroelectric disorder arising from the Sr2+ (r = 1.18 Å) substitution instead of the A-site Bi3+ (r = 1.36 Å) and Na+ (r = 1.39 Å) with differences in radius and valence state. In general, the reduction in size of PNRs is linked with the weakened interaction among PNRs and enhanced dynamics. Smaller PNRs enable a faster response to external electric fields45, which is responsible for the obviously reduced strain hysteresis of the BNST-35 ceramic compared with the BNST-30 ceramic. The absence of long-range ferroelectric ordering in the BNST ceramics and the decay in ferroelectricity by the introduction of Sr can be also reflected in the X-ray diffraction and Raman results (Supplementary Fig. 6), which indicate the pseudo-cubic (nonpolar) phase structure in the average long-range scale for both the samples and the improvement of lattice symmetry with the increase of Sr content. Rietveld refinement results show a phase coexistence with the dominated P4bm phase and small amounts of R3c phase, and the increase of Sr content leads to the increase of P4bm phase.

More intriguingly, many grains in the BNST ceramics were found to exhibit an inhomogeneous microstructure with complex and irregularly aligned domains in the core region. Low-magnification ceramic grain images also display the inhomogeneous microstructure (Supplementary Fig. 7), and the BNT ceramic is used as a counterpart to discuss the formation mechanism of cores in the BNST ceramic grains. The core size is in the range of 400–800 nm, as shown in Fig. 2c. The line scan analysis by energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) indicates that the enrichment of (Bi, Na) and deficiency of Sr in the core region contribute to the heterostructure, as shown in Fig. 2d. In Fig. 2e, the selected area electron diffraction (SAED) patterns for the matrix region taken along the [100] and [110] zone axes show the coexistence of ½{ooe} and ½{ooo} superlattice spots, which corresponds to the P4bm symmetry with the in-phase a0a0c+ type BO6 tilting and R3c symmetry with the anti-phase a-a-a- type BO6 tilting, respectively46,47. This structure feature is consistent with the uniform compositions reported before48. In order to show the local polarization structure directly, the atomic-resolution images along the [100] direction characterized by the high angle angular dark field-scanning transmission electron microscopy (HAADF-STEM) were used to quantitatively analyze the local polarization mapping at different regions, as shown in Fig. 2f, g. There is a coexistence of [001]- and [011]-oriented (the projection component of rhombohedral phase on the (100) projection plane) polarization vectors on the lattice scale for the matrix region, and obviously, the PNRs sharing the P4bm (labeled as ‘T’ in the figure) and R3c (labeled as ‘R’ in the figure) symmetries are randomly distributed and exhibit a gradual structural transformation. The dynamically fluctuating two-phase PNRs can undergo a fast polarization reorientation under the action of electric field, giving rise to a low strain hysteresis. Differently, for the core region, there is no ½{ooe} spots while much stronger ½{ooo} spots, revealing an R3c structure. This result can also be well confirmed by the preference for polarization vector orientation along the [011] direction, the formation of which should be ascribed to a lower Sr/(Bi, Na) element ratio in the core region and responsible for the stronger overall ferroelectric polarization. Difference in ferroelectric polarization (or permittivity) inside and outside the core region can cause an uneven electric field distribution in order to satisfy the polarization coupling49. Higher local electric field in the matrix region can facilitate the nonpolar-to-polar transition, which is beneficial to enhance the strain output. In a word, such a segregated domain structure in the BNST grains helps to induce a low-hysteresis high strain because the ferroelectric core can act as a seed to facilitate the nonpolar-to-polar transition for high strain value50, while the highly-dynamic matrix can provide a low-hysteresis polarization and strain response.

Defect engineering

It is worth noting that the high unipolar strain of the BNST ceramics is obtained under the negative electric field. Actually, in addition to the segregated domain structure discussed above, the defect engineering plays a crucial role in producing the high unipolar strain under the negative electric field. Given that no acceptor or donor doping was conducted to the BNST system, the defect dipoles in the BNST ceramics were thought to stem from the A-site vacancies (VA″, average valence of A-site cations) induced by the volatilization of Bi and Na elements during sintering and charge-compensating oxygen vacancies (VO∙∙). It should be mentioned that the BNST ceramics in this work were sintered at a high temperature of 1180 °C without buried calcined powders, providing a basis for the formation of VA″ and VO∙∙. Besides, in the Ti-containing ceramics, the reduction of Ti ions may occur during sintering, which could also lead to the generation of VO∙∙ and defect dipoles. Inductively coupled plasma-optical emission spectroscopy (ICP-OES) was utilized to analyze the chemical compositions of the BNST ceramics (Supplementary Table 1). The contents of Bi, Na, and Sr are normalized based on the Ti content, and (Bi, Na) depletion can be detected for both the samples. Obvious local chemical inhomogeneity and even existence of VA″ can also be confirmed by the drastic change in the intensity of the A-site atom columns in the HAADF-STEM image, as shown in Fig. 3a–c. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis was used to identify the presence of VO∙∙ and the valence state of Ti ions in the BNST ceramics. As shown in Fig. 3d, the O1s XPS spectra mainly exhibits two resolved peaks and the shoulder at ~531 eV is an indication of the existence of VO∙∙. Fitting the Ti2p XPS spectra (Supplementary Fig. 8) suggests the absence of Ti3+ peak for both the samples, i.e., no obvious reduction of Ti4+ during sintering of the BNST ceramics. It implies that VA″ and VO∙∙ play dominant roles in the defect structure of the BNST ceramics. Moreover, the concentration of VO∙∙ was found to decrease with increasing x, which could be attributed to the reduced (Bi, Na)/Sr element ratio in the lattice.

The (VA″−VO∙∙) defect dipole is along the [110] crystallographic directions, and it has been demonstrated that in the BNT-based relaxor ferroelectric ceramics, the polarizability of (VA″−VO∙∙) defect dipole is stronger than that of the electric dipoles arising from the electronic and ionic displacements, which can significantly affect the polarization and strain behaviors14. In the first-cycle bipolar S-E curve at room temperature for the fresh BNST-30 and BNST-35 ceramics (E of 5 kV/mm, frequency of 1 Hz), as shown in Fig. 4a,b, we can observe a higher strain output at −5 kV/mm (Spol−) than that at 5 kV/mm (Spol+), implying the existence of an internal bias field along the negative direction caused by defect dipoles. For a comparative experiment, the BNST-35 ceramic sintered in buried calcined powders was prepared to avoid the evaporation of Bi and Na elements and inhibit the formation of VA″ and VO∙∙ as much as possible, which has been shown to be effective in previous studies9,11. Indeed, its first-cycle bipolar S-E curve at room temperature (E of 5 kV/mm, frequency of 1 Hz, Supplementary Fig. 9) shows a more symmetric shape. It suggests that bipolar strain asymmetry of the BNST ceramics is sensitive to the defect structure.

From the perspective of Landau-Ginzburg-Devonshire phenomenological theory, the multiple coexistence of ferroelectric phases is conducive to the flattening of free energy profile, meaning a low barrier for PS rotation among different states. The coexistence of P4bm and R3c phases in our BNST ceramics with randomly distributed PNRs determines the low energy barrier for PS rotation, and a shallower free energy well is expected with the increase of Sr content. Meanwhile, the internal bias field caused by aligned PD can break the symmetry of the free energy profiles, causing the system to descend into a potential well in which the PS aligns close to the direction of PD, as shown in Fig. 4c, which presents the schematic diagrams of the free energy profiles for the BNST ceramics in the defect-free state and the state with PD. On this basis, the effects of electric field cycling on the polarization and strain behaviors of the fresh BNST ceramics are worthy of research. Intriguingly, the strain asymmetry of bipolar S-E curve is visibly enhanced by the room-temperature bipolar electric field cycling (E of 5 kV/mm, frequency of 1 Hz) for the fresh BNST-30 and BNST-35 ceramics, which increases obviously in the first three cycles, and then becomes stable (Fig. 4a, b). It is impressive that a negative strain is acquired under the positive electric field, indicating an obvious lattice compression, which could arise from the interaction between aligned PD and PS switching (see “Insights into the effects of aligned PD”). Based on the bipolar P-E and S-E curves shown in Fig. 4a, b, we summarized the changes of Pr+/Pr− and Spol−-Spol+ with cycling number for the fresh BNST-30 and BNST-35 ceramics (Supplementary Fig. 10) to evaluate the polarization and strain asymmetry. The enhanced asymmetry of P-E loops with cycling number can be demonstrated by the gradually decreased Pr+/Pr− from 0.94 to 0.63 and from 0.89 to 0.69 for the BNST-30 and BNST-35 ceramics, respectively. Combined with the decrease of negative coercive field (EC−) with cycling number (Supplementary Fig. 11), it is believed that there is a gradual alignment of PD and enhanced internal bias field along the negative direction during every bipolar electric field cycle, which is stabilized after several cycles. The one-way-trip alignment of PD driven by the external electric fields and internal bias field are energetically favorable owing to the tilted free energy profiles. In terms of the bipolar S-E curve, the increased strain asymmetry is accompanied with the increase in Spol− and decrease in Spol+. After five cycles, theSpol− of the BNST-30 and BNST-35 ceramics increases from 0.490 to 0.540% and from 0.336 to 0.382%, meanwhile, the Spol+ decreases from 0.161 to −0.002% and from 0.089 to 0.005%, respectively. Additionally, we found that the negative unipolar electric field cycling (E of −5 kV/mm, frequency of 1 Hz) on the fresh BNST ceramics at room temperature acts to enhance the Suni (Supplementary Fig. 12), which is counterintuitive to the traditional idea that strain response of ferroelectric ceramics degrades gradually upon repeated cycling due to the domain stabilization effect. The results further support our hypothesis that proper electric field cycling can promote the PD alignment in the fresh BNST ceramics and enhance the internal bias field along the negative direction, leading to the increase of bipolar strain asymmetry and strain output under the negative electric field.

The reorientation of defect dipoles is an energy- and time-consuming diffusion process through vacancy migration. By calculating the energy assumption of PD reorientation in the Sr-doped BNT lattice with the R3c and P4bm symmetries, it is found that the PD reorientation energy barrier in the P4bm lattice is lower than that in the R3c lattice and the increase in Sr content is conducive to the decrease of PD reorientation energy barrier (Supplementary Fig. 13). Therefore, the dominant P4bm phase and pseudo-cubic structure of our BNST ceramics have a positive effect on the one-way-trip alignment of PD under the action of external electric field and internal bias field. However, the convergence of strain asymmetry in several cycles indicates that the alignment of PD is limited at room temperature when fixing the external electric field. With the increase of temperature, the PD reorientation could become easier owing to the lowered activation energy of oxygen vacancy migration, conducive to the further alignment of PD under electric field. However, the elevated temperature could also lead to the decoupling of defect dipoles, i.e., the thermally-activated defect dipoles are difficult to maintain the alignment state. This motivates us to explore the effects of temperature on the PD reorientation and bipolar strain behaviors of the BNST ceramics under the stimulation of electric field. Before that, temperature-dependent Raman spectra were measured to determine the variation of crystal symmetry with temperature for the BNST ceramics (Supplementary Fig. 14). As a case study, Fig. 4d displays the contour map of Raman intensity for the BNST-30 ceramic in the temperature range of 30–120 °C. The specific Raman spectra at 30 °C and 120 °C and corresponding peak fitting results are shown in Fig. 4e. With the increase of temperature, a continuous softening behavior can be observed for both the samples. For the B2 mode (related to the strength of the Ti–O bond) located around 300 cm−1, the position gradually moves toward lower wavenumbers, along with the increase in the FWHM value. This signifies the gradual decrease in the ferroelectric ordering50,51.

Figure 4f, g show the variable-temperature bipolar S-E curves (E of 5 kV/mm, frequency of 1 Hz) of the BNST-30 and BNST-35 ceramics after the room-temperature bipolar electric field cycling, respectively. The variable-temperature bipolar P-E curves were also provided (Supplementary Fig. 15). We summarized the variations of Spol−, Spol+, Spol−-Spol+, Pr+/Pr−, EC+/EC− with temperature for both the samples (Supplementary Fig. 16). It is surprising that the Spol− and strain asymmetry (Spol−-Spol+) for both the samples increase as the temperature increases from room temperature to 80 °C. In the meantime, there is also an enhanced polarization asymmetry, as evidenced by the decreased Pr+/Pr− and increased EC+/EC−. It demonstrates the alignment of PD by the bipolar electric field cycling at high temperatures, and below 80 °C, the promoting effect of aligned PD on strain response can greatly offset the negative effect induced by the enhanced ergodicity at elevated temperature. With the further increase in temperature from 80 to 120 °C, the reduced Spol−, strain asymmetry and polarization asymmetry reveal the degraded influence of aligned PD, which can be attributed to the decoupling of defect dipoles and stronger random fields in the ceramics. Notably, at the same temperature, the bipolar S-E and P-E curves measured in the cooling process display a higher Spol−, larger Spol−-Spol+, lower Pr+/Pr− and higher EC+/EC− compared to their counterparts measured in the heating process. It is supposed that under the stimulation of electric field, the sufficient oxygen vacancy migration at high temperatures facilitates the formation of oriented defect dipoles during the subsequent cooling process. As the temperature decreases to room temperature, the bipolar S-E curves with a huge strain asymmetry can be obtained for both the samples. After the coupling of bipolar electric field cycling and heat treatment, the Spol− of BNST-30 ceramic reaches 0.671% with a η ~ 30%, while the BNST-35 ceramic shows the Spol− ~ 0.482% and η ~ 10%.

To further examine the existence of defect dipoles and the effects of aligned PD, we observed the frequency dependence of room-temperature bipolar P-E and S-E curves of the BNST-30 ceramic after the bipolar electric field cycling and heat treatment (Supplementary Fig. 17). Intriguingly, the change of measurement frequency from 10 to 0.1 Hz leads to an increase in the polarization and strain asymmetry, as evidenced by the decreased Pr+/Pr−, increased EC+/EC− and Spol−-Spol+. For the defect-engineered ferroelectric ceramics, it is usually accepted that the oxygen vacancies possess more time to migrate in short-range within the lattice at a low frequency, causing the aligned PD being disturbed and thus the reduced internal bias field and polarization/strain asymmetry52. The opposite trend shown in our study demonstrates the high stability of aligned PD along the negative direction in the BNST ceramics. The improved strain response and Spol−-Spol+ with the decrease of frequency should be attributed to the more sufficient field-induced nonpolar-to-polar transition and lattice compression/stretching due to the aligned PD (see “Insights into the effects of aligned PD”) at a low frequency. Besides, it was found that the lower the frequency, the smaller the remnant polarization and strain, which should arise from the more efficient collapse of field-induced polar state on removal of the electric field. Additionally, a room-temperature positive unipolar electric field cycling (E of 5 kV/mm, frequency of 1 Hz) experiment was performed on the BNST-30 ceramic showing a large negative strain under the positive electric field (Supplementary Fig. 18). The negative strain can be totally reversed by a long-term positive unipolar electric field cycling. It means that the aligned PD along the negative direction can be destroyed by applying an opposing electric field. These results further verify the significant effects of aligned PD in our BNST ceramics.

Insights into the effects of aligned PD

As discussed above, the aligned PD induces the highly-asymmetric bipolar S-E curves of the BNST ER ceramics and helps to produce a large unipolar strain under the negative electric field. In Fig. 5a, we present the schematic illustration of PD alignment under the action of electric field cycling and heat treatment. Figure 5b gives the schematic diagram of strain contributions from the reversible nonpolar-to-polar transition and aligned PD in the BNST-30 ceramic, as a case study. Herein, the mechanisms behind the negative strain under the positive electric field require an in-depth exploration. At a point, no long-range ferroelectric ordering exists while there is an obvious PD alignment. From a point to b point, the increase in the applied positive electric field leads to the continuous compression of the aligned PD, i.e., lattice contraction, which is the origin of negative strain. In the meantime, the PD impedes the field-induced nonpolar-to-polar transition. However, if the applied positive field is high enough, the tensile strain caused by the field-induced nonpolar-to-polar transition can compete with the compressive strain from the aligned PD, which inhibits the continuous increase in the negative strain. From b point to c point, the lattice contraction induced by defect dipoles weakens, leading to the decrease in the negative strain. It should be emphasized that the depressed defect dipole polarization cannot be fully recovered to their initial state8, leaving a residual negative strain at c point. With the application of negative electric field in the c-d route, the aligned PD is stretched and promotes the field-induced nonpolar-to-polar transition in their surrounding lattices, giving rise to a giant strain at d point. From d point to e point, the hysteresis effect of the induced polar state back to the initial nonpolar state and the defect dipole recovery causes a positive residual strain at e point. Phase-field simulations were further conducted to confirm the significant effects of aligned PD on the polarization and strain behaviors of the BNT-based ER samples (Supplementary Fig. 19).

Insights into the effects of aligned PD.

a Schematic illustration of PD and PS in the lattice, and their orientation distributions in the virgin state, under the action of external electric field, and after the electric field cycling and heat treatment. b Schematic diagram of strain contributions from the field-induced nonpolar-to-polar transition and lattice contraction/stretching due to the aligned PD in the BNST-30 ceramic, that has undergone the electric field cycling and heat treatment. c, Evolution of ferroelectric domain structure in the fresh BNST-30 ceramic with the applied voltages. d, [100]-oriented SAED patterns measured under different voltages and e, the calculated d010, d001, and c/a.

To provide an intuitive analysis of how the nonpolar-to-polar transition develops under the stimulation of electric field and the influence of PD, in-situ TEM observation with respect to voltage (specimen configuration shown in Supplementary Fig. 20) was performed on the fresh BNST-30 ceramic along the [100] direction. Figure 5c shows the evolution of domain structure in the matrix region of the BNST-30 grain with the applied voltages (TEM sample thickness is ~5 μm). With the increase of voltage from 0 to −40 V, the intersecting tweed-like PNRs gradually evolved into parallel lamellar nanodomains with high-density domain walls, relating to the enhancement of polar ordering. During the removal of the voltage, the domain structure was relaxed back to the initial state, indicating a reversible nonpolar-to-polar transition. When the voltage switched back and forth between 0 and 40 V, a similar domain evolution pattern can be observed. However, it is worth noting that the degree of polar ordering under 40 V is visibly weaker than that under −40 V, implying an asymmetric field-induced response. For the determination of crystal structures and lattice parameters, the [100]-oriented SAED patterns were observed under different voltages, as shown in Fig. 5d. Obvious ½{ooe} superlattice spots reveal the P4bm phase structure while the application of negative or positive voltages shows little effect on the intensity of ½{ooe} spots. It infers that there is no significant structural transformation from P4bm to R3c caused by the applied voltages. The [011] splitting of elementary reflection spots is also the evidence for the (011)-type 90o tetragonal twining. The calculated lattice parameters including d010, d001, and c/a were summarized in Fig. 5e. Regardless of the electric field direction, the d001 increases while d010 decreases with the increase of voltage, leading to the improvement of c/a. Noticeably, at the same voltage level, a negative voltage can induce a larger c/a value, i.e., a larger lattice distortion, than a positive voltage. It is believed that the increase in the polar ordering and lattice stretching are the main contributions to electric-field-induced strain. Importantly, the PD in the ceramic impedes the polarization rotation and lattice stretching under the positive voltages, which are promoted under the negative voltages, leading to the asymmetrical strain response. In-situ measurement by the piezoelectric force microscopy (PFM) also demonstrates the asymmetrical field-induced response at the domain scale, as evidenced by the faster polarization switching and higher PFM amplitude under the negative voltages (Supplementary Fig. 21).

In summary, we proposed a strategy of integrating the segregated domain structure with defect engineering in the BNT-based ER ceramics to greatly enhance the unipolar strain output while maintaining an ultralow strain hysteresis and excellent stability. Giant unipolar strains of 1.03% (−8 kV/mm) and 0.78% (−10 kV/mm) were achieved in the BNST-30 and BNST-35 ceramics, accompanied with the low hysteresis of 27% and 4.4%, respectively. For the BNST-35 ceramic, the near-linear unipolar strain response was obtained in the range of −3 to −5 kV/mm, displaying the electric-field-independent piezoelectric coefficient of ~ 1000 pm/V and ultralow hysteresis of <10%. The ceramics are of practical importance for high-precision lead-free ferroelectric actuator applications. The proposed strategy could also be applied to other ferroelectric materials to break the trade-off between electrostrain value and strain hysteresis. Moreover, we reported that the coupling of electric field cycling and heat treatment has the ability to promote the alignment of PD in the defect-engineered ergodic relaxor ferroelectric ceramics without the need of long periods of poling-aging treatment, and the interaction between the aligned PD and PS switching was revealed on the domain and lattice scales, providing a deep understanding about the structural origins of asymmetric bipolar strain curves.

Methods

Ceramic preparation

The (Bi0.5Na0.5)1-x/100Srx/100TiO3 (BNST-x, x = 30 and 35) ceramics were prepared by the solid-state method starting from Bi2O3 (Aladdin, 99.9%), Na2CO3 (Aladdin, 99.5%), SrCO3 (Aladdin, 99.95%), and TiO2 (Aladdin, 99%). The powers are weighed and mixed in ethanol for 8 h at 300 rpm. After drying overnight, the mixture was calcinated at 800 °C for 3 h with a heating rate of 5 °C/min. The BNST calcined powders were milled in ethanal for 24 h at 300 rpm, and then mixed with 3 wt% PVB. The dried powders were pressed into the pellets with a diameter of 10 mm. After burning out the PVB at 600 °C for 1 h, the pellets were sintered at 1180 °C for 2 h with a heating rate of 5 °C/min to gain the BNST ceramics. Notably, the pellets were placed on a layer of calcined powders in a covered alumina crucible, but the pellets were not buried in the calcined powders. For a comparative experiment, the BNST-35 ceramic sintered in buried calcined powders was also prepared with the same heating procedures.

Structure and property characterization

The phase structures of the BNST ceramics were tested by a D8 Advance X-ray diffractometer (XRD, Bruker AXS, Germany, Cu-Kα radiation). Raman spectra were collected by a Horiba HR Evolution confocal Raman microscope (HORIBA Scientific, France) equipped with a Linkam THM 600 heating stage to provide the in-situ thermal field. The observation of ceramic grain morphology and elements distribution was conducted by the scanning electron microscope (SEM, MIRA3, TESCAN, Czech Republic) equipped with an energy dispersive spectroscope (EDS). Back scattered electron (BSE) images can be obtained accordingly. The analysis of oxygen vacancy and elemental valence state was carried out by the X–ray photoelectron spectrometer (XPS, ESCALAB 250Xi, USA). Inductively coupled plasma-optical emission spectrometer (ICP-OES, PerkinElmer Optima 8300, USA) was used to analyze the ceramic compositions. The focused ion beam (FIB) milling technology was carried in this work to prepare the TEM sample. A double spherical aberration-corrected transmission electron microscope (Thermo Fisher Spectra 300, USA) was used to observe the domain morphology, SAED patterns, and atomic-scale HAADF-STEM images. The equipped EDS apparatus was used to acquire the elements distribution. Based on the HAADF-STEM images, the polarization vector, polarization magnitude and polarization angle were calculated and extracted by the customized MATLAB scripts. For the in-situ TEM observation, Pt-electrodes were deposited on both sides of the TEM sample and an electrochemically etched tungsten tip was used as a movable electrode. The polarization distributions and field-induced polarization switching behaviors were characterized by the piezoresponse force microscopy (PFM) mode of an atomic force microscope (AFM, NanoManTM VS, Veeco, USA). Before the PFM measurements, the ceramics were thoroughly polished and thinned to ~ 0.10 mm thickness.

For electrical characterization, the BNST ceramics were first polished to a thickness of ~ 0.5 mm and then Ag paste was coated on both faces of the ceramics and baked in air at 500 °C for 15 min to form the electrodes with a diameter of ~ 8 mm. The temperature-dependent dielectric spectra in the temperature range of RT-400 °C were measured using the commercial dielectric measurement system (DMS-500, Partulab Technology Co., Ltd, China). The P-E and S-E curves of the BNST ceramics at different electric fields, temperatures and frequencies were characterized using a commercial ferroelectric tester (TF Analyzer 2000, AixACCT systems, Germany) equipped with a linear variable differential transformer (LVDT). The specific measurement conditions of P-E and S-E curves have been annotated in the main text.

Specially, the field-induced strain response of the BNST-35 ceramic under uniaxial compressive stress was measured using a custom-built sample holder. A 100 g mass block serves as a load on the BNST-35 ceramic (thickness of ~ 0.5 mm, contacting area of ~ 0.785 mm2) to provide a uniaxial compressive stress, and the strain was measured as the relative displacement by an LVDT located well outside the sample holder.

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) remains a progressive and life-threatening disease despite major therapeutic advances over the past three decades. Alongside evolving treatment strategies, the growing complexity of PAH management has been accompanied by an expansion of global clinical research activity, creating challenges for clinicians and researchers to synthesize emerging evidence and identify meaningful trends.MethodsThis study provides a descriptive analysis of PAH-related clinical studies registered on international platforms integrated into the World Health Organization International Clinical Trials Registry Platform (ICTRP/WHO) between January and December 2025. Trials were characterized according to study design, therapeutic pathway, geographic distribution, funding source, and methodological features, including endpoint selection and incorporation of patient-reported and digital outcomes.ResultsA total of 54 PAH-related studies were registered in 2025, of which 64.8% were interventional and 33.3% observational. Most trials involved pharmacological interventions and were conducted predominantly in high- and middle-income countries, with limited representation from low-income regions. Academic institutions and hospitals accounted for the majority of sponsors, while direct pharmaceutical industry sponsorship was identified in a minority of registrations. Therapeutic investigations extended beyond classical vasodilator pathways, with increased focus on transforming growth factor/bone morphogenetic protein (TGF-β/BMP) signaling modulation, metabolic and cardiorenal pathways, and selective tyrosine kinase inhibition. Methodologically, trials increasingly incorporated composite endpoints, imaging-based assessments, biomarkers, patient-reported outcome measures, and, in a subset, digital health and remote monitoring strategies.ConclusionClinical trials registered in 2025 reflect a PAH research landscape characterized by stable trial volume, diversification of therapeutic targets, and progressive methodological refinement. The growing use of multidimensional endpoints and patient-centered and digital tools suggests a increasing integration of therapeutic response. Introduction

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is a condition associated with high morbidity and mortality, with a substantial impact on quality of life and life expectancy that results from complex pathophysiological alterations affecting the pulmonary circulation and the right ventricle [1]. Since the 1990s, the treatment of PAH has incorporated therapies targeting central molecular pathways, including the endothelin-1, nitric oxide (NO), and prostacyclin systems, and, more recently, the bone morphogenetic protein (BMP)/activin axis [2–7]. These approaches are associated with improvements in functional, hemodynamic, and prognostic outcomes; however, PAH remains a chronic condition that is characterized by disease progression and the need for multiple combined therapeutic strategies [8].

In parallel with therapeutic advances, the growing complexity of PAH management has been accompanied by a marked expansion of clinical research activity worldwide. According to data from the International Clinical Trials Registry Platform (ICTRP/World Health Organization [WHO]), between 2015 and 2025, 502 PAH-related studies were registered across international platforms (including ClinicalTrials.gov, the EU Clinical Trials Register, and national registries linked to the WHO ICTRP), encompassing different study designs, with an annual average of 44.9 ± 15.2 studies per year, of which approximately 62% involved pharmacological interventions. The majority of these registrations were concentrated in high-income countries (approx. 71%), with substantially lower representation from low- and middle-income regions [9]. Over the last decade, the annual number of registered studies increased progressively until 2018 (n = 54), peaked in 2021 (n = 66, including 49 pharmacological trials), and subsequently remained stable between 43 and 54 studies per year from 2022 to 2025. While this sustained volume of research reflects intense scientific interest and innovation in the field, it also poses a significant challenge to clinicians and researchers to remain up to date with ongoing developments. The rapid succession of early-phase studies, evolving trial designs, and the parallel investigation of multiple therapeutic targets contribute to a fragmented although dynamic literature landscape, in which emerging trends are often difficult to discern from isolated initiatives, reflecting a progressive conceptual expansion of PAH research beyond classical vasodilator pathways (Fig. 3).

Conceptual expansion of pulmonary arterial hypertension research beyond classical vasodilator pathways toward broader biological domains encompassing growth factor signaling, inflammation, vascular remodeling, metabolism, right ventricular function, and multidimensional endpoints

During this period, recent studies have explored novel therapeutic targets, including activin signaling inhibitors within the transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) superfamily, such as sotatercept, as well as alternative formulations and routes of administration of prostacyclin pathway agents [10–12]. Simultaneously, there has been a increasing use of more complex trial designs that incorporate adaptive methodologies, composite endpoints, and multimodal assessments combining hemodynamic, functional, imaging, and patient-reported outcomes [13–15]. Despite these advances, important methodological challenges persist, including the absence of fully validated surrogate endpoints, premature discontinuation of phase III trials due to insufficient efficacy or safety concerns, and the geographic concentration of study populations in high-income settings, which may limit the generalizability of findings and equitable access to clinical research [10, 13, 15].

In this context, systematic analyses of ongoing clinical trials may represent a valuable strategy to synthesize emerging evidence and anticipate future directions in PAH research. In the study presented here, we use data from the ICTRP/WHO—which integrates clinical trial registries from multiple national and regional databases under harmonized criteria for transparency and periodic updating [16]—and provide a descriptive review of PAH-related studies registered between January and December 2025. By examining the characteristics, therapeutic targets, and methodological features of trials in progress, the aim of this analysis was to provide an updated overview of the current research landscape and to identify signals that may inform the future trajectory of clinical investigation in pulmonary arterial hypertension.

This article is a review and does not involve human participants or primary data collection. Therefore, ethical approval was not required.

Overall Landscape of Clinical Trials (2025)

In 2025, 54 PAH clinical trials were registered on international platforms (Electronic Supplementary Material 1). Of these, 35 (64.8%) were classified as interventional, 18 (33.3%) as observational, and one (1.9%) as an expanded access study. In terms of the type of intervention, 47 studies involved pharmacological interventions and seven involved non-pharmacological interventions, regardless of study design.

Among the 35 interventional trials, 26 (74.3%) reported geographic location and were conducted exclusively in high- or middle-income countries; the remaining nine trials (25.7%) did not report the country where the trial was conducted. No studies conducted exclusively in low-income countries were identified.

From a funding perspective, of the total set of 54 studies, 32 (59.3%) were sponsored by universities or hospitals, and eight (14.8%) were directly sponsored by the pharmaceutical industry. In the remaining 16 registrations (29%), funding was not clearly specified or involved combinations of institutional sources.

Observational studies, which accounted for 18 of the 54 identified trials (33.3%), included assessments of clinical phenotypes, real-world practices, and outcome monitoring in PAH. Among these, studies focusing on clinical phenotyping, such as the JPRN-UMIN000056065 trial, explored the characterization of subphenotypes and prognostic assessment in patients with PAH, including outcomes such as mortality and clinical progression.

From a methodological standpoint, analysis of the studies indicate that most remain based on conventional clinical endpoints, such as functional capacity, hemodynamic parameters, and mortality, with variable incorporation of patient-centered outcomes, including measures of quality of life and symptoms, as well as laboratory biomarkers such as NT-proBNP.

Based on this overall landscape, the analysis conducted in the present study is structured according to the main therapeutic pathways investigated in PAH, as summarized in Fig. 4. Diagnosis

In 2025, only two studies registered on international platforms addressed the diagnostic aspects of PAH. One of these evaluated advanced imaging methods for pulmonary structural characterization, using computed tomography pulmonary angiography combined with quantitative analysis of the distal vasculature through functional respiratory imaging (FRI) (trial NCT06907693). The primary endpoint is based on the ratio between the vascular volumes of small and medium pulmonary vessels (BV5A/BV10A), with the aim of identifying early microvascular alterations associated with PAH.

The second study corresponds to a Brazilian national observational registry with a focus on the diagnostic journey of PAH, with assessments of the time to diagnosis, number of physicians consulted, prior misdiagnoses, and functional class at the time of diagnostic confirmation (PANDORA; ClinicalTrials.gov ID: NCT06998329).

The first emerging therapeutic axis involves modulation of the transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β)/BMP signaling pathway, which has been investigated in recent translational initiatives. Studies focusing on ROC-101 (NCT07175038), LTP001 (CTIS2024-513613-12-00), HS235 (NCT07143448), and sotatercept (NCT06939647), for example, share the objective of restoring the balance between the TGF-β/Activin–Nodal (SMAD2/3) and BMP/growth and differentiation factor (GDF) (SMAD1/5/8) branches of this pathway, as imbalance is considered a central disturbance in PAH pathophysiology [16–19]. Accumulating evidence indicates that mutations or functional dysfunctions in components of the BMP/TGF-β network, such as BMPR2, ACVRL1 (ALK1), and ENG, result in loss of BMP signaling with compensatory hyperactivation of pro-proliferative TGF-β-mediated pathways, favoring vascular remodeling, inflammation, and, downstream, right ventricular overload [18, 20].

In this context, interventions aimed at rebalancing cellular signaling, such as ActRIIA-Fc receptor traps, have been supported by preclinical evidence and early human data demonstrating a reduction of SMAD2/3-mediated signaling and restoration of the SMAD1/5/8 pathway, in association with modifications in pulmonary vascular remodeling [16–19]. This rationale is also reflected in recent studies of sotatercept in distinct populations, including treatment-naïve patients, as exemplified by the SIRIUS trial (NCT07266519), a phase 2 study evaluating the effect of the drug on pulmonary vascular resistance in individuals with newly diagnosed PAH. The pediatric MOONBEAM trial (NCT05587712), registered in 2022, further illustrates the extension of sotatercept-based investigation to younger populations.

The second emerging therapeutic axis is the metabolic–cardiorenal pathway, represented by three empagliflozin-based clinical trials registered across international trials platforms: CTIS2024-517009–99-00 (EmPAH), NCT06992440 (EmPATH), and IRCT20120520009801N18. These studies explore a rationale that remains incompletely established in PAH: that myocardial energy efficiency and the balance of glucose and lipid metabolism may modulate right ventricular (RV) performance and directly affect prognosis. In animal models, empagliflozin has been shown to attenuate vascular remodeling and RV hypertrophy through activation of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor γ (PPARγ) and inhibition of the γ-secretase–Notch3 axis [21]. At the population level, a retrospective propensity score–matched analysis involving more than 12,000 patients with PAH associated the use of sodium-glucose transport protein 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors with an absolute 10.4% reduction in all-cause mortality over 5 years [22]. In addition, recent pathophysiological evidence provides support to the premise that modulation of the PGC-1α/AMPK–PPAR pathways, which are also involved in adaptive responses to exercise, may restore RV metabolic flexibility and favor fatty acid oxidation and efficient ATP production [23]. The use of imaging-based and cardiopulmonary exercise testing endpoints in these protocols reflects the search for more integrative physiological measures and reinforces the potential of this pathway as a target for adjuvant therapies in PAH.

The third emerging therapeutic axis involves selective tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) targeting pathways such as the platelet-derived growth factor receptor (PDGFR), Src, and BCR-ABL pathways, which are implicated in cellular proliferation and vascular remodeling in PAH. Although this therapeutic strategy has historically been associated with safety and tolerability challenges—particularly highlighted by the results of the IMPRES study published in 2013, which evaluated imatinib in PAH [24]—preclinical evidence and early clinical data continue to support interest in modulation of these pathways, especially through approaches seeking greater molecular selectivity or reduced systemic exposure [15].

In 2025, three PAH studies evaluating TKIs are included in registries, reflecting a renewed interest in this therapeutic class through the investigation of different molecular targets and routes of administration, as well as a broad range of safety, hemodynamic, functional, structural, and patient-reported outcomes. One of these studies, a phase 2 clinical trial conducted in the UK (ISRCTN94603622; REPIPAH), investigates oral imatinib in patients with PAH and incorporates continuous hemodynamic monitoring using CardioMEMS-derived total pulmonary resistance, alongside functional, structural, biomarker, and quality-of-life endpoints. In parallel, a proof-of-concept study (NCT06917196) evaluates inhaled, targeted imatinib nanoformulations in PAH, primarily focusing on formulation development, characterization, and exploratory assessments of pulmonary distribution. The third study, an open-label phase 3 extension study registered in Japan (JPRN-jRCT2051240238), evaluates the long-term safety of seralutinib, an inhaled PDGFR-targeted TKI, in adults with PAH previously exposed to the drug, with treatment-emergent adverse events as the primary endpoint and 6-min walk distance and NT-proBNP as secondary outcomes.

Beyond the three main therapeutic axes described above, additional targets are being explored in studies registered in 2025 that involve complementary pathways of PAH pathophysiology. These approaches include inflammation, extracellular matrix remodeling, autonomic modulation, and strategies aimed at optimizing the endothelin and prostacyclin pathways [20].

Within the inflammation and extracellular matrix axis, early-phase studies evaluate interventions with a focus on safety and pharmacological characterization. The phase 2 ViTAL-PH study with APL-9796 (a monoclonal antibody blocking ZIP12 [25]; NCT06846554) includes additional exploratory endpoints, such as telemetry parameters, 6-min walk distance, NT-proBNP, and quality-of-life measures. The STOP-PAH study with artesunate (a semi-synthetic artemisinin derivative with anti-inflammatory and vasodilatory properties [26]; NCT06872112) and the study with PF-07868489 (a monoclonal antibody antagonizing BMP9 [27]; NCT07073820) focus primarily on safety endpoints, laboratory assessments, and pharmacokinetics.

Within the sympathetic and hormonal–metabolic axis, studies evaluate interventions aimed at systemic hormonal modulation and pharmacological characterization. The REVIDAH study (CTIS2025-521694-14-00), a phase 4 trial conducted in Spain, investigates calcifediol supplementation in patients with vitamin D deficiency, with the primary endpoint defined as the occurrence of clinical improvement without worsening, based on a composite of functional, hemodynamic, and laboratory parameters. In parallel, zamicastat (BIA5-1058), which is a selective peripheral dopamine β-hydroxylase inhibitor is evaluated in a phase 1 study assessing pharmacokinetics, safety, and tolerability when co-administered with furosemide ([28]; NCT07173738).

Among therapeutic pathways already established in PAH, the vasoregulatory and oxidative NO–soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC) axis remains prominent, as its dysfunction continues to be a central determinant of disease, associated with imbalance of cyclic guanosine monophosphate–protein kinase G (cGMP–PKG) signaling and progression of pulmonary vascular remodeling [29, 30]. In 2025, investigations on this pathway remained predominantly focused on established pharmacological strategies, including phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitors (PDE5i) and sGC stimulators. Studies involving riociguat and tadalafil were identified. An observational study included in the ChiCTR2500096733 registry evaluates the switch from phosphodiesterase inhibitors to riociguat in patients with pulmonary hypertension associated with congenital heart disease at intermediate–high risk. The ChiCTR2500110083 study is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind trial investigating the pharmacodynamic effects, safety, and pharmacokinetic profile of DDCI-01 compared with tadalafil in patients with PAH. Among studies involving tadalafil, the ORIENTATION trial (NCT06968962) compares the efficacy of sequential versus initial combination therapy with tadalafil and ambrisentan, while the COMMODITIES trial (NCT07245680) evaluates initial oral dual therapy with the same agents in patients with PAH and cardiovascular comorbidities.

As an element of incremental innovation, the introduction of a novel long-acting PDE5i, DDCI-01, was observed and evaluated in the ChiCTR2500110083 study. This compound is supported by previously published preclinical evidence demonstrating attenuation of pulmonary vascular remodeling, reduction of mean pulmonary arterial pressure, and improvement in RV functional parameters in experimental models, with a differentiated pharmacodynamic profile compared with that of conventional PDE5i [31]. In addition, early clinical data indicate a prolonged elimination pharmacokinetic profile, with a reported terminal half-life of approximately 35–40 h, supporting its characterization as a long-acting PDE5i [32].

The endothelin and prostacyclin pathways remain represented by studies evaluating clinical efficacy, therapeutic strategies, pharmacokinetics, and safety within established drug classes. Within the endothelin axis, studies involving ambrisentan include the ALEPH trial (NCT06987097), which evaluates its efficacy in patients with early-stage PAH characterized by low clinical risk, absence of prior treatment, and borderline hemodynamic abnormalities, and the COMMODITIES study (CTIS2023-509891-40-00), which investigates initial combination therapy strategies. Investigations involving bosentan include observational switch studies (NCT07013149) and bioequivalence trials (ChiCTR2500103552).

Within the prostacyclin axis, studies involving treprostinil include trials evaluating efficacy and hemodynamic and ventricular outcomes (JPRN-jRCT2031240628; NCT07177703), as well as expanded access programs (NCT06939647). The ACTRN12625000563460 study evaluates the pharmacokinetics and safety of the transdermal formulation TRX-248, while studies with selexipag focus on bioequivalence (NCT06932198).

Methodological Advances and Emerging Trends

The analysis of 54 pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) clinical trials mapped between January and December 2025 demonstrates a consistent methodological characteristics, with an expanded evaluative scope and greater incorporation of imaging techniques and patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs). Although traditional hemodynamic parameters served as the primary endpoint in only 13.0% of studies (7 of 54, these outcomes remain widely used as secondary or exploratory endpoints or as components of composite endpoints. Overall, an increasing use of multidimensional assessments is observed, integrating structural, functional, and patient-centered aspects, reflecting clinical research designs more closely aligned with the complexity of PAH and with a comprehensive evaluation of therapeutic impact.

Personalization and Expansion of Endpoints

A subset of interventional trials registered in 2025 reorganized the assessment of therapeutic response through increased use of composite endpoints and integrated metrics, replacing isolated outcomes. This approach reflects a increasing emphasis on prioritizing risk stratification and the combination of clinical and functional parameters for longitudinal disease monitoring [13, 14, 33].

This concept is exemplified by the COMMODITIES trial (NCT07245680), a phase 3, randomized, interventional study that adopts a strategy previously evaluated in the AMBITION trial [34], based on the use of ambrisentan, tadalafil, or their combination at initiation of therapy. The study is conducted in a specific population of patients with PAH and cardiovascular comorbidities. The primary endpoint consists of risk profile assessment using a noninvasive four-stratum risk stratification method, while secondary endpoints include functional and hemodynamic parameters and the occurrence of clinical events; taken together, these endpoints enable a broader evaluation of the impact of initial combination therapy.

In observational studies, risk stratification is employed for methodological or evaluative purposes. The ChiCTR2500100857 study analyzes an adaptation of the four-stratum model in a retrospective Chinese cohort, whereas the NCT07013149 study longitudinally evaluates the impact of switching endothelin receptor antagonists from ambrisentan to bosentan on risk category during follow-up.

In parallel with the reorganization of clinical endpoints, the incorporation of patient-centered outcomes is also observed in studies registered in 2025. Patient-reported outcome measures and quality-of-life assessments were included in 17 of the 54 analyzed trials (approx. 31%), indicating an expansion of the evaluative scope beyond traditional clinical and functional parameters. Among PAH-specific questionnaires, emPHasis-10 was the most frequently used instrument, identified in eight studies; this questionnaire focuses on assessing the functional and psychosocial impact of the disease [35]. The PAH-SYMPACT (Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension-Symptoms and Impact) questionnaire, employed in three studies, was mainly used in educational or behavioral contexts, enabling measurement of symptoms and their impact on daily activities from the patient’s perspective [36]. The CAMPHOR (Cambridge Pulmonary Hypertension Outcome Review) outcome measure was identified in two studies, with emphasis on evaluating quality of life and patient experience over follow-up [37].

In addition to disease-specific instruments, generic quality-of-life measures, notably the EQ-5D-5L (European Quality of Life 5 Dimensions 5 Level), were also incorporated in three studies, allowing cross-contextual comparisons of well-being and disease burden across different clinical settings [38, 39]. Complementarily, some protocols explored dimensions related to health literacy and disease knowledge, such as the SAVOIR study (NCT07120789), which investigates the relationship between PAH knowledge, treatment adherence, and quality of life.

Overall, the incorporation of patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) reflects an expansion of the metrics used to assess therapeutic response, integrating functional, psychosocial, and patient experience dimensions into clinical follow-up [14, 15, 36, 37].

Integration of Imaging and Biomarkers

The incorporation of cardiovascular imaging techniques and structural assessments of the RV has occurred selectively in recent PAH clinical trials, particularly in early-phase interventional or mechanistic studies. In these protocols, parameters derived from advanced echocardiography and cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (CMR) are used as primary or secondary endpoints, complementing traditional clinical and functional measures [14, 40]. This trend reflects an expansion of the set of evaluated outcomes, historically centered on hemodynamic parameters, to include more sensitive structural and functional measures of cardiopulmonary adaptation—such as RV volumes and function assessed by CMR—while pulmonary vascular resistance remains the central hemodynamic parameter.

This rationale is illustrated by studies involving empagliflozin, such as EmPAH (CTIS2024-517009-99-00) and EmPATH (NCT06992440), in which echocardiographic and CMR metrics focusing on RV function constitute the central endpoints, while functional capacity, hemodynamic parameters, biomarkers, and patient-centered measures are evaluated as secondary endpoints. A similar approach is observed in other recent interventional trials, including a phase 3 study conducted in Iran (IRCT20120520009801N18), which integrates echocardiography, invasive hemodynamics, and functional capacity in the assessment of therapeutic response to empagliflozin. In this respect, the NCT07177703 trial with treprostinil uses cardiac magnetic resonance imaging to evaluate RV stroke volume as a structural endpoint in patients with intermediate-risk PAH.

In studies focused on established therapies, cardiac imaging is incorporated mainly in secondary or exploratory analyses. The COMMODITIES trial (CTIS2023-509891-40-00) combines noninvasive risk stratification with echocardiographic, hemodynamic, and functional parameters, whereas the ALEPH study (NCT06987097) integrates echocardiographic measures and invasive hemodynamics into the evaluation of clinical progression in patients with low-risk PAH.

Observational and real-world protocols, including a study evaluating pulmonary artery denervation (PADN) (NCT07039604), and recent methodological investigations, including the integration of the VExUS score into risk stratification (NCT07266012), further reinforce the use of cardiovascular imaging for physiological characterization of treatment response and prognostic refinement, without formal validation of these parameters as independent surrogate endpoints.

Taken together, the integration of imaging parameters, hemodynamics, and serum biomarkers reflects the progressive adoption of multiparametric evaluation models in PAH, similar to those used in other chronic cardiovascular conditions such as heart failure and cardiomyopathies. However, heterogeneity in the selection, definition, and hierarchical prioritization of these parameters across protocols limits their comparability and broader applicability. Methodological standardization remains a prerequisite for the validation of structural endpoints as potential surrogates and for the consolidation of integrated metrics of clinical benefit [14, 40, 41].

Digitalization and Continuous Data

The incorporation of digital technologies has emerged as a distinct methodological trend in PAH research. In 2025, five registered studies (approx. 9% of the 54 analyzed trials) explicitly integrated digital health strategies, remote monitoring, or continuous data collection as central components of the study design.

Among these, REPIPAH (ISRCTN94603622) uses continuous hemodynamic monitoring through the CardioMEMS device, enabling serial assessment of pulmonary vascular resistance in the outpatient setting. Similarly, the ViTAL-PH (NCT06846554) study incorporates physiological telemetry and the remote collection of functional and quality-of-life data throughout thefollow-up. The Nor-SScCare (NCT06865118) study adopts a structured home-monitoring model that combines functional testing, wearable sensors, and systematic assessment of adherence, usability, and quality of life. In another approach, the NCT06941441 study evaluates the feasibility of remotely performed 6-min walk testing, combined with accelerometry and actigraphy-derived continuous physical activity metrics, potentially enabling remote and more granular assessment of functional status in PAH. The OPENLINE (NCT06850792) study integrates digital platforms for longitudinal monitoring of treatment adherence, symptoms, risk stratification, and quality of life using validated instruments.

These initiatives are situated within a broader context of clinical research digitalization, and the results are supported by external evidence demonstrating the technical feasibility and reproducibility of wearable sensors, biosensors, and remote monitoring strategies for capturing physiological, functional, and behavioral parameters in cardiovascular and chronic diseases [42, 43]. In addition, the findings from systematic reviews and meta-analyses indicate that structured telemedicine interventions, particularly when combined with education and systematic feedback, are associated with improvements in treatment adherence and health literacy, although methodological heterogeneity across studies is reported [44].

Overall, the studies registered in 2025 indicate a increasing use of continuous data rather than isolated point-in-time assessments, which has the potential to enhance monitoring of therapeutic response and treatment adherence.

Open-Label Extension Studies

In 2025, two clinical trials (approx. 3.6%) were registered as open-label extension studies. These include JPRN-jRCT2051240238, which evaluates the long-term safety of seralutinib in patients previously exposed to treatment, and CTIS2025-521970-33-00, which investigates the long-term safety and tolerability of sotatercept in a previously treated population.

Although underrepresented among studies registered during the evaluated period, open-label extension trials play a relevant role in assessing long-term safety, durability of therapeutic effect, and maintenance of clinical benefit in chronic PAH interventions [45, 46].

Persistent Gaps

Although the ideal clinical endpoint in pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) trials is mortality, its use as a primary endpoint is severely constrained by the rarity of the disease, which poses substantial methodological challenges, including the need for very large sample sizes and prolonged follow-up to achieve adequate statistical power. Consequently, mortality has historically been replaced by surrogate endpoints, most notably invasive hemodynamic parameters and the 6-min walk distance, which became widely adopted as measures of treatment efficacy. From 2015 onward, the emergence and consolidation of composite clinical endpoints—such as time to clinical worsening or time to first morbidity–mortality event—represented an important methodological advance, enhancing the clinical relevance and robustness of clinical trials. However, this refinement has also increased operational complexity, requiring larger populations, longer follow-up periods, and, as a result, substantially higher study costs.

In this context, despite the methodological advances observed up to 2025, the PAH research landscape continues to face a critical gap in terms of the development and formal validation of reliable surrogate endpoints. Invasive hemodynamic parameters are still recurrently used as secondary endpoints, components of composite endpoints, or mechanistic markers; however, their routine use is limited by the fact that these measures are costly, resource-intensive, and operationally complex, requiring specialized infrastructure and expertise, which restricts their scalability and feasibility, particularly in large multicenter or real-world studies. Addressing this gap will be essential to enable more efficient, accessible, and methodologically robust clinical trials in the future of pulmonary hypertension research.

Significant gaps remain in the representativeness of populations evaluated in registered studies. Among the 54 studies analyzed, only three included individuals younger than 18 years, all of which were observational or non-pharmacological in nature: a pediatric cardiac rehabilitation study based on telehealth (NCT06819059), registered retrospectively after completion and already published [47]; a registry focused on phenotyping and prognosis in patients with PAH and cardiopulmonary comorbidities (JPRN-UMIN000056065); and a Chinese registry addressing the diagnosis and management of pulmonary vascular diseases with inclusion starting at 14 years of age (NCT06954727). No interventional pharmacological trials were specifically designed for pediatric populations.

Similarly, specific clinical subgroups remain underrepresented in therapeutic studies. In 2025, observational protocols were identified that focused on populations with congenital heart disease (ChiCTR2500096733); connective tissue diseases such as systemic sclerosis (NCT06865118); diabetes mellitus associated with PAH (NCT07082933); vasculitis with pulmonary involvement (NCT07184814); and environmental exposures related to air pollution in pregnant women with pulmonary hypertension (NCT07164157). In addition, the EXPOSPAH study (NCT07172334) is an observational registry evaluating the exposome in PAH and chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH), combining environmental biomarkers in hair and urine with exposure questionnaires to compare environmental profiles between groups.

Four studies include populations compatible with pulmonary hypertension associated with interstitial lung disease (group 3). NCT07175038 (ROCSTAR) is a phase 2a study evaluating ROC-101, an oral molecule described as a ROCK1/ROCK2 inhibitor, administered in addition to standard of care for 24 weeks, with a long-term extension. NCT06917196 (PROMPTLY) is a proof-of-concept, single-center study (Pavia, Italy) investigating an inhaled formulation of imatinib (nanoformulation/“targeted” imatinib; XHALIP) in the context of pulmonary hypertension associated with interstitial or post-inflammatory pulmonary fibrosis, with the aim to generate exploratory signals of feasibility, safety, and potential effects on cardiorespiratory parameters.

In parallel, studies evaluating inhaled prostacyclin analogs, such as NCT06939647 and JPRN-jRCT2031240628, are investigating treprostinil in populations with overlap between PAH and interstitial lung disease, reflecting growing interest in modulating vasoreactivity and vascular remodeling in mixed phenotypes.

Although these studies expand current understanding of clinical, environmental, and prognostic determinants of the disease, most adopt observational or exploratory designs, focusing on phenotypic characterization, survival outcomes, remote monitoring, or risk assessment, without testing specific therapeutic strategies. As a result, the generated evidence remains limited in guiding treatment decisions in non-classical populations, restricting extrapolation of findings from interventional trials to scenarios with greater clinical heterogeneity.

From a geographic perspective, the participation of Latin America in multinational trials is observed; however, only seven studies (approx. 13%) registered in 2025 include countries from this region, predominantly Brazil, with no protocols conducted exclusively in other Latin American countries. No registered studies conducted on the African continent were identified, reinforcing the concentration of PAH clinical research in high-income countries.

These findings indicate that, despite observed methodological progress, relevant gaps persist in geographic equity, population diversity, and validation of surrogate endpoints. Addressing these limitations will be essential to ensure that advances in trial design, risk stratification, and therapeutic innovation translate into consistent and sustainable clinical benefits throughout patient follow-up.

Perspectives and Implications for Patients

The set of PAH clinical trials registered on international platforms between January and December 2025 reflects current methodological approaches within the field. Investigations on novel therapeutic pathways, the use of composite and multiparametric endpoints, and the incorporation of digital technologies in a subset of protocols indicate an expansion of evaluative approaches. This landscape points toward an increasing inclusion of structural, functional, and patient-reported measures, beyond isolated functional outcomes.

From a funding perspective, only a fraction of trials registered in 2025 received direct pharmaceutical industry sponsorship (8/54 studies; approx. 15%), with the predominance of such funding being for interventional studies. Most protocols were sponsored by universities or hospitals (32 studies; approx. 59%), while funding in 14 of the registered studies (approx. 26%) was either not clearly specified or involved combinations of institutional sources. This profile highlights the predominance of academic initiatives in PAH clinical research, with industry participation concentrated in specific stages of therapeutic development. However, indirect forms of industry support to academic or hospital-based studies cannot be reliably identified through the information available in trial registration platforms.

Among the 54 analyzed studies, 18 had an observational design (33.3%). Among interventional trials, early and intermediate phases of development predominated, consistent with a research landscape in which mechanistic hypotheses, emerging interventions, and follow-up models are still being tested prior to broader clinical application.

From a clinical standpoint, this profile is reflected in endpoint design. A proportion of interventional studies incorporated composite endpoints combining functional, hemodynamic, structural, and quality-of-life measures, thereby expanding assessment of therapeutic response beyond isolated outcomes. These protocols include indicators of clinical stability, cardiac remodeling, and disease impact on daily life during follow-up.

For patients, the implications are twofold: on one hand, new therapeutic strategies move closer to being implemented in clinical practice, particularly with the advancement of TGF-β modulators and metabolic therapies; on the other hand, the experience of research participation becomes more integrated, with increasing use of digital platforms, telemonitoring, and instruments that capture the patient’s own perspective.

More than ever, the design of future trials will need to balance scientific rigor, clinical relevance, and global representativeness, so that the promise of innovation translates into tangible and equitable impact on the lives of people with PAH.

Conclusion

The integrated analysis of studies registered on international platforms throughout 2025 describes a scenario of stability in the global volume of trials, accompanied by diversification in the therapeutic targets investigated, the endpoints adopted, and the assessment tools employed. As this review focuses specifically on trials registered in 2025, these observations should be interpreted as a cross-sectional overview rather than a formal comparison with previous years. An expansion in the use of strategies targeting the TGF-β/BMP pathways, the metabolic axis, and modulation through tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) is observed, along with the incorporation of composite endpoints integrating imaging, hemodynamic parameters, and biomarkers. In parallel, tools such as cardiac magnetic resonance imaging, advanced echocardiography, patient-reported outcome instruments, and digital data collection are being used with increasing frequency. Together, these elements indicate an expansion of therapeutic response assessment beyond isolated vasodilatory control, incorporating structural and functional parameters of the right ventricle and dimensions related to patient experience.

From a clinical perspective, phase II–III studies incorporate endpoints integrating structural imaging parameters, serum biomarkers, and patient-reported measures, bringing trial design closer to real-world clinical practice contexts. The inclusion of remote monitoring and continuous data allows longitudinal assessment of therapeutic response and treatment adherence, while open-label extensions and adaptive designs contribute to characterization of durability of effect in chronic therapies. Interpretation of these findings, however, remains conditioned by population representativeness and heterogeneity of the metrics employed.

In summary, data from 2025 indicate the coexistence of methodological advances with persistent limitations related to endpoint standardization and inclusion of diverse populations.

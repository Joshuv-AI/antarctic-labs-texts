Encircling Skin Flap Technique for the Reconstruction of Superficial Philtrum Defects in the Upper Lip
Authors: Siqi Huang, Bo Yu, Ban-Cheng Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The philtrum is the most important aesthetic unit of the upper lip. For the repair of defects in philtrum, it is a difficult challenge for surgeons. A 35-year-old male presented with a round black papule, approximately 1 cm in diameter, on his philtrum that had persisted for over 30 years. The diagnosis is nevus. Surgical resection was performed to remove the papule, reaching the subcutaneous fat layer. Subsequently, a circular defect of approximately 1 cm in diameter was evident. We employed an encircling skin flap technique. Surgical resection achieved satisfactory results. Local skin flap is the preferred method to repair the skin and soft tissue defect in the Middle Region of human body.

Introduction

The philtrum, a critical aesthetic component of the upper lip, encompasses the philtral crests extending laterally from the lip crest towards the nasal columella sides, and the Cupid’s bow—presenting an m-shaped arched contour. This region, being relatively small and endowed with a unique tissue structure, poses unique challenges when it comes to repairing skin and soft tissue defects.

Various repair techniques have been employed to address these defects. Direct closure is one such method, but its application is limited to small defects with a transverse diameter of less than 0.2 cm. Any larger defects can result in significant displacement of adjacent tissue.[1] Skin grafting offers another avenue, but it has its own setbacks. For instance, the grafted skin may differ in colour and texture from the surrounding tissue, and if the graft is too thin, it can lead to pitting deformity in deeper defects. Additionally, for male patients, the absence of facial hair can affect aesthetics. Not to mention, the need to sacrifice donor site skin results in a secondary wound.[23]

Therefore, local skin flaps have become the preferred method for repairing skin and soft tissue defects in the philtrum region. Among these flaps, the encircling skin flap technique has emerged as a particularly effective and aesthetically pleasing option. This technique not only offers a durable repair but also preserves the functional and aesthetic integrity of the upper lip.

In the following sections, we delve deeper into the encircling skin flap technique, discussing its principles, surgical application, and the challenges it addresses in the realm of dermatologic surgery.

Surgical technique

Lateral views will also confirm the normal appearance of the philtrum [Figure 1k and l]. Ten months post-operatively, patients typically report high satisfaction with the surgical outcome, noting that the scar has nearly disappeared and the wound has healed well [Figure 2a and b].

Discussion

In the surgical repair of defects in the philtrum, surgeons encounter a unique challenge. Various repair techniques have been described, including O-t plasty, bilateral peri-alar advancement flap for midline upper lip defect closure,[4] O to z reconstruction,[5] and the use of island pedicle flaps.[6] However, each of these methods has the potential to disrupt the normal structural integrity of the philtrum, possibly leading to cosmetically unacceptable outcomes. Furthermore, they may affect the facial beard growth, which is an important factor in male patients’ satisfaction.

The hairy preauricular free flap approach, while allowing for the restoration of a bearded area,[7] is technically demanding and may result in donor site scarring. Additionally, there are concerns regarding flap survival.

The encircling skin flap technique described in this paper offers several advantages. Firstly, it belongs to the category of local skin flaps, which allows for the reduction of the wound surface area through folding and suturing techniques. This not only reduces the amount of tissue that needs to heal by secondary intention but also accelerates the healing process. Secondly, by encircling the flap without severing the pedicle, blood flow and nutrition supply to the flap are maintained, reducing the risk of flap necrosis. This is particularly beneficial in areas where vascularity may be compromised. Thirdly, compared to simple excision and secondary healing of pigmented nevi, the encircling flap technique significantly reduces the defect area undergoing secondary healing. This, in turn, reduces the risk of scar contracture and other cosmetically undesirable outcomes. Lastly, this technique can be performed without significantly altering the fundamental structure of the philtrum. For male patients, this is particularly advantageous as it preserves hair follicles, allowing for normal beard growth after healing. This is a crucial factor in ensuring patient satisfaction and maintaining a natural-looking appearance.

The advantage of secondary healing has been shown in some cases. Successful treatment of intradermal nevi in the eyebrow area using shear surgery in combination with electrocautery and curettage has been reported.[8] Post-surgery, patients exhibited visible depressions at the wound sites, but these gradually shallowed and eventually blended with the surrounding skin, leaving no notable indentations. Intradermal nevi consist of epidermal structures nestled within the dermis, encapsulated by the basement membrane. During melanocyte proliferation, these nests expand and exert pressure on adjacent tissues, causing deformation and shortening of elastic fibres. Provided the lesional tissue can be fully excised without compromising surrounding healthy tissue within a certain deformation space, the surrounding connective tissue can then fill the void created by the elastic fibre contraction. The minimal scarring observed in patients post-surgery may be attributed to limited damage to elastic fibres and subsequent regenerative capabilities of the surrounding connective tissue.[8]

The encircling skin flap technique combined with a small area of secondary healing solves the problem of skin repair in a limited space. While not changing the structure and retaining hair, so the removal of benign tumours on the eyebrow is also suitable. In the skin structure with a relatively large residual skin area around the skin lesion, such as the skin on the back and abdomen, it is the most preferred surgical choice to completely close the skin defect after removing the skin lesion, which can reduce the risk of infection during the secondary wound healing.

In summary, the encircling skin flap technique offers a viable and effective solution for repairing defects in the philtrum, especially in male patients. It combines the benefits of local flaps with the preservation of facial hair growth, making it a valuable addition to the surgical armamentarium.

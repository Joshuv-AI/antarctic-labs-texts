Oxymatrine possibly inhibits differentiation and maturation of oligodendrocytes in the remyelination of the toxin-induced demyelination animal model
Authors: Mei Li, Fei Huang, Jian Song, Renzhang Liang, Zhongwei Zhou, Yinglan Lin, Lili Hu, Jinghai Gu, Hua Yang, Shaozhang Hou, Huisheng Ma, Peng Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Multiple sclerosis (MS) is characterized by demyelination. In demyelinated MS lesions, the recruitment, differentiation, and maturation of oligodendrocyte progenitor cells (OPCs) into oligodendrocytes (OLGs) are inhibited, leading to remyelination failure. Promoting OLG differentiation and maturation has emerged as a promising strategy for enhancing remyelination in MS. Here, we investigated whether Oxymatrine (OMT), a pharmacologically active compound derived from the traditional Chinese herb Sophora flavescens, regulates OLG differentiation and maturation, and remyelination. In this study, OMT was applied to OLG cultures in vitro and injected into the corpus callosum of a toxin-induced demyelination rat model in vivo. Our results showed that OMT potentially inhibited OLG maturation in vitro. Furthermore, in the demyelination model, remyelination was possibly impaired, and OLG maturation was likely suppressed by OMT, which possibly is due to OMT-induced apoptosis in OLGs. Although OMT possibly impairs remyelination in this model, the exploration of Chinese herbal compounds for MS therapy remains a novel and promising field for future interventions. These findings underscore the complexity of herbal pharmacology and suggest that compounds like OMT may exert dualistic effects on neurodegeneration depending on disease stage, cellular targets, or microenvironmental cues, warranting cautious translational exploration.

Introduction

Multiple sclerosis (MS) is an autoimmune disease of the central nervous system (CNS), characterized by chronic inflammation and demyelination. Although remyelination occurs during the early stages of MS, it often fails as the disease progresses, resulting in chronic demyelinating plaques and axonal degeneration (Frischer et al. 2015). Oligodendrocyte progenitor cells (OPCs) are responsible for differentiating and maturing into oligodendrocytes (OLs), which are essential for myelin regeneration in the CNS (Jakovcevski & Zecevic, 2005). In MS lesions, both intrinsic and extrinsic inhibitory factors suppress OLG differentiation and maturation, thereby impairing remyelination (Dulamea 2017; Goldschmidt et al. 2009; Gruchot et al. 2019). Recent studies have shown that enhancing OLG differentiation and maturation in MS lesions can help mitigate disease progression (Jadasz et al. 2013). Therefore, promoting OLG differentiation and maturation is considered a promising therapeutic strategy for remyelination in MS.

Chinese herbal medicine has recently gained attention as a potential treatment for neurological disorders (Kong et al. 2024; Lu et al. 2024). Sophora flavescens Ait, a traditional Chinese medicinal plant, exhibits a range of pharmacological properties including antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, anti-tumor, and anti-arrhythmic effects (Du et al. 2023; Ma et al. 2018). Oxymatrine (OMT), a tetracyclic quinolizidine alkaloid, is one of its major active components (Zheng et al. 2014). OMT has long been used for its anti-inflammatory, anti-tumor, antioxidant, and anti-apoptotic properties (Ling et al. 2011; Zhang et al. 2014). Recent research has also revealed its neuroprotective effects in neurological disorders, such as ischemic brain injury. OMT modulates pathways including TLR4, JAK/STAT, and NF-κB (Guzman et al. 2013; Lu et al. 2016), and suppresses pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 (Dong et al. 2013; Mao et al. 2012; Wen et al. 2014). Additionally, it attenuates oxidative stress via the NF-κB pathway (Cheignon et al. 2018; Luo et al. 2014). Moreover, OMT has shown potential in reducing neurotoxicity in primary neuronal cultures and in improving memory deficits in rats (Dong et al. 2019). It has also been reported to promote retinal ganglion cell (RGC) survival and axon regeneration in glaucoma models by inhibiting ROCK activity.

In MS, chronic inflammation impairs OLG maturation and remyelination. The expression of various pro-inflammatory cytokines correlates with demyelination, and their accumulation in MS lesions contributes to OLG death (Eng et al. 1996). Several of these cytokines are directly cytotoxic to OLGs. Consequently, under chronic inflammatory conditions, OLG differentiation and maturation are arrested (Elbaz and Popko 2019; Zveik et al. 2024). However, the effects of OMT on remyelination in MS remain largely unknown. Given OMT’s properties, we investigated its role in a demyelinated animal model. Our study showed that OMT potentially inhibited OLG differentiation and maturation in vitro. Moreover, in the toxin-induced demyelination rat model, OMT possibly impaired remyelination and suppressed OLG maturation, likely due to OMT-induced apoptosis in OLGs. Although OMT did not facilitate remyelination in the current study, the therapeutic potential of Chinese herbal medicine in MS remains a promising avenue for future research.

Materials and methods

Primary cell culture

Primary cell cultures were performed as previously described with minor modifications (Bsibsi et al. 2012). Briefly, single-cell suspensions were obtained from the forebrains of 1–2-day-old Sprague Dawley rats (SD, Laboratory Animal Center of Ningxia Medical University, Ningxia, China) by mechanical and enzymatic dissociation. After culturing for 10–12 days in poly-L-lysine (PLL, 5 mg/ml, Table 1)-coated tissue culture flasks, microglia and OPCs were sequentially detached by orbital shaking. Microglia were removed by shaking at 150 rpm for 2 h, followed by overnight shaking at 150 rpm to isolate OPCs (Bsibsi et al. 2012). For maturation assays, OPCs were seeded at a density of 3.0 × 10⁴ cells per well on 24-well plates containing 14 × 14 mm glass coverslips (Table 2). Cells were cultured in NBT medium consisting of 96% Neurobasal medium (Table 1), 2% B27 (Table 1), 1% Glutamine (Table 1), 1% Penicillin-Streptomycin (Table 1), and 0.02% T3 (Table 1, Maier et al. 2005), supplemented with FGF-2 (10 ng/ml; Table 1) and PDGF-AA (10 ng/ml; Table 1) for 2 days. To induce OLG maturation, growth factors were withdrawn, and cells were cultured in NBT medium alone for 3 days. Finally, coverslips were mounted with DAPI-containing mounting medium (Table 1) and analyzed using a fluorescence microscope (Table 2). Oligodendrocytes were identified based on morphology, and at least 250 cells were manually scored as MBP-positive or MBP-negative. Lysolecithin-induced local demyelination animal model

As previously described, 9-week-old female Sprague Dawley rats (250–270 g; SD, Laboratory Animal Center of Ningxia Medical University) were used to induce focal demyelination via stereotactic injection of 1% lysolecithin (Table 1) into the corpus callosum. Rats were anesthetized with gaseous isoflurane (Table 1) and secured in a stereotactic frame after shaving the scalp. Coordinates used were: 1 mm lateral to the midline, 1.8 mm anterior to bregma, and 4 mm ventral. After local disinfection with iodophor, the skull was drilled to create a 0.8 mm burr hole. Using a Hamilton syringe, 1 µL of 1% lysolecithin was injected into the corpus callosum for over 5 min. The wound was disinfected and sutured (4 − 0). Isoflurane (1–2%) was used postoperatively for analgesia. Ten days later, the same coordinates were used to inject 0.5 µL of either saline or OMT (100umol/L). To terminate the experiment, rats were deeply anesthetized with 5% isoflurane and decapitated. Brains were collected on ice, fixed in 4% PFA for 72 h, and dehydrated in 20–30% sucrose solution. Following cryo-embedding, brains were sectioned at 18 μm thickness. A minimum of three animals were included in each treatment group. All procedures were approved by the Ethics Committee of Ningxia Medical University (Ethics Approval Number: 2024–3423, Ningxia, China).

Sudan black B staining

Cryosections were incubated in 2.5 mg/ml Sudan Black B solution (Table 1) for 5 min, followed by triple washing in 70% ethanol. Sections were then rinsed in distilled water to stop staining and examined using an optical microscope.

Immunohistochemistry

Sections were permeabilized with 1% Triton X-100 (Table 1) at 37 °C for 20 min and blocked with HBSS containing 1 mM HEPES (Table 1), 1% horse serum (Table 1), 10% goat serum (Table 1), 0.1% BSA (Table 1), and 0.25% Triton X-100 for 1 h at 37 °C. Sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with a primary antibody (Table 3) in blocking buffer, followed by Alexa-conjugated secondary antibodies (Table 3) overnight at 4 °C. After three washes in 0.1% TBST, sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with another primary antibody (Table 3) and subsequently with the respective Alexa-conjugated secondary antibodies (Table 3). Nuclei were stained with DAPI-containing mounting medium and imaged using a high-resolution laser confocal microscope (Table 2).

Apoptosis assay

Sections were permeabilized with 1% Triton X-100 at 37 °C for 20 min and blocked with HBSS containing 1 mM HEPES, 1% horse serum, 10% goat serum, 0.1% BSA, and 0.25% Triton X-100 for 1 h at 37 °C. Primary antibody (Table 3) against NKX6.2 was applied in blocking buffer overnight at 4 °C, followed by a 2-hour incubation with appropriate Alexa-conjugated secondary antibodies (Table 3). After triple washing with TBST, apoptosis was assessed using a One-Step TUNEL Apoptosis Staining Kit (Table 1), according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Statistics

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0.1 and IBM SPSS Statistics 27. All data are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). One-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test was used to assess statistical significance. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Oxymatrine possibly inhibits differentiation and maturation of OLGs in vitro

To investigate the role of OMT (Fig. 1) in myelination, we first examined its effect on OLG differentiation and maturation in primary OPC cultures grown on PLL-coated surfaces. OPCs were derived from mixed glial cultures of neonatal rat forebrains (Fig. 2A). Cells were maintained with PDGF-AA and FGF-2 for 2 days, followed by incubation with OMT at various concentrations (50 µmol/L, 100 µmol/L, 400 µmol/L, and 800 µmol/L) for 3 days. MBP expression and the formation of elaborate myelin membranes were used as indicators of OLG differentiation and maturation, respectively (Fig. 2A). Accordingly, the percentages of MBP-positive cells and cells bearing elaborate myelin membranes were quantified (Qin et al. 2017; Stoffels et al. 2013; Wang et al. 2018). Consistent with previous findings (Qin et al. 2017; Stoffels et al. 2013; Wang et al. 2018), OLGs cultured on PLL naturally differentiate and form myelin-like membranes (Fig. 2A, B). Compared to controls, addition with 50 µmol/L and 100 µmol/L OMT resulted in an approximately 25% reduction in MBP-positive OLGs. In cultures treated with higher concentrations (400 µmol/L and 800 µmol/L), the proportion of MBP-positive cells was markedly reduced by approximately 40%, compared to control (Fig. 2C). In parallel, a significant decrease in the number of myelin membrane–forming cells was observed at 50 µmol/L, 400 µmol/L, and 800 µmol/L OMT, compared to control (Fig. 2B, D). Oxymatrine possibly fails to promote remyelination likely by inducing the apoptosis of OLGs to prevent maturation of OLGs in a toxin-induced animal model

Focal demyelination in the rat corpus callosum was induced by a single stereotactic injection of 1% lysolecithin. After 10 days post-lesion (DPL), spontaneous remyelination typically ensues, becoming robust by 28 DPL. To assess the effect of OMT on remyelination, OMT was intralesionally administered at 0 DPL, and samples were examined at 5 DPL (Fig. 3A). Sudan Black B staining revealed reduced remyelination in the OMT-treated group compared with controls and saline-injected controls (Fig. 3B, C). Immunohistochemical analysis showed that the percentages of NKX6.2-positive (oligodendrocyte lineage marker) and CC1-positive (mature oligodendrocyte marker) cells were significantly decreased in OMT-administrated lesions compared to controls (Fig. 3B, D, E). This reduction possibly suggests that OMT may impair both OLGs’ survival and their maturation. Additionally, OMT addition appeared to increase apoptosis among oligodendrocytes, as evidenced by the elevated number of NKX6.2-positive cells co-labeled with apoptotic markers compared to saline-treated controls (Fig. 4B, C). Discussion

Remyelination failure is a major contributor to disease progression in MS. Several studies have demonstrated that Chinese herbal medicine may offer therapeutic benefits for MS (Guo et al. 2023; Sun et al. 2019; Yang et al. 2023). OMT, a bioactive compound derived from Sophora flavescens, has been reported to suppress inflammation and oxidative stress and to protect neurons in ischemic injury models (Lan et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2020). In the context of MS, chronic inflammation disrupts OLG differentiation and maturation, thereby impairing remyelination. In demyelinated lesions, pro-inflammatory cytokines contribute to OLG cytotoxicity and death. Given OMT’s potent anti-inflammatory properties, this study aimed to evaluate its potential effect on remyelination. Contrary to expectations, our findings indicate that OMT possibly inhibits OLG maturation and remyelination, potentially through the induction of OLG apoptosis. Specifically, primary OLG cultures with OMT showed the reduced numbers of MBP+ cells and MBP+ cells with myelin membrane formation compared with control, while in a toxin-induced demyelination model, OMT administration led to decreased numbers of NKX6.2⁺ and CC1⁺ cells and increased apoptotic markers in OLGs.

In previous studies, intraperitoneal administration of OMT in experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE) models has been shown to prevent demyelination, potentially through modulation of the gut microbiota (Zhang et al. 2022). OMT has also been reported to delay EAE onset and modulate immune responses, including T-cell regulation via microbiome alterations (Liu et al. 2014; Blais et al. 2021; Mangalam et al. 2017). Furthermore, OMT suppresses pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α (Dong et al. 2013; Mao et al. 2012; Wen et al. 2014). Surprisingly, despite these anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective effects, the current study reveals that OMT may exert cytotoxic effects on OLGs under certain conditions, impairing remyelination in vivo and maturation in vitro. One possible explanation is that OMT exhibits context-dependent effects. For example, previous literature has reported both protective and toxic outcomes of OMT in liver and nervous system tissues (Li et al. 2021; You et al. 2020), a duality that aligns with the traditional Chinese medicine principle of “yin and yang.” As stated in classical Chinese medical theory: “A toxic medicine, if properly used, can be harmless.” Notably, OMT has been associated with intestinal toxicity in healthy mice, but not in EAE models, suggesting differential tolerability based on disease context.

In present study, we used the toxin-induced demyelinated animal model and primary OLG cultures to show that OMT may inhibit OLG differentiation and maturation both in vitro and in vivo. Furthermore, OMT appears to impair remyelination by possibly inducing OLG apoptosis in the toxin-induced demyelinated animal model. OMT is known to have multiple biofunctions, including suppression of inflammation and oxidative stress, modulation of gut microbiota and immune responses, and cytotoxic effects. These properties may regulate the inflammatory microenvironment and regulate OLG proliferation. However, in the present study, we only observed OLG apoptosis in vivo. The broader biofunctions of OMT and the signaling pathway mediating OLGs apoptosis remain undetermined and require further investigation. Moreover, only three animal models were included in each research in vivo group, which limits the statistical robustness and statistical analysis of the findings, although this limitation of sample size was considered necessary to adhere to animal welfare principles. Despite these limitations, immunofluorescent analyses of in invitro and in vivo model consistently shown that OMT possibly inhibits differentiation and maturation of OLGs. Future studies should address the mentioned limitations to strengthen the validity of the findings.

Taken together, our results suggest that while OMT has shown immunomodulatory benefits in EAE models, its role in remyelination—particularly in toxin-induced demyelination—may be detrimental, potentially through the induction of OLGs apoptosis and inhibition of their maturation.

Conclusion

Although our current study indicates that OMT may impair OLG maturation and remyelination, likely by inducing OLG apoptosis in a toxin-induced demyelinated animal model, prior evidence supports its beneficial effects in EAE models, where systemic administration delays disease progression and prevents demyelination. In the present study, however, we only observed OLG apoptosis in vivo. The broader biofunctions of OMT in preventing OLG maturation, and the signaling pathways mediating OLGs apoptosis, remain undetermined, which need further investigation. Moreover, only three animal models are included in each research group, limiting statistical robustness and statistical analysis, but these limitations were considered necessary to adhere to animal welfare principles. Despite these limitations, immunofluorescent analyses of in vitro and in vivo model consistently shown that OMT possibly inhibits OLG differentiation and maturation. Future studies should address these limitations to strengthen and validate our findings. These findings suggest that the effects of OMT may be context-dependent, highlighting the need for further investigation to delineate its therapeutic potential and limitations in remyelination in MS.

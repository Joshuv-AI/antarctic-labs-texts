Complete chloroplast genome of Cephalanthera erecta (Thunb.) Blume (Orchidaceae) and its phylogenetic analysis
Authors: Hong Zhu, Yingchao Dai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe report the chloroplast (cp) genome of Cephalanthera erecta (Thunb.) Blume, a terrestrial orchid endemic to East Asia, to clarify its phylogenetic relationship. The 162,011 bp genome has a typical quadripartite structure: a large single-copy (LSC: 88,944 bp), a small single-copy (SSC: 19,177 bp), and a pair of inverted repeats (IRs: 26,945 bp). Annotation identified 135 genes, including 88 mRNAs, 38 tRNAs, eight rRNAs, and one pseudogene. Phylogenetic analysis places C. erecta in a well-supported clade with C. longibracteata, C. falcata, and C. nanchuanica, confirming their close evolutionary relationship. This findings provide genomic resources, clarifies its phylogenetic position, aiding conservation and evolutionary studies of East Asian orchids.Keywords: Cephalanthera erecta, chloroplast genome, phylogenetic relationship

Introduction

Cephalanthera erecta (Thunb.) Blume 1859, a terrestrial orchid species endemic to East Asia, is widely distributed in warm-temperate and temperate regions in central and southern China, central and southern Korea, and Japan (Chung et al. 2018). Chloroplast (cp) genomes have been widely recognized as powerful tools for phylogenetic inference, DNA barcoding, genome evolution study, and species conservation strategies (Wen et al. 2021; Zhu and Li 2024). As the most common species within the genus Cephalanthera, C. erecta has garnered significant attention. However, no cp data have been reported for this species to date. To elucidate the taxonomic and evolutionary relationships within the genus Cephalanthera, we sequenced and assembled the complete cp genome of C. erecta, and performed comparative genomics analyses to characterize its characteristics and phylogenetic position. These results will provide valuable insights into future research on phylogenetic analyses and genetic diversity within Cephalanthera species.

Materials and methods

Plant materials

Fresh leaves of C. erecta were collected from Baibuling, Zhejiang Qingliangfeng National Nature Reserve, Hangzhou, China (coordinates: 30.1029°N, 118.8920°E; altitude: 1005 m) in April 2025 (Figure 1). The sample was identified and collected by the authors, and a specimen was deposited in the Herbarium of Zhejiang Academy of Forestry (HZJAF, contact: Hong Zhu, Email: 1107401987@qq.com) under the voucher number HZ2025-QLF-02.

Plant morphological characteristics of C. erecta. The photo was taken by the first author Yingchao Dai. The species is characterized by 2–4 leaves with a length of 2–8 cm, in contrast to 6–8 leaves measuring 6–14 cm in its closest relative.

DNA extraction and sequencing, genome assembly, and annotation

Total genomic DNA was extracted using a plant DNA kit (Tiangen Biotech, Beijing, China). The quality and integrity of the extracted DNA were initially evaluated via agarose gel electrophoresis. Subsequently, the DNA was sheared into fragments using ultrasonication. Libraries were constructed and subjected to quality control, after which qualified samples were sequenced using the Illumina Novaseq 6000 platform (San Diego, CA) with paired-end sequencing (PE150). Raw reads were processed for quality filtering using fastp v. 0.23.4 (Chen 2023) and followed by genome assembly with SPAdes v. 3.10.1 (Bankevich et al. 2012). Organelle genome annotation was conducted using GetOrganelle v.1.7.7.1 (Jin et al. 2020). Circular cp genome maps were visualized using online tool OGDraw v. 1.2 (Greiner et al. 2019). The cis/trans-splicing gene maps were generated using CPGView (Liu et al. 2023).

Phylogenetic analysis

To investigate the phylogenetic placement of C. erecta within the genus Cephalanthera, we retrieved cp genome sequences of 22 related taxa from the GenBank database. These taxa belong to the family Orchidaceae and include eight species from the genus Cephalanthera Rich., five species from the genus Cephalanthera Zinn, one species from the genus Aphyllorchis Blume, one species from the genus Diplandrorchis S. C. Chen, seven species from the genus Listera R.Br. Additionally, Erythrodes blumei (Lindl.) Schltr., was designated as outgroup for phylogenetic analysis. We aligned these sequences using the program MAFFT v. 7.429 (Katoh et al. 2019) and constructed a phylogenetic tree using the maximum-likelihood (ML) algorithm. To do this, we used the software RAxML v. 8.2.12 (Stamatakis 2014) and performed 1000 bootstrap (BS) replicates based on the GTRGAMMA parameter model.

Results

After quality control, the obtained Clean Data contained 22,472,694 reads and 6,741,808,200 bases, with Q20 and Q30 being 99.40% and 97.54%, respectively. The total length of this cp genome is 162,011 bp, with an average coverage of 269.65× and GC content of 37.20% (Figure 2, Figure S1). A large single-copy (LSC: 88,944 bp), a small single-copy (SSC: 19,177 bp), and a pair of identical inverted repeats (IRs: 26,945 bp) regions made up the typical quadripartite structure of the genome. The base compositions of the cp genome were uneven (31.17% A, 18.77% C, 18.42% G, and 31.46% T). A total of 135 unique genes were annotated, including 38 transfer RNA (tRNA) genes, eight ribosomal RNA (rRNA) genes, 88 messenger RNA (mRNA) genes, and one pseudogene. Specifically, 15 genes harbored a single intron (including ndhA, ndhB, petB, petD, atpF, rpl16, rpl2, rps16, rpoC1, trnA-UGC, trnG-GCC, trnI-GAU, trnK-UUU, trnL-UAA, and trnV-UAC), while three genes (rps12, clpP, and ycf3) contained two introns. Thirteen cis-splicing genes and one trans-splicing gene, rps12 were identified (Figures S2 and S3). Additionally, the cp genome sequence of C. erecta has been deposited in the NCBI database under the accession number PV752076.

According to the phylogenetic analysis (Figure 3), C. erecta within the Cephalanthera occupies an independent branch, and forms a sister relationship with the small clade composed of three other species (C. nanchuanica, C. falcata, and C. longibracteata) in the same genus, supported by an ML BS value of 100.

Discussion and conclusions

Phylogenetic analysis demonstrates that C. erecta forms a strongly supported clade with C. nanchuanica, C. falcata, and C. longibracteata, indicating a shared recent common ancestor. This evolutionary relationship is consistent with their morphological traits of white or yellow spurred flowers, yet, C. erecta exhibits distinct lip apex morphology and spur positioning, suggesting functional adaptive differentiation. Additionally, comparative genomic analysis reveals striking disparities in cp gene content: C. erecta encodes 88 protein-coding genes (PCGs), 38 tRNAs, and eight rRNAs, whereas its close relatives contain only 79 PCGs, 30 tRNAs, and four rRNAs. Such differences in gene composition likely reflect lineage-specific functional divergence and adaptive evolution within Cephalanthera. Furthermore, intergeneric phylogenetic relationships show that Cephalanthera and Epipactis each form reciprocally monophyletic sister clades, constituting sister groups, supporting their common origin and providing molecular evidence for the adaptive radiation in terrestrial orchids.

In this study, the complete cp genome of C. erecta was successfully assembled and annotated for the first time. The results confirm its close phylogenetic relationship with C. longibracteata, C. falcata, and C. nanchuanica, and reveal the evolutionary link between the genera Cephalanthera and Epipactis. This research not only enriches the genomic resources of Orchidaceae but also provides a molecular foundation for taxonomic revision, conservation strategies, and adaptive evolution studies of endemic orchids in East Asia.

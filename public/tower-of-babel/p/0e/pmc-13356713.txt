Efficacy of microencapsulated organic acids-cinnamon blend as alternatives to antibiotic growth promoters: Assessing performance, gut histomorphometry, ileal digestibility, immunity, and antioxidant status in broilers
Authors: Maryam Sahraei, Reza Mahdavi, Soudabeh Moradi, Leili Jamshidi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study evaluated a microencapsulated blend of organic acids and cinnamon extract (OAs-C) as a natural alternative to antibiotic growth promoters. A total of 360 day-old male Ross 308 chicks were assigned to four dietary treatments: a negative control (NC), a positive control with antibiotics (PC), and two supplement levels at 1.5 or 3.0 g/kg. The 3.0 g/kg dose significantly increased body weight gain (BWG; P = 0.005) and improved the cumulative FCR (P = 0.014). Feed intake (FI), Newcastle disease virus antibody (NDV) titers or serum antioxidant parameters, specifically malondialdehyde (MDA), total antioxidant capacity (TAC), superoxide dismutase (SOD), and glutathione peroxidase (GPx) remained statistically unaffected by the OAs-C treatments (P > 0.05). Morphometric analysis of the jejunum revealed that villus height (VH) and villus width (VW) were not significantly influenced by the 1.5 and 3 g/kg OAs-C. The PC and 1.5 g/kg groups significantly reduced jejunal crypt depth (CD; P = 0.033). Compared to the control, birds supplemented with 1.5 g/kg OAs-C achieved the highest VH/CD ratio (quadratic P-value = 0.021). Furthermore, the 3.0 g/kg inclusion level significantly expanded the villus surface area (VSA) with linear (P = 0.030) trend. Apparent ileal Ash digestibility showed significant linear (P = 0.041) and quadratic (P = 0.038) effects. Supplementation with 3.0 g/kg OAs-C induced significant linear and quadratic increases (P < 0.01) in the digestibility of crude protein (CP) and calcium (Ca). Regarding apparent ileal phosphorus (P) digestibility, a significant quadratic response (P < 0.001) was observed, with the 1.5 g/kg dose providing the optimal result. In conclusion, the microencapsulated OAs-C blend enhances broiler growth and feed efficiency primarily by optimizing intestine morphology and improving nutrient utilization.Keywords: Acetic, Formic, Butyric, Propionic, Cinnamaldehyde

Introduction

Rising concerns regarding the proliferation of antibiotic-resistant pathogens and the presence of chemical residues in meat products have fundamentally reshaped modern poultry nutrition (Abd El-Ghany, 2024; Khan and Iqbal, 2016; Ricke, 2003). For more than half a century, nontherapeutic antibiotic growth promoters (AGPs) were extensively utilized to enhance bird performance and prevent subclinical infections (Khan and Iqbal, 2016; Ricke, 2003). However, the subsequent implementation of total bans on AGPs across numerous jurisdictions has created an urgent and critical need for viable and sustainable feed alternatives. Among the most promising candidates are probiotics, prebiotics, organic acids, exogenous enzymes, and phytogenic compounds (Abd El-Ghany, 2024; Ali et al., 2021; Khan and Iqbal, 2016).

OAs are generally defined as weak carboxylic acids that effectively lower gastrointestinal pH to inhibit pathogenic (like Salmonella and Escherichia coli) colonization while simultaneously preserving the hygienic quality and safety of the feed and maintaining physiological stability (Gerzilov and Hristakieva, 2025; Ricke, 2003). These effects are significantly augmented by the inclusion of cinnamon extract. Cinnamon bioactives, particularly cinnamaldehyde and eugenol, act as powerful digestive stimulants by increasing the secretion of endogenous enzymes (Ali et al., 2021; Fathi et al., 2025; Yaqoob et al., 2022).

Most related studies have used only 1-3 organic acids blend, or have focused on phytogenic feed additives alone. Fewer investigations have examined the simultaneous combination of organic acids and phytogenic feed additives together. To our knowledge, no previous study has reported the use of all five organic acids in combination with cinnamon simultaneously. Each component has shown antimicrobial effects and improved nutrient digestibility. A chitosan-based matrix, unlike conventional lipid coatings, likely offers superior protection from early degradation and allows targeted release in the distal gut, an important factor for maintaining mucosal structure. Therefore, we aimed to investigate their possible synergistic effects on growth performance, intestinal morphology, antioxidant status, immune system, and nutrient digestibility in broilers.

Material and methods

Experimental design, animals and diet

All experimental procedures were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of the Department of Animal Science, Razi University, Iran. A total of 360 one-day-old Ross 308 male broiler chicks were randomly assigned to four treatments with six replicates of 15 birds each. Treatments included a negative control (NC), a positive control (PC; 0.5 g/kg bacitracin citrate, based on manufacture’s recommendation and previous studies), and NC supplemented with 1.5 or 3.0 g/kg of a microencapsulated organic acids-cinnamon blend (Parsid Plus®). The active ingredient profile of Parsid Plus® (Holding PARSA, Qom, Iran) included citric acid (25%), formic acid (25%), acetic acid (20%), propionic acid (20%), butyric acid (5%), and cinnamon extract (2%). These sensitive compounds were encapsulated within a chitosan-based matrix using a specialized spraying and cooling technique to protect them from rapid degradation and oxidation during storage. The 42-day trial was conducted in floor pens on deep litter in a closed house system. Birds received mash diets formulated according to Ross 308 recommendations and had free access to feed and water. Production performance

Growth performance was evaluated at 42 days of age. Body weight gain (BWG), feed intake (FI), and feed conversion ratio (FCR) were determined on a replicate basis. Mortality and body weight of dead birds were recorded daily, and a broiler-appropriate mortality-adjusted calculation was applied to correct performance data.

Intestinal morphology

At day 42, one bird per replicate (six birds per treatment) with body weight close to the pen average was selected for intestinal morphology evaluation. A 2-cm jejunal segment, collected 5 cm proximal to Meckel’s diverticulum, was fixed in 10% buffered formalin, dehydrated, embedded in paraffin, sectioned at 5 μm (rotary microtome; Model CUT4055, microTec Laborgeräte GmbH, Germany), and stained with hematoxylin and eosin. Villus height (VH), villus width (VW), and crypt depth (CD) were measured using an Olympus BX51TF light microscope equipped with an image analysis system. Antioxidant parameters and antibody titers against newcastle disease virus

To evaluate humoral immunity, birds were vaccinated against Newcastle disease virus (NDV; CEVAC® NEW L, La Sota sratin, CEVA-Phylaxia, Hungary) on day 30 via drinking water. Blood samples were collected from one bird per replicate on days 28 and 40, and serum antibody titers were determined using the hemagglutination inhibition (HI) test. At day 42, one bird per replicate was selected for antioxidant analysis. Serum and whole blood samples were collected to determine malondialdehyde (MDA), superoxide dismutase (SOD), glutathione peroxidase (GPx), and total antioxidant capacity (TAC). SOD and GPx activities were measured using commercial kits, and all antioxidant indices were determined spectrophotometrically.

Nutrient digestibility

Apparent ileal digestibility of DM, CP, Ash, Ca, and P was determined using titanium dioxide (0.5%) as an indigestible marker during the final 72 h of the experiment. At day 42, two birds per replicate were euthanized, and ileal digesta samples were collected from the region between Meckel’s diverticulum and 5 cm anterior to the ileocecal junction. Samples were pooled by replicate and stored at −20°C.

Digesta samples were freeze-dried and analyzed for DM, CP, Ash, Ca, and P. DM and CP were determined according to AOAC procedures, Ash by muffle furnace, Ca by atomic absorption spectrophotometry, and P by the ammonium vanado-molybdate method. Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed by a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) using SAS 9.4 software. The pen served as the experimental unit for BWG, FI and FCR. For jejunal morphology, blood indices, antibody titers, and ileal nutrient digestibility, individual birds selected from each replicate were considered the experimental units. When an overall effect was detected (P ≤ 0.05), means were compared using Duncan’s multiple range test. Orthogonal polynomial contrasts were performed to evaluate linear and quadratic trends of the response variables relative to increasing inclusion levels. Results are presented as means with the pooled standard error of the mean (SEM). Statistical thresholds were established at P ≤ 0.05.

Results and discussion

Production performance

As shown in Table 2, dietary inclusion of 3 g/kg OAs-C increased the cumulative body weight gain compared to both the NC and PC treatments (P = 0.005). The feed intake recorded for birds in the PC group was lower than that of birds fed the NC diet (P = 0.008). Consequently, birds supplemented with 3 g/kg OAs-C improved the FCR relative to the NC group (P = 0.014). Analysis of orthogonal polynomial contrasts revealed no linear or quadratic trends for these cumulative growth indices. The improvements observed in growth performance, especially at the 3 g/kg dose in the present study can primarily be explained by the maximum VSA (Table 2), providing a greater surface area for the greater load of nutrients, and superior ileal digestibility of CP (Table 2), which can be effective by increasing the supply of nutrients to the birds. A larger VSA, coupled with more efficient protein breakdown, provided the necessary physiological framework for the birds to achieve better growth. Beyond the measured parameters, several complementary mechanisms may also help explain the observed responses. The antimicrobial activity of organic acids, especially formic and acetic acids, may reduce the intestinal burden of pathogenic bacteria, which may decrease nutrient competition between host and microflora (Gerzilov and Hristakieva, 2025; Ricke, 2003). These effects are complemented by cinnamaldehyde, a potent antimicrobial agent (Ali et al., 2021; Friedman, 2017). Cinnamaldehyde, the principal active compound of cinnamon, may further contribute to the observed performance improvements by stimulating digestive secretions and enhancing the activity of endogenous enzymes such as amylase, lipase, and proteases, which may improve nutrient breakdown and feed efficiency (Ali et al., 2021; Yaqoob et al., 2022). The improved performance observed in the present study may also be related to the microencapsulation technology applied to the OAs-C blend. This delivery system may protect volatile and sensitive compounds from premature absorption in the upper gastrointestinal tract and may enable a more controlled release along the jejunum and ileum, where nutrient absorption is most active and microbial pressure is higher (Abd El-Ghany, 2024). This targeted release may enhance the overall efficacy of the bioactive compounds.

Antioxidant parameters and antibody titers against NDV

Dietary OAs-C inclusion did not alter MDA, SOD, GPx or TAC (Table 2). Similarly, antibody titers against NDV measured at 28 and 40 days of age, as well as the calculated titer difference, remained unaffected by the treatments. The lack of systemic response suggests that bioactives like cinnamaldehyde and butyric acid primarily exerted localized antioxidant and metabolic effects within the intestinal mucosa-consistent with the improved VSA and digestibility observed in this trial, rather than through circulating blood markers (Fathi et al., 2025; Sikandar et al., 2017). Furthermore, the lack of severe environmental or pathogenic stressors during the experimental period likely restricted the physiological demand for a significant immuno-stimulatory surge (Ali et al., 2021). Despite these facts, the experimental treatments demonstrated a numerical improvement in antibody titers against NDV compared to the NC group.

Intestinal morphology

Different dietary levels of OAs-C did not influence VH or VW in broiler chickens. Dietary supplementation with 1.5 g/kg OAs-C increased the VH/CD (P = 0.028) compared to the NC group (Table 2). Birds receiving the 3 g/kg OAs-C treatment showed an increased VSA relative to the NC group (P = 0.041). The CD was lower in birds fed the antibiotic and 1.5 g/kg OAs-C diets compared to the NC group (P = 0.033). Analysis of orthogonal polynomial contrasts revealed quadratic responces for VH (P = 0.018) and VH/CD (P = 0.021), while the VSA followed a linear respnonce (P = 0.030). These morphological enhancements are likely driven by the metabolic and protective functions of the bioactive blend. Butyric acid potentially acts as a paramount nutritional substrate, serving as the primary energy source for enterocytes and stimulating epithelial cell mitosis in the crypts. This trophic effect could accelerate villus elongation and expand the total intestinal VSA (Abd El-Ghany, 2024, Abd El-Ghany, 2024; Khan and Iqbal, 2016; Sikandar et al., 2017). Furthermore, the antimicrobial action of organic acids may suppress pH-sensitive pathogens, reducing the secretion of toxic microbial metabolites that otherwise cause villus atrophy (Gerzilov and Hristakieva, 2025; Ricke, 2003). The structural optimization might also be linked to the upregulation of tight junction proteins like Occludin and Claudins by cinnamaldehyde and butyric acid, which potentially strengthen the mucosal barrier against inflammatory damage (Abd El-Ghany, 2024; Ali et al., 2021). Additionally, bioactive compounds in cinnamon may provide localized antioxidant protection, safeguarding the delicate villi from oxidative stress and lipid peroxidation during digestion (Ali et al., 2021; Fathi et al., 2025; Friedman, 2017). Collectively, the increased VSA and VH/CD ratio provided a superior physiological framework for the improved nutrient utilization recorded elsewhere in this study.

Nutrient digestibility

Regarding apparent ileal digestibility, the dry matter digestibility was not affected by the dietary treatments (Table 2). Inclusion of 3 g/kg OAs-C increased the digestibility of CP (P = 0.002) and Ca (P < 0.001). Both levels of the bioactive blend improved the digestibility of Ash compared to the NC and PC groups (P < 0.001). The digestibility of P was higher in birds supplemented with 1.5 g/kg OAs-C (P = 0.022). Linear and quadratic responces were detected for CP (Linear P = 0.001; quadratic P = 0.002), Ash (P = 0.041; P = 0.038), and Ca (P = 0.012; P < 0.001). However, A quadratic trend was detected for P (P < 0.001). Based on quadratic regression analysis, the estimated optimal inclusion levels for maximizing apparent ileal digestibility of Ash and phosphorus were 1.93 and 1.56 g/kg diet, respectively. This improvement in nutrient utilization likely resulted from the pH-lowering properties of the organic acid components. Formic, acetic, and propionic acids potentially function as acidifiers, facilitating the conversion of inactive pepsinogen into its active form, pepsin, thereby improving gastric proteolysis and subsequent amino acid release (Abd El-Ghany, 2024; Gerzilov and Hristakieva, 2025). Complementing these effects, cinnamaldehyde may act as a digestive stimulant, triggering the secretion of endogenous enzymes, including pancreatic amylase and intestinal proteases (Ali et al., 2021; Yaqoob et al., 2022). Collectively, the synergistic blend likely creates a more favorable gut environment by suppressing microbial competition for nutrients and enhancing the birds intrinsic digestive capacity (Gerzilov and Hristakieva, 2025; Khan and Iqbal, 2016). Furthermore, the optimization of mineral digestibility is likely attributed to citric acid, which potentially acts as a potent chelator. By forming soluble complexes with mineral ions, it prevents the formation of insoluble calcium-phytate aggregates, thereby increasing the bioavailability of Ca and P (Gerzilov and Hristakieva, 2025; Kazempour and Jahanian, 2017). Butyric acid could further support mineral retention by providing a direct energy substrate for enterocytes, which helps maintain epithelial integrity and supports the active transport mechanisms required for efficient absorption (Abd El-Ghany, 2024; Sikandar et al., 2017).

We conclude that dietary inclusion of OAs-C enhances growth performance and feed efficiency by optimizing jejunal morphology and improving ileal nutrient digestibility coefficients, thereby serving as a practical nutritional strategy to maintain broiler productivity in modern antibiotic-free production systems.

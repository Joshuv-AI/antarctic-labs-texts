Chinese crop diseases and pests named entity recognition based on variational information bottleneck and feature enhancement
Authors: Runqing Huang, Meijing Li, Huilin Zheng, Ziqi Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractChinese crop diseases and pests named entity recognition (CCDP-NER) is a critical step in extracting domain-specific information in the field of crop diseases and pests, playing a significant role in promoting agricultural informatization. To address challenges such as noisy data, erroneous annotations, and ambiguous entity boundaries in the crop disease and pest domain, this study proposes a deep learning-based CCDP-NER model. The model employs a bidirectional gated recurrent Unit (BiGRU) to capture long-range semantic dependencies and integrates multi-level dilated convolutional neural networks (DCNNs) to extract local fine-grained features, thereby constructing a global-local collaborative representation. Innovatively, the variational information bottleneck (VIB) technique is introduced to filter noise by constraining mutual information, reducing the impact of input noise on feature extraction while simultaneously enhancing the correlation between extracted features and labels, thereby improving model robustness. Additionally, an entity boundary detection module is incorporated to identify the head and tail positions of entities, enhancing boundary recognition accuracy. Experiments conducted on a constructed crop diseases and pests dataset demonstrate that the proposed model effectively identifies crop disease and pest entities, achieving an F1-score of 90.64%. This research holds significant value for applications such as agricultural knowledge graph construction and agricultural question-answering systems.Keywords: Named entity recognition, Crop diseases and pests, BiGRU, DCNN, Entity boundary detection, VIBSubject terms: Computer science, Information technology

Introduction

With the rapid development of digital agriculture, agricultural informatization has become a key research hotspot1,2. Crop diseases and pests are significant factors limiting agricultural production and threatening food security. To accurately acquire intelligence on crop diseases and pests and enable timely prevention and control, retrieving knowledge from massive unstructured agricultural text data is essential3,4. Chinese crop diseases and pests named entity recognition (CCDP-NER) focuses on identifying entities such as crops, diseases, and pests from unstructured agricultural text. This task provides critical support for precise management of agricultural diseases and pests and has been widely applied in downstream tasks such as agricultural knowledge quizzes and knowledge graph construction. These applications empower agricultural practitioners to formulate more targeted control strategies, promoting sustainable agricultural development.

However, CCDP-NER research still faces significant challenges. In contrast to general-domain Named Entity Recognition challenges, agricultural texts have numerous technical terminology and hybrid entities including numbers, letters, and Chinese characters, such as “小麦根腐病” (wheat root rot), “吡虫啉” (imidacloprid), and “苏南4号” (Su Nan 4). Additionally, agricultural texts are often plagued by substantial textual noise and manual annotation errors, which further exacerbate the difficulty of accurate recognition. To address these issues, we propose a CCDP-NER model based on variational information bottleneck and feature enhancement (VIBFE). The model captures long-range semantic dependencies through a bidirectional gated recurrent unit (BiGRU) and integrates multi-level dilated convolutional neural networks (DCNNs) to extract multi-granularity local features, thereby constructing a global-local collaborative representation. Moreover, the variational information bottleneck (VIB) technique is employed to establish a mutual information constraint mechanism, which suppresses text noise interference while enhancing the semantic correlation between extracted features and labels. To address the challenge of entity boundary recognition, an entity boundary detection module is introduced to identify the head and tail of entities, resolving issues of entity confusion and ambiguous boundaries. Related work

Chinese named entity recognition

Earlier NER methods relied mainly on dictionary-based and rule-based approaches. Dictionary-based methods5 achieved entity recognition by matching terms in a predefined dictionary with words in the target text sequence. While this approach is relatively straightforward, its effectiveness diminishes as the number of entities and the diversity of terms increase, making accurate entity recognition more challenging. On the other hand, rule-based approaches6 often required domain experts to manually construct extraction rules tailored to specific domains. These methods performed well in narrow domains but struggled with generalization and adapting to broader, more diverse contexts. Both approaches, therefore, heavily relied on manual input, consuming considerable time and effort. In contrast, machine learning-based approaches enabled more efficient entity recognition through statistical models or algorithms. Notable models such as the Hidden Markov Model (HMM)7 and Conditional Random Field (CRF)8 became widely used for entity extraction. Notwithstanding significant advancements in identification accuracy, conventional machine learning techniques nonetheless necessitated expensive and laborious feature engineering, constraining their scalability and adaptability to novel or intricate datasets. Deep learning approaches have been extensively utilized in NER tasks, yielding remarkable results due to their ability to operate without the need for manually designed features and their end-to-end learning capabilities9. By leveraging neural networks to automatically extract relevant features, these methods significantly reduce the need for manual effort and resource allocation, positioning deep learning as a dominant approach in the field of NER research. Huang et al.10 were the first to apply the BiLSTM-CRF architecture to the NER task, significantly improving recognition accuracy. Qin et al.11 combined manually designed feature templates with a CNN-BiLSTM-CRF model, introducing a hybrid approach for Chinese-English cybersecurity entity recognition that effectively extracts both local character-level and global text-level features. Wu et al.12 introduced an attention mechanism and designed an enhanced BiLSTM-CRF model that successfully addressed long-range dependency issues, improving the performance of Chinese clinical NER. Li et al.13 proposed the BERT-BiLSTM-GAM-CRF model, which incorporates a syntactic dependency-guided self-attention mechanism into BERT’s Transformer framework. Qiu et al.14 designed a geologic entity recognition method, which combines features such as pinyin, radicals, and positional information to significantly improve the model’s performance. Xu et al.15 introduced an adaptive lexical weighting approach that assesses the connection between words and sentences using an improved global attention mechanism, alleviating the mismatch between lexical information and sentence semantics. Yu et al.16 proposed a document-level NER model, which captures global dependencies by constructing a document graph and encodes cross-sentence contexts using a specific attention mechanism, effectively addressing sentence-level ambiguity issues.

Agricultural named entity recognition

Currently, investigations into deep learning within the agricultural sector predominantly concentrate on computer vision tasks, including the classification of images pertaining to crop and fruit pests and diseases17, the identification of plant species18, and implementations in smart agriculture19. In contrast, studies on agricultural NER remain relatively scarce. In recent years, the fast progression of neural network technology has increasingly facilitated the application of deep learning to agricultural NER tasks, leading to novel research avenues and technical innovations in the domain. Guo et al.20 proposed the ACE-ADP model, which fine-tunes BERT to generate domain-specific contextual representations and employs adversarial training to enhance generalization ability for Chinese agricultural NER. Zhang et al.21 introduced the APD-CA model, which integrates similar words from a dictionary into the BiLSTM-CRF framework to improve character representations for apple pest and disease NER. Wang et al.22 proposed a Soft-lexicon method based on attention and TF-IDF for agricultural pest and disease NER, which improves recognition accuracy by incorporating topic features into lexical information. Guo et al.23 achieved good results on agricultural pest and disease NER datasets by integrating glyph features of Chinese characters and using a channel fusion architecture to capture multi-granular local contextual features. Tang et al.24 proposed ResiAdvNet, which integrates progressive residual architectures and adversarial training, utilising MacBERT pre-training and residual block aggregation to highlight multi-layer information, effectively implemented for potato pest and disease recognition.

Named entity recognition based on large language models

In recent years, large language models (LLMs) such as GPT-4 and LLaMA-3 have achieved remarkable progress in the field of natural language processing25. Through extensive pretraining, these models have developed strong capabilities in language understanding and contextual awareness. They have been widely applied to tasks such as text generation, translation, and question answering, demonstrating outstanding performance26. Compared to traditional methods, LLMs demonstrate greater adaptability in handling NER tasks, particularly in scenarios with limited annotated data, where they can still accurately identify key entities within complex semantics. Wang et al.27 proposed GPT-NER, a method that reformulates the NER task as a text generation problem, enabling LLMs to identify entities by marking their positions with special tokens and introducing a self-verification mechanism to mitigate hallucinations, which proved especially effective in low-resource and few-shot scenarios. Liu et al.28 have explored leveraging LLMs and retrieval-augmented generation to enhance domain-specific NER tasks, employing strategies like in-contextual learning and fine-tuning to effectively customize taxonomies and improve entity recognition performance. Li et al.29 combined retrieval mechanisms with Chain-of-Thought reasoning to propose an innovative framework that integrates information retrieval and systematic reasoning, effectively improving performance on few-shot named entity recognition tasks. Liu et al.30 proposed a method for enhancing NER by leveraging external knowledge generated by large language models, in which an entity linking strategy is used to expand knowledge coverage and a self-training mechanism is employed to filter pseudo labels, effectively mitigating non-entity interference and significantly improving performance across multiple benchmark tasks.

Methods

VIBFE consists of five main layers: the embedding layer, information bottleneck layer, feature extraction layer, feature fusion layer, and CRF layer, along with an entity boundary detection module. The overall architecture of the model is shown in the Fig. 2. The input text is first converted into word vectors through the embedding layer. These word vector representations are then passed through the information bottleneck layer, which establishes a mutual information constraint mechanism. This mechanism effectively suppresses textual noise and enhances the semantic correlation between the input and the target labels, thereby improving the robustness of VIBFE. Next, the feature extraction layer employs BiGRU and DCNN to extract global and local contextual features, respectively. The entity boundary detection module is used to precisely identify the head and tail boundaries of entities. Subsequently, the feature fusion layer integrates the obtained global and local contextual features with entity boundary information using an adaptive gating mechanism and multi-head attention mechanism. This improves VIBFE’s focus on key information and its sensitivity to entity boundaries. Finally, the fused feature representations are passed into the CRF layer for decoding, generating the most probable label sequence and assigning corresponding labels to each character.

Embedding layer

To generate more accurate word-level encoding vectors for Chinese characters, this study introduces the pre-trained BERT-wwm31 language model. BERT-wwm improves the superior performance of the BERT32 model by incorporating a whole-word masking strategy. Unlike the static masking mechanism in the BERT model, BERT-wwm uses a whole-word masking strategy, which means that when part of a word is masked, the entire word is masked simultaneously. This mechanism enables the model to understand the overall semantics of words in context more comprehensively, especially when dealing with languages such as Chinese, where it can more accurately capture the semantic relationships of complex words. For example, in the agricultural text “高粱顶腐病” (top rot of sorghum) in Table 1, the BERT model might mask “高” (tall) as a separate character, weakening the general understanding of the meaning of the phrase. In contrast, in the BERT-wwm model, “高粱” (sorghum) is masked as a whole, and the resulting word-level vectors can more accurately capture the contextual semantic information of the phrase. Compared to BERT, which only masks a single character at a time, BERT-wwm preserves the integrity of the lexicon through the whole-word masking strategy, enabling the model to more effectively learn contextual information and semantic relationships between words, thus improving the performance of entity and relation extraction tasks. Feature extraction layer

BiGRU and DCNN were selected for global and local contextual feature extraction in this study, mainly because of their excellent performance in the NER task.

BiGRU

GRU (Gated Recurrent Unit) is an evolution of the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network. Its core consists of an update gate and a reset gate. The update gate controls the proportion of the previous hidden state passed to the current time step, while the reset gate determines how much of the previous hidden state is forgotten or reset. This gating mechanism helps GRU effectively tackle the issues of gradient vanishing and explosion. Feature fusion layer

BiGRU is primarily used to extract the global semantic information of the text, while DCNN focuses on capturing local semantic features. After obtaining the global and local contextual features as well as the encoded entity boundary position information, the model performs adaptive integration through a feature fusion layer. This fusion layer mainly consists of an adaptive gating mechanism and a multi-head attention mechanism. Specifically, the adaptive gating mechanism assigns different weights to the extracted global and local features, thereby enhancing the model’s focus on key information. CRF layer

CRF is a conditional probability-based discriminative model widely applied in sequence labeling tasks, especially NER. CRF models the conditional probability distribution of random variables under given input conditions and ensures the global consistency of the generated label sequence by fully considering the dependencies between contextual labels. In traditional methods, the outputs are often used to independently predict labels for each time step. However, this approach may result in invalid label sequences. For example, the beginning of an entity should be labeled “B,” but it might be incorrectly labeled “I.” To address this issue, CRF incorporates a state transition matrix to model the relationships between label transitions. This approach optimizes the label assignment across the entire sequence, ensuring that the predicted label sequence adheres to constraints such as those defined by the “BIO” tagging scheme, thereby improving the model performance in sequence labeling tasks.

Experiments

Dataset

Corpus collection and preprocessing

Due to the absence of public datasets for CCDP-NER, we have constructed a specialized dataset tailored to this task. First, we collected a large volume of textual data from specialized websites such as the China Crop Resources Information Network and Friends of Agriculture using web scraping techniques. Next, we performed rigorous data cleaning and preprocessing on the collected texts, including the removal of special characters and non-textual elements. The cleaned data was then manually annotated with entity labels to ensure accuracy and high-quality annotations. Finally, we applied a 7:2:1 data split strategy, dividing the data set into training, validation, and test sets, which resulted in the creation of AgriCNER, a CCDP-NER dataset that contains approximately 200,000 characters.

Data annotation

We used the YEDDA annotation tool to label the entities and converted the annotated entities to the standard BIO format. In this notation, ’B’ indicates the beginning of an entity, ’I’ indicates the inside of an entity, and “O” indicates a character or fragment not related to entities. Under the guidance of agricultural experts, we identified and annotated five categories: crop, pesticide, fertilizer, disease, and pest. The final dataset contains 6010 crop disease and pest entities, including 2102 crop entities, 1383 pesticide entities, 1253 fertilizer entities, 615 pest entities, and 657 disease entities. Recognition performance for each entity category

To more comprehensively evaluate the practical performance of the proposed model on the CCDP-NER task, we conducted an in-depth analysis of its recognition performance across different entity categories. As shown in Fig. 9, the F1 score for the crop entity class is the highest, reaching 96.23%. In contrast, the pesticide entity class has the lowest F1 score, only 84.48%. This may be attributed to the complex terminology, diverse naming conventions, and inconsistent expressions commonly found in pesticide entities, which increase the difficulty of recognition. Additionally, the pest and disease entity classes suffer from data imbalance, with 973 and 839 training samples respectively, which is likely one of the main reasons their recognition performance is slightly lower than that of the crop class. Despite the presence of some degree of class imbalance in the dataset, the proposed VIBFE model still demonstrates relatively strong recognition performance on entity categories such as pesticide and fertilizer, which are both highly domain-specific and have relatively few samples. This indicates that the model possesses a certain level of robustness and generalization capability in handling complex and low-frequency entities.

However, there is a significant difference between the two models in recognizing the crop entity “紫甘蓝” (red cabbage): the BERT-CRF model failed to explicitly label “red cabbage” as a crop entity, whereas the VIBFE model accurately recognized it as such. This indicates that VIBFE is better at identifying nested entities and distinguishing fine-grained entity categories. The improvements in boundary recognition and semantic differentiation enable VIBFE to perform better when processing complex agricultural texts. In addition, there are notable differences in how the two models represent domain-specific semantic information. Figure11 illustrates the visualization of attention associations between the character “蓝” (blue) and other characters. As shown in Fig. 11b, the BERT-CRF model exhibits a relatively dispersed attention distribution for the character “蓝”, attempting to attend to multiple positions in the sentence to capture broader contextual information. However, this dispersed attention may introduce redundancy, thereby affecting the model’s ability to accurately extract entity boundaries. In contrast, as shown in Fig. 11a, the proposed VIBFE model exhibits a more focused attention distribution, primarily centering on the core entity “紫甘蓝烧心病”. This focus closely aligns with the goal of the CCDP-NER task, indicating that the model has a stronger ability to capture key semantic features related to entity recognition.

Recognition performance on public datasets

To evaluate the generalization ability of the VIBFE model, we conducted tests on two public benchmark datasets - Resume36 and MSRA37 - and additionally introduced an agricultural domain dataset, CropDiseaseNer1, which contains annotated entities such as diseases, pests, control agents, control methods, symptoms, affected areas, and crops. The inclusion of the CropDiseaseNer dataset allows us to further assess the model’s performance in real-world and domain-specific scenarios.

As shown in Tables 7 and 8, VIBFE achieved the highest F1 scores across all three datasets, with 96.18% on the Resume dataset, 95.36% on the MSRA dataset, and 70.07% on the CropDiseaseNer dataset. Compared with other models, VIBFE’s F1 score was at least 0.65% and up to 1.72% higher on the Resume dataset, at least 1.24% and up to 2.18% higher on the MSRA dataset, and at least 1.23% and up to 7.03% higher on the CropDiseaseNer dataset. These results demonstrate that VIBFE performs consistently well across multiple datasets and exhibits excellent generalization capability.

Conclusions

This paper proposes a NER model based on variational information bottleneck and feature enhancement, aiming to improve the performance of CCDP-NER tasks. The model enhances the accuracy of CCDP-NER by integrating global and local contextual features, entity boundary detection module and VIB. Specifically, the VIBFE model achieves deep fusion of global semantic dependencies and local fine-grained features through a collaborative representation mechanism combining BiGRU and multi-level DCNNs. Additionally, the model introduces an entity boundary detection module that marks the start and end positions of entities, thereby enhancing its ability to handle complex texts. Notably, the VIBFE model incorporates the VIB mechanism, which compresses input features through mutual information constraints, effectively suppressing noise propagation while strengthening the potential correlation between features and entity labels. To further advance research in the CCDP-NER field, this paper also constructs a new dataset for CCDP-NER providing data support for related studies. Experimental results demonstrate that the VIBFE model achieves significant performance improvements in CCDP-NER tasks, validating its effectiveness.

Although VIBFE has achieved some success in CCDP-NER, there are still several shortcomings in the experimental process that need to be improved. First, the size of the dataset used is relatively small. The AgricNER dataset contains about 200,000 characters, but the amount of data is still limited compared to the complex body of knowledge in the field of crop pests and diseases, which may restrict the performance of the model. Second, data annotation is difficult and costly, and the manual annotation and its review process is both time-consuming and laborious, affecting the efficiency of dataset construction. Finally, while the VIBFE model improves the accuracy of entity recognition, the parallel feature extraction of multi-level DCNNs and BiGRU in its architecture increases computational resource consumption.

Future research directions can be explored in the following aspects: First, the generalization capability of the model can be further enhanced by expanding the volume of agricultural text data and constructing larger-scale datasets. Second, annotation strategies can be optimized to balance the distribution of different entity categories, thereby mitigating data imbalance issues. Third, leveraging the advantages of large models, deep semantic information can be extracted through transfer learning and multi-task learning methods, improving the recognition accuracy of disease and pest entities in complex agricultural texts. Furthermore, multi-modal data fusion techniques can be explored, such as integrating image and text information to build a more comprehensive intelligent diagnosis system for crop diseases and pests, offering new ideas and technical support for the intelligent development of the agricultural sector.

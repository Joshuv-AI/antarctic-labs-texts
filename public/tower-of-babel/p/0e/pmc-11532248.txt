Welding defects extraction method by fusing saliency information of mid-level and underlying level images
Authors: Bing Zhu, Zhefan Wang, Yuyan Ma, Weixin Gao, Siyu Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractX-ray non-destructive testing (NDT) technology is extensively utilized in the welding industry for the detection of weld defects. This paper proposes a novel defect segmentation algorithm to address the challenges of X-ray defect detection images, including low contrast, blurred edges, significant noise, and pronounced background variations. Traditional detection methods often struggle to extract low-contrast defects from weld images, so this approach integrates both underlying and mid-level image information to enhance accuracy. The process begins with a visual saliency model that generates a rough saliency map from underlying details. Next, a Pulse Coupled Neural Network (PCNN) is used to compute the saliency map at the mid-level. Finally, these two saliency maps are combined using a pixel-minimum method to produce the final image saliency map. Experimental results show that this method is highly accurate, broadly applicable, and capable of rapid defect extraction within the welding area.Keywords: Defect detection, Fusion model, PCNN, Image saliency

Introduction

Pipeline transportation plays a pivotal role in oil and natural gas transportation. During the production of oil and gas pipelines, various defects may arise, including cracks, pores, slag inclusions, and lack of penetration, e.g. The defects pose significant risks the safety of pipeline transportation. Therefore, non-destructive testing of pipeline welds is of paramount practical significance.

Currently, radiographic testing is the most commonly used non-destructive testing technique in the industry. This method allows for scanning potentially defective weld areas using highly penetrating X-rays without compromising the pipe's structure. As these rays pass through materials of varying densities, the attenuation coefficients differ, creating contrasting light and dark images on the X-ray film.

The quality of the weld is assessed by manually identifying defects based on the grayscale differences in the radiographic image. However, this process is labor-intensive, heavily reliant on the inspector's technical expertise, inefficient, and prone to errors. Thus, defect detection through digital images is essential.

We are currently in a critical phase of transforming the manufacturing industry towards digitalization and intelligence. Various industries are continually evolving and moving toward automation. Automatic extraction of weld defects, as a key component of intelligent manufacturing, has significant potential in this field, highlighting the high academic value of the research presented in this paper.

C Li et al. used grayscale and other features to extract defects at the pixel level [1,2]. However, the approach only achieved the optimal effect for extracting specific types of defects. Z H Yan et al. extracted defects at the superpixel level based on image fusion, adaptive morphological filtering and other approaches [[3], [4], [5]]. The approach was unable to achieve the function of real-time recognition due to the complexity of the algorithms.

Mid-level image information encompasses rich image structure information. Therefore, mid-level image information is used to locate local information and analyze differences, colors, and spatiotemporal frequencies. On the other hand, underlying image information captures underlying details of the algorithm and design. Underlying image information can better express the internal information of the region of interest.

In 1990, Eckhorn proposed a connection model illustrating the spiking phenomenon based on the synchronized spiking phenomenon in the cat's cerebral cortex [6]. Similar experimental results were obtained in experiments on the cerebral cortex of monkeys [[7], [8], [9]]. The PCNN model was derived by modifying Eckhorn's model [10].

When PCNN is used for image processing, one pixel corresponds to one neuron. Neurons with similar inputs pulse at the same time in PCNN. As a result, PCNN can compensate for the spatial incoherence and small changes in the amplitude of the input data to aid in retaining the regional information of the image. The feature is advantageous for image segmentation.

Unlike traditional multi-layer networks, PCNN is capable of pattern recognition, image segmentation, and target classification without the need for training. This unique feature makes it especially suitable for real-time image processing applications.

Result

Experimental setup

In the initial experiments, the proposed method was evaluated using a self-constructed dataset comprising 278 images of welding defects. Subsequently, we applied the method to the publicly available GDXray dataset. We also compared the results with four established methods: the ITTI model [14], the CA method [15], the LC method [16], and the SIM algorithm [17]. Finally, we assessed the computational efficiency of each method, focusing on execution time and memory usage, to provide a comprehensive evaluation of their performance in terms of speed and resource consumption.

Discussion

The proposed segmentation algorithm, which integrates visual saliency with PCNN, demonstrated promising results on the image dataset developed in this study. However, when applied to dense stomatal defects, the algorithm is prone to stomatal sticking, resulting in suboptimal segmentation outcomes. In terms of computational efficiency, the algorithm proposed in this paper utilizes only 1.9961 MB of memory, representing the best performance among all the evaluated algorithms. However, its execution speed is not optimal. Future work will focus on two key improvements: first, optimizing the parameters of the visual saliency model to enhance the algorithm's noise resistance and further improve segmentation accuracy; second, reducing the computational complexity to increase processing speed.

Conclusions

The study proposes welding defect extraction method that integrates underlying image information and mid-level image information. First, the PCNN network is employed to generate a saliency map of the mid-level information. Next, the visual saliency model is applied to obtain a saliency map from the underlying information. Finally, these two levels of information are integrated using a pixel-minimum-based method, resulting in the final saliency map. The proposed algorithm demonstrates outstanding performance on the private dataset, achieving an accuracy of 93.17 %, a precision of 96.46 %, and a recall of 94.14 %. Furthermore, it exhibits excellent memory efficiency, utilizing only 1.9961 MB of memory. On the public dataset, the algorithm also achieves an accuracy of 92.12 %. These results highlight the algorithm's effectiveness and resource efficiency in various testing conditions. The proposed approach was validated on a welding database, and the experimental results indicate that it exhibits high accuracy and strong applicability in detecting volumetric defects within the welding area, making it an effective image preprocessing technique.

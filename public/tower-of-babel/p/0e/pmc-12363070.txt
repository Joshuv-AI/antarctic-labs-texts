Level of job satisfaction and its associated factors among midwives working in public health institutions, Northwest Ethiopia: a multi-center study
Authors: Nebiyu Solomon Tibebu, Berihun Agegn Mengistie, Saron Abeje Abiye, Paulos Abeje, Selamawit Tilahun, Sosina Belay, Geremew Azene, Belayneh Ayanaw Kassie
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionMidwives are practitioners of midwifery and are recognized as indispensable professionals for lowering maternal mortality worldwide, particularly in low-income countries. Midwives who aren’t satisfied with their job challenge the prevention and control of maternal mortality and morbidity and had a surge in unfavorable patient outcomes. Therefore, this study aimed to assess the level of job satisfaction and its associated factors among midwives working at public health institutions in northwest Ethiopia.MethodsAn institutional-based cross-sectional study was conducted from February 1st to July 15th, 2022. A total of 422 midwives participated in the study. A simple random sampling method was used to select all eligible participants. Data were collected using a semi-structured, pretested, and self-administered questionnaire. Data version 4.6 software and SPSS 25 were used for data entry and analysis, respectively. Both bivariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses were conducted to find factors associated with midwives’ job satisfaction. The statistical association was decided based on the adjusted odds ratio (AOR) with its 95% confidence interval (CI) and p-value of < 0.05.ResultsThe prevalence of midwives’ satisfaction with their job was 46.7% (95% CI: 41.8, 52.0). Institutions followed democratic leadership (AOR = 2.03, 95% CI: 1.02, 4.40), having good relationships with their superiors (AOR = 2.01, 95% CI: 1.00, 5.02), being satisfied with payment and benefits (AOR = 4.00, 95% CI: 1.45, 9.12), and safe job security (AOR = 5.66, 95% CI: 4.8, 12.68) were found to be statistically significant factors associated with midwives’ job satisfaction.Conclusion and recommendationsIn this study the level of midwives’ job satisfaction was low. Thus, setting strategies in exercising democratic leadership, promoting good relations with superiors, providing incentives opportunities, and ensuring work security will have significant role in improving midwives’ satisfaction with their job.Keywords: Democratic leadership, Job satisfaction, Safe job security, Midwives, Public health institutions, Ethiopia

Introduction

Globally, the midwifery workforce is identified as the essential profession for lowering maternal morbidity and mortality [1–3]. However, according to the World Midwifery Report, approximately 900,000 midwives are in shortage around the globe, primarily in low- and middle-income countries [4]. This global shortage of the midwifery workforce could increase preventable maternal and neonatal morbidity and mortality [4]. Even though it was scarce, many midwives were also found to be less satisfied with their job, and every year, over 36% of midwives leave their jobs [5, 6]. In Ethiopia, the prevalence of midwives’ job satisfaction ranges 21% − 94.4% [6–8].

Midwives’ job satisfaction is recognized as a major priority in every healthcare system, as it correlates favorably with patient satisfaction [9]. Job satisfaction is defined as an employee’s emotional response to various job-related factors that result in pleasure, comfort, confidence, rewards, personal growth, and various positive opportunities, such as upward mobility, recognition, and appraisal done on a merit basis with monetary compensation [5, 10, 11].

According to different studies, job satisfaction of Midwives associated with different factors such as educational level, supportive supervision, salary, professional development and promotion, management and leadership, working environment, autonomy at work, inadequate staffing levels, work demands, medicolegal issues, being a midwife, recognition at work, workplace security, marital status, sex, and work experience [7, 12–17].

A wide range of consequences are thought to follow from low job satisfaction among midwives, including poor job performance, lower productivity, higher turnover and intention to quit, costs associated with recruitment and orientation of new health professionals, the loss of skilled health professionals, periods of short staffing accompanied by exhaustion for remaining midwives, and the potential for an increase in unfavorable client outcomes and decreased organizational performance [18–20].

There are numerous ways to increase midwives’ job satisfaction. Support within the team, a strong relationship with coworkers, praise and support from superiors, autonomy, a working environment, engagement with women, and the ability to promote normal birth are significant drivers that positively affect midwives’ job satisfaction [14, 21–24]. Heavy workloads, a lack of employees and resources, conflicts in work-life balance, and low pay are all factors that lower midwives’ job satisfaction [14, 24–26].

The government of Ethiopia attempted to increase health professionals’ job satisfaction by raising their income, duty payment, risk allowance, and introducing Job Evaluation and Grading (JEG). However, we have assessed that most midwives are not satisfied with their jobs and it’s a major unsolved concern for them. There is also a scarcity of evidence on the midwives’ job satisfaction. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to assess the level of midwives’ job satisfaction and its associated factors among midwives working at public health institutions in Northwest Ethiopia.

Methods and materials

Study design, setting and period

An institutional-based cross-sectional study was conducted in public health institutions found in Central Gondar zones from February 1st to July 15th, 2022. The Central Gondar Zone is one of the thirteen zones in the Amhara regional state. It’s located 738 km from Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, and 182 km from Bahir Dar, the capital town of Amhara regional state. The Central Gondar zone covers thirteen Woreda, and it has a total population of 2,896,928. In central Gondar zone there are 14 districts, 8 hospitals, and 86 health centers. Maternal health care services were provided in all health facilities. There are 650 permanently employed midwives working in these public health institutions.

Study participants and eligibility criteria

The study participants were permanently employed midwives who worked in Central Gondar Zone public health institutions. All midwives who were working in different obstetrics and gynecology wards and available during the data collection period were included in the study. Those midwives who were temporarily employed or provided free service during data collection and had less than six months of work experience were not included in the study.

Sample size determination and sampling procedure

The sample size for this study was determined by using single population proportion formula by considering a 53% prevalence of job satisfaction conducted in Addis Ababa [9], 95% level of confidence, and a 5% margin of error.

= (1.96)2*0.53 (1-0.53)/ (0.05)2 = 383. Where, n = required sample size, α = level of significant, Z = standard normal distribution curve value for 95% confidence level = 1.96, P = proportion, d = margin of error.

Finally, by considering the 10% non-response rate, the minimum adequate sample size for this study was 422. The study was conducted in public health institutions (hospitals and health centers) in the in the central Gondar zone. There were 94 health facilities (eight hospitals and 86 health centers) in the study area. From these five hospitals, 40 health centers were selected using a lottery method. Using the midwife’s registration book as the sampling frame, the sample size was distributed proportionally to the respective hospitals and health centers. Finally, the study subjects were selected using a simple random sampling method (Fig. 1).

Study variables

Dependent variable

Midwives’ level of job satisfaction (Satisfied/ Dissatisfied).

Independent variables

Socio-demographic characteristics

(age, sex, religion, marital status, education level, work experiences, average monthly salary, level of health institution)

Intrinsic motivator factors

(achievement, advancement, recognition and reward, growth and nature of work) and extrinsic or hygienic factors (benefit and payment, leadership style, organizational policy and strategy, physical work environment, relationship with colleagues, relationship with superior, and work security).

Operational definitions and measurements

The dependent variable, midwives’ job satisfaction, was measured using a 29-item job satisfaction scale [27]. Each question is scored on a 5-point Likert scale: 1 for “strongly disagree,” 2 for “disagree,” 3 for neutral,” 4 for “agree,” and 5 for “strongly agree” [27]. To determine the level of satisfaction, the mean value was taken as a cutoff point to determine the level of satisfaction. Midwives who had a score of the mean or above were considered satisfied, whereas participants who scored below the mean were considered dissatisfied with their job [21, 28].

Achievement (low or high), advancement (poor or good), nature of work, growth as well as recognition and reward each of them have their own specific group of questions on a five-point Likert scale and categorized as low or high and poor or good, based on the mean scores of each domain [27].

Data collection procedure and quality assurance

Data were collected using a semi-structured, pretested, and self-administered questionnaire. The questionnaire was first prepared in English and translated to the local language, Amharic, then back to English to check for its consistency. Before the actual data collection, a pretest was conducted on 5% of the sample size to check the responses, language clarity, and understanding of supervisors and data collectors. Five diploma midwives collected the data, and two master’s holders supervised the data collection. Before data collection, training was given to data collectors and supervisors for half a day for a better understanding of the overall process of the data collection procedure. The questionnaire was checked for completeness daily by the supervisors and the principal investigator during the actual data collection period.

Data processing and analysis

Before data analysis, the data were cleaned, cross-checked, coded, and entered into Epidata Version 3.46, then exported to SPSS Version 25 for analysis. Both descriptive and analytic statistical procedures were conducted. Binary logistic regression was used to identify statistically significant independent variables and to control for possible confounder variables having a p-value less than 0.2 in the multivariable logistic regression analysis. In the multivariable logistic regression analysis, variables having a p-value of < 0.05 with a 95% confidence interval for the adjusted odds ratio were considered statistically significant with the outcome variable. In the final model, model fitness was checked using Hosmer and Lemeshow goodness of fit.

Out of a total of 422 participants, only 404 provided a complete response, with a response rate of 95.7%. Among the respondents, 241 (59.7%) were males, and 353 (87.6%) of the participants level of education had a bachelor of degree. The majority of the respondents, 328 (81.2%), were Orthodox followers. The mean age of the study participants was 33.39 years old (SD ± 4.6). Almost half of the study participants, 207 (51.2%), were unmarried (Table 1).

Level of job satisfaction related characteristics

The overall level of midwives’ job satisfaction among midwives working at Central Gondar Zone public health institutions was 46.7% (95% CI: 41.8, 52.0). Factors associated with midwives’ job satisfaction

Bivariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses were conducted to identify factors associated with midwives’ job satisfaction. On bivariable logistic regression, midwives whose age was above 34 years, midwives who worked at health centers, midwives whose level of education was master’s degree (MSc) or bachelor degree (BSc), having good achievement, having high advancement, performing work themselves, getting good recognition and good growth, practicing democratic leadership, having good relationships with colleagues and supervisors, working in a safe environment, getting satisfactory payment and benefits, and working in a comfortable environment had an association with midwives’ job satisfaction. However, on the multivariable logistic regression analysis, practicing democratic leadership, having a good relationship with their supervisor, getting satisfactory payment and benefits, and working in safe security were independently associated factors for midwives’ job satisfaction.

This study found that the odds of midwives’ satisfaction with their job were 2.03 times higher among midwives whose institutions follow democratic leadership styles as compared to midwives who worked in institutions that have undemocratic leadership (AOR = 2.03, 95% CI: 1.02, 4.40).

The odds of midwives’ satisfaction with their job were 2.01 times higher among midwives who had a good relationship with their supervisor compared to midwives who had not (AOR = 2.01, 95% CI: 1.00, 5.02). Likewise, the odds of midwives’ satisfaction with their job were 4.00 times more likely to be satisfied among midwives who were satisfied with their job’s payment and benefits compared to midwives who were dissatisfied (AOR = 4.00, 95% CI: 1.45, 9.12). This study also found that midwives’ satisfaction with their job was 5.66 times higher among midwives who felt safe work security compared to their counterparts (AOR = 5.66, 95% CI: 4.8, 12.68) (Table 3).

Discussion

This study assessed midwives’ job satisfaction and associated factors among midwives working in central Gondar zone public health institutions. Thus, the level of satisfaction with their job was 46.7% (95% CI: 41.8, 52.0). The findings of this study are almost consistent with studies of 43.57% in Tigray, Ethiopia [6], 44.2% in Harari, Ethiopia [29], and 48.6% in Iran [30]. However, the findings of this study were lower than those of 82.6% in Tanzania [31], 58% in Turkey [32], 85% in Australia [33] and 54% in Gondar, Ethiopia [13]. While this study finding was higher than those of 22.6% in Oromia, Ethiopia [34], 41.4% in Western Ethiopia [35], and 41% in Pakistan [36]. This discrepancy might be due to the differences in the socio-demographic characteristics of the study subjects, study population, study setting, study period, and tools used to measure the outcome variable. The lower value for this discrepancy from Turkey study, the study participants were working in private hospitals and they were selected using non-probability sampling, which might increase the level of job satisfaction. However, the current study was conducted in government health facilities, where midwives have lower salaries for their work. In addition to this, the current study used 29-itemed intrinsic and extrinsic factors to measure the level of job satisfaction, whereas other studies used the Minnesota Satisfaction Questionnaire (MSQ) and other satisfaction measuring tools [13].

In this study, practicing democratic leadership, having a good relationship with their supervisor, getting satisfactory payment and benefits, and working in a safe environment were factors that were significantly associated with midwives’ level of job satisfaction.

Regarding factors affecting it, this study found that democratic leadership style was a significant predictor of midwives’ job satisfaction. Thus, the odds of midwives’ satisfaction with their job were 2.03 times higher among midwives whose institutions follow a democratic leadership style as compared to midwives who worked in institutions that have undemocratic leadership. This study was supported by the previous studies conducted in Ethiopia [9, 37], Rwanda [38], and the United States [39]. The plausible explanation is due to the fact that a democratic leadership style can increase midwives’ job satisfaction by creating a positive and empowering work environment that values their contributions, fosters open communication and collaboration, and provides opportunities for autonomy and professional growth [11, 37]. On the contrary, an autocratic leadership style can have a negative impact on midwives’ level of job satisfaction by limiting their empowerment, communication, engagement, and motivation in the workplace [37]. It is important for leaders to consider the effects of their leadership style on their team members’ well-being and job satisfaction to create a positive and supportive work environment.

The odds of midwives’ satisfaction with their job were 4.00 times higher among midwives who were satisfied with their job’s payment and benefits. This finding is consistent with the previous studies conducted in Ethiopia [9, 13], and Iran [11]. The possible explanation could be that providing balanced financial and non-financial payments and benefits opportunities results in ensuring financial security, motivation, promotion, and career growth, all of which are fundamental predictors of job satisfaction [17]. Therefore, it’s essential for healthcare organizations to prioritize fair and competitive compensation and benefits packages to support the well-being and job satisfaction of their midwifery staff.

Midwives who had a good relationship with their supervisor were two times more likely to be satisfied with their job as compared to their counterparts. This finding of this study was similar to those of other studies [12, 40]. This is due to the fact that good relationships with superiors promote a smooth and supportive relationship, recognition, open communication, trust, respect, professional development, and team collaboration, which results in being satisfied with their job. Therefore, it’s important for healthcare organizations to build positive relationships between midwives and their superiors, which is a crucial approach for retaining and enhancing midwives’ satisfaction [5].

This study also found that midwives’ satisfaction with their job was 5.66 times higher among midwives who felt safe work security compared to their counterparts. This finding was consistent with the studies conducted in Senegal [41] and Iran [11]. The likely explanation might be that working in a safe environment and taking workplace security measures can help create a safe and supportive environment for midwives, leading to increased job satisfaction and overall well-being. In sum, creating a conducive, autonomous, and safe working atmosphere has an indispensable contribution to enhancing midwives’ job satisfaction and, ultimately, could improve the quality of maternity care services [42].

Strengths and limitations of the study

To our knowledge, this is the first multi-centered study conducted among midwives in Northwest Ethiopia. This study also came up with independent contributing factors that have important policy implications. However, this study was not without limitations; satisfaction was assessed at one point in time; however, satisfaction is a process and not a single event. This might underestimate or overestimate their level of satisfaction with their job. Due to the nature of the cross-sectional study design, causal relationships are not determined.

Conclusion

This study indicates that the midwives’ level of job satisfaction was low. Practicing democratic leadership, having a good relationship with their supervisor, getting satisfactory payment and benefits, and working in safe security were found to be significantly associated factors with midwives’ job satisfaction.

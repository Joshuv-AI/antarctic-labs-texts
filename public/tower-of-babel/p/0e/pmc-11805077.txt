Domestic Chicken Eggshell-Derived Bone Substitute: Synthesis, Characterization and In Vitro Cell Viability
Authors: Sruthy Prathap, K. S. Rajesh, Nebu G. Thomas, Jeyachandran Venkateshan, M. S. Prathap, Suprith Surya
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Hydroxyapatite (HA) is a material with excellent bioactivity and chemical similarity to bone. It can be extracted from natural sources or fabricated synthetic sources.Objective:To synthesize HA from domestic chicken eggshells and to characterize it using Fourier transform infrared (FTIR), X-ray powder diffraction (XRD), scanning electron microscopy (SEM), energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy, and cell viability.Materials and Methods:Domestic chicken eggshells (Indian breed “Aseel”) were used to synthesize HA through the chemical precipitation method. The processing was conducted at an elevated temperature (900°C). Characterization of the sample was performed using, FTIR, SEM, EDS, and XRD. The cell viability was tested in vitro using methyl thiazolyl tetrazolium assay.Results:Phosphate and calcium ions were confirmed by FTIR and EDS. The carbonate peaks observed were typical of biological apatite. XRD revealed its crystalline property. SEM analysis confirmed the particle size to be less than 100 μm and the presence of a porous structure. The material was found to be noncytotoxic and favored cell growth.Conclusion:The synthesized HA showed a crystalline nature and had good cell viability. Hence, it may be considered as a material for various biomedical applications.

INTRODUCTION

Regeneration of osseous defects is a real challenge for clinicians and requires the use of bone substitutes. Autologous bone graft is considered the most successful technique for bone regeneration. Nevertheless, it often results in patient discomfort, donor site morbidity, and limited availability of bone.

There is an increasing trend for the use of synthetic bone substitutes, which are called alloplasts. Since, the first documented use of a synthetic bone graft was reported in 1892 by Van Meekeren, materials classified as bioceramics have come to extensive use.[1] Hydroxyapatite (HA) is the most extensively, used bioceramic simulates natural bone in chemical composition, crystalline morphology and bioactivity.[2] The bioactivity of HA was the outcome of its osteoconductive attribute, where the graft acted like a scaffold allowing migration and juxtaposition of osteoblasts at the surface of material.[3] HA has an element of calcium phosphate with the chemical formula Ca10(PO4)6(OH)2, which can be synthesized using a variety of methods including precipitation, sol-gel, hydrothermal, and others.[4]

Eggshells are primarily composed of calcium carbonate.[5] Some native chicken breed shells tend to have more minerals than the broiler eggshells (Alam et al 2020).[6] Although there are numerous studies on eggshell-derived HA, there are very few on its synthesis from shells of indigenous chicken breeds. Here we have chosen shells of an Indian indigenous chicken breed namely “Aseel” (origin: western ghat regions of southern India).

The present study is aimed at synthesizing HA utilizing eggshells of an indigenous chicken breed by chemical precipitation method.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Domestic chicken eggshells were cleaned thoroughly, and the inner membranes were removed after which they were dried and powdered in a domestic blender and passed through a sieve. 1 gm of eggshell powder was calcined in a muffle furnace at 900°C for 2 hours to decompose the organic matter and form calcium oxide releasing carbon dioxide. Calcium oxide having the property of being highly hygroscopic gets converted to calcium hydroxide soon after its exposure to the atmosphere.

Calcium hydroxide was weighed and mixed with distilled water to form 0.3 M suspension and reacted with 0.5 M diammonium hydrogen phosphate solution at a stochiometric ratio of Ca/p = 1.67. The unwanted ions from the product were removed by washing repeatedly with distilled water. Following that the sedimented material was filtered and dried in hot air oven at 100°C [Figure 1].[7] The synthesized material was subjected to characterization using Fourier transform infrared (FTIR), X-ray powder diffraction (XRD), scanning electron microscopy (SEM) with gold spluttering, and spectroscopic elemental analysis (EDS).

FT-IR

FTIR (SHIMADZU) spectra analysis in the range from 4000 to 500 cm-1 was employed to characterize the different functional components (groups) present in the material with an intensity mode of % transmittance. Around 45 scans at a resolution of 2–4 cm-1 were conducted.

SEM – EDX

SEM analysis was performed using EVO MA18 with Oxford EDS (X-act), with magnification ranging from 1x to 1,00,000x. The procured material was analyzed at 1 K, 5K, and 10 K magnification.

XRD

Rigaku Miniflex 600 (5th generation) with X-ray generation up to 40 k V and current (15 mA) was used. This apparatus analyses polycrystalline materials including powder, thin films and metals both qualitatively and quantitatively, identifies the phases, and determines the percentage of crystallinity, crystal size, strain, and lattice parameter.

Cell viability

L929 cells were seeded and allowed to attach overnight at suitable conditions. Different concentrations of the sample were tried. Methyl thiazolyl tetrazolium (MTT) reagent was added and incubated for 24 hours. The absorbance was recorded at 570nm using microplate reader (FLUOstar Omega, BMG Labtech). The percentage of viable cells in the sample was calculated to the untreated control.

RESULTS

FTIR

HA samples exhibited well-defined absorption bands in the infrared spectra [Figure 2a]. The spectra showed the phosphate (PO4)3- group, hydroxyl (OH-), and carbonate (CO32-) groups. The structural OH1- vibrational mode was present in the samples at 3309.46 represented by a weak broad peak.

SEM analysis

SEM analysis [Figure 2b-d] revealed flake-shaped crystalline structures and the particle size was less than 100 μm in size. Pore sizes of different ranges were detected using SEM (3.926 μm v and 17.58 μm).

Spectroscopic analysis

Spectroscopic analysis revealed different elements with their weight percentage C(38.34%), O(38.88%), P(8.96%), Ca(13.82%).

Spectroscopic elemental analysis displayed the presence of calcium, oxygen, phosphorus, and carbon.

XRD

Strongest peak was noticed at 31.54 and 31.68 (925). Other peaks at 33.9 and 39.81 and some minor reflections in the 20 range (600 to 658) were detected. Some minor peaks were also observed at 27.56 (675) [Figure 2e].

Cell viability

Eggshell-derived HA was found to be noncytotoxic and favored cell growth and multiplication (rat fibroblasts) [Figure 2f] with increasing concentrations.

At concentrations 12.5,25,50 and 100μg /ml ,cell viability of 189.6%,167.3%,232.7% and 255.9% was found respectively.

DISCUSSION

In the present study, carbonated HA was synthesized by the chemical precipitation method and characterized using FTIR, XRD, SEM, and cell viability tests.

FTIR: HA contains phosphate and hydroxyl groups which are active in the vibrational range of the infrared spectrum and generate peaks at fixed wavelengths. This property was displayed even if they were amorphous, crystalline, or doped products.[8] It has been reported that the peaks obtained in the region of 515.96, 555.62, and 1019.83, represented the presence of a P-O bond. The peaks obtained in the region of 869.6, 1646.67 and 3309.46 confirmed CO3 2-, absorbed H2O and OH – groups.[9] The findings were found in agreement with previous literature on HA extracted from eggshells.[10]

SEM: Natural bone has a complex hierarchical structure and uniform thickness of bone crystals, ranging from 2 to 5 nm. At 1 K magnification, the newly synthesized material appeared to have rod-shaped morphology in the micrographs. These findings were similar to previous reports.[11] Padmanabhan et al.[12] generated HA of width of 70–90 nm and length 400 nanometer using sol-gel process. Rahim et al.[13] (2020) in their SEM observations found an effective pore removal with increase in temperature, subsequently increasing the hardness and density and transformation to β-TCP.

EDS analysis confirmed presence of Ca and Ph, similar to the study by Hsu et al.[14]

XRD: A few sharp peaks were detected showing crystalline nature. A good agreement between the experimental data for eggshell-derived HA (ref code: 09-432) was noted. Similar peaks have been reported previously in the studies of HA synthesis.[15] The collected patterns were compared with the computerized databases of the Joint Committee of Powder Diffraction Standards (JCPDS).[16] Kumar et al. (2023) derived HA from eggshells of two Indian indigenous chicken breeds and confirmed HA as the major phase with a small trace of β-tricalcium phosphate.[17]

Cell Viability: With increasing concentrations of the material, an improved cell viability was observed, which encourages further in vivo studies.

Ganesan et al.[18] used HA soaked in phosphate buffer saline and found good dissolution, bioactivity, and cytocompatibility. Kumar and Girija[19] confirmed the apatite-forming ability and biocompatibility of HA.

CONCLUSION

From the present study, it can be concluded that HA powder can be synthesized from eggshells. XRD revealed strong peaks of the HA phase. The processing conditions were suitable for the synthesis of high-quality HA from domestic chicken eggshells. The phase purity of the eggshell-derived HA was confirmed by FTIR. The synthesized HA showed a good crystalline nature and had good cell viability. Hence, it may be considered as a material for various biomedical applications.

Vaccine diluent affects expression levels of immune related genes in caecal tonsils of layer chickens vaccinated with live attenuated Salmonella Typhimurium vaccine
Authors: Samiullah Khan, Andrea R. McWhorter, Daniel M. Andrews, Gregory J. Underwood, Kapil K. Chousalkar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, Salmonella Typhimurium (ST) vaccine diluent effects on caecal tonsils gene regulation in layer chicks were investigated. A live attenuated ST vaccine was reconstituted in Marek's and/or water diluents, chicks vaccinated and caecal tonsils were assessed for global gene expression at days 7 and 14 post-vaccination. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) involved in the activation of the intestinal immune network for IgA production were upregulated earlier (e.g. day 7 post-vaccination) in the water diluent group, while in the Marek's diluent group, this pathway was enriched by the upregulated DEGs at day 14 post-vaccination. The commonly upregulated biological pathways between the two diluents were cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction, toll-like receptor signaling pathway and cytosolic DNA-sensing pathway. At day 7 post-vaccination, 28.5% and 40.2% of the significantly upregulated genes were unique to Marek's and water diluents, respectively, whereas 31.3% were common between the Marek's and water diluent groups, showing the role of the diluent in priming the vaccine for host immune system modulation. At day 14 post-vaccination, 52.7% and 15.4% of the upregulated DEGs were unique to Marek's and water diluents, respectively. Overall, the data showed that the live attenuated ST vaccine reconstituted in Marek's diluent affected the regulation of immune system-related genes in the caecal tonsils of chicks, with a difference in pattern when water was used as a diluent.

Introduction

Salmonella enterica serovars have a close association with poultry (Ford et al. 2018; Gole et al. 2017; Laidlow et al. 2022). In Australia and other parts of the world, the contamination of foods, in particular chicken meat and eggs, with host universal non-typhoidal serovars represent a significant public health concern (Fearnley et al. 2011; Gole et al. 2014). Poultry often do not exhibit clinical signs of disease (Khan and Chousalkar 2020a); thus, producers have focused on different strategies to mitigate Salmonella on farm. Control of Salmonella cannot be achieved at the farm level through the use of a single strategy; therefore, biosecurity, feed additives and effective farm decontamination procedures are often coupled with vaccination (Galán-Relaño et al. 2023). Live attenuated Salmonella Typhimurium vaccines are registered for use in poultry (Jia et al. 2020). In Australia, an Aroa- live ST vaccine (Vaxsafe ST) is registered for use in broilers, layers and broiler breeders, which is delivered by coarse-spray at day-old, drinking water at 2–4 weeks, and in long-lived birds, at 8–12 weeks of age via intramuscular injection.

It has been shown that wild-type Salmonella Typhimurium infection in chickens upregulates genes involved in caecal immune response and gross lesions in caeca (Khan and Chousalkar 2020b). The live attenuated Salmonella Typhimurium vaccine has shown a significant seroconversion at day 14 post-vaccination both in commercial broilers and layers vaccinated intramuscularly or subcutaneously (Groves et al. 2015). Wild-type Salmonella causes dysbiosis in the chicken gut microbiota (Juricova et al. 2013; Khan and Chousalkar 2020a); however, the changes produced by a live ST vaccine are minimal (Khan et al. 2024). This shows that vaccines may not stimulate the gut mucosal immune system in the way that wild-type Salmonella serovars do.

Vaccination of layer chickens is a key strategy for mitigating wild-type Salmonella in the human food supply chain. The live attenuated Salmonella Typhimurium vaccine (Vaxsafe ST) registered in Australia is designed to elicit an immune response that can protect chickens against wild-type Salmonella Typhimurium. Previously, it was shown that Vaxsafe ST resulted in a significantly elevated antibody response in the blood after an intramuscular injection around week 10–12 of chicken age (McWhorter and Chousalkar 2018). At the transcriptome level, various Salmonella vaccines affect the expression of immune system genes. For example, a Salmonella Enteritidis subunit chitosan nanoparticles-based vaccine up-regulated the expression of IL4, TLR2, TLR4, TGF -ß and INFγ in the caecal tonsils of layers (Renu et al. 2020). A bivalent live attenuated vaccine composed of Salmonella Enteritidis and Salmonella Typhimurium in specific pathogen-free (SPF) chickens resulted in the up-regulation of IFNγ in the caeca (Jan et al. 2024). However, Vaxsafe® ST effects on the regulation of the caecal tonsils immune response at the transcriptome level have not been previously investigated.

Vaccine diluent might affect the vaccine colonisation ability in the host cell gut (Jia et al. 2024). Marek's diluent, which is commercially used for Marek's disease vaccine and Salmonella Typhimurium live attenuated vaccine reconstitution for intramuscular injection, is composed of proprietary ingredients including potassium phosphate, phenol red, sucrose and water for injection. Generally, live attenuated bacterial veterinary vaccines are reconstituted in sterile water prior to administration. Our hypothesis was that rehydration of a live Aroa-deficient Salmonella Typhimurium vaccine in nutrient-rich media containing aromatic amines could enhance vaccine recovery from a freeze-dried state, improving antigenic expression and colonisation of the caecum, and stimulating improved caecal immunity. Previously, it was shown that reconstituting a live Aroa-deficient Salmonella Typhimurium vaccine in Marek's diluent enhanced its colonisation in layer chicken gut (Jia et al. 2024). Irrespective of vaccine diluents, vaccinating chicks at day 1 of hatch does not mount Salmonella specific antibody-response measurable in blood through ELISA. Therefore, there is a knowledge gap about the regulation of genes in the caeca when chicks receive vaccine at day of hatch using a nutritive vaccine diluent. This study was conducted to understand whether the reconstitution of Aroa-deficient Salmonella Typhimurium vaccine in Marek's diluent will enhance gene expression in the caecal tonsils of layer chickens.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

The data included in this study originated from samples collected from an animal trial that was approved by the Animal Ethics Committee at the University of Adelaide (approval number S-2020-076) in accordance with the guidelines specified in ‘Australian code for the care and use of animals for scientific purposes, 8th edition (2013)’.

Caecal tonsils collection from chicks for gene expression study

Considering their vital role in the regulation of the gut immune system in response of vaccination; caecal tonsils were collected from 1- and 2-week-old layer chicks. Immediately after cervical dislocation, approximately 120 mg caecal tonsil samples from the blind end of the caecum were collected from each chicken and stored in RNAlater at −80 °C during a vaccine study, the data of which have been published previously (Jia et al. 2024; Khan et al. 2024). As previously described (Khan et al. 2024), Isa-Brown layer chicks were hatched, vaccinated at the day of hatch or left control and reared until 2 weeks of age as per the Isa Brown Management Guide. Chicks in different treatment groups were housed in separate rooms in raised pens. Chicks were vaccinated via the oral route with Vaxsafe® ST on the day of hatch using water, buffered peptone water and Marek's as the diluent with 3 different doses of vaccine (Jia et al. 2024; Khan et al. 2024). Based on the hypothesis of this study, where our interest was only to understand the effects of Mareks diluent reconstituted live attenuated Salmonella Typhimurium vaccination on chicken immune system regulation, we targeted only samples collected from chickens that received 107 CFU vaccine dose reconstituted in Mareks and water diluents. The 107 CFU dose was selected as the vaccine label dose for vaccinating chickens in commercial poultry production systems. Water diluent was selected as a control diluent, whereas Marek's diluent was used because of its nutritive nature and has already been registered as a vaccine diluent for Salmonella Typhimurium at week 8 of chicken age and for Marek's disease vaccination at day of hatch. From the chicks using water and Marek's as diluents and their controls (n = 32), caecal tonsils collected in RNA-Later at days 7 and 14 post-vaccination were processed for RNA-sequencing. The collected caecal tonsil tissue samples in RNA-Later at −80 °C were processed for RNA extraction within 3 months of collection. There were 2 treatment groups (Marek's and water diluents) and two sampling time-points (day 7 and 14 post-vaccination) and from each treatment group, there were a total of 4 samples processed for RNA-sequencing.

Caecal tissue RNA extraction via TRIzol method

Total RNA was extracted from approximately 120 mg of caecal tonsil tissue using a modified method of TRIZol (Invitrogen). Briefly, tissue samples stored in RNAlater at −80 °C were thawed on ice and weighed into 2 mL Eppendorf tubes and maintained on ice. Into individual samples, 1 mL of TRIZol was added, and maintained on ice. To preserve RNA integrity, 10 µL beta mercaptoethanol was added into each sample, homogenised using a IKA T10 basic homogenizer while maintaining the 2 mL tubes on ice in 30 mL container. The samples were incubated on ice for 7 min. In the next step, 0.2 mL of bromochloropropane (BCP) was added into each sample and incubated on ice for 5 min. The samples were centrifuged for 15 min at 12,000 × g at 4 °C to separate the RNA from the DNA and proteins. From the top aqueous layer containing RNA, 0.5 mL was transferred into 1.5 mL tube, and the samples were maintained on ice. To precipitate RNA, 0.5 mL chilled propanol (100%) was added into each sample, mixed and incubated for 10 min on ice. The samples were centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was discarded, the RNA pellet (clearly visible) was washed with 1 mL of 75% ethanol (chilled), and the samples were centrifuged for 5 min at 7500 × g at 4 °C. The supernatant was discarded, and the RNA pellets of individual samples were allowed to dry for 10 min on ice. The individual RNA pellets were dissolved in 50 µL PCR grade water and proceeded for RNA purification as detailed below.

RNA purification using RNeasy Mini Kit

As per the protocol of the RNeasy Mini Kit, all the steps were performed at room temperature. To perform, RNase-Free DNase digestion step, 2.5 μL DNAse I (Qiagen), 10 μL buffer RDD and 37.5 μL PCR grade water were added into individual RNA samples and incubated at room temperature for 10 min. To purify the RNA, 350 μL of Buffer RLT was added to individual samples and mixed by inversion. Next, 250 μL ethanol (absolute) was added into individual samples and mixed by pipetting. Individual samples (700 μL) were transferred to spin columns and centrifuged at 8000 × g for 15 s. RNA in the spin columns was washed twice with RPE buffer, and the samples were centrifuged as per the manufacturer's recommendation. RNA was eluted in 50 μL of RNAse-free water and maintained on ice until tested its quality and concentration in TapeStation 2200 (Agilent Technologies).

RNA quality and quantity testing

The RNA samples were assessed in a Nanodrop 1000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Melbourne, Australia) for quantity and purity. The original RNA samples were stored at −80 °C and, within a month, were transported on dry ice to SAGC (South Australian Genomic Centre) for RNA-sequencing and basic bioinformatics analyses. SAGC performed quality check on the total RNA using 2200 TapeStation. The quantity was assessed by Qubit 2.0 fluorometer (Life Technologies, Australia).

The RNA samples (n = 32) were processed by SAGC (South Australian Genomic Centre) for RNA-Sequencing and basic bioinformatics analyses. Several reports (Khan and Chousalkar 2020b; Kumar et al. 2020; Russell et al. 2021) have shown a positive correlation of RNA-Sequencing and reverse transcriptase qPCR data; therefore, for this experiment, further validation of the transcriptomic data through qPCR was not performed.

RNA-sequencing of chicken caecal tissue

cDNA synthesis and poly(A) selection

Illumina's TruSeq Stranded mRNA Library Prep Kit was used for processing of chicken caecal tonsil RNA samples. The process included mRNA purification via poly-T oligo-attached magnetic beads, RNA fragmentation, first-strand complementary DNA (cDNA), second-strand cDNA synthesis and cDNA purification. The steps involved in RNA-sequencing were in-line with the standardised protocol of SAGC (Supplementary File S1). The RNA samples were sequenced using Illumina NovaSeq 6000 with 150 bp paired-end reads and 2500 million reads coverage per sample. Libraries were generated according to the Universal Plus mRNA-seq protocol (NuGen Technologies) that included 10 cycles of amplifications.

Bioinformatics analysis

Quality control and sequence data evaluation

Pre-processing of sequencing data was completed on the SAHMRI (South Australian Health and Medical Research Institute) High Performance Computing (HPC) involving Quality control (QC) of raw fastq files, alignment of raw (fastq) files to the chicken reference genome assembly (GCA_016699485.1) using STAR and quantification of gene expression with featureCounts.

Reference genome assembly and gene models for alignment and quantification of gene counts

Alignment to the reference chicken genome assembly (GCA_016699485.1) was very high, with over 75% of the reads from each sample aligning to unique loci (gene models). The quantification involved counting the number of reads aligning to the Gallus gallus genome corresponded known gene models. The samples had good quantification with at least 38M reads assigned to known gene models.

Functional annotation of significantly regulated DEGs

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) with log2 fold changes greater or less than 1.5 were annotated to immune system pathways in the ClueGO and CluePedia plugins in Cytoscape software (Cline et al. 2007). For significantly enriched pathways visualisation, p < 0.05 was chosen, and the KEGG immune system pathways were visualised as nodes with bigger size reflected its regulation significance. Venn diagrams of the diluent unique or common genes were created in Venny 2.1 (Oliveros 2007).

Results

Vaccine effects on regulation of genes involved in caecal immune response

All the RNA samples (n = 32) had good integrity, with the RNA integrity number (RIN) ranging from 8.7 to 9.8 on a scale of 0–10 (Supplementary File S2). To investigate the effects of a live ST vaccine reconstituted in Marek's and water diluents on chicken caecal tonsils immune system regulation, RNA-sequencing was performed on caecal tonsils collected at days 7 and 14 post-vaccination. Based on the hypothesis of the experiment, which was to understand the vaccination effects on caecal immune system gene regulation, the significantly regulated genes were annotated in Kyoto Encyclopaedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways specific for the immune system (Supplementary File S3). For accurate interpretation of the data, the cut off criteria for selecting the significantly regulated genes were log2 fold change >1.5 or <−1.5 and FDR < 0.05. The gene annotation data showed that Marek's diluent reconstituted vaccine resulted in the upregulation of genes (n = 214) in the caeca involved in cytokine–cytokine reception interaction, nucleotide-binding oligomerization domain (NOD)-like receptor signaling pathway, the Toll-like receptor signaling pathway and the cytosolic DNA-sensing pathway at day 7 post-vaccination (Figure 1a). The topmost upregulated genes included MMP7, IL4I1, SLC13A2, S100A12, EXFABP, CCL26 and MMP9 (Table 1; Supplementary File S3). There were 39 significantly down-regulated genes in the Marek's diluent vaccinated treatment group at day 7 post-vaccination that did not annotate to any KEGG immune system pathway (Supplementary File S3). The topmost significantly down-regulated genes included C8orf22, ABCB5, CYP2AC2, AOX2, CALB1, ALDOB and CCDC92B.

Discussion

The main objective of the current study was to understand the effects of vaccine diluents on gene regulation in the caecal tonsils of 1–2-week-old chicks vaccinated with a live attenuated Salmonella Typhimurium vaccine. The data showed that vaccination affected the regulation of several immune system-related genes when the vaccine was reconstituted in Marek's or water diluent with a difference in gene regulation pattern. For example, genes involved in the humoral immune response showed delayed regulation in chicks that received the vaccine in Marek's diluent compared to the water diluent.

RNA-sequencing data revealed that Vaxsafe ST reconstituted in Marek's and water diluents, when administered to chicks, regulated many genes involved in immune system pathways, with distinct differences in the patterns of gene regulation between the two diluents. For example, in the Marek's treatment group, at day 7 post-vaccination, the regulated genes mainly annotated to KEGG immune system pathways of cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction, cell adhesion molecules (CAMs), the Toll-like receptor signaling pathway, the NOD-like receptor signaling pathway, and the cytosolic DNA-sensing pathway. These data show that vaccination resulted in up regulating many genes involved in the mucosal response, with a difference in the pattern of regulation due to the nature of the diluent used for vaccine reconstitution. Although not significantly quantifiable Salmonella specific antibody in the blood through ELISA after the oral vaccination of layer chicks at day 1 of hatch were reported earlier (Jia et al. 2024), the live attenuated ST vaccine reconstituted in Marek's and water diluents resulted in the up-regulation of genes involved in the caecal tonsils immune system both at days 7 and 14 post-vaccination. This shows that the live attenuated ST vaccine is beneficial for the protection of layer chickens.

Toll-like receptors (TLRs), such as TLR2, TLR4, TLR5, TLR15 and TLR21 are involved in Salmonella recognition following infection (MacKinnon et al. 2009; Paul et al. 2013). Interestingly, in the current study, the Salmonella Typhimurium vaccine reconstituted in Marek's diluent resulted in the upregulation of SPP1, IL8L2, IL8L1, IL1B and CCL4, which were mapped to the Toll-like receptor signaling pathway. TLR15 was also upregulated, with a log2 fold change of 3.05. Additionally, TLR1A was significantly (FDR < 0.05) upregulated, but the log2 fold change was 1.22, which was below the cut-off threshold for gene annotation data analysis. Other non-annotated genes that were upregulated and involved in the Toll-like receptor signaling pathway included CXorf21 (log2 fold change 1.62). TLR4, TLR1B, TLR7 and TLR1A, which are involved in the Toll-like receptor signaling pathway did not significantly regulated at day 7 in the guts of chickens that received the Salmonella vaccine reconstituted in Marek's diluent. A recent study has shown the upregulation of TLR1A, TLR1B, TLR2, TLR4, TLR5, TLR7 and TLR15 at days 7 and 14 post-wild-type Salmonella Enteritidis challenge in the caecum of laying hens (Jiang et al. 2023). Overall, the data showed that live attenuated ST vaccination in chickens up-regulate many genes from the Toll-like receptor pathway for protecting the host against wild-type Salmonella infection.

In the chicken caecum, wild-type Salmonella cells are recognised by TLRs followed by the induction of cytokines and chemokines (Rychlik et al. 2014). IL1B is a proinflammatory cytokine that has been shown to be upregulated in the intestine of Salmonella Typhimurium challenged broilers (Fasina et al. 2008). CD4, which is an important gene in the production of CD4+T lymphocytes (T helper cells), was upregulated irrespective of the vaccine diluents in the current study. IL6, which was previously shown to be a key upregulated gene in the caeca of chicks challenged with wild-type Salmonella Typhimurium (Khan and Chousalkar 2020b), did not significantly regulated in this vaccine study.

The live ST vaccine reconstituted in Marek's and water diluents and administered to day-old chicks resulted in the upregulation of genes involved in cytokine‒cytokine receptor interactions, with a difference in the pattern of gene regulation. For example, CXCR1, IL13RA2, CXCL13L2, CSF3R, IL18R1, IL8L2, IL8L1, IL1B, IL1R2 and IL22, which were upregulated in the Marek's treatment group, did not significantly change in the water diluent treatment group at day 7 post-vaccination. However, some of the genes whose expression was upregulated at day 7 in the Mareks's treatment group were up-regulated in the water diluent group at day 14 post-vaccination. This shows that the diluent shifted the timing of the regulation of certain genes within the caecal tonsils of layer chickens. On the other hand, IL2RG, CCL5, CCR6, CD4, CD40LG, TNFRSF13C, CXCL13L2, TNFSF8, XCR1 and CSF3R only upregulated in the water diluent treatment group. All these genes play roles in overall host immune system regulation; however, their different pattern of regulation in the current study show the role of diluent effects on host immune system modulation in Salmonella vaccination.

The most significant pattern in the regulation of genes in the chickens vaccinated using Marek's and water diluents was for the genes annotated to the ‘intestinal immune network for IgA production’. Genes involved in this biological pathway were upregulated at day 7 post-vaccinated in the water diluent treatment group showing diluent-specific effect on vaccine priming. In the Marek's diluent treatment group, this biological pathway was upregulated at day 14 post-infection. This delayed regulation might show the potential role of Marek's diluent in stimulating the immune response later, which may last longer compared with the water diluent. However, to test this hypothesis, further studies are required. The difference in the pattern of the regulation of genes relative to the two different diluents was further clarified by the data mapping using Venn diagrams. Secretory IgA in the layer chicken gut has been linked to protection against wild-type Salmonella Enteritidis (Mon et al. 2021). Interestingly, the consistent upregulation of MMP7 in the Marek's diluent group at both days 7 and 14 post-vaccination further supports the use of this diluent for Salmonella vaccine reconstitution. MMP7 has been shown to be the most upregulated gene in the caeca of layer chickens (Matulova et al. 2013).

Overall, the data showed that reconstituting live attenuated ST vaccines in Marek's and water diluents and vaccinating day-old chicks with it resulted in the up-regulation of numerous genes from multiple immune system pathways. This shows that although the vaccine does not result in an increase in Salmonella specific antibody measurable in the blood using ELISA, it still results in stimulation of the caecal tonsil immune system. Pattern observed in the difference in regulation of genes attributed to Marek's or water diluent showed that both diluents are appropriate; however, Marek's can be preferred because of its effects on the delayed on-set of genes regulation from the IgA related pathway. Lastly, Marek's diluent can also be tested as a potential diluent for other live attenuated poultry vaccines.

Conclusions

The transcriptome analysis of the caecal tonsils of layer chickens vaccinated orally with a live attenuated Salmonella Typhimurium vaccine reconstituted in Marek's and water diluents showed immune system regulation, with a pattern of difference attributed to the diluent. The Marek's diluent reconstituted vaccine resulted in the delayed induction of genes involved in mucosal IgA production, which could play a role in a longer duration of immunity.

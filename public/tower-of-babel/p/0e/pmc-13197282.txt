Exploration of the signaling pathway involving OsCSN8 in regulating the leaf angle of rice
Authors: Xinhai Yu, Zihao Wang, Chunwen Li, Chuhan Zhang, Yanxi Liu, Chunyu Zhang, Miao Xu, Ming Wu, Liquan Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLeaf angle serves as a crucial determinant of rice plant architecture. The erect leaf phenotype is widely regarded as ideal for achieving high yields. We observed that OsCSN8 overexpression induced leaf erectness at the heading stage, indicating that OsCSN8 regulated leaf angle. This study focused on OsCSN8, a core subunit of COP9 signalosome (CSN), analyzing leaf angle phenotypes in OsCSN8 mutants under varied phytohormone treatments. Integrating Western blot, Bimolecular Fluorescence Complementation, and Yeast Two-Hybrid data elucidated the molecular mechanism: OsCSN8 modulates the second complete leaf angle by interacting with OsBZR1, a central brassinosteroid (BR) signaling transcription factor. Furthermore, at the heading stage, OsCSN8 affected the leaf angle of rice flag leaf by regulating the thickness of adaxial parenchyma and the number of cell layers. These findings reveal the functional role of OsCSN8 in the regulation of rice leaf angle and offer valuable genetic resources for breeding programs focused on agronomic trait improvement.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00122-026-05264-z.

Introduction

Rice is a globally dominant grain. It is the staple food for nearly half the world’s population. Rice yield is a critical determinant of food security, and plant architecture serves as a key factor affecting yield. Therefore, more research now targets the “ideal plant type.” Erect leaves and smaller leaf angles are especially important in this ideal type (Singh et al. 2022). Rice yield depends heavily on leaf function. Therefore, leaf morphology directly affects both the transpiration rate and photosynthetic efficiency (He et al. 2018). Erect leaf phenotype represents one of the key morphological characteristics of plant architecture. It allows plants to capture more light energy and photosynthesize more efficiently in dense planting. This helps increase yields (Guo et al. 2023). Additionally, an optimal leaf angle in rice allows plants to acclimate to the cultivation environment. Consequently, it is necessary to investigate the mechanism of OsCSN8’s function in regulating the development of leaf angle in rice.

The COP9 signalosome (CSN) is a protein complex that is highly conserved in higher eukaryotes (Dohmann et al. 2008; Wei et al. 2008), usually in the form of a stable eight-subunit (CSN1 to CSN8) complex. CSN complexes regulate diverse physiological processes in plants (Bai et al. 2021; Han et al. 2023), including protein ubiquitination and degradation (Qin et al. 2020), photomorphogenesis (Nishimura et al. 2025), and the signaling pathways of various phytohormones, such as ethylene, abscisic acid, and auxin. It has been reported that conditional knockdown of CSN8 in mouse peripheral T lymphocytes disrupts CSN complex formation. Consequently, CSN8 plays a pivotal role in maintaining the structure of the complex. The relatively simple structure of CSN8, which contains only one proteasome-COP9-initiation factor 3 (PCI) domain subunit (Deng et al. 2000), predicts that the PCI domain may play an important role in CSN8, which relies heavily on the PCI domain in maintaining the structural integrity of the complex. This structural domain has been found to be predominantly present within the CSN complex, eukaryotic initiation factor 3 (eIF3) (Glickman et al. 1998) and the 26S proteasome (Pick et al. 2009). Furthermore, the PCI domain has the capacity to mediate the interaction of CSN with other coregulators, thereby contributing to the regulation of multiple signal transduction pathways.

Leaf angle is a key agronomic trait that determines rice yield. In addition to genetic factors, two main mechanisms also influence leaf angle. One regulatory pathway relates to mechanical tissues (Xu et al. 2021). The other regulatory pathway involves phytohormone signals. Phytohormone regulation is the primary form of leaf angle regulation. The plant phytohormone brassinosteroid (BR) is known to play crucial roles in regulating the leaf angle (Li et al. 2020). BR also impacts early development of lamina joints significantly. OsBZR1 is a crucial component of the BR signaling pathway. BR suppresses OsGSK2 kinase function, causing dephosphorylation of OsBZR1 and its migration to the nucleus. This process triggers the activation of downstream BR-responsive genes, influencing rice growth and development. Leaf angle alteration is controlled primarily by three mechanisms: modifications in the shape of lamina joint cells, cell division, and cell wall synthesis (Huang et al. 2021; Li et al. 2023). Enhanced expansion or proliferation of adaxial parenchyma cells (Zhang et al. 2015) and abnormal formation of vascular bundles (Ning et al. 2011) can result in changes to leaf angle. For instance, upregulation of the cell elongation gene ILI1 stimulates elongation of proximal cells and adjusts leaf inclination (Zhu et al. 2021). CSN8 serves as a crucial regulator of the cell cycle (Liu et al. 2013) and may influence tumor progression and cell proliferation (Ju et al. 2013). However, further research is required to confirm if OsCSN8 regulates the cell cycle. Therefore, it is essential to investigate whether OsCSN8 affects rice leaf angle by controlling cell division in the lamina joint.

BR interacts with other phytohormones, such as gibberellic acid (GA) (Tong et al. 2014; Unterholzner et al. 2015) and jasmonic acid (JA) (Wang et al. 2020), to jointly regulate leaf angle development. GA serves as a positive regulator of rice leaf angle (Luo et al. 2016), operating either independently or synergistically with BR. In contrast, JA treatment inhibited the expression of genes associated with BR biosynthesis and reduced endogenous BR levels, resulting in a decreased leaf angle. Additionally, the activation of GSK3-like kinases, which serve as negative regulators of the BR signaling pathway, partially counteracted the inhibitory effect of JA on leaf angle (Gan et al. 2015). Therefore, this study addresses two central questions: Which phytohormone pathway regulates rice leaf angle through OsCSN8, and what is the regulatory mechanism?

Materials and methods

Plant material and culture conditions

The OsCSN8 gene (LOC_Os04g34930) is situated on chromosome 4 of the rice genome. OsCSN8 produces a full-length mRNA of 1204 bp, which contains a 591 bp coding sequence that encodes a protein of 196 amino acids. Among the subunits of the CSN complex in rice, OsCSN8 exhibits the lowest molecular weight and least conservation, predominantly localizing to the nucleus. The construction method and source of the OsCSN8 mutants (Fig. S1) were provided by our laboratory. The seeds of wild type (Oryza sativa subsp. japonica), OsCSN8-reduced (oscsn8) mutants, and OsCSN8 overexpression (OsCSN8-OE-1; OsCSN8-OE-2) mutants were divided into three groups. Then, 0.8% (w/v) agar medium was prepared, and the 5 seeds of wild type (WT) and OsCSN8 mutants were sown on the medium. The seeds were placed in a light incubator (Shenzhen Lvheng Lighting Technology Co., Ltd., Shenzhen, China) with a light irradiation of 203 μmol m−2 s−1 and a photoperiod of 12 L:12 D. The incubation temperature was set at 28℃, relative humidity was maintained at 95%, and the seeds were incubated for a period of 48 h. Uniformly germinated rice seeds were sown in vermiculite and immediately supplied with rice nutrient solution to fully submerge the roots. The nutrient solution was refreshed at 12-h intervals. The seedlings were then grown hydroponically for 12 days under identical light and temperature conditions. The formulation of the rice total nutrient solution was conducted in accordance with the conventional nutrient solution formulation as outlined by the International Rice Research Institute (IRRI). Exposure of rice seedlings to 0.1 μM BR led to a marked increase in the second leaf angle, with seedlings exhibiting normal growth and no signs of chlorosis (Fig. S2). In contrast, treatment with brassinazole (BRZ), showed a limited effect on leaf angle reduction, likely because the second leaf angle was already minimal under hormone-free conditions. Based on these observations, 0.1 μM BRZ was chosen for further experiments, as it reduced the second leaf angle relative to WT controls while preserving normal leaf morphology (Fig. S3). Control seedlings were cultured in nutrient solution without hormone supplementation.

Phenotypic analysis of rice

Seeds of WT and OsCSN8 mutant lines were imbibed for 48 h and subsequently exposed to hormone treatment for 12 days, after which the second complete leaf angle of rice was measured. Measurements of flag leaf angle and cytomorphological analysis of the flag leaf pulvinus were carried out at the heading stage, specifically when the panicle had emerged approximately 2 cm from the flag leaf sheath. All measurements for each group were replicated three times with a sample size of 5 (n = 5).

Protein extraction and western blot

The second complete leaf lamina joint tissue (0.3 g) was cryogenically pulverized in liquid nitrogen. The powder was homogenized in Extraction Buffer (1 mL plant RIPA lysate with 10 μL PI, 10 μL PMSF, and 3 μL DTT) and vortex-mixed. Following centrifugation at 4℃ for10 min, the supernatant was collected. Supernatants were combined with 5 × SDS-PAGE loading buffer and denatured by boiling for 10 min.

Western blot was performed essentially as described by (Mahmood et al. 2012). Protein samples underwent electrophoretic separation using 10% SDS–polyacrylamide gels. Resolved proteins were electrotransferred onto PVDF membranes employing a Bio-Rad Trans-Blot Turbo blotting system. Non-specific binding sites on the membranes were blocked using 4% skim milk. Membranes were then incubate with specific primary antibodies: rabbit polyclonals antibodies included OsCSN2, OsCSN5, OsBZR1, and an anti-plant actin mouse polyclonal antibody. After primary antibody incubation, PVDF membranes were treated with goat anti-rabbit and goat antimouse IgG secondary antibody (HRP). Target protein bands were ultimately detected via chemiluminescence. All antibodies were sourced from Wuhan ABclonal Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China).

Total RNA extraction and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction

RNA was isolated from seedlings using a Spectrum Plant Total RNA Kit (Sigma-Aldrich, Darmstadt, Germany). First-strand cDNA synthesis was carried out from 5 µg of total RNA using the StarScript II First-Strand cDNA Synthesis Mix with gDNA Remover (GenStar, Beijing, China). Quantitative PCR (qPCR) assays were conducted in triplicate using StepOnePlusTM real-time PCR device (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Applied Biosystems, Waltham, MA, USA). Reactions contained 2 × RealStar Green Fast master mix with ROX dye (GenStar, Beijing, China). Transcript abundance for target genes was normalized against the GAPDH reference gene (JN848809) and quantified using the comparative Ct method. Primer sequences are provided (Tab. S1).

Spatiotemporal positioning

A translational fusion of OsCSN8 with green fluorescent protein (GFP) was constructed and effectively expressed in rice. Subcellular localization of the OsCSN8-GFP fusion protein was observed in the leaves, coleoptiles, and roots of 9-day-old transgenic seedlings using laser scanning confocal microscopy. Fluorescence images were captured using a Leica TCS SP8 CARS confocal microscope (Leica, Wetzlar, Germany) with a 10 × 0.40 eyepiece and 20 × and observed with an objective lens of 0.70. GFP signals were excited at 488 nm and emission was collected between 500 and 530 nm.

Bimolecular Fluorescence Complementation

Target gene sequences were amplified using specific oligonucleotide primers (Table S2). The vectors pSAT4A-cEYFP-N1 and pSAT4A-nEYFP-N1 were linearized by EcoRI (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) and BamHⅠ (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). The construction of the recombinant plasmids OsCSN8-cEYFP and OsBZR1-nEYFP was achieved by utilizing the ClonExpress II One Step Cloning Kit-C112 (Vazyme, Nanjing, China). The recombinant plasmids OsCSN8-cEYFP and OsBZR1-nEYFP, vectors nEYFP and cEYFP, were transferred into EHA105 (Coolaber, Beijing, China), respectively. The negative controls comprised OsCSN8-cEYFP + nEYFP, and the experimental groups comprised OsCSN8-cEYFP + OsBZR1-nEYFP. Agrobacterium was mixed in equal proportions, infested the onion epidermis for 30 min, and then incubated in the dark for a period of between 16 and 48 h. The onion epidermis was then subjected to a further incubation with Agrobacterium. Yellow fluorescent protein (YFP) signals were monitored using excitation wavelength of 514 nm and emission wavelength of 527 nm.

Yeast Two-Hybrid

Target gene sequences were amplified using specific oligonucleotide primers (Tab.S3). The pGADT7 and pGBKT7 plasmids were procured from Myriad Plasmid Platform (Wuhan, China). The primers used in the experiment are shown in Table S3. The experimental group comprised the pGADT7-OsCSN8 + pGBKT7-OsBZR1, the positive control pGADT7-T + pGBKT7-53, and the negative control pGADT7 + pGBKT7, pGADT7-OsCSN8 + pGBKT7, pGADT7 + pGBKT7- OsBZR1 and pGADT7-T + pGBKT7-lam. These were co-transformed into yeast strain AH109 (Coolaber, Beijing, China) for Yeast Two-Hybrid analysis. Transformed yeast cells were cultivated in SD-Trp-Leu medium and maintained at 30℃ for a period of three days. Subsequent validation was conducted by incubating the cells in SD-Trp-Leu-His-Ade medium for a duration of four to five days at 30℃.

Statistical analysis

The data are expressed as the standard error of the mean for the control group and the experimental group. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) test followed by Duncan’s test. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05. The results of western blot were analyzed using ImageJ software (1.52V). Statistical analyses employed Excel 2019 and SPSS 28.0, while graphical representations were generated with GraphPad Prism 8.

Results

OsCSN8 is involved in the regulation of leaf angle in seedling rice

This study examined the role of OsCSN8 in regulating the leaf angle of rice by treating WT and OsCSN8 mutant seedlings with exogenous hormones and observing their leaf angle phenotypes. In 14-day-old rice seedlings, the second complete leaf angle of oscsn8 mutants was found to be significantly greater than that of OsCSN8-OE and WT (Fig. 1A). Compared to the control group that did not receive added plant hormones, BR treatment increased the second complete leaf angle in rice seedlings. Furthermore, a reduction in the expression level of the OsCSN8 protein results in rice seedlings displaying an increased angle in the second complete leaf. (Fig. 1B). In contrast, treatment with BRZ, an inhibitor of brassinosteroid, was found to inhibit the increase in the second complete leaf angle in rice seedlings (Fig. 1C). Consequently, the OsCSN8 protein functions as a negative regulatory factor for the leaf angle in rice, and its modulation of rice leaf angle may be contingent upon the BR signaling pathway.

The cytomorphological analysis to investigate the role of OsCSN8 in regulating leaf angle in rice

OsCSN8 controls leaf angle through BR signaling at the seedling stage. In order to explore if OsCSN8 also regulates leaf angle at the heading stage, we sectioned lamina joint (Fig. 2) and the flag leaf angle (Fig. S4) from naturally grown WT and OsCSN8 mutants. Compared to WT and OsCSN8-OE mutants, oscsn8 mutants exhibit an increase in parenchyma thickness and the number of parenchyma cell layers on the adaxial side of the lamina joint, accompanied by the presence of large sclerenchyma clusters. In contrast, the sclerenchyma clusters in OsCSN8-OE and WT plants are significantly smaller than those observed in oscsn8 mutants. These findings suggest that reduced levels of OsCSN8 protein result in an increase in the number of parenchyma cells, parenchyma thickness and the size of sclerenchyma clusters in the adaxial lamina joint. These cytological alterations may be associated with changes in leaf support structures, a hypothesis that requires further validation.

Western blot analysis

The predicted secondary structure of OsCSN8 consists primarily of alpha helixs, β-sheets, and random coils (Fig. S5A). The alpha helices are mainly located on the surface of the protein (Fig. S5B). In higher plants, impairment of CSN8 results in the destabilization of CSN1, CSN3, CSN5, CSN6, and CSN7, with the CSN2 and CSN4 subunits remaining largely stable (Gusmaroli et al. 2007). This study focused on the OsCSN2 and OsCSN5 subunits for western blot analysis in the OsCSN8 mutants to ascertain whether alterations in the leaf angle of rice seedlings are regulated independently by the OsCSN8 subunit or are contingent upon its role in forming a stable CSN complex. Furthermore, phenotypic observations of leaf angle indicate that OsCSN8 may influence rice leaf angle through its involvement in the BR signaling pathway. As a key transcription factor within this pathway, OsBZR1 regulates a series of target genes associated with cell elongation and division. Consequently, this study also performed western blot analysis to assess OsBZR1 protein levels in the OsCSN8 mutants.

The expression level of OsCSN2 in oscsn8 mutants did not differ significantly from that of WT. In contrast, the expression level of OsCSN5 in oscsn8 mutants was down-regulated compared to WT. These results corroborate the findings of Gusmaroli, who demonstrated that loss of function of OsCSN8 triggers the destabilization of OsCSN5 subunit, while having no effect on OsCSN2 subunit. Under natural conditions (Fig. 3A), the expression level of OsBZR1 in OsCSN8 mutants was significantly upregulated. Following BR treatment (Fig. 3B), the expression level of OsBZR1 was increased in OsCSN8 mutants. Under BRZ treatment (Fig. 3C), the expression of OsBZR1 in OsCSN8 mutants was significantly down-regulated. However, no significant difference in the expression level of OsBZR1 was observed between oscsn8 and OsCSN8-OE mutants under either natural or phytohormones treatment. This suggests that OsCSN8 may facilitate the transition of OsBZR1 to an active state or nuclear localization, rather than influencing the protein level of OsBZR1.

Analysis of relative expression of related genes in OsCSN8 mutants under different treatments

The present study investigates the molecular mechanism by which OsCSN8 regulates rice leaf angle, with a focus on proteomic differences in the lamina joint of WT and OsCSN8 mutants. Analysis of differential protein expression levels indicates that OsCSN8 plays a significant role in cell wall biogenesis (Fig. S6). Notably, differential proteins such as OsBC1L4, OsWRKY53, and OsCESA7 are essential for cellulose biosynthesis and cell wall structure (Chen et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2016). Additionally, expansion proteins like OsEXPA10 and OsEXPB4 facilitate cell wall relaxation and expansion (Che et al. 2016). These findings suggest that alterations in cell wall biosynthesis may substantially influence rice leaf angle and overall plant architecture. Furthermore, studies indicate that OsCSN8 can regulate the cell cycle (Guo et al. 2021). For instance, CSN8 is crucial for cell proliferation in mouse hepatocytes (Lei et al. 2011) and contributes to maintaining an appropriate duration of the G1 phase (Liu et al. 2013). To determine whether the regulation of rice leaf angle by OsCSN8 is associated with the cell cycle and cell wall biosynthesis, we conducted verification using qRT-PCR (Fig. 4).

Gene expression in relation to the second complete leaf lamina joint cells of WT and the OsCSN8 mutants under different conditions. A Gene expression under natural condition. B Gene expression under BR treatment. C Gene expression under BRZ treatment. Data are presented as mean ± SD from three independent biological replicates. Different letters above the bars indicate statistically significant differences in gene expression as determined by Duncan’s multiple range test (p < 0.05)

Under natural conditions (Fig. 4A), the expression of OsBC1L4 and OsWRKY53 genes related to cell wall cellulose biosynthesis in oscsn8 mutants was significantly up-regulated. Additionally, the expression of OsEXPA10 and OsEXPB4 genes, which are related to cell expansion, was also significantly increased. The cell cycle genes (Shultz et al. 2007; Qi et al. 2012) OsMCM2 and OsCYCT1-3, along with the tubulin gene OsTUBA1 were also increased. Following BR treatment (Fig. 4B), the expression of OsEXPA10, OsTUBA1, OsWRKY53 (Tian et al. 2017), and OsCESA7 in the oscn8 leaf lamina joint was significantly upregulated. These findings suggest that BR may regulate the rice leaf angle by enhancing the expression of genes involved in cell wall cellulose biosynthesis and cell elongation in lamina joint cells.

Functional analysis of OsCSN8 and OsBZR1 protein interactions

Predictive analysis of OsCSN8 and OsBZR1 interactions

Protein–protein docking is facilitated via HDOCKlite v1.1 Local Edition server. Utilizing HDOCK for docking (Fig. S7A), model 1 exhibits the lowest binding energy, with a docking score of -277.37 and a confidence score of 0.9274. This indicates that the probability of OsCSN8 and OsBZR1 binding in this configuration is notably elevated. The free energy of binding, calculated using the MM/GBSA protocol from the HawkDock server, resulted in a value of -68.13 kcal/mol.

It is widely accepted that hydrogen bond is an important type of intermolecular interaction. Indeed, the prevailing hypothesis suggests that the binding affinity of the ligand is known to increase by an order of magnitude for each additional hydrogen bond. The docking pockets of OsCSN8 and OsBZR1 were visualized by PyMOL (Fig. S7B), revealing the formation of seven pairs of hydrogen bonds between the two proteins. Consequently, there is possibly an interaction between OsCSN8 and OsBZR1.

The validation of Bimolecular Fluorescence Complementation between OsCSN8 and OsBZR1

The interaction between OsCSN8 and OsBZR1 was first validated by Bimolecular Fluorescence Complementation experiments. The recombinant vectors, OsCSN8-cEYFP and OsBZR1-nEYFP, were successfully generated (Fig. S9; Fig. S10). The OsCSN8-cEYFP and OsBZR1-nEYFP vectors exhibited yellow fluorescence in the nucleus following co-culture in onion epidermal cells. In contrast, no yellow fluorescence was detected in the nucleus of OsCSN8-cEYFP and nEYFP, cEYFP and OsBZR1-nEYFP, and control vectors of cEYFP and nEYFP (Fig. 5). It demonstrates an interaction between OsCSN8 and OsBZR1, thereby suggesting a potential role for OsCSN8 in the regulation of OsBZR1 activity.

The validation of Yeast Two-Hybrid between OsCSN8 and OsBZR1

The existence of interactions between OsCSN8 and OsBZR1 could be initially demonstrated based on Bimolecular Fluorescence Complementation experiments, followed by Yeast Two-Hybrid experiments (Fig. 6). The yeast strain demonstrated robust growild typeh in the SD-Trp-Leu medium, thereby signifying the successful transformation of the pGADT7-OsCSN8 and pGBKT7-OsBZR1 vectors into the yeast strain and their subsequent normal expression. In the ensuing experiment, the positive yeast strain was transferred to the SD-Trp-Leu-His-Ade medium containing 40 mM 3-AT. The yeast strain exhibited normal growild typeh, thereby indicating that OsCSN8 can interact with OsBZR1.

Discussion

OsCSN8 functions as a negative regulator of leaf angle in rice

Spatial expression profiling showed that OsCSN8 exhibits pronounced tissue-specific expression in 9-day-old rice seedlings (Fig. S8). Notably, OsCSN8 was undetectable in coleoptiles, while weak GFP fluorescence was observed in the leaves of OsCSN8-OE mutants. This expression pattern aligns closely with the functional assignment of OsCSN8 in leaf angle regulation. Although abundant OsCSN8 expression was also observed in roots, this does not diminish its role in shoot morphogenesis; rather, it points to functional pleiotropy mediated by tissue-specific expression, enabling OsCSN8 to participate in both root development and leaf angle control. Collectively, these observations suggest that leaf-specific expression of OsCSN8 is essential for its regulatory function in determining the second leaf angle.

Plant hormones regulate the differentiation and maturation of lamina joint cells (Zhou et al. 2017). The second leaf angle of oscsn8 was significantly larger than that of WT and OsCSN8-OE mutants. This indicates that OsCSN8 functions as a negative regulator of rice leaf angle. Additionally, BR promoted an increase in the leaf angle of rice seedlings, with OsCSN8 mutants exhibiting a significantly greater increase compared to WT. This result implies that the regulation of OsCSN8 in leaf angle likely depends on BR signal transduction. In heading rice, a decrease in OsCSN8 protein expression leads to an increase in the thickness of the adaxial parenchyma, a greater number of cell layers, and an enlargement of sclerenchyma clusters in the rice flag leaf lamina joint. This change promotes an increased tilt angle of the rice flag leaf. However, the present study mainly elucidates the molecular mechanism by which OsCSN8 regulates leaf angle at the seedling stage. While cytological observations were performed at the heading stage, a full assessment of agronomic performance across the entire growth cycle remains to be conducted. In future studies, multi-year and multi-location field experiments will be carried out to systematically evaluate the impact of OsCSN8 on yield and related agronomic traits.

OsCSN8 regulates leaf angle by interacting with the BZR1 transcription factor in the BR signaling pathway

OsBZR1 serves as a fundamental transcription factor within the BR signaling pathway, orchestrating plant growth, development, and stress responses through the regulation of downstream genes. The expression of OsBZR1 showed no significant difference in the OsCSN8 mutants. (Fig. 3). Furthermore, the interaction between OsCSN8 and OsBZR1 suggests that OsCSN8 may regulate OsBZR1 not through modulating its protein expression level, but rather by affecting its phosphorylation status or nuclear localization. Consequently, OsCSN8 may further influence rice leaf angle by interacting with OsBZR1 within the BR signaling pathway. Proteomic analysis and qRT-PCR results indicate that the regulation of leaf angle by OsCSN8 is linked to the expression levels of genes associated with cell wall biosynthesis. Based on this, it is speculated that the possible mechanism by which OsCSN8 regulates the leaf angle of rice seedlings (Fig. 7). However, further investigation is needed to determine elucidate how OsCSN8 regulates OsBZR1 in BR signal transduction.

Conclusions

It has been determined that OsCSN8 plays an instrumental role in preserving the stability of the CSN complex. However, it is plausible that OsCSN8 does not solely rely on the CSN complex for functionality. The present study demonstrated that OsCSN8 functions as a negative regulator of leaf angle in rice. In the process of sensing BR signal, OsCSN8 interacts with OsBZR1, potentially regulating the transcriptional activity of OsBZR1 on downstream target genes. This mechanism is distinct from the previously established role of CSN in regulating hormone receptors or E3 ligases. Thus, in turn, resulted in the regulation of leaf angle in rice seedlings. The interaction between OsCSN8 and OsBZR1 demonstrates the important role of OsCSN8 in regulating rice growild typeh through its involvement in the BR signaling pathway. Furthermore, OsCSN8 was found to regulate leaf angle by affecting the cell wall biosynthesis of lamina joint in the proximal axis of the flag leave during the heading stage. It is proposed that, in accordance with the aforementioned mechanism, OsCSN8 may be utilized as a molecular breeding target in the development of new rice varieties characterized by optimal leaf angles. This approach is expected to enhance the crop’s photosynthetic use efficiency and adaptability to a changing environment. By optimizing plant morphology, it is possible to enhance crop yields. Moreover, this approach opens a new avenue for the development of ideal rice varieties.

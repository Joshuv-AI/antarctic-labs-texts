Triangular fuzzy game modelling for internal innovation in platform-driven manufacturing enterprises under uncertainty
Authors: Yongquan Guo, Han Zhang, Hua Zou, Deyu He, Chuan Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEnvironmental uncertainty poses a significant barrier to collaborative innovation in platform-driven manufacturing enterprises. However, existing studies often overlook the platform’s dynamic coordination role, and the quantification of uncertainty. This paper investigates the collaborative interaction processes and key influencing factors within the internal innovation ecosystem of platform-based manufacturing enterprises under environmental uncertainty using a triangular fuzzy evolutionary game model. Subsequently, we conducted numerical examples and case validation of the model results, which demonstrated that: (1) External environmental uncertainty primarily affects organizational collaborative stability through three pathways: punitive fluctuations, cognitive divergence, and information arbitrage. (2) The difference between collaborative and non-collaborative benefits drives strategic choices.The risk of free-riding and unilateral revenue growth can significantly weaken the willingness to cooperate. (3) Platform resource integration mitigates the impact of uncertainty through a dual mechanism: On one hand, it reduces collaboration costs by leveraging global resources, reusing technologies, and digitizing processes; on the other hand, it enhances incentives through additional benefits such as two-way collaboration compensation, effectively expanding the feasible probability range of the collaboration strategy. (4) The double-edged sword effect of fines is reflected in two aspects: on the one hand, they compel employees to engage in cooperation through coercive constraints; on the other, they diminish the dynamism of innovative groups.This paper for the first time constructs a triangular fuzzy game model involving ‘platform-innovation group-employees’, integrates platform governance variables and realizes the quantification of uncertainty, providing strategic guidance for collaborative innovation within platform-based manufacturing enterprises, thereby fostering the development of their internal innovation ecosystems, and it aligns with the trend of sustainable and people-oriented manufacturing transformation. Furthermore, this study identified specific ways model results improve operational performance, offering practical insights for manufacturing enterprises.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-47051-2.Keywords: Platform-enabled manufacturing enterprises, Synergistic innovation, Triangular fuzzy numbers, Game theorySubject terms: Environmental social sciences, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The platform-enabled transformation has disrupted the traditional functional-matrix organizational structure of manufacturing companies1. It has transformed the original business departments into internal innovation teams. This has altered the traditional top-down corporate innovation process, giving rise to an internal innovation ecosystem centered on internal innovation teams and involving all employees. Within this system, the key to generating innovative outcomes lies in the collaborative innovation between internal innovation teams and corporate employees2. However, this process is subject to various factors and faces significant challenges posed by environmental uncertainty. The main manifestations are as follows: First, innovation teams and employees engage in innovation activities based on their perceptions of user value3, but it is difficult for the two parties to reach consensus, leading to discrepancies in value perception. Second, as the platform facilitates the rapid aggregation and circulation of diverse resources4, the environment in which innovation actors operate undergoes continuous transformation. Third, if platforms fail to update data in a timely manner or the information they filter is not sufficiently accurate, this can create data barriers, leading to information asymmetry. Fourth, humans cannot make completely rational choices because they cannot obtain all possible knowledge about optimal goals and cannot guarantee that the likelihood of achieving a particular goal will be maximized. Due to numerous uncertain factors, the willingness and decision-making effectiveness of internal innovation subjects (innovation teams and employees) in platform-enabled manufacturing enterprises are affected, thereby hindering the enhancement of platform-enabled enterprises’ core competitiveness of .Therefore, under conditions of environmental uncertainty, analyzing the decision-making mechanisms of internal innovation subjects in platform-enabled manufacturing enterprises and clarifying the factors that influence their strategic choices are important issues. The triangular fuzzy evolutionary game model in this study essentially aligns with the core concept of people orientedness. Unlike traditional game theory, which assumes complete rationality, triangular fuzzy numbers account for cognitive fuzziness, limited rationality, and subjective uncertainty among decision-makers. These are precisely the human traits that need to be retained and enhanced in the industrial 5.0 era. Therefore, this model provides a mathematical basis for quantifying human uncertainty in platform governance and offers a new perspective on how platform governance evolves to promote human-machine collaboration. Theoretical basis and literature review

To precisely analyze the collaborative logic of internal innovation within the platform in uncertain environments, this chapter will conduct a comprehensive review from three aspects: theoretical foundation, research progress, and framework reconfiguration, laying a solid theoretical foundation for model construction. Firstly, in response to the limitations of traditional game theory in handling ambiguous information in innovation decisions, this chapter will analyze the applicability of fuzzy game theory and triangular fuzzy numbers in organizational decision-making, and clarify their core advantages in quantifying subjective uncertainty; Secondly, it will systematically review the research status of fuzzy games in the field of innovation cooperation, revealing the imbalance characteristic of existing research that ‘emphasizes external factors and neglects internal factors’, and identifying the three core gaps: application scenarios, variable setting, and uncertainty quantification; Finally, based on the theories of platform governance and ecosystem architecture, this chapter will reconfigure the theoretical framework of internal innovation within platform enterprises, clarify the governance essence of the platform and the self-organizing characteristics of internal innovation, and identify the intersectional gap between fuzzy game theory and platform governance theory, thereby establishing a ‘theory - gap - innovation’ logical chain for subsequent model construction.

Analysis of the applicability of fuzzy game theory and triangular fuzzy numbers

In the field of organizational decision-making, traditional game theory has long relied on the core assumptions of ‘complete rationality’ and ‘deterministic gains’5. The internal innovation process within manufacturing enterprises is essentially a dynamic system full of high uncertainty and information asymmetry. Given this inherent characteristic, the fuzzy game theory integrates the fuzzy set theory to construct a mathematical framework for handling fuzzy information, addressing issues related to subjective cognitive biases and fuzzy information, and creating a new mathematical framework6. In management decision-making scenarios where historical data is lacking, or the environment is highly complex, fuzzy logic can more accurately reflect human reasoning than traditional binary logic7.

Among various fuzzy tools, triangular fuzzy numbers (TFNs) have become the core tool of this study due to their unique advantages in innovative decision-making. Compared with trapezoidal fuzzy numbers and interval fuzzy numbers, triangular fuzzy numbers only require the establishment of three anchors: ‘pessimistic value’, ‘most likely value’, and ‘optimistic value’8. Their simple structure closely aligns with decision-makers’ cognitive patterns when assessing innovation risk, thereby reducing cognitive load in complex decision-making environments9. As a classic tool in operational research decision-making methods, triangular fuzzy numbers offer a good balance between computational simplicity and the ability to capture cognitive uncertainty, and are particularly suitable for environments with incomplete information10. For example, when an internal innovation team of an enterprise predicts the market return of a new product, defining a fuzzy return range such as [100,000, 150,000, 200,000] is far more practical and operable than providing an exact probability distribution.

Recent empirical studies have further verified that triangular fuzzy numbers not only can effectively quantify qualitative indicators such as ‘technical feasibility’ and ‘market acceptance’ in product innovation11, but also possess strong robustness, and extreme outliers in subjective evaluations can be effectively smoothed12. Therefore, the previous studies that use triangular fuzzy numbers to convert language variables in innovation cooperation into computable fuzzy returns provide a solid theoretical basis for this study to construct a ‘fuzzy perception model of returns and costs’.

The rise of privacy computing technology provides a new technical realization path for fuzzy game theory. The traditional fuzzy game addresses the ambiguity of the decision maker’s subjective cognition, while privacy computing technology characterizes data governance capabilities in the technical dimension, eliminating concerns about data sharing through the ‘data available but invisible’ mechanism. Fuzzy logic captures the cognitive biases and bounded rationality of innovation subjects, privacy computing technology eliminates privacy barriers and trust deficiencies in data sharing, and the two work together to shape the payment matrix of platform governance.Federated learning-empowered smart manufacturing and product lifecycle management13.

Research progress on fuzzy games in innovation cooperation

A review of the existing literature reveals that research on innovative cooperation fuzzy games exhibits a non-equilibrium characteristic, with more studies from an external perspective and fewer from an internal enterprise perspective. Most of the existing research focuses on cross-organizational cooperation such as supply chain collaboration and industry-university-research alliances, and widely adopts fuzzy evolutionary game methods to analyze the strategic interaction and strategy selection of innovation entities at the macro level, and explores the evolution paths of factors such as government subsidies and digital transformation on the stability of cooperation. This research trajectory mainly focuses on external knowledge acquisition and cross-border cooperation14. In the supply chain field, fuzzy games have been applied to solve problems such as uncertain demand, logistics optimization, and supplier selection in closed-loop supply chains15,16. But these studies often assume that the participating entities have a relatively high level of decision rationality.

In contrast, research on the ambiguous game within the internal innovation ecosystem of platform-based manufacturing enterprises is extremely scarce. Although platform transformation enables internal entities to share organizational resources, their decision-making remains influenced by cognitive biases. Industry 4.0 emphasizes automation and efficiency, while Industry 5.0 shifts towards human-centric manufacturing, emphasizing that technology enhances human capabilities rather than replacing them. Digital innovation management has shifted from traditional hierarchical control to an internal process arrangemen17. According to the cognitive bias theory, individuals will exhibit nonlinear preferences in uncertain situations18. In internal innovation, this manifests as value cognition differences, namely, differences in the assessment of platform resource value between the innovation team and employees. However, existing models do not account for the ambiguity in the game caused by cognitive biases19.The triangular fuzzy method employed in this study precisely addresses the uncertainty in human cognition, providing a tool for people-oriented governance and effectively compensating for this research deficiency. Additionally, information asymmetry leads to misallocation of resources. For manufacturing enterprises empowered by the platform, data is their key resource. However, the environment’s uncertainty makes the definition of data ownership vague, and users’ concerns about information security are increasing. This makes it even more difficult to achieve data sharing within the system. This limits the flow and sharing of data elements. How to coordinate the data and resources of all parties and effectively release the value of data is an important issue20.Internal innovation within enterprises faces unique ‘resource frictions’, and these key issues have not yet been incorporated into the existing game analysis framework, resulting in a disconnection between theory and practice21.

Based on the above analysis, the current research still has deficiencies in the following areas: Firstly, in terms of application scenarios, the existing research mostly focuses on cooperative innovation from an external perspective, neglecting collaborative scenarios in which digital platforms drive internal innovation ecosystems within organizations. Especially, without accounting for the development trend of human-centered intelligent manufacturing, the core influence of human cognition in the internal innovation game has been overlooked. Secondly, in terms of variable settings, the existing models mostly follow the traditional binary framework of cost and benefit in game theory, lack structural integration of platform-specific governance, and rarely consider quantifying uncertainty.,and fail to effectively respond to the quantitative demands for human cognitive uncertainty in the context of people-oriented principles.

Reconstruction of the theoretical framework for internal innovation in platform-based enterprises

To go beyond the phenomenon description of platform-driven manufacturing enterprises, this study analyzes the internal innovation operation mechanism from the perspectives of platform governance theory and ecosystem architecture theory. The platform is not merely a technical architecture, but also a core governance mechanism. This governance mechanism coordinates the ecosystem’s operations by allocating decision-making power and control22. Existing research indicates that leading enterprises enhance organizational innovation flexibility by platform transformation internal resources23.

In this study, the platform serves as a ‘governance entity’ by integrating resources through the dual dimensions of basic resource sharing and technological empowerment, thereby regulating the collaborative process among innovation groups and employees.This governance mechanism, on the one hand, involves the open sharing of explicit and implicit knowledge, physical resources, and data among platform participants. On the other hand, the platform focuses on technological tools such as digital collaboration systems, data encryption, and intelligent matching algorithms to break down barriers to data sharing and protect privacy. This dual-dimensional integration mechanism is consistent with the core viewpoints of platform governance theory22 and also responds to academic calls in the field to quantify the enabling mechanisms of platforms at the micro level23. Its strategic core lies in balance and control24, and its value lies in promoting the ‘friction interaction’ capability25. Therefore, the platform no longer manages innovation through administrative instructions but reduces transaction costs to achieve ‘empowerment’, which constitutes the key assumption that distinguishes this model from traditional hierarchical management.

From the perspective of ecosystem architecture, an ecosystem is a self-evolving system composed of multiple partners that deliver value propositions. It differs fundamentally from hierarchical organizations in that it relies on complementary assets to achieve value co-creation26. The transformation of manufacturing enterprises has led to the evolution of organizational architecture from vertical hierarchies to modularization. The layered modular architecture theory explains how heterogeneous resources can generate innovation through flexible reorganization27. This theoretical perspective is consistent with Haier’s practice, where internal units have transformed into self-organizing micro-enterprises28. With the change in task dependence, coordination mechanisms must shift from authoritative to market types29. Therefore, the relationship between innovation groups and employees has shifted to a contractual, collaborative arrangement based on value adjustment mechanisms and penalty functions. This theory deconstructs the evolutionary process into a dynamic circular system. The core view is that evolution does not tend toward a static equilibrium but is influenced by cognitive biases. A non-equilibrium process driven by ‘evolutionary power’ such as resource constraints, the ‘variation’ dimension in the framework directly corresponds to the fuzzy cognition of innovation entities, that is, corresponding to fluctuations in revenue and cost perception; The ‘choice’ dimension corresponds to the mechanism of the platform regulating ‘choice pressure’ through resource integration, providing theoretical explanations for the dynamic revision of evolutionary paths.

From the perspective of logical transmission paths, the driving effect of fuzzy cognition on evolutionary dynamics is concretely reflected in that environmental uncertainty triggers significant variations in innovation entities ‘perception of synergy benefits. This variation is presented in the form of interval fluctuations of triangular fuzzy numbers. As a core evolutionary force, it breaks the convergence conditions of traditional deterministic equilibria and promotes the selection of strategies into the dynamic adjustment process of repeated games. Cross-theoretical integration and the unique contributions of this study

This research aims to bridge the gap between fuzzy game theory and the platform governance theory. Platform theory scholars have expounded on the necessity of governance structures but rarely quantified decision-making dynamics at the micro level, while game theory scholars have provided mature mathematical tools but often deviate from the specific context of platform governance, leaving the two theories unable to connect effectively. The academic community has clearly called for future research to deeply explore the ‘dynamic capabilities’ of managing the platform ecosystem, especially how governance rules specifically affect the cooperative behavior of complementary parties. This research is a direct response to this academic call. Existing research has confirmed that collaborative innovation can improve the operational performance of manufacturing enterprises31,32, but there is a lack of relevant quantitative correlation between innovation parameters and actual operational indicators such as production efficiency, resource utilization rate and production flexibility, which makes it difficult for enterprises to transform their innovation strategy into operational efficiency optimization.

The unique contributions of this research are mainly reflected in three dimensions: First, scenario innovation. It has for the first time constructed a triangular fuzzy game model for the interaction among ‘platform-innovation group-employee’, filling the gap in the application of fuzzy game theory in the internal innovation ecosystem of enterprises; Second, variable innovation. It breaks through the traditional ‘cost - benefit’ binary analysis framework and creatively introduces platform governance variables such as resource integration. The degree of resource integration is decomposed into two aspects: basic resource sharing and technological empowerment. Mathematically, the dual-path mechanism of platform empowerment is revealed. This reveals how the platform’s empowerment capacity shapes the equilibrium solution of internal game dynamics, directly aligning with the academic suggestion for in-depth exploration of the micro-mechanism of platform network effects. Third, quantitative innovation. Based on the quantitative advantage of triangular fuzzy numbers in uncertainty, this study integrates them into evolutionary game models to address the shortcomings of traditional deterministic models in characterizing cognitive biases and information ambiguity. This method more scientifically reconstructs complex innovation environments, which is highly consistent with the academic consensus that ‘platform uncertainty has multidimensional characteristics and requires complex modeling techniques’.Fourth, the practical value has increased. The model’s quantitative results, along with a detailed analysis of the case’s action mechanism, clarify how platform governance parameters relate to core operational indicators of manufacturing enterprises. These indicators include production efficiency and resource utilisation rate. The model provides a useful tool for turning innovation collaboration strategies into clear operational steps, making the research conclusions more practical.

Mathematical models

On the premise of having a clear theoretical foundation, this chapter will focus on the two core tasks of ‘how to quantify uncertainty’ and ‘how to construct a game model in the platform context’, gradually completing a complete logical loop from the definition of fuzzy tools to the construction of the game model. First, define the basic definitions and operation rules of triangular fuzzy numbers, clarify their unique advantages compared to traditional probability theory and interval analysis, and provide mathematical tools for quantifying cognitive differences and information asymmetry in innovative decision-making; second, based on the actual context of the platform’s internal innovation ecosystem, propose assumptions related to game participants, strategy choices, and revenue and cost, clarify the economic connotations and real-world mappings of each parameter; on this basis, respectively construct the game payment matrices in the scenarios of no platform and platform resource integration, derive the expected revenue functions and optimal solutions of mixed strategies, and reveal the regulatory mechanism of platform governance variables on the game equilibrium. Finally, through quantitative sensitivity analysis and Monte Carlo simulation, preliminarily verify the rationality of the model and the positive impact of platform resource integration on collaborative strategies, providing model support for subsequent numerical examples and case validations.

Triangular fuzzy numbers

Triangular fuzzy numbers are one of the methods used to represent fuzzy information in a simple and direct manner.

From the perspective of their application scenarios, compared to traditional probability theory, the two differ fundamentally in both mathematical structure and interpretation33. The main differences are as follows: traditional probability theory primarily addresses uncertainty arising from the randomness of events, while fuzzy set theory, including triangular fuzzy numbers, focuses more on addressing uncertainty caused by imprecise information34. Specifically, triangular fuzzy numbers align with the cognitive characteristics of innovative decision-making. The environmental uncertainty analyzed earlier includes factors that are difficult to quantify, such as decision-makers’ cognitive biases and bounded rationality. This leads to assessments of future market trends, the likelihood of technological breakthroughs, and how to respond to competitors, which are often not single precise values but rather fuzzy intervals and tendencies. For example, if a decision-maker believes that the market acceptance of a new technology ‘may range between 30% and 60%, with the most optimistic estimate being 50%,’ triangular fuzzy numbers are used to convert the three points of ‘pessimistic-normal-optimistic’ or some linguistic evaluations (such as ‘high,’ ‘medium,’ and ‘low’) into specific (a, b, c) fuzzy numbers35, thereby retaining the original judgment’s fuzzy interval and core tendency.

Additionally, triangular fuzzy numbers have the following advantages over interval analysis. Interval analysis only has upper and lower bounds at the ends of the interval, and its numerical definition is based on binary logic, i.e., ‘belongs to’ or ‘does not belong to’ the interval, with arithmetic operations performed according to the boundaries. Triangular fuzzy numbers provide a formalized definition of numerical intervals as triplets, enabling the distinction of ‘high-probability values’ and preserving the ‘probability structure’ after operations, thereby effectively quantifying uncertainty. Kilincci and Onal’s vendor selection experiments found that triangular fuzzy numbers only need three anchors: pessimistic, most likely, and optimistic to complete information extraction. Cognitive load assessment showed that its easy-to-understand score was 18% higher than that of the trapezoidal form, and 34% higher than that of the type-2. The simplicity of parameters and interview-friendliness are reflected here35.

Thirdly, triangular fuzzy numbers describe environmental uncertainty in a manner that is readily accepted by people and applied in practical operations. Compared to trapezoidal fuzzy numbers, interval-type fuzzy numbers, and hesitant fuzzy sets, triangular fuzzy numbers have a simpler and more straightforward concept, containing only three parameters: lower bound, most likely value, and upper bound36. This makes them easier for decision-makers and analysts to understand and use. Furthermore, research on green technology strategies indicates that triangular fuzzy numbers can be used in strategic choice studies involving multiple uncertainties and complex situations. For example, triangular fuzzy numbers can be used to assess automotive companies’ selection and application of green technology innovation strategies, demonstrating their applicability to strategic decision-making that considers long-term impacts and multiple stakeholders37,38. The multi-case study conducted by Siweiec et al. reveals that when decision-makers are simultaneously faced with triple uncertainties regarding technology, market, and policy, the application of triangular fuzzy numbers can elevate the questionnaire response rate from 63% to 82% while reducing model prediction errors39. Consequently, the information fidelity capability in strategic decision-making scenarios is validated hereby. Sugapriya et al. further noted that trapezoidal and interval type-2 fuzzy numbers are prone to support set drift after multiple-step operations, which makes subsequent analytical differentiation challenging to achieve closure. In contrast, triangular fuzzy numbers consistently retain their three-parameter structure during addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division operations, thereby providing an analytically tractable mathematical basis for the proof of equilibrium existence36.

Basic hypothesis

The collaborative innovation process between innovation ecosystems and employees within a platform enterprise is a repeated dynamic game.Environmental uncertainty can impact the internal innovation process of platform-based manufacturing enterprises. For example, first, environmental uncertainty leads to information ambiguity, which in turn causes differences in value perception and results in fluctuations in implicit returns. Second, regarding collaboration costs, environmental uncertainty exacerbates decision-making complexity, leading to resource misallocation risks and causing changes in the rigidity of collaboration costs. Third, environmental uncertainty affects the contract execution risks of collaborative innovation partners, leading to fluctuations in the effectiveness of punitive deterrence. Fourth, environmental uncertainty causes market fluctuations, leading to divergent returns. Based on the above analysis of this paper, there are two research subjects: the enterprise internal innovation group and enterprise employees. The first dimension of α, α₁, reflects the cross-departmental sharing ability of production factors and technology. This ability, through the leveraging of organizational assets and manufacturing technology, enhances resource utilization and reduces operational costs57. Therefore, α₁ represents resource utilization and operational costs. α₂ reflects the supporting ability of digital tools and intelligent algorithms. Information technology resources promote knowledge sharing, process integration, and operational coordination, which can enhance production flexibility and accelerate market response speed57,58. Therefore, α₂ represents production flexibility and market response speed.

The x/y variables have a significant positive correlation with innovation performance, that is, the improvement of the collaborative probability can effectively promote production efficiency and the efficiency of new product launch59–61. Therefore, it is represented as production efficiency and new product launch efficiency.

F refers to the governance intensity of the platform against opportunistic behaviors such as free-riding and information fraud. Some studies have pointed out that this variable is related to operational stability and process compliance rate62,63. Therefore, it is represented as operational stability and process compliance rate.

E1/D1 includes the costs generated during the collaboration process of the innovation group and employees64,65. This variable is related to manufacturing operation costs and resource waste rate.

ɤ reflects the degree of trust erosion. Some studies have pointed out that trust erosion will directly lead to the loss of team effectiveness and productivity decline66,67. Therefore, this variable is related to collaborative efficiency and on-time order delivery rate.

A1/B1 represents the value increment created by the innovation group and employees through collaborative creation68. This variable is related to profit margin and new product market share. A reasonable profit distribution ratio has a positive impact on the coordination of the entities, and a reasonable value distribution mechanism is the key to maintaining the stable operation of the ecosystem69.

Quantitative sensitivity analysis with sensitivity curves

Figure 3 presents the results of 10,000 Monte Carlo simulations. It shows the probability distributions of the innovation group’s and employees’ collaborative strategies in both the platform-free and platform-present scenarios. From the results, it can be seen that integrating platform resources has a significant positive regulatory effect on the stability and enthusiasm of collaborative strategy selection. In the platform-free scenario, the peak of the x~ distribution of the collaborative probability of the innovation group is concentrated in the range of 0.4 to 0.45, with an average of 0.4325 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.0398–0.9217. The peak of the y~ distribution of the collaborative probability of the employees is concentrated in the range of 0.35 to 0.40, with an average of 0.4012 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.0516–0.8742. Both exhibit fluctuations, with standard deviations of 0.2531 and 0.2348, respectively. In the platform integration scenario, the peak of the x* distribution of the collaborative probability of the innovation group shifts to the range of 0.55 to 0.60, with an average of 0.5682, an increase of 31.4% compared to the platform-free scenario, and a 95% confidence interval of 0.0512–0.9803. This indicates that the platform’s human-oriented governance mechanism has shifted the probability of collaboration from the “unstable human game” to the “sustainable human-machine symbiotic range”, verifying the stability of human subjectivity under technological enhancement. The peak of the y* distribution of the collaborative probability of the employees shifts to the range of 0.5 to 0.55, with an average of 0.5237, an increase of 30.5% compared to the platform-free scenario, and a 95% confidence interval of 0.0589–0.9617. The standard deviations of x* and y* are 0.2154 and 0.2017 respectively, and both have shrunk relatively (CV reduction ≈ 15–18%).This indicates that technological empowerment does not reduce the volatility of human decision-making by restricting choices, but rather enhances decision confidence through cognitive support.

Monte Carlo simulations under different subjects.

In the absence of a platform, the probability distribution of collaboration among innovation groups and employees shows a left-skewed and broad-peak shape, with peaks at 0.4 and 0.35, respectively. The dispersion is high, and the fluctuations are intense, indicating that the collaborative system lacks a stable mechanism in the absence of a platform, and the group strategy selection shows a high degree of randomness. In the presence of a platform, the probability distribution of collaboration shifts significantly to the right, and the concentration increases. The peak of the innovation group reaches 0.55 (a shift of 37.5%), and the peak of the employee group reaches 0.5 (a shift of 42.9%). The 95% confidence interval narrows by approximately 30%, confirming the platform’s role in reshaping the collaborative system’s structure. When extended to the industrial 5.0 scenario, human-oriented governance creates a predictable human-machine collaboration environment, allowing human innovators to freely explore and innovate within the boundaries of certainty. This indicates that the platform amplifies the collaborative benefits through the matching efficiency coefficient θ, suppresses the non-cooperative benefits through the default penalty coefficient ϕ, weakens the long-term non-cooperative benefits through the trust attenuation coefficient γ, and combines the benefit distribution coefficient α and the additional incentive Eb. These measures fundamentally change the payment matrix and strategy space of the evolutionary game, causing the probability of dual-group collaboration to converge to a stable range.

It should be noted that this study does not focus on pure-strategy equilibrium, primarily because of the platform’s uncertainty and dynamic nature of innovation. Pure strategy equilibrium requires decision-makers to possess complete rationality and complete information symmetry, which contradicts the ambiguity of innovation decisions and the limited rationality of decision-makers. On the other hand, mixed strategy, through probabilistic strategy selection, can not only depict decision-making preferences under uncertainty but also capture the dynamic evolution process of strategies, making it more suitable for the research context. The model solution results show that the probability interval of the mixed-strategy equilibrium effectively reflects the joint influence of uncertainty and platform governance variables, and its conclusions have stronger practical explanatory power and practical guidance value than those of the pure-strategy equilibrium. Comparisons of model parameters show that improved platform resource integration increases the manufacturing system’s resource utilization rate. Higher collaboration probability between innovative groups and employees boosts production and new product launch efficiency. Moderate punishment enhances operational stability. However, an excessively large punishment coefficient reduces production flexibility. Overall, this quantitative relationship provides enterprises with a direct basis for optimizing operational indicators.

Comparison of system dynamic evolution trajectories.

Regarding the convergence speed and stability of the system, the probability of collaboration between the innovation team and employees in the absence of the platform scenario needs approximately 82 iterations to converge to a steady state value of 0.5000, and the number of iterations for the convergence of the probability of collaboration among employees is approximately 83. Case study

To further verify the practical applicability of theoretical models and numerical conclusions, this chapter selects Hai’er Smart Home, a typical platform for internal innovation ecosystem, as a case study. Through the coding logic of grounded theory and the cross-validation of theory-case, it deeply analyzes the collaborative mechanism of internal innovation on the platform and the path of platform governance.

Background and research methodology

This study selected Hai’er Smart Home as a case study to validate and supplement conclusions derived from numerical examples. During its operation, this autonomous innovation cluster implements self-regulation through algorithmic dynamic auctions, floating penalty mechanisms, and implicit incentives. This aligns with key characteristics of internal corporate innovation ecosystems-self-organization, self-regulation, and self-sustaining profitability-while leveraging Haier’s HOPE platform to achieve resource coordination. Furthermore, the analysis draws upon textual materials from Haier’s Official information and related papers. And the enterprise faces typical environmental uncertainties, such as the impact of the pandemic and semiconductor shortages, thereby reflecting the broader external uncertainty. Key statements were employed to validate the interactive strategic processes and critical influencing factors of innovation entities within uncertain environments.

Research methods and data sources

An exploratory case study approach was adopted, combined with grounded theory coding logic. Through a three-level coding process of ‘open coding-main axis coding-selective coding’, core categories and logical relationships were extracted from the case data. At the same time, the coding results were cross-verified with the parameter influence laws and equilibrium results of the triangular fuzzy evolutionary game model, achieving a closed loop of ‘theoretical derivation - case verification - mechanism explanation’.

The data for this study were sourced from Hai’er Home Appliances Co., Ltd.‘s annual reports for 2020 to 2024. These reports are official documents disclosed by the company in accordance with the requirements of the ‘Securities Law of the People’s Republic of China’ and the ‘Administrative Measures for Information Disclosure of Listed Companies’, covering core information directly related to the research topic, such as business operations, financial performance, collaboration mechanisms, and salary policies. The authenticity, completeness, and authority of the data are fully supported by regulatory oversight in the capital markets and third-party auditing. It is worth noting that using publicly available annual report data for enterprise case studies has become a well-established research paradigm in management and economics. Many current academic studies on the same topic71,72 have obtained core data from publicly disclosed documents of listed companies and conducted case analyses, thereby verifying the applicability and reliability of such data in case studies.

To ensure the accuracy and consistency of data extraction, this study adopted the standardized process of ‘double coding + cross-validation’: Firstly, two researchers independently located the parameters corresponding to the original text, extracted the core data, and marked the chapter page numbers, forming an independent coding table; then, three subject group mentors with experience in enterprise management and financial research were invited to conduct cross-review around three core dimensions: ‘adaptability of data and parameter definitions’, ‘completeness of original text citations’, and ‘consistency of quantitative indicators’. Regarding the 3 points of disagreement during the coding process, consensus was reached through a joint review of the original annual report text, verification of financial statement notes, and audit explanations, which conform to the rigorous norms of academic research data coding and effectively avoid subjective biases arising from a single entity’s extraction.

Mechanism explanation

Platform resource integration reshapes the foundation environment for collaborative innovation

The integration of platform resources forms a progressive, closed-loop chain of resource sharing, efficiency improvement, and cost optimization, thereby creating an efficient foundation for collaborative innovation. This mechanism is highly consistent with the influence laws of model parameters α and θ. The coding results show that the platform conducts resource allocation through multiple dimensions such as global R&D resource collaboration, digital integration of supply chains, and integration of business resources through mergers, in order to enhance the degree of resource sharing, increase the probability of collaborative innovation among the innovation groups, and the positive adjustment of parameter α on the core conclusion of enhancing the probability of collaboration is confirmed. In general, platform resource allocation depends on two core dimensions: basic resource sharing and technology empowerment. Basic resource sharing breaks down resource isolation barriers and makes it easier to obtain key resources, such as technical data. Technology empowerment relies on digital tools and algorithms to optimize resource matching and shorten the collaboration cycle.

From the platform’s functional perspective, the cross-industry, cross-regional resource-sharing network is first established, breaking down barriers to isolated innovation resources and enabling innovation groups and employees to conveniently access key resources such as technology, talent, and data. This inclusiveness of resources reflects the evolution of platform governance towards a people-oriented artificial intelligence model, transforming hierarchical resource control into platform-based resource sharing. This enables innovative data to no longer be exclusive to the core team, but to be accessible to all those involved in the innovation activities. At the same time, the universal sharing of resources across the platform enables employees to enhance their skills. Ordinary front-line workers can integrate on-site practical experience with the intelligent scheduling system to optimize production processes. Researchers can match their innovative ideas with the technical resource library to conduct innovative experiments. The integration of platform resources has achieved the initial upgrade of employees’ skills.

The application of digital tools and intelligent algorithms optimizes the efficiency of resource matching (corresponding to parameter θ in the model), shortens the collaborative innovation cycle, and improves the convergence speed of the system, resulting in the effective reduction of the collaborative innovation cost E1 and the improvement of the output efficiency of a single model in the domestic market. Technology empowerment reflects the core essence of technology, adapting to human needs and is the key to platform governance, evolving from simple resource sharing to humanized technology integration. The platform does not simply open up technical resources; instead, it builds a data-accessible but undiscoverable human-machine collaborative data system through technological means, thereby reducing the technical barriers to employees using artificial intelligence for collaborative work. Employees only need to focus on their own professional skills without having to master complex algorithms, and can collaborate with machines for innovation. Humanized technology empowerment not only improves the efficiency of resource integration and collaboration but also enables employees to acquire the digital collaboration skills necessary for practical application, thereby upgrading employees’ digital skills and promoting collaborative innovation.

By leveraging multi-brand technical reserves and aggregating global preferred resources, the efficiency of resource utilization is maximized, supporting collaborative innovation and promoting the system’s evolution from decentralization to scale. The core of this mechanism lies in the fact that the platform resource integration reshapes the cost and benefit structure of collaborative innovation, making the equilibrium condition of model collaborative benefits being greater than collaborative costs more easily met, providing the material foundation for the continuous advancement of collaborative innovation.

The improvement of platform resource integration directly promotes resource utilization, production flexibility improvement and operating cost reduction through the dual path of resource revitalization and process optimization. Haier’s practice shows that the collaborative optimization of basic resource sharing and technology empowerment can systematically improve the core operating indicators of the manufacturing system.

Cost - strengthening synergy dynamics through compensation adaptation and revenue distribution

The sustainability of collaborative innovation relies on the effective operation of the dynamic reinforcement mechanism, which regulates through both cost compensation and revenue distribution, balancing the interests of the collaborative entities and forming a closed loop with the logical operations of the model’s parameters A2, ɤ, Eb1, and Eb2. The coding results show that the platform significantly enhances the motivation to participate of the collaborative entities through the dual measures of ‘cost coverage and revenue incentives’, thereby increasing collaborative willingness.

On the cost side, the platform addresses external shocks such as chip shortages and market fluctuations by generating opportunity costs (model parameter A2) through independent support for emerging businesses and resource allocation for overseas operations, achieving dynamic adaptation through non-collaborative compensation Eb2. The independent innovation willingness of the innovation community increases, and the problem of ‘excessive opportunity cost leading to a retreat in collaboration’ is thus alleviated. This cost-adaptive mechanism not only reduces the cost pressure on collaborative innovation but also lowers the opportunity and trial-and-error costs for innovation teams and employees in the process of human-machine collaboration and the development of digital skills. For innovation teams, the non-cooperative benefits of the platform provide sufficient research and development resources and trial-and-error space for developers of human-machine collaboration tools, avoiding the situation in which overly specialized tools do not match the actual skill levels of frontline employees. For employees, cost coverage eliminates the economic pressure of learning human-machine collaboration skills, laying a solid foundation for the widespread popularization of human-machine collaboration and the improvement of employees’ skills.

On the revenue side, the platform achieves fair revenue distribution through dynamic adjustment of the revenue distribution coefficient, combined with bidirectional collaborative compensation, enhancing the fairness of collaborative revenue distribution. The sensitivity analysis of model parameters shows that for every 20% increase in revenue differences, the probability of collaboration increases by 25%. However, the platform further amplifies the incentive effect of revenue differences through mechanisms such as revenue sharing and scale effect sharing, thereby forming a distribution pattern of ‘more work, more rewards’ and ‘collaborative win-win’. This optimization of revenue distribution links it to the contributions of human-machine collaboration and to the improvement of employees’ skills, serving as a driving force to motivate employees to enhance their skills and engage in human-machine collaboration.

The core of this mechanism lies in the platform’s dynamic balance of costs and revenue, which optimizes the payment matrix in the model, makes the collaborative strategy the preferred choice for employees in the innovation community, and continuously strengthens synergy motivation.More importantly, the platform has evolved from simple cost coverage and fixed income distribution to cost coverage in human-machine collaboration and dynamic income distribution linked to skill contributions. This aligns perfectly with the development logic of human-centered artificial intelligence. The entire mechanism design is centered on “enhancing human capabilities rather than replacing them”, and driven by both cost adaptation and income incentives. This platform is no longer merely a mechanism for balancing the interests of collaborative innovation entities, but a core carrier for enterprises to achieve human-machine collaboration and continuous improvement of employees’ digital skills.The optimization of cost and compensation matching, as well as the improvement of revenue distribution, essentially aims to reduce the collaborative costs (E1/D1), expand the collaborative revenue gap (A1 - A2/B1 - B2), and thereby promote the improvement of operational indicators such as production efficiency and profit margins.

Knowledge sharing and value transformation drive the collaborative innovation cycle

The sharing of tacit knowledge and the transformation of value are the core drivers of the continuous occurrence of collaborative innovation. This mechanism achieves continuous increases in collaborative value through a virtuous cycle of knowledge flow, efficiency improvements, revenue growth, and knowledge re-creation. It closely aligns with the functional laws of tacit knowledge sharing and with the model’s individual unilateral collaborative benefits. The coding results show that leveraging tacit knowledge through technologies such as cross-brand reuse, platform research and development transformation, and community interaction iteration shortens product iteration cycles and improves contextual sales performance. This verifies the model’s conclusion that knowledge transformation positively affects collaborative benefits. Under the human-centric artificial intelligence paradigm advocated by Industry 5.0, this knowledge transformation logic takes building a two-way empowering knowledge ecosystem between humans and machines as its main task. The platform realizes the internalization of technology into human capital by converting explicit technical knowledge into human-machine collaborative operation skills that employees can directly apply in production scenarios; at the same time, the platform captures the tacit knowledge generated by employees during practical operations, makes it explicit, and feeds it back into algorithm iterations, thereby forming a mutually reinforcing closed loop. Essentially, it constitutes a dynamic, evolving and mutually enhancing social-technical system.

The operational logic of the specific mechanism is reflected in three levels. Firstly, manufacturing enterprises link global patent achievements through external platforms, establish internal technology-sharing platforms, overcome barriers to knowledge flow, elevate individual-level tacit knowledge to the organizational level, and provide intellectual support for collaborative innovation. Secondly, knowledge sharing promotes technological integration and improves the efficiency of collaborative innovation. The innovation group and its employees achieve technological breakthroughs in a short period, thereby creating differentiated competitive advantages. Thirdly, the scenario-based application of technological innovation generates actual revenue; the growth of unilateral collaborative benefits stimulates the willingness to share knowledge; and a cycle mechanism of knowledge sharing, value creation, revenue feedback, and sharing reinforcement is formed. At the same time, the transparency of platform information inhibits free-riding rent-seeking, reduces unilateral profit-making by innovation entities, reduces opportunistic risk in the knowledge-sharing process, and ensures the sustainable operation of the value transformation mechanism.

Knowledge sharing and value transformation—enabled by technological reuse and demand matching—drive improvements in operational indicators such as new product launch efficiency and product quality consistency. This interaction creates a cycle: knowledge sharing accelerates innovation, improves performance, and fuels further sharing. Haier’s experience shows that when a platform enables knowledge flow, innovation results are quickly converted into operational advantages.

Rule constraints and willingness enhancement ensure synergistic efficiency and control

The stability of collaborative innovation relies on the effective implementation of a risk management mechanism. This mechanism works through rule constraints, risk prevention, and the synergy of willingness enhancement, ensuring efficient and controllable collaborative processes. It aligns with the impact patterns of the penalty F and parameter ϕ in the model. The coding results show that the platform, through clear rule design and the utilization of diversified incentives, has reduced the rate of transaction pricing violations, extended the collaborative cooperation period, and significantly enhanced the robustness of the collaborative system.

At the rule-constraint level, the platform sets a reasonable penalty F and optimizes the penalty-intensity coefficient ɸ, creating a constraint environment in which the cost of violation exceeds the benefit of violation. This verifies the conclusion that moderate penalties improve performance rates. The platform establishes clear, unified penalty rules to define the behavioral boundaries for human-machine collaboration. It strictly curbs speculative behaviors such as sharing collaborative benefits without participating in skill improvement. This helps establish a fair order foundation for human-machine collaboration, eliminates employees’ concerns about “their skill improvement results being stolen”, and lays the foundation for all employees to actively participate in human-machine collaboration and accumulate relevant skills.

At the willingness enhancement level, the platform optimizes through a flat organizational structure and reduces the collaborative costs of employees through the application of intelligent tools, combined with core employee shareholding, independent project commissions, and individual performance rewards, etc., reducing the core talent turnover rate and enhancing the willingness of employees to collaborate. The platform further directly links the degree of human-machine collaboration, skill improvement achievements, and incentives. Employees who master advanced human-machine collaboration skills can receive shareholding incentives, and those involved in human-machine collaboration innovation can share two-way compensation. This promotes a transformation in employees’ skills from passive compliance acquisition to active advancement and improvement, perfectly aligning with the core concept of binding technology empowerment with human benefits.

Oscillations in collaborative strategies caused by unstable penalties in the model are avoided by using standardized penalty mechanisms. The core of this mechanism lies in rule constraints that prevent opportunistic risks and ensure collaborative order. Willingness enhancement stimulates entities’ initiative, thereby enhancing collaborative vitality. The two complement each other, leading to an evolution from an unstable to a stable equilibrium in the model of the collaborative system, and ensuring the continuous advancement of collaborative innovation through the dual guarantees of institutional and emotional aspects.

The synergy between rule constraints and willingness promotion achieves a balance between operational stability and innovation vitality. Haier’s practice shows that moderate punishment can improve process compliance rates and operational stability; excessive punishment may inhibit innovation vitality; and strengthening incentive measures for collaborative willingness can further improve production efficiency, delivery rates, and other indicators.

Collaborative trust management mechanism reduces profit loss through trust suppression

The collaborative trust management mechanism serves as a crucial supplement to the sustainability of collaborative innovation. The core logic and model of the collaborative trust management mechanism closely align with the influence pattern of the parameter ɤ, resolving the trust deficiency caused by insufficient coordination and reducing profit losses. The coding results show that insufficient coordination leads to trust differences among cross-regional innovation groups within departments, shrinking collaborative benefits, and the failure to transmit timely cross-departmental collaborative information, resulting in trust loss and reduced profits. This verifies the model’s core conclusion that trust attenuation is negatively correlated with collaborative benefits.

From an operational perspective, this mechanism operates through three layers: First, information transparency is used to address the root cause of the trust deficiency. In global multi-brand collaborations, transparency in technology sharing and profit distribution data reduces trust erosion caused by opaque decision-making processes. This transparentization logic constitutes the fundamental mechanism for building a mutual trust relationship between humans and machines. The platform discloses key information, such as the collaborative rules between humans and machines, the paths for skill investment and income conversion, etc., to eliminate employees’ concerns about no returns on skill investment and secretive operations in the collaborative process. It establishes a transparent foundation of trust for human-machine collaboration, stimulating employees’ willingness to actively learn human-machine collaboration skills and participate in technical collaboration.

Second, responsibility clarification strengthens the foundation of trust. Market chain collaboration clarifies responsibility division, mutual shirking leading to trust breakdown, and reduced communication costs. The platform further clarifies the boundaries of responsibility in human-machine collaboration. This responsibility division transforms cognitive trust into execution trust, enabling employees to dare to attempt advanced human-machine collaboration skills.

Third, mechanism standardization consolidates trust. Through ecological brand cooperation, by establishing trust relationships, partners can conceal resource use situations, thereby avoiding situations in which collaborative value is lost. From establishing a transparent mechanism to clarifying responsibility allocation, and finally to the formation of an institutionalized guarantee system, this evolution represents a shift from platform governance to people-oriented governance. Through an ecological trust mechanism that binds long-term interests and enables the utilization of employees’ skill development achievements across the entire ecosystem, it strengthens the perception that skill development brings long-term benefits, providing core trust support for the sustainability of human-machine collaboration and the full-cycle development of employees’ skills.

By reducing trust attenuation, the trust management mechanism drives improvements in operational indicators that matter to our teams. The study’s quantitative model clearly shows this effect. As a result, cross-departmental collaboration becomes smoother, and our market responses accelerate, unlocking new opportunities. Additionally, the manufacturing system enhances its ability to withstand risks. Haier’s experience in practice validates the study’s model rule: reducing the parameter ɤ leads to measurable operational gains. Overall, these results confirm that trust-as an implicit operational asset-empowers stable operation and fuels ongoing performance growth. The core of this mechanism lies in enhancing the parameter ɤ to alleviate situations in which employees and innovation groups are not coordinated, thereby making the collaborative equilibrium state more stable.

Hai’er Smart Home’s practice aligns with the model’s conclusion. The five mechanisms drive improvement in core operating indicators—resource utilization, production flexibility, and operation stability—by influencing the model’s core parameters (α, E1/D1, A1/B1, F, and ɤ). In this case, Hai’er achieved systematic improvement of manufacturing performance by optimizing governance parameters. This aligns with the model’s parameter optimization logic, leading to collaborative and operational improvements. The case also provides empirical support for the model: dual-dimensional resource integration, reasonable punishment mechanisms, and suppression of trust decay all verify the model’s quantitative law and operational gains.

Conclusions

Main conclusions

This paper constructs a triangular fuzzy game model to elucidate the collaborative mechanisms and key influencing factors among internal innovation agents within enterprises operating in uncertain environments. Through theoretical deduction, numerical examples, and case studies of Haier’s smart home, this paper explores in depth the key influencing factors of collaborative innovation mechanisms between innovation groups and employees. Combined with the connotation of “people-oriented artificial intelligence” in Industry 5.0, this model highlights its insight into the trend of industrial transformation. The main research conclusions are as follows:

First, the uncertainty of the external environment exerts influence on the collaborative system through three core pathways and has a significant negative impact on manufacturing operational performance. It forms a cumulative impact through three core paths: punishment fluctuations, cognitive differences, and information arbitrage. Punishment fluctuations weaken the effectiveness of the constraint mechanism, cognitive differences hinder the alignment of collaborative goals, and information arbitrage disrupts the fairness of income distribution. The three paths collectively increase the instability of the collaborative system, ultimately manifesting as deteriorations in operational indicators such as reduced resource utilization, decreased production flexibility, and increased operational costs, thereby confirming the systematic impact of environmental uncertainty on manufacturing operation performance.The triangular fuzzy numbers in the model do not represent the random errors or system deviations generated during the machine operation process. Instead, they quantify the true fuzziness of human cognition, providing a precise mathematical language and operational basis for conceptually human-centered artificial intelligence, and transforming the abstract demand for “preserving the subjective initiative of human decision-making” into a describable, quantifiable governance parameter.

Second, the core driver of strategic choice is the imbalance between synergistic and non-synergistic benefits, which will directly hamper operational performance. The strategies chosen by the employees in the innovation group are driven by differences in benefits. When the incremental revenue from collaborative innovation is significantly higher than the collaborative costs and non-synergistic benefits, both parties tend to choose the collaborative strategy, thereby improving the improvement of production efficiency, new product launch efficiency and other operating indicators. The risk of free-riding and the increase in unilateral benefits lead to a significant decrease in the willingness to collaborate. As a result, resource misallocation and production process blockage lead to a decline in operational performance. The triangular fuzzy number simulation confirms that, in an uncertain environment, subjects’ perceptions of benefit differences directly determine the probability range for the collaborative strategy. This is consistent with the simulation results, which show that reducing employees’ cognitive burden has a greater contribution to the stability of cooperation. This supports the values advocated by Industry 5.0 - placing skill development above efficiency.

Third, the platform mitigates uncertainties through a dual-dimensional resource integration mechanism, which aligns with the human-oriented thinking of technology. This mechanism includes (1) Basic resource sharing, which refers to pooling global resources, reusing technology, and digitalizing processes to reduce collaboration costs for innovation groups and employees-acting as the core ‘revenue engine’ to boost collaboration; and ensuring the material foundation for human innovation rights. (2) Technological empowerment, which means applying technologies like privacy computing to enable secure data sharing and address privacy concerns, thereby serving as the system’s ‘stabilizer’ by maintaining steady collaboration rates, and maintains the psychological safety of human decision-making rights. The platform supplements these with additional incentives, such as compensation for two-way collaboration. Together, these elements expand the feasible range of collaboration strategies.The numerical example and case verification show that the dual mechanism can significantly improve core operational indicators, such as production flexibility, on-time delivery rate, and resource utilization rate, and that the improvement in platform resource integration degree is positively correlated with these indicators.

Fourth, Punishment mechanisms are a double-edged tool, and their appropriate use is key to ensuring operational stability. An appropriate degree of punishment can effectively restrain opportunistic behaviors such as free-riding and information arbitrage, and increase the probability of performance. However, excessive punishment will increase the innovation vitality of innovation entities, reduce their willingness to participate in high-risk innovation projects, and indirectly undermine the sustainability of collaborative innovation, leading to a rigid production process, slower market response, and ultimately undermining operational performance. Case studies have shown that standardized, transparent punishment mechanisms are more effective at maintaining collaborative stability and operational flexibility than simply increasing punishment intensity, and that optimal penalty intensity is positively related to the operational stability of the manufacturing system. This double-edged sword effect indicates that Industry 5.0 should shift from constraint-based governance to transparency-based governance. That is, by making algorithms interpretable to reduce cognitive uncertainty, rather than relying on coercive measures. This aligns with the practical logic of trust management in case studies, which ensures the credibility of human-machine collaboration through transparency of information and clear accountability.

This study employs triangular fuzzy numbers to quantify environmental uncertainty. Compared with deterministic game models, this approach enables the expression of multiple strategic options through probability, thereby revealing the critical regulatory role of parameter fuzziness in game equilibria, and a logical chain of parameter optimization, collaborative improvement, operational excellence has been established. This method is applicable to innovative decision-making scenarios with high dynamics and information ambiguity, enabling the insights of this model not only for internal collaborative innovation within enterprises but also for alignment with broader industrial transformation trends.

Practical suggestions

Based on the above analysis, this paper puts forward the following countermeasure suggestions from the perspectives of platform enterprises, internal innovation groups, and enterprise employees, integrating the governance path of human-centric intelligent manufacturing:

Based on the core findings that the penalty fluctuations in the model weaken the effectiveness of constraints, cognitive differences hinder the alignment of collaborative goals, and information arbitrage undermines the fairness of returns, it is recommended that manufacturing enterprises establish a three-dimensional governance system of ‘standardization + consensus + transparency’. Standardization means clearly defining specific scenarios of ‘mild violations’ (such as delayed collaborative response), ‘moderate violations’ (such as partial resource diversion), and ‘severe violations’ (such as malicious free-riding, information fraud), and setting a stepped penalty standard of ‘warning and performance deduction’, ‘economic penalty and deadline rectification’, ‘canceling collaborative qualification + industry notification’ to avoid varying penalty intensities. At the same time, the principle of algorithmic transparency governance should be followed. The interpretability of the algorithm should be prioritized to explain the logic of violation determination, and trust should be established through transparency rather than relying excessively on punitive constraints. Consensusization means establishing a cross-departmental ‘collaborative goal consensus group’ with user needs as the core, to sort out the core goals, technical standards and division boundaries of each collaborative scenario (such as R&D - production collaboration, production - sales collaboration), synchronizing task progress and deviation warnings in real time, allowing the innovation group and employees to form a unified understanding of the collaborative goals, reducing execution deviations. Transparency means building a ‘collaborative end-to-end data sharing platform embedded with cognitive enhancement design, clearly requiring real-time upload and full accessibility of data such as resource usage records (such as the duration of R&D equipment usage, the trajectory of technical document invocation), task progress (such as node completion time, quality acceptance results), and detailed distribution of returns (such as the basis for calculating collaborative incremental returns, the proportion of each entity’s share) for all personnel. In the intelligent decision-making module of the platform, artificial intelligence is positioned as a “decision assistant” rather than an “automatic decision-maker”, in order to retain the final decision-making power of humans. Blockchain technology is used to encrypt and archive key data, ensuring its immutability.

Build a dual-dimensional platform for resource integration that combines basic resource sharing with technological empowerment, and accurately identify and address operational pain points. This approach increases revenue by optimizing resource allocation and enhances ecological stability by supporting sustainable collaboration. For different operational pain points, formulate differentiated resource integration plans: For enterprises with low resource utilization rates, they should prioritize expanding the scope of basic resource sharing, establish a platform spanning departments and regions. Include technology, talents, equipment, and data as basic resources. Clearly define rules for requesting and accessing resources. Specify how income is divided and set assessment criteria for resource use. Link participation and utilization rates of resource sharing with the collaboration contribution scores of innovation groups or employees.Implement skill enhancement incentive measures by prioritizing the reduction of employees’ learning costs rather than merely increasing economic rewards, in order to ensure that resource sharing can directly enhance collective performance and reduce collaboration costs. For enterprises that lack production flexibility and slow market response capabilities, efforts should focus on increasing the proportion of technology-enabled operations. To enable technological empowerment, build a data-sharing system for security. Base this on technology. Use a mechanism that makes data available without making it directly visible. This eliminates concerns about sharing among innovation entities. Deploy intelligent algorithms to pair resources with needs. Use digital collaboration systems to improve matching efficiency. Reduce negative fluctuations in employee collaboration. In this way, technology stabilizes collaboration. Employee collaboration. In this way, technology stabilizes collaboration. Enterprises with high operating costs should strengthen the synergistic effect of dual-dimensional integration, reduce synergistic costs through technology reuse and process digitization, and expand the profit differential through two-way synergistic compensation, thereby achieving the dual goals of cost control and efficiency improvement.

Centering on the core of benefit differences, design a ‘precise incentive and cost diversion’ two-way driving mechanism. Based on the conclusion of the model that ‘the difference in collaborative and non-collaborative benefits determines strategy selection’, establish a three-dimensional quantitative contribution measurement system of resource input dimension, value output dimension, and collaboration quality dimension. Link the contribution score directly to returns, and the collaborative share ratio of the innovation group fluctuates accordingly. At the same time, a ‘collaborative project co-investment right’ is provided, and the collaborative returns of employees are calculated based on performance bonuses. When the cumulative score meets the standard, it can be directly exchanged for promotion qualifications, making the benefits difference between collaboration and non-collaboration concrete. The incentive mechanism of ‘revenue difference optimization - operation index binding’ is designed to strengthen the collaborative power, directly link the contribution score with revenue, and dynamically adjust the innovation group and employees.

Research limitations

Although the two-player game framework effectively captures the core collaboration mechanism, it inevitably simplifies the multi-agent heterogeneity and network complexity of real platform-driven manufacturing enterprises. Future research can extend the model to a tripartite or multi-agent game structure to better reflect the real innovation ecosystem.

Although this study has made certain contributions at both the theoretical and practical levels, there are still several limitations:

This study employs the triangular fuzzy evolutionary game model, focusing on three types of uncertainties: cognitive divergence, information asymmetry, and fluctuating punishment. It also takes into account key platform governance variables such as resource integration, matching efficiency, and trust decay. However, multiple external factors, such as macroeconomic fluctuations, industry competition, and policy changes, have not been considered in this model. Moreover, the interaction between external factors (such as organizational culture and individual employee characteristics) and internal factors has not been fully considered. The model assumes that the strategies of the innovation group and employees adjust at the same speed. However, in reality, due to differences in resource holdings and risk tolerance across entities, there are differences in their decision response speed and strategy adjustment flexibility. Future research can further expand the interaction dimensions of variables and incorporate interaction variables such as macroeconomic indices and internal organizational culture types into the model. Additionally, introducing heterogeneity parameters for entities to quantify their decision response and strategy adjustment flexibility. This study aims to clarify the optimal allocation of platform resource integration degree α by separating it into basic resource sharing α₁ and technology empowerment α2, and to examine their distinct synergistic effects. Quantitative methods, such as structural equation modeling and regression, identify optimal values of α₁ and α2 across industries, their interaction effects, and their impact coefficients on collaborative innovation. The parameter settings of this study are based on Haier’s annual report data and existing literature. The logical deduction of experts’ judgment also provides support. However, there is a lack of multi-enterprise, large-sample empirical data verification. Although case studies validate the theoretical model, the universality of single-case study conclusions may be limited. At the same time, this study adopts a combination of theoretical derivation, numerical simulation, and case study methods. However, quantitative research methods such as questionnaire surveys and econometric models have not further tested the influence intensity of variables. Future research suggests expanding the data collection scope by extending the research range to platform enterprises across multiple industries, such as services and technology, and using these as research samples. Data can be collected through multiple channels, such as company annual reports, industry statistics, and enterprise survey questionnaires, and quantitative methods, such as structural equation models and regression analysis, can be used to test the significance of variable influence coefficients at the 5% level.

This study quantifies environmental uncertainty through triangular fuzzy mathematics and constructs a tripartite evolutionary game model of “platform innovation group employee”, which has achieved the core research goal of revealing the evolution law of platform driven internal innovation collaboration in manufacturing enterprises. The core propositions of “three-dimensional impact path of environmental uncertainty” and “platform dual mechanism mitigation effect” have been verified through numerical simulation and the Hai’er case study. The parameter assignment in the study is based on Haier’s annual report data and industry practice, and the strategic probability interval focuses on cross scenario general rules such as “platform empowerment to collaborative probability increase”, rather than precise numerical predictions for specific enterprises, which is in line with the research positioning of “theoretical model law verification”. However, estimating key triangular fuzzy number parameters based on actual micro data of a certain enterprise and verifying the consistency between the strategic probability interval derived from the model and the actual distribution of the enterprise’s development strategy remains an important direction for future research.

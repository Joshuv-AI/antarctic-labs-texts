Clinical efficacy of ulinastatin and budesonide combination therapy in pelvic fracture complicated by pulmonary contusion
Authors: Hongyu Lu, Jian Jia, Xizhi Ma, Danyang Li, Yue Hu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe aim of this study is to investigate the clinical efficacy of combination therapy with ulinastatin and budesonide as an adjuvant treatment in patients with pelvic fractures complicated by pulmonary contusion.MethodsA total of 136 individuals with pelvic fractures complicated by pulmonary contusion were enrolled from the Pelvic Ward of the Department of Trauma at Tianjin University Tianjin Hospital, Tianjin City, between July 2021 and July 2024. Participants were randomly assigned to a study group or a control group. The control group received budesonide suspension via aerosol inhalation (2 mg per dose, every 6 h). The study group received the same treatment along with intravenous administration of ulinastatin injection (1.0 × 105 U per dose, administered one to three times daily based on clinical condition). Both groups underwent treatment for two weeks. Clinical parameters assessed included the pulmonary contusion simple score, visual analogue scale (VAS), lung function and alveolar oxygenation function indices, lung function-related cytokine levels, immune function and oxidative stress indices, and the incidence of complications. The data were compared between the two groups.ResultsThe study group demonstrated statistically significant improvements in pulmonary contusion simple score, VAS, lung function and alveolar oxygenation indices, lung function-related cytokine levels, immune function, and oxidative stress indices compared to the control group (p < 0.05). However, no significant difference in overall complication rates was observed between the groups (p > 0.05).ConclusionsThe combination of ulinastatin and budesonide demonstrated significant efficacy in improving lung function indices, modulating immune responses, and reducing inflammation and oxidative stress in individuals with pelvic fractures complicated by pulmonary contusion. The combination therapy exhibited a favorable safety profile, may serve as a potential treatment approach.Keywords: Budesonide, Immune function, Lung function, Oxidative stress, Pulmonary contusion, Pelvic fracture, Ulinastatin

Introduction

Pulmonary contusion is a common complication associated with pelvic fractures, occurring in approximately 12–25% of individuals with such injuries [1–3]. This condition often induces a series of pathological changes, including pulmonary parenchymal hemorrhage and edema, which subsequently contribute to ischemia and hypoxia of lung tissue, ventilatory dysfunction, and decreased lung compliance. In severe cases, pulmonary contusion may gradually progress to pulmonary infection, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), or even result in mortality [4]. Although surgical intervention of pelvic fractures has been shown to mitigate pulmonary complications, pulmonary injury often serves as a contraindication to the surgical intervention of pelvic fracture [5, 6]. Consequently, effective management of pulmonary contusion is crucial for optimizing outcomes in patients with pelvic fractures.

The current treatment strategies are primarily conservative, focusing on maintaining respiratory patency, improving circulation, and managing associated injuries. The key therapeutic objectives include pain management, early repair of lung injury, and prevention of complications; however the efficacy of these methods is limited in achieving optimal outcomes [7].

Ulinastatin, a broad-spectrum protease inhibitor, is widely used in clinical practice for its organ-protective properties. Budesonide, a glucocorticoid, exerts anti-inflammatory, antiallergic, and immunosuppressive effects by inhibiting inflammatory factor activity in alveolar macrophages, thereby reducing lung injury [8, 9]. Although both agents have demonstrated individual therapeutic benefits, limited research exists on their combined use for the treatment of pulmonary contusion. This study aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of ulinastatin and budesonide combination therapy in individuals with pelvic fractures complicated by pulmonary contusion, as described in the following sections.

Equipment, participants, and methods

Study design

This study was designed and conducted as a prospective, single-center, single-blind, randomized controlled clinical trial.

Participant selection

Inclusion criteria:

Participants were eligible for inclusion if they:

① Had a confirmed diagnosis of pulmonary contusion based on CT imaging; ② Were above 18 years of age; ③ Were admitted to the hospital within 24 h of sustaining and injury; ④ Had concurrent pelvic fractures.

Exclusion Criteria:

Participants were excluded if they:

① Exhibited an allergy to chemical or pharmaceutical agents used in this study; ② Had pre-existing underlying lung diseases;③ Experienced life-threatening complications. ④ Were hospitalized for less than 14 days.

A total of 136 patients with pelvic fractures and pulmonary contusions, admitted to Tianjin University Tianjin Hospital in Tianjin City between July 2021 and July 2024, were included in this study. Ethical approval for the study was obtained from the hospital’s ethics committee (No. 2024 Medical Ethics 238), and all participants provided written informed consent prior to enrollment.

The cohort consisted of 73 males and 63 females. Among the participants, 85 sustained injuries in motor vehicle accidents, while 51 experienced falls from heights. All participants underwent surgical intervention for their pelvic fractures, and 78 participants also sustained injuries in anatomical regions other than the pelvis and lungs. This study was conducted with approval from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Tianjin University Tianjin Hospital (No. 2024 Medical Ethics 238). This study was conducted in accordance with the declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Pharmaceutical agents and equipment

Ulinastatin for injection: Specification: 2 mL × 1 × 105 IU; Batch number: 032402054; Approval number: SFDA approval number H20040476; Manufacturer: Guangdong Techpool Biochemical Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. Budesonide suspension for inhalation: Specification: 0.5 mg × 2 Ml; Batch number: 24091031; Approval number: SFDA approval number H20140474; Manufacturer: AstraZeneca Pty Ltd, Australia.

Equipment: AU5800 fully automatic biochemistry analyzer, manufactured by Beckman Company, United States; CytoFLEX Flow Cytometer, manufactured by Beckman Coulter, USA; SPM-A lung function test spirometer, manufactured by CONTEC, Japan, and PT1000 blood gas analyzer, manufactured by Wuhan Easy Diagnosis Biotechnology Co., Ltd.

Grouping and treatment

Participants were randomly assigned into two groups using the random number table method: control group (n = 68) and study group (n = 68).

Post-admission, all participants received standard care, including hemostasis, oxygen therapy, antimicrobial therapy, cough suppression, bronchodilation, and nutritional support. Chest drainage and ventilator-assisted breathing were administered as required, based on the individual clinical conditions.

In the control group, participants received the aforementioned standard care along with budesonide suspension administered via aerosol inhalation (2 mg per dose, every 6 h). In the study group, participants received the same therapy as the control group, with the addition of ulinastatin administered intravenously (1 × 105 IU per dose, administered 3 times daily based on the clinical condition). Both groups continued therapy for a duration of two weeks.

Observation indices and efficacy assessment

Primary indices: The primary indices for evaluation included, PCSS (pulmonary contusion simple score), VAS (visual analogue scale), pulmonary function, and alveolar oxygenation function indices.

Subgroup indices: These included lung function-related cytokines, immune function and oxidative stress indices, and as well as the complications and prognosis.

① Clinical efficacy: Treatment efficacy was evaluated by comparing pre- and post-treatment outcomes using PCSS and VAS scores [5, 6]. Four indices are evaluated in the PCSS system, including the evaluation of respiratory rate, sputum production, rales, and chest X-ray. Scores of 1 to 6, 7 to 12, and 13 to 18 points indicate grade I, II, and III injuries, respectively. The VAS score was used to assess the degree of pain in both groups, with a score ranging from 0 to 10, where a higher score indicates significant levels of severe pain.

② Lung function indices: Lung function was assessed using a spirometer, which measured the following parameters before and after treatment: VT (volume of tide), MVV (maximal voluntary ventilation), and RSC (respiratory system compliance).

③ Alveolar oxygenation function indices: Before and after treatment, RR (respiratory rate) and FiO2 (fraction of inspired O2) of the individuals was measured by blood gas analyzer, and SpO2 (percutaneous arterial oxygen saturation) was detected by pulse oximeter. Arterial blood gas analysis was used to assess A-aDO2 (alveoli-arterial oxygen pressure difference) and PaO₂ (partial pressure of oxygen in arterial blood). In addition, OI (oxygenation index) and ROX (respiratory rate oxygenation) index were calculated as OI = PaO₂/FiO₂, ROX index = (SpO2/FiO2)/RR.

④ Lung function-related cytokine levels: The levels of SP-A (pulmonary surfactant-associated protein A), KL-6 (Krebs Von den Lungen-6), and SP-D (pulmonary surfactant-associated protein D) in individuals were detected before and after treatment.

⑤ Immune function indices: Flow cytometry was used to detect Th17 (T helper cell 17), Th22 (T helper cell 22), and Treg (regulatory T cells) populations before and after treatment, and the Treg/Th17 ratio was calculated.

⑥ Oxidative stress indices: Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay was used to detect HMGB1 (levels of high mobility group box-1 protein), MIF (macrophage migration inhibitory factor), and PON1 (paraoxonase-1) in patients before and after treatment. An automated biochemical analyzer was used with the dimethyl-phenol orange method to detect TAS (total antioxidant state) and TOS (total oxidant status). The OSI (oxidative stress index) was calculated using the formula: OSI = TOS (μmol H2O2 Eq/L) / TAS (mmol Trolox Eq/L).

⑦ Complications and prognosis: Complications arising during and post-treatment were documented, including ARDS, pneumothorax, and pulmonary infections. The prognosis of participants was statistically analyzed, categorizing outcomes into discharged, in cured condition, transfer to another facility, and mortality. The total incidence rate of complications was calculated using the formula: Total incidence rate of complications (%) = (number of adverse reaction cases) / (total number of cases) × 100%.

Statistical methods

All the data collected in this study were analyzed using SPSS 26.0 software. Normally distributed measurement data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and the comparisons were examined by Student t test. The categorical data were expressed as n(%), and the differences between the two groups were examined by chi-square analysis or Fisher's exact test. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Comparison of lung function indices

After two weeks of treatment, the MVV, VT, and Crs levels increased significantly in both groups compared to baseline values. The study group exhibited higher levels of improvement in these parameters than the control group (p < 0.05, Table 2).

Comparison of alveolar oxygenation function indices

After two weeks of treatment, the OI and ROX index were significantly elevated in both groups compared to baseline values. The A-aDO2 decreased significantly compared to baseline. Changes in the study group were greater than those in the control group (p < 0.05, Table 2).

Comparison of lung function-related cytokine levels

After two weeks of treatment, the SP-A, SP-D, and KL-6 levels in both groups were decreased compared with baseline values. The SP-A, SP-D, and KL-6 levels in the study group were lower than those in the control group (p < 0.05, Table 2).

Comparison of immune function indices

After two weeks of treatment, the Th17, Th22, and Treg levels, as well as Treg/Th17 ratio improved in both groups compared to baseline values. The Th17, Th22, and Treg levels in the study group were lower than those in the control group (p < 0.05, Table 2).

Comparison of oxidative stress indices

After two weeks of treatment, the MIF, HMGB1, and OSI levels decreased significantly in both the groups as compared with baseline values, while PON1 levels increased. The changes in the study group were greater than those in the control group (p < 0.05, Table 2).

Comparison of complications and prognosis

There was no significant difference in the overall complication rate (11.76% vs. 19.21%) between the study group and the control group (p > 0.05), including ARDS (4 vs. 6), pneumothorax (3 vs. 4), and pulmonary infection (1 vs. 3). There was no significant difference in the number of individuals who were cured and discharged (49 vs. 53), individuals who were transferred to another facility (14 vs. 12), and individuals who succumbed (5 vs. 3) between the control group and the study group (p > 0.05).

Discussion

The progression of pulmonary contusion in individuals varies widely, depending on the nature and severity of trauma, ranging from mild discomfort to sudden fatal outcomes [10–12]. For individuals suffering from pelvic fractures and pulmonary contusions, the current clinical management strategies include surgical intervention, conservative pharmacological therapy, and supportive care measures, such as mechanical ventilation. However, there is a lack of consensus regarding optimal treatment protocols with therapeutic efficacy varying significantly among patients due to individual differences [13–16].

Ulinastatin has been identified as a protective agent for cardiopulmonary function by stabilizing lysosomal membranes and inhibiting neutrophil protease activity. It is extensively used in treating respiratory diseases and other diseases with favorable efficacy [17]. Qiao et al. reported that ulinastatin improves the condition of individuals with pulmonary contusion by reducing the systemic level of HMGB1 and regulating lung ventilation function and compliance [18]. Short-term combined administration of glucocorticoid can further reduce the damage to pulmonary endothelium caused by pulmonary edema and improve pulmonary circulation. Inhaled budesonide is beneficial for clearing airway secretions and improving lung function by improving airway hyper-responsiveness [19, 20]. Hydrogen inhalation therapy significantly mitigated inflammatory responses associated with lung contusion in animal study. Hydrogen inhalation therapy may be a supplemental therapeutic strategy for treating lung contusion [21].

The combined use of ulinastatin and budesonide demonstrated significant therapeutic synergy. Patients in the study group exhibited lower PCSS and VAS scores compared to the control group, which were consistent with the results of Ju et al. [22] This indicates that the combined therapy of hormones and ulinastatin alleviates the clinical symptoms and promotes pain resolution. Indicators such as OI, A-aDO2, and ROX index can all be used as important indices of lung ventilation [23].

In this study, the oxygenation and pulmonary function indices in the two groups improved after two weeks of treatment, with the study group exhibiting improved results than the control group. These findings suggest that the combined use of ulinastatin and budesonide could significantly improve the oxygenation status, lung function, and respiratory endurance of individuals with pulmonary contusion. It is hypothesized that this effect may be associated with the regulation of immune function and the inflammatory response in the context of comprehensive treatment.

The pathophysiological phase of pulmonary contusion includes exudation, hyperplasia, and fibrosis, with pro-inflammatory mediators, such as MIF, immune responses, and oxidative stress being implicated in the direct lung injury associated with blunt thoracic trauma. The inflammatory cascade mediated by immune cells, such as Th17 and Th22, can damage the air-blood barrier, which is composed of epithelial-mesenchymal-endothelial cells. Pulmonary surfactants such as SP-D and SP-A interact with inflammatory exudates, exacerbating the formation of pulmonary edema and contributing to reduced lung compliance [24]. ARDS is the pathological state of alveolar and capillary damage caused by acute lung injury. Pneumothorax is a pathological condition in which gas enters the pleural cavity, causing an increase in pressure within the pleural cavity. Pulmonary infection inflammatory diseases occurring in the terminal airways, alveoli, and interstitium of the lungs. All these conditions can induce dyspnea, refractory hypoxemia, cough, chest pain, and shock.

In a previous study by Xue et al. it was reported that a combination therapy using ulinastatin and budesonide reduces inflammation in patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, correcting cytokine imbalances, and improving lung function [25]. In this study, it was also observed that the post-treatment levels of MIF, SP-D, SP-A, Th17, and Th22 in the study group were lower compared to the control group, indicating that this combination therapy regulates immune disorders, stabilizes alveolar surfactant membranes, and prevents alveolar atrophy and collapse. In previous studies, PON1, an antioxidant enzyme that catalyzes the hydrolysis of lipid peroxides has been shown to prevent free radical damage. The elevated expression levels of HMGB1 protein are associated with increased oxygen free radical production, correlating with the severity and prognosis of pulmonary contusion. OSI serves as a marker of cellular oxidative stress [26, 27].

Besides, there was no difference of the complications between two groups showed that the implement of combined therapy is safe for clinical practice.

The results of this study, indicate that the combined therapy of ulinastatin and budesonide is more effective in reducing serum PON1, HMGB1, and OSI compared to monotherapy. There was no significant difference in the complications and prognosis after two weeks of treatment between the two groups. However, the mortality rate of the study group exhibited a reduced trend in comparison with the control group, indicating that the combination therapy had a significant inhibitory effect on oxidative stress in lung injury without increasing adverse reactions, demonstrating a favorable prognosis and safety profile. Further studies with larger sample sizes and extended follow-up periods are necessary to validate these findings.

There were also several limitations in this study. Firstly, it was a study in single center. Secondly, it was a study with limited follow-up time. Thus, all results should be interpreted cautiously. It should be investigated by further research in multicenter.

Conclusion

In summary, the combination of ulinastatin and budesonide is observed to enhance lung function, regulate immune response, and reduce inflammatory and oxidative stress-related injuries in individuals with pelvic fractures complicated by pulmonary contusion. Moreover, with a favorable safety and efficacy profile, this combination may serve as a potential treatment approach.

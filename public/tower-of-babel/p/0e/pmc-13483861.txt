CD25 genetic ablation on lymphoid cell lines to obtain models of stimulation through the Interleukin-2 beta/gamma receptor
Authors: Ernesto Relova-Hernández, Ana Beatriz Díaz-Bravo, Arleen Cantero-Rodríguez, Rydell Alvarez-Arzola, Nayeli Hernández-Álvarez, Tania Carmenate, Gertrudis Rojas
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractInterleukin-2 (IL-2) has pleiotropic roles within the immune system. Its functional versatility depends upon the interactions with diverse arrays of receptor subunits differentially expressed on immune cell subsets. The balance between stimulation of regulatory T cells (Tregs) and effector lymphocytes determines the immunological outcome. While Tregs display constitutively a trimeric IL-2 receptor composed by alpha (also known as CD25), beta and gamma subunits, resting and memory CD8 + T cells express a dimeric beta/gamma receptor. The effects of IL-2 signaling through the trimeric receptor are routinely studied in vitro using either mouse CTLL-2 or human Kit225 lymphoid cell lines. We obtained equivalent IL-2-responsive cellular models displaying dimeric beta/gamma receptors. The purpose was achieved through genetic ablation of CD25 expression on CTLL-2 and Kit225 cells using CRISPR/Cas9 edition. The usefulness of the stable CD25-KO cell lines thus generated was illustrated by setting up proliferation assays, and characterizing a panel of IL-2-derived recombinant muteins, including agonists, super-agonists and antagonists. Problems and pitfalls of such assays, as well as the ways to mitigate them, were depicted. Stat5 phosphorylation assays based on KO cell lines were also performed. These cellular models, and the experience accumulated during their use, could contribute to the evaluation of the output of IL-2 engineering strategies developed across different laboratories.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-61486-7.Keywords: CTLL-2, CRISPR/Cas9, IL-2 biological activity, in vitro cell proliferation assays, Interleukin-2 muteins, Kit225Subject terms: Biotechnology, Immunology

Introduction

Interleukin-2 (IL-2) is a member of the common gamma chain (γc) superfamily of cytokines, and plays a pivotal role in T cell biology1. High doses of IL-2 have been introduced into the clinical practice for the treatment of metastatic cell renal carcinoma and metastatic melanoma several decades ago2. The potent antitumor effect of IL-2 administration was limited to a subset of patients, mainly due to its adverse effects3 and the undesired expansion of regulatory T (Treg) cells4. On the other hand, IL-2 at low doses has been used to treat a variety of autoimmune and inflammatory conditions5,6.

The functional versatility of IL-2 on immune cell populations, and the subsequent complexities of its therapeutic handling, are linked to the existence of diverse receptors, differentially expressed on particular cell types7. Tregs constitutively express a trimeric IL-2 receptor (IL-2R) formed by alpha (also known as CD25), beta (CD122) and gamma (CD132) subunits, which results in a high affinity (KD in the range of 10− 11 mol/L) and efficient uptake of the limiting amount of the cytokine present under physiological conditions. On the other hand, resting effector cells lack CD25 expression and display the dimeric beta/gamma IL-2R with intermediate affinity for IL-2 (KD in the range of 10− 9 mol/L). Therefore, they are less sensitive to IL-2-mediated stimulation, requiring higher concentrations to respond. Once activated, effector cells transiently express CD25, become more responsive, and eventually get exhausted and die. Such a complex IL-2R-centered regulation keeps the basal state of self-tolerance, while allowing the development of potent immune responses upon strong antigenic stimulation, as well as their downmodulation to avoid harmful effects.

Besides pharmacological dosage management, IL-2 itself has been engineered in multiple ways to fine-tune its binding capacity to particular receptor subunits, in order to segregate functions, reinforcing either immunoregulatory or immunoenhancing roles8. While disrupting the binding interface with CD25 has given rise to weak agonists biased to effector cells9–13, increasing the interaction with CD122 has resulted in potent super-agonists also biased to effector functions14,15. Signaling attenuation, through interruption of the interface with either CD122 or CD132, has rendered antagonists and partial agonists with unique functional profiles16–18. The emergence of so many IL-2-derived variants dictates the need of simple and standardized methods to evaluate their biological activity. Whereas the induction of proliferation of mouse CTLL-219 and human Kit22520 cells accurately reflects the functionality of wild-type (wt) IL-2 and its variants on lymphoid cells displaying the trimeric IL-2R, such classical proliferation assays are no longer useful when the research questions are focused on the differential effect of each mutein on cells having the dimeric IL-2R version.

Here we report the generation and characterization of stable CD25-knockout cell lines through CRISPR/Cas9 genetic edition of both CTLL-2 and Kit225 cells. The new cell lines keep the expression of a responsive dimeric beta/gamma IL-2R and are thus suitable models to evaluate the biological activity of IL-2-derived molecules on mouse/human immune cell populations harboring this receptor. Since they only differ from the parental cell lines in the absence of CD25, the combined use of proliferation assays based on wt cells and their CD25-KO counterparts allows a strict comparison of the activity of a given mutein on target cells displaying either the trimeric or dimeric receptor. The broad usefulness of the tools developed by us has been illustrated through the characterization of a panel of IL-2-derived muteins with diverse structural modifications and biological effects. Problems and pitfalls associated with proliferation assays based on wt cells and/or their CD25-KO counterparts are described, and the strategies to mitigate the risk of artifacts are presented.

Results

Genetically modified CD25-KO CTLL-2 and CD25-KO Kit225 cell lines were generated using the CRISPR/Cas9 system

The workflow to obtain CD25-knockout cell lines through lentiviral transduction with vectors encoding single guide RNA sequences targeting either mouse or human CD25 gene is depicted in Fig. 1a-d. This protocol was useful to achieve genetic ablation of CD25 expression in both mouse CTLL-2 (Fig. 2) and human Kit225 (Fig. 3) lymphoid cell lines. Viral titers between 2 × 106 and 2 × 107 particles/mL were consistently obtained in supernatants of packaging HEK-293T cells co-transfected with each lentiviral genetic construct and suitable auxiliary plasmids. Lymphoid cell lines were thus directly transduced with lentiviruses-containing supernatants to avoid any viral concentration/purification step. This protocol guaranteed a high multiplicity of infection (MOI) in the range 50–500, while keeping the procedure as simple as possible.

The use of non-purified virus preparations introduced the risk of transduction interference by any component of the HEK-293T cell culture supernatant. For CTLL-2 transduction, two different culture feeding additives -fetal bovine serum (FBS) and tryptone- were tested, in order to minimize undesired effects. Media composition during the assembly of lentiviral particles was indeed shown to influence the subsequent transduction efficiency. Even though a variable fraction of the cells became CD25-negative after a single transduction round of CTLL-2 cells with viruses packaged in the presence of 10% FBS, it was accompanied by clearly defined CD25 + cell populations comprising between 20 and 80% of the total cell number (Fig. 2a). This was in sharp contrast with the output from transfections done with tryptone-packaged viruses, where CD25 expression was ablated in the vast majority of the cells, as shown by flow cytometry (Fig. 2a).

Differences in transduction efficiency were reflected in the output of a single round of limiting dilution segregation of transduced cells. While homogeneous populations of CD25-negative cells were consistently found in all the tested wells derived from transductions with tryptone-packaged lentiviruses, only a few wells from the procedure done with FBS-packaged viral particles (2/9) contained truly CD25-KO cells (Fig. 2b). Since the above-described differences were thought to be related to a decreased transduction efficiency in the presence of FBS, and not to a differential effect on edition events after virus entry, it was assumed that any homogeneous CD25-KO CTLL-2 cell line, independently of its origin, was potentially useful as a model of mouse lymphoid cells having dimeric beta/gamma IL-2 receptor. Therefore, C5FBS cell line (prepared with FBS-packaged viruses), which showed total absence of CD25 + cells during the initial screening, was selected for further experiments.

Due to the advantage in terms of global transduction efficiency, tryptone-packaged lentiviruses were subsequently used for genetic modification of human Kit225 cells. Only one of the three transduction replicates showed a clearly defined CD25-negative population (26% of the cells) (Fig. 3a), indicating a lower initial transduction efficiency as compared to CTLL-2 cells. Nevertheless, the procedure was continued, and cells from only three limiting dilution screening wells kept intact CD25 expression (Fig. 3b). One well comprised a mixture of CD25-positive and negative cells, and most wells (14/18) contained homogeneous CD25-KO populations (Fig. 3b). One of them (K6) was selected for further characterization.

CD25-KO CTLL-2 and CD25-KO Kit225 cell lines were stably deficient of CD25 expression and free of undesired phenotypic changes

Stability and homogeneity of the CD25-deficient phenotype was a prerequisite for further use of CD25-KO cell lines (Fig. 1e), since the expansion of CD25 + residual cells would preclude their applications as models bearing IL-2R beta/gamma. After five consecutive passages every 48 h in the absence of the selection agent, both selected CD25-KO cell lines were shown to keep CD25-deficient phenotype (Fig. 4a). The fact that lymphocytes can rearrange the expression levels of different IL-2R subunits in response to diverse stimuli21,22 raised the concern of whether CD25-deficiency could result in a compensatory change in CD122 expression levels, the other specific component of IL-2/IL-15R. The coexistence of both modifications would complicate the interpretation of results of stimulation of parental cell lines and their CD25-KO counterparts with mutated IL-2 variants biased to recognition by particular receptor subunits. CD122 expression in wt cell lines and their CD25-KO versions, assessed by flow cytometry, was shown to be in the same order (Fig. 4b), indicating the potential of the knockout cells as clean models of pure CD25-deficiency in an otherwise normal IL-2-responsive lymphoid cell context. Additionally, testing several CD25-KO CTLL-2-derived cell lines in two independent experiments reinforced the idea that there was no trend towards compensatory CD122 expression increase upon CD25 ablation (Suppl. Figure 1). The absence of CD25 expression was kept along 30 consecutive passages (Fig. 4.c-d) confirming the stability of the new cell lines.

Interestingly, a small difference between the growth rate of wt and genetically modified cell lines was found (Fig. 4.e-f). This difference could not be simply attributed to the absence of the high affinity trimeric IL-2R in KO cells because all cell lines were routinely grown in the presence of a no-alpha IL-2-derived mutein23(displaying a severely disrupted CD25 interface)24. However, the effects of a minor residual CD25-dependent engagement of the high affinity trimeric IL-2R in wt cells could not be absolutely ruled out. Alternatively, genome integration of lentiviral DNA and/or off-target effects of genetic edition could have resulted in subtle modifications of cell physiology, which might explain the slower growth rate. Since the replication of procedures described herein to grow CD25-KO cell lines in many laboratories would rely on the use of commercially available wt IL-2, phenotypic stability of selected cell lines was also tested after growing them during 21 days in the presence of wt IL-2 (Fig. 4g). No reversion of the CD25 deficiency was observed, indicating that homogeneity and stability of the genetically modified cell lines are not affected under the potential selective pressure of stimulation by a CD25 binding-competent IL-2.

Taken together, the above-described results indicated that CD25-deficient cell lines are stable, free of gross undesired phenotypic alterations, and useful as suitable models to characterize the effects of IL-2-derived molecules on lymphoid cells harboring exclusively the dimeric beta/gamma IL-2R.

CD25-KO cell lines were useful to confirm the ability of Interleukin-2-derived muteins with disrupted CD25 interfaces to induce a response through the dimeric IL-2 receptor

A first application of CD25-KO lymphoid cell lines was the characterization of two different CD25-non-binders derived from human IL-2, named by their authors No-apha (NA)9 and 3 × 25 muteins. Each one has a distinctive set of disrupting mutations in the alpha subunit binding interface (Fig. 5a). The original design of this kind of molecule was aimed at shifting the functional balance between immunoregulatory and immunoenhancing actions of IL-2, precluding its preferential use by Tregs that display the trimeric receptor, while leaving signaling through the dimeric beta/gamma receptor (highly abundant in effector cells) unaffected. IL-2 muteins were tested as fusion proteins with a human IgG1 Fc domain. The IL-2 moiety of every fusion protein contained the replacement K35E, already known to reduce the biophysical liabilities of IL-2-based recombinant proteins26.

Measurement of the ability of IL-2 to induce proliferation of mouse CTLL-2 cells, which display the trimeric IL-2R, is the classical indicator of its functionality. Human IL-2 cross-reacts with all the components of mouse IL-2R, which explains responsiveness of this mouse cell line to the human cytokine. Therefore, such in vitro proliferation assays are widely accepted since decades ago as the gold standard test to quantitate the activity of IL-2 preparations27. When a CTLL-2-based colorimetric assay was used, both NA/Fc and 3x/Fc behaved as weaker agonists, in comparison with the similarly formatted fusion protein containing IL-2 (Fig. 5b). Values of specific biological activity, calculated on the bases of the cytokine concentration required to achieve half-maximal proliferation, decreased from more than 106 to 103-104 U/mg upon the introduction of disruptive CD25 interface mutations, confirming the expected effects on signaling through the trimeric IL-2R (Fig. 5f). A similar assay (Fig. 1f), based on CD25-KO CTLL-2 cells, revealed their markedly lower sensitivity to stimulation as compared to the wt cell line. Higher concentrations of all fusion proteins were required to induce cell proliferation (Fig. 5c), highlighting the substantial contribution of CD25 to the strength of receptor-mediated signaling. In this experimental setting, ideally suited to test the effects on cells displaying the dimeric IL-2R, both NA/Fc and 3x/Fc kept the same activity levels of IL-2/Fc, predicting conserved stimulation of mouse effector cells. Specific activity, calculated in an analogous manner to the one used in classical CTLL-2-based assays, was in the range of only 102-103 U/mg (Fig. 5f). This metric allowed a quick comparison with the reference values provided by the internationally accepted CTLL-2 proliferation tests.

The combination of assays based on CTTL-2 cells and their CD25-KO counterparts was useful to show the modified functional profile of IL-2-derived CD25-non-binders in a simple and reproducible way, validating the original design of the muteins and their suitability for in vivo experiments in mouse models. Similar experiments with human cells confirmed the expected behavior of fusion proteins in a scenario that is closer to the final application of these molecules in human therapy (Fig. 5d-e). While the proliferation-inducing ability of NA/Fc and 3X/Fc on Kit225 wt cell line was estimated in the range 103-104 U/mg, several orders of magnitude lower than the one of IL-2/Fc (above 106 U/mg), both muteins showed the same activity than the reference fusion protein IL-2/Fc (103-104 U/mg) on CD25-KO Kit225 cells displaying the dimeric IL-2R (Fig. 5f).

Proliferation assays based on CD25-KO cell lines underscored the increased potency of IL-2-derived CD122 super-binders

Another application of the above-described proliferation assays based on CD25-KO cells was the characterization of a panel of IL-2-derived muteins having improved binding affinity for CD122, known as either superkines14 or superbetas28, and designed to enhance signaling strength through the dimeric beta/gamma receptor on effector cells in the absence of CD25. This is another way of shifting the functional balance between immunoregulatory and immunostimulant actions of IL-2 towards the latter, without affecting the intrinsic CD25 binding ability (see the previous section). Several CD122 super-binders were tested in the current study: a K35E-containing Fc-fused version of the superkine H9 previously obtained through yeast display-based directed evolution14, and a panel of similarly formatted variants selected/designed after phage display screening15. This group includes a wide variety of changes at the beta subunit binding interface (Fig. 6a).

Classical CTLL-2-based proliferation assays failed to reveal any advantage of CD122 super-binders over IL-2/Fc, presumably due to the dominant role of CD25-mediated interaction in cell stimulation, which masks the contribution of beta chain binding (Fig. 6b). Genetic ablation of CD25 expression in CD25-KO CTLL-2 cells revealed a substantial increase (above two orders of magnitude) of the ability of all CD122 super-binders to stimulate them through the dimeric beta/gamma receptor in comparison to the IL-2/Fc, which induced a weaker response (Fig. 6c). Two different levels of super-agonism were distinguished. While fusion proteins containing H9 moiety and other muteins resembling H9 in terms of changes at the hydrophobic core (Fig. 6a) consistently exhibited activities above 2 × 105 U/mg, SB830/Fc and SB834/Fc represent a different family of structural solutions, and their specific activity was in the range between 104 and 105 U/mg (Fig. 6f). Proliferation assays based on knockout cells were thus powerful enough to reveal subtle differences among CD122-targeted super-agonists, beyond showing the mere superiority over the reference IL-2-containing fusion protein, whose activity was only in the range of 102 U/mg.

Similar results were obtained with human cells. The advantage of fusion proteins comprising CD122 super-binders to induce proliferation was only evident on CD25-KO Kit225 cells (Fig. 6e) and not with the parental cell line (Fig. 6d). In this scenario, the activity increased by more than 70-fold: from less than 104 U/mg for IL-2/Fc to more than 105 for the super-binders (Fig. 6f). The ranking of super-agonists’ potency in this experimental setting was not identical to the one based on proliferation of mouse cells (see above). This was attributed to possible differences in the affinity to human CD122 (used as selector molecule during panning)15 and to its mouse counterpart.

Besides the bulk differences between CD122 super-binders and the reference protein IL-2/Fc, it was interesting to compare H9/Fc with its closely related variant H9L92/Fc, which differs from the former by a single mutation (F92L). This change had been shown to remove biophysical liabilities associated to H9 sequence15. H9L92/Fc was always slightly more active than H9/Fc (Fig. 6f), but such minor differences were not accentuated in the CD25-KO context, where beta chain affinity becomes dominant, confirming that the residue at position 92 influences the global folding of the molecule rather than its interaction with CD12215.

CD25-KO cell lines allowed the comparison of IL-2-derived antagonist muteins in different contexts

Another use of CD25-KO cell lines was the evaluation of IL-2-derived antagonists. Unlike the previously tested agonist muteins, all able to stimulate cells trough their IL-2R complexes, molecules with disrupted CD132 binding interface (critical for signal transduction) are expected to have severely reduced biological activity. If their interaction with the gamma subunit of the IL-2R (also known as CD132) is fully interrupted, the resulting molecules could behave as antagonists, precluding the access of fully active IL-2 to the receptors. Theoretically, IL-2-derived antagonists could block both trimeric and dimeric receptors, compromising the stimulation of Tregs and effector cells. Such opposite effects complicate the prediction of the net immunological outcome. The availability of CD25-KO versions of lymphoid cell lines represented a step forward towards the discrimination of the antagonism on each cell class.

This was illustrated by the characterization of an Fc-fused version of the antagonist H9-RETR, which combines a disrupted gamma chain binding interface with additional replacements that enhance beta chain binding16, and has been shown to inhibit the proliferation of effector lymphocytes. Another variant, sharing mutations in the gamma chain interface with H9-RETR but keeping the original beta subunit interface (RETR), was also tested, in an attempt to dissect the relative contribution of each set of mutations to the observed biological effect (Fig. 7a). Neither wt CTLL-2/Kit225 cell lines nor their CD25-KO counterparts were stimulated by any of the fusion proteins containing gamma interface-disrupting mutations, showing that the first requisite for efficient antagonism (lack of residual signaling ability) was accomplished (Fig. 7b).

When CTLL-2 cells were treated with a mixture of the antagonists and a fixed concentration of signaling-competent IL-2/Fc, proliferation induced by the latter was inhibited in a dose-response manner by both H9-RETR and RETR (Fig. 7c). However, only H9-RETR at the highest tested concentrations was able to block the IL-2-induced proliferation of CD25-KO CTLL-2 cells (Fig. 7d), highlighting the importance of improving beta chain binding to obtain a super-antagonist able to affect cells displaying the dimeric beta/gamma receptor. CD122 affinity was thus crucial to determine the sensitivity to IL-2R antagonism in the absence of CD25 binding, in the same way that it dictates the potency of stimulation through the dimeric beta/gamma receptor (see the previous section). Similar results were obtained when analyzing antagonism on human Kit225 cells and their CD25-KO counterparts (Fig. 7e-f). The above-described results provided a simple validation of the molecular design of the antagonist H9-RETR, which is aimed at controlling effector responses.

Functional properties were reproducible among diverse CD25-KO cell lines

Since there is certain risk of non-specific fortuitous genetic changes related to lentiviral integration and off-target genetic edition, which are difficult to discard without an extensive genomic/transcriptomic study, a simple approach was chosen to strength the link between the intended genetic modification and the output of functional assays involving IL-2-derived muteins. A panel of representative fusion proteins containing different classes of muteins was tested in proliferation assays based on two additional knockout cell lines independently derived from the parental CTLL-2 cell line (C10T and C22T), and two obtained through edition of Kit225 cells (K10 and K17). The already characterized CD25-KO C5FBS and K6 cell lines, as well as both wt parental cells, were included as controls. The response induced by each protein variant reproduced the one observed in previous experiments, and the results with the three analyzed CD25-KO cell lines on each background showed a total coincidence (Suppl. Figures 2–3). In the case of knockout cells derived from CTLL-2, the two newly tested cell lines, unlike C5FBS, had been obtained using tryptone-packaged lentiviruses. Their similarity supported the initial idea that, despite quantitative differences in transduction efficiency depending on the viral source, the method used to produce lentiviral particles did not influence functional properties upon genetic edition.

Proliferation assays problems and pitfalls

Even though both CD25-KO CTLL-2 and Kit225 cell lines, as well as their wt counterparts, were useful for the intended proliferation assays, some artifacts were eventually observed. Prior knowledge about them could help the researchers to optimize their experimental designs. Interestingly, the frequency, nature and impact of such artifacts were variable depending on the base cell line. CTLL-2 and Kit225 cells not only differ in their mouse/human origin. While CTLL-2 originated from long-term cultured cytotoxic CD8 + cytotoxic T cells generated through allogeneic stimulation19, Kit225 cell line was derived from a CD4 + T cell leukemia20. All these differences create diverse physiological contexts for IL-2R function beyond receptor composition itself.

Proliferation assays based on CTLL-2 cells (either wt or CD25-KO) were easy to perform. Experimental artifacts were uncommon. However, the remarkably low sensitivity CD25-KO cells to IL-2 stimulation had to be considered during experimental design. Only high concentrations (in the range of µg/mL) of IL-2-derived molecules were able to induce proliferation of knockout cell lines, unless mutations increasing CD122 binding affinity were included in the molecular design. Therefore, it was not possible to obtain the complete sigmoidal curves for most agonist variants (Figs. 4c and 5c), and a serial dilution factor of only two-fold had to be used in order to detect proliferation at several points of the curve (Fig. 8a). This was in sharp contrast with highly sensitive wt CTLL-2 proliferation assays, which typically worked in the ng/mL range and below, rendering full sigmoidal curves (Figs. 4b and 5b).

Common problems faced during in vitro proliferation assays based on IL-2 dependent cell lines. Kit225-based assays showed higher sensitivity to IL-2-mediated stimulation in the absence of CD25 binding, allowing to depict typical sigmoidal curves in most cases (Figs. 4d and 5d). However, these experiments were much more prone to suffer a wide variety of technical problems. Both the wt cell line and its CD25-KO counterpart depend on the presence of IL-2 for long-term culture, but their dependency is not so strict as the one of their CTLL-2 counterparts. They can survive around one week without receiving signals through the IL-2R20, and proliferate during the first few hours after removing IL-2 from the culture media. Therefore, in contrast to CTLL-2 cells, which were immediately used after washing out the maintenance medium containing an IL-2 variant23, a long starvation period of Kit225 cells (at least 16 h in FBS-supplemented RPMI), was required before using them for proliferation assays. Otherwise, the high basal cytokine-independent proliferation overcame any effect of the tested molecules, which were equivalent to the negative control protein IL-6/Fc (Fig. 8b). A long incubation time with the colorimetric reagent also caused the loss of cytokine-dependent effects. After 6 h, non-specific reduction of resazurin contained in Alamar Blue resulted in the uniform development of a high signal in every well (Fig. 8c). That is why, the experiments had to be carefully monitored and read within the first 6 h after adding the colorimetric reagent. Non-specific signals tended to be high with Kit225 cells, but using the proper incubation time in every step, specific activity could be detected over such elevated background levels (Fig. 8d).

Finally, positional artifacts affected the output of proliferation assays based on both cell lines and their knockout counterparts. This is illustrated for one of them in Fig. 8e, where the non-specific background in wells located in the external plate border was very high and resulted in an unacceptable variability when these values were averaged with their replicates at internal positions of the plate. The problem was even more accentuated for Kit225 cell lines, but disappeared when the use of all wells in the plate borders for growing cells was avoided. These wells were only filled with culture media to reduce evaporation at the internal positions of the plate.

Stat5 phosphorylation was an alternative functional readout of CD25-KO-based assays

Although the final readout of most of the experiments was cell proliferation, CD25-KO lymphoid cell lines were also useful to assess signaling induced by IL-2R occupancy15. The JAK-Stat5 pathway is rapidly activated upon IL-2 binding and has been used to detect early signaling events29. Stat5 phosphorylation was indeed measured by flow cytometry in experiments of stimulation of CD25-KO cell lines to evaluate a critical individual IL-2R signaling event upstream the final proliferative response.

The shape of dose-dependent curves of Stat5 phosphorylation resembled those of proliferation induced by recombinant fusion proteins containing IL-2-derived muteins on either wt or CD25-KO cell lines (Fig. 9). While wt cells were highly sensitive to treatment with IL-2/Fc and with the beta super-binder SB834/Fc, higher concentrations (> 2000x) of the alpha non-binder NA/Fc, classified as a weak agonist, were required to induce equivalent levels of Stat5 phosphorylation (Figs. 9a, c, e). These results confirmed the dominant role of alpha chain binding in cells able to display the trimeric receptor. On the other hand, similar experiments showed that CD25-KO cell lines are less sensitive to stimulation by both IL-2/Fc and NA/Fc, whose ability to induce Stat5 phosphorylation in the absence of the alpha chain was in the same range (Figs. 9b, d, e). In that context the beta super-binder SB834/Fc displayed a clear signaling advantage over the other variants (by more than 40- and 220-fold in CTLL-2 and Kit225 backgrounds, respectively), as expected for a dimeric IL-2R-biased super-agonist (Fig. 9b, d and e). No phosphorylation-inducing ability was detected for RETR/Fc, the protein having a disrupted interface with the gamma receptor subunit, on any of the cells, which is compatible with the crucial role of gamma chain binding in inducing biological responses.

Discussion

In vitro evaluation of biological activity is an important step in every research project aimed at developing IL-2-related immunotherapeutic candidates. Moreover, this kind of experiment is a routine procedure along manufacture process of IL-2-based products, due to its simplicity and standardization potential30. However, the output of classical proliferation assays based on cells displaying the trimeric alpha/beta/gamma receptor like CTLL-219, Kit22520 and HT-231 is only mechanistically linked to the intended use when the target cells are those expressing constitutively high levels of this receptor form, as in the case of Treg-directed therapies. Manipulation of other immune cell populations displaying the dimeric beta/gamma IL-2R requires dedicated cellular models to assess responses mediated by such a receptor version.

Other cell lines like Jurkat32 and YT33, expressing the dimeric beta/gamma IL-2R, change receptor composition upon stimulation. Therefore, they are not ideal cellular models. CD25-negative NK-type YT-1 cells were useful to evaluate the activity of IL-2-derived super-agonists in a scenario of beta/gamma IL-2R-mediated signalling14. However, activation of these cells induces CD25 expression, and sorting to exclude CD25 + cells was required. Their incomplete removal can affect the experimental output. Similar enrichment strategies have been applied not only to cell lines, but to lymphocyte populations from different sources. This approach allows testing IL-2 variants on the same cell type intended to target. However, cell purification can be stressful34, manipulation of samples from human donors introduces the risk of contamination with human pathogens35, and functional variability between donors36 complicates the interpretation of the results.

Genetically engineered reporter systems allowed the selective introduction of IL-2R subunits on otherwise non-responsive non-lymphoid cells. HEK-Blue IL-2 cells were generated by InvivoGen through transfection with the human IL-2R alpha, beta and gamma genes, and a Stat5-inducible reporter gene. An assay based on these cells has been used to test the activity of IL-2-based prodrugs37. InvivoGen further generated a variant of these cells known as HEK-Blue CD122/CD132 expressing only IL-2R beta and gamma chains (https://www.invivogen.com/hek-blue-cd122-cd132). Genetic ablation of CD25 expression in lymphoid IL-2-responsive cell lines, already displaying beta and gamma IL-2R subunits, combines the advantages of a simple genetic manipulation of rather homogeneous cell populations with the linkage to the original downstream signaling pathways in a more natural cellular context. The first attempt to do so was based on targeting the synthesis of anti-CD25 intracellular antibodies (intrabodies) with a retention signal at the endoplasmic reticulum of Jurkat cells, where they produced a phenotypic knockout of the alpha chain without deleting its gene, allowing the expression of a functional beta/gamma IL-2R upon activation38.

Another intrabody against CD25 was used to ablate its expression on Kit225 cells39. The use of these models has been limited up to now. The advent of CRISPR/Cas technology greatly increased the chances to generate permanent tailor-made knockouts40. CD25-KO CTLL-2 cells were obtained in that way for the first time some years ago41. The authors sorted CD25-negative populations after transduction and mixed them with wt CTLL-2, to test their relative responsiveness to IL-2-derived muteins.

The current work expanded this initial experience to the generation and long-term culture of stable CD25-KO cell lines that can be kept as homogeneous and robust models of beta/gamma IL-2R-mediated responses of lymphoid mouse cells. This approach was extended to the creation of a new CD25-KO Kit225 cellular model of human origin (not explored before), ideally suited for translational research. The usefulness of both knockout cell lines, together with their wt counterparts, has been assessed in proliferation assays, able to underscore the unique functional profiles within a panel of known IL-2-derived muteins.

Although phenotypic CD25 deficiency was shown along the generation of knockout cell lines, and during their subsequent long-term culture, no sequencing of the targeted alleles was performed to visualize directly gene edition details. Cell lines were shown to be free of gross phenotypic defects in terms of growth and responsiveness to IL-2 and other molecules derived from it, but subtle modifications in their physiology due to off-target genetic edition and/or lentiviral integration cannot be easily ruled out. However, highly similar functional responses of several independent cell lines with the same intended genetic modification point to specific effects of targeting CD25 gene in the absence of other undesired artifacts.

One important modification of the typical protocols used to maintain CTLL-2 and Kit225 cell cultures, as well as their knockout derivatives, in our laboratory is the addition of the No-alpha IL-2 variant9 to the media (instead of wt IL-2). This choice had two major reasons: a practical one related to the availability of a high-quality source of this molecule produced for clinical trials23,42, and theoretical considerations about its suitability to obtain CD25-KO cell lines. While wt IL-2 with intact binding ability to the alpha IL-2R subunit could preferentially amplify the residual CD25 + population during the process of knockout generation, through the high affinity interaction with the trimeric receptor; the use of a variant unable to bind the alpha chain would result in proper stimulation of CD25-KO cells through the dimeric receptor without conferring any advantage to cells escaping CD25 ablation. This would in principle facilitate the workflow to obtain fully CD25-deficient cell lines. Even though we recommend the use of this kind of IL-2-derived mutein with a disrupted CD25 interface during the initial stages of CD25-KO lymphoid cells’ generation whenever it is possible, routine use of these lines in most laboratories should rely on the addition of commercially available wt IL-2 for maintenance. Therefore, we demonstrated phenotypic stability of CD25-KO cell lines upon long-term culture in the presence of wt IL-2, which guarantees their usefulness in any laboratory.

Proliferation assays based on our CD25-KO cell lines took advantage of the use of Alamar Blue, a non-radioactive, non-toxic, and sample-sparing colorimetric reagent, with only minor modifications of the recommended protocols43. These assays have already been used for the early characterization of CD122-biased IL-2R super-agonists15,28. The clear advantage of variants with increased affinity for CD122 over wt IL-2-containing molecules in these cellular models was validated by further in vitro studies with mouse/human lymphocytes and in vivo experiments in mice15,28. The biological activity of IL-2-derived variants on CD25-KO model lymphoid cell lines was thus shown to predict its capacity to stimulate immune effector cells in vivo and their subsequent anti-tumor effects. The performance of other muteins in similar CD25-KO cells-based proliferation assays also matched with their designs and the results reported by other authors in diverse experimental settings9,25.

In the case of IL-2-derived antagonists, the availability of CD25-KO cell lines offered an opportunity to explore directly the different behavior of signaling-incompetent variants through the assessment of their capacity to inhibit IL-2-induced proliferation. Disruption of the interface with the signaling gamma chain resulted in either downmodulation of effector responses16 or preferential Treg inhibition and enhancement of the anti-tumor response17. Parallel assessment of biological activity on wt lymphoid cell lines and CD25-KO modified cells provided a simple clue towards elucidating the molecular bases of such a dichotomy. While mutations affecting the interaction with gamma chain gave rise to a selective antagonist targeting only cells displaying the trimeric IL-2R (like Tregs), the same replacements combined with other changes increasing the affinity for CD122 rendered a super-antagonist, now inhibiting also the expansion of cells that express the dimeric IL-2R (like resting CD8 + lymphocytes). These findings explained the shift of the functional balance towards immune downmodulation, already observed for the mutein combining changes at both interfaces16.

It is important to remark that the rationale behind the design of many IL-2-derived muteins (differential activation of Tregs having a trimeric IL-2R versus effector cells displaying a dimeric receptor) is a useful over simplification of a more complex reality. Even though this reductionist thinking has led to the discovery of promising therapeutic candidates, transient expression of CD25 in activated effector cells complicates the scenario. For instance, CTLL-2 cell line, the classical model to assess stimulation through the alpha/beta/gamma receptor typically displayed by Tregs, is indeed derived from long-term cultured activated cytotoxic T cells, which under these conditions express CD25. Therefore, the pairs of wt and CD25-KO lymphoid cell lines tested here provide reliable models of stimulation though trimeric and dimeric receptor-bearing cells, not directly equivalent to Tregs and effector cells at any stage of development or activation status. A rigorous analysis of the functional implications of the results obtained in this experimental setting must take into account the complex dynamics of regulated expression of the different receptor subunits in each cell type.

The methods described here are simple and versatile. Although the typical readout of the assays was cell proliferation, the feasibility of assessing Stat5 phosphorylation was also shown. These experiments could still be optimized in terms of finding the ideal incubation times to reach maximal phosphorylation levels. CD25-KO lymphoid cell lines could in principle be useful to follow the whole signaling cascade induced by IL-2R occupancy29. Moreover, the current experience could be extended to the ablation of CD25 expression on other lymphoid cell lines of interest. A different, although technically overlapping, field of application of the lentiviral vectors created here could be the genetic modification of primary T cells belonging to defined subpopulations, Genetic ablation of CD25 expression in these particular cellular contexts could reveal new insights on the role of diverse receptor arrays in functional responses of immune cells. Ex vivo modification of cells, followed by assessment of their functions in vivo, could provide valuable information connected to other therapeutic approaches, like gene therapy and/or adoptive cell transfer procedures. Such applications might represent additional ways to modulate the balance between divergent immunological effects, besides the use of biased IL-2-derived muteins.

In summary, precise CRISPR/Cas-mediated genomic edition to ablate CD25 expression on lymphoid cell lines of both mouse and human origin has been achieved with a rather simple lentiviral transduction protocol. The resulting CD25-KO model cell lines are stable, and useful to assess the responses mediated by the dimeric beta/gamma IL-2 receptor to a variety of IL-2-derived muteins with unique functional profiles. This experimental setting, which simulates the IL-2 receptor array on resting immune effectors, has proven to provide valuable information about IL-2-derived attenuated agonists, super-agonists and antagonists. Its use could allow the strict head-to-head comparison of diverse IL-2-derived muteins generated across different laboratories. CD25-KO lymphoid cells described here, and the assays based on their stimulation, can thus complement and accelerate ongoing strategies of IL-2 engineering aimed at guiding the power of the immune system in the desired direction.

Methods

Cell lines and culture conditions

Mouse CTLL-2 (cytotoxic T cell line isolated from a C57BL-6 mouse, ATCC TIB-214)19, human Kit225 (T lymphocytic leukemia cell line isolated from a patient, ATCC CVCL_5443)20 and their CD25-KO counterparts (obtained during the current work) were grown in suspension 6 well plates in RPMI 1640 media (Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10% (v/v) heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum (FBS), 2 mmol/L glutamine (RPMI/FBS/Gln) plus 1 µg/mL of either recombinant IL-2 no-alpha mutein23 or commercial human IL-2 (NovaTeinBio, USA) at 37 °C under a humidified 5% CO2 atmosphere. Cells were kept in culture by diluting the culture 1: 12.5 in fresh cell culture media.

HEK-293 T cell line (ATCC CRL-3216) for lentivirus packaging was grown in 75 cm2 flasks for adherent cells in DMEMF12 media (10 mL/flask) supplemented with 5% (v/v) of heat-inactivated FBS and 2 mmol/L glutamine. Cells were expanded when 80% of confluence was reached. The expansion protocol was the following: supernatant was removed, and cells from each flask were detached with 2 mL of trypsin solution during 3 min at room temperature (RT). 5–10 mL of DMEMF12 media supplemented with 5% (v/v) of heat- inactivated FBS were used to wash trypsin out. Cells were centrifuged at 160 g and resuspended in a suitable media volume to continue the culture in three flasks.

Assembly of lentiviral particles

DNA sequences encoding single guide RNA specific for either the exon 2 of mouse CD25 gene (GTGTCTGTATGACCCACCCG) or for the exon 2 of human CD25 gene (GGTTTCCGCAGAATAAAAAG) were designed by Sanjana Lab, USA (http://guides.sanjanalab.org/). Lentiviral particles containing the lentiviral vector lentiCRISPR2 neo (Addgene, USA) with the suitable targeting insert coding for each single guide RNA (cloned through BsmBI restriction site) were assembled in HEK-293T cells co-transfected with such genetic construct and the auxiliary plasmids pLPI, pLPII and pLP/VSV-G (Invitrogen, USA). The lentiviral vector and the auxiliary plasmids were mixed in a 2:1:1:1 proportion (12 µg: 6 µg: 6 µg: 6 µg) in 1 mL of DMEMF12 FBS-free media with 82 µg of the transfection agent polyethyleneimine (PEI). The mixture was incubated 20 min at RT. The supernatant of 80% confluent HEK-293T cells, grown in 75 cm2 flasks, was replaced with 10 mL of DMEMF12 media/flask, the above-described DNA/PEI complexes were added to each flask, and incubated during 4–6 h. After feeding each flask with 1 mL of FBS or tryptone stock solution (at 40% (m/v) in culture media), cells were grown for 72 h. Supernatant was collected, filtered (through 0.45 μm pore size) and lentiviruses were quantified using a commercial ELISA kit (LISIDA, Cuba). Supernatant containing lentiviral particles was frozen at -80 °C until use.

Generation of CD25-KO CTLL-2 cell line

Genetically modified CD25-KO CTLL-2 cell line was generated by transduction with lentiviral particles containing the lentiviral vector lentiCRISPR2 neo (Addgene, USA) encoding a single guide RNA targeting the exon 2 of mouse CD25 gene. 20 000 CTLL-2 cells/well were incubated in 96 well tissue culture plates with the HEK-293T cell supernatant containing the lentiviral particles (see the previous section) diluted 1:2 in RPMI 1640 culture media during 18 h. The process was performed by triplicate in independent wells with each lentiviral preparation (packaged in the presence of either FBS or tryptone). After transduction cells were grown in 24 wells tissue culture plates with RPMI/FBS/Gln plus 1 µg/mL of IL-2 no-α mutein23 and G418 at 250 µg/mL as selection drug. Once enough cells were available (> 1 million) they were collected for flow cytometry screening (see below). The remaining cells were used for limiting dilution segregation. Cells were plated at 0.8 cell/well in 96 well plates and grown for 15 days in the same supplemented media with selection drug. Cells from some wells (nine derived from each transduction condition) were expanded to 24 wells plates to collect more than one million of each for flow cytometry screening. Selected CD25-negative cell lines were expanded, now without G418, frozen and kept at -80̊C. One of them, named C5FBS, was grown for 35 passages for stability assessment.

Generation of CD25-KO Kit225 cell line

Genetically modified CD25-KO Kit225 cell line was generated by transduction with lentiviral particles containing the lentiviral vector lentiCRISPR2 neo (Addgene, USA) encoding a single guide RNA targeting the exon 2 of human CD25 gene. 20 000 Kit225 cells/well were incubated in 96 well tissue culture plates with the HEK-293T cell supernatant containing the lentiviral particles diluted 1:2 in RPMI 1640 culture media during 18 h. The process was performed by triplicate in independent wells with the lentiviral preparation packaged using tryptone as feeding additive. After transduction cells were grown in 24 wells tissue culture plates with RPMI/FBS/Gln plus 1 µg/mL of IL-2no-α mutein23 and G418 at 250 µg/mL as selection drug. Once enough cells were available (> 1 million) they were collected for flow cytometry screening (see below). The remaining cells were used for limiting dilution segregation. Cells were plated at 0.8 cell/well in 96 well plates and grown for 15 days in the same supplemented media with the selection drug. Cells from 18 wells were expanded to 24 wells plates to collect more than one million of each for flow cytometry screening. Selected CD25-negative cell lines were expanded, frozen and kept at -80̊C. One of them, named K6, was grown for 35 passages for stability assessment.

Flow cytometry screening

The following antibodies were used for flow cytometry analysis: FITC-conjugated anti-mouse CD25, clone PC61.5 (Invitrogen, USA); PECy5 conjugated anti-mouse CD25, clone PC61.5, APC-conjugated anti-mouse CD25, clone PC61.5, PE-conjugated anti-human CD25, PerCPCy5.5-conjugated anti-human CD25, clone BC96, and PE-conjugated anti-mouse CD122, clone 5H4 (eBioscience, USA); and PE-conjugated anti-human CD122, clone mik-β2 (BD Pharmingen, USA). On the other hand, FITC-conjugated anti-human Fc polyclonal antibody (Dako, USA); PerCPCy5.5-conjugated, anti-human TNFα, clone MaB11, FITC-conjugated anti-human/mouse STAT3, clone Stat3Y705-B12, and PE-conjugated anti-mouse Foxp3, clone NRRF-30 (Invitrogen, USA); and PE-conjugated anti-human Stat3, clone 4/P-Stat3 (eBioscience, USA) were used as isotype controls.

The cells were harvested by centrifugation 160 g during 2 min and washed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Centrifugation was repeated, and the pellet was incubated during 20 min at RT with the antibody diluted in 50 µL of FACS Buffer (PBS plus 2% (m/v) bovine serum albumin (BSA) and 0.05% (m/v) sodium azide). Cells were washed twice with FACS buffer. All the experiments were read in a Gallios cytometer (Beckman Coulter, USA). Acquired data were analyzed with FlowJo v10.0.7 (https://www.flowjo.com/).

Production of the recombinant fusion proteins

Purified pCSE plasmids44, comprising the DNA sequence coding for each of the cytokines/mutated cytokine variants fused to the gene of a modified human IgG1 Fc domain (with the replacements L234A and L235A, known as LALA version) were used to transfect HEK-293T cells adapted to grow in suspension in F-17 Freestyle media (Invitrogen, USA) supplemented with 8mmol/L glutamine, following protocols described elsewhere44. Briefly, 50 µg of each plasmid were diluted in 1.25 mL of F-17, and mixed with 1.25 mL of F-17 containing 125 µg of polyethyleneimine. The mixture was incubated during 20 min at RT, and added to 50 mL of the suspension of growing HEK-293T cells (at 2 × 106 million/mL). Cells were grown during 48 h at 37 °C with shaking at 120 RPM, under a humidified 5% CO2 atmosphere. Then, each flask was fed with 50 mL of supplemented F17 media and 2.5 mL of tryptone stock solution (prepared at 40% (m/v) in F-17), and cells were grown during 5 additional days. Supernatant of transfected cells was collected, and recombinant fusion proteins were purified using a single step of affinity chromatography with HiTrap Protein A HP column (Cytiva, Sweden) with a chromatographic system ÄKTA go (Cytiva, USA) following instructions of the manufacturer. Proteins were quantified by the Bicinchoninic acid assay (BCA, Pierce Biotechnology, USA) following the instructions of the supplier.

Proliferation and inhibition assays

CTLL-2, Kit225, and their CD25-KO counterparts were harvested by centrifugation, and washed three times with RPMI/FBS/Gln. In the cases of Kit225 and its CD25-KO version, a starvation of at least 18 h in culture without any IL-2 source was performed before going ahead. For the proliferation assays, 104 cells/well were seeded in 96 wells plates (flat bottom) and grown in the presence of different concentrations of the fusion proteins comprising the IL-2 muteins under investigation, diluted in RPMI/FBS/Gln. A similarly formatted IL-6-containing fusion protein was used as negative control. CTLL-2 and its CD25-KO version were grown in such conditions during 48 h, while the growth period for Kit225 and its CD25-KO counterpart was extended to 72 h. Alamar Blue reagent (Invitrogen, USA) was added (20 µL/well) and incubated during either 18 h (for wt and CD25-KO CTLL-2) or 6 h (for wt and CD25-KO Kit225). The original blue color of Alamar Blue reagent switched to pink upon incubation with living cells. The absorbances at 540 and 630 nm were measured with a microplate reader (Dialab, Austria) and the doses-response curves were built using the difference (Abs540 nm - Abs630 nm) as function of the protein concentration. It is important to remark that the protein concentration refers to the cytokine/mutated cytokine portion within the fusion proteins, calculated considering the total protein concentration in each sample, determined with BCA, and the fraction that the cytokine moiety represents in the fusion protein. The inhibition assays were performed as described above, except that in these cases, fusion proteins containing the muteins under study were mixed with IL-2/Fc at a single stimulating concentration previously selected for each cell line: 2 ng/mL for CTLL-2 and Kit225, 5 µg/mL for CD25-KO CTLL-2, and 0.5 µg/mL for CD25-KO Kit225.

Stat 5 phosphorylation assays

Cells were harvested by centrifugation, and washed three times with RPMI 1640. A starvation of 6 h in culture without any IL-2 source was performed before going ahead. 2.5 × 105 cells/well were seeded in U-bottom 96-well plates, and incubated during only 15 min in the presence of different concentrations of the recombinant fusion proteins under study. Supernatants were removed by centrifugation and the cells were fixed and permeabilized with cold 90% (v/v) methanol during at least 20 min. Cells were kept at -20 °C in cold methanol until used. Methanol was removed by centrifugation and the cells were washed once with PBS/BSA 2% (FACS buffer). Staining with either PE-conjugated anti-pStat5 (Biolegend cat. 936904, clone A17016B.rec) or its isotype control (Invitrogen cat. 12-4714-B2, clone P3.6.2.8.2) was performed during 30 min at 4 °C. All the experiments were read in a Gallios cytometer (Beckman Coulter, USA). Acquired data were analyzed with FlowJo v10.0.7 (https://www.flowjo.com/).

Dose-response curves were built by plotting the percentage of pStat5 + cells against cytokine concentration. EC50 were estimated as the cytokine concentrations required to reach half-maximal percentage of pStat5 + cells.

Data analysis

Bar graphs corresponding to the comparison of proliferation of the knockout cells and their wt counterparts show the media and SD of three independent data points. Statistical analyses were performed using Student’s t test (*p < 0.05; **p < 0.01; ***p < 0.001; ****p < 0.0001). Dose-response curves representing the output of proliferation, inhibition, and phosphorylation assays show the mean and SD of two independent values. Graphs were constructed and statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism v9 (https://www.graphpad.com/).

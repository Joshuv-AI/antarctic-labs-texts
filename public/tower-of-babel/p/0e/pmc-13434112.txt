Athletes’ performance and injury management in sports training using association rules and data mining techniques
Authors: Lvbo Chen, Bin Mo, XiaoYan Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOptimizing athletic performance while minimizing injury risk remains a central challenge in sports science. Although large volumes of athlete monitoring data are now available, existing studies primarily rely on modeling or isolated statistical analyses, with limited exploration of interpretable pattern discovery for understanding multi-factor relationships. This study addresses this gap by applying association rule mining techniques, including Apriori, FP-Growth, and Eclat, to identify conditional relationships within a comprehensive sports training dataset comprising demographic, physiological, psychological, and training-related variables. Unlike prior work focused mainly on classification accuracy, this approach emphasizes interpretable rule extraction to identify key combinations of training intensity, recovery status, sleep, and prior injury history associated with performance and injury outcomes. Rule quality is evaluated using standard association measures, while validation is conducted through classification-based metrics. The results show that association rule mining can reveal stable and high-impact patterns that may inform monitoring interpretation, support hypothesis generation, and help prioritize factors for future prospective validation. This study highlights the value of interpretable data mining frameworks for exploring association structures in sports training data within a non-causal analytical setting.Keywords: Sports analytics, Association rule mining, Athlete performance, Data mining, Pattern discovery, Athlete monitoringSubject terms: Classification and taxonomy, Computational models, Data acquisition, Data integration, Data mining, Data processing, Literature mining, Machine learning, Software, Standards, Statistical methods

Introduction

Sports have always been a powerful reflection of human potential, combining passion, discipline, and teamwork in the quest of excellence. In the fast-evolving world of sports, the significance of athletics is rapidly increasing as new domain based on various data-driven insights to reshape how athletes train, compete, and recover1. The real challenge is understanding how different factors are associated with each other and identifying patterns within training and performance data2. By identifying such patterns, coaches and athletes can derive data-informed insights for training and recovery. The identification of interpretable associations among athlete variables supports analytical understanding in sports science3. Understanding athletic performance patterns and injury occurrence remains a central objective in sports science. Modern athlete monitoring systems generate large volumes of multidimensional data, including physiological metrics, training load variables, psychological indicators, and injury histories4. Although such data provides opportunities for advanced analytics, transforming them into interpretable knowledge for analytical understanding remains a significant challenge. Coaches and sports scientists require analytical methods that not only characterize outcome patterns but also explain how combinations of variables are associated with performance and injury occurrence5,6.

As far as known, most of the previous studies on sports analytics have dealt with modelling methods including regression analysis, machine learning classification and deep learning methods. Association rule mining (ARM) is an interpretable approach that is used to derive multi-factor relationships in complex data sets that can be used to achieve high accuracy yet are not a black-box model7, Even though ARM has been successfully applied in various fields like market basket analysis8, target detection9 and social network influence detection, it has yet been under-investigated in sports training analytics. Existing research using data mining in sports tends to focus on a single aspect of the sport, either training load, biomechanics or injury incidence, and does not systematically investigate interactions between demographic, physiological and/or contextual variables. In addition, there is a lack of studies that comparatively evaluate different ARM algorithms in a unified sports training framework. Consequently, little is known about the performance of the various rule mining techniques for discovering meaningful and strong patterns related to athlete performance and injury outcomes in practice. This study is thus directed by the following research questions to overcome the found gaps in interpreting sports performance analytics: The questions are directed towards identifying multiple factor associations that are statistically validated for the athlete training data and assessing the strength of the associations and consistency of the extracted rules for various strategies of mining.

This study addresses these gaps by applying and systematically comparing three association rule mining algorithms including Apriori, FP-Growth, and Eclat on a comprehensive sports training dataset. We aim to emphasis key multi-factor patterns that are related to injury occurrence, progression to recovery and performance outcomes rather than just predicting injury occurrence. We combine rule evaluation metrics with model validation to evaluate rule strengths, statistical validity and consistency. Moreover, the paper demonstrates that interpretability based on rules can be combined with the data-driven approach to analysis, providing interpretable insights that can guide analytical understanding, which is often missing in a black box machine learning framework. The main outcomes analyzed in this study include (i) injury incidence and (ii) classification of injury while performing various tasks during an athlete training session. In this setup, Injury_Occurred is used as the main target variable to be evaluated, with other variables such as injury type, injury severity and recovery stage are used as descriptive attributes and not used as target variables. The structured monitoring indicators were based on recovery stage, workload balance, and psychological readiness, and used to determine performance state. For injuries when recorded, severity was classified according to the type of injury recorded, and records of the progression of the recovery stage, as descriptive outcome indicators rather than as causal factors. Each outcome variable was categorized according to session level information, and each outcome was dichotomized at a specific threshold, consistent with the rest of the dataset. These definitions enable systematic extraction of conditional multi-factors patterns which preserve the causal interpretation of the relation in between the factors without going beyond the observed relation. The results are presented as session rather than subject level longitudinal effects.

Related work

Recent literature in sports analytics has exploited rule mining and classification techniques to enhance training, performance evaluation, action analysis and injury prevention10. Also, new developments in sports science, epidemiology, and machine learning have paid more attention to the enhanced approaches to injury prediction, recovery modeling, and methodological validation of athlete monitoring systems.

Association rule mining for training optimization

A study11 developed a sports-training decision system based on an enhanced Apriori association-rule algorithm which performed well in decision making about the training program. Similarly, based on auxiliary decision support model12 introduced based on association-rule mining and the weighted voting approach by employing posterior probability weighting to combine classification ones for measuring training effectiveness. Furthermore, ARM13 refined the compression and hashing Tricks) to support the mining of multi-dimensional association analysis of athletes’ training data is then combined with a fuzzy feature selection random-forest classification algorithm (trained and tested on improved whale algorithm) to analyze the quality of the training and recovery of college athletes. A few works concentrate on unsupervised approaches and computations improvements in sports data mining14, introduced a heuristic Apriori algorithm specific for physical education data (with transactional pruning and hashing too), and it drastically reduces the time of rule mining in fitness test data and it does not lose in accuracy. Another study15 suggested data mining technology for scientific training of an athlete in the higher education of sports or education. It takes multi-dimensional association rules and attributes mining to enhance training and to modify the training programs. Fuzzy decision-making and whale algorithms and random forest are utilized in the smart recovery assessment of the system. Body composition training and recovery optimization optimized physical fitness of the athletes and other health indicators, and the plan was also practical among athletes. In addition to strictly monitored systems16, subgroup discovery algorithms used to detect multi-week neuromuscular threshold trends that are correlated with the risk of injury. The identified rule-like conditions were like those of association rule logic and showed how understandable antecedent-outcome structures can be used to interpret risks.

These studies focus on applying Apriori-based and enhanced ARM techniques to training decision systems, fitness evaluation, and physical education datasets.

Injury analytics and risk pattern discovery

In the area of injury and recovery, conditional patterns of recovery have been discovered via association rules. In addition17, used unsupervised learning (DBSCAN, isolation forest) and association-rule mining to analyze more than two decades of NBA injury data. They did deduce, for instance, a confidence > 50% significant players with lower limb strains were recovering faster than historical, indicating they are doing position/class injury targeted rehab well. Sarlis et al.18 also used anomaly detection and association rules to the NBA, correlated recovering less to injury types and player salaries to speed of recovery even. Meanwhile19 proposed a big-data risk assessment framework that leverages association-rule mining and clustering on sports injury data, aiming to discover the relationship between different injury types and the injury severity, to provide personalized rehabilitation strategies. On one hand, on classification model20 predicted short-track speed-skating injuries a week in advance using random forests. Their composite model (load and physiological markers) demonstrated modest performance, suggesting that the feature set enhanced performance beyond that of the load-only model. These studies highlight the potential of data mining to find hidden risk patterns in athlete’s injuries (via association rules) and to train models for injuries prevention, however accuracy model still an issue in real world applications. K-mean clustering21 performed on the biomechanics of the CMJ to profile hidden movement strategy patterns associated with injury risk, in military recruits, thereby demonstrating unsupervised classification of movement strategies to flag individuals at high risk of injury. Furthermore22, used data mining algorithm used for the Min-Max injury risk prediction and prevention algorithm is developed to analyze such factors as training load, biomechanics and environmental conditions. It estimates the chances of injury with model-based predictions and offers individual preventive measures. The algorithm estimates the best and worst cases injury probabilities, which allow practitioners to recognize possible results and unpredictability. It assists in determining those athletes who have higher risks of injuries and shows the low-risk cases during simulation experiments. The study23 examined the prediction of ACL injury on 880 elite female players by using various classifiers tested in 100 repeated cross-validations and permutation tests. Even though the highest-performing linear SVM had a small average AUC-ROC of about 0.63 (standard deviation 0.51 0.69), permutation testing revealed that it was performing above chance. The results suggest the significance of repeated cross-validation and statistical tests in interpretation of moderate discrimination performance. The approach of modeling in clinical practice was expanded upon by24, who formed an MRI-based injury classification model together with machine learning regressors that predicted the hamstring recovery time in professional football players. The study showed that combining measures with clinical reliability measures is valuable, with the value of MAE of 9.8 days and R2 of 0.48 with high reliability (Cohen 0.93 or above on most measures). Also25, demonstrated that even with 96.3% total accuracy with SVM and SMOTE, the sensitivity was only 25% indicating how accuracy can be deceptive in unbalanced injury data and how performance measures should be richer. Temporal validation has become significant also. In a comparison of models in 2-week and 4-week injury windows26, found that AUC-PR (up to 0.83) is a more informative statistic compared to raw accuracy when the models are imbalanced.

Hybrid Rule-Based and Supervised Classification Models

Rule-based classification has also contributed to the performance assessment of athletes. SmartTrain model proposed27 based on SVM classifier equipped with association-rule mining (Apriori) results to estimate individual athletic performance from training data. By discovering relationships and supplying the results to an SVM, SmartTrain reached 89.3% accuracy– much better most landmarks. In team sports28, proposed a Sports Action Mining framework, which relied on FPGrowth to transform positional data to feature sequences and further employed a random-forest classifier. This resulted in 93.3% balaned accuracy in detecting complex action game play of soccer. Similarly, another study29 also integrates an Apriori rule mining into a decision-tree classification to investigate the fitness-test results of the Chinese students where their proposed system demonstrated a strong capability of classifying the athletic levels of a learner and generating individualized training programs. These experimental cases illustrate the power of a hybrid approach; association rules provide an interpretable explanation of factors governing performance, whereas supervised learners yield high level accuracy in determining athlete fitness and skill. In30, they compared more extensive models and found 72 models to predict lower-extremity soft-tissue injury. The last UBAG[SMO] model obtained AUC 0.700 ± 0.088 and integrated SHAP-based interpretability that places a focus on the transparent reporting of the TP/TN rates and the imbalance-aware evaluation. On the same note31, also compared a variety of classifiers on repeat 10-fold cross-validation and multi-metric evaluation, with AUC values reaching 0.86 and highlighted stratified results among positions. GPS-based in workload-based injury prediction32, suggested a GPS-based system combining feature selection, learning with imbalance sensitivity and repeated cross-validation with AUC of about 0.85 in professional groups. Lastly33, created a prospective multi-club ensemble model with inner cross-validation and outer next-season temporal validation with an AUC-PR value of 0.759 versus 0.589 of logistic regression. The study also showed calibration, hazard ratios and clinical utility and is one of the prototypical examples of contemporary validation in the field of injury epidemiology.

NBA-specific performance and contextual analytics

In recent literature, the analytics in NBA sports have been making more use of data mining and statistical modeling as a tool to reveal the dynamics of performance in various game scenarios. The study used data mining model to examine quarter-by-quarter patterns of the NBA player performance, and it was found that offensive efficiency, defensive performance and other metrics of overall impacts like Net Rating have a significant impact on the result of the game, especially in different conditions of regular season and playoffs38. Following the contextual performance analysis, a different research put forward the estimation of clutch competency metric to measure player performance in the last few minutes of tightly contested games, and the advanced modeling is essential to assess high-pressure performance and compare the findings with league recognition standards39. Moreover, the study of injury analytics has investigated the long-term and short-term impacts on player performance on a large-scale longitudinal data (based on paired statistical testing and analysis of effect sizes), assessing the performance changes between the pre- and post-injury periods40. Collectively, these papers signal the growing importance of data science in basketball analytics, but they pay much attention to model and statistical inferences but limited the scope to the interpretable pattern discovery methods like association rule mining to advance strategic decision-making.

The change that is being analyzed is the shift of single-metric accuracy reporting to imbalance-aware, multi-metric validation. Some applied injury studies have indicated that high accuracy can be accompanied by low recall/sensitivity in rare events of monitoring (youth soccer monitoring), which supports the necessity of reporting sensitivity/specificity, AUC-PR and consistency. Second, validation rigor is also becoming homogeneous: repeated-CV and stability summaries have become more frequently used (e.g., repeated CV/repeated runs), but few studies on sports-injury have stronger generalization evidence such as temporal validation and calibration/clinical utility, which is explicitly shown in the 2026 professional football cohort study. Third, interpretable frameworks are becoming accessible in two complementary ways (a) explicit rule discovery (Apriori ARM in NBA injury economics; subgroup-discovery thresholds in elite football neuromuscular testing) and (b) interpretable ML feature-importance/explainability pipelines (e.g., SHAP in youth soccer, weighted importance in rugby). These strands endorse utilizing positioning association-rule outputs as risk-pattern discovery validity pipeline, as opposed to causal analysis.

Proposed research methodology

In this paper, we utilize the association rule mining to analyze and summarize actionable patterns from large-scale sports training datasets (data) to better understand the determinants that contribute to an athlete´s performance and injury susceptibility41. The dataset is systematically organized in assorted categories such as demographic information, personal data, training reports, physiological and biometric records, metrics of performance, stress and recovery markers, injury and illnesses documentation, psychological measures, and environmental conditions. Each type consists of a few potential features – age, gender, type of training session, HR metrics, agility scores, sleep quality, injury history, environmental conditions and many others. The heterogeneous and multidimensional nature of these attributes allows them to be used to find strong association rules which give more insight into the underlying unstructured information. The study focuses on a dichotomous dependent variable (Injury_Occurred (Yes/No)) that is well defined. Distinct categorization of variables has been done for pre-event monitoring variables (e.g., training intensity, workload, physiological measures, psychological state) and for post-event variables and/or contemporaneous (e.g., injury type, injury severity, recovery stage, medical assessment) for conceptual soundness. The interpretation needed to be restricted to the variables that occurred prior to the event, and that were only included in the rules. The descriptors related to the injury, which were used to make up the antecedent rule, were also not used when the target was Injury_Occurred and were only used in the descriptive/exploratory pattern analysis.

The method begins with a complete set of data preparation that is converted to data cleaning, continuous attributes discretization, and encoding categorical attributes used by existing association rule mining models like Apriori, FP-Growth, and Eclat as flow in the framework of Fig. 1. Systematic searching among combinations of features within categories is then done to develop rules that are directed by finding interpretable patterns related to training conditions, fatigue states, injury occurrence, and recovery progression42. All rules are checked pairwise using the most popular metrics, including support, confidence, lift and conviction to determine the statistical significance, and the efficiency of their turn model is cross-validated with accuracy, precision, recall and F1-score43.

Association rule learning

After the preprocessing, the association rule learning is employed to find out the hidden pattern and relationship in the data. This study uses three association rule mining algorithms such as Apriori, FP-Growth and Eclat- to mine frequent multi-factor performance patterns. Instead of repeating the standard algorithmic derivations, the emphasis of the current research is on comparing the effectiveness of these algorithmic derivations in a sports training scenario45. The maximum length of the rule of five items was used to implement algorithms to be reasonable in terms of the pattern complexity and interpretability. This enables detection of the higher-order relationships between training intensity, psychological preparedness, history of injuries, and recovery condition46.

Experimental setup

On the experiment, a large dataset of sports training that incorporates the demographic features of the athletes, the physiological measurements, the training history and the history of injuries were embraced. Data transparency

The data set is a collection of 5,000 records of athlete-sessions that are repeated measurements of various athletes in a variety of training situations. The number of unique athletes and their identities cannot be determined because of anonymization. Records are representative of a single training or monitoring session, and they have demographics, physiological, psychological, recovery, and injury-related variables. No temporal metadata is given in the dataset, and, hence, it is impossible to know the exact period the observations were made (e.g., one season or multi-season coverage of the study area). Consequently, the analysis considers each athlete-session as a separate observational unit, which is in line with the exploratory data mining research with anonymized monitoring datasets. The data was accessed at a publicly available research repository and does not contain any personally identifiable information. The data is tabularized, with each row corresponding to one athlete-session case, and columns corresponding to the measured attributes and outcomes, including a binary variable of whether there was an injury or not. Although the repetition of measures in the context of athletes may be present, they cannot be maximally explicitly modeled, because the identifiers of the subjects are not provided. There are no subject identifiers or time indicators, which constrain the modelling of within-athlete associations or longitudinal implications.

Implementation details

Various implementation-level optimization and tuning strategies were introduced to ensure that Apriori, FP-Growth and Eclat models are robust and efficient. To test the model, the minimum support and confidence limits were varied within ranges of predetermined support (0.02–0.05) and confidence (0.50–0.70) to check the stability of a rule and eliminate spurious relationships. The upper limit of rule length was five items put in place to create a trade-off between the richness of patterns and interpretability in sports training. To prevent overfitting and to obtain interpretable rules, the following strategies were used: pruning of rare items, and redundancy elimination. Apriori was reduced to optimization of candidate pruning to minimize computational cost and FP-Growth to the FP-tree compression and conditional pattern filtering to increase the memory efficiency. The analysis of Eclat was carried out using the vertical TID-list intersection to speed up the support computation, and under same split strategy holdout with 80 − 20% split as reported in Table 3. Along with that, the cross-split validation (5-Folds) was also performed to validate consistency of the rules and stability across the different splits of data. All these innovations resulted in effective calculations, model stability and applicability of extraction sports performance patterns.

Performance measures

To prove the usability of the learned rules in the sport training domain, estimate evaluation metrics using confusion-matrix-based formulation, as shown in Fig. 2.

Analysis of performance measures.

Accuracy:

The accuracy is the ratio of correctly predicted training outcomes (e.g., injured/not injured, stage of recovery, level of performance) to all cases investigated. For sports training this indicates how much trust one can have in association rules when modelling test day situations49.

Precision:

Precision measures the ratio of the actual occurrence of the predicted class (e.g. high risk of injury) to the hypothesized occurrence, in cases where the rule has fired. In the field of sports, there is a need for high precision to prevent unwarranted interventions, nor over cautious adaptations of the training schedule50.

Recall:

Recall (or sensitivity) evaluates how well the rule set determines all real instances of interest. For analysis, the high recall makes it so that most risky situations are alerted, aiding proactive intervention51.

F1-Score:

The F 1 -score is a harmonic mean of precision and recall; it is used to indicate a balance between the precision and recall, especially in settings where the priorities in false-positive and false-negative predictions are both important as would be the case in athlete monitoring and management52.

These metrics are constructed based on a rule prediction mechanism: for each (athlete-session) instance in the data, if the antecedent conditions of a rule are tested as true, the rule predicts the consequence. These predictions are contrasted with the real result yielding true positive (TP), false positive (FP), true negative (TN) and false negative (FN), that allow the calculation of all the previous measures.

A multi-level pruning framework was introduced as the rule explosion phenomenon was seen at the lower levels of support, which were implemented as a structured framework, reported in Table 5. The first step involved a search for support confidence grid with varying minimum support between 0.02 and 0.05 and confidence between 0.50 and 0.70 to identify stable operating regions. Such sensitivity analysis was used to avoid excessive generation of low relevance patterns. The lift-based filtering was then implemented and the rules with a lift that was less than one or equal to one were discarded to take away non-informative or free association. Eliminations of redundancy were also introduced to further simplify the rule set, by deleting rules subsumed by others, or where there exist more combinations of antecedents, which can never be used to explain a new thing. This was filtered further using a domain-based filtering mechanism that only left the rule that covered critical outcome variables such as injury occurrence, recovery stage or performance measure to simplify the results to be easier to interpret. Lastly, the robustness of the rules was tested by bootstrap resampling and only retained the rules that appeared in 70% of the resampling runs. This type of filtering of the compound decreased the total extracted rules by approximately 62% without decreasing statistically significant and tactical relevant performance patterns. The developed pruning methodology was such that the resulting set of rules was computationally feasible. Moreover, to enhance the practical interpretability of extracted rules the rule usefulness was measured based on subgroup-level proportions and absolute occurrence rates and not by the intuitive narratives only.

The general comparison of the algorithm performance, as reflected in Table 9 highlights the strength and finer variations of Apriori, FP-Growth and Eclat in generating useful and dominant association rules out of monitoring training sports dataset. Although association rule mining is basically an unsupervised method of finding patterns, the rules obtained were experimented in an organized rule based model that assisted in establishing the credibility of the rules in functioning. Based on the rule of the form X→Y, in all cases when the antecedent condition X was established as in an example of an athlete-session, the rule resulted in a prediction of outcome Y. This allowed us to create a confusion matrix by comparing observed with predicted rules generated. The rules were generated only on a training subset and tested on unknown test data, to make sure that there is no leakage of information. Performance was measured based on consistency, class balance management and trade-offs between false positive and false negative by computing accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. These measures were implemented to reformulate the technique as a supervised classifier, but to quantify the consistency of the rule-based decision support under held-out circumstances. To further ensure our approach was rigorous, we employed 5-fold cross-validation, sensitivity analysis with support thresholds, statistical validation with significance testing and effect size estimation, rule pruning strategies to test the presence of explosion, rule ranking (confidence → lift → support) and algorithmic consistency study with Jaccard similarity. Integrating statistical testing, evaluating the effect size, and assessing stability, only meaningful and replicable associations are kept and hence decrease the chances of spurious results that are mostly linked to large-scale rule mining. Altogether, these results underline that the high-impact associations are defined by a strong combination of the lift, the statistical significance, and the reproducibility of the results during validation procedures. Notably, the observed patterns are to be viewed as the relationships of conditional co-occurrence and not causality, due to the cross-sectional and non-temporal characteristics of the dataset. The correlation between gender and injury occurrence was identified in various extracted rules but statistical support proved that the gender on its own was not an independent and high impact factor. Instead, the patterns related to gender were important only when they were used along with such contextual factors as high training intensity and previous injury history. This is an indication that gender can serve as a moderating variable in the multi-factor performance dynamics as opposed to being a causal factor that determines the risk of injury. Notably, in a recovery stage and training load condition, in which the control was maintained using rule filtering; isolated effects of gender had been reduced in various instances, and this means that past tendencies could have been due to interaction effects and not necessarily independent physiological variations.

The statistical validation framework assists in the possibility of the association rules extraction. Because of the incidental relationship of the two variables in large data sets, the inferential testing and the effect size analysis in the method is effective for removing the spurious relationship. It allows for much more emphasis on practically significant patterns than can be done using frequency dependent measures, so that the rules that are retained are structurally significant relationships. Also, the process of determining the moderating multi-factor interactions that determine the conditional dependencies not merely as simple pair associates is also facilitated by the validation procedure. The framework combines statistical rigor with the rule-based mining and helps to mitigate the dependency on other descriptive statistics such as support and confidence, enhancing the scientific credibility and applicability of the results to the sports training setting. The results support the concept that the association rule mining and the inferential validation might give statistically valid informative information in the field of sports performance optimization and injury risk management.

The findings also indicate that the associations of injuries and performance are conditional and occur due to multiple factors interrelating with each other and not via individual variables. These results, within the constraints of session-level cross-sectional data, establish the usefulness of statistically based rule assessment of interpretable sports training analytics but should be interpreted intervention-based.

Discussion and research questions findings

The extracted patterns represent conditional co-occurrence relationships observed in the data. These findings should not be interpreted as causal relationships. Although the rules were statistically examined using significance testing and effect size filtering, the cross-sectional study design does not allow conclusions about temporal direction or underlying mechanisms. The observed associations may also be influenced by factors such as the type of sport, training stage, and individual physiological differences. Therefore, the identified patterns are presented as descriptive observations that highlight possible interactions among variables rather than fixed or causal relationships. Because many candidate rules were generated and lower support thresholds were used, the possibility of spurious associations due to multiple comparisons was carefully considered. To reduce this issue, the analysis included rule pruning, lift-based filtering, sensitivity analysis of support thresholds, and cross-validation across different data partitions. Only statistically consistent and stable rules were considered during interpretation. However, these findings should not be viewed as clinically validated indicators or direct recommendations. Instead, they provide an interpretable basis for further exploration and future examination using longitudinal or externally validated sports monitoring datasets. To support this aim, the study formulated four research questions focusing on multi-factor association patterns, algorithmic consistency, robustness, and practical interpretability within a sports training context. Overall, the identified patterns represent conditional statistical associations, and, due to the non-temporal cross-sectional nature of the dataset, they should not be interpreted as causal effects, validated risk factors, or intervention outcomes. The findings related to each research question are summarized below.

RQ1: What multi-factor association patterns emerge between training load, psychological monitoring variables, and injury occurrence in athlete training sessions?

The multi-factor rules involving the occurrence of injuries with high training intensity were statistically proved to be present when interacting with the previous injury history and low recovery stage. Psychological variables, especially high levels of stress and low motivation, reinforced injury-related associations to work with variables of workload. Regulations that had been statistically validated had lift values that were above one and moderate effect sizes, depicting significant conditional dependence. These results imply that the presence of injury is a product of multi-dimensional relations and no single training factors.

RQ2: How consistent are the extracted association rules across different mining algorithms (Apriori, FP-Growth, and Eclat)?

There was high structural agreement between Apriori, FP-Growth and Eclat as shown by algorithmic comparison. The Jaccard similarity scores are higher than 0.80 when they are compared in pairs and imply that the rules are very overlapping. Although there were minor differences in the number of rules and stability indicators, the basic multi-factor tendencies were similar. This confirms that the specified associations are a component and part of the data structure but are not always the result of a specific mining algorithm.

RQ3: How stable are the identified rule sets under varying support thresholds and cross-validation partitions?

Sensitivity analysis of all support levels showed the systematic and monotonic reduction of the number of rules with the increase in the minimum of support and preservation of the high-lift patterns of core. The five-fold cross-validation was used to determine the consistency of the rules derived to different subsets of data and the retention ratios of rules were slightly different to establish consistency. The findings indicate that the findings are robust to parameter tuning and sampling variance that reduces the likelihood of overfitting and spurious correlations.

RQ4: To what extent can statistically validated association rules provide interpretable insights for athlete monitoring interpretation frameworks?

The interpretable conditional profiles, such as the presence of high intensity, past injury and lower recovery stage were obtained using the verified and reduced rule sets. These rule structures provide explicit signaling of decision support that can be utilized to guide the monitoring strategies without the need to invoke black-box models. Statistical validation and strength checks will foster confidence in the usefulness of the identified patterns in the systems of athlete performance management.

Moreover, the extraction of the association rules obtained must be interpreted taking into consideration several methodological limitations. First, relationships can be affected by the potential confounding factors because the characteristics of athletes, training load, psychological state, and recovery conditions are usually intertwined. Due to the observational nature of the analysis and lack of simultaneous control over all covariates, not all of the associations may be due to independent effects, and some may be due to indirect effects. Second, large-scale association rule mining is prone to spurious patterns especially when many candidate rules are considered with comparatively low levels of support. To address this problem, the study used multi-stage pruning, statistical validation and stability analysis but the residual false-positive associations cannot be completely ruled out. Lastly, the data set consists of cross-sectional, non-temporal observations, which do not allow making statements about the directionality or causal processes. In turn, the specified rules are to be viewed as conditional co-occurrence patterns, as opposed to the approved risk factors or cause-effect relationships. These results are also in agreement with the hypothesis that may be used to undertake future longitudinal or experimental studies.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates the value of association rule mining for identifying interpretable and statistically validated patterns in sports training data. By applying and comparing Apriori, FP-Growth, and Eclat algorithms, the analysis identifies consistent multi-factor associations among training load, recovery stage, psychological readiness, prior injury history, and observed performance and injury-related outcomes. The integration of statistical validation, rule pruning, sensitivity testing, and cross-validation strengthens the robustness and reliability of the extracted rules. The findings indicate that athlete performance and injury outcomes are associated with conditional interactions among multiple factors rather than isolated variables. The study contributes a structured framework for extracting stable and interpretable association rules within a sports analytics context. The comparative evaluation of the three algorithms confirms that core patterns remain consistent across mining strategies, supporting the stability of the identified relationships. Several limitations should be acknowledged. The dataset represents a specific athlete cohort and may not fully reflect diverse sports populations. The absence of athlete identifiers and temporal information limits the ability to model within-subject correlations or longitudinal dependencies. Therefore, potential intra-athlete variability may not be fully captured, which could influence the generalizability of the findings. The observational and cross-sectional structure of the data limits causal interpretation and temporal analysis of outcome progression. Although internal validation procedures were applied, external dataset validation was not conducted. Future research should incorporate longitudinal data to enable temporal and sequential rule mining. Expanding the dataset across multiple sports and competitive levels would improve representativeness and generalizability. Hybrid approaches that integrate interpretable rule mining with modeling frameworks may provide a useful direction for future methodological investigation. Overall, this study provides a statistically grounded and interpretable approach to sports performance analytics, offering insights that may support data-informed validation and further investigation, rather than prescriptive or causal conclusions.

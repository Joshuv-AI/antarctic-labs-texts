Genome editing or small molecule inhibition of KMT5A in CAR-T cells enhances antitumor immunity
Authors: Xiaoling Tian, Guolong Liu, Qiudao Wang, Na Zhang, Yanting Shen, Bing Du, Yuxuan Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAdoptive T-cell therapy has emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy for cancer treatment. However, clinical challenges persist, including the limited ability of CD8+T cells to infiltrate solid tumors and efficiently eliminate tumor cells. Given the critical role of epigenetic mechanisms in antitumor immunity, targeting epigenetic regulators represents a critical step toward optimizing adoptive T-cell therapies for solid tumors.MethodsTo investigate the role of KMT5A in CD8+T cell function, we employed CRISPR screening to identify KMT5A as a negative regulator. We then genetically deleted KMT5A in human CD8+T cells and systematically evaluated its impact on the antitumor efficacy of chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-T cells using xenograft models. Furthermore, we used the small-molecule inhibitor UNC0379 to pharmacologically inhibit KMT5A, meticulously assessing the consequent effects on CAR-T cell activation, cytotoxicity, and antitumor activity.ResultsWe report that lysine methyltransferase KMT5A acts as a negative regulator of CD8+T cell function, identified via CRISPR screening. KMT5A deletion in human CD8+T cells significantly enhances the antitumor efficacy of CAR CD8+T cells in xenograft models. In vitro immunophenotyping reveals that KMT5A deletion improves effector functions, cytokine secretion, and early activation of CD8+T cells. Mechanistically, KMT5A depletion increases the expression and chromatin accessibility of multiple effector-related genes in CD8+T cells. KMT5A-mediated histone H4 modifications and chromatin remodeling suppress CD8+T cell effector functions, partially via inhibition of the transcription factor SP1. Notably, pharmacological inhibition of KMT5A using the small-molecule inhibitor UNC0379 enhanced activation, cytotoxicity, and antitumor activity in human CD8+T cells—a novel finding in this study.ConclusionsOur findings establish KMT5A as an epigenetic regulator that impairs CD8+T cell function. These findings demonstrate that genetic or pharmacological (eg, UNC0379) targeting of KMT5A in CD8+T cells represents a viable therapeutic strategy to augment effector functions and improve adoptive T-cell therapies, particularly CAR-T cells, for solid tumors.Keywords: Adoptive cell therapy - ACT

Introduction

Immunotherapy has become a cornerstone therapeutic strategy for cancer treatment.1 Adoptive cell therapy (ACT), a form of immunotherapy, augments antitumor immunity through the adoptive transfer of ex vivo-modified immune cells (eg, T cells or natural killer cells).2 Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy—an ACT modality wherein engineered synthetic receptors (CARs) redirect lymphocytes to target and eliminate cells expressing specific antigens3—has shown durable clinical efficacy in hematologic malignancies, including refractory lymphoma and leukemia.4 5 However, CAR-T therapy remains limited in solid tumors, including tumors refractory to CAR-T therapy, those with early relapse post-treatment, or cases exhibiting limited initial efficacy and rapid progression. This is primarily due to a tumor microenvironment in which CD8+T cells are chronically exposed to antigens and immunosuppressive signals from stromal and myeloid cells, driving T-cell exhaustion and impairing effector function.6 7 Emerging evidence indicates that epigenetic regulators modulate T-cell differentiation8 9 and tumor infiltration, while additionally controlling the expression of cytotoxic molecules such as surface receptors and granzymes, which critically influence tumor cell clearance. Targeting these epigenetic mechanisms to enhance T-cell effector function represents a promising strategy to improve the efficacy of T cell-based therapies for solid tumors.

CRISPR gene editing technology has emerged as a strategy to enhance T-cell function or reverse CD8+T cell dysfunction, thereby improving immunotherapy efficacy in patients with cancer.10 11 Large-scale CRISPR screening has been employed to identify functional gene targets in primary T cells.12,14 This technology enables the targeted identification of functional genes and regulatory elements at the whole-genome level.15 However, although whole-gene screening facilitates unbiased discovery, it necessitates a large number of cells and is challenging to execute in certain specific screening environments (eg, in vivo animal models). In contrast, focused sublibrary screening provides a streamlined strategy, allowing targeted interrogation of gene subsets aligned with specific research objectives. This method is adaptable to in vivo studies through sublibrary size optimization, enhancing the physiological relevance of screening outcomes.16

In this study, we employed an epigenetically-focused single-guide RNA (sgRNA) library to identify epigenetic regulators for cancer immunotherapy using a lentivirus-mediated approach with screens of tumor infiltration and degranulation. The screen prioritized 10 genes, including KMT5A—a previously understudied epigenetic regulator in CD8+T cell biology. Knockout of KMT5A markedly enhanced effector functions and tumor-killing efficacy in CAR-T cells. Mechanistically, KMT5A ablation attenuated its suppression of cytotoxic functions via H4K20me1-mediated chromatin remodeling, thereby impairing tumor cell resistance to T-cell-mediated killing. Notably, pharmacological inhibition of KMT5A with the small-molecule compound UNC0379 recapitulated these effects, augmenting effector activity and antitumor potency in CD8+CAR T cells. In conclusion, our findings establish KMT5A targeting as a translatable strategy to enhance epigenetic-driven adoptive T-cell therapies, providing a foundation for clinical development in solid tumors.

Results

Tumor infiltration and degranulation screen identifies KMT5A as an epigenetic regulator of CD8+T cells

To systematically investigate the epigenetic regulators that govern the antitumor activity of T cells, we performed a high-throughput CRISPR knockout screen using the PC3 xenograft model. We designed and synthesized a human sgRNA library (HKO) that included 1101 epigenetic regulators related to transcription and translation and 100 non-targeting controls (NTCs) (online supplemental table 1) (4,504 sgRNAs listed). The HKO library was cloned into a lentiviral transfer plasmid containing an sgRNA expression cassette (figure 1a) and packaged into lentiviral particles. To establish a screening platform, we infected activated human primary CD8+T cells isolated from peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMC) of healthy donors with HKO lentiviral libraries and introduced Cas9 protein by electroporation (figure 1a). 3 days after electroporation, cells were analyzed by flow cytometry and a significant upregulation of T-cell activation markers (CD154, CD69) was observed (Extended Data online supplemental figure 1a,b), demonstrating the successful activation of CD8+T cells.

The limited infiltration of T cells into the solid tumor microenvironment affects the efficacy of therapies employing T cells. Therefore, we performed an in vivo tumor infiltration screen associated with epigenetic regulatory factors. Adoptive transfer of edited human CD8+T cells into NSG heterologous tumor model mice bearing PC3 tumors (figure 1a). 3 weeks after T-cell transfer, adoptively transferred CD8+T cells were quantified in tumors, spleens, and lymph nodes via flow cytometry and confirmed the presence of adoptively transferred CD8+T cells in these tissues. (Extended Data online supplemental figure 1c). Subsequently, genomic DNA (gDNA) was extracted and subjected to high-throughput sequencing, revealing sgRNAs significantly enriched in tumors (false discovery rate <0.5%). The statistically significant correlation between two biological replicates (Spearman ρ=0.24, p<0.0001) indicates a consistent trend in sgRNA enrichment patterns and supports the reliability of the in vivo CRISPR screening results (Extended Data online supplemental figure 1d). sgRNA-targeted genes were ranked based on multiple criteria, including significant enrichment in 50% of the samples (p≤0.05), targeting by multiple independent sgRNAs (≥2 significant sgRNAs), and ranking by Robust Rank Aggregation (RRA) score (rank≥50), and generated a list of the top scoring genes, which included EPC1, CBX1, SIRT7, BRD9, CBX7, METTL13, ING4, and KMT5A (figure 1b, Extended Data online supplemental figure 1e, online supplemental table 2). MAGeCK algorithm analysis17 further prioritized CBX1, SIRT7, ASH21, KMT5A, and METTL13 as top candidates (figure 1c, Extended Data online supplemental figure 1f). sgRNAs targeting KMT5A, BRD9 and CXXC1 were significantly enriched, whereas those targeting JUN were depleted (Extended Data online supplemental figure 1g). The concordance among multiple sgRNAs targeting these genes indicates that the phenotype results from on-target effects, rather than off-target mechanisms (Extended Data online supplemental figure 1h). Collectively, this screen identified key epigenetic regulators of in vivo CD8+T cell tumor infiltration.

To enhance the rigor of our screening approach, we conducted an in vitro degranulation screening assay. Human CD8+T cells were transduced with the HKO sgRNA library lentivirus after 24 hours and electroporated with the Cas9 protein. After 3 days of activation and 12 hours of serum-free culture, HKO-infected CD8+T cells and PC3 cells were co-cultured at a 1:1 effector-to-target (E:T) ratio in the presence of anti-CD107a antibodies (figure 1d). CD107a (lysosomal-associated membrane protein-1) is a lysosomal membrane glycoprotein that is transiently expressed in cell membranes during the degranulation process of T cells and serves as a functional marker for cytotoxic CD8+T lymphocyte activity.18 We sorted the top 5% of CD107a+cells (figure 1e), and subsequently performed high-throughput sequencing analysis on them. Enriched sgRNAs (≥1 sgRNA per sample; online supplemental table 3) identified potential negative regulators of T-cell degranulation. Cross-referencing hits from both screens revealed 10 candidate genes, including KMT5A, BRD9, and METTL13, as potential targets for adoptive T-cell therapy (figure 1f,g). In summary, convergent T-cell CRISPR screening identified 10 epigenetic regulatory factors as potential adoptive T-cell therapy targets for solid tumors.

KMT5A ablation enhances tumor infiltration and improves tumor control of CD8 CAR-T cells

Among the 10 overlapping candidates from both screens, we focused on KMT5A, the sole methyltransferase catalyzing histone H4 lysine 20 monomethylation (H4K20me1), which is critical for genome stability and DNA damage repair.19,21 KMT5A is also involved in several tumors, including breast tumors22 and clear cell renal cell carcinoma23 and is a therapeutic target for solid tumor treatment,24 but its role in immune regulation remains poorly characterized. To examine the role of KMT5A in antitumor immune responses in T cells, we analyzed KMT5A expression on normal human T cells via the human protein atlas online database and found activated CD8+T cells had increased levels of KMT5A (Extended Data online supplemental figure 2a). We further explored the correlation of KMT5A with tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) in prostate cancer using the TISIDB database. We observed a significant negative correlation (p<0.001) between KMT5A expression and TILs in a prostate cancer cohort (498 samples) (http://cis.hku.hk/TISIDB/index.php) (Extended Data online supplemental figure 2b). These data indicate that KMT5A inhibits CD8+T cell activation and tumor infiltration in patients with prostate adenocarcinoma.

To investigate the function of this gene in immunotherapy model, we designed sgRNAs targeting KMT5A that scored in both screens, as well as PD1 (as a positive reference)13 and an NTC sgRNA (sgCTRL) as a negative reference (online supplemental table 4). Previous studies have shown that 4-1BB-based CAR-T cells exhibit enhanced T-cell persistence and reduced exhaustion.25 These cells also demonstrate robust therapeutic safety and efficacy.26 27 Therefore, we generated second-generation 4-1BB-based PSMA-targeting PSMA. In brief, PSMA-CAR-T cells with the 4-1BB co-stimulatory domain were generated by transducing CD8+T cells with PSMA-CAR lentivirus, followed by electroporation with Cas9 protein and sgRNAs (sgKMT5A, sgPD1, or sgCTRL). 4 days after electroporation, we confirmed efficient target editing of KMT5A and PD1 in PSMA-CAR-T cells (Extended Data online supplemental figure 2c,d) and detected changes in their messenger RNA (mRNA) and protein levels (figure 2a, Extended Data online supplemental figure 2e–g). To assess PSMA-specific antitumor responses, we conducted both an in vitro co-culture experiment and in vivo specific antitumor experiments (Extended Data online supplemental figure 3a). We observed a significant increase in degranulation of PSMA-PC3 cells by PSMA-CAR-T cells compared with PC3 cells in the in vitro co-culture assay (Extended Data online supplemental figure 3b). Meanwhile, in in vivo experiments, tumor-bearing mice were treated with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) or PSMA-specific CAR-CD8+T cells (Extended Data online supplemental figure 3a). Tumor regression was more pronounced in mice treated with PSMA-CAR-T cells compared with the group treated with PBS. Furthermore, PSMA-CAR-T cells induced significantly greater regression in PSMA-PC3-derived tumors than in PC3-derived tumors (Extended Data online supplemental figure 3c).We confirmed that endogenous PSMA protein expression in PC3 cells is extremely low, despite their PSMA mRNA levels being approximately threefold higher than those in 293T cells, consistent with previous reports.4 Despite the low expression level, the antigen remains detectable by high-affinity PSMA-CAR T cells. Notably, PC3 cells with PSMA overexpression exhibit significantly enhanced sensitivity to PSMA-CAR targeting (Extended Data online supplemental figure 3d). Overall, these data support a degree of antigen specificity in the PSMA-CAR T-cell system. The antitumor activity of PSMA-CAR T cells is largely mediated through PSMA recognition, indicating target specificity, though low expression in PC3 cells limits conclusions on absolute specificity.

Subsequently, the edited cells were adoptively transferred into NSG mice bearing PSMA-PC3 tumors 24 days after tumor inoculation. At 50 days post-transfer, KMT5A-ablated or PD1-ablated PSMA-CAR-T cells exhibited significantly enhanced antitumor activity compared with controls, reducing tumor volume by 55% and 50%, respectively (figure 2b,c). Flow cytometric analysis revealed a significant increase of TIL population in tumors treated with KMT5A knockout CAR-T cells, while there was no significant change in the spleen (figure 2d, Extended Data online supplemental figure 4a). Additionally, quantitative PCR (qPCR) analysis demonstrated upregulated expression of effector cytokines GZMB and IFN-γ in KMT5A knockout TILs (Extended Data online supplemental figure 4b).

The use of an independent sgRNA targeting KMT5A (sgKMT5A-2) resulted in comparable reductions in tumor growth (Extended Data online supplemental figure 4c) and tumor infiltration (Extended Data online supplemental figure 4d,e), confirming that the observed functional gains are on-target and specifically mediated by KMT5A deletion. These findings suggest that CRISPR-mediated targeting of KMT5A enhances the antitumor efficacy of PSMA-CAR-T cells against PSMA-PC3 tumors.

KMT5A ablation alters the immunological profile of human CD8 T cells

To further validate the impact of KMT5A knockout on the immunological characteristics of CD8+T cells, we observed that KMT5A-deficient T cells exhibited significantly higher levels of the activation marker CD69 (figure 3a), alongside elevated expression of intrinsic activation markers Lymphocyte Activation Gene 3 (LAG3) and programmed cell death protein-1 (PD-1) (figure 3b,c).28,30 Antigen-induced T-cell differentiation into subpopulations—naive T cells, effector T cells, stem cell memory T cells (Tscm), central memory cells (Tcm), effector memory T cells (Tem), and terminally differentiated effector T cells (Teff).9 Among these, Tscm cells demonstrate enhanced longevity and antitumor activity.31 32 CCR7 and CD45RO have been used to delineate the T-cell subpopulations: Tscm (CD45RO-CCR7+), Tcm (CD45RO+CCR7+), Tem (CD45RO+CCR7−) and Teff (CD45RO−CCR7−). KMT5A knockout in PSMA-CAR-T cells significantly increased the frequency of Tcm and Teff subsets (Extended Data online supplemental figure 5a). Additionally, KLRG1 and CD127 also serve as markers of T-cell differentiation.33 CD127+KLRG1− are memory precursor effector cells (pre-Tem), while KLRG1+CD127− are Teff. Our results indicate that KMT5A-depleted CAR-T cells display a notable increase in TEFF populations (Extended Data online supplemental figure 5b). Collectively, these data imply a role for KMT5A in T-cell activation and differentiation processes.

To validate the antigen-specific cytotoxicity of KMT5A knockout CD8+T cells, we performed in vitro co-culture assay and found that KMT5A knockout CD8 T cells exhibited greater potency in killing PSMA-PC3 tumor cells compared with wild-type (sgCTRL-edited) T cells (Extended Data online supplemental figure 5c). This enhanced cytotoxicity was not observed in PC3 cells lacking PSMA expression, confirming the antigen-specific nature of the system. As an independent validation, we conducted additional co-culture experiments using PMSA-PC3 cells stably expressing bright red fluorescent protein (Crimson+) as target cells (figure 3d), which yielded consistent results. We found that KMT5A knockout PSMA-CAR-T cells enhanced surface CD107a expression (figure 3e), suggesting that KMT5A ablation potentiates antigen-specific in vitro cytotoxicity. Subsequently, it was observed through imaging analysis that KMT5A knockout CAR-T cells markedly decreased the quantity of PSMA-PC3 cells in comparison with CTRL cells (Extended Data online supplemental figure 5d,e). This observation was further validated by quantification, demonstrating a reduction in tumor cell numbers across both groups, although with a notably greater decrease within the KMT5A knockout group (Extended Data online supplemental figure 5f). Additionally, we measured the ability of KMT5A knockout T-cell’s ability to produce interferon (IFN)-γ and tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α (cytokines mediating CD8+T cell cytotoxicity),34 35 and found KMT5A knockout PSMA-CAR-T cells produced higher levels of IFN-γ+ (figure 3f) and TNF-α+ (figure 3g). ELISA analyses further corroborated this observation (figure 3h–i). Notably, these results were recapitulated using a second sgRNA (sgKMT5A-2), which also showed elevated CD107a expression (Extended Data online supplemental figure 5g) as well as increased expression of IFN-γ, TNF-α, and Granzyme B (GZMB) (Extended Data online supplemental figure 5h–j). Together, these data confirm that the observed enhancement in T-cell cytotoxicity is on-target and specifically mediated by KMT5A deletion. To confirm the role of KMT5A in different CAR-T cell contexts, we constructed second-generation 4-1BB-based MLSN-targeting CAR-T cells. Consistently, we observed that KMT5A knockout MLSN-CAR-T cells enhanced the in vitro cytotoxic activity of these cells against SK-OV-3 ovarian cancer cells (Extended Data online supplemental figure 6a–e). Together, KMT5A ablation enhanced the degranulation of T cells, promoted cytokine production, and enhanced the antitumor effects of CAR-T cells.

KMT5A knockout CD8+T cells increased effector-related gene expression

To investigate the downstream regulatory mechanism of KMT5A in modulating CD8+T cell effector functions, we performed mRNA sequencing (mRNA-seq) on KMT5A knockout and CTRL CD8+T cells. Differential expression analysis (figure 4a) revealed substantial transcriptional alterations, including the significant upregulation of genes associated with cell growth and differentiation (eg, CD69, KLRG1, IL-2RA, Osm, SOCS3), immune infiltration (eg, CCL3, CCL4), and effector functions (eg, EOMES, FOS, BATF3, GZMB, GZMH, GZMK, IFN-γ, TNF-α) (figure 4a,b, Extended Data online supplemental figure 7, online supplemental table 5). While the expression of immunosuppressive gene FOXP3, the T-cell stemness genes LFF1 and TCF7, and the T-cell quiescence gene FOXO1 was notably downregulated (figure 4a,b, Extended Data online supplemental figure 7). We validated five genes, GZMB, GZMH, CD86, CCL3 and FOXP3, by qPCR analysis and confirmed that these genes were differentially expressed (figure 4c). Gene Ontology (GO) analysis of upregulated genes in KMT5A knockout cells revealed enrichment in immune-related processes, including antigen processing/presentation via major histocompatibility complex class II, T-cell activation, and immune response regulation (figure 4d). Conversely, downregulated genes were associated with adaptive immunity, transmembrane receptor tyrosine kinase signaling, and bacterial response pathways (figure 4e, online supplemental table 6). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that KMT5A ablation enhances CD8+T cell effector function by transcriptionally activating cytotoxicity, cytokine signaling, and immune activation pathways while suppressing genes linked to quiescence and immune tolerance.

KMT5A-mediated H4K20me1 modification and chromatin remodeling inhibit T-cell effector function in human CD8+T cells

H4K20me1 is a well-characterized substrate of KMT5A.21 To explore KMT5A’s epigenetic regulatory role in CD8+T cell effector function, we hypothesized that KMT5A ablation enhances effector activity by disrupting H4K20 monomethylation (H4K20me1) at effector gene loci and remodeling chromatin architecture. To test this hypothesis, we employed cleavage under targets and tagmentation (CUT&Tag) to profile H4K20me1 modifications and chromatin accessibility in KMT5A-depleted CD8+T cells. CUT&Tag analysis revealed 3,000+differentially accessible open chromatin regions (OCRs) linked to H4K20me1. Of these, 1,000+OCRs exhibited elevated H4K20me1 occupancy, while 2,000+OCRs displayed reduced occupancy in KMT5A knockout CD8+T cells (figure 5a). Integrated analysis of genes bound to H4K20me1 and genes whose expression was upregulated in the KMT5A knockout transcriptome revealed 71 overlapping genes, including effector-associated genes ITGB1, FGR, CD74 and TERT (figure 5b, online supplemental table 7). We validated the increased expression of ITGB1 and FGR in KMT5A knockout CD8+T cells using CUT&Tag-qPCR (Extended Data online supplemental figure 8a). GO analysis of the biological processes enriched in these 71 genes with the Database for Annotation, Visualization and Integrated Discovery (figure 5c, online supplemental table 8) revealed that the reduction in H4K20me1 occupancy in these genes and was associated with positive regulatory pathways for wound healing, protein kinase B signaling (AKT), calcium ion transport and cell migration (online supplemental table 9). Additionally, the reduced H4K20me1 regions in ITGB1 and FGR were accompanied by increased chromatin accessibility (figure 5d), indicating that ITGB1 and FGR are targets of KMT5A-mediated H4K20me1. To assess how KMT5A knockdown alters the epigenetic landscape of T cells, we performed transposase-accessible chromatin sequencing (ATAC-seq), and ATAC-seq analysis of the effector genes IFN-γ and GZMB showed that KMT5A knockdown cells had elevated chromatin accessibility as well as reduced H4K20me1 occupancy on the IFN-γ or GZMB genes (figure 5e). Motif analysis revealed enrichment of the transcription factors SP1 (specificity protein 1) and IRF1 (interferon regulatory factor) in chromatin regions with increased accessibility in KMT5A knockout CD8+T cells (Extended Data online supplemental figure 8b). qPCR confirmed significant upregulation of SP1 and IRF1 in KMT5A knockout cells (Extended Data online supplemental figure 8c), demonstrating that KMT5A ablation attenuates its repression of SP1 and IRF1, thereby enhancing chromatin accessibility and transcriptional activation. Knockdown of SP1 led to a significant reduction in the expression of effector molecules, including IFN-γ and GZMB, in KMT5A-knockout CAR-T cells (Extended Data online supplemental figure 8d,e), demonstrating that SP1 is required for the augmented effector response triggered by KMT5A ablation. Since both sgRNAs showed similar editing efficiency and functional activity, we randomly selected sgKMT5A for off-target analysis. We predicted potential off-target sites in the human genome with up to three mismatches and no PAM constraints, identifying a total of 28 candidate loci. Deep sequencing revealed no detectable off-target editing in KMT5A knockout CD8+T cells (Extended Data online supplemental figure 8f), indicating that sgKMT5A exhibits high on-target specificity and minimal off-target effects. Taken together, KMT5A ablation reduces H4K20me1 modification at SP1 or IRF1, increases chromatin accessibility, and amplifies T-cell cytotoxicity.

Pharmacological inhibition of KMT5A in CD8 CAR-T cells attenuates tumor growth

It has been shown that future research should prioritize developing compounds targeting these regulators via small-molecule library screening or structure-based drug design.13 33 Notably, Veschi et al36 reported that UNC0379—a small-molecule KMT5A inhibitor—improved survival outcomes in a preclinical neuroblastoma xenograft model. However, the functional and transcriptional consequences of KMT5A inhibition by UNC0379 in CD8+T cells remain unexplored. To evaluate the pharmacological impact of UNC0379, we initially assessed its IC50 in CD8+T cells (Extended Data online supplemental figure 9a). To mitigate potential cytotoxicity, CD8+T cells were treated with a sub-IC50 dose of UNC0379 (5 µM) for 72 hours (Extended Data online supplemental figure 9b). Western blotting demonstrated that this dose reduced both KMT5A protein levels and associated H4K20me1 histone modification, without affecting other histone methylation marks such as H3K9me3 and H3K27me3 (Extended Data online supplemental figure 9c). These findings are consistent with previous reports,37 and support the conclusion that UNC0379 selectively inhibits KMT5A, in line with its reported function as a selective, substrate-competitive inhibitor of the enzyme.38 Flow cytometry analysis revealed that UNC0379-treated cells exhibited marked upregulation of activation markers (CD69, figure 6a), effector molecules (KLRG1, figure 6b), degranulation markers (CD107a, figure 6c), and cytotoxic mediators (GZMB, figure 6d), confirming enhanced effector function. Furthermore, UNC0379 significantly elevated phosphorylation of pro-survival signaling components AKT and mTOR (figure 6e), while reducing expression of pro-apoptotic proteins (BAX, PARP) and increasing anti-apoptotic BCL-2 levels (Extended Data online supplemental figure 9d). Flow cytometry further corroborated reduced apoptosis in treated cells, as indicated by diminished Annexin V+7-Aminoactinomycin D (7-AAD)+populations (Extended Data online supplemental figure 9e). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that UNC0379 effectively inhibits KMT5A in CD8+T cells, augmenting their activation, cytotoxicity, and survival via the AKT/mTOR pathway.

To assess the impact of KMT5A inhibition by UNC0379 on tumor growth in vivo, NSG mice bearing prostate cancer xenografts received intravenous injections of UNC0379-treated or control CAR-T cells. Tumor growth was monitored longitudinally (figure 6f). UNC0379-treated CAR-T cells demonstrated significant tumor growth suppression (figure 6g; Extended Data online supplemental figure 9f,g), enhanced tumor infiltration (figure 6h), and increased intratumoral production of IFN-γ and TNF-α (figure 6i,j), without affecting splenic CAR-T cell populations (Extended Data online supplemental figure 9h). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that pharmacological KMT5A inhibition suppresses tumor progression and augments antitumor immunity, underscoring its therapeutic potential for clinical translation in cancer immunotherapy.

Discussion

Based on the current immunotherapy for solid tumors is not satisfactory. To enhance the function of T cells in ACT, it is necessary to find new epigenetic targets.2 In this study, we performed parallel in vivo tumor infiltration and in vitro degranulation screens using epigenetic regulatory libraries to identify epigenetic regulators that enhance adoptive T-cell therapies targeting solid tumors. Specifically, genetic ablation or pharmacological inhibition of KMT5A in CD8+T cells significantly enhanced effector function and antitumor efficacy in vitro and in vivo (figure 7). KMT5A, a member of the lysine methyltransferase family, has been implicated in the pathogenesis of several solid cancers and has emerged as a potential therapeutic target to improve cancer treatment outcome.22 23 39 Analysis of TISIDB’s data revealed that KMT5A expression was significantly negatively correlated with TIL levels in patients with prostate cancer. Our experimental results also showed that KMT5A ablation promotes T-cell infiltration into tumors and enhances the antitumor efficacy. This suggests that KMT5A inhibits the tumor infiltration and persistent effects of CD8+T cells in this malignancy.

Illustration summary of KMT5A regulation of CAR-T cell antitumor responses. (a) KMT5A mediates H4K20me1 modification and chromatin remodeling in CD8+T cells, functioning as a negative regulator. Both KMT5A deletion and its inhibition with small molecules improve the expression of effector-associated genes and enhance tumor control both in vitro and in vivo. (1) KMT5A deletion in CAR-T cells via genome editing reduces H4K20me1 at effector-associated genes, enhancing T-cell activation, effector differentiation, and T-cell cytotoxicity. Motif analysis reveals that SP1 and IRF1 binding sites are enriched in more accessible regions of CART cells’ genome. (2) Pharmacological inhibition of KMT5A using UNC0379 augments CD8+T cell proliferation, anti-apoptosis and cytotoxicity, partially through the AKT/mTOR pathway. CAR, chimeric antigen receptor; GZMB, Granzyme B; IFN, interferon; IRF1, interferon regulatory factor; SP1, specificity protein 1; TNF, tumor necrosis factor.

Understanding the mechanisms of CD8+T cell differentiation is critical to improving therapies for cancer and other diseases. On antigen stimulation, naïve CD8+T cells are rapidly activated and undergo robust expansion, giving rise to effector T cells and memory T cells. We demonstrated that KMT5A knockout markedly enhanced CAR-T cell activation (CD69, PD-1, and LAG3 upregulation) and degranulation response (CD107a upregulation). Notably, KMT5A knockout also promoted the expansion of Tcm (CD45RO+CCR7+) and Teff (CD45RO−CCR7−) CAR-T populations. These data suggest that KMT5A knockout promotes the activation, cytotoxicity, and differentiation functions of CAR-T cells. This finding aligns with previous studies underscoring the capability of Tcm cells in sustaining CAR-T cell persistence and antitumor efficacy.40 In metastatic melanoma patients, Tcm-like TILs have been correlated with complete remission and T-cell persistence.2 Notably, we observed an increase in both Tcm and Teff proportions following KMT5A depletion. This suggests that KMT5A loss promotes early T-cell activation, which may concurrently promote Tcm cell generation and drive effector (Teff) differentiation. Consistent with this the elevated Teff proportion correlated with improved tumor-killing efficiency in vitro, indicating that enhanced terminal effector function contributes to the overall functional improvement of CAR-T cells. It is worth noting that traditional surface markers may not always accurately represent the functional or developmental state of CAR-T cells after epigenetic manipulation.41 Therefore, based on both transcriptomic profiling and functional assays, our findings suggest that KMT5A targeting may help maintain a favorable balance between effector function and stem-like memory potential. Hence, taken together, targeting KMT5A represents a promising strategy to enhance both the potency and durability of CAR-T cell therapy. What’s more, intratumoral heterogeneity and phenotypic diversity in the tumor microenvironment necessitate combinatorial immunotherapy strategies. CAR-T cells combined with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) are under clinical evaluation for treating hematologic and solid tumors.42 Cancer vaccines paired with cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) or PD-1/programmed death-ligand 1 inhibitors demonstrate enhanced priming of tumor-specific T cells.43 44 Similarly, TIL therapy combined with PD-1-targeted checkpoint blockade demonstrated promising efficacy in immunotherapy-naïve patients with melanoma, head and neck cancer, and cervical cancer. Therefore, future studies investigating KMT5A ablation alongside established combinatorial immunotherapies could augment anticancer immunity, enhance T-cell infiltration, mitigate exhaustion, and induce a shift from immunologically “cold” (poor immune recognition) to “hot” (lymphocyte-rich) tumors. This approach holds promise for generating more effective CAR-T cells. Of particular interest are those engineered with 4-1BB costimulatory signaling, as they may significantly improve clinical outcomes, especially in the treatment of solid tumors.

Mechanistically, our mRNA-seq data revealed that KMT5A knockout upregulated genes associated with immune cell infiltration, effector function, and differentiation in CD8+T cells, which are mainly enriched in signaling pathways related to immune regulation. Furthermore, CUT&Tag and ATAC-seq analyses demonstrated that KMT5A negatively modulated CD8+T cell effector functions via H4K20me1 modification and chromatin remodeling. GO analysis indicated that KMT5A knockout diminished H4K20me1 enrichment at loci involved in wound healing, AKT (protein kinase B) signaling, calcium ion transport, and cell migration. DNA methylation, histone modification, and chromatin remodeling have been implicated in regulating the differentiation and function of CD8+T cells.9 And H4K20me1 suppresses chromatin accessibility and transcriptional activation of target loci.45 Similarly, our findings demonstrate that KMT5A knockdown reduces H4K20me1 levels, thereby upregulating genes marked by H4K20me1 and underscoring its role in transcriptional repression. Integrative Genomics Viewer analysis of CUT&Tag, mRNA-seq, and ATAC-seq data confirmed reduced H4K20me1 occupancy at the ITGB1 (Integrin Subunit Beta 1), FGR (FGR Proto-Oncogene, Src Family Tyrosine Kinase), IFNG, and GZMB loci, accompanied by increased chromatin accessibility and transcription. These findings suggest that these genes are targets of KMT5A-mediated H4K20me1, thereby demonstrating that KMT5A knockdown enhances T-cell effector function through H4K20me1 modification and chromatin remodeling. This finding aligns with previous studies indicating that hypomethylation of the IFN-γ locus in activated CD8+T cells correlates with enhanced transcription.35 46 Moreover, motif analysis of ATAC-seq data revealed enrichment of SP1 and IRF1 transcription factor binding motifs in chromatin regions with enhanced accessibility in KMT5A knockout CD8+T cells. These data suggest a functional interaction between KMT5A and SP1/IRF1 in T cells. SP1 is a constitutive transcription factor that activates genes in the MAPK, JAK/STAT, and PI3K/AKT pathways, which govern T-cell activation, differentiation, persistence, and migration—processes critical to antitumor immunity.47 IRF1 regulates type I IFN-α/β responses and antiviral immunity.48 A recent study revealed KMT5A-mediated regulation of SP1 transcriptional activity in vascular smooth muscle cells and human umbilical vein endothelial cells increasing target gene expression.49 Similarly, our data show that KMT5A ablation amplifies SP1 activity in CD8+T cells, augmenting antitumor responses. Furthermore, SP1 partially mediates the enhanced effector program observed in KMT5A-deficient T cells, as evidenced by the significant attenuation of this phenotype—including reduced expression of IFN-γ and GZMB—following SP1 knockdown. Collectively, KMT5A regulates H4K20me1-dependent chromatin remodeling in human CD8+T cells, which in turn regulates the expression of the corresponding transcription factors, and enhances antitumor immunity through the synergistic regulation of effector genes through multiple pathways.

Interestingly, small-molecule inhibitors enhance CAR-T cell antitumor efficacy while mitigating adverse effects.50 The KMT5A inhibitor UNC0379 has shown preclinical efficacy in inflammatory and malignant models, including pulmonary fibrosis,38 ovarian cancer,51 neuroblastoma,52 and glioblastoma.53 Here, we report the first evidence that UNC0379 enhances CD8+T cell function and exerts antitumor effects both in vivo and in vitro. UNC0379-treated CD8+T cells exhibited enhanced AKT/mTOR pathway activation via reduced KMT5A and H4K20me1 expression. This not only promoted T-cell activation but also inhibited apoptosis by upregulating BCL-2 expression and suppressing BAX and PARP expression, collectively extending T-cell survival. Additionally, future studies should assess the efficacy of UNC0379 both as a monotherapy and in combination with immune checkpoint blockade within the framework of ACT. It is essential to ascertain whether UNC0379 can induce a potent antitumor T-cell response in vivo while further elucidating its molecular mechanisms of action in T cells, thereby broadening its therapeutic potential in cancer immunotherapy.

While our study demonstrates promising findings, several limitations warrant acknowledgment: (1) CRISPR screening was conducted in an allogeneic tumor system rather than a humanized mouse model; (2) validation using patient-derived T cells was not performed. (3) No evaluation of KMT5A knockout in CD4 CAR-T cells within a mixed CD4/CD8 context in vivo, or potential compensation by CD4 cells when KMT5A is intact. Nevertheless, the robustness of our conclusions is supported by the concordance between in vitro and in vivo datasets. Overall, the study reveals that lysine methyltransferase KMT5A acts as a negative regulator of CD8+T cell function, and its deletion or inhibition via the small-molecule inhibitor UNC0379 enhances the effector functions and antitumor activity of CAR-T cells, specifically 4-1BB costimulated CAR-T, providing a novel strategy to optimize adoptive T-cell therapies for solid tumors.

Methods

Detailed methods are described in the online supplemental method, here in brief.

Mice

Male NSG mice (6–8 weeks, GemPharmatech) were used in experiments approved by the East China Normal University (ECNU) Animal Care Committee (ID: m20210238). Mice were kept in specific pathogen-free (SPF) conditions and euthanized with CO2 for tissue collection.

Isolation and culture of human CD8+ T cells

PBMCs from healthy donors were obtained from SAILY BIO (Shanghai, China; #200425, #XW0801328). Human CD8+T cells were isolated using CD8 MicroBeads (Miltenyi Biotec, #130-045-201).

HKO library cloning

We developed a human epigenetic sgRNA library targeting 1,101 genes involved in transcription and translation, with four sgRNAs per gene to minimize off-target effects. The library also includes 100 NTCs. Oligos from IDT were cloned into lentiGuide-Puro (Addgene, #52963) using Gibson Assembly (NEB, E2611), transformed into Escherichia coli DH5α, and plated on ampicillin-LB agar. Plasmid DNA was extracted using an EndoFree Maxi Plasmid Kit (TIANGEN).

Production of HKO library lentivirus or CAR lentivirus

HEK293T cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (P/S), transfected with HKO/PSMA-CAR/MLSN-CAR, pMD2.G(Addgene #12259), psPAX2 (Addgene #12260) using PEI, and viral supernatant was collected 48 hours post-transfection.

HKO library lentivirus transduction and Cas9 electroporation

Primary human CD8+T cells were stimulated with T-cell TransAct (Miltenyi Biotec, #130-111-160) for 24 hours, transduced with concentrated HKO sgRNA library lentivirus at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.3, electroporated with Cas9 protein (IDT) after 24 hours, and cultured in X-VIVO medium with 100 U/mL interleukin (IL)-2 for 3 days, followed by serum-free X-VIVO medium for 12 hours at 1×10⁶ cells/mL.

In vitro degranulation screen

HKO sgRNA library lentivirus-infected CD8+T cells were resuspended at 1×10∧6 cells/mL in X-VIVO medium with 2 nM monensin and anti-CD107a antibody (1:400). These cells were co-incubated with PC3 cells at a 1:1 ratio for 2 hours at 37°C. After staining with anti-human CD8 (1:50) for 30 min, the top 5% of CD107a+CD8+ T cells were sorted using BD FACS Aria (BD Bioscience).

Preparation of gDNA for high-throughput sequencing

Viral library-infected and Cas9-electroporated CD8+T cells or tumor cells with TILs were collected, and gDNA was isolated using a TIANamp Genomic DNA Kit (TIANGEN). sgRNAs were amplified in a two-step PCR. After purification and quantification using a Tiangel Purification Kit, PCR products and HKO plasmid samples underwent high-throughput sequencing on a HiSeq 4000 instrument (Illumina) by XI’AN CyanSnow Gene.

In vitro co-culture of tumor cells and T cells for the T-cell killing assay and cytokine detection

PSMA-PC3 cells were co-cultured with KMT5A knockout PSMA CAR-T cells (1:1) in X-VIVO medium with IL-2 (100 U/mL). After 24 hours, cell counts and killing assays were performed. Supernatants were analyzed for IFN-γ and TNF-α by ELISA.

In vitro degranulation assay

KMT5A knockout, SP1 knockout, KMT5A/SP1 double knockout, or CTRL PSMA-CAR-T cells were incubated with PSMA-PC3 cells (1:1 ratio) in X-VIVO with monensin and anti-CD107a. After 2 hours, cells were stained with anti-CD8 antibody and analyzed by flow cytometry for CD107a+CD8+ T cells.

Tumor transplantation, adoptive transfer, and tissue processing

For the in vivo CD8 T-cell screen, NSG mice (n=3) were injected with 5×10∧6 PC3 cells. 10 days later, viral library-infected T cells were injected intravenously (1:1 ratio). 3 weeks post-transfer, tumors were isolated, digested, and filtered for gDNA extraction.

To test the efficacy of the adoptive transfer of KMT5A-knockout CD8+T cells, NSG mice were injected with 5×10∧6 PSMA-PC3 cells. After 24 or 15 days, mice were treated with T cells (1:1 ratio). Tumor size was measured at indicated time points. At the experimental endpoint, tumors and spleens were isolated, processed and analyzed by flow cytometry.

For the drug efficacy experiment, human CD8+T cells were activated, transduced with PSMA-CAR lentivirus, and treated with dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) or UNC0379 (5 µM) for 72 hours. Treated CAR-T cells were transferred into NSG mice with established PAMA-PC3 tumors (1:1 ratio, 10 days post-inoculation). Tumor size was monitored every 2 days. At the endpoint, tumors and spleens were harvested, processed, and analyzed by flow cytometry for TILs and splenic CD8+T cells.

Quantitative PCR

Total RNA of T cells or CAR-T cells was isolated with TRIzol (Thermo Fisher Scientific, #15596026), reverse-transcribed (TAKARA, #RR036A), and analyzed by qPCR using SYBR Green (YEASEN, #HB210910). Primers in online supplemental table 10.

Messenger RNA sequencing

Total RNA from KMT5A-knockout or CTRL CD8+T cells was isolated with TRIzol, fragmented, and reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA. After adapter ligation, size selection, and UDG treatment, libraries were PCR-amplified and sequenced (2×150 bp PE150) on Illumina NovaSeq 6000 (LC-Bio Technology, Hangzhou, China).

Flow cytometry and data analysis

Cells were surface-stained with antibodies in PBS+2% FBS for 30 min on ice. For intracellular cytokine staining, cells were fixed/permeabilized using Cytofix/Cytoperm (BD), then stained. Analysis was done on FACSAria II (BD) using FlowJo 7.6.1. Antibodies used are in online supplemental methods.

Accessible chromatin sequencing

ATAC-seq on sgCTRL/sgKMT5A-transduced CD8+T cells was performed using TruePrep Kit (Vazyme, #TD501), and libraries were sequenced (PE150,~600M reads/sample) on Illumina NovaSeq 6000 (Novogene).

Cleavage under targets and tagmentation

CUT&Tag was performed on sgCTRL/sgKMT5A-transduced CD8+T cells using NovoNGS CUT&Tag 3.0 High-Sensitivity Kit (Novoprotein, # N259-YH01) with anti-H4K20me1 antibodies. Libraries were sequenced (PE150, 6 Gb raw data) on Nova-PE150 (Novogene).

T-cell pharmacological treatment

Cell viability was assessed using CCK-8 (YEASEN, #40 203ES60).

For flow cytometry and western blot analyses, CD8+T cells were activated for 24 hours, infected with PSMA-CAR lentivirus for another 24 hours, and then treated with DMSO or UNC0379 (5 µM) for 72 hours. Expression of T-cell markers was assessed by flow cytometry. Protein levels were evaluated by western blot.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Details of the statistical analyses are indicated in the figure legends and Methods. Statistical analyses for comparing two groups were performed using unpaired two-tailed t-tests; two-way analysis of variance was used for multiple comparisons (GraphPad Prism V.7). Significance levels are indicated as *p<0.05, **p<0.01, and ***p<0.001. Data are presented as mean±SEM. Results are based on two or three independent experiments, as indicated in the figure legends.

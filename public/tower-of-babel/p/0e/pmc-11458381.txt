1. Sex Differences in Chronic Cough Epidemiology: The Korean Cough Study Group.

Koo HK, et al.

This study aimed to reveal sex differences in the prevalence and severity of chronic cough in South Korea, stratified by age and etiology. It included 625 adult patients from 19 respiratory centers. Results showed a female predominance (67%), with a male-to-female ratio of 1:2.03. Female patients were older and more likely to have asthma/cough variant asthma (CVA). There was no difference in cough duration or severity between sexes. The proportion of females with cough increased with age, particularly in the 50s, 60s, and 70–89 age groups. Understanding these differences could enhance cough-related pathophysiology and treatment strategies.

2. Examining the Relationship Between Polystyrene Microplastics and Male Fertility: Insights From an In Vivo Study and In Vitro Sertoli Cell Culture.

Tae BS, et al.

This study investigated the effects of polystyrene microplastics (PS-MPs) on the reproductive system of male mice. Thirty mice were divided into six groups and exposed to PS-MPs via oral gavage for 28 and 56 days. Sperm analysis and protein evaluations were conducted. Results showed significant differences in sperm concentration, motility, and normal sperm proportion between groups. Increased levels of TGF-β, HIF2α, and NOX-4 were observed. In vitro, PS-MPs penetrated Sertoli cells and increased inflammatory protein expressions. The study concluded that PS-MPs induce reproductive dysfunction in male mice, providing insights into PS-MPs-associated toxicity in mammals.

3. Validity and Reliability of the Korean Version of Reduced Morningness–Eveningness Questionnaire: Results From a General Population-Based Sample.

Chu MK, et al.

The study evaluated the validity and reliability of the Korean version of the reduced Morningness–Eveningness Questionnaire (rMEQ) for assessing chronotype. The rMEQ, a concise version of the original MEQ, includes five items. A total of 3,030 individuals participated, with an average rMEQ score of 14.0 ± 3.4. The rMEQ scores showed a strong positive correlation with the MEQ scores (r = 0.859) and a significant negative correlation with the midpoint of sleep on free days (r = –0.388). The internal consistency of the rMEQ was 0.609. The study concluded that the Korean rMEQ is a valid and reliable tool for assessing chronotype.

4. Outpatient Renal Function Screening Before Contrast-Enhanced CT Examinations.

Yoon SH, et al.

Iodinated contrast media can cause acute kidney injury, especially in patients with eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m2. This retrospective study included 2,560 outpatients without recent eGFR measurements before contrast-enhanced CT. On the day of CT, 1.1% had eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m2, often with histories of gout and renal disease. The ACR and ESUR guidelines identified 92.6% and 96.3% of patients with renal insufficiency, respectively, with high negative predictive values. These results support selective renal function screening before contrast-enhanced examinations.

5. Advancing Research, Writing, and Publishing in Nursing: Addressing Challenges and Improving Standards.

Koçyiğit BF, et al.

Nurses make up nearly 50% of the global health workforce. The World Health Organization advocates for expanding their roles to ensure equitable healthcare and meet rising service demands. Specialization in nursing has increased educational opportunities, including PhD programs, which prepare nurse researchers and educators. This growth emphasizes evidence-based practice and high-quality academic writing. Nurses’ involvement in developing evidence-based guidelines is crucial for practical and relevant recommendations. Advancing nursing relies on rigorous research methods and addressing issues like inadequate writing skills and plagiarism. Strategies to enhance nursing research include specialized education, open-access publishing, and social media use for broader dissemination.

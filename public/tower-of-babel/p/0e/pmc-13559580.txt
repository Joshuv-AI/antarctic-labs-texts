Non-small cell lung cancer and tumor-educated platelets: screening of biomarkers and construction of a prognostic model
Authors: Yandong Zhao, Tianjun Tang, Jie Li, Linxuan Chen, Xin Gu, Qiaofeng Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLung cancer is a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, emphasizing the urgent need for effective early detection strategies. Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) provides a unique perspective on tumor pathogenesis, focusing on concepts such as “long-term stasis leading to accumulation”. Tumor-educated platelets (TEPs) offer potential as biomarkers due to their ability to reflect cancer heterogeneity and facilitate less invasive diagnostic approaches. This study aims to identify TEP-related prognostic biomarkers for non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and to construct and validate a multigene prognostic model by integrating platelet transcriptomic data with tumor tissue datasets.MethodsWe performed comprehensive analysis of gene expression datasets obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) repositories to characterize transcriptomic differences among lung cancer specimens, normal tissue samples, and TEPs. Using R software, we identified Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and subsequently applied a multi-stage analytical pipeline to TEP-associated DEGs, incorporating univariate Cox proportional hazards regression, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression, multivariate Cox regression, and stepwise regression modeling to pinpoint genes with prognostic significance. These prognostically relevant genes served as the foundation for developing a risk stratification model. We computed individual risk scores across both training and validation cohorts, enabling patient stratification into high- and low-risk categories. Model robustness was assessed through internal cross-validation and external validation procedures, while predictive performance was quantified using risk calibration metrics and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis.ResultsThrough systematic bioinformatics screening, we identified a four-gene prognostic signature comprising NELL2, C4orf48, PRAM1, and KLHL35, which served as the foundation for developing our risk stratification algorithm. Rigorous internal cross-validation and external cohort validation substantiated the moderate predictive performance of this signature. Comprehensive clinicopathological correlation analysis revealed that elevated risk indices, advanced pathological staging (stage III–IV), increased primary tumor dimensions, regional lymph node metastasis, and distant organ dissemination each demonstrated statistically significant associations with diminished overall survival (OS) outcomes in lung cancer patients. The clinical nomogram exhibited acceptable calibration, with calibration plots showing reasonable concordance between predicted and observed survival probabilities across all time points. Discriminative capacity assessment via time-dependent ROC analysis yielded area under the curve (AUC) values consistently surpassing 0.6, confirming moderate prognostic discrimination. Furthermore, decision curve analysis (DCA) demonstrated that our integrated multi-gene model conferred potential net clinical benefit compared to individual prognostic variables across the full spectrum of clinically relevant threshold probabilities (0–1 range), thereby establishing its potential utility for risk-informed clinical decision-making.ConclusionsThis study identified NELL2, C4orf48, PRAM1, and KLHL35 as candidate TEP-related prognostic biomarkers for non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). The developed prognostic model shows preliminary potential for patient stratification, but its clinical application, particularly as a platelet-based liquid biopsy tool, requires further validation in independent TEP-based cohorts.

Key findings

• A four-gene tumor-educated platelet (TEP)-related prognostic signature (NELL2, C4orf48, PRAM1, KLHL35) was identified for non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). The risk score showed preliminary stratification ability, with area under the curve >0.6 at 1–3 years.

What is known and what is new?

• Tumor-educated platelets represent a promising liquid biopsy source for cancer detection and monitoring because they can reflect tumor-associated molecular alterations.

• This study integrated platelet transcriptomic data (GSE89843) with tumor tissue datasets (The Cancer Genome Atlas and GSE30219) and identified a four-gene TEP-related prognostic signature comprising NELL2, C4orf48, PRAM1, and KLHL35 for preliminary risk stratification in NSCLC.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The identified four-gene TEP-related signature provides preliminary evidence that platelet-associated transcriptomic alterations may contribute to prognostic stratification in NSCLC and offers candidate biomarkers for further investigation.

• Prospective validation in independent TEP-based cohorts with complete survival data and long-term follow-up is required before the model can be considered for clinical application.

Introduction

According to the 2024 World Health Organization (WHO) Cancer Statistics Report (1), lung cancer ranks first in global cancer incidence (12.4%) and mortality rate (18.7%). Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is the predominant histological subtype. Accurate prognostic stratification is critical for guiding individualized treatment decisions in NSCLC patients. According to a Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER)-based analysis using the eighth edition of the tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) staging system, the 5-year survival rate for patients with stage I NSCLC ranges from 73% to 90% (2), whereas that for patients with stage IIIA–IVA NSCLC is approximately 10% to 36% (3). TEP RNA biomarkers have been used to distinguish patients with NSCLC from those with other types of solid tumors and to differentiate patients with NSCLC from healthy individuals and individuals with non-cancerous inflammatory diseases (4). Currently, tumor-educated platelets (TEPs) have emerged as a promising liquid biopsy source for cancer detection (5). Unlike traditional tissue biopsies, which are invasive and subject to sampling bias, TEPs can be obtained minimally invasively from peripheral blood, allowing for serial sampling and real-time monitoring of tumor dynamics. Furthermore, TEPs can absorb and reflect tumor-derived biomolecules, providing a window into cancer heterogeneity (6). However, the challenge lies in identifying robust NSCLC-specific prognostic biomarkers from TEP-derived transcriptomic data and validating their clinical relevance in tumor tissues.

The perspective of TCM on tumors may offer a novel approach. Precision medicine and TCM’s principle of treatment based on syndrome differentiation both emphasize individualization, holistic consideration. Many ancient TCM texts describe the syndrome differentiation and treatment of NSCLC in terms of healthy energy deficiency, evil excess, qi stagnation, phlegm turbidity, blood stasis, and toxic heat. These factors ultimately lead to the dysfunction of lung qi in dispersing and descending, causing stagnation of lung qi, obstructed blood flow, accumulation of phlegm, and the formation of lumps in the lungs over time. Hence, the formation of blood stasis is a critical link in the development of NSCLC. The classic TCM literature The Inner Classic of the Yellow Emperor states that ‘When blood and qi are impeded, accumulation occurs over time’, implying that stagnation of blood and qi leads to the formation of masses over a prolonged period. This theory of ‘long-term stasis leading to accumulation’ has been refined over more than two millennia, with profound theoretical and practical implications. How can we explore NSCLC biomarkers from the TCM perspective of “long-term stasis leading to accumulation”?

In TCM, ‘blood stasis’ primarily manifests as abnormal changes in platelet morphology and function, which shares similar pathological changes with TEPs in the context of tumor development (6). TEPs are platelets of which its RNA and protein levels are altered through direct or indirect interactions with various signaling molecules or receptors within the tumor microenvironment (7). These TEPs circulate in the body, promoting tumor growth, metastasis, and angiogenesis (8). Studies have shown that TEPs hold potential as liquid biopsy markers for the diagnosis, prognosis, prediction, and monitoring of NSCLC (4). How, then, can we identify effective molecules from TEPs that can predict the diagnosis and prognosis of NSCLC?

Therefore, this study focuses on NSCLC, utilizing machine learning combined with bioinformatics to screen for TEP biomarkers in NSCLC patients and construct a prognostic model. The aim is to provide new insights for the early diagnosis of NSCLC. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-1075/rc).

Methods

Data sources

Training set: gene expression matrices for 513 NSCLC patients and 59 normal lung tissue samples were downloaded from the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA, https://gdc.cancer.gov/). High-throughput sequencing technology was used for differential gene identification and key gene screening. Validation set: the GSE30219 dataset, consisting of 85 NSCLC patients and 14 normal lung tissue samples, was downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). This dataset, using chip sequencing technology on the GPL570 platform, was used for key gene validation. NSCLC platelet data: the GSE89843 dataset, comprising 234 normal control samples and 402 NSCLC platelet samples, was downloaded from GEO. High-throughput sequencing technology was used to provide genes relevant to the research focus. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. All data utilized in this study were accessed on October 15, 2025. Furthermore, the authors did not have access to information that could identify individual participants during or after data collection. The overall flowchart of the research is shown in Figure 1.

In this study, GSE89843 was used to identify TEP-related candidate genes, whereas TCGA and GSE30219 tumor tissue datasets were used for prognostic model construction and validation because publicly available TEP cohorts with complete survival information are limited.

Differentially expressed gene (DEG) screening

The R package DESeq2 (9) was used to screen for DEGs in NSCLC and control samples from the TCGA dataset (DEGs1, NSCLC vs. normal), and in NSCLC TEP samples and control samples from the GSE89843 dataset (DEGs2, NSCLC platelets vs. normal). The screening criteria were set to P.adj <0.05 and |log2FC| >1. The VennDiagram package was then used to identify the intersecting genes between DEGs1 and DEGs2.

Construction of the prognostic risk model

To assess the prognostic utility of candidate genes for overall survival (OS) in NSCLC patients, we implemented a systematic multi-step analytical framework. Initially, we conducted univariate Cox proportional hazards regression on variables satisfying the proportional hazards assumption (PH.test >0.05) to examine correlations between gene expression profiles and OS outcomes, thereby preliminarily screening prognosis-associated genes within the training cohort. These candidates underwent further dimensionality reduction via least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression, executed through the glmnet package (10) in R with family parameter specified as “Cox”. Survival-related variables derived from both univariate Cox and LASSO procedures were subjected to PH assumption verification (PH.test > 0.05) prior to multivariate Cox regression modeling. When any covariates in the multivariate Cox model exhibited P>0.05, we employed stepwise variable selection methodology. Ultimately, independent prognostic determinants were identified through the multivariate Cox proportional hazards framework, forming the basis of our multi-gene prognostic signature. Patients were stratified into high- and low-risk subgroups using median risk score as the cutoff threshold. Survival disparities between risk categories were visualized through Kaplan-Meier survival curves generated with the survminer package (11), with statistical significance determined by log-rank testing (P<0.05). Model discriminative capacity was quantified by generating time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves at 1-, 2-, and 3-year intervals using the survivalROC package (12), with area under the curve (AUC) serving as the performance metric.

Validation of the prognostic risk model

Model generalizability was assessed using an independent cohort of NSCLC patients from the GSE30219 dataset. Individual risk indices were calculated and patients were dichotomized into high- and low-risk categories based on the median risk score threshold. Performance evaluation encompassed Kaplan-Meier survival analysis, log-rank statistical testing, ROC curve construction, and AUC quantification.

Construction and analysis of the nomogram

To integrate clinicopathological parameters with our risk signature for enhanced prognostic prediction, we performed univariate Cox regression analysis on the training cohort (TCGA NSCLC specimens), incorporating variables including patient age, sex, tumor stage, and calculated risk scores, followed by multivariate Cox modeling. The nomogram was constructed exclusively from variables retained in multivariate analysis, with initial candidate selection informed by univariate Cox results. Leveraging the rms package in R, we developed a clinical prediction nomogram capable of estimating 1-, 2-, and 3-year survival probabilities based on the selected predictors. Nomogram performance was comprehensively evaluated through calibration plot analysis, ROC curve assessment, and decision curve analysis (DCA) to determine clinical utility.

Immune infiltration

We employed the single sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) algorithm from the GSVA package (13) to assess the infiltration patterns of 28 distinct immune cell populations in TCGA-NSCLC specimens, comparing high- and low-risk patient cohorts. The Wilcoxon test enabled us to identify immune cells showing significant infiltration differences between these groups (P<0.05). Subsequently, using the psych package (14), we examined correlations among these differentially infiltrated immune cells and their relationships with prognostic genes across all TCGA-NSCLC samples (|cor| >0.3, P<0.05).

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

To elucidate the biological processes and signaling cascades involving prognostic genes in NSCLC pathogenesis, we conducted GSEA on TCGA-NSCLC data. We first calculated correlation coefficients between prognostic genes and all other genes using the psych package, then ranked genes in descending order of correlation strength. Using the c2.cp.v2025.1.Hs.symbols.gmt reference gene set from MSigDB (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb), we performed enrichment analysis through the clusterProfiler package (15) with significance criteria of |Normalized Enrichment Score (NES)| >1, false discovery rate (FDR) <0.25, and P<0.05. The five most statistically significant pathways were selected for presentation.

Additionally, using logFC values derived from differential expression analysis comparing high- and low-risk cohorts, we applied the same GSEA approach to identify biological pathways characteristic of each group.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.4.2). Differentially expressed genes were identified using the DESeq2 package, with an adjusted P value <0.05 and |log2 fold change| >1 considered statistically significant. The proportional hazards assumption was assessed before Cox regression analysis, with a PH test P value >0.05 indicating no evidence of violation. Univariate Cox proportional hazards regression and LASSO Cox regression with 10-fold cross-validation were used for preliminary variable selection. Candidate variables were subsequently entered into a multivariable Cox regression model, followed by stepwise regression to determine the final prognostic genes. Hazard ratios and corresponding 95% confidence intervals were reported.

Risk scores were calculated using the regression coefficients and gene expression levels, and patients were divided into high- and low-risk groups according to the median risk score. Survival differences were evaluated using Kaplan-Meier curves and the log-rank test. The discriminatory performance of the prognostic model was assessed using time-dependent receiver operating characteristic curves and the corresponding areas under the curve at 1, 2, and 3 years. Calibration curves and decision curve analysis were used to evaluate the calibration and potential clinical utility of the nomogram, respectively.

Differences in immune cell infiltration between the high- and low-risk groups were evaluated using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Spearman correlation analysis was used to examine correlations among differentially infiltrated immune cell types and between immune cell infiltration and prognostic gene expression. Correlations with an absolute correlation coefficient >0.3 and P<0.05 were considered statistically significant. For gene set enrichment analysis, |normalized enrichment score| >1, false discovery rate <0.25, and P<0.05 were used as the significance criteria. All statistical tests were two-sided, and unless otherwise specified, P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

When the model reached its optimal configuration (with the smallest lambda), the number of selected genes was eight, namely MAOB, CD69, NELL2, DEFA3, C4orf48, PRAM1, WASF1, and KLHL35. Construction of nomogram model

To elucidate the combined prognostic value of clinicopathological parameters and our risk stratification system, we performed univariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis on the TCGA NSCLC training cohort, incorporating variables including patient age, sex, tumor staging parameters, and calculated risk indices. This preliminary screening revealed that Risk Score, T classification, N classification, and pathological Stage demonstrated statistically significant associations with clinical outcomes (P<0.05) (Figure 9A). To determine which factors served as independent prognostic determinants within the training dataset, we subjected the univariate-significant variables (Risk Score, T classification, N classification, and pathological Stage) to proportional hazards assumption verification (PH test >0.05), followed by multivariate Cox regression modeling. The multivariate analysis identified Risk Score, T3 classification, and N1 classification as retaining independent prognostic significance (P<0.05) (Figure 9B). While multivariate Cox results informed the statistical validity of our model, the clinical nomogram was constructed using all variables that achieved significance in univariate screening to maximize clinical applicability. Consequently, the final predictive nomogram integrated four univariate-significant features: Risk Score, T classification, N classification, and pathological stage. Discussion

This study explores NSCLC biomarkers based on the TCM theory of ‘long-term stasis leading to accumulation,’ focusing on TEPs. The research identified four TEP genes significantly associated with NSCLC: NELL2, C4orf48, PRAM1, and KLHL35. Using these four genes, we constructed a model to predict the 1-, 2-, and 3-year survival probabilities of NSCLC patients and an independent prognostic model.

Due to the specific precursor mRNA splicing, TEPs produce unique mRNA expression profiles that can be used for tumor diagnosis. Given that platelets typically have a lifespan of 7–10 days, they can accumulate tumor-derived transcripts and protect them from degradation by free plasma RNases (7). Consequently, TEPs-related mRNAs can serve as blood biomarkers for tumors. The advantage of this approach lies in the ease of collection, isolation, and analysis of platelets, which lack a nucleus; thus, functional changes are primarily reflected in alterations to RNA or protein profiles (7). TEP RNA biomarkers have been used to distinguish NSCLC patients from those with other types of solid tumors, as well as to differentiate NSCLC from healthy individuals and non-cancerous inflammatory diseases (4). A recent review focusing on lung cancer further summarized the potential of TEPs to reflect tumor biology, tumor progression, and treatment-related changes through platelet transcriptomic alterations (18). Consistently, Shao et al. reported that TEP RNA could serve as a diagnostic biomarker for ground-glass opacity-related lung adenocarcinoma, providing recent lung cancer-specific evidence for the potential value of TEP-derived RNA biomarkers (19).

This study identified KLHL35, C4orf48, PRAM1, and NELL2 as potential specific targets for NSCLC. KLHL35 is derived from the Kelch-like family proteins (KLHLs), which contain BTB/POZ, BACK, and Kelch domains (20). Currently, there are 42 known members of the KLHL protein family, which play significant biological roles in various life processes, including immune response, skeletal muscle maintenance, and brain development (21,22). Abnormalities in KLHLs are closely associated with tumorigenesis and cancer progression. For instance, overexpression of KLHL37 can promote the development of colorectal cancer (23), increased expression of KLHL7 is significantly associated with the invasive phenotype of breast cancer (24), and silencing KLHL5 can inhibit the proliferation and survival of ovarian cancer cells (25). These studies suggest that KLHL proteins not only regulate the malignant proliferation of tumor cells but also possess clinical oncogenic potential.

PEST domain-containing protein tyrosine phosphatase-interacting protein 1 (PRAM1) is expressed in mature granulocytes and serves as a molecular marker of granulocyte maturation, also exhibiting immunomodulatory functions (26). Studies have found that downregulation of PRAM1 in lung adenocarcinoma tissues may be associated with the pro-tumor effects of neutrophils (27).

C4orf48, also known as NEL-like protein 2 (NELL2) interacting cell ontogeny regulator 1 (NICOL1), and NELL2, encode members of a repeat sequence family rich in epidermal growth factor-like (EGF-like) domains (28). They involved in various cellular biological processes, including cell growth, differentiation, and regeneration. They play significant roles in tissue repair and angiogenesis in both cancer and cardiovascular diseases (29).

Therefore, KLHL35, C4orf48, PRAM1, and NELL2 may be involved in tumor angiogenesis and invasive migration. As targets for NSCLC, these genes exhibit a certain degree of specificity and efficacy.

The collective biological functions of these four TEP-derived genes are consistent with certain pathophysiological features described in the TCM concept of “long-term stasis leading to accumulation.” In modern oncology, the TCM notion of “blood stasis” (Xue Yu) may be interpreted to encompass not only macroscopic hemodynamic alterations but also features such as aberrant angiogenesis, immune microenvironment remodeling, and chronic inflammation. In this study, GSEA revealed that C4orf48 was enriched in translation and ribosome pathways, while NELL2 was enriched in mitochondrial energy metabolism pathways. These findings are consistent with enhanced proliferative activity and metabolic reprogramming—processes that may correspond to the uncontrolled cellular proliferation inherent to the TCM concept of “accumulation”. Meanwhile, KLHL35 and PRAM1 exhibited immunomodulatory associations, with PRAM1 showing a strong correlation with MDSCs and neutrophils in immune infiltration analysis, indicating a link to an immunosuppressive and pro-inflammatory microenvironment. Furthermore, GSEA revealed enrichment of cell cycle and translation-related pathways in the high-risk group, consistent with enhanced proliferative activity. Taken together, these findings suggest that the four identified biomarkers may not only have prognostic value but also provide a potential molecular framework for interpreting TCM-related pathophysiological concepts in NSCLC within a transcriptomic context. However, these genes may be associated with NSCLC progression and immune regulation, but their functional roles require further experimental validation.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, although the candidate genes were initially screened from TEP data (GSE89843), the prognostic model was constructed and validated exclusively using tumor tissue datasets (TCGA and GSE30219). This discrepancy arises because publicly available TEP datasets lack the comprehensive survival information required for time-to-event analysis. Consequently, the actual performance of this four-gene signature as a liquid biopsy-based prognostic tool remains to be validated in independent cohorts of NSCLC patients with matched TEP sequencing and clinical follow-up data. Second, the moderate discriminatory ability (AUC >0.6) suggests that the model’s clinical utility is preliminary and requires further optimization. Third, the specific biological functions and mechanisms of these four genes in NSCLC remain incompletely understood, and further experimental studies are needed to elucidate their roles in tumor progression and immune regulation. Fourth, the retrospective nature of the data and the absence of external validation in a prospective cohort limit the generalizability of our findings. In addition, selection bias, batch effects, platform heterogeneity, and incomplete clinical information in public datasets may also affect the robustness of the results. Future studies with prospectively collected samples—particularly TEP-based cohorts with long-term survival records—are warranted to determine whether this signature can be translated into a clinically actionable assay. This need is consistent with recent reviews emphasizing that TEP-based liquid biopsy still requires methodological standardization, independent cohort validation, and clearer clinical implementation pathways before routine clinical use (30).

Conclusions

This study identified four TEP-related genes (KLHL35, C4orf48, PRAM1, and NELL2) as candidate prognostic biomarkers for NSCLC. A prognostic model based on these genes demonstrated moderate discriminatory ability (AUC >0.6) for 1-, 2-, and 3-year OS in both training and validation cohorts. The model shows preliminary potential for risk stratification; however, its clinical utility—particularly as a liquid biopsy tool—requires prospective validation in independent TEP cohorts with long-term follow-up data. Further experimental studies are also needed to elucidate the biological functions of these genes in NSCLC.

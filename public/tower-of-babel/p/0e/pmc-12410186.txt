A Man With Abdominal Pain
Authors: Amman Malik, Juber Ahmed, Dana Mahdi, Zahra Al-Essah, Ibrahim Hamami, Rashad Riasat Haque, Ali Usamah Butt, Adnan Malik
Published: 2025

Presentation

An 18-year-old man presented to the emergency department with a 6-hour history of vomiting and abdominal pain, which had migrated from the umbilicus to the right iliac fossa.

Physical Examination

Upon examination, it was observed that the patient was febrile with abdominal tenderness in the right lower quadrant, maximal over McBurney point. Laboratory studies revealed a white-cell count of 23,460 per cubic millimeter (normal range, 4000-11,000) and C-reactive protein of 88 milligrams per litre (normal range, <11).

Diagnosis

Acute Appendicitis

Because of inaccessibility to ultrasonography, plain radiographs of the abdomen were obtained, which revealed a calcified mass overlying the right iliac crest (Fig).FigureAnnotated plain radiograph of the abdomen, showing a calcified mass overlying the right iliac crest.

Treatment

Emergent laparoscopic appendicectomy was undertaken, which revealed a large appendicolith lodged in the distal appendix, with a necrotic proximal appendix, close to perforation. A Robinson drain was placed because of the extensive inflammation and purulent free fluid in the right iliac fossa. The patient was continued on intravenous antibiotics postoperatively, after which an uneventful recovery was made.

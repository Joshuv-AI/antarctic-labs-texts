Melatonin increases superoxide dismutase 2 (SOD2) levels and improves rat ovarian graft function after transplantation
Authors: Karla Krislane Alves Costa Monteiro, Luciana Lamarão Damous, Marcos Eiji Shiroma, Lara Termini, José Cipolla-Neto, Ricardo dos Santos Simões, Rinaldo Florencio da Silva, José Antonio Turri, Edmund C Baracat, Jose Maria Soares-Junior
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOvarian cryopreservation is a promising technique despite being hindered by damage from freezing and thawing. Melatonin can mitigate this outcome.ObjectiveThis study aimed to evaluate the effect of melatonin on the follicular dynamics of ovarian tissue in a cryopreserved cell culture.MethodsThree-month-old adult female Wistar rats (n = 24) weighing approximately 250 g were oophorectomized and divided into two groups (n = 12): the control group (CG) and the melatonin group (MG). In the CG, slow cryopreservation was performed according to the standard protocol with Medium M2 and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO). In MG, melatonin diluted in ethyl alcohol vehicle at a concentration of 0.1 μm was added to the culture medium. In both groups, the ovaries were cryopreserved by slow freezing and kept in liquid nitrogen for 24 h. Subsequently, after thawing, the ovaries were reimplanted in the retroperitoneum, one on each side of the great vessels (inferior vena cava and aorta). After 30 days, the animals were euthanized during the diestrus phase; then, the grafts were removed and processed for histomorphometric and immunohistochemical analyses, whereas the blood was subjected to biochemical analysis. Student’s t test was used to assess the difference between the groups.ResultsThe FSH levels in MG (83.79 ± 32.37) were lower than those in CG (120.52 ± 36.59), p = 0.03. The FSH/AMH ratios were also lower in MG (3.53 ± 1.13) than in CG (6.52 ± 2.85), p = 0.001. The SOD2 immunoexpression was higher in MG than in CG regarding all parameters except for the degenerated follicles (follicular cells and internal thecal cells): CG (16.80 ± 4.80 [13.36–20.24]) and MG (14.91 ± 4.04 [12.01–17.79]) p = 0.351. Statistically, the difference in intact follicles (theca + interstitium) between CG (6.60 ± 2.59 [4.75–8.45]) and MG (9.31 ± 3.09 [7.09–11.51]) was significant (p = 0.049), with a small difference in the expression of regular antral follicles.ConclusionsMelatonin can improve the quality of cryopreserved tissues, as evidenced in this study, and the evaluation of cryopreserved ovarian grafts, as shown in the melatonin group with better hormonal parameters and greater immunohistochemical expression of the SOD2 antioxidant. Thus, damage is reduced during cryopreservation and transplantation is improved.Keywords: Melatonin, Ovarian transplantation, Rats, Cryopreservation, Fertility preservation, Ovary, Transplantation, Tissue preservation

Background

The prognosis of young women with cancer has significantly improved due to advancements in oncological disease treatment [1, 2, 3], resulting in higher cure rates [4]. An estimated 60,000 new cases of cancer in women under 40 years of age are reported in the USA each year, four thousands of which are prepubescent or teenage patients [5]. Chemotherapy and radiotherapy, however, can harm the ovary and jeopardize the patients’ fertility [6]. Animal models have been created to evaluate potential solutions to freezing and thawing processes to prevent damage to fertility, which is a major concern.

Oocytes, embryos, or ovarian tissue can be frozen as a method of preserving reproductive potential [3-7]. We cannot induce ovulation in prepubertal girls, and because the cryopreservation of oocytes or embryos requires a two- to four-week delay at the commencement of cancer treatment, these fertility preservation approaches are not currently part of the standard management of young patients who will undergo cytotoxic treatment [7, 8]. Ovarian tissue cryopreservation is still regarded as an experimental method for limited thawed tissue quality [9-12]. Following transplantation, follicular activation in the graft proceeds more quickly than in the host, possibly resulting in differences in granulosa cell maturation and oocyte formation. These differences may be caused by hypoxia and oxidative stress [13-15], as well as by a deficiency of anti-Müllerian hormone in the host [4].

The structural morphological alterations induced by freezing and thawing may include oxidative stress and specifically influence the thickness of the inner theca cell layer [4, 11]. Using melatonin in cryopreservation media after autologous rat ovary transplantation, Shiroma et al. [16] demonstrated that melatonin can increase the number of mature follicles, type I collagen, angiogenesis, and proliferation while decreasing follicle apoptosis. However, the impact of melatonin on ovarian graft function and its potential to mitigate oxidative stress throughout freezing and thawing procedures were not discussed by the authors. In this field of experimental research, new techniques have been proposed, among which, we highlight the use of melatonin to reduce oxidative stress and improve the quality of the implanted graft [16-18].

Since 1993, melatonin has been known to scavenge free radicals and reactive oxygen species (ROS), which are generated during metabolic processes and by damaging molecules in cells, including lipids, proteins, and DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) [19]. To protect against ROS, cells develop antioxidant defense agents that include the enzymes superoxide dismutase (SOD), catalase (CAT), and glutathione peroxidase (GPx) [20], which are fundamental for balancing and controlling possible damage related to excess oxidative stress.

The superfamily of superoxide dismutases (SODs) is one of the main families of antioxidant enzymes whose function is to catalyze the dismutation of superoxide radicals into oxygen peroxide and molecular oxygen [21-23]. Superoxide dismutase 2 (SOD2), which is located in the mitochondrial matrix, plays an indispensable role in maintaining redox homeostasis. This enzyme represents one of the first obstacles that superoxide radicals (O2•-) face when they reach the cytoplasm, and through the detoxification of these ROS, SOD2 helps prevent damage to biological molecules [24-27]. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the effects of melatonin, which acts as an antioxidant through its influence on the SOD2 enzyme and on ovarian function in cryopreserved rat ovarian tissue.

Materials and methods

This study was approved by the local Ethics Committee on the Use of Animals (CEUA-FMUSP 024/15). The sample size was calculated using the Altman nomogram [28] and the results from a previously published study; the study concerned melatonin treatment and follicular expression of vitrified ovarian grafts and was conducted with both control and study groups [29]. The calculations yielded a sample size of 24 animals in total for 2 groups considering 80% power and a statistical significance of 5%.

Accordingly, the sample consisted of 24 three-month-old female Wistar rats (Rattus norvegicus albinus) weighing approximately 250 g each. The animals were kept under adequate conditions of hygiene, temperature, and feeding and in a controlled “zeitgeber time” (ZT) 12/12 h light/dark regime in the laboratory [30], where ZT0 starts at 6 am.

Groups

The 24 animals were randomly divided into two groups: (1) the control group (CG) and (2) the melatonin group (MG). During the procedures, there was a loss of two animals in each group, with 10 animals remaining in each group (n = 10). Slow cryopreservation of the ovaries was performed in the CG according to the standard protocol with M2 medium, dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), and ethyl alcohol vehicle [31]. In the MG, melatonin (SigmaAldrich, Saint Louis, MO, USA) diluted in ethyl alcohol vehicle at 0.1 μm concentration was added to the culture medium [13, 14], which also followed the aforementioned standard protocol.

Only animals with regular estrous cycles of 4 to 5 days were used, and estrus was standardized for surgical procedures [16, 18]. Initially, vaginal smears were collected daily between ZTs 2 and 4 for characterization of the estrous cycle by the Shorr–Harris technique [30]. The ZT stands for zeitgeber time and a 12 h/12 h light/dark cycle; ZT0 stands for the transition from dark to light; and ZT12 stands for the transition from light to dark. Therefore, vaginal smears were collected 2 to 4 h after the lights were turned on.

Oophorectomy and ovarian graft implantation procedures

The animals were weighed and then given injections of xylazine (15 mg/kg) and ketamine (60 mg/kg) intraperitoneally to induce anesthesia [32]. After the abdominal cavity was opened, the ovaries were identified, and their pedicles were clamped and immediately connected with 4 − 0 nylon thread to identify the site. Subsequently, the ovaries were removed bilaterally at the junction of the uterine horns, and then hemostasis was assessed. Next, the ovaries were washed with saline solution (0.9% NaCl), and the oviducts were resected with periovarian fat fragments [16].

In both groups, the ovaries were cryopreserved by slow freezing and kept in liquid nitrogen for 24 h [9, 16, 31]. After this period, the samples were thawed at room temperature (25 °C). The ovaries were reimplanted in the retroperitoneum of their respective donors, one on each side of the great vessels (inferior vena cava and aorta), without vascular anastomosis and fixed by means of a simple stitch with nonabsorbable thread (nylon 4 − 0).

After postoperative day 15 of the transplant, vaginal smears were collected daily, always at the same time between ZTs 2 and 4 until postoperative days 30 to 35 when euthanasia was performed with the animals in the diestrus phase (days 30–35 of the estrous cycle).

Blood collection and biochemical analysis

After 30 to 35 days, vaginal smears were obtained, animals in the diestrus phase were decapitated, and blood was immediately collected for hormonal determination. Blood samples were promptly centrifuged at 4 °C (1,500 × g) for 10 min, and the supernatants were frozen at -20 °C for subsequent analyses via radioimmunoassay using a coated-tube, double-antibody technique (ICN Biomedicals, Inc., Costa Mera, CA).

The biochemical determinations were performed on rat blood with the following kits: (a) FSH (E-EL-R0391, Elabscience, Texas, USA) with a detection range of 3.13 ∼ 200 ng/mL and a sensitivity of 1.88 ng/mL; (b) LH (E-EL-R0026, Elabscience, Texas, USA) with a detection range of 1.56 ∼ 100 mIU/mL and a sensitivity of 0.94 mIU/mL; (c) estradiol (E-OSEL-R0001, Elabscience, Texas, USA) with a detection range of 3.13 ∼ 200 pg/mL and a sensitivity of 1.17 pg/mL; (d) progesterone (E-EL-0154, Elabscience, Texas, USA) with a detection range of 0.31 ∼ 20 ng/mL and a sensitivity of 0.15 ng/mL; (e) AMH (E-EL-R3022; Elabscience, Texas, USA) with a detection range of 62.50 ∼ 4000 pg/mL and a sensitivity of 37.50 pg/mL; (f) Inibin b (E-EL-R1027; Elabscience, Texas, USA) with a detection range of 15.63 ∼ 1000 pg/mL and a sensitivity of 9.38 pg/mL; and (g) FSH (E-EL-R0391; Elabscience, Texas, USA) with a detection range of 3.13 ∼ 200 ng/mL and a sensitivity of 1.88 ng/mL. We calculated the LH/FSH ratio and the FSH/AMH ratio [31]. The samples were assayed in triplicate and processed on the same day. The cross-reactivity with other steroids in both assays was less than 0.01%.

Morphological and immunohistochemical analyses

The ovaries were fixed for 24 h in 10% formaldehyde, phosphate-buffered at pH 7.2, dehydrated in increasing concentrations of ethyl alcohol, cleared in xylene, and then embedded in paraffin. The paraffin-embedded samples were cut into 3-μm sections. Some sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) for histomorphometric analysis, whereas the other tissues underwent immunohistochemistry to determine SOD2 expression. The immunohistochemical assays were performed at the Cancer Innovation Laboratory, Centro de Investigação Translacional em Oncologia, Instituto do Câncer do Estado de São Paulo Octavio Frias de Oliveira—ICESP, Universidade de São Paulo, Brazil, by automated reaction using the UltraView Universal DAB Detection Kit® (Ventana Medical Systems, Inc., Roche, Tucson, AZ, USA) and the Ventana BenchMark GX equipment (Roche Diagnostics, Mannheim, Germany), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. In summary, the slides were subjected to an antigenic recovery process, carried out with a tris-based buffer, pH 8.0 (Ultra Cell Conditioning Solution—Roche Diagnostics, Mannheim, Germany) under heat for 30 min. Next, rabbit polyclonal antibodies directed against SOD2 protein (ab 13533, Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA) were used in a 1:1000 concentration for 32 min. Positive reactions were visualized with a cocktail (UltraView Universal HRP Multimer—Roche Diagnostics, Mannheim, Germany) containing peroxidase conjugated anti-mouse and anti-rabbit secondary antibodies in the presence of DAB (3,3-Diaminobenzidine), resulting in a brown precipitate. The slides were counterstained with hematoxylin for 20 min. This entire process was performed inside the Ventana BenchMark GX equipment in a closed system. Samples of ovary tissues previously tested for SOD2 expression by immunohistochemistry were used as reaction controls and were incubated with or without anti-SOD2 antibodies (data not shown).

Analysis of parameters

Each H&E-stained section of an animal was divided into four fields where follicles were counted at a 10X magnification with concomitant performance of the following two histomorphometric analyses: evaluation of follicles (degenerated and mature) and of corpora lutea. To assess follicular development, ovarian follicles were counted and classified as atretic or degenerated (irregular), mature (with a single voluminous antrum), or corpora lutea. Degenerated follicles were defined as those having a wrinkled zona pellucida and disarranged follicular cells with pycnotic nuclei, regardless of the size of the follicular cells. Viable mature follicles were those that did not present degenerative changes [31].

For the immunohistochemical analysis, SOD2 immunoreactivity was evaluated in eight different fields per ovarian section/animal using a light microscope at 400X magnification. The results are expressed as a percentage of the positive area (arbitrary unit). The readings were obtained by two independent investigators blinded to the groups [31] (Fig. 1). Photomicrographs were captured, and measurements were carried out with the aid of a computerized system consisting of a light microscope (Carl Zeiss) adapted to a high-resolution camera (AxioCam MRC from Carl Zeiss) and a color video monitor. The measurements were obtained using the Carl Zeiss image analysis program AxionVisionREL 4.6 .

Statistical analysis

Prior to the statistical analysis, distribution tests were carried out to determine the normality of each variable. For the continuous quantitative parametric variables, the Student t test or ANOVA was used for comparing means when applicable; for nonparametric variables, the Mann‒Whitney test or the Kruskal‒Wallis test was used; and for categorical variables, the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was used. A logistic regression analysis was also performed to determine the main factors associated with the melatonin effect on ovarian tissue. All tests were run using STATA 16 SE software, and the statistical significance level to reject the null hypothesis was set at p < 0.05.

Discussion

Ovarian transplantation is one of the main options for preserving the fertility of patients treated for cancer among other conditions that can lead to premature ovarian failure (POF). Rat experimental models are good options for assessing fertility and reproduction, and they have the advantage of having a short estrous cycle, that is, one whose mean duration is four to five days [33]. The great limitation of this model is damage caused by free radicals during cryopreservation, which is made apparent by increased expression of superoxide dismutase. This enzyme is important for inducing the formation of an appropriate antioxidant and transporting it to an adequate location, thereby preventing cell damage. Concomitantly, we observed improvements in the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis, given the slight decrease in FSH levels, the partial improvement in ovarian reserve, and the decreased ratio of FSH to anti-Mullerian hormone levels.

In the female reproductive system, melatonin action is widespread and has been studied extensively in terms of ovarian activity, ovulation, pregnancy, and childbirth [13, 15]. Melatonin acts on the hypothalamus, on the hypophysis, and directly on the ovary [33-35]. It is a hydrophilic and lipophilic molecule [13, 15, 34] that spreads easily across several subcellular compartments, such as membranes, the cytoplasm, the nucleus, and the mitochondria [13-15]. In the ovary, two specific receptors for melatonin (MT1 and MT2) have been identified [33]; these receptors act by reducing cyclic adenosine monophosphate [36-38]. The same receptors have also been detected in human ovarian follicles [36]. These proteins act on ovarian function, modulating steroidogenesis and noticeably increasing the synthesis of progesterone [36] and its receptor [37]. Our results show that melatonin also plays an important role in ovarian cryopreservation and in oxidative stress.

Melatonin and its metabolites act as direct free radical scavengers through several scavenging mechanisms, upregulating the transcription and activity of antioxidative enzymes and binding to transition metals to prevent the production of hydroxyl radicals. Melatonin is a well-known and potent antioxidant. Furthermore, because melatonin is highly concentrated in the mitochondria, it shields DNA, lipids, and proteins from oxidative damage. The mechanisms of the antioxidant actions of melatonin have been extensively reviewed elsewhere [34]. Melatonin exhibits antioxidant properties in neurological diseases, organ transplantation, chronic diseases, surgery, cancer, and reproduction in several tissues and organs in different experimental models [39]. The primary mechanism by which melatonin increases antioxidant activity involves was found to increase the concentration of SOD and transform the superoxide anion radical into hydrogen peroxide, which in turn is transformed into water and oxygen by CAT and GPx. These enzymes are the first line of antioxidant defense against free radical-induced toxicity tissue damage [39]. This effect probably explains the high SOD2 expression in ovaries cryopreserved with melatonin. The higher SOD2 expression indicates a stronger antioxidant response, which correlates with lower tissue damage. Although SOD2 expression itself does not directly indicate the extent of damage, it provides valuable insight into the defensive capacity of the tissue against oxidative stress.

The great challenge in ovarian transplantation is the damage caused by oxidative stress and hypoxia during cryopreservation and graft reimplantation. In addition to the damage caused by the freezing process, to achieve successful transplantation, it is necessary to reduce hypoxic injury, as the graft is avascular and suffers the effects of ischemia until revascularization in the ischemia‒reperfusion process [40]. Therefore, our group conducted several studies involving preconditioning of the ovary in vivo [9] and cell therapy with stem cells derived from female rat adipose tissue to induce neovascularization and promote angiogenesis. The results, however, didn’t show improvement in long-term graft survival [36].

Além disso, testamos várias técnicas de criopreservação, e a mais promissora foi a usada neste estudo [15]. Usamos uma sequência de estudos realizados usando células-tronco: (a) primeiro, conduzimos o estudo injetando células-tronco diretamente nos ovários de ratos, que foram posteriormente transplantados frescos; esta técnica provou ser viável [41], mas no ovário criopreservado, danificamos o tecido [12]; (b) então, usamos a terapia secretoma com células-tronco, e os resultados mostraram que havia pouca diferença na qualidade do enxerto transplantado [52] (c) finalmente, em um estudo anterior, usamos uma técnica envolvendo a aplicação de células-tronco em uma esponja absorvível [42, 43], que melhorou o enxerto ovariano transplantado, como evidenciado por um aumento no número de folículos maduros, a quantidade de colágeno tipo I, proliferação celular e angiogênese, e uma redução na apoptose [16]. Essas mudanças positivas apontam para a melatonina como um agente promissor para preservar o tecido durante a criopreservação. No entanto, não houve confirmação hormonal ou um mecanismo de ação sugerido para a melatonina neste tipo de tecido, lacunas que mais tarde foram preenchidas pelo nosso estudo.

The first report on the use of melatonin in the treatment of female rats via ovarian autotransplantation [44] involved a decrease in ovarian necrosis and malondialdehyde levels in tissue and plasma and an increase in superoxide dismutase and glutathione peroxidase. In another publication, animals subjected to xenotransplantation and treated with melatonin orally and vitamin E showed improvement in the quality of the ovarian graft, as determined by the follicle count, apoptosis index, expression of vascular endothelial growth factor, and proliferative cell nuclear antigen [6]. In alignment with these reports, the present study revealed an increase in reactivity to SOD in the follicles and corpora lutea of the melatonin group. However, these studies differ from ours in that their animals were treated with melatonin, whereas in our study, melatonin was used in the culture medium during cryopreservation. Our study showed that melatonin strongly increased SOD expression.

Several reports on the reduction in damage caused by free radicals in several diseases, including cancer, reinforce the positive effects of melatonin. Directly or indirectly (via a decrease in free radicals), melatonin may inhibit the mitochondrial apoptotic pathway by reducing BCL2 (B-cell lymphoma 2) expression and caspase-3 activity [6, 17]. The strength of our study lies in evaluating melatonin as a potent antioxidant in cryopreservation medium through SOD2 expression. Other studies have shown that melatonin action is more intense than that of vitamins C and E [14, 15, 39, 45], given that their metabolites also act to clear free radicals via an antioxidant cascade effect [12, 15].

The excess free radicals interact with lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids, resulting in the loss of membrane integrity, functional and structural changes in proteins, and nucleic acid damage [15, 34] and causing cell damage and a diminished ovarian reserve. These outcomes were paralleled by lower FSH values and a lower FSH to anti-Mullerian hormone (AMH) ratio in the control group (vehicle) than in the group treated with melatonin. A balance between free radicals and antioxidants in the ovarian follicle appears to be essential for the proper functioning of the oocyte and granulosa cells [13, 15, 34] and for decreasing the number of degenerated follicles, which are undergoing atresia. When evaluating the LH/FSH ratio used to assess the pituitary-ovarian axis, we noted a change in the pattern, with a relative increase in LH release compared to FSH. Animals that present a disorder in ovarian steroids related to ovarian transplantation may develop an abnormal ratio with LH predominance [46]. Moreover, since the correlation of FSH and of AMH with low ovarian reserve was positive and negative respectively, the FSH/AMH ratio was calculated. Although AMH alone has a predictive value that is similarly sensitive to oocyte production and to ovarian reserve, it is not as specific as the FSH/AMH ratio [47].

Theca interna cells are important for androgen production and essential for follicular steroidogenesis and estrogen production [48, 49, 50]. The latter is controlled by LH, which determines estrogen proliferation and activity. When LH levels are very high, as is the case with polycystic ovary syndrome [51], follicular growth is disrupted, and aromatase activity is reduced. In our model, we found no significant changes in the LH level in comparison with those in the control group; however, we did observe changes in the level of FSH, which is a receptor in theca cells, although its primary function is in granulosa cells [44]. The FSH is a marker of ovarian reserve: the greater the serum concentration, the smaller the number of follicles. Hence, melatonin prompts a decrease in hormone levels, which is reflected in the ovarian reserve, as evidenced by the transformation of FSH into AMH. This fact suggests that a greater number of follicles may have survived cryopreservation with melatonin treatment.

Conclusions

Melatonin may enhance the quality of cryopreserved ovarian grafts, as evidenced by the hormonal parameters and immunohistochemical expression of SOD2, which revealed less damage during transplantation in the melatonin group. Future research is needed for a better evaluation of the improvement capacity of melatonin in the reproductive performance of grafts.

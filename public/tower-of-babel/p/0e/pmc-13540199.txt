Association of albumin-based inflammatory indices with adverse neonatal outcomes in intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy: a retrospective comparative study
Authors: Sevil Cicek, Bilge Kapudere, Yasemin Beyza Kaya Parspancı, Zehra Tavukcuoglu, Berkay Soyupak, Merve Amine Eyiceoglu, Feyza Kutay Yılmaz, Reyhan Ayaz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo evaluate whether albumin-based inflammatory indices are associated with perinatal and neonatal outcomes in pregnancies complicated by intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP).MethodsThis retrospective comparative study included 277 pregnant women who delivered at a tertiary referral center between January 2020 and December 2025 (ICP, n = 90; controls, n = 187). Albumin-based inflammatory indices were calculated from routine laboratory parameters obtained during the week of delivery. Perinatal and neonatal outcomes were compared between groups. Within the ICP group, associations between inflammatory indices and adverse outcomes were evaluated, and multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed to identify independent predictors within the ICP group of neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) admission.ResultsGestational age at delivery and neonatal birth weight were significantly lower in the ICP group, while rates of preterm birth, low birth weight, and NICU admission were significantly higher compared with controls. Albumin-based inflammatory indices did not differ significantly between groups. Within the ICP group, low birth weight (< 2500 g) was associated with higher NAR (p = 0.031), whereas LAR (p = 0.094) and MAR (p = 0.129) were not statistically significant. NICU admission was associated with higher leukocyte-to-albumin ratio (LAR) (p = 0.036), neutrophil-to-albumin ratio (NAR) (p = 0.032), and monocyte-to-albumin ratio (MAR) (p = 0.017) in univariable analyses. In multivariable logistic regression analysis, higher LAR (OR 1.818, 95% CI 1.026–3.223; p = 0.041) and preterm birth < 37 weeks (OR 6.119, 95% CI 2.145–17.457; p = 0.001) remained independently associated with NICU admission. Exploratory ROC analysis performed within the ICP group demonstrated limited discriminatory performance for all evaluated indices, with modest accuracy observed for LAR (AUC = 0.67) and MAR (AUC = 0.65), whereas other indices, including NPAR (p = 0.455) and CAR (p = 0.065), showed poor and non-significant discriminatory performance.ConclusionAlbumin-based inflammatory indices demonstrated limited overall clinical utility in pregnancies complicated by ICP. Among these indices, leukocyte-to-albumin ratio was associated with NICU admission, suggesting a possible association between leukocyte-related inflammatory burden and neonatal outcomes. However, this finding should be interpreted with caution given the limited discriminatory performance of the evaluated indices and the potential for residual confounding. Further prospective studies are needed to validate this observation.Keywords: Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy, Albumin, Inflammation, Biomarkers, Neonatal outcomes, NICU

Introduction

Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP) is a multifactorial liver disorder unique to pregnancy that most commonly presents during the second and third trimesters. The condition is characterized by maternal pruritus without an accompanying rash and is diagnosed primarily by elevated serum total bile acid (TBA) concentrations, with or without abnormalities in liver biochemical markers. The reported incidence of ICP varies widely across different geographic regions and populations, reflecting the influence of genetic, environmental, and hormonal factors [1–3].

Although maternal symptoms typically resolve after delivery, ICP has been consistently associated with an increased risk of adverse perinatal outcomes. Elevated bile acid levels have been linked to spontaneous preterm birth, fetal distress, meconium-stained amniotic fluid, and neonatal respiratory morbidity. Several large cohort studies and meta-analyses have demonstrated that the risk of these adverse outcomes increases with rising serum bile acid concentrations, underscoring the clinical importance of close fetal surveillance in affected pregnancies [4–6].

Despite its clinical relevance, the pathophysiological mechanisms underlying ICP remain incompletely understood and do not fully explain the marked variability in maternal disease severity or neonatal outcomes [3]. Importantly, bile acid concentrations alone may not adequately capture the full spectrum of fetal risk. Adverse perinatal outcomes have been reported even among women with comparable bile acid levels, suggesting that additional maternal, placental, or inflammatory factors may contribute to fetal vulnerability [4–6].

Although ursodeoxycholic acid is widely used to alleviate maternal symptoms in intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy, its effectiveness in preventing adverse perinatal outcomes appears limited. This observation further supports the hypothesis that mechanisms beyond symptomatic bile acid reduction may play a role in determining neonatal outcomes. The placenta has been proposed as a critical interface mediating the effects of maternal cholestasis on the fetus, and placental dysfunction has been increasingly recognized as a potential contributor to adverse neonatal events in ICP [7, 8].

In recent years, inflammation-based indices derived from routine laboratory parameters have attracted growing interest as integrative markers reflecting both systemic inflammatory burden and nutritional status. Albumin-based inflammatory indices, in particular, have been investigated in various hepatic and systemic inflammatory conditions; however, their clinical relevance in pregnancies complicated by intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy remains unclear. Given their accessibility and low cost, these indices may represent potential adjunctive markers for risk stratification if shown to be associated with adverse perinatal outcomes.

Recent studies have also explored the role of complete blood count–derived and liver-related inflammatory indices in intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy. For example, Karagün et al. reported that indices such as the alanine aminotransferase-to-platelet ratio may have diagnostic value in mild ICP, highlighting the growing interest in accessible biomarkers in this population [9].

The primary objective of this study was to evaluate the association between albumin-based inflammatory indices and neonatal outcomes, particularly NICU admission, in pregnancies complicated by intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy. Secondary analyses included comparisons between ICP and control groups and evaluation according to disease severity.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

This retrospective comparative study was conducted at Goztepe Prof. Dr. Suleyman Yalcin City Hospital, a tertiary referral center. Pregnant women who delivered between January 2020 and December 2025 were screened for eligibility.

A total of 277 pregnant women were included in the study, comprising 90 women diagnosed with intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP) and 187 healthy pregnant women who served as the control group.

Diagnosis of intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy

The diagnosis of intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy was established by a perinatologist based on the presence of maternal pruritus without an accompanying rash and fasting serum total bile acid (TBA) levels ≥ 10 µmol/L, with or without elevation of liver transaminases.

Gestational age at diagnosis was recorded only for women in the ICP group

All patients diagnosed with ICP were managed according to a standardized institutional protocol. Ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) was initiated as the first-line medical treatment for all symptomatic patients to alleviate maternal pruritus and improve liver function. Antenatal corticosteroids (betamethasone) were administered for fetal lung maturity to patients with a high risk of delivery before 34 weeks of gestation. The timing of delivery was determined based on serum total bile acid (TBA) levels and fetal surveillance; patients with mild ICP (TBA < 40 µmol/L) were generally scheduled for delivery between 37 0/7 and 38 6/7 weeks, whereas those with severe ICP (TBA ≥ 40 µmol/L) or non-reassuring fetal status were considered for delivery starting from 36 0/7 weeks.

Control group

The control group consisted of healthy pregnant women who received routine antenatal care and delivered at the same institution during the study period. Inclusion criteria for the control group were singleton pregnancy, normal liver function tests, and absence of pruritus or clinical suspicion of cholestasis throughout pregnancy.

Women with pre-existing liver disease (including viral hepatitis, autoimmune hepatitis, cirrhosis, or cholelithiasis), hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, insulin-requiring gestational diabetes, fetal growth restriction, fetal structural anomalies, multiple gestations, or spontaneous preterm labor unrelated to intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy were excluded.

Serum total bile acid levels are not routinely measured in asymptomatic pregnant women with normal liver function tests at our institution; therefore, bile acid levels were not available for women in the control group. Disease severity stratification based on TBA was performed only within the ICP group.

Both study groups were derived from the same obstetric population and healthcare setting, which may reduce but not eliminate selection bias.

Data collection

Demographic, obstetric, and laboratory data were obtained retrospectively from the electronic medical record system. Collected variables included maternal age, body mass index, gravidity, parity, gestational age at diagnosis and delivery, complete blood count parameters, liver function tests, serum albumin, fibrinogen, and C-reactive protein levels.

Calculation of inflammatory indices

Albumin-based and composite inflammatory indices were calculated using routine laboratory parameters obtained during the week of delivery in both groups. Serum albumin was recorded in g/L and converted to g/dL (g/L ÷ 10) for the calculation of albumin-based ratios. Complete blood count parameters (neutrophil, lymphocyte, monocyte, and platelet counts) were expressed as ×10⁹/L, and C-reactive protein (CRP) was expressed in mg/L. The indices were calculated as follows: LAR = white blood cell count (×10⁹/L) / albumin (g/dL); NAR = neutrophil count (×10⁹/L) / albumin (g/dL); MAR = monocyte count (×10⁹/L) / albumin (g/dL); CAR = CRP (mg/L) / albumin (g/dL); NPAR = neutrophil percentage (%) / albumin (g/dL); SII = (neutrophil count × platelet count) / lymphocyte count; SII/albumin = SII / albumin (g/dL); SIRI = (neutrophil count × monocyte count) / lymphocyte count; SIRI/albumin = SIRI / albumin (g/dL). All laboratory measurements used for the calculation of inflammatory indices were obtained during the week of delivery in both the ICP and control groups, ensuring comparable gestational timing between groups. Therefore, no additional adjustment for gestational age at sampling was performed.

Perinatal and neonatal outcomes

Perinatal and neonatal outcomes included gestational age at delivery, preterm birth (< 37 weeks), birth weight, low birth weight (< 2500 g), 1-minute and 5-minute Apgar scores, umbilical cord pH, and neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) admission.

Ethical approval

This study was approved by the Non-Interventional Clinical Research Ethics Committee of Goztepe Prof. Dr. Suleyman Yalcin City Hospital (Approval No: 2025/0101, Date: 13 August 2025). Due to the retrospective design of the study, the requirement for informed consent was waived. All procedures were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, and patient data were anonymized prior to analysis.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

The normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Normally distributed variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation and compared using the independent samples t-test. Variables with a non-normal distribution were expressed as median (minimum–maximum) and compared using the Mann–Whitney U test.

Categorical variables were presented as numbers and percentages and compared using the Pearson chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed within the ICP group to evaluate the ability of inflammatory indices to discriminate neonatal outcomes, including NICU admission.

The area under the curve (AUC) with 95% confidence intervals was calculated, and optimal cut-off values were determined using the Youden index.

Multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed to evaluate factors associated with NICU admission. Results were presented as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals.

A p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Composite adverse perinatal outcome (CAPO) was defined as the presence of at least one of the following: preterm birth (<37 weeks), low birth weight (<2500 g), NICU admission, umbilical cord pH <7.2, or 5-minute Apgar score <7.

Results

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics

A total of 277 pregnant women were included in the study, consisting of 90 women with intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP) and 187 healthy controls. Maternal age and body mass index were similar between the ICP and control groups (p = 0.812 and p = 0.298, respectively). Gravidity and parity were significantly lower in the ICP group compared with controls (p = 0.006 and p < 0.001, respectively).

The mean gestational age at diagnosis among women with ICP was 31.3 ± 5.8 weeks. Gestational age at delivery was significantly lower in pregnancies complicated by ICP than in controls (36.4 ± 1.8 vs. 38.6 ± 1.3 weeks, p < 0.001). Neonatal birth weight was also significantly lower in the ICP group (2890.5 ± 591.1 g vs. 3220.3 ± 409.1 g, p = 0.001).

Both 1-minute and 5-minute Apgar scores were significantly lower among neonates born to mothers with ICP (p = 0.021 and p < 0.001, respectively). Umbilical cord pH values did not differ significantly between the groups (p = 0.611). Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics are summarized in Table 1.

Pregnancy and neonatal outcomes

Use of assisted reproductive technology was significantly more frequent in the ICP group compared with controls (7.8% vs. 1.1%, p = 0.006). Mode of delivery did not differ significantly between the groups (p = 0.595).

Preterm birth before 37 weeks’ gestation occurred significantly more often in pregnancies complicated by ICP than in controls (42.2% vs. 9.6%, p < 0.001). Similarly, the incidence of low birth weight (< 2500 g) was significantly higher in the ICP group (20.0% vs. 2.1%, p < 0.001).

Neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) admission was significantly more common among neonates born to mothers with ICP (28.9% vs. 10.7%, p < 0.001). Laboratory parameters and albumin-based inflammatory indices

Women with ICP had significantly lower hemoglobin levels and serum albumin concentrations compared with controls (p = 0.001 and p < 0.001, respectively). White blood cell count and neutrophil count were modestly but significantly lower in the ICP group (p = 0.029 and p = 0.049, respectively), while platelet counts were similar between groups (p = 0.371).

Liver enzyme levels, including aspartate aminotransferase, alanine aminotransferase, and gamma-glutamyl transferase, were significantly higher in the ICP group compared with controls (all p < 0.001). Alkaline phosphatase and C-reactive protein levels were comparable between groups (p = 0.072 and p = 0.582, respectively).

Association between inflammatory indices and perinatal and neonatal outcomes

In subgroup comparisons within the ICP group, none of the evaluated albumin-based indices were significantly associated with preterm birth < 37 weeks (p = 0.118, p = 0.245, and p = 0.432 for LAR, NAR, and MAR, respectively). Low birth weight (< 2500 g) was associated with higher NAR (p = 0.031), whereas LAR (p = 0.094) and MAR (p = 0.129) were not statistically significant. NICU admission was associated with higher LAR (p = 0.036), NAR (p = 0.032), and MAR (p = 0.017). No significant associations were observed for Apgar score at 5 min < 7 or umbilical cord pH < 7.2 (p = 0.459 and p = 0.356 for LAR, respectively). CAPO was significantly more frequent in the ICP group (51.1% vs. 21.4%, p < 0.001). These results are presented in Table 5. Additionally, when composite adverse perinatal outcome (CAPO) was evaluated within the ICP group, higher LAR values were observed in the CAPO-positive group compared with the CAPO-negative group (p = 0.034), while NAR and MAR did not show a significant association.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis for neonatal outcomes within the ICP group

To further evaluate the predictive performance of inflammatory indices for neonatal outcomes, ROC analysis was performed within the ICP group to evaluate the discriminatory ability of inflammatory indices for NICU admission. For NICU admission, leukocyte-to-albumin ratio (LAR) demonstrated modest discriminatory performance with an AUC of 0.67 (95% CI: 0.55–0.79, p = 0.010). At a cut-off value of 2.9, sensitivity and specificity were both approximately 65%. Monocyte-to-albumin ratio (MAR) also showed statistically significant but limited performance (AUC = 0.65, 95% CI: 0.52–0.78, p = 0.023), with similar sensitivity and specificity values.

Overall, these findings indicate that although certain indices reached statistical significance, their ability to discriminate NICU admission remains limited (Table 6). The ROC curves are presented in Fig. 1.

Due to the limited number of events, the number of variables included in the multivariable model was restricted to avoid overfitting. Discussion

In this retrospective comparative study, we explored whether albumin-based inflammatory indices are associated with perinatal and neonatal outcomes in pregnancies complicated by intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP). Our findings show that pregnancies affected by ICP had higher rates of adverse outcomes, particularly preterm birth, low birth weight, and NICU admission. Although most albumin-based inflammatory indices were not associated with disease severity or differed from controls, LAR was identified as a factor associated with NICU admission; however, this finding should be interpreted with caution given the limited discriminatory performance and the potential for residual confounding. In addition, composite adverse perinatal outcome (CAPO) was significantly more frequent in the ICP group; within the ICP cohort, higher LAR values were associated with CAPO-positive pregnancies (p = 0.034), whereas the association for MAR did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.093).These findings further support the association between inflammatory burden and adverse neonatal outcomes.

The relationship between ICP and adverse perinatal outcomes observed in our cohort is consistent with previous studies reporting increased risks of preterm delivery, impaired fetal well-being, and neonatal morbidity in pregnancies complicated by cholestasis. Evidence from large cohort studies and meta-analyses indicates that the risk of adverse outcomes rises with increasing bile acid concentrations, particularly above 40 µmol/L; however, unfavorable outcomes may still occur even at lower bile acid levels [8–12]. This variability suggests that bile acids alone may not fully capture the complexity of fetal risk in ICP.

While serum bile acids remain central to diagnosis and clinical decision-making, they do not fully explain the diversity of neonatal outcomes. In our study, albumin-based inflammatory indices were not associated with biochemical disease severity, supporting the idea that inflammatory pathways may represent a distinct dimension of risk. Variability among international clinical guidelines regarding diagnosis, monitoring, and timing of delivery further reflects the ongoing uncertainty in optimal risk stratification in ICP [13, 14].

Increasing evidence points toward placental involvement in the pathophysiology of ICP-related adverse outcomes. Placental phenotype alterations described in ICP suggest that placental dysfunction may contribute to fetal compromise independently of bile acid levels [15]. Experimental studies also indicate that bile acids may directly affect trophoblast function through mitochondrial dysfunction, apoptosis, and impaired autophagic processes, offering a potential mechanistic explanation for fetal vulnerability in ICP [16].

Abnormal liver enzymes are commonly observed in ICP and reflect hepatocellular and cholestatic involvement; however, their interpretation requires consideration of physiological pregnancy-related changes and clinical context [17]. In addition, systemic inflammation has been proposed as an important contributor to disease progression in hepatic disorders, supporting the potential relevance of inflammation-based biomarkers in ICP [18].

Among the evaluated indices, LAR was the only parameter independently associated with NICU admission. Although LAR remained independently associated with NICU admission, residual confounding or mediation by gestational age cannot be excluded, given the strong relationship between preterm birth and neonatal outcomes. Since preterm delivery was more frequent in the ICP group, this association may partly reflect indirect effects related to earlier gestational age rather than a direct inflammatory mechanism. Furthermore, albumin-based inflammatory indices did not differ between ICP and control groups and were not associated with disease severity, suggesting limited pathophysiological relevance. Therefore, this finding should be considered exploratory and requires validation in prospective studies.

Importantly, gestational age at delivery may play a central role in this association. Preterm birth is a well-established determinant of NICU admission, and the higher rate of preterm delivery in the ICP group raises the possibility that the observed relationship between LAR and NICU admission may be partially mediated by earlier gestational age rather than reflecting a direct causal inflammatory mechanism. Therefore, the inclusion of preterm birth in the multivariable model should be interpreted cautiously, as it may act as a mediator rather than a true independent predictor. However, the multivariable model included a limited number of covariates, and therefore the possibility of residual confounding cannot be excluded. Additional adjustment for relevant clinical variables may further clarify the independent contribution of LAR. Although NAR and MAR showed associations in univariable analyses, these relationships did not persist after adjustment, suggesting that LAR may better reflect the combined effect of inflammatory activation and reduced physiological reserve. Elevated leukocyte counts indicate inflammatory activation, while lower albumin levels reflect both inflammation and nutritional status. Together, these components may capture a more integrated biological signal related to placental dysfunction or altered maternal–fetal immune interactions, however, these mechanisms remain speculative and should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating.

Despite this association, the overall discriminatory performance of albumin-based indices remained limited. In our exploratory ROC analysis, both NPAR and CAR demonstrated poor discriminatory performance for predicting NICU admission, with NPAR showing particularly low accuracy (AUC = 0.55, p = 0.455), while CAR also failed to reach statistical significance (p = 0.065)These findings suggest that no single inflammatory marker is sufficient for clinical prediction in ICP. Instead, such indices may serve as complementary tools when interpreted alongside established clinical and biochemical parameters, including gestational age and bile acid levels. Previous studies investigating liver-related inflammatory indices such as APRI and De Ritis ratio have similarly reported inconsistent findings regarding their diagnostic and prognostic value in ICP. For example, Yılmaz EBS et al. evaluated APRI and De Ritis ratio in pregnancies complicated by ICP and reported variable diagnostic and prognostic performance [19]. Similarly, recent studies evaluating inflammation-based indices in ICP populations have reported limited predictive performance, supporting the findings of the present study.

ROC analyses further confirmed the limited discriminatory performance of these indices, indicating that their clinical utility as standalone predictive markers is restricted. These findings highlight that statistically significant associations do not necessarily translate into clinically meaningful predictive performance. However, ROC analyses were based on unadjusted values and should be interpreted with caution, as potential confounding factors such as gestational age may influence the observed performance. Therefore, these findings do not represent independent predictive accuracy.

Similarly, recent studies evaluating inflammation-based composite markers in ICP have reported heterogeneous findings, suggesting that these indices may reflect general systemic responses rather than disease-specific mechanisms. This is consistent with our results, which demonstrate limited clinical utility of albumin-based inflammatory indices in predicting adverse neonatal outcomes [20–23].

This study has several strengths, including the use of a well-defined control group from the same obstetric population, standardized timing of laboratory assessment, and comprehensive evaluation of multiple inflammatory indices in relation to clinically relevant neonatal outcomes.

However, certain limitations should be acknowledged. A significant difference in gestational age at delivery between the ICP and control groups represents a major limitation of this study. Because the groups were not matched for gestational age, this variable may have acted as an important confounding factor influencing key neonatal outcomes, including birth weight and NICU admission, as this variable is a major determinant of neonatal outcomes and may act as a confounding factor in group comparisons. Although LAR remained independently associated with NICU admission in multivariable analysis, the strong association between preterm birth and neonatal morbidity suggests that residual confounding or mediation by gestational age cannot be entirely excluded. Serum bile acid levels were not available for the control group, which reflects routine clinical practice in asymptomatic pregnancies. However, this introduces a potential risk of misclassification bias, as subclinical or undiagnosed intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy cannot be fully excluded. In addition, this limitation may affect the interpretability of comparisons between ICP and control groups, including analyses such as ROC-based discrimination. Therefore, findings involving comparisons between ICP and control groups should be interpreted with caution.

The retrospective and single-center design may limit generalizability. The retrospective design and the tertiary referral setting may have introduced selection bias, which could limit the generalizability of the findings. Inflammatory markers were assessed at a single time point, preventing evaluation of dynamic changes during pregnancy.

Since inflammatory indices did not differ between ICP and controls or correlate with disease severity, systemic inflammation may not be a primary driver of ICP pathophysiology. Thus, our findings regarding LAR should be considered exploratory and speculative, requiring further validation in prospective studies to account for the potential risk of type I error due to multiple comparisons. Therefore, statistically significant findings should be interpreted with caution and considered hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.

In summary, although albumin-based inflammatory indices demonstrated limited overall diagnostic and prognostic value in pregnancies complicated by ICP, LAR was associated with NICU admission; however, this finding should be interpreted with caution given the limited discriminatory performance and potential for residual confounding. This result should be considered exploratory and requires validation in larger prospective and multicenter studies. Multiple comparisons were performed without formal adjustment, which may increase the risk of type I error. Although clinical management was based on a standardized institutional protocol, in which treatments such as ursodeoxycholic acid and antenatal corticosteroids were administered when clinically indicated, detailed patient-level data regarding these interventions were not consistently available for analysis. Accordingly, treatment-related subgroup analyses were beyond the scope of this retrospective study. Additionally, serum bile acid levels were not available for the control group; therefore, subclinical or undiagnosed cholestasis cannot be completely excluded, which may have influenced the interpretation of ROC analyses.

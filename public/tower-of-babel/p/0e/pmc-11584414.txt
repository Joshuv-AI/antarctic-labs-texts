A groundbreaking electrocardiographic observation: “Isoelectric horizontal ST-segment with sharp ST-T angle” a novel sign for acute coronary syndrome
Authors: Maitri M Patel, Sumantkumar G Patel, Jigar K Patel, Dhruvkumar M Patel, Poojan J Prajapati, Harsh D Patel, Jayesh S Prajapati, Lalitkumar B Patel, Mukundkumar V Patel
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractConsecutive suspected acute coronary syndrome (ACS) cases were categorized into three groups: Group-1 (ST-depression), Group-2 (T-inversion), and Group-3 (remaining cases). Group-3 was subclassified into isoelectric horizontal ST-segment with sharp ST-T-angle (IHST) positive and IHST negative groups. They underwent serial high-sensitive Troponin-I testing. ACS incidence was 77.96 %, with 33.02 %, 15.96 %, and (15.04 %, 35.96 %) of patients in Groups 1, 2, and 3 (IHST positive, IHST negative), respectively. The presence of IHST exhibited higher sensitivity than T-inversion and greater specificity than ST-depression in detecting ACS. The presence of IHST sign additionally detected 15.04 % of ACS. The presence of IHST was significant among group-3 (p = 0.008) as well as the overall ACS cases (p = 0.048).Keywords: Electrocardiogram, Isoelectric horizontal ST segment with sharp ST-T angle, Acute coronary syndrome, Dr Patels' IHST ECG Sign

Introduction

The standard 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) is a mandatory investigation after clinical examination during chest pain triage.1,2 With the availability of high-sensitive Troponin-I (HSTr-I), its serial measurement with a raised value 99 percentile above the upper limit of the reference range in either a single sample or consistent increase in values over time confirms the diagnosis of ACS and classify them into STEMI and non-STEMI ACS.3, 4, 5 However, a significant number of NSTEMI patients exhibit normal ECGs. Horizontal ST segments with less than 1 mm of depression are described as subtle signs of subendocardial ischemia or normal variants.6, 7, 8 In normal ECG, the ST segment is never truly horizontal but exhibits a slight deviation of less than 1 mm and ends with a T wave as a blunt angle.8 We authors(M V Patel,D M Patel, M M Patel, L B Patel, J K Patel) frequently observe isoelectric horizontal ST segments with sharp ST-T angles (IHST) in ECGs of ACS patients (Fig. 1) and coined this sign as Dr Patels' IHST ECG sign. The present study aims to evaluate the diagnostic value of the Dr Patels' IHST ECG sign in patients with acute coronary syndrome.

Methods

This prospective observational study was conducted from May 2020 to March 2022. All the consecutive patients of age above 21 years with chest pain suggestive of acute coronary syndrome were enrolled in the study after written consent. ECG and serum HSTr-I were ordered at the time of admission and repeated after 3 h.

The patients were classified according to ECG findings into three groups. Group 1 and group 2 comprised ST depression of ≥1 mm and T wave inversion of ≥1 mm in at least two contiguous anatomical leads respectively, while group 3 comprised of rest of the patients with apparently normal ECG. Subsequently, all cases were categorized as confirmed ACS (raised HSTr-I) and non-ACS chest pain (normal range HSTr-I). Serum HSTr-I level above 99 percentile of the upper normal laboratory reference range was considered as raised HSTr-I. The ADVIA Centaur high-sensitive Troponin-I kit was employed in conjunction with the SIEMENS ADVIA Centaur CP immunoassay system for the quantification of serum HSTr-I. Group-3 cases were further sub-classified into “IHST positive” and “IHST negative” according to the presence or absence of the sign in at least two contiguous anatomical leads respectively. Cases having STEMI or secondary ST-T ECG changes were excluded from the study.

Epi Info™ Web Survey Version 1.6 software was used to analyze the data. Student t-test with paired p-value was used for comparison of continuous variables and Chi-square test for categorical differences between the groups. Logistic regression was performed to assess whether the presence of IHST sign added diagnostic information to clinical parameters to predict ACS.

Results

Out of 1120 suspected ACS cases, 421 were excluded during initial screening (354 STEMI, 25 LBBB, 15 RBBB, 16 LVH, and 11 RVH). Patients enrolled in this study were predominantly males (63.66 %) with a mean age of 48.2 ± 12.5 years. Diabetes mellitus was the most common cardiovascular risk factor followed by dyslipidemia, hypertension, smoking/tobacco chewing, family history of coronary artery disease, and prior MI in descending order in this study. The incidence of ACS was 77.96 % (545/699). The frequency of ACS was 180 (33.02 %) in group 1, 87 (15.96 %) in group 2, 82 (15.04 %) in group 3 (IHST positive), and 196 (35.96 %) in group 3 (IHST negative).

The presence of IHST signs exhibited higher sensitivity than T inversion, while it was less sensitive than isolated ST-segment depression for ACS detection. Additionally, the new sign exhibits higher specificity than ST depression but lower specificity than T inversion. Notably, the IHST sign shows significant positivity within Group-3, with 22.52 % (82/364) in suspected ACS cases and 29.46 % (82/278) in confirmed ACS cases (p-value = 0.008). Furthermore, it identifies an additional 11.73 % (82/699) in suspected cases and 15.04 % (82 out of 545) in confirmed ACS cases (p-value = 0.048). Discussion

To date, the authors have noted a lack of exploration regarding the significance of IHST in detecting ACS within existing literature. Despite its subtle manifestation, the presence of IHST can be readily discerned during ECG interpretation by vigilant attending physicians.6,7 In our research, the presence of IHST sign revealed a notable detection of ACS cases, even among patients with seemingly normal ECGs but suspected of ACS. HSTr-I, a cardiac enzyme marker indicative of cellular necrosis renowned for its exceptional sensitivity, proves valuable in precisely diagnosing ACS during chest pain triage.3,9,10

The ST segment represents the plateau (phase two) of the action potential (AP). It is plausible to theorise that any modification of AP may result in an ST segment deviation.8 Normal ST-segment is isoelectric due to resting ventricle in repolarization. ST-segment depression represents a “current of injury” from the depolarized subendocardial myocardium that generates electrical currents that travel toward the recording electrodes.8 It is hypothesized that when the ventricular ischemia is uneven, transmural in some part, and subendocardial in some portion, it might result in horizontal ST-segment and sharp ST-T angle due to two opposite electric currents, one towards and the other opposite to the surface electrodes.8 This is the proposed electrophysiology of the IHST sign of myocardial ischemia.

Holubkov et al also revealed that using at least two ECG signs of myocardial ischemia in any set of contiguous leads causes remarkably higher odds of significant angiographic CAD than those without concomitant ECG abnormalities.11 Similar to the previous studies, our study showed low sensitivity and partial low specificity of individual ECG signs of ST-segment depression or T-wave inversion.12,13 But when these signs were considered together sensitivity increased significantly and specificity remained unchanged for predicting ACS. In the current study, the sensitivity of ECG for ACS diagnosis ranged between 15.96 % and 48.99 % and its specificity ranged from 55.84 % to 88.39 %, which matches previous studies.14,12,13

However, it's important to acknowledge the limitations of our study, including the small sample size and the utilization of the HSTr-I test, which although highly sensitive, carries a minimal risk of false positive results that could impact our findings. The strong part of our study is the presence of an IHST sign in significant number of probable and confirmed ACS cases.

Conclusion

The presence of Dr Patels' ECG Sign (IHST) is an unexplored subtle ECG sign for the diagnosis of acute coronary syndrome. Our study demonstrated its significance as an added ECG sign of myocardial ischemia.

Three filters for the enhancement of the images acquired from fluorescence microscope and weak-light-sources and the image compression
Authors: Man Jia, Jingmei Xu, Ruoxi Yang, Zongan Li, Ling Zhang, Ye Wu
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractFluorescence images enhancement is important as it can provide more information for medical diagnosis. In this work, we design three simple yet useful filters based on the combinations of mathematical functions, which are proved to be effective in strengthening the images acquired from the fluorescence microscope. Using these filters, detailed objects can be found in the dark sections of the fluorescence images. In addition, these filters can be used to enhance the low-light image, which provide satisfactory visual information and marginal profile for the blurred objects in the image. Moreover, these filters have been used to enhance the image with high degradation by the Gaussian noise, where clear edge profile can be extracted. Finally, we have shown that these filters can be utilized for the image compression. Compression ratio can be obtained to be 0.9688. This study shows the making of the filters with dual functions for the image enhancement and the image compression. Our designed filters are showing the potentials in the field of biomedical imaging and pattern identification.Keywords: Images enhancement, Low-light images, Fluorescence microscope, Image compression, Adaptive filters, Image contrast, Mathematical functions

Introduction

Fluorescence imaging has been significantly used in both clinics and research [[1], [2], [3], [4]]. The general difficulty is found in low-quality imaging of the bio-tissues due to the existing of biofluid or chemicals, which can substantially scatter or absorb the fluorescence. As a result, the images acquired are generally losing the details or key objects. In order to overcome this issue, the technique of image enhancement is used. The approach of the image enhancement can restore a compromised image to or close to its original visual quality while preserving its features. An image with high quality can be used in machine analysis and human perception. This in return can result in saving the image analysis time as well as the medical expenses.

One type of technique for getting effective image enhancement would be using histogram equalization [[5], [6], [7], [8], [9], [10]]. The histogram equalization approach can easily lead to losing details and presenting unnatural impression. In order to solve this problem, many researchers have actively come up with the approach of modified histogram equalization. H. Rahaman et al. used a neoteric tripartite histogram equalization to enhance low contrast image. They considered the image is composed by three sub-images whose histograms are calculated. Moreover, they used the snipping procedure in order to constrain the contrast enhancement. Finally, they used the independent operation of the equalization of the three sub-images and composed them into a single image [5]. D. Vijayalakshmi et al. use variational histogram equalization to overcome the issue of over-amplification and artifacts generated from the histogram spikes in the uniform background images [6]. O. O. Sule et al. used a two-stage histogram equalization for feature preservation in retinal fundus images [7]. B. S. Rao proposed dynamic histogram equalization approach based on improved particle swarm optimization [8]. I.S. Isa. et al. used adaptive histogram equalization to obtain enhanced MR images [9]. A. Paul et al. proposed fuzzy dissimilarity histogram equalization for infrared images enhancement [10].

Another important approach for the image enhancement is employing Retinex theory [[11], [12], [13], [14], [15], [16], [17], [18], [19]]. The Retinex theory was first proposed by Land and Mccann to simulate the perception of light and color in the human visual system, where the human visual system can restore reflectance and illumination from an image [[11], [12], [13]]. It is considered that the image can be divided into two layers: an illumination layer and a reflectance layer. The enhanced signals can be generated by the adaptive correction of the illumination layer. Since then, various frameworks based on the Retinex theory have been developed by many groups. In the following, we will discuss several representative works. R. Cai et al. proposed a retinex model inspired by the visual cortex, which can be used to extract contour and texture gradient in a robust manner [13]. X. Zhou et al. proposed method based on Retinex theory and modified pulse coupled neural network for processing the infrared images [14]. They introduced an illumination item to increase the attention for low-illumination areas, obtaining more details to the image. Y. Wang et al. established a novel global attention based Retinex network for the enhancement of the low-light images [15]. In their framework, the rich features are more conducive to learn the mapping relationship between low-light images to normal-light images. This leads to the detail recovery of dark regions in the images. Z. Shen et al. used pseudo-Retinex decomposition for underwater image enhancement. In their work, a relationship was established between the underwater imaging model and the Retinex model. With this method, they could use terrestrial images to replace the underwater images for training. The pseudo-illumination and pseudo-reflection maps were estimated via self-supervision. The pseudo -reflection image and pseudo-illumination image were reconstructed and thus the enhanced image was obtained [16]. X. Zhang et al. established a robust structure and a texture aware model for the low-light image enhancement. It would decompose a single image into the illumination and reflectance [17]. It was solved by a special alternating minimization, resulting in closed-form global optimal solutions. S. Du et al. used a dual-constrained Retinex decomposition model in order to solve illumination and reflectance [18]. This would remove the noise caused during imaging. Y. Wang et al. combined attention mechanism and the Retinex theory in order to obtain the enhancement of the low-light images [19]. They developed an attention map in order to evaluate the degree of the image and guide enhancement in a region-adaptive manner. This overcomes the issue of over-enhancing and leads to the enhancement of the underexposed regions. Q. Tang et al. combined the Retinex theory and Taylor series expansion for nighttime image dehazing [20]. They decomposed the atmospheric light image from the input image using the Retinex theory. Taylor series expansion was used to estimate the pointwise transmission map. Using image fusion and color transfer, the atmospheric light image and dehazing image were processed to acquire the final haze-free image.

Another representative technique of the image enhancement is constructing filters based on the fuzzy logic theory. In these frameworks, the low-quality image is considered a system with vagueness. A single value of each pixel divided by the length of the gray scale is used as the input of the fuzzy system. The fuzzy IF-THEN rules are used for calculating the fuzzy output. The output will be defuzzified to obtain a reasonable value. This fuzzy set theory has been widely utilized for enhancing the image contrast [[21], [22], [23], [24], [25]].

All these efforts help to resolve the limitation of over-enhancement, noise amplification and color imbalance. It can be noted that all these frameworks are more or less establishing a filter based on mathematical functions. This is inspiring. A further increase in seeking the image enhancement techniques can be noted by the using of the mathematical functions. We are trying to establish a simple and flexible filter associated with the mathematical functions. In this filter, the parameters can be set empirically and the users can optimize the values in order to make it useful for different images. We try to establish a robust framework that can generate enhanced signals.

With the proper selection of the mathematical functions, we consider that a filter that can be built up to keep the image details and suppress the blur of the image. Moreover, the noise contained in the image will not be amplified.

The development of multifunctional filters is our eternal research goal. Acquiring the image compression has been pursued by many research groups [[26], [27], [28], [29]]. The technique of the image compression can help to reduce the large volume of the image for fast transmission [[26], [27], [28], [29]]. We aim to develop new filters good for the image enhancement and the image compression.

Herein, we designed three filters for the image enhancement and compression based on the mathematical functions, including hyperbolic secant function, sine function, logarithmic function, hyperbolic cosecant function, inverse hyperbolic function, hyperbolic sine function and hyperbolic secant function. We used these filters to process images acquired from fluorescence microscope and the weak-light condition. We also used these filters to compress images.

The remainder of this article is arranged as follows. Section 2 is about the building of the three filters which are called as the sech-sin filter, the log-acosh-sinh filter and the cosh-sec-asinh-cos filter, irrespectively. The acquired images using our filters are illustrated and discussed in Section 3. The limitation of these filters is discussed in section 4. Finally, we summarize our main findings.

Method

Our algorithms are composed by two frameworks. In the first framework, we will use a wavelet lifting transform [30] to process the image. This wavelet lifting transform is composed by several steps, including column/row transform, signal splitting, signal prediction, and signal update. In the step of signal splitting, the original signal was split into two sets of sub-signal. In the step of the signal prediction, some data redundancy was eliminated. In the step of signal update, certain data correction was performed. By doing the wavelet lifting transform, we intend to keep the key data-profile of the signal and dump the noise or unwanted signal. In the second framework, a set of special functions are combined to create a strong-filtering effect.

Processing images degraded by the Gaussian noise

Gaussian noise is common in practical applications. It can be generated along with the condition of low illustration or low exposure when using a camera to capture the images. The technique of the image enhancement may help to recover the original profiles of the images. Limitation of the designed filters

There is room for improvement of our filters. First, our filters can keep some of the noise or amplify the noise. This hinders the clear presentation of the features in the images. Robust filters that can totally remove the noise is still needed to be developed. Second, the images can be covered by the combination of different kinds of the noise. The enhancement of those noisy or degraded images is challenging, which may be our future study. Third, our proposed frameworks can be used for the image compression, however, the image contrast is decreased after compression. Therefore, our filters have to combine with advanced techniques including those based on the Retinex theory or the fuzzy control theory to get enhanced contrast when performing the image compression. Fourth, our filters generate gray images, which may not provide vivid and direct information comparing to the colored images. Moreover, the users may have to learn programming in order to use our filters. In our future study, our frameworks may be packed in a graphical user interface which can provide quick access for the users.

Finally, it should be noted that significant work has been done in the field of the low-light image enhancement when deep learning is involved [[32], [33], [34], [35], [36], [37]]. These advanced techniques rely on a training system to get remarkable success in the enhancement of various real-world images. They can increase pleasing brightness as well as suppress the artifact/noise in a considerable balance. They are envisioned to play an important role on the applications of the effect of enhancement on high-level vision tasks like object detection and classification, which would be our future endeavor.

Conclusions

We have illustrated that three filters based on the simple functions including hyperbolic secant function, sine function, logarithmic function, inverse hyperbolic cosine function, hyperbolic sine function, hyperbolic cosine function, secant function, inverse hyperbolic sine function, and cosine function, can be used for the image enhancement. They are effective for enhancing those images obtained from the fluorescence microscope and the weak-light condition. The proposed approach recovers the detailed feature hiding in the image even when the image is in poor illumination. Another application of our filters is shown in the useful extraction of the contour of the images contaminated by the Gaussian noise. Moreover, we demonstrate that our filters can be used for compressing the image. The compression ratio can be achieved to be the value of 0.9688. This study has shown that our designed filters have the dual functions for the image enhancement and the image compression. Our future work will explore how to process and generate the colored images through our filters and how to control and adjust the output color and light based on the user input. We will study how to suppress the noise and bring the considerable brightness simultaneously.

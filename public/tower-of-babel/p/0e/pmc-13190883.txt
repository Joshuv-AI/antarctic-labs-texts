Crosstalk between circRNAs and the MAPK signaling pathway in cancer progression
Authors: Mohammad Zahedi, Ehsan Maghrebi-Ghojogh, Fatemeh Ahangari, Maryam Zamani Sani, Alireza Mafi, Kiavash Hushmandi, Seyed Ali Olianasab, Salman Daneshi, Mina Alimohammadi, Seyedeh Mahdieh Khoshnazar, Najma Farahani, Mehrdad Hashemi, Afshin Taheriazam
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractNoncoding RNAs (ncRNAs), including long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs), circular RNAs (circRNAs), and small ncRNAs, play critical roles in regulating biological processes and disease development. Among these, circRNAs, characterized by their single-stranded, covalently closed structures, act as protein templates, microRNA sponges, and transcriptional regulators, exhibiting high stability and distinct expression profiles. CircRNAs play an important role in several diseases, including autoimmune disorders, cardiovascular diseases, neurological conditions, and cancers. In cancer, they influence key mechanisms like cell cycle development, angiogenesis, apoptosis, proliferation, motility, and metastasis. Meanwhile, the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathways—especially the Ras/Raf/MEK/ERK cascade—are central to controlling cell survival, proliferation, and tumor development, with hyperactivation of ERK linked to cancer progression. Despite the important roles of both circRNAs and MAPK pathways in oncogenesis, the mechanisms underlying their interplay remain poorly understood. This review aims to fill this knowledge gap by examining the bidirectional interactions between circRNAs and MAPK signaling in cancer and explores therapeutic implications, focusing on strategies such as circRNA modulation (targeting their functions as transcriptional regulators or microRNA sponges) and MAPK pathway inhibition to control cancer progression.Keywords: Cancer progression, CircRNAs, MAPK signaling pathway, diagnostic biomarkers, Therapeutics targets

Introduction

Noncoding RNAs (ncRNAs) represent a substantial portion of the transcriptome and play key biological roles in several cellular processes. Long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs), circular RNAs (circRNAs), and small ncRNAs are among the increasing number of unique ncRNAs discovered thanks to recent advancements in sequencing techniques and data analysis [1]. Single-stranded, covalently closed RNA molecules known as circRNAs are found in many species. CircRNAs perform biological functions as protein templates and microRNA (miR) sponges. Additionally, intronic circRNAs (ciRNAs) and exon–intron circRNAs (ElciRNAs) act as transcriptional regulators [2–4]. CircRNAs exhibit a longer half-life and greater resistance to RNase R compared to linear RNAs [5], which makes them suitable candidates for both therapeutic targets and diagnostic biomarkers. Several studies have shown their unique expression signatures and critical biological functions in a range of illnesses, including autoimmune disease [6], cardiovascular disease [7], neurological disorders [8], and cancer [9–11].

CircRNAs are well acknowledged to play key roles in cell cycle progression, angiogenesis, and apoptosis, as well as in cell proliferation, motility, and metastasis [4]. CircRNAs may be crucial modulators of long-term proliferative signaling and the development of cancer [12]. Circ-FOXO3 is one of the examples. By forming a ternary complex with CDK2 and p21, the tumor suppressor circ-Foxo3 prevents the cancer cell cycle from progressing [13]. Another example of strong carcinogenic activity is the interaction between circ-Amotl1 and c-myc. Circ-Amotl1 can up-regulate c-myc targets, enhance c-myc stability, and improve the retention of nuclear c-myc [14]. One well-known example of a circRNA that may be translated into a protein is Circ-ZNF609 [15]. As an oncogene, circ-ZNF609 overexpression results in enhanced tumor development, whereas knockdown results in the opposite effects in mouse xenograft models [16].

Mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) cascades are important signaling pathways that control several cellular functions, such as stress responses, apoptosis, differentiation, and proliferation. The tumor cell survival depends heavily on the Ras/Raf/MAPK (MEK)/ERK pathway, the most significant signaling cascade among the MAPK signal transduction pathways. The formation and progression of cancer are significantly influenced by the hyperactivation of ERKs [17].

Even though circRNAs and the MAPK signaling pathway play critical roles in cancer, their interplay remains one of the least studied areas. Despite significant advances in understanding the individual roles of circRNAs and MAPK signaling in cancer progression, the mechanisms by which they interact and contribute collectively to tumorigenesis are still poorly understood. This gap highlights the need to study how they interact, which could lead to the discovery of new cancer therapies. Thus, in this study, we reviewed the bidirectional interaction between circRNAs and the MAPK pathway resulting in cancer progression. We intend to identify new ways to understand how circRNAs control MAPK signaling and how MAPK activity regulates circRNA functions. This will be achieved by developing new models and strategies.

MAPK signaling pathway: molecular activation and role in cancer

Mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) cascades mediate the transduction of extracellular stimuli to diverse cellular outcomes such as proliferation, differentiation, inflammation, and apoptosis. There are three major families of MAPKs in mammalian systems, namely, ERK, JNK, and p38, acting in three-tiered kinase modules (MAPKKK → MAPKK → MAPK).

Figure 1 outlines mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) cascades—ERK, JNK, and p38. Extracellular stimuli such as growth factors, mitogens, stress, and inflammatory cytokines activate upstream receptors (RTKs, GPCRs, integrins). This activation triggers small GTPases (RAS, RAP), followed by MAP3Ks, MAP2Ks, and MAPKs. These sequential steps result in outputs like cytokine production, apoptosis, cell migration, proliferation, and differentiation. The figure illustrates how diverse stimuli converge on MAPK cascades to regulate cellular functions involved in stress and inflammation responses [18, 19].

An overview of the MAPK signaling pathways and upstream stimuli

Protein kinases downstream of cell surface receptors and/or interactions with a small GTPase are the primary routes by which MAPKKK is activated [20]. First, MAPKKK activates MAPKK by directly phosphorylating it. Then, the activated MAPKK phosphorylates a conserved tripeptide TxY motif at the activation site of MAPK, thereby activating MAPK. When the MAPK is activated, it phosphorylates a variety of substrates in the nucleus and cytoplasm, altering gene expression and protein function to elicit the appropriate biological response [21].

Members of the ERK (extracellular signal-regulated kinase) family (e.g., ERK1/2 and ERK5) are characterized by a conserved TEY (threonine–glutamate–tyrosine) motif located within their activation site, which is essential for their phosphorylation and functional activation. ERK1 and ERK2 predominantly consist of a catalytic kinase domain, whereas ERK5 contains a significantly longer sequence extending from the carboxy-terminal region beyond the kinase domain, suggesting additional regulatory or functional capacities [22–24].

The ERK1/2 signaling module is important in the cellular response to growth factors and mitogens, primarily mediating processes related to cell proliferation and differentiation. This pathway is activated at the plasma membrane by many cell surface receptors, including receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs), G-protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs), and integrins. These receptors, upon ligand binding, stimulate downstream signaling through small GTPases, notably RAS and RAP, which serve as molecular switches to relay signals to the MAPK cascade [21]. Once activated, RAS and RAP engage upstream kinases in the MAPK cascade, beginning with MAPKKKs such as members of the RAF family, as well as MOS and Tpl2. These kinases phosphorylate and activate the MAPKKs, specifically MEK1 and MEK2, in the case of the ERK1/2 pathway. MEK1/2, in turn, phosphorylates ERK1/2 on both threonine and tyrosine residues within the TEY motif, resulting in their full activation. Activated ERK1/2 then translocates into the nucleus to regulate transcription factors and downstream gene expression programs that are essential for mitogenic and differentiative responses [21].

Among the most crucial downstream signaling pathways of MAPKs is the JNK pathway, which is primarily regulated by oxidative stress, UV radiation, inflammatory cytokines (e.g., TNF-α, IL-1β), and DNA damage. A conserved TPY motif in the activation segment of these three JNKs distinguishes them from other members of this family. Upstream kinases such as MEKK1/4, MLK2/3, ASK1, and TAK1 phosphorylate and activate MKK4 and MKK7, which further phosphorylate JNK. Activated JNK translocates to the nucleus, where it phosphorylates central transcription factors c-Jun, ATF2, and Elk-1, thereby regulating the genes that are accountable for apoptosis, inflammation, and cellular stress response [25].

In cancer, JNK signaling is abnormally activated, causing tumor growth and cancer formation by promoting apoptosis resistance and an inflammatory tumor microenvironment. Rho family GTPases Cdc42 and Rac are also involved in the activation of JNK. The GTPases are molecular switches that exist in an inactive GDP-bound state as well as an active GTP-bound state, and add additional regulatory complexity to the JNK signaling pathway [21, 26].

One of the strongest downstream effectors of JNK signaling is the AP-1 (Activator Protein-1) transcription factor complex. The complex, consisting of Jun and Fos family proteins, is not only activated in part through c-Jun and family members being phosphorylated by JNK but also mediates gene expression that affects cell growth, differentiation, and apoptosis, and thus cellular response to inflammation and stress [27].

During recent times, several negative regulatory components of the JNK pathway have been identified. Some examples include proteins such as the dual-specificity phosphatase MKP7 that dephosphorylates JNK and certain isoforms of p38 selectively, which suppress JNK signal transduction. Heat shock protein Hsp72 also suppresses JNK activity and therefore controls stress-induced apoptosis. Evi1 oncoprotein also suppresses JNK activation and therefore suppresses stress-induced cell death. These negative regulators serve to counteract activation of the pathway and contribute to the overall tight regulation of JNK signaling [28, 29].

Despite significant advances, there remains much to be elucidated regarding the particular regulation and scaffolding of the JNK pathway. Areas needing research priority are the identification of putative scaffold proteins—CrkII, filamin, β-arrestin-2, JIP2, and JIP3—and upstream MAP3Ks MLK family members, ASK2, MEKK2, MEKK4, and TAK1. Redundancy and context-dependent activation of these proteins reflect an elaborate, well-regulated network of signaling [21, 26].

The p38 MAPK pathway represents another large branch of the MAPK signaling pathway. It is primarily activated by environmental and physiological stress stimuli such as ultraviolet (UV) radiation, heat shock, osmotic shock, oxidative stress, and pro-inflammatory cytokines like TNF-α and IL-1β. p38 is phosphorylated and activated by upstream kinases MKK3 and MKK6, which are phosphorylated by MAP3Ks such as ASK1, TAK1, MLK2/3, MEKK1-4, and TAO1/2 [30, 31]. The four isoforms of the p38 family are p38α (MAPK14), p38β (MAPK11), p38γ (MAPK12), and p38δ (MAPK13), with distinct expression patterns and substrate preferences. Several downstream targets of phosphorylated p38 include protein kinases MK2/3, MSK1/2, and PRAK, as well as transcription factors ATF2, Elk-1, and p53, which play important roles in regulating inflammation, differentiation, cell cycle arrest, and apoptosis [21].

Among the p38 MAPK isoforms, p38α is the most abundant and widely studied [32]. p38β is downregulated and often shows overlapping functions with p38α. In contrast, p38γ and p38δ display more restricted expression patterns and appear to serve specialized roles [33, 34]. Specifically, p38β is enriched in the brain, p38γ in skeletal muscle, and p38δ in kidneys, pancreas, small intestine, and testis [35, 36]. Several studies suggest that p38α and p38β cooperate in diverse processes, including sex determination, cardiac development, regulation of mitotic entry, and T-cell activation [37]. p38γ and p38δ have been implicated in tissue regeneration and immune responses [38]. However, there is no genetic evidence indicating that they substitute for p38α functions [39]. Interestingly, suppression of p38α can sometimes trigger compensatory activation of p38γ and/or p38δ [40]. Overall, the distinct and overlapping contributions of the four isoforms remain to be fully clarified.

Functionally, the p38 MAPK pathway is an important regulator of cellular stress response and immune signaling. Acute p38 activation in normal physiology leads to cell survival and repair, but persistent or inappropriate activation leads to apoptosis, senescence, or tumor suppression. The function of p38, however, in cancer cells also becomes context-dependent, shifting from tumor-suppressive to tumor-promoting depending on tissue type, microenvironment, and signal intensity [41]. For example, chronic activation of p38α has been implicated in chemoresistance, invasion, and angiogenesis in breast and colorectal cancers, while reduced p38 activity in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and melanoma is implicated in uncontrolled cell growth and metastasis. The p38 signaling pathway regulates transcriptional programs and governs cell fate under stress conditions through its interactions with other MAPK branches, such as ERK and JNK. In brief, the double and highly context-dependent functions of p38 MAPK emphasize its complexity in cancer biology. Dissection of the molecular pathways that determine whether p38 acts as a tumor suppressor or tumor promoter can unveil novel therapeutic opportunities, particularly with the targeting of stress [42, 43].

As previously stated, the dysregulation of the MAPK signaling pathway due to genetic and epigenetic modifications contributes to the development of several human cancers. The primary cause of MAPK signaling hyperactivity and continuous signal transduction which eventually results in tumor progression is mutations in several MAPK signaling pathway components, including upstream membrane receptors such as EGFR, signal transducers such as RAS, and downstream kinases such as BRAF [44].

Increased expression of ERK has been found in a several human tumors, including lung, breast, colon, and ovarian cancer. By activating transcription factors to increase VEGF transcription, ERK/MAPK signaling pathways can raise VEGF expression in tumor cells and encourage the development of blood vessels [17]. Moreover, benign cystadenomas and normal ovarian surface epithelium had higher levels of MAPK phosphatase-1 (MKP-1) expression than invasive carcinomas, low malignancy potential tumors, and borderline tumors [45]. Moreover, over 40% of cancer cases are caused by overactivation of the conventional MAPK pathway (Ras/RAF/MEK/ERK (MAPK) pathway) [46, 47].

Mutations in upstream regulators such as KRAS, BRAF (in particular the V600E mutation), and EGFR are some of the key drivers of constitutive activation of the MAPK pathway in human cancers. The KRAS mutations (e.g., G12D, G13D, Q61H) lock RAS in an active GTP-bound conformation, resulting in continual stimulation of RAF kinases and persistent phosphorylation of ERK. The BRAF V600E mutation mimics activation loop phosphorylation, leading to constitutive MEK/ERK signaling regardless of upstream stimulation. Similarly, EGFR activating mutations (e.g., exon 19 deletions and L858R substitutions) induce downstream RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK activation. These oncogenic mutations not only drive unchecked growth but also serve as therapeutic targets—e.g., BRAF inhibitors (vemurafenib, dabrafenib) and MEK inhibitors (trametinib, cobimetinib) have shown clinical activity in tumors with MAPK hyperactivation. However, adaptive resistance generally develops by feedback reactivation of ERK signaling or compensatory activation of parallel pathways, highlighting the requirement for combination therapeutic strategies against several MAPK nodes [48]. As a result, members of the RAS kinase subfamily are being examined as potential therapeutic targets in the MAPK pathway. One gene family that frequently undergoes mutations in cancer is RAS. KRAS mutations are the most common and are frequently found in colorectal and pancreatic ductal adenocarcinomas. Human cancers are closely linked to BRAF and RAS mutations that dysregulate MAPK signaling [49]. The interaction between RAF and RAS is a key event in the activation of MAPK pathways. Active, GTP-bound RAS brings RAF to the plasma membrane, promoting its dimerization and conformational activation. However, RAS binding is not sufficient for total activation of RAF, as other cofactors and post-translational processes (e.g., phosphorylation and release of 14-3-3 protein) are required to lock the kinase’s active conformation. Mutations in RAS or RAF disrupting this finely tuned regulation cause constitutive MAPK signaling, which drives oncogenesis and drug resistance in a variety of cancers.

CircRNAs : a brief insight into biogenesis and functions

For circRNA biogeneis exon-intron boundary and 3’ UTR are essential cis-elements [50]. The production of circRNAs primarily occurs through a non-canonical splicing event known as back-splicing mechanisms: [1] lariat-driven circularization (exon skipping) [2], intron pairing-driven circularization, and [3] RNA binding protein (RBP)-driven circularization [51]. Other unique groups of circRNAs, including intergenic circRNAs, rt-circRNAs, and f-circRNAs can also be generated by alternative processes and are commonly linked to clinical disorders [52]. CircRNAs are classified into three classes based on their composition and cycling mechanisms: circular intronic RNAs (ciRNAs) [3], exon-intron circRNAs (ElciRNAs) [2], and exonic circRNAs (ecircRNAs) [53, 54].

Numerous circRNAs perform significant biological roles by modulating protein activity, functioning as microRNA or protein inhibitors (also known as “sponges”), or being translated themselves [55]. A major mechanism through which circRNAs exert their function is by acting as competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) which means sponging microRNAs (miRNAs) to regulate gene expression [56]. For instance, CDR1as, one of the most well-known circRNAs, functions as a miR-7 sponge by containing almost 70 preferentially conserved miR-7 target sites [57, 58]. CDR1as increases the expression levels of miR-7-targeting proto-oncogenes (EGFR, CCNE1, and PIK3CD) by acting as a miR-7 sponge. In other words, the tumor-inhibitory effects of miR-7 are blocked by overexpression of CDR1as [59]. Furthermore, glioma tissues and cell lines have high expression of hsa_circ_0046701, which is able to stimulate cell invasion and proliferation by controlling ITGB8 expression through sponging miR-142-3p [60]. Similarly, circGFRA1 acts as a ceRNA in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) by sponging miR-34a, thereby modulating the expression of GFRA1. This highlights its potential for TNBC diagnosis and treatment [61]. In a parallel fashion, circ-PDE8A serves as a ceRNA for miR-338 in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC), where it regulates MACC1 and drives tumor invasion via the MACC/MET/ERK or AKT signaling pathways. Therefore, exosomal circ-PDE8A could serve as a valuable diagnostic or prognostic marker for PDAC [62].

Apart from functioning as miRNA sponges, circRNAs can also impact cancer cell behavior by interacting with proteins [56]. For instance, circ-Foxo3 interacts with p21 and CDK2 to form a ternary complex that blocks CDK2 activation and thereby inhibits the cell cycle [13]. In breast cancer, circ-Amotl1 is found at elevated levels and acts as an oncogene that accelerates cancer progression by binding to c-myc and increasing the expression of c-myc target genes. Indeed, the circ-Amotl1/c-myc complex boosts c-myc’s affinity for the promoter sites of genes like HIF-1γ, Cdc25a, ELK-1, and JUN [14].

Modulating transcription is another way that circRNAs can affect cancer cells’ activity. As an example, CircSMARCA5 binds to its genomic locus to form a circR loop, causing transcriptional pausing and generation of a truncated nonfunctional SMARCA5 protein. This reduces SMARCA5 expression and impairs DNA damage repair, suggesting that targeting circSMARCA5 could sensitize breast cancer cells to cytotoxic drugs [63].

Interestingly, several circRNAs can change cancer cell behavior by encoding peptides. For example, Liu et al. found that circ-EGFR can encode a polymeric novel protein complex, called rolling-translated EGFR (rtEGFR). It co-localizes with EGFR at the plasma membrane and prevents its endocytosis and degradation, thereby sustaining EGFR signaling and promoting glioblastoma progression [64]. In addition to the miRNA sponge, transcriptional regulation, interacting with RBPs and translational effects, circRNAs are also involved in alternative splicing [65].

The circRNA–MAPK axis: mechanistic interplay

CircRNAs primarily interact with the signaling pathway via different mechanisms, including [1] acting as miRNA sponges to release MAPK-related mRNAs from repression, [2] modulating upstream activators (such as RAS/RAF/MEK), [3] binding directly to signaling proteins to stabilize or scaffold MAPK components, and [4] participating in feedback loops that integrate MAPK signaling with circRNA expression. CircRNAs mainly regulate the MAPK signaling pathway by using the competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) mechanism. This type of regulation is predominantly achieved through the sponging of microRNAs that target MAPK pathway proteins expression and activity. When a circRNA binds to a miRNA, it prevents the miRNA from binding to its usual target mRNAs . For example, recently, it was shown that circ-MAPK4 promotes gliomas by sponging miR-125a-3p, which confirms this circRNA’s role in affecting the downstream signaling of the MAPK pathway [66]. Another example is circ-CEP128, which is a ceRNA for miR-145-5p and decreases its availability. This can affect the MAPK signaling pathway as the inhibition from the miR-145-5p is not present, and can cause the tumor progression related to the MAPK activation [67, 68]. The detailed interactions between circRNAs and MAPK signaling will be discussed in the next section.

CircRNAs regulating MAPK signaling in cancers

Several cellular processes are regulated by the MAPK signaling pathway, including cell growth, proliferation, differentiation, migration, and apoptosis. Many cancers are thought to develop and progress because of dysregulation of the MAPK pathway [69]. The circRNA pathway is also dysregulated in many cancers [70]. It has been suggested that circRNAs can serve either as oncogenes or tumor suppressors, depending on their influence on MAPK signaling pathways (Fig. 2). A study has found that circMAPK1 could target and suppress of MAPK signaling, inhibiting gastric cancer progression and invasion. However, the MAPK pathway-related circRNAs remain under-researched. Oncogenic circRNAs

Circ_0006528

The PRELID2 gene gives rise to the circular RNA hsa_circ_0006528, also known as circPRELID2, which may contribute to the development of drug resistance in cancer cells. According to previous expression profiling studies, circ_0006528 was among the most significantly upregulated circRNAs in adriamycin-resistant breast cancer cells compared to drug-sensitive tissues. Given its overexpression, it may play a role in the mechanisms that enable cancer cells to withstand adriamycin therapy. However, its precise function and potential as a therapeutic target require further investigation [71, 85, 86].

Gao et al. demonstrated that circ_0006528 is significantly upregulated in breast cancer tissues compared to adjacent normal tissues and is associated with advanced disease stages and poor patient prognosis. Mechanistically, circ_0006528 functions as a molecular “sponge” for miR-7-5p, a microRNA known for its tumor-suppressive effects. By binding to miR-7-5p, circ_0006528 prevents it from inhibiting its target gene, Raf1. Activated Raf1 phosphorylates MEK1/2 on two serine residues, which converts MEK into its active kinase form. MEK phosphorylates ERK1/2 on Thr202/Tyr204 (ERK1) or Thr185/Tyr187 (ERK2). This dual phosphorylation fully activates ERK, which can then translocate to the nucleus, phosphorylate transcription factors (such as ELK1, c-Fos, c-Myc), and regulate cytosolic substrates controlling proliferation, differentiation, or survival. Through this circ_0006528/miR-7-5p/Raf1 axis, cells experience enhanced MAPK/ERK signaling. This activation promotes breast cancer cell growth, invasion, and metastasis. Functional studies showed that circ_0006528 enhances DNA synthesis and tumor cell aggressiveness, while its downregulation induces G2 phase cell cycle arrest and increases cell death in breast cancer cells. These findings suggest that circ_0006528 plays a crucial role in breast cancer progression by modulating the miR-7-5p/Raf1/MAPK/ERK axis, making it a potential biomarker and therapeutic target in breast cancer management [71]. The study further reveals the molecular mechanism underlying the oncogenic role of circ_0006528, particularly its function as a ceRNA or molecular sponge for miR-7-5p. By sponging miR-7-5p, circ_0006528 prevents miR-7-5p from suppressing its target gene, Raf1, thereby activating the MAPK/ERK signaling pathway. This activation leads to the development, progression, and migration of breast cancer cells [71].

Circ-CEP128

Among the growing list of identified circular RNAs (circRNAs), circ-CEP128 has emerged as a significant player, particularly in the context of cancer and cellular signaling. Several studies have highlighted the involvement of circ-CEP128 in various cancers, often acting as an oncogene, contributing to tumor development and progression. Circ-CEP128 is overexpressed in bladder cancer tissues and blood samples, with its expression levels higher in advanced tumor stages and cases with lymph node metastasis. Functionally, circ-CEP128 acts as a “sponge” for miR-145-5p, directly binding to this microRNA and reducing its availability. MiR-145-5p targets and negatively regulates MYD88, a protein overexpressed in bladder cancer and linked to the MAPK signaling pathway [67, 68]. The overexpression of circ-CEP128 lowers miR-145-5p and enhances MYD88. Once upregulated, MYD88 recruits IRAK kinases (IRAK1/4), which phosphorylate and activate TRAF6. TRAF6 ubiquitination leads to recruitment and activation of downstream kinases, including TAK1, which can phosphorylate MAPKKs (MKKs). TAK1 phosphorylates MKKs, such as MKK4/7 (for JNK) or MEK1/2 (for ERK). MKKs then phosphorylate MAPKs (ERK, JNK, or p38) on conserved Thr/Tyr residues in their activation loops. Activated MAPKs translocate to the nucleus and phosphorylate transcription factors like AP-1 (c-Fos/c-Jun), NF-κB, and others. Therefore, this pathway facilitates the tumor progression.

Silencing circ-CEP128 increases miR-145-5p levels, leading to downregulation of MYD88 and suppression of MAPK signaling. This results in inhibited cell viability and mobility, induction of G0/G1 cell cycle arrest, and increased apoptosis. Functional assays, including wound healing and migration tests, demonstrated that circ-CEP128 silencing significantly impairs bladder cancer cell migration through the miR-145-5p/MYD88/MAPK axis. These findings suggest that targeting circ-CEP128 could serve as a promising therapeutic strategy to inhibit bladder cancer progression by modulating this signaling pathway [67].

Circ_0074027

Gao et al. demonstrated that circ_0074027 is upregulated in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and contributes to tumor progression by promoting cell growth, migration, and metastasis while inhibiting apoptosis. Mechanistically, circ_0074027 functions as a molecular sponge for miR-185-3p, reducing its availability and thereby increasing the expression of downstream oncogenes BRD4 and MADD. MADD functions as an adaptor protein linking extrinsic apoptotic signals (via TNF receptor family) to MAPK pathway activation. Overexpression of MADD can enhance ERK phosphorylation, resulting in boosted cell proliferation and reduced apoptosis. Mechanistically, MADD interacts with Ras or Raf complexes and facilitates the phosphorylation cascades along the MAPK axis. In vitro experiments showed that overexpression of circ_0074027 enhanced proliferation and invasive behaviors in NSCLC cell lines, whereas silencing circ_0074027 suppressed these malignant traits. These findings suggest that circ_0074027 plays a crucial oncogenic role in NSCLC through the miR-185-3p/BRD4/MADD pathway and may serve as a promising therapeutic target [87].

Hsa_circ_0002476

In cancer cells, hsa_circ_0002476 increases proliferation, migration, and invasion, which suggests it contributes to tumor growth and metastasis. Several studies indicate that hsa_circ_0002476 may significantly enhance the malignant progression of NSCLC. On the other hand, hsa_circ_0002476 inhibits apoptosis, which leads to cancer cell survival. It is believed that this anti-apoptotic effect contributes to tumor progression and resistance to therapy. Through miRNA sponging, hsa_circ_0002476 modulates the expression of target genes, thereby influencing various signaling pathways, such as the MAPK pathway. Despite the lack of direct links between the MAPK pathway and circRNAs, the modulation of microRNAs by circRNAs significantly impacts many cellular pathways, including the MAPK pathway. Therefore, by the action of hsa_circ_0002476 working as a micro RNA sponge, it does indeed affect the MAPK pathway [88].

Hsa_circ_0000190

Hsa_circ_0000190, derived from the ZKSCAN1 gene, is significantly upregulated in NSCLC tissues and cell lines. It facilitates tumor progression by reinforcing the EGFR-MAPK/ERK cascade, which is critical for tumor cell proliferation, survival, and migration. EGFR activation enhances C190 expression via a MAPK/ERK-dependent mechanism, and conversely, its expression is reduced by MEK1/2 inhibitors or EGFR knockdown. Overexpression of C190 upregulates ERK1/2 phosphorylation, therefore establishing a self-reinforcing positive feedback loop to maintain oncogenic signaling regardless of EGFR mutation status. CRISPR/Cas13a-mediated knockdown of C190 reduces ERK1/2 phosphorylation and tumor cell proliferation and migration both in vitro and in vivo [72].

Moreover, C190 contributes to immune evasion by facilitating the upregulation of soluble PD-L1 and promotes cell cycle progression by upregulating cyclin-dependent kinases, further enhancing the aggressiveness of NSCLC [89]. Taken together, all these findings position C190 as a representative paradigm for the circRNA-mediated reinforcement of MAPK, in which circRNAs amplify the MAPK cascade through feedback-transcriptional loops and sustain malignant phenotypes [90].

Circ-MAPK4

Circular RNA MAPK4 (circ-MAPK4), originating from exon 2 of the MAPK4 gene, is markedly upregulated in glioma tissues compared to normal brain tissue. Its circular structure confers resistance to RNase R digestion and leads to predominant cytoplasmic localization, characteristic of many functional circRNAs. It is important to note that circ-MAPK4 is distinct from the MAPK family and MAPK1 (ERK2) or the canonical ERK1/2 pathway. Circ-MAPK4 promotes glioma progression by acting as a ceRNA that sponges miR-125a-3p, a microRNA known for its tumor-suppressive role through inhibition of the p38/MAPK signaling pathway. By sequestering miR-125a-3p, circ-MAPK4 prevents the microRNA from suppressing p38/MAPK phosphorylation, thereby inhibiting apoptosis and enhancing glioma cell proliferation, migration, and survival. Experimental silencing of circ-MAPK4 results in increased apoptosis, reduced cell proliferation, and decreased tumor growth both in vitro and in vivo, underscoring its critical role in glioma pathophysiology. These findings position circ-MAPK4 as a promising therapeutic target for this aggressive brain tumor [66].

CircRNA NRIP1

CircRNA NRIP1 (Nuclear Receptor-Interacting Protein 1) is highly expressed and tumorigenic in renal carcinomas, esophageal squamous cell cancers, and notably papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC). Studies show its overexpression correlates with advanced tumor stages and increased malignancy in PTC. CircRNA NRIP1 promotes PTC progression by sponging tumor-suppressor miR-195-5p, preventing its protective effects and enabling cancer cell growth and spread. Additionally, CircRNA NRIP1 activates key carcinogenic pathways, including MAPK. Experimental evidence from cell cultures and mouse models confirms its role in promoting tumor development, highlighting its potential as a diagnostic biomarker and therapeutic target in PTC [73].

Hsa_circ_0005909

Circular RNA hsa_circ_0005909 derived from the XPR1 gene through a process known as back-splicing. It is a closed-loop RNA molecule, meaning it lacks free 5’ and 3’ ends, which makes it more stable than linear RNAs and resistant to exonuclease degradation. Circ_0005909 is primarily located in the cytoplasm of cells, where it functions as a molecular “sponge” for microRNAs, regulating gene expression at the post-transcriptional level [91].

Hsa_circ_0005909 has been identified as a key regulator in the progression of osteosarcoma (OS) through multiple mechanisms. One study revealed that circ_0005909 promotes OS progression by functioning as a molecular sponge for miR-936, which in turn regulates the expression of High Mobility Group Box 1 (HMGB1). This interaction enhances OS cell viability, colony formation, migration, invasion, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), ultimately contributing to tumor growth and metastasis. Inhibition of circ_0005909 was shown to suppress these malignant behaviors both in vitro and in vivo, indicating its potential as a therapeutic target for OS treatment [91].

A study revealed an oncogenic role of circ_0005909 in osteosarcoma (OS) via the miR-338-3p/HMGA1 axis. Circ_0005909 was found to be upregulated in OS tissues and cell lines, acting as a molecular sponge for miR-338-3p. Knockdown of circ_0005909 reduced cell viability and colony formation. HMGA1 was identified as a direct target of miR-338-3p; its protein levels decreased following circ_0005909 knockdown, but this effect was reversed by inhibiting miR-338-3p. Additionally, circ_0005909 knockdown suppressed the phosphorylation of ERK, indicating its function in activating MAPK-ERK pathway, which is overactivated in OS cells. This inhibition was reversed by HMGA1 overexpression, suggesting that circ_0005909 promotes these signaling pathways through HMGA1 [76].

Hsa_circ_0006117

In pancreatic cancer (PC) tissues, Hsa_circ_0006117 expression was significantly increased, according to Liu et al. The gene maps to protein tyrosine phosphatase receptor type A (PTPRA). This study’s key finding suggests that hsa_circ_0006117 plays a crucial role in PC development and progression. This result may be of great importance for oncology as it may enable the development of novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets for PC. They made this inference by conducting an integrated analysis of differentially expressed circRNAs in PC, according to data from two Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets. Due to its circular structure and its high resistance to RNase R degradation, hsa_circ_0006117 displays excellent structural stability, enabling its preferential cytoplasmic localization, where it exerts its oncogenic activity. To promote PC development, hsa_circ_0006117 functions as a ceRNA to compete with miR-96-5p. The binding traps miR-96-5p, reducing its inhibitory effect on KRAS, a key oncogene in PC [78]. As a result, the expression of KRAS is elevated, which results in the activation of the KRAS/MAPK pathway [92–94]. This signaling pathway drives tumor cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. Experimental downregulation of hsa_circ_0006117 using siRNA significantly reduced the malignancy of pancreatic cancer cells. This was confirmed by decreased proliferation (CCK-8 and colony formation assays) and diminished invasiveness and migration (wound healing and transwell assays). Knockdown of hsa_circ_0006117 also reduced KRAS mRNA and protein levels, and suppressed MAPK pathway activity, as evidenced by reduced phosphorylation of MEK1/2 and ERK1/2. Overexpression of KRAS partially restored malignant phenotypes and MAPK pathway activity in hsa_circ_0006117-silenced pancreatic cancer cells. Therefore, hsa_circ_0006117 is considered a promising therapeutic target and biomarker for pancreatic cancer due to its critical role in pathogenesis via the KRAS/MAPK signaling pathway [78].

Hsa_circRNA_101705

The TXNDC11 gene on chromosome 16 produces the circular RNA hsa_circRNA_101705 (circTXNDC11), which is highly stable due to its closed-loop structure and predominantly localized in the cytoplasm. CircTXNDC11 is overexpressed in renal cell carcinoma (RCC) tissues and cell lines, correlating with advanced TNM stages and lymph node metastasis. It promotes RCC progression by activating the MAPK/ERK signaling pathway through upregulation of MEK and ERK phosphorylation, enhancing cancer cell proliferation, invasion, and tumor growth. Knockdown of circTXNDC11 suppresses these oncogenic behaviors both in vitro and in vivo, and its effects are reversed by the ERK inhibitor SCH772984. These findings position circTXNDC11 as a promising biomarker and therapeutic target in RCC [79].

Hsa_circ_0003204

Circular RNA hsa_circ_0003204 is produced by reverse splicing, where the downstream splice acceptor joins the upstream splice donor to form a closed loop. Its circularity has been confirmed using PCR and Sanger sequencing. This circular RNA has been identified as a key regulator of the progression of cervical cancer (CC). It is significantly upregulated in human CC tissues and cell lines compared with surrounding normal tissues. The hsa-circ-0003204 molecule enhances cell growth, migration, and metastasis while simultaneously suppressing apoptosis. Conversely, knockdown of hsa_circ_0003204 inhibited cell growth, reduced migration and invasion, and increased apoptosis in vitro. Western blot analysis showed that overexpression of the active substance increased phosphorylation of key signaling elements of MAPK, including ERK and p38, while knockdown resulted in reduced phosphorylation. According to in-vivo xenograft studies, mice implanted with CC cells expressing hsa_circ_0003204 had noticeably larger tumors in terms of both volume and weight. According to these results, hsa_circ_0003204 may be a useful biomarker and therapeutic target for the management of cervical cancer [80].

CircRNA UBAP2

Circular RNA UBAP2 (circUBAP2), encoding ubiquitin-associated protein 2, is a critical regulator in various cancers and acts as a ceRNA. CircUBAP2 can sponge tumor-suppressor miRNAs to upregulate oncogenes, thereby promoting tumor progression, metastasis, and drug resistance. In prostate cancer (PCa), circUBAP2 enhances tumor growth via the miR-1244–MAP3K2–MAPK pathway. Due to its widespread upregulation and central role in oncogenic pathways, circUBAP2 is a promising biomarker and therapeutic target across multiple cancer types, underpinning ongoing research efforts to develop novel cancer treatments [81].

CircRAD23B

CircRAD23B (hsa_circ_0087862) is derived from the RAD23B gene at chr9:110068659–110,081,160 and is significantly upregulated in multiple cancer types, including NSCLC [82], ovarian cancer [95], and esophageal cancer [96]. Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) confirmed that its expression is elevated in tumor tissues compared to normal tissues across these malignancies.

In NSCLC, CircRAD23B is overexpressed in cancerous lung tissues and cell lines. Its upregulation correlates with lymph node invasion, lower differentiation grade, and shorter overall survival. The study revealed that CircRAD23B, functioning as a substrate for the competing ceRNA sponging miR-142-3p, leads to increased MAP4K3, a key regulator of the MAPK signaling pathway. This finding underscores the functional significance of CircRAD23B in cancer progression. Western blot analysis confirmed that the knockout of CircRAD23B reduces the phosphorylation of ERK1, JNK, and p38, resulting in the inactivation of the MAPK pathway. This inactivation highlights the potential therapeutic effect of targeting CircRAD23B. Functional tests, including CCK-8 viability, colony formation, and transwell invasion assays, showed that knockdown of CircRAD23B inhibits the proliferation and motility of tumor cells. In vivo, xenograft models have also confirmed that inhibition of CircRAD23B slows tumor growth [82].

Hsa_circ_0002124

According to the report, NUSAP1 is a target of miR-122 involved in cell cycle processes associated with HCC progression [97]. Significant increases in NUSAP1 expression were also observed in HCC cells and tissues, and increased NUSAP1 levels correlated with shorter survival in HCC samples [98]. The study by Ribbeck et al. They found that NuSAP1, a protein involved in stabilizing microtubules and in spindle assembly [99], was associated with the progression of cancer. In a study by Fang et al., it was found that hsa_circ_0002124 was significantly upregulated compared to normal liver cells in HCC tissues and cell lines, particularly in HepG2, Huh7, 97 L, and 97 H cells. The results of functional assays demonstrated that overexpression of hsa_circ_0002124 significantly enhances tumor growth, while knockdown induces apoptosis and arrests the cell cycle at the G1 phase. The study confirmed the oncogenic role of hsa_circ_0002124 in hepatic cancer by increasing uncontrolled cell proliferation and resistance to apoptosis [83]. Hsa_circ_0002124 increases HCC progression by acting as sponges for key miRNAs regulating oncogenic proteins, thereby activating the MAPK signaling pathway. Its overexpression increases MAPK activity and c-Myc expression, while knockdown reduces them. Functional assays showed that hsa_circ_0002124 significantly influences cancer cell viability, proliferation, apoptosis, and cell cycle progression. Bioinformatics analyses confirmed its involvement in cancer drug response pathways, supporting its potential as a biomarker for diagnosis of early HCC and a target for novel therapies [83].

CircFAT1

CircFAT1 is a stable circRNA derived from exon 2 of the FAT1 gene, which is involved in cell adhesion, migration, and polarity. Due to its circular structure, circFAT1 is resistant to degradation and functions differently depending on cancer type, acting either as a tumor suppressor or oncogene. In NSCLC, circFAT1 is overexpressed and linked to poor prognosis. It promotes immune evasion by increasing regulatory T cell (CD4⁺ and Foxp3⁺) infiltration while suppressing cytotoxic CD8⁺ T cells. Mechanistically, circFAT1 sponges miR-30e-5p to upregulate USP22, enhancing tumor proliferation and metastasis. In cervical cancer, circFAT1 drives progression by interacting with miR-409-3p, leading to increased CDK8 expression and activation of ERK1/2 and p38 MAPK pathways. Silencing circFAT1 reduces cell proliferation, invasion, and tumor growth both in vitro and in vivo. Overall, circFAT1 plays complex, context-dependent roles in cancer biology and represents a promising target for future research and therapeutic interventions [84, 100–103].

Tumor suppressor circRNAs

CircRNA 0001313

CircRNA 0001313 (circ-0001313) is a key regulator in several cancers, including colon cancer and NSCLC (Fig. 3). It mainly functions as a ceRNA for miRNA sponging to modulate gene expression and signaling pathways. In colon cancer, circ-0001313 is highly expressed and promotes tumor growth by sponging miR-510-5p, which upregulates AKT2 and activates the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway. This pathway enhances cell proliferation, survival, and resistance to apoptosis. In NSCLC, circ-0001313 drives tumor progression via the miR-452/HMGB3/ERK/MAPK axis. By binding miR-452, it increases HMGB3 expression, activating the ERK/MAPK pathway and promoting cancer cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. Overexpression of circ-0001313 increases ERK1/2 and p38 MAPK phosphorylation, confirming its oncogenic role. Overall, circ-0001313 is a promising biomarker and therapeutic target. Targeting it or its downstream pathways could provide new cancer treatment strategies [77].

Hsa_circRNA_0000567

CircSETD3 (hsa_circ_0000567) is a circular RNA derived from exons 2–6 of the SETD3 gene on chromosome 14q32.2. Its circular structure confers stability and resistance to RNase R digestion. CircSETD3 acts mainly as a molecular sponge for miRNAs such as miR-421 and miR-641, regulating targets like BMF and PTEN to influence cell proliferation, apoptosis, and tumor growth. It is downregulated in tumors such as HCC, cholangiocarcinoma, and bladder cancer, where its low expression correlates with poor prognosis, indicating a tumor suppressor role. CircSETD3 notably modulates the MAPK signaling pathway through the circSETD3/miR-421/MAPK14 (p38 MAPK) axis in HCC. By binding miR-421, circSETD3 prevents suppression of MAPK14, leading to increased MAPK14 expression. This upregulation reduces proliferation markers (Cyclin D1, PCNA) and inhibits tumor growth and progression. Functional assays, including dual-luciferase reporter tests and rescue experiments, support this mechanism. In vivo xenograft models showed that circSETD3 overexpression results in smaller tumors, higher MAPK14 levels, decreased proliferation, and reduced EMT marker expression (N-cadherin, vimentin), indicating suppressed metastasis. Overall, circSETD3 functions as a critical tumor suppressor regulating cancer cell development via MAPK14 and represents a promising therapeutic target in HCC and other cancers with dysregulated MAPK signaling [74].

CircSLC8A1

circSLC8A1, a circular RNA with a covalently closed-loop structure produced by back-splicing of the SLC8A1 gene, has been the focus of numerous investigations in cancer, particularly in prostate cancer [75]. Notably, circSLC8A1 has been found to suppress bladder cancer development via modulating PTEN, as demonstrated by Lu et al. [104]. This promising role of circSLC8A1 in inhibiting bladder cancer progression highlights its therapeutic potential. CircSLC8A1 expression is closely associated with the clinical and histological stages of bladder cancer, with reduced expression observed in bladder tumor tissues and cell lines. CircSLC8A1 upregulation has been shown to suppress bladder cancer cell migration, invasion, and proliferation [104]. Wang et al. discovered that circSLC8A1 acts as a miRNA sponge, directly interacting with miR-21 and modulating its function [75]. This regulatory role of circSLC8A1 is a key aspect of its potential as a tumor suppressor. Importantly, the circSLC8A1/miR-21 interaction has been linked to cancer-promoting pathways such as the chemokine signaling pathway and the MpoAPK signaling pathway, as well as to Gene Ontology (GO) terms related to cell migration and proliferation regulation. These findings underscore the potential of circSLC8A1 to function as a tumor suppressor by modulating these pathways and thereby inhibiting PCa cell growth and progression [75].

Clinical relevance and translational potential

CircRNAs have found at relatively high levels in body fluids such as saliva, plasma, serum, and exosomes [4, 105], making them as promising candidates for noninvasive liquid biopsy biomarkers in cancer. Circ-MAPK4 is markedly upregulated in glioma tissues and correlates with advanced disease and poor prognosis. By sponging miR-125a-3p, it activates p38/MAPK signaling and enhanced glioma cell proliferation and survival [66]. Its detection in tumor tissue and plasma suggests diagnostic and prognostic value for glioma. Circ_0006528 (circPRELID2) is highly expressed in breast cancer and drives tumor aggressiveness through the circ_0006528/miR-7-5p/Raf1 axis, which results in sustained MEK–ERK activation. Elevated circ_0006528 levels correlate with advanced stage and poor survival, which supports its role as a liquid-biopsy biomarker. Circ-CEP128 is overexpressed in bladder cancer tissues and blood samples, where it promotes tumor progression via the miR-145-5p/MYD88/MAPK axis. High circ-CEP128 expression correlates with advanced stage and lymph-node metastasis. This suggests both diagnostic and prognostic value. Hsa_circ_0006117, upregulated in pancreatic cancer, activates the KRAS/MAPK pathway by sponging miR-96-5p, which normally represses KRAS. Its strong association with tumor invasiveness and progression supports its utility as a noninvasive biomarker. Additional MAPK-related circRNAs, including hsa_circ_0005909 in osteosarcoma, hsa_circRNA_101705 (circTXNDC11) in renal carcinoma, and circRAD23B in NSCLC, ovarian, and esophageal cancer, also exhibit diagnostic and prognostic potential by modulating MAPK-ERK or MAP4K signaling cascades.

Beyond diagnostics, targeting circRNAs that modulate MAPK signaling presents new therapeutic opportunities. Silencing circ-MAPK4 in glioma cells suppresses MAPK/ERK activation and leads to decreased proliferation and increased apoptosis. Therefore, this circRNA may act as a therapeutic target in MAPK-driven tumors. Knockdown of circ_0006528 has been reported to inhibit ERK phosphorylation and reduce invasion and migration of cancer cells by further underscoring the potential of circRNA-directed therapy. Similarly, circ-CEP128 interference attenuates MAPK pathway activation which suggests its regulatory role could be exploited for therapy approaches. Emerging technologies, including antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs), RNA interference (RNAi), and synthetic circRNA mimics, are being investigated to modulate circRNA expression. Such approaches could enable selective targeting of circRNAs that sustain MAPK signaling. These approaches offer a novel therapeutic avenue for cancers that are characterized by MAPK pathway hyperactivation.

Conclusions and future perspectives

In conclusion, the cross-modulation between circRNAs and the MAPK signaling pathway profoundly influences cancer progression, tumor cell growth, invasion, and therapeutic resistance. CircRNAs such as circ-MAPK4, circ_0006528, circ-CEP128, and circ_0006117 regulate MAPK signaling by miRNA sponging or regulation of upstream mediators like Raf1, MYD88, and KRAS. These interactions finally affect MAPK components including ERK, JNK, and p38, and this mechanism is common among different cancers. Conversely, tumor-suppressive circRNAs such as circSETD3 reinforce this regulatory axis by restoring MAPK14 (p38) function and inhibiting malignant progression.

Importantly, circRNAs may not only regulate but also be regulated by MAPK-driven feedback mechanisms which creates complex bidirectional interactions that remain underexplored. For instance, hsa_circ_0000190 forms a positive feedback loop with the EGFR–MAPK–ERK pathway in NSCLC, in which MAPK activation increases circRNA expression and, in turn, circRNA upregulation further enhances MAPK activity. Circ_0006528, by promoting the miR-7-5p/Raf1/MAPK/ERK axis in breast cancer, and circ-CEP128, which activates MAPK signaling through the miR-145-5p/MYD88 pathway in bladder cancer, may be part of feedback systems that sustain continuous pathway activation. In glioma, circ-MAPK4 regulates the miR-125a-3p/p38-MAPK pathway, while in pancreatic cancer, hsa_circ_0006117 enhances KRAS/MAPK signaling, which suggest that circRNAs can act both upstream and downstream of MAPK cascades. Likewise, hsa_circ_0005909 in osteosarcoma and circUBAP2 in prostate cancer activate ERK and MAP3K2, respectively. On the other hand, tumor-suppressive circRNAs such as circSETD3, which restores MAPK14 (p38) activity, illustrate the opposite regulatory direction. Together, these examples reveal the complex two-way communication between circRNAs and MAPK components, where circRNAs can both regulate and be regulated by MAPK signaling. Understanding these feedback and feed-forward loops will be essential for explaining how MAPK-related circRNAs maintain tumor growth, invasion, and therapeutic resistance, and for designing precise interventions targeting this regulatory axis. In this regard, RNA-based inhibition approaches, such as siRNA, antisense oligonucleotides, and CRISPR-guided depletion, offer various possibilities to interfere with MAPK hyperactivation by targeting oncogenic circRNAs or restoring tumor-suppressive ones.

Despite these advances, several limitations remain. First, in vitro studies continue to dominate the literature on circRNA–MAPK interactions, with few studies exploring in vivo validation. The specificity of delivery, the safety of long-term use, and the efficiency of delivery for circRNA-targeted therapies, along with several other factors, remain poorly understood. Second, while it is clear that MAPK activation affects circRNA abundance, the upstream regulatory mechanisms that drive the biogenesis of circRNAs under oncogenic MAPK signaling are poorly defined. Third, RNA-based therapies still face significant hurdles to achieve effective and tumor-specific delivery due to off-target activity, immune activation, and intracellular stability. The focus of future research should be on comprehensive in vivo profiling, characterization of upstream MAPK-dependent splicing and circularization events, and developing targeted delivery systems, including lipid nanoparticles or exosome-based vectors. Elucidation of these aspects will be key in translating circRNA-directed interventions into clinically actionable therapies. Overall, understanding circRNAs as integral amplifiers of the MAPK signaling cascade gives a common framework for biomarker discovery and therapeutic innovation in cancer.

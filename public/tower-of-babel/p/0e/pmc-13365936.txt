IFPHOR0087 Providing Public Health Input into Planning Application Decision Making: Implementation and Impact of the Bury Hot Food Take Away Matrix
Authors: Lee Buggie, Joanna Goldthorpe, Dawn McDermott, Laura Spencer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionBury has one of the highest densities of hot food takeaways in the UK, increasing from 127 to 165.7 per 100,000 residents between 2020 and 2026. This growth aligns with local patterns of childhood overweight and obesity, particularly in deprived communities where up to 44% of children are affected. Bury Council therefore developed and implemented the “Hot Food Takeaway Matrix” to inform planning decisions by integrating granular population health intelligence.MethodsUsing postcode linked Lower Super Output Area (LSOA) indicators, a simple dashboard was created using MS Excel and Power BI to draw on local data incorporating deprivation levels, existing takeaway density, school proximity (within 400 metres), and locations where clustering would reduce high street diversity and potentially be detrimental to community wellbeing.FindingsObservations of planning decision-making show that data from the matrix is easily accessed and understood. The matrix supports Bury Council’s Hot Food Takeaway Supplementary Planning Document by providing reproducible, transparent criteria that strengthens planning decisions , reduces the likelihood of successful appeals and demonstrates the likely effects of proposed takeaway developments in exacerbating existing inequalities and obesogenic environments.DiscussionThe matrix is now embedded within Bury’s wider public health and strategic planning system, supporting appeal-proof decision-making and contributing to Bury’s 2030 vision for healthier communities. This case study demonstrates how local authorities can operationalise public health evidence within the planning system to proactively shape healthier food environments and reduce health inequalities.

Two Novel Mutations in the BCKDHB Gene Cause Intermediate Maple Syrup Urine Disease
Authors: Hui Zhu, Yi Zhong, Shuyao Zhu
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

Maple syrup urine disease (MSUD; OMIM# 248600), a sporadic and autosomal recessive genetic heterogeneous disorder, is characterized by a functional deficiency of the branched-chain α-ketoacid dehydrogenase (BCKDH) complex caused by biallelic heterozygous pathogenic variants of the BCKDHA, BCKDHB, or DBT gene.[12] Its key clinical characteristics are ketoacidosis, motor delay (MD), intelligence disability (ID), feeding difficulties, and the unique odor of maple syrup in urine.[12] Herein, we present a Chinese girl of Han nationality with an intermediate type of MSUD caused by a novel compound heterozygous pathogenic variant in the BCKDHB gene, which, to our knowledge, has not been described.

A girl aged 1 year and 3 months presented to the pediatric neurology outpatient department with MD, ID, and repeated unprovoked seizures. She was born naturally at full term, with a length of 49 cm, a head circumference of 33 cm, and a birth weight value in the normal range. She was born in good condition and was the second baby of nonconsanguineous healthy parents. The couple’s first child was a healthy boy. She could not raise her head at 5 months of age and could not sit alone at 1 year and 3 months of age. The first seizure occurred when she was about 7 months old. It manifested as generalized tonic–clonic seizures, which occurred once or twice in several days and each lasted from 30 s to 1 min. She was breastfed after birth without feeding difficulties. At 10 months of age, she stopped breastfeeding and started to be fed formula and complementary foods. But coincidentally, since then, she has had repeated vomiting and recurrent bronchopneumonia. Physical examination showed a slow reaction, no skeletal or facial deformities, low muscle tone and normal muscle strength in the extremities, normal neurophysiological reflexes (such as knee reflexes, ankle reflexes, myoclonus, etc.), and failure to elicit pathological signs (including Babinski’s sign, Brinski’s sign, Obenham’s sign, etc,). Plasma organic acid analysis showed 415.39 µmol/L leucine (normal <270 µmol/L) and 383.33 µmol/L valine (normal <300 µmol/L). Urine organic acid analysis indicated elevated levels of 2-ketoisocaproic acid, 2-keto-3-methylvaleric acid, and 2-hydroisovaleric acid. Electroencephalogram revealed sharp-slow waves in the left frontal, occipital, and meso-posterior temporal regions [Figure 1]. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the brain was normal. The Gesell Developmental Scale and Stanford-Binet Intelligence Scale scores of 60 and 63, respectively, were defined as mild ID. Based on the above clinical findings, she was diagnosed with intermediate MSUD. To further identify etiology, ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid blood samples of the girl and her parents were collected for genetic analysis by whole-exome sequencing. The specific methods described in previous studies were followed.[3] Single-nucleotide mutation sequencing results showed a compound heterozygous variation in the BCKDHB gene on chromosome 6, including a missense mutation (NM_000056.3, c.446T>C, p.Phe149Ser) and a nonsense mutation (NM_000056.3, c.673_675del, p.Leu226del). Sanger sequencing verified that the two variations were inherited from her unaffected father and mother, respectively [Figure 2]. Based on the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics guidelines,[4] the two variants were classifed as “Likely Pathogenic” with pathogenicity is very strong (PVS1) (loss-of-function [LoF] is the known mechanism of MSUD type Ib) + PM2 (absent from controls in Genome Aggregation Database, 1000 Genomes Project, or Exome Aggregation Consortium). Furthermore, copy number variation sequencing results revealed that the proband carried a deletion variation GRCh37/hg19 15q13.1-13.3 (chr15:29673929-31362454) × 1, spanning 1.688 Mb, and an amplification variation 17q25.1(chr17:71189172-71503756) × 3, spanning 314.585 Kb, which were de novo variations. The variants were classified as “variants of uncertain significance.” Genetically, she was diagnosed with MSUD type Ib. At the time she was diagnosed with MSUD, she had begun treatment with branched-chain amino acids (BCAA)-free protein diet, while the changes in her BCAA levels were monitored. Meanwhile, valproic acid was given as antiepileptic therapy. After 6 months, epilepsy was well controlled. After 1 year of follow-up, her plasma BCAA levels were in the normal range, motor development was close to that of normal children of the same age, and intelligence was improved, as well as growth and language were well developed. After 3 years, her neurological and intellectual functions were completely normal.

Clinically, MSUD has five different phenotypes: classic (the liver BCKDC activity in this type children is often lower than 2% in healthy children), intermediate (accounting for 20%), intermittent (accounting for 20%), thiamine-responsive (BCKDA activity is 2%-40%), and E3 deficient (the activity of BCKDA is 25% lower than that of normal children). MSUD type Ib (OMIM# 248611) is caused due to pathogenic variations in the BCKDHB gene.[5] Nevertheless, the clinical manifestation and phenotype of MSUD do not hinge upon the genotype, but rather hinge upon the activity of the BCKDH complex, leucine tolerance, and metabolic response to disease.[6] Intermediate MSUD is uncommon and has only been found to be caused by variations in the BCKDHA and DBT genes,[6] while those caused by variations in the BCKDHB gene have not been described. In this study, we present a girl with intermediate MSUD caused by pathogenic variations of the BCKDHB gene, clinically presenting with ID, MD, epilepsy, elevated plasma branched-chain amino acids (BBAAs) level, abnormal electroencephalogram, and normal brain MRI. Two new pathogenic variations in the BCKDHB gene, c.446T>C (p.Phe149Ser) and c.673_675del (p.Leu226del), were detected in our patient. Her genotype and phenotype corresponded to MSUD type Ib and intermediate type, respectively. She had a good prognosis without neurological sequelae after treatment. This is the first time it has been described in a case of BCKDHB-associated MSUD. MSUD of various phenotypes caused by BCKDHB gene mutation show brain MRI abnormalities.[17] Diffuse bilateral symmetric high-signal white matter, pale matter, and thalamus on cerebral MRI are the traits of MSUD.[8] Intracapsular MRI without high signal in the hind limb can be a clear distinction between classic and intermediate types.[5] Interestingly, our patient’s brain MRI was normal. The mechanism is unknown and needs further research.

In brief, we describe a Chinese Han girl with intermediate MSUD caused by two novel pathogenic variants in the BCKDHB gene, exhibiting some previously unreported clinical manifestations.

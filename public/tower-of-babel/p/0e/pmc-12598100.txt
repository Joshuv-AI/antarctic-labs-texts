Omega‐3 Long Chain Polyunsaturated Fatty Acid Supplementation for Prevention of Retinopathy of Prematurity in Pre‐Term Low Birth Weight Infants: A Pilot Study
Authors: Salma Afroz, Md Mahbubul Hoque
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and AimsRetinopathy of prematurity (ROP) is a vasoproliferative disorder of the retina affecting preterm infants that potentially leads to blindness. Omega‐3 long‐chain polyunsaturated fatty acids (LCPUFA) have anti‐inflammatory and antiangiogenic effects, thereby reducing pathological neovascularization and preventing the incidence of ROP. This study was done to evaluate the effect of available omega‐3 LCPUFA supplementation for the prevention of ROP among Bangladeshi infants.MethodsThis pilot study was conducted in the Neonatology Department of a tertiary care hospital in Bangladesh from November 2022 to March 2024 among preterm neonates < 34 weeks of gestation with birth weight < 1800 g. Initially, 76 preterm neonates were enrolled in this study. Among them, 71 preterm neonates were randomly allocated to the intervention group and the control group. The intervention group received a cap. omega‐3 LCPUFA enterally (40 mg/kg/day). It was added to their enteral feeds within 48 h of the first enteral feed and continued from the starting point to 4 weeks. The control group received no supplementation.ResultsThe incidence of ROP was almost similar between the omega‐3 LCPUFA group and the control group (37.1% in the intervention group vs. 41.6% in the control group, p = 0.69). However, severe ROP incidence was significantly reduced in the omega‐3 group. Three patients developed severe ROP in the omega‐3 LCPUFA group, in contrast to nine patients in the control group (23.1% vs. 60%, p = 0.049). On logistic regression analysis, while considering risk factors for ROP, omega‐3 LCPUFA supplementation was not found to have a significant association with the incidence of ROP.ConclusionOmega‐3 LCPUFA supplementation does not reduce the overall incidence of ROP; however, it may lower the risk of severe ROP. This reduction in severe ROP cases may help decrease the need for invasive treatments and improve long‐term visual outcomes in preterm infants. Nonetheless, the study lacks sufficient statistical power to confirm this conclusively.Keywords: Bangladesh, omega‐3 LCPUFA, preterm birth, retinopathy of prematurity

Introduction

Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) is a multifactorial disorder that affects the developing retinal vasculature of pre‐term low birth weight infants [1]. The rate of ROP has increased as the survival rate among preterm newborns has increased due to continuous improvement in neonatal care. Every year, around 32,300 infants worldwide are diagnosed with irreversible vision impairment caused by ROP [2]. The World Health Organization's Vision 2020 program identified ROP as a leading cause of childhood blindness, particularly in low‐ and middle‐income countries [3].

ROP is a vasoproliferative disorder characterized by aberrant retinal vessel development in premature infants, commonly associated with supplemental oxygen therapy, which may lead to irreversible retinal damage, including detachment and macular distortion [4]. The spectrum of ROP ranges from a mild, transient change in the retina to severe progressive vasoproliferation. Severe ROP can lead to retinal detachment, permanent vision loss, smaller brain volumes, and poor psychomotor development [5, 6].

The prevalence of ROP in developing countries ranges from 7% to 37%, influenced by gestational age and birth weight [7]. In Bangladesh, an estimated 25,000 infants are born annually weighing ≤ 1500 g, placing them at high risk for ROP [8]. Local studies report incidence rates between 19.5% and 31.9%, highlighting its significant national burden [9, 10]. While early‐stage ROP may regress spontaneously, severe cases require intervention. Laser photocoagulation and intravitreal anti‐VEGF are current treatment options but may result in adverse effects such as peripheral vision loss, myopia, cataracts, and impaired angiogenesis [11, 12]. Preventive strategies are essential to reduce complications and preserve long‐term visual outcomes [13, 14].

Inflammation and oxidative stress are now recognized as key contributors to the pathogenesis of ROP. In its early phase, oxidative stress induces endothelial cell apoptosis, leading to retinal vaso‐obliteration. Nitro‐oxidative stress further disrupts prostanoid metabolism, exacerbating avascular retinal development. Inflammatory mediators amplify this response, promoting retinal cell death and choroidal degeneration [15]. Omega‐3 long‐chain polyunsaturated fatty acids (LCPUFA)—particularly docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) and eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA)—exert protective effects against these mechanisms [16]. DHA, which constitutes up to 30% of total retinal fatty acids, is essential for retinal structure and function [17, 18]. EPA modulates inflammation by counteracting arachidonic acid (AA)‐derived pro‐inflammatory mediators and enhancing vascular and endothelial health [19].

Omega‐3 fatty acids, especially DHA, are transferred to the fetus mainly during the last trimester, supporting brain and retinal development. Preterm infants miss this crucial period and have lower DHA levels at birth, risking impaired growth [20, 21]. After birth, both preterm and term infants have a limited ability to convert precursors to DHA [16]. Breastmilk provides 14–28 mg DHA/kg/day, below the in‐utero accretion rate of 45 mg/kg/day [22]. DHA content in breastmilk varies worldwide, with Bangladeshi mothers' levels (0.3%) lower than those in Korea, China, and the Philippines [23]. DHA intake among Bangladeshi lactating women (30 mg/day) is also much lower than in other Asian countries. The recommended DHA intake for breastfeeding women is 200–300 mg/day [24]. Therefore, preterm neonates require external supplementation of omega‐3 LCPUFA lipid emulsions [25]. Parenteral omega‐3 lipid emulsion is not yet available in Bangladesh. Lipid emulsions available in Bangladesh are soybean oil‐based. Soy oil‐based emulsions contain mainly omega‐6 fatty acids which may elevate pro‐inflammatory cytokine levels and oxidative stress in the body [16]. Intervention trials with omega‐3 supplements have reported no serious adverse effects except nausea, belching, and loose stool [25, 26].

Several studies have demonstrated that both parenteral and enteral omega‐3 LCPUFA supplementation can reduce the incidence of any stage and severe ROP [6, 14]. Additionally, omega‐3, particularly DHA, has been shown to improve gestational outcomes by lowering the risks of pre‐eclampsia and preterm birth [27]. Bangladesh faces a substantial burden of preterm births, with thousands of infants annually at high risk for developing ROP [8, 9, 10]. Although advances in neonatal care have improved survival, preventive strategies for ROP remain inadequate. Current management largely depends on invasive treatments, which are often expensive, inaccessible, or unavailable in many parts of the country. Due to limited availability and awareness of omega‐3 LCPUFA supplementation, most physicians rely on omega‐6‐rich soybean oil‐based emulsions, which may exacerbate systemic inflammation and oxidative stress, thereby worsening retinal injury [28]. Given this context, this study aims to assess the effect of the currently available formulation of omega‐3 supplementation in Bangladeshi preterm infants, with the goal of establishing evidence‐based, cost‐effective preventive care tailored to the country's resource and nutritional constraints.

Methods

Study Design and Setting

This pilot study was conducted among preterm low‐birth weight infants admitted to the Department of Neonatology, Bangladesh Shishu Hospital & Institute, Dhaka. Data collection was done from November 2022 to March 2024.

Sample Criteria

Preterm neonates with a gestational age between 28 and 34 weeks and birth weight < 1800 g were included to focus on infants at high risk for ROP, as they are most vulnerable to this condition. Infants aged less than 8 days and those with initiated enteral feeding were selected to ensure that the omega‐3 LCPUFA supplementation could be administered early, at a critical stage for retinal and brain development. Ultrasonography of the cranium was performed on all infants to rule out any pre‐existing intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH) or periventricular leukomalacia that might interfere with the assessment of ROP.

Infants with major congenital anomalies or other eye conditions such as cataracts were excluded to avoid confounding effects that could complicate the interpretation of ROP outcomes. Infants with severe bleeding manifestations, such as IVH of any grade or GI bleeding, were excluded to reduce the risk of additional complications that could skew results and impact the infant's overall health. Additionally, infants on mechanical ventilator during the study were excluded to ensure that the study focused on those whose outcomes could be attributed more directly to the effects of omega‐3 LCPUFA supplementation, rather than other acute interventions or complications.

Sample Size

This was a pilot study, and the sample size was determined based on a retrospective study rather than an RCT‐based calculation, which limits the statistical power of the study.

The initial sample size calculation indicated 35 patients per group. Post hoc analysis revealed that Cohen's h was approximately 0.71 and observed power 0.99, suggesting that with 35 patients per group, there was a high probability of detecting a statistically significant difference between the two proportions.

We also accounted for a 10% dropout rate, based on the likelihood of patients being lost to follow‐up. After adjustment, the sample size was increased to 38 patients per group. However, with a final sample size of total 76 patients (71 completing the study), the statistical power may have been limited, particularly for subgroup analyses (Figure 1).

Sampling Method

The sample for the study was initially selected using nonrandom purposive sampling from preterm neonates admitted to the Neonatology Department of Bangladesh Shishu Hospital & Institute. Once the eligible participants were identified, randomization was performed using a 1:1 block randomization method to ensure an equal distribution of infants into two groups: the intervention group (omega‐3 LCPUFA supplementation) and the control group (standard care).

The block randomization process involved preparing sealed envelopes, with each block containing eight envelopes—four for the intervention group and four for the control group, ensuring equal allocation of participants. The envelopes were placed in a box and thoroughly mixed. During enrollment, one envelope was randomly drawn at a time, and participants were assigned to the respective group based on the envelope's contents. This process was repeated until the desired sample size for both groups was reached. The 1:1 block randomization ensured balanced group sizes, minimizing potential imbalances in baseline characteristics and reducing bias in the study.

Study Procedure

Neonates meeting the inclusion criteria were included in the study after obtaining informed written consent from parents. The purpose, procedure, importance, and benefit of the study were explained to the parents. Neonates were randomized for intervention to receive either enteral omega‐3 LCPUFA or no supplement. A detailed history of each patient was taken from the parents. All the postnatal events were followed up and carefully recorded in the data collection sheet. Both groups of patients were receiving enteral nutrition by maternal or donor breastmilk. In the intervention group, preterm infants were given the daily dose of 40 mg/kg DHA entrally or by nasogastric tube along with breastmilk. Omega‐3 was started within 48 h of initiation of the first enteral feed and continued from the starting point to 4 weeks. Contents of Omega‐3 capsule was given by insulin syringe entrally. Each capsule contains 1 mL omega‐3 LCPUFA oil, therefore 100 units of one insulin syringe are filled with 120 mg DHA. So, 1 unit of insulin syringe contained 1.2 mg DHA. Each Capsule OMG‐3 contains omega‐3 acid ethyl esters 1000 mg which is a combination of 120 mg DHA and 80 mg Ecosapentaenoic acid. Capsule OMG‐3 is marketed by Drug International Bangladesh. DAR (Drug Administration Registration) no (210‐0422‐061), Mfg. Lic. (Manufacturing license) no (127 & 389). In the control group treatment was given according to standard protocol. Follow‐up regarding the clinical course, and oxygen supplementation along with other complications were recorded in the questionnaire. Oxygen saturation (SPO2) was monitored by pulse oximeter. The target range for SPO2 for both groups was from 88% to 92%. The pulse oximeter was continued until the patient no longer needed oxygen and breathing room air for 48 h. During the hospital stay, if any patient developed features of feeding intolerance like abdominal distension, vomiting, pre‐feed aspirate > 30% of previous feed, we managed the patient by keeping nothing per oral, and the omega‐3 supplements were withheld until resolution of symptoms and then feeds were started gradually along with omega‐3 supplements.

Patients of both groups underwent their ROP screening at the National Institute of Ophthalmology, Agargaon. 1st ROP screening was done at 20 days in babies with gestational age ≤ 30 weeks and at 30 days in babies with gestational age > 30 weeks, according to National Guidelines for Screening and Management of ROP, Bangladesh. ROP screening was done by an ophthalmologist of the National Institute of Ophthalmology, who was masked about the supplementation.

Procedure of ROP Screening

Pupils of the particpents were dilated with 0.8% tropicamide eye drop and 5% phenylephrine eye drop and were instilled twice 5 min apart. The examination was performed 20−25 min later. A surface anesthetic drop of oxybuprocaine 0.5% was applied at the conjunctival sac immediately before screening. ROP screening was done using a binocular indirect ophthalmoscope and a + 20D lens. A sterile wire lid speculum and scleral indent for eye rotation were used. One drop of Tobramycin eye drop was instilled in each eye after the procedure. Patients who had ROP were classified into stages (1−5), zone (I−III), and presence or absence of plus disease (characterized by arterial tortuosity and venous dilation in at least two quadrants of the posterior pole in infants with ROP). Lowest the zone and highest stage of ROP are considered as the highest risk for severe ROP. Severe ROP was defined as ROP requiring Laser therapy or intravitreal Bevacizumab. A follow‐up eye examination was planned according to the location and severity of ROP. Immediate treatment was planned for A‐ROP. Preterm neonates whose ROP screening showed zone I any stage with plus disease or zone I stage 3 with or without plus disease or zone II stage 2 or 3 with plus disease assigned for laser therapy or intravitreal anti‐VEGF therapy (Bevacizumab). Treatment was initiated within 48 h of diagnosis. Screening of ROP was continued until the retina was completely vascularized or ROP was fully regressed (Figure 1).

Statistical Analysis

All the data were processed and analyzed by Statistical Package for Social Science (SPSS 27.0 IBM Corporation, New York, USA). Data were expressed as numbers and percentages for categorical variables or as means and ranges for quantitative variables. All data was presented in a suitable table or graph. Categorical data were analyzed by the Chi‐square (χ 2) test and Fisher's exact test. Student t‐test was used for continuous data. For all statistical tests p‐value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Ethical Consideration

Before the commencement of the study, the research protocol was approved by the Ethical Review Committee (ERC) of Bangladesh Shishu Hospital & Institute, Dhaka, (approval no. admin/BSHI/2022/2464(9), approval date 17/10/2022). The aims and objectives of the study, as well as the risks and benefits of this study, were explained to the parents. Then informed and written consent was obtained from the legal guardians of each child. It was assured that all information and records would be kept confidential.

Results

A total of 76 patients were included in the study. Among them, four patients died and one patient left against medical advice. Therefore, 71 patients reached the final ROP screening. Considering the baseline characteristics of all infants (n = 76), no statistically significant difference was noted between the groups. Male participants were predominant in both groups, 55.3% in the intervention group, and 68.4%. in the control group. Similarly, birth weight distributions and mean birth weights did not exhibit significant disparities between the two groups (all p > 0.05). Moreover, gestational age, categorized into three groups, showed comparable frequencies and mean values between the intervention and control groups indicating no significant distinction (Table 1).

Discussion

This study provides some insights into the effects of omega‐3 LCPUFA supplementation on ROP in preterm infants. In terms of baseline characteristics, no significant differences were found between the omega‐3 and control groups, which aligns with findings from Khalesi et al. [30]. Similarly, key ROP risk factors—including the duration of oxygen therapy, RDS, apnea, PDA, frequency of C‐PAP use, BPD, and sepsis—were comparable between groups, consistent with studies by Beken et al. and Yang et al. [6, 29, 31]. Although some studies have reported an increased occurrence of BPD in DHA‐supplemented infants born before 29 weeks of gestation [6, 29, 32], this trend was not observed in our study, likely due to the exclusion of infants younger than 29 weeks, who are at higher risk for BPD.

Although in our study, there was no significant difference in baseline oxygen therapy duration observed between the control and intervention groups, increased total oxygen therapy duration was significantly associated with the development of ROP. This finding aligns with previous randomized trials, which suggest that while initial oxygen exposure may not differ markedly between groups, prolonged oxygen supplementation even after discharge from hospital, particularly at high concentrations, increases the risk of severe ROP, especially in infants born before 32 weeks of gestation [33, 34]. Additionally, a meta‐analysis reported a positive association between extended oxygen exposure and increased ROP risk, further supporting the role of oxygen therapy as a critical modifiable factor in ROP pathogenesis [35]. We also found that gestational age below 32 weeks and birth weight under 1500 g were significantly associated with an increased risk of ROP, consistent with findings from previous studies [9, 36, 37, 38].

In this study, the overall incidence of ROP was slightly lower in the intervention group compared to the control group, although this difference was not statistically significant. However, multivariate analysis found no statistically significant association between omega‐3 LCPUFA supplementation and ROP incidence. Similarly, Bernabe‐García and Yang et al. also reported reductions in ROP incidence with fish oil supplementation without statistical significance [29, 39, 40]. The intervention group in our study exhibited a significantly higher rate of spontaneous ROP regression, especially in severe or treatment‐requiring ROP, suggesting that while omega‐3 LCPUFA supplementation may not completely prevent ROP onset, it may promote a milder, self‐limiting disease course [39, 41]. This aligns with the finding of Beken et al. and Pawlik et al., where that fish‐oil emulsions reduced ROP severity but did not significantly affect overall incidence [31, 39]. Also, Tu et al. and Pawlik et al. found significant decreases in severe ROP with parenteral omega‐3 lipid emulsions [14, 30]. Moreover, Hellström et al. and Bernabe‐García et al. corroborated these results with significant reductions in severe ROP after omega‐3 supplementation [6, 40]. This apparent paradox—where omega‐3 does not significantly reduce ROP incidence but appears to lessen severity—may be explained by the biphasic pathogenesis of ROP. Omega‐3 LCPUFAs may have limited influence during the initial vaso‐obliterative phase, especially in severe prematurity or uncontrolled oxidative stress. However, during the proliferative phase, their anti‐inflammatory and angiostabilizing properties may limit pathological neovascularization and promote more organized vessel growth via vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) modulation, contributing to spontaneous regression [29, 42]. Another important consideration is that reactivation can occur after a prolonged period of ROP regression [43]. However, as long‐term follow‐up was not conducted, the clinical significance of the higher rate of spontaneous ROP regression observed in the intervention group in our study remains uncertain.

The lack of statistical significance in ROP incidence reduction in our study, despite a decrease in severity, suggests a possible dose‐dependent effect of omega‐3 LCPUFA supplementation. We administered 40 mg/kg of DHA enterally in the intervention group, which was the only formulation available in our country. This finding aligns with Belal et al., who also reported no significant impact on ROP incidence using a similar DHA dose [44]. In contrast, studies using higher doses (50–75 mg/kg), particularly in combination with AA, have shown significant reductions in the risk of severe ROP [6, 40]. Evidence also indicates that DHA supplementation may be more effective when AA levels are maintained, highlighting the need for further research to optimize dosage and nutrient balance for ROP prevention [36].

While several studies have demonstrated the benefits of omega‐3 LCPUFA supplementation in reducing ROP risk, our study is the first to evaluate the effect of omega‐3 LCPUFA among Bangladeshi infants at risk of ROP—many of whom are managed primarily with invasive interventions. Importantly, it also explores the feasibility of such supplementation in resource‐limited settings. As a pilot study, these findings highlight the need for further research to optimize dosing strategies and nutrient combinations to enhance the preventive potential of omega‐3 LCPUFA in preterm infants.

Limitations

This study has several limitations that should be considered. First, as a pilot study, the sample size was based on a retrospective analysis, limiting its statistical power. Additionally, the small block size used in randomization—due to the limited patient pool at our center—may have introduced bias by allowing prediction of treatment allocation despite masking. Baseline imbalances, including higher rates of comorbidities such as apnea, sepsis, and BPD in the control group, could have confounded ROP outcomes. While our findings provide preliminary insights, larger prospective trials are needed for validation. Second, due to the study's limited power, we could not confirm the effect of DHA on severe ROP conclusively. Additionally, as the research was conducted at a single institute, the findings may not be generalizable. Furthermore, we were unable to measure DHA and EPA levels in breastmilk and blood before supplementation. Further multicenter trials with a larger sample size and longer duration are essential to validate these findings.

Conclusion

This pilot study evaluated the effect of omega‐3 LCPUFA supplementation in preterm low birth weight infants for the prevention of ROP. While omega‐3 LCPUFA supplementation did not significantly reduce the overall incidence of ROP, it demonstrated a reduction of ROP risk in severe or treatment‐required cases. However, although the p‐value for severe ROP was significant, the study was not sufficiently powered to confirm this effect conclusively. Further research with larger sample sizes, multi‐institutional settings, and longer follow‐up periods is necessary to validate these findings and assess the broader applicability of omega‐3 LCPUFA supplementation in preterm infants.

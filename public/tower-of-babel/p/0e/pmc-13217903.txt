Predictive value of serial dynamic lipid monitoring for pathologic complete response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy in luminal breast cancer: a retrospective study integrating metabolic and clinical indicators
Authors: Yi Zeng, Yu Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionNeoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) achieves a low pathological complete response (pCR) rate in Luminal breast cancer. Static lipid parameters have limited predictive value. This study evaluated the predictive role of dynamic cholesterol trajectory monitoring for pCR.MethodsThis retrospective study enrolled 201 patients with Luminal breast cancer who received NAC. Exclusion criteria comprised statin users and patients who discontinued treatment. Blood lipids were dynamically monitored at 8 time points following each chemotherapy cycle, and the linear slope (mg/dL/week) was calculated. pCR defined as ypT0/Tis ypN0, served as the primary endpoint. Multivariate logistic regression was employed to construct a predictive model that integrated lipid slope with key clinicopathological features. Internal validation was carried out using the bootstrap method with resampling. Model calibration and clinical usefulness were assessed using calibration curves and decision curve analysis (DCA). Furthermore, using gene set enrichment analysis and the Kaplan Meier plotter database, the association between hyperlipidemia related gene sets and breast cancer patient prognosis was validated in an external cohort.ResultA rising TC slope was inversely associated with pCR (non-pCR: 0.04 vs pCR: -0.01, p < 0.001). The standardized TC slope independently predicted pCR (HR = 0.40, 95% CI: 0.25–0.64, p < 0.001). A nomogram integrating TC slope, T stage, and HER2 status achieved an AUC of 0.816, outperforming clinical-only (AUC = 0.728) and lipid-only (AUC = 0.773) models (p < 0.05). High expression of hyperlipidemia-related genes correlated with poorer prognosis (p = 0.003). The calibration curve indicated good consistency between predicted and observed probabilities, and DCA demonstrated the model's clinical net benefit at threshold probabilities between 0.4 and 0.8.ConclusionDynamic TC trajectory is a novel metabolic biomarker predicting NAC response in Luminal breast cancer. Integration of dynamic lipid monitoring with clinical features may guide personalized treatment and early intervention.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12905-026-04440-z.

Introduction

Luminal breast cancer constitutes the most common clinical subtype due to the positive expression of estrogen receptor (ER) and/or progesterone receptor (PR) [1]. As a critical strategy for locally advanced or high-risk breast cancer, neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) seeks to reduce tumor stage, conserve the breast, and offer in vivo insights into drug responsiveness [2]. Nonetheless, the response rate to NAC in luminal patients is markedly lower than in HER2-positive or triple-negative subtypes, with a low rate of pathological complete response (pCR) [3, 4]. Consequently, many patients endure substantial chemotherapy-related toxicity without attaining profound remission, posing a central challenge in clinical practice [5]. Hence, accurately identifying potential beneficiaries among luminal subtypes is crucial for optimizing therapeutic strategies.

Beyond tumor biology, the role of host systemic metabolism—especially lipid metabolism—in modulating breast cancer treatment response has garnered increasing attention [6]. Fundamental studies demonstrate that cholesterol and its metabolites regulate cellular proliferation, survival, and tumor microenvironment functionality [7]. Clinical evidence indicates a potential link between hyperlipidemia and adverse breast cancer outcomes [8]. Retrospective studies further found that elevated blood lipid indicators detected at specific time points of NAC (e.g., baseline or a certain cycle) may have a potential association with lower pCR rates, suggesting that dynamic changes in blood lipids may serve as a critical regulatory node for therapeutic efficacy [9].

However, a critical limitation in existing research is its predominant reliance on static lipid measurements at isolated or sparse timepoints (e.g., solely at baseline or post-treatment) [10], overlooking the potential for continuous evolution of lipid profiles driven by NAC as a robust metabolic stressor. Chemotherapeutic agents can perturb hepatic metabolism or trigger inflammatory cascades, thereby generating intricate and individualized dynamic trajectories of lipid levels across the entire therapeutic course. Isolated measurements fail to capture longitudinal trends (e.g., progressive elevation or decline) and fluctuation rates, thereby limiting information richness and potentially missing critical biological signals [11]. Cross-sectional approaches are inadequate for elucidating potential time-dependent causal links between dynamic lipid fluctuations and pCR, impeding the establishment of early predictive models grounded in metabolic trajectory analysis.

In response to the major challenge in predicting NAC response for Luminal-type breast cancer and the deficiencies in current studies, this research seeks to investigate the association between dynamically monitored blood lipid changes and pCR. The research question centers on whether this blood lipid kinetic pattern, based on serial monitoring, is independently correlated with attaining pathological complete response. Based on these findings, we will combine blood lipid kinetics slope, baseline features, tumor biology parameters, and surgical data to construct and validate an integrated predictive model for clinical use, aimed at personalizing treatment strategies and enhancing patient outcomes.

Methods

Patients

This retrospective study enrolled breast cancer patients who received surgery followingNAC at the Affiliated Huizhou Hospital, Guangzhou Medical University from January 2020 to December 2024. All patients received the standardized EC*4-T*4 regimen (epirubicin 90 mg/m2 and cyclophosphamide 600 mg/m2 every 3 weeks for 4 cycles, followed by docetaxel 100 mg/m2, every 3 weeks for 4 cycles). The study inclusion criteria included the following: (1) ER and PR expression ≥ 10%; (2) No prior use of lipid-altering medications (e.g., statins, fibrates, niacin preparations, or cholesterol absorption inhibitors); (3) Complete clinicopathological data and documented lipid profiles. The subject exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Dose reduction due to severe adverse events (SAEs); (2) Premature treatment discontinuation; (3) Protocol-deviated regimen modifications; (4) Failure to undergo definitive surgery post-chemotherapy.

This is a retrospective observational study. All enrolled patients underwent NAC as part of their actual clinical management. The decision to use NAC for some patients traditionally staged as early was grounded in individualized clinical evaluation and multidisciplinary team discussion. The specific indications were preoperative imaging indicating a substantial axillary nodal disease burden where R0 resection via upfront surgery was challenging, and a strong patient preference for breast conservation, for which neoadjuvant chemotherapy was a viable downstaging strategy to enable breast conserving surgery. Consequently, this cohort represents luminal breast cancer patients from real world practice for whom NAC was chosen based on locally advanced characteristics or a strong desire for breast preservation.

Collection and processing of clinicopathological information

Clinicopathological characteristics were collected, including age, T stage, N stage, Ki67 status, HER2 status, surgical approach, postoperative pathology, and lipid levels (measured at 2 weeks after each chemotherapy cycle), including triglyceride (TG), total cholesterol (TC), high-density lipoprotein (HDL), and low-density lipoprotein (LDL).

All immunohistochemical (IHC) slides underwent independent blinded review by three specialized breast pathologists. In accordance with the 2018 American Society of Clinical Oncology/College of American Pathologists (ASCO/CAP) guidelines [12], the percentage of ER/PR-stained tumor cells was documented. Ki67 assessment was performed using the standardized "typewriter" visual evaluation method, as recommended by the 2021 International Working Group Guidelines for Ki67 Evaluation in Breast Cancer [13]. To ensure the robustness of HER2 classification, in accordance with the 2024 CSCO guidelines, IHC 0 was defined as no staining or ≤ 10% of invasive tumor cells with incomplete, faint membrane staining; IHC 1 + as > 10% of invasive tumor cells with incomplete, faint membrane staining; IHC 2 + as > 10% of invasive tumor cells with weak to moderate intensity, complete membrane staining or ≤ 10% of invasive tumor cells with strong, complete membrane staining [14]. Based on recommendations from the Chinese CACA guidelines, 20% was used as the threshold for determining high/low Ki67 and PR expression [15].

Serum lipid levels

Serum concentrations of TC, TG, LDL and HDL were measured using a fully automated biochemical analyzer at our institution two weeks after each chemotherapy cycle. Lipid variability was quantified by calculating the linear slope of sequential lipid concentration measurements.

Validation with an external database

A detailed gene set (HP_HYPERCHOLESTEROLEMIA) related to increased serum lipid concentrations was identified via Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) (http://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb/index.jsp). Notably, GSEA is a platform that performs enrichment analysis based on predefined functional gene sets to identify coordinated change patterns within these sets in transcriptomic data. It does not itself directly furnish individual patient clinical outcome data. Afterwards, Kaplan Meier plotter [Breast Cancer] (http://kmplot.com/analysis/index.php) was applied to verify the relationship between the expression of these genes and the prognosis of early stage breast cancer patients.

Study endpoints and definitions

The primary endpoint was pCR, defined as the absence of invasive cancer residues in both the breast primary tumor and sampled axillary lymph nodes (ypT0/is ypN0) of the surgically resected specimen following NAC, with the presence of ductal carcinoma in situ being permissible. Due to the currently relatively short follow up period in this cohort, long term survival outcome data are insufficiently mature and thus were not analyzed in this study.

Statistical analysis

The study outcomes were pCR. Specifically, pCR was referred to as ypT0/is ypN0. SPSS 29.0 and R 4.5.1was used for statistical analyses. Differences in categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test. Differences in continuous variables were analyzed using Student's t-test (normally distributed) or Mann–Whitney U-test (non-normally distributed). Collinearity between covariates was assessed by calculating the variance inflation factor (VIF). Variables with a p < 0.05 from the univariate analysis were incorporated into a multivariate logistic regression model to identify independent factors associated with pCR. Model stability was assessed via bootstrap resampling with 500 iterations. A predictive nomogram for pCR probability was developed using the final multivariable model. Model calibration and clinical usefulness were assessed using calibration curves and decision curve analysis (DCA). Statistical significance was defined as a two-tailed p-value < 0.05.

Result

A cohort of 201 eligible patients was analyzed, comprising 72 pCR and 129 non-pCR. The median age was 49 years (range 25–73). Significantly steeper lipid trajectories were observed in non-pCR patients for both total cholesterol (TC slope: 0.04 vs −0.01; p < 0.001) and LDL (LDL slope: 0.04 vs 0.00; p < 0.001), indicating an inverse association between rising lipid trends and treatment response. Clinically relevant differences were observed in Ki67 expression, HER2 status and T stage distribution (p < 0.001). No significant associations were found for age, lymph node (N) status, mutation status, or surgical approach (Table 1). Baseline lipid profiles for all patients are presented in Supplementary Table 2. No statistically significant differences were observed between the pCR and non-pCR groups in baseline total cholesterol, triglyceride, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, or low-density lipoprotein cholesterol levels (p > 0.05). TC_slope_std maintained independent predictive significance in the multivariable model (OR = 0.40, 95% CI: 0.25–0.64; p < 0.001), demonstrating superior predictive capacity compared to conventional biomarkers. Significant independent associations persisted for T stage (OR = 0.35, 95% CI: 0.16–0.77; p = 0.009) and HER2 status (OR = 0.39, 95% CI: 0.19–0.82; p = 0.012), whereas the association for LDL_slope_std became non-significant (p = 0.456). The Ki67 status showed borderline significance (OR = 2.28, p = 0.090). The final multivariable model identified dynamic TC changes, T stage, and HER2 status as key predictors (Table 2).

To test the general applicability of the predictive value of TC slope, we conducted subgroup analyses. Supplementary Figure S2 shows that a consistent, significant inverse correlation between the TC slope and pCR was present in all subgroups. Discussion

This study revealed deep associations between lipid trajectory evolution during NAC and pCR in Luminal breast cancer through continuous dynamic monitoring. Crucially, an upward TC trajectory (positive slope) emerged as a robust independent predictor (adjusted OR = 0.40; p < 0.001), outperforming conventional markers including T stage and HER2 status. This suggests that chemotherapy—acting as a systemic metabolic stressor—may perturb lipid homeostasis, driving sustained cholesterol accumulation [16] that remodels tumor biology [17]. Cholesterol-derived metabolites (e.g., oxysterols) activate LXR/RXR signaling to enhance tumor cell survival [18], while concurrently disrupting cellular integrity and sculpting immunosuppressive microenvironments via immunomodulatory effects [19]. Notably, the LDL slope—though significant in univariable analysis (OR = 0.48; p < 0.001)—lost independent predictive value in the multivariable model (p = 0.456), underscoring the primacy of TC as a pivotal metabolic node. Rising TC signifies not only disrupted cholesterol synthesis/clearance equilibrium, but also tumor cell adaptation through membrane lipid raft remodeling that potentiates drug efflux pump activity [20, 21].

In this study, the observed pathological complete response rate was 35.8% (72/201), exceeding the average pCR rate (approximately 10–16%) historically reported for Luminal breast cancer [22]. This difference may be accounted for by the distinct patient population characteristics in this cohort. Firstly, Table 1 shows that 88% (63/72) of patients who attained pCR had a Ki67 ≥ 20%, suggesting the inclusion of a significant number of highly proliferative Luminal B tumors, a subtype generally more chemosensitive [23]. This is consistent with reports that Luminal B tumors can achieve higher pCR rates (around 30%) [24]. Then, 82% (59/72) of patients in the pCR group had T1-2 stage disease, indicating the inclusion of a substantial proportion of patients with relatively early-stage cancer and lower tumor burden, a factor associated with higher response rates [25].

The integrated prediction model constructed in this study showed robust performance upon internal validation (bootstrap corrected AUC = 0.793), suggesting that combining dynamic lipid profiles with clinical characteristics is effective for predicting pCR in Luminal breast cancer. In contrast to studies dependent on single time point lipid measurements, the TC slope from dynamic monitoring offers predictive signals during the early phase of therapy, establishing a crucial window for adjustments based on response-guided treatment strategies [26]. Multivariate analysis further identified HER2-low status as an independent negative predictor of pCR in this model. This echoes emerging evidence that HER2-low Luminal tumors might exhibit unique biological behavior, with potentially differential responses to conventional chemotherapy compared to HER2-zero tumors [27]. In conclusion, dynamic lipid profiles offer distinct incremental value to the conventional clinicopathological predictive framework.

Validation in external databases offers molecular biological substantiation for the association between lipid levels and therapeutic efficacy. Figure 4 demonstrates that high expression of multiple genes linked to hyperlipidemia, such as CAV1, LIPA, and IRF5, is significantly associated with unfavorable prognosis in breast cancer patients receiving NAC. For example, patients exhibiting high CAV1 expression had significantly lower 5-year recurrence-free survival (HR = 1.48, p = 0.003), indicating that CAV1 promotes disordered cholesterol metabolism [28], resulting in the reprogramming of lipid metabolism in cancer cells, encompassing increased sphingolipid clearance and changes in ceramide metabolism. Thereby creating a microenvironment conducive to tumor advancement and resistance to chemotherapy [29], which compromises the efficacy of neoadjuvant chemotherapy. However, the mechanisms by which genes such as LIPA, CNF, and NUP107 affect the survival outcomes of breast cancer patients remain unclear. Subsequent research is needed to further explore the associations between these genes and breast cancer. The expression patterns of these genes provide biological validity (form a mechanistic link) for the rising TC trend observed in this study, confirming that the dynamic alterations in lipid levels fundamentally represent quantifiable phenotypic manifestations of the interplay between the tumor's inherent genomic properties and host metabolism. This offers a potential molecular explanation for the influence of lipid metabolism on chemotherapy sensitivity. A crucial future direction is to assess if these GSEA identified genes could function as readily measurable plasma biomarkers and therapeutic targets, in order to translate this foundational discovery into risk stratification tools or intervention strategies for clinical practice.

While this study centered on dynamic lipid alterations during neoadjuvant chemotherapy, its findings prompt a broader consideration of the potential link between lipid metabolism and response to systemic therapies in breast cancer. A particularly pertinent question is whether variations in lipid levels could also predict response to neoadjuvant endocrine therapy in patients with Luminal breast cancer. Biologically, aromatase function is intimately linked to cholesterol metabolic pathways [30], and lipid abnormalities might affect intratumoral estrogen levels and the surrounding microenvironment [31]. Thus, although the present work mainly established the predictive value of kinetic lipid parameters for chemotherapy response, this discovery offers a theoretical rationale for future investigation into their utility in the context of breast cancer endocrine therapy. Future studies could validate, in cohorts undergoing neoadjuvant endocrine therapy, whether dynamic metrics like the TC slope similarly hold predictive potential for therapeutic efficacy. This would extend the concept of metabolic intervention from chemotherapy to the wider treatment spectrum for Luminal breast cancer.

Future research efforts should prioritize several interconnected directions. One crucial avenue involves examining whether interventions using lipid-lowering drugs, for example PCSK9 inhibitors, can reverse chemotherapy resistance in patients with dyslipidemia [32]. Our observation that the LDL slope lost significance in the multivariate model (p = 0.456), suggests that LDL may not itself be the direct effector molecule. Another important direction is the establishment of early warning thresholds based on dynamic lipid profiling. A representative threshold could be a total cholesterol increase exceeding ten percent by the second treatment cycle, which could inform subsequent modifications to treatment intensity. Finally, a comprehensive understanding may be achieved by integrating dynamic ctDNA monitoring with metabolomics data. This integrated approach is essential to delineate the complex interactive network linking cholesterol metabolism and genomic instability.

The model holds notable value for clinical translation. n high-risk individuals exhibiting a sustained TC slope increase of more than 0.04 mmol/L per cycle, the early co-administration of statins may be considered. Clinical research has demonstrated that atorvastatin can overcome cholesterol-mediated chemoresistance by inhibiting HMG-CoA reductase and obstructing the mevalonate pathway [33]. A more proactive intervention strategy involves modifying the treatment from a standard three-week regimen to dose dense chemotherapy in an attempt to overcome underlying resistance mechanisms. Concurrently, this early predictive signal can provide crucial evidence for informing subsequent decisions regarding the use of CDK4/6i to intensify adjuvant endocrine therapy. This intervention paradigm, grounded in dynamic lipid monitoring, holds promise for creating new pathways in the treatment management of patients exhibiting poor chemosensitivity.

Several limitations of this study must be objectively stated. The retrospective design, while controlling for confounders via stringent exclusion criteria, failed to systematically collect metabolic covariates including body mass index (BMI), dietary patterns, and physical activity habits. The HER2-low expression subgroup (IHC 1 +/2 + and ISH-) constituted 42% of the cohort. This subgroup might possess unique biological characteristics [34], requiring larger sample sizes to verify subgroup specific findings. Moreover, the absence of tumor tissue lipidomic profiling and assessment of cholesterol metabolism in immune cells within the tumor microenvironment hinders the establishment of a direct causal chain between increasing TC and chemoresistance. The current findings, obtained from a single-center study with a relatively small sample, require validation in larger, prospective multicenter cohorts. While survival analysis revealed an association between lipid metabolism related genes and prognosis, indirectly supporting the clinical observations, constraints of public data resources prevented us from locating an independent neoadjuvant chemotherapy cohort with matched pre-treatment transcriptomic data, lipid profiles, and pCR outcomes to directly test the predictive performance of this gene signature for pCR. Future prospective studies aimed at collecting multi-omics data are required to more directly clarify the precise mechanisms through which lipid metabolic reprogramming influences chemosensitivity.

Conclusion

The evolving TC profile represents a novel metabolic marker for predicting NAC response in locally advanced Luminal breast cancer. Our integrated model extends precision medicine beyond the conventional "tumor-centric" paradigm, advancing it into the dimension of "dynamic host-tumor interactions". Future work should validate real-time lipid-monitoring-guided interventions within prospective clinical trials, with the ultimate goal of establishing a "monitoring-prediction-intervention" closed-loop strategy, thereby pioneering a personalized treatment approach for patients with metabolic high-risk Luminal breast cancer.

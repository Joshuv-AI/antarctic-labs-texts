Safety and Efficacy of Transscleral Cyclodiode Laser in Advanced Glaucoma: A Retrospective, Single-center Observational Cohort Study
Authors: Yasaman Salari, Divya Venugopal, Tooba Sohail, David Burton, Jan van der Hoek, Pouya Alaghband
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAims and backgroundGlaucoma is a leading cause of irreversible blindness worldwide. Its global prevalence is projected to exceed 110 million individuals by 2040 (Tham et al., 2014). In 2020, the disease accounted for an estimated 3.6 million cases of blindness and 4 million cases of moderate to severe visual impairment. Between 3 and 17% of patients develop refractory glaucoma, in which progression continues despite maximal medical and surgical treatment (Saunders et al., 2016). Optimizing management of these patients is essential to reduce the global burden of visual disability.MethodsThis was a single-center, observational, retrospective cohort study conducted at York and Scarborough Teaching Hospitals. We included patients with refractory glaucoma treated with a single session of transscleral cyclodiode laser between 2019 and 2023. Baseline demographics, intraocular pressure (IOP), and medication use were recorded. Treatment success was defined as ≥20% reduction in IOP and a final IOP ≤ 21 mm Hg at 12 months posttreatment or cessation of oral antihypotensive medication and/or reduction in the number of topical antiglaucoma drops.ResultsA total of 108 patients had cyclodiode laser treatment between 2019 and 2023. A total of 62 eyes from 57 patients were included. The average age of the patient was 71.5 ± 15.8 years. The mean baseline IOP was 36.4 ± 12.4 mm Hg, which significantly reduced to 19.0 ± 9.0 mm Hg at 12 months (p < 0.001). There was no significant correlation between baseline and posttreatment IOP. The average number of topical medications decreased from 3.4 ± 1.2 to 2.5 ± 1.4 (p < 0.001).ConclusionTransscleral cyclodiode laser achieved a 77.4% success rate in reducing IOP ≤ 21 mm Hg and ≥20% reduction. It was an effective and well-tolerated option for managing refractory glaucoma.Clinical significanceThis study supports the use of a single session of transscleral cyclodiode laser as a viable standalone treatment for patients with advanced, treatment-resistant glaucoma. Its ability to significantly lower IOP and reduce the need for systemic medications, with a favorable safety profile, makes it especially valuable for elderly and frail patients who are poor candidates for further surgery.How to cite this articleSalari Y, Venugopal D, Sohail T, et al. Introduction

Background and Rationale

Glaucoma is a chronic and progressive disease of the optic nerve, often associated with increased intraocular pressure (IOP), which contributes to structural damage through mechanical strain, disrupted axonal transport, and reduced blood supply to the optic nerve head.1,2 A meta-analysis estimated that in 2020, glaucoma was responsible for approximately 3.6 million cases of blindness and 4.1 million cases of moderate to severe visual impairment worldwide, accounting for 8.4% of global blindness and 1.4% of visual impairment.3 By 2040, the global prevalence is projected to rise to 112 million individuals.4

Refractory glaucoma refers to cases in which IOP remains uncontrolled despite maximally tolerated medical therapy (topical and systemic), failed surgical interventions, or a combination of both. These cases may also include patients at high risk of failure for glaucoma filtration surgery. Ongoing optic nerve damage from inadequate IOP control may lead to further visual field loss and irreversible blindness. Approximately, 3–17% of glaucoma patients are at risk of significant progression due to refractory glaucoma.5 Therefore, optimizing treatment for patients with refractory glaucoma is critical to preserving vision.

Currently, IOP is the only modifiable risk factor in glaucoma management.6–8 Glaucoma treatments include pharmacological therapy, laser procedures, and surgical interventions, chosen based on disease severity and patient characteristics/choices.8 Among laser therapies, cycloablative procedures, also known as, cyclodestructive or cyclophotocoagulation treatments, aim to reduce aqueous humor production by targeting the ciliary body epithelium.9 It was first introduced in 1930s. Cyclodestruction can be delivered via various approaches, including transscleral, endoscopic, micropulse, and Nd:YAG (neodymium-doped yttrium aluminum garnet) laser techniques.9,10 Transscleral cyclodiode laser is often reserved for refractory cases where conventional treatment options are ineffective or unsuitable.9,11 However, the literature lacks robust long-term data on its effectiveness, particularly in achieving target IOPs (≤21 mm Hg), with reported outcomes varying widely depending on parameters, such as total energy, laser duration, power settings, number of applications, and treated arc circumference.12–14

Objectives

This study evaluates the effectiveness of a single application of cyclodiode laser in managing refractory glaucoma. By adhering to the recommended laser settings from the UK National Glaucoma Survey and Wills Eye Institute, this study seeks to contribute clearer evidence on anticipated IOP outcomes and safety of cyclodiode laser treatment.13,14

Methods

This was a single-center, observational, retrospective cohort study conducted at York and Scarborough Teaching Hospitals between January 2019 and December 2023. This study was deemed by the local governance to be exempt from ethical approval, as retrospectively obtained and anonymized data were used for this noninterventional study (registration number: CSCS/OPH/095). Patients were identified through the electronic medical records system. Inclusion criteria were diagnosis of refractory glaucoma (confirmed by fellowship-trained glaucoma specialists DSB, JvDH, and PA), defined as persistently elevated IOP despite maximally tolerated medical therapy or previous IOP-lowering surgeries, and having undergone a single session of transscleral cyclodiode laser photocoagulation during the study period. Patients who had previous cyclodiode treatments, underwent combined surgical interventions, or had insufficient follow-up data were excluded.

Baseline data collected included patient demographics (age, gender, and ethnicity), type of glaucoma, baseline IOP, oral acetazolamide use, and the number of topical glaucoma medications used prior to treatment. Follow-up data, including IOP measurements, the use of oral acetazolamide, postoperative complications, and the number of topical medications, were recorded at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months after treatment.

All procedures were performed under general anesthesia using parameters based on the UK National Glaucoma Survey recommendations, ensuring consistency in treatment delivery.13 The initial median power settings used were 1250 mW for dark brown irides and 1500 mW for all other irides for 2000 ms.13 Energy levels during the procedure were adjusted intraoperatively using the audible “pop” as a feedback mechanism to guide optimal power titration.13

Success of cyclodiode intervention was defined as achieving an IOP reduction of ≥20% and an IOP ≤21 mm Hg at 12 months, or a reduction in the number of topical antiglaucoma drops and/or cessation of oral antihypotensive medication.

Data were analyzed using Microsoft Excel (version 15.28, released in 2016) and STATA (version 18.0, released in 2023). Student's t-test was used to calculate p-values for the normally distributed data and the Wilcoxon signed-rank test for not normally distributed data. R² values were derived from linear regression analyses using the same programs. All estimates are reported with 95% confidence intervals to indicate the reliability of the results.

Results

Between 2019 and 2023, 108 patients underwent cyclodiode laser treatment for refractory glaucoma. A total of 101 eyes from 92 patients were initially identified. Twenty-four eyes of 22 patients were excluded due to prior glaucoma procedures or incomplete baseline data. During follow-up, 10 eyes (13%) required additional intervention and were not included in the final outcome analysis. Five patients (five eyes) were lost to follow-up. The final study cohort therefore comprised 62 eyes of 57 patients, all of whom underwent a single episode of cyclodiode laser treatment and no additional glaucoma interventions during the 12-month follow-up period. Of the cohort of 62 eyes of 57 patients, 60 eyes completed a 12-month follow-up, while one patient died before this time point. The number of eyes available for analysis at each visit was 62 at baseline, 58 at 1 month, 48 at 3 months, 50 at 6 months, and 60 at 12 months, as shown in Figure 1. The declining numbers at intermediate time points reflect variability in follow-up attendance and scheduling rather than true attrition, except for one patient who died before completing follow-up.

All patients in this study were White Caucasians. There were 24 females and 33 males in this study. Among the 62 treated eyes, 34 were pseudophakic. Prior glaucoma procedures included selective laser trabeculoplasty (SLT) in 9 eyes, trabeculectomy in 14 eyes, glaucoma aqueous shunt implantation in 2 eyes, and peripheral iridotomy in 8 eyes. Changes in IOP

The mean IOP was 36.4 ± 12.5 mm Hg at baseline, decreasing to 20.0 ± 11.6 mm Hg at 4 weeks, 21.1 ± 9.4 mm Hg at 3 months, 18.2 ± 9.9 mm Hg at 6 months, and 19.0 ± 9.1 mm Hg at 12 months posttreatment. Overall, the mean IOP reduced from 36.4 ± 12.4 to 19.0 ± 9.1 mm Hg over 12 months (p < 0.001). Changes in the mean IOP from baseline and the number of patients at each follow-up point are shown in Figure 1.

Linear regression analyses were used to evaluate whether baseline IOP could predict IOP at different follow-up time points: 1, 3, 6, and 12 months after treatment (Table 2). At 1 month, a statistically significant but clinically weak correlation was observed (R2 = 0.067, p = 0.05). At 3 months, there was a borderline nonsignificant trend (p = 0.07). At 6 months, baseline IOP showed a stronger and highly significant correlation (R2 = 0.264, p < 0.001), while by 12 months the association was no longer significant (p = 0.1).

Changes in the Number of Topical Glaucoma Agents and Oral Acetazolamide

The number of topical glaucoma agents used before cyclodiode treatment ranged from 0 to 5, with a mean of 3.4 ± 1.2. This decreased to 2.5 ± 1.4 at the 12-month follow-up (Fig. 2). The reduction in the average number of topical medications was statistically significant, with a mean decrease of 0.9 ± 1.7 (p < 0.001). Additionally, the number of patients requiring oral acetazolamide dropped significantly, from 39 before treatment to 2 at the 12-month follow-up (Fig. 2). Notably, these two patients initially demonstrated a reduction in IOP after the procedure. However, during the 1-year follow-up period, glaucoma was uncontrolled; hence, acetazolamide was restarted until their next surgical intervention.

Comparison of the mean number of topical glaucoma medications and oral acetazolamide used at baseline and 1-year post-cyclodiode laser treatment

Changes in the Number of Topical Glaucoma Agents and Oral Acetazolamide by Glaucoma Subtypes

Across the cohort, reductions in medication burden varied by glaucoma subtype. Patients with neovascular glaucoma (n = 22) demonstrated the most pronounced and statistically significant improvement, with mean medications decreasing from 3.8 to 2.0 (Δ −1.77, p < 0.01), alongside a drop in oral acetazolamide use from 64 to 18%. In secondary open-angle glaucoma (n = 21), the mean number of medications decreased from 3.1 to 2.6 (Δ −0.63), and oral acetazolamide use fell from 71 to 38%, although this reduction was not statistically significant. By contrast, primary open-angle glaucoma (n = 12) showed minimal change in medications (2.8 → 2.8; Δ −0.08, p = 0.85), with a modest reduction in oral acetazolamide use (50 → 42%). Primary angle-closure glaucoma (n = 7) remained stable in medication requirement (3.5 → 3.0; Δ ≈ 0), with oral acetazolamide use decreasing only slightly (57 → 43%).

Effect of Laser Energy and IOP Reduction

In this study, an initial standard median power setting of 1250 and 1500 mW was used. This was applied over a standard duration of 2000 ms. The power was titrated based on the “pop” cues. The average power used was 2086 ± 385.5 mW, and the average duration was 2243 ± 488.9 ms. The logistic regression analysis indicated that neither the power of the laser, which was adjusted based on the audible sounds, nor the duration of treatment significantly influenced the success of the treatment (p = 0.1).

Cumulative Probability of Treatment Success Following Cyclodiode Laser

The cumulative probability of treatment success following cyclodiode laser was estimated using Kaplan–Meier (KM) survival analysis as shown in Figure 3. Success was defined as an IOP < 21 mm Hg with a ≥20% reduction from baseline. At 12 months, the KM-estimated probability of success was 26% (95% CI, 14–48%). The number of eyes remaining at risk declined over time, with 62 at 1 month, 34 at 3 months, 10 at 6 months, and 7 at 12 months. These findings highlight an early reduction in success rates, with limited data contributing to the survival estimates beyond 6 months.

KM Survival Analysis Based on Glaucoma Subtypes

Kaplan–Meier survival analysis of cyclodiode laser success (IOP < 21 mm Hg and ≥20% reduction from baseline) demonstrated broadly similar outcomes across glaucoma subtypes (Supplementary Fig. 2). At 12 months, the KM-estimated probability of success was 26% (95% CI, 4–48%) for neovascular glaucoma (n = 22), 29% (95% CI, 0–62%) for primary angle-closure glaucoma (n = 7), 25% (95% CI, 0.5–50%) for primary open-angle glaucoma (n = 12), and 27% (95% CI, 5–49%) for secondary open-angle glaucoma (n = 21). The overall log-rank test showed no significant differences among groups (χ2 =0.12, p = 0.99). Numbers at risk at 12 months were low (4, 0, 1, and 2 eyes for the respective groups), limiting the precision of late-time estimates.

Visual Acuity

In this cohort, most patients presented with advanced or end-stage glaucoma, and many had very limited vision at baseline, which precluded reliable visual field testing. The mean baseline visual acuity across all eyes was 1.19 ± 0.90 logMAR. At 12 months, visual acuity data were available for 58 eyes. At final follow-up, visual acuity remained similarly limited, with a mean of 1.17 ± 0.90 logMAR, which did not differ significantly from baseline (Wilcoxon signed-rank test, p = 0.39).

Cup-to-disc Ratio

Cup-to-disc ratio measurements were available for 55 eyes at baseline and 57 eyes at 12 months. At presentation, the mean ratio was 0.82 ± 0.09, reflecting the advanced stage of disease in this cohort. By the final follow-up, the mean ratio had increased to 0.85 ± 0.08; however, this change was not statistically significant (Wilcoxon signed-rank test, p = 0.19).

Complications of Cyclodiode Laser Postintervention in This Study

Overall, all postoperative complications were mild and self-limiting. Anterior uveitis, cystoid macular edema, and corneal abrasion were each observed in three different cases, but all resolved with appropriate management. Postoperative ocular pain was reported in four cases, of which three resolved. One patient experienced persistent postoperative pain; they had a history of preexisting ocular pain prior to the laser treatment.

Patients were followed up until January 2025 for the assessment of late-onset cyclodiode laser-related complications. Of the cohort, 52 were reviewed in our glaucoma service at various time points. One patient had passed away by 2025, and others had significant comorbidities that precluded glaucoma follow-up at different time points. Importantly, no late complications, such as persistent hypotony, chronic inflammation, or loss of vision, were observed during the follow-up period.

Discussion

This study included 62 eyes from 57 patients with refractory glaucoma who received only one application of transscleral cyclodiode laser treatment and no other IOP-lowering interventions during the 12-month follow-up period. The patient cohort in this study is representative of the patients who undergo cycloablative procedures elsewhere, often involving patients with end-stage ocular disease and frail, elderly individuals.

The average laser power level used was 2086 ± 385.5 mW, and the average application duration was 2243 ± 488.9 ms. At 12 months posttreatment, a single application of cyclodiode laser, the mean IOP reduced from 36.4 ± 12.4 to 19.0 ± 9.1 mm Hg (p <0.001). At 12 months posttreatment, there was a statistically significant reduction in the number of glaucoma medications, from 3.4 ± 1.2 to 2.5 ± 1.4 (p < 0.001). Additionally, the number of patients who required oral acetazolamide reduced from 39 to 2 at 12 months post-cyclodiode laser treatment.

The outcomes observed in this study are consistent with previously reported data supporting the sustained IOP-lowering effect of transscleral cyclodiode laser therapy in refractory glaucoma.12,15–17 This study also reiterates the importance of cyclodiode laser in easing the medication burden.12,18

Various protocols are available for cyclodiode laser use. However, no standardized guideline is currently available.16 In 2011, a survey of UK ophthalmologists performing cyclodiode lasers revealed significant procedural variability among surgeons.13 The laser parameters used in our study are based on the findings of this survey and the recommended parameters by the Wills Eye Institute.13,14 Our analysis revealed no significant association between power settings and IOP outcomes. While our average energy was relatively high compared with some published protocols, this finding indicates that greater energy levels do not necessarily translate into improved efficacy. Importantly, titrating energy intraoperatively based on visual and auditory cues (such as the “pop” sound), as advocated in the literature,19,20 may help balance efficacy with safety by reducing the risk of serious complications such as hypotony and phthisis that are linked to excessive energy delivery. This contrasts with the findings of previous studies, which indicated that higher power levels might yield greater IOP reduction, thus adding to the debate on optimal cyclodiode laser settings.12,21

Although baseline IOP showed a weak but statistically significant correlation with IOP at 1 month and a borderline trend at 3 months, the explained variance was very small (R2 = 0.067 at 1 month), and therefore these early findings are unlikely to be clinically useful for predicting individual outcomes. A stronger association was seen at 6 months (R2 = 0.264, p < 0.001), but this predictive value was not maintained at 12 months, where the correlation was no longer significant. These findings suggest that baseline IOP may influence early IOP reduction following cyclodiode laser, but it is not a reliable long-term prognostic marker. This adds nuance to the existing literature by highlighting that treatment success is multifactorial and cannot be determined by baseline IOP alone.

While our study did not include a comparator group, several other studies have evaluated repeated transscleral cyclodiode laser sessions or alternative interventions such as micropulse transscleral cyclophotocoagulation, ultrasound cycloplasty, or glaucoma drainage devices. These studies have consistently demonstrated meaningful IOP reductions and safety profiles broadly comparable to our results.15,21–24 Taken together, these findings suggest that although our study lacked a direct comparator arm, our outcomes are in line with those reported in the wider literature, supporting the validity and generalizability of our results. Future prospective comparative studies would be valuable to clarify the relative effectiveness of the cyclodiode laser against these alternative modalities.

Strengths of This Study

In this study, we only included a single application of transscleral cyclodiode laser treatment and no other IOP-lowering interventions during the follow-up period. Multiple studies have assessed more than one application of cyclodiode.15–17,22–40 By doing so, we have eliminated the confounding factors introduced by different numbers of applications or combinations with other IOP-lowering treatments. This provided a clearer understanding of the treatment efficacy as a standalone intervention, isolating its direct impact on IOP control. Moreover, exploring the outcomes of a single treatment is particularly important for patients who may have limited tolerance for repeat procedures, such as elderly or frail individuals with advanced ocular disease. If a single application proves effective, it could reduce the overall treatment burden, lowering the frequency of procedures and associated risks, which is particularly valuable for improving quality of life and long-term management in this patient population.

In comparison to Zhekov et al., who used fixed power without titration and reported a 5.3% incidence of hypotony,12 our titration-based approach resulted in no cases of hypotony or phthisis. Despite employing higher average powers overall, the absence of these complications suggests that careful titration is a critical factor in optimizing safety. These findings reinforce the importance of individualized titration strategies over fixed energy settings, especially in high-risk populations.

Limitations of This Study

Our study was a retrospective review with inherent limitations due to potential biases in data collection, which may be incomplete or inconsistent, especially in terms of IOP measurements, medication adherence, or follow-up assessments.

This study included 62 eyes, as it included a niche group of individuals with refractory glaucoma who failed to respond to conventional interventions. A great proportion of patients were excluded as there was insufficient follow-up data for them; they required more than one session of cyclodiode laser treatment or had other IOP-lowering interventions during the follow-up period.

A small sample size lowers the statistical power of the study, meaning there is a greater chance of not detecting a true effect (type II error) and limits the generalizability of this intervention. Additionally, as all included patients were Caucasian, the generalizability of our findings is limited, particularly as glaucoma subtypes and treatment responses to laser may differ across ethnic groups, and eyes with more pigmented irides may exhibit a different safety profile.

By excluding patients who required repeat transscleral cyclodiode treatments or alternative IOP-lowering interventions, the study may introduce selection bias. These exclusions could potentially overestimate the success rate of the treatment by including only those patients who responded well to the initial treatment. Including patients with multiple cyclodiode laser applications or patients who had other IOP-lowering interventions and comparing their results to the population in this study group could provide a more comprehensive view of real-world outcomes. The lack of a comparator group, such as eyes undergoing repeat laser or alternative therapies, limits the ability to contextualize our findings against other treatment options.

The skill and experience of the operators may have varied; however, they were all supervised by fellowship-trained glaucoma specialists. Nonetheless, the operator variability can introduce operator bias.

Another limitation of our study is the 12-month follow-up period. While this timeframe provides useful information on short- to medium-term outcomes after cyclodiode laser, it may not be sufficient to capture late-onset complications that can occur beyond the first year. Extending follow-up is further challenged by the specific characteristics of our study population and the stringent inclusion criteria, which limit the feasibility of larger-scale studies. In addition, the advanced age and frailty of many patients make prolonged follow-up difficult, as some may not survive long enough to complete extended observation. Future studies with longer follow-up, where feasible, are warranted to more fully assess long-term safety and durability.

Similarities of This Study with the Available Literature

A comprehensive overview of previously published studies evaluating transscleral cyclodiode laser outcomes is summarized in Supplementary Table 1.12,15–17–22,46 Our study corroborates the universal finding that transscleral cyclodiode laser effectively lowers IOP and reduces the burden of medications. The success rate and safety profile align closely with studies reporting single treatment outcomes.12,37 The lack of correlation between energy settings and efficacy matches the findings of Kaushik et al.37

Differences from the Available Literature

Our study reported a small percentage of complications, in contrast to higher rates of hypotony and phthisis bulbi in older studies such as Walland.17 This may reflect improved protocols. The exclusive focus on single treatments isolates the efficacy of a single application, which differs from studies allowing multiple treatments and introduces fewer confounding variables.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that a single application of transscleral cyclodiode laser, performed using UK guideline-recommended parameters, is an effective and well-tolerated treatment for patients with refractory glaucoma. At 12 months posttreatment, a significant and sustained reduction in IOP was achieved in 77.4% of eyes, accompanied by a notable decrease in the number of topical medications and a dramatic reduction in the use of oral acetazolamide. The absence of serious complications, such as hypotony or phthisis, highlights the procedure's safety profile. Importantly, no significant correlation was found between laser energy or duration and treatment success, supporting the growing body of evidence that titrating laser energy based on intraoperative cues, rather than uniformly applying higher powers, may optimize outcomes while minimizing the risk of complications. By focusing solely on patients who underwent a single session of cyclodiode without additional interventions, this study offers a clear view of the standalone effectiveness of the treatment. These findings support the use of transscleral cyclodiode laser as a valuable and minimally invasive option in the management of advanced and treatment-resistant glaucoma, particularly in patient populations less suitable for further surgical intervention.

Clinical Significance

This study highlights the clinical utility of a single session of standardized transscleral cyclodiode laser photocoagulation as an effective and well-tolerated intervention for patients with refractory glaucoma. The laser parameters employed followed current UK National Glaucoma Survey and Wills Eye Institute recommendations, ensuring standardized and reproducible methodology. The absence of a significant correlation between energy levels and treatment success suggests that carefully titrated lower energy settings may be sufficient to achieve desired IOP control, potentially reducing the risk of serious complications, such as hypotony or phthisis. The findings suggest that a single, standardized cyclodiode laser treatment can offer meaningful IOP control with minimal burden, which may translate into improved quality of life and reduced need for intensive follow-up or complex therapeutic regimens.

Therapeutic application of EUS-guided intraportal autologous bone marrow transplantation for decompensated liver cirrhosis
Authors: Ting Cai, Bing Chen, Xin-meng Li, Aojian Deng, Yi-cun Shen, Xue-er Yang, Yang liu, Lun-xi Liang, Xiao-ming Liu, Fen Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and aimsTo compare the efficacy and safety of different treatment regimens for decompensated liver cirrhosis (DLC), focusing on the novel method of endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)-guided intraportal autologous bone marrow transplantation (ABMT).MethodsIn this study, 65 DLC patients were assigned to one of three groups: EUS-POV ABMT (n = 22), PEV ABMT (n = 20), or conventional treatment (n = 23), and followed for 12 months. An exploratory analysis investigated the impact of graft quality (CD34 + cell proportion) on outcomes in the ABMT cohort.ResultsThe EUS-POV group and PEV group that received ABMT showed different degrees of improvement in routine blood tests, transaminases, albumin levels, Child-Pugh scores, and coagulation function compared with the conventional treatment group, the clinical symptoms were relieved, with no apparent adverse reactions. The EUS-POV group was more effective than the PEV group in improving albumin levels and activated partial thromboplastin time (P < 0.05); notably, histopathological improvement was observed in the EUS-POV group. An exploratory subgroup analysis based on CD34 + cell proportion (dichotomized by an ROC-derived cutoff) did not reveal a significant difference in the trajectory of liver function improvement between high and low CD34 groups. Importantly, linear mixed-model analysis revealed that the transplantation route, not the CD34 + cell proportion, was the primary factor associated with therapeutic efficacy (Interaction effect: P = 0.936).ConclusionsEUS-guided intraportal ABMT is a safe and highly effective treatment for DLC, demonstrating clear advantages over peripheral infusion due to superior targeting and sustained clinical benefits. The success of ABMT appears to be influenced more critically by the cell delivery route than by the progenitor cell content of the graft.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13287-025-04740-5.Keywords: Endoscopic ultrasound, Decompensated liver cirrhosis, Portal vein, Bone marrow transplantation, Cell therapy

Introduction

Decompensated liver cirrhosis (DLC) is the end stage of various chronic liver diseases with high lethality. Conventional drug treatment for DLC is characterized by poor therapeutic effect and prognosis. Liver transplantation is the most effective treatment for DLC currently. However, various factors, which comprise a paucity of donor liver organs, a high cost, and multiple postoperative complications, limit its application.

Bone marrow stem cells can differentiate into hepatocyte-like cells under specific conditions, repairing damaged hepatocytes [1–3] and restoring liver function. The advantages of autologous bone marrow include being easily accessible and not requiring in vitro induction of differentiation. After a series of explorations and practices, the technology of autologous bone marrow infusion for the treatment of DLC has achieved gratifying achievements [4–7] and has a wide range of applications. This study was designed to provide more treatment options for patients with DLC. It may also be used as an adjuvant therapy while waiting for the liver source before transplantation.

CD34 is a well-established surface marker for hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs), and its level is a key metric for assessing the quality of a bone marrow graft [8]. As HSPCs have been implicated in tissue repair and regeneration, including potential contributions to liver regeneration [9, 10], we hypothesized that the quality of the autologous bone marrow, as indicated by the CD34 + cell ratio, might influence the therapeutic efficacy of ABMT. Therefore, this study incorporated an exploratory analysis of the CD34 + cell ratio to evaluate its potential as a predictive biomarker.

Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) -guided intraportal vein puncture is a new diagnostic and treatment technology that has emerged in recent years [11]. With the maturation of this technology, approaches such as EUS-guided portal vein pressure measurement [12], EUS-guided fine needle aspiration from portal vein tumor thrombus [13], EUS-guided portal injection chemotherapy [14] have been widely used in the clinic. Given the safety and feasibility of EUS-guided intraportal vein puncture technology, we designed this prospective controlled study to pioneer the combination of EUS-guided intraportal vein puncture and autologous bone marrow infusion in the treatment of DLC. The aim of the present study is to provide more therapeutic choices for patients with DLC, and it can also be used as an adjuvant therapy while waiting for the liver source before liver transplantation.

Methods

Inclusion criteria

Patients with DLC (clinical diagnosis of decompensated liver cirrhosis manifested by chronic liver disease with hypohepatia, ascites and/or esophageal varices, and/or portal hypertension) who were admitted to the Third Xiangya Hospital of Central South University between September 2020 and January 2022; age over 18 years; able to tolerate the endoscopy after evaluation of the anesthesiologist; voluntary and signed the informed consent form.

Exclusion criteria

Pregnancy or lactation; with severe anemia or coagulation dysfunction that cannot be improved by treatment; recently been complicated by gastrointestinal bleeding, failed hemostatic treatment, spontaneous peritonitis, hepatic encephalopathy, hepatorenal syndrome or in the acute phase of infection; mental or neurological disorders; combined with severe cardiac, pulmonary, renal, hematological illness and other diseases; malignancies. In addition, key exclusion criteria included pre-existing portal vein thrombosis (PVT), which was an absolute contraindication for the EUS-guided intraportal infusion procedure. The presence of PVT was screened for in all potential participants via Doppler ultrasonography prior to enrollment.

Patient grouping

A total of 65 patients with DLC were included (Fig. 1). Patients were randomized into one of three groups: the EUS-guided intraportal infusion group (EUS-POV group, N = 22) and the peripheral venous infusion group (PEV group, N = 20) that underwent autologous bone marrow transplantation (ABMT) plus a conventional internal medicine regimen including drugs for anti-HBV virus, liver cell protection, transaminase and jaundice reducing drugs. In the negative control group (conventional treatment group, N = 23), patients received only the conventional medical treatment described above without ABMT.

Bone marrow harvest

Considering the smooth implementation of our previous study [15], we further improved the steps of bone marrow harvest and EUS-guided portal vein puncture. In this study, we again did not use G-CSF before operation due to the risk of splenic rupture [16]. Patients in the EUS-POV and PEV groups were sent to the sterile operating room after preoperative preparation. The left/right posterior iliac spine was selected as the puncture point, followed by routine disinfection and draping. After local anesthesia, a bone marrow puncture was performed, and 60 ml of bone marrow was extracted and transferred to sterile bone marrow infusion bags which contained the filtration device to filter fat and impurities and 20 ml of heparin saline (62.5 U/ml) to prevent bone marrow from coagulating. 1 ml of bone marrow was collected for low cytometry to count the total number of nucleated cells and the proportion of CD34+ cells. The total number of nucleated cells in 60 mL bone marrow was 600–1200 × 106 and the percentage of CD34+ cells ranged from 0.04% to 1.73%. Cell viability was not less than 97–99%. Autologous bone marrow infusion was performed in the same sterile operating room immediately.

EUS-guided portal vein puncture

This step was performed by an experienced physician with extensive experience in EUS operations. Instruments and equipment used in this study include an EUS system (EU-ME2, Olympus Corporation, Tokyo, Japan), linear-array scanning EUS (UCT-260, Olympus Corporation, Tokyo, Japan), and 19G FNB-needle (Cook Medical, pro-core Winston-Salem, NC, USA; here we choose 19G FNB-needle but not 22G FNB-needle for it can inject ABM more smoothly and obtain satisfactory pathological tissue). After intravenous anesthesia, the endoscopic ultrasound was inserted through the mouth to the junction of the gastric fundus and cardia; under EUS guidance, the puncture needle was punctured through the stomach and liver into the left branch of the portal vein; the needle core was slowly removed, and blood was aspirated to confirm the correct needle position within the vessel. The total 80 ml of patient’s fresh autologous bone marrow (60 mL of fresh autologous bone marrow and 20 ml of heparin saline) was injected into the portal vein at an approximate rate of 4 ml/min under continuous ultrasonic monitoring. After the infusion was completed, the needle was flushed with 2 ml of heparinized saline. Then, the needle core was installed, and the puncture needle was removed slowly. After the puncture needle was removed, the puncture point in the left liver was compressed with the front end of the ultrasound probe for about 5 min. It was re-examined to determine the presence of intrahepatic or perihepatic hemorrhage or hematoma before withdrawing the endoscope. After the operation was completed, the patient was closely observed.

Autologous bone marrow infusion through peripheral veins

The collected autologous bone marrow was infused into the median cubital vein through a blood transfusion device. After the infusion was completed, the patient was closely observed.

Liver biopsy

Liver tissues were obtained by 19G FNB-needle puncture under EUS systems when the autologous bone marrow infusion was completed and at the time of 12 months after treatment, they were fixed in formalin solution and sent to the Department of Pathology at the hospital for HE and Masson staining. Improved or worsened liver fibrosis and necroinflammatory activity were assessed by a single pathologist using the Ishak-modified histologic activity index (HAI) scoring system [17]. The necroinflammatory grade is scored from 0 to 18, and the fibrosis stage is ranged from 0 to 6. A histological response was defined as a 2-point or greater reduction from baseline in the Ishak-modified HAI score. No change was defined as a change in HAI score of + 1, -1, or 0, and worsening was defined as an increase in the HAI score of 2 or more points.

Endpoints and follow-up

The endpoint of the follow-up period was 12 months after the treatment. The laboratory test results, clinical symptoms, treatment-related adverse reactions, and postoperative survival before treatment (baseline) and 1, 3, 6, and 12 months after treatment were all examined. Assessment of ascites was primarily performed using color Doppler ultrasound. Computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) were not routinely employed as standardized assessment tools for all patients in this study.

Safety assessment

All patients were monitored for adverse events (AEs). AEs were defined as any unfavorable medical occurrence during the trial, regardless of causal relationship to the intervention. The severity of AEs was graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0 [18–20]. Events of special interest included puncture-related complications (bleeding, infection), PVT, progression of liver failure, hepatic encephalopathy, and gastrointestinal bleeding.

Patient cohort for CD34 + analysis

Of the 42 patients who underwent ABMT (22 in the EUS-POV group and 20 in the PEV group), bone marrow aspirate samples from 27 patients (15 in the EUS-POV group and 12 in the PEV group) were available for CD34 + cell proportion analysis. The primary reasons for missing data were the unavailability of laboratory test results or levels below the detection threshold of the assay. Consequently, all subsequent analyses regarding CD34 + cell proportion are based on this subset of 27 patients.

CD34 + cell analysis

Given the potential role of graft quality, the proportion of CD34 + cells in the bone marrow aspirate was analyzed. To define a clinically relevant cutoff for CD34 + cell proportion, a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed. The outcome of “clinical response” at the 12-month follow-up was defined as an improvement of ≥ 2 points in the Child-Pugh score, a well-established threshold for meaningful clinical improvement in cirrhosis [21]. The optimal cutoff value (0.16%) was determined by maximizing the Youden Index. To rigorously assess whether CD34 + cell proportion was an independent predictor of the longitudinal change in liver function, a linear mixed-effects model was fitted. The model included Child-Pugh score as the dependent variable, with fixed effects for time, CD34 group (dichotomized by the 0.16% cutoff), treatment route, and the critical interaction term between time and CD34 group. A random intercept for each patient was included to account for repeated measures.

Statistical analysis

IBM® SPSS® (version 22.0) software (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, United States) was used for statistical analysis. Numerical variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation. The χ2 test and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) were used to compare the baseline categorical data. One-way ANOVA for repeated measures and least significant difference (LSD) post hoc test was used to compare the difference between baseline data and individual points during the follow-up period. Comparison of differences between groups was performed by the mean relative changes from baseline and independent t-test, or Fisher exact test when necessary. Differences were considered statistically significant with a P-value < 0.05. In subgroup analysis, patients receiving ABMT were divided into Low and High CD34 groups. The dichotomization cutoff (0.16%) was not pre-specified but was derived from the present cohort using ROC curve analysis to maximize the predictive accuracy for a 12-month clinical response (Child-Pugh improvement ≥ 2 points).

Liver function tests

Transaminases and bilirubin

In the conventional treatment group, alanine transaminase (ALT), aspartate transaminase (AST), total bilirubin (TBil), and direct bilirubin (DBil) remained gradually elevated after treatment (Table 3). Yet, AST was significantly increased at 1 year of follow-up compared with that before treatment (P < 0.05), indicating a continuous deterioration of liver function. In the EUS-POV group, AST levels were significantly improved at 1 and 3 months compared to preoperative levels (both P < 0.05). In the PEV group, the improvement of ALT, AST, TBil, and DBil was not as evident as that in the EUS-POV group (P > 0.05). That is, TBil and DBil showed a transient and mild increase at 1 month after the treatment.

Comparing the differences of parameters between the groups before and after the treatment (Supplementary Figure S2), ALT and AST in the EUS-POV group decreased significantly at 6 months compared with that of the conventional treatment group (P < 0.05 and 0.01, respectively). In the PEV group, DBil showed an obvious increase at 1 month compared with the EUS-POV group (P < 0.05).

The serum albumin levels, ascites, and Child-Pugh scores

There was no significant improvement in serum albumin (ALB) levels after treatment in the conventional treatment group, and ascites and Child-Pugh scores were gradually increased (Table 3; Fig. 2A-C). In the EUS-POV group, ALB remained significantly elevated from 1 to 12 months postoperatively (P < 0.05). Ascites and Child-Pugh scores showed a significant decrease compared with the preoperative period at 3 months and 1 month postoperatively, respectively (P < 0.05, 0.01, respectively). In the PEV group, ALB increased significantly at 1 month (P < 0.05), and then gradually decreased to the preoperative level; Child-Pugh scores decreased significantly at 1 month compared with the preoperative period (P < 0.05) and then gradually increased to the preoperative level; the changes of ascites were similar to those of Child-Pugh score (not statistically significant).

The differences in the changes of the above tests between the groups were shown in Fig. 2D-F. ALB in the EUS-POV group increased significantly from 1 month compared with that in the conventional treatment group (P < 0.01), and ascites and Child-Pugh scores decreased significantly from the 1 month (P < 0.01 and 0.001, respectively). The trend of ALB, ascites, and Child-Pugh scores in the PEV group was similar to that of the EUS-POV group at 1 month. However, the improvement of ascites lacked statistical significance (P < 0.05, >0.05, and < 0.05, respectively). Figure S3 visually shows each group’s composition of Child-Pugh scores at different follow-up time points.

Coagulation function

The indicators of coagulation function in the conventional treatment group showed no significant improvement after treatment (Table 3). Prothrombin time (PT), thrombin time (TT), and fibrinogen (FIB) in the EUS-POV group showed a tendency to significantly improve compared with the preoperative period in 1, 12, and 6 months after the intervention, respectively (P < 0.05). The PEV group mainly showed improvement in TT at 6 months (P < 0.05).

Comparing the differences between the groups before and after treatment of each index (Supplementary Figure S4), PT and activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT) in the EUS-POV group decreased significantly at 1 month and 12 months, and TT at 12 months after surgery, compared with the conventional treatment group (P < 0.05). TT in the PEV group decreased significantly at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months compared with the conventional treatment group (P < 0.05), whereas APTT increased significantly at 1 month compared with the EUS-POV group (P < 0.05).

Pathological changes of the EUS-POV group

Liver tissue biopsy was performed on patients in the EUS-POV group, and the pathological changes of the liver before treatment and at 12 months after treatment were compared (Fig. 3). Overall, an improvement of the HAI score was seen in 76.19% (16/21) of the recipients, no change in HAI score was seen in 23.81% (5/21) of the patients, no patient’s HAI score was worse than before.

Exploratory analysis: impact of CD34 + cell proportion

This analysis was performed on the 27 patients with available CD34 + data. To investigate the influence of graft quality, an exploratory analysis was conducted on the CD34 + cell proportion. The optimal cutoff value, determined by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis using a 12-month clinical response (defined as a ≥ 2-point decrease in the Child-Pugh score) as the state variable, was 0.16% (AUC = 0.68, sensitivity = 73.3%, specificity = 66.7%) (Fig. 4A, B). Based on this cutoff, 15 patients who received ABMT were classified into the High CD34 group and 12 into the Low CD34 group.

A linear mixed-effects model was employed to assess the association between CD34 group and the longitudinal change in liver function. The analysis revealed no significant interaction effect between the CD34 group and time (F(4, 31.68) = 0.20, p = 0.936). This indicates that the trajectory of Child-Pugh score improvement over the 12-month follow-up was not significantly different between the two groups (Fig. 4C). The model-adjusted estimated marginal means, demonstrating parallel trends, are shown in Fig. 4C, and the individual trajectories are presented in Fig. 4D. In contrast, the treatment route remained a significant independent predictor of outcome in the model (F(1, 22.15) = 6.63, p = 0.017).

Discussion

Mesenchymal stem cells possess the capabilities of tissue regeneration and self-renewal, and can repair the liver through mechanisms such as differentiation into hepatocyte-like cells, immunomodulation, and anti-fibrosis [22]. However, liver regeneration requires stem cells and other stromal cells. Autologous bone marrow contains mesenchymal stem cells, hematopoietic stem cells, endothelial progenitor cells, and stromal cells, which can synergistically exert beneficial effects on DLC [23, 24]. Therefore, whole bone marrow transplantation that “seeds with the soil” should be performed [25].

In the clinical practice of bone marrow transplantation to treat DLC, the infusion route of bone marrow, the number of cells used for transplantation, and homing capability may affect the efficacy of transplantation. Low homing efficiency is a major limitation of this therapy. Therefore, selecting an optimal infusion route and improving homing efficiency is critical.

The clinical methods of bone marrow cell infusion mainly include peripheral vein [7], percutaneous transhepatic portal vein route under abdominal ultrasonic guidance [26], interventional radiology-guided hepatic arterial infusion [25], and surgical placement of implantable portal vein access ports [27]. In contrast, the EUS-guided intraportal vein method is clearly superior to previous approaches. Indeed, the portal vein supplies 80% of the blood flow to the liver. Compared to peripheral veins and hepatic arteries, its massive, sluggish blood flow is more favorable for cell homing and colonization. Besides, EUS is not interfered with by ascites and bowel gas, thus making it possible to display intrahepatic and perihepatic blood vessels clearly. To help with the diagnosis and treatment of DLC, patients can also have a gastroscopy, liver biopsy, esophageal-gastric variceal ligation, and other procedures done simultaneously.

Using cellular magnetic resonance imaging, Zhong et al. [28] found the portal vein route is more conducive than the tail vein route for the migration of mesenchymal stem cells to the liver of cirrhotic rats, which is consistent with the study of Li et al. [29]. Moreover, Hu et al. [30] believe that intraportal bone marrow transplantation is the best strategy to improve liver function in patients with liver cirrhosis.

We innovatively combined EUS-guided intraportal vein puncture technology with autologous bone marrow infusion and compared it with peripheral intravenous infusion of autologous bone marrow and conventional treatment. We found that EUS-guided intraportal vein infusion of autologous bone marrow has apparent advantages in improving patients’ routine blood tests, liver function, and coagulation function compared to conventional treatment and peripheral venous infusions of autologous bone marrow. Most indicators can still be maintained at a satisfactory level one year after operation, with no noticeable adverse effects, and the liver pathology has also been ameliorated. The peripheral venous bone marrow infusion has less effective targeting, a more minimal increase in liver function, and a shorter maintenance period than the portal vein approach, despite the fact that it can improve several indices of DLC. Therefore, it is not recommended as the first choice for bone marrow transplantation in the treatment of DLC. Liver transplantation plays an undoubtedly vital role in the treatment of DLC. Nonetheless, infusion of autologous bone marrow through the portal vein under EUS guidance is also safe and effective in treating DLC. It can slow down disease progression, improve the quality of life, and extend the window of opportunity for patients who planning to undergo liver transplantation.

In an exploratory analysis, we investigated whether the quality of the bone marrow graft, as reflected by the CD34 + cell proportion, influenced therapeutic outcomes. Contrary to the hypothesis that a higher stem cell dose would enhance regeneration [8], our data did not show a significant association between CD34 + cell proportion and the rate of liver function improvement. This suggests that within the context of autologous BM transplantation for cirrhosis, the efficiency of cell delivery and homing to the liver site (which is superior via the portal route) may be a more critical determinant of success than the initial dosage of progenitor cells in the graft. This finding warrants further investigation in larger, dedicated studies.

This study has several limitations. First, it was conducted at a single center with a relatively small sample size. Second, although pre-existing portal vein thrombosis was an exclusion criterion, the incidence of new-onset PVT was not prospectively monitored as a predefined endpoint. A retrospective review did not identify any symptomatic cases, but future trials should incorporate standardized radiographic assessments (e.g., contrast-enhanced CT/MRI) to definitively evaluate this risk. Despite these limitations, our findings provide promising preliminary evidence for the safety and efficacy of EUS-guided intraportal ABMT. Future multi-center, large-scale randomized controlled trials are warranted to confirm these results. Furthermore, mechanistic studies exploring biomarkers to track the homing and engraftment of transplanted cells could elucidate the underlying therapeutic mechanisms and optimize treatment strategies.

Conclusion

In conclusion, EUS-guided intraportal infusion of autologous bone marrow is a safe and effective therapeutic strategy for patients with decompensated liver cirrhosis, demonstrating significant advantages over peripheral venous infusion in terms of sustained biochemical and clinical improvement. The transplantation route appears to be a paramount factor for success.

Remembrance of our Founder and Editor Emeritus
Authors: Surendra Marur
Published: 2026

Dr. Robert Ferguson, a distinguished clinical internist and educator whose leadership and innovations in medical education shaped generations of physicians, passed away on October 22, 2025, at the age of 79.

Dr. Ferguson earned both his BA and MD degrees from Cornell University and Cornell University Medical College in 1972. He completed his internship and residency at North Shore University Hospital in New York, followed by a Chief Residency in Medicine at Mount Sinai Hospital in Hartford, Connecticut. He later served as Chief of Medicine there for nearly 15 years.

In 1992, Dr. Ferguson moved to Baltimore to become Chief of Medicine at Union Memorial Hospital, a role he held for two decades. During this time, he also served as Program Director of the Internal Medicine Residency Program, and later joined the University of Maryland School of Medicine as a Clinical Professor in the Department of Medicine. In these positions, he profoundly influenced countless medical students and residents.

Dr. Ferguson’s impact extended nationally. In 2000, he was the inaugural leader of the Community Hospital Interest Group and was elected to the APDIM Council, serving from 2004 to 2007. In these roles, he identified key challenges faced by internal medicine training programs in community hospitals—such as limited opportunities to share best practices, difficulty recruiting faculty for teaching, lack of research infrastructure, and barriers to meeting scholarly activity requirements. To address these issues, he established the Community Hospital Education Research Network (CHERN) and launched the Journal of Community Hospital Internal Medicine Perspectives (JCHIMP) in 2011.

In 2014, Dr. Ferguson joined the faculty at Greater Baltimore Medical Center, continuing his work with JCHIMP while teaching pre-clinical medical students from the University of Maryland School of Medicine and mentoring GBMC’s internal medicine residents until his retirement in 2024. Now, medical students in their clinical years from the University of Maryland find GBMC an exceptional medical experience and JCHIMP remains a vibrant peer reviewed pubmed-indexed medical journal published six times per year.

His contributions were widely recognized. In 2014, he was awarded Mastership by the American College of Physicians, and in 2018, he received the APDIM Dema C. Daley Founders Award for his leadership in internal medicine education.

Dr. Ferguson educated, inspired, and collaborated with countless individuals at local, state, and national levels. His philosophy emphasized innovation, collaboration, and commitment to learners and patients. We are deeply grateful for his extraordinary contributions to medical education and the lives he touched.

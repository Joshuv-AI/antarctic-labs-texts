Background

Cardiac metastases are uncommon and frequently present a diagnostic dilemma. Endomyocardial biopsy (EMB), once limited to transplant surveillance, has become the gold standard for definitive etiological diagnosis of intracardiac masses and can be safely performed in experienced centers, even in high-risk clinical scenarios.

Take-Home Message

•This case illustrates a rare presentation of cardiac metastasis from colorectal cancer and highlights the pivotal role of multimodality imaging and intracardiac echocardiography–guided biopsy in the diagnostic work-up of intracardiac masses, as well as the importance of dedicated operators for sampling unusual and high-risk cases.

Case Summary

A 55-year-old man with ulcerative colitis was diagnosed with rectal adenocarcinoma and treated with chemoradiotherapy with capecitabine and oxaliplatin and resection (ypT3 ypN0 ypM0). During oncological follow-up, an increase in carcinoembryonic antigen levels prompted further investigation. Colonoscopy excluded local recurrence. Whole-body computed tomography revealed a filling defect within the right ventricle (RV).

Transthoracic echocardiography showed a heterogeneous mass attached to the RV and preserved RV function. Contrast-enhanced echocardiography showed contrast uptake within the lesion. Further assessment with cardiac magnetic resonance confirmed a 36 × 48 mm RV mass arising from the free wall, with endoluminal growth, isointense signal on T1- and T2-weighted sequences, and contrast enhancement.

With no other evidence of malignancy, the patient was referred for EMB at our center for histologic diagnosis, performed by a dedicated operator experienced in tissue sampling from all cardiac chambers, including tumors. Given the unusual location of the mass and the risk of perforation from involvement of the RV free wall, the biopsy was performed under intracardiac echocardiography guidance. Multiple tissue samples were successfully obtained from the mass (Figure 1, Video 1, Video 2, Video 3, Video 4) without complications, and the patient remained awake throughout the procedure.

Histopathologic analysis confirmed cardiac metastasis from colorectal adenocarcinoma.

Discussion

Cardiac metastasis from colorectal cancer is rare, and isolated RV involvement without extracardiac disease is exceptional. Multimodality imaging aids in lesion characterization, but histopathologic confirmation is essential for definitive diagnosis and management.1,2

EMB is pivotal in selected cardiac conditions, including unexplained acute heart failure, myocarditis, cardiomyopathies, infiltrative diseases, and cardiac tumors. Although invasive, EMB is generally safe when performed at experienced centers. Major complications occur in approximately 1% to 5% of cases, while periprocedural mortality is very low (0%-0.07%). Procedural safety is strongly influenced by institutional and operator experience, with lower complication rates in high-volume centers.3

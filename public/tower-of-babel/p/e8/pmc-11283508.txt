Attention mechanism-enhanced graph convolutional neural network for unbalanced lithology identification
Authors: Aiting Wang, Shuyu Zhao, Kai Xie, Chang Wen, Hong-ling Tian, Jian-Biao He, Wei Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, we propose a novel method for identifying lithology using an attention mechanism-enhanced graph convolutional neural network (AGCN). The aim of this method is to address the limitations of traditional approaches that evaluate unbalanced lithology by improving the identification of thin layers and small samples, while providing reliable data support for reservoir evaluation. To achieve this goal, we begin by using Principal Component Analysis (PCA) with maximum and minimum distance clustering (Max-min-distance) to correct the logging curves, which compensates for the low resolution of thin layers and enhances the accuracy of stratigraphic representation. Subsequently, we transform the logging data into graph-structured data by connecting distance similarity points and feature similarity points of the logging samples. We then use the graph convolutional network (GCN) to identify lithology, leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data to enhance the ability to identify lithology in small sample datasets. Additionally, our model incorporates a channel and spatial attention mechanism that assigns weights to the graph structure during lithology identification, improving the model’s capability to discern differences across samples. To evaluate the performance of our model, we constructed a lithology dataset comprising five wells and conducted experiments. The results indicate that our approach achieves a maximum accuracy of 97.67%, surpassing the performance of a singlestructure model in lithology identification. In conclusion, our proposed method provides a promising and effective approach for unbalanced lithology identification, significantly improving accuracy levels.Subject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In the realm of petroleum exploration and development, lithology identification play a crucial role in shaping the accuracy of geological modeling and reservoir determination1,2. While cuttings logging and core drilling are conventional methods for identifying lithology, they are both costly and time-consuming. In contrast, logging is widely favored due to its efficiency3.

With the gradual depletion of conventional natural gas and oil reservoirs, the petroleum industry has shifted its focus towards developing unconventional reservoirs4. However, large sample of unbalanced lithologies in unconventional reservoirs, the non-linear noise interference of logging equipment presents a significant challenge to the reliability of traditional lithology interpretation methods in unconventional reservoirs5. Moreover, traditional classification algorithms struggle to meet the demands of complex and heterogeneous environments such as unconventional reservoirs6,7.

In response to these challenges, machine learning has emerged as an indispensable tool for lithology identification8,9. Various machine learning algorithms offer distinct advantages in lithology identification. For example, the Potential Scale Reduction Factor (PSRF) algorithm, which integrates mean shift and Random Forest (RF) algorithms, is particularly well-suited for borehole lithology identification10. Li et al.1 proposed utilizing feature decomposition, selection, and transformation to enhance the effectiveness of feature extraction. Ren et al.3 combined fuzzy theory, decision trees, and the K-means++ algorithm to minimize the interference of redundant information on identification results.11 employed support vector machines for automatic lithology identification. Other methods include but are not limited to Support Vector Machine (SVM)12,13, Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost)14, convolutional neural networks (CNN)15,16, and recurrent neural networks (RNN)17. Supervised learning methods eliminate human factors in lithology identification, thereby enhancing accuracy and efficiency. However, these methods necessitate a substantial amount of labeled data to build models and may yield subpar results if data is scarce or class distribution is uneven18.

To surmount this challenge, semi-supervised learning has been applied to lithology identification19. This approach leverages both labeled and unlabeled samples for training20,21. Asghar et al.22 and Yuan et al.23 employed Euclidean distance in active learning to eliminate similar samples and utilized unlabeled logging data. Wang et al.24 enhanced Tri-training with tree-based models and successfully applied it to semi-supervised radar image classification. Lu et al.25 utilized Graph SAmple and aggreGatE (GraphSAGE) to handle spatial data structures, capturing spatiotemporal relationships to enhance the accuracy and interpretability of classification tasks. Zhao et al.26 proposed a classification-enhanced semi-supervised generative adversarial network model (CE-SGAN) using a pseudo-labeling mechanism to enhance classification. Previous methods for addressing the imbalanced class issue in lithology prediction have primarily been designed for conventional reservoirs, where lithology classes are typically more balanced and distinct. However, unconventional reservoirs present unique challenges, including large samples of unbalanced lithologies, thin layers27, and non-linear noise interference of logging equipment. These characteristics require specialized approaches for lithology prediction that can accurately capture the diverse and complex lithologies present in unconventional reservoirs. Traditional methods may struggle to effectively address these challenges and accurately predict the full spectrum of lithologies in unconventional reservoirs. Therefore, the development of tailored machine learning and semi-supervised learning techniques specific to unconventional reservoirs is essential for improving the accuracy of lithology prediction in these complex and heterogeneous environments.

To address these issues, we have devised a unbalanced lithology identification method based on attention mechanism-enhanced graph convolutional neural network. The objectives of this study are outlined as follows:

To tackle the low resolution issue stemming from thin layers in logging, we have adopted a PCA improved by Max-min-distance. This method extracts the primary information from the data, eliminates noise and redundant information, thereby enhancing the resolution of logging curves. It transforms the original data into fewer principal components, reduces the dimensionality of the data, and simplifies the analysis process.

Methodology

In this section, we first present a general overview of the proposed algorithm’s flow and, subsequently, elaborate on the algorithm theory of Max-min-distance, PCA and AGCN.

The algorithmic process of this study can be divided into three parts: in part A, logging curves are corrected using PCA improved by Max-min-distance to reduce logging errors caused by low resolution in thin layers. In Part B, the logging data are converted into graphically structured data using static distance graphs and dynamic distance graphs, to maximize the use of labeled and unlabeled data. In Part C, the static distance graphs and dynamic distance graphs are input into a graph convolutional neural network and the weights are adjusted dynamically by an attention mechanism to improve the lithology identification. Logging curve data correction

In actual exploration, the logging data obtained represents the average lithological response of the measurement probe over the range of the sounding, and not the true logging values at the point of measurement28. This means that the lithological information of a thin layer may be concealed by the surrounding lithology, leading to low resolution of the logging curve’s thin layer. This phenomenon is called the boundary effect of the logging curve29.

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is a dimensionality reduction technique commonly used to simplify datasets by transforming them into a new coordinate system while preserving most of the variance in the data. In the context of logging curve data correction, PCA aims to enhance the quality of the data by removing noise and extracting meaningful patterns (Wang et al. 2020).

However, the linear transformation performed by PCA may not adequately capture nonlinear relationships present in the data, especially when dealing with small, subtle thin layers in lithologies. These nonlinearities can be crucial for accurately characterizing lithological responses, and PCA’s linear transformation might not fully preserve them.

This is where the Max-min-distance approach comes into play. Its objective is to select objects as far apart as possible as clustering centers, addressing a challenge in K-means methods where cluster seeds may be selected too close together during initial value selection30. By doing so, it effectively identifies and emphasizes the differences between different classes or formations in the logging curve data. This approach is particularly beneficial when dealing with unbalanced samples or features from different formations, as it ensures that the clustering criteria consider the specific characteristics of each class. As a result, the Max-min-distance approach improves upon traditional PCA by providing a more nuanced and accurate representation of the underlying lithological features, including small, subtle thin layers, thus enhancing the overall quality of the corrected curve data. Lithology identification using AGCN

In order to achieve adaptive adjustment of the weighting relationships between dynamic and static graphs, we introduced spatial and channel attention mechanisms33. Traditional methods for feature extraction often struggle to effectively capture subtle connections within uneven geological information, as it is typically localized and spatially correlate34. However, by incorporating a spatial focusing mechanism, our model can prioritize important features within unbalanced geological regions, thereby enhancing the expressiveness of these features. Furthermore, unbalanced lithology is influenced by multiple factors, and logging curves at different depths or locations may correspond to different geological features. To address this complexity, we utilize a channel focusing mechanism to dynamically learn the significance of each channel and improve the model’s ability to capture key information about unbalanced geology. Additionally, traditional classification methods face challenges such as sample imbalance and overfitting when dealing with limited samples of unbalanced geology, where categories exhibit minimal differences. However, by combining spatial and channel attention mechanisms, our model amplifies the distinctions between lithological samples, enabling it to sensitively discern subtle differences in thinly layered samples and enhance its capability to recognize small sample sets. The optimized diagram of the attention mechanism is depicted in Fig. 4:

Within the graph, we not only have data associated with each node but also the network structure itself, indicating how the nodes are interconnected. In the context of identifying unbalanced rockiness using a graph structure, we encounter highly similar nodes as well as nodes with extremely low or high heights, making it challenging to extract meaningful information about the network structure. To tackle this issue, we introduce Graph convolutional network (GCN).The graphical neural network structure is shown in Fig. 5.

Graph convolutional neural network architecture for lithology recognition.

GCN is a powerful semi-supervised learning neural network based on the principle of neighbourhood feature information transfer35, which is achieved by weighted averaging the features of each node with those of its neighbouring nodes, where the weights are determined by the weights of the connecting edges as well as the degree of the node. In this way, each node is able to aggregate information from its neighbours and integrate it into its own feature representation, thus achieving information exchange and feature fusion between nodes. Moreover, it demonstrates strong generalization capabilities when handling various types of rock data, providing accurate rock classification outcomes for unbalanced lithologies.

Conclusion

Based on the analysis of the experimental results, several important conclusions can be drawn regarding the method proposed in this study.

Firstly, to address the issue of low resolution in thin-layer logging curves, the Max-Min-distance-PCA method was employed for curve correction. The corrected model consistently outperformed the uncorrected data model, highlighting the crucial significance of preprocessing techniques in enhancing the quality and reliability of input data.

Secondly, in this research, the transformation of logging data into a graph structure based on dynamic and static distance graphs was carried out. By incorporating information from similar depths and feature similarity points, the recognition ability of the model was significantly enhanced, particularly for small sample data. Subsequently, the utilization of a graph convolutional network (GCN) for predicting the graph data resulted in substantial accuracy improvements. These findings underscore the effectiveness of leveraging the inherent spatial relationships within logging data through graph-based representations.

Additionally, the introduction of channel and spatial attention mechanisms expanded the discrimination between samples and dynamically adjusted the strength of connecting relationships in the graph structure. The incorporation of attention mechanisms led to an impressive model accuracy of up to 97.67%, illustrating the importance of selectively focusing on relevant features and enhancing the discriminative ability of the model.

In summary, the AGCN model proposed in this study exhibits enhanced stability and reliability in non-equilibrium lithology recognition, specifically in scenarios involving thin layers and small samples. In practical reservoir evaluation, the model only requires adjustment of network parameters without modifying the network structure, rendering it highly versatile and applicable.

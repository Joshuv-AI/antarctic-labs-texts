Empyema necessitans
Authors: Anu Menon, David Wisa, Chenyang Zhan, Mohit Chawla, Or Kalchiem‐Dekel
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Key messageEmpyema necessitans should be suspected in any patient presenting with constitutional symptoms, pleural effusion, and a subcutaneous chest wall mass. Thoracic sonography is a readily available tool, which can expedite diagnosis and timely management.Keywords: computed tomography, empyema necessitans, thoracic sonography

A 78‐year‐old man presented with a 1‐week history of tender back swelling. One year earlier, he underwent thoracoscopy with right lower lobe resection for lung cancer. Thoracic sonography followed by computed tomography (CT) revealed subcutaneous and pleural fluid pockets communicating through the chest wall (Figure 1). Drainage catheters placed into both pockets yielded purulent fluid, growing Streptococcus pneumoniae. Systemic antibiotics, totaling 3 months in duration, led to rapid symptom improvement as well as radiographic resolution 4 weeks later.

Sonographic and CT imaging revealing empyema necessitans with an abnormal communication (*) between the pleural space (P) fluid pocket and the subcutaneous (SC) fluid pocket. (A) Sonographic view using a curvilinear ultrasound probe at 7 MHz. (B) Axial CT image. (C) Coronal reformatted CT image. (D) Sagittal reformatted CT image. (E + F) Axial and coronal reformatted CT images obtained 4 weeks post‐discharge showing resolution of the intrapleural and soft‐tissue fluid collections.

Empyema necessitans is a rare complication of empyema caused by decompression of pus from the pleural space into the subcutaneous tissue of the chest wall, forming a pathologic communication. 1 The most common presentation is of a focal, enlarging chest wall swelling. 1 Prior thoracic surgery may be a risk factor due to surgery‐related compromise of the chest wall. CT chest with contrast is an invaluable diagnostic study; however, thoracic ultrasonography can serve as a readily‐available diagnostic modality (Panel A, Figure 1). Consensus on best management is lacking; therapy typically includes antimicrobials and percutaneous drainage. Intrapleural fibrinolytics and surgery are considered on a case‐by‐case basis. 2

We present a case of empyema necessitans, 1 year after thoracoscopic surgery, in which thoracic sonography served as an expeditious diagnostic tool.

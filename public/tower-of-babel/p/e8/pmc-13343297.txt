Maternal Satisfaction With Delivery Services and Associated Factors at Public Health Centers in Horro Guduru Wollega Zone 2024: Institutional‐Based Cross‐Sectional Study
Authors: Abdi Fikadu, Firezer Belay Keno, Worku Fikadu, Emiru Merdassa, Lencho Kajela Solbana, Debelo Etana, Segni Mulugeta Tafasa, Wabi Tamasgen, Adisu Tafari Shama, Desalegn Wirtu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and AimMaternal satisfaction is a multidimensional concept that encompasses satisfaction between the personal and the physical environment of the delivery room, and it is one indicator of the quality of healthcare. There is a limited study done at health center as a whole Ethiopia and there is no evidence about the problem in this study area. The purpose of this study was to assess the extent of maternal satisfaction with delivery services and related characteristics among mothers who gave birth in the Horro Guduru Wollega Zone public health centers in western Ethiopia in 2024.MethodsAn institution‐based cross‐sectional study design was conducted among 384 participants in selected health centers of Horro Guduru Wollega zones from January 08 to March 09, 2024. Systematic random sampling technique was used, and a tool was prepared using reviewing different literatures. Multivariable logistic regression was used to identify factors associated with maternal satisfaction.ResultsThis study shows overall level of maternal satisfaction with delivery services was 45.1% with 95% CI (40.3, 49.2). Moreover, being housewives (AOR = 2.99 CI 1.52, 5.90), waiting health care provider less than 50 min (AOR = 2.31 CI 2.22, 4.38), asking permission before applying any procedures and examination (AOR = 4.44, CI 2.67, 7.39) and pain management during delivery (AOR = 7.27, CI 3.02, 17.48) were factors significantly associated with mothers’ satisfaction with care during delivery.Conclusion and RecommendationLess than half of mothers were satisfied with skilled delivery services and being a housewife; waiting time, pain management, and asking permission before applying any procedure and examination were factors associated with mother's satisfaction. To raise patient satisfaction, health center administrators and medical staff must provide patient‐focused services.Keywords: delivery service, Horro Guduru Wollega, maternal satisfaction, western Ethiopia

Background

The problem of poor maternal satisfaction is one of contributing factor for maternal‐ related death globally [1]. Research indicates that the degree of maternal satisfaction with delivery service provided during labor and delivery differs across various regions globally; including research done in Pakistan 61% [2], in Jordan Hospitals 17.8% [3], and result from Kenya shows satisfaction with Delivery Services was 54.5% [4]. A diverse range of findings concerning mothers’ satisfaction with the care provided during labor and delivery was identified in Ethiopia. A result from St. Paul Millennium Medical College Hospital was 19% [5], result from University of Gondar Referral Hospital was 47.6% [6], and study from public hospitals of North Shoa Zone was 28.2% [7].

Delivery care services affects mothers satisfaction with immediate and long‐term use of healthcare services [8]. Once a mother is unsatisfied with the delivery services she receives, she may choose to give birth to her unborn child at home with a traditional birth attendant instead, which increases the risk of different disease and increases the risk of maternal death [9]. Adverse experiences, like traumatic childbirth, can cause women to harbor painful, angry, or fearful memories that increase their vulnerability to mental health issues [8, 9].

Factors including the existence of cultural customs, discussing the delivery location with a health worker during ANC, obtaining consent prior to the procedure, encouraging companions to stay with mothers, never leaving mothers alone or unattended [8, 10, 11], and waiting times were factors associated with mothers satisfaction in Ethiopia [9].

The Ethiopian government has been attempting to increase basic healthcare services’ accessibility throughout the country as a part of an initiative by improving access to maternal health services. However, there is a long way to go towards achieving Sustainable Development Goal on reduction of maternal mortality [12, 13]. Despite the Ethiopian government's attempts to provide access to high‐quality maternal health care through various programs, the low standard of treatment has drawn criticism [13].

Although research on mother satisfaction and associated with it has been conducted in several Ethiopian hospitals, there is limited study done at health center and there was no study conducted at Horo Guduru Wollega zone. In addition, variables related to mothers professional preference to attend delivery services were added.

Moreover, the study's finding will offer scientific evidence regarding mother's satisfaction with delivery care in public health centers in Horo Guduru Wollega Zone and assist in providing information for policymakers. Furthermore, this research will serve as a foundational dataset for upcoming studies.

Methods and Materials

Study Area and Period

This study was conducted in Horo Guduru Wollega zone from January 08, 2024 to March 09, 2024. Horo Guduru Wollega zone is located in the Oromia Regional State to the west of Addis Ababa, the capital city of Ethiopia. Shambu, the capital of Horo Guduru Wollega zone is located at 315 km away from Addis Ababa. The zone has a total population of more than 862,369 of which around 422,560 are men and are 439,808 women. Currently, there are 1 General hospital and 2 district Hospitals, 50 health centers, and 187 health posts providing maternal and child health care services in the Zone. Hospitals and Health centers provide delivery services (HGW Zonal Health Department Woreda base plan report 2024).

Study Design

A cross‐sectional study design based in an institution was carried out.

Source Population

Mothers who gave birth at all public health centers in Horo Guduru Wollega zone were source of population.

Study Population

Mothers who gave birth at selected public health centers throughout the data collecting period were chosen by systematic selection as the study population.

Eligibility Criteria

Inclusion Criteria

Mothers who gave birth at the selected public health centers during the research period were included.

Exclusion Criteria

Postnatal mothers who experienced mental or severe health issues, as well as those referred from selected health centers to hospitals due to complications along with mothers who had cesarean deliveries were excluded.

Independent/Explanatory Variable

Individual and obstetrics factors: Age, education status, occupation status, perceived provider competence, obstetric history, wanted status of pregnancy; Structural factors: Infrastructure accessibility, hygienic conditions of medical facilities, accessibility to medications, supplies, and services, availability and sufficiency of human resources, and sex of service providers; Process variables include waiting times, pain management, privacy, confidentiality, and client‐provider communication, delivery position of mothers and asking permission before applying any procedure.

Operational Definition

Maternal satisfaction: In terms of the quality of care (process, result, and physical environment), mothers reported their level of satisfaction with health care services [11, 14].

Satisfied: Mothers who scored 75% or above on the 32 Patient Satisfaction Questionnaire will be classified as “satisfied” for their overall satisfaction level. Responses of “very satisfied” and “satisfied” will be classed as satisfied [14, 15].

Unsatisfied: Mothers with an overall satisfaction score of less than 75% were classified as “unsatisfied.” “Very dissatisfied,” “dissatisfied,” and “neutral” responses are all categorized as unsatisfied [14, 15].

Privacy: Measures taken to assure privacy of laboring mothers by screened area, giving private room, and keeping their personal information [14].

Emotional support: Verbal affirmation, acceptance, and support during time of stress by health care provider or companion for laboring mothers [11].

Provider competence: Mothers’ reported opinion or view about health care providers’ ability and confidence during labor and delivery, and is measured by five Likert scale.

Availability of medicine and supplies: the presence of oral or parenteral antibiotics drugs, parenteral oxytoxic drugs, parenteral anti convulsant and assisted vaginal delivery (vacuum extraction) [16].

Pain management: reducing pain by nonpharmacology (rubbing back) and pharmacologically by giving pethidine and other drugs [16].

Data Collection Tool and Procedure

A standardized questionnaire with three elements, modified from the Donabedian quality assessment framework and other comparable studies, was translated to local language (Afan Oromo) and used to collect data from January 8 to March 09, 2024. The first one included the socio‐demographic features of mothers, next section covered the medical history of mothers giving birth, and the final section included the respondents’ satisfaction with the method, and results of the healthcare facility. It has delivered via a 5‐point Likert scale [14].

Data Quality Assurance

Training was given for five Diploma Nurses (working in other health center's) for 2 days on purpose of study, procedures of data collection, objectives of the study, ethical issue, use of measurement scales, and how to approach the study subjects by investigator. A pre‐test was conducted on 5% [17] of the total sample size at a place out of study area Bako town and Cronbach's alpha coefficient result is 0.776. The data collected during the pretest was not included in the final analysis.

Data Analysis and Process

Data were entered to Epi Data version 3.1 and analyzed using SPSS (Statistical Package for Social Science) version 25. A variable that has missing value was not included in binary logistic regression. A variable that has p‐value < 0.25 on bivariable analysis was candidate to a multivariable logistic regression model. Finally, the finding of multivariable logistic regression model was reported with 95% CI at p‐value < 0.05 as a cut off points considered statistically significant. Multi‐collinearity test was performed to see the presence of collinearity among independent variables using variance inflation factor (VIF, 1.010–1.2221) and Hosmer and Lemshow model of good fitness was checked in the final model and indicated with (p = 0.51).

Factors Associated With Mothers Satisfaction on Delivery Service

This study identified mothers who were housewives were 2.99 of odds as compared to government employee (AOR = 2.99, 95% CI: 1.52,5.90) and those mothers who were asked permission before applying any procedures and examination were 4.44 more likely to be satisfied than those who weren't asked (AOR = 4.44, 95% CI: 2.67, 7.39).

Mothers whose pain had been managed during labor and delivery were 7.27 times more satisfied than those whose pain hadn't been managed (AOR = 7.27, 95%CI: 3.02, 17.48) and those spent less than 15 min waiting to be seen by the health care provider were 2.31 more likely to be satisfied than those who spent greater than 15 min (AOR = 2.31, 95% CI 1.22, 4.38) (Table 6).

Discussion

The proportion of mother's satisfaction with delivery services was 45.1% (95% CI: 40.3%, 49.2%). This study is similar with study done in Sri Lanka 48% [18] and University of Gondar Referral Hospital, 47.6% [6]. However, this study is higher than study done at Jordan hospital 17.8% [3], predictors study in Asmara Public Hospitals 20.8% [19] and St. Paul Millennium Medical College 19% [5]. This difference may be due to mothers differing expectations, study period, socio‐demographic and study setting of countries, raised awareness, decrease work load of health care providers and a better care [8, 10, 17, 20].

The result of this study was low as compared to west Shoa Zone Public health Facilities 60.8% [11], public health centers in Yeka sub city [8] and public health facilities at Adama town 74.8 [10]. The possible explanation for this may be due to subjective nature of satisfaction, type of health facilities. Another reason might be that the majority of those studies were carried out in hospitals with a variety of experts, including specialized care, and adequate services, supplies, and human resources [8, 11, 21].

This study revealed occupation status of household, time spent waiting to be seen by the health care provider, asking permission before applying any procedures and examination and pain management during delivery were factors associated with maternal satisfaction with delivery services.

Housewives were more likely to be satisfied than mother mothers who were governmental employees. This study was in line with study conducted in Nepal [21] and public health centers in Yeka Sub City [8]. Additionally, women who waited less than 15 min to visit a health care practitioner were more satisfied than mothers who waited longer. It is similar with studies completed at public health facilities in eastern Ethiopia and the Amhara region and St. Paul Millennium Medical College [5, 9, 22]. The possible explanation for this may be long waiting period resulted in negative perception of care, frustration, and absence of reassurance.

In this study, mothers who were asked consent before the procedure and examination were 4.4 odds of satisfying with delivery services. This study is in line with study done at west Shoa zone public health facilities [11]. The possible explanation for this might be asking for consent increase positive birth experience, respects the patient's autonomy and bodily integrity both instrumentally and intrinsically, and mothers who did not give consent prior to the procedure are likely to perceive that the provider undermined them and didn't consider their right to participate in their decision‐making.

Mothers whose pain had been managed during labor and delivery were 7.27 odds of satisfying with delivery services. It is in line with study conducted in Jordan, Tabriz hospitals and Asrade Zewude Memorial Primary Hospital [3, 23, 24]. The possible explanation for this may be may be pain management has better cope and reduce stress with labor and delivery.

Conclusion and Recommendation

Less than half of mothers expressed satisfaction with professional delivery services. This study also revealed that; occupational status of household, waiting time, pain management during labor and delivery and asking permission before applying any procedure and examination were significantly associated with mother's satisfaction. As one of the indicators of respectful maternity care, the management of health centers and medical staff must provide patient‐oriented services to raise satisfaction levels.

Spatial pattern and landscape change prediction of blowouts in sandy grassland
Authors: Ziyue Guo, Liang Gao, Huidong Shi, Ting Yu, Hongtao Jiang, Chunxing Hai, Yunhu Xie, Li Zhu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBlowouts are a common type of wind-eroded landform found in sandy and desertified areas. They also represent a major degradative surface process affecting grassland ecosystems. Blowouts exacerbate changes in surface morphology through their effects on other surface phenomena including vegetation. In this paper, Xilingol League sandy grassland blowouts are taken as the research object, and the U.S. Keyhole satellite data and China’s Gaofen-1 satellite data are used as the data source, and the blowouts are extracted based on the 3 S technology for a total of six periods of high-resolution remote sensing image data in the study area from 1962 to 2023. The Landscape Pattern Index method and Fuzzy Land Use Simulation (FLUS) modelling applied to changes over the last six decades provided spatial evolution parameters for predicting future blowout distributions. Results showed that blowouts affecting the Xilingol grassland area increased by 16.81% over the past 60 years. The patch density (PD) increased by 0.9 per hectare. The mean proximity index (PROX_MN) and mean Euclidean nearest neighbour distance (ENN_MN) showed a tendency to decrease and then increase indicating initial expansion and then merging of adjacent blowouts to create the present landscape. The FLUS model used ten factors to predict changes in blowout distributions from 2023 to 2033. Factors included digital elevation model (DEM), slope, aspect, normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI), mean annual temperature, mean annual precipitation, population density, real GDP, distance to water, and distance to impervious surfaces. It was found that grassland area decreased by 6217.12 hm2 and blowout area decreased by 102.91 hm2. Results of this study can expand understanding of blowout morphodynamics in ecologically sensitive areas.Keywords: Sandy Grassland, Blowout, Spatial pattern, FLUS modelSubject terms: Ecology, Boreal ecology, Grassland ecology

Introduction

In the context of global climate change, desertification, primarily due to perturbations in hydrological factors, has become a salient environmental risk affecting sandy grasslands1. Desertification and soil degradation severely reduce potential productivity of land and lead to degradation of ecosystems and their associated ecosystem services2,3. In 2019, China conducted the sixth nationwide survey on land desertification. As of that year, the area of desertified land in China was 2,573,700 km2 and the area of sanded land was 1,687,800 km2. The average vegetation cover of sanded land was 20.22%, and the sanded land with vegetation cover exceeding 40% had significantly expanded4. The report demonstrated the urgent necessity of managing sanded land sustainably and over long term time scales.

Desertification begins with blowouts which also signal the beginning of fixed dune formation. Local, regional and global studies have described spatial alterations5, morphological features6,7 and evolution8,9 of blowouts. Much international research has focused on coastal blowouts10. Chinese scholars have described coastal blowouts around Haidan Island, Fujian Province11 but have primarily focused on blowouts affecting continental sandy grasslands12. Some scholars use Fragstats software13 to select landscape pattern indices relevant to ecology in order to derive changes in the spatial distribution of blowouts. Huercha used ALOS satellite remote sensing and field survey data to construct 12 landscape indices entered into Fragstats to show that that sandy grassland blowouts around Hunshandak occurred over a broadly distributed but locally clustered area14. A study of blowouts affecting the northern edge of the Taklamakan Desert using six indicators, including the number of patches (NP), patch density (PD) in the patch type index and landscape level index, found that blowouts in initial stages of development occupied small localized areas but added up to a large number of patches15. The studies described above only considered changes in blowouts over relatively short timescales. Few studies have considered large scale or long term morphological changes in blowouts. Continued research has found that airflow is the main factor affecting blowout occurrence and development. Recent research has focused on combining high-resolution remote sensing image data with wind tunnel test data, numerical simulations, modelling and other pertinent methods16–18. Du Huishi and other scholars for example used surface numerical simulation, cellular automata (CA), gray prediction, Markov chain methods, FLUS and other models in their “Land-cover landscape changes of blowout distribution in the Hulun Buir grassland” to predict blowouts from 2012 to 202219. Although the CA-Markov model can simulate land use changes, it is difficult to meet the research needs with higher requirements for simulation accuracy and dynamics.

One of four major grasslands in China, the Xilingol grassland represents a typical arid to semi-arid pastoral area and forms a critical ecological barrier around the Beijing-Tianjin and northern regions of China20. In recent years, development, over-utilisation of mineral resources and increasingly arid conditions associated with global climate change have severely disturbed the Xilingol grassland through desertification21 evident as blowouts or increases in grassland sand.

To clarify the spatial distribution and evolution of blowouts in the sandy grassland of Xilingol over a large area and a long time scale, this paper innovatively utilizes long-term, high-resolution remote sensing image data to extract morphological data of blowouts, thereby improving the accuracy and persuasiveness of the data. It employs the classic ecological landscape analysis method, the landscape pattern index method, to gain a deeper understanding of the complexity and heterogeneity of the spatial pattern of blowouts by quantifying their spatial structural characteristics. In combination with the FLUS prediction model, which boasts high-precision and multi-scale simulation capabilities, this study enhances the accuracy of predicting the spatial pattern and landscape changes of blowouts in the study area from 2023 toatively 2033. This research innov combines dynamic simulation of land use change with quantitative analysis of spatial patterns, adopts high-precision remote sensing image data and prediction models, and comprehensively utilizes multiple indicators of landscape pattern indices. It aims to comprehensively reveal the spatial distribution, change patterns of blowouts in sandy grasslands, and their impacts on the future spatial distribution of blowouts. The findings are expected to provide more comprehensive basic data and scientific evidence for the management of blowouts and the sustainable utilization of grassland resources.

Site description

The Xilingol sandy grassland is located in Xilingol League in the east-central part of the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region. From east to west, the area consists of meadow grassland, typical grassland and desert grassland. This research focused on Dahekou Town, Duolun County (Fig. 1), an area along the southern edge of the Otindag dune field which typically experiences blowouts. Given its location and average elevation (42°33’94 “N-42°92’66"N, 116°44’63"E-116°91’23"E, 1350 m), the area’s climate represents a transition from semi-arid to semi-humid in the middle temperate zone experiencing continental monsoon conditions. The study area specifically experiences average annual temperature of 2° C, an average annual precipitation of 150–400 mm, prevailing north-westerly winds, average annual wind speed of 3.6 m/s, and a maximum wind speed of 24 m/s. Landforms consist of high flat hills, and blowouts are mostly developed on the sand belt22, soils occur as mobile, wind-sandy soils located in the northern and central part of the study area. Predominant vegetation species include Leymus chinensis, Stipa grandis and Stipa sareptana, among others.

Data sources and processing

To study the long-term changes of blowouts, given the practical constraints of data availability, the remote sensing image data used in this study have different time intervals, specifically 1962, 1972, 1982, 2013, 2018, and 2023. This ensures that the selected data can fully reflect the key changes in blowouts over the past 60 years. Three-phase remote sensing image data for 1962, 1972 and 1982 were collected from the U.S. Geological Survey’s Keyhole satellite data sources (https://earthexplorer.usgs.gov/). Three-phase remote sensing imagery for 2013, 2018 and 2023 derived from Gaofen-1 satellite data (China Resource Satellite Application Center: https://www.cresda.com/zgzywxyyzx/index.html and Center for Resource and Environmental Sciences and Data, Chinese Academy of Sciences: http://www.resdc.cn (Table 1).

Using ENVI5.6 and ArcGIS10.5, we conducted pre-processing of remote sensing images including georeferencing, orthorectification, image fusion, image mosaicking, and clipping (Fig. 3). Taking the US Landsat-8 (OLI) data as the reference image, we selected georeferencing control points (buildings, road intersections, etc.) within the study area to georeference the remote sensing image data, ensuring consistency in data spatial reference. We then mosaicked and clipped the images according to the administrative boundaries of the Dahekou Town to ensure consistency in data range. Finally, we fused the high-resolution panchromatic band with the low-resolution multispectral data to ensure consistency in spatial resolution between the two data sources (2 m). Blowouts were identified by visual inspection as sandy grassland, sand accumulation areas behind pits, and sand patches on bare ground23. Software also generated a raster map of blowouts (10 × 10 m output pixel size). Blowout observation and validation was carried out in the field over the study period to verify accuracy of results and interpretations.

Land-use changes arise from natural and human drivers operating over different time scales. Relevant studies on land use25–27 and data available on Xilingol sandy grassland blowouts suggested a total of 10 natural and human factors influencing blowouts. FLUS model

Fuzzy landscape use simulation (FLUS) models predict land-use based on CA using an artificial neural network (ANN) and joint roulette algorithm. The ANN’s occurrence probability calculation module uses the land use raster data as an initial input and refines results according to raster data representing normalized driving factors. Validation of FLUS model accuracy

This paper selects the FLUS model to conduct predictive analysis on the evolution of blowouts in the study area. Before the analysis, the accuracy of the FLUS model is first tested, and a confusion matrix is generated using ENVI to complete the classification accuracy assessment. The confusion matrix is an n-by-n matrix structure that precisely presents classification accuracy data in a matrix form. Each row of the matrix represents the true distribution of a specific category, and each column reflects the proportion of samples in that category predicted by the model to be in each category. By accurately matching and calculating the differences between the actual locations of ground objects and the displayed locations of classification, it can precisely quantify classification errors and more intuitively compare classification results with actual land use information30,31. In this study, the land use data of the study area in 2013 is taken as the base period, and the driving factors in 2013 are added. Combining the land use transfer matrix and neighborhood factor weights, and setting relevant parameters, the simulation results of land use in 2018 are obtained, which are then verified for accuracy against the actual land use conditions in 2018.

Over the total 67,326.04 hm2 area of the study area, 151 blowout patches were extracted in 1962. Patches increase to 781 in 1972 (greater than four fold increase). This decade experienced the greatest increase in blowout patches observed over the 60-year study period. NP values showed stabilization from 1972 on but increased to 816 in 2023. The interval experiencing the greatest increase in CA over the 60-year study period matched that showing the greatest increase in NP. CA increased by 1634.39 hm2 or 55.84% from 1962 to 1972. Although NP increased from 1972 to 2013, CA decreased significantly, with a decrease of 2257.77 hm2 in 2013 compared to 1972 (half the total CA blowouts in 1972). CA from 2013 to 2023 showed an increasing trend from 2303.50 hm2 to 2434.97 hm2 (increase of 131.47 hm2) and overall stabilisation. The area of monolithic blowout PA from 1962 to 2023 (Figs. 5 and 6) gives NP ranging from 1 to 10 hm2 and only exceeded 10 hm2 in 1962 (71 and 80, respectively). The number of monolithic blowouts with a 1–10 hm2 PA declined from 515 to 461 from 1972 to 2023 (decrease in the total number of blowouts from 65.94 to 56.50%). However, the number of individual 1–10 hm2 PA blowouts from 1972 to 2023 all exceeded 50%, indicating that blowout PA occurred mainly over small areas (1–10 hm2) over the last 50 years. From 1972 to 2023, the number of patches representing less than 1 hm2 PA increased from 149 to 313. This approximate factor of 1 increase indicates continued formation of new blowouts or revitalisation of SP. The number of blowouts with an area larger than 10 hm2 reached a maximum of 117 in 1972 and a minimum of 36 in 2013. This number has increased steadily over the last decade to 42 in 2023, indicating general stabilization of large-scale blowouts due to land management.

AREA_MN represents the total area of patches divided by the total number of patches and thus characterises the degree of landscape fragmentation and complexity in landscape spatial structure. To a certain extent, the index can reflect degree of anthropogenic disruption. The smaller the value, the more fragmented the landscape. AREA_MN for the study area showed a decreasing trend from 19.38 hm2 in 1962 to 2.85 hm2 in 2013 (85.29% decline). The most significant change from 19.38 hm2 to 5.84 hm2 (decrease by a factor > 2) occurred from 1962 to 1972. The AREA_MN indicator increased steadily over the last ten years to 2.98 hm2 in 2023 (increase of 0.13 hm2).

Increases in complex, discontinuous patches can reflect natural factors or human activities that alter an originally more contiguous landscape. The degree of fragmentation in any case indicates loss of original grassland. Soil disruption leads to bare ground and sand patches more vulnerable to wind erosion. Under the action of wind erosion, pits form and may expand to blowouts. Trends in PLAND proportions from 1962 to 2023 were consistent with changes in CA. Patches accounting for 4.35% of the landscape in 1962 increased to their maximum value of 6.77% in 1972. This increase of 2.42% was followed by a decrease to 2.98% in 2023 (3.15% decline). The LPI varied modestly over the last 60 years peaking in 1962 and 1972 at 0.20%, and then decreasing to its lowest value of 0.09% in 2013. This value persisted in 2018 but rose to 0.18% in 2023. The area of blowout patches may expand due to limitation of downward incision in blowouts during active development. A change in the direction of erosional incision causes the number of patches to remain unchanged, but their area grows. Expansion may also reflect decreases in the degree of fragmented blowout patches. Adjacent blowouts appear to merge, and the number of patches decreases while their area increases. Lastly, other environmental factors may contribute to the observed increases in newly developed blowouts and patch area.

Distribution patterns of blowouts

Results showed that blowout PD in the study area generally increased over the last 60 years (Table 5). Blowout PD of 0.22 hm− 2 in 1962 increased to 1.21 hm− 2 in 2023 (4.5 fold increase). Changes in PD tracked changes in NP consistent with overall blowout trends in the study area. The PROX_MN index estimates connectivity between patches of the same type with larger values indicating good connectivity between landscapes. Calculations generated PROXA_MN values (within 1000 m search radius) exhibiting an overall decreasing trend from 1962 to 2018. The PROXA_MN value of 94.81 in 1962 fell to 36.66 in 2018 (61.33% decrease). This suggests a degree of discretization in the spatial distribution of blowouts and comports with fragmentation trends indicated by the AREA_MN variable. The ENN_MN values showed irregular cyclical fluctuations. A maximum value of 416.29 m occurred in 1962 followed by a decrease to 130.83 m in 1972. This 68.57% decline represents the largest decrease in ENN_MN over the 60 year study period. The ENN_MN index did not vary significantly from 1972 to 2023. As shown in Fig. 7, the ENN_MN of blowouts in 2023 was 67.1% less than that in 1962. This indicates that after 60 years of evolution, blowouts form closer to each other and show greater clustering. An 8.7% increase in ENN_MN from 2019 to 2023 reflects effective management of blowouts after 2018. These techniques limit the risk of blowouts linking up into a larger area of sandy land.

Discussion

The arid and semi-arid grassland ecosystem is highly sensitive and vulnerable. Under the influence of natural factors such as climate change, as well as human factors such as overgrazing or vehicle rolling, it is prone to forming “blowouts,” which are often critical phenomena indicating the fragmentation and initial desertification of grassland landscapes. They are also markers of the activation of fixed sandy lands. This paper utilizes the landscape pattern index method and the FLUS model to reveal the spatial characteristics and future changes of blowouts, which is of great significance for understanding the spatial variations of blowouts. In the discussion, the potential impact of changes in blowouts during different periods is analyzed from the perspective of policy formulation, and their formation mechanisms are explored. This study’s results are compared with existing research to reveal the commonalities and characteristics of current spatial changes in blowouts. The limitations in predicting the evolution of blowouts are analyzed, and future research directions are clarified. It is expected that this will provide a more comprehensive understanding of the changes in the spatial pattern of blowouts and valuable insights for research and practice in related fields.

Analysis of landscape patterns

The landscape pattern index method can effectively detect changes in spatial scale and distribution of a given process over a long time scales. The method has successfully characterized blowouts in Hulunbeier sandy grassland and Hunsandak sandy grassland5,14,19. The present study detected a five fold increase in NP and dramatic increase in CA from 1962 to 1972. During this period of rapid national-scale economic development, the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region invested significant resources in developing large-scale pastoral agricultural and water conservation projects. Under the joint action of human activities and natural factors, wind erosion intensified and blowouts expanded.

In 2000, the town of Dahekou implemented multiple ecological management policies, particularly the Beijing-Tianjin Sandstorm Source Control Project and the Grain for Green Policy, as well as ecological migration and population transfer measures in sandy areas. These efforts led to improvements in the ecological environment, with a significant increase in forest coverage, effective management of desertified land, and participation in the Million-Mu Mongolian Pine Afforestation Project around 2010. As a result, the formation and expansion of blowouts were significantly curbed32. By 2013, the number of blowouts had increased but blowout area had decreased by 41.41% compared with that detected for 1982. The decline likely reflects activities of the Hunsandak Desertification Prevention and Control Area of Inner Mongolia proposed in the 11th and 12th Five-Year Plans33,34. Desertification continued to decline with interventions according to 2017 data which recorded 35 blowouts and an increase in vegetation cover from 5% to 25–40%.

Spatial distribution of sandy grassland blowouts

A study on the number and spatial distribution of blowouts along the southern edge of the Otindag dune field detected widely distributed blowouts in 2008 with localised aggregation and merging of neighbouring blowouts thus indicating risk of blowouts linking up to form larger sandy areas16. The results of the present study similarly detected an increase in the density of blowouts, a decrease in fragmentation and an increase in ENN_MN in the study area from 1982 to 2013. Results thus confirm risk of formation of linked dune landscapes. Studies on changes in the area and number of blowouts along the southern edge of the Otindag dune field from 2010 to 2016 and the southeastern part of the Otindag dune field from 2010 to 2021 detected ongoing increase in the area and number of blowouts35,36. By contrast, the present study found that both the area and number of blowout patches decreased and then increased from 2013 to 2023. This may relate to the extent of the landscape area and number of extracted blowouts. Hu Rina extracted about 150 blowouts in a landscape area of 1410.05 hm[236. Zhou Yanguang primarily investigated changes in smaller scale aspects of four different blowout morphologies over a 67,326.04 hm2 landscape area with 800 extracted blowouts35. Alternatively, differences in image resolution and blowout extraction methods used by different studies can result in inconsistent blowout designations that affect area estimates. Furthermore, while the landscape pattern index method can quantify the spatial distribution characteristics of blowouts, some indices may oversimplify complex surface processes, making it difficult to fully reflect the true situation of blowout evolution. Additionally, the correlation between different indices may lead to information redundancy, resulting in varying research outcomes.

Predictive analysis of sandy grassland blowouts

The FLUS model, which combines ANN with CA, enhances simulation accuracy while maintaining high computational efficiency, and is widely used in Land-Use and Land-Cover Change (LUCC) studies. The FLUS model supports the setting of different simulation scenarios to assess the impact of various factors on land use changes, providing a more comprehensive basis for reference. Additionally, its ability to directly read, write, and output in TIFF format simplifies data processing procedures. Consequently, the FLUS model exhibits significant advantages over other traditional statistical prediction methods, such as the CA-Markov model, in terms of model mechanism, simulation accuracy, convenience in data processing, and multi-scenario simulation capabilities. However, the weight setting of driving factors in the FLUS model is relatively subjective and requires a significant amount of historical data based on the experience of other scholars. In this study, blowouts, as a type of land use, were predicted using ten driving factors input into the model, but the determination of parameters may be subjective and may not fully encompass all factors that could influence blowout evolution, such as wind speed, distance from residential areas, and distance from roads. Furthermore, the absence of restricted areas in land use transitions may also impact the results. Therefore, there is room for further improvement in simulation accuracy in future research.

The approach described here offers effective monitoring of blowouts in sandy grasslands and provides a scientific basis for prevention of grassland desertification or other degradative changes. Prediction of blowouts from land use data can also inform effective planning for sustainable development of sandy grassland area around the world.

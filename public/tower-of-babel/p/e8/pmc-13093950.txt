Multiple B vitamins dynamics during pregnancy and the risk of immediate postpartum anemia: a longitudinal birth cohort study
Authors: Jieling Wu, Mengtong Yang, Litao Guo, Peisi He, Di Wang, Ruikun He, Chuhui Qiu, Baolin Liang, Chaoming Deng, Yong Guo, Qiang Sun, Zhongxia Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPostpartum anemia is a global health issue linked to maternal mortality and sepsis. While iron deficiency plays a role, its contribution is limited, highlighting the need for better biomarkers. Although some studies suggest potential links between certain B vitamins and anemia, their relationship with immediate postpartum anemia (IPPA) remains unclear. We aimed to explore the longitudinal trajectory patterns of B vitamins (B1, B2, B3, B5, B6, and B9) during pregnancy and their potential associations with IPPA, and to develop a preliminary predictive model based on identified factors.MethodsWe recruited 200 women in second trimester, measuring B vitamin levels longitudinally through pregnancy and postpartum. IPPA was defined as hemoglobin < 11 g/dL within 24 h postpartum. Latent class trajectory models (LCTM) identified B vitamin trajectories, and logistic regression analyzed associations with IPPA. A predictive model for IPPA risk was developed using the Kolmogorov-Arnold network (KAN), with performance evaluated via the area under the curve (AUC), the net reclassification index (NRI), integrated discrimination improvement (IDI), and decision curve analysis (DCA).ResultsIPPA was diagnosed in 52.5% of participants. Unique trajectories of vitamins B1 (OR 0.25, 95% CI 0.10–0.59) and B3 (OR 0.21, 95% CI 0.08–0.57) were associated with reduced IPPA risk. Single-time-point analyses indicated that second-trimester levels of B1 (OR 0.82, 95% CI 0.67–0.98) and B3 (OR 0.84, 95% CI 0.73–0.97) may be associated with lower IPPA risk. The predictive model, integrating second-trimester levels of B1, B3, and iron, achieved an AUC of 0.78 (95% CI 0.76–0.79). The KAN-based predictive model showed an NRI of 0.32 (95% CI 0.27–0.37) and an IDI of 0.08 (95% CI 0.07–0.09), indicating superior predictive performance compared to the base model.ConclusionsOur exploratory findings suggest that B vitamins, particularly the previously unrecognized vitamin B3, may play a protective role against IPPA, with the second trimester emerging as a potential critical window for monitoring. The developed predictive model shows promise as a screening tool. These results require validation in larger prospective studies but point to modifiable nutritional factors that could inform future prevention strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12986-026-01097-1.Keywords: B vitamins, postpartum anemia, pregnancy, trajectories, dried blood spot

Introduction

Postpartum anemia is a significant global health concern, affecting 22–50% of women in developed countries and 50–80% in developing countries [1]. It has considerable effects on maternal health, increasing the risks of fatigue [2], depression [3], and cognitive impairment [4]. These repercussions may impair breastfeeding duration and maternal-infant bonding, potentially affecting infant health [5]. Furthermore, severe postpartum anemia elevates the risk of postpartum sepsis [6] and maternal mortality [7], highlighting the need for effective prevention and treatment of postpartum anemia.

The underlying causes of postpartum anemia remain poorly understood. While iron deficiency is the leading cause of anemia during pregnancy, accounting for over 72% of cases, it contributes to only 20%-37% of postpartum anemia [8]. Evidence suggests potential harms associated with routine iron supplementation [9], making the necessity for such interventions in preventing postpartum anemia more ambiguous. Current strategies for early screening and prevention of postpartum anemia are limited, underscoring the urgent need to identify more effective biomarkers and interventions for early detection and prevention to reduce postpartum burden.

In recent studies, various previously unrecognized functions of B vitamins have been discovered in the prevention and treatment of numerous diseases [10–13]. This indicates that there remains significant potential for B vitamins to be further explored in promoting health and preventing illness. While evidence is emerging for the role of certain B vitamins (e.g., riboflavin and B6) in anemia during pregnancy [14, 15], their association with postpartum anemia remains largely unexplored. There is a clear need for more comprehensive investigations into the relationships between multiple B vitamins and postpartum anemia risk.

Pregnancy is a critical period characterized by dynamic changes in metabolic and nutritional requirements. Despite this, research exploring nutritional assessment through longitudinal trajectory analysis, an approach that could better capture physiological and nutritional fluctuations during pregnancy, has been limited. Such a method could more accurately identify high-risk populations for enhanced monitoring and early preventive strategies. By employing multi-time-point measurements obtained through dried blood spot (DBS) sampling [16] and longitudinal trajectories identified through latent class trajectory modeling (LCTM) [17], we can pinpoint specific patterns in B vitamin levels. These trajectories can help identify distinct longitudinal patterns of nutrient status and pinpoint high-risk subgroups who may benefit from closer monitoring. This approach serves as a valuable hypothesis-generating exercise, informing the timing and focus of future intervention studies aimed at preventing postpartum anemia.

Therefore, this study was designed to explore the longitudinal trajectory patterns of B vitamins, aka vitamin B1, B2, B3, B5, B6, and B9 during pregnancy, and their associations with immediate postpartum anemia (IPPA). Subsequently, to evaluate the potential clinical utility of any observed patterns, we developed a predictive model using the Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN) that incorporated biomarkers from the gestational window highlighted in the initial exploratory phase, with the goal of providing a preliminary assessment of its screening performance.

Methods

Study design and population

We conducted a population-based prospective study in Southern China to examine the impact of maternal vitamin levels on the health outcomes of pregnant women and their infants. Between July 2021 and August 2022, we recruited 257 healthy pregnancies through convenience sampling at the maternity clinics of Guangdong Women and Children Hospital. Specifically, obstetricians identified potentially eligible women during prenatal visits, and research staff stationed at the clinic then approached them in person, explained the study and obstained written informed consent. Recruitment followed the inclusion criteria: age ≥ 18 years, gestational age between 19 and 28 weeks, residency in Guangzhou, and plans to deliver at the hospital. We excluded pregnant women with: (1) a history of severe neurological or psychiatric disorders; (2) liver or renal disease; (3) chronic conditions such as diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, or cancer; as well as those with acute or chronic infectious diseases or other significant organic diseases.

Demographic and lifestyle data, including maternal age, educational background, iron and folate supplement usage, were collected using a self-designed enrollment questionnaire administered through face-to-face interviews. Obstetrical related variables, including gravidity, parity, mode of delivery, and postpartum hemorrhage, were obtained from the medical records. Participants also provided self-reported pre-pregnancy weight and had their height measured upon enrollment to calculate pre-pregnancy body mass index (BMI). BMI categories were defined as follows: underweight (BMI < 18.5 kg/m2), normal weight (18.5 ≤ BMI < 24.0 kg/m2), overweight/obese (BMI ≥ 24.0 kg/m2). Maternal age was stratified into three categories: < 25 years, 25–34 years, and ≥ 35 years. Parity, gravidity, postpartum hemorrhage, iron and folate supplement usage, and mode of delivery were treated as dichotomous variables. Educational background was categorized into three groups: secondary level or below, high school and college, and undergraduate or above. Assessments were conducted longitudinally via face-to-face interviews during the second trimester, third trimester, and within 48 h postpartum. The study followed the reporting guideline (STROBE).

Measurement of B vitamins levels

Maternal blood samples were collected at three time points: the second trimester (between 19 and 28 weeks of gestation), the third trimester (between 31 and 40 weeks of gestation), and 48 h postpartum. We analyzed these samples to measure the levels of B vitamins (B1, B2, B3, B5, B6, and B9). Quantitative measurements of B vitamins were performed using dried blood spot (DBS) techniques, which have been validated for accuracy and precision in previously published studies [18]. A 20 µL of whole blood was applied onto the center of a DBS cassette and allowed to dry for 3 h in a sealed foil pouch away from light, and stored at -80℃ until analysis. Before analysis, samples were loaded into the DBS MS500 Sample Rack (CAMAG Switzerland). The cassette was transferred to each card was then transferred to the internal standard (IS) Spray Module and sprayed with 10 µL IS solution over 10 s. After drying for 30 s, the sample was transferred to the extraction platform, where 15 µL of extract (methanol: water = 7:3) was extracted at a flow rate of 50 µL per minute. The eluate (15 µL) was loaded into the sample ring (10 µL) and injected into the integrated liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS) instrument (full ring injection) for vitamin analysis. Vitamin levels were analyzed using an AB Sciex ExionLC system coupled to a Scix Triple Quad 6500 + mass spectrometer.

Outcome assessment

Blood samples for hemoglobin concentration measurement were collected within 24 h post-delivery. Hemoglobin levels was measured using an autoanalyzer (Mindray BC-7500CS) at the laboratory of the hospital. Immediate postpartum anemia (IPPA) was diagnosed when the hemoglobin concentration fell below 11 g/dL within 24 h of the postpartum period [19–21].

Statistical analysis

The normality of variable distributions was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Continuous variables were presented as means ± standard deviation (SD) or as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR), as appropriate. Categorical variables were described in terms of frequencies and percentages. Comparisons of categorical variables between groups were performed using χ2 tests, while nonparametric Kruskal-Wallis tests were utilized to compare continuous variables between groups. Only participants who completed all three visits were included in the analysis.

Latent class trajectory modeling (LCTM)

Latent class trajectory models (LCTM) were employed to identify trajectories of B vitamin concentrations over time, utilizing second-order polynomial models. Posterior probabilities for each trajectory were calculated for every participant based on their gestational age, and participants were assigned post hoc to the trajectory with the highest probability. The optimal number of trajectory clusters was determined based on the minimum Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC), while ensuring that posterior probabilities by class were greater than 0.70 and that each class comprised at least 2% of the population [17]. This analysis was performed using the lcmm package in R. The identified trajectories served as the basis for subsequent exploratory association analyses.

Logistic regression and sensitivity analysis

Multivariable logistic regression models were employed to explore the associations of trajectories and single-period B vitamin concentrations with IPPA risk. Analyses were performed using the glm function in R. This analytical phase was conceived as exploratory and hypothesis-generating, given that the specific trajectories and time points of interest were identified through data-driven methods in the preceding LCTM analysis. For each analysis, two models were fitted: Model 1 (univariate, unadjusted) and Model 2 (adjusted for maternal age, pre-pregnancy BMI, educational level, parity, mode of delivery, and corresponding period-specific iron and vitamin B9 concentrations or trajectory patterns). Confounders were selected a priori based on previous studies [22] and their established biological plausibility as potential confounders of the vitamin-anemia relationship.

To assess the robustness of the primary associations to uncertainty in latent trajectory class assignment, we conducted a sensitivity analysis using a multiple imputation adaptation of the Bolck-Croon-Hagenaars (BCH) three-step procedure [23]. Individual posterior probabilities of class membership, derived from the trajectory models were used to generate 50 imputed datasets where class assignment was probabilistically determined via a custom multinomial imputation function implemented with the mice package. Logistic regression models were then fitted to each imputed dataset, and the resulting parameter estimates and variances were pooled across all 50 imputations using Rubin’s rules, thereby producing final odds ratio estimates and standard errors that appropriately incorporate the variability arising from the probabilities class assignment process.

Mediation analysis

Mediation analyses were conducted using the mediation package of R software, employing two regression models: a logistic model to examine the relationship between the outcome (IPPA) and the exposure (vitamin B3 and B6) as well as the mediator (iron), and a weighted linear model to assess the impact of the exposure on the mediator while adjusting for potential confounders. The estimates for direct and indirect effects were obtained using the regression-based approach proposed by VanderWeele [24]. The mediation package provides point estimates, 95% confidence intervals, and P-values for the direct, indirect, and total effects. Both regression models were adjusted for maternal age, pre-pregnancy BMI, educational level, parity, and mode of delivery.

Development and evaluation of the predictive model

Building on the exploratory associations observed, we developed and evaluated a predictive model to assess the potential clinical utility of combining identified factors for IPPA risk stratification. Due to the limited guidelines for postpartum anemia, a base model was constructed using five risk factors (maternal age, pre-pregnancy BMI, parity, iron supplement usage, and folate supplement usage) identified in previous studies [25–27] to establish a reference model representing currently known and routinely available risk factors. These eight risk factors, five established clinical factors plus second-trimester levels of vitamins B1, B3, and iron, served as inputs to a Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN) to develop the enhanced predictive model. Unlike MLP [28], KAN places learnable activation functions on network edges, offering potential advantages in both accuracy and interpretability.

To prioritize clinical interpretability while maintaining predictive performance, we employed a deliberately simple KAN architecture with a single hidden layer containing 8 neurons. The model’s behavior was determined by several key hyperparameters (Supplementary Table S3): (1) polynomial order and grid intervals controlled the flexibility to capture non-linear biomarker-anemia relationships; (2) network structure parameters (hidden layers, neurons per layer) balanced predictive capacity with interpretability; (3) training parameters (epochs, batch size, L-BFGS optimizer, learning rate) governed the learning process; and (4) functional components (SiLU activation function, MSE loss) enabled non-linear predictions and performance optimization. All hyperparameter values were selected through systematic testing. The model was trained using the Limited Memory Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shanno (L-BFGS) optimizer, minimizing the Mean Square Error (MSE) loss between the predicted probabilities and actual IPPA outcomes. Model evaluation employed 10 iterations of 5-fold cross-validation with varying data splits to ensure robustness. Given KAN’s enhanced interpretability, particularly with fewer parameters, we pruned the predictive model by removing nodes with minimal incoming or outgoing connections. The visual representation of the predictive models post-pruning is illustrated in Figure S3. Subsequently, potential mathematical relationships were explored through automatic symbolic regression using a predefined function library. These symbolic formulas can elucidate the compositional structure and variable dependencies within the predictive models, ultimately enhancing interpretability.

The discriminative ability of each model was evaluated and compared by calculating the Area Under the Curve (AUC), which quantifies a binary classifier’s ability to distinguish between classes by measuring the area under the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve. Calibration plots were employed to evaluate consistency between predicted and observed probabilities. Sensitivity, specificity, positive likelihood ratio, and negative likelihood ratio were determined using the maximum Youden index criterion. The Youden index (calculated as sensitivity + specificity − 1) identifies the cutoff threshold that maximizes the overall discriminatory power of a binary classifier. This criterion balances the trade-off between sensitivity and specificity, providing a single optimal threshold for clinical decision-making. To further assess the clinical benefits and utility of the predictive model compared with the base model, the net reclassification index (NRI), integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) [29, 30], and decision curve analysis (DCA) [31] were employed. The NRI, IDI, and DCA were used to evaluate the clinical benefits and utility of the predictive model compared with the base model. Specifically, the dataset was divided into a training set (70%) and a validation set (30%) to calculate NRI and IDI separately, ensuring a robust evaluation of the model’s performance on both development and validation data. NRI and IDI served as alternatives to the AUC for evaluating improvements in risk prediction [29, 30]. DCA was employed to quantify net benefits at various threshold probabilities, with curves plotted for both the treat-all-patients scheme (representing the highest clinical costs) and the treat-none scheme (representing no clinical benefit) [32]. The threshold probability is defined as the point at which a patient perceives the benefits of treatment for intermediate-to-high-risk disease equivalent to the harms of overtreatment for low-risk disease, thereby reflecting how patients weigh the benefits and harms associated with their decisions. The highest curve at any given threshold probability represents the optimal decision-making strategy to maximize net benefit [31].

Missing values for continuous variables were imputed using linear interpolation, while those for categorical variables were filled in using plural interpolation. All statistical tests were two-sided. Given the exploratory nature of the association analyses, P-values are presented as unadjusted measures of association strength alongside effect estimates and confidence intervals [33]. Statistical analyses were conducted using R software version 4.3.1 (R Project for Statistical Computing), Python version 3.9.7 (Python Software Foundation) and PyTorch version 1.10.2 (Meta AI Research). Data from the cohort were analyzed from July 2023 to March 2024.

Results

Baseline characteristics

Of the 257 women initially recruited in the second trimester, 34 were lost to follow-up by the third trimester, and a further 21 were lost postpartum. After excluding an additional 2 participants with missing postpartum hemoglobin data, a total of 200 participants (77.82% of the original cohort) with complete data across all three time points were included in the final analysis (Figure S1). The average follow-up period was 14 weeks. The baseline characteristics of the eligible participants are summarized in Table 1. Among the 200 participants, 105 (52.5%) were diagnosed with IPPA. The median (IQR) age, pre-pregnancy BMI and gestational age of the participants were 29.00 (27.00–32.00) years, 20.50 (18.97–22.43) kg/m2, and 25.00 (24.00–26.00) weeks, respectively. No significant differences were observed between the IPPA and normal groups regarding maternal age, pre-pregnancy BMI, education level, parity, gravidity, mode of delivery, iron supplementation, or folate supplementation. The distribution of B vitamin concentrations between the IPPA and normal groups is available in the supplementary materials (Table S1).

Trajectories of B vitamins during pregnancy and the IPPA risk

The trajectories of vitamin B1 and B2 were categorized into three distinct patterns: a consistently low concentration throughout the follow-up period (steady-lowest), a trajectory marked by an initial decline followed by an increase (decreasing-increasing), and one characterized by an initial rise followed by a decline (increasing-decreasing) (Fig. 1a).

Trajectories of B vitamins in maternal blood from second trimester to postpartum 48 h. a) Trajectories of vitamin B1 and B2; b) Trajectories of vitamin B3 and B6; c) Trajectories of vitamin B5 and B9

For vitamin B3, three trajectories were identified: a continuous decrease (decreasing), an initial increase followed by a decrease (increasing-decreasing), and a stable trajectory at the lowest level (steady-lowest). Similarly, vitamin B6 displayed three trajectories, defined by a decreasing trend (decreasing), an increasing tendency (increasing), and a stable pattern at the lowest level (steady-lowest) (Fig. 1b).

Additionally, vitamin B5 and B9 exhibited four patterns: an initial increase followed by a decrease (increasing-decreasing), an initial decrease followed by an increase (decreasing-increasing), relatively stable levels at a medium concentration (steady-medium), and stable levels at the lowest concentration (steady-lowest) (Fig. 1c).

Table 2 presents the results of multiple logistic regression analyses examining the association between empirically derived B vitamin trajectories and the risk of IPPA. Participants in the steady-lowest trajectory group were used as the reference group for these analyses. The initial analysis suggested that membership in the decreasing-increasing trajectory of vitamin B1 (OR 0.25, 95% CI 0.10–0.59; P = 0.002), the decreasing trajectory of vitamin B3 (OR 0.21, 95% CI 0.08–0.57; P = 0.002), and the decreasing trajectory of vitamin B6 (OR 0.23, 95% CI 0.06–0.81; P = 0.022) was associated with a reduced risk of IPPA.

To account for uncertainty in trajectory class assignment, we performed a sensitivity analysis using a multiple imputation adaptation of the BCH methods. In this more conservative analysis, the association for vitamin B1 remained statistically significant (OR 0.30, 95% CI 0.13–0.70; P = 0.006). The association for vitamin B3 showed a consistent protective effect with marginal statistical significance (OR 0.38, 95% CI 0.15-1.00; P = 0.051), while the association for vitamin B6 was attenuated and no longer statistically significant (Supplementary Table S5).

Association of single-period B vitamin concentrations with IPPA risk

To examine the associations at specific time points, concentrations of vitamins B1, B3, and B6 in the second trimester, third trimester, and postpartum period were analyzed separately. As shown in Table 3, higher concentrations of vitamin B3 in the second trimester were associated with a reduced risk of IPPA (OR 0.84, 95% CI 0.73–0.97; P = 0.016), while no significant associations were observed for vitamin B3 in the other single periods. Similarly, higher concentrations of vitamin B1 (OR 0.82, 95% CI 0.67–0.98; P = 0.042) and vitamin B6 (OR 0.88, 95% CI 0.79–0.97; P = 0.016) during the second trimester were significantly associated with a decreased risk of IPPA, with no significant relationships in other periods. The results of analyses for all other B vitamins at all time periods are provided in the supplementary materials (Table S2).

Mediation analyses

Given the associations observed between certain B vitamins (B3 and B6) and IPPA in the priori exploratory analyses, we conducted exploratory mediation analyses to investigate whether iron levels could potentially explain part of these observed statistical associations. As illustrated in Figure S2, the results suggested that iron might mediate a portion of the associations for vitamin B3 and B6 with IPPA risk, accounting for 15.2% and 53.2% of the total associations, respectively. No significant relationship was observed between vitamin B1 and iron, precluding a similar mediation analysis for this vitamin.

Development and evaluation of a proof-of-concept predictive model

Building upon the exploratory findings for vitamin B1 and B3, we constructed a proof-of-concept predictive model to explore the potential additive value of these biomarkers, along with mid-pregnancy iron and anemia status, in discriminating IPPA risk.

To evaluate the potential clinical relevance of our exploratory findings and to place the performance of the KAN-based proof-of-concept model into context, we constructed a series of nested comparison models within the same dataset. The progression began with a base model comprising established risk factors such as age, pre-pregnancy BMI, parity, and the usage of folate and iron supplements, which achieved an AUC of 0.64. Adding second-trimester iron concentrations to this base model resulted an iron-only model with an AUC of 0.63. Building further upon this, a mid-pregnancy model was developed by incorporating the second trimester concentrations of vitamin B1 and B3, the vitamins highlighted in our prior exploratory analyses, along with concurrent anemia status; this model achieved an AUC of 0.73 (Fig. 2a).

Abbreviations: ROC, receiver operating characteristic; KAN, Kolmogorov-Arnold Network

In our sample, we developed a proof-of-concept model based on the KAN. This model incorporated second-trimester levels of vitamin B1, B3, and iron, as well as anemia status during pregnancy (see hyperparameters in Table S3). In cross-validation, it achieved an AUC of 0.78 (95% CI 0.76–0.79, P < 0.001), with a sensitivity of 73.1%, a specificity of 66.8%, a positive likelihood ratio of 2.20, and a negative likelihood ratio of 0.40. The calibration curves indicated reasonable agreement between the predicted and observed probabilities in both the training and validation sets (Fig. 2b-c). To quantify improvement, the KAN-based predictive model showed an NRI of 0.32 (95% CI 0.27–0.37, P < 0.001) and an IDI of 0.08 (95% CI 0.07–0.09, P = 0.018) compared to the base model, a finding corroborated in the validation set (Table S4). DCA suggested that, over a range of threshold probabilities (≥ 0.2), the use of this proof-of-concept model could provide more clinical net benefit than the base model or simple “treat-all/treat-non” strategies in this dataset (Fig. 2d-e).

Discussion

This study provides the first systematic exploratory analysis of the association between multiple B vitamins and the risk of IPPA in a Chinese longitudinal birth cohort. Our findings suggest potential inverse associations for vitamin B1 and B3 with IPPA risk, with vitamin B3 emerging as a previously unrecognized factor of interest. Trajectory analysis indicated that the second trimester may represent a critical window for monitoring B vitamin status. Building on these exploratory observations, we developed a predictive model which demonstrated promising discriminatory and calibration abilities in our cohort, offering a preliminary tool to address the gap in IPPA risk prediction.

Postpartum anemia, a prevalent nutrition-related condition and significant public health concern, affected 52.5% of our participants, consistent with previous studies [26]. While iron has traditionally been a primary focus for preventing and treating anemia during pregnancy, its role in postpartum anemia is limited. Moreover, the impact of B vitamins on anemia has been underreported, leading to their significance being underestimated in clinical practice. Our exploratory results revealed that vitamin B1 and B3 may serve as additional markers for IPPA risk, independent of iron levels, with associations that were observed after accounting for classification uncertainty.

In this study, our analyses identified vitamin B3 as a newly recognized factor of interest for lower IPPA risk. Although the association approached, but did not reach, conventional statistical significance in our most conservative sensitivity analysis, the substantial effect size, consistency across analytical approaches, and supportive biological plausibility warrant its discussion as a high-priority candidate. Mediation analyses showed that vitamin B3 had both significant direct and indirect effects on IPPA risk, with only a 15.19% mediating effect through iron. Vitamin B3 is an essential precursor of NAD+[34], which has been negatively associated with anemia risk in a community-based study involving Chinese women [35]. We hypothesized that the observed inverse association of vitamin B3 with IPPA might be explained through the intermediate substance of NAD+. Higher hemoglobin was linked to Sirtuin 1 (SIRT1) levels, which was the activation of NAD-dependent deacetylase [36]. Animal studies have showed that replenishing NAD+ positively affected the most primitive blood stem cells and protected against hematological failures [37]. Taken together, this existing evidence suggests that NAD+ could be a plausible biological mediator, offering a mechanistic hypothesis to explain the association between vitamin B3 and IPPA observed in our study. This hypothesis, however, requires direct testing in future research that measures NAD+ levels alongside B vitamins and hemoglobin.

Vitamin B1 demonstrated a suggestive inverse association with IPPA risk in our analyses, complementing the observation for vitamin B3. The biological plausibility of these findings is supported by existing mechanistic evidence. Deficiency in thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP), the active coenzyme form of vitamin B1, may impair ribose phosphate production through the pentose phosphate pathway, a process essential for DNA synthesis in erythroid cells, and reduce succinyl coenzyme A, a key substrate for heme synthesis [38]. Regrading vitamin B6, while the association was attenuated and no longer statistically significant in our sensitivity analysis accounting for classification uncertainty, it remains a biologically plausible candidate based on prior literature. Previous studies have demonstrated that vitamin B6 deficiency can disrupt the initial enzymatic step of heme synthesis and impair iron utilization in erythropoietic cells [39], and our preliminary mediation analyses suggested a potential link through iron status. Therefore, despite the lack of robust statistical support in the present study, the established biological role of vitamin B6 in erythropoiesis justifies its inclusion in the research agenda for IPPA, meriting targeted investigation in larger, hypothesis-driven cohorts. Collectively, these exploratory findings highlight vitamin B1 and B3 as the most notable nutritional factors associated with reduced IPPA risk in our study, while underscoring the need for additional research to clarify the potential contributions of other B vitamins.

Anemia has a complex etiology. While iron deficiency is a major risk factor during pregnancy, its role is less pronounced in the postpartum period [8]. Moreover, increasing evidence highlights the potential harms of routine iron supplementation during pregnancy, the US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) concludes that current evidence is insufficient to determine the balance of benefits and harms of iron supplementation on maternal and infant health outcomes [9]. In this context, our exploratory identification of vitamins B1 and B3 as potential factors for early screening of IPPA risk gains particular relevance. Due to their water-soluble nature and safety in supplementation, these vitamins present a promising and potentially lower-risk alternative for intervention, warranting further investigation for clinical application. This potential is underscored by the population-level relevance of B vitamin status. To contextualize our biochemical findings, suboptimal intake of B vitamins is a recognized public health concern. Nationally, insufficient dietary intake of thiamine (vitamin B1) and riboflavin (vitamin B2) is highly prevalent among Chinese adults [40], and studies in pregnant women suggest that levels of vitamin B1, B2, and B6 can be low or decline during gestation [41, 42]. These nutrients, primarily obtained from foods such as whole grains, legumes, nuts, seeds, and animal products, point to a widespread and modifiable aspect of maternal nutrition. Therefore, the associations we observed between vitamins B1 and B3 with IPPA risk may offer not only mechanistic insights but also carry potential public health significance. They suggest a possible targeted nutritional approach—addressing a common deficiency pattern with a supplement profile generally considered safe—for further investigation into postpartum anemia mitigation.

To design such targeted interventions effectively, it is essential to first identify which women are at highest risk and when they are most vulnerable. Our longitudinal trajectory analysis directly addresses this need by identifying distinct, time-sensitive patterns of B vitamin status that are differentially associated with IPPA risk. We found that certain trajectories were associated with significantly lower risk, while others were not. This demonstrates that tracking B vitamins, rather than relying on a single measurement, is crucial for risk stratification. These trajectory-defined subgroups provide a data-driven framework for prioritizing individuals in future research aimed at validating these associations, assessing their underlying dietary determinants, and testing whether targeted nutritional support during the critical second-trimester window can effectively prevent IPPA.

Interestingly, the trajectories suggesting a potential protective effect for vitamin B1 and B3 consistently exhibited higher concentration levels during the mid-pregnancy period. This observation led us to hypothesize that the second trimester may represent a critical window during which B vitamin status could be most relevant to subsequent IPPA risk. To explore this possibility further, we examined whether vitamin concentrations at specific time points showed corresponding associations. Our exploratory single-point analyses indicated that the inverse associations for vitamins B1, B3 and B6 appeared most pronounced when measured during the second trimester. While these findings are exploratory, the consistency between trajectory patterns and single-time-point measurements points to the second trimester as a potential focus for future monitoring and research.

The second trimester is a pivotal stage for fetal growth and development, making it crucial to meet maternal nutritional needs to promote both maternal and fetal health. Furthermore, the extended time frame between the second trimester and the postpartum period allows for the early detection and correction of micronutrient deficiencies, thereby potentially reducing the risk of postpartum anemia and other related adverse pregnancy outcomes as early as possible. Our exploratory findings suggest that vitamin status during this critical window may be particularly relevant. Specifically, vitamin B1, B3 and B6 showed the most consistent inverse associations with IPPA risk in our cohort. Beyond these nutrients, we also observed intriguing patterns for others, such as a specific vitamin B5 tentatively linked to lower risk. Collectively, these exploratory observations indicate that the relationship between B vitamin status and IPPA may involve multiple nutrients and warrants more comprehensive investigation in larger, adequately powered cohorts.

Despite the high prevalence and adverse effects of postpartum anemia, early screening guidelines remain limited. Universal screening presents significant cost and logistical challenges, while selective testing is hindered by the lack of reliable, validated risk assessment screening tools. Additionally, the optimal timing for assessing postpartum anemia continues to be a subject of ongoing debate. Building on the exploratory associations observed in our study, we conducted a comprehensive evaluation to assess the potential of integrating these biomarkers into a clinically usable tool. This aimed to address the current challenges in identifying the most effective factors and timing for predicting postpartum anemia risk. Initially, we developed a base model using five economically feasible risk factors that have been reported in prior studies to be associated with postpartum anemia [25–27]. However, the suboptimal performance of this base model highlighted the difficulties in identifying high-risk populations using routine factors, underscoring the urgent need for a more effective screening tool. Moreover, focusing exclusively on iron during the early stages to prevent postpartum anemia did not yield improved efficiency in the iron-only model compared to the basic risk factor model, further emphasizing the limited role of iron in the development of postpartum anemia. To explore the potential for enhanced early screening, we incorporated vitamin B1 and B3—both highlighted in our exploratory analyses—into the iron-only model, resulting in the development of a mid-pregnancy model. This modification significantly improved the model’s efficiency compared to the iron-only model. During the sensitive and critical window of the second trimester, the mid-pregnancy model proved to be an effective screening tool for risk assessment in IPPA. To optimize screening performance throughout gestation, we developed a final predictive model. This model utilized eight factors (age, pre-pregnancy BMI, parity, anemia throughout pregnancy, vitamin B1, B3, and iron measured in the second trimester) within the KAN framework, as outlined in Eq. (1). The final model achieved the highest AUC, establishing it as the most effective screening tool for continuous assessment throughout pregnancy.

The performance of the predictive model was evaluated using several comprehensive indicators. Both the AUC and calibration plot demonstrated that the predictive model exhibits significant discriminative and calibrating capabilities. Specifically, the KAN-based predictive model showed an NRI of 0.322 (95% CI 0.272–0.373) and an IDI of 0.081 (95% CI 0.070–0.092), indicating superior predictive performance compared to the base model. Furthermore, DCA confirmed that our model offers greater clinical net benefit across a range of clinically relevant threshold probabilities, as shown in Fig. 2d-e. Built upon the key biomarkers and timing suggested by our exploratory study, this predictive model represents a step toward translating etiological insights into a practical screening tool. Its components are largely accessible in routine care, suggesting potential for future clinical integration. More importantly, it provides a framework for early risk stratification, which could help identify high-risk women for enhanced monitoring and inform the design of future intervention studies aimed at preventing postpartum anemia.

A key strength of our study is that it is the first to comprehensively examine the role of multiple B vitamins, they are vitamin B1, B2, B3, B5, B6, and, B9, in reducing the risk of IPPA. The prospective design of our study features multiple time-point measurements of B vitamins in maternal blood from the second trimester to postpartum, effectively capturing the long-term longitudinal changes in B vitamin levels throughout pregnancy. The LCTM methodology enables us to identify and characterize early divergent patterns of B vitamins, which may help identify women at higher risk of IPPA. Furthermore, the use of DBS technology enhances the feasibility of longitudinal monitoring during pregnancy, facilitating the identification of high-risk groups from a population-based perspective.

Several limitations of this exploratory study should be acknowledged. First, the trajectory identification and subsequent association analyses are inherently data-driven. The number and shape of trajectories were not pre-specified but emerged from the data, and the associations tested were generated from these observed patterns. While this approach is valuable for hypothesis generation, the findings require validation in pre-specified, hypothesis-driven studies before any causal inference can be drawn. Second, our primary analyses treated the most likely trajectory class as a fixed variable, which does not incorporate the uncertainty in class assignment. To address this, we conducted a rigorous sensitivity analysis using a multiple imputation adaptation of the BCH three-step method. While this more conservative approach appropriately increased the standard errors, leading to an expected attenuation of statistical significance for some associations, the core findings remained consistent in direction and effect magnitude. Third, the modest sample size may have limited our statistical power to detect smaller effect sizes and increases the uncertainty around our estimates. This underscores the need for replication in larger studies. Forth, we utilized DBS sampling rather than venous blood with gold-standard assays. Although our DBS method is rigorously validated [18] and performed in accordance with established standards (T/CAGR 009-2022), and provides robust relative measures for within-study comparisons, future studies would benefit from direct comparison with standard clinical measurements. Lastly, as with many observational studies, residual confounding by unmeasured or unknown covariates cannot be entirely ruled out, despite our adjustment for several key covariates. In summary, this study provides preliminary, hypothesis-generating evidence. The trajectories and associations identified are exploratory and must be validated in independent, prospective studies with pre-specified hypotheses before they can inform clinical practice.

In summary, our exploratory study suggests that B vitamins, particularly vitamin B1 and B3 may serve as potential biomarkers for IPPA risk, with measurements during the second trimester appearing especially relevant. Based on these insights, we developed a predictive model that showed promising performance as a preliminary screening tool in our cohort. This work represents an initial step toward addressing the significant research gap in postpartum anemia risk prediction. To move from these exploratory findings to clinical application, future research, particularly well-powered prospective studies and intervention trials, is required to validate these associations and determine whether supplementation during the identified window can effectively reduce IPPA risk.

Dynamic topology-aware multimodal hypergraph fusion network for load forecasting in novel power systems
Authors: Xiaolong Lv, Qin Ma, Jie Du
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractExisting load forecasting methods for novel power systems face critical bottlenecks regarding insufficient multimodal data fusion along with weak dynamic topology adaptability and rigid modality weight allocation. A Dynamic Topology-Aware Multimodal Hypergraph Fusion Network (DTA-MHFN) is proposed to address these challenges. First, a Multimodal Hypergraph Network (MHN) is constructed. This network models user electricity consumption time series units and grid topology physical units as distinct hypernodes. Structural and behavioral hyperedges are then built based on physical connections and behavioral correlations. This design achieves explicit representation of cross-modal high-order associations and overcomes the limitation of traditional graph neural networks that only model pairwise correlations. Second, a Dynamic Topology Awareness Module (DTAM) is designed. It monitors topological time variations by calculating the cosine similarity of topological adjacency matrices across adjacent time windows. A gated recurrent unit is integrated to capture evolutionary features and adaptively update the hypergraph convolution weights. This mechanism resolves the issue of sudden prediction accuracy degradation under perturbation scenarios caused by static topology modeling. Finally, a Gradient-guided Adaptive Modality Weight mechanism (GAMW) is proposed. It quantifies the contribution of bimodal features using the proportion of backpropagation gradient magnitudes. Fusion weights are dynamically allocated through an attention mechanism. This strategy avoids feature redundancy or critical information loss triggered by static weights. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed model reduces the MAPE by 7.2% on the IEEE 33-bus system and smart meter datasets. The prediction stability in dynamic topology scenarios improves by 12%. The comprehensive performance significantly outperforms existing mainstream methods. This provides technical support for load forecasting in new power systems and holds significant theoretical and practical value for advancing multimodal data fusion and adaptation to time-varying topologies.Keywords: Novel power systems, Load forecasting, Multimodal fusion, Hypergraph network, Dynamic topology awarenessSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

The accelerated transformation and deep advancement of novel power systems establish load forecasting as a fundamental technology for operational dispatch and demand-side response alongside energy optimization1,2. Forecasting accuracy directly dictates the secure operation and economic efficiency of the power grid. Load data exhibits quintessential multimodal characteristics within novel power systems. The current study focuses specifically on bimodal fusion involving user electricity consumption time series and grid topological structures. The profound fusion of these two modalities holds significant importance for capturing bidirectional interaction patterns between users and the grid to enhance forecasting precision3,4. Existing load forecasting methods encounter severe challenges when processing multimodal data fusion. Traditional approaches mostly employ simple concatenation or fixed weighting strategies to fuse user behavioral features with grid topological features. These techniques fail to effectively represent cross-modal high-order associations among users and transformers as well as feeders and substations5,6. This inadequacy leads to fragmented multimodal information and insufficient fusion. Such fragmentation severely restricts further improvements in forecasting accuracy7,8. Furthermore, grid topology possesses pronounced time-varying characteristics. Line switching and node addition or removal along with network reconfiguration and distributed generation integration all alter the topological structure9,10. Current methods universally adopt static topology modeling. Such approaches struggle to adapt to topological variations in real time. This inflexibility triggers a sudden drop in forecasting accuracy under perturbation scenarios11,12. Achieving sufficient fusion of multimodal data and adaptive awareness of dynamic topologies has therefore emerged as a critical scientific problem requiring urgent resolution in load forecasting for novel power systems13,14.

Deep learning technologies have achieved remarkable progress in the load forecasting domain recently. Researchers have explored the effectiveness of intelligent forecasting frameworks across multiple dimensions including time series modeling and multi-source data fusion alongside graph neural network applications15,16. Lv et al.17 integrated variational mode decomposition with time series networks to enhance the forecasting capability for non-stationary load sequences. In the realm of multi-source data fusion, Miao et al.18 designed a deep feature interactive network for multi-source heterogeneous data utilized in fault diagnosis. Zhou19 and Xu et al.20 developed heterogeneous dynamic multi-view graph neural networks to achieve effective cross-domain information fusion. Regarding graph neural network applications, Lv et al.21 presented a multi-area short-term load forecasting approach based on spatiotemporal graph neural networks. Tan et al.22 engineered a multi-task learning framework to facilitate joint forecasting of multi-node loads. Building upon this foundation, Wu et al.23 introduced spatiotemporal adaptive personalized federated learning into multi-energy load forecasting to effectively resolve cross-regional data distribution discrepancy issues. To address data scarcity and uncertainty challenges, Xu et al.24 proposed a multi-output framework to tackle data limitations in time series forecasting. Wang25 and Ryu et al.26 designed a Bayesian transformer network and a quantile-mixer method to provide novel solutions for constructing probabilistic load forecasting systems and enhancing model robustness.

Although the aforementioned methods have attained notable advancements in forecasting precision and model generalization capability, existing research continues to face three primary challenges. Traditional graph neural networks including graph convolutional networks and graph attention networks can only capture pairwise associations between nodes within the grid topology. They fail to explicitly represent cross-modal high-order association structures. This failure leads to inadequate multimodal information fusion. Static topology modeling methods struggle to accommodate the time-varying nature of grid topologies. This inability results in a significant decline in forecasting performance under topological perturbation scenarios. Fixed weight allocation strategies cannot quantify the dynamic contribution degrees of user behaviors and grid topological features across different scenarios. This rigidity easily causes feature redundancy or the loss of critical information. These limitations restrict the adaptability and robustness of load forecasting in complex power grid environments. Related work

Deep learning methods for load forecasting

The prevailing research in the load forecasting domain primarily relies on the automated extraction and modeling capabilities of deep learning models regarding load time series features. This research stream achieves effective capture of spatiotemporal dependencies within load data through the design of various neural network architectures27,28. Regarding the capture of temporal dependencies, several studies initially focused on enhancing the model fitting capacity for long-term evolutionary patterns. Heng et al.29 subsequently embedded adaptive signal decomposition technologies including VMD and CEEMDAN into LSTM and TCN-LSTM architectures. This integration significantly approaches the precision limits of short-term load forecasting by effectively stripping away non-stationary interference terms. Hasanat et al.30 constructed a parallel multi-channel network framework to achieve hierarchical extraction of load features through the collaborative modeling of 1D CNN and Bi-LSTM. Building upon this foundation, Kumar et al.31 introduced an evolutionary learning-driven load forecasting model to optimize demand response management strategies and further empower smart grid dispatch with high-precision forecasting results. Although the aforementioned methods have achieved remarkable success in time series modeling, they still face challenges regarding inadequate single-modality information representation and the inability to effectively fuse grid topological structure features.

Graph neural networks and multi-source data fusion

The increasing informatization of novel power systems has prompted researchers to explore the application potential of graph neural networks to achieve explicit modeling of grid topological structures32. This method uses graph operators for multi source data fusion. Although originating from crime prediction33 or fault diagnosis, these models share mathematical foundations with load forecasting in modeling heterogeneous spatiotemporal topologies. Similarly, Wu et al.23 coupled spatiotemporal graphs with federated learning to resolve regional data silos and distribution discrepancies. This innovative integration further broadened the application boundaries of multi-energy load forecasting. Dong et al.5 explored a graph collaborative embedding diagnostic method within a hyper-feature space to strengthen the interaction capability of multimodal features. Zhou et al.20 concurrently developed heterogeneous dynamic multi-view graph neural networks to significantly enhance the fusion depth of cross-domain information. Despite these advancements, standard paradigms remain restricted by pairwise interactions. Specifically, spatiotemporal graph neural networks such as ST-GNN4 and STAP-FL23, heterogeneous graph networks5, and multiview graph fusion methods like MTL-MNLF22 decompose relational topologies into simplified node pairs. These frameworks struggle to capture concurrent interactions among multiple entities simultaneously. Conversely, hyperedges provide the non substitutable advantage of encapsulating high order cross modal correlations within a unified mathematical formulation, effectively mitigating information fragmentation.

Dynamic adaptation and uncertainty modeling in load forecasting

The dynamic characteristics of grid operating environments have prompted researchers to investigate the adaptive capabilities and uncertainty quantification of load forecasting models. This research trajectory enhances model adaptability to complex scenarios by integrating mechanisms including probabilistic forecasting and dynamic updating34. Wang et al.25 alongside Ryu et al.26 extensively explored the potential of deep neural networks in distribution fitting to construct reliable forecasting confidence intervals. They introduced a Bayesian transformer network and designed a quantile-mixer module to establish a novel probabilistic modeling method for multi-energy and short-term load forecasting. Aprillia et al.35 utilized a quantile regression random forest to achieve statistical load forecasting and risk assessment. Furthermore, the evolution of novel power systems closely aligns with next-generation networks, where real-time adaptation is paramount. Recent literature suggests that synergizing artificial intelligence with digital twins provides transformative frameworks for network optimization, forecasting, and security36. Additionally, digital network twins offer critical insights into managing dynamic structural complexities37. The aforementioned methods have achieved notable progress in dynamic adaptation. Existing research nevertheless fails to systematically resolve the sudden decline in forecasting accuracy under scenarios involving time-varying topological structures. Current approaches fundamentally lack effective awareness and adaptive updating to adequately manage dynamic alterations in grid topology.

Methods

This paper proposes a Dynamic Topology-Aware Multimodal Hypergraph Fusion Network (DTA-MHFN), whose overall framework is shown in Fig. 1. Multimodal hypergraph network

Existing load forecasting methods universally adopt traditional graph neural networks including graph convolutional networks and graph attention networks to model grid topological structures. These approaches can only capture pairwise associations between nodes. They fail to explicitly represent cross-modal high-order association structures among users and transformers as well as feeders and substations. Traditional graph neural networks define binary relationships between nodes through adjacency matrices where each edge connects exactly two nodes. This modeling mechanism fragments multimodal information during the transmission process. The insufficient fusion depth between user electricity consumption behavioral features and grid topological structure features severely restricts improvements in forecasting precision. This paper proposes a multimodal hypergraph network (MHN) to overcome these obstacles. This network explicitly represents cross-modal high-order associations through a hypergraph structure to break through the limitation of traditional pairwise association modeling and achieve sufficient multimodal information fusion.

As illustrated in Fig. 2. The MHN module initially constructs a multimodal hypergraph structure. This process defines user electricity consumption time series units and grid topology physical units as distinct hypernodes. Structural hyperedges are built based on physical connection relationships. Behavioral hyperedges are simultaneously constructed relying on the statistical correlations of user electricity consumption patterns. A hypergraph convolution operator is subsequently designed to aggregate hypernode features via hyperedge weights to facilitate high-order association information transmission. A hyperedge weight learning mechanism is then introduced to adaptively adjust the importance of different hyperedges. Deep fusion of multimodal features is ultimately achieved through multi-layer hypergraph convolutions. Explicit modeling of the hypergraph structure effectively resolves the inadequate information transmission issue inherent in traditional graph neural networks. This foundation supports subsequent dynamic topology awareness and modal weight allocation.

Dynamic topology awareness module

Existing load forecasting methods universally adopt static topology adjacency matrices to model grid structures. Grid topology possesses pronounced time-varying characteristics where line switching and node addition or removal along with network reconfiguration and distributed generation integration all alter the topological structure. Static topology modeling methods fix the topology adjacency matrix during the training phase and fail to perceive dynamic alterations in the topological structure. This inability leads to severe prediction deviations for models under topological perturbation scenarios. This mismatch between static modeling and dynamic topology severely restricts the adaptability and robustness of load forecasting models in complex power grid environments. A dynamic topology awareness module (DTAM) is designed to resolve the sudden drop in forecasting accuracy caused by static topology modeling under perturbation scenarios. This module aims to achieve an adaptive mapping of topological changes to feature weights and prediction results through a topological change monitoring and adaptive weight updating mechanism.

As illustrated in Fig. 3 the DTAM initially calculates the cosine similarity of topological adjacency matrices across adjacent time windows to monitor topological structural changes. A topological update signal is triggered when the similarity falls below a preset threshold. The module then captures topological temporal evolutionary features through a gated recurrent unit and embeds the topological change patterns into a hidden state vector. These evolutionary features are subsequently mapped to the hypergraph convolution weight space to adaptively update the convolution parameters. End-to-end training ultimately achieves collaborative learning between topological change awareness and prediction model optimization. This module enables the model to respond to topological structural changes in real time through topological change monitoring and adaptive weight updating to significantly improve forecasting stability under perturbation scenarios.

Gradient-guided adaptive modality weight mechanism

Existing load forecasting methods universally adopt fixed weight allocation strategies when fusing user behavioral features with grid topological features. The contribution degrees of bimodal features exhibit significant differences across various scenarios. User behavioral features dominate load changes during peak load periods with a contribution degree exceeding eighty percent. The contribution degree of topological features rises above sixty percent during topological reconfiguration periods. Fixed weight allocation leads to redundancy caused by over-weighting non-dominant features or results in critical information loss due to insufficient weighting of dominant features. This severely restricts prediction accuracy. A gradient-guided adaptive modality weight (GAMW) mechanism is proposed to address these issues. This mechanism aims to quantify the dynamic contribution degrees of bimodal features utilizing backpropagation gradients. It adjusts fusion weights in real time through an attention mechanism to evade feature redundancy or omission issues triggered by static weights.

As shown in Fig. 4, the GAMW mechanism initially calculates the gradient magnitudes of the loss function with respect to each modality feature during the backpropagation process. These gradients serve as quantitative indicators for contribution degrees. Modality weight coefficients are subsequently generated through gradient normalization and softmax transformation. These weight coefficients are applied to the multimodal feature fusion process to achieve adaptive weighting. A weight smoothing constraint is ultimately added to avoid weight oscillation and ensure stability during the fusion process. This mechanism enables the model to adaptively adjust the modality fusion strategy based on real-time scenario features through gradient-guided dynamic weight allocation. This significantly enhances prediction accuracy and robustness.

The experiments were conducted on a workstation equipped with an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU with 24GB of video memory alongside an Intel Core i9-13900K CPU and 64GB of RAM. An early stopping strategy with a patience value of 20 epochs was implemented to prevent overfitting. All experiments were repeated five times. These results validate the efficacy of the dynamic topology awareness module. It effectively monitors topological time variations and adaptively updates hypergraph convolution weights. It achieves a synergistic enhancement of steady-state accuracy and dynamic stability. Consequently the comprehensive performance significantly surpasses existing mainstream methods.

Discussion

Hyperparameter evaluation

A sensitivity analysis is conducted on the temperature parameter τ and the topology adaptation coefficient within this study. Figure 6a illustrates the performance variations of the model on the IEEE 33-bus dataset under different τ values. Experimental results indicate that the model achieves optimal performance when τ equals 1.0. An excessively small τ value leads to an overly sharp weight distribution. Such a condition causes drastically abrupt modal switching and negatively impacts prediction stability. Conversely an excessively large τ value results in an overly smooth weight distribution. This outcome weakens the influence of the dominant modality and reduces prediction accuracy. Figure 6b demonstrates the performance changes of the model on the topology reconfiguration dataset across various values. Experimental results reveal that the model attains optimal stability in topological disturbance scenarios when equals 0.5. A minimal value causes insufficient topology update intensity and fails to effectively address topological changes. An overly large value forces the model to focus excessively on topological variations and potentially introduces noise interference.

Robustness analysis under extreme scenarios

To thoroughly evaluate the predictive robustness of the model under extreme real-world conditions, additional stress-test experiments were conducted on the IEEE 33-bus system dataset. Table 4 presents the quantitative comparison of model performance under varying degrees of sensor data loss (10%, 20%, 30% missing rates) and large-scale topological changes (simultaneous cascading outages of 5 critical branches).

As illustrated in Table 4, under ideal conditions, all baseline models maintain relatively low forecasting errors. However, their performance deteriorates rapidly under extreme perturbations. First, to simulate massive sensor data loss, random masking was applied to the input temporal features. The experimental results suggest that traditional sequential and static graph models exhibit acute vulnerability to data sparsity. For instance, the MAPE of VMD-GRU and ST-GNN degrades significantly, exceeding 7.2% and 6.7% respectively at a 30% data loss rate. In contrast, the proposed DTA-MHFN maintains a highly competitive MAPE of 3.82% under the same severe missing rate. This resilience may indicate that the GAMW effectively quantifies the degradation of the temporal modality. By utilizing backpropagation gradients, it adaptively redistributes fusion weights toward the intact topological structures, thereby preventing catastrophic prediction failures caused by single-modality feature collapse. Furthermore, large-scale topological changes were simulated via cascading line outages. Under these severe structural perturbations, models relying on static adjacency matrices (ST-GNN, VMD-GRU) experience severe performance drops, yielding PSI values below 0.4. The proposed DTA-MHFN, conversely, maintains a high PSI of 0.78. This stable performance is consistent with the robust adaptive capabilities provided by the DTAM, which rapidly monitors structural similarity shifts and maps evolutionary patterns to the hypergraph convolution parameter space. These findings robustly demonstrate that the proposed multimodal hypergraph framework possesses exceptional generalization and fault-tolerance capabilities, ensuring highly reliable load forecasting even during complex grid emergencies.

Conclusion

This study proposes a dynamic topology-aware multimodal hypergraph fusion network to address the core bottlenecks of insufficient multimodal data fusion and weak dynamic topology adaptability alongside rigid predictive modal weight allocation in novel power system load forecasting. The proposed model effectively resolves the key limitations of traditional methods by constructing a multimodal hypergraph network to achieve the explicit representation of cross-modal high-order correlations. A dynamic topology awareness module is designed to enable the adaptive update of time-varying topological features while a gradient-guided adaptive modal weight mechanism is introduced to accomplish the dynamic fusion of bimodal features. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed network achieves optimal performance across the IEEE 33-bus system and the Irish smart meter dataset alongside the provincial power grid topology reconfiguration dataset. The MAPE experiences a 7.2% reduction whereas prediction stability in dynamic topological scenarios is enhanced by 12%. Ablation studies validate the effectiveness of each individual module. Furthermore efficiency analysis indicates that the model strikes a favorable balance between computational complexity and prediction performance. This research provides a novel technical pathway for load forecasting in modern power systems. It holds significant theoretical value and practical significance for advancing multimodal data fusion and time-varying topology adaptation. Future research will explicitly explore integration methodologies for additional distinct modalities, including meteorological conditions and macro-economic indicators, to further enhance the comprehensiveness of the network model. Subsequent investigations will also address model adaptability in scenarios characterized by high penetration rates of distributed energy resources.

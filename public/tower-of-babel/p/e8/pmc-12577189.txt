Efficacy and safety of DEB-TACE combined with HAIC and donafenib in the treatment of unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma: a dual-center retrospective study
Authors: Xuexian Zhang, Xinyi Fang, Ruidong Wang, Bin Xiong, Jian Long, Qikun Guo, Menglan Chu, Wei Luo, Shangbo Shi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis retrospective study aimed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of drug-eluting bead transarterial chemoembolization (DEB-TACE) combined with hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy (HAIC) and donafenib in the treatment of unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (uHCC).Materials and methodsThis dual-center retrospective study collected and analyzed clinical data from 87 patients with unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (uHCC) who received either DEB-TACE combined with HAIC and donafenib or DEB-TACE combined with donafenib between November 2022 and December 2023 at two hospitals in China. Patients were stratified into two groups based on whether HAIC was administered: the DEB-TACE + H + D group and the DEB-TACE + D group. Treatment efficacy was evaluated using objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS). Prognostic factors for PFS and OS were assessed using Cox proportional hazards regression models. Treatment safety was evaluated by the incidence and severity of adverse events (AEs).ResultsIn terms of treatment efficacy, the DEB-TACE + H + D group showed significantly higher objective response rate (ORR; 69.0% vs. 44.4%, P = 0.021) compared with the DEB-TACE + D group. Moreover, the disease control rate (DCR) in the DEB-TACE + H + D group was higher than that in the DEB-TACE + D group (90.5% vs. 73.3%, P = 0.039).The median progression-free survival (PFS) was significantly longer in the DEB-TACE + H + D group than in the DEB-TACE + D group (9.00 months vs. 7.00 months, P = 0.0078), as was the median overall survival (OS) (19.00 months vs. 13.00 months, P = 0.0031).Cox regression analysis identified treatment method as the only independent prognostic factors for PFS, while tumor diameter, BCLC staging, and treatment method were independent predictors of OS. Regarding safety, there were no significant differences between the two groups in the incidence or severity of adverse events (AEs) (P > 0.05), and most AEs were mild to moderate in intensity.ConclusionCompared with DEB-TACE combined with donafenib, the combination of DEB-TACE, HAIC, and donafenib demonstrated superior tumor control and survival benefit in patients with uHCC, with an acceptable safety profile. Further prospective randomized controlled trials are warranted to validate the long-term efficacy and safety of this combination strategy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-14879-2.Keywords: Unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma, Drug-eluting bead transarterial chemoembolization, Hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy, Efficacy, Safety

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) ranks among the most prevalent malignant tumors globally and is associated with substantial morbidity and mortality [1]. Approximately 70% of HCC patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage, having lost the opportunity for surgical resection [2]. The reasons for patients being deemed unsuitable for surgical resection included large tumor burden, multifocal lesions, vascular invasion, intrahepatic or extrahepatic metastases, impaired liver function, and insufficient future liver remnant [3]. Transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) is a commonly employed treatment method for patients with unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (uHCC) [4]. Nonetheless, a significant number of patients develop tumor recurrence or progression following TACE treatment [5]. As a result, many clinical practice guidelines for HCC regard TACE as a palliative rather than curative treatment method. Therefore, not only has the refinement of TACE techniques become increasingly important, but the integration of systemic therapies is also necessary to further enhance its therapeutic efficacy [6]. Drug-eluting bead transarterial chemoembolization (DEB-TACE) is a therapeutic approach that employs non-absorbable microspheres loaded with chemotherapeutic agents to embolize tumors. This technique enables the slow release of chemotherapy drugs, aiming to enhance the overall antitumor effect. In contrast, conventional TACE (cTACE) is characterized by a more rapid release of chemotherapy agents, which may lead to an initial high concentration followed by a quick decline, potentially failing to maintain drug levels above the therapeutic threshold [7]. Several studies have demonstrated that DEB-TACE offers superior clinical efficacy and a lower incidence of adverse events compared with cTACE in the treatment of hepatocellular carcinoma [8, 9]. As hepatocellular carcinoma is primarily supplied by the hepatic artery, hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy (HAIC) enables the direct delivery of high concentrations of chemotherapeutic agents to the tumor via the hepatic artery. Compared with systemic administration, this approach achieves higher intratumoral drug concentrations and greater selectivity, thereby enhancing antitumor efficacy [10]. In comparison with TACE, HAIC has been associated with improved tumor response, prolonged survival in patients with advanced HCC, and a more favorable safety profile [11].

Although DEB-TACE induces tumor cell necrosis by obstructing tumor-feeding arteries and creating a localized hypoxic and ischemic environment, this hypoxia may paradoxically stimulate the upregulation of pro-angiogenic factors such as vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), thereby promoting tumor neovascularization, which may in turn lead to recurrence and metastasis [12]. As such, reliance on locoregional therapies alone, such as TACE or HAIC, may be insufficient to fully suppress the biological progression of the disease, particularly in patients with unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma.

Systemic therapy, particularly molecular targeted agents represented by multi-targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), may help overcome this limitation.

Sorafenib was the first TKI approved for the treatment of unresectable HCC and remains a standard first-line therapy [12]. Donafenib, a new-generation oral small-molecule multi-kinase inhibitor, represents a strategic modification of sorafenib. By potently inhibiting the activities of various receptor tyrosine kinases, including VEGF, PDGF, and multiple Raf kinases, donafenib effectively curbs tumor cell proliferation and angiogenesis, thereby exerting a robust antitumor effect [13]. A randomized, open-label, active-controlled phase II–III clinical trial (ZGDH3) demonstrated that donafenib was superior to sorafenib in improving overall survival (OS) in patients with advanced HCC, with a favorable safety and tolerability profile [14]. In June 2021, donafenib was approved in China for the treatment of uHCC in patients who had not previously received systemic therapy [15].

Therefore, the combination of systemic therapy with locoregional treatments such as DEB-TACE and HAIC may exert synergistic effects through multiple pathways and mechanisms, leading to improved tumor control rates while reducing the risk of treatment-related tumor escape and distant recurrence.

To the best of our knowledge, no prior studies have systematically investigated the therapeutic potential of combining DEB-TACE, HAIC, and donafenib in the management of uHCC. Therefore, we conducted this retrospective, dual-center analysis to comprehensively assess the efficacy and safety profile of this multimodal regimen in patients with uHCC.

Materials and methods

Patient selection

This study was approved by the institutional ethics committees of both participating centers and was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Given the retrospective nature of the study, informed consent from patients was waived.

We retrospectively collected clinical data from 101 patients with uHCC who received either DEB-TACE combined with HAIC and donafenib (DEB-TACE + H + D) or DEB-TACE combined with donafenib (DEB-TACE + D) as initial treatment between November 2022 and December 2023 at two hospitals in China. Patients were categorized into two groups based on whether they received HAIC.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) age between 18 and 80 years, with an expected survival of more than 3 months; (2) newly diagnosed uHCC confirmed by imaging or histopathology; (3) Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) stage B or C; (4) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) score of 0–1 prior to DEB-TACE; (5) Child–Pugh class A or B.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) presence of severe underlying cardiac, pulmonary, or renal disease; (2) concurrent malignancies at other sites; (3) allergy to iodinated contrast agents precluding tolerance of DEB-TACE or HAIC; (4) incomplete clinical follow-up data.

DEB-TACE procedure

DEB-TACE procedures were performed by experienced interventional radiologists. The technique was as follows: A 5 F Yashiro catheter (Terumo, Japan) was introduced via the femoral artery and advanced to the celiac and hepatic arteries for digital subtraction angiography (DSA) to identify the tumor-feeding arteries. If areas of the Liver showed absent vasculature or incomplete tumor staining, selective angiography of the superior mesenteric artery, left gastric artery, and inferior phrenic artery was performed to detect ectopic hepatic arterial branches and extrahepatic collateral vessels supplying the tumor. Subsequently, a 2.7 F RAPIDTHRU microcatheter (Jiangsu Hengrui Medicine Co., Ltd., Jiangsu, China) was selectively advanced into each tumor-feeding artery for embolization.

Under fluoroscopic guidance, CalliSpheres microspheres (100–300 μm in diameter; Jiangsu Hengrui Medicine Co., Ltd., Jiangsu, China) loaded with 50 mg of epirubicin hydrochloride were slowly injected at a rate of 1 mL/min into the tumor-feeding arteries. If residual tumor staining persisted after using one vial of CalliSpheres, additional embolization was performed using an appropriate amount of 300–500 μm diameter blank 8Spheres microspheres (Jiangsu Hengrui Medicine Co., Ltd., Jiangsu, China) until the embolization endpoint was reached, defined as near stasis of blood flow in the tumor-feeding arteries.

Furthermore, in cases with hepatic arteriovenous shunts, embolization of the shunt was performed prior to tumor artery embolization using appropriately sized particulate embolic agents, selected according to the shunt’s location and flow velocity.

HAIC procedure

Following completion of DEB-TACE, the microcatheter was left in place within the tumor-feeding artery to facilitate continuous HAIC. If tumors were present in both the left and right hepatic lobes, the microcatheter was positioned in the proper hepatic artery for continuous infusion.HAIC was administered according to the FOLFOX regimen as follows: oxaliplatin was infused arterially at a dose of 130 mg/m² or 85 mg/m² over 2–3 h; calcium folinate at 400 mg/m² or leucovorin at 200 mg/m² was infused arterially over 1–2 h; and 5-fluorouracil was administered as a bolus of 400 mg/m² followed by continuous infusion at 2400 mg/m² over 23–46 h.

Donafenib therapy

Donafenib was initiated within one week after DEB-TACE at a dose of 200 mg orally twice daily. Donafenib administration was suspended three days prior to the subsequent DEB-TACE procedure. In the event of drug-related adverse events (AEs) during donafenib treatment, AEs were graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0 (NCI-CTCAE v5.0) [16]. For grade 1 or 2 AEs, donafenib was continued at the current dose with close monitoring. In cases of grade 3 or 4 AEs, the dose was reduced to 200 mg per day. If the patient remained intolerant to AEs despite dose reduction, donafenib was discontinued.

Follow-up and outcomes assessment

All patients returned for follow-up evaluations every 4 to 6 weeks. Follow-up assessments primarily included physical examinations, laboratory tests, and contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Tumor responses were evaluated according to the modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (mRECIST) [17], and categorized as complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), or progressive disease (PD). Objective response rate (ORR) was defined as the sum of CR and PR, while disease control rate (DCR) comprised CR, PR, and SD. Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the interval from the initial DEB-TACE or DEB-TACE plus HAIC treatment to either disease progression or death. OS was defined as the time from initial treatment to death or last follow-up. Patients exhibiting residual viable tumor or recurrence on contrast-enhanced MRI or CT and classified as Child–Pugh class A or B underwent repeat treatment with DEB-TACE combined with HAIC or DEB-TACE alone. In both groups, patients achieving CR continued donafenib monotherapy, whereas those with PR received additional DEB-TACE combined with HAIC or DEB-TACE alongside ongoing donafenib administration. The cutoff date for follow-up of all patients included in this study was December 2024.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 25.0 and R 4.2.2. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Survival curves for PFS and OS were generated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and differences between groups were assessed by the log-rank test. Potential prognostic factors for PFS and OS were evaluated using Cox proportional hazards regression models. Variables with a P < 0.10 in univariate analysis were included in the multivariate analysis. Model discrimination was assessed using Harrell’s concordance index (C-index), with higher values indicating better predictive accuracy. The proportional hazards assumption for each covariate and the global model was evaluated using Schoenfeld residuals. Moreover, to control for the risk of type I error rate arising from multiple comparisons, Bonferroni correction was applied by dividing the significance threshold (α)(P < 0.05) by the number of independent tests performed for each analysis, and only P-values below the adjusted threshold were considered statistically significant (P < 0.025).

Treatment efficacy

Tumor response

The ORR in the DEB-TACE + H + D group was 69.0%, significantly higher than the 44.4% observed in the DEB-TACE + D group (P = 0.021). Similarly, the DCR was significantly greater in the DEB-TACE + H + D group compared to the DEB-TACE + D group (90.5% vs. 73.3%, P = 0.039). However, after Bonferroni adjustment, the difference in DCR between the DEB-TACE + H + D group and the DEB-TACE + D group was no longer statistically significant (P > 0.025). Survival analysis

In the DEB-TACE + H + D group, the median follow-up duration was 16 months, whereas it was 12 months in the DEB-TACE + D group. The median progression-free survival (mPFS) was significantly longer in the DEB-TACE + H + D group compared to the DEB-TACE + D group (9.00; HR: 8.09–9.91 vs. 7.00; HR:5.13–8.87 months; P = 0.0078) (Fig. 2A). Similarly, the median overall survival (mOS) was significantly prolonged in the DEB-TACE + H + D group relative to the DEB-TACE + D group (19.00; HR: 15.49–22.50 vs. 13.00; HR: 10.74–15.26 months; P = 0.0031) (Fig. 2B).

Analysis of prognostic factors

Cox proportional hazards regression analysis identified treatment method (HR = 0.51; 95% CI, 0.32–0.81; P = 0.005) as the only independent prognostic factors for PFS (Table 3, Supplementary Figures S1). Tumor diameter (HR = 3.11; 95% CI, 1.27–7.67; P = 0.013), BCLC staging (HR = 5.69; 95% CI, 1.27–25.45; P = 0.023) and treatment method (HR = 0.32; 95% CI, 0.17–0.62; P < 0.001) were identified as independent prognostic factors for OS (Table 4, Supplementary Figures S2). The multivariate Cox regression models demonstrated good discriminative ability, with Harrell’s C-index values of 0.781 for PFS and 0.820 for OS. Moreover, Schoenfeld residual analyses indicated that the proportional hazards assumption was satisfied for all covariates and for the models as a whole (Supplementary Figures S3 and S4). For the stratified analysis, none of the variables reached statistical significance for either PFS or OS among patients with Child-Pugh class A. In contrast, within the Child-Pugh B subgroup, BCLC staging was identified as an independent risk factor for progression-free survival (PFS), whereas treatment method served as an independent protective factor for PFS; tumor diameter was the only significant risk factor for overall survival (OS) (Supplementary Table S1).

Safety

There were no statistically significant differences in the overall incidence or severity of AEs between the DEB-TACE + H + D and DEB-TACE + D groups (any grade: 85.7% vs. 77.8%, P = 0.340; grade 3 or 4: 23.8% vs. 31.1%, P = 0.446). Similarly, no significant differences were observed in the incidence or severity of individual AEs between the two groups (P > 0.05). The most common AEs in both groups included fever, abdominal pain, nausea and vomiting, and elevated total bilirubin. Importantly, no grade 4 or 5 AEs occurred in either group (Table 5).

Discussion

Our study demonstrated that compared with DEB-TACE combined with donafenib, the addition of HAIC to DEB-TACE plus donafenib significantly improved ORR and DCR, as well as prolonged mPFS and mOS. These findings suggest that the combination of DEB-TACE, HAIC, and donafenib offers superior tumor control and survival benefits for patients with uHCC.

The underlying biological mechanism of the combined therapy in this study may be as follows: ischemia and hypoxia induced by DEB-TACE can upregulate the expression of VEGF and PDGF, thereby promoting tumor angiogenesis and potentially leading to recurrence. The addition of HAIC enhances local drug exposure in residual tumor regions, while donafenib, as a multi-targeted kinase inhibitor, effectively suppresses VEGF and PDGF signaling pathways, thereby inhibiting tumor neovascularization and reversing the adverse tumor microenvironment induced by TACE [18].

The majority of patients with HCC are deemed unresectable at diagnosis, posing a significant clinical challenge. There is an urgent need to explore additional therapeutic strategies to improve patient outcomes, including various combinations of TACE or DEB-TACE, HAIC, targeted therapies, and immunotherapies.

Numerous prior studies have probed into the efficacy of TACE combined with targeted drugs in the treatment of HCC. The TACTICS trial [19] demonstrated that TACE combined with sorafenib significantly prolonged the PFS of HCC patients compared with TACE alone. Nevertheless, in terms of OS, TACE combined with sorafenib did not exhibit significant benefits. A study by Liu et al. [20] reported that TACE combined with apatinib yielded higher ORR and DCR compared to TACE alone in patients with uHCC, along with significantly prolonged median time to progression (TTP) and OS. Zhou et al. [21] demonstrated that TACE combined with lenvatinib significantly prolonged mOS and mPFS compared to TACE alone in patients with uHCC. Additionally, the combination therapy markedly improved ORR and DCR. Compared with cTACE, DEB-TACE employs non-absorbable microspheres as embolic agents, which enhance embolization efficacy and prolong the contact time between chemotherapeutic agents and cancer cells, thereby potentiating antitumor activity and improving therapeutic outcomes [22]. Similarly, several studies have explored the efficacy of combining DEB-TACE with targeted therapies in the treatment of HCC. Liu et al.’ s study [23] showed that compared with DEB-TACE alone, DEB-TACE combined with sorafenib in the treatment of uHCC significantly prolonged the median TTP and OS of patients and also significantly increased the ORR and DCR. Another randomized, prospective, multicenter study [22] demonstrated that the mPFS and mOS of patients with uHCC treated with DEB-TACE combined with apatinib were significantly prolonged. In addition, compared with patients who received only DEB-TACE, the ORR and DCR of patients treated with DEB-TACE combined with apatinib were also significantly increased. A central retrospective study by Xia et al. [24] showed that DEB-TACE combined with lenvatinib in the treatment of uHCC was more effective than lenvatinib alone in improving OS, PFS, and ORR. These studies suggest that TACE or DEB-TACE combined with molecular-targeted drugs in the treatment of HCC has a potential synergistic effect.

Donafenib, a tritium derivative of sorafenib, can inhibit the activities of angiogenesis-promoting vascular endothelial growth factor receptors and platelet-derived growth factor receptors, thereby suppressing tumor growth [15]. A recent study by Wan et al. [25] showed that HAIC combined with donafenib was superior to HAIC alone in terms of ORR, DCR, and PFS in patients with uHCC. A study by Lu et al. [26] on TACE combined with donafenib and immunotherapy for uHCC showed that the triple-therapy group of TACE combined with donafenib and toripalimab had a higher ORR and DCR than the double - therapy group of TACE combined with sorafenib, and the mPFS and mOS were also significantly prolonged. The results of another multicenter retrospective study [27] showed that compared with the TACE combined with donafenib group, the mPFS and mOS of patients with uHCC treated with the triple-therapy of TACE combined with donafenib and programmed death-1 (PD-1) inhibitor were longer, and the ORR and DCR were higher. These studies suggest that the combined use of donafenib with other treatments can significantly improve the tumor response and survival of uHCC patients. However, currently, no studies on DEB-TACE combined with donafenib in the treatment of uHCC have been identified.

HAIC, a treatment method for advanced HCC, plays a crucial role in the treatment of uHCC patients [28]. Recently, multiple studies have indicated that the combined use of HAIC with other treatments can significantly improve the prognosis of HCC patients. A study by Huang et al. [29] demonstrated that in patients with large or giant HCC, DEB-TACE combined with HAIC had a higher ORR, longer PFS, and OS than DEB-TACE alone. A multicenter study by Cai et al. [30] showed that adding HAIC to the combination of DEB-TACE and lenvatinib significantly improved the ORR, TTP, and OS of patients with large HCC with main portal vein tumor thrombus compared with DEB-TACE combined with lenvatinib. Lu et al.’ s study [31] showed that DEB-TACE combined with HAIC and lenvatinib in the treatment of uHCC showed significantly higher ORR and DCR than DEB-TACE combined with lenvatinib and presented longer mPFS and mOS. Another retrospective study [32] showed that TACE-HAIC combined with targeted, and immunotherapy significantly increased the ORR and DCR of uHCC patients and prolonged the mOS compared with TACE combined with targeted and immunotherapy. Our research results further corroborate these findings, indicating that the addition of HAIC to the combination of DEB - TACE and donafenib can significantly increase the ORR and DCR of uHCC patients and prolong the mOS.

The most prevalent adverse events in our study encompassed fever, abdominal pain, nausea and vomiting, and elevated serum bilirubin. These adverse events were manageable, and no patients experienced grade 4 or 5 adverse events. Moreover, there were no significant disparities in the incidence and severity of adverse events (AEs) between the two groups of patients, which is congruent with previous safety reports on the treatment of DEB-TACE combined with HIAC and lenvatinib [30, 31]. This safety profile buttresses the clinical application of DEB-TACE combined with HAIC and donafenib in the treatment of uHCC patients, indicating that this treatment regimen is not only efficacious but also well-tolerated.

Cox proportional-hazards regression analysis identified several independent prognostic factors associated with PFS and OS. That is, the treatment method was deemed a significant factor affecting PFS. reatment of DEB-TACE combined with HAIC and donafenib was a favorable prognostic factor for PFS. For OS, tumor diameter, BCLC staging, and treatment method were significant influencing factors. Smaller tumors, earlier BCLC stage, and the treatment of DEB-TACE combined with HAIC and donafenib were favorable prognostic factors for OS. These prognostic factors are consistent with previous research results [27, 33]. In addition, these results further accentuate the importance of early-stage disease and comprehensive treatment strategies in enhancing the survival rate of uHCC patients.

Notwithstanding the valuable insights furnished by this study on the combined treatment of DEB-TACE, HAIC, and donafenib for uHCC, certain limitations persist. First, this study is retrospective in nature, and treatment decisions were influenced by the attending physicians’ preferences and patients’ financial situations, which may have introduced selection bias and led to potential deviations in the results. Second, the relatively short follow-up period may have limited the ability to fully assess the long-term efficacy of treatment, particularly with respect to overall survival. Third, the small sample size may have compromised the statistical power of the study and constrained the scope of stratified analyses. Therefore, larger-scale, multicenter prospective studies with longer follow-up are needed to confirm and expand upon these preliminary findings.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that triple therapy with DEB-TACE, HAIC, and donafenib significantly improves treatment outcomes in patients with uHCC, including higher ORR and DCR, as well as prolonged survival, compared with DEB-TACE plus donafenib. This combination regimen offers a promising therapeutic option for uHCC and may represent a more effective treatment approach. However, large-scale randomized trials are warranted to validate these findings. The favorable safety profile further supports its potential clinical application.

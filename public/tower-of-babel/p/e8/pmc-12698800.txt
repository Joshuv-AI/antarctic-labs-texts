Upregulated NNMT sustains angiogenesis in clear cell renal cell carcinoma through activation of the SDF-1/CXCR4–ERK–CCL20 axis
Authors: Wenfu Zhang, Yang Yu, Bo Geng, Xin Shao, Wei Zhang, Zhuolun Li, Yunhui Chan, Nan Zhang, Jinpeng Wang, Yunfeng Nan, Kaifeng Wang, Meng Dong, Weikang Ma, Bosen You, Xuedong Li, Enyang Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNicotinamide N-methyltransferase (NNMT) is involved in the development of several cancers. Tumor stem cells are present in various cancers and are linked to treatment resistance, tumor recurrence, and metastasis. However, the role of NNMT in regulating the stemness characteristics of clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) is not yet fully understood.MethodsNNMT expression was identified as differentially expressed through the analysis of TCGA and GEO datasets (GSE105261 and GSE66272). Functional significance was explored using xenograft models. We performed qPCR, western blot, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) and additional assays to examine the effects of NNMT on ccRCC stem cells. The potential molecular mechanism of ccRCC stem cells in promoting angiogenesis was investigated through RNA-seq analysis. The expression of C-C motif chemokine ligand 20 (CCL20) was validated through western blot and ELISA analyses. Regulation of angiogenesis was assessed using tube formation assays and Matrigel plug assays.ResultsNNMT was significantly upregulated in ccRCC tumor tissues and impaired the prognosis in ccRCC patients. In vivo studies revealed that NNMT depletion significantly inhibited tumor growth, whereas increased NNMT levels enhanced tumor progression. Additionally, NNMT enhanced the stemness characteristics of ccRCC cells and maintained C-X-C chemokine receptor type 4 (CXCR4) expression through the activation of Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 3 (STAT3) phosphorylation. Moreover, NNMT facilitated the recruitment and differentiation of ccRCC stem cells towards endothelial cells via the stromal cell–derived factor 1 (SDF-1)/CXCR4 axis, thereby promoting vessel function and tumor growth.ConclusionNNMT functions as an oncogene, strengthening the stemness properties of ccRCC cells and sustaining angiogenesis. These results indicate that NNMT may be a viable therapeutic target and prognostic biomarker for ccRCC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s13402-025-01115-z.

Introduction

Clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) presents with distinct features, including elevated levels of angiogenesis and robust vascularization [1]. Due to the pronounced neovascularization in advanced ccRCC, therapeutic strategies have largely centered on inhibiting Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF) signaling through tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) or VEGF-specific monoclonal antibodies [2–4]. Multiple tyrosine kinase inhibitors are widely employed as standard first-line therapies for metastatic cancers and demonstrate notable benefits in extending progression-free survival [5, 6]. Nonetheless, most patients eventually develop resistance and relapse, while a subset shows primary refractoriness to targeted therapy [1, 7]. Therefore, a comprehensive delineation of the mechanisms governing ccRCC progression and angiogenesis is required to enable the development of more effective therapies.

Nicotinamide N-methyltransferase (NNMT) is an enzyme that transfers a methyl group from S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) to nicotinamide, resulting in the formation of S-adenosylhomocysteine (SAH) and N-methylnicotinamide (MeNAM) [8, 9]. After oxidation by aldehyde oxidase, MeNAM is eventually expelled from the body via urine. Recent studies have shown that NNMT negatively regulates intracellular SAM levels, the primary methyl donor involved in protein and deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) methylation [10]. Previous reports showed that NNMT represents a promising biomarker and therapeutic target for cancer diagnosis and therapy [11–13]. Elevated NNMT expression promotes the migration and invasion of ccRCC cells, whereas NNMT knockdown suppresses tumor growth and metastasis [14]. In addition, NNMT consumes SAM to methylate nicotinamide into 1-methylnicotinamide, thereby reducing histone H3 lysine 27 trimethylation (H3K27me3) levels, activating Wingless/Integrated (WNT) signaling, and maintaining cancer stem cell stemness during the early progression of gastric cardia adenocarcinoma [12]. Nevertheless, it remains unclear whether NNMT sustains ccRCC cells stemness through SAM consumption and consequently modulates ccRCC progression.

Cancer stem cells constitute a minor subset of cancer cells and are critical drivers of tumor heterogeneity and resistance to therapy [15, 16]. Evidence from studies using both clinical samples and established cell lines indicates that C-X-C chemokine receptor type 4 (CXCR4) functions as a marker for normal renal progenitors as well as tumor progenitor populations in ccRCC [17, 18]. CXCR4⁺ ccRCC cells possess sphere-forming ability and exhibit higher tumorigenic potential than CXCR4⁻ cells [19]. CXCR4, the receptor for the chemokine stromal cell–derived factor 1 (SDF-1) that drives metastasis, regulates cellular migration mainly via soluble chemotactic gradients [17]. Upon interaction with SDF-1, CXCR4 induces the separation of the heterotrimeric G protein into Gα and Gβγ subunits and mediates the exchange of guanosine diphosphate for guanosine triphosphate on the G protein, which subsequently activates downstream signaling [20]. CXCR4 expression in malignant epithelial and hematopoietic tumor cells indicates that the SDF-1/CXCR4 pathway contributes to cancer biology and critically mediates the metastasis of CXCR4+ cells to SDF-1-rich organs, including lymph nodes, lung, liver, and bone [21]. Few studies have explored how the NNMT-regulated SDF-1/CXCR4 pathway contributes to the progression of ccRCC.

In this study, we revealed that NNMT maintains the stemness of ccRCC cells, promotes ccRCC progression, and regulates the differentiation of ccRCC stem cells into pericytes through the SDF-1/CXCR4 axis to support vessel function and tumor growth.

Materials and methods

Data collection

This study utilized a comprehensive dataset comprising 1021 RNA-sequencing profiles, including 128 normal kidney tissues and 893 ccRCC samples, alongside 872 DNA methylation profiles consisting of 205 normal and 667 tumor specimens. All these datasets, along with matched clinical data for ccRCC patients, were retrieved from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) repository (https://gdc.cancer.gov/). Additionally, chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing (ChIP-seq) datasets were sourced from the publicly available GSE123398 database.

Cell culture

The ccRCC cell lines 769-P, 786-O, OS-RC2, and ACHN, along with the human immortalized proximal tubule epithelial cell line HK2, human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVEC), and HEK293T cells, were sourced from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, Manassas, VA). 769-P, 786-O, OS-RC2, ACHN, HEK293T, and HUVEC cells were cultured in 1640 medium, while HK2 cells were maintained in DMEM/F12 medium. Both media were supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and antibiotics (100 units/ml penicillin and 100 µg/ml streptomycin). The cells were kept at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2.

Patients and samples

Between October 1, 2023, and April 1, 2024, forty ccRCC tissue specimens, verified by histopathological analysis, were collected from the Department of Urology at the Second Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University. In addition, the matched samples of adjacent non-tumorous tissue were also gathered. This study received approval from the hospital’s institutional review board and adhered to the ethical guidelines outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. All patients provided written informed consent prior to participation. The ethics approval reference number for the study is YJSK2023141, and the ethics approval was granted on September 2023.

Xenograft model in vivo

Female athymic BALB/c nude mice, aged four weeks, were obtained from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). For the in vivo tumorigenesis study, lentiviruses for NNMT knockdown (NNMT_KD) or negative controls transfected 769-P cells (3 × 105) were injected into the kidney. Lentiviruses for NNMT overexpression (NNMT_OE) or negative controls transfected 786-O cells (3 × 105) were stereotactically injected into the kidney. Bioluminescence imaging was employed to monitor tumor growth. After 60 days of ccRCC cells injection, mice were euthanized under isoflurane anesthesia via cervical dislocation. All animals remained healthy throughout the study. Ethical approval was secured from the hospital’s institutional review board prior to the experiment. The researchers obtained prior approval from the hospital’s institutional review board. The ethics approval reference number for the study is YJSDW2023-064, and the ethics approval was granted on September 2023.

HUVEC tube formation assay

HUVECs were employed in the tube formation assay. Matrigel (BD Biosciences, CA, USA) was dispensed into 24-well plates (Jetbiofil, Guangzhou, China) and allowed to solidify at 37 °C for 30 min. Afterwards, 2 × 10⁴ HUVECs suspended in 200 µL of conditioned medium were seeded into each well and incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO₂ for 4 h. Bright-field images were taken using a Leica DM2500P microscope (Germany). The degree of tube formation was assessed by observing the formation of cellular networks.

Western blot analysis

Western blotting was performed as previously described [22]. Protein samples (30–50 µg) were lysed, run on SDS-PAGE, and transferred to PVDF membranes. After incubation with primary and then HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (Zsbio Store-bio, Beijing, China), bands were detected using a ChemiDoc™ system.

Lentivirus packaging and transfection

Lentivirus packaging and transfection were carried out following the same procedure as previously described [23]. Lentivirus particles were produced by cotransfecting pLKO.1 (for shRNA plasmids) and pWPI (for overexpression) vectors with psPAX2 and pMD2. G plasmids into HEK293T cells using CaCl2 transfection. After 48 h, lentivirus particles were harvested, filtered, and stored at -80 °C or used immediately. Cells with depleted endogenous PP2A and reconstituted with stable expression of either wild-type (WT) PP2A or PP2A-L309A.

RNA extraction and qRT-PCR

RNA extraction and qPCR were performed as described previously [22]. Total RNA was isolated using TRIzol (TaKaRa, Japan), and cDNA was synthesized with the PrimeScript RT kit. Quantification was done using the SYBR PrimeScript RT-PCR Kit (Roche, USA), with GAPDH as the internal control. The 2−△△Ct method was used to analyze the qRT-PCR data.

RNA sequencing analysis

RNA sequencing was conducted on three groups of 769-P stem-like cells (769-P-SLCs) after SDF-1 treatment. Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol (Invitrogen, USA) and treated with RNase-free DNase I (Takara, Japan). RNA quality and purity were checked by agarose gel electrophoresis, Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer, and NanoDrop spectrophotometer. For each sample, 1.5 µg of RNA was used to construct libraries with the NEBNext® Ultra™ RNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina® (NEB, USA). Unique index codes were assigned to each sample. Sequencing was performed on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (Beijing Allwegene Technology, China) to generate paired-end 150 bp reads. The raw data are available at the Genome Sequence Archive (GSA-Human: HRA009941) via https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn/gsa-human.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

ELISA was conducted on conditioned media collected from each cell culture. The activity of PP2A, and the levels of SAM, SAH and C-C motif chemokine ligand 20 (CCL20) were measured using ELISA kits (Ruifan, China).

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

IHC was performed as previously described [1]. Paraffin-embedded tissue slices fixed in formalin were first treated with xylene to remove paraffin, followed by washes in ethanol and rehydration with double-distilled water. Antigen retrieval was conducted by heating the slides in sodium citrate buffer at 95 °C for 15 min. Subsequently, the sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies targeting NNMT and CD31. After washing with PBS, the tissues were treated with an HRP-conjugated polymer antibody for 30 min, followed by color development using DAB chromogen.

Immunofluorescence (IF)

IF was utilized to detect protein expression and distribution within cells. Cells were plated in 24-well plates and, after an overnight incubation, fixed following standard protocols. Primary antibodies against NNMT, CXCR4, and Phosphorylated Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 3 (p-STAT3) were prepared in 1% BSA and applied to the cells overnight at 4 °C. After washing three times with PBS, the cells were incubated with TRITC Phalloidin at room temperature for 1 h. Secondary antibodies, either goat anti-rabbit IgG or goat anti-mouse IgG, were then added. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI, and fluorescence images were obtained using a Leica DMi8 microscope (Germany).

Gene set enrichment analysis

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was conducted with GSEA version 4.0 (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/index.jsp/). The reference gene set used was the annotated c2.cp.kegg.v7.4.symbols.gmt file.

Matrigel plug assay

1 × 10⁶ 769-P were cultured in media for 24 h. The supernatant was collected and centrifuged to eliminate cells. Conditioned medium was combined with phenol red-free Matrigel at a 2:7 ratio (total volume 0.9 ml) and injected subcutaneously along the mice’s dorsal midline. After seven days, the Matrigel plugs were retrieved.

Endothelial complex formation and coculture of ECs with CcRCC stem cells

To investigate the effect of NNMT gene expression on ccRCC stem cells recruitment to endothelial complexes, NNMT knockdown 769-P-SLCs and NNMT-overexpressing 786-O-SLCs were separately added to established endothelial cell complexes. The integration of ccRCC stem cells (GFP+) into the endothelial complexes was monitored and captured via imaging, followed by quantitative analysis.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad version 10.0 (GraphPad Software, CA, USA). Differences between groups were assessed with Student’s t-test. Data visualization, including heatmaps, was done in R (v4.3.0) using the pheatmap package. A p-value below 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

NNMT is overexpressed in ccRCC and detrimentally influences the prognosis of ccRCC patients

Cancer is characterized by DNA methylation alterations, including hypomethylation of large chromatin domains, which contributes to genomic instability and aberrant gene regulation [24]. To explore differentially expressed genes and DNA methylation-driven genes within ccRCC, we retrieved the TCGA and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets (GSE105261 and GSE66272) for our analysis and obtained 11 differentially expressed DNA methylation-driven genes (DEMDGs) (Fig. 1A-C). Hypomethylation of oncogenes has been identified as an adverse prognostic biomarker at the initial diagnosis of ccRCC [25]. Accordingly, we prioritized genes exhibiting concomitant hypomethylation and upregulation in ccRCC. Of the four hypomethylated and upregulated genes analyzed, only NNMT showed significant prognostic value, with higher expression correlating with worse patient outcomes (Fig. 1D-E, Supplementary Fig. 1A-F). Using TCGA-ccRCC dataset, NNMT methylation levels were analyzed and found to be significantly lower in ccRCC tissues compared to normal counterparts (Fig. 1F). Analysis of TCGA-ccRCC and GEO datasets (GSE66272 and GSE105261) showed higher NNMT levels in tumor tissues compared to matched normal samples (Fig. 1G). Elevated NNMT mRNA expression and increased protein expression were detected in ccRCC samples compared to peritumoral tissues (Fig. 1H-J). Furthermore, patients with ccRCC exhibiting higher NNMT expression tended to have worse prognoses compared to those with lower expression (Fig. 1K). In agreement with TCGA and GEO data, NNMT levels were markedly higher in RCC cell lines (769-P, ACHN, OS-RC2, and 786-O) than in the normal proximal tubule epithelial cell line HK2 (Fig. 1L). In conclusion, we discovered a distinct pattern of NNMT overexpression in ccRCC. These findings indicate that NNMT may play an important role in ccRCC development and progression.

NNMT enhances the stemness characteristics of ccRCC cells

To assess NNMT’s function in vivo, we developed nude mouse xenograft models using 769-P cells transfected with NNMT knockdown (NNMT_KD) lentivirus, and negative controls, respectively (Fig. 2A). At the same time, the mice received bioluminescence analyses every 7 days. Bioluminescent imaging further demonstrated that NNMT_KD effectively suppressed tumor growth (Fig. 2B, C). Subsequently, we established orthotopic mouse models bearing ccRCC xenografts using 786-O cells transfected with either NNMT overexpression (NNMT_OE) lentivirus or negative control lentivirus (Fig. 2D). Bioluminescent imaging further revealed that NNMT_OE significantly promoted tumor growth (Fig. 2E, F).

Tumor stem cells show a measurable association with tumor aggressiveness and metastatic potential [26]. In view of the critical role of cancer stemness in tumor aggressiveness, we subsequently investigated the impact of NNMT on the stemness properties of ccRCC cells. Knockout of NNMT reduced the ability of 769-P cells to form spheres, and sphere-forming capacity was enhanced in NNMT-overexpressing 786-O cells (Fig. 2G, H). We further found that knocking down NNMT diminished CXCR4, Nanog homeobox (NANOG), and Octamer-binding transcription factor 4 (OCT4) expression in 769-P-SLCs, while NNMT overexpression enhanced the expression of these markers in 786-O stem-like cells (786-O-SLCs) (Fig. 2I). We also observed a strong correlation between NNMT and CXCR4 expression. Specifically, CXCR4 levels decreased following NNMT knockdown in 769-P-SLCs, whereas CXCR4 expression was upregulated with NNMT overexpression in 786-O-SLCs (Fig. 2J). Taken together, our findings suggest that NNMT functions as an oncogene and enhances the stemness characteristics of ccRCC cells.

NNMT regulates PP2A activity to modulate CXCR4 expression

To identify the potential mechanisms underlying NNMT-mediated effects in ccRCC, we analyzed mRNA-sequencing profiles from the TCGA-ccRCC dataset. GSEA revealed that the JAK/STAT signaling pathway exhibited marked enrichment in ccRCC tissues with high NNMT expression. The normalized enrichment score (NES) reached 1.49 (p < 0.001, Fig. 3A).

The p-STAT3 ChIP-seq analysis revealed that p-STAT3 had wide-spread binding to the genome (Fig. 3B). A bioinformatics examination of the CXCR4 promoter region identified two potential p-STAT3 DNA-binding elements (DBEs) according to the JASPAR database (Fig. 3C). Given that NNMT can significantly influence CXCR4 expression and that p-STAT3 has been shown to bind to the CXCR4 promoter region, we aimed to investigate whether NNMT regulates CXCR4 expression via the JAK/STAT signaling pathway. We found that knockdown of NNMT in 769-P cells significantly reduced STAT3 phosphorylation and CXCR4 expression, whereas overexpression of NNMT in 786-O cells promoted STAT3 phosphorylation and CXCR4 expression (Fig. 3D). In addition, our results demonstrated that NNMT knockdown led to a decrease in nuclear accumulation of p-STAT3. In contrast, overexpression of NNMT in 786-O cells significantly enhanced the nuclear accumulation of p-STAT3 (Fig. 3E).

To investigate how NNMT affects STAT3 phosphorylation levels and associated metabolic pathways, we measured the levels of NNMT substrates and products, specifically SAM and SAH. Knockdown of NNMT in 769-P cells led to an increase in intracellular SAM levels and a decrease in SAH levels. Conversely, overexpression of NNMT in 786-O cells resulted in reduced SAM levels and elevated SAH levels (Fig. 3F). NNMT competes with leucine carboxyl methyltransferase 1 (LCMT1) for the methyl group provided by SAM, the main methyl donor in biological systems [27]. Inhibiting NNMT increases the availability of methyl groups, allowing LCMT1 to methylate PP2A more effectively [10, 28]. NNMT knockdown and overexpression upregulated and repressed PP2A activity, respectively (Fig. 3G). Furthermore, PP2A directly participates in the regulation of STAT signaling by dephosphorylating the serine residues on STAT3 in vitro [29–31]. PP2A-L309A led to a decrease in PP2A activity, an increase in STAT3 phosphorylation levels, and elevated CXCR4 expression in ccRCC cells (Fig. 3H-J). These results suggest that the methylation of PP2A is essential for maintaining its activity. The above results indicate that NNMT regulates PP2A activity, thereby modulating STAT3 phosphorylation levels and increasing CXCR4 expression.

NNMT enhances the recruitment of ccRCC stem cells to endothelial cells in a CXCR4-dependent manner

We next examined the role of NNMT in modulating endothelial cell–mediated recruitment of ccRCC stem cells. NNMT knockdown in 769-P-SLCs reduced the recruitment of GFP-labeled 769-P-SLCs to endothelial cell (EC) complexes, whereas NNMT overexpression in 786-O-SLCs significantly increased the recruitment of GFP-labeled 786-O-SLCs to EC complexes (Fig. 4A, B).

Since NNMT regulates CXCR4 expression through PP2A methylation and CXCR4 is the receptor for the chemokine SDF-1, we next investigated whether NNMT affects the SDF-1–mediated differentiation of ccRCC stem cells into pericytes. We found that 769-P-SLCs with relatively high endogenous NNMT expression exhibited elevated levels of the pericyte marker alpha-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) and showed a pronounced adherent growth phenotype following SDF-1 treatment, whereas 786-O-SLCs with relatively low endogenous NNMT expression displayed only a modest increase in α-SMA expression under the same conditions (Fig. 4C, D). Consistently, NNMT knockdown reduced the differentiation of 769-P-SLCs into pericytes, while NNMT overexpression enhanced the differentiation of 786-O-SLCs into pericytes (Fig. 4C, D).

To further examine whether NNMT influences ccRCC stem cells migration towards EC through CXCR4, we co-cultured ccRCC stem cells and HUVECs in separate transwell chambers. We observed that NNMT enhanced ccRCC stem cells migration towards HUVECs, an effect dependent on CXCR4 (Fig. 4E-G). Tumor growth was significantly inhibited, and microvascular density within the tumor was reduced following CXCR4 knockdown (Fig. 4H, I).

NNMT regulates the SDF-1/CXCR4–ERK–CCL20 signaling pathway to promote and sustain angiogenesis

We next investigated the changes in biological functions following CXCR4 activation in ccRCC stem cells. To this end, we treated 769-P-SLCs with SDF-1 and subsequently performed RNA sequencing (RNA-seq). GSEA analysis indicated that both the RENAL_CELL_CARCINOMA and CHEMOKINE_SIGNALING_PATHWAY were significantly enriched in the SDF-1-treated group, with normalized enrichment scores (NES) of 1.4328 and 1.2997, respectively (Fig. 5A). As illustrated in Fig. 5B and C, among the 18 protein-coding genes, CCL20, which encodes a secretory protein, showed relatively high expression levels following SDF-1 treatment. Given that the SDF-1-CXCR4 axis is widely recognized for triggering various intracellular signaling cascades, such as ERK, PI3K, mTOR, PKC, and PKA pathways, we next explored which pathway mediated SDF-1-driven CCL20 production. 769-P-SLCs were exposed to selective inhibitors: ERK inhibitor SCH772984, PI3K inhibitor LY294002, mTOR inhibitor rapamycin, PKC inhibitor GO6983, and PKA inhibitor H89 dihydrochloride. Notably, only SCH772984 markedly suppressed SDF-1-induced CCL20 expression, whereas the other inhibitors had no noticeable impact (Fig. 5D). These findings indicate that ERK signaling is essential for SDF-1-mediated CCL20 induction. NNMT knockdown markedly suppressed SDF-1-induced CCL20 expression and secretion in 769-P-SLCs, while NNMT overexpression notably enhanced CCL20 expression and secretion in 786-O-SLCs (Fig. 5E, F). The Matrigel plug assay results indicated that knockdown of NNMT significantly suppressed angiogenesis, while supplementation with CCL20 effectively restored angiogenic activity (Fig. 5G). The tube formation assays further demonstrated that NNMT knockdown compromised the ability of 769-P-SLCs to induce tube formation in HUVECs. However, the addition of SDF-1 partially rescued the impaired tube formation capacity in NNMT-knockdown 769-P-SLCs (Fig. 5H). NNMT overexpression enhanced the ability of 786-O-SLCs to promote tube formation in HUVECs, and supplementation with SDF-1 further augmented the tube formation capacity of 786-O-SLCs (Fig. 5I). The results collectively suggest that SDF-1/CXCR4 activation induces CCL20 via ERK signaling. Furthermore, NNMT modulates SDF-1-induced CCL20 expression and secretion, thereby promoting and sustaining angiogenesis.

Discussion

Our previous studies have shown a significant increase in microvascular density within the tumor tissue [1]. Microvascular density is commonly employed to assess tumor angiogenesis, with higher levels often linked to worse outcomes in many solid tumors. CcRCC are highly vascularized tumors, with approximately 20% of patients displaying innate resistance to therapies targeting VEGF [32, 33].

Existing therapies for human ccRCC involve combinations of anti-angiogenic drugs, mTOR inhibitors, and immunotherapy approaches, which have improved patient survival rates. Nevertheless, these therapies seldom achieve full remission, with most tumors advancing within a two-year period. In this study, we provide evidence that NNMT drives the chemotaxis of ccRCC stem cells toward endothelial cells through the regulation of CXCR4, where they differentiate into pericytes and contribute to the maintenance of vasculature. In addition, activation of the SDF-1/CXCR4 signaling pathway further induces the expression and secretion of CCL20, thereby promoting angiogenesis. These findings highlight a previously unrecognized mechanism through which NNMT not only enhances stemness but also drives vascular remodeling in ccRCC. This mechanism may, at least in part, account for the failure of anti-angiogenic therapies in ccRCC. Further investigations are required to elucidate the association between NNMT and resistance to anti-angiogenic therapies.

Overexpression of NNMT has been implicated in the pathogenesis of multiple malignancies, including lung, liver, kidney, thyroid, prostate, bladder, and colon cancers, with its elevated activity contributing to tumor aggressiveness by promoting cancer cell proliferation and survival [11–13, 34]. Nicotinamide metabolism driven by NNMT is predominantly elevated in gastric mucinous phenotype cell populations, which is implicated in tumor initiation and inflammation-driven angiogenesis [12]. NNMT mainly functions by methylating NAM, using SAM as the methyl donor to produce SAH [13]. Research has shown that NNMT catalyzes the methylation of nicotinamide to produce 1-methylnicotinamide using S-adenosylmethionine, resulting in decreased H3K27 trimethylation and activation of the WNT signaling pathway. This process maintains the stemness of AQP5+ stem cells during the malignant progression of esophagogastric junction adenocarcinoma [12]. NNMT depletes the methyl groups required by LCMT1 for the O-methylation of its substrates, thereby promoting the accumulation of stable N-methylated products (MNA). Inhibition of NNMT disrupts MNA formation, thereby rescuing LCMT1-mediated methylation of the PP2A catalytic subunit (C), which subsequently leads to the activation of the PP2A phosphatase [10]. In this study, we found that NNMT regulates PP2A activity, promoting STAT3 phosphorylation, enhancing the nuclear translocation of p-STAT3, and increasing its transactivation activity, thereby facilitating the expression of CXCR4.

As the receptor for SDF-1, CXCR4 binds to SDF-1, triggering a signaling cascade that ultimately directs the migration of CXCR4+ cells along the SDF-1 concentration gradient [35]. In the present study, we show that ECs, through the secretion of SDF-1, not only enhance the migration of ccRCC stem cells towards the ECs but also promote the differentiation of ccRCC stem cells into pericytes, thereby contributing to the maintenance of angiogenesis. Additionally, SDF-1 upregulates the expression of CCL20 through the SDF-1/CXCR4 signaling axis, thereby further facilitating angiogenesis.

Despite these findings, our study has several limitations. One limitation is that, although we employed xenograft and Matrigel plug models to investigate NNMT-mediated angiogenesis, these systems cannot fully recapitulate the complexity of the human tumor microenvironment. Another limitation is that, while our results suggest that NNMT may contribute to resistance to anti-angiogenic therapies, clinical evidence linking NNMT expression to therapeutic outcomes in ccRCC patients is still lacking. These limitations highlight the need for further in-depth investigations.

Conclusion

In summary, we found that renal cancer stemness is regulated by NNMT, which promotes the migration and differentiation of ccRCC stem cells towards endothelial cells via the SDF-1/CXCR4 axis, thereby sustaining angiogenesis in ccRCC. Our data suggest that NNMT may serve as a potential therapeutic target for ccRCC patients (Fig. 6).

B1 cells drive pulmonary emphysema progression through ADAM10
Authors: Ruchao Lv, Zishuo Li, Chenzi Zhao, Mengke Wang, Fanyu Shi, Changqing Lu, Jielin Duan, Xia Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe pathogenic role of B cells in pulmonary emphysema remains incompletely understood. This study aimed to delineate the specific B cell subsets and molecular mechanisms driving disease progression.MethodsUsing single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), we characterized lung B cell heterogeneity in a murine model of emphysema induced by porcine pancreatic elastase (PPE). Functional roles were assessed via B cell depletion and pharmacological inhibition of a disintegrin and metalloprotease 10 (ADAM10).ResultsEmphysema triggered the formation of pulmonary tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS) enriched with proliferating BAFF⁺ B cells. scRNA-seq revealed a selective expansion of B1 cells, primarily the B1b subset, which was accompanied by increased ICOS expression on lung CD4⁺ T cells. B cell depletion attenuated alveolar destruction, abolished TLS, reduced serum autoantibody levels, and improved lung function. Mechanistically, ADAM10 was specifically upregulated in B1 cells. Functional studies demonstrated that ADAM10, through activation of the Notch signaling pathway, was essential for the proliferation of B1 cells and their capacity to activate CD4+ T cells. Pharmacological inhibition of ADAM10 recapitulated the therapeutic effects, reducing B1 cell numbers, suppressing TLS formation, diminishing ICOS⁺ CD4⁺ T cells and autoantibody titers, and ultimately ameliorating lung pathology and function.ConclusionOur findings identify ADAM10 as a critical regulator of B1 cell-driven immunopathology in emphysema, highlighting the ADAM10-Notch axis in B1 cells as a potential therapeutic target.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12931-026-03640-3.Keywords: Emphysema, Tertiary lymphoid structures, B1 cells, ADAM10, Autoantibody

Introduction

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a heterogeneous disease characterized by emphysema and small airway disease, which poses a significant global health burden [1]. Current therapies, such as bronchial dilations and inhaled corticosteroids, mainly target airway inflammation to alleviate symptoms, slow lung function decline, and prevent acute exacerbations [2]. However, beyond smoking cessation, no treatments exist that can halt or reverse disease progression, particularly the structural destruction of emphysema, underscoring the critical need to elucidate the underlying mechanisms.

Although the importance of innate immune cells in COPD has long been recognized, emerging evidence suggests that lymphocytes, particularly B cells, play an indispensable role in its development and progression [3, 4]. Researchers have observed an increase in the number and size of B-cell-rich pulmonary tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS) in both patients with COPD and cigarette smoke-induced COPD mouse models [5–7]. It has also been suggested that B cell counts, as well as TLS number and size, correlate with COPD progression and severity, especially in the later stages of the disease [6, 8]. Recent spatial transcriptomic studies have further confirmed that B cells within these structures exhibit an abnormal activation signature directly associated with autoimmune-mediated emphysema pathogenesis [9]. In addition to the accumulation of B cells in the lung, autoantibodies have been detected in COPD lung and blood samples [10–12]. Several studies have shown that autoantibody levels correlate positively with emphysema severity, suggesting that they may contribute to alveolar destruction [13, 14]. Therefore, focusing on decoding the mechanisms underlying the emphysema phenotype may facilitate the development of precise and effective treatments.

A disintegrin and metalloprotease 10 (ADAM10) is a zinc-dependent, membrane-anchored protease which regulates immune responses through extracellular cleavage of transmembrane proteins [15]. ADAM10 critically regulates B cell development and is essential for determining marginal zone B (MZB) cell fate. Upon BCR engagement, the serine/threonine kinase Taok3 is activated, promoting the translocation of ADAM10 to the cell surface. Surface-localized ADAM10 cleaves the Notch2 receptor, thereby activating the Notch signaling pathway and driving the differentiation of type 1 transitional B cells into MZB cells [16, 17]. In addition, ADAM10 can also influence interactions between B and T cells. When ADAM10 is deficient in B cells, ICOSLG cannot be effectively cleaved, leading to its excessive accumulation on the B cell surface. This triggers hyper-internalization and degradation of ICOS on the T cell surface, thereby inhibiting the differentiation and function of T follicular helper cells [18, 19]. In autoimmune disease models, such as systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), ADAM10 deficiency can significantly reduce autoantibody levels and slow disease progression [19]. Furthermore, adenovirus-mediated overexpression of ADAM10 in the lungs of wild-type mice was found to significantly increase the mean linear intercept (MLI), demonstrating that ADAM10 contributes to the development of emphysema [20].

In the present study, we used a porcine pancreatic elastase (PPE)-induced emphysema model to investigate the role of B cells. We found that B1 cells expanded in emphysematous lungs, as along with increases in the number and distribution of TLS. Subsequently, we found that ADAM10 critically regulated B1 cell development by initiating Notch signaling. Similarly, B cell depletion and ADAM10 inhibition alleviated emphysema, concomitant with the disruption of TLS and reductions in serum autoantibodies. These findings indicated that ADAM10 critically regulates B1 cell development to drive emphysema progression.

Results

TLS occurs in the lungs of elastase-induced emphysema mice

To investigate the mechanisms of emphysema pathogenesis, we established a murine model via weekly intratracheal instillation of PPE for four weeks, with analyses performed two days after the final instillation (Fig. 1A). Pathological assessment of lung sections by H&E staining revealed hallmark features of emphysema in model mice, including destruction of alveolar walls and inflammatory cell infiltration, which were quantified by significant increases in the inflammatory score and MLI compared with the controls (Fig. 1B). Concurrently, lung function tests demonstrated a pattern consistent with obstructive lung disease with elastic destruction, characterized by significant increases in enhanced pause (Penh), expiratory time (Te), frequency (F), expiratory flow at 50% of vital capacity (EF50), and pause (PAU), along with a decrease in peak expiratory flow resistance (Rpef) (Supplementary Fig. 1A). Furthermore, PAS staining indicated enhanced mucus secretion, as evidenced by notable higher PAS scores in the lungs of emphysema mice (Supplementary Fig. 1B).

In addition, the development of TLS was observed exclusively in the lungs of emphysema mice. Immunofluorescence staining confirmed that these structures were densely populated with CD3+ T and CD20+ B cells (Fig. 1C). Within these TLS, we identified an increase in the population of CD20+ B cells that were positive for BAFF (Fig. 1D). Moreover, a significantly higher proportion of B220+ B cells was positive for the proliferation marker Ki67 in emphysematous lungs compared with controls, indicating active B-cell proliferation within the TLS (Fig. 1E). Collectively, these findings demonstrated that elastase-induced emphysema is associated with the formation of TLS enriched with proliferating BAFF+ B cells, suggesting a potential role for these structures in disease progression.

B1 cells expand in emphysematous lungs

To further characterize the immune landscape underlying emphysema, we re-clustered these transcriptomic data (GSE 237749) and identified B cells based on the high levels of Ig transcripts (Fig. 2A). This population uniformly expressed pan-B cell signature genes (Cd79b). We determined that lung cells contained immature B cells (Cd19+, Ighd−), mature naïve B cells (Ighd+), memory B cells (Cd80+, Bcl2+), Plasma cells (Prdm1+, Mzb1+) and B1 cells (Cd19+, Spn+, Vim+) (Fig. 2B). Control B cells were maximally enriched in immature B and mature control, whereas the emphysema lungs were more abundant than control in memory B (38.57% vs13.66%) and B1 (26.28% vs. 0.73%) cells (Fig. 2C and Supplementary Fig. 2A-C). Gene Ontology analysis for B1 cells revealed that significantly upregulated genes were enriched in oxidative phosphorylation, antigen receptor signaling, and membrane raft organization in B1 cells compared with other clusters (Fig. 2D). Moreover, Adam10 was identified as a highly expressed gene in B1 cells of emphysema mice (Fig. 2E). The results revealed a selective enrichment of B1 cells in the lungs of emphysema mice. Consistent with the single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) findings, flow cytometric analysis revealed a significant increase in the frequency of B1 cells (defined as CD19+CD23−IgM+CD43+) within lung lymphocytes of emphysema mice. Further stratification demonstrated that this expansion was predominantly driven by a substantial rise in B1b cells (CD5−), while the B1a (CD5+) subset remained unchanged (Fig. 2F, Supplementary Fig. 2E). Parallel analysis of the T-cell compartment (Fig. 2G) revealed transcriptional signatures of T-cell activation and differentiation in emphysema. CD4+ T cells showed upregulation of genes associated with T helper 17 cells (Il17a and Rorc), whereas CD8+ T cells exhibited enhanced expression of cytotoxic effector molecules (Gzmb and Gzmk) (Fig. 2H, Supplementary Fig. 2D). Flow cytometric analysis confirmed T cell activation in emphysema, showing a significant increase in the total number of CD3+ T cells (Supplementary Fig. 2G) and a specific elevation in the frequency of ICOS-expressing cells within the CD4+ T cell population (Fig. 2I, Supplementary Fig. 2F).These results suggested a distinct immune alteration in emphysematous lungs, characterized by a specific expansion of B1 cells (particularly the B1b subset) and concomitant activation of CD4+ T cells, as evidenced by ICOS upregulation.

B cells aggravate emphysema pathology

To determine whether B cells are required for inducing emphysema progression, we depleted B cells via intravenous injection of CD20 antibodies into mice. Administration of antibodies led to > 95% B cell depletion four weeks later (Fig. 3A-B). Histopathological analysis revealed that B cell depletion significantly ameliorated emphysema pathology (Fig. 3C), which were quantified by significant decreases in both the inflammation score and the MLI (Fig. 3D-E). Furthermore, PAS staining indicated a significant reduction in mucus hypersecretion, as evidenced by a lower PAS score (Supplementary Fig. 3A). Critically, the formation of TLS, which were prominent in emphysematous lungs, was abolished upon B cell depletion (Fig. 3F, G). Concurrently, depletion of B cells did not alter the total CD3+ T-cell population or the CD4+ to CD8+ T-cell ratio in pulmonary tissue (Supplementary Fig. 3B). In contrast, B-cell depletion was associated with a decline in the number of ICOS-expressing CD4+ T cells (Fig. 3H) and a concomitant decrease in autoantibody levels, notably anti-proliferating cell nuclear antigen antibody (anti-PCNA) and anti-ribosomal P antibody (ARPA) (Fig. 3I). In the meantime, the results revealed that B cell depletion significantly reduced the proportions and absolute counts of eosinophils and neutrophils but had no influence on alveolar macrophages compared with emphysema groups (Supplementary Fig. 3C-D). These data suggested the immunopathogenic roles of B cells for inducing emphysema.

B-cell depletion improves lung function

We next compared the transcriptomes for the total lung cells among the three groups. Gene Ontology analysis revealed that significantly upregulated genes were enriched in oxygen transport and gas exchange signaling pathway in anti-CD20 groups compared with emphysema groups (Fig. 4A). The levels of erythrocyte development related genes (including Hbb-bs, Hbb-bt and Slc4a1) significantly increased in the B-cell deletion groups compared with the emphysema groups (Fig. 4B). GSEA also showed upregulation of pathways associated with electron transport chain, respirasome, and TGF-beta signaling pathway and downregulation of signaling related to BCR activation after B-cell deletion (Fig. 4C and Supplementary Fig. 4A).

Consistently, lung function tests revealed that emphysematous lungs—characterized by elevated Penh and prolonged Te, along with reduced Rpef, exhibited significant improvement after B-cell depletion therapy (Fig. 4D). Other metrics, including F, EF50, and PAU, showed comparable amelioration (Supplementary Fig. 4B). Taken together, these results underscore the essential role of B cells in driving the progression of lung tissue destruction during emphysema.

ADAM10 upregulation in B1 cells correlates with emphysema development

Given the critical role of ADAM10 in B cell biology, we investigated its expression in emphysematous lungs. Our scRNA-seq data revealed a significant and specific upregulation of Adam10 expression, predominantly within the B1 cell subset (Fig. 5A). Since ADAM10 is a key sheddase for Notch receptors and Notch signaling is crucial for B cell function, we performed GSEA, which confirmed a significant enrichment of the Notch signaling pathway in lung B cells from emphysema mice (Fig. 5B, Supplementary Fig. 5A). Consistently, the expression levels of key Notch target genes, including the Notch2-selective target Dtx1 and the general Notch receptor targets Hes1 and Hes5, were all significantly elevated in B1 cells from emphysema mice compared with controls (Fig. 5C).

To validate this finding, we performed multiplex immunofluorescence staining for IgM (a marker for B1 cells) and ADAM10 in the lung tissues. We observed a significant increase in the number of IgM+ADAM10+ double-positive cells in the lungs of emphysema mice compared with controls. Notably, these cells were predominantly localized to the periphery and marginal zones of the TLS (Fig. 5D, E). This finding was further confirmed using flow cytometric analysis of isolated lung cells, which demonstrated a significant increase in the surface expression of ADAM10 specifically on B1 cells from emphysematous lungs compared with those from control mice (Fig. 5F and Supplementary Fig. 5B). These findings suggested that ADAM10 is associated with B cell-induced emphysema progression.

ADAM10 inhibition attenuates emphysema progression

To further elucidate the role of ADAM10 in emphysema pathogenesis, mice were treated with an ADAM10 inhibitor to further determine whether they are indispensable for PPE-induced emphysema (Supplementary Fig. 6A). Histopathological examination of lung tissues revealed that ADAM10 blockade significantly reduced the mean linear intercept, inflammation score, and PAS score (Fig. 6A-C and Supplementary Fig. 6B, C).Moreover, the formation and maintenance of TLS were markedly suppressed upon ADAM10 inhibition (Fig. 6D, E). These findings revealed that targeting ADAM10 significantly alleviates lung injury in emphysema models. Lung function tests demonstrated that inhibition of ADAM10 significantly improved lung function in emphysema mice, as reflected by decreased Penh and Te, along with increased Rpef following ADAM10 inhibitor treatment (Fig. 6F). Consistent improvements were also observed in other functional parameters, including F, EF50, and PAU (Supplementary Fig. 6D). Furthermore, longitudinal monitoring of hemoglobin (Hb) levels revealed a pronounced decline in emphysematous mice during the progression of emphysema (Fig. 6G), and this reduction was markedly attenuated in mice treated with ADAM10 inhibitors, indicating that ADAM10 inhibition helps preserve lung oxygen-carrying capacity during emphysema progression. Consistent with the therapeutic effects of ADAM10 inhibition on emphysema development, qPCR analysis confirmed that the expression of key Notch target genes (Hes1, Hes5, Hey1) was significantly upregulated in the lungs of emphysema mice, and this upregulation was markedly reversed by ADAM10 inhibitor treatment (Fig. 6H).

Therapeutic targeting of ADAM10 ameliorates the lung immune microenvironment

We next examined the inflammatory cell composition in mouse lung tissue.The results showed that ADAM10 inhibition led to a significant reduction in the number of B1 cells, without markedly altering the total B and T cell population (Fig. 7A and Supplementary Fig. 7A, B), Consistent with this, ADAM10 expression on B1 cells was notably lower in the treatment group than in the emphysema controls (Fig. 7B, C). In parallel, the proportion of ICOS-positive cells among CD4⁺ T cells were substantially decreased upon ADAM10 blockade (Fig. 7D, E). The serum levels of anti-PCNA and ARPA also significantly decreased after ADAM10 blockade (Fig. 7F). Furthermore, ADAM10 inhibition significantly reduced the proportions and absolute number of eosinophils and neutrophils, whereas alveolar macrophages remained largely unaffected (Supplementary Fig. 7C). Furthermore, immunofluorescenceresults showed upregulation​ of ADAM10 in emphysematous​ lungs, which was decreased following ADAM10 inhibitor treatment (Supplementary Fig. 7D, E). Collectively, these findings revealed that therapeutic targeting of ADAM10 ameliorates emphysema pathology.

As reported that ADAM10 regulates B cell differentiation via Notch and cleaves ICOSLG on B cells [18]. Our results also revealed that ADAM10 inhibitor significantly reversed the upregulation of ICOSLG in B cells in emphysema mice (Supplementary Fig. 8B).To determine whether ADAM10 function in B1 cells is related to Notch signaling during emphysema development, we established an in vitro co-culture system. After two days of co-culture with naive T cells, the percentage of proliferating B cells and T cells were significantly higher in the B1 group isolated from emphysematous lungs than either B2 groups from emphysematous group or the control group. This enhanced proliferative capacity was markedly suppressed by both ADAM10 and Notch inhibitors (Fig. 7G and Supplementary Fig. 8C, D). Furthermore, B1 cells induced higher expression of ICOS on T cells, an effect that was significantly attenuated when either ADAM10 activity or Notch signaling was inhibited (Fig. 7H and Supplementary Fig. 8E). Collectively, these findings indicate that the ADAM10-Notch signaling axis is essential for maintaining the immunostimulatory function of B1 cells.

Upregulation of ADAM10 in B1 cells from patients with COPD

To further determine whether ADAM10 as a critical regulator of B1 cell-driven immunopathology in emphysema, we re-clustered these transcriptomic data from patients with COPD and healthy controls available and identified B cells based on the high levels of Ig transcripts. A total of 2711 cells that passed the quality control were displayed on the UMAP space (Fig. 8A).We determined that lung B cells contained immature B cells (IGHM+, VPREB3+), mature naïve B cells (IGHD+,YBX3+), unswitched memory B cells (CD44+, TNFRSF13B+), switched memory B cells(ITGB1+, S100A10+), germinal center B cells (RFTN1+, RGS13+) and B1 cells (CRIP1+, SPN+, VIM+) (Fig. 8B). Lungs of patients with COPD were more abundant than control in B1 cells (Fig. 8C and Supplementary Fig. 9A-B). Gene Ontology analysis for B1 cells revealed that significantly upregulated genes were enriched in B cell receptor signaling pathway, B cell activation and membrane raft organization and T cell co-stimulation in B1 cells compared with other clusters (Fig. 8E). Moreover, compared with control samples, ADAM10 expression is increased in B1 cells from patients with COPD (Fig. 8D). These findings indicate that increased ADAM10 expression in B1 cells is associated with the pathogenesis of COPD.

In summary, the data indicate that ADAM10 upregulation on B1 cells activates Notch2 signaling, promoting B1 cell activation and differentiation. These activated B1 cells then interact with T cells, facilitating pulmonary TLS formation and driving autoantibody production, which collectively contribute to alveolar destruction and emphysema progression in COPD (Supplementary Fig. 9C).

Discussion

The functional impact of B cells in COPD continues to be debated, as evidence suggests they may contribute to autoimmune-mediated pathology while also playing a critical role in host defense during exacerbations [21, 22]. In our study, we observed a significant expansion of B1 cells and the presence of B-cell-containing TLS in the lungs of emphysema mice. Functionally, B1 cells produce natural antibodies, primarily IgM, IgA, and IgG3, which facilitate the clearance of apoptotic cells and their components [23]. Notably, aberrant expansion of B1 cells is associated with autoimmune diseases such as lupus [24]. Our in vitro co-culture experiments further demonstrated that emphysema-derived B1 cells possess a strong ability to stimulate naïve CD4⁺ T cell activation and proliferation, defining a direct cellular mechanism by which B1 cells may orchestrate local immune responses in emphysema.

Our findings demonstrated that B cells contribute to COPD emphysema. Depletion of B cells alleviated emphysema symptoms, including parenchyma dilation/destruction, mucus production, and lung inflammation, resulting in improved lung function. There also appeared to be a reduction in serum PCNA and ARPA levels, which are autoantibodies associated with systemic autoimmunity, such as SLE [25, 26], indicating broad suppression of pathogenic autoantibody production.

Furthermore, single-cell sequencing of the B1 cell subset implicated ADAM10 as a crucial mediator in emphysema. ADAM10 deficiency in B cells has been suggested to lead to compensatory upregulation of ADAM17, which in turn drives the overproduction of TNF-α and the subsequent destruction of secondary lymphoid follicle structures, as evidenced by the rectified tissue structure observed in TNF-knockout ADAM10B−/− mice [27]. ADAM10 is also known to regulate several key aspects of conventional B2 cells, including MZB cell fate determination [16, 17], T-B cell interactions via ICOSLG shedding [18, 19], plasma cell differentiation [28], and B cell survival via BAFF receptor cleavage [29, 30]; however, its specific role in B1 cells remains largely unexplored. Our study provides novel insights into the function of ADAM10 in B1 cells. We found that ADAM10 was highly expressed in emphysema-associated B1 cells and was mechanistically linked to their pathogenic function.This elevation aligns with established regulatory mechanisms, as inflammatory signaling pathways (e.g., IL-1α/p38/ERK/PI3K) and nuclear receptors (e.g., PPARα) are known to directly drive ADAM10 transcription, suggesting that the inflammatory environment of the emphysematous lung may promote ADAM10 upregulation in B1 cells through these conserved pathways [31]. Inhibition of ADAM10, or its downstream Notch pathway, significantly suppressed the proliferation of B1 cells and their ability to induce ICOS expression on co-cultured T cells. This establishes a specific “ADAM10-Notch” functional axis that drives the expansion and T cell-stimulatory capacity of B1 cells in emphysema. Our findings revealed that ADAM10 blockade significantly reduced the number of B cells, particularly B1 cells, and alleviated the severity of emphysema, supporting the hypothesis that ADAM10 promotes emphysema progression by expanding the pathogenic B1 cell population via Notch signaling and facilitating aberrant T-B cell interactions, thereby sustaining TLS formation and autoantibody production.

Particularly, autoantibodies targeting oxidative stress-induced carbonylated proteins may drive autoimmunity in COPD and contribute to emphysema [32, 33]. Therefore, profiling a wider panel of established COPD-relevant autoantibodies would strengthen our conclusions. In addition, previous findings have confirmed the association of B1 cells with autoimmunity [34, 35]. Based on our results, we propose that B cells play an important role in emphysema pathogenesis. This insight could help guide late-stage COPD treatment, given that TLS formation and emphysema progression typically occur during the mid-to-late stages of COPD [6, 8]. Moreover, we identifiedthe ADAM10-Notch signaling axis as a key mediator of this process and propose that it drives emphysema pathogenesis by expanding B cells and promoting TLS formation. The significant improvement in lung function and the upregulation of oxygen-related pathways following ADAM10 inhibition underscore its potential therapeutic value in emphysema. Based on these findings, future studies should further clarify the mechanistic role of ADAM10 in human COPD B cell subsets and explore its potential as a therapeutic target in emphysema.

Materials and methods

Animals and experimental models

Wild-type C57BL/6 mice were purchased from Vital River Laboratory (Beijing, China). Mice were housed and fed in specific pathogen-free (SPF) facilities at Capital Medical University. The emphysema mouse model was developed using intratracheal instillations of 0.5 U of PPE (Sigma, St. Louis, MO, USA) dissolved in 50 µLsterile PBS (The control group received sterile PBS (50 µL) using the same protocol. All mice were anesthetized with 1.5–2.0% isoflurane using a mask before each intratracheal instillation, and they were sacrificed two days after the last instillation. All experimental protocols were approved by the Ethics Committee for Animal Studies of Capital Medical University (AEEI-2021-272).

Histological staining

Following euthanasia, the left lungs were collected from the challenged animals, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde(Solarbio, China), and paraffin-embedded for histological analysis. Four-micrometer-thick tissue sections were cut and stained with H&E and the AB-PAS Staining Kit (Solarbio) to evaluate lung inflammation, MLI, and mucus secretion. Images were acquired using a Nikon microscope (Nikon, Japan) and NanoZoomer-SQ (Hamamatsu, Japan).

Immunofluorescence

After deparaffinization, lung sections were subjected to heat-induced antigen retrieval using citrate buffer (pH 6.0). Following blocking with Immunol Staining Blocking Buffer (Beyotime) for 1 h at room temperature, sections were simultaneously incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. After thorough washing, sections were exposed to secondary antibodies (Supplementary Table S1). Images were acquired using a TCS SP8 STED confocal microscope (Leica Microsystems, Germany).

TLS detection and quantification

TLS were defined as tertiary lymphoid structures with the presence of segregated T cell and B cell areas and were identified using immunofluorescence. TLS in H&E-stained lung sections were then labeled. The number of dense TLS was quantified per 10×high-power field (HPF) in each section. TLS density was calculated as the number of TLS per mm2 in each lung region. TLS quantification was performed using NanoZoomer-SQ (Hamamatsu).

B cell depletion

B cells were depleted using an anti-mouse CD20 monoclonal antibody (clone MB20-11, catalog BE0356, BioX Cell), which has been shown to rapidly and sustainably deplete murine B cells. To achieve depletion, mice received a single intravenous injection of the CD20 antibody (100 µg/mouse) into the tail vein two days prior to intratracheal PPE instillations.

ADAM10 inhibition

The ADAM10 inhibitor used was GI254023X (HY-19956, MedChemExpress, USA) [36, 37]. For in vivo studies, 6-8-week-old C57BL/6J mice received the inhibitor via intraperitoneal injection at 30 mg/kg (200 µL per mouse), four times per week throughout the 4-week modeling period. Control mice received an equal volume of vehicle solvent. The working solution was prepared by diluting the stock solution (100 mg inhibitor in 100 µL DMSO) with a solvent containing 10% DMSO, 40% PEG300, 5% Tween-80, and 45% saline.

ELISA

The total serum concentrations of anti-PCNA and ARPA, BAFF and APRIL were measured using commercial ELISA kits according to the manufacturer’s protocols (Beijing Weioukai Technology Co., Ltd, China). The results were analyzed using an EnSpire Multimode Plate Reader (Perkin Elmer, Waltham, MA, USA) at 450 nm.

Hemoglobin detection

Hb concentrations were assessed weekly using a hemoglobin assay kit (TC0207; Leagene, Beijing, China). Blood samples (20 µL) were collected from the jugular vein of mice and anticoagulated with an equal volume of EDTA. The assay was performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, blank, standard, and test tubes were prepared. A 20 µL aliquot of each blood sample was mixed with the detector working solution. After incubation at room temperature for 4 min, the results were analyzed using an EnSpire Multimode Plate Reader (Perkin Elmer) at 410 nm. The Hb concentration (g/L) of each sample was determined according to manufacturer’s instructions.

Quantitative reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction

Briefly, total RNA was isolated using TRIzol reagent (Cat. No. 15596018; Thermo Fisher Scientific), and cDNA was synthesized using the 5× All-In-One RT MasterMix (Applied Biological Materials, Richmond, BC, Canada). qPCR was performed on an ABI Prism 7500 Sequence Detection System (Thermo Fisher Scientific) using BlasTaq 2× qPCR MasterMix (Cat. No. G891; Applied Biological Materials), according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The complete list of primer sequences is provided in Supplementary Table S2.

Lung function test

The whole-body plethysmography (WBP, Tow Intelligent Technology, Shanghai, China) was used to measure the lung function indexes in mice. The environment was kept quiet and the mice were placed in the WBP chambers for acclimatization. The test was started until mice were in a steady state. Penh, Te, F, EF50, PAU and Rpef were detected and recorded.

Flow cytometry

Single-cell suspensions from lung samples of mice were prepared by enzymatic digestion followed by RBC lysis buffer treatment (eBioscience, China). Tissue samples were triturated and filtered through a 70-µm nylon cell strainer (BD Falcon, Bedford, MA, USA). After counting, cells were resuspended and incubated in the dark with Zombie NIR™ Fixable Viability Kit (BioLegend, USA) for 10 min at room temperature, followed by washing with PBS. An Fc receptor binding inhibitor (BioLegend) was used to block nonspecific FcγR-mediated antibody binding, followed by PBS washing and staining with respective antibodies (Supplementary Table S1). For cell from coculture in vitro, cells were collected and stained with respective antibodies (Supplementary Table S1). Data were acquired using a BD LSRFortessa X-20 cell analyzer (BD, USA), while quantification was performed using Flowjo software v10.8.1 (Tree Star, USA).

ScRNA-seq analysis

As our previous study, the mouse emphysema model was established via weekly intratracheal instillation of PPE four times. scRNA-seq analysis of whole lung cells isolated from one mouse for either control group and emphysema group [38]. The human samples were obtained from the “Adams_Kaminski_2020” dataset. Specifically, 13 samples from the Normal group and 13 samples from the COPD group were included, with each sample containing more than 20 B cells. A unique molecular identifier (UMI) count matrix was processed using the R package Seurat (version 3.3.1). To remove low-quality cells and potential doublets, cells with UMI or gene counts outside the range of mean +/- 2 standard deviations, assuming a Gaussian distribution of UMI/gene numbers, were filtered out. Cells in which > 10% of the counts belonged to mitochondrial genes were also discarded. Library size normalization was performed using the Normalize Data function in Seurat to obtain normalized counts. To remove batch effects in single-cell RNA-sequencing data, mutual nearest neighbors (MNN) correction was performed using the R package batchelor. Graph-based clustering was performed to group cells according to their gene expression profiles using the FindClusters function in Seurat. Marker genes for each cluster were identified using the FindAllMarkers function (test.use = bimod) in Seurat, which identifies positive markers for a given cluster compared with all other cells. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using the FindMarkersfunction (test.use = MAST) in Seurat. A minimum cell-percentage threshold of 0.25% in addition to P-value < 0.05 and |log2foldchange| > 0.58 were set as the thresholds for significant differential expression. GO enrichment and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses of DEGs were performed using R software based on a hypergeometric distribution.

In vitro experiment

Naïve T cells and CD19⁺were isolated from the spleens of naive mice. B1 and B2 cell subsets were isolated from the lung tissue of PPE-induced emphysema model mice. All cell types were purified by FACS AriaⅢ Cell Sorter (BD, USA). For the co-culture, 5 × 105 B cells were seeded with 1 × 106 T cells per well of 96-well plate. The following five experimental groups were established: (1) T cells + control; (2) T cells + B2 cells; (3) T cells + B1 cells; (4) T cells + B1 cells + 10 µM ADAM10 inhibitorGI254023X (HY-19956, MedChemExpress, USA); (5) T cells + B1 cells + 50 nMNotch inhibitorBMS-906,024 (HY-15670, MedChemExpress, USA).Cells were treated by ADAM10 inhibitorat 10 µM [39] and Notch inhibitorat 50nM [40]. Cells were incubated for 48 h and analysed by flow cytometry.

CFSE-based proliferation assays

The isolated cells were washed with sterile PBS and resuspended at a density of 1 × 106 cells/mL. Cells were labeled with CFSE (1:1000 dilution, Invitrogen)under light-protected conditions, followed by washing with 5 volumes of pre-warmed PBS. Cells were incubated for 48 hoursand CFSE dilution was measured by flow cytometry. After the incubation period, cells were harvested and analyzed by flow cytometry. The proliferative response was assessed based on CFSE dilution in gated CD19⁺ B cells and CD3⁺CD4⁺ T cell populations.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0 software (GraphPad Software, USA). Unpaired t-tests were used to evaluate statistical differences between the two treatment groups. One-way ANOVA was used to assess differences among three or more treatment groups. Two-way ANOVA was used for comparisons involving two or more experimental groups with two independent categorical variables. All data are presented as mean ± SEM, and statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

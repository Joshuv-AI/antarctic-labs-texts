Dapagliflozin reduces proteinuria in IgA nephropathy patients receiving immunosuppressive therapy
Authors: Shujun Wang, Lawei Yang, Peijia Huang, Jingjing Zheng, Qian Wang, Hua-Feng Liu, Yongzhi Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors reduced proteinuria in patients with IgA nephropathy; however, their efficacy in patients at high risk of progression receiving immunosuppressive agents and renin angiotensin-aldosterone system inhibitors remains unclear. After 3 months of low-dose steroid alone or combined with mycophenolate mofetil, as well as renin angiotensin-aldosterone system inhibitors treatment, 105 biopsy-proven IgA nephropathy patients with proteinuria greater than 0.5 g/d were included in this study. The changes in proteinuria and renal function were analyzed at 3 and 6 months after initiating dapagliflozin. Among 105 patients, the MEST-C lesions of M1, E1, S1, C1, C2 and T1 were more frequent and accounted for 74.3%, 61.0%, 80.0%, 50.5%, 8.6% and 40%, respectively. Baseline characteristics were similar between 49 patients in the dapagliflozin groups and 56 patients in the control groups. After 6 months of therapy, the total remission rate in the dapagliflozin group was higher than that in the control group (55.1% vs. 33.9%, p < 0.05), the odds ratio (95% confidence interval) was 2.390 (1.085, 5.262). Besides, the median proteinuria level in the dapagliflozin group was significantly lower than that in the control group (307.92 (164.75, 616.31) mg/g vs. (505.58 (248.97, 864.96) mg/g), p < 0.05). The mean increases in serum albumin in the dapagliflozin and control groups were 2.94 g/L and 0.66 g/L, respectively (p < 0.05). Furthermore, adverse events were similar in the dapagliflozin and control groups. For IgA nephropathy patients with severe renal pathology and are being treated with low-dose steroid or combined with mycophenolate mofetil, dapagliflozin can further reduce proteinuria.Keywords: IgA nephropathy, sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors, immunosuppressive agents, proteinuria

Introduction

Immunoglobulin A nephropathy (IgAN) is the most prevalent primary glomerular disease worldwide [1,2]. Approximately 30% of patients with IgAN progress to end-stage renal disease (ESRD), but the therapeutic approaches for IgAN is limited, with unsatisfactory treatment effects [3]. Renin-angiotensin system (RAS) inhibitors, including angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEIs) or angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs) are the traditional cornerstones of IgAN management [4,5]. Immunosuppressive therapy is usually reserved for patients who are at high risk of progressive chronic kidney disease (CKD) and have not responded to supportive measures by RAS inhibitors [6]. A Chinese clinical trial suggested that low-dose steroids with mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) may be effective for treating IgAN [7]. Our retrospective analysis also showed that combining steroids with MMF significantly reduced proteinuria and stabilized renal function in patients with severe IgAN [8]. However, even when receiving RAS inhibitors combined with immunosuppressive therapy, some patients are still at risk of developing ESRD.

Proteinuria is a prominent characteristic of IgAN, and the risk of renal failure is raised as the degree of proteinuria increases [9]. However, recent studies have found that even patients with low levels of proteinuria (0.5 g/d < proteinuria > 1.0 g/L) exhibit decreased estimate glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) and an increased risk of kidney failure [10]. Therefore, the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) 2024 Clinical Practice Guideline for the Management of IgAN (public review draft) suggested that additional treatment should be started in all patients with proteinuria > 0.5 g/d. However, some patients with IgAN receiving combined immunosuppressive with optimized supportive therapy continue to exhibit persistent proteinuria of more than 0.5 g/d. Hence, more combination therapy choices are required to maximize treatment effectiveness in these patients.

Sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) have been shown to exert protective effects in patients with CKD [11,12]. In the DAPA-CKD study, dapagliflozin significantly reduced proteinuria by 26% and the risk of major adverse renal events by 71% in patients with IgAN. However, IgAN patients at high risk of progression who were receiving steroids or immunosuppressive drugs were not included in this trial [13]. Hence, in this study we investigated whether the addition of SGLT2i in IgAN patients with persistent proteinuria more than 0.5 g/d, despite receiving RAS inhibitors combined with steroids or steroids + MMF, could further reduce proteinuria and delay the progression of renal failure risk.

Methods

Study design

This was a retrospective single-center study. Our study was conducted in compliance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Affiliated Hospital of Guangdong Medical University (PJKT2023-108). Informed consent was obtained from all the patients. We reviewed the medical records from our IgAN follow-up registration database (Siyuan Hospital Customer Relationship Management, Siyuan Technology Industrial Co., Ltd, Changsha, China) at the Affiliated Hospital of Guangdong Medical University. The database contained 1636 patients with biopsy-proven IgAN from January 2019 to December 2022. Among them, 750 patients received glucocorticoid with or without MMF (Figure 1).

Study population flow chart. IgAN: Immunoglobulin A nephropathy; MMF, mycophenolate mofetil; Scr, serum creatinine; eGFR, estimated glomerular filtration rate.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) newly diagnosed IgAN patients who were in the initial immunosuppressant treatment phase (receiving glucocorticoid and MMF therapy less than three months), when the glucocorticoid dose was still greater than 0.3 mg/kg/d, and the MMF dose was greater than 1 g/d; (2) the glucocorticoid or MMF dose was increased, or the immunotherapy regimen was changed, during follow-up; (3) proteinuria ≥ 3.0 g/d or the presence of other systemic diseases, such as diabetes, malignant tumor, severe hepatitis, or severe infections; (4) bilateral renal artery stenosis; (5) patients being treated with finerenone; (6) patients with a follow-up period of less than 6 months; and (7) patients did not have regular renal function or the urine monitoring. Ultimately, adult biopsy-proven IgAN patients who had undergone an initiation phase of glucocorticoid with or without MMF for at least 3 months and then entered a maintenance phase of reduced-dose glucocorticoid (0.1 ∼ 0.3 mg/kg/d) or MMF (0.5 ∼ 1.0 g/d) but still had a proteinuria > 0.5 g/d, serum creatinine (Scr) levels < 265 μmol/L, and eGFR > 25 mL/min/1.73 m2 were included. All participants had already received ACEI/ARB treatment for at least 3 months before receiving glucocorticoid or MMF.

All participants were divided into two groups based on whether or not were treated with dapagliflozin. The patients in the dapagliflozin group were administered dapagliflozin at a daily dose of 10 mg.

Data collection

Demographic and clinical data were obtained, including sex, age, blood pressure, and revised Oxford Classification MEST-C scores [mesangial hypercellularity (M), endocapillary hypercellularity (E), segmental glomerulosclerosis (S), tubular atrophy/interstitial fibrosis (T), and crescent (C)]. Medication usage during the follow-up period, including the administration of RAS blockers, steroids, and immunosuppressants, was collected.

Laboratory data included urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR), hematuria, hemoglobin, albumin (ALB), Scr, urea, eGFR, fasting blood-glucose (FBG), potassium, sodium, and phosphorus. The eGFR was calculated with the chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration (CKD-EPI) formula using Scr.

Adverse event data were also collected, including hypoglycemia, urinary tract infection, liver function impairment, gastrointestinal reaction, etc. The adverse events were evaluated as follows: (1) Hypoglycemia: A decrease in serum glucose, below 2.8 mmol/L, is considered hypoglycemia. (2) Urinary tract infection (UTI): If the white blood cells in the sediment of the midstream urine centrifuged are >10/HPF, and the number of colonies in the midstream urine culture is ≥105/mL, accompanied by urinary tract irritation signs, it can be diagnosed as urinary tract infection. (3) Liver function impairment: Any abnormality of alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase or total serum bilirubin is diagnosed as liver function impairment. (4) Headache: Pain in the frontal, parietal, temporal and occipital regions. (5) Gastrointestinal reaction: Those who meet the evaluation results of “affirmative”, “very likely” or “likely” for nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, indigestion, upper abdominal pain, constipation, gastritis, flatulence, abdominal distension, gastroesophageal reflux and abdominal discomfort as stipulated in the “Measures for the Administration of Reporting and Monitoring of Adverse Drug Reactions” of China.

Curative effect evaluation

We assessed dapagliflozin efficacy by comparing changes in ACR and eGFR between the two groups after 3 and 6 months treatment. Complete remission of proteinuria was defined as UACR < 300 mg/g/Cr and stable renal function (eGFR decreased by no more than 50%). Partial remission of proteinuria was defined as an ACR reduction of at least 50% but greater than 300 mg/g/Cr from values recorded at enrollment and stable renal function (eGFR decreased by no more than 50%). Ineffective treatment was defined as a decrease in proteinuria that did not meet the above criteria or an increase in proteinuria. Total remission rate = (CR + PR)/total cases ×100%.

Statistical analysis

Continuous data were assessed for normality. Normally distributed data are presented as mean (standard deviation), and nonnormally distributed data are presented as median (interquartile range). Categorical data are summarized as percentages and were evaluated by Chi-square test. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 26.0 software (IBM Corporation, New York, United States). Paired t test was applied for parametric comparisons, while Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used for non-parametric comparisons. A two-sided P value < 0.05 represented statistical significance.

Results

Baseline characteristics

Between January 2019 to December 2022, we identified 105 patients with biopsy-proven IgAN who have already received low-dose glucocorticoid with or without MMF for at least 3 months but still exhibited a proteinuria > 0.5 g/d. These 105 patients were grouped according to dapagliflozin use. As shown in Table 1, there were 49 participants in the dapagliflozin group and 56 participants in the control group. The two groups had similar baseline characteristics, except that serum ALB levels were slightly lower in the dapagliflozin group. There were no statistically significant differences in age, sex, systolic/diastolic pressure, immunosuppressive regimen, cumulative dosage of glucocorticoid and MMF, hematuria, ACR, FBG, urea, Scr, serum uric acid, or eGFR between the groups. The frequencies of MEST-C lesions scores were M1 74.3%, E1 61.0%, S1 80.0%, C1 50.5%, C2 8.6%, and T1 40% in these total of 105 patients, respectively, suggesting that high histological MEST-C score at diagnosis. There were no significant differences in the percentages of M1, S1, T1-2, and C1-2 lesions between the two groups (p > 0.05), but there were significantly fewer E1 lesions in the dapagliflozin group than in the control group (p < 0.01).

Dapagliflozin reducing proteinuria in IgAN patients receiving immunosuppressive therapy

As shown in Table 2, dapagliflozin lowered proteinuria among patients who were already receiving glucocorticoid with or without MMF. After 6 months of therapy, the total remission rate, defined as urinary protein remission, in the dapagliflozin group was 55.1%, which was significantly higher than that seen in the control group 33.9% (p = 0.029), the odds ratio (95% confidence interval) was 2.390 (1.085, 5.262). In addition, as shown in Figure 2A–D, the median proteinuria level in the dapagliflozin group was 307.92 (164.75, 616.31) mg/g, which was a slightly lower than in the control group (505.58 (248.97, 864.96) mg/g) after 6 months of treatment (p < 0.05). Meanwhile, the changes of the median proteinuria had a decreasing trend, as the median proteinuria levels at baseline and after 3 and 6 months of dapagliflozin treatment were 851.63 (547.02, 1403.21) mg/g, 640.30 (300.80, 1029.35) mg/g, and 307.92 (164.75, 616.31) mg/g, respectively. Importantly, these changes over time within the dapagliflozin group was significant (p < 0.001). Meanwhile, the mean reduction in ACR of dapagliflozin group −493.24(-784.93, −335.25) was significantly greater than that of the control group −273.74 (−497.69, −14.01) (p < 0.001). Additionally, the mean increase in ALB in the dapagliflozin group (2.94 g/L) was slightly higher than that in control group (0.66 g/L) at six months (p < 0.05). These results suggest that dapagliflozin can further reduce proteinuria in patients with IgAN during the maintenance treatment phase of reduced-dose glucocorticoid with or without MMF.

Adverse events

In the dapagliflozin group, two patients developed hypoglycemia, and two patients developed urinary tract infections (Table 5).

Discussion

In the present study, we found that, for IgAN patients at high risk of disease progression, if proteinuria is still greater than 0.5 g/d during the maintenance treatment phase with reduced-dose glucocorticoid with or without MMF, the addition of dapagliflozin can further reduce proteinuria. This findings provides insights into the use of dapagliflozin, in combination with RAS inhibitors and corticosteroid-based immunosuppression, represents a promising approach to reduce proteinuria for IgAN patients at high risk of disease progression.

Significant controversy surrounds the use of glucocorticoid and MMF use in patients with IgAN. Current international guidelines recommend that glucocorticoids only be considered for use in patients with IgAN who remain at high risk of progressive CKD despite supportive care with ACEI/ARBS treatment (KDIGO Glomerular Disease Work Group, personal communication, 2021) [4]. However, the TESTING study had revealed a significant reduction in proteinuria and risk of kidney failure when steroids were added to the treatment regimen of patients with IgAN [14,15]. In addition, recent studies have suggested that combining low-dose steroids with MMF may reduce proteinuria and the risk of kidney failure in patients with IgAN [7,16]. M, S, T, and C scores are strongly associated with proteinuria [17]. The 105 patients in our study had a high prevalence of MEST-C lesions ((M1, 74.3%; E1, 61.0%; C1, 50.5%; C2, 8.6%). Our findings indicate that low-dose steroids with or without MMF therapy decreases proteinuria and stabilizes eGFR in patients with IgAN with severe renal pathology. These results suggest that targeting the immune system with corticosteroid-base immunosuppression is effective in patients with IgAN at the early stage of disease. However, given the relatively short study period, we were unable to evaluate the lifetime kidney failure risk associated with immunosuppressive therapy in these patients.

Proteinuria is a critical predictor of prognosis in IgAN, even modest reductions in proteinuria are associated with improved long-term renal outcomes. A series of studies have demonstrated the renal protective effects of SGLT2i, especially in patients with diabetes [18,19]. Subgroup analysis of the DAPA-IgAN cohort also confirmed the role of SGLT2i in reducing proteinuria in chronic nephritis, and shown that the mean percentage difference in ACR was −35% after 4 month of dapagliflozin treatment. However, DAPA-IgAN cohort did not include any participants receiving immunotherapy [13]. On the contrary, we included IgAN patients at high risk of progression, all of these patients have received corticosteroid-base immunosuppression also the supportive care with RAS inhibitors. Notably, we found that the antiproteinuric effect of dapagliflozin was sustained, the addition of dapagliflozin could further reduced the mean ACR −493.24(-784.93, −335.25) at 6 months. This indicating that the additive antiproteinuric benefit of dapagliflozin is independent of RAS blockers and immunosuppressants. Further, there were no cases of major hypoglycemia in patients with IgAN receiving dapagliflozin, confirming that dapagliflozin was well tolerated in this population. SGLT2i have been shown to affect a number of pathophysiological processes involved in the development and progression of kidney disease. For instance, it is known that these inhibitors ameliorate glomerular hypertension through tubuloglomerular feedback, which in turn contributes to a decrease in proteinuria [20,21]. In vitro and in vivo studies also have demonstrated that SGLT2i ameliorate multiple pathological processes, including endothelial dysfunction [22,23], podocyte loss [24], mitochondrial damage [25], fibrosis [26], inflammation and oxidative stress [27]. It is likely that these mechanisms may act together to modulate the immune-mediated injury in IgAN, and thus to slow the progression to kidney failure. However, further studies are required to elucidate the therapeutic mechanisms underlying protective effects of SGLT2i on IgAN.

Consistent with the DAPA-CKD trial, we observed a slight increase in eGFR to 0.49 mL/min/1.73 m2 after dapagliflozin treatment for 6 months. The mean age of the participants in the dapagliflozin group in our study was 33 (28, 46) years, which is younger than that of the DAPA-CKD study participants 52.2 (13.1) years, and the mean eGFR was 72.23 (42.67, 110.51) ml/min/1.73 m2, which is higher than the mean eGFR of 44.3 (12.4) ml/min/1.73 m2 reported in the DAPA-CKD study [28]. In addition, cellular proliferation in biopsy lesions was more frequent in our study: M1 (73.5%), E1 (42.9%), C1 (55.1%), and C2 (8.2%). This indicates that participants in our study were in the early stage of the disease. Thus, it is possible that comprehensive treatment is more likely to be effective when IgAN in initiated early, rather than when the kidneys accumulate significant irreversible fibrosis.

In conclusion, our results provide important clinical evidence for IgAN patients at high risk of progressive loss of kidney function be treat with SGLT-2 inhibitor. For the renal benefits of adding dapagliflozin, SGLT2 inhibitors may be prescribed as optimized supportive measures to high risk patients with IgAN in the future.

Limitations and strengths

Several limitations of the present study should be considered. Firstly, due to a small scale, and non-randomization retrospective single-center study, there is a baseline imbalance in serum ALB levels and E1 lesions between groups, which may impact the interpretation of the results. Future larger-scale, multi-center and randomized controlled studies with long-term are needed to validate the role of dapagliflozin in IgAN patients with severe renal pathology. Secondly, it is necessary to further explore the potential mechanisms underlying SGLT2i benefits in IgAN patients.

Potential ecological risk and accumulation of heavy metal(loid)s in soils of Macao in China as a non-industrial and tourist City
Authors: Yao Ma, Boran Shao, Wenjun Li, Ying Li, Yang Wu, Ni Xiong, Bingzheng Zhou, Yibing Ma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith rapid industrialization, the accumulation and contamination of heavy metal(loid)s in soil often has occurred. However, the accumulation of heavy metal(loid)s in soil in non-industrial and tourist cities with high population densities is not well known. Therefore, the Macao Special Administrative Region (Macao SAR) was taken as an example to investigate the spatial and temporal changes in the characteristics of soil heavy metal(loid)s in non-industrial and tourist cities. The total concentrations of heavy metal(loid)s (As, Co, Cd, Cr, Cu, Pb, Ni, Hg, and Zn) in soil as well as the ecological risk in different functional areas and geographic locations were studied by combining statistical analysis, the land accumulation index, and the potential ecological risk index. Finally, the data in 2018 and 2023 were compared. The results show that in terms of spatial distribution, the high CVs (coefficients of variation) of 0.67 and 0.73 for Cr and Hg, respectively, showed Cr and Hg were more likely to have point sources of contamination. The skewness value of 0.16 for Zn suggests that the distribution may be slightly skewed and flat, with a more uniform distribution, probably due to the high-density distribution of the population and atmospheric deposition. Compared with other areas, heavy metal(loid) concentrations in soils were generally higher in the Macao Peninsula, which may be related to the higher population of waste incineration plants and residential areas located there. Geographically, Hg concentrations in soils were higher near the airport and transportation areas than in other areas. From 2018 to 2023, heavy metal(loid)s (except Zn) in the soils of Macao did not increase significantly but showed a decreasing trend due to tourism-oriented activities, high rainfall, the absence of industry, and the COVID-19 pandemic. Cr, Cd, Zn, and Hg occur more frequently in the lower concentration ranges than the other heavy metal(loid)s and generally show decreasing trends. Although the potential ecological risk of heavy metal(loid)s in Macao was generally low, As had the highest potential risk. Most of the lightly contaminated sites (62%) had a higher geo-accumulation index of Zn than for the other heavy metal(loid)s. This paper can be used as the data support for future research on soil heavy metal(loid) distribution and government policies for non-industrial cities.Keywords: Heavy metal(loid) accumulation, Tourist cities, Potential ecological risk, Spatial-temporal distribution, Source identificationSubject terms: Environmental sciences, Environmental chemistry, Environmental impact

Introduction

In recent years, the rapid development of cities has triggered a series of environmental issues that cannot be ignored. As an important part of the ecosystem, the health of urban soil is directly related to the sustainable development of cities1. Soil heavy metal pollution is an unavoidable environmental problem in urbanization2–4. So far, worldwide, soil heavy metal(loid)s (although As is not a heavy metal, it has metal-like properties, and is, hereafter, collectively referred to as a heavy metal with other elements) in industrialized cities are generally well studied5,6. Soil heavy metal(loid)s mainly come from industrial and traffic emissions, predominantly from industry, and the contaminated soils often exhibit strong potential ecological risks. However, the situation of heavy metal(loid)s in non-industrial cities dominated by tourism is unknown. In China, some tourist cities retain some industries, and industrial and transportation emissions are still important sources of metals in urban areas, with the increasing source share of transportation7,8. However, the situation in rainy zones such as the southeastern coast of China is not yet known. Although some researchers have also focused on non-industrial cities or non-industrial areas of integrated cities in recent years9,10there is a lack of reliable long-term monitoring. No systematic study on the trend of soil heavy metal(loid)s has been done in these areas, especially in some cities with high-intensity tourism. Therefore, the study of soil heavy metal(loid)s in non-industrial and tourist cities is necessary for sustainable development.

It is well known that soil heavy metal(loid) contamination is primarily caused by industrialization and urbanization. While numerous studies have reported contamination levels in industrialized cities, the potential contribution of urbanization-related sources-such as domestic waste and traffic emissions-in urban areas remains unclear and largely unreported. The Macao SAR is a highly urbanized city in China, known for its historical, cultural, and tourism-oriented character11. Therefore, we selected Macao as a representative case. The objective of this study is to investigate the extent of soil heavy metal(loid) contamination in such fully urbanized but non-industrialized cities and to explore its spatial and temporal characteristics.

Materials and methods

Study area

The Macao SAR (22°06’39″ − 22°13’06″N, 113°31’33″ −113°35’43″E) is located on the southeast coast of mainland China, on the west bank of the Pearl River Delta. Its soil is dominated by loamy sand and sandy loam. The average annual temperature of Macao is 22.8 °C, with a subtropical monsoon climate. Macao consists of the Macau Peninsula, Taipa Island, and Coloane Island, with nearly half of its soil area formed by land reclamation. Currently, Macao has a total population of 686,400. The per capita GDP (Gross Domestic Product) is 134,000 USD (United States Dollar), the highest in Asia. Tourism is an important economic pillar of Macao, and the gambling industry, combined with the development of tourism, has become one of the unique mainstream economies of Macao12.

Sampling site distribution and sample collection

Soil samples were collected in December 2023. Before sampling, on the basis of analyzing the land use map, soil type map, and geological map of the study area, a total of 29 sampling sites were laid out using ArcGIS 10.8 software on the base map of Macao, and, excluding the areas that could not be sampled, such as the Macao International Airport and the Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macao Bridge highway port area (marked in gray). During the field sampling process, appropriate adjustments were made according to the actual environment around the preselected sampling sites, and the Global Positioning System (GPS) was used to determine the actual coordinates of the sampling sites and generate a schematic map of the sampling sites (Fig. 1). Based on functional zoning, the sampling sites were classified into five categories: conservation area (n = 4), parkland (n = 9), coastal zone (n = 5), transportation area (n = 4), and commercial and residential area (n = 7).

Distribution of sampling sites in Macao.

In the five-point sampling method around the sampling site, the soil was collected at a depth of 10 cm after removing debris, mixed in equal amounts, and then put into polyethylene plastic bags, with the original weight of the samples greater than 1500 g, and 29 soil samples were collected. The sample number, sampling location, sampling date, and the surrounding environmental conditions were recorded during sample collection.

Measurement methods

Soil pH was measured using a pH meter at the ratio of soil: water as 1:2.5. Soil organic matter (SOM) was determined using the potassium dichromate volumetric method. Soil oxidation-reduction potential (ORP) was determined using the in situ potentiometric method. Soil electrical conductivity (EC) was determined using the conductivity method. Soil total salt (dissolved solids) concentration (TDS) was determined using the mass method.

Air-dried soil samples (0.25 g each) were digested using a mixture of hydrochloric acid (HCl, 10 mL), nitric acid (HNO₃, 5 mL), hydrofluoric acid (HF, 5 mL), and perchloric acid (HClO₄, 3 mL). The resulting digests were dissolved in 2 mL of 1:1 (v/v) HCl and subsequently diluted with ultrapure water. The total concentrations of Co, Cd, Cr, Cu, Ni, Pb, and Zn in the soil samples were determined by inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS; Agilent 7500, Agilent Technologies). The method exhibited standard recoveries ranging from 75 to 125%, with limits of repeatability (r) between 0.2 mg/kg and 66.1 mg/kg, and limits of reproducibility (R) ranging from 0.4 mg/kg to 186 mg/kg. For Hg, soil samples were digested using a nitric acid–hydrochloric acid mixture under reflux in a boiling water bath. For As, soil samples were digested using aqua regia. The concentrations of Hg and As were determined by atomic fluorescence spectrometry (AFS-8220, Beijing Titan Instruments Co., Ltd.). Under repeatability conditions, the limits (r) were 0.08 mg/kg (As) and 0.05 mg/kg (Hg); under reproducibility conditions, the limits (R) were 0.15 mg/kg (As) and 0.12 mg/kg (Hg).

Soil contamination risk assessment

Geo-accumulation index

The geo-accumulation index (Igeo) has been widely used to evaluate the heavy metal contamination in soils, including contamination levels and characteristic13. The Igeo is calculated using the following formula:

Cn = content of element n in soil;

Bn = geochemical background value for element n;

K = factor (typically 1.5) for variations in background values that may be caused by local rock variations.

The following groupings are used to describe the Igeo classes: level 0: practically unpolluted (Igeo < 0), level 1: unpolluted to moderately polluted (0 < Igeo < 1), level 2: moderately polluted (1 < Igeo < 2), level 3: moderately to strongly polluted (2 < Igeo < 3), level 4: strongly polluted (3 < Igeo < 4), level 5: strong to very strongly polluted (4 < Igeo < 5) and level 6: very strongly polluted (Igeo > 5).

Potential ecological risk index

The ecological risk was determined by the potential ecological risk index (RI). The RI integrates the toxicity and concentration of a wide range of heavy metal(loid)s in soils to assess the potential risk to ecosystems14especially when considering the impacts of heavy metal(loid)s on biodiversity and human health. The formula (2) for computation of RI is as follows:

Ei = the potential ecological risk factor for the given substance;

Ti = the “toxic-response” factor for the given substance (As = 10, Cd = 30, Cr = 2, Cu = 5, Ni = 5, Pb = 5, Zn = 1, Hg = 40, Co = 5)15;

Ci = measured value of this heavy metal in soil, mg/kg;

Bi = risk control standard for this element16.

The following classes were used to deleneated the risk factor Ei: low potential ecological risk (Ei < 40); moderate potential ecological risk (40 ≤ Ei < 80); considerable potential ecological risk (80 ≤ Ei < 160); high potential ecological risk (160 ≤ Ei < 320); and very high ecological risk (Ei ≥ 320). The following classification scheme is used to delineate the RI values: low ecological risk (RI < 150); moderate ecological risk (150 ≤ RI < 300); considerable ecological risk (300 ≤ RI ≤ 600); and very high ecological risk (RI > 600).

Data analysis

In this study, the MS Excel 2019 and statistical software (SPSS 27.0.1) were used to analyze the descriptive statistics and test the normal distribution of heavy metal(loid) concentrations in the surface soil of the study area. Origin 2022 was used to draw the distribution maps of heavy metal(loid) concentrations for the different land uses and the maps of the results of the pollution evaluation. The partial correlation analysis was conducted using the ppcor package in R (version 4.4.3), and the correlation coefficient matrix was visualized with the ggplot2 package. The maps of China and Macao were obtained from the Standard Map Service Platform of the Ministry of Natural Resources of China (http://bzdt.ch.mnr.gov.cn/). The spatial distribution maps were generated using the Ordinary Kriging interpolation method in ArcGIS 10.8. Prior to interpolation, data were tested for normality and transformed where necessary. Semivariograms were fitted and validated through cross-validation to ensure interpolation reliability. Kriging with few samples may reduce accuracy; its assumption of spatial stationarity may not hold in heterogeneous environments.

Results and discussion

Current status of heavy metal(loid) distribution in soils in Macao

Statistical description of heavy metal(loid)s

To obtain a macroscopic understanding of the distribution pattern of heavy metal(loid) concentrations in Macao soils, mathematical descriptive statistics of the concentrations of nine heavy metal(loid)s were computed (Table 1). Compared with the bordering Guangdong Province17the mean values of Cd, Pb and Zn were higher in Macao (see Table 7). This may be because Macao has a higher population density, and, therefore, generates more solid waste and transportation emissions, which in turn affects the increase in atmospheric deposition. The skewness (S) and kurtosis (K) provide information about the shape of the data distribution. The skewness value of Cr was as high as 1.44 and the kurtosis value was 2.81, which indicates that the distribution may be seriously right-skewed, with extremely high values occurring. While Zn had a skewness value of 0.16 and a kurtosis value of −1.04, indicating that the distribution may be slightly left skewed and flat. The CVs of Cr and Hg were higher, 0.67 and 0.73, respectively, which indicated that the concentrations of these two heavy metal(loid)s varied greatly among samples. They were more likely to have point sources of contamination. The CVs of Co, Pb, and Zn were lower, and their extreme ratios (Max/Min) were relatively small, indicating that their concentrations vary over a relatively small range. Compared with some industrial cities like Du’an Autonomous County of Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region in China18 which has CVs in the range of 0.703 to 4.82, the CVs of heavy metal(loid)s in the soil of Macao were all relatively low. This may be because Macao has fewer industrial emissions, and, thus, point source pollution is less common, so there are fewer instances of high values, and the distribution of the individual heavy metal(loid)s in the soil is relatively more homogeneous.

Risk assessment of heavy metal(loid)s contamination of soil in Macao

Igeo of heavy metal(loid)s

Table 2; Fig. 2 show the statistics of the ground accumulation index of nine heavy metal(loid)s in Macao. Among them, all were non-polluted except Zn. At all the sites As showed no pollution, 80% or more of the sites showed no pollution for Co, Ni, Cu, Cr, and Hg, and the remaining sites were only mildly polluted. The most serious pollution was found for Zn, its Igeo mean value was 1 level (mild pollution), and the number of sites showing pollution was 18, accounting for 62% of the total number of sites, of which 3 were moderately polluted. Cd and Pb also had one site with a medium degree of contamination, and moderately polluted sites accounted for 55%, and 45%, respectively, of the sites.

Ecological risk index of heavy metal(loid)s

Due to the low Igeo in Macao, no obvious pollution was observed, and the potential ecological risk assessment of the nine heavy metal(loid)s was done (Table 3). The combined potential risk (RI) was 69.52, which was in the low-risk category, and, thus, it is concluded that the risk of heavy metal(loid) contamination in Macao is low. In terms of single-factor potential risk (Ei), all the heavy metal(loid)s belong to the low-risk category except As, which exceeded the low-risk threshold by 0.25%, and contributed the most to the risk with 57.69%, so the combined potential ecological risk mainly came from As. Cr, Co, and Pb contributed 25.80%, 13.09%, and 1.41%, respectively, which were the higher contributors, and were also the main contributors to the risk. The contribution rates of other heavy metal(loid)s were less than 1%. Several studies have shown that the dominant element in the RI of industrial and mudflat urban areas is Cd19which is not quite consistent with the results from Macao. This may be due to the different functionality of different cities, e.g., high atmospheric deposition due to traffic and population in Macao, and, thus, the change in the percentage of potential risk from each heavy metal(loid).

Partial correlation analysis between heavy metal(loid)s and other soil physicochemical properties in Macao

Figure 3 shows the correlation analysis of nine heavy metal(loid)s with other physicochemical properties of soil in Macao. Soil pH has a significant effect on the forms, adsorption capacity, and migration transformation of heavy metal(loid)s. At the same time, heavy metal(loid)s react with other chemical components in the soil, changing the chemical nature of the soil and the conditions of the reaction, thus, affecting the pH of the soil. However, different heavy metal(loid)s have different degrees of influence20,21. In this study, pH was found to have some correlation with Cr, and Zn.

Heatmap of partial correlation coefficients among soil physicochemical properties and heavy metal(loid)s, controlling for the effects of all other variables. Significant correlations are indicated by p < 0.05 (*), p < 0.01 (**) and p < 0.001 (***).

Organic matter in soil (SOM) can form stable complexes or chelates with heavy metal(loid)s, which affects the bioavailability and mobility of heavy metal(loid)s22,23. Therefore, soil organic matter (SOM) exhibited a significant negative partial correlation with As (r = −0.61) and Pb (r = −0.47), indicating its critical role in immobilizing these elements.

Distinct partial correlations were also detected among different heavy metal(loid)s, such as between Ni and As, and between Zn and Hg, suggesting potential co-mobilization or common sources. This may be due to the small area of Macao, where several heavy metal(loid)s have common geological backgrounds, and are subjected to the same environmental modes.

Regional characteristics of heavy metal(loid) distribution in Macao

Spatial distribution characteristics of soil heavy metal(loid)s in Macao

Figure 4 shows the spatial distribution of a total of nine heavy metal(loid)s (As, Cu, Co, Cr, Cd, Hg, Ni, Pb, and Zn) in Macao, except for the Macao International Airport and the Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macao Bridge highway port area. Generally, the concentration of each heavy metal(loid) in the Macao Peninsula was more homogeneous and higher than the rest of Macao, because the Macao Peninsula is the primary residential area of Macao with more anthropogenic activities and has the largest waste incineration plant in Macao. Additionally, the concentration of each heavy metal(loid) was relatively high in the southern part of Coloane and tends to decrease gradually from south to north. The levels of As, Cd, Co, Cu, Ni, Pb, and Cr were particularly high, which may be related to the improper disposal of materials or industrial wastes in some specific industrial areas in the region24. The distribution of Hg was more pronounced in the coastal areas of Macao, or the areas close to the water bodies. The enrichment of this element was related to ocean currents, industrial emissions, and long-range atmospheric transport. Soil around airports may have higher levels of mercury, mainly due to aircraft emissions and mercury released during the combustion of aviation fuel, especially in areas with frequent aircraft landings and takeoffs. This is consistent with O’Connor et al.25 who found coal combustion activities emit large amounts of Hg into the environment. The distribution of Zn was prevalent in several areas of Macao, especially in areas with heavy traffic, and may be related with vehicle emissions, tire wear, and urban runoff26. This is broadly similar to the distribution pattern of Hg.

Spatial distribution of the total amount of nine heavy metal(loid)s in Macao soils.

Characteristics of heavy metal(loid) distribution in different functional areas of Macao

In Table 4, heavy metal(loid)s were generally lower in the parkland areas, especially for As and Ni. For example, the concentration of Ni was more than 50% lower than in the other functional areas, suggesting that parkland areas may be less affected by Ni contamination. Except for Co and Pb, all other elements had the highest concentrations in commercial and residential areas, which may be related to more human activities and domestic waste in these areas. Coastal zones also had higher levels of Cu, Cd, and Zn, which may be related to marine activities and harbor operations as well as population density. Co was higher in the transportation zone with mean values of 9.28 mg/kg which may be related to traffic emissions, wear and tear on vehicles, and road maintenance materials, while transportation activities increase the Co content of airborne particulate matter, entering the soil through atmospheric deposition. Co and Cr in the conservation areas showed greater variability, which is related with the specific environmental conditions at different sites. Overall, transportation and commercial and residential areas are the main input areas for heavy metal(loid)s, mainly due to dense population and high anthropogenic emissions.

Analysis of heavy metal(loid) principal components and sources in Macao soil

The heavy metal(loid)s in soil generally originate from five sources: industrial emissions, the use of pesticides and fertilizers, urbanization, transportation activities, and the natural geological background9,31,34,35. In Tables 5 and 6; Figure 7, the coefficients for PC1, PC2, and PC3 are derived from the Component Score Coefficient Matrix. The formulas are: PC1=(0.111*Co) + (0.160*Ni) + (0.175*Cu) + (0.193*Cd) + (0.07*Pb) + (0.132*Cr) + (0.173*As) + (0.146*Hg) + (0.175*Zn), PC2 = (−0.085*Co) + (−0.260*Ni) + (0.174*Cu) + (−0.096*Cd) + (0.407*Pb) + (−0.194*Cr) + (−0.188*As) + (0.319*Hg) + (0.123*Zn), PC3 = (0.778*Co) + (0.034*Ni) + (−0.065*Cu) + (−0.012*Cd) + (0.073*Pb) + (−0.572*Cr) + (0.156*As) + (−0.006*Hg) + (−0.193*Zn). Each heavy metal(loid) showed a strong association to several of the sources, which is because most of these heavy metal(loid)s originate from the soil-forming parent material of Macao soils. Table 6 indicates that in Macao the heavy metal(loid)s results from the high degree of urbanization, because its mainstay industry only is tourism. As a non-industrial city, there was no pathway for heavy metal(loid) deposition in Macao from industries. Atmospheric deposition of solid wastes from human activities and emissions from transportation become the main causes of heavy metal(loid) formation in Macao.

Our results were compared with the 2018 environmental survey data of Macao27 and regional soil background values33. Since no specific background values have been reported exclusively for Macao, we referred to soil sample data from Guangdong Province, which borders Macao. The soil samples from Guangdong were digested using a mixed acid system consisting of HNO3-HF-HClO4, and the resulting solutions were analyzed by atomic absorption spectrometry (AAS), atomic fluorescence spectrometry (AFS) and inductively coupled plasma emission spectrometry (ICP-OES). These data were obtained from regional geochemical surveys conducted by authoritative geological institutions. In Table 7, Cd, Pb, and Zn were the heavy metal(loid)s that had increased the most compared to the background values for Guangdong Province (since there were no separate soil background values for Macao, the values for Guangdong Province are used). These three elements are mostly from transportation emissions, which also confirms the contamination sources in Table 7.

It has been shown in several other studies that heavy metal(loid)s mainly enter the soil through atmospheric deposition (about 30%)36,37 and surface runoff (about 40%)38,40. The erosive action of seawater and rainwater may transport 10–20% of the heavy metal(loid)s41. Uptake by plants, transformation, and immobilization by microorganisms account for about 10–20%42. However, in the current study, atmospheric deposition was a much higher percentage of the heavy metal(loid) accumulation pathway. From 2018 to 2023, Macao’s per capita waste generation was 1.74–2.22 kg/day, much higher than the global level of 0.79 kg/day12. Therefore, the atmospheric deposition due to waste incineration and solid waste accumulation in Macao were higher than the global level, which also supports the current results.

Macao is located in the western region of the Pearl River Estuary and is strongly influenced by the maritime climate, resulting in relatively low atmospheric concentrations of heavy metal(loid)s12. The subtropical climate with abundant rainfall also brings to Macao a strong intensity of ground scouring and surface runoff, which carries some of the heavy metal(loid)s in the soil to the ocean43. This has led to a decreasing trend in soil heavy metal concentrations over the past five years (2018–2023), especially for As, Co, and Cr, whose average values were even below the soil background. The Macao region also has a strong natural purification capacity, as the oceanic climate may contribute to the diffusion and dilution of pollutants in the atmosphere, reducing the deposition rate of heavy metal(loid)s.

The Report on the State of the Environment of Macao 202212, published by the Macao SAR Government, points out that in 2022, due to the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic, socio-economic activities in Macao have decreased, resulting in a decrease in the amount of municipal solid waste and the annual average concentrations of several air pollutants. The report also mentions that land transportation is the main source of air pollutants in Macao and that the government is accelerating the promotion of the use of electric vehicles to reduce pollution. Therefore, due to the implementation of various policies and the COVID-19 pandemic, the decrease in the concentration of heavy metal(loid)s in soil during these five years is also justified.

Conclusions

In summary, spatially, among the unevenly distributed heavy metal(loid)s, the spatial distribution of Cr and Hg was more variable, suggesting that they are more likely to be affected by point sources of pollution. On the contrary, the distribution of Zn was generally more homogeneous, which may be related to the high population density and atmospheric deposition. Soil heavy metal(loid) concentrations near waste incineration plants and residential areas on the Macao Peninsula were higher than that in other areas. Similarly, soil Hg concentrations near the airport and transportation areas were higher than that in other areas. From 2018 to 2023, heavy metal(loid)s (except Zn) concentrations in Macao soils kept a stable balance and did not increase significantly, and even more showed a decreasing trend, which may be related to the COVID-19 pandemic, tourism-oriented economy, high rainfall, and the lack of industry in Macao. The heavy metal(loid)s of Cr, Cd, Zn, and Hg often were at lower concentration ranges and showed a decreasing trend in general. The potential ecological risk of heavy metal(loid)s (except As) in Macao was generally low. Based on the geo-accumulation index, Zn usually accumulated more in the sampling sites with a tendency to be contaminated.

Importantly, there was a decreasing trend in soil heavy metal(loid) accumulation in Macao, which sets an example of the soil management model. However, in the area around airports, waste disposal sites, and densely populated areas, the source controls of the heavy metal(loid)s should be paid more attention. Therefore, this also suggests that heavy metal(loid) accumulation in non-industrial and tourist cities can be zero or negative if the important sources of heavy metal(loid)s are rationally managed.

However, although there are the limited accessible land in the densely populated Macao region, the study systematically revealed the spatial distribution of heavy metal(loid)s in soils across different functional zones of Macao, providing valuable reference data for understanding soil pollution in non-industrial and tourist cities. Future studies could improve and validate the spatial patterns identified here by increasing sampling density or including samples from other urban areas.

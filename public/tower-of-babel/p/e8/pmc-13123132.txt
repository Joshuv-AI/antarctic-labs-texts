Complete genome sequence of Streptococcus shenyangsis isolated from buccal mucosa

Objective

The genus Streptococcus represents a dominant and metabolically diverse bacterial group within the human oral microbiome, encompassing both commensal and opportunistic species that influence host–microbe interactions [1–4]. Within this genus, the mitis group streptococci comprise a cluster of closely related species that predominantly inhabit the human oral cavity [5]. Although numerous oral streptococcal species have been taxonomically characterized, genomic information for many remains limited, resulting in an incomplete understanding of their ecological adaptations and evolutionary relationships [6, 7].

Streptococcus shenyangsis, first described from the human upper respiratory tract, shows close phylogenetic relatedness to Streptococcus oralis subsp. dentisani, a representative member of the oral mitis group frequently isolated from the human oral cavity [8, 9]. Given that S. oralis is a core mitis-group species with established roles in early dental biofilm formation, its genetic similarity to S. shenyangsis indicates that S. shenyangsis may also occupy related ecological niches within the oral microbiome. However, only one draft genome is currently available for this species, limiting comparative genomic analysis and accurate placement within the S. mitis group.

To address this gap, we sequenced and assembled the complete genome of S. shenyangsis strain KHUD_015, isolated from the buccal mucosa of a healthy Korean adult. Comparative analysis with the type strain D19T was performed to explore potential genomic variation and strain-specific features. This work expands the currently limited genomic repertoire of S. shenyangsis and provides a high-quality reference genome that facilitates comparative genomics across oral streptococci. The resulting dataset offers a foundation for future investigations into the population structure, metabolic potential, and ecological roles of S. shenyangsis within the complex microbial community of the human oral cavity.

Data description

The S. shenyangsis strain KHUD_015 was obtained from the buccal mucosa of a healthy Korean female adult and cultivated on Trypticase soy agar supplemented with defibrinated sheep blood under anaerobic conditions at 37°C. Genomic DNA was isolated using the Wizard HMW DNA Extraction Kit (Promega, USA). Species identification was verified by sequencing the full-length 16S rRNA gene (~ 1.5 kb) amplified with universal bacterial primers (forward: 5’-AGAGTTTGATCCTGGCTCAG-3’; reverse: 5’-GGTTACCTTGTTACGACTT-3’) [10], followed by BLAST analysis against the GenBank database.

Whole-genome sequencing of S. shenyangsis strain KHUD_015 was performed using the PacBio Sequel II platform. A Single-Molecule Real-Time (SMRT)bell library was constructed following the manufacturer’s instructions (Pacific Biosciences). A single SMRT cell run produced 207,540 HiFi reads, with an average read length of 7,924 bp and an N50 value of 9,197 bp (Data file 1). De novo genome assembly was carried out using the Microbial Genome Analysis pipeline in SMRT Link v11.0 (https://www.pacb.com/support/software-downloads/) with default settings. Gene annotation was performed using the NCBI Prokaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline (PGAP).

The complete genome of S. shenyangsis KHUD_015 consists of a single circular chromosome of 2,153,473 bp with a G + C content of 40.0% (Data file 2 and 3). The genome encodes 1,906 protein-coding genes, four rRNA operons (5 S, 16 S, 23 S) and 59 tRNAs. Benchmarking Universal Single-Copy Orthologs (BUSCO) v5.5.0 [11] analysis using the “lactobacillales_odb10” dataset (304 genomes, 402 BUSCOs) yielded 99.8% completeness (Data file 4). Average Nucleotide Identity (ANI) analysis showed 94.57% similarity between strain KHUD_015 and the reference genome (GCA_006385785.1). Taxonomic assignment was further validated by digital DNA–DNA hybridization (dDDH) and genome-based phylogenetic analysis using the Type (Strain) Genome Server (TYGS). The dDDH values (d0: 76.5%, d6: 74.8%) exceeded the 70% species threshold, with a minimal G + C content difference (0.11%) (Data file 5). Genome-based phylogeny based on the Genome BLAST Distance Phylogeny (GBDP) approach showed robust clustering of KHUD_015 with the S. shenyangsis type strain D19ᵀ (100% bootstrap support; Data file 6).

Comparative genomic analysis against the only publicly available S. shenyangsis genome (ASM638578v1, isolated from the oropharynx) identified 1,506 core gene clusters shared between the two strains and a total of 586 strain-specific clusters (Data file 7). Of these, 349 clusters comprising 353 genes were unique to strain KHUD_015, while the remaining 237 clusters were specific to the reference strain ASM638578v1 (Data file 8). Functional annotation of these unique gene clusters revealed enrichment in COG functional categories related to DNA replication and repair (L), transcription (K), and cell wall/membrane/envelope biogenesis (M). Additional genes were assigned to defense mechanisms (V) and inorganic ion transport and metabolism (P). The presence of unclassified genes (S) suggests potential niche-specific functions that may support adaptation to the oral environment.

The genome of S. shenyangsis strain KHUD_015 represents the first complete genome derived from an oral isolate of this species and expands its limited genomic representation. This high-quality genome provides a foundational resource for comparative and functional genomic analyses, particularly in relation to its phylogenetic proximity to S. oralis and other mitis group streptococci. Limitation

This study is based on the genomic characterization of a single Streptococcus shenyangsis isolate (KHUD_015) obtained from a healthy Korean adult. Therefore, the genomic features described here may not fully represent the pangenomic diversity or global population structure of the species. Additional genomes from diverse hosts, geographic regions, and oral niches will be required to comprehensively elucidate intraspecies variation and ecological adaptation.

Joint model based on intratumoral and peritumoral computed tomography radiomics integrated with clinical features for predicting the spread through air spaces in lung adenocarcinoma: a multicenter study
Authors: Chen-Zheng-Ren Bao, Rong Zhang, Sheng-Yao Deng, Zi-Wei Liu, De-Hua Chen, Jin-Song Sun, Qiu-Gen Hu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe presence of spread through air spaces (STAS)-positive tumors is associated with an elevated risk of local and distant recurrence following sublobar resection, a risk that is not observed in patients undergoing lobectomy. Accurate assessment of STAS necessitates postoperative pathological diagnosis. The objective of this study was to develop a joint machine learning model that combines intratumoral and peritumoral computed tomography (CT) radiomics with clinical features for the preoperative prediction of STAS in lung adenocarcinoma, thereby facilitating optimal selection of surgical strategy and treatment plan to mitigate the likelihood of secondary surgery for patients.MethodsThis retrospective study collected data from patients with lung adenocarcinoma from three centers. The dataset from one center was randomly divided into a training group and an internal validation group at a ratio of 7:3, while the datasets from the two other centers were used as the external testing cohort. The random forest machine learning algorithm was employed to construct independent radiomics and clinical feature models. Subsequently, logistic regression was applied to develop a mixed model integrating intratumoral, peritumoral, and clinical features. The performance of the model was evaluated via area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) analysis, and the results were visualized through nomogram representation. Additionally, calibration curves and decision curves were employed to assess both the goodness of fit and clinical utility of the model.ResultsThe results revealed that vacuole sign (P<0.001), crescent sign (P=0.002), gender (P=0.021), air bronchogram (P=0.001), spiculation (P=0.032), and consolidation tumor ratio (CTR) (P=0.005) were significantly correlated with STAS status. The joint model integrating intratumoral, peritumoral, and clinical features demonstrated exceptional performance in predicting STAS in lung adenocarcinoma. In the training cohort, the model achieved an AUC of 0.985 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.9697–1.000], with a sensitivity of 93.8% and a specificity of 93.7%. The internal validation cohort exhibited an AUC of 0.988 (95% CI: 0.9703–1.000), sensitivity of 90.9%, and specificity of 95.7%. In the external test cohort, the model maintained robust predictive performance with an AUC of 0.851 (95% CI: 0.7952–0.9073), a sensitivity of 84.6%, and a specificity of 74.3%. The model outperformed individual clinical and radiomics models, as well as other common machine learning classifiers such as extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), support vector machine (SVM), and multilayer perceptron (MLP) in terms of AUC and overall accuracy. The nomogram visualization of the joint model provides an intuitive tool for clinicians to assess the risk of STAS preoperatively, facilitating the formulation of precise treatment strategies.ConclusionsThe joint model developed in this study, integrating intratumoral, peritumoral, and clinical features, serves as a robust tool for predicting the occurrence of STAS in patients with lung adenocarcinoma. This model can aid in the formulation of precise and appropriate surgical strategies.Keywords: Lung adenocarcinoma, spread through air spaces (STAS), radiomics, prognostic model, peritumoral features

Introduction

Lung cancer ranks second in incidence among cancers worldwide and has the highest mortality rate. Among the various pathological types, adenocarcinoma of the lung is the most common (1). In 2015, the World Health Organization (WHO) classification of lung cancer introduced spread through air spaces (STAS) as a novel invasive pattern for lung adenocarcinoma (2). Evidence suggests that STAS-positive tumors are associated with an elevated risk of local and distant recurrence following sublobar resection, a risk not observed in patients undergoing lobectomy (3). Given that STAS assessment necessitates postoperative pathological diagnosis, the preoperative identification of STAS holds significant clinical implications (4).

Radiomics, as an emerging data-mining technology, is predicated on the extraction of a myriad of quantitative features from medical imaging data through computational techniques. These features encompass morphological characteristics, intensity patterns, textural attributes, and functional information. They are often difficult for the human eye to recognize or quantify, and so radiomics are used to transform digital medical images into discernible pathophysiological features (5-7). The analytical pipeline of radiomics involves several key stages: procurement and preprocessing of medical imagery, delineation of regions of interest (ROIs), extraction of features, selection of salient features, and the construction and validation of predictive models (8,9).

The majority of studies in the field have focused on intratumoral lesions, with far less attention being accorded to the nuanced alterations within the peritumoral zone. Emerging evidence suggests that peritumoral radiomics is capable of distinguishing between benign and malignant lesions and can offer valuable clinical insights into alterations of the tumor microenvironment, such as angiogenesis, lymphangiogenesis, and the invasion of microvessels and lymphatics. These features may be indicative of the biological behavior of tumors, aiding in the characterization of tumor aggressiveness and the prognostication of outcomes (9-12). Peritumoral radiomics has only been sparsely applied in the prognostic prediction of patients with STAS lung adenocarcinoma. A few studies have demonstrated that joint models outperform individual clinical models or pure radiomics models in predicting the histological grading and invasiveness of malignant tumors (13,14). The aim of this study was thus to develop a combined machine learning model that fuses intratumoral and peritumoral computed tomography (CT) radiomics and clinical features to predict STAS in lung adenocarcinoma before operation so as to inform the selection of the surgical strategy and treatment plan. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD + AI reporting checklist (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-2024-2836/rc).

Methods

Patients

We retrospectively collected data from patients with primary lung adenocarcinoma confirmed by surgical pathology from March 2020 to March 2024. The inclusion criteria were (I) a preoperative thin-section CT scan and (II) complete clinical, radiological, and pathological information. Meanwhile, the exclusion criteria were as follows: (I) history of previous lung surgery, history of radiotherapy and chemotherapy for previous malignancies, poor quality of CT images; (II) pathological results lacking STAS assessment; (III) presence of ≥2 tumors in one patient; and (IV) presence of other malignancies.

In compliance with the ethical standards of Chencun Hospital, Affiliated to Shunde Hospital of Southern Medical University (The First People’s Hospital of Shunde), this study enrolled 183 cases from this center, 97 and 86 of whom were positive and negative for STAS, respectively. Among these patients, 70% were randomly allocated to the training dataset, while 30% were allocated to the internal validation dataset. In order to verify the performance of the optimal model, an additional 187 cases were recruited from two other centers: the Shunde Hospital of Southern Medical University (The First People’s Hospital of Shunde) and the Foshan Shunde District Traditional Chinese Medicine Hospital (Guangzhou University of Traditional Chinese Medicine Shunde Traditional Chinese Medicine Hospital). The inclusion and exclusion criteria for the external test were the same as those for participants recruited from Chencun Hospital. This multicenter study adopted a single central institutional review board (sIRB) model to improve efficiency by eliminating duplicate reviews and to ensure consistency in ethical standards across all participating sites. The analyses in this study were exclusively retrospective in nature and used deidentified clinical data, with no additional patient intervention or contact being required. The participating institutions completed data-sharing agreements compliant with local regulations. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Chencun Hospital Affiliated to Shunde Hospital of Southern Medical University (The First People’s Hospital of Shunde District, Foshan City) (approval No. 100901). The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the analysis.

Histological assessment and analysis

All tumor tissues acquired from surgical resection were fixed with 10% formalin, paraffin-embedded, sectioned at a thickness of 5 mm, and then stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E). The tumor sections were analyzed by two experts with over 15 years of experience in pathological diagnosis who had no knowledge of the patients’ clinical information. In cases of disagreement, a consensus was reached through discussion. STAS was defined as the presence of detached tumor cells that were not in direct contact with the main tumor mass and observable in the alveolar spaces surrounding the periphery of the main tumor in the lung parenchyma.

CT image acquisition and analysis of radiological characteristics

All patients underwent thin-section CT scans of the entire lung with the Aquilion Prime 80-slice scanner (Canon Medical Systems, Otawara, Japan), the Ingenuity Core 128 64-slice scanner (Philips Healthcare, Amsterdam, the Netherlands), the SOMATOM Definition AS 64-slice scanner (Siemens Healthineers), or the SOMATOM Definition Flash 64-slice multidetector spiral CT scanner (Siemens Healthineers). The scanning protocol included breath-hold training prior to the procedure, and full lung scans were conducted after a deep inhalation and breath-hold. The scanning parameters were as follows: tube voltage =120 kV, tube current =250–300 mA, field of view (FOV) =350–400 mm, pitch =0.8–1.0, collimation =0.6×128, and reconstruction thickness =1.5 mm. After the scan, multiplanar image reconstruction was carried out, and the images were subsequently transmitted to the Picture Archiving and Communication System (PACS).

The imaging analysis and feature collection were conducted by two radiologists with over 10 years of experience in diagnostic imaging who had no knowledge of the patients’ clinical information or the presence of STAS. In cases of disagreement, confirmation was provided by a senior radiologist with 20 years of diagnostic experience and a higher professional title. The radiological features of lung adenocarcinoma were observed and recorded, such as tumor CT value, tumor location, spiculation, tumor density, air bronchogram sign, tumor-lung interface, maximum tumor diameter, lobulation, pleural indentation sign, crescent sign and vacuole sign, and consolidation tumor ratio (CTR). The CTR is calculated as the ratio between the maximum diameter of the solid part of the tumor and the maximum diameter of the tumor. In this study, tumor density was categorized into three grades: pure ground-glass nodules, mixed ground-glass nodules, or solid nodules. The air bronchogram sign was classified as none, no luminal narrowing, irregular luminal narrowing, or luminal obstruction. The imaging window settings were unified as follows: window width at 1,500 Hounsfield units (HU) and window level at –600 HU.

Radiomics data preparation

Medical Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine (DICOM) images were downloaded from the PACS, and all images were subjected to resampling and standardization processes. Subsequently, two radiologists, each with over 5 years of experience in diagnosing lung cancer, used the ITK-SNAP (version 3.8) software to delineate the ROI for the tumor, taking care to avoid large blood vessels, bronchi, and other structures outside the tumor. This resulted in the acquisition of a three-dimensional volume of interest (3D-VOI). Centered on the tumor, an automatic delineation method was applied to expand outward by 2, 4, 6, 8, and 10 mm, respectively, to obtain 3D-VOIs for five peritumoral regions. The delineation results within the tumor and in the peritumoral areas were reviewed and corrected by senior physicians with more than 10 years of professional experience. Feature extraction and feature selection

We extracted 1,834 radiomics features from each 3D-VOI, including 360 first-order statistical features, 14 shape features, 440 gray-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM) features, 320 gray-level size-zone matrix (GLSZM) features, 320 gray-level run-length matrix (GLRLM) features, 100 neighboring gray-tone difference matrix (NGTDM) features, and 280 gray-level dependence matrix (GLDM) features. We normalized all radiomics features to a common scale to eliminate the effects of dimensional differences. The intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was used to evaluate the reliability of the features, with only those with an ICC of 0.75 or higher being retained. We then applied the Mann-Whitney test to assess statistical significance and selected features with a P value below 0.05.

To ensure high reproducibility, we calculated the Spearman rank correlation coefficient for feature interdependence, retaining only one feature when any two features had a correlation coefficient exceeding 0.9. To maximize the preservation of characteristic representation, we employed a greedy recursive deletion strategy for feature selection, which involved the iterative removal of the most redundant feature in the current set.

We employed a least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression model for feature selection and model construction. Using a 10-fold cross-validation approach based on the minimization criterion, we fine-tuned the regularization parameter λ to achieve the minimum cross-validation error. The model subsequently retained features with nonzero coefficients, which were then employed to construct the regression model and aggregated into a radiomics signature. Furthermore, based on the retained features, we calculated the radiomics score for each patient through their linear combination. The entire LASSO regression modeling process was implemented via the scikit-learn library in Python.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted with SPSS 26.0 software (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R 4.0.3 statistical software (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing). For nonnormally distributed continuous data, the results are presented as the median and interquartile range. Comparisons between groups were performed with the Mann-Whitney test. Categorical data are expressed as numbers and percentages, and comparisons between groups for unordered categorical data were conducted via the Chi-squared test or the Fisher exact probability method. Comparisons for ordered categorical data were conducted with the Mann-Whitney test. A P value less than 0.05 was considered to indicate statistically significant differences.

Construction and validation of prediction model

In this study, we integrated radiomic features processed by LASSO into a random forest machine learning model to construct a predictive model. The optimal peritumoral model was selected and merged with the intratumoral radiomics to form an integrated intratumoral-peritumoral model. Diagnostic efficacy was evaluated via accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity. The clinical model was developed starting from univariate analysis, with meaningful clinical and radiological parameters screened at a threshold of P<0.05. Subsequently, multivariate logistic regression was performed to identify independent prognostic factors, from which the clinical prediction model was constructed. Ultimately, the clinical model was combined with the integrated intratumoral-peritumoral model to form the joint model, which was visualized with a nomogram. The performance of the joint model was assessed via the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, and the performance of the joint model with the clinical models and the intratumoral-peritumoral model was evaluated. To evaluate the calibration effect of the nomogram, a calibration curve was plotted, and the Hosmer-Lemeshow test was used to assess the calibration accuracy. Additionally, decision curve analysis (DCA) was conducted to determine the clinical utility of the predictive model. To comprehensively evaluate the performance of our proposed method in predicting STAS in lung adenocarcinoma and to better demonstrate the strengths and applicability of our approach, we compared our method with other common machine learning classifiers, using the same datasets as applied in our study to ensure the fairness of the comparison. To verify the generalizability of the joint model, we retrospectively recruited patients from the other two centers who met the inclusion criteria as an external validation cohort. The predictive model was constructed with the same methodology as that used to develop the model on the training group, and the performance of the model was evaluated according to the area under the curve (AUC).

Construction of the clinical feature model

We selected the variables with P values below 0.05 from the univariate analysis and subjected them to multivariate analysis (Figure 3) to further investigate the independent predictors associated with positive STAS status. The findings revealed that vacuole sign, crescent sign, gender, air bronchogram, spiculation, and CTR were independent predictors for positive STAS. These significant predictors were then integrated into a random forest machine learning model to develop a predictive model for clinical characteristics. Within the training cohort, this model demonstrated an AUC of 0.967 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.9394–0.9946], a sensitivity of 92.2%, and a specificity of 90.5%. In the internal validation cohort, the AUC for the model was 0.895 (95% CI: 0.7911–0.9995), with a sensitivity of 84.8% and a specificity of 87.0%. Construction of the integrated radiomics model

A random forest machine learning algorithm was used to develop six distinct models encompassing both the tumor’s interior and the peritumoral zones at intervals of 2, 4, 6, 8, and 10 mm from the tumor edge. The ROC curves for the training sets were delineated, as illustrated in Figure 4B. Through a comparative analysis of the peritumoral radiomics models, the most efficacious peritumoral model was determined and subsequently merged with the intratumoral radiomics model, culminating in the construction of an integrated model incorporating both intratumoral and peritumoral radiomics features.

The AUC values for the prediction of STAS by the intratumoral radiomics model were 0.959 in the training cohort and 0.872 in the validation cohort. For the peritumoral radiomics models at distances of 2, 4, 6, 8, and 10 mm from the tumor margin, the AUC values in the training group were 0.960, 0.961, 0.973, 0.964, and 0.967, respectively, while in the validation group, they were 0.781, 0.833, 0.883, 0.806, and 0.787, respectively. Consequently, we selected the 6-mm peritumoral radiomics model and combined it with the intratumoral radiomics model, producing the integrated model. This integrated model achieved an AUC of 0.964 (95% CI: 0.9350–0.9926) in the training group and 0.912 (95% CI: 0.832–0.991) in the validation group, as depicted in Figure 4C. We further visualized the feature importance for the model, as depicted in the Figure 5. In our rigorous evaluation of feature importance within the radiomic analysis, three features emerged as the paramount: “peri_wavelet_LLL_glcm_Idm”, which captures the intrinsic texture characteristics through the wavelet-transformed GLCM with the information measure of correlation; “intra_square_glcm_Imc2”, which reflects the textural homogeneity within the tumor’s core as assessed by the GLCM’s maximum correlation; and “peri_gradient_firstorder_10Percentile”, which indicates the spatial distribution of gradients at the peritumoral margin. Their preeminence underscores the value of integrating multifaceted radiomic features in enhancing the model’s prognostic accuracy.

The feature importance for the integrated model. The analysis of feature importance identified “peri_wavelet_LLL_glcm_Idm”, “intra_square_glcm_Imc2”, and “peri_gradient_firstorder_10Percentile” as the top three predictive features, indicating their prominent role in the model’s predictive capacity.

Construction of the joint model and performance evaluation

Upon establishing the intratumoral–peritumoral integrated model, we merged it with the clinical-features model to develop a novel joint model using logistic regression. We conducted external validation using data from the two other hospitals to ensure the predictive model’s generalizability. The ROC curve, calibration curve, and decision curve for the joint model are depicted in Figure 6. The findings indicated that the predictive efficacy of the joint model (AUC =0.985; 95% CI: 0.9697–1.000) exceeded that of the clinical-features model (AUC =0.967; 95% CI: 0.9394–0.9946) and the intratumoral-peritumoral integrated model (AUC =0.976; 95% CI: 0.9548–0.9970). To facilitate clinical application, the joint model was subsequently visualized in a nomogram, as shown in Figure 7, and the confusion matrix for the joint model is presented in Figure 8. The model correctly identified 60 out of 64 positive cases of STAS, achieving a sensitivity of 93.8%. Among the 63 STAS-negative cases, it correctly identified 59, resulting in a specificity of 93.7%. The overall accuracy of the model was 93.7%, indicating its excellent performance in distinguishing between positive and negative cases of STAS. These results were in line with those observed in the external testing cohort (Figure 6B), indicating that the joint predictive model possesses superior reproducibility and reliability. Comparison with other classifiers

To comprehensively evaluate the performance of our proposed model, we compared it with several common machine learning classifiers, including extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), support vector machine (SVM), and multilayer perceptron (MLP). The experimental results (Table 4) demonstrated that our method outperformed these classifiers on multiple datasets, particularly in terms of accuracy. In the training set, our joint model achieved higher accuracy, specificity, and AUC compared to XGBoost, SVM, and MLP. In terms of sensitivity, our model was comparable to XGBoost and MLP but slightly inferior to SVM. Additionally, our joint model demonstrated excellent performance in the internal validation set, further highlighting its superior overall performance.

Discussion

In the 2015 classification by the World Health Organization (WHO), STAS was formally designated as the fourth distinct mode of dissemination for lung cancer, complementing local tumor invasion, lymphatic and hematogenous metastasis, and distant metastasis (2). STAS is characterized by the dissemination of lung cancer cells as single cells, micropapillary clusters, and solid nests into the alveoli and bronchioles adjacent to the primary tumor. Researchers, including Uruga et al. (15), have performed more nuanced semiquantitative evaluations of STAS. Furthermore, studies leveraging machine learning and deep learning techniques for radiomic analysis of STAS in lung adenocarcinoma have emerged (16,17). These investigations offer critical insights into the preoperative assessment of STAS in patients with lung adenocarcinoma, facilitating the selection of optimal surgical strategies by surgeons and potentially reducing the incidence of postoperative recurrence.

STAS is a biological behavior of lung adenocarcinoma referring to its propensity to spread circumferentially around the tumor. The predominant focus of research endeavors aimed at diagnosing STAS in lung adenocarcinoma has been on traditional imaging features, quantitative parameters, and intratumoral radiomic characteristics, with the radiomic features of the peritumoral region being largely ignored (4,18,19). In our study, we leveraged the confluence of conventional radiological attributes alongside radiomic features extracted from within and surrounding the tumor to assess their collective predictive value in the differential diagnosis of STAS in lung adenocarcinoma. In this study, we conducted manual segmentation of the ROI within the tumor and subsequently automated the segmentation of peritumoral zones at incremental distances from the tumor core (2, 4, 6, 8, and 10 mm). Following the extraction and selection of features, a comparative analysis was conducted to assess the diagnostic performance of peritumoral radiomics models in STAS diagnosis, culminating in the identification of the most effective peritumoral range (6 mm). Subsequently, we integrated these features with those from the intratumoral domain to forge a novel radiomic model, which exhibited superior predictive capabilities, with an AUC of 0.964 (95% CI: 0.9350–0.9926), surpassing the efficacy of either modality in isolation. In the integrated model, the feature “peri_wavelet_LLL_glcm_Idm” was derived from the gray-level co-occurrence matrix and included low-frequency information obtained through wavelet transformation to characterize the uniformity and texture smoothness of the image. In the context of predicting air space dissemination, this feature may reflect the homogeneity and consistency of the peritumoral microenvironment. Generally, a heterogeneous architectural pattern is associated with an elevated risk of tumor invasion and subsequent dissemination. The feature “intra_square_glcm_Imc2” provides insights into the internal structural characteristics of the tumor tissue. A high degree of contrast and a complex texture within the tumor may indicate more aggressive biological behavior, thereby increasing the risk of air space dissemination. The feature “peri_gradient_firstorder_10Percentile” is based on the gradient information of the image, with a particular focus on the 10th percentile of gray-value changes in the surrounding area. This feature typically serves to describe the distribution of local gradient intensity within the image. Elevated gradient values could signify increased activity and alterations in the tissues surrounding the tumor, such as the extent of tumor cell infiltration. Analyzing these changes allows for a more accurate prediction of the dynamics within the peritumoral microenvironment, which is crucial for assessing the likelihood of air space dissemination.

Joint models have significant advantages in predicting tumor invasiveness, histological grading, and other aspects. Therefore, this study constructed a joint model to integrate clinical features and radiomic features, aiming to achieve in-depth integration of multidimensional information and thereby comprehensively improving the accuracy of disease prediction. In this study, the integrated model demonstrated outstanding predictive performance, achieving an AUC of 0.985 (95% CI: 0.962–1.000) with a sensitivity of 93.8% and a specificity of 93.7%, which was significantly higher than that of the clinical feature model (AUC =0.967) and the intratumoral-peritumoral radiomics model (AUC =0.976) (DeLong test P<0.001). In this study, we evaluated the performance of the joint model in the classification task of STAS using a confusion matrix, achieving a sensitivity of 93.8% and a specificity of 93.7%. Compared with the model in Jiang et al.’s study (20), which yielded an AUC for STAS prediction of 0.754 (with a sensitivity of 0.880 and a specificity of 0.588), our model provides a superior overall performance and is more effective in identifying positive cases of STAS. This finding strongly confirms the great potential of multidisciplinary collaborative analysis in the field of disease prediction, indicating that integrating data from different fields, such as the clinical and imaging fields, through joint analysis can build more powerful predictive models and provide more reliable and accurate decision-making support tools for clinical practice.

By comparing our proposed model with other common machine learning classifiers, such as XGBoost, SVM, and MLP, we found that our model excels in terms of AUC and overall accuracy. However, the sensitivity could be improved further. These comparative results not only validate the effectiveness of our model but also provide valuable insights for future research. Our future work will focus on further optimizing the model’s sensitivity while maintaining high accuracy, thereby enhancing the model’s reliability in practical applications. Additionally, we will use additional datasets and application scenarios to further verify the model’s generalization ability and stability.

To facilitate a more intuitive understanding for clinicians, this study visualized the joint model. Using the nomogram for visualization, we converted multiple key predictive variables and their regression coefficients into scaled line segments and displayed the relationships between these predictive variables on the same plane. This tool can help clinicians more intuitively and accurately calculate the disease risk of patients based on individual characteristics (such as gender, age, laboratory indicators, and imaging features). Clinicians can locate the corresponding points for each key indicator of the patient on the nomogram and determine the disease risk probability by quickly summing the scores matched to these points. For the joint model, the nomogram can integrate clinical features, imaging features, and radiomic features to provide an intuitive assessment tool for the preoperative prediction of STAS in lung adenocarcinoma, thereby facilitating more precise treatment strategies.

To further verify the practical application value of the joint model and its stability in different data environments, we specifically introduced an external cohort for testing. This external cohort was completely inaccessible during the model training phase, fully ensuring the objectivity and fairness of the test. The test results were encouraging: the joint model still maintained a robust predictive performance in the external test cohort, with no significant decline in the AUC value, indicating its good generalizability. This means that the model can effectively adapt to data differences in the clinical environment and accurately predict the risk of STAS from actual clinical data. It is worth noting that several studies have previously attempted to predict STAS using various machine learning methods or deep learning models. For example, Wang et al. (21) employed an XGBoost model, which achieved an AUC of 0.973 (95% CI: 0.964–0.982), while Liu et al. (22) used a deep learning model based on automatic segmentation, which attained an AUC of 0.880 (95% CI: 0.780–0.979). In comparison with these models, our model demonstrated superior performance in prediction. These comparative results not only validate the effectiveness of our model but also provide valuable insights for future research. In our future work, we plan to conduct a comprehensive comparison of various machine learning and deep learning models on a unified dataset. This will help further enhance the reliability of our model in practical applications.

In our multivariable analysis, gender, spiculation, air bronchogram sign, crescent sign, vacuole sign, and the CTR emerged as independent predictors for the occurrence of STAS in lung adenocarcinoma, with CTR recognized as an independent risk factor (Figure 9). Kim et al. and Zhang et al. posited that the proportion of solid components within a tumor can more precisely forecast the likelihood of STAS than other radiographic features, such as overall tumor size (23,24). We support the notion that the solid component of a tumor is a more accurate reflection of its heterogeneity than the ground-glass component, and patients with tumors exhibiting a higher CTR are at an increased risk for STAS, warranting more vigilant surveillance and the adoption of more proactive therapeutic approaches. Kim et al. (23) reported there being a correlation between STAS and the air bronchogram sign. In our study, a significantly higher proportion of bronchial obstruction was observed in STAS-positive cases (P<0.001). We believe that the occurrence of air space dissemination leads to the obstruction of distal bronchi.

The underlying pathology of the vacuole sign in lung cancer is predominantly attributed to the preservation of lung structural frameworks, such as the alveoli, that have not been compromised or supplanted by the tumor. Additionally, some vacuoles represent aerated spaces within tumor necrosis cavities and mucin-filled glandular structures. In our study, a significantly higher prevalence of the vacuole sign was observed in cases negative for STAS than in those that were positive (P<0.001). This finding suggests that patients with lung adenocarcinoma exhibiting an augmentation of solid components are at an increased likelihood of having STAS.

This study is the first to incorporate the crescent sign (Figure 10) as a predictor for the occurrence of STAS in invasive lung adenocarcinoma. The crescent sign is characterized by the presence of smooth, arcuate, or crescent-shaped notches on the lesion’s surface and can only be definitively identified via sequential examination on multiplanar reformatted imaging (25). Pathologically, it is observed as the interlobular septa invaginating to varying extents toward the lesion, thereby obstructing the proliferation of neoplastic cells. Within the context of this study, a significantly elevated prevalence of the crescent sign was noted among individuals with STAS-negative disease (P<0.001). A study conducted by Yagi et al., leveraging 3D reconstruction and advanced multiplex immunofluorescence staining techniques, demonstrated that STAS tumor cells are capable of detaching from the primary tumor mass, traversing air spaces, and re-establishing adhesion to the alveolar walls, rather than existing in a free-floating state (26). The increased incidence of the crescent sign within the STAS-negative patient cohort in our study can be attributed to the presence of interlobular septa impeding the migration of tumor cells, consequently diminishing the propensity for STAS development. This suggests that patients lacking the crescent sign may have an increased susceptibility to STAS.

In terms of other CT findings, multiple studies have demonstrated a close association between STAS and the invasiveness of the tumor (27-29). For example, the research conducted by Han et al. (30) discovered a certain association between STAS and both lymphovascular and pleural invasion. In our study, spiculation was identified as an independent risk factor correlated with STAS. The lobulation sign is indicative of the tumor’s expansive growth pattern, suggesting variability in the proliferation rates of cells in disparate areas surrounding the neoplasm.

Conclusions

This study not only elucidated the correlation between conventional CT imaging features and the STAS status of lung adenocarcinoma but also leveraged a random forest machine learning algorithm to assess the potential for STAS through the analysis of extensive radiomics characteristics. Characterized by high predictive precision, the random forest model proficiently manages high-dimensional datasets, exhibits reduced susceptibility to overfitting, and adeptly discerns intricate nonlinear interrelationships among features. We developed a machine learning model predicated on the amalgamation of intratumoral and peritumoral CT radiomics data with clinical parameters to forecast the propensity for STAS in patients with lung adenocarcinoma. This model exhibited exemplary efficacy and was assessed through external validation, which confirmed its broad applicability. Such validation substantiates the viability of the predictive model in practical clinical contexts.

However, that this retrospective multicenter study was limited to patients who had undergone surgical resection of their tumors, potentially resulting in selection bias. Additionally, prediction based on observed CT imaging features involves a degree of subjectivity, which could lead to discrepancies between the predicted and actual outcomes of STAS. Consequently, subsequent research endeavors should incorporate a more comprehensive suite of methodologies and datasets to study STAS, including the performance of positron emission tomography-computed tomography scans and the collection of extensive pathological data. Furthermore, prospective research designs and follow-up studies are warranted to more thoroughly investigate and assess the influence of STAS on patient outcomes.

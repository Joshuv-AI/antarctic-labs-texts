Evaluation of Patient Comfort and Quality of Life Using Implant-Supported Overdentures: A Qualitative Study
Authors: Preeti Budhwar, Mahima, Simran Ahmed, Juberahamad R. Attar, Sirisha Kommuri, Vilas Patel
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPurpose:This study aims to evaluate patient comfort and quality of life improvements in individuals using implant-supported overdentures, focusing on their subjective experiences.Methods:A qualitative study design was employed, involving semi-structured interviews with 20 patients using implant-supported overdentures. Data were analyzed using thematic analysis to explore the impact on comfort, oral function, and psychological well-being.Results:Findings revealed significant improvements in mastication, speech, and overall satisfaction. Two major themes emerged: enhanced oral functionality and elevated self-esteem. However, some participants reported adaptation challenges during the initial weeks.Conclusion:Implant-supported overdentures significantly enhance patient comfort and quality of life, addressing both functional and psychosocial aspects. Further studies are needed to generalize these findings to larger populations.

INTRODUCTION

Tooth loss impacts both functional and psychological aspects of an individual’s life. Conventional dentures, although widely used, often fail to provide adequate retention and stability, leading to compromised oral function and reduced quality of life.[1] Implant-supported overdentures have emerged as a promising solution, offering improved stability, retention, and patient satisfaction compared to traditional dentures.[2]

While quantitative studies have demonstrated functional benefits, qualitative insights into patients’ lived experiences remain scarce. Understanding these experiences is crucial to improving patient-centered care.[345] This study aims to explore the impact of implant-supported overdentures on patient comfort and quality of life through qualitative methods, shedding light on their subjective perceptions.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design

A qualitative study design was adopted to gain an in-depth understanding of patients’ experiences with implant-supported overdentures. Ethical approval was obtained from the institutional review board.

Participants

Twenty participants (12 males, eight females; aged 45–75 years) using mandibular implant-supported overdentures for at least six months were recruited. Purposive sampling ensured diversity in age, gender, and socioeconomic status. Inclusion criteria included edentulous patients with no systemic contraindications for implants and those who had completed post-prosthesis adaptation.

Data collection

Semi-structured interviews were conducted by a trained interviewer, using an open-ended question guide. Questions focused on comfort, mastication, speech, aesthetics, and psychosocial well-being. Interviews were audio-recorded with consent and transcribed verbatim.

Data analysis

Transcripts were analyzed using Braun and Clarke’s thematic analysis. Two researchers independently coded the data to identify recurring themes. Discrepancies were resolved through discussion, ensuring credibility and reliability.

RESULTS

The study included 20 participants (12 males, 8 females) with a mean age of 60 ± 10 years. Most had been edentulous for over three years (mean 3.5 ± 1.2). DISCUSSION

This study provides valuable qualitative insights into the transformative impact of implant-supported overdentures on patients’ lives. Findings align with previous quantitative research that highlights improved oral functionality and psychological well-being.[34]

Enhanced oral functionality

The ability to chew a wider variety of foods was a recurrent theme, corroborating earlier studies that demonstrated enhanced masticatory performance with implant-supported overdentures.[5] Improved speech clarity further underscores the functional superiority of these prostheses.

Psychological benefits

The psychosocial benefits observed, including increased self-esteem and social confidence, are consistent with existing literature on patient satisfaction with implant-supported overdentures.[6] This aspect is critical, as oral health is closely linked to overall mental well-being.[7]

Adaptation challenges

Initial adaptation issues reported by some participants mirror findings from previous research. Proper patient education and follow-up care can mitigate these challenges.[8]

Clinical implications

The study highlights the importance of considering patient-reported outcomes in clinical practice. Dentists should prioritize patient education and provide realistic expectations regarding the adaptation process.[891011]

Limitations

While this study provides rich qualitative insights, its findings are limited by the small sample size and single-institution setting. Future research should include larger, diverse populations and integrate mixed-methods approaches to enhance generalizability.

CONCLUSION

Implant-supported overdentures significantly improve patient comfort, functionality, and quality of life. The study demonstrated enhanced mastication (90%), speech (85%), aesthetics (95%), and self-esteem (92%), confirming their superiority over conventional dentures. While initial adaptation challenges were noted in a small percentage, these issues resolved within weeks, emphasizing the importance of patient education and follow-up care. Implant-supported overdentures effectively address both functional and psychosocial needs, contributing to better nutrition, social confidence, and overall satisfaction. These findings highlight their transformative potential, reinforcing the need for wider adoption in edentulous patients to enhance oral health and well-being. Further studies on larger cohorts are recommended.

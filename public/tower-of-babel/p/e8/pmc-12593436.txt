Tirzepatide for fertility-sparing treatment in obese/overweight patients with endometrial cancer and atypical hyperplasia: a phase II single-arm clinical trial protocol
Authors: Jing Zheng, Jie Yang, Jiaxin Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionApproximately 3–5% of patients with endometrial cancer (EC) are diagnosed under the age of 40 years, with 70% of these individuals being nulliparous. Consequently, fertility-sparing treatment for eligible young patients with endometrial lesions is increasingly being accepted. Obesity is a well-known factor closely associated with the incidence and prognosis of endometrial lesions. As obesity rates rise, weight loss interventions become particularly important in the treatment of these patients. Tirzepatide is currently the most effective glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist for weight control. This trial aims to explore the synergistic effects and safety of tirzepatide combined with standard fertility-preserving treatment for endometrial lesions.Methods and analysisThis single-arm, phase II clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety profile of tirzepatide as an adjunctive therapy to standard fertility-sparing treatment in obese or overweight patients with atypical hyperplasia (AH) or EC. The study will enrol a total of 45 patients, each receiving a combination of standard fertility-sparing treatment for EC and AH alongside tirzepatide. The primary outcomes include the reversal rate and time to remission (in months) of endometrial lesions, which are determined through endometrial pathology sampling every 3 months, as well as the percentage of weight reduction. Secondary objectives include evaluating changes in body morphology and composition, alterations in metabolic parameters during the study, as well as reproductive outcomes and the rate of tumour recurrence during follow-up.Ethics and disseminationThis study has received approval from the Ethics Committee of Peking Union Medical College Hospital (I-25PJ1055). Introduction

Endometrial cancer (EC) is one of the most common malignancies of the female reproductive tract, with an incidence rate that has been increasing annually.1 EC and atypical hyperplasia (AH) predominantly occur in postmenopausal women. However, previous studies indicate that approximately 25% of patients with EC are premenopausal, with 3% to 5% of these patients being under the age of 40 years.2 Among this group, about 70% have not yet had children at the time of diagnosis.3 In recent years, as childbearing age has been delayed, fertility-preserving treatment has become a focal point for patients with AH and early-stage EC who wish to maintain their fertility.

Obesity is defined as a body mass index (BMI) greater than or equal to 30 kg/m². Considering the characteristics of the Chinese population, obesity is defined as a BMI of 28 kg/m2 or greater.4 5 Over the past 40 years, global obesity rates have nearly tripled, according to data published by NH Phelps et al in 2024, which show that the global obesity rate among women was only 8.8% in 1990, but this figure rose to 18.5% by 2022.6 Among women, obesity exhibits the strongest association with EC compared with other malignancies.7 For every 5 kg/m² increase in BMI, the risk of developing EC increases by 50%.8 Additionally, obesity is associated with inferior prognosis in patients with EC.9 Given the close relationship between obesity and the incidence, progression and prognosis of EC, the importance of weight loss interventions in the treatment of endometrial lesions has become increasingly apparent in recent years. Weight loss treatments primarily include nutritional counselling, pharmacotherapy and surgical interventions. Currently, nutritional counselling is the primary treatment method for patients with endometrial lesions,10 but its efficacy is often limited due to social and psychological factors. At present, fertility-preserving treatment for EC primarily involves hormonal treatment such as high-dose progestin, which often leads to increased appetite and weight gain, further complicating weight management.

Since the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs), including liraglutide, semaglutide and tirzepatide, for weight loss treatment, GLP-1RAs suppress appetite by activating the GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R) signalling pathway in the central nervous system and stimulate metabolic activities in the gastrointestinal tract, pancreas and liver, thereby promoting insulin secretion, reducing glucagon secretion, delaying gastric emptying and inhibiting gastrointestinal motility.11 12 Tirzepatide is a novel glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP)/GLP-1 dual receptor agonist, and extensive clinical studies have demonstrated that tirzepatide achieves more pronounced weight loss effects compared with semaglutide across all patients and subgroups, with this effect maintained over a 12 month follow-up period.13 Tirzepatide is currently the most effective GLP-1RA for weight control.

Currently, the efficacy evaluation of GLP-1RAs in fertility-preserving treatment for obesity-related endometrial lesions is primarily based on in vitro studies and animal experiments,14 with clinical trials still in the patient recruitment phase of phase II. An animal study in obese mice with EC suggested that tirzepatide altered key gene pathways in tumour tissue, including those related to metabolism and inflammation, while inhibiting proliferation and promoting apoptosis.14

Objective

This study is designed to investigate the therapeutic potential of tirzepatide in fertility-preserving management of obesity-associated endometrial neoplasms. We will assess the synergistic antitumour efficacy, safety profile and impact on the molecular and tumour immune microenvironment of tirzepatide during treatment.

Methods and analysis

Study design

A single-centre, one-arm clinical trial will be conducted to evaluate the safety and efficacy of tirzepatide in fertility-preserving treatment for patients with endometrial neoplasms who are obese or overweight with weight-related comorbidities. The study also assesses the synergistic effects on tumour treatment. The study will enrol a total of 45 patients who are obese or overweight with at least one weight-related comorbidity, have a pathological diagnosis of EC or atypical endometrial hyperplasia and have fertility desires. The treatment regimen includes standard endometrial preservation therapy combined with tirzepatide. Endometrial assessments will be conducted regularly within 1 year following clinical treatment protocols, observing changes in weight, endometrial reversal rates, body morphology, glucose and lipid metabolism, and other relevant indicators. Endometrial sampling will be performed concurrently during assessments to conduct molecular biological testing, including spatial transcriptomics and methylation analysis. Intervention

Standard treatment for EC and atypical endometrial hyperplasia

Patients will receive standard treatment according to current international guidelines. The treatment regimen includes oral high-dose progestins, such as medroxyprogesterone acetate/megestrol, or a gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonist combined with letrozole or the levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine device. The specific treatment plan will be selected by the physician and the patient in consultation based on shared decision-making and individual circumstances.

Tirzepatide dosing regimen

According to the drug label, the initial dose is 2.5 mg administered subcutaneously once weekly. After 4 weeks, the dose is increased to 5 mg subcutaneously once weekly. Based on patient tolerance and weight management outcomes, the dose may be increased in increments of 2.5 mg after maintaining the current dose for at least 4 weeks. The maintenance dose can be 5 mg, 10 mg or 15 mg, administered subcutaneously once weekly. According to the label and previous clinical trials, the treatment duration should not exceed 52 weeks. If complete remission occurs within 52 weeks and the patient desires fertility, tirzepatide should be discontinued during assisted reproduction, pregnancy and lactation.

Evaluation

Baseline data, including patient age, height, initial weight, body composition and body morphology, as well as glucose and lipid metabolism levels, such as fasting blood glucose, glycated haemoglobin, total cholesterol, triglycerides, low-density lipoprotein (LDL), high-density lipoprotein (HDL), insulin resistance index; and inflammation levels, including C-reactive protein (CRP); pathological type, medical history and family history, will be collected in detail on patient enrolment. During the trial, patients will undergo monthly reassessments of weight, body composition, body morphology, glucose and lipid metabolism levels and inflammation levels. An endometrial sample will be collected for pathology evaluation and further molecular study every 3 months during hormonal treatment. After the treatment concludes, patients will be followed up every 3 months for up to 3 years, every 6 months between 3 and 5 years and annually thereafter. During follow-up, annual data on body composition analysis and peripheral blood samples will be collected for glucose and lipid metabolism-related tests. Additionally, fertility status and tumour recurrence will be monitored. Blood and endometrial samples collected during the trial will be stored in the sample bank of Peking Union Medical College Hospital.

Sample size calculation

This study is a single-arm Phase II clinical trial. Based on previous data, the complete response rate of fertility-sparing treatment for endometrial lesions in overweight and obese patients is approximately 60%.15 16 We have set the expected response rate at 0.80, the minimum acceptable response rate at 0.60, the significance level at 0.05 and the power at 90%. Using the single-stage A’Hern method and the clinfun package in R 4.4.0, the calculated sample size is 45.

Recruitment of patients

Relevant information about this study will be displayed in the outpatient and inpatient areas in the form of posters. All obese or overweight patients with endometrial lesions who are considering fertility-sparing treatment will be evaluated for eligibility to participate in the study. Members of the research team will inquire about the patient’s willingness. Once the informed consent is obtained, basic information will be collected.

Data management and statistical analysis

An electronic case report form will be used to collect data. All investigators must be authorised to access the data and are required to maintain the data’s confidentiality. A comprehensive data management plan, including eCRF, is implemented to ensure data security and storage. This plan includes conducting regular quality checks and validation procedures, as well as implementing strict protocols for data security and storage. The datasets used and/or analysed can be obtained from the principal investigator on reasonable request. Upon completion of data collection, the data will first be organised and cleaned to ensure completeness and accuracy. According to the indications from the FDA of China for the drug tirzepatide, this study includes populations who are obese and overweight with at least one weight-related comorbidity. After medication, obese individuals may experience more pronounced improvement in metabolic and inflammatory profiles due to a greater reduction in the adipocyte percentage, potentially leading to a more potent drug effect. In contrast, individuals with related comorbidities may exhibit attenuated responses due to factors such as insulin resistance, liver and kidney dysfunction and possible dose limitations. Therefore, in the early stage of the trial, we will enrol these two groups and also analyse them separately. Independent two-sample t-tests will be conducted to compare glucose and lipid metabolism levels, CRP levels, specific drug dosages and endometrial pathology types, examining whether there are differences between the two groups. In the mid-trial phase, we will also assess whether there are differences in endometrial reversal time and reversal rates between the two groups. This will guide the recruitment of subsequent trial participants and the statistical analysis of the results. If necessary, subgroup analyses relevant to the study will be conducted. Statistical analysis will be conducted using software such as SPSS (V29.0) and R (V4.4.0). Descriptive statistics, including mean, SD and median, will be calculated for continuous variables, while frequencies and percentages will be calculated for categorical variables. Categorical variables will be analysed using X2 tests, while continuous variables will be assessed using t-tests or the Mann-Whitney U test, depending on their distribution characteristics. Mixed models will be employed to manage complex data, using maximum likelihood or Bayesian approaches to estimate model parameters while accounting for missing data. Statistical significance will be determined by a two-sided p value of <0.05 and a 95% CI, with effect sizes reported to assess the practical significance of the results.

Harms

Previous studies have shown that the side effects of tirzepatide are usually mild and tolerable. The most common adverse reactions are primarily concentrated in the gastrointestinal system, including nausea, diarrhoea, decreased appetite, vomiting, constipation, indigestion, abdominal pain, belching, bloating and gastro-oesophageal reflux. Most of these reactions are mild to moderate and typically diminish gradually with prolonged treatment. In terms of the cardiovascular system, an increase in the heart rate may occur. Severe adverse reactions include hypersensitivity reactions, acute kidney injury, severe gastrointestinal diseases, acute gallbladder diseases, pancreatitis, thyroid C-cell tumours and hypoglycaemia.

Additionally, for fertility-sparing treatment of endometrial lesions, if the treatment is ineffective and the lesions persist or progress, hysterectomy may still be required or new tumours may develop in other organs, potentially threatening the patient’s life. During treatment, repeated evaluations of the endometrium may lead to complications such as endometrial thinning, damage, intrauterine adhesions and failure to achieve successful fertility.

During the study, adverse effects in patients will be closely monitored, recorded and reported as required. Symptomatic treatment will be provided based on clinical practice.

Discussion

This single-arm, phase II clinical trial assesses the efficacy of the novel weight-controlling medication tirzepatide in the management of obese women with uterine endometrial neoplastic disease who wish to preserve their uterus. The study will evaluate both the antitumour effects and weight loss benefits of tirzepatide.

It is currently known that the occurrence and progression of EC are closely related to obesity. It is also negatively associated with the success rate of fertility-preserving management in EC.17 A study showed that obese patients who lost less than 10% of their body weight had poorer response and pregnancy outcomes, as well as a higher rate of recurrence, compared with those who successfully lost weight.18 Several potential mechanisms underpin this relationship. First, the increased visceral fat in obese individuals exerts both local and systemic effects, promoting abnormal endometrial proliferation and tumourigenesis.19 20 Additionally, elevated levels and activity of aromatase in obese patients make them more susceptible to oestrogen-induced endometrial proliferation.21 Furthermore, obesity contributes to imbalanced body metabolism and chronic inflammatory states through the secretion of adipokines.22 This state of inflammation is associated with upregulated cellular stress, which in turn enhances genetic instability and DNA damage.

Current weight loss treatments commonly used in the management of endometrial lesions include nutritional counselling and pharmacotherapy.10 However, as the standard treatment for fertility preservation in endometrial lesions, oral progestins may lead to weight gain due to factors such as increased appetite and water-sodium retention. This, in turn, further limits the effectiveness of nutritional counselling alone. Since the FDA approved related medications for weight loss treatment, an increasing number of patients are receiving pharmacotherapy. Tirzepatide, as the first GLP-1/GIP dual receptor agonist, demonstrated greater benefits in blood glucose control and weight reduction compared with GLP-1 single receptor agonists like semaglutide and dulaglutide, induced by the combined activation of both receptors.23,26 The latest results from the SURMOUNT-5 trial, published in May 2025, indicate that in obese individuals without diabetes, tirzepatide led to greater reductions in both weight and waist circumference compared with semaglutide. In the tirzepatide group, the average percentage of weight loss over 72 weeks reached 20.2%, compared with 13.7% with semaglutide. The average weight reduction with tirzepatide was 22.8 kg, whereas semaglutide resulted in a 15.0 kg reduction. Additionally, tirzepatide reduced waist circumference by an average of 18.4 cm, compared with 13 cm with semaglutide.26

The pre-clinical model provides the currently known data evaluating tirzepatide for the treatment of endometrial lesions.14 The study introduced an obesity-driven EC mouse model, and after treatment with tirzepatide, significant differences in both body weight and tumour mass were observed. Further analysis revealed that tirzepatide effectively alters the host and tumour environment, reducing body weight by modulating glycolysis, fatty acid metabolism and cytokine and chemokine production, ultimately inhibiting the growth of EC tumours.

However, currently, there are limited clinical data on the application value of GLP-1RA in the conservative treatment of endometrial lesions. Our study aims to explore the effects and safety of tirzepatide in weight management and its synergistic effects in fertility-sparing management for obese or overweight patients with EC/EIN. The innovation of the trial lies in the combination of fertility-preserving treatment with pharmacological weight loss therapy, as well as the selection of a novel GLP-1/GIP dual receptor agonist. This approach seeks to further enhance the success rate of fertility-preserving treatments in eligible patients with obesity or overweight EC/EIN. Nevertheless, the limitation of this study is that it is a single-arm study, and large-scale randomised controlled trials are needed in the future to further validate the findings.

Opposite regulation of immune genes in blood and skin highlights tissue-specific dynamics of mpox virus
Authors: Mostafa Rezapour, Sean V. Murphy, David A. Ornelles, Patrick M. McNutt, Anthony Atala
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Mpox re-emerged globally in 2022 with atypical clinical features and efficient human-to-human transmission, which underscores the need to better understand host immune responses. We conducted an integrative transcriptomic analysis of mpox virus (MPXV) infection in nonhuman primates by leveraging RNA-Seq datasets from whole-blood and skin. We applied the Generalized Linear Model with Quasi-Likelihood F-test and Magnitude-Altitude Score (GLMQL-MAS), a method that combines rigorous statistical testing with a ranking metric to identify and prioritize differentially expressed genes (DEGs), and the Cross-Magnitude-Altitude Score (Cross-MAS) gene selection strategy, which integrates results across multiple time points to identify reproducible signatures, to define compact and robust markers of infection. Longitudinal analysis of whole-blood revealed a staged immune trajectory: early induction of interferon-stimulated genes (IFI27, ISG15) was followed by proliferative and hematopoietic programs and later enrichment of adaptive immune signatures, including B cell activation and TNF signaling. A minimal six-gene panel (IFI27, ISG15, MYO7B, HEY1, VASH1, CNTD2) distinguished infected from control animals with 100% accuracy in both training and independent held-out test sets. In contrast, skin transcriptomes showed upregulation of keratinocyte-related genes (KRT10, KRT1, SOSTDC1) and downregulation of immune mediators (IL1B, CXCL8, S100A8, ISG15), which suggests epithelial remodeling with limited local immune activation. Cross-tissue comparisons revealed that under stringent criteria (BH-adjusted p-value < 0.05 and |log₂FC|> 1), only 10 genes were commonly significant between whole-blood and skin, and all (100%) exhibited opposite regulation. When thresholds were relaxed for exploratory purposes (unadjusted p-value < 0.05 and |log₂FC|> 1), 58 common significant immune-related genes were identified from the curated nCounter® Host Response Panel, of which 74.1% were oppositely regulated. Shared immune genes such as ISG15, MX1, IFIT2, and OAS2 were upregulated in blood but suppressed in skin, and Reactome enrichment of discordant genes highlighted interferon and cytokine signaling pathways. These contrasts suggest that MPXV may trigger systemic interferon activation while suppressing local antiviral responses in lesions; however, because they are derived from independent secondary datasets in different species, they should be regarded as hypothesis-generating signals that require confirmation in matched, longitudinal studies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-23342-y.

Introduction

Mpox, caused by the mpox virus (MPXV), is a zoonotic orthopoxvirus that re-emerged with unprecedented global impact in 2022–2023, resulting in over 80,000 confirmed cases across more than 110 countries1–4. Unlike earlier outbreaks restricted to endemic regions in Central and West Africa, the recent Clade IIb outbreak was marked by atypical clinical presentations and efficient human-to-human transmission5. These features have renewed urgency in characterizing host–pathogen interactions, particularly through molecular profiling strategies that can uncover dynamic immune responses and guide the development of diagnostic and therapeutic interventions.

Nonhuman primate (NHP) models have been recently used to characterize MPXV pathogenesis and evaluate vaccine responses. Aid et al.1 profiled whole-blood transcriptomes of rhesus macaques during acute infection and described the induction of innate and adaptive immune programs, including inflammatory and extracellular matrix remodeling pathways. Ye et al.2, in a separate study, analyzed skin biopsies from cynomolgus macaques challenged with MPXV following immunization with a multivalent mRNA vaccine, and revealed protective effects against clinical disease, viremia, and histopathological damage. While both studies provided important insights, neither performed an integrative time-resolved or cross-compartmental transcriptomic analysis, nor did they fully exploit the potential of their respective datasets for biomarker discovery.

To address these gaps, in the current study, we performed a multi-step integrative analysis leveraging publicly available datasets from Aid et al.1 and Ye et al.2. Our study had three major goals. First, we performed a comprehensive analysis of whole-blood transcriptomes using the Generalized Linear Model with Quasi-Likelihood F-test and Magnitude-Altitude Score (GLMQL-MAS)6–9, a framework that combines statistical testing with a ranking metric to identify and prioritize differentially expressed genes (DEGs) at each time point. We then applied Cross-Magnitude-Altitude Score (Cross-MAS)10, an extension of this framework that integrates results across multiple contrasts to identify genes that are either common or unique across different time points and rank them as reproducible transcriptional signatures. Using this approach, we defined a minimal set of genes consistently regulated during the peak of MPXV replication and immune response. The discriminative power of these genes, which were identified from a training set and validated on a biologically held-out test dataset, was evaluated using logistic regression11,12. Second, we analyzed the skin dataset to derive an independent set of cutaneous gene signatures at day post-infection (DPI) 10, which was the only available time point in the skin dataset. This analysis revealed transcriptional programs associated with MPXV-induced skin pathology. Finally, we integrated the whole-blood and skin transcriptomes at DPI 10 to perform, to the best of our knowledge, the first cross-tissue transcriptomic comparison for MPXV infection. This comparison enabled us to identify concordant and discordant immune responses between circulating whole-blood and peripheral skin compartments.

Building on these foundational studies by Aid et al.1 and Ye et al.2, our study was designed to extend them in scope and resolution. In relation to Aid et al.1, who mapped the progression from innate activation to adaptive immunity in whole-blood, we refine these broad pathway trends into a compact and reproducible gene set with potential diagnostic value. In relation to Ye et al.2, who demonstrated cutaneous protection conferred by vaccination and characterized local skin responses, we apply GLMQL-MAS to highlight transcriptional programs associated with lesion pathology. Moreover, by examining whole-blood and skin side by side at a shared disease stage, we identify candidate cross-compartment contrasts, such as differential regulation of interferon-stimulated genes, that were not apparent in the individual studies.

However, since our analysis relied on secondary datasets generated in different nonhuman primate species and sequencing platforms, the cross-compartment contrasts reported here should be considered hypothesis-generating rather than definitive. Our goal is to provide a framework that identifies robust transcriptional signatures and generates testable predictions about systemic and local immune responses to MPXV infection, which future experimental validation will be required to confirm.

Materials

Whole-blood RNA-Seq data from MPXV-infected Rhesus Macaques (GSE234118)

We used publicly available transcriptomic data from Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) accession GSE234118, which profiles whole-blood RNA-Seq from Macaca mulatta (rhesus macaques) infected with mpox virus (MPXV, hMPXV/USA/MA001/2022; Lineage B.1, Clade 2b)1. Eighteen adult macaques were challenged via intravenous, intradermal, or intrarectal routes with varying doses of MPXV. In the current study, we focused exclusively on whole-blood samples collected during the first 21 days post-infection, corresponding to the acute phase of mpox infection. According to the original study by Aid et al.1, viral loads and skin lesions peaked between days 7–10. By restricting our analysis to this window, we aimed to capture the primary pathogenesis and early immune response.

Total RNA was extracted from whole-blood collected in PAXgene RNA tubes using the MagMAX RNA Isolation Kit. Following globin transcript depletion, cDNA synthesis was performed using the Clontech SMART-Seq v4 Ultra Low Input RNA Kit1. Libraries were prepared using the Nextera XT DNA Library Kit and sequenced with 100 bp paired-end reads on an Illumina NovaSeq 60001. Reads were aligned to a composite reference genome (Mmul10 and MPXV) using STAR (v2.7.9a), transcript quantification was performed with htseq-count1.

These sample IDs are provided in the GEO metadata and correspond to individual animals from which whole-blood RNA was collected. These samples were used for exploratory analysis, feature selection, and model development.Test dataset: This group included samples with the IDs T461M, T462M, T448F, T441F, T409F, T445F, T437F, T443F, and T465M, as listed in the GEO metadata. These samples were held out for independent validation of all analytical results.

Skin RNA-Seq data from MPXV-infected Cynomolgus Macaques (GSE281290)

In addition to the whole-blood dataset, we used another publicly available transcriptomic data from GEO accession GSE281290, which profiles RNA-Seq from skin tissues of Macaca fascicularis (cynomolgus macaques) following infection with mpox virus (MPXV-B.1-China-C-Tan-CQ01; Clade 2b)2. In the original study by Ye et al.2, MPXV-seronegative cynomolgus macaques were challenged intravenously with MPXV, and skin biopsies were collected 10 days post-infection (DPI 10), the time point at which skin lesion formation was most pronounced in animals2. Total RNA was extracted from skin tissue using TRIzol reagent. RNA-Seq libraries were prepared and sequenced using the Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform. Transcript abundance was quantified was performed using DESeq2 (v1.6.3)2.

Note that the skin dataset included a total of four MPXV-infected animals and three control animals. Due to the limited sample size, we did not divide this dataset into separate training and test sets. Instead, all samples were used collectively for differential expression and downstream analysis.

Methodology

Transcriptomic profiling of whole-blood samples

To capture the temporal dynamics of host response during acute MPXV infection, we analyzed whole-blood RNA-Seq data from rhesus macaques collected at DPIs 1, 3, 7, 10, 14, and 21. Pre-infection/control samples were used as the baseline group. Each DPI group was contrasted against the control samples to reveal time-specific transcriptomic alterations linked to MPXV pathogenesis. We applied the GLMQL-MAS method6–9, which is particularly suited for RNA-Seq count data due to its ability to account for overdispersion13. Count normalization was performed using the Trimmed Mean of M-values (TMM)14, and a design matrix was constructed to model temporal variables. Gene-level dispersion was estimated to capture inter-sample variability.

Generalized Linear Models (GLMs)15 were fitted for each gene, and quasi-likelihood F-tests16 were used to evaluate model fit between full and reduced models. This approach allows for rigorous detection of transcriptional shifts without relying on strict distributional assumptions. Following MAS-based ranking, we performed functional enrichment analysis to contextualize the differentially expressed genes. Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment21 was carried out using the clusterProfiler R package (v4.14.6). Gene symbols were mapped to Entrez IDs using the bitr() function, and enrichment was conducted on Biological Process (BP) terms using enrichGO(). Results were filtered using BH correction, with BH adjusted p-values < 0.05 considered significant. Pathway enrichment analysis22 was conducted using the GProfiler Python API (gprofiler-official, v1.0.0), specifically querying Reactome (REAC) pathways.

Given the time-resolved nature of MPXV infection, we sought to identify genes that were most consistently and prominently altered across critical infection stages. For this, we applied the Cross-MAS ranking method (Algorithm 2 in10), focusing on samples from DPIs 3, 7, and 10, which correspond to the period of peak viral replication and immune response. The Cross-MAS method identifies genes based on the lowest maximum MAS rank observed across these DPI comparisons. This min–max optimization highlights genes that are repeatedly among the top-ranking across multiple time points, which suggests consistent and strong transcriptional regulation. Genes with top Cross-MAS ranks reflect those with both high absolute fold change (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$|{\text{log}}_{2}\text{FC}|$$\end{document}) and strong statistical support (low BH-adjusted p-values), marking them as robust indicators of MPXV-driven immune dynamics in whole-blood.

Transcriptomic profiling of skin tissue samples

To complement our analysis of systemic host responses, we investigated the transcriptomic landscape of skin during acute MPXV infection using the RNA-Seq dataset from cynomolgus macaques (GSE281290). Unlike the whole-blood dataset, skin tissue samples were collected only at a single post-infection time point, DPI 10, which corresponds to the peak of skin lesion formation and tissue-level pathology2. To identify transcriptional changes associated with MPXV infection in the skin, we contrasted sham-treated (control) animals with infected animals at DPI 10. The GLMQL-MAS framework, as described in section “Transcriptomic profiling of whole-blood samples”, was used to perform differential expression analysis.

Count data were normalized using TMM, and differential expression testing was carried out using GLMs with quasi-likelihood F-tests, followed by BH correction. We applied the same |\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\text{log}}_{2}$$\end{document} FC|> 1 and BH adjusted p-value < 0.05 thresholds to identify significantly regulated genes. Subsequently, we used clusterProfiler for GO enrichment and GProfiler for Reactome pathway analysis, following the same enrichment procedures used for whole-blood samples.

Cross-tissue comparison of transcriptional responses at DPI 10

To examine the concordance and divergence of MPXV-induced responses across distinct biological compartments, we compared transcriptional profiles between whole-blood and skin tissue at DPI 10. As GLMQL-MAS was previously applied independently to each dataset to identify DEGs between samples from infected and control animals, we only quantified the extent of transcriptional agreement between the two tissues by categorizing the shared DEGs based on their direction of regulation. Each commonly significant gene was classified as (i) significantly upregulated in both tissues, (ii) significantly downregulated in both tissues, or (iii) discordantly regulated (upregulated in one tissue and downregulated in the other).

This comparative analysis was intended to evaluate whether MPXV infection drives similar or distinct transcriptional programs in circulating blood versus skin tissue. Understanding these cross-tissue transcriptional dynamics may offer insight into compartment-specific immune activation, potential biomarkers detectable in whole-blood that reflect cutaneous pathology, or localized versus systemic effects of MPXV.

To ensure robust identification of discordantly regulated genes, we first applied stringent thresholds of |\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\text{log}}_{2}$$\end{document} FC|> 1 and BH-adjusted p-value < 0.05 within each dataset. To complement this stringent analysis, we also performed an exploratory comparison using a relaxed threshold of unadjusted p-value < 0.05, restricting our analysis to the curated nCounter® Host Response Panel (Cat# XT-HHR-12), which includes 773 immune response genes. The goal of this secondary step was not to redefine statistical significance but to assess whether discordant regulation persisted within a focused predefined immune gene set.

Although the six-gene panel was derived from the training dataset and validated on a held-out test set, and the focus on the minimal number of consistently dysregulated genes already reduced the risk of overfitting, we performed permutation testing to further assess the robustness of its classification performance.

In the permutation analysis, sample labels were randomly shuffled 1000 times while maintaining the original gene expression profiles. At each iteration, a logistic regression classifier was retrained on the permuted data, and classification accuracy was recalculated, which yields an empirical null distribution of accuracies expected under random label assignments. The performance of the six-gene panel on the true, unshuffled labels was then compared to this null distribution.

In the test dataset, the observed classification accuracy was 100%. By contrast, the permutation-derived null distribution had a mean accuracy of 75.59% with a standard deviation of 2.29. The empirical p-value, defined as the proportion of permutations achieving accuracy equal to or greater than the observed, was < 0.001. These results suggest that the separation achieved by the six-gene panel lies well outside the range of accuracies expected under random label assignment, which indicates that the finding is unlikely to be explained by overfitting. Discussion

This study presents a comprehensive and integrative transcriptomic analysis of MPXV infection in nonhuman primates, which leveraged two publicly available RNA-Seq datasets from whole-blood (Aid et al.1) and skin (Ye et al.2). By applying GLMQL-MAS and Cross-MAS across early post-infection time points, we identified robust and reproducible gene expression signatures that distinguish infected from control animals. Notably, we demonstrated that a compact set of genes selected from peak infection stages could achieve 100% classification accuracy across independent cohorts.

Furthermore, through cross-tissue comparisons at DPI 10, we observed discordant regulation of key immune genes between circulating whole-blood and peripheral skin. Because this observation was derived from computational integration of secondary datasets generated in different nonhuman primate species and using different sequencing platforms, it should be regarded as a hypothesis rather than a confirmed biological phenomenon. Our analysis provides a framework for generating testable predictions about compartment-specific immune regulation, which future validation will be required to confirm.

The temporal whole-blood transcriptomic analysis uncovered a highly dynamic and coordinated immune response to MPXV infection that evolved distinctly over the course of the acute phase. Early infection stages were characterized by rapid induction of antiviral and interferon-stimulated genes, which is consistent with innate immune priming in response to viral entry. This antiviral axis, marked by genes such as IFI27 and ISG15, emerged before the peak in the number of differentially expressed genes. The transient and robust upregulation of MYO7B, a gene not classically linked to immune function, but strongly induced during early infection, points to the possibility of infection-induced cytoskeletal remodeling38.

As infection progressed, the host response transitioned from antiviral activation toward cell cycle and proliferative pathways, suggesting a potential hematopoietic compensatory mechanism or leukocyte expansion39–41. This transcriptional shift was not only temporally aligned with peak viral burden and clinical severity but also functionally captured in enrichment patterns associated with mitosis and chromosomal segregation. The induction of such programs may represent emergency hematopoiesis or the expansion of specific immune cell subsets in response to systemic inflammation42. Notably, this shift was not sustained indefinitely by DPI 21, signatures of adaptive immune activation became more prominent, including processes related to B cell proliferation and TNF signaling. This progression reflects a staged immune defense trajectory, transitioning from innate sensing to proliferative mobilization and ultimately adaptive engagement.

The application of GLMQL-MAS and Cross-MAS enabled the distillation of this complex temporal landscape into a compact set of reproducible gene signatures with exceptional classification performance. The ability of a six-gene panel, including IFI27, ISG15, MYO7B and HEY1, VASH1, CNTD2 to accurately distinguish infected from control animals across independent cohorts highlights its diagnostic potential. The cutaneous transcriptomic landscape at DPI 10 revealed a markedly different immune architecture from that observed in circulation. Although numerous genes were significantly upregulated in skin, the dominant biological signals were not immunological but structural, with enrichment for pathways involved in epidermal development and keratinocyte differentiation. Genes such as KRT10, KRT1, and SOSTDC1, which are central to epidermal integrity and cornification, were among the most upregulated, which suggests that MPXV infection elicits a strong epithelial remodeling program. This regenerative or hyperproliferative response may represent a compensatory mechanism to counteract virus-induced tissue damage or barrier disruption. Concurrent enrichment of pathways such as “Formation of the cornified envelope” and “Regulation of TLR by endogenous ligand” suggests that barrier remodeling and shifts in immune sensing mechanisms may be central features of the cutaneous response43.

Immune-related transcriptional programs in the skin were largely suppressed. Antiviral and inflammatory genes, including IL1B, CXCL8, ISG15, S100A8, and S100A9, were significantly downregulated, despite being strongly upregulated in whole-blood. GO enrichment of downregulated genes underscored this trend and revealed significant suppression of pathways related to viral defense, cytokine signaling, and inflammatory regulation. Given the small sample size and single time point, these patterns should be interpreted cautiously as hypothesis-generating signals rather than definitive evidence of immune suppression.

The direct comparison of transcriptional responses between whole-blood and skin at DPI 10 revealed clear opposing regulation of immune-related genes. Under identical statistical thresholds, only ten genes were commonly and significantly differentially expressed in both tissues, and all exhibited discordant regulation. Notably, ISG15, IFIT2, IFIT1, MX1, OAS2, GZMA, IFI6, APOBEC3A, and S100A8 were all strongly upregulated in whole-blood while being concurrently downregulated in skin. This pattern highlights a robust divergence in immune signaling across compartments. The systemic upregulation of ISGs such as ISG15, MX1, IFITs, and OAS2 in whole-blood likely reflects heightened circulating antiviral defenses, and the repression of these same genes in skin suggests either local immune evasion by the virus or tissue-intrinsic regulatory constraints. Moreover, when used as features for PCA and logistic regression, this discordant gene set effectively separated infected from control animals in both whole-blood and skin datasets. However, without matched experimental validation, these contrasts should be regarded as a computational hypothesis.

By relaxing the statistical threshold to include genes with an unadjusted p-value < 0.05, 52.1% of the genes that were significant in both whole-blood and skin remained oppositely regulated. This consistency lends statistical support to the presence of compartment-specific antagonism during MPXV infection, but the biological interpretation remains provisional until confirmed with orthogonal assays. The top enriched pathways of the genes, i.e., “Interferon alpha/beta signaling,” “Interferon Signaling,” “Cytokine Signaling in Immune system,” and “Antiviral mechanism by IFN-stimulated genes,” demonstrated a highly coherent immune signature. These pathways encompassed ISGs such as ISG15, MX1, IFI6, and IFIT2, which reinforces the hypothesis that systemic interferon-driven immune activation occurs in tandem with localized immunomodulation or suppression in the skin.

To further test whether this discordant regulation was representative of broader immune trends, we examined expression patterns from the curated nCounter® Host Response Panel, which targets 773 immune-related genes. Importantly, 74.1% of the significant immune genes showed opposing regulation between whole-blood and skin. This pattern was not limited to ISGs but extended to inflammatory and immunomodulatory mediators such as IFIT3, OAS2, IL1RN, and S100A8. While these results highlight consistent compartment-specific patterns in the datasets analyzed, they remain provisional and require independent experimental validation.

Several nonexclusive mechanisms may explain why interferon stimulated genes are upregulated in whole-blood but downregulated in skin. Orthopoxviruses encode immune modulators that antagonize interferon pathways at sites of replication44. For example, in vaccinia virus, the soluble interferon binding protein B18R functions as a decoy receptor that neutralizes type I interferons and prevents signaling45,46. Chemokine binding proteins sequester chemokines and alter inflammatory recruitment47, and interleukin 18 binding proteins reduce IL 18 mediated responses48. Mpox virus retains orthologs of these genes, including the B18 family interferon binding protein49, which may suggest conservation of these strategies. Poxviruses also interfere with intracellular signaling. The JAK-STAT cascade mediates interferon induced transcription of antiviral genes50. Vaccinia virus VH1 phosphatase dephosphorylates STAT1 and blocks its nuclear accumulation51, while proteins such as A46R inhibit adaptor dependent innate pathways that intersect with interferon responses52. Although these mechanisms have not yet been confirmed in mpox virus, the presence of orthologous genes indicates that similar strategies may contribute to suppression of interferon stimulated genes in skin despite systemic induction in whole-blood.

Keratinocyte intrinsic features and tissue specific context provide additional explanations. Interferon kappa is constitutively expressed in keratinocytes and influences local antiviral and barrier associated responses53. The interferon lambda receptor is enriched on epithelial cells and produces a restricted antiviral state that is generally less inflammatory than type I interferon signaling54. These epithelial biases may favor barrier integrity over widespread immune activation and help explain reduced interferon signatures in skin. Viral tropism further strengthens this effect. Mpox virus replicates efficiently in keratinocytes and causes cytopathic changes that concentrate viral burden and antagonists within the epidermis55. Histopathologic analyses of mpox lesions show epidermal necrosis, keratinocytic ballooning, and detection of viral particles by electron microscopy, consistent with direct epithelial infection56. Taken together, these viral and host factors offer a plausible mechanistic basis for the observed downregulation of interferon stimulated genes in skin while they remain strongly upregulated in whole-blood, although this interpretation remains a hypothesis until validated in matched experimental systems.

When we compared our results with the original studies, Aid et al.1 and Ye et al.2, several points of agreement and extension became apparent. Our whole-blood results broadly corroborate the temporal arc reported by Aid et al.1, with early induction of interferon and innate immune programs followed by rising lymphocyte and plasma-cell signatures, and the strongest transcriptome perturbation centered around the first seven days after challenge. We extend those observations in three ways. First, by applying GLMQL-MAS at each DPI and then Cross-MAS across DPIs 3, 7, and 10, we translated pathway-level trends into compact, gene-level features with reproducible out-of-sample performance, yielding a six-gene panel (IFI27, ISG15, MYO7B, HEY1, VASH1, CNTD2) that perfectly separated infected from controls across independent cohorts. Second, we resolved an explicit temporal switch from antiviral ISGs such as IFI27 and ISG15 at DPIs 1–3, to cell-cycle and mitotic programs at DPIs 7–14, and finally to adaptive immune signatures by DPI 21, aligning with but making more granular the trajectory described by Aid et al.1. Third, we highlighted MYO7B as a transient and reproducible early marker that peaks at DPIs 3–7 before declining, which points to a cytoskeletal remodeling axis not emphasized in the original study. Taken together, our analysis confirms the overall kinetics described by Aid et al.1 while adding reproducible minimal markers and a quantifiable timeline that may facilitate diagnostic or mechanistic follow-up.

Our skin findings at DPI 10 extend the observations of Ye et al.2 in several important ways. By applying GLMQL-MAS, we identified compact gene-level signatures capable of cleanly separating infected from control samples, which provides minimal discriminatory features that were not defined in the original study. In addition, we resolved a two-track cutaneous response at DPI 10, characterized by strong epithelial remodeling, marked by upregulation of genes such as KRT10, KRT1, and SOSTDC1, alongside selective reduction of antiviral and inflammatory mediators including ISG15, IFIT2, IL1B, and S100A8/A9.

Nevertheless, the integration of whole-blood and skin transcriptomes at a shared disease stage revealed insights not apparent in either dataset alone. By aligning systemic and local responses, we identified a coordinated but decoupled architecture: robust systemic interferon activation in whole-blood occurring in parallel with epithelial remodeling and selective immune suppression in skin. Neither Aid et al.1 and Ye et al.2 directly tested this cross-tissue antagonism, yet this integrative view suggests that systemic antiviral activation can coexist with localized immunomodulation in lesions. At the same time, important design differences between the underlying datasets must be acknowledged, including species (rhesus versus cynomolgus), vaccination status, challenge dose and route, and sequencing platform. These factors could influence baseline immune tone, the amplitude and timing of interferon responses, and epithelial remodeling trajectories. Our observation that ISGs such as ISG15, MX1, IFIT2, and OAS2 were upregulated in whole-blood but reduced in skin should therefore be regarded as a hypothesis-generating pattern rather than a definitive biological antagonism.

This raises testable predictions that circulating markers such as IFI27 and ISG15 may serve as early systemic indicators of infection, while keratinocyte associated programs reflect lesion pathology and repair. The juxtaposition of these compartment specific signatures underscores the importance of viewing mpox pathogenesis across tissues and motivates future matched, longitudinal studies to clarify whether the apparent divergence reflects viral evasion, tissue intrinsic regulation, or both. Although our findings derive from nonhuman primate models, which remain the most relevant system for recapitulating mpox disease dynamics, the absence of publicly available human transcriptomic datasets prevents direct validation of these signatures in patient samples. The translatability of these signatures should therefore be considered preliminary, but they provide a rational framework for identifying systemic and cutaneous biomarkers in future human studies. The same classes of interferon stimulated genes and keratinocyte associated programs observed here have been implicated in human orthopoxvirus biology, which increases confidence that these pathways are likely to be conserved. While our conclusions are based on nonhuman primate models, they generate hypotheses testable in clinical cohorts or organ tissue equivalents (OTEs)57, and we highlight the need for such efforts to establish human specific diagnostic and therapeutic implications.

While our analysis identifies gene signatures that could have implications for vaccine development, biomarker discovery, and clinical diagnosis, we stress that these possibilities are preliminary and require careful validation in larger, matched, and ideally human/OTE datasets. Our findings also raise the possibility that compartment-specific biomarkers could aid in prognosis and monitoring of mpox virus infection. For example, interferon-stimulated genes detected in blood may serve as indicators of systemic immune activation, while keratinocyte-associated transcriptional programs in skin could reflect local lesion pathology and repair. These compartmentalized signals may ultimately help refine disease monitoring strategies; however, such applications remain provisional until validated with additional experimental and clinical data.

Limitations of the study

This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings. First, all analyses were conducted using nonhuman primate models. While these provide valuable insights into MPXV pathogenesis, they may not fully capture the complexity of human immune responses. Differences in host genetics, immune regulation, and disease progression could influence the translatability of the identified gene signatures. Second, the skin dataset was relatively small (n = 4 infected, n = 3 control), which limits statistical power and increases the potential influence of inter-individual variation. Third, the whole-blood and skin datasets were generated in different macaque species (rhesus vs. cynomolgus) and under distinct experimental protocols, challenge conditions, sequencing platforms, and normalization workflows. Although we applied harmonized criteria within each dataset, these technical and biological differences could contribute to the observed cross-tissue contrasts. Fourth, the absence of matched longitudinal sampling across whole-blood and skin prevented a true time-resolved cross-compartment analysis, restricting our ability to assess how transcriptional divergences evolve during infection. Finally, we did not perform wet-lab validation (e.g., qRT-PCR, RNAscope, or NanoString) of the discordant immune genes. The reported whole-blood-skin antagonism should therefore be regarded as a hypothesis generated from computational integration rather than a confirmed biological phenomenon.

Conclusions

This study provides a comprehensive, time-resolved, and compartment-specific transcriptomic dissection of MPXV infection in nonhuman primates. By integrating whole-blood and skin RNA-Seq datasets and applying a robust statistical and machine learning-guided framework, we identified reproducible gene signatures that capture the dynamic immune response to MPXV and distinguish infected from control animals with perfect accuracy. Our longitudinal analysis of whole-blood revealed a temporally coordinated immune trajectory, from early interferon-mediated antiviral activity to proliferative hematopoietic expansion and eventual engagement of adaptive immunity. A compact six-gene whole-blood signature (IFI27, ISG15, MYO7B, HEY1, VASH1, CNTD2) emerged as a highly discriminatory and biologically meaningful marker of infection.

Transcriptomic profiling of MPXV-infected skin at the peak of lesion formation exposed a markedly different landscape. Upregulation of keratinocyte-associated genes and concurrent suppression of immune mediators pointed to a structural regeneration program accompanied by local immune suppression. The divergence in gene regulation between whole-blood and skin was highlighted by cross-compartment comparisons, where majority of commonly significant immune genes exhibited opposite regulatory trends. However, because these contrasts were derived from secondary datasets generated in different species and sequencing platforms, they should be regarded as hypothesis-generating rather than validated biological phenomena. Pathway enrichment analysis of discordantly regulated genes revealed systemic activation of interferon and cytokine signaling pathways, including ISG15, MX1, IFI6, and IFIT2, alongside their coordinated suppression in skin. This interpretation remains provisional and requires orthogonal validation in matched or analogous samples.

Together, these findings generate the hypothesis that MPXV elicits spatially distinct immune environments, with systemic antiviral defenses occurring in parallel with local modulation in infected tissue. This hypothesis underscores the limitations of relying solely on whole-blood -based biomarkers to infer tissue-level immune dynamics and emphasizes the need for future studies that incorporate spatially resolved and experimentally validated approaches.

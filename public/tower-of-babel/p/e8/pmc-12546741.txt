Machine learning driven aggregation aware bitmap MAC protocol for energy efficient data transmission in WSNs
Authors: Fuhid Alanazi, Mohammad N. Alanazi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
An Aggregation-Aware Energy-Efficient Bit-Mapping Medium Access Control Protocol (AABMP) is proposed for efficient data transmission in Wireless Sensor Networks and the Internet of Things. The main objective of the proposed method is to effectively manage the data transmission from sensor nodes to cluster heads in an energy-efficient manner. AABMP aggregates the data by estimating the mean value of a sliding window of the previous few samples and calculates the deviation of the current reading with respect to mean value. The deviation aware method is proposed for the transmission decision from sensor node to cluster head node. The deviation is predicted for real-time dataset using various machine learning methods. Based on performance evaluations, the most effective ML method is integrated with the bit-mapping MAC protocol in an energy-efficient manner. The aggregation-aware ML approach reduces the number of transmitted packets by efficiently identifying redundant data. The proposed approach is evaluated using the Intel LAB dataset (https://db.csail.mit.edu/labdata/labdata.html) of 55 real sensor nodes. The proposed method demonstrates practical applicability for energy-efficient data transmission in IoT-oriented WSN deployments. For the efficient analysis, the precise number of transmitted packet is estimated based on transmission probability. The algorithm is proposed for the estimation of transmission probability. For performance analysis, we have compared multiple ML methods to determine the most suitable one. This optimal method is then integrated with the bit-mapping-based energy-efficient piggybacking scheme. AABMP is also compared with existing MAC protocols. The results demonstrate its superiority in terms of energy savings across both worst-case and best-case scenarios.

Introduction

Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) and the Internet of Things (IoT) have achieved significant advancements in past few decades. WSN/IoT can effectively handle the existing challenges and it can be used for real-time data collection and intelligent decision-making in various domains such as healthcare, environmental monitoring, and industrial automation1. The development of smart sensors causes an exponential increase in the volume of data generated which requires efficient mechanisms for the data transmission, aggregation, and processing. In early years the WSNs relied on simple data forwarding techniques. However, recent approaches are more advanced due to integration of Machine Learning (ML) and effective Medium Access Control (MAC) protocols which is used to optimize the network performance and energy efficiency. In addition, the integration of energy-aware mechanisms has further enhanced the adaptability of WSNs in resource-constrained environments. It ensures the prolonged network lifetime and improved transmission efficiency. MAC protocols plays an important role for managing the energy consumption in WSN/IoT as well as minimizing redundant transmissions. The traditional contention-based MAC schemes often suffer from high energy overhead due to excessive retransmissions and idle listening, while time-scheduled approaches provide better control at the cost of increased latency. In previous research works, several aggregation-based and energy-efficient data transmission methods have been proposed to address these challenges by reducing redundant data at the source, thereby minimizing the unnecessary packet transmissions. In recent years also, the researchers have explored bit-mapping and piggybacking mechanisms to enhance the transmission efficiency. However, the existing solutions struggle to balance real-time data accuracy, energy efficiency in highly dynamic environments2. Therefore, energy efficiency and performance optimization is primary need for real time application in WSNs without compromising data accuracy. Sensor nodes deployed in large-scale applications often operate under severe energy constraints. The efficient data transmission strategies are crucial to increase the lifetime of overall network. The redundant data transmission significantly contributes to unnecessary energy consumption which leads to a depletion of the node batteries. In real-time application scenarios such as industrial automation and environmental monitoring, low-latency data aggregation while maintaining accuracy of the sensed environment is a key challenge. To address the above discussed issues, an intelligent aggregation-aware mechanism is proposed which dynamically adapts the varying network conditions and application-specific requirements. Related works

The rapid advancements in sensor networks, edge impulse based computing, high capacity of processor and controller, and in-memory computing have encouraged the researchers to develop some innovative solutions for various applications for healthcare, wireless communication, and artificial intelligence applications. In the literature study work, we have shortlisted relevant literature closely related to the domain of the proposed work. The recent studies have explored the role of wearable devices in healthcare monitoring. Giordano et al.1 proposed SepAl, a low-power wearable system integrating digital biomarkers and Tiny Machine Learning (TinyML) to detect sepsis onset. This work highlights the potential of edge computing in medical diagnostics. Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have been leveraged for resource management in dynamic wireless sensor networks. Giang et al.4 introduced HGNN, a hierarchical GNN architecture designed for joint resource management in wireless sensor networks, improving quality of service and power allocation. In-memory computing has gained attention for energy-efficient AI applications. Hérissé et al.5 proposed a time-domain mixed-signal matrix-vector multiplication technique for low-power edge-AI, improving efficiency in AI-driven applications. Similarly, Lin et al.6 developed an 11T1C computing-in-memory macro that optimizes bit-level sparsity with adaptive conversion time and computation voltage. The use of deep learning in sensor-based applications has also been explored. Yu et al.7 proposed RepMobile, a MobileNet-like network with structural reparameterization for human activity recognition using sensor data, optimizing accuracy and low-latency processing. Recent advancements in TinyML and edge-computing platforms have enabled real-time machine learning inference on low-power IoT devices. Tolani et al.8 demonstrated a practical implementation of adaptive traffic prediction and control using the Edge Impulse platform on Arduino Nano 33 BLE Sense. This reinforces the feasibility of deploying ML models directly on sensor hardware in dynamic environments.

Tang et al.9 addressed privacy concerns in federated learning by developing an optimized power control mechanism for privacy-preserving over-the-air federated edge learning, ensuring device sampling and differential privacy. For multimedia content dissemination in wireless sensor networks, Wang et al.10 designed an information-centric wireless sensor network (ICWSN) approach that enhances content caching and dissemination using learning automata.

Advancements in neuromorphic computing have also been explored. Liu et al.11 proposed a sparsity-aware in-memory neuromorphic computing unit that integrates hybrid spiking and artificial neural networks, improving energy efficiency and computational performance. In the field of computing-in-memory, Shah et al.12 presented an efficient 6TP SRAM-based CIM macro with column ADCs for binarized neural networks, enhancing energy-efficient AI inference. A binary label \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$y_i$$\end{document} is assigned:15\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} y_i = {\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} 1, & \text {if anomaly is detected} \\ 0, & \text {otherwise}. \end{array}\right. } \end{aligned}$$\end{document}

Random forest classification model

Given the feature vector \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textbf{X} = [T_i, H_i, L_i, V_i]$$\end{document}, we train a Random Forest classifier \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$f(\textbf{X})$$\end{document} such that:16\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \hat{y} = f(\textbf{X}), \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat{y}$$\end{document} is the predicted anomaly label.

The classifier is trained using a set of decision trees \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\{T_1, T_2,..., T_M\}$$\end{document} with M trees, where each tree outputs a prediction \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_j(\textbf{X})$$\end{document}. Transmission probability computation

For each sensor parameter X, the transmission probability \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$P_X$$\end{document} is calculated as:18\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} P_X = \frac{1}{N} \sum _{i=1}^{N} \mathbb {1}(\left| X_i - \mu _X \right| > \tau \cdot \sigma _X). \end{aligned}$$\end{document}

The average transmission probability across all sensors is given by:19\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} P_{avg} = \frac{1}{4} (P_T + P_H + P_L + P_V). \end{aligned}$$\end{document}

Model evaluation metrics

The classification accuracy A of the Random Forest model is computed as:20\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} A = \frac{1}{N} \sum _{i=1}^{N} \mathbb {1}(\hat{y}_i = y_i), \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where N is the total number of test samples.

The transmission probability for each sensor is shown in Fig. 9. The results presented in the figure shows the probability of sensor data transmission across different threshold values for temperature, humidity, light, and voltage. The probability values indicate how reliably each parameter is transmitted based on the set threshold levels. At lower threshold values (0.2 and 0.4), all parameters exhibit higher transmission probabilities, meaning the data transmission is more frequent and less restrictive. Notably, light and voltage maintain the highest transmission probabilities, followed by humidity and temperature. As the threshold increases to 0.6 and 0.8, the transmission probability declines, with light still maintaining relatively high transmission rates, whereas temperature and humidity experience significant drops.

Conclusion

The comparative analysis of the existing MAC protocol and proposed machine learning-based classification methods for sensor data processing shows important findings. The machine learning-based transmission probability based on aggregation for temperature, humidity, light, and voltage sensor data shows that Random Forest and XGBoost outperform SVM and Decision Tree in terms of classification accuracy. The confusion matrices indicate that Random Forest and XGBoost exhibit higher correct classification rates, particularly for medium and high sensor values, while SVM struggles with misclassifications. The MAE, RMSE, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R^2$$\end{document} comparisons further proves that the Random Forest and XGBoost provide the best balance between accuracy and efficiency. The energy consumption model is also developed for transmission probability. The performance evaluation of different existing TDMA, EATDMA, EBMA, and proposed AABMP energy consumption model is analyzed. The analysis result shows that the AABMP is the most energy-efficient protocol across different scenarios in terms of variation of event occurrence probability, number of nodes, number of rounds, packet size, and continuous monitoring nodes. The results confirm that TDMA exhibits the highest energy consumption, followed by EATDMA and EBMA, while AABMP consistently outperforms them in both best-case scenario (25% less energy consumption) and worst-case scenario (5% less energy consumption). The overall analysis proves that AABMP a superior choice for energy-efficient communication in sensor networks. Future work will focus on scaling the simulation to larger IoT networks; however, the current evaluation using 55 nodes demonstrates effectiveness in typical IoT-WSN scenarios.

The fatigue status feature of bicycle movement based on deep learning and signal processing technology
Authors: Yingchun He, Yih-haw Jan, Fan Yang, Yunru Ma, Xin-Yuan Chen, Chun Pei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCycling is a common and effective home-based rehabilitation exercise. Accurate and accessible assessment of the onset of fatigue is essential to achieving optimal exercise benefits and preventing overuse injuries. To obtain fatigue-related parameters in different age groups, we applied deep learning algorithms and signal processing technology to analyze cycling movement features for the people aged over 45. 20 healthy adults aged over 45 and 20 aged 18–30 were recruited. Participants were asked to ride a stationary exercise bike at their self-regulated pedaling speeds for 10 min and wear a COSMED K5 device to collect physiological signals. The Keypoint RCNN (KR) algorithm and three signal processing methods (Fourier transform, short-time Fourier transform, and multiscale entropy analysis were used to analyze the cycling movement data. Based on time–frequency analysis, subjects’ movement status change points were identified when fatigue occurred. Four movement status parameters were calculated, including the peak frequency before/after the movement status change point and the complexity index average (CIA) before/after the movement status change point. Inter-group and intra-group movement features, movement status, and physiological data were compared to determine fatigue-related features. Results showed that the peak frequency (p = 0.005), the peak frequency before/after the change point (p = 0.008/0.019), the CIA after the change point (p = 0.014), the maximum heart rate, maximal oxygen consumption, metabolic equivalents, and energy efficiency exhibited significant inter-group differences. The KR algorithm demonstrated outstanding performance in keypoint detection, achieving an accuracy of 86.5%, significantly outperforming OpenPose. With an inference speed of 30 FPS, it fulfills the demands for real-time monitoring. In addition, CIA valuses before and after change pointsshowed significant differences in the the middle-aged and elderly people group. Introduction

The increasing adoption of home-based rehabilitation reflects the global challenges posed by aging populations and escalating chronic disease burdens. In China, projections indicate a 38.3% surge in demand for rehabilitation interventions by 2030, driven predominantly by conditions such as osteoarthritis and post-stroke complications1. While traditional clinical rehabilitation remains constrained by resource limitations and prohibitive costs2, home-based approaches have gained prominence, often incorporating aerobic exercises to improve physical conditioning. Cycling has emerged as a cornerstone of household rehabilitation programs due to its standardized biomechanical patterns, adjustable joint stress, and measurable performance parameters3,4. Nevertheless, the lack of professional oversight in unsupervised home environments raises significant safety concerns, including risks of musculoskeletal injuries and cardiovascular strain associated with prolonged cycling fatigue5,6. Nevertheless, prolonged cycling has the potential to induce exercise fatigue, which can affect functional performance and overall health. Exercise-induced fatigue is a crucial physiological protective mechanism and serves as a foundation for adaptive changes during physical activity7. Therefore, precisely assessing the onset of fatigue during cycling is important for self-training and health management among middle-aged and elderly peoples. In other words, the development of a real-time and precise fatigue monitoring system has become imperative.

When exercise-induced fatigue occurs, it manifests in varying degrees across the nervous, muscular, psychological, and physiological systems, with symptoms such as increased heart rate and slowed movements, as detailed in Table 1. These multidimensional characteristics enable cycling fatigue assessment through four biomarker categories: Fatigue during exercise is usually assessed by subjective psychological evaluations, such as Rating of Perceived Exertion (RPE)8, or by physiological, biochemical, and kinesiological parameters. Biochemical parameters are primarily extracted from blood, saliva, and urine substances, such as serum creatine kinase, proteins, and urea, to monitor subjects’ metabolic status. However, these parameters require interpretation from medical professionals9. Physiological parameters, including biological signals such as heart rate(HR), electroencephalography (EEG), electromyography (EMG), and respiratory gases, are usually collected by wearable devices10. Heart rate is the most commonly used parameter to evaluate exercise fatigue. Above mentioned methods are susceptible to clinical practitioners’ subjective experiences, require specific conditions, and cause physical discomfort. Therefore, they may not be suitable for community or home-based continuous monitoring.

The multifaceted manifestations of fatigue in cycling.

Researchers have tried to use movement parameters to identify fatigue-related features. One popular approach is to analyze body acceleration signals. For instance, Cuesta-Vargas11 used embedded sensors in a smartphone to measure acceleration changes during 30 s of sitting and standing to assess physiological fatigue for breast cancer survivors. Marotta et al.12 analyzed 39 accelerometer-based fatigue identifications for lower limb exercises such as running, jumping, and walking. They suggested that changes in biomechanical parameters can be used to assess fatigue at an individual level. Darch et al.13 also found a strong correlation between fatigue and tibial acceleration, indicating that acceleration changes could be used as a fatigue marker.

Traditional approaches to exercise fatigue assessment have primarily relied on physiological or psychological indicators. With technological advancements, fatigue detection methodologies have diversified, not only improving recognition accuracy but also enabling potential applications in home-based settings.

Signal processing techniques offer a cost-effective and non-invasive avenue. For instance, surface electromyography (sEMG) can assess muscle fatigue by analyzing median frequency shifts14. Similarly, Entropy analysis extracts complexity features from EEG signals, attaining 90% accuracy15. The ECG signal introduces the STFT technique to differentiate between different levels of exercise fatigue16.While these methods excel in affordability and ease of use, their robustness in dynamic environments requires further optimization.

Machine learning-driven approaches leverage automated feature extraction and complex model architectures to enhance detection precision and robustness. The Support Vector Machine (SVM)17, for example, achieves 92.25% accuracy in small-sample scenarios by integrating sEMG and inertial measurement unit (IMU) data. However, the dependency on large annotated datasets and high computational complexity limits real-time deployment. Additionally, insufficient temporal feature extraction capabilities (e.g., SVM’s weakness in capturing sequential patterns) remain a critical bottleneck.

In recent years, a rapid influx of deep learning pose estimation algorithms has been applied to human motion capture. Marotta et al.12 have proposed that deep learning algorithms can enhance fatigue recognition by reducing the reliance on feature engineering. Moreover, Russell et al.18 combined acceleration and HR, and constructed a fatigue prediction model with a convolutional neural network. These studies suggest that utilizing deep learning to detect changes in physical activity. Zhang et al.19 further advanced this domain by proposing a hybrid CNN-LSTM framework for multimodal fatigue assessment. Their system synergistically processes time–frequency representations of sEMG signals to simultaneously achieve lower limb motion classification and muscle fatigue detection. Validated across diverse exercise modalities (e.g., cycling, stair climbing), the framework demonstrated robust performance with classification accuracies ranging from 78.85% to 88.35%, showcasing its adaptability to heterogeneous movement patterns. In contrast to other studies that have looked at recognising fatigue, Swapnali used a machine learning approach for smartphone-based detection of human fatigue levels, which recognises fatigue while grading the level of fatigue with an accuracy of 91.78%20.

Building upon existing methodologies, contemporary research has increasingly prioritized multimodal data fusion techniques for fatigue identification. Related work

Keypoint RCNN algorithm

Human pose estimation techniques exhibit diverse architectures, each tailored to specific application scenarios (Table 3). After systematic evaluation, this study selected the Keypoint RCNN(KR) algorithm for analyzing cycling kinematics due to its superior performance in spatial feature extraction and occlusion robustness. Unlike Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) or Transformer models, which are optimized for sequential data (e.g., text or time-series), KR excels at processing multi-dimensional spatial coordinates inherent in joint trajectories.

Specifically, while LSTM and Transformer models excel at modeling temporal dependencies in 1D sequential data, cycling motions produce complex 2D/3D spatiotemporal patterns involving dynamic joint interactions. Region-based convolutional networks such as KR are better suited for capturing these spatial relationships—particularly intricate joint configurations (e.g., knee trajectories).

As a computer vision analysis method, the KR algorithm is an advanced deep learning model for human joint detection and pose recognition. Derived from the Mask-RCNN architecture25, it incorporates an additional keypoint detection branch. The model employs a ResNet-50 backbone network and directly extracts 17 human body keypoints from input videos without requiring physical sensor attachments. Pre-trained on the COCO dataset for 24 epochs, its hyperparameter configuration includes: a learning rate of 0.001, batch size of 8, and optimization using Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) with momentum.

Comparing to the commonly used OpenPose algorithm, KR’s calculation process is more straightforward, making it an efficient tool for home-based scenarios Our previous research26 showed that KR demonstrated excellent reliability (ICC1,3 = 0.988) and validity (ICC2,1 = 0.865) when capturing knee movement in a pedaling task .

Signal processing technology

Time–frequency analysis and MSE (Multiscale Entropy) analysis are integral components of signal processing techniques. We employed Fourier Transform (FT) to characterize the key frequency features of acceleration signals.

Information processing technologies are effective methods for KR data analysis. Multiscale entropy (MSE) provides complexity information but not temporal information. By comparing data sequence similarity levels at different scales, MSE provides information about the complexity of action behavior. When fatigued, the physiological conditions required to maintain exercise will adaptively decrease, as is the overall complexity. Therefore, MSE is widely applied to extract human fatigue characteristics from various physiological signals, including EEG27,28, EMG29,30, and segmental acceleration data31,32 collected from different clinical populations. We have previously analyzed the acceleration data of the KR algorithm using MSE. Unfortunately, MSE is unable to derive temporal information directly, namely the point in time when the complexity changes26.

The short-time Fourier transform (STFT) is used to obtain the temporal information. STFT is a time–frequency analysis method. It divides a signal into windows and approximates it as stationary for a short period. Then, the Fourier transform is done on each short period to get the signal’s distribution at different times and frequencies. In this study, the STFT was used to obtain the movement status change point. For participants with large changes, these change points can be obtained directly through STFT, otherwise, it is difficult to obtain directly. To solve this problem, we propose a novel approach that combines STFT with MSE. Using time points derived from STFT to assess the complexity before and after the transformation before and after movment state serves to address this limitation. The inclusion of distinct age cohorts enhances the model’s generalizability by accounting for age-dependent physiological variances, such as fatigue thresholds. Specifically, older adults exhibit reduced joint range of motion (ROM) and lower cadence stability during cycling, introducing unique kinematic patterns that contrast with younger counterparts. These disparities not only strengthen the model’s robustness but also highlight its capacity to capture nuanced, age-specific movement dynamics.

Methods

Participants

The appropriate sample size for the study was determined using GPower V.3.1 software. A minimum of 20 participants was required to achieve an effect size greater than 0.9, with a two-tailed test at 80% power and a 5% significance level. The study recruited 20 middle-aged and elderly people over 45(nine males and 11 females) and 20 young adults aged 18–30(12 males and eight females). Basic subject information can be found in Table 5. Exclusion criteria included inability to stand or walk unaided, recent lower extremity, spinal, or brain surgery within the past six months, reported neurological, cardiac, or pulmonary diseases, cognitive impairments, use of medications affecting cardiovascular responses to exercise, other physical or mental discomfort, difficulty tolerating a respiratory mask, and unsuitability for exercise as determined by the 2014 Physical Activity Readiness Questionnaire (PAR-Q). All procedures were ethically reviewed and approved by the Biomedical Research Ethics Committee of Fujian Medical University, with the approval number 2022FUMCIRB160. In this study, we have obtained written informed consent from all participants for the publication of recognizable images (Fig. 2 and Fig. 3a, b, c) with other data details.

Data processing and analysis

The Redmi K40 smartphone’s video sampling frequency was set to 30 Hz. The KR algorithm analyzed the video and generated coordinate pixel-displacement CSV data files for 17 key points. Previous research conducted in the participant’s lateral direction had encountered an issue of overlap, which obscured the other side and led to non-recognition33. Therefore, this study analyzed the right knee joint’s vertical direction (Y-direction) data. A second differential was necessary to obtain the pixel acceleration data, as the output of the KR algorithm is the pixel displacement data as part of preprocessing(Fig. 4). In this study, time–frequency features (The Peak Frequency, energy) and entropy features (multi-scale entropy values) were extracted from acceleration data after time–frequency and entropy analysis. The Visual Signal software (Yitch, version 1.6 STD, Taiwan) was used for all operations, including differentiation, filtering, FT, STFT, and MSE.Cycling involves periodic motion, which creates periodic patterns in the data. However, the individual differences in the cycles of the participants make it challenging to compare complexity on the same scale. To express the complexity of the data, this study chose to use the average complexity index (CIA) of the first periodic for all participants. This index provides a standard measure of complexity that can be used to compare the data across different participants. The entire movement Complexity Index Average (E-CIA) was derived from the area under the entire movement MSE (E-MSE) curve and then averaged. CIA was calculated according to Eq. According to ACSM guidelines, exercise intensity in this experiment was estimated using %HRR. Analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics 26 with a significance level of P < 0.05. Indicators were tested for normality. Comparisons between groups were made using t-tests or Mann–Whitney U-tests, and comparisons within groups were made using paired t-tests and paired rank-sum tests, with effect sizes calculated (Cohen’s d, Hedges’ g, or r) and 95% confidence intervals added for the indicators of interest.

Discussion

This study proposes a noninvasive, universal, and interference-free method for determining fatigue status based on deep learning posture estimation algorithms. The Keypoint RCNN algorithm is highly efficient for detecting and positioning essential points and can accurately locate keypoints in the image during movements, such as knees or ankles26. By analyzing thesekeypoints’position and movement changes, we have identified essential parameters related to movement status changes while pedaling at a self-regulated speed on a stationary bike. Our results showed that the CIA-AC parameters were significant after both inter and intra-group comparisons of the various parameters. We concluded that the CIA-AC could be used to determine fatigue in people aged over 45 while exercising. This is the first time that KR algorithm data and MSE analysis can objectively extract the fatigue feature of stationary bike exercise for the first time. This method might be able to help assess the movement status of individuals more accurately.

CIA-AC is an entropy feature index derived from multi-scale entropy analysis and is also the motion state parameter in this paper.The significant reduction in CIA-AC following the movement status change point provides a novel, physiologically-grounded biomarker for fatigue. The observed decline in complexity aligns with well-established principles of neuromuscular fatigue36. During fatiguing contractions, the central nervous system alters motor unit recruitment strategies: there is a decrease in the recruitment of fast-twitch motor units and an increase in the synchronization of active motor units to maintain force output37. The shift from a complex, asynchronous firing pattern to a more simplified and synchronized one is directly captured by a reduction in MSE values32.Whereas traditional metrics like peak frequency (PF) primarily reflect quantitative changes in movement speed. CIA-AC captures the qualitative deterioration of movement control. This explains why CIA-AC, rather than PF or CIA-BC, proved to be the most sensitive discriminator of fatigue in our study, particularly in the older adult group who are more susceptible to neuromuscular coordination deficits. The convergence of our findings—where the decline in CIA-AC coincided with sharp changes in HR and frequency (Fig. 5)—strengthens the argument that this metric is not an algorithmic artifact but a valid representation of underlying physiological strain. Therefore, CIA-AC can be interpreted as a non-invasive proxy for the loss of movement adaptability and efficiency in the neuromuscular system due to fatigue. It is also important to note that CIA-AC is not intended to predict HRmax or VO₂max. These peak physiological measures reflect cardiovascular and metabolic capacity, which is a different domain from the neuromuscular control and movement quality that CIA-AC captures. A individual may have high cardiovascular fitness but poor movement economy, or vice versa.

A key objective of this study was to establish the CIA-AC , rather than to develop a predictive model for immediate deployment. The rationale for this approach is twofold. First, the validation of a novel biomarker requires a clear demonstration of its sensitivity (e.g., significant within-group changes in the elderly) and specificity (e.g., ability to discriminate between groups under fatigue), which we have robustly demonstrated in our results. Second, building a generalizable predictive or classificatory model requires a much larger and more diverse dataset, specifically designed for that purpose with pre-defined endpoints for model training and testing. Our current study, with its controlled design and focus on mechanistic insight, provides the essential proof-of-concept that justifies and informs the collection of such a large-scale dataset for future model development.

In this study, we developed a non-invasive fatigue assessment approach based on phone videos, which is cost-effective and easy-to-operate. A novel prameter CIA-AC was identified from video-based body keypoints and validated as a sensitive fatigue biomarker. This workflow requires only a smartphone, making it a promising tool for home-based fatigue monitoring, especially for those elder people who are vulnerable to fatigue-related injuries.

We have demonstrated that the core algorithm (Keypoint RCNN) operates at 30 FPS on a consumer-grade smartphone (Redmi K40), proving the technical feasibility of real-time processing in a device already available in many households. The logical next step, as rightly pointed out, is to translate this technological proof-of-concept into a user-friendly application. This will require future research focused on human–computer interaction, including usability testing with target users in real home environments to optimize interface design and user feedback mechanisms. The real-world application potential of this method is significant. For example, it can be integrated into a smart kinetic cycling system to capture the user’s riding posture in real time via a built-in camera, combined with an edge computing module (e.g., Raspberry Pi or mobile phone chip) for local data processing. When CIA-AC is detected to be below the threshold, the system can automatically reduce the resistance or send vibration alerts to the user via Bluetooth. In addition, the method can be encapsulated into an SDK for health management APP to call, achieving fatigue status cloud synchronisation and long-term trend analysis, providing scientific guidance for family rehabilitation. This solution not only avoids the discomfort of wearing devices, but also reduces the hardware cost, which is suitable for large-scale promotion.

We compared movement data to observe the differences in movement between the over-45 and young groups. PF, PF-AC, PF-BC, and CIA-AC can determine the movement status of different groups. TheCIAmeasures the movement status complexity, where higher entropy indicates a more complex status. The statistical results of theCIAandCIA-BC for inter-groups were insignificant, which means that the movement status of the two groups could notbe distinguished. When pedaling at a self-regulated speed, the cardiopulmonary system responds directly to exercise fatigue by increasing heart rate. However, the muscle movement does not change immediately when heart rate rises.This implies a delay in the muscles’ response to fatigue compared to the cardiopulmonary performance. For example, in Fig. 4 the heart rate continues to rise after 358 s, while in the STFT, the frequency drops for a period and then remains basically unchanged.

The signal for acceleration recorded a considerable length of continuous pedaling. In contrast, unstable pedaling due to fatigue only lasts for a short time. As a result, it became challenging for the CIA and CIA-BC to identify any changes in the movement status. TheCIA-AC was notably lower in the over-45 group compared to theCIA-BC.This indicates that older adults experience more disturbances and uncertainties during movement, leading to a decline in theCIA-AC. These results are consistent with previous studies that found that entropy decreases as muscle fatigue occurs during exercise based on MSE analysis32.This may be due to older adults experiencing a decline in their coordination ability during exercise, which can cause their exercise status to become more unstable. Meanwhile, in the young group, although the entropy also decreased after the change point, the magnitude of the decrease was relatively small, indicating that young people have better coordination and stability during exercise. As a result, they are better equipped to handle the challenges of changes in movement status.

In the group over-45, both CIA-BC and CIA-AC showed noticeable differences, distinguishing the changes in older adults’ overall exercise process status. Therefore, theCIA-AC in the over-45 group can identify the intra- and inter-group differences. This makes it a crucial feature for monitoring the movement status of older adults. However, in the young group, theCIA-BC andCIA-AC were insignificant. Under the same conditions, young participants have better cardiopulmonary, metabolic, and physiological conditions due to age factors, and they experience less fatigue. This finding provides a simple, efficient, and accurate assessment tool for monitoring movement status, which is particularly suitable for elderly people at home.

Prudence is warranted in the application and interpretation of CIA-AC results, owing to the current absence of a rigorously defined threshold. In over-45 group, the average CIA-AC before fatigue was 0.36 ± 0.05, and after fatigue it was 0.29 ± 0.06, with an entropy value decrease of approximately 19%.This aligns with the > 15% entropy reduction threshold for fatigue proposed in accelerometer studies32. Although defining a universal cutoff requires larger cohorts, our data indicate CIA-AC < 0.30 warrants clinical attention in over-45 cyclists.

This study has several limitations. Firstly, the sample size of 20 participants per group, while consistent with sample sizes employed in similar exploratory studies in the field of movement analysis and fatigue research32, may limit the generalizability of the findings to broader populations. Future studies with larger and more diverse cohorts are warranted to confirm these findings and enhance their external validity. Secondly,theCIAandCIA-BC cannot distinguish the movement status on Inter-group. A larger sample or an improved MSE calculation model needs to be conducted for more detailed research.Thirdly, there are potential biases in the experimental design: only the right knee vertical direction data were analysed without considering the effect of the left knee occlusion on the detection of critical points; moreover, the data acquisition was done in a controlled light environment, and the change of light in real applications may lead to the degradation of the model performance. In the future, the robustness needs to be improved by multi-view camera layout and data enhancement (e.g., modelling occlusion, light perturbation).Fourth,the movement statusparameterCIA-AC and the movement status change point were both extracted manually. In the future, machine learning algorithms can automatically identify the change point after inputting a large amount of data, further improving efficiency.For example, using KR to extract the right knee coordinate sequence; smoothing and normalising the coordinate data (e.g. filtering and denoising); and using LSTM or Transformer to analyse the pattern of change in the trajectory (e.g. motion amplitude attenuation, cyclic disorders) and quantify the fatigue level. Lastly, physiological data mainly served as a verification standard in this study, and the direct connection between KR data and physiological data has not yet been explored. The effect of psychological factors on fatigue RPE was also measured in this study but also not directly linked in this model. Further research may be needed to establish a link between physiological-psychological and KR data for multimodal analyses.

Conclusion

In this study, we demonstrated the effectiveness of the Complexity Index Average after Change Point (CIA-AC) in distinguishing cycling status across different age groups. Our findings revealed that the young and older adult group (> 45 years) exhibited significantly lower CIA-AC values compared to the younger cohort (18–30 years) (*p* = 0.014, Cohen’s *d* = 0.07), while maintaining 86.5% reliability in keypoint detection accuracy using the Keypoint RCNN model. These results position CIA-AC as an objective biomarker of fatigue that quantitatively reflects exercise-induced physical decline in aging populations. Through integration of the KR algorithm with advanced signal processing techniques, this work presents a viable non-invasive fatigue monitoring framework, potentially enabling novel approaches for home-based rehabilitation training and personalized health management.

Our immediate future work will therefore focus on overcoming these critical limitations by: (1) expanding the analysis to include multiple joints (e.g., both knees, ankles, hips) to create a more robust whole-body assessment model; and (2) conducting rigorous robustness testing by collecting data under diverse lighting conditions and simulating occlusions, which will be essential for developing effective compensation algorithms. (3) Use ROC curve analysis to determine a robust CIA-AC threshold with a predetermined sensitivity and specificity.

Future research should focus on three primary directions

Personalized energy expenditure modeling-Developing dynamic prediction frameworks by combining CIA-AC with physiological parameters (e.g., heart rate, VO₂max) to optimize exercise efficiency evaluation. Adaptive intensity regulation-Creating algorithms that dynamically adjust cycling resistance or exercise intensity thresholds based on real-time CIA-AC feedback to prevent over-fatigue;Intelligent exercise prescription design-Generating customized rehabilitation plans through integration of fatigue characteristics with health metrics (e.g., BMI, medical history) to enhance scientific rigor in home training programs.

An efficacious fabrication of Tara gum reduced/stabilized gold nanoparticles and its post functionalization with quercetin for sensitive and selective optical detection of iodide ion
Authors: Titilope John Jayeoye, Fredrick Nwude Eze, Opeyemi Joshua Olatunji, Nongnuj Muangsin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractHerein, we reported Tara gum (TG), mediated synthesis of stable and dispersed gold nanoparticles (AuNPs). TG was applied for the first time as the reducing/capping agent for AuNPs synthesis, through heating with gold salt, stirring at 80 °C for 1 h under NaOH support. Reddish color AuNPs solution, exhibiting absorption maximum at 521 nm, with an average particle size of 10.5 ± 1.8 nm, was obtained. The synthesized TG-AuNPs was characterized using various equipment, including TEM, FESEM, ATR-FTIR, Raman spectroscopy, XRD, TGA/DTG, DLS/zeta potential and XPS, which confirmed the dispersal of small-sized AuNPs in TG polymeric structure. TG-AuNPs was further modified with Quercetin (QT), a notable polyphenolic compound, to realize TG-AuNPs-QT nanoparticles which was applied as optical nanoprobe for Iodide ion (I−) detection. The addition of silver ion [Ag+], to the TG-AuNPs-QT probe results in color change, from red to yellow, followed with an absorption spectra blue shift from 521 nm to 420 nm, owing to QT mediated in-situ reduction of Ag+ to its nanoparticles, which was deposited on pre-formed AuNPs, yielding Au@Ag nanostructure. However, when I− (various concentrations), were pre-mixed with an optimized Ag+ concentration, reverse color and absorption spectra perturbations were observed, which is attributed to the high binding interaction between Ag+ and I−, forming AgI complex in solution. At the optimal I− detection conditions, the analytical response applied, (Ao-Ai)@405 nm, was linear with I- concentrations from 1.0 to 65.0 μM, with a limit of detection/quantification (LOD/LOQ) of 250 nM and 0.82 μM, respectively. Practical applications of the probe was demonstrated on water and diverse food samples, with satisfactory accuracy.Keywords: Tara gum, Gold nanoparticles, Quercetin, Optical probe, Silver ion, Iodide ion detection

Introduction

Iodine is an important trace element that is needed for the optimal functioning of the human body, owing to its role in thyroid function especially in the biosynthesis of thyroid hormones (THs) (Sorrenti et al., 2021), which are heavily implicated in maintaining healthy body development and homeostasis. Dietary intake from foods is the chief form in which Iodine is supplied to the human body, including foods like, fish, seafoods, seaweeds, diary products like, milk, yogurt and eggs also contain iodine. The world health organization recommends a daily iodine consumption of 150 to 250 microgram for adults human and pregnant/lactating women (N. Wang et al., 2019). Proper balance of Iodine ion (I-) in the human system is important as deficiency of iodine can cause goiter and hypothyroidism (Zimmermann & Boelaert, 2015), cognitive impairment (Tuccilli et al., 2018) and some form of cancers (Lv et al., 2021). Consequently, detection and monitoring of I- in different matrices has attracted huge interest from scientists. Reported detection strategies include: inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) (Rosen et al., 2022), ion chromatography or IC (M.-H. Wang et al., 2015), electrochemical methods including cathodic stripping voltammetry (Khunseeraksa et al., 2020), amperometric flow injection method (Alqibthiyah et al., 2024) and fluorescence based detections (Chen et al., 2009; Du et al., 2013). Others based on nanomaterials applications have equally been successful, such as, bimetallic-based colorimetric sensor (Li et al., 2024), where biopolymer-gelatin was applied for gold salt reduction and those based on Cu2+ supported Au@Ag core shell nanoparticles (Zeng et al., 2015). For example, (Fu et al., 2022), reported a highly sensitive Histidine derived gold nanoclusters (His-AuNCs) based on I- enhancement of its oxidase-like activity, in the presence of 3,3′5,5′ tetramethylbenzidine (TMB), with a LOD of 3 nM. While these previous works have been impressively utilized for I- detection, some indeed suffer from severe limitations, especially those based on the use of huge analytical equipment such as ICP-MS and IC, require operational skills, series of sample preparation regimen, while also lacking on-site or point-of-use applicability. Moreover, detection systems based on electrochemistry require high skill in handling electrodes, while fluorescence-based assays may suffer from autofluorescence of fabricated materials and toxicities of common fluorophores. Hence, a detection system that combines high sensitivity, simplicity, rapidity and possible deployment for on-site use is regularly in high demand.

Nanomaterials based analytical detections holds great promise in diverse analytes detection, owing to their unique optical properties. Their sheer small sizes in the nanoscale dimension (1–100 nm), makes them highly susceptible to various ligands conjugation and modifications which can be tuned for specific analyte binding (Jayeoye & Muangsin, 2024; Jayeoye, Panghiyangani, et al., 2024). In the recent decade, keen interest has been shown by materials Chemist to promoting eco-friendly or environmentally sustainable synthesis of materials. Here, the use of nature derived substrates is promoted over chemically made starting materials. The use of biopolymers in nanomaterials fabrication has received impressive scientific success/progress, consequently, in this work, Tara gum (TG), a popular polysaccharide biopolymer, is exploited as the reducing and stabilizing agent for the first time, without support from any chemical reductants.

TG is an important and highly valuable biopolymer belonging to the class called galactomannans, including guar gum, Funugreek, Alfalfa gum and Locust beam gum. It is commonly extracted from the seed of Caesalpinia spinosa tree. In the food industry, the colorless powder is used as an emulsifier, thickener and as stabilizer of labile food components, attributed to its excellent water dispersity, solubility and stability (Mukherjee et al., 2023; Raj et al., 2024). Structurally as shown in Scheme 1, TG contains regular repeating units of (1–4) joined ꞵ-D-mannopyranosyl units attached to a single (1–6) α-D-galactopyranosyl units. Thus, TG contains galactose and mannose as the monosaccharide units, with the M/G ratio often used as a key feature of each galactomannan. TG applications in nanomaterials fabrication have been sparingly documented. Eze et al., developed TG and broken rice berry derived silver nanoparticles for multifaceted biological applications (Eze et al., 2022), while quercetin grafted TG prepared through the free radical grafting method using ascorbic acid and hydrogen peroxide pairs have been used for AuNPs synthesis (Eze et al., 2023). Similarly, we have exploited the in-situ chemical oxidative synthesis strategy using TG and poly vinyl alcohol blend (PVA) blend as stabilizer to realize AuNPs/poly aminobenzene boronic acid/TG-PVA colloidal nanocomposite for gold ion detection in aqueous environment (Jayeoye, Singh, et al., 2024). In all the above TG based nanomaterials synthesis, TG was never used as the sole reductant/stabilizer. As a result, we hereby report the use of TG for AuNPs without any supportive material, for the detection of important I- anion. We hypothesized that under heating/alkali pH adjustment of aqueous TG solution, the reductive capacity of the biopolymer can be intensely activated, so that the addition of gold salt and its subsequent reduction to its nanoparticles form is achieved within a short time frame. The stable TG-AuNPs, now devoid of any reductive species, may be compensated for through QT functionalization, to obtain a new nanomaterial (TG-AuNPs-QT), which can be readily tuned at ambient conditions for I− detection through Ag+ supplementation. In fact, the QT functionalization step imbues the detection probe (TG-AuNPs-QT), with impressive sensitivity, while the presence of TG, assisted in material stability and robustness for I- detection even in complex matrices.

Schematic illustration of TG reduced/stabilized gold nanoparticles.

In this work, we reported the synthesis and characterization of TG derived AuNPs and its potential application for the detection of I- in aqueous solution. TG-AuNPs synthesis parameters were optimized, to achieve optimal synthesis condition that is reproducible. After successful synthesis of TG-AuNPs, the colloidal solution was post modified with quercetin (QT), a notable polyphenolic compound to realize TG-AuNPs-QT. The highly hydrophilic TG-AuNPs could improve the aqueous stability and dispersity of QT-a well-known hydrophobic drug through hydrogen bonding (Rao et al., 2023). The synthesized ruby red and highly stable TG-AuNPs-QT probe, responded swiftly to Ag+ ion charging, marked by a naked eye color change from red to yellow, and an absorption spectra blue shift from 521 nm to 400 nm. In the presence of I-, owing to its high binding affinity to Ag+, a reverse color formation and absorbance perturbations were obtained. The detection probe was applied to I- detection in water and diverse food samples, while demonstrating impressive accuracy.

Thermal stability was investigated on a thermogravimetric Analyzer TGA8000, Perkin Elmer, USA, using a scanning program method. Small mass of the samples (TG and TG-AuNPs) was placed on the sample pan, heated from 50 to 1000 °C, at 10 °C/min under nitrogen atmosphere at 20 mL/min. X-ray diffractometer (XRD) was carried out on Empyrean PANalytical, from Netherland. Synthesis of TG-AuNPs

Before TG-AuNPs synthesis, 0.5 % (v/v) of TG solution was prepared under heating at 50 °C, and vigorous stirring until a perfect solution was formed without lumps. The prepared TG solution was stored in the refrigerator overnight before use. 25 mM concentration of gold salt was prepared in water, by weighing appropriate mass, which was dissolved in ultra-pure water. TG-AuNPs was prepared as follows, into a beaker, 23 mL of 0.25 % TG, was measured and was maintained under heating at 80 °C (optimized synthesis temperature), under vigorous stirring. Further, 1.25 mL of 25 mM gold salt (optimized gold concentration), was added, followed by the addition of 0.25 mL of 0.5 M NaOH solution, for 1 h (optimized synthesis time). The final volume of the synthesis pot was 25 mL and was maintained by adding ultra-pure water. Synthesis parameters were optimized according to achieve highly stable and dispersed nanoparticles. Synthesized TG-AuNPs was transferred into an amber bottle and was stored in a refrigerator before QT functionalization.

Functionalization of TG-AuNPs with QT

Synthesized TG-AuNPs was diluted appropriately, from where 30 mL was measured into a beaker maintained under stirring at room temperature (RT). 4.5 mL of QT, 0.4 mM, prepared in ethanol (optimized QT concentration), prepared in ethanol was added and was made up to 36 mL with ultra-pure water. The TG-AuNPs-QT colloidal solution was stirred for 2 h at RT, to allow QT mix with TG-AuNPs. The solution was stored at 4 °C, before use.

Optical detection of I- based on TG-AuNPs-QT

For I− detection, into a 1.5 mL Eppendorf tube, 200 μL of different concentrations of potassium iodide (KI) prepared in ultra-pure water, was pipetted, followed by the addition of 50 μL of silver nitrate solution 2.8 mM (optimized Ag+ concentration), 50 μL of 0.1 M NaOH solution was injected, and appropriate volume of water was added. The mixture was vortex mixed, after which 400 μL of TG-AuNPs-QT was injected, then vortex mixed. The final volume of the detection system is 1.4 mL. The photo images and the absorption spectra of the solution was acquired after 10 min RT incubation.

Real sample application of TG-AuNPs-QT for I- detection in water, kelp and other food samples

The practical application of the TG-AuNPs-QT colorimetric probe for I- detection was demonstrated on Tap water and Kelp (seaweeds) food sample. Laboratory tap water was allowed to run for a few minutes before it was taken in a beaker. The water sample was boiled for some minutes and was allowed to cool to RT. It was then passed through 0.22-μm membrane filter. The prepared water sample was spiked with various concentrations of I- and was used for I- detection following the steps in Section 2.5. Results and discussion

Synthesis of TG-AuNPs

TG mediated synthesis of small sized, stable and dispersed AuNPs was investigated through the addition of gold salt to a beaker containing TG, to which NaOH solution has been injected. To achieve an optimal synthesis condition, parameters affecting the AuNPs synthesis steps were immediately optimized.

Optimization of synthesis parameters

The concentration of gold salts in the final synthesis reaction container could affect the AuNPs yield and its optical features, while keeping the concentration of TG constant. As mentioned in Section 2.3, the final volume of the synthesis reaction pot was maintained at 25 mL. The volume of gold salt (with prepared concentration of 25 mM) added was varied from 0.25 to 1.5 mL, so that the final concentrations of gold salt ranged from 0.25 to 1.5 mM. As shown in Fig. 1A, as the gold concentration increases, the absorption spectra intensity increased with the formation of a sharp peak at 521 nm, which is characteristic of small sized spherical AuNPs (Borse & Konwar, 2020), as shown in Fig. 1A(a). Similarly, the photo images changed from faint red to reddish color from 1.0 mM (Fig. 1A-a inset), confirming the generation of more AuNPs arising from the reduction of more gold salt to its nanoparticles mediated by TG. The plot of absorbance ratios at 521 nm and at longer wavelength 650 nm, could provide better information about the nano colloids, intrinsic optical property. As shown in Fig. 1A(b), the ratio increased with gold salt concentrations, peaked at 1.25 mM, then dropped afterwards. This shows that the best synthesis yield based on visual observations and absorption spectra monitoring was achieved at 1.25 mM and was thus selected. This concentration provided the best balance between the reductant (TG) and gold salt salts so that maximum nanoparticles formation was obtained and were appropriately stabilized by TG.

The synthesis reaction temperature could also affect AuNPs synthesis. The temperature can enhance the rate of the reaction, by catalyzing the rate of biopolymer breakdown to its smaller units, which may improve its reductive efficacy. Biopolymers based nanoparticles synthesis at ambient conditions are generally poor, owing to the high inter-planar binding interactions, which are rigid, thus limiting the free movement of bonds and by extension impact their reductive capacity of precursor metal ions in aqueous environment (Fredricks et al., 2023). To minimize this limitation and ensure good AuNPs yield, the use of strong chemical reducing agents (sodium borohydride) or highly efficient polyphenolic compounds, to support AuNPs synthesis reaction at ambient conditions are often proposed (C. Wang et al., 2022). From the foregoing, the temperature of the synthesis reaction pot, would exert significant impact on the AuNPs synthesis processes in the presence of TG, and was consequently varied from RT (30 °C) to 90 °C. As depicted in Fig. 1B (a), the absorption spectra increased as the temperature increases, showing that increased temperature is beneficial to TG-AuNPs synthesis, which is supported by the color transitions (Fig. 1B-a inset). At low temperature, the polymeric chains of TG may not be easily broken, thus less reductive species are available in solution for gold salt reduction, while high temperature ensures more reductive monosaccharides from TG breakdown are available for gold salt reduction, in the reaction system. The absorbance ratio at 521 to 650 nm was also plotted to identify the colloidal solution with the best optical feature. Fig. 1B-b shows that the ratio increased with a peak at 80 °C, dropping at 90 °C, which may be attributed to possible disruption in TG stabilization effect, which may result in slight particles aggregation. Thus, 80 °C, was selected at the optimum synthesis temperature of TG-AuNPs.

Finally, the synthesis kinetics was monitored for 100 mins, using 1.25 mM gold salt concentration and at 80 °C, while keeping TG concentration fixed. The absorption spectra increased quite rapidly (Fig. 1C-a), while the plot of absorbance at 521 nm versus time in minutes (Fig. 1C-b) maintained consistent increment with a peak at 60 mins (as marked by the red rectangle), without showing any significant differences (P ≥ 0.05), afterwards. This shows the synthesis processes is completed at 60 mins and was thus selected. The TG-AuNPs synthesized under the optimal condition was stored in the refrigerator at 4 °C, and the stability under this storage was tested by talking the absorption spectrum over 26 weeks. As shown in Fig.S1, the absorption spectra remained sharp at 521 nm, without any observation of peaks at 650 nm or beyond often used to describe AuNPs aggregation peaks. This shows TG provided perfect reduction and stabilization effect on the synthesized TG-AuNPs.

Mechanism of TG-AuNPs synthesis

TG reduced/stabilized AuNPs was synthesized based on gold salt injection into a vigorously stirred solution of TG maintained at 80 °C, whose pH has been adjusted to around 7 using NaOH solution. At this pH, with high temperature and stirring, the multitudinous hydroxyl groups of TG are deprotonated, while some glycosidic linkages joining TG monosaccharides are broken, exposing highly reductive sugars (galactose and mannose). These conditions facilitate swift reduction of gold salt to its nanoparticles, on injection into the TG solution. AuNPs synthesis generally follows the popular particles nucleation and growth mechanism where numerous nuclei are formed, under appropriate reaction conditions, then the particles are clustered together under random attachment, which then grow and matured based on Oswald ripening, while being stabilized by the oxidized species of the precursor materials (Thanh et al., 2014). As depicted in Scheme 1, under the stated synthesis conditions, a redox reaction is set up between TG and gold salt, after which TG undergoes oxidation while the gold salt follows a reduction reaction to its nanoparticles. Based on the Nef-Isbell mechanism the hydroxyl groups at the distal ends of TG will undergo oxidation to carboxylate ions COO− (Jayeoye et al., 2021; Laksee et al., 2017) which surround the synthesized TG-AuNPs. This would prevent facile particles aggregation owing to electrostatic repulsion between similar charged TG-AuNPs in solution. The visual observation of reddish color TG-AuNPs with an absorption maximum at 521 nm is an indication of the successful reduction of gold salt to its nanoparticles mediated by only TG aqueous solution, without support from any additional chemical reducing agents. This reaction is green or eco-friendly, where natural materials are deployed for the nano materials fabrication. Fig.S2 shows the absorption spectra and photo images of TG, gold salt and TG-AuNPs. As observed, only the mixture of TG and gold salt showed a sharp peak at 521 nm (UV–Vis speatra-Fig.S2), which is attributed to the localized surface plasmon resonance of small sized AuNPs, while the photo images revealed colorless for TG, slight yellow color for gold salt and red color for TG-AuNPs.

Characterization of TG-AuNPs

The use of diverse analytical instruments can reveal intrinsic information on the microstructural and morphological features of materials. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was used to observe the images of TG-AuNPs as shown in Fig. 2A-a. The particles are spherical and dispersed, without any clumpiness or aggregation. Thsis shows the synthesis process is effective and that TG under appropriate conditions could be used for AuNPs synthesis without need for supporting reducing agents. The size distribution of particles obtained from TEM, as plotted with an image J software shows the AuNPs are more distributed between 5 and 10 nm, with about 62 % frequency (Fig.S3). FESEM could be used to reveal particles morphology similar to what is obtained from TEM. As shown in Fig. 2A-b the FESEM images of TG, shows plain surface, while that of TG-AuNPs revealed small specs of whitish AuNPs interspersed in the TG polymer surface (Fig. 2A-c), as emphasized with the orange color arrows. The energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) spectrum is shown in Fig. 2A-d, where prominent peaks of O, Au and C are present with weight percentages of 45.19, 32.79 and 22.02 % respectively (Fig. 2A-d inset). The elemental mapping of the TG-AuNPs surface shows the presence of O, Au and C, as shown in Fig.S4. The average particles size of TG-AuNPs obtained from a dynamic light scattering (DLS) system is 31.5 ± 2.2 nm (Fig. 2A-e), with a zeta potential value of −35.3 ± 1.8 mV, showing that the particles are highly stable. In fact zeta potential value within ±30 mV, is assigned to colloidal solutions with good stability (Sikora et al., 2015).

These peaks are significantly shifted and less broadened in TG-AuNPs (Fig. 2B-a orange line), especially in the regions marked I, II and III. This shows TG functional groups are involved in the TG-AuNPs. This trend is further supported by the Raman spectra of TG and TG-AuNPs (Fig.S5). As can be observed, a sharp peak at 1016.5 cm−1 from C-OH stretching/bending of galactomannans (Yuen et al., 2009), was observed in TG (Fig.S5), while in TG-AuNPs, peaks at 304.5 and 2142.6 cm−1, were observed in addition to the broad peaks around 950–1200 cm−1 which results from TG polymeric chains interaction with gold salts to yield TG-AuNPs.

The X-ray diffractogram of TG as shown in Fig. 2B-b, reveals sharp 2θ peaks at 7.9, 15.6 and 20.4 degree (Fig. 2B-b blue line), which are characteristic peaks of amorphous TG polymeric backbones (Chi et al., 2018). However, the sharpness reduced in TG-AuNPs (Fig. 2B-b orange line), with the emergence of 2θ peaks at 20.5 degree from TG and other prominent peaks at 38.5, 44.7, 65.1 and 78.2 degrees attributed to the (111), (200), (220) and (311) diffraction peaks of face centered cubic (fcc) crystalline structure of AuNPs (Jankowski et al., 2022). This shows, the XRD reflects the presence of TG and crystalline AuNPs in the fabricated TG-AuNPs.

The thermal properties of TG and on being used as the base material for AuNPs synthesis, to form TG-AuNPs was investigated as shown in Fig. 2B-c and d. The TGA profile shows three sharp degradation profiles, as detailed in Table S2. For TG (Fig. 2B-c blue line), the TGA profile shows three distinct degradation profiles, with inset temperature at 50.2 °C, such that stage I (50.2–294.5 °C), stage II (294.5–390.2 °C) and stage III (390.2–1000 °C). Similarly, TG-AuNPs (Fig. 2B-c orange line), exhibited onset temperatures at 50.3 °C, with three thermal degradation profiles as follows. Stage I (50.3–230.0 °C), stage II (230.0–376.8 °C) and stage III (376.8–1000 °C). The se three stages are signature of biopolymers thermal degradation trends with stage I, attributed to the loss of water vapor from polymer structures or water evaporation stage, stage II assigned to the breakdown in the polymer network well-ordered arrangement and stage III as the complete loss of the material or charring of the material. It is also known as the ash stage (Shah et al., 2024). The ash percent after complete charring of the material is a reliable tool for estimating the material stability. The higher the ash percent, the more stable the material. As shown in Table S1, the ash percent of TG is 2.83 % while that of TG-AuNPs was 10.61 %, showing that the impregnation of AuNPs into TG polymer networks, arising from using TG as the base materials for AuNPs synthesis, enhanced its thermal stability almost five times. This is adequately supported by the DTG profile (Fig. 2B-d), where a single sharp peak at around 320 °C, was observed for TG (Fig. 2B-d blue line), showing most of the thermal breakdown of the material occur around that temperature. However, three peaks could be identified in TG-AuNPs (Fig. 2B-d orange line), which corresponded to the TG profile of TG-AuNPs highlighted prior.

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) is another very versatile characterization equipment that can provide information on the elemental composition and the possible oxidation states of available elements identified in fabricated nanomaterials. The full survey profile of TG-AuNPs (Fig. 3A), shows the presence of Au, C and O with 2.9, 62.5 and 34.6 % respectively. Expansion of these peaks shows deconvoluted Au 4f peaks (Fig. 3B) at 83.7 and 87.6 eV, which are assigned to the Au 4f7/2 and Au 4f5/2. The spin-orbit differences between the two Au binding energies is about 3.7 eV, matching that reported for Au0 (Jasmin et al., 2017). Functionalization of TG-AuNPs with quercetin

From the outset, the objective of the work was to design a stable sensitive and selective colloidal optical probe for I− detection based on the interaction between Ag+ and I−. The appropriate optical probe displays rapid color change on Ag+ charging, which can be obstructed after I− addition. Thus, we first tested TG-AuNPs and TG-AuNPs functionalized with QT (TG-AuNPs-QT) as shown in Fig. 4A. Under NaOH addition for pH control, TG-AuNPs shows sharp peak at 521 nm (Fig. 4A-a) with characteristic red color (Fig. 4A-a inset). The addition of Ag+ to TG-AuNPs led to a slight increment in absorption intensity (Fig. 4A-b) and color (Fig. 4A-b inset). This shows that under the prevailing reaction condition, TG-AuNPs is not sensitive to Ag+ charging at a high concentration (150 μM). However, after QT functionalization of TG-AuNPs, no significant absorption spectra changes was observed (Fig. 4A-c), so does the color image (Fig. 4A-c inset). On Ag+ charging, TG-AuNPs-QT probe experienced a blue shift in absorption spectra (Fig. 4A-d), with a sharp peak at 410 nm, which is assigned to AgNPs, shell deposited on pre-formed TG-AuNPs, and was confirmed by the development of sparkling yellow color (Fig. 4A-d inset), over a very short time frame. Hence the functionalization of TG-AuNPs with QT is justified to improve detection mechanism sensitivity. Afterwards, QT functionalization on TG-AuNPs was optimized as shown in Fig. 4B. As can be seen, the addition of QT solution to TG-AuNPs under RT stirring, did not bring sudden change in the absorption spectra for QT concentrations tested within 0.0 to 50.0 μM (Fig. 4B-a to f), notably after this concentration the TG-AuNPs changed color to yellowish red color at 66.7 μM QT charging as marked by the arrow (Fig. 4B-g inset). Consequently, the final concentration of QT in the TG-AuNPs solution (TG-AuNPs-QT) was maintained at 50.0 μM. Application of TG-AuNPs-QT as optical probe for I- detection

The optical probe TG-AuNPs-QT was applied for I− detection in aqueous solution based on the strong Ag+ binding to I−. Thus, the parameters affecting the detection system was summarily optimized to achieve high sensitivity, and method robustness. The concentration of Ag+ used and the incubation time for color and absorption spectra change on interacting with TG-AuNPs-QT probe are two key parameters affecting the detection system. The addition of varying concentrations of Ag+ to the pH adjusted TG-AuNPs-QT probe, results in absorption spectra enhancement or increment, with the emergence of a sharp peak at about 405 nm, as shown by the red arrow, Fig. 5A. Interestingly, the color of TG-AuNPs-QT changed from reddish color to yellow (Fig. 5A inset). The yellow color formation and the sharp absorption peaks blue shift, are attributed to the formation of small sized AgNPs on initially formed TG-AuNPs, to realize Au@AgNPs nanostructure (Rani et al., 2023). The position of the absorption spectra is dependent on the Ag shell thickness. As can be seen, as the red color of AuNPs faded, sharp absorption peak of AuNPs reduced, while the emergence of yellow color was marked with an absorption spectrum at 405 nm. The choice of appropriate Ag+ is key to obtaining impressive analytical parameters for the detection system. As marked with a purple arrow, Fig. 4A inset, with 100.0 μM Ag+, sparkling yellow color of the nanomaterial was obtained so that a wider linear range for I- may be obtained, and was thus selected. The reaction kinetics of 100.0 μM Ag+ charging on TG-AuNPs-QT is shown in Fig. 5B, where a progressive increment in the absorption spectra was noted from 0.5 min with a peak at about 10 min, as shown in Fig. 5C (red rectangle). This shows the impact of 100.0 μM Ag+ injection into the TG-AuNPs-QT probe, stabilized at about 10 mins. Thus, the incubation time for the detection system was fixed at 10 min.

Sensitivity

Analytical detection sensitivity is defined as a measure of the detection probe response to a slight change in analytes concentration. It is the wish of analysts to realize a detection system that can respond to even analytes small or low concentrations. Using 100.0 μM Ag+ concentration, the prior mixing of varying concentrations of I- (0.0 to 125.0 μM), The sharp yellow color induced on TG-AuNPs-QT in the presence of 100.0 μM Ag+, progressively diminished (Fig. 5D inset), while the absorption spectra intensity reduced and red shifted until the emergence of sharp AuNPs peaks at 521 nm (Fig. 5D). This is attributed to the high binding affinity between Ag+ and I-, forming AgI complex in solution, as reflected by their low solubility products (Ksp = 8.3 ˟ 10−17 Mol2L−2) (Kang et al., 2019) according to the soft acid property of Ag+ (Shoeib et al., 2001) and I− as a soft base, using the hard-soft-acid-base (HSAB) theory (Zhang et al., 2018). The analytical response Ao-Ai@405 versus I- concentrations is plotted over the full I- concentration range as shown in Fig. 5E, revealed consistent increment in the response axis as I- concentrations. The plot was linear within 1.0–65.0 μM, and the regression equation fitted perfectly into Ao-Ai@405 = 0.0068[I−] + 0.0452, with r2 value of 0.9942 (Fig. 5E). Ao is the absorbance of the detection system in the absence of Ag+ and Ai is the absorbance values of the various I− concentrations charged. The limit of detection (LOD) was estimated using the equation, 3α/S where, and the limit of quantification (LOQ) 10α/S, where α is the standard deviation of the regression from calibration plot and S is the slope of the plot. The calculated LOD was 0.25 μM or 250 nM, and LOQ estimated value was 0.82 μM. The estimated analytical strength of the TG-AuNPs-QT probe for I− detection was compared with reported methods, as shown in Table S3. The fabricated TG-AuNPs-QT showed impressive LOD and wide linear range, suitable for I− determination in real samples. On comparing the LOD, linear range and the synthesis protocol of the developed detection probe with reported studies (Table S3), the LOD achieved (0.25 μM), is close to other AuNPs derived, colorimetric detection probes reported (0.20 μM based on AuNBPs/CTAB, 0.29 μM based on Au—Pt bimetallic nanoparticles). It is relevant to mention that AuNBPs, synthesis involves the use of toxic sodium borohydride (Liu et al., 2023), the present TG-AuNPs-QT probe is eco-friendly all out, using biopolymer and phenolic compounds, for nanomaterial fabrication step. The detection time (10 min) in the present work is faster than other reported AuNPs based assays for I- detection, in Table S3. For example, the assay time for the AuNBPs/CTAB probe is 20 min. While electrochemical methods are equally an attractive detection technique, the complexity in preparation of the materials on electrode surfaces and the handling of the electrochemical workstations may limit the widespread practical application of the developed methods. Moreover, the possibility of auto-fluorescence and the use of toxic reagents during preparation sets notable challenges and limitations on the fluorescence-based methods. It is worth mentioning also that analytical fluorimeters are much more expensive than a UV–Vis machine, hence the read-out device used in the present work, makes it more easily available for wider applications since the machine is one of the most commonly available equipment in most research laboratories.

Mechanism of I− detection based on TG-AuNPs-QT

QT functionalization on TG-AuNPs ensured the enrichment of the nanoparticles with multitudinous highly reductive polyphenolic compound, which are firmly held together in solution by TG polymeric structures. The realized TG-AuNPs-QT was then applied as optical probe for I− detection. As can be seen in Fig. 6A, the addition of Ag+ into the TG-AuNPs-QT under NaOH adjusted reaction environment leads to rapid in-situ reduction of Ag+ to its nanoparticles, owing to the high reductive efficiency of QT in aqueous environment after NaOH based pH adjustment. The formed AgNPs, immediately imparted distinct color change to the initial TG-AuNPs-QT probe forming Au@Ag nanostructure (Fig. 6A-section I), with yellowish color and absorption spectra characteristic of AgNPs. However, when Ag+ and I- were premixed before the addition of TG-AuNPs-QT probe in presence of NaOH support, the high binding interaction of Ag+ and I− results in the depletion of free Ag+. Consequently, in-situ AgNPs formation is prevented or obstructed depending on the analyte concentration, thus the nanomaterial exists as red color at high I− concentration (Fig. 6A-section II), depending on I− concentration injected. The proposed mechanism was further substantiated through TEM images collation of the reaction mixture under I− concentrations at 0.0, 50.0 and 125.0 μM, as shown in Fig. 6B, C and D respectively. As depicted, the TEM images show highly dispersed particles, which confirmed the detection strategy is not based on particles aggregation mechanism, that has been mostly exploited for AuNPs based analytical detections. Furthermore, the hydrodynamic diameter (Dh) from DLS shows progressive particles reduction as I- ions concentration increased from 0.0 μM, or blank sample in the presence of Ag+ only, with Dh of 49.7 ± 2.4 nm (Fig. 6B-DLS result), to I− at 50.0 μM, Dh value of 42.7 ± 3.2 nm (Fig. 6C-DLS result) and I− at 125.0 μM, Dh value of 39.1 ± 2.8 nm (Fig. 6D-DLS result). The above results confirmed the reduction of AgNPs shell (which is directly related to the particle sizes) that is deposited on AuNPs, as I− is injected in the reaction system.

Precision and selectivity

The precision of the TG-AuNPs-QT probe towards I- detection was demonstrated through charging different concentrations of I- on the probe, then collecting the absorption spectra and the corresponding phot images. Precision in method development is expressed generally as reproducibility and repeatability, using the relative standard deviation (%RSD). As shown in (Fig.S9 and 10) I- Concentrations at 20.0 and 80.0 μM were collected on the same day, n = 7 (intra-day precision), while the blank was collected as reference. The absorption spectra shows that there are no major differences in the collected spectra, while the photo images (Fig.S9 and 10-inset), can be clearly identified as same. The same concentrations were repeated over three consecutive days (inter-day precision). The RSD obtained were (2.3 & 3.2 %) for intra-day and (3.1 & 4.1 %) for inter-day precision. This shows the detection strategy is highly reproducible. Further the probe sensitivity after 4 °C storage in the refrigerator, over time was tested to I− detection at 80.0 μM, shows reliable response over 2 months (Fig.S11). This shows ones the TG-AuNPs-QT probe is synthesized, it can be stored in the refrigerator over 2 months without losing its sensitivity to I− ion detection, once Ag+ and NaOH solution are freshly prepared.

Selectivity is another important analytical parameter of great importance in method development. The selectivity was demonstrated by charging different anions on the detection TG-AuNPs-QT probe, under the previous optimal detection conditions for I-, then collating the absorption spectra and the photo images. Accordingly, I- was maintained at 80.0 μM, while the possible interfering anions were fixed at 10-fold higher concentrations, except Br-, which was fixed at 2.5-fold that of I-. As detailed in Fig.S12, the absorption spectrum plummeted significantly, only in the presence of I-, while other tested species did not effect significant impact on the detection system (Fig.S12, 1–14). This is owing to the high binding interaction between Ag+ and I− over other halides or anions. An additional sample to test the response of I- when in contact with other interfering anions was labelled All. This contained the concentration of single interfering anions mixed with I-. As seen, the All sample demonstrated similar response to I− alone (Fig-S12-All line), confirming the high sensitivity and specificity to I- over the tested anions (David et al., 2021). The photo images (Fig.S12 inset), show only I- and All sample effected red color on the detection system, while others show yellow color. The plot of Ao-Ai@405 against tested anions as shown in Fig.S13, revealed that, only the I- and All samples gave response values that is significantly different from other anions (P ≥ 0.05). This confirms the preferential binding of I− to Ag+ in comparison to other anions, even in simulated scenarios where I− co-existed with other anions. While it is common for selectivity test to be conducted only on diverse anions during I- detection method development ((Li et al., 2024)), it must be mentioned that owing to the diversity of real samples matrices, such as proteins-rich food samples, the inclusion of common amino acids in food samples, as possible interfering species is recommended.

The summarized analytical features of the TG-AuNPs-QT probe for detection I- is shown in Table 1. Accordingly, the obtained parameter in the present study is within acceptable range with reference to the AOAC, 2016 guidelines for standard method performance requirements, considering analyte (I− concentration) less than 150.0 μM or 0.001 %.

Real sample application of TG-AuNPs@QT for I- detection

The real sample application of the TG-AuNPs-QT probe was demonstrated for I- detection in tap water and food sample (Kelp powder), after sample preparations, from Section 2.6. The processed samples were charged on the detection system and were equally spiked with standard I- and the recoveries were estimated. The absorption spectra/photo images of the real samples and the spiked I− concentrations are shown in Fig.S14 and S15 for tap water samples and Kelp samples respectively. As shown, the yellowish color of the TG-AuNPs-QT in the presence of Ag+, the absorption spectrum remained almost unchanged for the unspiked Tap water sample (Fig.S14-b), in comparison to the blank, confirming that I− was not detected in the Tap water sample. However, the absorption spectra diminished as I− concentrations were spiked at 10.0 and 40.0 μM, which was correlated to the photo images (Fig.S14-onset). Thus, the detection strategy could respond to I− ion in water sample. The practical applications of the TG-AuNPs-QT was further extended to I− detection in food sample (Kelp powder), as shown in Fig.S15. Interestingly, the addition of Kelp powder sample after sample preparation (Fig.S15-b), showed significant perturbation in the absorption spectra in comparison to the blank (Fig.S15-a), validating that the kelp sample is rich in I-. After I- spiking on the kelp sample, progressive absorption spectra reduction was observed (Fig.S15-c and d), which was well related to the samples photo images (Fig.S15-inset). Table 2 summarized the I- detection in Tap water or kelp sample and was compared to the results from ICP-MS. Accordingly, the recoveries ranged between 98.6 and 104.9 %, which is within the acceptable recovery range (80–110 %), according to the AOAC, 2016 guidelines. The application of the probe was further extended to I- detection in diverse food samples, including, wheat fluor, sweet potato, Yard long bean, spinach stem, dried prunes, Egg yolk, Cheese sausage, powder milk, shrimp and Wakame seaweed. The sample preparation steps have been detailed in Section 2.6, however, adequate caution must be taken in the choice of sample digestion method to apply, so that the analyte integrity is preserved without loss. For example, Plaisen et al., 2018 used muffle furnace digestion at 600 °C, for organic components removal in rice samples for bromide ion (Br−) detection (Plaisen et al., 2018). Lixia et al., 2023 applied food samples digestion in a digestion tube at 400 °C, for 1 h, in I− detection (Lixia et al., 2023), while microwave digestion of food samples, for I− detection was adopted by Zhong et al., (Zhong, X., Li, C., Chen, H., & Deng, P. (2024). The foregoing confirm that samples digestion at 250 °C in a high temperature oven, may not induce analyte volatilization and loss. However, strict monitoring of the digestion process is advised, especially in highly sticky samples such as dried prunes, egg yolk, where discomforting smokes may saturate the room. Digested samples were prepared, diluted and charged on the TG-AuNPs-QT in the presence of Ag+ under optimal conditions, after which the absorption spectra/photo images were collected, as shown in Fig. S16. The obtained absorption spectra (n = 3) were converted to I- concentrations using the calibration plot equation. Significant spectra shift in comparison to the blank were observed in Wakame seaweed sample, shrimp, powder milk, dried prune, egg, while other samples exhibited slight changes, depicting varying iodide concentrations in food samples.

Conclusion

Herein, we showcased the exploration of TG biopolymer, a popular food addictive, as an environmentally friendly material for the synthesis of AuNPs. TG served as the reductant of gold salt and the stabilizer of the realized nanomaterial. The fabricated TG-AuNPs was characterized in detail, using relevant analytical techniques, to showcase the role of TG polymer network in housing AuNPs, thus providing exceedingly high stability to the realized TG-AuNPs. For a more robust, sensitive and reproducible detection method for I-, TG-AuNPs was further functionalized with Quercetin (QT), to obtain TG-AuNPs-QT nanoprobe, which was then applied as optical detection probe for I-. The detection strategy exploited the high binding interaction between Ag+ and I−, such that Ag+ was used as TG-AuNPs-QT modulating species. The addition of Ag+ imparted notable naked eye observable color and absorption spectra perturbations on TG-AuNPs-QT probe from red to yellow/ absorption maximum blue shift, while pre-mixing of Ag+ and I− before the detection probe addition resulted in reversal of Ag+ changes on TG-AuNPs-QT probe. This is because of high binding interaction between Ag+ and I− to form AgI complex in solution. Consequently I- was detected within 10 min, showing linear range within 1.0–65.0 μM, and an estimated LOD of 250 nM and LOQ of 0.82 μM. The TG-AuNPs-QT probe was successfully applied to I- detection in Tap water and kelp sample showing satisfactory recoveries, while the practicality was further extended to I- detection in various food samples and was compared to the results obtained using an ICP-MS, for method validation, with acceptable relative errors. The need for carefully monitoring of samples digestion step, probe's pH sensitivity, controlled storage condition are surfacing limitations which must be improved in future works. Moreover, the complexity of real sample matrices (food samples), must be tested to improve the method robustness and reliability for the analyte detection.

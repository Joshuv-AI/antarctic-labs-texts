LINC00341 promotes fracture healing through downregulation of miR-133a-3p
Authors: Shen Chen, Longhui Xu, Bin Cheng, Qiang Li, Lin Li, Shenyi Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to investigate the role of LINC00341 in delayed fracture healing (DFH) and its regulatory mechanism through miR-133a-3p.MethodsThis study analyzed serum samples from 131 patients with tibial fractures, including 67 patients with normal fracture healing (NFH) and 64 with DFH. The levels of LINC00341, miR-133a-3p and osteogenic marker-related mRNAs were verified by RT-qPCR assays. Cell viability and apoptosis were detected by CCK-8 and flow cytometry. Dual-luciferase reporter and RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assays were performed to confirm the binding between LINC00341 and miR-133a-3p.ResultsThe expression of LINC00341 was found to be downregulated in DFH patients. This downregulation of LINC00341 inhibited the mRNA expression of osteogenic markers, reduced osteoblast viability, and increased apoptosis in MC3T3-E1 cells. Additionally, miR-133a-3p was identified as a target miRNA of LINC00341. Reducing miR-133a-3p effectively eliminated the negative impact of LINC00341 downregulation on osteoblast proliferation and differentiation.ConclusionLINC00341 enhances fracture healing by targeting miR-133a-3p, indicating its potential as a potential target for clinical diagnosis and therapy.Keywords: LINC00341, miR-133a-3p, Fracture healing, Osteogenic differentiation

Background

Fractures are a prevalent form of traumatic injury in clinical practice, with tibial fractures being particularly common [1]. In the majority of instances, bone structure can undergo complete restoration owing to the remarkable regenerative potential of bone tissue. Nevertheless, approximately 10% of fracture patients experience impaired healing processes [2]. Bone healing constitutes a sophisticated physiological and metabolic cascade, precisely regulated by hormones, immune function, and therapeutic approaches at the tissue, cellular, and molecular levels [3, 4]. Notwithstanding the advancements achieved in fracture healing therapy, the present success rate still stays at a comparatively low level. Traditional interventions for fracture healing, such as surgical fixation and pharmacological support, have shown limited efficacy in complex cases [5], emphasizing the need to explore underlying molecular mechanisms for novel therapeutic strategies. Therefore, there is an urgent need to deeply explore the molecular mechanisms of DFH to enable early intervention and reduce the incidence of poor fracture healing.

Bone regeneration, as a sophisticated multi-phasic process, involves non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) play a pivotal role. Accumulating evidence indicates that ncRNAs are involved in the regulation of skeletal disorders via diverse biological mechanisms [6–10]. Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), as non-protein-coding molecules, participate in bone formation, differentiation, and healing via diverse molecular pathways [11–13]. Recent investigations increasingly reveal that lncRNAs modulate the differentiation and functionality of skeletal cells [14]. Through RNA sequencing analysis, a total of 1,331 predicted lncRNAs were detected across the osteogenic differentiation trajectory [15]. Yang et al. have demonstrated that LINC00341 is downregulated in osteoarthritis patient tissues and cultured osteoarthritis chondrocytes [16]. The overexpression of LINC00341 induced an upregulation of typical osteoblastic genes [17]. Despite advancements, the molecular basis of delayed fracture healing remains unclear, and currently, there are no effective strategies to promote accelerated bone healing.

Drawing from the aforementioned investigations, we hypothesized that LINC00341 may play a role in DFH. To validate this hypothesis, we enrolled patients with fractures and analyzed the predictive value of LINC00341 for delayed healing. Additionally, we performed in vitro experiments to explore potential mechanisms. Our research may offer a molecular basis for treating delayed bone healing.

Materials and methods

Participants

The study recruited 131 patients with tibial fractures who received treatment at Zhangzhou Zhengxing Hospital, comprising 67 patients with NFH and 64 patients with DFH. All participants were aged 18 and older and had no history of previous fractures or osteoporosis. Informed consent was obtained from each patient involved in the study, which was approved by the ethics committee of Zhangzhou Zhengxing Hospital. All research procedures adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki.

Cultivation of cells

MC3T3-E1 cells were cultured in DMEM medium. The cells were incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO₂, and the complete medium was replaced every 48 h.

Plasmid construction and transfection of cells

The transfection vectors, including si-LINC00341, miR-133a-3p mimics, miR-133a-3p inhibitors, and their respective negative controls were purchased from RiboBio (China). When MC3T3-E1 cells were cultured to the logarithmic growth phase, various vectors were transfected using Lipofectamine 3000 transfection reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The culture medium was refreshed at 6-hour intervals, and the cells were continuously cultured for subsequent experimental collection.

RNA extraction and RT-qPCR

Total RNA was isolated using TRIzol reagent. RT-qPCR analysis was performed on an MX3000p Real-time PCR instrument. GAPDH and U6 served as internal reference genes for LINC00341 and miR-133a-3p, respectively. The mRNA expression levels of osteogenic marker-related proteins in the cells were detected.

Cell viability assay

Transfected cells were seeded into culture plates, and cell viability was assessed using the Counting-8 (CCK-8) assay kit (Dojindo, Japan) at 0, 24, 48, and 72 h of culture, respectively.

Cell apoptosis assay

Transfected cells were rinsed with PBS. Then 10 µl of Annexin V-FITC and 10 µl of PI were added to the cells, which were incubated for 30 min under dark conditions. Finally, flow cytometry was employed to quantify apoptotic cells.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

Fragments of LINC00341 harboring the miR-133a-3p binding site were inserted into pcDNA3.1 vectors. These vectors were then co-transfected into MC3T3-E1 cells. After 48 h post-transfection, the cells were analyzed using the Dual Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, Wisconsin, USA) to measure luciferase activity.

RNA Immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay

RIP assays were performed in accordance with the protocols provided in the Magna RIP Kit (Millipore, USA). MC3T3-E1 cells were harvested and lysed, followed by incubation with magnetic beads that were coated with anti-Ago2 antibody or rabbit anti-IgG antibody (as a negative control) to form immune complexes. The magnetic beads were subsequently washed to eliminate impurities, and RNA was isolated using Trizol reagent. The LINC00341 and miR-133a-3p levels were then quantified by RT-qPCR.

Statistical analysis

This study used SPSS 23.0 and GraphPad Prism 9.0 for statistical analysis. Student’s t-test was used to analyze the differences between two group. Analysis of variance (ANOVA) was conducted to assess differences among multiple groups. The predictive value for delayed tibial fracture healing was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. P < 0.05.

Our analysis demonstrated no significant differences between the two groups regarding age, body mass index (BMI), gender, fracture side, smoking, and alcohol consumption (P > 0.05, Table 1). Analysis of general information on patients with fracture healing

RT-qPCR analysis revealed that DFH patients had lower serum LINC00341 expression compared to NFH patients (P < 0.0001, Fig. 1A). LINC00341 could effectively distinguish between NFH and DFH suffers with high diagnostic significance, as evidenced by ROC curves (sensitivity: 92.19%, specificity: 74.63%, AUC = 0.875, Fig. 1B).

Expression and prediction of LINC00341 in DFH (A) Relative expression of LINC00341 in serum samples from patients with NFH (n = 67) and DFH (n = 64). (B) ROC curve analysis evaluating the diagnostic potential of LINC00341 for DFH. **** P < 0.0001

Silencing LINC00341 affects fracture healing by inhibiting osteoblast proliferation and promoting apoptosis

si-LINC00341 was transfected into MC3T3-E1 osteoblasts to down-regulate LINC00341 levels. The expression of LINC00341 significantly decreased after transfecting MC3T3-E1 cells with si-LINC00341, while no change was observed with si-NC (P < 0.0001, Fig. 2A). Moreover, inhibition of LINC00341 expression suppressed cell viability and enhanced the apoptosis rate in MC3T3-E1 cells (P < 0.0001, Fig. 2B-C). Furthermore, silencing of LINC00341 inhibited the expression of osteogenic differentiation markers such as alkaline phosphatase (ALP), osteocalcin (OCN), collagen I and runt-related transcription factor 2 (RUNX2) mRNA (P < 0.0001, Fig. 2D). The results indicate that silencing LINC00341 may negatively impact the fracture healing process by inhibiting cell proliferation and promoting apoptosis, suggesting that it could be a significant factor contributing to delayed fracture healing.

Regulation of osteoblast activity by abnormal expression of LINC00341. Effects of silencing of LINC00341 on mRNA expression of LINC00341 (A), cell viability (B), apoptosis (C), and osteogenic marker-related mRNA (D). n = 3 independent experiments, **** P < 0.0001

LINC00341 targeted miR-133a-3p

An online bioinformatics prediction tool identified miR-133a-3p as the target microRNA for LINC00341, with its binding site depicted in Fig. 3A. To examine the targeting relationship, dual-luciferase reporter gene assays were performed. The results showed that miR-133a-3p mimics reduced luciferase activity in LINC00341-WT cells (P < 0.001, Fig. 3B). In contrast, the luciferase activity in LINC00341-MUT cells was no affected by the levels of miR-133a-3p (Fig. 3B). RIP assays provided further confirmation of the interaction between LINC00341 and miR-133a-3p (P < 0.0001, Fig. 3C). In the control group, the enrichment levels of both LINC00341 and miR-133a-3p were low. However, treatment with an anti-Ago2 antibody significantly increased the enrichment level of LINC00341, suggesting that it binds to the Ago2 protein. Similarly, the enrichment level of miR-133a-3p was also significantly, indicating its binding to the Ago2 protein as well. This evidence supports the conclusion that both LINC00341 and miR-133a-3p can bind to the Ago2 protein, further affirming their interaction within the RNA interference pathway (Fig. 3C).

LINC00341 targeted miR-133a-3p. (A). The predicted binding site. (B) Dual-luciferase reporter assay. (C) RIP assays. n = 3 independent experiments, **** P < 0.0001

LINC00341 alleviates cell inhibition and apoptosis through miR-133a-3p regulation

RT-qPCR assays revealed that miR-133a-3p expression was approximately double in si-LINC00341-treated group compared to control group. In contrast, treatment with miR-133a-3p inhibitor led to a decrease in miR-133a-3p expression, which approximated the control level (P < 0.0001, Fig. 4A). Additionally, the miR-133a-3p inhibitor could reverse the decrease in cell viability and the increase in apoptotic cells rate caused by silencing LINC00341 (P < 0.001, Fig. 4B-C). Furthermore, si-LINC00341 treatment resulted in a significant reduction in the mRNA expression levels of ALP, OCN, Collagen I, and RUNX2, while the miR-133a-3p inhibitor partially restored the expression of these genes (P < 0.0001, Fig. 4D).

Discussion

Fracture healing encompasses multiple phases, including the inflammatory response, neovascularization, and osteogenesis. Various individual factors also modulate the healing process [18]. Delayed healing occurs when a fracture does not heal within a normal timeframe, with a higher incidence observed in fractures of the tibia, neck of the femur, and navicular bone [19]. This condition may increase the risk of refracture and even lead to disability in severe cases. This trend has spurred an increasing in a growing number of studies focusing on the molecular mechanisms behind DFH. Exploring serological indicators that can predict delayed fracture healing is crucial for guiding timely clinical interventions and optimizing patient outcomes. In recent years, lncRNAs have received much attention in predicting the prognosis of fracture surgery [20, 21]. Given the special anatomical location of the tibia and the characteristics of its surrounding tissues, the incidence of delayed healing is relatively high. Therefore, an in-depth investigation into the mechanisms by which lncRNAs influence fracture healing will bring significant benefits to clinical practice.

This study demonstrated a significantly higher serum expression of LINC00341 in NFH patients compared to DFH patients. This finding is consistent with the observations of Yang et al., who reported that LINC00341 expression is down-regulated in chondrocytes from osteoarthritis patients and is associated with chondrocyte apoptosis and cartilage destruction [16]. The higher LINC00341 expression observed in the NFH group may be associated with its role in promoting fracture healing, whereas the lower expression in the DFH group may be associated with delayed healing. The down-regulation of LINC00341 may delay the healing process by affecting the key processes such as cell proliferation, migration and differentiation. Additionally, several studies have suggested that LINC00341 may serve as a potential clinical biomarker for prognosis [22, 23]. In this study, ROC curve analysis showed that the expression level of LINC00341expression had high diagnostic accuracy (AUC = 0.875, sensitivity 92.19%, specificity 74.63%). Similar to other serum non-coding RNAs or metabolic parameters identified as diagnostic markers for delayed fracture healing [24, 25], LINC00341 shows high accuracy in distinguishing NFH from DFH, supporting its potential as a clinical indicator.

We investigated the role of LINC00341 in osteogenic differentiation. Using si-RNA-mediated knockdown of LINC00341, we observed a significant decrease in cell viability and an increase in the apoptotic cell rate. These findings suggest that LINC00341 is crucial for maintaining cell viability and inhibiting apoptosis. Additionally, the interfering of LINC00341reduced the mRNA levels of ALP, OCN, Collagen I, and RUNX2, which are key players in osteoblast differentiation and function [26]. These results indicate that LINC00341 plays a vital role in osteogenic differentiation by promoting cell viability, inhibiting apoptosis, and regulating osteogenic gene expression. Our findings not only deepen our understanding of the function of LINC00341 but also offer new potential targets for treating delayed bone healing.

Recent studies have further identified that specific ncRNAs modulate osteogenic differentiation and bone healing through targeting key regulatory genes, highlighting their potential as therapeutic targets in skeletal disorders [27, 28]. lncRNAs exert a pivotal influence in fracture healing by regulating microRNAs (miRNAs). For instance, a specific lncRNA can promote the healing of femoral neck fractures by inhibiting miRNA-19a-3p [29]. LINC00339 can target and negatively regulate miR-16-5p, and affect fracture healing by regulating osteoblast function [30]. Additionally, lncRNA Rhno1 regulates osteoblast differentiation by modulating the interaction between miR-6979-5p and bone morphogenetic protein 2 (BMP2), which is vital for fracture healing [31]. Numerous studies have demonstrated that miR-133a-3p can participate in osteoblast differentiation. Similarly, miR-1271-5p has been shown to be upregulated in patients with delayed pilon fracture healing, where it regulates MC3T3-E1 cell activities by targeting ZBTB7A, further supporting the role of miRNAs as key regulators in fracture healing processes [32]. Overexpression of miR-133a-3p attenuated the expression of osteogenesis-related genes (Runx2, Osterix, ALP, and bone bridging proteins) in bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs), and reduced ALP activity and mineralization capacity [33]. Also, miR-133a-3p expression were upregulated in BMSCs derived from SAMP6 mice (a model of senile osteoporosis). miR-133a-3p overexpression inhibited osteogenic differentiation of odontoblasts, whereas knockdown of miR-133a-3p reversed the effect [34]. In this study, we first validated the targeted binding relationship between LINC00341 and miR-133a-3p through dual-luciferase reporter assays and RIP experiments. Additionally, our experimental results demonstrated that the miR-133a-3p inhibitor could mitigate the effects of LINC00341 silencing on cell viability and apoptosis. Meanwhile, in the detection of osteogenic differentiation markers, inhibiting miR-133a-3p reversed the effects caused by LINC00341 silencing. Collectively, these findings suggest that LINC00341 regulates the expression of osteogenic differentiation-related genes by suppressing miR-133a-3p.

Briefly, this study explored the pivotal function of LINC00341 in fracture healing and its underlying molecular mechanisms. LINC00341 expression was downregulated in DFH patient, and it regulated osteoblast activity by sponging miR-133a-3p. Decreased LINC00341 levels inhibited osteoblast proliferation, promoted apoptosis, and delayed fracture healing, whereas miR-133a-3p inhibitors reversed the effects of LINC00341 silencing. Recent studies have explored novel interventions for fracture healing, such as targeting specific molecular pathways to enhance osteogenesis [35]. Importantly, prior research has emphasized that translating molecular findings into clinical practice requires rigorous validation of targets in preclinical and clinical settings [36], which underscores the need for further studies to confirm the therapeutic potential of LINC00341/miR-133a-3p.These findings offer potential molecular candidates for promoting fracture healing and clinically predicting/treating delayed fracture healing.

This study has certain limitations that should be noted. First, all clinical samples were collected from a single center (Zhangzhou Zhengxing Hospital), which may introduce regional population bias due to potential differences in genetic background, lifestyle, and clinical management practices among different populations. Therefore, the generalizability of our findings needs to be validated by multi-center studies with larger and more diverse cohorts. Second, our in vitro experiments were conducted using the MC3T3-E1 osteoblastic cell line, which, while a widely used model for osteogenic research, cannot fully replicate the complex in vivo microenvironment of fracture healing. This microenvironment involves intricate interactions between various cell types (e.g., endothelial cells, immune cells), extracellular matrix components, and signaling molecules, as well as processes like angiogenesis and inflammatory responses that are critical for successful bone repair. Thus, future in vivo studies using animal fracture models are warranted to further confirm the regulatory role of LINC00341/miR-133a-3p in fracture healing and validate its potential as a therapeutic target.

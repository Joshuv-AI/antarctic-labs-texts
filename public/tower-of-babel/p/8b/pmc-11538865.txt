Oral supplementation with lactic acid bacteria improve the intestinal epithelial barrier and gut microbiota of broiler chicks to alleviate Salmonella Enteritidis infection
Authors: Yuting Yang, Jiajun Hong, Zheng Zhang, Minghao Zheng, Jingang Zhao, Xiaowei Fang, Xiongyan Liang, Jing Liu, Yuying Yang, Guangming Tian, Chun Fang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Lactic acid bacteria (LAB) play a key role in regulating the balance of gut microbiota and serve as a suitable alternative to antibiotics. This study aims to evaluate the characteristics of 2 LAB isolates Lactiplantibacillus plantarum Lp71 (L. plantarum Lp71) and Enterococcus faecium Ef72 (E. faecium Ef72), and their roles in alleviating Salmonella Enteritidis infection. Sixty 1-day-old chicks were randomly divided into 4 groups which treated with or without L. plantarum Lp71 and E. faecium Ef72 mixture for 21 d, and then intestinal samples were collected for gut microbiota analysis, pathological and immunohistochemical analysis at 24 h post infection with or without Salmonella Enteritidis on the 22nd d. The results showed that L. plantarum Lp71 and E. faecium Ef72 had the ability to anti-acid and anti-bile salt. Salmonella Enteritidis infection damaged the intestinal epithelial barrier and reduced the expression level of tight junction proteins (ZO-1, Claudin-1, Occludin). Oral supplementation with L. plantarum Lp71 and E. faecium Ef72 mixture could alleviated the damages to intestinal epithelial barrier by Salmonella Enteritidis infection. Salmonella Enteritidis could cause abnormal Akkermansia muciniphila proliferation and decrease the diversity of cecal microbiota in chicks. These conditions could have further led to reduce gut microbiota health index (GMHI), and improve microbial dysbiosis index (MDI). Moreover, oral supplementation with L. plantarum Lp71 and E. faecium Ef72 mixture could effectively prevent the aforementioned infection outcomes and increase the abundance proportions of the several key functions in metabolic pathways metabolic pathways such as transcription and signal transduction mechanisms. In summary, L. plantarum Lp71 and E. faecium Ef72 could be the probiotics candidates that used to prevent the damage from enteric pathogens such as Salmonella Enteritidis in broiler chicks.

INTRODUCTION

Salmonella subsp. enterica Enteritidis (Salmonella Enteritidis) is one of the most important pathogens and has serious effects on poultry, such as high chick mortality and decreased egg production in adult chickens (Meijerink et al., 2022). Antibiotics are commonly fed to poultry as antibiotic growth promoters and infectious disease control methods (Selim et al., 2024). Salmonella Kentucky ST 198 is widely prevalent in some countries or regions due to its antibiotic resistance (Liu et al., 2024). The rapid growth of the poultry industry has been accompanied by issues such as antibiotic residues and resistance due to the overuse of antibiotics, posing significant risks to both poultry and consumers (Selim et al., 2024).

To reduce or replace the use of antibiotics, the development of probiotics, such as lactic acid bacteria (LAB), has gained significant attention. Numerous studies have shown that optimizing gut microbiota is a key strategy to alleviate food-borne pathogen infections. Short chain fatty acids regulate the gut microbiota by promoting beneficial microbes to mitigate pathogen infections (Morrison and Preston, 2016). Adding the prebiotic Galacto-oligosaccharides (GOS) to the diet accelerates the eradication of Salmonella in the gut (Richards et al., 2024). Meijerink found that long-chain glucomannan (GM) reduces Salmonella Enteritidis infection by increasing the relative abundance of intestinal lactic acid bacteria (Meijerink et al., 2022). Animal intestinal beneficial microbiota can improve the morphology of intestinal mucosa and promote immune response (Hussein et al., 2020). And the active substances produced can inhibit the growth of harmful pathogens (Yosi and Metzler-Zebeli, 2023; Selim et al., 2024). The use of LAB is an effective strategy to reduce pathogenic microorganism infections and improve food safety. LAB secrete lactic acid and acetic acid, which exert competitive inhibition against pathogenic microorganisms such as Escherichia coli, Salmonella, and Helicobacter pylori (Shi et al., 2017). Multiple studies have shown that LAB have a beneficial impact on the diversity of the gut microbiota and the maintenance of a healthy balance (Dinan and Cryan, 2017; Ma et al., 2022). At present, the mainstream speculation about the action mechanism is that probiotics and their metabolites will lead to changes in the structure of microbiota in the host, and then affect the ability to resist pathogenic microorganisms.

To enhance the methods for the prevention and control of animal diseases, it is of great significance to study the role of probiotics in balancing gut microbiota. The purpose of this study is to evaluate the effects of mixtures of Lp71 and Ef72 on preventing Salmonella Enteritidis from damaging the intestinal epithelial barrier and disrupting the gut microbiota in broiler chicks. The results of this study hold significant theoretical importance for advancing research on the role of LAB in intestinal health and for the prevention and control of infection caused by Salmonella Enteritidis.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Experimental Animals and Bacterial Strains

A total of 60 one-day-old broilers (average BW 40±5g) were purchased from the Hubei Yukou Poultry Industry Co., Ltd in Jingzhou, China. Salmonella Enteritidis CGMCC50760 was cultured with LB medium at 37 °C, and the strain was kindly provided by Professor Weihuan Fang from the Laboratory of Molecular Microbiology and Food Safety, College of Animal Sciences, Zhejiang University. The isolated strains Lactiplantibacillus plantarum Lp71 (L. plantarum Lp71) and Enterococcus faecium Ef72 (E. faecium Ef72) were cultured with MRS medium at 37 °C. These strains were stored at -80 °C with liquid medium mentioned above, which contains a final concentration of 15% glycerol in the Animal Pathogenic Microbiology Laboratory, College of Animal Science and Technology, Yangtze University.

Identification and Characteristic Evaluation of Probiotics

The 16S rRNA of L. plantarum Lp71 and E. faecium Ef72 were amplified by polymerase chain reaction (PCR), respectively. After sequencing, the 16S rRNA sequences of L. plantarum Lp71 and E. faecium Ef72 were blasted in NCBI, and the phylogenetic trees were constructed by MEGA11, respectively. The growth ability of 2 probiotic strains was assessed by the growth curve. The overnight-cultured strains were inoculated into fresh MRS medium at a 1:100 dilution, and the OD600 nm value was measured every hour using a microplate reader. The resistance ability of L. plantarum Lp71 and E. faecium Ef72 to acid stress and bile salt stress was tested using a survival assay with pH 2.5 MRS broth or 0.5% bile salt MRS broth, as reported previously (Feng et al., 2019). Briefly, overnight-cultured bacteria were collected by centrifugation, washed once with PBS, and then resuspended in PBS. Then, 50 μL of the bacterial suspension was added to 950 μL of pH 2.5 MRS broth or MRS containing 0.5% bile salt, and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h after gently mixing by vortex. Plate counting method was used to calculate the number of bacteria before and after treatments with serial dilution. The survival rate was defined as the ratio of bacteria number after treatment to the initial bacteria number. Data are presented as mean ± SD of triplicates from 3 independent experiments.

Monitoring of Feeding Procedure and Growth of Chicks

Sixty chicks were randomly divided into 4 groups: A, B, C, and D, with 15 chicks in each group. They were fed at 32 to 34 °C and 23L:1D for 21 d. All 4 groups were regularly fed the same basic diet, and had free access to feed and drinking water every day. The chicks in groups B and D were supplemented with 1 mL of L. plantarum Lp71 and E. faecium Ef72 bacterial suspension at concentration of 1.0 × 109 CFU/mL per day per chick by oral administration as previously reported (Khan et al., 2024). The chicks in groups A and C were supplemented with 1 mL of PBS as control. Daily body weight and death of chicks were recorded, and the growth curve of chicks was drawn according to the daily body weight. On the 22nd d, every chick in groups C and D were orally administered 1mL of Salmonella Enteritidis CGMCC50760 suspension at the concentration of 106 CFU/mL, and chicks in groups A and B were given 1 mL of PBS as control. At 24 h after infection, 4 chicks in each group were randomly selected for euthanasia and sample collection under aseptic conditions. The duodenum samples were collected in 50 mL tubes containing 4% paraformaldehyde for H&E and immunohistochemical staining (Wang et al., 2024), and the contents of the cecum were collected for gut microbiota analysis. HE Staining and Immunohistochemical Technique

HE staining was used to observe the morphological changes in the small intestine. The duodenum tissue was fixed with paraformaldehyde and dehydrated, followed by paraffin embedding, sectioning into thin slices. Deparaffinization to water for paraffin sections. After treating with high-definition constant staining pretreatment solution, the sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin. The sections were then dehydrated and mounted using neutral gum.

Immunohistochemical technique was used to analyze the effects of different treatments on the expression of small intestinal tight junction proteins. After deparaffinization to water, antigen retrieval was performed on the paraffin sections, followed by blocking of endogenous peroxidase. Serum working solution was used to block non-specific binding, followed by incubation overnight at 4°C with antibodies against intestinal tight junction proteins ZO-1, Claudin-1, and Occludin. The secondary antibody used was HRP-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG. After DAB staining, the cell nuclei were counterstained with hematoxylin. The sections were then dehydrated, cleared, and mounted, followed by observation under a microscope. The protein expression intensity was quantified for the target proteins using ImageJ software.

Bioinformatics Analysis of 16S rRNA Full-Length Sequencing Data

Changes in gut microbiota were evaluated using 16S rRNA full-length sequencing. Sixteen cecal contents samples were collected and genomic DNA was extracted as a template for full-length amplification of 16S rRNA using degenerate primers containing 5′ M13 universal tail sequences (Katiraei et al., 2022). The third-generation microbial diversity was analyzed based on the PacBio sequencing platform. The genomic DNA of 16 samples was sent to Biomarker Technologies for species annotation and abundance analysis of the Effective CCS sequence. All analysis is done on BMKCloud (www.biocloud.net).

Cecal microbiota diversity analysis. According to the number of samples and the number of annotated species, the relative abundance accumulation curve of genus-level species was drawn to determine whether the sample quantity was sufficient. The Usearch software was used to cluster Reads at a 97.0% similarity level (Edgar, 2013), and a Venn diagram was drawn to visually display the number of common and unique features among each group. The Alpha diversity was analyzed by the Chao1 richness estimator and Shannon-wiener diversity index. Differences in species diversity among samples were exhibited by principal coordinates analysis (PCoA) (Dubois et al., 2010), and significant differences in beta diversity among samples of different groups were tested by Anosim analysis.

Analysis of cecal microbiota composition and structure. The characteristic sequences were annotated by taxonomy to obtain the community composition of different samples at various levels (phylum, class, order, family, genus, species). The species abundance tables at phylum, family, and species levels were drawn to analyze the microbiota changes in different samples. Line Discriminant Analysis Effect Size (LEfSe) was used to compare biomarkers that were statistically different among multiple subgroups (Segata et al., 2011). Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) was used to analyze the relative abundance of the 4 groups of samples.

Predictive analysis of gut microbiota function. Using PICRUSt2 (Phylogenetic Investigation of Communities by Reconstruction of Unobserved States), the 16S rRNA gene sequences of the samples were used to predict the functional abundance in the COG (Clusters of Orthologous Groups of proteins) functional database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/COG/).

Analysis of Gut Microbiome Health Index and Microbial Dysbiosis Index

To assess health status, a gut microbiome health index (GMHI) based on the species levels of gut microbiota samples from 16 cecal contents was measured. The GMHI aims to assess the balance between 2 groups of microbial species associated with good and poor health conditions. GMHI indicates the extent to which a chick's fecal metagenomic sample depicts taxonomic properties of micro-organisms associated with health (GMHI > 0) or unhealthy (GMHI < 0) (Gupta et al., 2020).

To determine the extent of microbiota dysregulation, a microbiota dysregulation index (MDI) was calculated for each item (Xu et al., 2020). The MDI was calculated using the following formula:MDI=lg(totalabundanceoforganismsincreasedinTreatmentgrouptotalabundanceoforganismsdecreasedinControlgroup)

The treatment groups are Group B, Group C, and Group D, with the corresponding control groups being Group A, Group A, and Group C, respectively.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism software. The T-test was used for analysis between the 2 groups. Comparisons between 2 or more groups were made using ANOVA. Analysis of similarities (Anosim) was used to analyze the similarities between multidimensional data groups. The parametric factorial Kruskal-Wallis sum-rank test was used to test the differential taxa between the 4 groups with P < 0.05, and linear discriminant analysis (LDA) was used to estimate the size of the influence of the abundance of each species on the differential effect. The ns indicated no significant difference, the symbols * and ** indicated significant difference (P < 0.05) and highly significant difference (P < 0.01), respectively.

Group A has 20 unique OTUs, Group B has 16 unique OTUs, Group C has 12 unique OTUs, and Group D has 19 unique OTUs. The data shows that the intestinal microbial species of the chicks in the 4 groups were different. Salmonella Enteritidis decreased the abundance of characteristic OTU, and feeding probiotics effectively prevented the reduction of characteristic OTU abundance. DISCUSSION

L. plantarum and E. faecalis as nutritional supplements have many beneficial effects on the healthy breeding of broilers, and L. plantarum can reduce the damage of coccidia by acting as an antioxidant and enhancing host immunity (Mohsin et al., 2022). Feeding L. plantarum can improve the metabolic activity and nutrient utilization of poultry (Gao et al., 2017). Dietary addition of Bacillus subtilis had positive effects on feed efficiency, intestinal absorption capacity, and immune-related gene expression (Soltan et al., 2024). Lei Wang evaluated that L. plantarum has good potential in enhancing growth ability and promoting intestinal integrity in broilers (Lefter et al., 2023). However, feeding the L. plantarum Lp71 and E. faecium Ef72 mixture in this study could not significantly enhance the growth ability of chicks aged 1 to 21 d (Figure 3). It may be due to the difference of feeding conditions that the colonization efficiency of LAB is different or the metabolites of strains are strictly dependent on the strains difference. We adopted the more economical method of direct feeding microorganisms (DFM), which is added to drinking water. In order to obtain the highest level of free and active probiotics, there is a need to study the packaging structure of probiotics with a balance of encapsulation and effect release efficacy (Zhang et al., 2024b).

Salmonella Enteritidis colonizes the cecum of chicks, disrupts the intestinal mucosa, and causes a significant increase in the number of intraepithelial cytotoxic T cells and splenic macrophages (Meijerink et al., 2021). The intestinal epithelial barrier is the first line of defense against invading pathogens, and maintaining its integrity is critical. The integrity of the small intestinal villi and the tight junctions between intestinal epithelial cells are important components of the intestinal barrier. Disruption of this relationship can result in intestinal inflammation, tissue damage, and nutrient loss (Li et al., 2024). Through HE and IHC analysis, we can visually observe the serious damage caused by Salmonella Enteritidis to the structure of the small intestine of chicks (Figure 4). This may be related to reduced expression levels of intestinal tightener protein. We found that administering LAB prior to a Salmonella Enteritidis challenge restored tight junction protein expression to normal levels and alleviated the damage to the small intestine structure caused by the infection. E. faecium has been shown to reduce the effects of Salmonella Enteritidis colonization in broilers by improving intestinal integrity (Olsen et al., 2022). This is consistent with our research. But in addition to assessing the integrity of the gut, the balance of the composition of the gut microbiota is more noteworthy.

It is well known that maintaining the balance of the gut microbiota is essential for preventing infections with disease-causing bacteria in the gut (Allegretti, 2014; Zhang et al., 2015). Based on the relative abundance analysis results of phylum-level species (Figure 6A), the abundance of Bacillota decreased significantly after Salmonella Enteritidis was challenged, and Verrucomicrobiota had the highest abundance proportion. Generally, Bacillota has the highest relative abundance among normal chicks (Zhang et al., 2024a). Many members of Firmicutes are beneficial bacteria, and these microorganisms produce acetate, lactate, and antimicrobial substances (Sakamoto et al., 2021; Peng et al., 2023). It can prevent pathogens from interfering with health, and the more common beneficial members Lactobacillus and Ruminococcus belong to this phylum. Verrucobacteria can break down polysaccharides (such as mucopolysaccharides and cellulose) (Orellana et al., 2022) and produce short-chain fatty acids (propionic acid, butyric acid, etc.) (Lee et al., 2021). These substances are important for gut health and the regulation of the immune system (Clemente et al., 2012). However, the excessive proportion of Verrucobacteria will reduce the diversity of other species and have adverse effects on the host. Species abundance analysis at the species level showed (Figure 6C) that Salmonella Enteritidis infection causes a relative abundance of A. muciniphila up to 50%. A. muciniphila is an important genus of symbiotic bacteria in the human gut (Zhai et al., 2018). It plays a crucial role in maintaining healthy immune processes, including promoting intestinal epithelial barrier integrity, regulating immune responses, and suppressing inflammation (Xi et al., 2020; Ghosh and Mani, 2022; Segers and de Vos, 2023). However, abnormal proliferation of Akkermansia may lead to a significant reduction in the number of non-mucus-consuming species (Zhai et al., 2018), thereby causing damage to the intestinal epithelial barrier and triggering intestinal inflammation and other related issue. The significant reduction in gut microbiota diversity caused by Salmonella Enteritidis may be attributed to the abnormal proliferation of A. muciniphila, which reduces the species diversity of other microbiota, such as a decrease in the abundance of L. johnsonii. L. johnsonii is a probiotic that can improve Escherichia coli-induced cell damage by inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome activity(Zou et al., 2020). Feeding the L. plantarum Lp71 and E. faecium Ef72 mixture before the challenge could significantly improve the decrease of bacterial diversity caused by Salmonella Enteritidis. The species distribution results at the family level (Figure 6B) showed that the L. plantarum Lp71 and E. faecium Ef72 mixture could restore the abundance of most bacterial groups, such as Oscillospiraceae and Lachnospiracea. Oscillospira has been identified as a candidate for next-generation probiotics, with the ability to produce short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) such as butyrate (Gophna et al., 2017; Yang et al., 2021). Oscillospira is significantly reduced in inflammatory bowel disease(Konikoff and Gophna, 2016), which is consistent with the findings of this study. Lachnospira is found in the intestines of most healthy individuals and is involved in the metabolism of various carbohydrates, particularly pectin (a complex dietary fiber and prebiotic). Through fermentation, it produces acetate and butyrate, providing the host with a primary energy source (Moro Cantu-Jungles et al., 2019).

Salmonella Enteritidis caused significant changes in the health of the gut microbiota (Figure 8A). The MDI results quantifying the degree of gut microbiota dysbiosis were consistent with the previous results. The addition of L. plantarum Lp71 and E. faecium Ef72 reduced gut microbiota dysbiosis, but Salmonella Enteritidis exacerbated it. The addition of LAB significantly improved the dysbiosis induced by Salmonella Enteritidis.

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that the L. plantarum Lp71 and E. faecium Ef72 mixture can enhance the intestinal epithelial barrier and gut microbiota in broiler chicks, helping to alleviate Salmonella Enteritidis infection. Salmonella Enteritidis caused severe damage to the small intestinal epithelial barrier, abnormal proliferation of A. muciniphila, and a significant reduction in cecal microbiota diversity. GMHI and MDI analyses confirm the above conclusions. The L. plantarum Lp71 and E. faecium Ef72 mixture can mitigate the adverse effects of Salmonella Enteritidis on the dysbiosis of the intestinal epithelial barrier and cecal microbiota.

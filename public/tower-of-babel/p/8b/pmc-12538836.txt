Big data dimensionality reduction-based supervised machine learning algorithms for NASH diagnosis
Authors: Onder Tutsoy, Huseyin Ali Ozturk, Hilmi Erdem Sumbul
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIdentifying the Non-Alcoholic Steatohepatitis (NASH) that can cause liver failure-based morbidity remains a challenging research problem since there is no confirmed and effective approach for its early and accurate diagnosis yet. A large amount of medical data is collected to diagnose the NASH where the majority of them are redundant.MethodsThis paper initially focuses on selecting the most informative blood test data among the collected big data with the Pearson correlation statistical approach and modified Particle Swarm Optimization with Artificial Neural Networks (PSO-ANN) machine learning algorithm. Then, a gradient based Batch Least Squares (BLS) and a search-based Artificial Bee Colony (ABC) machine learning algorithms are implemented to optimize the NASH prediction models. Confirmed operational NASH diagnosis supervise the statistical and machine learning algorithms to develop accurate prediction models.ResultsTwo machine learning algorithms were trained and also validated with the varying number of selected input features. The results yielded that the trained BLS machine learning model is able to diagnose benign and malignant cases with 100% and 98% accuracies, respectively. The trained ABC machine learning algorithm diagnoses the benign and malignant cases with 90.5% and 94.3% accuracies, respectively.Keywords: Big data, Dimension reduction, Feature selection, Prediction model, NASH disease, Supervised machine learning

Introduction

NASH has recently emerged as a significant public health threat around the world and become an intense research focus aiming to investigate new insights, novel therapeutic and diagnostic strategies to manage the disease [1]. It is estimated that between 2 and 6% of the world population has NASH, where between 21 and 50% of them have advanced fibrosis [2]. NASH is a sub-entity of the non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) which can lead to cardiovascular health problems, chronic kidney disease and certain extrahepatic cancers where the corresponding mortality rate is around 15–20 per 1000 person-years and continue to grow substantially in patients with NASH [3]. Although a great number of clinical trials has been performed in the past two decades, there is still no pharmacotherapy for the NAFLD [4]. Therefore, early and accurate diagnosis of the NASH play an important role to lower its progression to irreversible stages [5]. Liver biopsy is a common diagnostic tool, but it is unsuitable for large scale screening due to sampling error, sample inefficiency, variabilities in the observations and occurrence of adverse effects [6]. Thus, non-invasive tests such as serum and ultrasound-based methods are extensively utilized [7]. In order to accurately estimate the existence of the NASH, this paper focuses on selecting the most informative non-invasive dataset with the statistical and machine learning approaches and develops highly accurate predictive machine learning models.

Statistical approaches are extensively utilized to diagnose patients with the NASH. Lee et al. collected medical history, laboratory tests and magnetic resonance (MR) data of 127 patients and applied to well-known logistic regression model to formulate an MR elastography-based NASH scoring equation [8]. Barritt et al. evaluated the NASH severity and patient phenotypes with the chi-squared statistics and analysis of variance test which revealed that the Alanine transaminase (ALT) is the major factor that determines the liver biopsy [9]. Masarone et al. focused on differentiating the NASH patients with 307 metabolomics signatures data and implementing partial-least-square discriminant analysis [10]. Zheng et al. performed a persistent normal ALT-based univariable and multivariable logistic regression statistical analysis to distinguish the NASH and non-NASH patients [11]. It is revealed that 30.4% of the patients with persistent normal ALT has histologically confirmed NASH. Even though these statistical approaches provide basic insights about the existence and severity of the NASH, they are lack of intelligibility since they do not have a memory and they are usually sensitive to small changes in the input data.

As opposed to the statistical approaches, machine learning algorithms are capable of learning the complex patterns by mapping the input data to target output. Recently, Lee et al. trained two NASH diagnosis models with the gradient boosting algorithm whose inputs were clinical data, biomarkers and NASH grade [12]. They reported that the Area Under the Curve (AUC) score ranges between 0.72 and 0.83 and also stated that the added biomarkers do not contribute to the efficiency of the developed models. Similarly, Docherty et al. applied eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) and random forest machine learning algorithms whose inputs were 14-dimensional test data and the output was the confirmed liver NASH histology [13]. The data was imputed with the K-Nearest Neighbour (K-NN) machine learning approach where the trained prediction model produced 0.76 AUC, 0.81 sensitivity and 0.81 precision. In a further research, Yasar et al. implemented gradient-boosted Decision Tree (DT) machine learning algorithm trained with the obesity history, type 2 diabetes mellitus and metabolic disorder data [14]. The developed machine learning model yielded 0.78 AUC and 1% precision at 10% recall. Moreover, Yaghouti et al. trained a bunch of machine learning models with the Support Vector Machines (SVM), Adaptive Boosting (AdaBoost), XGBoost and Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM) [15]. A dataset of 181 patients was used and input features are selected with the chi-square and mutual information statistical approaches which yielded 0.81 accuracy, 0.86 sensitivity, 0.7 specificity, 0.81 precision and 0.83 F1 scores.

In terms of dimension reduction for the input data, Dhanka et al. applied various filter, wrapper and embedded feature selection techniques to reduce the data dimensionality and mitigate overfitting for accurate biomarker identification [16]. This research revealed that metaheuristic machine learning algorithms achieve 12–15% higher accuracies in classifying the cardiovascular, cancer, diabetes and metabolic disorders. Similarly, Kumar et al. used Genetic Algorithm (GA) to select the most informative input dataset and trained a number of traditional machine learning approaches for diagnosing thyroid disorders [17]. They reported 97% prediction accuracy with the GA based Random Forest (RF) machine learning algorithm. In addition, Kumar et al. employed a bunch of dimension reduction approaches including the Linear Regression (LR), SVM and K-NN where they are hybridized with the metaheuristic Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) approach [18]. The LR-PSO produced 96.3% accuracy, 94.4% sensitivity and 97.2% specificity. Zamanian and Shalbaf employed feature forward selection, minimum redundancy maximum relevance and mutual information approaches for the NASH data dimension reduction and trained the RF, LR and DT machine learning algorithms [19]. The RF machine learning algorithm outperformed the others by producing 81% accuracy, 82% AUC, 85% precision and 89% recall. Ghosh et al. considered twelve dimension reduction approaches including the correlation-based and embedded approaches for the NASH data dimension reduction and they are combined with the tree-type machine learning algorithms [20]. The research determined the marker genes diosmin, esculin, lapatinib and phenelzine as the most related ones. Yoghouti et al. applied the sequential forward selection, chi-square analysis of variance and mutual information approaches to reduce the dimension of the NASH data and trained the machine learning approaches [15]. The RF machine learning model with sequential forward selection approach generated the best results with 81% accuracy, 86% sensitivity, 70% specificity, 70% precision and 83% F1-score.

Although these machine learning approaches are able to predict the NASH disease, since the trained background model structures are unknown, they easily lose their effectiveness in case of the big data, uncertainties and poor data quality. Thus, the richest input data should be selected for both the training and the validation of the developed prediction models. Feature selection from the raw NASH data

This section aims to determine the most informative dataset with the Pearson correlation and PSO-ANN-based feature selection approaches for the diagnostic machine learning models. In this research, two different feature selection approaches having two distinctive properties are considered. The Pearson correlation essentially quantifies the linear relationship between each input dataset and the target output defined in Sect. "Introduction of the raw data". However, the PSO-ANN does not focus on each input dataset, it aims to select a specific number of input data among the whole input dataset by optimizing a cost function such as the sum-squared prediction error. This paper compares the performance of the statistical Pearson correlation and hybrid PSO-ANN machine learning algorithm, that can take into account the effects among the input features.

Machine learning algorithms for nash diagnosis

This section provides the BLS and ABC machine learning algorithms. The BLS is based on the one-shot learning method and therefore, it does not require a recursive process. Since it instantly utilizes whole data for unknown parameter learning, it can reduce the effects of the unknown uncertainties in the data. However, a rank-reduced basis can cause singularity in the parameter learning and to avoid such a situation, the most informative input features should be selected. In contrast to the BLS, the ABC is a swarm type bioinspired optimization algorithm which focuses on selecting the best unknown parameters by minimizing a cost function through a search process. The ABC is an effective machine learning approach in case where the data has poor gradient information and dimension of the data is small. However, it performs poorly with the uncertain and large dimensional data.

Conclusion and future works

This paper focused on handling the big data with the Pearson correlation and PSO-ANN optimization approaches. In addition, gradient-driven BLS and search-based bioinspired ABC machine learning algorithms were modified to optimize the NASH prediction models. The raw blood test data was analysed in terms of the gender and various age groups which yielded that females are more prone to NASH diseases (Fig. 2) and people are more susceptible to NASH as the age increases (Fig. 3). Pearson correlation and PSO-ANN optimization approaches selected the most informative NASH features where 5 of the 10 features were determined commonly (Table 3). In this paper, same BLS cost function given by Eq. (11) was optimized by the ABC algorithm and their performance were compared under the equal conditions. This ensured us that the two machine learning algorithms are compared under the equal conditions. The results showed that the trained BLS machine learning algorithm outperformed the trained ABC machine learning algorithm (Fig. 10). Even though the trained BLS yielded higher prediction accuracies, its validation results were poorer than the ABC algorithm (Fig. 9). Moreover, it was noticeable that the validation performance of the ABC algorithm reduced steadily and slowly as the number of the selected features grew compare to the BLS algorithm (Fig. 11). This confirmed that selecting the most informative features considerably contributed to the validation performance of the machine learning algorithms. Further analysis revealed that the BLS algorithm has higher sensitivity for the testing, but the ABC algorithm has larger specificity and accuracy with the test data (Table 4). These results also confirmed that the BLS algorithm can effectively learn from the training data, but the ABC algorithm has superior generalization ability to unseen data.

As the future works, advanced NASH prediction models including the personal and environmental differences will be incorporated and evaluated. In addition, effectiveness of the treatment and pharmacological approaches will be considered and sophisticated artificial intelligence approaches which can make long-term stable predictions and treatment policies will be developed. Moreover, explainable Artificial Intelligence (AI) approached such as SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP), which is a game theoretic approach to explain the outputs of the machine learning models, and Local Interpretable Model agnostic Explanations (LIME), that can make predictions individually comprehensive, will be employed to interpret the developed NASH model estimates.

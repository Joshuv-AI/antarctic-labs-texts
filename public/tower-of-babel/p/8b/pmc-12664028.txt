Transcriptomic insights into the heart and cerebellar vermis of pigeons: Breed-specific differences driven by selective breeding
Authors: Yingli Long, Xiangshuai Zhang, Dan Chen, Qianchuan Yang, Keren Long, Xun Wang, Huaqiao Tang, Gang Ye, Ling Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo reveal the molecular regulatory differences in physiological adaptations of pigeons with different breeding objectives, this study compared the transcriptomic differences in the hearts and cerebellar vermis of homing pigeons (HP) and meat pigeons (MP). Phenotypic analysis showed that the heart index of HP was significantly higher than that of MP, which helps enhance functions such as blood circulation, supporting the metabolic demands for long-duration activity. However, no significant differences were observed in the cerebellar vermis indices. Transcriptomic analysis identified 162 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in the heart and 192 in the cerebellar vermis. Differential genes in the heart were primarily enriched in pathways related to circulatory system processes, ventricle development, fatty acid metabolism, and hypoxia response, including key genes related to angiogenesis and metabolism, such as HSP90AA1, ACACB, and LPIN1, suggesting that HP have stronger cardiovascular function and energy metabolism capacity. Differential genes in the cerebellar vermis were enriched in extracellular matrix organization, synaptic organization, axon guidance, and NRF2 pathway, indicating enhanced cerebellar neural adaptability and oxidative stress resistance in HP. Notably, circadian rhythm-related genes (PER2, PER3) were downregulated in both the hearts and cerebellar vermis of HP, which may affect the adaptive growth and remodeling of the heart. Alternative splicing analysis revealed 149 and 151 significant splicing events in the heart and cerebellar vermis, respectively, with exon skipping being the predominant splicing type. These results systematically reveal the tissue-specific molecular characteristics of the heart and cerebellar vermis in MP and HP, providing new insights into the molecular basis of their cardiac and metabolic adaptation.Keywords: Heart, Cerebellar vermis, Transcriptomic, Meat pigeon, Homing pigeon

Introduction

Pigeons (Columba livia) have undergone long-term artificial selection for distinct breeding purposes, resulting in three major types: fancy, meat and homing pigeons. Of which, meat pigeons have become the fourth poultry species in China (Ji et al., 2022), accounting for more than 80 % of the total number of global breeding (Kokoszyński, et al., 2020); while the homing pigeon industry valued at approximately 20 billion RMB. The breeding of meat pigeons focuses on efficient meat production, whereas homing pigeons have been selected for superior flight capabilities. Nevertheless, some homing pigeons culled due to suboptimal flight capabilities still possess certain value for egg production and meat utilization.

The differences in breeding objectives have resulted in distinct functional adaptations across various tissues, including skeletal muscles, hippocampus, etc. For instance, compared to meat pigeons, homing pigeons possess a significantly larger hippocampus. This anatomical characteristic is closely associated with their superior navigation and spatial memory capabilities. In addition, homing pigeons have higher content of skin with subcutaneous fat, which helps protect them from low temperatures during flight, while having lower fat content in their breast and leg muscles due to their higher level of physical activity. Moreover, homing pigeons exhibit significantly larger pectoral muscle fiber dimensions, including cross-sectional area, perimeter, and Feret diameters (Kokoszyński et al., 2020). Apart from above adaptation alteration, reported studies found that heart and cerebellar vermis play crucial roles in function adaptation (Lee et al., 2014; Hastings et al., 2024). The heart, as the central part of the circulatory system is responsible for pumping blood, supplying oxygen and nutrients to various tissues (Kim, 2022). During poultry production, high-density rearing environment commonly triggered stress responses and increased possibility of heart diseases (Sosnówka-Czajka and Skomorucha, 2022). In contrast to meat pigeons, homing pigeons exhibit better cardiac function, with superior cardiac output, supporting their flight by efficiently delivering oxygen and nutrients to muscles (Peters et al., 2005). Furthermore, the avian brain exhibits a high degree of plasticity in response to changes in external environments (Mehlhorn and Petow, 2020). The cerebellum, as a major motor structure of the brain, is made up of two hemispheres connected at the midline by the cerebellar vermis. The cerebellar vermis is a structure essential for controlling and coordinating body posture (Coffman et al., 2011). Using positron emission tomography (PET) technology, an investgation group found that pigeons exhibit a significant increase in cerebellar activity during flight, further supporting the unique cerebellar function involved in flight (Balanoff et al., 2024). In contrast, studies have shown that traditional caging systems limit behavioral expression, resulting in relatively smaller cerebella in caged poultry, which in turn affects their spatial learning and working memory abilities (Mehlhorn and Petow, 2020).

Gene expression serves as a critical component of the genetic basis governing evolutionary changes in diverse phenotypic traits. Existing studies have revealed divergence in gene expression levels between homing pigeons and meat pigeons in tissues such as the heart, hippocampus, and pectoral muscles. For example, the lactate dehydrogenase A (LDHA) gene plays an important role in energy metabolism, promotes the conversion of pyruvate to lactate and maintains NAD+/NADH balance, ensuring energy supply to the myocardium under sustained high metabolic demand (Erdem et al., 2025). Furthermore, LDHA reduce the level of succinyl-CoA and inhibit the succinylation modification of thioredoxin reductase 1 (TXNRD1), enhancing its antioxidant activity and reducing reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation (Chen et al., 2022), thus alleviating myocardial oxidative stress. The lactate produced by LDHA metabolism can also upregulate the expression of N-myc downstream-regulated gene 3 (NDRG3), activate the ERK signaling pathway, and promote adaptive growth and remodeling of the myocardium (Dai et al., 2020). Polymorphisms in the LDHA gene have been shown to be significantly associated with superior flight performance in homing pigeons (Ramadan et al., 2018). RNA-sequencing (RNA-Seq) further reveals that genes associated with memory and learning, including MFSD2A, KIRREL3, KCNAB2, and MAPK8IP2, are upregulated in the hippocampus of homing pigeons (Shao et al., 2020). Notably, MFSD2A encodes a transporter protein responsible for the brain’s uptake of docosahexaenoic acid (DHA). A decrease in DHA levels is linked to neuronal cell loss in the hippocampal region and subsequent cognitive dysfunction (Zhao and Zlokovic, 2014). Recent research revealed that SYNE1 and INSC genes were upregulated in the pectoral muscles of high-flying pigeons compared to meat pigeons, these genes exert negative regulatory effects on their growth and development (Hou et al., 2021). However, the differences in gene expression profiles in the cerebellar vermis and heart resulting from the distinct breeding objectives of homing and meat pigeons remain poorly understood.

This study conducted transcriptome sequencing of the heart and cerebellar vermis in homing pigeons and meat pigeons, revealing differences in their gene expression profiles. These findings provide new insights into understanding the effects of breeding objectives on the physiological adaptations of pigeons and offer new perspectives for genetic improvement and health management in poultry production.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics statement

This research was approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee in the College of Animal Science and Technology, Sichuan Agricultural University, Sichuan, China (Approval No. DKY-2024202002).

Experimental animals and collection of heart and cerebellum tissues

Sixteen adult female meat pigeons (MP, 2 years old) and homing pigeons (HP, 2 years old) were provided by the Feng Mao pigeon breeding plant (Mianyang, China) and Ya’an homing pigeon association (Ya’an, China), respectively. The pigeons were euthanized following body weight measurement. The heart, cerebellum, and cerebellar vermis of each pigeon were dissected and weighed. Organ indexes were calculated using the formula: organ index = organ weight / body weight × 100 %. Tissues for RNA sequencing were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at –80 °C.

Total RNA extraction and RNA-sequencing

Total RNA was isolated from the cardiac muscle tissues of the left ventricle and cerebellar vermis from 3 homing pigeons and 3 meat pigeons by HiPure Universal RNA Kit (Magen, Guangzhou, China) according to the manufacturer's protocol. The integrity and concentrations of total RNA were assessed using the Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer system (Agilent Technologies) and the Nanodrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, Massachusetts, USA). Total RNA was purified with poly-T oligo-attached magnetic beads and was employed to construct the RNA-Seq library by using NEBNext® Ultra™ RNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina (NEB, USA). After library quality was evaluated, 12 cDNA libraries were sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq platform, and producing 150 bp paired-end reads. RNA-Seq data are available in the GSA database (BioProject ID: PRJCA042686).

Quantification of gene expression and differential expression analysis

The raw reads were filtered to exclude adapters and low-quality sequences. The cleaned reads were mapped to the pigeon genome (Cliv_1.0, NCBI) with Hisat2 (version 2.0.5). The mapped reads of each sample were assembled by the StringTie (version 1.3.3b). FPKM (Fragments Per Kilobase of transcript per Million mapped reads) values were calculated by FeatureCounts (version 1.5.0-p3) for gene expression quantification. Differential expression analysis between two pigeon breeds was performed using DESeq2 (version 1.16.1), with padj≤0.05 and |log2FoldChange|≥1.

Functional enrichment and Protein-Protein Interaction analysis

Functional enrichment analysis of DEGs was implemented by using the online tool Metascape (https://metascape.org/). Gene Ontology (GO) terms and Kyoto Encyclopaedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways of DEGs was implemented by the hypergeometric test, in which p-value is calculated. GO terms and KEGG pathways with P < 0.05 were considered to be significantly enriched. The protein-protein interaction (PPI) network of DEGs was constructed based on the STRING database (https://string-db.org/) and subsequently visualized with Cytoscape (version 3.10.3).

Alternative splicing analysis

Alternative splicing (AS) events were quantified and analyzed for differential splicing using rMATS software (version 3.2.5). The software can identify five types of AS events: Skipped Exon (SE), Alternative 5′ Splice Site (A5SS), Alternative 3′ Splice Site (A3SS), Mutually Exclusive Exons (MXE), and Retained Intron (RI). Each AS event corresponds to two isoforms: Exon Inclusion Isoform and Exon Skipping Isoform. The expression levels of the two isoforms were calculated separately, normalized by their effective length, and the corrected expression levels were obtained. The ratio of Exon Inclusion Isoform to the total expression of both isoforms was then calculated, with the ratio corresponding to IncLevel1 (homing pigeon group) and IncLevel2 (meat pigeon group) in the result file. The rMATS statistical model was used to quantify the expression of alternative splicing events in different samples (with biological replicates). A likelihood-ratio test was then applied to calculate the p-value, reflecting the differences between the two groups at the IncLevel. Next, the Benjamini-Hochberg algorithm was used to adjust the p-values, yielding the False Discovery Rate (FDR) values. Finally, a threshold of FDR < 0.05 was applied to select significantly differential splicing events, ensuring that the identified differential AS events were reliable and of high confidence. After identifying the differentially spliced events, differential analysis was performed on the genes involved in these AS events. Subsequently, GO term and KEGG pathway analyses were conducted on the identified differentially spliced genes (DSGs). GO terms and KEGG pathways with P < 0.05 were considered to be significantly enriched.

Quantitative real-time PCR validation

Total RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA with PrimeScript™ RT Kit with gDNA Eraser (Takara, Otsu, Japan). Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qPCR) assays were conducted on a CFX96 Real-Time PCR Detection System (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA) using SYBR Premix Ex Taq II (Novoprotein, Shanghai, China). The 2-△△Ct method was used to calculate the relative gene expression levels, and ACTB was selected as a reference gene. The primer sequences were in Supplementary Table S1.

Statistical analysis

Independent sample t-tests were used to compare phenotypic data (R software, version 4.5.0) and qPCR data (SAS software version 9.0), with statistical significance set at P < 0.05.

As shown in Fig. 1A, heart indexes are conspicuously increased in homing pigeons compared with meat pigeons. Transcriptome analysis

To explore the gene expression profile in the heart and cerebellar vermis of homing and meat pigeons, we generated a total of 84.83 Gb raw sequence data from twelve libraries. Following quality control, 549.21 million clean reads (82.39 Gb data) were obtained for further analysis. The correlation coefficients of every two samples within twelve samples were calculated according to expression level (Fig. 2A). The results indicate that gene expression data in this study displays a tissue-specific pattern. The correlation coefficients are higher between samples from the same tissue, even if these samples may originate from different pigeon breeds. It is also confirmed by principal component analysis (PCA) (Fig. 2B).

Functional enrichment and protein-protein interaction analysis

To reveal the potential functions of the DEGs in the heart and cerebellar vermis, functional enrichment analysis was performed using the online tool Metascape. As shown in Fig. 4A, some DEGs in the heart are enriched in GO terms and KEGG pathways related to the regulation of cardiac structure and function, such as circulatory system process, cardiac ventricle development, blood circulation, circadian rhythm, regulation of heart rate, response to decreased oxygen levels, etc. (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4A and Supplementary Table S4). In contrast, DEGs in the cerebellar vermis are enriched in extracellular matrix organization, NRF2 pathway, regulation of synapse organization, axon guidance, neuron projection guidance, etc. (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4B and Supplementary Table S5). A PPI network comprising DEGs in the heart and cerebellar vermis of homing and meat pigeons was generated via the STRING database and visualized using Cytoscape (Fig. 4C-D). The heart-DEGs PPI subnetwork contained 41 nodes and 85 edges, including HSP90AA1, NOX1, FASN, etc. (Fig. 4C). The cerebellar vermis-DEGs PPI subnetwork contained 21 nodes and 27 edges, including COL2A1, ALPL, THBS1, etc. (Fig. 4D).

Alternative splice analysis

As Fig. 5A-B depicted, we detected 149 and 151 differentially alternative splice (DAS) events in the heart and cerebellar vermis between meat and homing pigeons, respectively. Among five types of AS events, SE is the most significantly enriched type (heart: 56.4 %; cerebellar vermis: 43.6 %). Functional enrichment analysis found that DSGs in the heart were involved in actomyosin structure organization, actin filament-based process, actin-mediated cell contraction, etc. (P < 0.05). In contrast to the heart, DSGs in cerebellar vermis were enriched in regulation of membrane potential, positive regulation of excitatory postsynaptic potential, protein localization to membrane, learning or memory, etc. (P < 0.05).

Discussion

This study revealed transcriptomic differences in the hearts and cerebellar vermis of homing and meat pigeons, reflecting physiological adaptations driven by divergent breeding objectives. These distinct breeding goals have resulted in significant physiological and metabolic differences, with particularly pronounced changes observed in the heart. The cardiovascular system is responsible for transporting oxygen-rich blood from the lungs to the muscles (Baggish and Wood, 2011), while the cerebellum is recognized as a crucial structure participating in motor control and learning (Celnik, 2015). In addition, cardiac function is regulated by a complex brain-heart axis (Liu, et al., 2025), modulating heart function and blood pressure through the interaction of neural, mechanical, and biochemical pathways (Valenza, et al., 2025). From the results of phenotypic differences, it was observed that the heart index of homing pigeons was significantly greater than that of meat pigeons. This result is strongly consistent with the phenomenon of ‘athlete's heart’, which describes cardiac hypertrophy induced by regular athletic training in animals and human athletes (Buhl, 2008). Homing pigeons exhibit a higher heart index, which enhances flight performance, whereas meat pigeons, raised long-term in cages, display a lower heart index. These results reflect a fundamental physiological divergence shaped by diametrically opposed selection objectives. Previous studies have shown that birds capable of sustained flight generally have larger hearts, and heart size is associated with flight ability (Nespolo, et al., 2018). A larger heart can pump more blood with each beat, providing more oxygen to meet the metabolic demands of prolonged flight (Nespolo, et al., 2018). For nervous system, previous research reported larger hippocampal index in the homing pigeons than in other pigeon breeds (Shao, et al., 2020). From our results, there are no significant differences in the cerebellum and cerebellar vermis indexes between homing and meat pigeons. These results implied that different parts of the central nervous system may have distinct evolutionarily responses for exercise adaptation.

The significant difference in heart index between meat pigeons and homing pigeons is likely attributed to their fundamental differences at the cardiac gene transcription level. Through transcriptomic analysis, 162 DEGs are identified in hearts, and these DEGs are primarily enriched in pathways related to muscle structure development, cardiac ventricle development, and blood circulation. Additionally, several genes related to fatty acid metabolism (ACACB, ACOX3, LPIN1) and hypoxia response (AQP1, PENK, ANGPTL4) were significantly upregulated in hearts of homing pigeons. Of which, ACACB is primarily involved in the regulation of fatty acid synthesis (Bhattacharjee, et al., 2020); LPIN1 plays a key role in lipid biosynthesis (Chambers, et al., 2021); and AQP1 is closely associated with cardiopulmonary function (Liu, et al., 2019). As an organ that undergoes continuous contraction, the heart requires a high energy demand. Under normal conditions, 40 %–60 % of ATP generation relies on fatty acids (FAs) (Yamamoto and Sano, 2022). Up-regulated expression of FA metabolism-related genes in homing pigeons indicated a difference in FA metabolism between the hearts of the two pigeon breeds. PPI network analysis revealed that HSP90AA1 is the key regulatory node in the heart DEGs network. Studies have shown that HSP90AA1 expression is significantly up-regulated in athletes following maximal aerobic effort, suggesting its activation as part of the exercise-induced cellular stress response (Hammad, et al., 2022).

Compared with other species, pigeons possess a relatively large and well-developed cerebellum (McMillan, 1990). Although there is no significant difference in cerebellar vermis indexes between homing and meat pigeons, a total of 192 DEGs were identified, exceeding the number detected in the heart. This suggests that the cerebellar vermis may exhibit greater molecular plasticity in response to motor adaptation. These DEGs were significantly enriched in pathways related to neurodevelopment and neuroprotection, including extracellular matrix organization, regulation of synapse organization, axon guidance, and the NRF2 signaling pathway. Previous studies have shown that exercise can activate the NRF2 pathway, upregulate antioxidant factors such as GSH and MnSOD, thereby enhancing the antioxidant capacity of neural tissues (Wang, et al., 2016). Exercise also improves cerebellar mitochondrial function, reduces oxidative stress and apoptosis-related markers (Marques-Aleixo, et al., 2015), which may help homing pigeons mitigate oxidative damage during prolonged flight and promote neuromuscular adaptation. Taken together, these transcriptional changes in the cerebellar vermis may represent an important molecular basis for motor adaptation in homing pigeons.

Importantly, our analysis also found that there are 14 shared DEGs in the heart and cerebellar vermis of homing and meat pigeons, which may provide new clues for the connection between the brain-heart axis. Particularly, compared to meat pigeons, the expression level of the two circadian rhythm-related genes (PER2, PER3), in the heart and cerebellar vermis of homing pigeons were downregulated. As coding products of core clock genes, PER proteins are involved in the rhythmic transcription-translation feedback loop (Liang, et al., 2022). It has been reported that there was a tendency for reduced induction of the PER genes in the hypertrophied heart of rats (Young, et al., 2001), which is similar to our results. Further studies have demonstrated that downregulation of PER2 promotes increased heart size and cardiomyocyte proliferation. Specifically, the protein encoded by PER2 can bind to the promoter of Wee1 and the intronic region of Cdk1/cyclin B1, leading to the upregulation of Cdk1/cyclin B1 and the suppression of Wee1 kinase activity. This results in the activation of Cdk1, ultimately enhancing cardiomyocyte proliferation and entry into mitosis (Tampakakis, et al., 2021). Although PER2 and PER3 have been implicated in a range of neurobiological processes, including synaptic plasticity (Wang, et al., 2009). However, the physiological significance of altered PER2 and PER3 expression in the cerebellar vermis remains to be elucidated, and whether these molecular changes represent an adaptive response to the physiological demands of sustained exertion requires further investigation.

AS is a crucial regulatory mechanism of gene expression that plays a significant role in generating phenotypic complexity (Chacko and Ranganathan, 2009). Identifying AS events between meat and homing pigeons provides valuable insight into the transcriptional regulation of gene expression during sports adaptation. The involvement of AS in various biological processes has been well established. For instance, AS is essential in regulating cardiac physiological adaptation (Gotthardt, et al., 2023) and is closely associated with neurogenesis and brain development (Su and Tarn, 2018). In our study, we found that the number of DAS events in the heart and cerebellar vermis of meat and homing pigeons is generally similar. Furthermore, SE is the most significantly enriched type in these two tissues, consistent with the fact that SE is the most common form of AS in metazoans (Wright, et al., 2022). In addition, some DSGs in the heart were enriched in the VEGFA-VEGFR2 signaling pathway, which has been shown to be activated by exercise, thereby promoting vascular formation, remodeling, and cardiomyocyte repair (Lee, et al., 2025; Ma, et al., 2025). In the cerebellar vermis, some DSGs were enriched in positive regulation of dendrite extension, cognition, learning or memory. This finding is consistent with the study by Daan et al., which reported that exercise promotes synaptic plasticity and neurogenesis (Mes, et al., 2020), implying that long-term flight training may effectively enhance the adaptation of neural networks in homing pigeons.

The larger sample size would enhance the robustness and generalizability of the omics data. Although previous studies have reported using three biological replicates for transcriptomic analysis (Li, et al., 2015; Sinpru, et al., 2021; Lattin, et al., 2022; Zhu, et al., 2023), a small sample size may increase the risk of false positives and limit the ability to detect subtle differences or low-abundance gene expression changes. Another limitation of our study is that, despite our efforts to control for environmental factors, the potential influence of unmeasured factors cannot be fully excluded. In addition, our analysis revealed a suite of DEGs in the heart and cerebellar vermis of meat versus homing pigeons. Functional studies of these genes are thus essential. Specifically, characterizing those associated with biological rhythms and neuroprotection will provide critical insights into the physiological differences shaped by distinct breeding objectives.

Conclusion

This study systematically compared the transcriptomes of the hearts and cerebellar vermis of homing pigeons and meat pigeons, revealing tissue-specific molecular regulatory patterns driven by different breeding objectives. Changes in pathways related to cardiovascular function may support the homing pigeons' adaptation in cardiac oxygen transport and energy supply, while the changes in the cerebellar vermis-related pathways may enhance their neural adaptability. These findings offered new insights into the molecular basis of their divergent cardiac and cerebellar vermi functional adaptations.

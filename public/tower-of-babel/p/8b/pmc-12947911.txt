Brain and Spinal Leptomeningeal Metastases in Prostate Cancer Detected by PSMA PET and MRI Fusion
Authors: Jiaqiong Wang, Russ Kuker
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A 66-year-old man with metastatic castration-resistant prostate adenocarcinoma experienced a rising PSA after multiple systemic therapies and radiation therapy to the cervical and thoracic spine for bone metastases. F-18 piflufolastat (Pylarify) PSMA PET/CT demonstrated widespread metastases, involving bones, lungs, mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes, liver, and intracranial and intraspinal sites. He subsequently developed worsening neurological symptoms. Brain MRI fused with PSMA PET revealed numerous radiotracer-avid scattered leptomeningeal enhancing foci intracranially. Cervical spine MRI fused with PSMA PET demonstrated multifocal radiotracer-avid intradural, extramedullary enhancing lesions. Biopsy of cervical intradural lesions confirmed metastatic prostate carcinoma.

A 66-year-old man was diagnosed with castration-resistant prostate adenocarcinoma and bone metastasis in 2016. From 2016 to 2025, he received leuprolide, abiraterone and prednisone, docetaxel, enzalutamide, carboplatin, and paclitaxel. For bone metastases, he underwent radiation therapy to the C7–T12 spine (2021) and C1–C3 spine (2024). However, his PSA increased to 64.4 ng/mL (August 2025). F-18 piflufolastat (Pylarify) PSMA PET/CT demonstrated widespread metastases involving bones, lungs, mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes, liver, and intracranial and intraspinal sites (A). The patient developed worsening neck pain, headache, difficulty ambulating, limb weakness, and impaired grip strength. Brain MRI revealed numerous small leptomeningeal enhancing foci within the right sylvian fissure, suprasellar cistern, paramesencephalic cisterns, bilateral cerebellar folia, and vermis (B), some possibly intraparenchymal. Fused PSMA PET/MRI T1 postcontrast images showed a radiotracer-avid lesion along the right inferior cerebellar surface (SUV 20.6, white arrows in B, C, and D) and additional smaller radiotracer-avid lesions, for example, one at the parafalcine right parietal lobe (yellow arrow in D).

Cervical spine MRI showed multifocal micronodular thickening of the pia and intradural, extramedullary enhancing lesions. A representative lesion at the right ventrolateral C7–T1 level was T2 hyperintense (white arrow in A), enhanced on postcontrast imaging, measured 1.1×0.8×1.5 cm, and caused severe cord compression (white arrows in B and C). Fused PSMA PET/MRI T1 postcontrast images confirmed this lesion was radiotracer-avid, SUV 47.5 (white arrow in D). Another radiotracer-avid 5 mm intradural extramedullary enhancing lesion was at the dorsal cranial-cervical junction, SUV 10 (yellow arrows in C and D). Biopsy of cervical intradural lesions confirmed metastatic carcinoma consistent with patient’s prostate primary. Leptomeningeal metastasis from prostate cancer is exceedingly rare, with an incidence of approximately 0.016%.1 The mean age of developing meningeal metastasis was reported as 63.0 years.2 Clinical presentation is varied and nonspecific, including headache, confusion, coma, or gait abnormalities.3,4 Neurological symptoms typically arise after a remission period averaging 7 years (range, 6 mo–21 y).5 Leptomeningeal dissemination is commonly preceded by axial skeletal metastases,5 with vertebral metastases considered an underrecognized pathway.6 Consistent with these reports, our patient developed multiple bone metastases before progression to leptomeningeal disease. The current standard imaging modality for diagnosing leptomeningeal carcinomatosis in prostate cancer is contrast-enhanced MRI.7 Our case is the first to report brain and spinal cord leptomeningeal carcinomatosis detected by F-18 piflufolastat (Pylarify) PSMA PET/CT, before MRI, and confirmed with PSMA PET/MRI fusion and biopsy. Prognosis in prostate cancer patients with leptomeningeal metastasis is extremely poor, with a systematic review reporting a median survival of 1.7 months after diagnosis.8 Treatment is generally palliative. Whole-brain radiation therapy may improve neurological symptoms. Hormonal therapy with leuprolide was reported to achieve stabilization and clinical improvement in a hormone-sensitive patient.9 Surgical debulking for decompression may preserve neurological function.10 In our case, the patient underwent posterior laminectomy with debulking of intradural spinal tumors at C7–T1. Although rare, leptomeningeal metastasis may become more prevalent with therapeutic advancements, and PSMA PET/MRI fusion may aid its detection.

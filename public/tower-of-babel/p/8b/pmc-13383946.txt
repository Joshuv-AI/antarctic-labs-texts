MicroRNAs expression changes associated with trauma-focused psychotherapy efficacy in treatment-resistant depression: An exploratory study
Authors: Lisa Buson, Rosana Carvalho Silva, Chiara Galbiati, Marco Bortolomasi, Monica Scaglia, Giulia Perusi, Maria Abate, Elisabetta Maffioletti, Massimo Gennarelli, Nicola Lorenzon, Mara Dierssen, European College of Neuropsychopharmacology (ECNP) Pharmacogenomics & Transcriptomics Network, Alessandra Minelli
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEarly life stress (ELS) increases vulnerability to major depressive disorder (MDD) and treatment-resistant depression (TRD) through lasting neurobiological and epigenetic alterations. MicroRNAs (miRNAs), key post-transcriptional regulators of gene expression, are emerging as potential biomarkers linking environmental stressors and depression pathophysiology. This study employed a miRNomic approach to investigate plasma miRNA changes associated with trauma-focused psychotherapy in TRD patients with a history of ELS. Twenty-four patients received either trauma-focused cognitive behavioural therapy (TF-CBT) or eye movement desensitization and reprocessing (EMDR) over eight weeks, with depressive symptoms assessed by the Montgomery-Åsberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS) at baseline (T0) and post-treatment (T8). Plasma levels of 188 miRNAs were quantified using qRT-PCR. Of these, 148 passed quality control and were included in the analysis. Twenty-two miRNAs were nominally significantly downregulated after psychotherapy, with miR-132-3p remaining significant after correction for multiple testing, and four (let-7f-5p, miR-195-5p, miR-29c-3p, miR-32-5p) showing trends toward significance. Pathway enrichment analyses identified 120 pathways involved in inflammation, neuroplasticity, and stress response, including Hippo, TGF-β, TNF, Wnt, PI3K-Akt, Neurotrophin, glutamatergic synapse, and dopaminergic signaling. Subgroup analyses identified nominal downregulation of miRNAs following EMDR (miR-132-3p and let-7f-5p) and TF-CBT (miR-32-5p and let-7f-5p), with none remaining significant after correction. Correlational analyses between miRNAs changes and treatment outcomes showed, in the whole sample, that miR-139-5p downregulation was nominally associated with greater improvements in overall depressive symptoms, as well as cognitive and neurovegetative symptom domains. Subgroup correlational analyses identified significant nominal associations between miR-125b-5p downregulation and improvements in overall depressive symptoms, mood, and neurovegetative domains following TF-CBT, and between miR-139-5p downregulation and overall depressive symptoms (trend significant) and neurovegetative symptom improvement following EMDR; however, none of these associations survived multiple-testing correction. These findings suggest that trauma-focused psychotherapies may induce peripheral miRNA modulation linked to clinical response, with miR-132-3p, miR-125b-5p, and miR-139-5p emerging as potential biomarkers of treatment response. The results support a role for miRNA-mediated neuroplastic and inflammatory mechanisms in the molecular underpinnings of psychotherapy efficacy in TRD patients exposed to ELS.Keywords: Early life stress, Major depressive disorder, Treatment-resistant depression, Trauma-focused psychotherapies, microRNAs, miRNomic

Introduction

Early life stress (ELS) is the term used to describe a variety of traumatic events that occur during childhood and adolescence, such as physical, emotional, or sexual abuse, and neglect, exerting harmful effects on health throughout life. Due to the high neuroplasticity of the neurodevelopmental window during which these adverse experiences occur, ELS is a critical contributor to the development and persistence of major depressive disorder (MDD) and other psychiatric disorders in adulthood (Bahk et al., 2017; Nelson et al., 2017; Spinhoven et al., 2014; Williams et al., 2016). About half of individuals with MDD report a history of ELS, and maltreated children are two to three times more likely to develop MDD in adulthood (Nelson et al., 2017). Moreover, ELS is also associated with an increased risk of treatment-resistant depression (TRD).

The association between ELS and MDD has biological bases involving dysregulations occurring at different levels (Carvalho et al., 2021). There is strong evidence that MDD has complex biological mechanisms that interact with environmental risk factors to influence its clinical course. MDD is characterized by dysregulated gene expression in both central and peripheral systems, involving alterations in monoamine and neurotransmitter systems, neuroplasticity and neurogenesis, neuroendocrine functions, and immune-inflammatory processes (Ciobanu et al., 2016). Stressful life events, particularly ELS, can induce epigenetic modifications that influence gene expression, affecting stress reactivity and emotional regulation. ELS has also been associated with increased inflammatory responses, evidenced by altered expression of cytokine-related genes in peripheral blood (Schwaiger et al., 2016) and enhanced glucocorticoid-induced inflammation (Menke et al., 2021).

Recent studies have highlighted the role of microRNAs (miRNAs) in MDD and stress-related disorders (Cătană et al., 2025). These small non-coding RNAs regulate gene expression, influencing biological processes by modulating mRNA stability and translation, impacting cellular functions and disease mechanisms, particularly in response to environmental stressors. They are involved in neurogenesis, neurodevelopment, and synaptic plasticity, as well as in the pathophysiology of several psychiatric disorders (Ding et al., 2023; Luoni and Riva, 2016; Maffioletti et al., 2014). miRNAs are also secreted into bodily fluids such as cerebrospinal fluid, blood, plasma, and serum. Alterations in circulating miRNAs have been reported in patients with MDD, highlighting their potential use as non-invasive biomarkers for diagnosis and monitoring of treatment outcomes (Ding et al., 2023). Changes in miRNA expression in response to stressful life events, including ELS, have been observed in both animal models and humans (Allen and Dwivedi, 2020). For example, increased levels of miR-15a have been reported in individuals exposed to childhood trauma, suggesting its role in chronic stress adaptation (Volk et al., 2016). Alterations in blood-derived miRNAs have been linked to MDD and modified by antidepressant therapies (O'Connor et al., 2012; Ghafouri-Fard et al., 2022; Vallelunga et al., 2014).

miRNAs may mediate the relationship between ELS and MDD. For instance, miR-450a-2-3p has been linked to emotional abuse and physical neglect in adolescents with MDD (Ran et al., 2022). miR-135-3p was found to be upregulated in individuals reporting high chronic stress and adverse childhood experiences compared to those with low stress exposure, particularly among African American young women (Holliday et al., 2025). Moreover, patients with MDD showed altered miRNA profiles compared to healthy controls, with miR-17 and miR-92 particularly associated with childhood traumas in depressed individuals (Mert et al., 2025).

MDD is the most prevalent psychiatric disease worldwide and, despite therapeutic advancements in pharmacological and non-pharmacological options, many patients remain inadequately treated (Kraus et al., 2019). Pharmacotherapy remains the first-line intervention, but only 40% achieve remission after the first treatment course and 30% after the second attempt, with one-third developing TRD (Jaffe et al., 2019; Oliveira-Maia et al., 2024; Pozuelo et al., 2025). Clinical factors, including earlier onset, severe symptoms, psychiatric comorbidities, suicidal tendencies, and stressful life events contribute to poor outcomes (Kraus et al., 2019; Kautzky et al., 2017).

In this context, trauma-focused psychotherapies, including trauma-focused cognitive-behavioural therapy (TF-CBT) and eye movement desensitization and reprocessing (EMDR), have been utilized in MDD and TRD patients with a history of ELS, showing therapeutic effectiveness (Hase et al., 2015, 2018; Minelli et al., 2019; Ostacoli et al., 2018).

There is little information regarding miRNAs changes associated with the treatment of MDD patients who experienced ELS. Most studies have focused on specific candidate-miRNAs approaches, analyzing only a few preselected miRNAs hypothesized to be linked to psychiatric disorders or trauma-related mechanisms, restricting the research to predefined hypotheses (Carvalho et al., 2021). Despite the inherent limitations of this approach, it was previously demonstrated that inflammation-related miRNAs are involved in both stressful life events and trauma-focused psychotherapy in depressed patients, confirming the direct link among inflammation, trauma, and stress. Specifically, lower baseline levels of miR-29a predicted non-response or worse symptomatologic improvement following psychotherapy, supporting its role as a biomarker of psychotherapy response (Maffioletti et al., 2021).

To the best of our knowledge, no prior study has employed a longitudinal miRNomic approach to evaluate miRNA changes in TRD patients with ELS undergoing trauma-focused psychotherapy. The present study employed a miRNomic strategy to comprehensively analyze plasma variations in 188 miRNAs among TRD patients, aiming to identify molecular correlates of treatment response.

Materials and methods

Study participants and clinical assessment

The study involved 24 TRD patients who voluntarily enrolled in the study. The diagnosis was made in accordance with the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fourth Edition (DSM-IV) classification system and confirmed through the Structured Clinical Interview for DSM-IV Axis I Disorders. The exclusion criteria were the following: (a) cognitive impairment or mental retardation; (b) history of bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, or schizoaffective disorder; (c) primary diagnosis of substance abuse, alcohol abuse or dependency, obsessive-compulsive disorder, personality disorder, or post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD); and (d) comorbidity with eating disorders; (e) comorbidity with alcohol and substance dependence; (f) neurological disorders (i.e., Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis, Alzheimer's and other dementias, epilepsy, stroke, brain tumors, traumatic conditions of the nervous system); (g) comorbidity with other severe medical illnesses and severe autoimmune diseases (i.e., cancers, Crohn's disease, rheumatoid arthritis, scleroderma, psoriasis, myasthenia gravis, Sjögren syndrome, systemic lupus erythematosus); (h) pregnancy.

Patients were referred to the Psychiatric Hospital ‘Villa Santa Chiara’ in Verona, Italy. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee for Clinical Trials of the province of Verona and Rovigo (N:234777/11.05.16). Participants received full explanation about study procedures and gave written informed consents to participate.

TRD was defined as failure to respond to at least two attempts with two or more classes of antidepressant drugs and to a trial with a tricyclic antidepressant (TCA), corresponding to stage III or higher according to the Thase and Rush staging system (Thase and Rush, 1997).

Assessment of ELS was performed with the Italian version of the Childhood Experience of Care and Abuse Questionnaire (CECA.Q) (Bifulco et al., 2005). All patients received trauma-focused psychotherapies and were randomly assigned, in a blinded manner, to either TF-CBT or EMDR. Each patient received three individual sessions per week, lasting 60 min each, over a period of 8 weeks, in addition to treatment as usual (TAU), for a total of 24 sessions. Psychotherapies were conducted by trained and experienced clinicians. Clinical depressive symptoms were assessed using the Montgomery-Åsberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS) (Montgomery and Asberg, 1979) and its corresponding dimensions according to the three-factor model, which includes mood, cognitive, and neurovegetative symptom domains. The improvement in symptoms was defined as the percentage reduction of MADRS scores from the baseline to T8 (ΔMADRS), and patients were classified as responders if the ΔMADRS at T8 was greater than >50%.

Circulating miRNA isolation and profiling

Peripheral blood samples were collected from all patients in K2 EDTA-coated tubes at T0 (baseline) and T8 (end of treatment). Blood tubes were first centrifuged at 1680 rcf for 15 min at room temperature to collect plasma samples, and then stored at −80°C.

Two hundreds μl of each plasma sample was used for miRNA extraction. Total miRNA isolation from plasma samples at T0 and T8 was performed using the NucleoSpin miRNA Plasma Mini kit for circulating miRNA (Macherey-Nagel, DEU), following the manufacturer's instructions. Before the RNA extraction, 10 pM of the exogenous spike-in control cel-miR-39 was added in each sample. RNA was then subjected to reverse transcription using the TaqMan™ Advanced miRNA cDNA Synthesis Kit (ThermoFisher, USA), according to the manufacturer's instructions. Plasma miRNA levels were assessed using TaqMan™ Advanced miRNA Human Serum/Plasma Card (ThermoFisher, USA), a panel of 188 target miRNAs, to comprehensively profile the circulating miRNAs that are most likely detectable in human plasma and serum, without a priori hypotheses, also including endogenous (hsa-miR-23a-3p, hsa-miR-652-3p) and exogenous (cel-miR-39-3p mentioned above) miRNA controls for normalization of data results. Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) assays, carried out on a QuantStudio 12K Flex (Applied Biosystems, USA), were performed in duplicate for each sample. MiRNAs showing Ct values > 32 in more than 3 samples were excluded from the analysis. Additionally, as a quality control (QC) procedure on each miRNA, we excluded values with excessive variance between the duplicates and/or showing an unconventional exponential trend in the amplification curves. Values passing the QC were then normalized by the geometric mean of Ct values of the more stable endogenous controls (hsa-miR-23a-3p and hsa-miR-652-3p in this case) and the exogenous spike-in cel-miR-39. The stability of the endogenous controls was evaluated using the NormFinder algorithm (RRID:SCR_003387). miRNA levels were calculated by the 2^-ΔCt method, whereas fold changes between T0 and T8 for each miRNA were determined using the 2^-ΔΔCt method.

Statistical analysis

To assess variations in depressive symptoms and miRNA expression levels (calculated as 2^-ΔCt) between T0 and T8, the Wilcoxon signed-rank test for dependent samples was applied, since data did not follow a normal distribution. p-values below 0.05 were considered significant at a nominal level and were adjusted for multiple testing by calculating the False Discovery Rate (FDR) using the Benjamini-Hochberg method. We considered for the statistical analysis only variations with a fold change (FC) < 0.9 or >1.1. Spearman's rank non-parametric statistical test was exploited to assess correlations among miRNAs variations and symptoms improvement. The variation of the score between visits (Δ MADRS) was used in the statistical analysis to evaluate the influence of symptoms changes. Moreover, correlation analyses were performed using the three-factor model of the MADRS (Suzuki et al., 2005). This model provides a more detailed description of depression severity because it regroups the symptoms in mood, cognitive, and neurovegetative dimensions.

All analyses were conducted using SPSS software package, version 21 (SPSS Inc., USA) while the FDR-adjusted p-values were corrected using R software version 3.6.1.

Gene target prediction and KEGG pathways analysis

For the identification of the biological processes involved, a target gene prediction and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis was conducted using the freely available online software mirPath v.3 (http://www.microrna.gr/miRPathv3). This tool allows the identification of miRNAs-regulated pathways by combining predicted miRNA interactions (in CDS or 3′-UTR regions) with target genes provided by the DIANA-microT-CDS algorithm or experimentally validated interactions derived from DIANA-TarBase. All analyses were conducted with reference to the KEGG molecular pathways database.

Results

Clinical assessment

A total of 24 patients were included in the present study, whose original cohort has been previously described in earlier publications from our group (Maffioletti et al., 2021; Carvalho et al., 2024). The mean age was 51.8 years, and 75% of the participants were female. Among the included participants, 14 patients received EMDR therapy, while 10 patients underwent TF-CBT. From T0 to T8, symptom severity, as measured by the MADRS, decreased in both treatment groups. Based on clinical response at T8, only two patients were classified as non-responders. Correlation between miRNA changes and treatment outcomes

In the overall sample, changes in miR-139-5p expression levels were significantly and positively correlated with symptoms improvement, as measured by the MADRS (r = 0.432, p = 0.039), indicating that greater reduction in miR-139-5p expression was associated with greater decreases in MADRS scores (Fig. 1). When specific symptom dimensions were examined, decreases in miR-139-5p expression were positively correlated with improvements in the cognitive (r = 0.437, p < 0.037) and neurovegetative (r = 0.479, p = 0.021) domains.

Correlation between changes in miR-139-5p expression levels and improvement in depressive symptoms in the overall sample. Changes in miR-139-5p expression were significantly correlated with symptom improvement, as measured by the Montgomery–Åsberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS).

Performing correlations analyses on the subgroup of patients based on the treatment received, we found that, in the TF-CBT subgroup, changes in miR-125b-5p expression were significantly correlated with overall symptom improvement, as measured by the MADRS (r = 0.733, p = 0.016), as well as with improvement in the mood (r = 0.634, p = 0.049) and neurovegetative (r = 0.758, p = 0.011) symptom domains. In the EMDR group, changes in miR-139-5p expression showed a trend toward a positive correlation with overall depressive symptom improvement at T8 (r = 0.547, p = 0.053) as measured by the MADRS. Moreover, a significant positive correlation was observed with neurovegetative symptom improvement (r = 0.689, p = 0.009).

None of the correlation analyses remained statistically significant after Benjamini-Hochberg correction for multiple testing.

Discussion

Our results revealed that 22 miRNAs were nominally significantly downregulated following trauma- focused psychotherapy, with only miR-132-3p remaining significantly changed after correction for multiple testing, and four others (let-7f-5p, miR-195-5p, miR-29c-3p, and miR-32-5p) showing trend-level downregulation. Target gene prediction and KEGG analyses of the nominally significant miRNAs identified 120 enriched pathways involved in MDD and stress-related processes. Subgroup analyses showed nominal downregulation of several miRNAs in both EMDR (7 miRNAs, including miR-132-3p and let-7f-5p) and TF-CBT (14 miRNAs, including miR-32-5p and let-7f-5p), but none remained significant after multiple-testing correction. MicroRNAs changes showed nominal significant positive correlations with symptom improvement as measured by MADRS, showing that miR-139-5p downregulation positively correlated with improvements in overall depressive symptoms, as well as in cognitive and neurovegetative domains. In subgroup analyses, TF-CBT was associated with significant correlations between miR-125b-5p downregulation and improvements in overall depression severity, as well as with mood and neurovegetative symptom domains, while EMDR showed a trend significant association between miR-139-5p downregulation and overall and neurovegetative symptom improvement. However, none of these associations remained significant after correction for multiple testing.

miR-132-3p showed the highest significance even after correction for multiple comparisons. miR-132 regulates neuronal differentiation and synaptic plasticity and is dysregulated in neurodegenerative and psychiatric disorders (Qian et al., 2017). In one study, widely prescribed antidepressants such as sertraline were reported to significantly reduce plasma miR-132 levels in patients with MDD (Ahmadimanesh et al., 2023). These findings suggest that both pharmacological and psychotherapeutic treatments may influence similar molecular pathways that could lower miR-132 expression and potentially contribute to symptom improvement.

let-7f-5p, part of the let-7 family, involved in neuronal processes, showed a trend-level downregulation after psychotherapy, consistent with our previous findings in MDD patients (Maffioletti et al., 2016; Bocchio-Chiavetto et al., 2013) and in TRD patients after electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) (Galbiati et al., 2025). let-7f was reported to be altered in TRD patients with ELS who underwent trauma-focused psychotherapy in a previous study by our research group (Maffioletti et al., 2021). These findings support miRNA's possible role in TRD and psychotherapy response while suggesting a link between inflammation and MDD pathogenesis, consistent with the literature (Ding et al., 2023). In our pathway analyses, target genes of downregulated miRNAs have been reported to be involved in inflammatory processes, including TNF signaling (Uzzan and Azab, 2021), extracellular matrix-mediated inflammation (Jaudon et al., 2021), and neurotrophic signaling (Abdolahi et al., 2022). Enrichment analyses highlighted pathways associated with miRNA downregulation after psychotherapy, consistent with those reported in psychiatric disorders and immune dysregulation, including Hippo (Taha et al., 2018), mTOR (Chen et al., 2024), neuroendocrine response to stress (Sze and Brunton, 2020), and fatty acid metabolism (Chen et al., 2025).

miR-195-5p, miR-29c-3p, and miR-32-5p also showed trends toward downregulation after psychotherapy. miR-195-5p is linked to cancers (Xu et al., 2022) and schizophrenia (Liu et al., 2024) and was reported to be similarly modulated during psychotherapy in TRD patients exposed to ELS (Maffioletti et al., 2021). Our group also previously reported miR-195-5p downregulation after ECT in TRD patients (Galbiati et al., 2025). miR-29c-3p, involved in neuroinflammation and neuronal differentiation (Lv et al., 2020), has been found to be upregulated in MDD patients (Prodan-Bărbulescu et al., 2024). Baseline levels of its homolog miR-29a were shown to predict psychotherapy response in TRD patients, with lower levels linked to poorer outcomes (Maffioletti et al., 2021), supporting that miR-29 family members may be affected by psychotherapy. miR-32-5p, related to neurodevelopment, was reported to be negatively associated with hippocampal volume in adolescents with MDD (Morgunova et al., 2025). Its trend-level downregulation in our cohort may support a role in stress-related molecular adaptation and recovery after psychotherapy. Taken together, these findings suggest that psychotherapy may induce peripheral miRNA changes converging on stress-regulatory and neuroplasticity pathways.

Correlation analyses showed a strong association between miR-139-5p downregulation and symptom improvement. miR-139-5p has been reported to be upregulated in exosomes from MDD patients and may mediate stress-induced depression-like behavior by reducing hippocampal neurogenesis (Wei et al., 2020). It has been proposed as a biomarker for anxiety and depression, influencing stress responses (Su et al., 2022). Its inhibition has been shown to exert antidepressant-like effects via the BDNF/TrkB pathway, making it a potential therapeutic target (Su et al., 2022).

In stratified analyses by treatment type, the correlation between miR-139-5p downregulation and symptom improvement was significant in the EMDR group, with strongest effects observed in the neurovegetative symptom domain. This finding suggests that EMDR may modulate specific molecular mechanisms, particularly those involved in neurovegetative recovery, the symptom cluster most closely linked with stress regulation (Gillespie et al., 2024). EMDR targets traumatic memories by restoring dysregulated neurobiological processes through mechanisms such as promoting neuroplasticity, reducing amygdala hyperactivity, and enhancing prefrontal cortex engagement. These mechanisms may help re-establish adaptive emotional and stress-regulation pathways, improving emotional regulation (Seok et al., 2024; Pagani and Carletto, 2017). By reprocessing traumatic memories, EMDR reduces negative emotions and somatic symptoms, activating cognitive mechanisms that reduce stress (Hase et al., 2018), which may partially contribute to the observed decrease in miR-139-5p levels in treated TRD patients.

In patients receiving TF-CBT, downregulation of miR-125b-5p was significantly associated with overall symptom improvement, as well as with improvements in mood and neurovegetative symptom domains. In a previous study conducted by our research group (Maffioletti et al., 2021), which investigated the effects of trauma-focused psychotherapies on the expression profiles of selected miRNAs, miR-125b was selected as a candidate miRNA based on existing literature demonstrating its association with inflammatory mechanisms and stress-related disorders (Wang et al., 2023; Olivieri et al., 2013). Although this study did not identify a significant association between changes in miR-125b expression and treatment outcomes, accumulating evidence indicates that this miRNA plays an important role in inflammatory processes.

miRNAs regulate gene expression post-transcriptionally and are key components of epigenetic regulation. In our previous study (Carvalho et al., 2024), we longitudinally assessed genome-wide methylation changes associated with trauma-focused psychotherapy in TRD patients exposed to ELS, revealing over 100 differentially methylated regions related to inflammation, with the strongest effects in EMDR-treated patients. Combined with current miRNA findings, these observations may speculatively point to a shared epigenetic signature, potentially involving DNA methylation and miRNA regulation, occurring after psychotherapy, with EMDR possibly influencing inflammatory and neuroplastic mechanisms and miRNAs potentially serving as biomarkers of treatment response. A multi-omics integration analysis combining genome-wide DNA methylation and miRNA data could provide further insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying psychotherapy response and help identify epigenetic pathways associated with treatment outcomes. However, such integrated analyses were beyond the scope of the current study and represent an important direction for future research.

Our study presents some limitations. The main one is the small sample size, reflecting the challenges of conducting longitudinal studies in TRD patients with varied ELS exposures, which may limit the generalizability of our findings. The sample size was further diminished after stratifying participants by treatment type, which additionally lowered statistical power and increased the risk of both type I and type II errors. Consequently, the treatment-specific correlation analyses should be interpreted with caution, given the limited sample size of each subgroup and all miRNA changes and correlation findings should be interpreted as exploratory. Another point to be considered is the potential influence of pharmacological treatment on the interpretation of the results, as psychotropic medications may affect miRNA expression. In our sample, participants were treated with multiple drugs; the majority were receiving antidepressants (primarily selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors and serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors), while approximately half were also treated with a mood stabilizer or with an antipsychotic. Although pharmacological treatments remained relatively stable throughout the study period, minimizing the potential confounding effect of medication changes on the longitudinal analyses, we cannot exclude effects of antidepressant types or concomitant psychotropic medications on miRNA expression. Finally, the absence of a control group represents a potential limitation of this study. However, our primary objective was to investigate longitudinal molecular changes associated with response to psychotherapy within a clinically homogeneous cohort of patients with TRD. Future studies employing different designs should include appropriate control groups to facilitate direct comparisons and strengthen the interpretation of findings.

In conclusion, this study provides novel evidence that trauma-focused psychotherapies, particularly EMDR, may be associated with changes in miRNA expression in TRD patients with a history of ELS. The downregulation of miR-132-3p, along with the trend toward reduced expression of let-7f-5p, miR-195-5p, miR-29c-3p, and miR-32-5p, together with pathway analyses implicating neuroplastic and inflammatory signaling, may suggest possible biological mechanisms through which psychotherapy might exert its therapeutic effects. The nominal associations between changes in miR-139-5p and symptom improvement following EMDR, as well as between miR-125b-5p downregulation and symptom improvement after TF-CBT, further emphasize the potential molecular effects of these therapeutic approaches. Our results contribute to the growing body of literature linking psychotherapy, molecular neurobiology, and epigenetic regulation, offering new perspectives on the identification of potential biomarkers to monitor treatment response in MDD and TRD.

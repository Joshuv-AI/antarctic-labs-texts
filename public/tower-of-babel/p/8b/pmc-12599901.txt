Development of an Improved Stacked U-Net Model for Cuffless Blood Pressure Estimation Based on PPG Signals
Authors: Jenn-Kaie Lain, Chung-An Wang, Jun-Hao Xu, Chen-Wei Lee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractGoal: This study presents an enhanced stacked U-Net deep learning model for cuffless blood pressure estimation using only photoplethysmogram signals, aiming to improve the accuracy of non-invasive measurements. Methods: To address the challenges of systolic blood pressure estimation, the model incorporates velocity plethysmogram input and employs additive spatial and channel attention mechanisms. These enhancements improve feature extraction and mitigate decoder mismatches in the U-Net architecture. Results: The model satisfies the Grade A criteria established by the British Hypertension Society and meets the accuracy standards of the Association for the Advancement of Medical Instrumentation, achieving mean absolute errors of 3.921 mmHg for systolic and 2.441 mmHg for diastolic blood pressure. It outperforms PPG-only spectro-temporal methods and achieves comparable performance to the joint photoplethysmogram and electrocardiogram one-dimensional squeeze-and-excitation network with long short-term memory architecture. Conclusions: The proposed model shows strong potential as a practical, low-cost, and non-invasive solution for continuous, cuffless blood pressure monitoring.Keywords: Blood pressure (BP), photoplethysmogram (PPG), and deep learning

Introduction

Despite advances in healthcare, cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) remain a leading cause of illness and death, especially among the elderly [1]. Hypertension, affecting nearly one-third of adults [2], [3], [4], is a major CVD risk factor. Often asymptomatic, it can lead to cardiac hypertrophy, heart failure, stroke, coronary artery disease, and other life-threatening complications. The World Health Organization (WHO) reports that 80% of hypertensive patients lack adequate treatment, with cases doubling from 650 million in 1990 to 1.3 billion in 2019 [5]. Nearly half remain unaware of their condition, making hypertension a silent killer. Continuous monitoring is vital, as early intervention could prevent 76 million deaths by 2050. However, a simpler, more accessible monitoring solution is urgently needed due to healthcare shortages [6].

Traditional blood pressure measurement methods include accurate but invasive catheter-based techniques, which involve complex procedures and risk of complications. Due to discomfort and medical demands, their use is limited to surgical or intensive care settings, making them unsuitable for routine blood pressure monitoring and daily management [7]. Non-invasive cuff-based monitors are common for home and clinical use due to their convenience. However, repeated inflation/deflation limits frequent or continuous use. Thus, developing cuffless, accurate, and continuous BP monitoring technologies remains a key focus in research and clinical practice [8], [9], [10].

PPG, which reflects blood flow and arterial pressure, has become key in BP estimation due to its simplicity and integration in wearables [11]. Using a light-emitting diode and photodetector, it captures blood volume changes via light intensity variations. Though electrocardiogram (ECG) and PPG were used together for BP estimation, waveform distortions from disease complicate feature extraction. To improve accuracy, a hierarchical artificial neural network and long short-term memory (LSTM) model extracted features and considered their time-domain changes [12]. Additionally, a multistage deep neural network using only PPG was developed, combining convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for feature extraction and LSTMs for temporal patterns, integrating systolic-diastolic relationships to enhance BP estimation accuracy and address cuff-based limitations [13]. Unlike earlier CNN-LSTM models, a filter-wrapper method and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator LSTM were proposed to refine BP-relevant features and improve accuracy [14]. The squeeze and excitation network (SENet)-LSTM model further enhanced performance by integrating PPG and ECG signals with SE blocks, enabling arterial blood pressure (ABP) waveform reconstruction [15]. These studies highlight the importance of accurate feature extraction from PPG signals using deep learning. While ECG-PPG hybrid approaches have demonstrated strong performance, they require additional electrodes, hardware synchronization, and more complex signal processing. These requirements increase cost, reduce portability, and limit the feasibility of continuous, cuffless, and user-friendly monitoring in daily life.

PPG waveform patterns change significantly with aging and vascular diseases, reducing the reliability of manually extracted features for BP estimation [16], [17]. Deep neural networks enable end-to-end time series-to-time series mapping, autonomously learning features and reconstructing ABP waveforms, eliminating the need for manual feature engineering. The one-dimension (1D) U-Net excels in blood pressure estimation with multi-scale feature extraction, skip connections, and denoising. It outperforms recurrent neural networks, is more efficient than transformers, and more robust than basic CNNs, making it ideal for PPG/ECG-based BP estimation. Translating PPG sequences into ABP waveforms is challenging, and a single 1D U-Net underperforms. A two-stage 1D U-Net first approximates PPG to an ABP waveform, then refines it into a more accurate output [18]. Regarding existing U-Net BP models, their performance does not fully meet the stringent British Hypertension Society (BHS) and Association for the Advancement of Medical Instrumentation (AAMI) standards, and they lack mechanisms to enhance feature representation or exploit morphological variations in the PPG signal. This study overcomes these limitations by integrating spatial and channel attention to enhance feature representation and using VPG inputs to enrich temporal-frequency information achieving BP estimation performance meeting BHS and AAMI standards.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section II details the materials and methods, including the PPG signal dataset, signal pre-processing, network architecture, and experimental settings, and introduces spatial attention to resolve decoder feature mismatch and channel attention to enhance feature extraction. Section III presents and discusses experimental results, highlighting the study's contributions and limitations. Finally, Section IV concludes the study.

Materials and Methods

Dataset

This study used a clinical dataset containing synchronously recorded PPG, ECG, and ABP signals, all sampled at 125 Hz with -bit precision [18], [19]. The dataset is a processed subset of the multiparameter intelligent monitoring in intensive care (MIMIC-II) dataset from PhysioNet [20], [21], complied by Kachuee et al., and includes records from patients [19]. The study considered blood pressure values ranging from mmHg (DBP) to mmHg (SBP), with SBP showing higher standard deviation, making its prediction more challenging [18].

Normalized signal segments of a duration of seconds employed to facilitate deep neural network training while maintaining computational efficiency. The model reconstructs ABP waveforms from PPG signals , and SBP and DBP are then extracted from the estimated ABP waveforms. A total of segments were extracted, with used for training and for testing. Importantly, low-quality PPG signals were retained to enhance the model's robustness across a variety of signal conditions.

PPG Signal Pre-processing

Blood pressure measurements include SBP, DBP, and mean arterial pressure (MAP). SBP reflects ventricular contraction force, while DBP indicates pressure during relaxation, influenced by vascular resistance. MAP, closer to DBP due to prolonged diastole, offers a comprehensive view of systemic hemodynamics and is widely used in critical care for continuous blood pressure assessment. Previous studies show that SBP estimation typically incurs larger errors than DBP or MAP [18], [19], due to rapid pressure changes during systole. Arterial stiffness from aging or vascular disease further distorts peak PPG morphology, complicating estimation. The ABP peak closely correlates with the PPG's rate of rise during ventricular contraction.

The velocity plethysmogram (VPG), the first derivative of the PPG signal, captures the rate of blood volume change and may enhance SBP estimation. Let denote the PPG segment and its derivative. The morphology of correlates with SBP peaks in the ABP waveform , suggesting that pressure is influenced by both blood volume and its rate of change. While CNNs extract morphological features, they often overlook derivative features like VPG. Integration both and as inputs enables deep models to better learn velocity-related features, improving SBP estimation accuracy.

Network Architecture

The Stacked U-Net

Blood pressure estimation from physiological signals is a complex task. Recently, stacked neural architectures have become prevalent, typically combining CNNs for morphological feature extraction and LSTM networks for temporal modeling. Unlike conventional models that sequentially connect feature extraction and regression networks, this study adopts a two-stage U-Net framework, as shown in Figs. 1 and 2, termed the ATTU-Net BP model, for ABP waveform estimation, in which denotes the batch size and each stage performs both feature learning and ABP waveform estimation, progressively improving performance.

The first-stage network of the ATTU-Net BP model simultaneously performs feature learning and ABP waveform approximation based on the pre-processed input signals.

The second-stage network of the ATTU-Net BP model concurrently performs feature extraction and ABP waveform refinement, using the approximated waveform generated by the first stage as input.

Based on [18], the U-Net BP model employs a similar two-stage framework. The first-stage approximation network uses a standard U-Net to translate PPG signals into ABP waveforms, while the second-stage refinement network uses MultiResUNet to enhance the approximated waveforms. However, the first-stage U-Net suffers from decoder feature mismatch due to fusing low-level skip-connected and high-level upsampled features. Therefore, optimizing the approximation network is vital for improving the overall performance and reliability of blood pressure estimation.

The Spatial Attention Mechanism

Recent advancements have addressed decoder feature mismatch in traditional U-Nets using attention mechanisms. Additive and scaled dot-product attention are two key approaches [22], [23]. While scaled dot-product attention uses dot products for score calculation, additive attention leverages nonlinear activations and feedforward networks, enabling better modeling of complex, localized relationships despite higher computational cost.

Scaled dot-product attention captures global dependencies, aiding sequence summarization and long-range context understanding. In contrast, additive attention excels in local alignment by focusing on relevant input-output token pairs. Its nonlinear combination and spatial selectivity make it well-suited for mitigating decoder feature mismatch in U-Net models, enhancing feature alignment and improving network performance, particularly in stacked U-Net architectures for blood pressure estimation. As shown in Fig. 1, additive attention layers are integrated between the encoders and decoders of the first-stage U-Net, enhancing feature alignment by prioritizing relevant encoder features and suppressing irrelevant activations.

The Channel Attention Mechanism

In addition to spatial attention via additive attention, channel-wise attention using the squeeze-and-excitation (SE) layer offers a promising enhancement. The SE layer adaptively recalibrates channel-wise features by applying global average pooling (squeeze) followed by fully connected layers (excitation) to generate channel weights [24].

Results and Discussion

In this study, we conducted a consistency evaluation of the developed blood pressure estimation model based on PPG signals, comparing it to standard blood pressure measurements. The objective was to assess the feasibility of the proposed strategies in improving BP estimation to meet the guidelines set by the BHS and AAMI as described in the standards for blood pressure measurement section in the supplementary materials.

Both the BHS and AAMI primarily use mean error as a performance metric, representing the average bias of a measurement compared to its ground truth. However, since mean error can be positive or negative and may cancel out, it can sometimes underestimate the true error. Therefore, Bland–Altman plots, regression plots, and MAE are also considered in the subsequent results and discussion.

As noted in [15], ECG signals capture the heart's electrical activity, while PPG signals measure blood volume changes. Combining ECG and PPG generally improves blood pressure estimation over PPG-only approaches. However, differences in sensing mechanisms complicate signal alignment, and using both signals requires extra sensors and increases hardware costs. The proposed ATTU-Net BP model, relying solely on PPG, achieves Grade A performance under BHS standards and satisfies AAMI accuracy requirements. Its MAE not only outperforms recent PPG-based methods [26], but also matches the performance of [15], which requires both ECG and PPG inputs. Importantly, this study reports results on a test dataset over ten times larger than [15], demonstrating superior robustness and generalization capabilities.

Conclusion

Hypertension significantly contributes to atherosclerosis and cardiovascular damage and is strongly linked to coronary artery disease. Regular blood pressure monitoring is among the most cost-effective public health measures. This study proposes a deep learning model, ATTU-Net BP, for non-invasive BP estimation using only PPG signals, incorporating VPG signals and spatial and channel attention mechanisms. The model meets the highest BHS and AAMI standards and rivals recent ECG-PPG-based models. By relying solely on PPG, it offers lower hardware requirements and greater usability for clinical and home settings.

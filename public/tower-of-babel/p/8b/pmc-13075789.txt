Transarterial Chemoembolization Combined With Camrelizumab and Rivoceranib for Unresectable Hepatocellular Carcinoma (CHANCE2005/CARES-005): A Randomized Phase II Trial
Authors: Hai-Dong Zhu, Wei-Jun Fan, Chang Zhao, Song Wang, Yu-Liang Li, Zhi-Cheng Jin, Zhe-Wei Zhang, Jin-He Guo, Hong-Tao Cheng, Qi Zhang, Jian Lu, Yong-Yi Zeng, Wei-Fu Lv, Hao Xu, Hai-Bo Shao, Wei-Guo Xu, Xu-Ya Zhao, Shan-Zhi Gu, Hai-Lan Lin, Wen-Heng Zheng, Long-Zhen Piao, Yu-Sheng Song, Jian-Bo Zhao, Yuan-Cheng Wang, Zhi-Guo Hou, Ying Sun, Ni Guan, Ming Huang, Wei-Zhu Yang, Jian-Song Ji, Gao-Jun Teng

ABSTRACT
AbstractPURPOSETransarterial chemoembolization (TACE) alone has shown limited efficacy in improving survival among patients with unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). This phase II trial compared TACE combined with camrelizumab (anti–PD-1 antibody) and rivoceranib (vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 2 inhibitor) versus TACE in unresectable HCC.METHODSPatients with unresectable HCC (Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer stage A to C without extrahepatic metastases) and Child-Pugh class A liver function were randomly assigned (1:1), stratified by macrovascular invasion, previous tyrosine kinase inhibitor treatment, and number of previous TACE procedures, to receive TACE combined with camrelizumab (200 mg once every 3 weeks) and rivoceranib (250 mg once daily; TACE-C-R) or TACE alone. The primary end point was progression-free survival (PFS) per composite criteria (progression per Response Evaluation Criteria in Cancer of the Liver version 5, transient deterioration to Child-Pugh class C, or TACE failure or refractoriness) in the intention-to-treat population.RESULTSBetween December 28, 2020, and October 29, 2023, 200 patients were randomly assigned (100 in each group). Median PFS per composite criteria was significantly longer with TACE-C-R than with TACE (10.8 months [95% CI, 8.8 to 13.7] v 3.2 months [95% CI, 2.4 to 4.2]; hazard ratio, 0.34 [95% CI, 0.24 to 0.50], P < .001). Grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 74.5% (70 of 94) of patients with TACE-C-R and 22.3% (23 of 103) of patients with TACE, with the most common being increased AST (29 [30.9%] and 13 [12.6%]) and increased ALT (23 [24.5%] and 14 [13.6%]).CONCLUSIONThe addition of camrelizumab and rivoceranib to TACE showed statistically significant improvement in PFS for patients with unresectable HCC, with a manageable safety profile. Follow-up for further overall survival analysis is ongoing.

INTRODUCTION

Transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) has been the established standard of care for patients with intermediate hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) for over two decades.1,2 It is also widely used in unresectable HCC including advanced stage without extrahepatic metastases in real-world practice, particularly in Asian countries per local guidelines.3-5 However, recurrence or progression after TACE is inevitable during the disease course, with a 5-year survival rate of 26%-35%.6,7 This underscores the need for novel therapies that offer enhanced survival benefits.

TACE-induced tumor necrosis is known to facilitate the release of tumor-associated neoantigens, reduce the secretion of immunosuppressive factors, and activate antitumor immune response.2,8,9 Meanwhile, TACE generates a hypoxic tumor microenvironment, promoting neovascularization within the residual tumor and increasing the expression of vascular endothelial growth factor.8 Antiangiogenic agents may potentiate antitumor immunity by normalizing the aberrant tumor vasculature and mitigating the immunosuppressive milieu within the tumor microenvironment.2,9 These underlying mechanisms suggest the potential of combining TACE with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) and antiangiogenic agents. Several large-scale retrospective studies demonstrated significantly longer median overall survival (OS) with this triplet combination (19.2-22.6 months) compared with TACE (15.7 months) or systemic therapy (ICIs plus antiangiogenic agents: 15.9 months) alone in patients with intermediate-advanced or advanced HCC.10,11 Recently, two phase III trials (EMERALD-1 and LEAP-012) confirmed the superiority of this combination strategy in progression-free survival (PFS) over TACE alone in patients who mostly had intermediate HCC.12,13 Data from another phase III trial (TALENTACE) were briefly disclosed at European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) Gastrointestinal Cancers Annual Congress 2025.14

Camrelizumab is a humanized anti–PD-1 monoclonal antibody. Rivoceranib (also known as apatinib) is a tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) targeting vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 2. Based on the positive results from the phase III CARES-310 trial (median OS, 23.8 months with camrelizumab plus rivoceranib v 15.2 months with sorafenib; hazard ratio [HR], 0.64 [95% CI, 0.52 to 0.79]),15,16 this first-line combination has been approved for treating advanced HCC in China and recommended by HCC Clinical Practice Guideline from ESMO.17 A retrospective study suggested the feasibility of this systemic therapy regimen combined with TACE, showing significantly better median OS compared with TACE alone (24.1 months v 15.7 months).18

Here, we conducted this trial to compare the efficacy and safety of TACE combined with camrelizumab and rivoceranib (TACE-C-R) versus TACE alone in patients with unresectable HCC.

METHODS

Study Design and Participants

CHANCE-2005/CARES-005 was a multicenter, randomized, open-label, phase II trial conducted at 22 centers (Appendix Table A1, online only) in China. Eligible patients were 18 years or older and had HCC confirmed by histopathology, cytology, or imaging; stage Ib to IIIa HCC per China Liver Cancer (CNLC) staging system19 (corresponding to Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer [BCLC] stage A to C without extrahepatic metastases) who had unresectable disease or refused surgery; at least one measurable lesion per Response Evaluation Criteria in Cancer of the Liver (RECICL) version 520; Child-Pugh class A (≤6 points) liver function; an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0 or 1; a life expectancy of at least 3 months; and adequate bone marrow, cardiac, hepatic, and renal function. Patients who had progression or residual lesions after the completion of surgery or locoregional therapy (radiotherapy, ablation, or up to two TACE procedures) at least 1 month before random assignment were allowed to be enrolled. Previous TKI treatment in the adjuvant or first-line setting at least 14 days before enrollment was permitted. Key exclusion criteria are listed in Appendix 1 (online only).

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declarations of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. The study protocol was approved by the ethics committee of all participating centers. Written informed consent was obtained from each patient.

Random Assignment and Masking

Patients were randomly assigned, in a 1:1 ratio, to receive either TACE-C-R or TACE alone. Random assignment was stratified by macrovascular invasion (yes v no), previous TKI treatment (yes v no), and number of previous TACE procedures (0 v 1-2). Dynamic random assignment was conducted by an independent third party. The study was open-label, and treatment allocation was not masked.

Procedures

Patients in both groups received conventional TACE containing epirubicin (no more than 40 mg per procedure) within 1 week after random assignment, and subsequent TACE treatments were administered on demand (for patients with evidence of residual viable lesions indicated by contrast-enhanced imaging examination9). Patients in the TACE-C-R group additionally received intravenous camrelizumab (200 mg once every 3 weeks) plus oral rivoceranib (250 mg once daily) starting within 2 weeks after the first TACE procedure until progression, intolerable toxicity, withdrawal of consent, or investigator's decision. Camrelizumab and/or rivoceranib treatment could be continued after progression at investigator's discretion based on benefits and tolerability. Treatment crossover from TACE to TACE-C-R was permitted beyond progression. Dose reduction was allowed for rivoceranib only to manage adverse events (AEs). Details of dose adjustments are described in the study protocol.

Radiologic response by contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging and/or computed tomography, Child-Pugh score, and serum alpha-fetoprotein level were assessed at baseline, every 6 weeks for the first 6 months, and every 3 months thereafter. Child-Pugh score was additionally assessed after each TACE procedure. For patients who discontinued the study treatment without radiologic progression, imaging examinations were conducted every 3 months until radiologic progression or initiation of new anticancer therapies. Radiologic response was locally evaluated by two senior radiologists with more than 10 years of experience at each center per RECICL 5 and modified RECIST (mRECIST).20,21 The third senior radiologist would participate in evaluation if there was discrepancy in radiologic response per either criteria. Survival was followed every 3 months. AEs were monitored from obtaining written informed consent until 30 days (90 days for camrelizumab) after the last study treatment, graded per Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0.

Outcomes

The primary end point was PFS per composite criteria (progression per RECICL 5, liver function deterioration to Child-Pugh class C within 1 week of TACE, or TACE failure or refractoriness22). Secondary end points were PFS per mRECIST; time to TACE-untreatable progression (TTUP); OS; objective response rate (ORR) and disease control rate (DCR), each assessed per RECICL 5 and mRECIST; duration of response (DoR); and safety. Detailed definitions of end points are given in Appendix 1.

Statistical Analysis

We assumed that the median PFS was 10.0 months with TACE and 15.4 months with TACE-C-R. With an HR of 0.65, a random assignment ratio of 1:1, a one-sided α of .05, a power of 80%, an estimated enrollment period of 12 months, and an estimated study period of 36 months, 84 patients were required for each group. Considering a dropout rate of 10%, each group needed 94 patients. The formal analysis would be performed after the occurrence of approximately 135 PFS events.

Efficacy was analyzed in all randomly assigned patients per the intention-to-treat principle. Safety was analyzed in all randomly assigned patients per actually received treatment. Statistical methods are detailed in Appendix 1. All statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4.

RESULTS

Patients

Between December 28, 2020, and October 29, 2023, a total of 200 patients (100 in each group) were randomly assigned (intention-to-treat population). One patient in the TACE-C-R group and two in the TACE group did not receive treatment, and five patients in the TACE-C-R group only received TACE, leading to 94 patients with TACE-C-R and 103 with TACE included for safety analysis (Fig 1). Of 200 patients, the median age was 58 years (IQR, 51 to 65) and the majority (87%) were male. Baseline characteristics were well-balanced between groups (Table 1).

Efficacy

As of the data cutoff date (October 16, 2024), the median follow-up duration was 14.3 months (IQR, 8.3-20.9). Twenty-five (25%) of 100 patients in the TACE-C-R group and eight (8%) of 100 patients in the TACE group remained on treatment. A total of 130 PFS events per composite criteria (Appendix Table A2) or 136 PFS events per mRECIST occurred. Median PFS per composite criteria was significantly longer in the TACE-C-R group compared with the TACE group (10.8 months [95% CI, 8.8 to 13.7] v 3.2 months [95% CI, 2.4 to 4.2]; HR, 0.34 [95% CI, 0.24 to 0.50], P < .001; Fig 2A). Median PFS per mRECIST showed similar results (8.8 months [95% CI, 6.2 to 10.8] v 3.1 months [95% CI, 2.3 to 4.2]; HR, 0.43 [95% CI, 0.30 to 0.62], nominal P < .001; Fig 3A). Post hoc subgroup analyses of PFS showed consistent trends favoring TACE-C-R (Fig 2B and Appendix Figs A1-A3). Moreover, a post hoc analysis excluding all patients with any previous anticancer therapy showed consistent results with the primary analysis (Appendix 1 and Appendix Fig A4). The median TTUP was 13.7 months (95% CI, 10.7 to 19.6) with TACE-C-R and 3.9 months (95% CI, 2.5 to 5.7) with TACE. Ninety deaths occurred. The median OS was 24.0 months (95% CI, 18.7 to 30.1) in the TACE-C-R group and 21.5 months (95% CI, 15.6 to not reached) in the TACE group (HR, 0.87 [95% CI, 0.57 to 1.32]; Fig 3B). Forty-nine (75%) of 65 patients with progression per composite criteria after applying the censoring rules in the TACE group crossed over to the TACE-C-R group.

In terms of tumor response per RECICL 5, the ORR was 65% (95% CI, 55 to 74) with TACE-C-R and 29% (95% CI, 20 to 39) with TACE and the DCR was 87% (95% CI, 79 to 93) and 63% (95% CI, 53 to 72), respectively. The median DoR from response per RECICL 5 to progression per composite criteria or death was 11.4 months (95% CI, 9.0 to 17.5) with TACE-C-R and 6.9 months (95% CI, 4.3 to 9.8) with TACE. Regarding tumor response per mRECIST, the ORR was 61% (95% CI, 51 to 71) with TACE-C-R and 29% (95% CI, 20 to 39) with TACE; the median DoR was 7.6 months (95% CI, 6.9 to 11.4) and 4.7 months (95% CI, 2.4 to 7.0), and the DCR was 85% (95% CI, 76 to 91) and 58% (95% CI, 48 to 68), respectively (Appendix Table A3).

Post hoc analyses of PFS, ORR, DCR, and DoR per RECIST 1.1 are shown in Appendix Table A3 and Fig A5.

Safety

Treatment exposure data in the safety analysis set are available in Appendix Table A4. The incidence of treatment-related AEs (TRAEs) was 99% (93 of 94) with TACE-C-R and 66% (68 of 103) with TACE; 70 (74%) and 23 (22%) patients had grade ≥3 TRAEs, respectively. The most common grade ≥3 TRAEs were increased AST (29 of 94 [31%]), increased ALT (23 of 94 [24%]), hypertension (13 of 94 [14%]), and decreased platelet count (11 of 94 [12%]) in the TACE-C-R group and increased ALT (14 of 103 [14%]) and increased AST (13 of 103 [13%]) in the TACE group (Table 2). Twenty-nine (31%) patients with TACE-C-R and three (3%) with TACE experienced serious TRAEs. Three (3%) patients in the TACE-C-R group died because of TRAEs, including one sepsis (possibly related to TACE), one cerebral hemorrhage (possibly related to rivoceranib), and one GI hemorrhage (undetermined relationship with rivoceranib). One (1%) patient in the TACE group died because of hepatic failure (possibly related to TACE).

DISCUSSION

This trial met its primary end point, demonstrating a significant improvement in PFS with TACE-C-R versus TACE alone in patients with unresectable HCC. The addition of camrelizumab plus rivoceranib to TACE resulted in a 7.6-month improvement in median PFS and a 66% reduction in the risk of progression across the entire study population, with consistent trends favoring TACE-C-R observed in various subgroups. Improvements in TTUP and ORR were also noted in the total population. These findings highlight the clinical impact of this combination strategy in inhibiting tumor growth and delaying disease progression. The most common grade ≥3 TRAEs were increased AST and increased ALT, which were gradually subsided, indicating tolerance.

The pattern for HCC management varies regionally.23 According to the BCLC staging system, HCC patients with macrovascular invasion are classified as advanced stage, for whom systemic therapy is generally recommended.24 However, under the CNLC staging system, the BCLC C stage is stratified as IIIa and IIIb based on the absence or presence of extrahepatic metastases. CNLC stage IIIa disease (with macrovascular invasion but no extrahepatic metastases) is eligible for TACE, which is supported by several local guidelines and expert consensuses.3,4,25,26 While the study design, eligibility criteria, and primary end point results of the TALENTACE trial were briefly disclosed,14 the baseline characteristics have not yet been formally published. In comparison with the EMERALD-1 and LEAP-012 trials, our study enrolled a population that might have highly worse prognosis, with macrovascular invasion (42% v 7% in EMERALD-1 v 0% in LEAP-012) and high tumor burden (beyond up-to-7 criteria: 90% v 52% in EMERALD-1).12,13 Notably, our study was the sole one including patients with Vp3-4 tumor thrombus (27%). These differences might explain the lower median PFS with TACE alone in our study compared with the other three trials. Furthermore, exploratory subgroup analyses based on BCLC stage consistently demonstrated greater PFS benefit with the addition of camrelizumab and rivoceranib to TACE.

Progression in the primary end point of this trial was defined as TTUP based on the RECICL criteria. Although mRECIST and RECIST 1.1 are widely recognized and were used in EMERALD-1 and LEAP-012,12,13 RECIST 1.1 solely relies on tumor size reduction without accounting for tumor necrosis,27 a critical aspect of response to locoregional therapies. By contrast, RECICL and mRECIST criteria incorporate tumor necrosis, offering a more comprehensive assessment of therapeutic efficacy in HCC.20,21 In addition, given the multicentric nature and intrahepatic metastases characteristic of HCC, the appearance of new intrahepatic lesions in untreated areas is not initially classified as “progressive disease” according to the RECICL criteria as these lesions typically reflect the natural biology of HCC rather than therapeutic failure. Importantly, the primary end point used in our study further integrated clinically relevant factors such as liver function, tumor markers, and TACE failure or refractoriness, providing a more accurate reflection of real-world clinical scenarios and therapeutic responses to TACE-based combination therapy. The TALENTACE trial adopted the same composite criteria as the assessment approach for the primary end point PFS. The results demonstrated the superiority of the experimental arm, whether by composite criteria or RECIST 1.1.14 This supports the definition of our primary end point.

A maximum of four TACE procedures were performed in EMERALD-1, whereas up to two TACE procedures per tumor were restricted in LEAP-012.12,13 In our study, on-demand TACE was administered according to a standardized protocol,4,10,26 consistent with the TALENTACE trial.14 The combination mode of TACE and systemic therapy also differed across studies. Durvalumab was initiated at least 7 days after the first TACE procedure, and bevacizumab was introduced at least 14 days after completing TACE in EMERALD-1.12 Pembrolizumab and lenvatinib were administered concurrently 2 to 4 weeks before TACE in LEAP-012.13 Atezolizumab and bevacizumab were initiated within 14 days to 8 weeks after the first TACE procedure in TALENTACE.14 In our study, patients received camrelizumab plus rivoceranib within 2 weeks after the first TACE procedure. The optimal timing of immunotherapy-based systemic therapy when combined with TACE is an important direction for future research.

Recently, it was announced that the LEAP-012 trial will be closed because of the low likelihood of achieving the protocol-specified threshold for OS at the future analysis.28 However, this study can still be considered as positive under its dual primary end point design since achieving statistical significance for PFS at the first interim analysis (which serves as the final analysis for PFS) would fulfill the criteria for a positive trial.13 The comparison of OS can be confounded by subsequent antitumor therapies after disease progression, especially in the context of the LEAP-012 trial, which enrolled a higher proportion (47% in the lenvatinib plus pembrolizumab plus TACE group and 48% in the placebo plus TACE group) of patients with low tumor burden (tumor burden score ≤6). In this study, although the median OS was estimated, the number of OS events was indeed insufficient to draw definitive conclusions. The high crossover rate of patients with disease progression from the TACE group to the TACE-C-R group might have further confounded the OS comparison. A comprehensive analysis of OS should be conducted once mature OS data become available in the future. The long-term data from the EMERALD-1 and the TALENTACE trials are also awaited for whether the addition of ICIs and antiangiogenic agents to TACE can improve OS.

The adverse event profile of TACE-C-R was consistent with the known safety profiles of each part of this triplet combination and of camrelizumab combined with rivoceranib.15,29,30 No new safety signals were identified. The most common grade ≥3 TRAEs were hepatic function abnormalities, hypertension, and decreased platelet count, which were also the predominant grade ≥3 TRAEs observed with camrelizumab plus rivoceranib in CARES-310.15 These events were generally reversible with symptomatic treatments and dose adjustments, which did not lead to a high discontinuation rate of both drugs. One patient in each group died because of TACE-related adverse event, whereas another two patients in the TACE-C-R group died as a result of rivoceranib-related hemorrhage events.

The significance of our study lies in the inclusion of a high proportion of patients with CNLC stage IIIa HCC (equivalent to the BCLC C stage without extrahepatic metastases), particularly those with Vp3-4 tumor thrombus, who were not eligible in EMERALD-1, LEAP-012, or TALENTACE.12-14 These patients could derive benefit from TACE combined with ICIs and antiangiogenic agents. In addition, a higher proportion of patients in the TACE group experienced BCLCp-C2 progression (new extrahepatic lesions and/or vascular invasion),24 which further supports the use of combination strategies in patients with high tumor burden and aggressive disease characteristics. The large-scale CHANCE2201 study has provided additional real-world evidence by directly comparing such a combination strategy with standard systemic therapy (ICIs plus antiangiogenic agents) in patients with advanced HCC.11 Collectively, these studies support the notion that TACE combined with ICIs and antiangiogenic agents may represent a promising treatment option for patients with unresectable HCC, including the BCLC C stage without extrahepatic metastases. In this context, we believe that CHANCE-2005/CARES-005 supplements critical evidence supporting this treatment strategy.

There are several limitations in this study. First, this study was open-label and efficacy was evaluated by individual investigational sites rather than a blinded independent review committee, which might introduce assessment bias. Second, this study was conducted exclusively in Chinese patients, mostly with hepatitis B virus–related HCC. Whether the results can be generalized to other populations with different etiologies needs further investigation. Third, the use of composite criteria allowed for more flexible investigator discretion in selecting postprogression treatment crossover for patients in the TACE group. Finally, the final OS benefit needs to be further determined given the immature data.

In conclusion, this trial demonstrated statistically significant improvement in PFS with the addition of camrelizumab and rivoceranib to TACE for patients with unresectable HCC and the safety profile was manageable. Follow-up is ongoing to confirm OS benefit with this combination strategy.

APPENDIX 1. SUPPLEMENTAL METHODS

Participants

Key exclusion criteria included the presence of portal vein tumor thrombus reaching or extending to the main trunk of the portal vein accompanied by complete occlusion of the main trunk of the portal vein or complete occlusion of both the left and right branches of the portal vein, history of immunotherapy or hepatic artery infusion chemotherapy, significant clinically meaningful bleeding within 6 months before random assignment or definite GI bleeding tendency (such as severe esophagogastric varices, locally active peptic ulcer, and vasculitis), uncontrolled hypertension, and active or history of autoimmune disease.

Outcomes

Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as time from random assignment to progression or any-cause death, whichever occurred first. Time to transarterial chemoembolization (TACE)–untreatable progression (TTUP) was defined as time from random assignment to intrahepatic progression per Response Evaluation Criteria in Cancer of the Liver (RECICL) version 5, liver function deterioration to Child-Pugh class C within 1 week of TACE, or TACE failure or refractoriness. Overall survival (OS) was defined as time from random assignment to any-cause death. ORR was defined as the proportion of patients with the best overall response of complete or partial response. Disease control rate (DCR) was defined as the proportion of patients with the best overall response of complete response, partial response, or stable disease. Duration of response (DoR) was defined as time from the first documented response per RECICL version 5 and modified RECIST (mRECIST) to progression per composite criteria and mRECIST, respectively, or any-cause death, whichever occurred first.

Statistical Analysis

Median PFS, TTUP, OS, and DoR were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and the corresponding 95% CIs were estimated using the Brookmeyer-Crowley method. PFS was compared between groups using the log-rank test stratified by random assignment stratification factors (excluding previous tyrosine kinase inhibitor treatment because of very limited pretreated patients). Hazard ratios and the corresponding 95% CIs were estimated using the stratified Cox proportional hazard model. The 95% CIs of ORR and DCR were estimated using the Clopper-Pearson method. Post hoc analyses of PFS, ORR, DCR, and DoR (defined as time from the first documented response per RECIST 1.1 to progression per RECIST 1.1 or any-cause death, whichever occurred first) per RECIST 1.1 were analyzed using the same methods as mentioned above. Post hoc subgroup analyses of PFS are presented as forest plots. Post hoc sensitivity analyses of PFS were performed after excluding all patients with any previous anticancer treatment.

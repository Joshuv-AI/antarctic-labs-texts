Management of De-Quervain's tenosynovitis by Agnikarma with Suvarana Shalaka: A case report
Authors: Poonam Garg, Manorma Singh, Suman Sharma, Rahul Sharma, Bharati Samota
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDe-Quervain's tenosynovitis is a painful condition characterized by wrist pain and swelling at the radial styloid process due to inflammation and thickening of the tendon sheath of the abductor pollicis longus and extensor pollicis brevis. Conventional treatment includes rest, analgesics, corticosteroid injections, and surgical release. In Ayurveda, this condition is correlated with Snayugata Vata, where Agnikarma (therapeutic heat) is recommended. A 55-year-old female presented with severe left wrist pain, swelling and difficulty in performing household work for 20 days. Finkelstein's test result was positive, with tenderness of grade-3 (withdrawal sign) and visible swelling. She underwent three sessions of Agnikarma using Suvarana Shalaka (gold rod) in a linear Bindu (point) pattern at weekly intervals. Outcomes were assessed using the visual analogue scale (VAS), Quick disability of the Arm, Shoulder and Hand (Quick DASH) score, and soft tissue tenderness scale. Complete pain relief and functional recovery of the wrist were achieved and sustained over an eight-month follow up period. Agnikarma alleviates Sroto-Avrodha(obstruction) enhancing Rasa-Raktadi Dhatu Samvahana (nutrient flow) and tissue extensibility. Suvarana Shalaka delivers continuous therapeutic heat through the conduction method without causing Dagdha Vrana (burn or blisters). This case report serves as a lead for future researches on the efficacy of Agnikarma with Suvarana Shalaka on Snayugata Vikara. It also suggests Agnikarma is a safe, cost-effective, day-care, drug-less treatment that obviates reliance on long-term use of analgesics and anti-inflammatory drugs.Keywords: Agnikarma, Case report, Snayugatavata, Suvarana Shalaka, Tenosynovitis

Introduction

De-Quervain's tenosynovitis, is stenosing tenosynovitis affecting the first extensor compartment of the wrist. It arises from thickening and myxoid degeneration of the tendon sheath of the abductor pollicis longus and extensor pollicis brevis, which are prone to entrapment, especially in cases of acute trauma or repetitive motion [1]. The repetitive grasping and twisting motions of the wrist exacerbate inflammation and restrict wrist functions of the dominant hand. Clinically, the condition is characterized by radial-sided wrist pain and tenderness that may progress to substantial disability [2]. The reported incidence of De-Quervain's tenosynovitis range from 0.31 to 0.94 per 1000 individuals, with a higher prevalence in females, mostly affecting the dominant hand [3]. In Ayurveda, this disease can be understood in the context of ‘Snayugata Vata’. The clinical features of which simulate a great deal with this disease entity [4].

In contemporary science, initial management typically involves conservative treatment such as oral and topical anti-inflammatory drugs, immobilization of the wrist with a thumb spica splint, and corticosteroid injections [5]. Where conservative treatments fail, surgical intervention is considered viz. De- Quervain release that is associated with potential risks and complications [6].

In Ayurveda, Agnikarma is specifically indicated for the management of Snayugata Vata [7,8].

Even with adequate management, relapses are inevitable in the contemporary system. This necessitates an alternative approach that offers better outcomes and sustained relief.

Agnikarma is a parasurgical procedure in which therapeutic heat is used. Various Dahana-Upkarana (materials used to transfer therapeutic heat) have been mentioned in Ayurvedic texts. They are selected based on their Dhatu level [9], physical properties, tissue penetration, and heat retention capabilities. It offers sustained relief in conditions that cannot be treated with oral medicine, Kshara Karma (alkaline substance application), or even with Shalya Karma (surgery) [10]. Here, Suvarana Shalaka (gold rod) was used as Dahana-upkarana. Being thermostable, it retains heat longer than other metals and exhibits minimal tissue reactivity, as therapeutic heat is delivered via conduction rather than direct application.

In this case report, De-Quervain's tenosynovitis was managed by Agnikarma with Suvarana Shalaka. It offers sustained pain relief and improves tissue circulation. Agnikarma is an immediate, drug-less, cosmetically favorable, long-lasting, minimally invasive day-care procedure.

Patient information

Case report

A 55-year-old female, homemaker visited the OPD, XYZ hospital, with complaints of pain in her left wrist radiating up to the thumb associated with visible and palpable swelling at the left radial styloid process for the last 20 days. The pain was aggravated by movements such as gripping, lifting, and twisting. No numbness or tingling sensations were reported in this area. There was no history of trauma, musculoskeletal disorders or systemic illness. However, she was engaged in daily strenuous household tasks, including cleaning heavy utensils, scrubbing floors, and lifting water buckets. She was diagnosed and initially treated. Following minimal clinical improvement, she presented to our hospital for further management.

Clinical findings

General examination

The patient weighed approximately 65 kg. The blood pressure was 132/76 mm Hg. The pulse rate was 86 beats/min, and the respiratory rate was 18 breaths per minute. The patient was afebrile.

Local examination

On inspection, the patient had visible swelling when compared with the contra-lateral wrist. No discoloration or scarring was observed.

On palpation, tenderness was localized to the radial styloid process of grade-3(with withdrawal sign), according to the soft tissue tenderness scale [11]. Palpable swelling of approximately 1 × 1 cm in size was observed at the radial styloid process. The Finkelstein's test was found positive as shown in Figure [3]. No crepitus was observed. Ulnar deviation and thumb flexion were restricted. The intensity of pain was rated 9/10 on the VAS scale [12]. Functional activity was 27/50 as assessed by the Quick DASH score [13].

Diagnostic assessment

The diagnosis of De-Quervain's tenosynovitis was confirmed based on the patient's medical history and clinical examination. On clinical examination, the pain was aggravated by adducting the thumb across the palm and forcing ulnar deviation against resistance. The Finkelstein's test was positive in confirming the diagnosis. No unusual changes were observed on radiographs (Antero-posterior and lateral views) of the left wrist joint.

Therapeutic intervention

Purva Karma (Pre-procedure)

Informed consent was obtained. The patient was positioned supine in the OPD couch. First, the tender spots were marked as shown in Fig. 1. Painting and draping were then performed.

Marking; most tender spots.

Pradhana Karma (procedure)

Bindu-type(dot) Agnikarma [14] was performed using the Suvarana Shalaka. The rounded tip (∼2 mm diameter) applied to tender spots as illustrated in Fig. 2. Therapeutic heat was transmitted continuously from an ignited candle(source) for approximately 40–50 seconds per spot, within the patient's tolerance, to avoid any thermal injury. Five tender spots (each ∼2 mm in diameter) were treated similarly. The total duration of each session was approximately 5–6 minutes. No skin discoloration was seen.

Agnikarma with Suvarana Shalaka.

Paschata Karma (Post-procedure)

Fresh Ghritkumari (Aloevera) leaf pulp was applied to soothe and mitigate the burning sensation as shown in Fig. 3. Three sessions of Agnikarma were carried out in the same manner at equal intervals of one week until the patient became symptom free. No oral medications were prescribed during treatment and follow-up.

Post-procedure topical application of Ghritkumari (Aleovera Pulp).

Follow-up and outcomes

There were four follow-up visits following the last session of Agnikarma. The first two follow-ups were conducted every 15 days interval. The third was after a one-month interval of the second follow-up. The last follow-up was done after 6 months of third follow-up. At first follow-up, the patient reported pain relief with no tenderness (grade-0) or swelling. The Finkelstein's test was negative. Despite clinical improvement, she refrained from heavy wrist activity due to concern over recurrence. At the second follow-up, the patient had no pain or tenderness (VAS score of 0/10, Tenderness scale Grade −0), and the patient was able to perform daily activities without any fear of relapse. The swelling had completely subsided. At the third follow-up, the patient was pain-free, with a negative Finkelstein test. The quality of life completely improved in relation to the functions of the affected wrist. No undue adverse effects were noted. The patient experienced no symptoms or any adverse effects at the final follow-up 6 months after the third follow-up. Discussion

Disease (Snayugata Vata)

In Ayurveda, De Quervain's tenosynovitis can be correlated with Snayugata Vata, where vitiated Vata affecting the Snayu leads to Stambha (stiffness), Shoola (pain), and Aksepanam (spasm), closely resembling its clinical features [4]. Although the exact pathophysiology of Snayugata Vata is not detailed in classical texts, it is understood as a form of Vata Vyadhi. Nidana Sevana [15] (Visama Chesta(erractic action), Ati Chesta(hyperactivity) and Vega Sandharna(suppression of natural urges) causes Dhatu Kshaya which results in vitiation of Vata Dosha and Marga Avarana (blocking channels) i.e. Kapha envelops Vyana Vayu [16]. This causes accumulation of Doshas at the site of Kha-Vaguniya which results in Guruta(heaviness), Ruja(pain), Sotha(swelling), Gati Sanga/Karma Kshaya(Restricted or diminished movements) results in manifestation of De-Quervain's Tenosynovitis.

Probable mechanism of Agnikarma

Probable mode of action (modern perspective)

Agnikarma (therapeutic heat application) induces a controlled rise in local temperature, resulting in a mild inflammatory response at the targeted site. This stimulation activates the thermoregulatory center, leading to vasodilatation and enhanced microcirculation. Consequently, there is improved blood flow with increased supply of nutrients and more efficient clearance of metabolic waste, thereby facilitating tissue repair and regeneration [10]. Its mechanism of action involves modulation at the site of tissue injury as well as activation of the Descending Pain Inhibitory System, which causes release of Endogenous opioid peptides followed by inhibition of P-substance and blockage of pain sensation.

Probable mode of action (Ayurvedic perspective)

In Ayurvedic literatures, Agnikarma is indicated as a therapeutic modality for managing painful conditions caused by vitiated Vata, particularly in disorders involving Snayu (ligaments and tendons), referred to as Snayugata Vata.

Teekshana(sharp), Ushna(hot), Sukshama (Penetrative), Ashukari (quick-acting) properties pacifies Kapha Avarana (removal of blockage of body channels) and Vata Dosha. It enhances Rasa-Raktadi Dhatu Samvahana and Dhatvagni(tissue metabolism). Improved Dhatvagni facilitates Ama Pachana (elimination of metabolic toxins) and promotes proper nourishment and regeneration of Dhatu and Upadhatu. These effects help reduce pain (Ruja), swelling, and tissue degeneration, thereby accelerating the healing process and improved Karma Shakti(Functional activity).

Conduction method

In conduction-based Agnikarma, therapeutic heat is transmitted from the source to the Dahana Upakarana, enabling precise and consistent thermal delivery. This indirect method ensures controlled energy dispersion and temperature uniformity, which are crucial for its efficacy. When executed with optimal heat regulation and timing, it maximizes therapeutic outcomes and patient comfort, thus establishing it as a superior technique.

Suvarana Shalaka and it's Probable mode of action

In Agnikarma, Suvarana Shalaka was used as Dahana-Upakarana. In Ayurvedic texts, repeated Agnikarma with Suvarana Shalaka is indicated after excision, in Kaphaja Arbuda. The gold rod used in this procedure was made up of 14 karat gold, measuring 7 cm in length with a 2 mm rounded tip. Owing to the high thermal conductivity of gold, the heat transfer during Agnikarma remains well-controlled, thereby minimizing tissue reactivity, reducing the risk of inadvertent burns. The localized tissue response to Agnikarma performed with Suvarna Shalaka is yet to be established. However, a few grey literatures have documented the impact of Agnikarma with Suvarana Shalaka on local tissues as in the case of cervical erosion, where complete healing was observed. This study documented healthy regeneration of squamous epithelium over the eroded area, ultimately leading to complete tissue recovery. Furthermore, the application of Agnikarma with a gold rod has been observed to enhance patient comfort and alleviate procedural anxiety when compared to other metallic Shalakas.

Assessment scale

Evaluation of treatment outcomes in De Quervain's tenosynovitis necessitates precise and sensitive assessment tools. The VAS, Quick DASH and Soft tissue tenderness scales effectively quantify pain intensity, tenderness and functional impairment. These scales offer concise, specific, and practical measures for outpatient follow-up [12] [13] [11].

As this is a single case report, the findings cannot be generalized. Proper standardization of the heating temperature and duration of Suvarna Shalaka is necessary to avoid burns or suboptimal results, while the cost and limited availability of gold instruments may restrict its wider use. Further large-scale randomized controlled studies are needed to compare the efficacy and safety of Agnikarma with conventional and other Ayurvedic treatments, along with evaluation of long-term outcomes and recurrence rates. The therapeutic effects of gold contact on body tissues also warrant further investigation.

Agnikarma, as part of Ayurvedic management, effectively reduced pain and improved functional outcomes in a patient with de-Quervain's tenosynovitis. This case report supports the efficacy of Ayurvedic management of painful musculoskeletal disorders. Agnikarma is an immediate, drug-less, minimally invasive, long-lasting, cost-effective, cosmetically favorable, and day-care alternative to conventional treatments that obviates reliance on the long-term use of analgesics and anti-inflammatory drugs.

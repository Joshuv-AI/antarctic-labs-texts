IFPHOR0096 Students as Changemakers: Fostering Interdisciplinary Engagement in Sustainable Healthcare
Authors: Harshini Suresh, Ashley Thian, Lana Kaedbey
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractUnderstanding the relationship between planetary health and human health is pivotal for the long-term sustainability of our healthcare systems. An audit of the Planetary Health Report Card (2025) revealed low levels of student engagement in sustainability in healthcare, despite discourse within public health.A student-led, cross-faculty conference aimed to encourage interdisciplinary collaboration in sustainability. This event featured an exhibition, showcasing perspectives on the future of healthcare. We used a mixed-methods program evaluation to assess the impact of this event within healthcare sustainability education.Data was collected concurrently via surveys (n = 50) and interviews (n = 20). Likert scale elements and qualitative free-text responses were implemented in conference feedback. Paired comparisons of means were used to analyse knowledge gained by participants, while thematic analysis was used to understand participant perspectives.Key findings showed a 26% increase in participants’ understanding of sustainability in healthcare. Emergent themes included “increased awareness of practical applications, “empowerment to engage with sustainability issues” and “appreciation for collaborative learning environments”. Exhibit feedback featured that a fixed 10-year timeline prompted reflection on current practices, goal-setting and improved collective responsibility in sustainability. It allowed for comparative analysis of engagement across different teaching methods used. Limitations included participant preference for increased specialised content depth, as well as overall number of attendants to the event.This conference provides replicable models for peer-led interdisciplinary collaboration within sustainability and education. Collected feedback allows for refinement of artefacts used within the conference to be incorporated into future sustainable-healthcare curricula while improving delivery of similar upcoming events.

Efficient cardiac MRI multi-structure segmentation for cardiovascular assessment with limited annotation by integrating data-level and network-level consistency
Authors: Sicong Guo, Xinyi Zhao, Junhong Ren, Minakshmi Shaw, Jiye Wan, Jianyao Su
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate segmentation of anatomical structures in cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) plays an irreplaceable role in the clinical management of cardiovascular diseases, serving as a cornerstone for precise diagnosis, individualized treatment planning, and long-term prognosis assessment. Although deep learning techniques have demonstrated promising performance in achieving automatic segmentation of cardiac MRI anatomical structures, their heavy reliance on large-scale labeled datasets for model training presents notable challenges in the field of cardiac imaging, as the annotations can only be provided by medical specialists with extensive experience. Against this backdrop, this work proposes a mutual ensemble framework integrating data-level and network-level consistency for semi-supervised learning to utilize limited labeled and abundant unlabeled data. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our approach can successfully harness unlabeled data to improve performance, outperforming existing segmentation methods under the same conditions.Subject terms: Cardiology, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Health care, Mathematics and computing, Medical research

Introduction

Analyzing cardiac function is central to clinical cardiology, guiding early diagnosis, risk stratification, treatment planning, and prognosis evaluation1. Quantitative functional parameters such as ventricular volumes, ejection fraction, myocardial mass, and wall motion are essential for the detection and longitudinal monitoring of cardiovascular diseases (CVDs), including heart failure, cardiomyopathies, ischemic heart disease, and valvular disorders. Accurate functional assessment enables the identification of subtle myocardial abnormalities before the onset of overt clinical symptoms, thereby facilitating timely intervention. Cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (CMR) has emerged as a gold-standard, non-invasive modality for comprehensive cardiac assessment due to its high spatial resolution, excellent tissue contrast, and multi-parametric imaging capabilities. It allows precise quantification of global and regional cardiac function, as well as myocardial tissue characterization2,3. Advanced CMR techniques, such as late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) and T1/T2 mapping, further provide detailed insights into myocardial fibrosis and microstructural abnormalities. In clinical practice, accurate and reproducible segmentation of cardiac structures is critical, as it directly underpins the derivation of key functional parameters used for diagnosis and treatment planning. Automated segmentation methods can substantially improve efficiency and consistency by reducing manual workload and inter-observer variability4.

The advent of deep learning has profoundly transformed the landscape of medical image segmentation. Convolutional neural network (CNN)-based architectures, exemplified by U-Net5 and its numerous derivatives6–8, have demonstrated remarkable success across a wide range of medical image analysis tasks. Nevertheless, CNN-based methods inherently struggle to capture long-range contextual dependencies due to the locality of convolutional operations and the limited receptive fields of convolutional filters. To overcome this constraint, self-attention mechanisms introduced in Vision Transformers (ViTs)9 have emerged as a powerful alternative, enabling the modeling of global interactions across spatially distant regions within an image. This paradigm shift has spurred growing interest in hybrid architectures that integrate the representational strengths of CNNs and ViTs for medical image segmentation10–12, achieving more comprehensive and anatomically consistent delineation of complex structures such as the heart.

Despite these methodological advances, the development of high-performance deep learning models for medical image segmentation remains constrained by the scarcity of annotated data. Generating high-quality pixel-level annotations is a labor-intensive and time-consuming process that demands substantial expertise from experienced clinicians13,14. In contrast to other imaging modalities and anatomical regions, such as abdominal CT, where large-scale, publicly available datasets exist15,16, annotated cardiac MRI datasets remain relatively limited due to the high costs and technical challenges associated with data acquisition and labeling. Furthermore, the diversity of imaging protocols, scanner vendors, and patient populations introduces significant domain variability, further complicating the development of generalizable segmentation models. These challenges are particularly pronounced in 3D volumetric data, where manual annotation of a single case may require several hours of expert effort17,18. Given the abundance of unlabeled medical images and the prohibitive cost of manual labeling, there is an urgent need for efficient annotation and learning strategies that can leverage limited labeled data alongside large-scale unlabeled datasets to train robust segmentation models.

In light of these challenges, semi-supervised learning has emerged as a compelling strategy for advancing medical image segmentation19, particularly in domains such as cardiac MRI, where expert-labeled data are scarce. By combining a limited set of annotated images with a larger pool of unlabeled data, semi-supervised approaches aim to maximize the diagnostic value of existing imaging resources while reducing the dependence on extensive manual labeling. This paradigm not only alleviates the burden on clinicians but also accelerates the development of robust, data-efficient segmentation models capable of supporting large-scale clinical deployment. One prominent semi-supervised strategy involves pseudo-labeling, a process where models generate provisional segmentation masks for unlabeled images, which are subsequently treated as ground truth to supervise iterative training. While this approach effectively leverages vast amounts of unlabeled data to expand the training set, the reliability of these pseudo-labels remains a critical bottleneck. Specifically, inaccuracies in model-generated annotations can lead to confirmation bias, where the model reinforces its own prediction errors, thereby introducing significant noise and uncertainty into the learning process20. To mitigate the propagation of such errors, recent research has extensively explored unsupervised regularization and consistency-based learning frameworks. These methods typically employ perturbation-invariant constraints or mean-teacher architectures to encourage the model to produce stable, consistent predictions across varying input conditions and noise levels21,22. Furthermore, advanced frameworks integrate dual-model mutual learning and shape-aware priors to filter out low-confidence predictions and ensure that the generated segmentation maps remain anatomically plausible23–26.

To address these challenges, our study proposes a semi-supervised framework tailored for cardiac MRI segmentation that leverages both labeled and unlabeled data to achieve accurate and robust delineation of cardiac structures. Unlike purely supervised models that require extensive expert annotation, our approach is designed to maximize the utility of existing unlabeled clinical datasets, thereby reducing the annotation burden while maintaining high segmentation precision. By integrating complementary learning mechanisms that capture both local structural details and global anatomical context, the proposed framework enhances the reliability of functional quantification across diverse patient populations and imaging protocols.

From a clinical standpoint, the adoption of semi-supervised learning frameworks holds substantial promise. By efficiently leveraging vast repositories of unlabeled imaging data routinely generated in hospitals, these methods can substantially enhance segmentation accuracy and reproducibility without demanding proportionate increases in annotation effort. Improved automatic delineation of cardiac structures directly contributes to more precise quantification of ventricular function, myocardial mass, and wall motion—parameters fundamental to diagnosing cardiomyopathies, assessing treatment response, and monitoring disease progression. Semi-supervised learning represents an important step toward annotation-efficient, scalable, and clinically applicable AI systems in cardiac imaging. Through improved generalization and reduced reliance on expert labeling, such approaches have the potential to bridge the gap between advanced image analysis algorithms and real-world clinical implementation, thereby enhancing both diagnostic precision and workflow efficiency in cardiovascular care.

Results

Experimental setup

To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed framework, we conduct comprehensive experiments on the publicly available ACDC dataset3, which contains short-axis cardiac MR images from 100 patients, comprising a total of 200 annotated 3D volumes. Each case includes manual segmentation masks for three key cardiac structures: the left ventricle (LV), the myocardium (Myo), and the right ventricle (RV). Following standard practice, the dataset is randomly divided into training and validation subsets, with 140 MR images (from 70 patients) used for training and the remaining 60 MR images (from 30 patients) reserved for validation. Due to the large inter-slice spacing and anisotropy of the 3D images, we adopt a 2D slice-wise segmentation strategy. Each 3D volume is preprocessed into 2D slices, which are resized to a resolution of 224 × 224 pixels, and their intensity values are normalized to the range [0, 1]. During inference, the 2D segmentation predictions are generated on a slice-by-slice basis and subsequently reassembled into 3D volumes to facilitate volumetric evaluation.

In the proposed framework, two complementary network architectures are employed as segmentation backbones: a CNN-based U-Net5 and a ViT-based Swin-UNet27, as detailed in section “Backbone network architectures”. To ensure a fair comparison, we adopt the publicly available implementation provided in ref. 28. All experiments are implemented in Python using the PyTorch library. The models are trained with a batch size of 16, where half of the samples are labeled, and the other half are unlabeled for semi-supervised learning. Optimization is performed using stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with an initial learning rate of 0.01 and a momentum of 0.9. To balance the supervised segmentation loss and the unsupervised consistency loss, a Gaussian ramp-up weighting function is employed, defined as λ=0.1*e−5(1−t/tmax), where t represents the current number of iterations and tmax represents the maximum number of iterations. This adaptive weighting strategy gradually increases the influence of the unsupervised term, allowing the model to benefit progressively from the unlabeled data as training stabilizes.

Discussion

Accurate and efficient segmentation of cardiac structures from MRI remains a longstanding challenge in cardiovascular imaging due to the high cost and expertise required for manual annotation. While deep learning-based methods have shown remarkable potential in automating this process, their dependence on large-scale labeled datasets limits their applicability in clinical practice, where annotated data are often scarce. To address this issue, our study introduces a semi-supervised framework that effectively leverages both labeled and unlabeled cardiac MRI data through mutual ensembling of CNN and ViT architectures.

The proposed framework integrates data-level and network-level consistency learning to fully exploit the information embedded in unlabeled data. Specifically, the combination of cross-supervision between heterogeneous backbones and self-ensembling within each model branch enables robust feature learning under limited supervision. The hybrid architecture capitalizes on the strengths of CNNs for local feature extraction and ViTs for global context modeling, allowing the model to more effectively capture both fine-grained details and broader spatial relationships. This combination contributes significantly to the framework’s ability to perform accurate segmentation even with limited labeled data. Our experimental results on the ACDC dataset demonstrate that SemiCoTr achieves superior segmentation accuracy compared with state-of-the-art semi-supervised methods, even when trained with as little as 5% labeled data. The ablation analyses further validate that both the multi-loss design and the hybrid CNN-ViT structure contribute synergistically to the observed performance gains.

From a clinical standpoint, the significance of these findings is twofold. First, by reducing the dependency on extensive manual annotations, our framework substantially enhances the efficiency and scalability of model development, making it feasible to train accurate segmentation networks using limited expert-labeled data supplemented by large volumes of routine clinical scans. Second, the improved segmentation precision directly benefits cardiac functional assessment, enabling more reliable quantification of ventricular volumes, myocardial mass, and wall motion, which are critical indicators for the diagnosis and management of heart diseases.

Despite the promising outcomes achieved by our method, there remain several avenues for future improvement. For instance, integrating active learning strategies could help identify the most informative samples from unlabeled datasets32, thereby improving both efficiency and accuracy in the training process. Furthermore, incorporating uncertainty estimation mechanisms could provide valuable insights into the model’s confidence in its predictions, enhancing interpretability and clinical reliability33–35. In addition to active learning and uncertainty modeling, another promising direction is the integration of pre-trained foundation models36,37, such as the Segment Anything Model (SAM)38,39 and Vision-Language Models40 into the semi-supervised framework. Leveraging such models allows the framework to benefit from rich prior knowledge learned from large-scale, diverse datasets, which can substantially improve segmentation performance in scenarios with extremely limited labeled data. By using SAM as an auxiliary model or a source of high-quality pseudo-labels, the network can achieve more accurate and anatomically consistent delineation while further reducing the reliance on costly expert annotations41. This approach also opens the possibility of rapid adaptation to new datasets or imaging protocols, enhancing the generalizability and scalability of semi-supervised medical image segmentation.

Future research will focus on extending the proposed framework to large-scale multi-center and multi-modality datasets to improve its generalization across diverse imaging protocols, devices, and patient populations. The evaluation of our method on additional large-scale multi-center datasets with diverse cardiac pathologies will be pursued in future work to systematically assess its cross-device and cross-disease generalization capability. Although the current study is conducted on a single cardiac imaging modality, the proposed framework could be adapted to other cardiac imaging settings, such as delayed enhancement-MRI2 and computed tomography angiographyagnostic42. Beyond imaging data, we also aim to explore the integration of multi-modal information, including radiology reports and structured patient clinical data, to further enhance cardiovascular analysis43. In addition, we plan to investigate the practical deployment of the segmentation results in real-world cardiovascular assessment scenarios.

In summary, the proposed semi-supervised framework bridges the gap between algorithmic performance and clinical practicality. By efficiently utilizing unlabeled imaging data and ensuring anatomically consistent delineation, SemiCoTr represents a promising step toward the development of annotation-efficient, generalizable, and clinically applicable AI systems for cardiac MRI analysis. Continued efforts toward integrating active learning, uncertainty modeling, and domain generalization will further advance the real-world deployment of AI-assisted tools, ultimately contributing to more precise diagnosis and personalized treatment in cardiovascular care.

This architecture approach allows us to capture diverse features and representation capabilities inherent in each model type. In the context of semi-supervised learning, we utilize self-ensembling techniques for both backbone networks. Consistency regularization plays a pivotal role here, ensuring that the outputs remain stable across different augmentations and between the two architectures. Backbone network architectures

Given the distinct learning paradigms of CNN and ViT for capturing local and global information, we leverage their respective advantages together in our SemiCoTr framework. The details of the backbone network architecture in our framework are introduced as follows:

CNN backbone

We use the U-Net5 with an encoder-decoder-based structure as the CNN backbone in our framework, as shown in Fig. 7a). The U-Net architecture consists of an encoder and a decoder, where the encoder utilizes information and the decoder up-samples the extracted features. The encoder part of the network performs down-sampling operations to extract feature information from the input image. It typically comprises two convolutional network layers with batch normalization, followed by a max pooling operation to reduce the spatial dimensions of the feature maps. This down-sampling process is repeated four times to capture hierarchical features at different scales. Each down-sampling operation reduces the spatial resolution of the feature maps while increasing the number of channels to capture more abstract representations. The decoder uses the high-level abstract features extracted by the encoder to perform up-sampling operations, gradually reconstructing the original resolution to generate the final segmentation result. It utilizes transposed convolutions or up-sampling layers to enhance the spatial resolution of the feature maps. Furthermore, it includes skip connections that link the feature maps from the corresponding levels of the encoder and decoder. This allows the decoder to access detailed spatial information from the earlier stages of the network. ViT backbone

We use the SwinUNet27, a UNet-like pure vision transformer as the ViT backbone in our framework, as shown in Fig 7a. The SwinUNet architecture integrates the Swin Transformer44 as the backbone for segmentation tasks. It employs a series of self-attention mechanisms to achieve powerful feature extraction capabilities for high-level representations, enabling the network to capture long-range dependencies and contextual information from the input image. The tokenized image patches are fed into a transformer-based U-shaped encoder-decoder architecture, equipped with skip connections for local-global semantic feature learning. Specifically, it utilizes a hierarchical Swin Transformer with shifted windows as the encoder to extract contextual features. At the same time, a symmetric Swin Transformer decoder with patch expansion layers is designed for upsampling and restoring the spatial resolution of the feature maps. Mutual ensembling framework for semi-supervised learning

As shown in Fig 6, our proposed framework is composed of two parallel segmentation networks based on CNN and ViT, to capture local and global information mutually from the original images. To utilize unlabeled data, our SemiCoTr framework incorporates both data-level and network-level perturbations mutually for consistency learning to ensure more efficient utilization of information. As summarized in Table 5, different semi-supervised segmentation frameworks adopt various strategies for data- and network-level perturbations. Our proposed SemiCoTr uniquely combines both within a unified mutual ensembling design. Specifically, for each backbone, we adopt a teacher model to ensemble the weights of the student models for consistency regularization to exploit data-level perturbations. Furthermore, we adopt the cross-supervision between two different backbone architectures together to exploit network-level perturbations.

Data-level perturbations: In our approach to consistency regularization within the framework of semi-supervised learning, we adopt a self-ensembling strategy inspired by the work of ref. 22. This method involves maintaining a teacher model whose weights are updated through an exponential moving average (EMA) of the student model’s weights. This setup allows us to effectively incorporate unlabeled data by promoting agreement between the outputs of the student and teacher models. Specifically, the teacher model is constructed as an average of the student model’s weights over various training epochs. This technique yields a more stable and accurate representation than relying solely on the final weights of the student model. Rather than directly sharing weights with the student model, the teacher model employs EMA for weight ensembling, represented mathematically as θt′=αθt−1′+(1−α)θt, in this equation, θ and θ′ represent the model weights of student model and teacher model, and α is the hyper-parameter of EMA decay. Through this self-ensembling mechanism, the student model learns from the teacher model by enforcing consistency in its output predictions. By aligning the student model’s outputs with those of the teacher model, we harness the benefits of both models, leading to improved performance in semi-supervised learning tasks. This synergy not only enhances the learning process but also ensures robustness against variations in the input data.

Implementation details

In the proposed framework, two heterogeneous segmentation networks are employed as described in the section “Backbone Network Architectures”, including a CNN-based model (UNet5) and a ViT-based model (Swin-UNet27), which are built upon fundamentally different learning paradigms. To ensure a fair and reproducible comparison, we adopt the unified open-source implementation provided in ref. 28 for both network architectures. All experiments are conducted using Python based on the PyTorch library. Data loading incorporates geometric augmentations such as random rotation, flipping, and cropping to enhance dataset diversity. A two-stream batch sampler is used to combine labeled and unlabeled data, ensuring a fixed ratio of labeled samples per batch. The batch size is set to 16, among which 8 samples are labeled for semi-supervised learning. Both networks are trained using the stochastic gradient descent (SGD) optimizer with an initial learning rate of 0.01 and a momentum of 0.9.

6633 Romosozumab Use in a Patient With Osteogenesis Imperfecta Type 3
Authors: Vedesh Kumar Babu, Sonal Phatak
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Abstract Disclosure: V.K. Babu: None. S. Phatak: None. Introduction: Osteogenesis Imperfecta (OI) is a rare genetic condition with qualitative and/or quantitative abnormalities in type 1 collagen. Its estimated prevalence ranges from 10,000 - 20,000 live births. Fractures are a hallmark feature of this condition. Biphosphosphonates have been widely studied in this patient population. Parathyroid Hormone (PTH) Analogues have also been used. Data from Sclerostin inhibitors is sparse and data from Denosumab is limited to children and pre-menopausal women. We present a post-menopausal female with Osteogenesis Imperfecta Type 3 who failed therapy with oral bisphosphonates, intravenous bisphosphonates, and eventually responded to Romosozumab followed by Denosumab. Clinical Case: This is a post-menopausal female with Osteogenesis Imperfecta Type 3 diagnosed at the age of 20 years. She has no similar family history, denied hearing issues, and reached menopause at 46 years of age. She received a combination of Alendronate, Risedronate and Zoledronic acid from 2004 (44 years old) to 2019 with the last dose of Zoledronic acid 5 mg in September 2019. She sustained multiple fragility fractures throughout that period, with the last one being a pelvis fracture in 2020. Her Serum PTH, Vitamin D, Albumin adjusted Calcium, Serum Phosphorus, Serum Protein Electrophoresis, and Celiac Panel were unremarkable. She was offered the option of continuing with bisphosphonate therapy, or switching to Romosozumab, PTH analogues, or Denosumab. She declined PTH analogues due to need for daily administration. The Sclerostin inhibitor (Scl-Ab), Romosozumab was started on July, 2021 for a duration of 12 months and switched to Denosumab, which is her current therapy. Her Bone Mineral Density (BMD) on Dual Energy Absorptiometry one year following the completion of Romosozumab therapy showed an increase at the Antero-Posterior Lumbar Spine of 20.7%, Left Femoral Neck of 35.0%, and Left Total Hip of 16.2% compared to two years prior with no reported fragility fractures since. Conclusion: Bisphosphonate therapy has been well-studied in patients with OI, especially type 1. Data on patients with OI type 3 is scarce. Our patient continued to sustain fractures despite therapy with oral and intravenous bisphosphonates prompting us to use Romosozumab, off-label. One case has been reported of Romosozumab use in a 65 year old man with OI type 1, with drastic (more than 20%) improvements noted in spine and hip BMD. Our patient with type 3 OI had comparable improvements in BMD with no fractures sustained since. In mouse models of OI, Scl-Ab improved the density, biomechanical strength, and osteoblastic apposition of long bones. A clinical trial with another Scl-Ab, Sertrusumab, for patients with OI types 1, 3, and 4 has been performed and results are awaited. Romosozumab can be considered off-label for patients with OI type 3 not responding to Bisphosphonate and/or PTH analogue therapy. Presentation: 6/2/2024

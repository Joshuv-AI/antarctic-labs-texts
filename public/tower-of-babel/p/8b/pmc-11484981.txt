Cross-cultural validation of the Spanish version of the Kidney AlloTransplant Immunosuppressive Therapy Adherence Questionnaire (KATITA-25)
Authors: Luana Cristina Lins de Medeiros Oliveira, Guillermo Pedreira-Robles, María José Pérez-Sáez, Marta Crespo, Anna Bach-Pascual, Sandra Rubio-Paez, Tania Curado-Soto, Alicia Rovira-Algara, Edoardo Melilli, Javier Jerez-Roig, Ester Oriol-Vila, Cristina Quintana Reyes, Maribel Diaz Jurado, Rand Randall Martins, Francisca Sueli Monte Moreira, Antonio Gouveia Oliveira
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe KATITA-25 (Kidney AlloTransplant Immunosuppressive Therapy Adherence) Questionnaire is a multidimensional self-administered scale developed in Brazilian Portuguese that measures the predisposition for non-adherence to immunosuppressive medication in candidate patients for kidney transplantation, in the pre-transplant setting. The aim of this study was the cross-cultural validation of the Spanish version of the KATITA-25 scale. The translation/back-translation method was used, followed by cognitive interviews and a pre-test. The Spanish version of KATITA-25 was administered to 163 candidate patients for kidney transplantation from 2 kidney transplant centres and 2 dialysis centres in Catalonia, Spain. The scale was re-administered to the first 79 patients included into the study, after a 2 to 4 weeks interval. Internal consistency was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha, test–retest reliability with the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC); construct validity with goodness of fit indices after confirmatory factor analysis using structural equation modelling. The Cronbach’s alpha of the Spanish version was 0.83, the ICC was 0.86. Confirmatory factor analysis showed adequate fit of the structural model. Overall, the analytical results closely resembled those obtained in the validation of the original scale. In conclusion, this cross-cultural validation study showed adequate reliability and construct validity of the Spanish version of the KATITA-25 questionnaire.Keywords: Kidney transplantation, Medication non-adherence, Psychometric scale, Cross-cultural validationSubject terms: Psychology, Health care, Nephrology

Introduction

Non-adherence to immunosuppressive therapy poses a significant clinical challenge, potentially leading to compromised renal graft function and loss1. This phenomenon is extremely common, as up to one-third of kidney transplant recipients may be nonadherent to immunosuppressive medications2, and the risk of nonadherence is known to increase over time3. However, some studies have indicated that 6 months after transplantation, 70% of recipients increasingly tend to skip doses4, and other studies have observed that nonadherence occurred from the third month after transplantation5,6.

Despite being a modifiable risk factor for adverse health outcomes7–11, medication non-adherence often evades detection or is recognized belatedly, when irreversible harm to the transplanted kidney has already ensued12,13. The intricate nature of measuring and identifying non-adherence primarily relies on patient self-reporting, further complicating detection efforts14. Numerous clinical questionnaires have emerged to identify medication non-adherence among patients15. However, these tools typically operate retrospectively, potentially missing the critical window for intervention before graft damage occurs. Typically, these tools entail structured interviews probing patients about instances of deviation from prescribed medication frequency, timing, and dosing.

The KATITA-25 scale, short for the Kidney AlloTransplant Immunosuppressive Therapy Adherence Questionnaire, originally developed in Brazilian Portuguese language, represents a new multidimensional self-administered questionnaire designed to gauge the propensity for non-adherence to potential immunosuppressive treatment among candidate patients for kidney transplantation in the pre-transplant setting. Diverging from conventional medication adherence assessment questionnaires that focus on instances of past non-compliance, KATITA-25 took a novel approach to medication adherence assessment by delving into the psychological factors that may influence patients’ attitudes toward medications to develop a psychometric scale aimed at evaluating patients based on their inclination towards non-compliance with prescribed medication.

A detailed description of the methodology for the development and validation of the original questionnaire was published in a separate paper16. The scale’s development involved a sample of 521 kidney transplant candidate patients from seven haemodialysis centres and six kidney pre-transplant outpatient clinics across three federal states in Brazil17. Administered as a self-administered pilot questionnaire, it comprised 72 statements, collected through literature review and patient focus groups, reflecting patients’ thoughts and feelings regarding medication-related behaviours, attitudes, beliefs, and barriers. Responses were rated on a 5-point Likert scale ranging from 0 (totally disagree) to 4 (totally agree). According to KATITA-25, three patient factors are primarily correlated to medication non-adherence. One factor pertains to unintentional non-adherence, encompassing instances of forgetfulness and a degree of indifference towards strict medication adherence. The remaining factors centre on intentional non-adherence, categorized as scepticism regarding medication effectiveness, and concern associated with medication safety. The total score of the instrument ranges from 0 to 100, with higher scores indicating a greater propensity for non-adherence to immunosuppressive therapy.

In a subsequent external validation study, 321 patients from six kidney transplant centres across three federal states in Brazil were enrolled18. These patients were recruited upon scheduling surgery and were monitored for a year. Their medication adherence to immunosuppressants was assessed at the 6- and 12-month marks using three independent methods: trough serum level of immunosuppressants, a self-report adherence scale, and medication refill records. This study focused on evaluating both construct and criterion validity. It demonstrated satisfactory concurrent validity through comparison with two existing scales, indicating alignment with established measures. Furthermore, the study showcased adequate predictive validity, highlighting the scale’s ability to forecast future adherence behaviours.

These two multicentre studies demonstrated robust psychometric properties of the KATITA-25 scale. Internal consistency reliability was high, with a Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.81, indicating strong coherence among scale items. Construct validity was adequate, supported by good convergent validity, as evidenced by differentiation among known groups, and structural validity, confirmed through confirmatory factor analysis. Criterion validity was also established, with satisfactory concurrent validity and predictive validity. The scale exhibited sensitivity to change, suggesting its responsiveness to variations in adherence behaviour over time. However, test–retest reliability was deemed unsatisfactory, attributed to the challenges posed by the Covid-19 pandemic imposing a 12-month interval between administrations of the scale in the same patients. Importantly, this study conducted in Spain with a shorter interval between administrations (2 to 4 weeks) demonstrated favourable test–retest reliability results.

The validation studies conducted thus far on KATITA-25 have provided compelling evidence of its reliability, convergent construct validity, and concurrent and predictive criterion validity. While confirmatory factor analysis has assessed its structural validity and multidimensionality, an additional crucial step remains: a cross-cultural validation study to affirm the scale’s construct validity and generalizability. This study aimed to address this gap by developing a Spanish version of the KATITA-25 questionnaire and evaluating its reliability, criterion validity, and construct validity among Spanish candidates for kidney transplantation. Through this endeavour, we sought to ascertain whether the scale properties remain consistent and applicable when administered to a distinct population with a different language and cultural context.

Methods

The study was planned as a multicentre study and conducted in four study centres: the kidney pre-transplant outpatient clinics at Hospital del Mar and Hospital Bellvitge, and the dialysis sectors of Hospital Mollet and Hospital Terrassa, all located in Catalonia, Spain. Before the start of the study, the study protocol was submitted and approved by the Research Ethics Committees of all the study centres, under authorization number CEIm 2021/10,184. Prior to participation, all patients provided informed consent in writing. The study was conducted in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.

The cross-cultural adaptation of the KATITA-25 questionnaire followed a systematic process outlined in the literature19,20, comprising five key steps: 1-Translation: The questionnaire was independently translated into Spanish by two professional translators with no affiliations to the study centres or authors. These translations were then compared, and a consensual version was derived; 2-Semantic Equivalence Validation: Interviews were conducted with professionals specializing in kidney transplantation and with patients to ensure semantic equivalence of the translated questionnaire; 3- Back-Translation: The Spanish text was translated back into Portuguese by a linguist without knowledge of the original text. This back-translation was then reviewed by a group of experts, including a member of the research team, a language expert, and a sociocultural expert; 4- Patient Feedback: The final Spanish version was administered to 10 patients on the renal transplant waiting list at Hospital del Mar. These patients, over 18 years old and capable of reading and communicating, provided feedback on their interpretation of each questionnaire item through individual interviews. Any unclear items were reformulated based on patient feedback; 5- Pre-Testing: A pre-test of the finalized Spanish version was conducted with 16 eligible patients to identify any difficulties encountered in responding to the questionnaire. This rigorous adaptation process ensured the linguistic and conceptual equivalence of the Spanish version of the KATITA-25 questionnaire, thereby enhancing its suitability for use in Spanish-speaking populations.

The multicentre validation study prospectively enrolled patients over 18 years of age, of both sexes, who were candidates for kidney transplantation and were on the waiting list for such a procedure. These patients were required to possess the ability to read and communicate effectively and provided written consent for participation. Individuals who were candidates for retransplantation were excluded from the study. Demographic information including age, sex, ethnicity, education level, and social support, along with clinical data related to kidney disease such as aetiology, current dialysis status, duration on dialysis, and number of blood transfusions, were collected from all participants. Additionally, each patient completed the translated questionnaire. A subset of the first 79 enrolled patients was invited to participate in the test–retest evaluation. This evaluation was conducted after a time interval ranging from 2 to 4 weeks, during which patients completed the translated questionnaire once again. This process allowed for the assessment of test–retest reliability.

Statistical methods

For this study, the sample size was determined to be 125 patients, a number based on the ratio of 5 patients per questionnaire item. This ratio, often proposed in the literature, ensures an adequate number of participants for robust statistical analysis21,22. For the test–retest analysis, a separate sample size of at least 60 patients was specified. This sample size calculation was designed to achieve 80% power, allowing the study to detect a correlation coefficient greater than 0.35 with an alpha error of 0.05. This level of statistical power ensures that the study has sufficient sensitivity to identify meaningful correlations between repeated measurements over time.

To characterize the study population, clinical and demographic variables were described using summary measures appropriate to the measurement scales. The Spanish version of the scale was assessed for construct validity by structural and convergent validity. In the former, an exploratory factor analysis with varimax rotation was performed to investigate whether the multidimensionality of the scale was reproduced in the Spanish version, which was further tested with confirmatory factor analysis using structural equation modelling with Satorra-Bentler correction for non-normality of item scores23, and assessment of model fit using the following indices: root mean square error of approximation (RMSEA)24, chi-square to degrees of freedom ratio, comparative fit index (CFI)25, Tucker-Lewis index (TLI)25, standardized root mean squared residual (SRMR) and coefficient of determination (CD). Construct validity was also assessed by convergent validity which, in the absence of validated scales measuring the same construct to compare their scores to KATITA-25 scores applied at the same time, was evaluated by differentiation by known groups, consisting of the comparison of KATITA-25 scores between levels of risk factors for medication non-adherence referred to in literature, using the Wilcoxon-Mann–Whitney test.

The criteria used to assess reliability, correlation, fit indices, and statistical significance were as follows: a Cronbach’s alpha coefficient value between 0.7 and 0.9 was considered good reliability, and a value above 0.9 excellent reliability26; an intraclass correlation coefficient value < 0.5 was considered poor reliability, 0.5–0.75 moderate, 0.75–0.9 good and > 0.9 excellent reliability27; a correlation coefficient value < 0.3 was considered weak correlation, 0.4–0.6 moderate and > 0.6 strong correlation; an RMSEA value < 0.10 was considered mediocre fit, 0.10–0.08 marginal, 0.08–0.05 acceptable and < 0.05 good fit and, In relation to the other fit indices, it was considered evidence of good fit CFI and TLI > 0.90, SRMR < 0.08, CD > 0.95 and chi2/degrees of freedom ratio < 228; a p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Reliability was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha coefficient for internal consistency and the intraclass correlation coefficient, two-way mixed model, absolute agreement, for temporal (test–retest) reliability, for the full scale and for each dimension. Item equivalence was assessed with item analysis, by presenting the median values and range of the item-test, item-rest and average inter-item correlations, in each dimension and for the total score, and by Cronbach’s alpha coefficient if-item-deleted, that is, the value of the coefficient when one item is deleted at a time. The assumption of independence of dimensions was tested using Spearman’s correlation coefficient between the scores of the three dimensions of the questionnaire.

Statistical analysis was performed using Stata 15 software (Stata Corp College Station, TX, USA). The presentation of the study methodology and results is in accordance with the COSMIN reporting guideline for studies on measurement properties of patient-reported outcome measures29.

Results

The translation-retroversion was carried out over a period of 2 months. None of the 10 participants in the cognitive interviews had doubts about the meaning or understanding of the content and there were no modifications to the instrument. In the pre-test, the patients had no difficulty in answering the questionnaire. The Spanish version of the KATITA-25 questionnaire is shown in Fig. 1.

The Spanish version of the KATITA-25 questionnaire.

The validation study was conducted from September 2022 to April 2024. A total of 207 pre-kidney transplant patients were assessed for eligibility. Of these patients, 7 declined participation, 27 were candidates for kidney retransplantation and 10 were unable to participate (2 illiterate, 2 with cognitive impairment, 4 with amaurosis, and 2 with inability to write after a stroke). Thus, 163 patients participated in the study. The mean age was 64.3 ± 13.9, 66.7% were male and 91.9% Caucasian. The most frequent diagnoses of kidney disease were diabetic nephropathy (24.5%) and hypertensive kidney disease (15.3%), with 19.6% of cases of unknown aetiology. The median time on dialysis was 2 years (Table 1). There were no statistically significant differences between centres in patients’ characteristics (age p = 0.39, sex p = 0.59, ethnicity p = 0.68, and duration of dialysis p = 0.46), while there were differences in the proportion on dialysis (p < 0.001).

Cronbach’s alpha coefficients in the second application of the questionnaire for the full scale and subscales were, respectively, 0.80, 0.80, 0.62 and 0.37. Discussion

The cross-cultural validation study presented here was essential for achieving comprehensive construct validation of KATITA-25. As per the COSMIN guideline29, cross-cultural validity assesses the extent to which the performance of items in a translated or cross-culturally adapted scale accurately mirrors that of the original version. In our investigation, conducted on a population distinct from the one in which the initial scale was developed, the evaluation results aligned closely with those of the original scale assessment across all aspects. The study centres were selected to reflect the diversity of settings to which kidney transplant candidates are exposed. As expected, patient characteristics were not different between centres, while frequency of dialysis was heterogeneous, as the practice of waitlisting patients before initiating dialysis varied across centres. Reliability indices were less than optimal for the Scepticism and Concern dimensions, which may be attributed to the small number of items in those dimensions as Cronbach’s alpha is sensitive to the number of items. Among the six goodness of fit indices of confirmatory factor analysis, only CFI and TCL were below the desirable value, but this should not question model adequacy because qualitative assessment of model fit should consider the results on all indices, not on individual indices32. Overall, the results of this study are consistent with the results observed in the original version. This congruence unequivocally confirms the cross-cultural validity of the Spanish version of KATITA-25.

The primary distinction between the results of the study conducted in Spain and the study in Brazil lies in the average total score of the scale, with Spain exhibiting approximately half the score observed in the Brazilian population. This variance is likely attributable to the significant age difference between the two populations, with the Brazilian cohort averaging 49 years and the Spanish cohort averaging 64 years. As adherence to medication tends to increase with age, the lower average scores among the older Spanish population would account for this discrepancy. This explanation finds support in the similarity of the slopes observed in the regression lines plotting the scale’s total score against age in both Brazilian and Spanish populations. The statistical analysis (p(interaction) = 0.52) indicates no significant difference in the relationship between age and adherence propensity across the two cohorts. Additionally, disparities in the proportion of transplant candidate patients undergoing dialysis were noted, with Brazil having a 100% dialysis rate compared to approximately 70% in Spain. Ethnic distribution also differed, with Brazil exhibiting a higher percentage of Afro-descendants than Spain. Notably, while these demographic variations existed, they did not exert a discernible influence on the validation results. However, it is evident that defining a cutoff point for predicting non-adherence, if desired, will necessitate tailoring to each specific population’s characteristics and demographics.

The decision to conduct cross-cultural validation in Spanish was driven by several factors. Firstly, Spanish is the fourth most spoken language globally, making it widely accessible to diverse populations. Secondly, Spain boasts one of the most dynamic kidney transplant regions globally, ensuring a robust and varied patient pool for validation studies. Moreover, the scarcity of clinical questionnaires translated into Spanish for assessing medication adherence, especially in the context of kidney transplantation, underscored the need for a validated tool in this language. As far as our knowledge extends, there were no existing instruments tailored specifically for assessing medication adherence in kidney transplant patients in Spanish. Hence, the development and validation of the Spanish version of KATITA-25 fill a crucial gap in clinical assessment tools, offering Spanish-speaking healthcare providers a valuable resource for evaluating medication adherence in this patient population.

Among the medication adherence questionnaires translated into Spanish, the Morisky Medication Adherence Scale (MMAS-8) is frequently referenced. However, its validity assessments in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus33 and arterial hypertension34 yielded suboptimal results, with Cronbach’s alpha coefficients of 0.40 and 0.68, respectively, and an intraclass correlation coefficient of 0.72. Another notable questionnaire, the Beliefs About Medicine Questionnaire (BMQ), evaluates beliefs regarding medications and includes both general and specific sections35. The Spanish version demonstrated good internal consistency (Cronbach’s alpha coefficients of 0.82 for Specific-Necessity and 0.73 for Specific-Concerns) in patients with chronic diseases, though test–retest reliability and criterion validity were not assessed36. Additionally, its evaluation in patients with inflammatory bowel disease focused primarily on structural validity37.

Other medication adherence questionnaires translated into Spanish cater to specific patient populations. For instance, the Adherence to Refill and Medication Scale (ARMS) questionnaire38, designed for patients with chronic diseases, underwent translation into Spanish. However, its cross-cultural validity remains unexplored39. Similarly, the Compliance Questionnaire Rheumatology (CQR) questionnaire, aimed at assessing medication adherence in patients with various rheumatic diseases, was translated into Spanish and evaluated for reliability and predictive validity40. It demonstrated adequate internal consistency, with a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.86, and an intraclass correlation coefficient of 0.76. A scale cutoff point predictive of non-adherence was proposed but lacked validation in an independent sample.

A single questionnaire, the Simplified Medication Adherence Questionnaire (SMAQ), initially designed for HIV-positive patients, underwent evaluation in kidney transplant recipients following its translation into Spanish41. In this evaluation, the serum level of tacrolimus was used as criterion for defining non-adherence to immunosuppressive treatment. The findings revealed a sensitivity of 44% and a specificity of 62% in classifying patients as non-adherent.

KATITA-25 distinguishes itself from other self-report scales by prospectively measuring non-adherence, even before the initiation of immunosuppressive therapy, in patients awaiting kidney transplantation. This unique approach makes it the first scale designed to assess the predisposition to adherence in pre-transplant patients. Given the global prevalence of kidney transplants and the critical importance of adherence to immunosuppressants post-transplantation, there is a clear need for effective tools to identify non-adherent patients, preferably before transplantation. The high cost of anti-rejection treatment, along with the risks associated with non-adherence, underscores the necessity for a clinical questionnaire that can identify predispositions for non-adherence in pre-transplant patients.

The availability of the Spanish version of the KATITA-25 questionnaire holds promise for enhancing the pre-transplant evaluation process. By identifying patients with a high predisposition for non-adherence, healthcare professionals can tailor interventions aimed at improving adherence during the pre-transplant period. These interventions may include measures to prevent non-adherence, health education initiatives, and motivational strategies to reduce the risk of adverse outcomes stemming from non-adherence. Moreover, studies have demonstrated that closely monitoring patients with initial unfavourable conditions or psychopathological conditions during the pre-transplant period can lead to successful transplantation outcomes42,43. Therefore, the implementation of the KATITA-25 questionnaire in pre-transplant assessments may contribute to optimizing patient care and transplant success rates.

Several methodological strengths bolster the validity and robustness of this study’s findings. Firstly, the translation and cross-cultural adaptation process adhered to recognized and standardized methodologies19,20, ensuring linguistic equivalence across different cultural contexts. Additionally, the sample size surpassed the recommended threshold, with more than 30% exceeding the minimum requirement of 5 respondents per questionnaire item21,22. This ensured adequate statistical power for analysis and interpretation of results. Moreover, the multicentre design of the study minimized the potential for selection bias and enhanced the generalizability of findings across diverse patient populations. However, it’s important to acknowledge certain limitations. The study population was confined to a single region of Spain characterized by high levels of human development, robust healthcare infrastructure, and extensive experience in kidney transplantation. As such, the results may not be fully representative of other regions within Spain or in other Spanish-speaking countries with differing healthcare systems and cultural contexts.

A significant contribution of the KATITA project is its revelation that psychological factors, such as attitudes, prejudices, and beliefs regarding medications, play a pivotal role in medication adherence. This insight suggests a paradigm shift from solely focusing on medication-taking behaviours to considering broader psychological determinants, potentially spurring research into psychometric scales tailored to diverse populations and contexts.

In conclusion, despite these limitations, the study successfully demonstrated the cross-cultural validity of the Spanish version of the KATITA-25 scale in assessing the predisposition for non-adherence to immunosuppressive therapy in kidney transplant candidates, even prior to transplantation. This underscores the scale’s potential utility in clinical practice for identifying at-risk patients and implementing targeted interventions to optimize medication adherence and improve transplant outcomes.

A decrease in glucose supply promotes cell death in bovine corpus luteum
Authors: Aoi JIMBO, Hiroki HASEGAWA, Masamichi YAMASHITA, Takeshi YAMAGUCHI, Kiyoshi OKUDA, Ryo NISHIMURA
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractGlucose (GLU) is an essential energy source for mammalian cells, and its depletion induces various cellular responses, including apoptosis. The present study examined whether a decrease in GLU levels induces apoptotic cell death, which typically occurs during regression of the bovine corpus luteum (CL). To investigate the possible mechanisms of luteal cell death induced by low GLU levels, we examined the effect of GLU-free medium on the expression of apoptosis-regulating factors (FAS, BAX, BCL2, CASP8, and CASP3). Luteal cells cultured in GLU-free medium showed reduced viability, which was rescued by GLU supplementation in a dose-dependent manner. Culturing without GLU increased the expression of FAS and CASP3 mRNAs. Inhibiting glucose transporter 1 (GLUT1) with cytochalasin B or STF-31 in the presence of GLU also induced cell death. Based on these findings, a decrease in GLU supply, which is induced by a decrease in blood flow to the CL during luteolysis, may promote apoptosis by stimulating the expression of FAS and CASP3 in the bovine CL.Keywords: Apoptosis, Cattle, Corpus luteum, Glucose

Luteal regression is characterized by a decrease in progesterone (P4) production (functional luteolysis), followed by a decrease in luteal size (structural luteolysis), during which cells of the corpus luteum (CL) undergo apoptosis [1,2,3,4,5]. In cows, luteolysis is initiated by uterine prostaglandin (PG) F2α released at the late luteal stage [6]. Luteolysis can also be induced by injecting PGF2α at the mid-luteal stage [7]. During both spontaneous and PGF2α-induced luteolysis, a decrease in luteal blood flow occurs in parallel with a decrease in systemic P4 concentrations [8,9,10,11]. Furthermore, capillaries in the bovine CL have been shown to regress during luteolysis [12, 13]. Glucose transporter 1 (GLUT1), a facilitative sugar transporter, is expressed in the bovine CL throughout the estrous cycle, and its expression is high only at the early luteal stage [14]. These findings imply that a decrease in glucose (GLU) supply caused by decreased blood flow into the CL is a characteristic feature of the luteal environment during luteolysis in cows. However, whether a decrease in GLU supply causes luteolytic cell death during luteolysis remains unclear.

Apoptosis is modulated by many regulators, such as a death receptor FAS [15,16,17,18,19], BCL2 family proteins [15,16,17,18] and caspases [17,18,19]. BCL2 family proteins modulate the release of apoptogenic factors, such as cytochrome c, apoptosis-inducing factor and Smac/Diablo, from mitochondria into the cytosol. The release of these factors determines whether apoptosis will proceed [18]. Among the BCL2 family proteins, BCL2 protects cells from apoptosis, whereas BAX accelerates this process [15,16,17,18, 20]. Caspases are a family of aspartic acid-specific proteases that activate a proteolytic cascade and rapidly induce cell death [18]. In the bovine CL, BAX and caspases (CASP3, 8, and 9) are involved in structural luteolysis in vivo [3, 21, 22], and similar findings have been reported in vitro using bovine luteal cells [23, 24]. Whether these apoptosis regulators are related to GLU supply in the CL remains unclear.

GLU is a hydrophilic molecule kept at 2.5–3.9 mM in the blood, and it is needed by nearly all cells in cows [25]. GLU is taken up by cells through a carrier protein, such as a glucose transporter (GLUT) [26]. We previously found that GLUT1 is expressed in the bovine CL and supports P4 production during luteal formation [14].

The present study was conducted to examine whether a decrease in GLU supply induces apoptotic cell death in the bovine CL. We also examined whether the GLU transporter, GLUT1, is related to low GLU-induced cell death.

Materials and Methods

Collection of CL

Ovaries with a midstage CL were collected from Holstein cows at a local abattoir within 10–20 min after exsanguination. Midstage CLs were identified through macroscopic observation of the ovaries and uterus, as previously described [27]. For the cell culture experiments, ovaries with a CL were submerged in ice-cold physiological saline and transported to the laboratory.

Cell isolation

Luteal tissue was enzymatically dissociated, and luteal cells cultured as previously described [28]. The luteal cells were suspended in a culture medium, DMEM, and Ham’s F-12 medium (D/F; 1:1 [v/v]; Nacalai Tesque; Kyoto, Japan; cat. no. 08460) containing 5% calf serum (Life Technologies, Grand Island, NY, USA; cat. no. 16170-078) and 20 μg/ml gentamicin (Life Technologies; cat. no. 15750-060). Cell viability was greater than 85%, as assessed via trypan blue exclusion. The cell suspension comprised approximately 70% small luteal cells, 20% large luteal cells, 10% endothelial cells or fibrocytes, and no erythrocytes.

Cell culture

The dispersed luteal cells were seeded at 2.0 × 105 viable cells in 1 ml (100 μl/well) in 96-well cluster dishes (Sumitomo Bakelite Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan; cat. no. MC-8096FS) for the detection of cell viability or at 4.0 × 105 viable cells in 1 ml (1 ml/well) in 24-well cluster dishes (Greiner Bio-One, Frickenhausen, Germany; cat. no. 662160) for the detection of mRNA expression. Thereafter, the cells were cultured in a humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2 in air at 38°C in a N2-O2-CO2-regulated incubator (ASTEC Co. Ltd., Osaka, Japan; APM-30D). After 12 h of culture, the cells were washed twice with glucose-free D/F media (Nacalai Tesque; cat. no. 09893) containing 0.1% bovine serum albumin (BSA; Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA; cat. no. A8022), 20 μg/ml gentamicin, and 5 ng/ml sodium selenite (Sigma-Aldrich; cat. no. S5261). The subsequent experiments were performed in fresh media with or without GLU (17.5 mM; Nacalai Tesque; cat. no. 08460).

Effect of GLU depletion on cell viability

Luteal cells were cultured in typical D/F (including 17.5 mM GLU) or glucose-free D/F media, both of which contained 0.1% BSA, 20 μg/ml gentamicin, and 5 ng/ml sodium selenite, for 48 h. After culturing, cell viability was determined using a Cell Counting Kit-8 (DOJINDO, Kumamoto, Japan; cat. no. CK04).

Dose-dependent effect of GLU on cell viability

Luteal cells were cultured with different concentrations of GLU (0.025, 0.25, 2.5, or 25.0 mM) in glucose-free D/F media containing 0.1% BSA, 20 μg/ml gentamicin, and 5 ng/ml sodium selenite for 48 h. After culturing, cell viability was determined.

Effect of GLU depletion on FAS, CASP8, CASP3, BCL2 and BAX mRNA expression

Luteal cells were cultured with or without GLU (25.0 mM) for 48 h. After culturing, total RNA was extracted to determine the FAS, CASP8, CASP3, BCL2 and BAX mRNA expression levels.

Effect of GLUT1 inhibition on cell viability

Luteal cells were treated with or without GLU (25.0 mM) in combination with a GLUT1 inhibitor: cytochalasin B (CB; nonspecific GLUT inhibitor; 10 μM; Sigma-Aldrich; cat. no. C6762) or STF-31 (GLUT1-specific inhibitor; 10 μM; Sigma-Aldrich; cat. no. SML1108) [14], whereafter they were cultured for 48 h. After culturing, cell viability was determined.

Cell viability assay

Cell viability was determined using a Cell Counting Kit-8 including WST-8 assay kit, according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, WST-8, a type of MTT (3-(4,5-dimetyl-2 thiazolyl)-2,5-diphenyl-2H-tetrazolium bromide), is a yellow tetrazolium salt that is reduced to formazan by live cells containing active mitochondria. After culturing with treatment in 96-well cluster dishes, a 10-μl aliquot containing WST-8 was added to each well. The cells were then incubated for 2 h at 38°C. The absorbance (λ: 450 nm) was measured using a microplate reader. Cell viability was expressed as a percentage of the control value or as a percentage of the value in glucose-free D/F media.

RNA isolation and cDNA synthesis

Total RNA was prepared from cultured luteal cells using RNeasy (Qiagen GmbH, Hilden, Germany; cat. no. 74106), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Total RNA (1 μg) was reverse-transcribed using a PrimeScript 1st strand cDNA synthesis kit (Takara Bio Inc., Shiga, Japan; cat. no. 6110A).

Real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR)

Gene expression was measured via real-time PCR using a 7500 FAST thermal cycler (Applied Biosystems, Tokyo, Japan) and KAPA FAST ABI prism qPCR kit (KAPA, Boston, MA, USA; cat. no. KK4604), beginning with 1 ng reverse-transcribed total RNA. The expression of β-actin (ACTB) was used as an internal control. Specific primers with 50–60% GC contents were synthesized (Table 1). The PCR conditions were 95°C for 10 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 sec and 60°C for 60 sec. Use of the KAPA FAST ABI prism qPCR mixture at elevated temperatures resulted in the reliable and sensitive quantification of RT-PCR products with high linearity (Pearson correlation coefficient r > 0.99). The relative expression level of each mRNA was determined with the 2−ΔΔCT method [29].

Statistical analysis

All experimental data are shown as the mean ± SEM. Cell viability was expressed as a percentage of the control (Fig. 1) or as a percentage of the value in glucose-free D/F media (Figs. 2 and 4). Statistical analysis for cell viability (Figs. 1, 2, and 4) was based on the absorbance (λ: 450 nm) detected in the Cell Counting Kit-8 assay. Statistical significance (set at P < 0.05) of the differences in cell viability (Fig. 1) and mRNA expression (Fig. 3) was assessed using Student’s t-test, and that in cell viability (Figs. 2 and 4) assessed using analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by a multiple comparison with Bonferroni correction.

Results

Effect of GLU depletion and the dose-dependent effect of GLU on cell viability

GLU depletion significantly decreased the viability of cultured luteal cells (Fig. 1; P < 0.05). GLU increased cell viability in a dose-dependent manner; significant increases were observed at concentrations of 2.5 and 25.0 mM (Fig. 2; P < 0.01). Based on this result, the subsequent experiments were performed with 25.0 mM GLU.

Effect of GLU depletion on FAS, CASP8, CASP3, BCL2, and BAX mRNA expression

The real-time PCR analysis results showed that 25.0 mM GLU decreased the mRNA expression of FAS (Fig. 3A; P < 0.05) and CASP3 (Fig. 3C; P < 0.05) but had no significant effect on that of BAX, BCL2, and CASP8 (Fig. 3).

Effect of GLUT1 inhibition on cell viability

Both GLUT1 inhibitors (CB and STF-31) significantly decreased cell viability in the presence of 25.0 mM GLU (Fig. 4; P < 0.05).

Discussion

The present results show that GLU depletion induced cell death in cultured bovine luteal cells and that adding GLU recovered cell viability in a dose-dependent manner. These results suggest that GLU is needed to maintain luteal cell function in cows. The bovine CL produces P4, which is essential for the establishment and maintenance of pregnancy [30]. When pregnancy is not established, the CL undergoes functional (decrease in P4 synthesis) and structural regression (luteal cell death) [31]. During luteolysis, luteal blood flow decreases with intra-luteal vascular occlusion [12, 13]. Based on these findings, the present results in which decreased GLU levels were found to induce luteal cell death suggest that decreasing the GLU supply to the CL, which is possibly caused by decreased luteal blood flow during luteolysis, is one of the causes of luteal cell death, as well as structural luteolysis. We have previously found that a decrease in GLU levels in bovine mid-luteal cell cultures did not decrease P4 production [14]. Thus, a decrease in GLU supply may promote structural luteolysis in cows.

GLU levels are maintained at 2.5–3.9 mM in the blood, functioning as a basic energy source for nearly all cells in cows [25]. GLU is a hydrophilic molecule; therefore, it needs a carrier, such as GLUTs, to be taken up by cells [26]. We have previously found that GLUT1 is expressed in the bovine CL and supports P4 production in bovine luteal cells [14]. GLUT1 is a tissue-specific transporter expressed in the CLs of rats [32, 33], mice [34], dogs [35], sheep [36], and cows [14, 37]. In the present study, we found that GLUT1 inhibition promoted luteal cell death. This result suggests that GLU supplied via GLUT1 supports luteal cell viability. GLUT1 is a facilitative sugar transporter that can move GLU from areas with high to low concentrations, and its expression in the bovine CL does not decrease during luteolysis [14]. However, GLU supply to the CL could decrease during luteolysis because blood supply to the CL decreases during luteolysis [11]. Thus, rather than the expression of GLUT1, a decrease in GLU supply may promote luteolysis in cows. In the present study, CB, a nonspecific GLUT inhibitor, induced cell death more noticeably than the GLUT1-specific inhibitor (STF-31) did. GLUT3 and GLUT4 mRNA expression was also reported in bovine CL [37]. Thus, in addition to GLUT1, other GLUTs may support GLU uptake to maintain luteal cells.

Apoptosis pathways are generally of two types: 1) the extrinsic pathway, in which FAS and its ligand (FASL) begin signaling for CASP8 activation, followed by CASP3 activation, and 2) the intrinsic pathway, which is regulated by BCL2 family proteins [17, 18]. Both pathways have been reported in bovine CLs [16, 38, 39]. The effect of GLU depletion on these apoptotic pathways has not been well examined in bovine cells, although GLU depletion has been reported to inhibit BCL2 mRNA expression and promote BAX mRNA expression in mouse photoreceptor cells [40]. In the present study, GLU depletion increased FAS mRNA expression without affecting that of BCL2 or BAX. These results suggest that GLU depletion induces the extrinsic pathway in bovine luteal cells, and that the apoptosis pathway generated by GLU depletion is different among cell types or animal species.

In the present study, GLU depletion increased CASP3 mRNA expression without the addition of FASL. This result implies the activation of some apoptotic pathways, such as the CASP9 pathway, rather than the FAS/FASL-induced pathway. CASP8 mRNA expression was not affected by GLU depletion. As CASP8 is activated by the FAS–FASL system [18, 19] and FAS mRNA expression was increased by GLU depletion in the present study, FASL present under GLU depletion is speculated to promote luteal cell death by upregulating CASP8. As neither BCL2 nor BAX expression was affected by GLU depletion, other pathways, including other BCL2 family proteins, such as BCL-xL and BAK, may be regulated by GLU depletion, resulting in an increase in CASP3 mRNA expression. Further studies are needed to clarify the precise apoptotic pathway activated by GLU depletion.

The overall results of the present study indicate that GLU depletion induces luteal cell death by increasing FAS and CASP3 mRNA expression, and that GLU supplied through GLUT1 supports luteal cells in the bovine CL. GLU depletion does not inhibit P4 synthesis in bovine mid-luteal cells [14]. In summary, the present results suggest that because bovine luteal blood flow decreases during luteolysis [11], the decrease in GLU supply (possibly due to the decreasing blood supply) promotes structural luteolysis in cows.

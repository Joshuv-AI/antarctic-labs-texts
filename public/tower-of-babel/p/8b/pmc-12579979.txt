Effectiveness of bivalent respiratory syncytial virus prefusion F protein-based vaccine in individuals with or without atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease: the DAN-RSV trial
Authors: Manan Pareek, Mats C Højbjerg Lassen, Niklas Dyrby Johansen, Sine H Christensen, Negar Aliabadi, Kristoffer G Skaarup, Daniel Modin, Brian L Claggett, Carsten S Larsen, Lykke Larsen, Lothar Wiese, Michael Dalager-Pedersen, Matias G Lindholm, Anne Marie R Jensen, Maria Dons, Katrine F Bernholm, Filip S Davidovski, Lisa S Duus, Camilla I Ottosen, Anne B Nielsen, Julie H Borchsenius, Caroline Espersen, Güldas Köse, Frederik H Fussing, Lars Køber, Scott D Solomon, Jens Ulrik Stæhr Jensen, Cyril Jean-Marie Martel, Bradford D Gessner, Claudia Schwarz, Elisa Gonzalez, Mette Skovdal, Pingping Zhang, Elizabeth Begier, Tor Biering-Sørensen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and AimsIt is not known whether the bivalent respiratory syncytial virus prefusion F protein-based (RSVpreF) vaccine reduces outcomes in individuals with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD). The aim was to evaluate the vaccine effectiveness (VE) of an RSVpreF vaccine vs no vaccine on respiratory and cardiovascular outcomes in persons with or without pre-existing ASCVD.MethodsWe conducted a prespecified secondary analysis of the DAN-RSV trial. Adults aged ≥60 years were randomized 1:1 to RSVpreF vaccine or no vaccine. Baseline and outcome data were collected through nationwide registries. The primary outcome was respiratory syncytial virus-related respiratory tract disease hospitalization. The principal major adverse cardiovascular event outcome was a composite of hospitalization for myocardial infarction, stroke, or heart failure. Heterogeneity in VE was assessed among participants with and without ASCVD.ResultsThe incidence rate of almost all outcomes was higher in participants with pre-existing ASCVD (n = 14 241) vs those without (n = 117 035). Vaccine effectiveness was generally consistent by ASCVD status (Pinteraction ≥ .05 for all but one interaction). Among persons without and with ASCVD, VE for the primary outcome was 80.0% [95% confidence interval (CI), 29.3–96.3] vs 100.0% (95% CI, −141.3 to 100.0), respectively (Pinteraction > .99). Vaccine effectiveness for major adverse cardiovascular events was 9.3% (95% CI, −15.1 to 28.6) in participants without, and 12.0% (95% CI, −34.6 to 43.3) in participants with, pre-existing ASCVD (Pinteraction = .90).ConclusionsThe VE of an RSVpreF vaccine vs no vaccine against respiratory and cardiovascular outcomes was similar among individuals ≥ 60 years of age with pre-existing ASCVD as compared with those without.

Introduction

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is associated with an increased risk of cardiovascular events, including stroke and acute coronary syndromes.1–7 The risk of symptomatic RSV-related illness, hospitalization, and poor outcomes appears to be particularly high among individuals with pre-existing cardiovascular disease.4,5,7–9

A bivalent RSV prefusion F protein-based (RSVpreF) vaccine containing stabilized prefusion F glycoproteins from RSV subgroups A and B demonstrated efficacy against symptomatic RSV infection and viral shedding.10 The RSVpreF vaccines further prevented RSV-associated lower respiratory tract illness and acute respiratory illness in adults ≥ 60 years of age in both the RENOIR trial (RSV Vaccine Efficacy Study in Older Adults Immunized against RSV Disease) and the AReSVi-006 trial (Adult Respiratory Syncytial Virus).11–13 However, these randomized studies did not have a sufficient number of cases for evaluation of hospitalization outcomes. Furthermore, it is conceivable that individuals with high a priori risk, e.g. those with a history of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD), have a different degree of benefit from vaccination.

The purpose of this prespecified secondary analysis of the DAN-RSV trial (A Pragmatic Randomized Trial to Evaluate Bivalent RSV Prefusion F Protein–based Vaccine Effectiveness for Preventing RSV Hospitalizations in Adults Aged 60 Years or Above) was to evaluate the effectiveness of the RSVpreF vaccine vs no vaccine against respiratory and cardiovascular events, including ASCVD events, in persons ≥ 60 years of age, with or without pre-existing ASCVD.

Methods

Trial design, participants, and procedures

The rationale, design, baseline characteristics, and primary results of the DAN-RSV trial are reported separately.14–16 In brief, DAN-RSV was an investigator-sponsored, pragmatic, open-label, parallel-group, individually randomized clinical trial conducted during the 2024/2025 Northern Hemisphere winter season. The trial enrolled individuals aged ≥60 years irrespectively of comorbidity status during November and December 2024 at more than 40 sites across Denmark. Participants were mainly recruited using electronic invitation letters sent through the Danish governmental digital mail system, Digital Post. All potentially eligible individuals with access to this mail system were invited to participate. Although there were no formal exclusion criteria, vaccination procedures were required to follow routine clinical guidelines, including assessment for contraindications.

Participants were individually randomized 1:1 to the RSVpreF vaccine or no vaccine. The RSVpreF vaccine contained RSV subgroup A stabilized prefusion F antigen (60 µg) and RSV subgroup B stabilized prefusion F antigen (60 µg) and was administered as a single intramuscular injection. The control group did not receive any vaccine as part of the trial. Participants could provide electronic informed consent prior to their scheduled study visit, or they could consent in-person at the study visit, in case of which randomization was performed on-site. Accordingly, only participants randomized to RSVpreF (and those who had not consented electronically beforehand) were required to attend a study visit.

The trial was approved by the Danish Medical Research Ethics Committees and the Danish Medicines Agency. It was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Council for Harmonization Good Clinical Practice guidelines.

Baseline evaluation and atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease

Baseline data were collected via linkage to the nationwide administrative health registries. International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision (ICD-10) codes and/or Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical (ATC) codes were used to define baseline conditions and vaccination status.14 Pre-existing ASCVD was defined as a history of ischaemic heart disease, cerebrovascular disease, or peripheral artery disease, all using ICD-10 codes.

Outcomes

Outcome data were also collected through linkage to the nationwide health registries.14 Access to electronic medial records was available for clinical outcome confirmation. Participants were followed for clinical outcomes from ≥14 days after the initially booked study visit date through 31 May 2025. The primary outcome was RSV-related respiratory tract disease hospitalization, analysed using the intention-to-treat principle. Key secondary outcomes included various other respiratory, hospitalization, and mortality outcomes. Detailed outcome definitions are presented in Supplementary data online, Table S1. All specific cardiovascular outcomes (except the secondary endpoints: RSV-related and all-cause cardiorespiratory hospitalization) were considered exploratory. All outcomes required overnight hospital stays. The principal major adverse cardiovascular event outcome for the present analysis was defined as a composite of hospitalization for myocardial infarction, hospitalization for stroke, or hospitalization for heart failure.

Statistical analysis

Baseline characteristics were displayed stratified for pre-existing ASCVD status and randomization group. Continuous variables were presented as medians and interquartile ranges. Categorical variables were presented as number (%). Comparisons between groups were performed using the Mann–Whitney U test or Pearson’s χ2 test, respectively.

All analyses were performed according to the intention-to-treat principle. All effectiveness endpoints were examined using vaccine effectiveness (VE) and based on the first outcome event only. Vaccine effectiveness was calculated as [1 − (incidence rate in the vaccine group/incidence rate in the control group)] × 100%, and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were constructed using the exact Clopper–Pearson method for binomial proportions. Vaccine effectiveness is the proportion of disease prevented by the vaccine. For example, a VE of 10% is mathematically equal to a relative risk of 0.90 for the intervention vs control. A VE = 0 signifies a null effect, a VE > 0 favours the intervention regimen, and a VE < 0 favours the control regimen. Of note, VE is capped at 100%, meaning that 100% of the disease is prevented by the vaccine; by contrast, a negative VE may extend to infinity. This also explains why the CIs may be asymmetrical. Event rates and VE were summarized by pre-existing ASCVD status and randomization group.

Treatment heterogeneity among participants with and without ASCVD was assessed using a Poisson model with a subgroup-by-randomized treatment interaction term; however, in case of low cell counts (<3), an exact Poisson model was used. A two-sided P < .05 was considered statistically significant. Although a minimum VE and a formal testing hierarchy were defined for the primary analysis, the results from this prespecified secondary analysis should be interpreted as exploratory in nature. Statistical analysis was performed using SAS Software, version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA), Stata MP, version 19.5 (StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA), and R, version 4.3.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 131 276 participants were randomized to either the RSVpreF vaccine (n = 65 642) or the control group (n = 65 634). Overall, 14 241 (10.8%) participants had a history of ASCVD. Of these, 9746 (7.4%) had ischaemic heart disease, 4673 (3.6%) cerebrovascular disease, and 735 (0.6%) peripheral artery disease. A total of 883 (0.7%) individuals had ASCVD affecting more than one vascular bed. Baseline characteristics in participants stratified by pre-existing ASCVD status are shown in Table 1; characteristics stratified by both ASCVD status and randomization group are shown in Supplementary data online, Table S2. Those with ASCVD were older, more often male, and generally had a more adverse cardiovascular and non-cardiovascular risk profile. Characteristics were well-balanced among participants with and without ASCVD randomized to the RSVpreF vaccine vs no vaccine.

Event rates in persons with and without atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease

The incidence rate of almost all reported primary, secondary, and exploratory outcomes was higher in participants with pre-existing ASCVD as compared with those without (Table 2). For example, the incidence rate of the primary outcome was 0.51 per 1000 participant-years (PY) among individuals with ASCVD and 0.37 per 1000 PY in those without. The rates of all-cause cardiorespiratory disease hospitalization were 60.18 and 23.89 per 1000 PY, respectively. The rate of the composite of hospitalization for myocardial infarction, stroke, or heart failure was 15.66 per 1000 PY in participants with ASCVD and 5.87 per 1000 PY in individuals without. Vaccine effectiveness in persons with and without atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease

Results were generally consistent among participants with and without ASCVD (Figure 1). The incidence rates for the primary outcome were lower in the RSVpreF group than in the control group among individuals without ASCVD [0.12 events/1000 PY vs 0.62 events/1000 PY, VE: 80.0% (95% CI, 29.3–96.3)] and among those with ASCVD [0 events/1000 PY vs 1.02 events/1000 PY, VE: 100.0% (95% CI, −141.3 to 100.0)] (Pinteraction > 0.99). For all-cause cardiorespiratory disease hospitalization, the incidence rates among persons without ASCVD were 22.54 events/1000 PY in the RSVpreF group vs 25.23 events/1000 PY in the control group [VE: 10.6% (95% CI, −0.5 to 20.5)]; among those with ASCVD, the incidence rates were 57.86 events/1000 PY vs 62.49 events/1000 PY [VE: 7.4% (95% CI, −14.1 to 25.8)] (Pinteraction = .77).

For the principal major adverse cardiovascular events outcome, the incidence rates were 5.58 events/1000 PY vs 6.16 events/1000 PY in the no ASCVD group [VE: 9.3% (95% CI, −15.1 to 28.6)] and 14.66 events/1000 PY vs 16.66 events/1000 PY in the ASCVD group [VE: 12.0% (95% CI, −34.6 to 43.3)] (Pinteraction = .90). Vaccine effectiveness estimates for myocardial infarction, hospitalization for heart failure, and hospitalization for atrial fibrillation were also consistent across ASCVD status at baseline (Pinteraction ≥ .05 for all). For stroke, ASCVD modified the treatment effect such that the vaccine appeared to prevent strokes more effectively among participants with ASCVD as compared with those without [without ASCVD: 3.04 events/1000 PY vs 3.16 events/1000 PY, VE: 3.9% (95% CI, −34.0 to 31.1); with ASCVD: 3.06 events/1000 PY vs 8.82 events/1000 PY, VE: 65.3% (95% CI, 23.9–85.8), Pinteraction = .02].

Discussion

In this prespecified, secondary analysis of the DAN-RSV trial, the incidence of both respiratory and cardiovascular events was higher among participants with pre-existing ASCVD than among those without. The VE of the RSVpreF vaccine vs no vaccine was generally consistent across these two subgroups. However, because of their higher a priori risk, the absolute benefits of the RSVpreF vaccine appeared more pronounced among individuals with ASCVD (Structured Graphical Abstract).

A recent clinical consensus statement from the European Society of Cardiology highlighted RSV as a pathogen associated with increased cardiovascular risk, with older individuals and those with coronary artery disease emphasized as susceptible risk groups.17 However, unlike influenza vaccination, the role of RSV vaccination for cardiovascular event reduction has not been established by high-quality randomized studies.18 Indeed, the intriguing concept of cardiovascular risk reduction with RSV vaccination has been based largely on extrapolations from observational studies linking RSV infection with cardiovascular events.4–6 The strategy is also appealing from a mechanistic standpoint as the pathophysiological consequences of RSV infection may trigger both atherosclerotic plaque rupture and thrombosis, with likely greater consequences among persons with pre-existing ASCVD.4,19

In the overall DAN-RSV trial, the primary outcome of RSV-related respiratory tract disease hospitalization occurred at a significantly lower rate in the RSVpreF vaccine group than in the control group.15 However, the finding was based on a limited number of events due to relatively infrequent RSV testing. While we observed absolute risk reductions that numerically favoured the RSVpreF group, the relative effects did not achieve statistical significance, possibly due to the limited number of cardiovascular events that may otherwise be directly affected by a reduction of the RSV burden. Systematic RSV testing was also not conducted as part of DAN-RSV and is infrequently conducted as part of routine care in older adults,20 and most individuals with laboratory-confirmed RSV are not admitted to hospital. Patients with ASCVD likely also have other, more important, determinants of adverse risk that are more directly linked to their cardiovascular pathology. Of the specific cardiovascular endpoints, stroke was significantly reduced in persons with ASCVD, with a significant effect modification of the effectiveness of the RSVpreF vaccine. This is supported by both the overall DAN-RSV results and by a prior registry-based, self-controlled case series analysis in which acute RSV infection temporally increased the risk of ischaemic stroke.6 However, the specific underlying mechanisms and the reasons as to why the beneficial effect of the RSVpreF vaccine did not appear to involve myocardial infarction remain unclear. Nevertheless, based on the numbers of statistical tests conducted, one cannot rule out that the effect on stroke was a chance finding. The burden of cardiorespiratory hospitalization attributable to RSV among high-risk individuals is substantial,21 and our study results suggest use of RSVpreF vaccination overall, with considerably larger absolute risk reductions among participants with ASCVD, although further evaluation among individuals with ASCVD is warranted. As the protective effects of the RSVpreF vaccine have been demonstrated over at least two full RSV seasons,12 and additional follow-up of the DAN-RSV trial is already planned,14 it is possible that more significant differences related to cardiovascular outcomes may emerge over time.

Strengths of this study included the large, well-characterized population and complete follow-up. Data were derived from the largest randomized RSV vaccine trial ever conducted. Use of the nationwide registries and the governmental digital mail system allowed for recruitment of virtually all participants who were willing. Some limitations also require mention, the most notable of which was the overall low RSV-related event rate and lack of systematic RSV testing in the trial, which may obscure interpretation of subgroup analyses. The trial was open label, but the investigators were not involved in vaccine administration, daily clinical management of the participants, or data collection. Well-defined objective outcomes like myocardial infarction, stroke, and death are also less likely to be affected by knowledge of the treatment to which a participant is allocated.22 The Danish registries are known for very high-quality data23; however, the reliance on administrative, routinely collected data may introduce misclassification bias. Nonetheless, this is not likely to have affected between-treatment group differences. Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease may comprise a heterogeneous group of persons, but stratified analyses were not conducted because of the low event rates. Finally, as this was an older, Danish population, which is predominantly white, generalizability to younger age groups and other ethnicities may be limited.

Conclusions

This prespecified analysis of the DAN-RSV trial found that the VE of an RSVpreF vaccine vs no vaccine against respiratory and cardiovascular outcomes was similar among individuals 60 years of age with pre-existing ASCVD as compared with those without. Moreover, the incidence of stroke was lower in the RSVpreF group than in the control group. As absolute risk reductions were higher for most outcomes among persons with ASCVD as compared with those without, this high-risk group may potentially derive greater absolute benefits with the RSVpreF vaccine.

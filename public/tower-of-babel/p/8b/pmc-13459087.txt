Author response to letter by Cunha-Filho & Guedes da Luz da Cunha on decreased anti-Müllerian hormone in women with superficial peritoneal endometriosis
Authors: Meaghan Griffiths, Andrew W Horne, Philippa T K Saunders
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We thank Professors Cunha-Filho and Guedes da Luz da Cunha for their interest and response (Cunha-Filho and Guedes da Luz da Cunha 2026) to our manuscript (Griffiths et al. 2026).

In their response, the authors highlight their research over recent years describing anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) levels in infertile women with endometriosis undergoing IVF (Lemos et al. 2008), in cumulus cells and follicular fluid from infertile women with endometriosis (De Conto et al. 2021), and identification of gene polymorphisms in AMH and related genes (e.g. GDF9) in women with peritoneal endometriosis and infertility (De Conto et al. 2017).

Professor Cunha-Filho’s original study measured AMH in a cohort of women found to have endometriosis when undergoing investigative laparoscopy for infertility. Their data show reduced AMH on day 3 of a stimulation cycle for IVF in 17 women with mild endometriosis, compared with 17 women with tubal obstruction infertility (Lemos et al. 2008). In comparison, our study reports AMH levels in 43 women with superficial peritoneal endometriosis (SPE) and 39 women with pelvic pain in the absence of endometriosis. Our cohort contains women who have had previous pregnancies or have never been pregnant; a very small number (two women in the SPE group) reported previous infertility. This is possibly due to these women never attempting to become pregnant (mean age was 27.7 for our SPE group); however, we did not have information on previous intent to conceive. Ultimately, our two studies are distinct but complementary, supported also by our similar reported AMH levels for women with mild/minimal endometriosis or SPE, 1.26 ng/mL (Lemos et al. 2008) and 1.02 ng/mL (Griffiths et al. 2026), respectively. Our study also represents the first of its size to report lower AMH levels in women with SPE.

Follow-up studies by Professor Cunha-Filho’s group have identified gene polymorphisms in AMH in a larger cohort of 74 women with peritoneal endometriosis and 70 control women undergoing IVF for infertility (De Conto et al. 2017). In contrast, we followed up our reduced AMH finding with investigation into endometriosis-associated inflammation as a potential underlying mechanism. We show in the same women elevated levels of IL17 and TNFalpha in the serum and IL23 in the peritoneal fluid (Griffiths et al. 2026). We propose that this inflammation could be a mechanism explaining the reduced AMH levels observed by ourselves and Professor Cunha-Filho and may be a contributing factor to endometriosis-associated infertility.

Together, our work and that of Professor Cunha-Filho contribute to a growing body of evidence describing the impacts of peritoneal endometriosis to fertility. We hope that the knowledge gained from our studies provides more information for clinicians to better counsel women with this subtype of endometriosis.

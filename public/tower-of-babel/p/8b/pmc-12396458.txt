Giant hydronephrosis in radiology: Learning from incidental findings to improve diagnostic accuracy
Authors: Lokesh Rana, Varun Bansal, Pooja Gurnal, Narvir S Chauhan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe giant Hydronephrosis is defined as dilated pelvic collecting system of kidney containing more than 1 liter of fluid or at least 1.6% of body weight or kidney occupying half of the abdominal cavity. The pelvic ureteric junction (PUJ) obstruction is the commonest cause and a rarely encountered entity nowadays because of availability of advanced diagnostic imaging. We herein report a case of 21-year-old female presenting with abdominal distension for past 6 months without significant pain or tenderness which on CT shows presence of right sided massive hydronephrosis and PUJ obstruction with approximately 20 liters of fluid in the collecting system. The giant Hydronephrosis a rare entity resulting from PUJ obstruction presenting commonly with distension only rarely pain is associated. Though Cross-sectional imaging confirm the diagnosis and help localizing the origin of the lesion but the massive size of lesion make it difficult to differentiate from pelvic benign cystic lesions particularly in females especially when the lesion is occupying almost entire abdominal cavity as in our case, which is rarely encountered.Keywords: Giant hydronephrosis, Pelvic-ureteric junction obstruction, Painless abdominal distension

Introduction

The Giant Hydronephrosis (GH) is defined as dilated pelvic collecting system of kidney containing more than 1 liter of fluid or at least 1.6% of body weight or kidney occupying half of the abdominal cavity and pelvic ureteric junction (PUJ)obstruction is the commonest cause [1,2]. The GH a rarely encountered entity nowadays because of availability of advanced diagnostic imaging which diagnose the condition in early stage [[2], [3], [4]]. Herein we report a case of giant hydronephrosis secondary to PUJ obstruction presenting with slow progressive abdominal distension managed by percutaneous nephrostomy followed nephrectomy and diagnosis confirmed.

Case presentation

A 21-year-old female patient presented to the outpatient department of urology with complaints of gradually progressive abdominal distension and discomfort for past 6 months without associated pain or fever. There was no history hematuria, pyuria, urinary tract infections or any surgical intervention. On examination the patient was average built and nutritional status with normal vital parameters and general physical examination. The systemic examination was normal except for diffuse abdominal distension which was soft, dull and non-tender. The laboratory urine analysis was normal and she had deranged renal function with serum creatinine of 2.1mg/dL. The ultrasound revealed a large cystic lesion with septations probably arising from right side occupying whole of the abdomen with non-visualized right kidney. The contrast enhanced computed tomography was done which showed massive and abnormal right kidney having large cystic lesion causing blowing out of calyces with thinned out renal parenchyma without dilated ureter. It was measuring approximately 26.5×26.5×28cm having HU value of water density without any excretion and drainage of the contrast into the massively dilated cystic collecting system. It was occupying almost entire abdominal cavity having mass effect on surrounding structure displaced small and large bowel, pancreas, spleen and left kidney (Fig. 1).

Contrast enhanced Computed tomography images shows giant Hydronephrosis (white arrow) of right kidney seen in axial (A), sagittal (B) and Coronal(C) planes measuring 26.5×26.5×28cm occupying whole with mass effect on intrabdominal structures.

Therapeutic interventions done were decompression of the giant hydronephrosis by percutaneous nephrostomy with drainage of 20 liter of urine, followed by laparoscopic nephrectomy (Fig. 2). The patient had uneventful postoperative recovery with normalized renal function blood parameters.

Discussion

Giant hydronephrosis occurring in patient of all ages is a rare urological condition commonly caused by PUJ obstruction defined as kidney containing more than 1 liter of fluid or at least 1.6% of body weight or kidney occupying half of the abdominal cavity [1,2]. The radiological diagnostic criteria being massive hydronephrotic pelvic calyceal system crossing midline, occupying more than half of the abdomen and more than 5 vertebrae in length [5]. The first case of GH was described in 1746 since than more than 600 cases have been reported worldwide in literature until now [3]. The commonest etiological factor is congenital PUJ obstruction and other being ureteric and kidney stones, aberrant vessel compression, flap like mucosal folds and high pelvic insertion of the ureter [[5], [6], [7]].

PUJ obstruction causing GH is commonly seen in pediatric and adolescent and rarely in adults as in our case [1]. The clinical presentation is with gradual painless progression of abdominal swelling due to presence of mass as in our case [7]. Other presentations may be vague abdominal pain, hematuria, fullness of flank and vague symptoms due to mass effect on surrounding abdominal structure nausea, dyspepsia, bowel and gastric obstruction, respiratory distress etc. [1,3,9,10].

With advent, availability and widespread use of diagnostic imaging modality the GH has become rare entity as hydronephrosis being diagnosed early in the course [1,8]. The ultrasonography and CT scanning are the main imaging modalities for diagnosis of GH but as in our case massive hydronephrosis limited the evaluation as large cystic mass was only visualized with non-visualization of right kidney [9]. Whereas the contrast enhanced computed tomography is considered ‘gold standard’ in the diagnosis of GH [3].

The differential diagnosis considered are abnormal ascites, intraabdominal cysts, large pseudocysts of pancreas, adrenal cysts and ovarian cystic tumors.

Treatment modalities as described in literature are percutaneous nephrostomy, reconstructive surgeries, nephrectomy depending upon patient condition and functional status of kidney. As in our case with loss of renal function and papery thin renal parenchyma percutaneous nephrostomy followed by simple nephrectomy was optimal choice and was done effectively in our case.

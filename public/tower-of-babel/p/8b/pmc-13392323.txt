Comparing the effectiveness of dexmedetomidine-assisted lumbar plexus block and epidural anesthesia for patients undergoing fixation for femoral shaft fracture
Authors: Dong Guo, Qingxi Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionTo evaluate the safety and benefits of different anesthesia techniques for older patients undergoing femoral shaft fracture fixation.MethodsWe analyzed data from 60 older patients with femoral shaft fractures undergoing external fixation. The patients were divided into two groups. Group A received dexmedetomidine-assisted lumbar plexus combined with sciatic nerve block anesthesia, whereas Group B received continuous epidural anesthesia. Systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, and mean arterial pressure were recorded at three time points: T0 (10 min after anesthesia), T1 (30 min after anesthesia), and T2 (30 min after surgery). We also analyzed the duration of the anesthesia block and postoperative complications, including headache, nausea, vomiting, constipation, urinary retention, and lower extremity venous thrombosis.ResultsThe results showed that diastolic blood pressure in Group A was significantly lower at T1 and T2 than in Group B. The anesthesia onset time was significantly longer in Group B. The incidence of postoperative complications and serum interleukin-6 levels were significantly lower in Group A than in Group B. No significant differences were observed between the two groups in other indices, such as mean arterial pressure and diastolic blood pressure.ConclusionDexmedetomidine-assisted lumbar plexus combined with sciatic nerve block anesthesia was associated with fewer postoperative complications, a shorter anesthesia onset time, and lower serum levels of the inflammatory factor interleukin-6 than the continuous epidural anesthesia in older patients.Keywords: Femoral shaft fractures, anesthesia, effectiveness, complications, dexmedetomidine

Introduction

Older patients undergoing fixation for femoral shaft fractures may face a variety of complications.1,2 These include an increased risk of infection due to advanced age and potential comorbidities, delayed bone healing, deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE), 3 and an elevated risk of delirium or confusion resulting from prolonged bed rest. 4 These special patients require stable vital signs during surgery within a short time to minimize postoperative complications. Inappropriate anesthetic dosing may prolong recovery and increase the risk of complications, whereas an insufficient dosage may reduce anesthetic effectiveness. Therefore, appropriate anesthetic dosing is crucial to the treatment plan.5,6

Dexmedetomidine-assisted lumbar plexus and sciatic nerve block enhances analgesia, reduces opioid use, improves hemodynamic stability, minimizes side effects, and improves postoperative pain management and patient satisfaction.7,8 Continuous epidural anesthesia provides prolonged pain relief, adjustable dosing, reduced opioid use, improved recovery, fewer side effects, and targeted analgesia with minimal discomfort.9,10 This study aimed to assess the safety and benefits of dexmedetomidine-assisted lumbar plexus and sciatic nerve block in older patients with femoral shaft fractures. We retrospectively collected the data, and the methods were performed as previously described. 11 Group A consisted of 30 patients who received dexmedetomidine-assisted lumbar plexus and sciatic nerve block anesthesia, whereas Group B consisted of 30 patients who underwent continuous epidural anesthesia. We compared these two anesthesia methods for older orthopedic patients by analyzing intraoperative indicators, postoperative inflammation, anesthesia onset time, and postoperative complications to identify the best approach for femoral shaft fractures.

Methods

Patients and data collection

The study included 33 male and 27 female patients, aged 54–71 years, with body weights ranging from 55 to 86 kg (S Table 1). Preoperative evaluations demonstrated normal heart, lung, liver, and kidney functions. Exclusion criteria included the following: (a) patients with marked disparities in American Society of Anesthesiologists Physical Status (ASA-PS) classification and preoperative cognitive status; (b) patients who declined to participate; and (c) patients with cerebral infarction, intracranial immune diseases, coagulation disorders, diabetes mellitus, and infection at the nerve block site (Figure 1). The patients were randomly divided in a double-blind manner to two groups. Patients in Group A received dexmedetomidine-assisted lumbar plexus combined with sciatic nerve block anesthesia, whereas patients in Group B received dexmedetomidine-assisted continuous epidural anesthesia. Routine measurements of systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), and mean arterial pressure (MAP) were recorded at various time points, along with an analysis of postoperative complications, including headache, nausea, vomiting, constipation, urinary retention, and lower extremity venous thrombosis, were assessed within 3 days after surgery. Intraoperative serum interleukin-6 (IL-6) levels were assessed using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kit.

Serum inclusion criteria for ELISA

All patients were diagnosed using imaging techniques evaluated by two independent imaging experts. Serum samples were obtained from the patients after surgery at Xingtai People's Hospital. A total of 100 µL of serum was incubated at 4°C overnight, after which 100 ng of recombinant IL-6 in carbonate buffer (pH 9.6) was added to 96-well Sandwich ELISA plates (IL-6/ER06126). Subsequently, 300 µL of blocking buffer (10 mM phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), pH 7.4, with 15% goat serum) was added, followed by a 2-h blocking period at room temperature. The plates were then incubated for 1 h at 37°C and washed 5 times. Next, 100 µL of diluted horseradish peroxidase (HRP)–labeled goat antihuman immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibody was added and incubated for an additional 1 h at 37°C. After three washes with washing buffer, the wells were treated with 50 µL of sulfuric acid (H2SO4) and 100 µL of 3,3',5,5'-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) for 15 min at 37°C to stop the reaction. A microplate reader set to 450 nm was used to measure the absorbance of each well 5 min later.

Ethics approval

This study was approved by the Xingtai People's Hospital Ethics Committee, and written informed consent was obtained from all patients. We confirm that this work adhered to the ethical principles of the 1975 Declaration of Helsinki. Before surgery, none of the patients received any medications, and an intravenous line was established for Ringer’s solution.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis and graphing were performed using GraphPad Prism version 9.0.0 (San Diego, CA, USA), and statistical analyses were performed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) version 22. To assess concentration differences between Group A and Group B, we conducted t tests (parametric tests) to evaluate age, sex, body mass index (BMI), operation time, SBP, DBP, MAP, IL-6 levels, anesthesia onset time, and postoperative complications between the two groups. The statistical results for SBP, DBP, and MAP were presented as x̅ ± SEM. A p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

The general data of all patients include basic clinical information such as age, sex, BMI, and operation time. These data are analyzed in Figure 1. There were no significant differences between the two groups in age, sex, BMI, operation time, or dexmedetomidine concentration (p > 0.05) (S Table 1).

Group A exhibited lower DBP levels at T1 and T2 than Group B. At T0, there was no significant difference in DBP levels between the groups (mean ± SEM: Group A = 63.37 ± 0.67; Group B = 64.70 ± 0.58, p = 0.1379). However, significant differences were observed at T1 (mean ± SEM: Group A = 53.67 ± 0.34; Group B = 58.23 ± 0.55, p < 0.001) and T2 (mean ± SEM: Group A = 53.50 ± 0.38; Group B = 57.53 ± 0.46, p < 0.001) (Figure 2) (S Table 2).

Comparison of DBP between Group A and Group B at T0, T1, and T2 points. The DBP level (mmHg) showed no significant difference at T0 (p = 0.1379), whereas significant differences were observed at T1 (p < 0.001) and T2 (p < 0.001).

DBP: diastolic blood pressure; T0: 10 min after anesthesia; T1: 30 min after anesthesia; T2: 30 min after surgery.

SBP differed significantly between the groups at T0 but not at T1 or T2, whereas MAP showed no significant differences at any time point. At T0, SBP was significantly different between Group A and Group B (mean ± SEM: Group A = 74.50 ± 0.35; Group B = 73.50 ± 0.34, p = 0.0439), whereas no significant differences were observed at T1 (mean ± SEM: Group A = 71.70 ± 0.32; Group B = 71.87 ± 0.21, p = 0.6652) and T2 (mean ± SEM: Group A = 58.50 ± 0.38; Group B = 57.53 ± 0.41, p = 0.0909) (Figure 3(a)) (S Table 3). Similarly, MAP did not differ significantly between the groups at T0 (mean ± SEM: Group A = 72.77 ± 0.36; Group B = 72.50 ± 0.41, p = 0.6281), T1 (mean ± SEM: Group A = 63.93 ± 0.43; Group B = 62.80 ± 0.40, p = 0.0563), and T2 (mean ± SEM: Group A = 58.50 ± 0.38; Group B = 57.53 ± 0.41, p = 0.0909) (Figure 3(b)) (S Table 4).

Comparison of SBP and MAP between Group A and Group B at T0, T1, and T2 points. The SBP level (mmHg) showed a significant difference at T0 (p = 0.0439), whereas no significant differences were observed at T1 (p = 0.6652) and T2 (p = 0.0909) point. The MAP level (mmHg) showed no significant differences at T0 (p = 0.6281), T1 (p = 0.0563) and T2 (p = 0.0909).

MAP: mean arterial pressure; SBP: Systolic blood pressure; T0: 10 min after anesthesia; T1: 30 min after anesthesia; T2: 30 min after surgery.

Group A exhibited a shorter anesthesia onset time and fewer postoperative complications compared to Group B. Specifically, the anesthesia block time was significantly shorter in Group A (mean ± SEM: Group A = 15.47 ± 0.25; Group B = 20.90 ± 0.38, p < 0.001) (Figure 4(a)) (S Table 5). Additionally, postoperative complications were significantly less frequent in Group A (mean ± SEM: Group A = 20.27 ± 0.43; Group B = 30.53 ± 1.13, p < 0.001) (Figure 4(b)) (S Table 6).

Anesthesia onset time and complications between Group A and Group B. The anesthesia onset time was significantly shorter in Group A (p < 0.001). The complications showed significantly decreased in Group A (p < 0.001).

Group A also demonstrated lower IL-6 expression. A comparison of serum IL-6 levels between the two groups revealed that the serum IL-6 level in Group A after the operation was significantly lower than that in Group B (mean ± SEM: Group A = 2009.90 ± 27.00; Group B = 3438.73 ± 137.00, p < 0.001) (Figure 5) (S Table 7).

The serum IL-6 levels between Group A and Group B. The serum IL-6 levels were significantly lower in Group A (p < 0.001).

IL-6: interleukin 6.

Compared with continuous epidural anesthesia group, dexmedetomidine-assisted lumbar plexus combined with sciatic nerve block showed improved outcomes in DBP levels at both 30 min after anesthesia and 30 min after surgery. No significant differences were observed in MAP and SBP. Furthermore, the dexmedetomidine-assisted approach also demonstrated advantages in terms of shorter anesthesia onset time, reduced postoperative complications, and lower serum IL-6 expression levels (Table 1).

Discussion

Aging reduces cardiopulmonary function, making anesthesia demands harder to meet clinical operations. Excessive anesthesia can lead to serious complications, including respiratory depression and postoperative cognitive dysfunction.12,13 Combined lumbar plexus and sciatic nerve block anesthesia is commonly used for fracture fixation. Clinical studies have demonstrated that this anesthetic technique helps maintain stable vital signs during surgery. However, it is also associated with certain complications, including lower limb thrombosis and postoperative issues such as headache, nausea, and vomiting.14,15

Dexmedetomidine, an alpha-2 adrenergic receptor agonist used for sedation, has been associated with hypotension, bradycardia, and sinus arrest. 16 Optimized anesthesia promotes better surgical outcomes and postoperative recovery by selecting the best drugs and methods, reducing pain and minimizing complications. Dexmedetomidine is a protective factor by reducing a sepsis-associated inflammatory response. 17 Studies show dexmedetomidine inhibits endotoxin-induced inflammation by reducing IL-6 and tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNF-α) levels and neutrophil aggregation, demonstrating anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects. 18 In the present study, we evaluated the anesthetic efficacy of dexmedetomidine-assisted lumbar plexus and sciatic nerve block in older patients with femoral shaft fractures. The treatment group (30 patients) showed that intraoperative dexmedetomidine infusion combined with nerve block anesthesia effectively reduced postoperative pain and complications. 19

A limitation of our research is that it is a single-center study, lacking multicenter analysis. The single-center design introduced limitations such as selection bias and limited generalizability. A single-center study may not fully reflect the diagnostic and treatment practices of other hospitals, leading to limited applicability of the research results across different clinical settings. This may result in insufficient data completeness and representativeness. However, our hospital's research also has its strengths, as it focuses on the unique characteristics of diseases in this region. The patient cohort mentioned above accurately reflects the real situation of orthopedic anesthesia in this region, along with the complications associated with various anesthesia techniques. For patient undergoing fixation for femoral shaft fracture, compared to continuous epidural anesthesia, dexmedetomidine-assisted lumbar plexus combined with sciatic nerve block anesthesia demonstrated a shorter onset time and fewer postoperative complications in this area. IL-6 is a key acute-phase marker reflecting surgical trauma and inflammation. It declines gradually and returns to baseline as recovery proceeds. Vital signs remained stable in all enrolled patients during the postoperative period. A notable limitation of this study is that we did not collect preoperative baseline IL-6 data, and all comparative analyses were limited to postoperative results only. 20

Conclusion

Dexmedetomidine-assisted lumbar plexus combined with sciatic nerve block represents a safe and effective anesthetic technique for older patients with femoral shaft fractures. Compared with continuous epidural anesthesia, it was associated with lower DBP, lower serum IL-6 levels, a shorter anesthesia onset time, and fewer postoperative complications, whereas no significant differences were observed in MAP and SBP. These findings may provide useful evidence to guide the selection of an optimal anesthetic approach for older patients undergoing femoral shaft fracture surgery.

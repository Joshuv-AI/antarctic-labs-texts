The Influence of the Conditions of Cultivation of Lactic Acid Bacteria on Their Antagonistic Activity Against Some Plant Pathogenic Bacteria
Authors: Olha Vasyliuk, Inna Garmasheva, Serhii Skrotskyi, Liudmyla Khomenko, Ihor Hretskyi, Natalia Senchylo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPlant diseases caused by pathogenic microorganisms result in significant damage to agriculture. Lactic acid bacteria (LAB), in particular strains of Lactiplantibacillus plantarum ( L. plantarum ), are used as one of the biocontrol methods against plant pathogenic bacteria due to high antagonistic activity associated with their metabolic potential. We have investigated the influence of nutrient medium components (various carbon and nitrogen sources) and cultivation conditions (temperature, duration, and pH) of L. plantarum strains on the level of their antagonistic activity against the test strains of plant pathogenic bacteria. The antimicrobial activity of LAB supernatants was maximal in the presence of 3% (30 g/L) sucrose as the main carbon source and 1% (10 g/L) tyrosine as the main nitrogen source in the nutrient medium against all investigated test strains of PPB. However, the use of such a carbon source as galactose or arabinose led to a decrease or even absence of antimicrobial properties of LAB against phytopathogenic bacteria. The optimal conditions for cultivation of lactobacilli were determined: cultivation temperature +30 ± 1°C, pH 7.8, and duration 72 h. Strains of L. plantarum 13c and 21c caused zones of inhibition in test pathogens from 298 to 291 mm. Whereas during 24‐h cultivation of LAB strains, their antagonistic activity was significantly lower, and the zones of inhibition decreased by 30%. The duration and temperature had a significant effect on increasing the antagonistic activity of L. plantarum strains, in contrast to the pH of the medium (p ≥ 0.05).Keywords: antagonistic activity, L. plantarum , phytopathogenic bacteria, plant pathogenic bacteria nutrient media optimization

Introduction

Agriculture is a globally significant economic branch of critical importance that occupies about 37% of the world's land area, according to the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) (FAO/WHO 2006). However, the increase in demand for agricultural products is accompanied by climate change, the depletion of natural resources, and the emergence of new pathogens causing plant diseases that negatively affect agricultural production (Jaffar 2023). Considering the growing demand for food products of plant origin, considerable attention is focused on the protection of agricultural products from the influence of various pathogenic organisms, such as fungi and bacteria (Hirozawa et al. 2023) (Manjarres et al. 2021), viruses, protozoa, and insects.

Phytopathogenic bacteria (PPB) not only cause plant diseases (necrosis, hypertrophy, mummification, and discoloration) (Nazarov et al. 2020) but they also lead to spoilage and rotting of stored crops (Francés et al. 2006). In particular, such pathogens as Erwinia carotovora and Xanthomonas vesicatoria , which contribute to the spoilage of fresh fruits and vegetables (Spadaro and Gullino 2004), Xanthomonas campestris , which causes black rot of agricultural crops, and Pectobacterium carotovorum , which causes bacterial soft rot, are a serious problem for agriculture nowadays (Steglińska et al. 2022). Pseudomonas syringae pv. syringae causes bacterial rot of blossoms and fruits in grapes (Whitelaw‐Weckert et al. 2011).

One of the main methods to combat plant pathogens is chemical fungicides and bactericides, which have been widely used for more than a century (Malik et al. 2021). However, some of them are prohibited for post‐harvest fruit processing, and some have been withdrawn from the market due to possible toxicological risks (Directive 91/414/CEE EU). The widespread use of synthetic chemicals and pesticides in food and agriculture is undesirable, as it contributes to the development of resistance in PPB. Most chemicals can accumulate in plants, negatively affect human health (Muri et al. 2009), inhibit the growth of beneficial soil microbiota, and disrupt the stability of ecosystems (Sindhu et al. 2016) (Jaffar et al. 2023) (Meena et al. 2020). Therefore, there is a growing need to develop alternative biological treatments for microbial control to replace chemicals and toxic pesticides for the purposes of soil, plants, planting material decontamination, and shelf‐life extension of agricultural products (Russo et al. 2017) (Linares‐Morales et al. 2018). In addition, biological methods of plant protection show a selective effect and do not have mutagenic activity. Thus, strains of lactic acid bacteria (LAB) are the basis of effective and environmentally safe microbiological preparations that can be used to protect plants and stimulate their life processes (Raman et al. 2022). LAB are promising candidates for the development of microbial biopesticides, as they include strains classified as safe by FAO; also, EFSA qualified their presumption of safety (FAO/WHO 2006). Remarkable antagonistic properties of LAB, especially strains of Lactiplantibacillus plantarum ( L. plantarum ), allow their use to suppress plant pathogenic (Shi and Knøchel 2023) (Vasyliuk et al. 2014) and opportunistic microorganisms (Ruiz et al. 2023) (Goel and Halami 2023) (Govindaraj et al. 2021). As a result, LAB are the main component of many microbial preparations intended for the prevention and control of microbial pathogens. LAB demonstrate a high antagonistic effect due to their capability to synthesize a number of metabolites: organic acids, hydrogen peroxide, bacteriocins (Arena et al. 2019) (Gajbhiye and Kapadnis 2016) (Fugaban et al. 2023). However, it should be noted that the accumulation of biologically active metabolites directly depends not only on the individual characteristics of the producer strain but also on the cultivation conditions used to obtain the target product (Yi et al. 2015). The biosynthetic activity of L. plantarum strains significantly depends on cultivation conditions, in particular the composition of the nutrient medium (carbon, nitrogen sources), pH, temperature, and cultivation time. In fact, the culture medium composition is closely related to the metabolic capabilities of the producer strain and significantly affects the biosynthesis of secondary metabolites, which are an important factor in LAB antagonistic activity against phytopathogens (Domínguez‐Gutiérrez et al. 2023).

Thus, one of the current directions of research is the search for optimal conditions for cultivating strains that produce biologically active metabolites with inhibitory properties: improving cultivation methods, studying the viability and activity of microorganisms under different growing conditions, and taking into account their trophic needs.

The aim of the present work was to investigate the effect of nutrients and cultivation conditions to increase their antagonistic activity against phytopathogenic microorganisms.

Results

MRS broth without carbon and nitrogen sources was the basic nutrient medium for the study of LAB antimicrobial action against plant pathogen strains. Selection of the optimal nutrient medium composition and physical growth parameters was carried out for the following seven strains of lactobacilli: six strains of the genus Lactobacillus sp. and one strain of the genus Leuconostoc mesenteroides . Research aimed at the selection of optimal conditions for LAB cultivation to increase their antagonistic activity against PPB through active synthesis of metabolites, in particular organic acids, antimicrobial compounds of protein nature, and hydrogen peroxide. At the first stage of the research, selection of the carbon source was carried out as it is one of the most important nutrient medium components that affect the synthesis of metabolites with antimicrobial activity. The following carbohydrates were studied: sucrose, lactose, fructose, galactose, maltose, and arabinose, all in a concentration of 1%. It was shown that the ability to cause the largest growth inhibition zones (12.6 ± 3.05 mm to 13.3 ± 1.04 mm) of phytopathogenic bacteria was found in two strains of lactobacilli— L. plantarum 21c and L. plantarum 13c. These strains were selected for further research (Table 1).

When galactose and arabinose were used as carbon sources, the antimicrobial activity of LAB cultures against PPB was lower or completely absent compared with use of other carbon sources. Strains L. plantarum 12c, L. plantarum 23c and L. plantarum 47c had rather low antagonistic activity against all test strains, regardless of the carbon source used. Inhibition zones in referent PPB strains were in ranges from 0.6 ± 1.03 mm to 7.3 ± 2.03 mm.

The highest antimicrobial activity of LAB strains was observed after their cultivation on growth medium enriched with sucrose. Strains L. plantarum 13c and L. plantarum 21c caused growth inhibition zones in test PPB cultures measuring as 13.3 ± 1.04 and 13.1 ± 3.02, respectively.

In this research we have also investigated the effect of different concentrations of carbon sources on the synthesis of LAB metabolites with antagonistic activity against phytopathogenic bacteria. Since at the first stage of the study, sucrose was identified as the optimal carbon source enabling the highest antimicrobial activity of lactobacilli against phytopathogens, it was expedient to determine its optimal concentrations. As a result of antagonistic activity comparison in strain L. plantarum 13c at different concentrations of sucrose in the nutrient medium, it was found that this strain demonstrated the highest level of antimicrobial activity against PPB after cultivation with 3.0% (3.0 g/100 mL) of sucrose in growth medium. Test pathogens X. campestris and Clavibacter michiganensis were the most sensitive to the antimicrobial effect of LAB at a given sucrose concentration. Growth inhibition zones in these pathogens reached 14.5 ± 0.02 mm and 14.0 ± 0.05 mm, respectively. After cultivation of L. plantarum 13c strain on a medium with 1.0%, 2.0%, 4.0%, 5.0%, and 6.0% sucrose, the following growth inhibition was observed in the abovementioned test PPB cultures: 11.0 ± 0.03 and 10.5 ± 0.05 mm (at 1%), 12.5 ± 0.04 mm and 12.5 ± 0.06 mm (at 2% and 4%), 10.0 ± 0.03 mm and 11.0 ± 0.05 mm at (5%), and 10.0 ± 0.04 mm and 11.5 ± 0.03 mm (at 6%), respectively (Figure 1).

After comparison of the antagonistic activity of L. plantarum 21c supernatant after cultivation on media with different carbon sources, the highest antagonistic activity against phytopathogens X. campestris (15.5 ± 0.05 mm) and P. carotovorum (15.5 ± 0.04 mm) was shown after cultivation in a medium with sucrose. Being cultivated on a medium with lactose, L. plantarum 21c demonstrated much lower antagonistic activity: in X. campestris —9.5 ± 0.03 mm and in P. carotovorum —8.0 ± 0.05 mm. On the medium with fructose as the only carbon source, growth inhibition activity was: in X. campestris —10.5 ± 0.06 mm and in P. carotovorum —8.0 ± 0.04 mm. When comparing the antagonistic activity of strain L. plantarum 21c at different concentrations of sucrose in the nutrient medium, a significant difference in the inhibition zones diameters in P. syringae was observed: at 3% sucrose (14.0 ± 0.05 mm) and at 6% (8.5 ± 0.05 mm). A noticeable difference in antagonistic activity was also found against X. campestris , where zones of growth inhibition after cultivation of the LAB strain at 3% sucrose measured as 14.5 ± 0.05 mm and at 5% as 11 ± 0.05 mm (Figure 1). A significant difference in the inhibition zones diameters was also observed for Pseudomonas fluorescens .

Thus, when cultivating L. plantarum 21c in a medium with 3% sucrose, the growth inhibition zones in P. fluorescens reached 12.5 ± 0.05 mm; at 1% and 2% sucrose concentrations, zone diameters reached 9.0 ± 0.04 mm.

It can be concluded that the highest antagonistic activity of lactobacilli against PPB was observed when LAB strains were cultivated in a medium with 3% sucrose as the main carbon source.

The next step of the study was to determine the influence of different nitrogen sources on the manifestation degree of LAB antagonistic activity against test pathogens. Peptone, yeast extract, meat extract, tyrosine, and corn extract in concentrations of 1% were used in this experiment. The highest growth inhibition potential caused in PPB by L. plantarum 13c and L. plantarum 21c strains was observed after cultivation on a medium with tyrosine (17.4 ± 0.03 mm and 18.2 ± 0.01 mm, respectively). The lowest antagonistic potential was observed after incubation on the media with corn or yeast extracts (Table 2).

It was found that strain L. plantarum 13c demonstrated the highest antimicrobial activity against X. campestris (23.0 ± 0.03 mm) and P. carotovorum (15.5 ± 0.04 mm) when it was cultivated in a medium with 0.5% tyrosine. It was established that strain L. plantarum 13c caused maximal inhibition zones in P. fluorescens (15.5 ± 0.05 mm) and P. syringae (16.0 ± 0.05 mm) when cultivated with 1% tyrosine; the best antagonistic activity against C. michiganensis (25.5 ± 0.04 mm) was observed on 1.5% tyrosine; against Pseudomonas corrugata (19.5 ± 0.05 mm)—on 2% tyrosine; against Agrobacterium tumefaciens (19.0 ± 0.05 mm)—on 3% tyrosine in the nutrient medium. Inhibition zones of pathogen X. vesicatoria measured as 19.0 ± 0.04 mm when L. plantarum 13c was cultivated with 2% tyrosine, 19.5 ± 0.05 mm—with 3%, and 20.5 ± 0.03 mm with other studied concentrations of tyrosine.

Thus, it was established that 1% tyrosine concentration in growth medium was optimal for LAB cultivation, leading to the best antagonistic activity of tested LAB strains against phytopathogens.

It was established that optimal cultivation conditions for strains L. plantarum 13c and L. plantarum 21c, which led to an increase in their inhibitory activity against PPB, were a growth temperature of 30°C, pH 7.8, and a cultivation time of 72 h (diameters of the growth inhibition zone of the test strains were 298.0 and 291.0 mm). It should be noted that strain L. plantarum 21c, when cultivated with such parameters as pH 6.8, cultivation time of 72 h, and temperature of 23°C, has shown the same high level of antagonistic activity.

Discussion

Today, LAB strains are widely used in various industries. They have a number of advantages over other microorganisms. In particular, lactobacilli have generally recognized as safe (GRAS) status, demonstrate favorable probiotic and technological properties, show antagonistic activity against conditionally pathogenic and plant pathogenic microorganisms, and produce a wide range of bioactive compounds and metabolites (Sharma et al. 2023). It is known that the antagonistic activity of LAB can vary depending on the nutrient medium composition and cultivation conditions. The influence of physical parameters of cultivation (pH, time, and temperature) and nutrient medium composition, which affect the quantitative and qualitative composition of metabolites and therefore impact the antagonistic and antifungal activity of LAB, is actively investigated nowadays.

In this research, we established that studied lactobacilli strains have demonstrated the highest antimicrobial effect against PPB when they were cultivated in an optimized environment with 3% sucrose as the main carbon source. These data are consistent with Aarti et al.; as a result of their research on the influence of different carbon sources on antimicrobial activity levels, it was shown that lactose is the optimal carbon source for stimulation of Lactobacillus pentosus antagonistic activity against test pathogens (Aarti et al. 2016). Whereas in the work of Ekhay et al., it was demonstrated that the antagonistic activity of LAB increased when glucose was added to the culture medium (Ekhay et al. 2013).

However, in the study of Veettil et al., it was established that 1% dextrose promotes the synthesis of LAB bacteriocins (Veetti and Chitra 2022). It is known from other studies that the maximum synthesis of lactic and acetic acids in microorganisms cultivated on media with sucrose occurred after 36–60 h of cultivation. Thus, it was shown that after 60 h of cultivation, Leuconostoc mesenteroides (T8AB6) synthesized 38.12 mg/mL of lactic acid, L. plantarum (T8N10)—32.22 mg/mL. At the same time, if fructose was used as a substrate, acid synthesis began later and occurred at different times depending on the strain. It is important that strain L. plantarum T10AG26 reached maximum lactic acid production (32.15 mg/mL) after 36 h of sucrose fermentation, while when cultivated with glucose and fructose, the maximum acid production occurred after 48 and 72 h of fermentation, respectively (Ouattara et al. 2019).

As a result of analyzing data obtained in this study and studies of other authors, it can be assumed that different carbon sources can induce or suppress the antagonistic activity of LAB supernatants depending on the strain.

Inhibitory effect of lactobacilli on plant pathogenic bacteria depends on the synthesis of organic acids, bacteriocins, diacetyl, hydrogen peroxide, short‐chain fatty acids, inhibitory enzymes, and antimicrobial biologically active peptides. In many works, it was demonstrated that organic acids (lactic, acetic, propionic, formic, and benzoic) are the main metabolites enabling lactobacilli to suppress the growth of a wide range of pathogenic microorganisms (Jaffar et al. 2023). The antibacterial effect of lactic acid is associated with slowing down of active membrane transport in pathogens, lowering their intracellular pH, and general inhibition of the metabolism, which ultimately leads to inhibition of pathogen growth (Rattanachaikunsopon and Phumkhachorn 2010). Studies of Steglińska et al. (2022) established that LAB inhibited the growth of 10 plant pathogens (in particular, P. carotovorum , F. oxysporum , and Rhophitulus solani ) due to synthesis of organic acids and ethanol. Another study showed the inhibitory effect of L. pentosus and Leuconostoc fallax strains on cruciferous diseases, including black rot caused by X. campestris and soft rot caused by P. carotovorum (Lin et al. 2020). In particular, it was shown that high antifungal activity of the L. plantarum strain depends not only on organic acids and phenolic compounds but also on synergistic interaction between other Lactobacillus metabolites (Nazareth et al. 2019).

The incubation time of microorganisms is one of the important factors that directly affects the qualitative and quantitative composition of LAB strains metabolites and accordingly their antimicrobial activity. As a result of our research, it was found that the antagonistic activity of strains L. plantarum 13c and L. plantarum 23c against test pathogens was the highest when they were cultivated for 72 h, which is consistent with the literature data. The influence of the cultivation time (12, 24, 48, 72 and 96 h) on the antagonistic activity of L. plantarum strain was investigated in other studies. It was shown that inhibition zones in plant pathogens were significantly larger when L. plantarum was cultivated for 48–72 h (Talluri and Lanka 2017). This may be related to an increase in the amount of synthesized organic acids. Thus, strains isolated from fermented cereals ( Lactobacillus fermentum , Lactobacillus casei , Lactobacillus brevis , Lactobacillus delbruckii , Lactobacillus acidophilus , L. plantarum , L. mesenteroides ) produced lactic acid with its peak concentration after 48 h of the incubation period. As the incubation period increased, the amount of lactic acid produced also increased until a gradual decline was observed after 48 h of cultivation (Adesokan et al. 2009).

It is known that the optimal cultivation temperature is specific not only for individual species but is even a strain‐specific parameter. Thus, it was shown that in both homofermentative strains L. casei NRRL B‐441 and L. casei subsp. rhamnosus NRRL B‐445, the most efficient synthesis of L (+)‐lactic acid occurred at 37°C, although in L. casei the highest concentration of lactic acid was obtained at 41°C. The maximum yield of lactic acid in L. casei was 4.1 g L−1 h−1; in L. casei subsp. rhamnosus —3.5 g L−1 h−1 (Talluri and Lanka 2017). Using the response surface methodology in the conditions of periodic fermentation of L. casei NRRL B‐441, a regression model was constructed that has shown that the maximum concentration of lactic acid was synthesized at a temperature of 35°C (Hujanen et al. 2001). According to other data, it is known that a number of species isolated during spontaneous fermentation of cereals, namely, L. fermentum , L. casei , L. brevis , L. delbruckii , L. acidophilus , L. plantarum , and L. mesenteroides , produce lactic acid at the optimal temperature of 30°C (Adesokan et al. 2009).

While selecting the optimal temperature for effective antimicrobial activity of L. plantarum strain LIPI13‐2‐LAB011, it was found that in this culture, the most active synthesis of antimicrobial metabolites occurs at 33°C. This result is consistent with another study, where the most optimal temperature for bacteriocin production by strain L. casei LA‐1 ranged from 33.5°C to 34.5°C (Kumar et al. 2013). The results of other studies show that the optimal cultivation temperature for L. plantarum ST71KS isolated from homemade goat feta cheese was 30°C–37°C; in this temperature range, the strain was producing the highest yield of antimicrobial metabolites active against Listeria monocytogenes strains 603 and 607 (Murua et al. 2013). This corresponds to the results of our study because the highest antagonistic activity against phytopathogens was reached when lactobacilli were cultivated at a temperature of 30°C. During the investigation of the temperature effect on acetic acid synthesis, it was found that L. plantarum strain T11G3 produced the largest amount of acetic acid (1.7 mg/mL) at a temperature of 30°C.

In addition to organic acids, LAB produce hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), which plays an important role in antagonistic activity due to its oxidizing effect on microbial cells leading to protein structure destruction (Jaffar et al. 2023). However, it has been shown that the antimicrobial effect of H2O2 is limited in LAB due to the low level of its synthesis by lactobacilli (Lay et al. 2016).

However, it should be noted that in vitro studies have shown that hydrogen peroxide alone has an antimicrobial effect and successfully inhibits the growth of Erwinia carotovora without affecting plant tissues.

Conclusions

In this research, we have studied the influence of cultivation medium components of LAB of the genera Lactobacillus and Leuconostoc on the level of their antagonistic activity against plant pathogenic microorganisms. It was shown that sucrose (at a concentration of 3%) was the optimal source of carbon compared with lactose, fructose, maltose, galactose, and arabinose. After the cultivation of LAB on galactose and arabinose, low or no anti‐PPB effect was observed in LAB supernatants. The antimicrobial activity of the supernatants was maximal in the presence of 1% tyrosine in the nutrient medium as the only source of nitrogen. It was found that after cultivation with tyrosine, LAB caused larger growth inhibition zones in plant pathogens (increased by 15.5–17.5 mm).

Our study has shown that the optimal cultivation conditions for L. plantarum 13c enabling the highest antagonistic activity against plant pathogenic bacteria were as follows: pH 7.8; cultivation time 72 h; growth temperature +30°C ± 1°C. This led to high antagonistic activity of cultivated strains, causing growth inhibition zones of phytopathogens up to 298.0 mm in diameter. The best cultivation conditions for another study, LAB strain L. plantarum 21c, were pH 6.8–7.8; cultivation time 72 h; temperature range 23°C–30°C. This strain caused growth inhibition zones in plant pathogenic bacteria up to 291.0 mm in diameter. However, when these cultures were cultivated for 24 h, their antagonistic activity was significantly lower, and growth inhibition zones decreased by 30%.

Materials and Methods

Seven strains of the genus Leuconostoc and Lactobacillus were the main objects of the present research. LAB were isolated from various fermented vegetables and fruits: pickled celery, cabbage, cucumbers, and apples.

All strains were stored in 30% (w/v) glycerol at −50°C. Before use, the culture was activated by three successive transfers in the MRS medium.

Test Organisms

P. syringae UCM IMV В‐10277 (ATCC 19310), P. fluorescens UCM IMV В‐8573, P. carotovorum UCM IMV В‐1095т (ATCC 15713), A. tumefaciens UCM IMV В‐1000, X. campestris UCM IMV В‐1049, C. michiganensis IMV 10 2, P. corrugata IMV В‐9069, and X. vesicatoria В‐1060 (IMV 7924) were used as phytopathogenic indicator strains of the Culture Collection of the Department of phytopathogenic bacteria, Zabolotny Institute of Microbiology and Virology NAS, Kiev, Ukraine. Strains of phytopathogenic bacteria were cultivated for 24 h at +30°C ± 1°C in potato broth (PB).

Selection of Best Carbon Source and Nitrogen

The influence of different source carbon (sucrose, lactose, fructose, galactose, maltose, and arabinose) and sources nitrogen (peptone, yeast extract, meat extract, and corn extract) on Lactobacillus was studied (Lay et al. 2016). The basic MRS medium containing each source carbon/nitrogen separately at a concentration of 1 g (g/100 mL) and pH 6.8 was sterilized before use by autoclaving for 30 min at 112°C (0.75 atm). The influence of the optimal concentration of the best carbon source (1‐6 g/100 mL) and nitrogen (0.3–3 g/100 mL) was determined. All samples were incubated at 30°C for 22 ± 2 h. A flask without any carbon/nitrogen source was kept as a control.

Cell‐free culture supernatants were obtained by centrifugation at 5000 rpm for 15 min (22331 AG, Eppendorf, Hamburg, Germany) from Leuconostoc and Lactobacillus cultures grown in broth at 30°C for 22 ± 2 h. Supernatants of strains LAB were sterilized through a 0.45‐μm pore filter (Merck Millipore) (Kalhoro et al. 2023). The CFS was used for antimicrobial testing by using the disk diffusion method (Rohmatussolihat et al. 2018). Antagonistic activity of CFS was evaluated by measuring the diameter of the inhibition zone against the phytopathogenic bacteria.

Effects of Duration, pH of Medium, and Temperature on the Antagonistic Properties of Strains Lactobacillus plantarum

The optimization of different physical parameters of cultivation on LAB antagonistic action (duration, pН of medium, and temperature) was investigated. Before sterilization, pH was adjusted to 5.6, 6.8, and 7.8 using 1‐M NaOH and 1‐M HCl and was sterilized by autoclaving for 0.75 atm. The incubation temperature was set to 23°C ± 1°C, 30°C ± 1°C, and 37°C ± 1°C. To select the optimal duration for increased antagonistic activity, microorganisms were cultivated for 24, 48, and 72 h.

The selection of the cultivation conditions of LAB to increase the antimicrobial activity was carried out using the compositions obtained with a three‐factor three‐level experimental design according to Box–Behnken plan. Data analysis was performed using trial‐version Statistica 14 (TIBCO Software Inc. 2020). Statistical Analysis

Data processing was performed using STATISTICA 7.0 (StatSoft Inc., Tulsa, OK, USA). Data analysis were performed using the program trial‐version Statistica 14 (TIBCO Software Inc. 2020). A three‐factor three‐level experimental design was performed according to the Box–Behnken design. Antibacterial data were presented as the mean (M) ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analyses were performed using one‐way ANOVA. The post hoc test using the criterion of LSD was used for assessing the reliability of quantitative indicators of differences in different strains. Statistical significance was defined as at p ≤ 0.05.

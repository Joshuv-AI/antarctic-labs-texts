PDC-DETR: a Parallel Dilated Convolutional Detection Transformer for preliminary screening of multiple macular degeneration
Authors: Yuhang Wang, Wenhao Wei, Xinghao Pei, Honglei Zhan, Yu Tang, Yahong Li, Jing Bai, Jinshan Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCurrent diagnostic approaches for macular degeneration in optical coherence tomography (OCT) images often lead to misdiagnosis due to their limited ability to capture the disease’s multiscale and irregular features. We present PDC-DETR, a Parallel Dilated Convolutional Detection Transformer, which introduces three major innovations for accurate and efficient macular degeneration analysis. First, a Parallel Feature-Optimized Attention Pyramid Network (PFOAPN) enables simultaneous modeling of global context and local details through multi-scale feature pathways. Second, a novel Wise-MPDIoU loss dynamically adjusts to variations in image quality while improving lesion localization accuracy. Third, the lightweight design ensures real-time clinical applicability, requiring only 38.2 MB parameters and 58.5 GFLOPs, while achieving 94.1% accuracy across five macular degeneration categories at 71 FPS. This study establishes a new benchmark for automated OCT-based retinal disease detection, providing both high diagnostic accuracy and practical clinical deployment potential.Keywords: Optical coherence tomography, Transformer, Macular degeneration, Feature extraction, Real-time processing

Introduction

Macular degeneration, glaucoma, and cataracts are among the leading causes of blindness worldwide [1, 2]. In particular, macular degeneration can severely impair central vision, making early detection and timely treatment essential to prevent irreversible vision loss [3, 4]. Optical coherence tomography (OCT) has become the preferred imaging modality for retinal diagnostics due to its non-invasive nature and high-resolution cross-sectional imaging capability [5–8]. However, the morphological features of macular degeneration are often irregular and multi-scaled [9, 10]. Manual interpretation of OCT scans is not only time-consuming but also highly subjective, frequently leading to misdiagnosis or missed diagnoses [11–13].

Artificial intelligence (AI) has demonstrated remarkable performance in medical image analysis and pathological recognition [14–16]. Deep learning, in particular, shows great potential in early detection, automated diagnosis and grading, treatment planning, and personalized medicine [17–19]. Among studies on macular degeneration using retinal OCT images, classification recognition and region segmentation represent two major research directions. Syed Ale Hassan et al. [20] developed a classification network based on the DenseNet architecture for detecting central serous chorioretinopathy (CSC). Ying-Chih Lo et al. [21] applied the ResNeSt101 model for macular prefovea recognition. Ghada Atteia et al. [22] employed Bayesian optimization to significantly enhance classification performance in detecting diabetic macular degeneration. Meanwhile, Yaqi Wang et al. [23] proposed the PGKD-Net to improve choroidal structure segmentation. Yang Jian et al. [24] introduced the RMPPNet for segmenting neurosensory retinal detachment in SD-OCT images. Although these models have achieved success in detecting individual types of macular degeneration, their generalization capability is limited by the substantial morphological variations among different macular diseases.

Xin Ye et al. [25] constructed five independent binary classifiers using ResNeSt101, each designed to identify a specific disease in retinal OCT images. Seda A Tuncer et al. [26] proposed a hybrid computer-aided diagnosis system based on a CNN-SVM architecture to classify diabetic macular edema (ME), choroidal neovascularization (CNV), and normal OCT images without requiring additional feature extraction or noise filtering. Nevertheless, few studies have achieved accurate quantification of lesion location and size. Han Lu et al. [27] used an enhanced feature fusion YOLOv5 model to localize choroidal neovascularization (CNV), macular edema (ME), and drusen, achieving high accuracy on a self-constructed dataset. Xiaoming Chen et al. [28] utilized multiple deep learning models including ResNet50 and YOLOv3 to collaborate on classification and localization tasks. Although these studies successfully identified the locations of maculopathies, their high computational complexity hindered real-time detection.

Given the strict real-time requirements in clinical settings and the growing need for cloud-based automatic screening in remote consultations, there is a pressing demand for algorithm lightweightness and fast inference speeds. Despite recent advancements, achieving real-time and rapid detection of macular degeneration remains a significant challenge.

Considering the technical challenges posed by the unique characteristics of retinal maculopathies and pathological regions, we propose a real-time macular degeneration detection method that accurately recognizes and classifies maculopathies in retinal OCT images.

In this paper, we propose PDC-DETR (Parallel Dilated Convolutional Detection Transformer), a novel real-time detector for macular degeneration that introduces three key innovations to overcome current diagnostic challenges:

Transformer-based Architecture: Combines self-attention mechanisms with parallel dilated convolutions to effectively capture both global context and local details in OCT images.

Method

Overall architecture

This paper proposes the PDC-DETR network for the detection task of retinal macular degeneration. Figure 1 shows the overall architecture of the PDC-DETR network.

Overall architecture of the PDC-DETR network

Our retinal macular degeneration detection framework builds upon RT-DETR [29], an efficient end-to-end Transformer-based detector developed by Baidu Flying Paddle and publicly available on GitHub (https://github.com/lyuwenyu/RT-DETR ). This architecture demonstrates superior performance characteristics particularly suited for medical image analysis, achieving real-time processing speeds comparable to YOLO variants while significantly reducing computational overhead through optimized architectural design. Compared to conventional detection models, RT-DETR exhibits faster convergence during training, typically requiring only 75–80 epochs with minimal data augmentation, as it eliminates the need for mosaic enhancement while maintaining competitive accuracy [29]. The model’s effectiveness in OCT image analysis stems from its multi-level self-attention mechanism, which enables comprehensive global context modeling while effectively suppressing imaging artifacts and noise that commonly degrade retinal scan quality. These combined advantages of computational efficiency, training stability, and robust feature extraction make RT-DETR particularly well-suited for clinical applications where both diagnostic accuracy and processing speed are paramount.

Despite the excellent performance of RT-DETR in capturing global information, there is some potential room for improvement when dealing with retinal macular degeneration detection. In order to further improve the performance of the model in detecting maculopathies at different scales, ResNet-18 was used as the backbone network in this paper to improve the depth and accuracy of feature extraction and meet the requirements of the real-time performance of the model. The backbone network’s last three output layers (S3, S4, and S5) are used as input for the hybrid encoder. To balance computational efficiency and real-time performance, the Attention based Intra-scale Feature Interaction (AIFI) in PDC-DETR processes only the deepest S5 feature outputs. Compared to S3 and S4, the S5 features contain richer semantic information, making them more effective for distinguishing the boundary features of different objects. The S5 features are converted into a vector structure, which is then processed by the multi-head self-attention mechanism within the AIFI module. The output is adjusted to a 2D feature, for further processing by the Parallel Feature Optimized Attention Pyramid Network (PFOAPN) in the subsequent encoder stage. The output object queries from the decoder are then optimized using an Wise-MPDIoU-aware query selection mechanism. The decoder iteratively refines the object queries using a cross-attention-based Transformer decoder, followed by multiple MLPs as detection heads to generate bounding boxes and confidence scores. During the model training phase, the Wise-MPDIoU loss function measures the difference between the predicted and actual boxes, guiding the model optimization process.

Parallel feature optimized attention pyramid network

A Parallel Feature Optimized Attention Pyramid Network (PFOAPN) is incorporated into the encoder part, as in Fig. 1. The PFOAPN includes three key components: the branch attention upsampling (BAUpsampling) module, the branch attention downsampling (BADownsampling) module, and the PDCC3 module. The BAUpsampling and BADownsampling modules are used to provide multiple feature extraction pathways for handling targets at different scales, as shown in Fig. 2. The traditional down sampling module reduces the resolution of the feature map by pooling or convolution, resulting in the loss of detail information and spatial information, which affects the model’s positioning accuracy and detail recognition ability.

Branching attention sampling module

We propose the BADownsampling module, which processes information across multiple branches and selectively retains critical information using an attention mechanism. This is illustrated in Fig. 2, which introduces two branches. Down Branch1 uses a 3 × 3 convolution with a stride of 2, while down Branch2 applies a 1 × 1 convolution after MaxPooling and Concatenation. After the halving operation, the two branches are fused and then multiplied by the attention branch. The attention branch is implemented by global pooling and nonlinear gate mechanisms. Similarly, up Branch1 with BAUpsampling employs transposed convolution to recover spatial resolution while maintaining relatively good details effectively. Up Branch2 performs a simple upsampling and then adjusts the number of channels through a convolutional layer to solve the problem of detail loss in traditional sampling. The branch attention up-and-down sampling module combines the multi-branch structure and the attention mechanism. This design can better preserve details and suppress noise, improving the richness and robustness of feature representation and improving the accuracy of detection and classification.

The structures of PDC and PDCC3 modules are shown in Fig. 3. The PDC module employs three parallel dilated convolutions with different dilatation rates to increase the perceptual capability of the model, which can effectively extract features at various scales. Dilated convolution introduces the “dilatation rate” concept [30], a hyperparameter that defines the interval size between neighboring pixels in the middle of the convolution kernel. When the convolution kernel processes feature data by controlling the dilatation rate, the actual area covered by the convolution kernel moving on the input can be effectively controlled. It is worth noting that increasing the receptive field by the dilatation rate, rather than expanding the convolutional kernel itself, enhances the feature perception capability without increasing the number of parameters. As shown in the lower subplot of Fig. 3, three dilatation convolutions with different dilatation rates are distributed in parallel, with the smaller dilatation rate producing a smaller and denser receptive field. The larger dilatation rate produces a larger but sparse receptive field. The parallel branching operation reduces the gridding effect that a single dilated convolution may cause. Multi-scale dilated convolution can cover the input image more uniformly, avoiding feature breaks and discontinuities caused by sparse sampling of information. Moreover, employing multiple dilated convolution kernels with different dilatation rates is a regularization mechanism, mitigating the risk of model overfitting. The number of channels in two branches is halved before concat to manage the computational cost associated with three parallel dilated convolutions.

PDCC3 module and its included PDC module with parallel dilated convolution with different dilatation rates

We propose an optimized parallel dilated convolution architecture, where the channel numbers in the two branches with dilation rates of 2 and 3 are halved following the PDC module design shown in the subplot of Fig. 3, reducing the computational overhead caused by traditional triple parallel dilated convolutions. Convolution after the fusion module restores the number of channels to the normal levels, balancing parameter efficiency with feature extraction capabilities. The PDCC3 module is inspired by the CSPNet network [31] and the C3 module in YOLOv5 [32] and aims to enhance feature characterization and facilitate the efficient exchange and sharing of feature information across the network. As shown in Fig. 3, the introduction of cross-stage connectivity in PDCC3 means that the hierarchy within the module allows for more efficient sharing and reuse of features. This design helps to reduce the loss of information as it is transmitted through the network and enhances the model’s adaptability to multi-scale target features. At the same time, the PDCC3 module also serves to balance the performance and computational efficiency of the model, which can enable the model to maintain high accuracy while having high operation speed and low resource consumption.

Wise-MPDIoU loss function

Traditional IoU loss metrics are less sensitive to aspect ratio changes and bounding box position changes, resulting in an unstable optimization process that makes dealing with class imbalance and complex scenarios challenging. This paper introduces the MPDIoU loss metric, which simplifies the computation by directly minimizing the distances of the upper-left and lower-right points between the predicted bounding box and the groundtruth bounding box. The minimized distance metric is insensitive to change in the aspect ratio of the bounding box [33]. It can handle the differences in macular degeneration shape and size more flexibly, which is particularly suitable for detecting macular degeneration with complex shapes and different sizes in retinal OCT images. By directly optimizing the vertex position of the bounding box, the position of the prediction box can be adjusted more accurately, and the positioning accuracy of the bounding box can be improved.

Experiment and discussion

Network architecture

We conducted experiments using the ResNet18 version of RT-DETR as the baseline model. The experiments were performed on a Windows 10 64-bit system equipped with an Intel Core i9-13900KF CPU and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU. The implementation utilized PyTorch deep learning framework with Python 3.9.5. The training configuration employed 640 × 640 input images with a batch size of 16, using 4 workers for data loading. We adopted the AdamW optimizer and trained the model for 70 epochs, following current best practices in the field to ensure reliable and comparable results. All hyperparameter selections were made in accordance with established methodologies to maintain experimental rigor.

Macular degeneration OCT dataset

Since existing macular degeneration datasets suffer from label inconsistency, subjective bias, lack of diversity, and class imbalance, this study developed a standardized dataset approved by the Ethics Committee of Dalian Central Hospital, with technical support from the hospital. Under the guidance of professional ophthalmologists, we established a macular degeneration OCT dataset containing diabetic macular edema (DME), central serous chorioretinopathy (CSC), macular hole (MH), choroidal neovascularization (CNV), and drusen. The dataset is based on the publicly available OCT2017 dataset and OCT images from [35] and Retinal-OCT-C8 dataset [36], with all lesion annotations manually performed by our team using annotation software and rectangular boxes to ensure high-quality ground truth. The dataset contains 5,400 images in total, with the following distribution: Drusen (1304 images), CNV (1685 images), CSC (778 images), MH (451 images), and ME (1,449 images). All images focus on the retina and macular region and were annotated through multiple rounds of review to resolve ambiguous features and ensure consistency. The dataset has been publicly released online [37], with training, validation, and test sets divided in a 7:1:1 ratio.

Training process for different models

The PDC-DETR model achieved 92.4% precision, 94.1% mAP50, and 55.5% mAP50-95, demonstrating superior detection performance. As shown in Fig. 5 (comparing subplots (a)-(b) with (c)- (d)), the training process of our RT-DETR-based model exhibits significantly smoother convergence than YOLO series detectors. This stability advantage originates from the Transformer architecture’s inherent properties - its anchor-free design and end-to-end prediction mechanism simplify the model structure and reduce training fluctuations. Figure 5 clearly demonstrates the superior convergence and performance of PDC-DETR, which surpasses all 12 comparison methods on both mAP50 and mAP50-95 metrics. Quantitative results in Table 1 further highlight PDC-DETR’s advantages over YOLO models with similar computational complexity, achieving higher mAP50-95 values by 1.9%, 2.3%, 1.7%, 0.5% and 2.2%, compared to YOLOv5m, YOLOv6s, YOLOv8m, YOLOv9-gelan-m, and Han, respectively. These results underscore PDC-DETR’s effectiveness in balancing detection accuracy and computational efficiency. In different RT-DETR variants, the baseline RT-DETR-R18 model showed better accuracy than both RT-DETR-R50 and RT-DETR-R101 despite having fewer parameters. This observation justified our selection of RT-DETR-R18 as the foundation architecture, which we further enhanced by integrating the PFOAPN module and Wise-MPDIoU loss function to develop the final PDC-DETR system for high-precision, real-time macular degeneration detection. PDC-DETR achieves 71 FPS with a 38.2 MB model size and 58.5 GFLOPs, surpassing YOLOv5m, YOLOv6s, YOLOv8m, YOLOv9-gelan-m and Han. Although YOLOv5n and YOLOv8n achieve the highest inference speed (FPS), they sacrifice model complexity and expressive capacity, resulting in a decline in accuracy for tasks such as feature extraction and object recognition. In contrast, PDC-DETR not only maintains a competitive inference speed but also outperforms them in accuracy, with improvements of 7.1% and 6.3%, respectively.

Ablation experiments

As shown in Table 4, our comprehensive ablation study systematically evaluates the contributions of PDC-DETR’s two key innovations through four progressive experimental configurations. The baseline RT-DETR-R18 with standard loss function establishes reference performance metrics. Quantitative analysis reveals that incorporating the PFOAPN module yields significant improvements of 3.3% in mAP50 and 0.9% in mAP50-95 while maintaining model efficiency (38.5 MB, 57.6 GFLOPs), with a remarkable 15.4 FPS enhancement demonstrating its effective parallel processing of multi-scale features. Subsequent implementation of the Wise-MPDIoU loss further improves detection accuracy (2.3% mAP50, 0.6% mAP50-95) without compromising efficiency (38.2 MB, 57.6 GFLOPs). The complete PDC-DETR configuration combines both components to achieve optimal performance − 4.1% higher mAP50 and 2.6% better mAP50-95 than baseline - while maintaining computational efficiency (only 1.5% increased computation, 0.7% reduced parameters) and achieved an improvement of 12.4 FPS. These results conclusively demonstrate that our PFOAPN architecture’s enhanced feature representation capability and Wise-MPDIoU’s optimized bounding box regression work synergistically to advance real-time detection performance, delivering both superior accuracy and speed while preserving the model’s lightweight characteristics. The systematic evaluation across all five critical metrics (mAP50, mAP50-95, FPS, GFLOPs, and Params) confirms PDC-DETR’s balanced improvements in detection precision and computational efficiency.

Conclusion

Given the challenges of high redundancy, noise artifacts, and multi-scale irregularities in retinal OCT images of maculopathies, we propose PDC-DETR, a real-time target detector based on a Parallel Feature Optimized Attention Pyramid Network (PFOAPN). This framework combines the global information processing capability of DETR’s Transformer-based self-attention mechanism with two key innovations to address these challenges. The PFOAPN architecture leverages a branching parallel processing mechanism to simultaneously capture fine-grained details and contextual information related to macular degeneration. By incorporating a dilated convolution module to expand the receptive field, PFOAPN enhances the discrimination between clinically relevant features and noise, improving feature representation and complex pattern recognition. We further introduce the Wise-MPDIoU loss function, a hybrid of WIoU and MPDIoU, to optimize bounding box regression, which improves localization accuracy while enhancing the model’s robustness and generalization across diverse OCT datasets. Experimental results demonstrate that PDC-DETR achieves real-time detection of macular lesions with high accuracy and computational efficiency. Its lightweight design reduces hardware requirements, enabling seamless deployment on edge devices, cloud servers, and embedded systems. Clinically, this model provides an automated tool for precise detection of macular degeneration, assisting ophthalmologists in early diagnosis, reducing manual workload, supporting treatment planning, and ultimately improving patient outcomes. By enabling rapid and accurate lesion localization in OCT images, PDC-DETR has the potential to enhance routine clinical workflows and facilitate large-scale screening in resource-limited settings.

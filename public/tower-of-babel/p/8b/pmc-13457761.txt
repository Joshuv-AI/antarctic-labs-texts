Methodological Reflections Regarding the LASSO-derived AAGC Model for In-hospital Mortality Prediction in Sepsis
Authors: Kapil D Soni
Published: 2026

The Editor,

I read with interest the prospective study by Gulia and colleagues, and commend the authors for the data collection effort and the attempt to follow TRIPOD.1 Several methodological reflections, however, suggest the reported AUC of 0.95 considerably overstates the model's true performance.2,3

The AAGC Model Double-counts APACHE II

APACHE II already contains age, creatinine, and a GCS-derived neurological score. Putting all four into one model is, in effect, fitting APACHE II twice. This explains the strong negative correlation the authors observed between APACHE II and GCS (r = −0.76), and it makes the individual odds ratios uninterpretable. The model should use either APACHE II or its components, not both.2

Categorization Throws Information Away

Splitting age at 60, grouping APACHE II into ordinal “risk categories,” and using qSOFA binary components alongside the continuous variables they were derived from (RR, SBP, GCS) reduces statistical power and biases estimates.2,4 Continuous variables should stay continuous, with non-linearity handled by restricted cubic splines.2

LASSO followed by Ordinary Refitting Undoes the Shrinkage

The authors selected variables with LASSO at λ_1se, then re-estimated coefficients without penalization. This discards the very shrinkage that makes LASSO useful out-of-sample, leaving inflated odds ratios and an over-confident nomogram.5 Either retain the penalized coefficients, or pre-specify a small set of predictors rather than letting the data choose.2

The Sample Size cannot Support 51 Candidate Predictors

With 86 deaths and 51 candidate variables, there are fewer than two events per parameter—an order of magnitude below what Riley et al. recommend.3 At this ratio, the variables LASSO selects are unstable from sample to sample, and apparent performance is unlikely to hold on independent data.

“APACHE Predictive Mortality” cannot be a Predictor of Mortality

The reported OR of 31.9 simply reflects regressing the outcome on a transformation of itself. This entry should be removed.

The clinical question is important, and the data are valuable. A pre-specified, fully continuous model using APACHE II or its components, with penalization retained and circular predictors removed, would give a more honest estimate of how well sepsis mortality can actually be predicted in this setting.

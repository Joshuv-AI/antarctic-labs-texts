Scenario based assessment of carbon storage and habitat quality under land use change in Shandong Province China
Authors: Kechen Lyu, Zhengjun Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractLand-use change exerts a profound influence on ecosystem services (ES), and accurately assessing its spatiotemporal dynamics is essential for achieving regional sustainability. Taking Shandong Province as a case study, this research integrates the PLUS and InVEST models to simulate the impacts of land-use changes on carbon storage and habitat quality in Shandong Province between 2000 and 2020, and to project their dynamics under different scenarios for 2030. The InVEST (Integrated Valuation of Ecosystem Services and Tradeoffs) model was employed to reassess variations in carbon storage (CS) and habitat quality (HQ). The main findings are as follows: (1) Cultivated land decreased by 12.3%, while construction land expanded by 51.04%, predominantly replacing farmland and forested areas, resulting in a distinct spatial pattern characterized by an “urbanized east and agricultural west.” (2) Carbon storage declined by approximately 63 million tons, primarily due to urban expansion. (3) Habitat quality experienced a 3.6% decrease, with significant ecological fragmentation identified in the central mountainous regions and the Yellow River Delta, driven by intensified urbanization and agricultural activities. (4) Future scenario simulations indicate that under the ecological conservation scenario, carbon storage could increase by 12.5% and habitat quality could reach 0.572 by 2040; in contrast, the natural development scenario suggests ongoing degradation. These findings highlight the trade-offs between land development and ecosystem services, emphasizing the necessity of reinforcing ecological zoning, compensation mechanisms, and the establishment of ecological corridors. Introduction

Ecosystem services (ES) represent the various benefits that humans derive from ecosystems, serving as a bridge between natural systems and human society1. The Millennium Ecosystem Assessment (MA) categorizes ES into four types: provisioning, regulating, supporting, and cultural services2,3. With the intensification of global urbanization, humanity is increasingly confronted with severe ecological crises, including biodiversity loss, global warming, water pollution, and intensified greenhouse effects4,5, all of which pose escalating challenges to human well-being and sustainable urban development6,7. Against this backdrop, carbon emissions and carbon storage (CS) have gained significant research attention, alongside growing interest in habitat quality (HQ). Terrestrial ecosystems play a pivotal role in absorbing atmospheric CO2 through vegetation, thus contributing to the mitigation of greenhouse gas concentrations and the enhancement of carbon sinks8,9. As a key indicator of regional ecological security, HQ reflects both biodiversity levels and ecosystem service capacity, and it has become an increasingly prominent research focus in the field of environmental security10. The MA emphasizes that both CS and HQ possess irreplaceable ecological value, and their degradation poses direct threats to food security, water supply, and climate stability11,12. Costanza et al. (1997) were the first to systematically estimate the global economic value of ES, revealing their substantial contribution to human well-being13. Under accelerated urbanization, optimizing ES provision at the regional scale has become essential for supporting land-based ecosystem protection and sustainable development.

Numerous studies have confirmed that land use and land cover change (LUCC) constitutes a key anthropogenic driver of terrestrial ecosystem transformations14. LUCC not only reflects the intensity and form of human activities, but also induces changes in regional carbon stocks through its influence on ecosystem structure, vegetation composition, land cover types, and soil CS capacity15–17. For example, Xue et al. investigated the impact of LUCC on HQ and observed a trend of initial decline followed by recovery in HQ across six major ecological zones in China between 1992 and 2020, underscoring the complex and dynamic nature of this relationship18. In another case, HernándezGuzmán analyzed land use transitions in western Mexico and found that increases in bare land were a major factor contributing to CS loss, thereby clearly demonstrating the influence of LUCC on CS19. Despite these insights, current research still has limitations in exploring the future impacts of land use and land cover change (LUCC) on ecosystem services (ES), especially under different climate scenarios, urbanization paths, and policy interventions. In particular, there is still insufficient exploration of the dynamic simulation of LUCC and its corresponding ES responses.Therefore, it is imperative to incorporate scenario-based LUCC simulation models—such as CA-Markov20–22 and FLUS23–25—with quantitative ES assessment tools, including the Integrated Valuation of Ecosystem Services and Tradeoffs (InVEST) model and matrix-based evaluation methods. By combining land use change (LUCC) modeling with ecosystem services (ES) assessment and utilizing feedback mechanisms, it is possible to conduct a comprehensive analysis of the potential ecological impacts of land use change on carbon sequestration, biodiversity, and other ecosystem service dimensions. Such an integrated framework can assist decision-makers in optimizing ecological red lines, improving land use efficiency, and achieving the coordinated and sustainable provision of ecosystem services in regional spatial planning.

Currently, the primary method for studying future land use dynamics relies on land change dynamics models. Among these, the “Patch-Generated Land Use Simulation” (PLUS) model developed by Liang et al. overcomes the key limitations of traditional cellular automaton (CA) models by introducing the “Land Expansion Analysis Strategy” (LEAS), thereby enabling more accurate characterization of urban land expansion mechanisms26. Building upon this advancement, Gao et al. integrated the PLUS model with the Markov model and multi-objective programming (MOP) to explore land use changes and associated ecological risks in Nanjing under four distinct scenarios: baseline development, rapid development, ecological land protection, and ecological-economic balance27. Other studies have also investigated the impacts of LUCC on CS, with a particular focus on urban-scale dynamic simulations under climate change scenarios28. For example, Wang studied the case of the Bortala Mongol Autonomous Prefecture in the Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region of China and developed a comprehensive modeling framework that combines system dynamics (SD) models, PLUS land use simulation models, and InVEST models for ecosystem service assessment. This method was used to predict land use changes and ecosystem service (ES) outcomes under various Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSPs) and Representative Concentration Pathways (RCPs)29. Due to its high simulation accuracy, ability to handle nonlinear relationships, support for multi-scenario analysis, and flexibility to adapt to policy environments, the PLUS model has become a powerful tool for simulating and predicting land use dynamics30,31. Therefore, in this study, we adopted the PLUS model based on previous research to simulate future land use change patterns under multiple scenarios and systematically assess their potential impacts on ecosystem services (ES).

Spatial simulation models have been widely applied in land system prediction and ecosystem service analysis. Compared with traditional CA-Markov and CLUE-S frameworks, the Patch-Based Land Use Simulation (PLUS) model demonstrates significant advantages in spatial expansion representation and scenario flexibility. Its built-in random forest algorithm efficiently captures nonlinear driving mechanisms, while the multi-type random seed cellular automaton (CARS) mechanism realistically simulates the growth boundaries of land patches, thereby enhancing spatial plausibility. Additionally, the model supports multi-scenario configurations and dynamic parameter constraints, making it particularly suitable for complex simulation tasks driven by policy objectives. The main innovations of this study are fourfold: (1) Model integration: We developed a coupled “PLUS–InVEST” framework that dynamically links land pattern changes with ecosystem service responses. (2) Scenario design: We established three transition pathways consistent with regional policies—natural development, farmland protection, and ecological protection—with transition parameters calibrated based on peer-reviewed literature. (3) Assessment indicators: Carbon storage (CS) and habitat quality (HQ) are jointly adopted to simultaneously quantify the stock and quality of ecosystem functions. (4) Results translation: Simulation outputs are used to define priority areas for ecological restoration and identify key areas for policy intervention, thereby enhancing the governance relevance of research findings.

Shandong Province, as a rapidly urbanizing region in China’s eastern coastal area, exhibits a spatially differentiated pattern of “urbanization in the east and agriculture in the west.” In recent years, the degradation of ecological barrier functions in the Lushan region and the decline in carbon sink capacity of the Yellow River Delta wetlands have further exacerbated this pattern. Therefore, this study undertakes the following tasks: (1) Analyze the spatiotemporal evolution of land use and land cover change (LUCC) in Shandong Province from 2000 to 2020; (2) Simulate the spatiotemporal distribution characteristics of carbon stock (CS) and high-carbon areas (HQ) during the same period; (3) Predict the trends in LUCC, CS, and HQ from 2030 to 2040 under three different scenarios. This study aims to provide scientific basis for land use planning and ecological conservation by examining the spatiotemporal dynamics of land use changes, their driving factors, and their impacts on ecosystem services.

Materials and methods

Overview of the study area

Shandong Province, located on China’s eastern coast (114°47’ 122°42’E, 34°22’ 38°23’N), covers approximately 15.8 × 104 km2. The region features a diverse range of landforms, including plains, mountains, hills, basins, deltas, coastal landscapes, and loess plateaus, with elevations ranging from − 62 m to 1,444 m (see Fig. 1). Administratively, Shandong Province is divided into 16 prefecture-level cities. Geographically, it is bordered by the Bohai Sea and the Yellow Sea to the east, Hebei Province to the north, Henan Province to the west, and Anhui Province and Jiangsu Province to the south, making it an important economic hub along China’s eastern coast. Shandong has a temperate monsoon climate, with an annual average temperature ranging from 11 °C to 14 °C and annual precipitation varying between 550 millimeters and 950 millimeters. The terrain structure follows a “higher in the center, lower on the periphery” pattern, transitioning from central mountainous areas to surrounding low-lying plains. The province boasts diverse ecological resources, including forests, wetlands, and grasslands. The eastern coastal regions feature extensive wetland resources, the central mountainous areas are dominated by a mix of natural and artificial forests, while the western plains primarily consist of agricultural cultivation zones with relatively high land development intensity.

Data source and preprocessing

This study selected land use data from three time points: 2000, 2010, and 2020. After reclassification, land use was divided into six categories: farmland, forest land, grassland, water bodies, construction land, and unutilized land. Scenario simulation and model design

This study uses the Random Forest (RF) algorithm to assess the occurrence probability of various land use types and predict future land use evolution and patch dynamics under multiple scenario settings. Specifically, historical changes in land use types are extracted through the Land Expansion Analysis Strategy (LEAS) submodule34, and an RF model is constructed based on 13 driving factors to systematically analyze their impact mechanisms on land use changes (Fig. 2).To construct scenario simulations of different land use conversion trends, this study adjusted the conversion probabilities for certain land use categories proportionally. Scenario S1 increases the conversion probability of construction land by 20% relative to the baseline trend, aiming to simulate the potential for intensified construction activities under accelerated urbanization policies. Scenario S2 explores the sensitivity thresholds of conversion trends under extreme policy perturbations by adjusting conversion probabilities between multiple land categories within a ± 40% range. This approach references the scenario setting strategies of Gao et al. (2022) and Liang et al.26, which similarly employed adjustment ranges of ± 20% to ± 50% to reflect potential disturbance spaces between regional development objectives and ecological conservation policies. Consequently, this study selected + 20% and ± 40% as reasonable and distinguishable disturbance magnitudes, ensuring policy realism in simulations while providing sufficient testing of model responsiveness.

Based on the land use data for Shandong Province in 2000 and 2010, the land use data for 2020 were simulated (Figure A1). A precision analysis was performed by comparing the simulated data with the actual land use data for 2020, resulting in a Kappa coefficient of 0.866 and an overall accuracy of 0.928. In addition to the Kappa coefficient and overall accuracy, the Producer’s Accuracy and User’s Accuracy for each land use category were computed to further validate the PLUS model performance. The Producer’s Accuracy was 87.2% for cropland, 82.4% for woodland, 83.1% for grassland, 90.2% for water bodies, 86.7% for construction land, and 80.3% for unused land. Corresponding User’s Accuracy values were 89.0%, 84.3%, 85.0%, 91.1%, 87.9%, and 83.0%, respectively. These results indicate that the simulated land classification results are consistent and robust across major land use types.

A Kappa value greater than 0.75 is generally considered to indicate high reliability, suggesting that the simulation results are highly reliable35. To comprehensively evaluate the accuracy of the PLUS model, in addition to the Kappa coefficient of 0.866 and the overall accuracy of 0.928, we further calculated the Producer’s Accuracy and User’s Accuracy based on the confusion matrix. The Producer’s Accuracy reached 85.1%, while the User’s Accuracy was 87.3% (Table A1), indicating that the simulated land classification results were consistent and robust across major land use types. Moreover, the driving factor importance ranking based on the Random Forest model was provided (Fig. 2), and all scenario-specific transition rules were detailed in Table A5 to ensure full transparency and reproducibility.

Three land use scenarios were constructed: Natural Development (ND), Cropland Conservation (CC), and Ecological Conservation (EC). Natural Development Scenario: Based on the land use change patterns in Shandong Province from 2000 to 2020, land use types are allowed to transition freely between each other without any restrictions. Cropland Conservation Scenario: Following the methodological framework proposed by Gao et al. (2022) for land-use simulations in Nanjing (Sustainable Cities and Society, 85, 104055), the spatial stability of cropland is defined as the primary regulatory target. Therefore, the transition probability from cropland to any other land-use type is set to zero. To represent cropland gains resulting from ecological restoration or land consolidation within the modelling domain, transition probabilities from all ecological land-use classes—i.e., woodland, grassland, and water bodies—to cropland are uniformly increased by 20%. This parameterisation explicitly incorporates the institutional constraints imposed by China’s food-security and permanent-basic-farmland protection policies. The present study applies this configuration intact to the Shandong modelling domain; the corresponding Boolean transition matrix is provided in Appendix Table A5. Ecological Conservation Scenario: Following the “ecology-first” transition logic proposed by Liang et al. (2021), the spatial influence of ecological-restoration policies is simulated by asymmetrically adjusting transition probabilities: conversions from construction land to ecological land-use classes (woodland, grassland, and water bodies) are increased by 40%, whereas transitions from any ecological land-use type to construction land are decreased by 40%. This parameterization operationalizes the institutional enforcement of ecological redlines and the enhancement of ecosystem services (Computers, Environment and Urban Systems, 85, 101569). The identical structural constraints were fully encoded within the PLUS modelling framework for Shandong Province.

The detailed Boolean transition matrix for each scenario is provided in Table A5, where ‘1’ denotes allowable conversion and ‘0’ indicates restricted conversion. In this matrix, land types are encoded as: a = cropland, b = woodland, c = grassland, d = water bodies, e = construction land, and f = unused land.

Results

Land use change spatiotemporal analysis

Current land use types

The results indicate that between 2000 and 2020, construction land expanded markedly, primarily through the conversion of cropland and grassland, which in turn caused declines in carbon storage and habitat quality in certain regions.

Between 2000 and 2020, the land use pattern in Shandong Province was dominated by cropland, which was primarily distributed in the western Huang-Huai-Hai Plain and the central and eastern lowland areas, together accounting for more than 65% of the total land area. Woodland was mainly concentrated in the central mountain areas, Jiaodong Hills, and other hilly regions. Grassland was sporadically distributed in transitional zones and some arid or saline-alkali areas. Water bodies were primarily distributed along rivers, lakes, and coastal mudflats in linear or patchy formations. From 2000 to 2020, significant changes occurred in land use types in Shandong Province. Woodland and grassland showed an annual downward trend, with reductions of approximately 9.79% and 38.57%, respectively. Water bodies and construction land continued to increase, with the area of water bodies rising from 6.16 × 103 km2 in 2000 to 9.74 × 103 km2 in 2020. Construction land expanded around key cities such as Jinan, Qingdao, and Yantai, as well as along major transportation corridors, growing from 2.01 × 104 km2 in 2000 to 2.73 × 104 km2 in 2020 (Fig. 3).

Spatiotemporal changes in ecosystem services

CS

The CS in Shandong Province showed a declining trend from 2000 to 2020, decreasing from 14.11 × 108t in 2000 to 13.92 × 108t in 2020. Among these, CS in cropland decreased from 9.67 × 108t in 2000 to 9.42 × 108t, while conversions between woodland and unused land led to reductions of 1.54 × 107t and 4.49 × 106t, respectively. The CS provided by grassland decreased from 1.20 × 107t to 1.98 × 106t.

In terms of spatial distribution, CS exhibited a notable “low in the east, high in the west” characteristic. Areas with higher CS density were primarily located in the central mountain and hilly regions of Shandong, such as Jinan, southern Zibo, and the Tai’an area, where the proportion of woodland and grassland was higher. Other significant areas included the coastal urban belt.

of the Jiaodong Peninsula (Weihai, Yantai), as well as parts of Linyi and Qingdao cities. The areas with the lowest CS were predominantly located in construction land across various cities, where vegetation coverage was lower. Between 2000 and 2010, the CS in the Bohai Bay area (e.g., northern Binzhou) increased significantly, while the coastal areas around Laizhou Bay saw the largest decline in CS per unit area (Fig. 5).

Spatiotemporal variation of HQ

From 2000 to 2020, the HQ index in Shandong Province declined from 0.52 to 0.49, exhibiting a spatial pattern characterized by “higher values in the central and eastern regions and lower values in the west” (Fig. 6). In 2000, areas with high HQ values (> 0.7) were concentrated in the central mountainous region and the Jiaodong Hills, accounting for 22.3% of the provincial land area. Low-value areas (< 0.4) were mainly distributed in the built-up areas of cities such as Jinan and Qingdao, as well as the agricultural belt of the northwestern Shandong Plain. By 2020, the extent of high-value areas had decreased. In the Jiaodong Hills, the expansion of construction land led to increased habitat fragmentation, resulting in a 12.5% reduction in HQ around the Laoshan area. Meanwhile, low-value areas expanded by 7.8%, forming a “three-core radiation belt” centered on Jinan, Weifang, and Dongying. The period from 2000 to 2010 marked a phase of rapid habitat degradation, with an average annual decline of 0.86%. From 2010 to 2020, the rate of degradation slowed to an average annual decline of 0.32%. Notably, along parts of the Bohai Bay coastline, HQ declined between 2000 and 2010, whereas the coastal areas around Laizhou Bay (eastern Dongying and northern Weifang) showed a significant upward trend during the same period (Fig. 6).

As shown in Fig. 6, certain regions exhibit a “mismatch” between ecosystem services and habitat quality, characterized by a higher proportion of “CS-dominant” or “HQ-dominant” coordination types. Combining the temporal trends from Figs. 3 and 5, areas such as the northern Shandong Peninsula and the southwestern Shandong Plain demonstrate a significant upward trend in CS performance, yet HQ levels show limited improvement or even decline. This phenomenon indicates that although ecological restoration and vegetation recovery projects (such as returning farmland to forests and afforestation) have been implemented in recent years in these areas, leading to enhanced carbon storage capacity, threats such as urban expansion, infrastructure development, or industrial pollution have not been mitigated simultaneously. Consequently, habitat quality has failed to improve correspondingly, resulting in a non-synergistic state where “ecological services enhance while habitat quality lags behind.”

Conversely, the Yellow River Delta and certain adjacent zones exhibit a “HQ-dominant” coupling pattern, where HQ significantly exceeds CS. These areas typically encompass protected wetlands or nature reserves. While they maintain high habitat integrity and biodiversity, their overall CS scores remain low due to limited land carbon storage capacity or slow vegetation type transitions. These areas underscore that carbon metrics alone cannot serve as the sole measure of ecological conservation success; comprehensive consideration of synergistic improvements across multiple ecological dimensions is essential.

Therefore, future regional ecological governance should prioritize spatially imbalanced coupling zones. This entails strengthening regulation of habitat threat factors in areas of intense human activity while introducing ecological engineering projects within nature reserves to enhance carbon sequestration capacity. Such an approach aims to achieve the dual objective of simultaneously improving carbon storage and habitat quality.

Discussion

Coupling analysis of land use change driving mechanisms and ecological effects

The spatiotemporal patterns of land use change from 2000 to 2020 reveal a compound driving mechanism in Shandong Province, characterized by “human activity dominance constrained by natural background.” The bidirectional conversion between cropland and construction land, accounting for over 55% of total transitions, highlights the transformative impact of urbanization on land resources. Although the annual expansion rate of construction land in Shandong (3.4%) is slightly lower than the national average (3.8%), it exhibits a distinctive “core city radiation + transportation corridor extension” expansion pattern. Notably, the rapid reduction of unused land at a rate of 7. 12% per year reflects both intensified land development and the potential ecological risks associated with marginal land exploitation. This underscores the need for multi-scenario, multi-scale land and spatial planning strategies to constrain disorderly urban sprawl and guide sustainable development.

The findings of this study are consistent with previous research by Rong on the Yellow River Lower Reaches and by Zhang on the Fujian Delta region36,37. To address scenario-specific land use transition logic, this study adopted clearly differentiated transition probability matrices based on authoritative literature and policy context. For the Cropland Conservation Scenario, the configuration strictly follows Gao et al. (2022), where the probability of cropland converting to any other land type is set to zero, and the probabilities from ecological lands (woodland, grassland, water bodies) to cropland are uniformly increased by 20%, reflecting the rigid institutional constraint of basic farmland protection under China’s food security framework. For the Ecological Conservation Scenario, the study draws on Liang et al. (2021), increasing the conversion probability from construction land to ecological land by 40% and reducing the reverse flow by 40%, simulating policy-driven ecological restoration paths such as “returning construction land to forests or wetlands.” The Natural Development Scenario uses the Markov transition matrix derived from observed LUCC data (2000–2020) without policy intervention. All matrices are detailed in Appendix Table A5, and the scenario logic is explicitly explained in the main text to ensure transparency, theoretical soundness, and reproducibility.

Although farmland conservation policies have to some extent promoted dual improvements in carbon stock (CS) and habitat quality (HQ), caution is warranted regarding potential counterproductive mechanisms. Specifically, increased farmland area may be accompanied by heightened agricultural intensity—such as more frequent application of chemical fertilizers and pesticides—potentially compromising regional water quality and biodiversity, thereby undermining the long-term HQ enhancement effects. Existing research indicates that intensive agricultural practices may lead to non-point source pollution and habitat fragmentation (e.g., Zhang et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2019). Therefore, when assessing the impact of farmland conservation on ecosystem services, potential negative externalities and compensatory mechanisms should be comprehensively considered. Future research could further incorporate indicators such as agricultural fertilizer application density and pesticide residue intensity, combined with modeling and simulation techniques, to identify the spatial distribution characteristics and early warning signals of ecological and environmental risks.

Furthermore, although the current InVEST framework evaluates Carbon Storage (CS) and Habitat Quality (HQ) independently, recent studies suggest potential dynamic feedbacks between them. Recognizing this, the study proposes incorporating System Dynamics (SD) or Multi-Agent System (MAS) frameworks in future research to model their interaction explicitly, thus enhancing the interpretability of ecosystem service changes under land-use transition and improving policy-relevant simulation capacity.

Land use transformation has led to significant carbon sink losses. The reduction in CS from cropland (2.5 × 107t) combined with the decline in woodland (3.85 × 107t) has resulted in a total provincial CS loss of approximately 1.9 × 108t. Spatially, the central mountainous and hilly regions of Shandong maintained relatively strong carbon sink functions due to higher vegetation coverage. In contrast, the Huang-Huai-Hai Plain experienced declining carbon density as a result of intensive agricultural development. HQ exhibited a clear degradation trend. Habitat fragmentation in the Jiaodong Hills, exemplified by a 12.5% decline in HQ around the Laoshan area, was directly associated with the encroachment of construction land into woodland. Meanwhile, the low HQ observed in the northwestern Shandong cropland belt can be attributed to landscape homogenization caused by intensified agricultural practices. These findings align with Ju’s research on the Shandong Peninsula38.

Policy implications from multi-scenario simulations

Based on the simulation results under the three scenarios, this study conducted a spatial overlay analysis of Carbon Storage (CS) and Habitat Quality (HQ) to identify high-risk zones where ecological degradation overlaps with construction land expansion.

In the business-as-usual scenario, regions such as the southwestern agricultural zone and northern coastal reclamation areas of Shandong showed simultaneous declines in HQ and expansion of urban land. These zones are recommended as ecological restoration priority areas, where targeted ecological engineering measures such as afforestation and wetland recovery should be implemented.

In the ecological protection scenario, although HQ improved significantly in the Tai-Yi Mountain and Yi River corridor, CS improvement remained limited. This mismatch suggests the need to designate these areas as clean water and efficient water supply coordination zones, where policies should aim to balance ecosystem connectivity and water provisioning services.

These region-specific suggestions bridge the gap between scenario outputs and actionable policy directions, enhancing the practical value of the modeling results.

According to the future land use simulations based on the PLUS model, different planning strategies will exert varying impacts on ecosystem services. Under the Natural Development Scenario, continued cropland loss and the expansion of construction land will lead to further degradation of ecosystem services, with declining CS and accelerated deterioration of HQ. Therefore, more proactive policy measures are required to safeguard ecosystem services. Under the Cropland Conservation Scenario, cropland is effectively preserved, and both CS and HQ show signs of improvement. However, potential negative impacts of agricultural intensification on HQ should not be overlooked39. It is essential to strengthen the promotion of agroecological technologies, reduce non-point source agricultural pollution, and enhance the stability of agricultural ecosystems.The Ecological Conservation Scenario offers a promising pathway for enhancing ecosystem services. Through woodland restoration and wetland rehabilitation, both CS and HQ are expected to improve significantly.

Moving forward, greater investment in ecological protection should be prioritized, including the implementation of policies such as “returning built-up land to woodland” and “returning cropland to lakes,” in order to increase the proportion of ecological land uses such as woodland and wetlands. At the same time, land use planning should be optimized based on local conditions. Construction land should be spatially arranged rationally to minimize damage to natural ecosystems40. For example, the development of compact cities can enhance land use efficiency while reducing encroachment on surrounding cropland and natural ecosystems. In addition, ecological monitoring and assessment should be strengthened to enable timely adjustments to land use and conservation strategies, aiming to achieve a win–win outcome for both economic development and ecological protection.

Outlook and recommendations

To enhance the linkage between policy recommendations and spatial identification results, this paper further compares the correspondence between HQ–CS (habitat quality–carbon storage) dual-high value areas in Fig. 3 and policy suggestions. For instance, in the southwestern mountainous region shown in Fig. 3, the HQ–CS index is significantly above average, providing the spatial foundation for constructing ecological corridors. This area should be prioritized as an “Ecological Conservation Guidance Zone.” In the central agricultural production belt, where HQ values are moderate and CS values are relatively high, promoting the demonstration and adoption of ecological agricultural technologies is advisable.

Conclusions

The coupled “PLUS–InVEST” framework developed herein structurally integrates land-pattern forecasting with ecological-function assessment, demonstrating high adaptability for spatial-planning policy simulation. The PLUS model incorporates non-linear driving mechanisms via a random-forest algorithm and allows flexible transition constraints within scenarios, thereby enabling the representation of multiple policy pathways. InVEST, in turn, provides robust quantitative estimation of ecosystem-service indicators. This study conducted a comprehensive analysis of land use change and ecosystem services in Shandong Province from 2000 to 2020 and simulated future developments from 2030 to 2040. The main conclusions are as follows:

From 2000 to 2020, land use in Shandong Province was dominated by cropland, mainly distributed in the western Huang-Huai-Hai Plain and the central–eastern low plains. Woodland and grassland areas declined annually, while water bodies and construction land continued to expand. Construction land grew primarily around central cities and transportation corridors, and the bidirectional conversion between cropland and construction land became the dominant pattern. Influenced by policy and economic activities, transitions between woodland and cropland exhibited regional variation. The period from 2000 to 2010 saw rapid expansion of construction land, while from 2010 to 2020, the dynamic degree of all land use types declined, indicating a trend toward stabilization.

Over the past two decades, land use changes in Shandong Province have had a profound impact on ecosystem services.

Looking ahead, different land use planning strategies will exert varying influences on ecosystem service outcomes. To achieve a win–win outcome between economic development and ecological protection, it is essential to increase investment in environmental conservation, implement policies such as “returning built-up land to woodland” and “returning cropland to lakes,” optimize land use planning, promote compact urban development, and encourage the adoption of agroecological technologies to reduce agricultural non-point source pollution. Strengthening ecological monitoring and assessment is also critical. Future research should focus on the long-term dynamics of ecosystem services under different land use policy scenarios and explore mechanisms for cross-regional ecological cooperation, thereby contributing to the sustainable development of Shandong Province. Future research will focus on elucidating the coupled feedback mechanisms between carbon storage (CS) and habitat quality (HQ). To address the current limitations of sequential interaction and mechanistic explanation, System Dynamics (SD) or Multi-Agent System (MAS) frameworks will be integrated, employing non-linear simulation approaches to capture temporal feedback and emergent dynamics.

To enhance the policy relevance of scenario-simulation outputs, this study identifies priority intervention zones by comparing spatiotemporal patterns of ecosystem services across the three scenarios and proposes spatially targeted regional governance measures. Specifically, areas exhibiting marked ecological degradation under the Natural Development scenario yet pronounced improvement under the Ecological Conservation scenario were delineated by overlaying spatially explicit declines in carbon storage (CS) and habitat quality (HQ) with projected construction-land expansion. The southwest Shandong cropland–woodland ecotone and the northern coastal tidal-flat zone exemplify such priority areas. Under the Ecological Conservation scenario, these regions display a significant increase in HQ and enhanced landscape connectivity. We therefore recommend their designation as “Ecological Restoration Priority Zones,” where policies should focus on returning construction land to woodland or wetlands and establishing ecological corridors to foster sustained habitat recovery.

Conversely, in transitional zones where HQ improves markedly but CS gains remain limited—such as the eastern Yi River basin and the interface between the Taiyi and Yimeng Mountains—land-use transitions are dominated by conversions between grassland and woodland, restricting carbon increments while substantially enhancing ecological integrity. These areas are proposed as “Trade-off Management Zones for Clean-Water and Efficient Water-Supply Systems.” In these zones, ecological infrastructure (e.g., water-retention facilities and bioretention systems) should be strengthened to synergize ecological protection with water-resource security objectives. The spatial delineations described above are explicitly annotated in Fig. 8, thereby increasing the congruence between simulation outcomes and actionable regional governance needs.

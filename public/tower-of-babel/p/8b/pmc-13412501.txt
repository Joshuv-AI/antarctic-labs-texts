Combined use of glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists and sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors for cardiovascular protection
Authors: Soumya Shanta Mohanty, R. R. Selvammathy, Hindustan Abdul Ahad, Edukulla Satheesh Kumar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Among the various treatments for type 2 diabetes during the initial period, glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) and sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors have been considered together as agents with separate cardiometabolic benefits, given their limited glucose-lowering effects. GLP-1 RAs are reported to inhibit atherosclerosis through several physiological processes, including increased blood flow in endothelial tissues, decreased inflammation, increased antioxidant levels, and improved lipid profile. There is a similar situation with SGLT2 inhibitors, and the explanation for this is different yet complementary, as these drugs cause a decrease in both cardiac preload and afterload and, at the same time, increase vascular permeability, diuresis, and more efficient energy usage by the heart. Thus, they have been affirmed by extensive clinical trials, which show that, although these drugs may harm patients, they do not result in significant cardiovascular events, have a lower treatment rate for heart defects, and are associated with a longer time before the patient’s renal function deteriorates. This translates into a continuing shift toward the dominion in terms of cardiometabolic protection – a strategy that is receiving strong backing from the majority of clinical guidelines across the world.

Introduction

The differences between these two mechanisms are so distinct that cardiovascular protection cannot be achieved simply by glucose-centric management alone in cardiovascular disease (CVD) through mechanism of action management of type 2 diabetes with cardiovascular protection by glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs).[1] These mechanisms have thrown treatment guidelines of diabetes and cardiology completely out of the window and opened the way into entirely new territories on cardiovascular protection. The rationale for including those antidiabetic agents on the market that could directly ameliorate major cardiovascular events into changing the pattern of diabetes care to encompass wider parameters due to the most prevalent acknowledgment that CVD below is probably the very important factor of morbidity and mortality among diabetic patients, with almost an unstoppable course of a left toward an endothelial dysfunctional state, chronic inflammatory state, or metabolic derail.[2] It takes multiple interacting mechanisms to explain how GLP-1 RAs exert cardioprotective effects. They stimulate the secretion of insulin in a glucose-dependent manner, control glucagon release, and delay gastric emptying; modes of action mimicked by GLP-1 in vivo.[3] Among the cardiovascular effects are the following, weight loss and decrease in systolic blood pressure; improvement of lipids; fall in oxidative stress; enhanced endothelial production of nitric oxide; and all of these happenings occur due to the GLP-1 RAs.[4] Effectively, these agents might prove anti-inflammatory through inhibition of their pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-6 (IL-6) and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α); hence, they reduce arterial inflammation and stabilize atherosclerotic plaques.[5] Hence, it is not surprising that so many cardiovascular outcome trials (CVOTs) had exceedingly glorious results on the major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) endpoints, most especially in stroke and myocardial infarction: LEADER (liraglutide), SUSTAIN-6 (semaglutide), and REWIND (dulaglutide). As a result, they have been propelled as one of the first-line therapies for patients who have diabetes and CVD or high atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk.[6]

Inevitably, such effects on cardiac metabolism, if at all existent, will be under regulation to a very small degree in that regard within sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor therapy, such as increased myocardial glucose uptake, which involves decreased ischemia-reperfusion injury, thus causing an improved overall state in the heart.[7] In addition, beyond the visible effects for cardiovascular well-being of the SGLT2 drugs upon glucose hunger, this armamentarium shows further cardio-resilience against failure via additional mechanisms.[8] These mechanisms counter glucose reabsorption in proximal tubular cells, thus culminating in unrestricted glycosuria and osmotic diuresis, relatively little weight loss, and blood-pressure improvement, all directly affecting volume status.[9] Such hemodynamics would lower preload and afterload in a manner that might be treated as beneficial against heart failure. Another mechanism for the renal action of SGLT2 inhibitors would be one that reduces intraglomerular pressure via tubuloglomerular feedback.[10] This will protect, upon being active, the characteristic cardiorenal syndrome of the diabetic population from inflicting damage to the heart. Most importantly, these compounds do influence cardiac energy metabolism, using ketone bodies as substrates for energy-rich substrates from ineffective myocardial cells, thus improving contractility and efficacy in the failing myocardium.[11] Almost overwhelming evidence has been provided through large studies, such as EMPA-REG OUTCOME (empagliflozin), CANVAS (canagliflozin), and DECLARE-TIMI 58 (dapagliflozin), regarding significant reductions in heart failure hospitalization and mortality because of their specific cardiovascular burden, even in non-diabetic persons, indicating their broad yet undefined cardioprotective potential.[12] The impressive results of SGLT2 inhibitors in treating heart failure brought them to the guidelines of reduced and preserved ejection fractions, placing SGLT2 as one of the few drug classes shown to be beneficial across the heart failure spectrum.[13] Such interventions have even been disregarded in actuality; that is, they did not offer substantial protection against major CVD through GLP-1 RAs and SGLT2 inhibitors, but, in contradistinction, in interplay with the contrary aspects of cardiovascular pathophysiology.[14] The argument about consideration of patients with long-standing diabetes – poor health with a high atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease risk – allures the backstage power of the intervention offered by the GLP-1RA in limiting myocardial infarction and stroke events.[15] On the other hand, SGLT2 inhibitors are extremely effective in preventing and treating heart failure, and they also protect renal function, thus being appropriate candidates for treatment in people who have already suffered from heart failure, chronic kidney disease, or are at high risk for fluid overload.[16] With increasing evidence, it appears probable that these two agents in combination would beneficially affect the cardiovascular and metabolic systems in an additive or perhaps even synergistic manner, with the different mechanisms they operate through acting in parallel pathways involved in glycemic control, weight loss, blood pressure reduction, vascular tone and fluid balance, and cardiac metabolism.[17] Early real-world activity, however, coupled with observational evidence, suggests that the efficacy of drug combinations to facilitate therapeutic synergy shows greater decline in glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), body weight, blood pressure, and cardiovascular events compared to their respective monotherapies.[18]

They usually have favorable safety profiles and are available in high amounts. Gastrointestinal (GI) adverse effects related to GLP-1 RAs, such as nausea and vomiting, tend to resolve with progressive use, but some question arises about possibly contributing to pancreatitis or gallbladder disease, which is not yet supported by evidence. SGLT2 inhibitors are, in general, well-tolerated but are linked with genital mycotic infections owing to glucosuria, volume depletion, and very infrequent but serious events of euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis.[19] Considering these associated risks, the evidence seems strong in favor of their use in patients who are at greater cardiovascular or renal risk.[20] The recommendation for the first-line use of GLP-1 RAs and SGLT2 inhibitors in diabetic patients with established cardiovascular risk, therefore, continues to increase the role these agents have in cardiometabolic medicine, according to the recent American Diabetes Association, European Society of Cardiology, and American Heart Association (AHA) guidelines.[21]

Studies are underway with such agents as new dual and multiagonist treatments for the future, hypothesizing that they would follow the success of GLP-1 RAs and SGLT2 inhibitors.[22] More interesting is the dual GLP-1/glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) agonist tirzepatide, which has elicited an arguably even stronger effect in metabolic control and weight management with a hint toward future benefit for cardiovascular protection.[23] These new classes of SGLT2 inhibitors and combinations are also under development, taking advantage of the maximum benefit while minimizing adverse effects.[24] These two developments-Glibenclamide-1 receptor agonists and SGLT2 inhibitors – bring pattern change in diabetes and CVD management, thus remaining the best evidence-based tools to tackle the interrelated burdens of metabolic dysfunction, CVDs, and renal deterioration.[25] Their complementary modes of action, solidly backed by clinical trials and the favorable safety profiles, are all attributes that distinguish them as first-line therapeutics in cardiometabolic medicine to enhance outcomes and quality of life for millions of patients around the world.[26]

Mechanisms of Cardiovascular Protection by Glucagon-like Peptide-1 Receptor Agonists

Atherosclerosis and the function of the endothelial lining in the aorta

In general, from a clinical routine view, the effect of GLP-1 RAs in the cardiovascular realm can be summarized as first delaying early atherogenic changes and second improving endothelial function.[34] One of the immediate effects of these agents is the enhancement of the endothelial release of nitric oxide, resulting in higher degrees of vessel relaxation and less stiffening. The other way these two agents work is by reducing oxidative stress in the blood vessel wall, thereby prolonging the life of endothelial cells.[35]

Another common effect of these medications, perhaps second to the most important, is to reduce the tendency of monocytes and other inflammatory cells to stick to the endothelium. Reduced expression of adhesion molecules, for example, vascular cell adhesion molecule-1 and Intercellular Adhesion Molecule-1, will be crucial in using those drugs to form an atherosclerotic plaque. Moreover, those drugs might also prevent smooth muscle cell proliferation inside arterial walls that support plaque formation.[36]

Indeed, GLP-1 RAs will provide less wall stress during the postprandial state with reduced hyperglycemia and better overall metabolic control. Because all these factors come into play together with enhancing vessel relaxation, reducing plaque build-up, and decreasing vascular inflammation, fewer cardiovascular complications have followed their patients over the years.[37]

Anti-inflammatory and Antioxidative pathways

The most important cause for the action of the GLP-1 RAs in cardiovascular protection is their tendency to reduce inflammation and oxidative stress, which are mainly responsible for plaque instability and vascular damage. Patients receiving these medications had a reduced level of inflammatory markers based on the outburst increase of CRP, IL-6, and TNF-α, among other inflammatory markers. GLP-1 RAs also inhibit the inflammatory response further down the line, following the activation of Nuclear Factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NF-κB), a very important pathway for the activation of downstream targets through the actions of inflammatory agents.[38]

GLP-1 receptor agonists and SGLT2 inhibitors, when used in combination, may exert additive or synergistic cardioprotective effects by converging on key intracellular pathways. The downregulation of NF-κB-mediated inflammatory signaling reduces vascular inflammation and oxidative stress.[39] At the same time, concurrent activation of the phosphoinositide 3-kinase/protein kinase B (PI3K/Akt) pathway promotes endothelial function, myocardial cell survival, and nitric oxide bioavailability. This integrated modulation of inflammatory and prosurvival pathways establishes a mechanistic basis for cardiovascular benefits observed with the combination use of GLP-1 receptor agonists and SGLT2 inhibitors in patients with type 2 diabetes.

From the perspective of oxidative stress, these drugs inhibit macrophage activity within the walls of blood vessels and inhibit their foaming and, therefore, improve plaque stability and limit progress. GLP-1 RAs decrease the generation of oxygen radicals and strengthen the antioxidant defense system, including superoxide dismutase enzymes and the mechanisms that mediate glutathione disposition.[40]

These antioxidants also safeguard the mitochondria and keep them healthy from damage caused by limited long-term oxidative injury. With inflammation and oxidative stress controlled, the endothelium will flourish and lower vascular remodeling while further increasing defense for the tissues during cardiovascular insufficiency. There will always be a reduced inflammatory and oxidative marker by GLP-1 RAS; even more importantly, such improvement also corresponds with better cardiovascular outcomes.[41]

Lipid profile modulation and weight reduction

As well as causing loss of body weight, GLP-1 RAs improve lipid levels and hence provide cardiac protection through other ways that have very strong propensities to influence long-term cardiovascular risk. In the majority, they will produce modest reductions in low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, triglycerides, and sometimes total cholesterol, with corresponding minor increases in HDL. Notably, improved insulin sensitivity is an important indirect action of these medications, but they also directly benefit the liver as they contribute to less fat synthesis and improve lipid disposition. Another effect is body weight loss; due to a reduction in gastric emptying and creating a sense of fullness in a person, combined with lowered total calories consumed, generally also leads to weight loss with GLP-1 RAs. As an example, semaglutide and tirzepatide produce larger-than-average losses of weight, resulting in positive changes in blood pressure, visceral fat, and markers of inflammation associated with altered lipid metabolism.[41]

Furthermore, they are useful in mitigating postprandial lipid peaks, which otherwise tend to exert shear stress on the endothelium, resulting in atheroma formation. Improvements in weight, lipid levels, glucose control, and inflammation combined create a much more favorable metabolic environment for long-term cardiovascular health.[42]

Improved cardiac metabolism and myocardial function

In an increasingly efficient way, GLP-1 RAs render the heart a bit less energy-demanding and offer it some kind of protection against adverse stress. With the heart using mostly fatty-acid oxidation pathways, the result is more oxygen demand and less efficiency in terms of energy generation, which, in diabetes, holds true. GLP-1 RAs essentially steer the heart back to the usage of glucose because it is a “cleaner” fuel in terms of economy for effort and use of oxygen. Improvement of this sort could lead, in addition, possibly to lower heart expenditure and to better cardiac output.[43]

These medications also turn on protective pathways, such as PI3K/Akt, with which heart cells withstand stress and prevent cell death, as well as improve their ability to contract. They can enhance the flow of blood toward the heart muscle or lessen damage resulting from the return of blood supply after having been deprived for a brief period of ischemia. Studies indicate improvements in left-ventricular function, less fibrosis, and better diastolic relaxation among patients who are under GLP-1 RA use.[44]

These agents are also shown to advance the exercise tolerance of some patients with heart failure and decrease the level of biomarkers such as N-terminal pro B-type natriuretic peptide. The improvement of mitochondrial function and the balance of oxidative species contribute more to the long-term protection of heart cells. Altogether, GLP-1 RAs provide more efficient cardiac metabolism, better performance of the myocardium, and retardation of heart failure progression in addition to their metabolic and vascular benefits.[45]

Major Cardiovascular Outcome Trials of Glucagon-like Peptide-1 Receptor Agonists

LEADER trial–liraglutide

The LEADER trial was an enormous study that truly changed how practitioners regard liraglutide in type 2 diabetes populations already at risk of CVD. Enrolling over 9300 patients followed for an almost 4-year period, they received either liraglutide or a placebo on top of the usual treatment.[46] Gradually, it became clear that there were fewer cardiac events in the liraglutide group; there was a 13% reduction in heart attack, stroke, or cardiovascular death combined. An even more impressive outcome was the decline in cardiovascular deaths by 22% in the liraglutide group compared to placebo. Liraglutide has shown effects on overall mortality and renal function. This outcome then fitted nicely with what was already known about the drug regarding weight, blood pressure, inflammation, and glucose stability. The LEADER became monumental in demonstrating that liraglutide does more than simply improve blood levels – it also protects the heart, a fact that has shaped treatment guidelines from then on.[47]

SUSTAIN-6– semaglutide

SUSTAIN-6 was set up to test cardiovascular safety for semaglutide, but results went far beyond just safety evidence. A little over 3,200 participants were followed for nearly 2 years, and semaglutide had a 26% reduction in main cardiovascular events compared to placebo. The more obvious benefit was a huge decrease in nonfatal strokes, with almost 40% lower rates.[48] This suggested that semaglutide may bestow strong protection on the blood vessels supplying the brain. As expected, patients lost weight and had improved glucose control, although with a fair bit of GI discomfort. An increase in retinopathy issues was noted, which was probably associated with the rapid lowering of blood glucose in already affected individuals.[49]

REWIND – dulaglutide

REWIND proves to be unlike any other GLP-1 RA trial in that it included a large cohort of patients who did not have any kind of prior CVD. Altogether, almost 10,000 participants from this study were observed for more than 5 years – a time span unusual for a trial of this type.[50] Dulaglutide provided a 12% reduction in the occurrence of main cardiovascular outcomes, and both the high-risk and lower-risk groups had benefits. Prevention of strokes accounted significantly for this overall benefit.[51] Patients also had modest weight loss, slightly lower blood pressure, and better glucose levels in the long run, as additional benefits apart from the cardiovascular outcomes. Dulaglutide also demonstrated some renal advantages. The results of REWIND provided further evidence to show that GLP-1 RAs can be used as a preventive measure against the occurrence of the first cardiovascular event in people who do not having heart disease at present. This added impetus to the directions taken to recommend the earlier use of dulaglutide.[52]

HARMONY outcomes – albiglutide

The Study HARMONY Outcomes, where albiglutide will be given to more than 9400 participants suffering from established CVD. Even though the follow-up was shorter at approximately 1.6 years, the trial confirmed a significant overall reduction of 22% in major cardiovascular events. Most of these advantages arose from the smaller number of patients with nonfatal heart attacks. The benefits associated with albiglutide included better glucose control and some weight loss. Although the drug was withdrawn later for commercial reasons, the findings were valuable because they supported the idea that GLP-1 RAs as a class offer cardiovascular protection. The trial fed into the idea that these benefits likely arose through some combination of anti-inflammation activity, enhancing endothelial function, restoring metabolic balance, and improving vascular responsiveness. This also helped to reassert that multiple GLP-1 RAs and not just one or two have clinically expressive effects on cardiovascular outcomes.[53]

Clinical Benefits and Limitations of Glucagon-like Peptide-1 Receptor Agonists

Cardio protection

Over time, GLP-1 RAs that were an integral part of diabetes therapy have been found to confer independent heart benefits beyond glucose-lowering. For years, the pattern of the various large studies, LEADER, SUSTAIN-6, REWIND, and HARMONY, was that patients taking these medications were having fewer heart attacks, strokes, or deaths due to heart disease compared to those on placebo.[54] Reasons seem to exist only in a mix of this-and-that rather than a lone mechanism. These drugs may help the vascular system in terms of improved vasoreactivity, diminished vascular inflammation, and decreased plaque accumulation, in addition to promoting body weight loss and mild blood pressure control – all of which have apparent benefits for the heart. Medications such as liraglutide, semaglutide, and dulaglutide have shown benefit even in people who did not already have heart disease, so many clinicians are now considering them earlier in treatment. Compared with older antidiabetic therapies, GLP-1 RAs offer a rare combination of good control of glucose, assistance with weight, and obvious cardiovascular protection. In many high-risk patients, these medicines have become an important long-term means to prevent a serious adverse cardiac event.[55]

Impact on heart failure

The situation is a bit different when it comes to heart failure. GLP-1 RAs appear to do well in atherosclerosis, with their effect on heart-failure outcomes being much less pronounced and consistent. Most of the large trials have shown some neutral effects on heart-failure hospitalizations, which, in stark contrast, have very clearly benefited from the SGLT2 inhibitors. However, not everything is neutral: Some smaller studies indicate the possibility of GLP-1 RAs helping the heart to metabolize energy with much more efficacy as they seem to promote glucose metabolism in the heart musculature, reduce oxidative stress, and enhance mitochondrial function.[56] Some reports have suggested that increases in ejection or diastolic functions mostly occur in those with obesity-related heart failure or heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), and weight reduction possibly contributes to this by lowering load on the heart. Nonetheless, the inconsistent nature of these improvements in large heart-failure outcome trials has led to their becoming subordinate to the guidelines mainly supporting SGLT2 inhibitors in treating heart failure. GLP-1 RAs should be viewed as providing help and assistance, but not as a primary reduction in heart failure events.[57]

Safety profile and adverse effects

Most patients tolerate GLP-1 RAs quite well, but a few side effects are pretty common, particularly at the beginning. Nausea, stomach upset, vomiting, and loose stools are among the most often recorded symptoms by the subjects, essentially due to the mechanism of the drug that slows gastric emptying. They usually decrease with time, particularly when dosage escalation is gradual. Concerns surrounding the possibility of pancreatitis are raised often, although from what is currently known, the risk is likely low. Cases of gallbladder-associated problems have also occurred, possibly secondary to swift weight loss. Hypoglycemia is rarely a problem unless the patient is on other medications, like sulfonylureas or insulin. Mild redness and irritation at the injection site may occur with the injectables, while the oral semaglutide may cause nausea and indigestion from time to time. Complications about the progression of diabetic retinopathy on semaglutide should be kept in mind, particularly in patients with already-existing significant eye disease and with rapid drops in sugar level. Collectively, this makes GLP-1 RAs safe for most individuals, with careful dose adjustments and patient education helping to mitigate many common side effects.[58]

Mechanisms of Cardiovascular Protection by Sodium-glucose Cotransporter 2 Inhibitors

Effects of hemodynamics

These agents operate practically by causing a body to gently shed superfluous rim and sodium salt. Because it causes glycosuria, it causes water to be lost with the glucose through urine. Total blood volume is slightly reduced due to the urine loss. It is a small effect, but it has meaning; it reduces pressure inside the heart and lightens the load that the pumping chambers have to handle with each beat. This explains how so many of our heart failure patients start to feel significantly better very early on, since their heart is simply no longer endowed with so much fluid.[58]

These compounds favorably allow arteries to be more flexible rather than stiff, letting blood flow easily through them. And unlike the old-fashioned water-holding pills that put the kidneys through their paces, these manage the levels of fluids without compromising the normal flow of good blood to the kidneys. Hence, it gets the heart less internal pressure, a lighter workload, and a smoother flow of blood.[59]

Sodium excretion and reduction in blood pressure

One of the key benefits that we would most definitely get would be a gentler but very significant drop in blood pressure. By actively stopping the kidneys from reabsorbing sodium, these medications would drive off salt and free water from the body. This is expected to lower blood pressure, not causing rapid reductions, nor will it lead to the release of stress hormones by the body.[60]

In particular, for our patients with diabetes, which oftentimes goes hand in hand with high blood pressure, a drop of a few points makes a tremendous difference because it noticeably reduces the amount of strain on their heart and vasculature. What makes this effect charming is that it comes without the negative side effects we usually see with other antihypertensive medications. These steady decreases in blood pressure, causing fluid overload and little weight loss, give the long-term heart some advantage.[61]

The heart works more efficiently and benefits from cardiac energy consumption

In a diabetic or a failing heart, fatty acid oxidation is efficient and oxygen-consuming. The heart is more likely to utilize ketones instead of glucose, which comes from the former energy source. It can be imagined as a cleaner combustion fuel, obtaining more energy from oxygen in this respect.[62]

Through this metabolic switching, the heart becomes more effective and efficient in pumping and relaxing. It protects the tiny power plants of heart cells (the mitochondria) against oxidative damage, which has practical implications for patients, certainly being able to be more active with less shortness of breath. Hence, this drug decreases physical work on the heart and at the same time boosts energy supply to the heart.[63]

Reduction of preload and heart afterload

They decrease preload and afterload-these loadings added to the heart due to excess fluid and enhanced vascular health, respectively.

It is the amount of blood returning to the heart. Less blood entering the heart reduces the preload (volume) that produces direct congestion and thus the sensation of breathlessness and swelling.

The afterload is the resistance that the heart must overcome to pump the blood out to the body. Greater resistance to the blood ejected in each heartbeat occurs with less flexible arteries.[64]

On top of that, because such medicines act on body fluids, they do not dehydrate the body or the bloodstream. Symptom improvement regarding breathing facilities and swelling in the feet is remarkably quick, thus allowing the individuals to move back into normal daily functioning.

Mechanisms of anti-inflammation and anti-fibrosis

In addition to handling fluids and pressures, the drugs are also useful in addressing the specific wear and tear on the heart. Low-grade, subacute inflammation becomes one of the main drivers of heart failure and atherosclerotic artery disease. These SGLT2 inhibitors will lower the inflammatory process the levels of several key markers within the blood that reflect stress within the blood vessel walls are also lowered.[65]

It might just as well be that they slow fibrosis activity, the collection of stiff, scar-like materials that makes the heart muscle into a rigid structure. In addition to reducing this scarring and protecting heart cells from damage, it helps maintain the heart muscle in a more supple condition. Thus, contraction and filling with blood are retained between beats, which is critical in preventing chronic progression toward heart failure [Figure 2].[66]

Major Cardiovascular Outcome Trials of Sodium-glucose Cotransporter 2 Inhibitors

The EMPA-REG Outcome

This study involved 7000 patients having type 2 diabetes and any kind of heart disease to build up the rapport between empagliflozin use and cardiovascular events. During slightly over 3 years, patients suffered fewer heart-related problems if on this drug. The most important reductions were deaths due to cardiovascular reasons, almost 38%, and fewer heart failure admissions.[67] These improvements were not just due to lower blood sugar. Empagliflozin helped get rid of extra fluid and salt so that heart strain was lightened, and it would, in turn, help the heart start using energy better. In this sense, it will stop kidney damage at a slower rate, which entails indirect heart protection. Such a set of data reshaped SGLT2 inhibitors in the eyes of practitioners to value cardioprotection; hence, they became one of the first antidiabetic drugs capable of reducing the risk of cardiovascular mortality.[68]

CANVAS program

The CANVAS program encompasses two related studies dialectically toeing the line, dealing with just over 10,000 type 2 diabetes patients at considerable risk of CVD. Throughout this program, after a very close to 4-year period, significantly fewer hospital admissions resulted from acute cardiac failure among canagliflozin patients. The slight beneficial effect upon important parameters, weight, and blood pressure in various organs occurred in the same way as the drug stabilized the kidneys. Concern rose with an increased risk of lower extremity amputations that usually involved disarticulation through the toes or the front of the foot. For this reason, upper care should be provided to such foot-cladlegged ones. With this risk, CANVAS concluded that the medicine in the broader family of SGLT2 inhibitors does assure a protective benefit to the heart and kidneys and is appropriate for patients suffering from long-standing diabetes and its concomitant cardiovascular risks.[69]

DECLARE–TIMI 58

This DECLARE–TIMI 58 included more than 17,000 people, compared to previous trials, where almost all the patients did not have established CVD. Instead, the main interest was the logo declared with relevance to heart failure. The dapagliflozin reduces the risk of hospitalization for heart failure by 27.5% over a time period that exceeds 4 years in a study arm, indicating the importance of delaying heart failure early before the first large event. The drug also significantly delays the decline in kidney function, most likely providing such a benefit through reductions in fluid overload, blood pressure, and haphazardly expanding myocardial stress.[70]

DAPA-HF

Contained in the 76-page report of the DAPA-HF trial were more than 6000 patients, all afflicted with heart failure and an EF <40. This formed a unique study to show that dapagliflozin could help the heart independent of blood sugar. Just about 18 months later, the drug showed to control the worsening of heart failure or death from cardiovascular causes by 26%. Patients on dapagliflozin enjoyed far better days in terms of activities and quality of life. The medicine worked by making the heart more efficient, necessary for congestion extrication and for those hypertrophy induced by the heart over the course of time, by plumping up some; yet also helpful in all this, there were guarantees that kidney function would continue. For the first time, an SGLT2 class of drugs revealed that they probably represented the major treatment of heart failure, being adopted into guidelines for both diabetic and nondiabetic patients.[71]

EMPEROR-reduced and EMPEROR-preserved

Empagliflozin has been studied in heart failure, both with reduced ejection fraction and alongside preserved ejection fraction, in the EMPEROR trials. EMPEROR-Reduced encompassed over 3700 patients and was able to reduce by 25% hospitalizations and/or cardiovascular death. EMPEROR-Preserved, with nearly 6000 patients enrolled, showed a 21% reduction in this combined outcome – a very encouraging finding, as HFpEF remains a big challenge for effective treatment. Here, likewise, empagliflozin ultimately minimized fluid congestion and was also a benefactor of lower arterial stiffness, thereby protecting more coordinated energy utilization within heart muscle cells. The clinical trials also showed strong protection against kidney decline. Combined, these trials confirmed effective help extended by empagliflozin throughout most heart failure phases, with or without accompanying diabetes condition.[72]

Clinical Benefits and Limitations of Sodium-glucose Cotransporter 2 Inhibitors

Heart failure benefits

SGLT2 inhibitors have revolutionized the medical approach to heart failure. In its implementation, quite a number of patients report quicker feelings of getting better. Such patients usually take the medications during the acute disability phase of heart failure with reduced ejection fraction and expect a response in the first few weeks. However, they feel better with those having hearts that are stiffer and those that do not relax well (HFpEF) in the 1st weeks. This is not purely contributed to blood sugar lowering alone; they also help in a gentle and steady way, a process for sending the extra fluid and salt out of the body.[73] The fluid levels rising in the body prevent stretching and struggling with each heartbeat. When the heart slightly lowers blood pressure, it does not have to push against such tight resistance. Thus, combined, they lower the daily “strain” that each heart suffers.[74] More importantly, SGLT2 inhibitors also regulate the metabolism of the heart’s energy. Conclusion

GLP-1 RAs and SGLT2 inhibitors are now revolutionizing the management of type 2 diabetes by affording profit beyond glycemic regulation to tangible cardioprotective effects; for instance, whereas GLP-1 RAs significantly curb atherosclerotic cardiovascular events in addition to improving endothelial function and promoting weight loss, SGLT2 inhibitors, on the other hand, reduce the risk of heart failure, lower admissions, and slow chronic kidney disease progression. With these HRAs, one gets grasped in the glory of having treatments that meet multiple facets of cardiometabolic risk, and hence, one would have to move beyond glucose-centricity to comprehensive cardiometabolic care.

Such therapies would make up a strategic advantage for patients with comorbidities in the areas of cardiovascular, renal, and metabolic diseases. Evidence is bound to be generated from clinical trials and recommendations of guidelines that specify their function in individualized treatment planning for superior long-term outcomes. Application of fixed-dose combinations or use of new combined agonists like GLP-1/GIP receptor agonists will even broaden the horizons in terms of better adherence and efficacy treatment.

Many significant leaps have been made, but gaps continue to exist, such as the long-term safety in diverse populations, optimal sequencing or combinations, and the costs involved, particularly in the real-world setting. Future investigation into personalized approaches, outcomes centered on patients, and the new therapeutic targets will carry the torch for clinical practice refinement. Integration of potentially high-risk patients in cardiometabolic care will definitely translate over time into reduced morbidity and mortality in patients with high risk, and the beginning of a new era in precision cardiovascular and metabolic medicine.

Identification and Experimental Validation of OS-Related Gene Sets Based on Integrated Analysis of Single-Cell and Bulk RNA Sequencing Data with Machine Learning in Patients with Sepsis
Authors: Linfeng Tao, Wei Tian, Ping Li, Yan Chen, Jun Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSepsis is a severe organ dysfunction syndrome caused by a dysregulated host response to infection, closely associated with poor prognosis. It disrupts the balance between oxidative and antioxidative systems, which may ultimately result in cellular dysfunction and death. However, the key regulatory genes involved in this process remain unclear and require further investigation. In this study, we analyzed a single-cell RNA sequencing dataset from the Single Cell Portal and an oxidative stress (OS) gene set from GeneCards. We employed multiple algorithms and correlation analysis to identify OS-related gene sets that were upregulated in sepsis. Subsequently, RNA expression datasets from the Gene Expression Omnibus were used to filter for overlapping genes that were upregulated in the sepsis group. Furthermore, we used three machine learning algorithms to identify the optimal characteristic genes and verified them with animal models. Analysis of both scRNA-seq and bulk RNA-seq datasets using various algorithms revealed a significant increase in OS activity scores following sepsis, with heterogeneity observed across different cell layers. TXN, NUDT1, MAPK14, and CYP1B1 were found to be closely associated with the elevated OS levels in sepsis. Furthermore, our animal experiments confirmed a significant increase in OS activity in septic mice, along with elevated expression of TXN, MAPK14, and CYP1B1. This study is the first to elucidate the heterogeneity of oxidative stress at the single-cell level in sepsis. Introduction

Sepsis is a life-endangering multi-organ failure syndrome with dysregulated host defense mechanisms against pathogens, having the characteristics of acute onset, rapid onset, and poor prognosis. Sepsis is one of the leading contributor to mortality in critically ill patients [1]. It is also the primary driver of disease burden as well as fatality rate in the world, which seriously threatens human health [1]. As sepsis progresses from mild to severe, it triggers a series of abnormal immune system responses. These reactions can lead to cell and tissue metabolic disorders, which in turn lead to coagulation dysfunction, abnormal microcirculation, and ultimately lead to multiple organ damage [2]. It has significant correlation with adverse clinical outcomes. While recent years have witnessed modest reductions in sepsis-related in-hospital mortality rates, problems such as high rehospitalization rate, cognitive dysfunction, and disability are still important factors affecting patients’ life [3]. Therefore, sepsis is not only clinically significant, but also brings significant economic burdens. At the same time, it is still a field of great concern and great research value. Currently, there is growing evidence of perturbations in redox homeostasis and oxidative stress in patients with sepsis, as well as oxidative damage markers [4, 5]. Oxidative Stress (OS) is characterized by the perturbation of redox equilibrium in biological systems, leading to an overproduction of Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) and reactive nitrogen species (RNS). This imbalance overwhelms the intrinsic cellular redox regulatory systems, resulting in cellular and molecular damage [2, 6]. ROS and RNS exert direct deleterious effects on cellular structures, comprising membranes, proteins, and DNA, as well as other critical biomacromolecules [4]. Oxi{Citation}dative stress serves as a critical instigator, driving the activation of transcription factors, and stimulating the overproduction of inflammatory mediators. This mechanism not only amplifies the inflammatory cascade but also exacerbates cellular and tissue damage, thereby exerting a critical influence on disease development of various inflammatory and OS-related diseases [7].

Although current studies have elucidated the critical role of oxidative stress in the pathogenesis of sepsis, the key regulatory genes involved remain to be explored. Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) is an emerging methodology that facilitates genome-wide analysis, cell-to-cell variability, profiling of transcriptional variations, and characterization of distinct cellular populations within tissue samples [7]. This demonstrates distinct superiority compared to conventional RNA-seq through the resolution of cellular heterogeneity within intricate tissue architectures.Furthermore, scRNA-seq has gained extensive application in both diagnosing diseases and predicting their outcomes. Its combination with bioinformatics methodologies has significantly advanced the understanding of metabolite involvement in diverse pathological conditions [8, 9]. Machine learning, a subfield of artificial intelligence, can be used to screen for more diagnostic biomarkers with huge datasets, when combined with other bioinformatics [10–12]. In Wang et al.’s study, the critical role of eight feature genes in constructing a diagnostic model for systemic sclerosis (SSc) was validated using the support vector machine (SVM), random forest (RF), and LASSO algorithms [13]. Du et al. employed multiple machine learning algorithms (xGBoost, Boruta, RF, and LASSO) to identify and validate the crucial roles of ANXA2 and COTL1 in oxidative stress following intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH), providing critical insights into the pathological mechanisms of ICH and potential therapeutic target development [8].

In this study, we for the first time revealed the heterogeneity of oxidative stress (OS) activity at the single-cell level in sepsis, demonstrating significant differences among immune cell subsets. By integrating multi-algorithmic computational analyses and machine learning frameworks, we observed significant upregulation of OS activity post-sepsis and identified key OS-related genes with significant diagnostic potential. These findings may serve as novel therapeutic targets, offering essential insights for future sepsis research and clinical translation.

Methods

Data Collection

In this study, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data were sourced from the SCP548 bulk set (https://singlecell.broadinstitute.org/single_cell), encompassing blood samples from 29 sepsis patients and 19 healthy controls [14]. Furthermore, bulk RNA-seq data were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (accessible at http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo), specifically from the GSE65682 dataset (comprising 802 samples, including 42 healthy and 760 sepsis cases) and the GSE66099 dataset (comprising 276 samples, including 47 healthy and 199 sepsis cases) [15]. The experimental datasets were integrated into a consolidated bulk RNA-sequencing dataset, following batch effect normalization and subsequent data integration employing the R packages “limma” and “sva” [16].

Oxidative stress-associated genes were obtained from the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/) with a selection threshold exceeding 20 points. A curated set of 193 high-scoring OS-associated genes was ultimately incorporated into the analytical pipeline, as detailed in Supplementary Table 1.

Analysis of scRNA-seq Dataset

Within this scRNA-seq dataset, we used the Seurat R package [17] to process the raw data into Seurat objects [17]. To maintain data integrity, we implemented rigorous quality control protocols by retaining cells with detectable gene counts between 50 − 4,000 and excluding those exhibiting mitochondrial gene expression levels exceeding 3% of total transcripts. We employed PCA as a dimensionality reduction technique, applying it to genes with above-average expression profiles and subsequently selecting the leading 20 principal components. Subsequently, we employed the “FindCluster” algorithm for cell clustering, with the resolution parameter configured to 0.65. The clustering results were visualized using Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP). To delineate cluster-specific marker genes, we performed differential expression analysis across all cell populations using Seurat’s “FindAllMarkers” algorithm. This approach systematically contrasts target clusters and the remaining cells, followed by biological interpretation of cell identities through integration with the CellMarker database (http://117.50.127.228/CellMarker/).

Evaluation of Os Related-Genesets (OSRGs) Activity

By leveraging AUCell [18], UCell [19], singscore [20], ssGSEA [21], and AddModuleScore [22] algorithmic frameworks, we evaluated cell-specific OS activity at a single-cell granularity and the total OS activity [8]. Cells were stratified into elevated and reduced OS activity cohorts using the median value of the precalculated scores as the cutoff threshold. Subsequent correlation study was employed to pinpoint genes functionally linked to OS activity. The ‘FindMarkers’ algorithm was implemented to perform differential gene analysis (DEG) associated with the upregulation of OS. Subsequently, a Venn diagram was generated to visualize the overlapping gene signatures identified through both analytical approaches. To functionally annotate the overlapping genes, we used Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis with the “clusterProfiler” package [23] in R software.

RNA-seq Dataset Analysis

The Ssgsea R package [24] was employed to perform Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA) on bulk RNA-seq profiles of blood specimens, facilitating the identification of OS-associated mechanisms in sepsis [24, 25].

Computational Identification of Optimal Feature Genes via Machine Learning

Leveraging an ensemble machine learning framework comprising Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) [26], Random Forest (RF) [27], Boruta [28], we systematically evaluated the pre-screened genomic candidates to identify the hub genes associated with OS activity. This multimodal framework synergistically integrates LASSO-driven parametric sparsity, RF-embedded decision tree entropy analysis, and Boruta’s permutation-based feature prioritization, thereby achieving comprehensive coverage of linear/nonlinear gene regulatory patterns, synergistically improving the reliability and precision in detecting sepsis-associated molecular signatures.

LASSO regression was executed using the “glmnet“ [29] package, which incorporates a regularization term to reduce coefficients and select the most significant features while discarding irrelevant or redundant genes. By adding regularization terms to the loss function, LASSO not only improves the predictive accuracy and interpretability of statistical models but also ensures robustness and prevents overfitting through 10-fold cross-validation. Random Forest [30] was utilized to further enhance prediction reliability. The RF algorithm builds an ensemble of decision trees through bootstrap aggregation and random feature subsets, then generates predictions through majority voting or mean aggregation. This approach emphasizes feature interdependencies and interactions. The RF algorithm was configured with an ensemble of 500 decision trees and employed default 10-fold cross-validation to evaluate predictive accuracy. The Boruta [31] algorithm was chosen for its thorough identification of relevant features linked to the target variable, retaining only the most crucial genes. It evaluates attribute significance by iteratively comparing initial attributes with randomly permuted shadow attributes using random forests. Boruta can handle high-dimensional data without preprocessing, is unaffected by attribute interdependencies, and provides a reliable selection of genes associated with sepsis, even though it lacks built-in cross-validation. Ultimately, the common genes identified by the three machine learning algorithms were chosen as the optimal feature genes in sepsis and visualized using a Venn diagram.

Intercellular Communication Networks and Transcriptional Factors

We implemented CellChat [32]-mediated integration of omics datasets to investigate alterations in theoretical cell-cell signaling networks, with the standard CellChatDB serving as the signaling interaction atlas. By pinpointing elevated ligand/receptor pairs in phenotypically distinct clusters, we delineated cell-subset communication patterns and uncovered signaling node activities linked to dysregulated molecular messengers. Subsequently, a protein interaction network (PPI) was generated by employing the Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes (STRING).

Gene Set Enrichment Analysis(GSEA) of the Feature

We conducted GSEA [33] on the selected feature genes to evaluate their associations with biological pathways or pathological conditions and underlying regulatory networks.

Animal Model of Sepsis

We obtained sexually mature male C57BL/6 mice (age: 6–8 weeks) from a certified vendor (Changzhou Kavans Experimental Animal Co., Ltd, China) prior to experimental procedures. Following a 7-day acclimation under standard conditions, caecal ligation and puncture (CLP)-induced sepsis modeling was performed in mice [3, 34]. Animals were anesthetized via isoflurane inhalation, after which the shaved abdomen was aseptically prepared with iodophor. Left lower quadrant incision (1 cm) exposed the cecum for ligation. The cecum was ligated 1.5 cm proximal to the blind end using 4 − 0 silk suture, followed by a single transmural puncture with a 26-gauge needle to create bilateral enteric continuity. A small drop of intestinal content was gently extruded to induce infection. Then, the ligated cecal segment was anatomically reinstated within the peritoneal compartment, followed by a multi-layered closure of the surgical site employing tiered tissue approximation techniques. At 12 h post-CLP surgery, mice exhibited signs of successful sepsis model establishment, including curling up, significantly reduced activity, lethargy, sluggish response, erect fur, purulent discharge from the corners of the eyes, and diarrhea.

The research utilizing animal models was ethically evaluated and authorized by the Ethics Committee of Suzhou Municipal Hospital, Suzhou, China.

Evaluation of OS Activity in Sepsis Mouse Model

24 mice were randomly allocated to two experimental groups: sham, CLP (n = 12/group). 24 h after operation, the mice were anesthetized and the blood were collected from the mice eye socket vein in mice.

Lipid Peroxidation MDA Assay Kit (S0131S, Beyotime Biotechnology, China), and superoxide dismutase (SOD)-WST-8 assay kit (ADS-W-KY011, Jiangsu Aidisheng Biological Technology Co.,Ltd, China) was used to test biochemical information.

Western Blotting

Total protein lysates were isolated with high-efficiency RIPA lysis buffer (P0013C, Beyotime) supplemented with a protease inhibitor mixture (P1045, Beyotime). Protein concentration determination was performed utilizing a bicinchoninic acid assay kit (ZJ101L, Epizyme Biotech). Normalized protein aliquots were applied to polyacrylamide gels for SDS‒PAGE electrophoresis, followed by electrophoretic transfer to PVDF membranes (IPVH00010, MerckMillipore). Post-blockade, membranes were exposed to optimized dilutions of primary antibodies at 4 °C for 12–16 h, subsequently probed with species-matched secondary antibodies under ambient conditions (25 ± 2 °C, 60 min). Chemiluminescent visualization was achieved by application of ECL substrate reagents (180–5001, Tanon), with signal acquisition conducted via a digital imaging platform (Tanon Chemi Dog 5200 T, China). The following primary antibodies were applied in the experimental design: anti-TXN (#A7638, ABclonal Technology, China), anti-MAPK14 (#ET1602-26, HUABIO, China), anti-CYP1B1 (#A1377, ABclonal Technology, China), anti-NUDT1 (#A5474, ABclonal Technology, China), and anti-GAPDH (#ab8245, Abcam, China).

Quantitative Real-Time PCR

Total RNA was isolated with RNAiso Easy reagent (TCH020, Takara), followed by cDNA synthesis via the PrimeScript™ Reverse Transcriptase Kit (RR047A, Takara) under standardized reaction conditions. Quantitative PCR was performed using TB Green (RR420A, Takara) and a Real-Time PCR Detection System (Gentier 96, Tianlong). Target gene transcriptional levels were quantified using the 2−ΔΔCt method normalized to GAPDH. Primer sequences are detailed in Supplemental Table 4.

Statistical Analysis

Raw data were analyzed computationally through R version 4.4.1 software and GraphPad Prism 9.0 analytical platform. Quantitative results were expressed as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). For comparative analyses, intergroup differences were assessed via the Student’s t-test or Wilcoxon’s rank sum test for dual cohorts, whereas multigroup comparisons were conducted via one-way ANOVA. Statistical significance thresholds were set at p < 0.05.

Complexity of OS-Related Features After Sepsis Via scRNA-seq Datasets

The schematic representation of the experimental workflow is presented in Fig. 1. Before going further, we controlled all samples (Fig. 2A) and finally selected 48 samples (29 from the sepsis cases and 19 from the control cases) for analysis. To explore specific cell classes with high oxidative stress (OS) activity to reveal the complexity and heterogeneity of OS after sepsis, we used UMAP plots to show the distribution of seven cell subsets between patients with sepsis and controls (Fig. 2B). According to the expression levels of standard immune cell signature genes, we divided the different cell clusters into seven different cell types (Fig. 2C-D), including: monocytes (labeled CD14 and LYZ), macrophages (labeled CD68), B cells (labeled CD79A), plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs; labeled JCHAIN and LILRA4), T cell (labeled CD3D and CD3E), natural killer cells (NK cells; labeled GNLY and KLRB1), and dendritic cells (DCs; labeled CD1C and FCER1A). Following GO enrichment by signature genes of subpopulations, we found that the top 3 GO terms by cell type were all related to their function (Fig. 2E). To delineate the divergences distinguishing the septic group from control group, comparative analyses of cellular subpopulation distribution disparities were conducted across predefined experimental groups. We found that the proportions of monocytes and macrophages were significantly increased after sepsis, while the proportions of dendritic cells were decreased, indicating disturbances of the immune system in sepsis (Fig. 2F).

By correlation, we investigated the top 100 genes associated with OS activity scores (Fig. 3E-F, Supplementary Table 2). All cells were then classified into two groups of high OS activity cells and low OS activity cells based on the median value of the total OS activity score (Fig. 3G). Identifying Optimal Genes Through Machine Learning

Three distinct machine learning algorithms were utilized to discern signature genes within the set of 15 candidate genes. In order to further compress the features and solve the collinearity problem, we first identified 12 key genes as optimal genes using the LASSO algorithm (Fig. 5A-B). Then utilizing the RF algorithm, we identified five pivotal genes exhibiting an importance score exceeding 8 (Fig. 5C-D). Following 1000 iterations of the Boruta algorithm, we subsequently discern 14 pivotal genes (Fig. 5E-F). Finally, through the integration of results from the three machine learning methodologies, we revealed four signature genes: TXN, NUDT1, MAPK14, and CYP1B1 (Fig. 5G).

Discussion

Previous studies have demonstrated that patients with sepsis experience severe redox imbalance and oxidative stress [5]. Under physiological homeostasis, a dynamic equilibrium is maintained between the generation of ROS and their elimination by intrinsic antioxidant defense mechanisms. However, in septic patients, an overproduction of ROS or impairment of the antioxidant system disrupts this balance, leading to oxidative stress5. Nevertheless, the relationship between OS-related molecules and sepsis remains largely unexplored. Some related studies have studied the characteristic genes used for diagnosis in different diseases through multi-algorithm machine learning strategies, which provides a methodological example for disease marker screening [8, 13]. In this study, machine learning was used to screen genes associated with oxidative stress in sepsis for the diagnosis of sepsis. These findings are expected to help researchers gain a deeper understanding of immune dysregulation in sepsis.

In this study, by analyzing scRNA-seq and bulk RNA-seq datasets, we observed a significant increase in OS activity scores following sepsis and identified four genes (TXN, NUDT1, MAPK14, and CYP1B1) closely associated with OS activity. Subsequently, we investigated the expression of these genes in a sepsis mouse model induced by CLP to further explore OS activity and OSRG alterations after sepsis. Compared with the sham group, septic mice exhibited elevated MDA expression and reduced T-SOD levels, indicating the occurrence of oxidative stress. Western blot and RT-qPCR analyses further demonstrated significantly increased relative expression levels of TXN, MAPK14, and CYP1B1 at both the protein and mRNA levels in septic mice. Overall, these findings indicate that OS-associated genes are potentially involved in the molecular mechanisms underlying sepsis pathogenesis.

Thioredoxin (TXN) is an endogenous redox protein that reduces intracellular hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) to water (H₂O) while converting oxidized thiols back to disulfide-linked thiols [35]. The primary function of TXN is to participate in cellular antioxidant defense mechanisms. By regulating cellular free radicals and ROS [36]. Studies have found that sepsis is closely linked to profound OS, and TXN serves a pivotal function in modulating oxidative stress and inflammatory responses [35, 37]. In our animal experiments, thioredoxin (TXN) was significantly upregulated in sepsis, and through PPI network analysis, we found that TXN had a physical interaction with key nodes of the MIF pathway: CD74、CD44、CXCR2 and CXCR4. Macrophage migration inhibitory factor (MIF), a pleiotropic cytokine, mediates inflammatory responses through receptor complexes composed of CD74, CD44, CXCR2, and CXCR4 [38, 39]. Susannah K et al. reported elevated TXN and macrophage migration inhibitory factor (MIF) levels in the plasma of septic patients exhibited a unique correlation [39]. Nevertheless, the precise function of TXN in the context of sepsis remains unknown. Our study reveals that TXN may exert its effects via the MIF pathway, but further research is needed to elucidate this mechanism.

Mitogen-activated protein kinase 14 (MAPK14, also known as p38-MAPK) is a cytokine-suppressive anti-inflammatory drug-binding protein and an osmotic regulatory protein kinase that is triggered by diverse cellular stress conditions. It plays a crucial role in the development of inflammatory diseases, neurodegenerative disorders, cardiovascular diseases, and cancer. Upon cellular stress, MAPK14 is phosphorylated by MKK3 and MK6, leading to the phosphorylation of downstream substrates, including gene regulatory proteins and MAPK-activated protein kinases (such as MAPKAPK2 or MK2). Inhibition of MK2 activation mediated by MAPK14 may help suppress inflammation and treat related diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis and cryopyrin-associated periodic syndromes [40, 41]. Previous studies have demonstrated a close association between MAPK14 and sepsis [42–45]. Ruixue Liu et al. found that MAPK14 is linked to ferroptosis and is significantly upregulated during sepsis [44]. Our single-cell trajectory analysis showed that MAPK14 expression was upregulated in the monocyte subpopulation but not significantly different in DC cells. Other studies have shown that MAPK14 is involved in all monocyte states during sepsis, suggesting that MAPK14 may be a key molecule in regulating monocyte differentiation and function [43]. This cellular heterogeneity suggests that p38 MAPK inhibitors may require the development of targeted delivery systems to avoid off-target effects on other immune cell subsets. Consequently, MAPK14 is implicated in the initiation and development of sepsis, yet its exact molecular mechanisms remain elusive and warrant further exploration.

Cytochrome P450 1B1 (CYP1B1) exhibits significant expression in non-hepatic tissues [49]. It is involved in the metabolism of various endogenous and exogenous substances [49, 50]. Previous study indicate that CYP1B1 is pivotal in tumorigenesis, modulation of metabolic pathways, and therapeutic interventions for metabolic disorders [46]. Relevant studies have indicated that CYP1B1 serves as a biomarker for sepsis diagnosis and poor prognosis, potentially promoting inflammation and apoptosis by targeting miR-18a-5p [47]. Juan Wu et al. found that the deficiency of CYP1B1 in mouse brain tissue is associated with the disruption of blood-brain barrier integrity and oxidative stress in the striatum [48]. Our findings revealed marked upregulation of CYP1B1 in septic mice. However, additional investigations are required to elucidate its precise role in oxidative stress mechanisms and therapeutic implications.

This study reveals a single-cell atlas of oxidative stress (OS) activity in sepsis, establishing TXN/MAPK14/CYP1B1 as core regulators spanning cell types, thereby offering a novel perspective for precision-targeted therapy. However, our research did not elucidate the specific regulatory mechanisms of these genes in sepsis OS pathology (such as upstream/downstream signaling pathways and transcriptional regulatory networks) or their direct functional roles (such as the impact of gene knockout/overexpression on OS phenotypes and organ function). Moreover, while animal models assessed gene expression and OS activity markers (MDA, SOD), they did not evaluate the therapeutic potential of intervening on these targets. Future studies require larger-scale, multi-center human cohort validation, deeper exploration of molecular mechanisms (functional experiments), and therapeutic intervention studies in models encompassing both sexes to advance these findings towards clinical translation.

Conclusions

This investigation indicates that oxidative stress-associated genes TXN, MAPK14, and CYP1B1 are potentially pivotal in sepsis pathogenesis. Nevertheless, comprehensive studies are essential to delineate the underlying molecular pathways and mechanisms. Subsequent research should prioritize elucidating oxidative stress dynamics in sepsis and uncovering novel therapeutic targets derived from these findings.

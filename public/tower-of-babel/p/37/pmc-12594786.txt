A forensic engineering framework for flood management of cascade reservoir systems
Authors: Masoumeh Zeinali, Omid Bozorg-Haddad, Mehdi Yasi, Hosein Alizadeh, Manizhe Zarei, Hugo A Loaiciga
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractExtreme floods are increasing in frequency and severity, exposing critical gaps in reservoir operation policies and post-event accountability. This study proposes a forensic engineering framework to evaluate and optimize flood management in cascade reservoir systems. The framework is applied to Iran’s Karkheh Basin, Iran, and combines scenario-based analysis, a dynamic Standard Operating Policy (SOP) extended to gated spillways, and real-time optimization using inflow forecasts. Five scenarios were analyzed for the April 2019 flood: (1) actual historical operation, (2–3) SOP-based rule curves driven by observed and forecasted inflows, (4) ideal hindsight operation, and (5) rolling-horizon real-time optimization with SVR (monthly) and LSTM (daily) inflow forecasts embedded in a Genetic Algorithm. Results show that, without the proposed Mashoureh Reservoir, relative downstream flood damage was reduced by 18%, 8%, 59%, and 39% under Scenarios 2–5, respectively, compared to Scenario 1. Inclusion of the Mashoureh reservoir reduces damage by 34%, 37%, 100%, and 55% according to scenarios 2–5. However, the impact of the Mashoureh reservoir was limited because 71% of the flood volume originated downstream this reservoir. These findings underscore the importance of combining structural and non-structural measures in the analysis of reservoir operation for flood-control purpose, and demonstrate the value of forensic scenario evaluation for flood preparedness, real-time operations, and planning of future infrastructure.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-25923-3.Keywords: Flood management, Forensic engineering, Reservoir operation, Flood damages, Cascade damsSubject terms: Climate sciences, Environmental sciences, Chemistry, Energy science and technology, Engineering, Materials science, Nanoscience and technology

Introduction

Flooding has emerged as one of the most frequent and destructive natural disasters worldwide since the early twenty-first century, imposing immense economic, environmental, and human losses each year1. This challenge is particularly acute in developing countries with arid and semi-arid climates, such as Iran, where socio-environmental vulnerabilities are more pronounced2. The frequency and magnitude of extreme flood events escalate under the influence of climate change, which requires management strategies that address dynamic operational and forecasting-based interventions3,4. Flood management approaches are generally categorized into structural methods—such as the construction of dams, diversion channels, and river engineering—and non-structural methods, including early warning systems, watershed management, and optimized reservoir operations. Among the latter forecast-based flood management has proven to be one of the most cost-effective solutions, particularly when pre-release strategies are guided by accurate hydrological forecasts5. On the one hand reservoir construction aims to increase system storage capacity; on the other hand non-structural approaches such as deriving rule curves or operational policies informed by long- and short-term forecasts can significantly improve system performance during flood events.

Significant advancements have been made in recent year with respect to the optimization of cascade reservoir operations under flood conditions. Researchers have explored real-time control strategies using various heuristic and AI-based approaches to account for hydrological uncertainty and improve system responsiveness. For instance, Phankamolsil et al. applied a DDPG‑based deep reinforcement learning framework to multi-reservoir operation planning in the Chao Phra Yai River Basin6. Their model integrates seven-dimensional predicted inflows, downstream conditions, adjacent reservoir releases, and storage states to dynamically adjust releases. Zhang et al. proposed an adaptive release mechanism based on ensemble streamflow prediction and rolling optimization7. These studies demonstrate the growing role of data-driven and hybrid methods for enhancing flood management efficiency Cheng et al. introduced a multi-objective cooperative control model for upstream–downstream reservoir systems under real-time constraints8. Liu et al. evaluated the trade-offs between local and system-wide objectives using multi-reservoir rule curves derived from historical flood patterns9.

Evaluating the effectiveness of existing management practices and identifying operational weaknesses are essential steps toward improving flood resilience. Forensic engineering offers a powerful methodology to investigate such failures by systematically analyzing causal mechanisms, both natural and anthropogenic10. Specifically, the field of forensic hydrology, introduced by Loaiciga11, focuses on examining the causes of hydrological disasters and distinguishing the role of operational errors from natural forcing. Subsequent developments in this field have enabled data-driven investigations of floods, droughts, sedimentation, and groundwater depletion12,13. Numerous forensic flood studies have examined major events across the globe, revealing critical lessons and shortcomings in reservoir operations. Examples include the Tabasco flood in Mexico14, the Brisbane flood in Australia15, and the catastrophic floods in Germany16,17. These investigations have leveraged hydrological monitoring data, satellite imagery, and modeling techniques to reconstruct flood events and evaluate alternative mitigation strategies. For instance, Ho et al. utilized data from 23 stations and remote sensing to analyze the 2011 flood in Thailand18. Delpasand et al. proposed a forensic framework using fourteen pre-release criteria to assess reservoir management during the 2019 flood in the Greater Karun Basin, Iran19. More recently, Zarei et al. developed a forensic engineering framework to evaluate reservoir operations under flood conditions in the Karkheh Basin, comparing Standard Operating Policy (SOP), Ideal Operation, and historical practices across multiple events20. Despite these advances, few studies have integrated forensic evaluation into forecast-based operational models to trace back performance gaps and quantify missed opportunities in real-time flood response.

This study introduces an enhanced forensic engineering framework for cascade reservoir systems. Prior studies have demonstrated the utility of forensic hydrology in post-event assessments, yet, there remains a need for integrated methodologies that combine data-driven forecasts, operational decision models, and quantitative performance evaluation across multiple scenarios. This study addresses these gaps by applying the proposed framework to the Seimareh–Karkheh–Mashoureh system in Iran during the extreme April 2019 flood. The proposed framework introduces the following innovations:

1. Integration of dual-scale inflow forecasts—monthly Support Vector Regression (SVR) and daily Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) models—with a rolling Genetic Algorithm (GA) optimizer for real-time operation.

2. Extension to hypothetical infrastructure: The integration of a proposed (non-existing) reservoir (Mashoureh) into a fully functional optimization and forensic framework, which is rarely addressed in prior literature. This allows assessment of structural vs. non-structural solutions under comparable conditions.

3. Forensic scenario-based evaluation of operational performance under the extreme April 2019 flood in the Karkheh River Basin, with and without the proposed Mashoureh Reservoir.

4. Quantification of relative flood damage across scenarios using performance indices and damage functions.

This study builds upon the authors’ previous work on forensic reservoir operation assessment under flood conditions in the Seimareh–Karkheh system20, where a modified Standard Operating Policy (SOP) was introduced. Previous research focused on rule-based retrospective assessment of reservoir performance, while the present study introduces a more advanced framework with the structural and non-structural innovations cited in items 1–4. Specifically, it expands the system to include the proposed Mashoureh Reservoir, enabling the assessment of structural augmentation. These advancements make it possible to perform a more comprehensive forensic evaluation of reservoir operations under extreme flood scenarios and demonstrate the added value of combining forecast-driven operations with system expansion.

This study aims to answer the following research questions:

- How did actual operations perform in comparison to optimized or ideal alternatives during the 2019 flood?

- Can forecast-informed, real-time control significantly reduce flood damages in the absence of new structural storage?

- What is the marginal benefit of incorporating the Mashoureh Reservoir compared to enhancing existing operations?

By coupling predictive modeling, optimization, and forensic evaluation, this study contributes to advancing integrated, adaptive flood management strategies in cascade reservoir systems.

Materials and methods

Modeling environment and data

The study area includes a cascade system in a watershed that includes reservoirs in operation and under study. Data sources include:

- Historical inflow, outflow, and storage data;

- Observed precipitation and runoff data for a flood event;

- Physical and operational parameters of reservoirs (storage capacity, release capacity, spillway characteristics);

- The dataset of reservoirs inflow used for training and testing the SVR and LSTM models, along with the predicted inflow results generated by these models.

Rolling horizon optimization

A rolling horizon optimization strategy is implemented. In this approach, the optimization is carried out iteratively across the event timeline to simulate real-time decision-making under dynamic flood conditions. At each time step (e.g., day t) the model generates optimal reservoir release decisions for the remaining flood period (days t to T), based on the most updated inflow forecasts. This approach is called Flood Informed Reservoir Operation (FIRO).

As the simulation progresses in time the earlier decisions (for days 1 to t − 1) become fixed and are no longer part of the optimization problem. Therefore, the number of decision variables decreases over time, as shown schematically in Fig. 1. This dynamic adjustment reduces computational complexity and reflects the real-world operational context, where only future actions remain controllable, while past actions are locked in.

Rolling horizon optimization scheme.

For example, at the start of the event (day 1), the optimizer considers the full 10-day planning window, but by day 5, only the decisions for days 5 to 10 remain in the optimization horizon. This allows the model to balance responsiveness to new forecasts with the stability of past decisions.

Inflow forecasting and integration

Reservoir performance is improved by integrating short- and long-term forecasts, with accuracy increasing closer to flood events. Two machine learning models are used:

- SVR (Support Vector Regression) for monthly inflow forecasting.

- LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) neural network for daily forecasts.

Under Scenario 5 the optimization is run in a rolling horizon where inflow forecasts are updated and past decisions are fixed. The number of active decision variables decreases as the time window progresses.

Forensic evaluation framework

Forensic evaluation of reservoir management under flood conditions involves comparing different operational scenarios with actual performance to identify the causes of flood damage. This study considers several operational scenarios, including those based on historical observations, standard operating policies with and without predictions, ideal operation, and proposed real-time optimal operations. In previous studies, such as20, a scientific review and failure analysis was conducted based on the prism analysis introduced by Noon22. This study introduces a forensic engineering framework for flood management, illustrated in Fig. 2. This framework involves selecting a past flood event for analysis, gathering relevant evidence, and identifying natural and human factors that contributed to the event. It further includes analyzing the roles of responsible organizations, available resources, and weaknesses in flood management, as well as recognizing errors in forecasting and the factors exacerbating flood damage. Various scenarios are then implemented, analyzed, and compared with actual flood performance.

Operational scenarios

Engineering approach was employed to reconstruct and assess five distinct operational scenarios under identical hydrological conditions. This scenario-based framework enables the isolation of both operational decisions and structural configurations (i.e., inclusion or exclusion of Mashoureh Reservoir), providing a robust basis for performance comparison. The five scenarios are described as follows:

Scenario 1 – Historical Operation: Simulates the actual recorded operations as implemented during the April 2019 flood event, based on historical inflow and release data.

Scenario 2 – SOP with Observed Inflows: Applies the Standard Operating Policy (SOP) using actual observed inflows to simulate a rule-based operation.

Scenario 3 – SOP with Forecasted Inflows: Implements the same SOP structure as Scenario 2 but driven by inflow forecasts, representing a forecast-informed rule-based approach.

Scenario 4 – Ideal Hindsight Optimization: Assumes perfect future knowledge (i.e., perfect foresight of inflows) to optimize reservoir releases retrospectively, serving as a performance benchmark.

Scenario 5 – Real-Time Forecast-Based Optimization: Integrates real-time inflow forecasts—monthly Support Vector Regression (SVR) and daily Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) models—into a rolling-horizon optimization framework using a Genetic Algorithm (GA).

Each of these scenarios is implemented for two system configurations:

A two-reservoir system comprising Seimareh and Karkheh reservoirs;

A two-reservoir system comprising Seimareh and Karkheh reservoirs;

A three-reservoir system that includes the proposed Mashoureh Reservoir in addition to the Seimareh and Karkheh reservoirs.

A three-reservoir system that includes the proposed Mashoureh Reservoir in addition to the Seimareh and Karkheh reservoirs.

By simulating all scenarios under the same flood inputs, the forensic approach facilitates a clear assessment of how different operational strategies and structural configurations influence flood control performance and downstream damage mitigation.

Performance indicators

Evaluating operational policies is necessary in reservoir management using optimization and simulation models, requiring performance indices to compare water resource conditions under different scenarios23. This study evaluates two-reservoir and three-reservoir systems using indices such as flood volume alleviation, peak discharge alleviation, peak discharge delay, and flood control readiness. Two main factors are considered in calculating the evaluation indices: (1) Flood occurrence period: This begins from the first observed rise in discharge in the reservoir inflow hydrograph or catchment. Two floods are analyzed: one from April 3 to April 6, 2019, and another from April 10 to April 13, 2019. The chosen flood period for comparison runs from April 3 to April 30, 2019. (2) The number of dams in each catchment: Inflows to downstream reservoirs are the sum of upstream dam outputs and catchment runoff, with the most downstream dam representing basin outflow and receiving special attention for operational performance. Case study

The framework developed in this study is applied to the April 2019 flood, which caused severe damage in the Karkheh Basin. Observed data and model simulations are used to reproduce hydrologic and operational conditions. The framework evaluates the trade-offs between structural expansion (adding Mashoureh reservoir) and improved real-time control. The Karkheh River Basin in southwestern Iran spans approximately 51,643 km2, with 55.5% comprising mountainous areas. Located between 46°06´–49°10´E and 30°58´–35°04´N, the basin includes major tributaries such as the Gamasiab, Qarasu, Seimareh, and Kashkan rivers. Results and discussion

The Karkheh River Basin’s cascade system comprises two existing reservoirs (Seimareh and Karkheh) and one proposed reservoir (Mashoureh), with Seimareh and Mashoureh reservoirsoperating in parallel and Karkheh in series. This study assesses flood management during the 2018–2019 water year under five operational scenarios, considering both the presence and absence of the Mashoureh reservoir. Scenarios 3 and 5 integrate inflow forecasts, showing that improved forecast accuracy allows Scenario 5 to closely match the ideal Scenario 4. Scenario outcomes were evaluated against 2019 operations using performance indicators and a forensic assessment of flood damages.

Forecast validation

As mentioned earlier, in Scenario 5, the genetic algorithm optimization was implemented with a rolling horizon that integrates long-term (SVR) and short-term (LSTM) inflow forecasts. This scenario allowed for dynamically updated decisions and demonstrated measurable improvements in flood mitigation. The SVR models achieved high correlations (> 0.85 for short-term forecasts) and NSE values above 0.60 for monthly forecasts; however, their accuracy decreased over longer horizons. In contrast, LSTM models outperformed SVR for daily inflows, with R2 values exceeding 0.90 for 1–7 day forecasts and lower RMSE values. They remained reliable for up to approximately 14 days, making them effective for real-time adjustments during the April 2019 flood event. Forecasting skill for the Karkheh reservoir was lower than that of the Seimareh and Mashoureh reservoirs due to more complex inflow sources, yet it remained sufficient to guide pre-release strategies and mitigate downstream flood impacts. Detailed performance metrics for both SVR and LSTM models at all three reservoirs and multiple lead times are presented in Appendix Tables A1–A8, including correlation, NSE, RMSE, and MAE values. Overall, SVR is suitable for long-term planning, whereas LSTM is more effective for short-term flood forecasting. Their complementary use within a regulatory optimization framework significantly enhances flood control efficiency and overall system resilience.

Analysis of operation in the two-reservoir system

Seimareh reservoir

Under Scenario 1 the Seimareh Reservoir increased its storage until early February 2019, followed by gradual drawdown ahead of major inflows. By early March, the level was 4.4 m below the spillway threshold (704.5 m). Outflows via the power plant reduced storage to near-minimum operating level (Figs. 5, 6). Prior to the April 3 flood, storage reached 694.61 m. Inadequate outflows post-flood limited flood-handling capacity, contributing to the April 11–12 event, when peak inflow reached 2359.86 m3/s. Despite bottom outlet operations and partial spillway use reservoir levels rose by 4.17 m (Figs. 7, 6). Support for Karkheh reservoir included power plant shutdown and spillway gate closure using stop logs. May saw gradual decline and power plant restart (May 13). Performance indices were 39% peak mitigation, 31% volume mitigation, and 32% readiness (Fig. 8). Scenarios 2 and 3 (SOP with observed/predicted flows) showed better peak mitigation (52%) but lower volume mitigation (23%). Readiness was comparable (32% and 31%), with slightly improved vulnerability but weaker reliability and resilience than Scenario 1. Scenario 4, with proactive drawdown starting in January, had superior results: 64% peak mitigation, 52% volume mitigation, and 56% readiness. All key performance metrics improved over Scenario 1. Scenario 5, optimized using forecasted inflows, began releases from December. It achieved 58% peak, 37% volume mitigation, and 37% readiness, and outperformed Scenario 1 across all performance metrics (Figs. 5, 7, 6 and 8).

Karkheh reservoir

Scenario 1, reflecting actual Karkheh Reservoir operations, involved delayed releases until March 2019. Storage increased over winter, with major inflows retained in February. Gradual releases began in March via hydropower (Fig. 9), but by early April, extreme inflows raised levels rapidly, forcing high spillway discharges (Figs. 10, 11 and 12). Power plant shutdown in mid-April shifted all releases to the spillway. By late May, storage rose to 222.23 m. Performance indices were: 62% peak flow reduction, 28% flood volume reduction, and 9% preparedness (Fig. 13). Scenarios 2 and 3 used earlier releases through the hydropower plant and Abbas Plain sluice, reducing March-end storage to 209.6 m and 210.8 m, respectively. Scenario 2 achieved better indices (66%/53%/27%), while Scenario 3 showed moderate improvement (62%/50%/25%) (Fig. 13). Scenario 4 applied early sluice releases from December, lowering storage to 191.5 m by February. With proactive management, it reached 221.38 m by May, yielding the highest performance: 90% peak reduction, 70% volume reduction, and 72% preparedness. Scenario 5 integrated SVR-based inflow forecasts, optimizing early releases. By March, storage was below the spillway threshold, avoiding uncontrolled spill. Analysis of operation in the three-reservoir system

The three-reservoir system comprises the Seimareh and Mashoureh reservoirs operating in parallel, with the Karkheh reservoir located downstream in series. This study assessed the system’s flood control performance during the 2018–2019 water year, comparing its operation with and without the proposed Mashoureh Reservoir. Five operational scenarios were evaluated: (1) actual historical performance, (2) standard operating policy (SOP) using observed inflows, (3) SOP with forecast-based inflows, (4) ideal operation, and (5) proposed optimized real-time operation.

Seimareh reservoir

Seimareh Reservoir’s operation corresponding to the three-reservoir configuration under Scenarios 1–3 matched that of the two-reservoir system; thus, only Scenarios 4 and 5 are analyzed for distinct management strategies.

Scenario 4: Early flood regulation began in February by lowering storage (Appendix B1). Between March 6 and April 14, water levels dropped below the power plant’s operational threshold, with releases continuing via the bottom outlet (Appendix B3). Pre-releases prior to April 3 expanded flood storage capacity (Appendix B2). As shown in Fig. 14, this strategy achieved 61% peak flow reduction, 52% flood volume reduction, and a 57% control preparedness index. Compared to Scenario 1, storage capacity and all performance indices (reliability, resiliency, and vulnerability) improved markedly.

Comparison of radar chart of evaluation indices for the operation scenarios of the Seimareh Reservoir (three-reservoir system).

Scenario 5: Forecast-driven optimization initiated gradual reservoir drawdown in December, with maximum energy generation used during April 12–30 to manage flood peaks. In May, operations were coordinated with downstream Karkheh Reservoir (Appendix B3). As shown in Fig. 14, peak flow and volume reductions were 59% and 39%, with 38% control preparedness. While flood storage improved compared to Scenario 1, performance gains were less than under Scenario 4. Reliability remained unchanged, though resiliency and vulnerability showed improvement.

Mashoureh reservoir

As Mashoureh Reservoir was absent during the historical flood (Scenario 1) its flood management role was assessed under Scenarios 2–5. Scenario 2: Without forecast-based control, discharges exceeded the safe threshold during April 12–14 (Fig. 15) and (Appendix B4). Despite continuous power generation (Figs. 16) and (Appendix B5), peak discharge reduction was 0%, flood volume reduction 31%, and control readiness 30%. Reliability reached 90%, but resiliency and vulnerability were weak in Fig. 16 (0.33% and 299%, respectively). Scenario 3: Similar to Scenario 2 in operations and outcomes, with 0% peak reduction, 30% volume reduction, and 29% readiness. Reliability remained 90%, while vulnerability slightly increased to 305% (Fig. 16). Scenario 4: Optimized operations maintained safe discharge levels from December to September, achieving peak efficiency from February to May. Performance metrics improved significantly: 90% peak reduction, 71% volume reduction, and 62% control readiness. Reliability and resiliency were 100%, with zero vulnerability (Fig. 15, 16). Scenario 5: Using long- and short-term forecasts, performance further improved—93% peak reduction, 72% volume reduction, and 62% readiness. Reliability, resiliency, and vulnerability all reached optimal values (100%, 0%, 0%) (Fig. 16).

The forensic assessment also identified atmospheric and climatic contributors to the 2019 flood. A significant atmospheric river, "Dena," originating from the Atlantic, transported moisture over 9,000 km to the Zagros Mountains, where it precipitated. This event marked a sharp shift from a prolonged drought (2005–2018) to extreme wet conditions31. Additionally, a convergence of a warm Sudanese front and a cold Mediterranean front formed a bipolar system over the Zagros Mountains, intensifying rainfall and inflows, particularly downstream of the Mashoureh Reservoir32.

Summary of results

This section presents and interprets the results of the forensic evaluation across five operational scenarios applied to the April 2019 flood event in the Seimareh–Karkheh–Mashoureh system. The reservoir inflow and outflow behavior, peak attenuation, timing of releases, storage dynamics, and the resulting downstream damage are analyzed for each scenario. Both system configurations—without and with Mashoureh Reservoir—are evaluated to isolate structural versus operational effects.

Scenario 1 – Historical Operation

Scenario 1 – Historical Operation

Historical operations during the April 2019 flood show limited pre-release activity and reactive control at both Seimareh and Karkheh reservoirs. Peak outflow from Karkheh reservoir reached over 2500 m3/s, causing substantial downstream flooding. Storage capacities were not optimally utilized, with Seimareh operating being near full before peak inflow, reducing its buffering capability. No operational coordination existed between upstream and downstream reservoirs. This scenario serves as the baseline for comparison.

Scenario 2 – SOP with Observed Inflows

Scenario 2 – SOP with Observed Inflows

Applying the extended SOP rule based on actual observed inflows resulted in moderate improvement. Seimareh released controlled volumes earlier than in Scenario 1, leading to reduced peak at Karkheh (~ 18% damage reduction without Mashoureh; ~ 34% with Mashoureh). However, lack of forecast anticipation limited further gains. The SOP rule performance was sensitive to coefficient calibration, and pre-flood storage conditions constrained its effectiveness.

Scenario 3 – SOP with Forecasted Inflows

Scenario 3 – SOP with Forecasted Inflows

Using inflow forecasts (SVR and LSTM) within the SOP rule provided earlier release opportunities. Although forecasts had uncertainty the proactive release timing improved performance. Peak release at Karkheh was reduced by ~ 8% (without Mashoureh) and ~ 37% (with Mashoureh). Interestingly, despite better anticipation, the damage reduction in the two-reservoir system was lower than Scenario 2 due to forecast errors and delayed peak arrivals.

Scenario 4 – Ideal Operation

Scenario 4 – Ideal Operation

This scenario assumes perfect foresight and full controllability. The GA optimizer minimizes maximum downstream release by coordinating all three reservoirs. Peak discharge at Karkheh was reduced by 59% (without Mashoureh) and fully eliminated (100% reduction) with Mashoureh. This scenario represents the theoretical upper bound of performance, showing how operational synergy and anticipatory coordination can significantly mitigate flood damages.

Scenario 5 – Real-Time Forecast-Based Optimization

Scenario 5 – Real-Time Forecast-Based Optimization

Scenario 5 employs real-time rolling optimization using updated SVR + LSTM forecasts in a GA framework. It balances feasibility and performance. Peak release was reduced by 39% (without Mashoureh) and 55% (with Mashoureh). The dynamic adaptation allowed for effective use of Seimareh’s capacity, although limitations at Karkheh (gate delays, outlet bottlenecks) constrained full mitigation.

Cross-Scenario Performance Summary

Cross-Scenario Performance Summary

- The addition of Mashoureh improved performance across all scenarios but was most effective when coupled with proactive operation.

- Forecast-based scenarios (3 and 5) outperformed static rule-based operation (Scenario 2) when forecast skill was accurate.

- Scenario 4 demonstrated that full coordination and timing are crucial—structural expansion alone (Mashoureh) cannot compensate for poor operation.

- Peak inflow at Karkheh could not be fully absorbed without dynamic early release at Seimareh, underscoring the need for forecast-guided policies.

Forensic insights and policy implications

This section synthesizes the cross-scenario results to answer the three research questions posed in the Introduction.

Q1 – How did actual operations perform in comparison to optimized or ideal alternatives during the 2019 flood? Historical operation (Scenario 1) led to high peak discharge due to delayed responses and lack of pre-release. Ideal operations (Scenario 4) reduced downstream damage by up to 100% (with Mashoureh Reservoir), showing that much of the observed impact could have been prevented through coordinated operation. Even real-time optimization (Scenario 5) yielded a 39–55% reduction, underscoring the operational potential.

Q2 – Can forecast-informed, real-time control significantly reduce flood damages in the absence of new structural storage? Yes. Scenario 5 demonstrated that combining inflow forecasts (SVR, LSTM) with rolling optimization could reduce flood damage by 39% without Mashoureh Reservoir. This suggests that improved operational capacity and forecast integration are cost-effective alternatives to large-scale infrastructure, particularly when floods bypass upstream reservoirs.

Q3 – What is the marginal benefit of incorporating the Mashoureh Reservoir compared to enhancing existing operations? The benefit of Mashoureh was conditional. Although it improved damage reduction across scenarios its effectiveness was limited by spatial rainfall distribution: 71% of the total flood volume entered the system from the Kashkan tributary—downstream of Mashoureh Reservoir. This indicates that structural expansion must consider spatial hydrology and cannot substitute for strategic real-time operation.

Conclusion

This study presented an integrated forensic engineering framework to evaluate and optimize flood management strategies in a cascade reservoir system, with a specific application to the Seimareh–Karkheh–Mashoureh system in Iran’s Karkheh River Basin. The framework incorporated dual-scale inflow forecasts—monthly SVR and daily LSTM models—into a rolling horizon Genetic Algorithm (GA) optimizer, allowing for real-time decision-making under extreme flood conditions. Five operational scenarios were examined, including actual historical operation, standard policy with observed and forecasted data, ideal operation, and real-time forecast-informed optimization. The results demonstrated that forecast-based optimization (Scenarios 4 and 5) significantly improved flood mitigation compared to historical operations. Without Mashoureh Reservoir, damage reductions relative to Scenario 1 were 18%, 8%, 59%, and 39% for Scenarios 2 through 5, respectively. When the Mashoureh Reservoir was included these improvements increased to 34%, 37%, 100%, and 55%, respectively. However, the marginal contribution of the Mashoureh Reservoir to downstream protection was limited due to the spatial concentration of rainfall (71%) downstream of the proposed dam in the Kashkan Basin, resulting in only 16% additional damage reduction at the Karkheh Dam. These findings lead to three key conclusions:

1. Forecast-informed, real-time reservoir operation offers a cost-effective and adaptive alternative to structural expansions, particularly under climate-driven hydrological variability.

2. Forensic scenario analysis provides valuable diagnostic insight into operational weaknesses and missed opportunities during extreme events, which is essential for improving system resilience.

3. The spatial distribution of rainfall must be a critical consideration in flood infrastructure planning, as location-specific hydrological dynamics can undermine the effectiveness of upstream interventions.

Overall, this research advances the field of flood risk management by combining machine learning, optimization, and forensic engineering in a novel operational framework. The approach provides both retrospective evaluation and forward-looking strategies for enhancing flood resilience in multi-reservoir systems and encompasses broader implications and insights such as:

- Forecast Integration: Inflows predicted using machine learning provided meaningful operational guidance, though forecast errors in Scenario 3 highlight the need for probabilistic or ensemble-based approaches.

- Outlet Constraints Matter: Karkheh Reservoir’s limited release capacity bottlenecked system performance. Gate operability is as critical as storage volume.

- Forensic Framework Value: By simulating both real and counterfactual scenarios, the forensic approach provides decision-makers with clear evidence of what operational strategies would have minimized losses.

These insights support a paradigm shift from purely structural flood management toward hybrid frameworks integrating real-time data, predictive analytics, and adaptive decision-making.

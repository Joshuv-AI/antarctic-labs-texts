Blockchain-based IoT integration for VAT photopolymerization additive manufacturing process security and transparency
Authors: Dhal A. Matoc, Bhavesh Kanabar, Amit Sata, Minal Shukla, Slaheddine Jarboui
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The interaction of blockchain technologies with additive manufacture (AM) processes enabled by IoT, specifically VAT Photopolymerization (VPP), offers innovative means for increased security, transparency, and traceability in industrial production data. The VPP-IoT system discussed in the present study is based on sensor data for monitoring and mitigating VOC emissions. To ensure secure flow and management of sensor data in this system, we implemented a tamper-proof and transparent access system for stakeholders based on blockchain-decentralized and immutable technology. Hyperledger Fabric was used to set up a private blockchain network that worked with IoT sensors to keep track of VOC emissions in real time. Smart contracts were designed to automate data validation, storage, and alert generation processes to maintain compliance with environmental and safety regulations. The blockchain-based system demonstrated high-performance metrics, such as an average transaction throughput of 487.2 tps and data integrity verification success of 99.99%. The results point toward blockchain-IoT integration’s potential to improve regulatory compliance, predictive maintenance, and real-time monitoring in VPP additive manufacturing for safer and more sustainable industrial practices. While experimental results established a strong positive correlation between VOC emissions and key printing parameters, such as exposure duration and light intensity, layer thickness showed little effect. This study has broader implications as it promotes the application of Industry 4.0 concepts in a more practical context for VPP additive manufacturing.

Introduction

Additive manufacturing (AM) has become a defining technology of Industry 4.0 by changing how complex, detailed, and accurate parts are made. These parts are used in many industries that need custom products, like biomedical, aerospace, and consumer goods. VPP, which includes techniques such as stereolithography, DLP, etc., works on the concept of curing photopolymer resins layer-wise using UV light directly from digital models to produce parts with exceptional dimensional accuracy and surface finish1,2. Despite many benefits that the VPP process brings to the table, a significant challenge related to the emission of volatile organic compounds during the process remains not widely discussed. These emissions are harmful, and they compromise workplace air quality for operators.

One of the innovative solutions that surfaced in recent literature is the adoption of real-time monitoring and mitigation strategies achieved by integrating IoT sensors into VPP to ensure safe and sustainable manufacturing practices3–7. However, these integrated systems open the possibilities for new opportunities, as well as challenges that require immediate attention. On one hand, VOC detection and air quality monitoring during VPP deliver process optimization, effective control, and reduced harmful emissions. On the other, the integrated IoT devices generate immense amounts of data, raising concerns about data security, integrity, and transparency.

This brings us to the central concern this research revolves around: the need for robust mechanisms that could guarantee the security of data transmission from point to point within VPP-IoT-enabled systems. Sensor data that varies from VOC measurements, resin temperature, etc., must be stored and managed safely with a high level of reliability and traceability. Without such features in place, the integrity of this data can lose dependability, which may lead to inadequate verification procedures, non-conformity to regulation, and potentially unprecedented dangers. Specifically, compromised VOC data could negatively influence the selection of ventilation measures, potentially exposing the worker environment to hazardous conditions8,9.

Hence, blockchain technology is the key to properly addressing the current challenges. With the immutable and decentralized framework, blockchain has evolved into a strong tool implemented across various sectors, including healthcare, smart energy systems, and supply chain management, going beyond how it was originally perceived as a foundation for digital currency10. Within the context of AM, blockchain exists as a distributed ledger that manages stored sensor data while ensuring security, traceability, and transparency. In other words, when blockchain is implemented in VPP-IoT systems, it will be possible for manufacturers to embrace higher data integrity so that the stakeholders can be provided with quality data that can be relied on11,12.

There are well-documented studies in literature regarding blockchain in AM13,14. Many of the topics focused on architecture15, supply chain management16,17 , enhancing security, and transparency18,19,20. Others recognized the benefits of blockchain in safeguarding sensitive digital design assets and intellectual property rights. Through its encrypted structure, blockchain ensures that records of design remain tamper-proof and accessible only by authorized personnel. Besides immutable record storing, blockchain decentralized architecture permits peer-to-peer collaboration among manufacturers, thereby facilitating secure exchange of manufacturing resources. Such frameworks are largely utilized in aerospace and automotive sectors15,21.

A recurring study also reported that auditable materials, process parameters, and component records can be documented in blockchain in a way that each manufacturing procedure is traced. This traceability supports product tracking and reinforces quality assurance, building trust among stakeholders and minimizing the risk of substandard parts. A relevant example can be found in the biomedical industry, where blockchain verifies that materials used to fabricate implants meet the requirements prescribed and standards.

Moreover, other common themes include the automation of different aspects of the AM workflow using smart contracts, according to the literature review conducted by Ferreira et al. in 202422 and Kumar et al. in 202323, with supplemental studies by Westphal et al. in 202324. Smart contracts are self-executing agreements stored on the blockchain to automate processes and transactions25. Their utility is helpful in cases where task execution and control conditions are predefined, so the blockchain performs interventions without regular human access and permission. Beyond automation, blockchain also offers lifecycle management of digital twins’ systems operations and enhances their reliability and traceability26.

Despite the significant advances in the implementation of blockchain technology in AM across domains, comprising supply chain, intellectual property protection, digital part management, and integration within digital twins’ frameworks. These studies remain conceptual or simulation-based, and they overlook the critical need for real-time, reliable monitoring of workspace environmental condition data within AM operations. Specifically, no experimental investigation to date has demonstrated the capability of a blockchain-based IoT system to track and validate VOC emissions in VPP AM, aiming to enhance operational safety and data transparency.

The present study addresses the gap by equipping a VPP-IoT-based system with a private Hyperledger Fabric network (PBFT) for secure and transparent real-time VOC monitoring. Using smart contract validation, AES-256 encryption, and automated alerts, the proposed framework seeks to deliver an environmentally accountable data management infrastructure for VPP AM.

A multidisciplinary approach combining experimental studies with IoT-enabled VPP systems, blockchain development, and data analytics is adopted in this paper. The setup consists of IoT sensors enabling VOC emission tracking, resin temperature and environmental condition monitoring, and a blockchain-based secure data recording and sharing platform. The framework continues to investigate the use of smart contracts in automating data validation and ensuring that set rules are followed.

The novelty of this research lies in its experimental realization of blockchain IoT-based integration within a VPP environment. Unlike most previous works that propose conceptual or simulated architectures, this study demonstrates a fully implemented Hyperledger Fabric network with PBFT consensus connected to IoT sensors for real-time VOC emission monitoring. The framework introduces a smart contract validation and alert system that enforces environmental and safety compliance autonomously. Furthermore, the quantitative benchmarking of blockchain performance establishes an empirical foundation for scalable, sustainable, and regulation-aligned adoption of blockchain-enabled VPP-AM.

Nevertheless, while numerous opportunities exist to implement blockchain in VPP, several challenges and areas of controversy need to be resolved to achieve breakthroughs. Blockchain’s suitability and efficiency as a technology for widespread industrial applications is a significant challenge. Many blockchain platforms rely on consensus mechanisms, which can be computationally expensive and reduce the transactions per second; thus, building a blockchain system with the capability to address these obstacles within the context of VPP AM is essential27,28. Additionally, challenges arise from the compatibility of VPP infrastructure with the different software applications and hardware instruments to be incorporated during the integration process. This issue often leads to the emergence of complexities in how blockchain-based solutions are implemented and managed since there are often no well-defined protocols to follow and because blockchain platforms are constantly undergoing development22,29.

This study is critical because the benefits are likely to extend beyond the laboratory and be realized in the transformation of monitoring and control of manufacturing processes in the context of AM. The insights gained in this research may contribute to the development of industry-wide best practices for standardizing blockchain integration into AM technologies. This research attempts to contribute to the progress of smart manufacturing and the realization of Industry 4.0 by addressing these challenges while ensuring safety and sustainability for AM processes13,14. Being an evolving field, the findings of this research are valuable for informing future innovations and shaping the future of industrial manufacturing.

Methods

This section provides a description of how blockchain can be applied to the IoT-VPP AM System to enhance data security, trust, and traceability. The methodology is designed based on an independent research study with pilot studies that correlates the blockchain technology with the IoT sensors in the regulation of VOC emissions in the VPP process. The methodology consists of several important elements: the key activities of the project will consist of the experimental setup, blockchain architecture design, data collection, cleanup, integrating blockchain into the system, and data analysis.

Experimental setup

The experimental setup has been made to provide an approach towards a VPP-AM setup in which a significant focus is being given to monitoring the VOC emissions, along with other paramount process parameters, during the experiments. The components of the setup included the following.

VPP-AM machine

The VPP-AM machine, which is a high-resolution Anycubic Photon Mono X 6 K with a 9.25-inch monochrome exposure screen, was used to conduct experiments. The light source has a 40-LED parallel array, and the highest printing speed is 80 mm/h. The light intensity (5,000 to 6,500 uW/cm²) was adjusted, and the spatial resolution in the machine (34 u²) gave the freedom to systematically investigate the effects of process parameters on VOC emissions.

Sensor array

A set of IoT sensors was connected to the experimental device to measure VOC emissions, resin temperature, and environmental conditions. The sensors included (Figs. 1 and 2):

I.VOC Sensor: A Sparkfun SGP30 Air Quality Sensor Breakout (Qwiic) was used to measure and determine VOC emissions throughout printing. The sensor gives out an index value of the concentration of VOCs in the air surrounding it. The sensor reports the readings of total VOCs (TVOC) in parts per billion (ppb), carbon dioxide (CO₂) equivalent in parts per million (ppm), and H₂.II.Temperature Sensor: The GY-906 MLX90614ESF-DCI Sensor Modules were used to measure the temperature of the resin vat. The temperature of resin is one of the most important factors influencing VOC emissions, as it influences the viscosity of the resin and its efficiency to cure.III.Environmental Sensor: A DHT22 temperature-humidity sensor received the temperature and humidity of the surroundings within the printing case. These are the levels that affect the rates of resin evaporation and the dynamics of VOC emission.

All the fabrication tests were undertaken in closed chambers at 23 ± 1 °C and 50 ± 2% RH. Randomization of print orders and resin-exposure combinations between experimental runs was used to eliminate systematic bias. Each trial was preceded by a 2-hour baseline, which was used to establish VOC balance and ambient stability. The sensor values have been recorded at a rate of 1 Hz, and they have been averaged with a three-sample moving average filter to reduce short-term filter changes.

Control unit

The minicomputer used as a central data acquisition and processing control unit was Raspberry Pi 4. The sensors communicated both through their PCI connector and I2C communication to the board, where they provided real-time data at set times. The data obtained was recorded and put aside to be analyzed later.

The accuracy of each sensor was ensured by calibrating it with laboratory reference instruments before data were collected. The photoionization detector to calibrate the SGP30 VOC sensor was also pre-calibrated, and a thermally calibrated thermocouple (at least 0.2 °C) was used to verify the temperature module in the GY-906 DCI sensor. The preprocessing script in Raspberry Pi 4 used calibration coefficients automatically.

Resin material

We used a commercially produced grey photopolymer resin in all our experiments to ensure that we have similarity in the material properties. The reason why we selected it is that it is compatible with the Anycubic Photon Mono X 6 K and is commonly applied in industries.

Safety and privacy protocols

All procedures conducted during the experiments adhered to institutional safety regulations and OSHA allowable exposures to VOC. The experimental condition was to be under inspection of forced ventilation (12 ACH air exchange rate), and personnel must be equipped with proper equilibrium personal protection.

Regarding the data privacy aspect, the blockchain-IoT system can store process-related sensor data only. None of the personal identifiable information (PII) or data of workers is collected, logged, and transferred. The private Hyperledger Fabric network uses membership service providers to control access based on roles, allowing only certain authorized organizational nodes (like machine operators, quality control personnel, and compliance officers) to view ledger data based on what they need for their jobs.

Blockchain architecture design

The blockchain architecture was developed to ensure secure and transparent data management in the VPP system that is enabled for IoT. It consists of three main layers (Fig. 3): the data acquisition, blockchain, and application layers.

Data acquisition layer

The data acquisition layer consists of IoT sensors collecting real-time data on VOC emissions, resin temperature, and environmental conditions. The gathered data is preprocessed and then recorded on the Raspberry Pi 4’s blockchain.

Blockchain layer

The purpose of this layer of the blockchain is to store and use the information obtained with the help of the IoT sensors in a safe way. The Hyperledger Fabric framework was recently adopted to create a private blockchain network due to its scalability and modular optimization, which allows it to support smart contracts30. The blockchain network will include the following:

I.Nodes: Each node within the network may represent a stakeholder in an AM process, e.g., a machine operator, a quality control team, or a regulatory authority. These contracts are automated and used in data validation, storage, and retrieval. Importantly, the contracts also impose preconditions, such as sensor calibration and data integrity checks, prior to significantly committing any records to the immutable ledger.III.Consensus Mechanism: This involved the employment of the Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance consensus algorithm to make sure that the nodes would be compliant with the state of the ledge31. PBFT has been selected since it is suitable in a private blockchain setting in cases where the trust of parties is comparatively low32.

The Hyperledger Fabric architecture was set up with Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT) consensus among three validating peers (one ordering node and two endorsing peers). Each block was formed after every 500 ms with a maximum of 50 transactions in each block. Although the full IoT load (4 sensor nodes transmitting 1 Hz) was applied to this network, the network was able to sustain an average throughput of 487.2 tps with an average block finalization latency of 1.18 s. The early-stage throughput value, which ranged from 430 to 450 tps, was associated with the pre-optimization tests conducted prior to changes in the batch timeout and endorsement policy.

Application layer

The application layer provides a user interface for accessing and interacting with data stored on the blockchain. A dashboard has been developed using React.js, enabling users to view emissions, temperature, and humidity in real time. Alerts are shown on the dashboard when VOC levels are above a permissible limit so that operators can act in time.

Data acquisition and preprocessing

Data is preprocessed before inserting into the blockchain to ensure both correctness and uniformity. The Raspberry Pi 4 aggregates sensor data and performs preliminary validation, including calibration verification and outlier detection. This preprocessed data is subsequently formatted into a JSON structure standardized for storage in the blockchain.

Data encryption

All data were encrypted with AES-256 encryption and were sent to the blockchain network to enhance the protection of data. Encryption ensures that the user cannot access the transmitted information without the proper decryption code.

To guarantee safe passage of information, AES-256 encryption was utilized with a dynamically selected session key that was generated in a key-generation procedure using SHA-256. The code of the encryption key was stored in the local secure storage of Raspberry Pi to be refreshed every now and then to eliminate replay/compromise. The sensor payloads were encrypted and hashed with SHA-256. To help limit access to schedules and timestamps of the ledger, Hyperledger Fabric Chaincode added access control through vaults. The sensor data streams were forwarded to off-chain storage, and the blockchain provided immutable reference to the information in the form of hash-based data on the ledger. This solution ensured the integrity of data through blockchain technology and ledger control.

Blockchain integration

The integration of blockchain technology with the IoT-enabled VPP system involved several key steps:

Blockchain data storage

The encrypted data are transferred into the blockchain network, after which the blockchain records them in the distributed ledger. The records are matched with a time stamp, and each one connects to the previous record, creating an indestructible chain of records. The blockchain aspect implies that once the data is stored, the process of altering or modifying it is impossible; as a result, the data integrity level is high.

The introduction of latency and energy overhead caused by the encryption pipeline and blockchain storage pipeline was parameterized and systematically quantified as to their practical feasibility of operation in real time. Also, the Raspberry Pi 4 control unit was profiled with the Keysight N6705C DC Power Analyzer at constant power during continuous operation and measured the data overhead due to metadata in the blockchain by comparing the size of the analog sensor payload with the final payment size.

Smart contract execution

Smart contracts play a critical role in automating data validation and storage. For example, our VOC validation contract verifies that sensor readings fall within predefined safety thresholds before blockchain commitment. If validation fails, the contract generates an alert and suspends data recording until the issue is resolved. Upon validation failure, the smart contract triggers an alert and suspends data recording until the anomaly is resolved (Fig. 5).

Data analysis

The implementation of blockchain technology has enabled a secure and transparent record of all experimental data, increasing the efficiency of the data analysis process. The analytical techniques used are listed below:

Correlation analysis

The relationship between VOC emissions and the printing parameters was found to have strength and direction, which was assessed by the Pearson correlation coefficient between the variables VOC emissions and printing parameters: layer thickness, exposure time, and light intensity. The blockchain’s storage of information may guarantee the presentation of the analysis on valid and indisputable records. We also conducted regression analysis to examine the quantitative relationship between VOC emissions and individual process parameters, as well as to analyze how variations in each independent variable affected VOC emissions. Furthermore, analysis of variance (ANOVA Type-II) was used to determine whether each of the parameters had any significant effect on the variance in VOC. These two approaches provide us with the measure of how big the effect is (with the help of regression coefficients) and whether it is significant (with the help of p-values). This makes the explanation of the impact on VOC emissions more stringent.

Time-series analysis

Time series analysis revealed trends and patterns in VOC emissions during the printing process. The blockchain immutable ledger has provided a reliable historical record, allowing for accurate analysis and prediction of trends in VOC levels.

Smart contract-based alerts

Smart contracts automated the detection of VOCs. If categories of VOC emissions exceeded predefined thresholds, the smart contract would trigger an alert on a web-based dashboard to prompt operators to take corrective actions.

Comparative baseline testing

To validate blockchain’s quantifiable impact, we established comparative baselines using MySQL 8.0 for data integrity testing (20 simulated tampering attempts) and manual operator monitoring for alert response time measurements (10 threshold violation events).

VOC emission analysis

Correlation analysis between VOC emissions and printing parameters

We measured correlations between the emissions of VOCs and printing parameters in terms of Pearson correlation coefficients. The duration of exposure had the strongest positive relationship with the VOC emissions (r = 0.89), with light intensity (r = 0.67) coming in the next position and layer thickness (r = 0.12) showing no relationship. There is a weak correlation between the VOC (ppb) and the layer thickness, whereas correlation between exposure duration and VOC (ppb) is stronger.

We produced scatter diagrams on associations between printing variables and VOC concentrations. Figure 6 illustrates the substantial variation of VOC (ppb) among the various layers due to their different thickness, yet the variation is not linear as indicated by the low value of the correlation coefficient. This outcome of the relationship is the absence of an apparent linear pattern between the two variables discussed above that causes relatively low values of their correlation.

Besides the correlation results, regression and ANOVA were performed to quantify the main impacts of process parameters on VOC emissions. A strong linear relationship was observed between VOC (ppb) and time of exposure (β₁ = 566.25, 95% CI [346.77, 785.73], R² = 0.5304, p < 0.001), followed by light intensity (β₁ = 78.42, 95% CI [35.18, 121.66], R² = 0.4267, p = 0.002). A stronger and statistically not significant relationship was also observed with layer thickness (β₁ = 15.21, 95% CI [− 0.35, 30.77], R² = 0.1395, p = 0.0549). Time-series analysis of VOC emissions

Displaying a time-series analysis of VOC emissions achieved during VPP-AM has indicated several distinctive patterns and trends that can serve as a clue to the dynamics of VOC emissions. In this case, we show the temporal variation in VOC emissions with the help of the findings of a single experiment (Fig. 10).

VOC (ppb) spiked quickly in the VPP process; in a few minutes the levels had a higher value in the region of 2900 ppb. The spike was experienced under the subsequent parameters of the process: exposure time of 2 s, light intensity at 100%, and a layer thickness of 50 m. This sudden rise in VOC may be due to the curing of the bottom layer, which has a longer exposure period compared to the normal exposure period. After the initial surge, VOC levels stabilized, indicating that the initial layers play a significant role in VOC emissions. While printing, it was noticeable that the VOC (ppb) was increasing slowly and nearly in a linear fashion following the initial stabilization in the graph. This difference would indicate that VOCs are emitted during printing, but at a slower rate compared to the initial burst. As a result of this spike, the levels of VOCs would gradually grow during the printing procedure, smaller than initially. The progressively higher VOC could be attributed to the accrued nature of a continuous polymerization reaction and cured resin over a specified period.

Discussion

The suggested blockchain-IoT combination introduced in the article refers to the different fields of application of current blockchain-AM studies on supply chain traceability (Alkhader et al., 2020), IP protection (Chan et al., 2023), and digital twins (Mandolla et al., 2019). They are integrated at the enterprise level; we have blockchain at the process control layer to measure real-time VOC emissions and environmental data during fabrication.

It produces important performance discrepancies. The delivered performance of Hyperledger Fabric, at 487.2 transactions per second (tps), is 16 to 32 times greater than that of Ethereum frameworks, which operate at 15 to 30 tps, and offers sub-second latency for real-time monitoring as reported by Kumar et al. (2023). However, we sacrifice radical decentralization to achieve deterministic finality suitable for regulated consortia. With 3.2% false and 96.8% alert accuracy with 127 alerts, our smart contracts showed operational maturity beyond conceptual frameworks that do not have empirical measures (Westphal et al., 2023).

The innovation application is the environmental monitoring focus (VOC tracking to monitor the health of workers). Excellent relationships between the duration of the exposure and the volume of VOC emissions (r = 0.87) and light intensity (r = 0.78), confirmed by irrevocable records, confirm that the process of reducing the emission needs the credibility of provenance, which supplements existing frameworks with the assurance of environmental compliance data.

The operational value of blockchain in comparison with its architectural novelty is seen in the comparative validations (Table 9). Organizations benefit from immutability during regulatory audits, as evidenced by a 9.6 times greater tampering detection rate for perfect systems compared to a rate of 13.0 for centralized systems. Additionally, the advantages of automation, which include a 99.3 times faster alert response, surpass those of human-dependent workflows in safety-critical environments.

The power usage of the private Hyperledger Fabric estimated network was 0.003–0.006 kWh per 1,000 requests, which was less than 0.15 kWh in all experiments. Conversely, monitoring enabled using blockchain demonstrated 18.25% VOC emission reductions, which shows that the low energy footprint of blockchain can be compensated by significant environmental benefits.

Easy scaling with the assistance of additional VPP printers is also supported by the architecture since the Hyperledger Fabric network can include new peers through standardized MQTT interfaces. Smart contracts ensure that all devices have data validation that is the same, and private channels and endorsement policies allow a safe interoperability system between a range of machines or facilities within industries.

There are essential constraints that should be investigated. The four-node PBFT network implemented (f = 1) offers only partial resilience; with production at 7–13 nodes, consensus latency is O(n²), or about 178.4 ms, with the PBFT network’s (n 2) complexity. Energy use (78 kWh/45 days, 2.4 overhead) is proportional to nodes. Scalability has not been tested beyond single-machine monitoring (487.2 tps), and it is theorized to support 80–160 machines with on-chain storage, although it reached a performance of 2.3 GB/45 days, which requires off-chain solutions (IPFS). Limited generalizability: This system can only utilize a restricted number of resins on a single machine, and it operates under specific parameters. VCs may vary between different systems and chemistries.

Possible future research directions are as follows: (1) federated architecture balancing transparency and confidentiality of multi-organization networks; (2) blockchain-authenticated data integration alongside ML-based predictive VOC control; (3) extension to other parameters (viscosity, quality metrics) to create omnipresent digital signatures; (4) discussion of energy-conscious blockchain applications, including proof-of-stake and DAG-based applications, to ensure scalability and lessen the operational footprint. New EU rules create compliance incentives for audible environmental records, serving as a distinct motivation separate from counterfeiting. Powder bed fusion and extrusion, in turn, have implications for industry, which may make blockchain a standard of safety-critical AM.

Conclusion

This study has expressed the possibility of integrating blockchain technology with IoT-enabled VPP-AM systems to address some of the most formidable problems in the current generation in terms of producing secure, transparent, and traceable data.

We took advantage of the decentralized and unchangeable structure of blockchain and realized the secure control of important process parameters. Our system is precise in monitoring and recording the VOC emissions, the temperature of the resin, and the environment during the VPP manufacturing process. Smart contracts and Hyperledger Fabric enabled the real-time validation of data, automatization of alerts, and adherence to environmental standards, a solid solution in the context of industrial manufacturing. The experimental evidence revealed that the significant positive correlation between VOC emissions and the key printing settings, exposure time, and light intensity was noted, whereas the layer thickness did not have a significant impact. This information is beneficial for streamlining VPP processes, leading to lower harmful emissions while maintaining print quality. The IoT-based system demonstrated strong high-performance indicators, including a transaction throughput of 487.2 tps and a data integrity success rate of 99.99%, thereby showcasing its stability and scalability for the industrial sector.

The research has more implications for future AM and Industry 4.0. Having control over data and its clear visibility leads to confidence among the stakeholders, the application of regulatory rules, and the improvement of safer and more sustainable production through the proposed framework. The proposed system will be scaled to incorporate other crucial parameters in future work, like the viscosity and efficiency of resin cures. These could be improved in a future study, and alternative studies conducted on energy-efficient consensus mechanisms that may augment blockchain-based solutions more on sustainability. The knowledge acquired throughout the course of this research will be one of the preconditions of the widespread introduction of the blockchain-IoT integration of AM to smart manufacturing and Industry 4.0.
